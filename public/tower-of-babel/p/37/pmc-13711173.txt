Postoperative root size predicts regurgitation in supravalvular aortic stenosis repair
Authors: Dominic P Recco, Lauren Marshall, Matthew Schildmeier, Kimberlee Gauvreau, Joseph Confessore, Luis G Quinonez, Pedro J del Nido, Christopher W Baird, Peter E Hammer, David M Hoganson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSupravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) repair carries a moderate risk for postoperative aortic regurgitation (AR). This may be related to the targeted sinotubular junction diameter z score (STJDZ) at reconstruction, traditionally sized to zero. We investigated relationships between achieved STJDZ and aortic valve function after repair.MethodsSixty-six children underwent patch repair from 2010 to 2024 at a median age of 1.2 years (interquartile range [IQR], 0.4, 3.5 years) via Brom (n = 53), Doty (n = 9), and McGoon (n = 4) techniques. Relationships between STJDZ and aortic valve function were assessed via Jonckheere-Terpstra nonparametric test for trend over median follow-up of 5.6 years (IQR, 2.4, 8.4 years).ResultsPreoperatively, AR was none/trivial in 52 (79%), left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) moderate or greater in 54 (82%), and median STJDZ −3.8 (IQR, −4.4, −2.9). At discharge, AR and LVOTO were mild or greater in 45 (68%) and 18 (27%), and median STJDZ was −0.3 (IQR, −1.3, −1.1). STJDZ increased by a median of 3.0 (IQR, 1.6, 4.4) preoperatively to postoperatively. Discharge AR was none/trivial at median STJDZ −1.3 (IQR, −1.8, −0.1) and worsened with increasing diameters (mild: 0.4 [IQR, −1.0, 1.8], mild+: 0.0 [IQR, −1.2, 1.5]; P = .038), with similar results at follow-up (P = .048). LVOTO worsened with lower median STJDZ (none/trivial: 0.2 [IQR, −1.2, 1.5], mild: −1.3 [IQR, −3.2, −0.1]; P = .015). Subanalysis of those with none/trivial preoperative AR (n = 52) showed that increase in STJDZ by ≥4 demonstrated worse follow-up AR (P = .016).ConclusionsAchieving STJDZ zero using patch repair techniques to relieve SVAS obstruction demonstrated mild or greater postoperative AR compared with none/trivial regurgitation at median z score −1.3. Mild LVOTO was noted with median z score −1.3. Targeting STJDZ of −1 in pediatric SVAS repair appears appropriate to balance residual regurgitation and LVOTO. Further follow-up is required to understand the long-term impact of this target on aortic growth.Key Words: cardiovascular surgery, congenital heart disease, congenital cardiac surgery, supravalvular aortic stenosis, aortic regurgitation

Regurgitation by discharge STJ diameter z score after supravalvular aortic stenosis repair.

Central Message

Targeting a sinotubular junction diameter z score of −1 instead of zero in pediatric supravalvular aortic stenosis repair demonstrates less postoperative aortic regurgitation and LVOTO.

Perspective

Rates of aortic regurgitation (AR) after supravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) patch repair remain moderately high. Traditionally, STJ diameter z score (STJDZ) is reconstructed to zero. Herein, achieving STJDZ zero demonstrated ≥mild postoperative AR. None/trivial AR and mild LVOTO occurred with median z score −1.3. Targeting STJDZ −1 in pediatric SVAS repair is appropriate to balance AR and LVOTO.

Supravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) is a rare form of left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) and accounts for 8% to 14% of congenital aortic stenosis in children.1 SVAS represents a heterogenous group of patients, including those with Williams-Beuren syndrome, non-Williams arteriopathies, and sporadic cases. SVAS is further classified by extent of involvement of the aorta: type I indicates discrete narrowing typically located at the sinotubular junction (STJ) and type II indicates diffuse obstruction of the ascending aorta and arch.2 Concomitant anomalies are common in more severe SVAS, with patients also presenting with multilevel left-sided obstruction, aortic arch hypoplasia, bicuspid aortic valve (AoV), pulmonary artery stenosis, and right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) obstruction. Although there is no exact definition, SVAS is usually described by a peak supravalvular velocity ≥2 m/s and/or STJ or ascending aorta diameter z score less than −2.0.3 Surgical approaches aim to enlarge the STJ and aortic root and repair any additional stenoses. There are 3 augmentation techniques most commonly used: (1) McGoon (single-patch, single-sinus, nonsymmetric), (2) Doty (pantaloon-patch, multisinus, nonsymmetric), and (3) Brom (3-patch, multisinus, symmetric).4,5 However, there is debate over the preferred surgical technique. Several studies have demonstrated comparable mortality and reintervention rates between the 3 patch techniques,6, 7, 8, 9, 10 whereas others have shown superior outcomes with multisinus repairs.11, 12, 13, 14, 15

Regardless of technique, excessive enlargement of the STJ/aortic root can result in malcoaptation of the AoV leaflets, and iatrogenic regurgitation can occur.16 It was observed that several patients developed new and substantial central aortic regurgitation (AR) with targeted enlargement of the sinuses, and it was postulated that these patients may have overenlargement of the STJ with creation of imbalance of the native leaflet size and the new STJ size. On a preliminary review of children undergoing SVAS repair at our institution from 2010 to 2024, we noted that only 20% of patients had mild or greater AR preoperatively, whereas roughly 70% had mild or greater AR at discharge (Figure 1, A and Online Data Supplement). Although there are multiple publications on SVAS repair, few report postoperative AoV function, and even fewer comment on less-than-severe AR. Despite misconceptions of low rates of significant AR after SVAS repair, between 30% and 100% of patients are left with mild or greater regurgitation postoperatively.2,10,13, 14, 15,17, 18, 19, 20 Furthermore, there remains moderate risk for LVOT- and AoV-related reintervention: 12% to 42% at 10 years.6,7,9, 10, 11, 12,16,18,21, 22, 23, 24, 25 The authors postulated that this risk may be partially attributable to the targeted STJ diameter at initial repair, traditionally sized to z-score zero. We hypothesized that underdeveloped native AoV leaflets in SVAS are inadequate for reconstruction to a normal target, resulting in poor coaptation, AR, and subsequent reintervention. Thus, the primary aim of this study was to evaluate the relationship between postoperative achieved STJ diameter and AoV function in children undergoing patch repair for SVAS. Understanding these relationships will provide the surgeon with measurable intraoperative targets for the reconstructed anatomy to reduce the risk of AR and future reintervention.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

The study was approved by Boston Children's Hospital IRB-P00046610 (approved January 20, 2025) with the need for patient consent waived. A single-center, retrospective review was conducted of all pediatric patients (≤21 years old) undergoing SVAS repair with patch plasty between January 2010 and December 2024. During this time frame, 84 patients met initial queried inclusion. Patients who underwent tissue resection without patching (n = 7, 8.3%), sliding aortoplasty (n = 2, 2.4%), previous SVAS repair (n = 2, 2.4%), and other (n = 3, 3.6%) were excluded. In addition, 4 (4.8%) patients with preoperative AR moderate or greater were excluded because of the overall aim of evaluating worsening AR in the setting of relatively normal preoperative AoV function. In addition, these 4 patients underwent concurrent AoV repair to address their regurgitation, dissimilar to the included cohort. A total of 66 patients with normal preoperative AoV function who underwent SVAS repair with patch plasty in the study time frame were included.

Outcome Measures

Data collected from medical records included patient demographics, preoperative echocardiogram measurements, previous procedures, operative details, and discharge and follow-up clinical and echocardiogram findings. The primary aim was to define relationships between postoperative aortic root diameters and AoV function and/or reintervention after SVAS repair. Secondary outcomes included intensive care unit and hospital length of stay, all-cause mortality, and need for reintervention.

Echocardiographic Measurements

Echocardiograms were reviewed to evaluate AoV function and proximal aortic dimensions preoperatively and postoperatively. A single sonographer in the cardiology department (M.S.) performed all measurements. Aortic diameter was recorded at 4 sites: AoV annulus, aortic sinus, STJ, and ascending aorta. Inner lumen diameters were taken at all locations. Of note, inadequate acoustic windows at preoperative and discharge imaging did not allow for all patients to have every measurement at both echocardiographic time points. Online Data Supplement outlines the number of data points at the aortic location for each acquired echocardiogram. Aortic measurement z scores were calculated on the basis of body surface area (BSA) using institutional regression models.26 Additional data included degrees of AR and LVOTO.

Mortality and Reintervention

Mortality was recorded at discharge and latest follow-up. Reintervention was defined as surgical reoperation or transcatheter procedure performed on the RVOT/pulmonary valve/pulmonary artery, LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta, or aortic arch during the study period after the index operation. For patients who underwent multiple reinterventions in the postoperative period, only the initial procedure was considered for analysis. Time to first reintervention was recorded.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics, operative details, and postoperative outcomes were summarized using frequencies and percentages if the variable was categorical, or median and interquartile range (25th and 75th percentiles) if continuous, with the number of nonmissing values indicated. Freedom from reintervention was estimated by Kaplan-Meier analysis, with 95% CIs reported. The Jonckheere-Terpstra nonparametric test for trend was used to compare median STJ diameter z scores (STJDZ) across increasing categories of AR and LVOTO and reintervention (Online Data Supplement). Analyses were performed with SAS statistical software (version 15.3; SAS Institute) or Stata (version 17; StataCorp).

Results

Baseline Characteristics and Preoperative Echocardiographic Measurements

For the overall cohort, 57% (n = 38) of patients were male, and most (n = 56) had tricuspid AoV morphology. Williams-Beuren syndrome was diagnosed in 32 (48%), and an additional 15 (23%) had other non-Williams elastin arteriopathy. Right-sided and left-sided (not including SVAS) obstruction was present in 46 (70%) with 32 (48%) patients undergoing preoperative cardiovascular intervention. Furthermore, 10 (15%) had evidence of renal artery stenosis, signifying severe diffuse disease. Further details regarding baseline characteristics are listed in Table 1. On preoperative echocardiogram, STJ diameter had the smallest z score (median −3.8; interquartile range, −4.4, −2.9), followed by the ascending aorta (−1.5 [−2.2, −0.5]), annulus (−0.8 [−1.4, +0.2]), and sinus (−0.5 [−1.4, +0.4]) (Figure 2, A). Most patients (n = 52, 79%) had none/trivial AR, but 14 (21%) had mild. Fifty-three (75%) patients had moderate or greater LVOTO (Figure 1, A and C). Operative Details

A total of 66 patients underwent SVAS repair with patch augmentation at median age 1.2 years [0.4, 3.5 years] and BSA 0.44 m2 [0.33, 0.61 m2]. SVAS repair was performed via Brom in 53 (80%), Doty in 9 (14%), or McGoon in 4 (6%). Most common patch material was glutaraldehyde-treated autologous pericardium (n = 43, 65%). Secondary procedures included patch augmentation of the pulmonary artery in 26 (39%), coronary in 22 (33%), and arch in 20 (30%). Additional operative details are listed in Table 1.

Postoperative Outcomes and Echocardiographic Measurements

Postoperative intensive care unit and hospital length of stay were median 4 days [2, 10 days] and 8 days [5, 18 days]. There were no early (<30 days from surgical date) deaths, and 1 patient underwent early reintervention for right pulmonary artery balloon dilation and stenting. Follow-up after hospital discharge was available for 65 (98%) patients. Median follow-up was 5.6 years [2.4, 8.4 years]. There were 2 (3%) late deaths. Thirty-two (48%) patients underwent late reinterventions that occurred at a median 297 days [119, 1253 days] after SVAS repair (Table 1). Overall freedom from reintervention was 45% (95% CI, 29%-59%) at 8 years postoperatively (Figure 3). Freedom from RVOT/pulmonary valve/pulmonary artery, LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta, and arch reintervention at 8 years postoperative were 61% (44%-73%), 82% (67%-90%), and 81% (68%-89%).

On discharge echocardiogram, median aortic root diameters z scores were all close to zero, the annulus at −0.3 [−1.2, +0.5], sinus +0.6 [−0.5, +1.6], STJ −0.3 [−1.3, +1.1], and ascending aorta −0.2 [−1.6, +0.9] (Figure 2, A). The STJDZ increased most from preoperative to postoperative, +3.0 [+1.6, +4.4], followed by the ascending aorta, +0.8 [−0.3, +2.4]. Most patients (n = 35, 53%) had mild AR, with 21 (32%) none/trivial and 10 (16%) moderate or greater. Sixty-four (97%) patients had mild or less LVOTO (Figure 1, A and C). Median time to latest echocardiogram was 3.7 years [1.0, 7.4 years]. At latest follow-up, one half of patients (n = 33, 50%) had none/trivial AR, whereas 25 (38%) had mild and 8 (12%) moderate or greater. Fifty-eight (88%) patients had mild or less LVOTO (Figure 1, A and C). See Online Data Supplement for all postoperative echocardiogram measurements.

Comparisons Between Discharge STJDZ and AR, LVOTO, and Reintervention

Comparisons between STJDZ at discharge and outcomes of interest are shown in Figure 4 and Online Data Supplement. Discharge AR was none/trivial at STJDZ −1.3 [−1.8, −0.1] and worsened with increasing diameters (mild: +0.4 [−1.0, +1.8], mild+: 0.0 [−1.2, +1.5]; P = .038), with similar results noted at follow-up (P = .048). LVOTO at discharge worsened with lower STJDZ (none/trivial: +0.2 [−1.2, +1.5], mild: −1.3 [−3.2, −0.1]; P = .015), although this was not statistically significant at follow-up (P = .16). Increase in STJDZ from preoperative to postoperative did not demonstrate a trend with degree of AR or LVOTO at discharge or follow-up (P > .05 each). Furthermore, neither STJDZ at discharge (P = .069) nor increase in STJDZ (P = .22) demonstrated a trend with LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta reintervention (Online Data Supplement).

Subanalysis of Patients With None/Trivial Preoperative AR

A subanalysis was performed on a subset of the initial cohort to evaluate whether patients with completely normal preoperative AoV function demonstrated different trends compared with those reported previously in patients with mild or less preoperative AR. Fifty-two patients with none/trivial preoperative AR were included. Baseline characteristics and preoperative echocardiogram measurements were grossly similar to the total cohort (Table 1, Figure 1, B and D, Figure 2, B, and Online Data Supplement), as were operative details and postoperative outcomes. Of note, when we compared the none/trivial (n = 52) and mild (n = 14) preoperative AR subcohorts, patients with none/trivial AR had greater rates of Williams-Beuren (58% vs 14%, P = .006), preoperative right-sided obstruction (77% vs 43%, P = .022), and concurrent arch augmentation (38% vs 0%, P = .016) and lower rates of previous LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta (8% vs 64%, P < .001) and aortic arch (2% vs 29%, P = .006) intervention (Table 1). These findings suggest that patients with preoperative none/trivial AR more often had type II SVAS and/or those with mild AR underwent interventions before SVAS repair to relieve associated obstructive processes, which may have influenced preoperative valve function.

Overall freedom from reintervention was 51% at 8 years postoperative (Figure 5). On discharge echocardiogram, median aortic root diameters z scores were all close to zero (Figure 2, B, and Online Data Supplement) with the STJDZ increasing most from preoperative to postoperative. Most patients (28, 54%) had mild AR, with 18 (35%) none/trivial and 6 (12%) moderate or greater. Fifty (96%) patients had mild or less LVOTO (Figure 1, B and D, and Online Data Supplement). Similar findings were noted at latest follow-up echocardiogram, except with more patients having none/trivial AR compared with mild.

Discussion

We have identified postoperative STJDZ and change in STJDZ from preoperative to postoperative as modifiable risk factors for AR after review of midterm outcomes for patients who underwent patch plasty for SVAS.

Surgical Technique

Our cohort was composed of patients with mild or less preoperative AR who underwent surgical repair at slightly younger and smaller size than those in previous reports.8, 9, 10,12,18,22,25,27 SVAS repair via patch plasty was performed using a multisinus repair technique in basically all cases, with autologous pericardium and pulmonary homograft being the most common materials. Our institution favors multisinus techniques to recreate a more symmetrical surgical result because of the previously described associations with lower incidence of residual obstruction, prevalence of moderate AR, and occurrence of reintervention at follow-up.13, 14, 15,19,27

Mortality and Reintervention

Early and late mortality and early reintervention were extremely low. At follow-up of 5.6 years, almost 50% underwent reintervention, with 35% of patients requiring a procedure on the RVOT/pulmonary valve/pulmonary artery and roughly 20% on the LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta and/or arch. The overall freedom from reintervention was 45% at 8 years postoperative, with the LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta and arch faring similar at ∼80% after SVAS repair. These short- and midterm outcomes are consistent with previous reports.6,7,9, 10, 11, 12,16,18,21, 22, 23, 24, 25 In fact, in the 5 reports with more than 100 patients (2 single-center, 1 multicenter, and 2 systematic reviews), 10-year survival rate ranged from 91% to 98% and 10-year freedom from reintervention from 83% to 91%,6,9,10,27,28 although the cumulative incidence of reintervention is as high as 20% to 40% in a majority of single-center series.7,12,14, 15, 16,18,21, 22, 23,25 Herein, neither STJDZ at discharge nor increase in STJDZ demonstrated trends with LVOT/AoV/ascending aorta reintervention. Because the primary aim of this study was to investigate a specific relationship with STJDZ, we did not evaluate other variables as predictors of reintervention. Several previous studies have identified concurrent AoV disease, diffuse SVAS, early era, co-existing pulmonary artery stenosis, augmentation technique, subvalvular stenosis, age <1 year at the time of surgery, William syndrome, and concomitant RVOT surgical repair as risk factors for late reintervention.7,9, 10, 11, 12, 13,15,18,22,24,25

AR: Previous Literature

Few clinical studies on SVAS repair report postoperative AoV function and even fewer comment on moderate or less AR. Despite misconceptions of low rates of significant AR after SVAS repair, between 30% and 100% of patients are left with mild or greater regurgitation postoperatively.2,10,13, 14, 15,17, 18, 19, 20 We found only 4 studies in the last 25 years that evaluated risk factors for postoperative AR and reintervention after SVAS repair, with most focusing on surgical technique. Stamm and colleagues15 found that patients with multiple-sinus reconstructions resulted in fewer reoperations and late deaths, lower residual gradients, and decreased prevalence of moderate AR at follow-up. Metton and colleagues14 demonstrated that the Brom repair was associated with a lower incidence of residual obstruction and moderate AR, and Deo and colleagues7 noted that diffuse SVAS was a risk factor for mortality and late reoperation, along with high postoperative gradient and significant AoV disease. Most recently, Lv and colleagues,27 in a systematic review and meta-analysis of 27 studies including 1162 patients, showed that a 3-patch method had a lower rate of AR compared with single-patch and 2-patch methods.

AR: Current Study

Most patients’ aortic roots were restored to normal dimensions, with the STJ diameter having the largest increase, as expected. In this study, basically all patients had mild or less LVOTO at discharge but, importantly, one half of patients developed worse AR, with 79% none/trivial preoperative down to 32% postoperative. This led to our hypothesis that underdeveloped native AoV leaflets in SVAS are inadequate for reconstruction to a normal target STJ diameter, resulting in poor coaptation, central AR, and subsequent reintervention. We found that discharge AR worsened with increasing STJDZ (none/trivial: −1.3, mild or mild+: >0), with similar results noted at follow-up. Surprisingly, at discharge, median STJZD for the mild category (+0.4) was slightly greater than the mild+ category (+0.0). This may be the result of modest sample size, with the mild+ group consisting of 6 patients. We suspect that sequentially larger diameters translate to more regurgitation, as demonstrated in the subgroup analysis (none/trivial: −1.3, mild: 0.4, mild+: 1.5), but a definitive conclusion may require a larger cohort to ultimately prove this trend. LVOTO at discharge worsened with lower STJDZ (none/trivial: 0.2, mild: −1.3), demonstrating the balance that needs to be struck. In a subset of patients with none/trivial AR preoperatively, increase in STJDZ by ≥4 from preoperative to postoperative demonstrated worse discharge and follow-up AR.

Clinical Implications

This is a newly reported relationship between achieved STJ diameter and postoperative AR after SVAS repair. Understanding these relationships provides the surgeon with measurable intraoperative targets for the reconstructed anatomy to reduce the risk of AR. Achieving a median STJDZ zero demonstrated mild or greater AR at discharge and follow-up, whereas patients with a median z score −1.3 showed none/trivial regurgitation. In contrast, mild LVOTO was noted with median STJDZ −1.3. Therefore, targeting an STJ diameter of z score −1 in pediatric SVAS repair appears appropriate to balance residual regurgitation and LVOTO. In this context, a patient-specific, patch-planning workflow is currently under development at our institution using preoperative 3-dimensional modeling and virtual surgery to accurately predict patch dimensions required to achieve target aortic size during SVAS repair with patch plasty. The platform incorporates mechanical properties of both the native aorta and patch material and accounts for material uptake at the suture line. In the meantime, a simplified method entailing basic mathematical equations with inputs of preoperative and target postoperative STJ diameter, number of desired patches, and estimated material stretch and suture uptake has been implemented to calculate the intended patch size(s). For example, for a 1.2-year-old toddler with BSA 0.44 m2 and preoperative STJ diameter 7.1 mm (STJDZ −3.8) (a representative patient of median age and size for this cohort), the traditional z-score zero diameter target is 11.4 mm, a difference of 13.5 mm in circumference. Accounting for roughly 1.5 mm of material loss at each suture line (2 per patch) and 5% stretch of autologous pericardium,29 performing a Brom technique would require 3 patches approximately 7.3 mm wide at the STJ. If targeting a z score of −1 (diameter 10.2 mm), 3 patches approximately 6.1 mm wide would be used instead, a difference of 4 mm of total patch material. We expect that a preoperative patch-planning strategy to assist surgeons intraoperatively will ultimately provide patients with the best-possible long-term repair outcome.

Limitations

This study is limited by its retrospective nature in a single-center institution. In addition, SVAS is a rare disease, with 4 to 5 cases performed each year at our institution. We elected to analyze all augmentation techniques due to modest study power and our hypothesis that postoperative AR after SVAS repair is related to STJDZ, not necessarily the number of patches used. However, asymmetric root enlargement techniques (ie, Doty and McGoon) may independently contribute to malcoaptation, which was not evaluated in this study. Lastly, there was incomplete data collected for some of the echocardiogram measurements as the result of poor acoustic windows and/or insufficient imaging of target anatomy, which may have impacted our outcomes.

Conclusions

Achieving STJDZ zero using patch repair techniques to relieve SVAS obstruction demonstrated mild or greater postoperative AR, compared with none/trivial regurgitation at median z score −1.3. Mild LVOTO was noted with median z score −1.3. Targeting STJDZ of −1 in pediatric SVAS repair appears appropriate to balance residual regurgitation and LVOTO. Further follow-up is required to understand long-term impacts of this target on aortic growth.

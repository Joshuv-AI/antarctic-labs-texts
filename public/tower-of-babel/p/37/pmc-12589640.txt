Bi-objective operation optimization of regional integrated energy system considering shared energy storage
Authors: Xiaobang Li, Liying Zhu, Liying Zhao, Kang Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the development of the sharing economy, the concept of “sharing” has begun to penetrate into the power grid, and companies have begun to explore service models for shared energy storage. Based on this, this article studies the optimization technology of regional integrated energy system (RIES) operation considering shared energy storage, which is conducive to promoting China’s energy strategy transformation, improving energy utilization efficiency and energy system flexibility. Firstly, the RIES fundamental structure integrating shared energy storage is constructed; Secondly, operational optimization objectives are established for both the RIES and the energy storage aggregator (ESA), aiming to minimize their respective operating costs. The constraints of the operational optimization model are analyzed; Based on the traditional sparrow search algorithm, the chaos sparrow search algorithm (COSSA) is constructed by combining Tent chaos and Gaussian mutation. Last, an illustrative case study demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed model, showcasing a reduction in RIES operating costs of 2.912536 million dollars enabled by shared energy storage participation. As a result, our method facilitates a more efficient operation of RIES.Keywords: Regional integrated energy system, Shared energy storage, Operational optimization, Operating costSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Solar energy, Wind energy

Introductions

The high investment cost of energy storage represents a significant barrier to its commercial development and widespread adoption in regional integrated energy system (RIES). Coordinating storage equipment operation through energy storage aggregator (ESA) enhances energy utilization while reducing costs, enabling optimized RIES operation1. In this context, this study aims to investigate regional comprehensive energy operation optimization based on shared energy storage technology. This methodology reduces user investment costs while enhancing renewable energy output stability through shared energy storage, critically advancing the "30·60" dual-carbon goal and accelerating modernized power system development.

RIES primarily resolves operational issues by establishing optimization objectives and models. These frameworks enable coordinated scheduling of distributed devices processing diverse energy vectors, including electricity, thermal energy, cooling energy, and natural gas2. Many studies have focued on energy coupling and distributed equipment models in the RIES operational optimization. Ref.3 employed scenario analysis to model renewable energy output stochasticity in RIES and optimized the operation of energy conversion and storage devices. This method not only reduced operational costs but also improved the energy utilization efficiency of the system. Ref.4 established a collaborative framework and dispatch model integrating power-to-gas conversion, carbon capture systems, distributed generation, and combined heat and power units. This integration configuration significantly enhanced renewable energy utilization rate and system economic benefits. Refs.5,6 primarily addressed uncertainty factors in equipment coupling and renewable energy generation output. These studies developed a comprehensive operations optimization model for integrated energy systems, enabling interval-based optimal control of unit outputs and consequently enhancing system operational economy in field tests. Refs.7,8 developed a standardized subdivision modeling framework for integrated energy systems based on energy hubs. This approach achieved granular representation of complex multi-energy flow structures during internal transmission processes and derived optimal energy supply configurations through global optimization. Refs.9,10 proposed a bus-based architecture for the combined cooling, heating, and power (CCHP) system, implementing device-level independent modeling to achieve flexible system configurability and generalized modeling framework.

Existing research on energy storage configurations mainly focuses on independent electricity users or distributed energy system users, but the high investment cost of energy storage is the main obstacle to its commercial development. Scholars have proposed the concept of shared energy storage based on the concept of a sharing economy and have conducted relevant research. Ref.11 utilized real-time electricity pricing to optimize the operational strategies across shared energy storage dispatch, user demand shifting, and grid scheduling, achieving a reduction in end-user energy expenditure and a decrease in regional power grid operational expenditures. Refs.12,13 addressed the challenges of significant uncertainty in photovoltaic (PV) output, substantial curtailment of solar power, and high costs associated with energy storage configurations. This was accomplished by developing an operational optimization model for community PV-energy storage systems that utilize shared energy storage, ultimately leading to a reduction in users’ energy costs. Ref.14 focused on community-integrated energy systems to optimize the user economics. It developed a collaborative user optimization model incorporating shared energy storage and PV equipment. Furthermore, the study explored the economic benefits of these systems under a shared energy storage operation mode, thereby verifying the effectiveness of this approach. Focusing on energy sharing, Refs.15,16 improved the economic efficiency and energy utilization of electric vehicles in building clusters through collaborative optimization and scheduling of independent charging stations and energy storage systems.

In summary, there is a lack of research on the use of the “sharing economy” concept to integrate distributed energy storage resources and optimize their operation in RIES. Motivated by the aforementioned work, therefore, this study adopts the role of “ESA” to provide energy storage scheduling and leasing functions to further reduce the annual operating cost of RIES.

The RIES basic architecture considering shared energy storage

Building upon the sharing economy paradigm, multi-regional integrated energy systems leverage the scale advantages of centralized energy storage to enable cross-system on-demand access to storage resources. This shared storage mechanism optimizes resource utilization efficiency while significantly enhancing operating economics through coordinated operations17. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the RIES integrates equipment across three functional layers: The source layer incorporates the power grid, PV systems, wind turbines (WT), and natural gas network; the conversion layer comprises heat pumps (HP), electric refrigerations (ER), electric boilers (EB), and gas boilers (GB); while the storage layer includes energy storage batteries, ice storage tanks (IST), and heat storage tanks (HST). To boost operational flexibility and cost-effectiveness, most RIESs incorporate energy storage systems. This study therefore focuses on storage-equipped RIESs, highlighting how ESA integrates distributed storage resources into the broader energy network through leasing mechanisms. This approach eliminates the need for costly large-scale storage power stations while enhancing storage utilization efficiency. Functioning as a third-party agent, ESA consolidates energy storage investors—both small and large—and represents them in electricity market activities.

Basic structure of RIES including shared energy storage.

The operational paradigms of energy storage systems manifest as two complementary dimensions: centralized storage stations and distributed decentralized facilities. Where centralized systems provide bulk storage capacity at grid level, distributed systems deliver localized storage solutions at end-user sites, forming an integrated architecture across spatial scales. The introduction of the ESA operational platform at the regulatory layer reconceptualizes distributed energy storage. This paradigm shifts the defining criterion from individual unit capacity to functional relativity within the integrated ESA framework, establishing system-wide operational context as the primary determinant. Within the ESA regulatory framework, any storage device constitutes distributed energy storage when functioning as an independent regulatory entity—agnostic to physical scale. These entities, designated as energy storage units, perform resource integration through aggregation processes: the systematic consolidation of distributed assets under platform governance.

Improving sparrow search algorithm

To mitigate premature convergence of the SSA near global optima, this work proposes the COSSA. The framework integrates three key innovations: (1) Tent chaotic initialization enhances population diversity through ergodic coverage of solution space, countering SSA’s local optima susceptibility; (2) Gaussian mutation operators refine local search precision for multi-parameter coupling in RIES; (3) Adaptive chaotic perturbation reactivates stagnant individuals by disrupting local basin attraction. Mode settings and modeling assumptions

This study investigates the impact of energy storage scheduling modes on RIES operation and validates the effectiveness of an optimization model incorporating shared energy storage. The proposed scheme serves as the baseline mode (Mode 2), while the RIES optimization scheme without shared energy storage functions as the comparative mode (Mode 1). Both schemes undergo simulation analyses for RIES operational optimization.

Mode 1 (M1): Energy storage capacities remain exclusively dedicated to respective RIESs. Each system operates in grid-connected mode under these principles: (i) Grid electricity procurement during generation deficits; (ii) Surplus energy storage in dedicated facilities; (iii) Objective: operational cost minimization.

Mode 2 (M2): RIES energy storage resources are leased to an ESA. ESA responds to these resources through “centralized dispatching + distributed response” and formulates a global charging and discharging plan: (i) Minimizes RIES operating costs; (ii) Enhances ESA economic benefits via coordinated dispatch.

During the simulation process, the following assumptions must be adhered to: (1) Ignore the physical loss differences of energy storage devices, assuming compatibility through standardized interfaces; (2) Control the load forecasting error within ± 5%; (3) TOU pricing for regional power grids remains stable during the optimization cycle.

Energy storage operation analysis

In independent operation mode M1, each RIES autonomously meets load demands exclusively through internal resources and configured multi-energy coupling equipment. Conversely, shared storage mode M2 enables interconnected RIESs to exchange electricity via an ESA, significantly enhancing supply flexibility. Figure 8 comparatively demonstrates the energy storage operations under both operating modes.

Energy storage operation results of REIS under different modes.

Figure 8 reveals distinct energy storage strategies between operation modes. In M1, RIES1-3 storage batteries maintain consistent cycling: charging during grid valley periods (00:00–08:00), discharging during peak pricing intervals (08:00–12:00 & 17:00–21:00), and remaining idle during normal pricing (12:00–17:00 & 21:00–23:00). Conversely, M2’s ESA-leased storage implements dynamic strategies: valley charging (00:00–08:00) and peak discharging (08:00–12:00 & 17:00–21:00) for grid arbitrage. Crucially during normal pricing (12:00–17:00), prioritized operations emerge: RIES1 storage cycles actively, RIES2 charges exclusively, while RIES3 discharges preferentially—all coordinated through ESA mediation.

Comparative analysis reveals that ESA-coordinated shared storage fundamentally transforms energy dispatch: storage strategies shift from autonomous load-driven operations (M1) to dynamic optimization incorporating regional grid pricing and cross-RIES load demands. This dual-objective optimization ensures ESA’s economic sustainability while simultaneously improving RIES operational economy and grid-stabilizing functions.

Algorithm validation

This study develops an optimization model for RIES incorporating shared energy storage, addressing multi-variable complex scenarios. Employing an enhanced SSA to refine the optimization process, we validate its efficacy through collaborative operation analysis of RIES1 under mode M2. Comparative simulations between baseline SSA and the improved algorithm (Fig. 10) demonstrate the latter’s superior convergence and stability.

Comparative analysis of different solving algorithms.

Figure 10a demonstrates that under 120-initial-population and 100-iteration conditions, the enhanced SSA initiates convergence at generation 44 for RIES1 collaborativ e optimization, whereas traditional SSA converges earlier at generation 36. Significantly, the enhanced SSA achieves a superior optimal solution (414.4568$) compared to traditional SSA (432.5523$), representing a 4.41% cost reduction. Figure 10b further verifies COSSA’s performance advantages: 15% accelerated convergence speed and 3.2% higher solution accuracy over particle swarm optimization (PSO). These findings collectively validate the enhanced SSA’s robust global search capability, effectively mitigating local optima risks while delivering precision-optimized outcomes.

Conclusion

In this study, a dual-objective model of RIES and ESA operation optimization is constructed from the perspectives of economic operations. On this basis, a tent chaos-Gaussian variational enhanced COSSA is developed for operational optimization, and independent vs. shared energy storage modes are established for each participation scenario. The results show that RIES1-3 exhibit cost reductions of 17.92%, 19.64%, and 19.78% respectively in M2 vs. M1, confirming the economic viability of the shared energy storage model. Unlike existing studies on RIES operation optimization, this study has the following novelties:

This paper integrates distributed energy storage resources to form ESA, and optimizes the scheduling of RIES, which is more adaptable to the characteristics of distributed energy scenarios in China.

ABSTRACT
AbstractIssue/problemAlthough schools and educational institutions are supposed to be safe learning and working environments the increase of safety incidents, especially physical, emotional, and sexual violence, bullying and harassment as well as disasters and injuries show that new understanding about safety culture is needed.Description of the problemSchool safety culture has not been the focus of assessment despite schools representing numerous organizations globally. Some researchers state that culture is a relatively stable construct that is shared amongst the group that operates within an organization and that culture has multiple dimensions. Also, culture is sometimes defined as a practical issue, as part of the organization’s functional operating culture ‘the way things are done here. Some researchers state that safety culture is largely unconscious, yet its manifestations are visible. However, educational organizations, such as primary, secondary and vocational schools as well as universities, schoolhouses and liberal education, cannot purposefully develop their safety unless they have knowledge of the level of their safety culture and its variation. Schools must find a systematic way to identify the risks and the near-miss cases as well as policy and acknowledgment advancement. The conference presentation will provide a holistic approach for assessing safety management and safety culture in educational institutions.ResultsThe presentation describes examples how actor-oriented safety plans can be drawn up in schools and early childhood education and care (ECEC) units. The model looks at the levels of safety culture from the perspective of four elements: management, prevention, social environment and competence assurance. For maturity models, typically, the model describes a safety culture through levels. In this model, levels are vulnerable, reactive, normative, proactive, and resilient. The model includes the anticipation of safety deviations, injury prevention and everyday practices.LessonsA systematically developed criteria framework for advancing a holistic safety culture contributes to the increased visibility of safety practices and facilitates the sustained development of safety culture among pupils, students, and educational staff.Key messages• Educational organizations cannot develop their safety unless they have knowledge of the level of their safety culture.• The comprehensive safety culture of schools can be assessed from the perspectives of management, prevention social environment and competence assurance.TopicSchool, assessment, safety culture.

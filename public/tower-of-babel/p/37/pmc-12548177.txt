Statistical analysis plan for TTVguideIT—a multicentre, patient- and assessor-blinded, non-inferiority, randomised and controlled phase II trial to compare standard and Torque Teno virus-guided immunosuppression in kidney transplant recipients in the first year after transplantation
Authors: Felix Herkner, Frederik Haupenthal, Sebastian Kapps, Konstantin Doberer, Harald Herkner, Franz König, Gregor Bond
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-interventional studies indicate an association between Torque Teno Virus (TTV) load in peripheral blood and immunosuppression related outcomes in kidney transplant recipients. This makes it a promising biomarker for guiding immunosuppression after kidney transplantation. The primary objective of the current trial is to demonstrate the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of TTV-guided immunosuppression.MethodsFor this purpose, a randomised, controlled, interventional, two-arm, non-inferiority, patient- and assessor-blinded, international multicentre investigator-driven phase II trial was designed. A total of 260 stable, low-immunological-risk adult recipients of a kidney graft with Tacrolimus-based immunosuppression and TTV infection after month 3 post-transplant are recruited in 13 academic centres in six European countries. Subjects are randomised in a 1:1 ratio stratified by centre (allocation concealment) to receive Tacrolimus either guided by TTV load or according to the local centre standard for 9 months. The primary composite endpoint includes the occurrence of infections, biopsy-proven allograft rejection, graft loss or death. Secondary outcomes include month 12 protocol biopsies, development of de novo donor-specific antibodies, health-related quality of life and drug adherence.DiscussionDetailed descriptions of the study design, planned statistical analysis and reporting of the results for the TTVguideIT trial are provided. This statistical analysis plan was submitted to the journal before the end of the trial and prior to data lock.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13063-025-09119-8.Keywords: Kidney transplantation, Torque Teno virus, Immunosuppression, Tacrolimus, Immunological monitoring, Personalised medicine, Infection, Graft rejection, Composite endpoints

Introduction

Background and rationale {Item 7 of SAP Checklist [1]

Scientific problem

Immunosuppression to reduce the risk of organ rejection is essential in post-operative treatment following kidney transplantation. However, existing immunosuppressive regimens insufficiently control rejection in the long run and have significant side effects like increased risk of infections [2]. Currently, monitoring of immunosuppression in clinical practice relies on measuring Tacrolimus (TAC) trough concentrations, the agent majorly in use today [2, 3]. TAC concentrations reflect toxicity rather than graft outcome [3, 4]. Torque Teno virus (TTV) is a non-pathogenic virus highly prevalent in immuno-compromised patients after transplantation and mirrors immune status [5]. The number of TTV copies in the peripheral blood is associated with both graft rejection and infections [5] making it a promising biomarker to guide the balance of adequate, but not excessive immunosuppression.

Objectives {8}

The primary goal is to demonstrate that the TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) is non-inferior with respect to safety compared to standard dosing (arm S) in stable adult kidney transplant recipients with low immunological risk in the first year after transplantation.

Non-inferiority hypothesis

The primary objective is to demonstrate the non-inferiority of TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) compared to standard dosing (arm S) in stable adult kidney transplant recipients with low immunological risk in the first year after transplantation.

Non-inferiority can be concluded if the upper limit of a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for the difference in proportion of the primary composite endpoint (Definition: Item 26a) at month 9 after randomisation (= Visit 7; post-transplant month 12) between the two treatment arms is less than 10% points.

Let pT and pS be the proportion (in %) of participants reaching the primary composite endpoint in the arm T and arm S, respectively, then the primary statistical hypothesis can be formulated as follows: H0: pT-pS ≥ 10% versus H1: pT-pS < 10%

Superiority hypothesis

If non-inferiority is reached, the study will demonstrate superiority of TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) compared to standard dosing (arm S) in the same endpoint by testing the hypothesis: H0: pT-pS ≥ 0 versus H1: pT-pS < 0.

A one-sided significance level alpha of 2.5% is used for non-inferiority and superiority testing of the primary endpoint.

Study methods

Trial design {9}

The TTVguideIT trial is a randomised, controlled, interventional, two-arm, non-inferiority, patient and assessor-blinded, multinational and investigator driven phase II trial. In month 4 post-transplant, subjects are randomised at an allocation ratio of 1:1 to receive TAC either guided by TTV load (arm T) or according to local centre standard (arm S) for 9 months. For more details about interventions, timeline and a graphical overview we refer to the publication of the study protocol, see Haupenthal et al. 2023 [6].

Randomisation {10}

Participants are allocated using random permuted blocks with variable block size, stratified by recruiting centres. Eligible patients are randomized 1:1 to either TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) or standard dosing (arm S). Electronic case report form (eCRF)-based randomization is used. Randomization is created via the program nQuery-Advisor®.

Sample size {11}

A total of 260 patients are included in the trial, 130 of them in the active group (arm T) and 130 in the control group (arm S). For the sample size calculation, we analysed the occurrence of the primary endpoint in recipients of a kidney allograft transplanted between 1 January 2012 and 31 December 2018 at the Medical University of Vienna, applying the trial-specific inclusion and exclusion criteria. Overall, 40% of patients experienced the primary endpoint between month 3 and 12 after transplantation (unpublished data). Therefore, we assume a composite rate of around 40% in the standard dosing (arm S). It is expected that TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) reduces the occurrence of the primary endpoint, and therefore rates between 20 and 30% are assumed to be reasonable. The non-inferiority margin has been fixed at 10% points. Thus, when the sample size in each group is 120, a two-group large-sample normal approximation test of proportions with a one-sided 2.5% significance level will have 90.42% power to reject the null hypothesis that arm T is inferior to standard arm S in favour of the alternative hypothesis arm T is not inferior to arm S. Due to the planned observation period of 9 months and the special patient population, a rather low drop-out rate is expected of about 2–5%. Adjusting for potential drop-outs, the sample size is fixed with 130 patients per group, i.e., 260 in total.

Framework {12}

The main goal of this study is to demonstrate that the TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) is non-inferior with respect to safety compared to standard dosing (arm S). Non-inferiority will be concluded if the upper limit of a two-sided 95%-confidence interval for the difference in proportion of patients at month 9 after randomization (= Visit 7; post-transplant month 12) between the two treatment arms is less than 10% points. If non-inferiority is reached, the study will demonstrate superiority of TTV-guided immunosuppression (arm T) compared to standard dosing (arm S) in the same endpoint. Superiority will be concluded if the upper limit of the 95%-confidence interval excludes 0 (if the difference is presented as T-S, see also hypotheses defined in item 8).

Please note that for secondary and exploratory endpoints no non-inferiority margins have been specified. Therefore, all secondary (except adverse events (AEs), serious adverse events (SAEs) and TAC trough level target changes, which will be only analysed descriptively, see Items 27a and b) endpoints will be tested for differences between Arm S and T and corresponding two-sided p-values and 95% confidence intervals are reported. These comparisons should not be considered as confirmatory as the multiple Type-I-error rate is not controlled.

Statistical principles

Statistical significance {16}

A one-sided significance level alpha of 2.5% is used for non-inferiority and superiority testing of the primary endpoint. For the analyses of all secondary endpoints two-sided p-values and two-sided 95%-confidence intervals will be reported (exceptions as in Item 12 above).

Multiplicity {17}

Hierarchical testing procedure

For the primary endpoint we will use a hierarchical testing procedure. This means we will first test the non-inferiority hypothesis at a one-sided alpha of 2.5%. After non-inferiority can be demonstrated, superiority will be tested also using a one-sided alpha of 2.5%.

Other endpoints

No adjustment for multiplicity. The secondary endpoints (e.g. including the components of the primary outcome) are needed to support the interpretation of the potential effects in the primary endpoint. The tests for these comparisons would require larger sample sizes to achieve the required power. This was the reason why a composite endpoint has been chosen and these analyses are considered supportive only without further multiplicity correction.

Confidence intervals {18}

Two-sided 95% confidence intervals for the primary and all secondary endpoints (exceptions see Item 12) will be calculated.

Definition of adherence {19a}

Medication adherence will be monitored prospectively by patient diary. Non-adherence is defined as failure of TAC intake more than once per week on average for twice-a-day and once every two weeks for once-a-day formulation. If missing entries of medical intake in the patient diary suggest non-adherence, the trial personnel has to verify if this is due to true non-adherence or due to inaccurate completion of the patient diary. Additional electronic drug monitoring using MEMS® Buttons (AARDEX® Group, Switzerland) on TAC blisters, the BAASIS® questionnaire, claimed prescriptions, psychological evaluation and TAC trough level variability will be accessed retrospectively.

Presentation of adherence {19b}

The number and percentage of patients fulfilling the above-stated definition of non-adherence according to patient diaries and investigator assessment of missing entries will be presented for arm S and T, respectively.

Description of protocol deviations {19d}

Deviations from the valid protocol are documented by the trial site, including a clear description and justification. Subject-related deviations are documented in the eCRF. Subject-independent deviations are documented on a separate form, which is stored in the investigator site file. Number of protocol deviations and discontinuations from the study drug and the study will be summarized by treatment arm and the types of deviations stated.

Analysis populations {20}

The primary analysis will be performed on a modified intention to treat principle. Additional analyses will be performed according to per-protocol analysis, and the dataset restricted to adherent patients.

Different analysis sets are defined as follows:

Modified intention to treat (mITT) set

This analysis set includes subjects who were randomized. According to the intent to treat principle, subjects will be analysed according to the treatment they have been assigned to during the randomization procedure. The mITT set includes all patients who are eligible for the study and with at least one TAC evaluation (and potential adaptation) during Visit 1.

Per protocol set

This analysis set comprises all subjects who received the study intervention and did not majorly violate the protocol in a way that might affect the evaluation of the effect on the primary objective. For examples of major protocol deviations see Item 19c and Hauptenhal et al. 2023 [6], e.g., deviations in relation to the inclusion or exclusion criteria, multiple consecutive examinations/visits that were missed, and repeated incorrect adjustment of the IMP according to TTV-load in the TTV guided group.

Adherent set

This analysis set comprises all subjects who received, and are deemed adherent to the study intervention according to the definition given in Item 19a.

Analysis methods

What analysis method will be used and how the treatment effects will be presented including adjustments for covariates {27a,b}

Statistical analysis of the primary endpoint

For the definition of the primary composite endpoint see item 26a.

The occurrence of the primary composite endpoint will be presented per treatment group as absolute numbers and percentages. The difference of proportions of the occurrence of the primary endpoint between the two treatment groups and a two-sided 95% confidence interval will be calculated. See also exemplary Table 4. For the primary analysis missing values will be considered as treatment failures in the composite primary endpoint.

Exemplary results table of the primary composite endpoint and main analysis

Non-inferiority will be concluded if the upper limit of a two-sided 95% confidence interval for the difference in proportion of patients reaching the composite primary endpoint by month 9 after randomization (= Visit 7; post-transplant month 12) between the two treatment arms is less than 10% points. Superiority will be concluded if the upper limit of the 95%-confidence interval excludes 0 (if the difference is presented as T-S, see also hypotheses defined in item 8).

As additional analysis confidence intervals will be calculated using Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel weights stratified by centre. Also logistic regression models will be using occurrence of the primary endpoint as dependent and treatment as independent variable adjusting for additional factors (such as sex and centre) and covariate (such as age at randomization). Odds ratios and corresponding two-sided 95% confidence intervals will be reported.

To explore the composite endpoint further, it will also be analysed as a time-to-event endpoint considering the time from randomization until time of first event. We will visualize these data by presenting Kaplan–Meier curves for each treatment arm. Furthermore, Cox-regression models will be performed adjusting for the same factors and covariates as used in the logistic regression models. Hazard ratios and corresponding two-sided 95% confidence intervals will be reported.

To assess the impact of the individual components of the composite endpoints, each component will be analysed descriptively (see description for secondary endpoints).

Statistical analysis of the secondary endpoints

General Overview

For binary secondary endpoints (such as “Rejection at month 12”) absolute (n = x) and frequencies in percentage (%) will be calculated per treatment group. 95%-confidence intervals will be calculated for rates, if appropriate. Such data will be visualized with bar charts. If appropriate, logistic regression models will be applied using treatment as an independent factor. The models will be adjusted for the same factors and covariates as outlined for the primary endpoint.

For time-to-event endpoints (such as “Overall survival”) Kaplan–Meier plots and in presence of competing risks cumulative incidence functions will be provided (if appropriate). The two groups will be compared with log-rank tests. Additionally Cox-Regression models will be performed adjusting for additional factors as described for the primary endpoint.

For the recurrent event “Infection” additionally methods for recurrent event data will be applied. This includes the Negative Binomial Regression models, Andersen-Gill (AG) models and Frailty models. We will provide two-sided 95% confidence intervals for the coefficients.

Continuous secondary endpoints (such as laboratory values) will be summarized by mean, standard deviation, median, first and third quartiles, minimum and maximum for each treatment arm separately. If a continuous endpoint is measured only at one time point after randomization, it will be analysed using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with the factor treatment group adjusting for the factors sex, centre and the covariate age at randomization (in years). Mean estimates will be provided, together with their corresponding two-sided 95% confidence intervals.

If repeated measurements are available for several visits after randomisation, a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) will be performed using patient as random factor and treatment arm as fixed factor. Models will be also adjusted for the same factors/covariate as described for the primary endpoint.

As additional analysis the time point V7 will be analysed using ANCOVA with the independent factor treatment group adjusting also for the same factors/covariate as described for the primary endpoint. The corresponding 95%-confidence interval for the mean difference between the two groups will be reported as well.

The analysis of the secondary endpoints will be performed on the mITT dataset.

Detailed description of analysis of selected secondary endpoints

The endpoints “Infection/rejection/graft-loss/death according to local PI” and “Primary endpoint, infection and rejection restricted to severe infection and rejection” are supportive analyses for the primary composite endpoint. The same analyses as outlined for the primary endpoint will be performed.

The endpoints “Infection/rejection/graft-loss/death plus development of malignoma”, “Infection/rejection/graft-loss/death according to blinded assessor plus development of malignoma” and “Primary endpoint including any infection” are considered exploratory. The same analyses as outlined for the primary endpoint will be performed.

Additionally for each composite component (e.g., infection type, death, graft loss, rejection type) the reason for the event will be reported descriptively as absolute numbers per group. See exemplary Table 5.

The endpoint “Overall Survival (Death)“ is defined as the time from randomisation to death, otherwise patients will be censored. Kaplan–Meier plots per group will be provided. The two groups will be compared with a log-rank test. Additionally, Cox-regression models will be performed adjusting also for additional factors as described for the primary endpoint.

The endpoints “Graft loss “, “Rejection according to blinded assessor”, “Rejection according to local PI”, “Severe rejection according to local PI/blinded assessor” and “Development of malignoma” are defined as the time from randomisation to event, otherwise patients will be censored. Kaplan–Meier plots and/or cumulative incidence functions per group will be provided.

The secondary endpoint “Infection according to blinded assessor” is expected to be the key component for the primary composite endpoint. This endpoint as well as “Infection according to local PI”, “Severe infection according to local PI/blinded assessor” and “SARS-CoV-2 infection/COVID-19” will be analysed as follows. We will fit a negative Binomial (NB) regression model including each infection endpoint as a dependent variable adjusting for variables as described for the primary endpoint with offsets accounting for the time a patient is actually under risk. Furthermore, we will perform the counting process model of AG and Frailty models. Additionally, the endpoint in NB and AG models will be defined as infection plus death to account for intercurrent events. To account for within-patient correlation, robust standard errors will be used in the Andersen-Gill model. Further adjustments for covariables will be conducted as described for logistic regression.

The time to the first infection will be analysed with the analysis methods for time-to-event endpoints (Cumulative incidence functions, log-rank test and Cox regression model). Patients who died will be censored at the time of death. Additional supportive analyses will be conducted for time to first infection where we will use a composite strategy (similar to the primary endpoint), where an event is defined as the time to the first of infection or death, whatever occurs first.

All aforementioned analyses will furthermore be conducted including COVID-19 as infections of interest, considering these infections in the various composite endpoint definitions.

Binary response variables “Rejection at month 12 protocol biopsy according to BANFF 2019 meeting report including/excluding BL-TCMR”, “Rejection at month 12 protocol biopsy according to molecular microscope”, “De novo DSA”, and Drug adherence measures will be summarized giving absolute numbers and percentages per group and depicted using bar charts. Logistic regression models will be applied using treatment as an independent factor. The models will be adjusted for the same factors and covariates as outlined for the primary endpoint. Additionally, Chi2-test (alternatively Fisher's Exact Test) will be reported for the 2 × 2 cross tables for exploratory purposes.

The individual time courses will be visualized, e.g., by using spaghetti plots. Additionally, the time courses per group are visualised by showing the trajectories of group mean ± standard deviation (or standard error) or box plots over time. The change from baseline to V7 will be visualized with waterfall plots.

Continuous endpoints will be summarized by mean, standard deviation, median, first and third quartiles, minimum and maximum for each treatment arm and time point (V1 to V7) separately. Mean estimates will be provided, together with their corresponding two-sided 95% confidence intervals.

The longitudinal data will be analysed using mixed models for repeated measurements, whereby the patient will be included as random factor and treatment arm as fixed factor. If appropriate the baseline value will be included as additional covariate. Different models will be explored using time either as fixed factor or as covariate. The interaction between time and treatment arm will be considered as well. Additionally MMRMs will be explored adjusting for the same factors/covariates as described for the primary endpoint.

For MMRMs we will use unstructured variance–covariance matrices. In case of convergence issues different structures might be explored and the deviation from the pre-planned analysis noted in the statistical analysis report.

When time is used as a fixed factor, it will be tested whether there is a difference between the two treatment arms at visit V7 and the corresponding 95%-confidence intervals for the LS-means will be reported. If baseline values of the respective dependent variable of interest are collected, they will be included as covariate in the respective MMRM.

As additional analysis the time point V7 will be analysed using an ANCOVA using the factor treatment group and adjusting for the baseline value. Models will be provided adjusting for the same factors/covariates as described for the primary endpoint.

The repeated measures of the secondary outcomes “TTV plasma load”, “TAC trough level” and “TAC dose” will also be summarized as the area under the curve (AUC) of single measurements over time. This one AUC value per patient will be analysed in the same manner as continuous outcomes measured only once per patient (“Rejection at month 12 protocol biopsy according to molecular microscope—Rejection score” and “full and/or abbreviated iBox”). As for other continuous variables mean, standard deviation, median, first and third quartiles, minimum and maximum will be provided for each treatment arm separately. Analyses will be conducted by means of an ANCOVA including treatment group as independent factor adjusting for sex, centre and age (in years).

Furthermore, differences in total eGFR slopes [7] between treatment groups will be explored.

“TAC trough level target changes” will be described in tabular form stating numbers of increase, decrease or no change at each time point for each treatment group and Sankey diagrams.

Methods used for assumptions to be checked for statistical methods {27c}

Data distribution (e.g., normal distribution) will be analysed by visual tools including histograms or Q-Q plots.

Homogeneity in treatment effects across centres (as assumed in the Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel test) will be investigated in the logistic regression models.

Proportional hazards (PH) assumption will be assessed graphically (log–log survival curves or scaled Schoenfeld residuals over time) and by adding time-dependent covariates to the model. If PH is not satisfied, accelerated failure time (AFT) models will be considered and distributional assumptions checked using graphical methods.

Details of alternative methods to be used if distributional assumptions do not hold {27d}

If distributional assumptions do not hold, data will be transformed, e.g., using logarithmic or square root transformation, or statistical analysis tools adapted to fit the distribution. The transformation and deviation from the SAP will be noted in the statistical report.

In addition to transforming the data, non-parametric tests such as the Mann–Whitney-U test might be performed to compare the Visit 7 values between the two groups. If this is the case, this will be noted in the statistical report.

For time-to-event analysis if deemed necessary the following analysis will be conducted in case of violations of the underlying assumptions: Robust standard errors will be calculated for AG models to allow for dependence of outcomes within patients, further frailty models will be applied taking these dependencies into account explicitly.

Any planned sensitivity analyses for each outcome {27e}

The primary analysis to establish non-inferiority will be performed following the modified intention to treat principle. Additional sensitivity analyses will be performed using the per protocol analysis set and the dataset restricted to adherent patients.

If there are substantial differences in the composition of the analysis sets, also sensitivity analyses for secondary analyses will be conducted using the per protocol analysis set and the dataset restricted to adherent patients for selected variables. This will be noted in the statistical analysis report.

Any planned subgroup analyses for each outcome including how subgroups are defined {27f}

Subgroup analysis will be performed in patients at risk for immunological (re-transplantation) and infectious (diabetes mellitus) events and according to age group (> 55 years of age), sex and study centre. If appropriate these factors will be included as additional covariates/factors in the regression models as described above.

Missing data {28}

For the primary analysis missing values will be considered as treatment failures (i.e., counted as events) in the binary composite primary endpoint. Patterns and reasons for missing data will be investigated and summarized in a table.

Due to the exploratory nature of the secondary endpoints, for these endpoints missing data will generally not be imputed. E.g., for longitudinal data this will be dealt with using models such as MMRM using all available data and specific assumptions such as missing at random. If a meaningful number of missing values is present in selected variables, imputation together with sensitivity analysis will be performed and noted in the statistical analysis report.

Additional analyses {29}

The following analyses are considered exploratory and will not necessarily be part of the summary of the results of the clinical trial in accordance with Article 37(4) of Regulation (EU) No 536/2014.

An additional endpoint analysis is performed, where infections are only counted for the primary endpoint after the second study visit (V2). Other variants of the primary endpoint include development of malignoma as an additional event in the composite endpoint and the respective endpoint according to the local investigator and the primary endpoint where infection is defined as any infection to occur.

Furthermore, severe infections and rejections according to the local investigator and blinded assessor, respectively, and including COVID-19, COVID-19 only, Plasma creatinine, proteinuria, abbreviated and/or full iBox scoring systems are investigated as outcomes.

Harms: sufficient detail on summarising safety data {30}

All adverse events (distinguishing between AEs and SAEs) will be reported in absolute and numbers in percent for the total cohort and per treatment arm as well as disaggregated by sex. Information on the type of event (medical/CTCAE term), severity according to CTCAE Grade (Version 5.0, published 27/11/2017, NCI), and causality (not related, unlikely, possibly, probably, certainly related, not assessable) will be provided. For SAEs the criterion/reason for reporting will be given.

Statistical software {31}

All statistical analyses will be conducted with statistical software like SAS 9.4 (or higher), R 3.6.3 (or higher) or IBM SPSS Statistics (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), and STATA (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Any deviation from the original statistical plan will be described and justified in the protocol and in the final report.

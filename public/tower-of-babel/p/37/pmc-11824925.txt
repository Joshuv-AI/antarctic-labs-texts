Antidepressant-induced Hyponatremia in Postmenopausal Females: A Case Series
Authors: Prabhat Kumar Agrawal, Sandipta Kumar Panda, Shiv Sagar Yadav, Ruchika Garg, Ashish Gautam
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHyponatremia is the most common electrolyte imbalance observed in hospitalized as well as outdoor patients. Antidepressants such as selective serotonin receptor inhibitors and tricyclic antidepressants are reported among the most common drugs causing hyponatremia, especially in the elderly and in patients with known risk factors for hyponatremia. However, there have been only a few case reports of hyponatremia associated with antidepressants such as mirtazapine and flupentixol/melitracen. The exact mechanism of hyponatremia with the use of antidepressants is not known; however, syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) is presumed to be the reason. Here, we report two cases of antidepressant drug-induced hyponatremia in two postmenopausal females with no other risk factor for hyponatremia: one associated with mirtazapine and the other with flupentixol/melitracen.

INTRODUCTION

Hyponatremia is the most common fluid and electrolyte abnormality in hospitalized as well as outdoor patients. Symptoms depend on the severity of hyponatremia and range from anorexia and nausea in mild hyponatremia to irritability, confusion, forgetfulness, gait and speech abnormality, convulsions, and coma, which can be life-threatening in moderate-to-severe hyponatremia. The exact mechanism of hyponatremia due to antidepressants is not known; however, SIADH is presumed to be the reason.[1] One of the most common causes of hyponatremia is drug-induced and among the most common culprits are antidepressants and anti-epileptics.

CASE REPORTS

Case 1

A 63-year-old female with no comorbidity presented to our emergency with gradually progressive irritability, confusion, slurring of speech for 2 days, and altered sensorium for 2 h. On examination, vitals were normal, and neurological examination was unremarkable except for Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of E2V3M5. On taking detailed history, it was revealed that she was taking tablet mirtazapine 7.5 mg twice daily for the last 2 months, prescribed to her for functional dyspepsia. She was also taking tablet pantoprazole 40 mg once daily. There was no history of any other drug intake or any other chronic organic or endocrine disease. Laboratory investigations such as complete blood count (CBC), liver function test (LFT), kidney function test (KFT), random blood sugar (RBS), and electrocardiogram (ECG) were normal except for a serum sodium value of 117 mEq/L. She was treated with hypertonic 3% saline and mirtazapine was stopped. The magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain revealed a normal study; the thyroid function test was normal, and the serum cortisol level was normal. Ultrasonography (USG) whole abdomen was unremarkable. Serum osmolality was 265 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality was 135 mOsm/kg, and urine sodium was 28 mEq/L. These findings suggested that she was a case of euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia. Her symptoms significantly improved over the next 2 days and her serum sodium level was 139 mEq/L after 3 days. We advised her not to take mirtazapine again.

Case 2

A 60-year-old female presented to our emergency with nausea, vomiting for 2 days, confusion, and slurring of speech for 1 day. On examination, vitals were normal, neurological examination was unremarkable, and GCS was E3V3M5. On evaluation, it was revealed that she was prescribed tablet placida (flupentixol 0.5 mg/melitracen 10 mg) for anxiety and atypical chest pain (ECG and coronary angiography were normal), which she has been taking twice daily for the last 6 weeks. There was no history of any other drug intake except for tablet tramadol 50 mg SOS (as needed). She had no comorbidity, and laboratory investigations such as CBC, LFT, KFT, RBS, and ECG were normal except for serum sodium level which was 116 mEq/L. Tablet flupentixol/melitracen was stopped and she was treated with hypertonic 3% saline. The MRI of the brain revealed a normal study; the thyroid function test was normal, and the serum cortisol level was normal. USG whole abdomen revealed normal study. Serum osmolality was 270 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality was 130 mOsm/kg, and urine sodium was 25 mEq/L. These findings again suggested euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia. Her symptoms significantly improved and her serum sodium level was 137 mEq/L after 3 days.

DISCUSSION

There have been frequent reports of association of antidepressants like selective serotonin receptor inhibitors (SSRIs) and serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors with hyponatremia, but reports of hyponatremia associated with mirtazapine or flupentixol/melitracen have been few and mostly in patients with risk factors like renal, endocrine, and central nervous system dysfunction or concomitant use of other drug like diuretics. In our cases, there was no such associated factor. On the Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale,[2] the score of both cases was 6, suggesting a probable association of mirtazapine and flupentixol/melitracen with hyponatremia. Mirtazapine is a central alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist and a potent antagonist of 5-HT2 and 5-HT3 serotonin receptors and H1 histamine receptors. Flupentixol is an antipsychotic with predominant dopamine D2 receptor-blocking activity, but at low doses, it acts as an antidepressant by binding to D2 autoreceptors and increasing postsynaptic activation via higher dopamine levels. Melitracen is a tricyclic antidepressant (TCA). Antidepressants like SSRIs are reported to cause SIADH,[34] and mirtazapine and flupentixol/melitracen are also thought to cause hyponatremia because of inappropriate ADH release;[5] however, the exact mechanism is not known.[16]

A systematic review in 2021 by Moscona-Nissan et al.[7] identified that among cohort and case–control studies involving 30,844 patients using mirtazapine, the incidence of hyponatremia was 3.26%. A class-by-class review of the literature suggested the risk of mirtazapine-induced SIADH and hyponatremia is moderate.[8]

Therefore, we suggest having a baseline sodium level and monitoring for hyponatremia regularly while advising mirtazapine, flupentixol/melitracen, and other antidepressants like SSRIs/TCAs or antiepileptics, especially in case of the elderly population.

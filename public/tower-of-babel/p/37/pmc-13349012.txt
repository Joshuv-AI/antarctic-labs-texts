Comparison of epcoritamab, lenalidomide, and rituximab versus usual care in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma
Authors: Lorenzo Falchi, Martin Hutchings, David Tybor, Junhua Yu, Abualbishr Alshreef, Anthony Wang, Kavita Sail, Zhijie Ding, Alex Mutebi, Poliana Patah, Nisha Kishore, Ali Rana, Elena Favaro, Kim Linton
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPatients with relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma (R/R FL) often experience multiple disease recurrences with progressively shorter duration of remission. Chemoimmunotherapy (CIT) is commonly used for R/R FL in second-line and later settings but is not curative; novel, improved treatments are needed. Epcoritamab, a CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibody, is approved as monotherapy for R/R FL after ≥ 2 lines of therapy (LOTs) and combined with lenalidomide and rituximab (R2) for R/R FL. Epcoritamab + R2 pivotal data demonstrated superiority over R2, but no comparison of epcoritamab + R2 versus CIT exists. Therefore, we conducted an adjusted comparative analysis of epcoritamab + R2 from arm 2 of the phase 1b/2 EPCORE NHL-2 trial (NCT04663347; n = 111; US, 23.4%) versus R2/CIT using COTA individual patient-level electronic health records (n = 380; US, 100%) of patients with R/R FL after ≥ 1 previous LOT. Epcoritamab + R2 improved response rates over R2/CIT (overall response, 96.9% versus 80.5%, P < .001; complete response, 90.2% versus 55.1%, P < .001). Epcoritamab + R2 improved duration of response (HR, 0.29 [95% CI, 0.18-0.47]; P < .001), duration of complete response (HR, 0.31 [95% CI, 0.17-0.55]; P < .001), progression-free survival (HR, 0.43 [95% CI, 0.28-0.68]; P < .001), and overall survival (HR, 0.33 [95% CI, 0.16-0.70]; P = .003) over R2/CIT. These findings support epcoritamab + R2 as a promising chemotherapy-free treatment option for R/R FL.

Introduction

Follicular lymphoma (FL), the most common indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), accounts for approximately 25% of all lymphomas in developed regions, 1 and is generally considered incurable. 2 Patients with FL have heterogeneous outcomes as some achieve long remissions 3 but others experience multiple disease recurrences with progressively shorter intervals between relapses; 4 , 5 those with progression within 24 months have particularly poor long-term survival. 6 There is currently no established treatment standard for patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) FL, and the choice of therapy depends on many factors, such as patient age, comorbidities, treatment history, duration of prior response, and patient treatment goals. 7 , 8 Patients with R/R FL have historically been treated with chemoimmunotherapy (CIT) but the efficacy is suboptimal 4 and cumulative toxicity is a concern. 9 The chemotherapy-free regimen of lenalidomide plus rituximab (R2) is an established treatment option for R/R FL in some countries, 4 yet its results are suboptimal in more contemporary series and more effective approaches are needed. 10 , 11

With the aim of improving clinical efficacy, preserving patient quality of life, and minimizing treatment-related adverse events, chemotherapy-free therapies for patients with R/R FL continue to be investigated. 8 Epcoritamab is a subcutaneously administered CD3 × CD20 T-cell–engaging bispecific antibody approved as a monotherapy for patients with FL or diffuse large B-cell lymphoma/high-grade B-cell lymphoma with 2 or more prior lines of therapy (LOTs). 12 , 13 EPCORE NHL-2 (NCT04663347) is a multi-arm phase 1b/2 trial exploring the safety and efficacy of epcoritamab combinations for patients with B-cell NHL. 14 In arm 2 of this study, patients with R/R FL received epcoritamab + R2 for up to 12 cycles followed by epcoritamab monotherapy for up to a total of 2 years of treatment, and the results showed deep and durable responses with a manageable safety profile. 14 Epcoritamab + R2 is newly approved in the United States as a fixed-duration regimen for up to 12 cycles for the treatment of R/R FL based on pivotal data from the phase 3 EPCORE FL-1 trial (NCT05409066). 15 Herein, we report the comparative efficacy of epcoritamab + R2 versus usual care (R2/CIT) in patients with R/R FL through trial and retrospective real-world treatment comparisons via inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW)-adjusted methods.

Methods

An adjusted comparative analysis of efficacy was conducted using individual patient-level data from patients aged ≥ 18 years with R/R FL treated with epcoritamab + R2 from arm 2 of the EPCORE NHL-2 study (NCT04663347; trial commencement: November 03, 2020; data cutoff: May 2024) and patients treated with usual care (R2/CIT) from COTA electronic health records (2010–2023; COTA Healthcare, New York, NY, USA). EPCORE NHL-2 arm 2 enrolled adults with R/R CD20 + FL who were administered epcoritamab with a 2-step-up (0.16 mg and 0.8 mg) dosing regimen in cycle 1 followed by 48 mg full dose cycles for up to 2 years. Patients included in arm 2 had received ≥ 1 prior antineoplastic agent, including ≥ 1 anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody-containing therapy, had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of ≤ 2, and FL grade 1 to 3 A. The COTA database contained real-world data (de-identified demographic, diagnostic, clinical, treatment, and outcomes data) submitted by geographically dispersed academic medical centers and community practice sites in the United States. Eligible patients from the COTA database were selected using the trial inclusion criteria and were restricted based on a diagnosis of R/R FL any time from January 2010 through December 2022 and had to have initiated their R2 or CIT treatment in the second-line and later settings after January 1, 2010.

A generalized estimating equation approach was used to fit a logistic regression among all patients from arm 2 of the NHL-2 trial and COTA patients satisfying the inclusion/exclusion criteria, using a robust sandwich variance estimator to account for the correlation between repeated observations on the same patient. IPTW was conducted to adjust for differences in baseline characteristics between the cohorts. Prognostic factors included in the model were age, sex, ECOG PS, prior LOTs, progression of disease within 24 months, double refractoriness, prior stem cell transplant, abnormal hemoglobin, disease stage at diagnosis, abnormal lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and refractory to immediate prior LOT. In the COTA cohort, LOT was defined per an external oncologist reviewed and vetted algorithm that was developed by COTA, Inc. using real-world data best practices and relevant industry and clinical guidelines. The first LOT began with the first regimen received for FL; all regimens received within 30 days of the initial regimen were combined into a single LOT if there was no documented progression. The LOT continued until a regimen was discontinued due to one of the following events: progression or inadequate response, addition of a new drug or regimen outside of the prespecified window, a gap in treatment occurred, or the patient underwent CAR T-cell therapy. Discontinuation due to one of these events resulted in the end of the current LOT and initiation of a new LOT. The LOT was not advanced by conditioning regimens, maintenance regimens, stem cell transplants within 1 year of preceding therapy, or the de-escalation or dropping of drugs from a regimen.

Study outcomes included overall response rate (ORR), complete response (CR), duration of response (DOR), duration of complete response (DOCR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Weighted logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate relative risks (RRs) and CIs for response outcomes and hazard ratios (HRs) and CIs for survival outcomes. Patients without disease progression or death (DOR, DOCR, PFS) or without death (OS) were censored at the last follow-up in the time-to-event analysis. All analyses were conducted using SAS (version 9.4) and R (version R.4.2.2).

As previously published, the EPCORE NHL-2 study protocol was approved by site-specific institutional review boards or institutional or central ethics committees before study initiation, all patients provided informed consent before enrollment, and the study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonization guidelines on Good Clinical Practice and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. 16 The COTA Vantage real-world database is a Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)-compliant database comprised of longitudinal data from electronic health records submitted from United States academic medical centers and community practice sites. The creation and use of COTA, Inc.’s de-identified real-world database is approved by WCG IRB for use in research (Study #1174746) consistent with CFR Title 45.46. Abstraction of data pertaining to the diagnosis, clinical management, and outcome of patients with cancer was performed by COTA and based on a review of source documentation and de-identified patient data in a manner compliant with HIPAA.

Results

In this study, data from a total of 491 patients, including those treated with epcoritamab + R2 (n = 111; United States [23.4%], Europe [73.9%], or Australia [2.7%]) or usual care (R2/CIT, n = 380, United States [100%]), were analyzed. Of the 380 patients treated with usual care, 47 (12.4%) received R2 and 333 (87.6%) received CIT. Of the 333 patients receiving CIT, 219 (65.8%) received bendamustine; 55 (16.5%) received cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (CHOP); 30 (9.0%) received cyclophosphamide, vincristine, and prednisone (CVP); 4 (1.2%) received fludarabine plus cyclophosphamide (FC); 1 (0.3%) received mitoxantrone; and 24 (7.2%) received other alkylator-based chemotherapy. These CIT regimens were rituximab-containing, except for 47 of 333 patients who received obinutuzumab. The median follow-up time was 25.0 months for epcoritamab + R2 and 47.6 months for usual care R2/CIT.

Before adjustment, there were imbalances in the proportions of patients in the epcoritamab + R2 group versus usual care group who were in the second-line setting (56.8% versus 76.3%) and the third-line and beyond setting (43.2% versus 23.7%). A higher proportion of patients who received epcoritamab + R2 versus usual care R2/CIT had high LDH and had received prior stem cell transplant (autologous for patients in the epcoritamab + R2 arm and allogeneic for patients in the usual care arm). Following adjustment, patient baseline characteristics were well balanced between epcoritamab + R2 and usual care R2/CIT groups, including the proportion of patients in the second-line (69.6% versus 71.0%) and third-line and beyond settings (30.4% versus 29.0%; Table 1). The effective sample sizes were 84 and 317 for the epcoritamab + R2 arm and the usual care R2/CIT arm, respectively (Table 1).

Upon adjustment, epcoritamab + R2 demonstrated superior efficacy and survival benefits when compared with usual care R2/CIT. Patients in the epcoritamab + R2 arm were 20% more likely to achieve an overall response versus the R2/CIT arm (ORR, 96.9% versus 80.5%; RR, 1.2; 95% CI, 1.1–1.3; P < .001) and 60% more likely to achieve CR (CR rate, 90.2% versus 55.1%; RR, 1.6; 95% CI, 1.4–1.8; P < .001; Figure 1). The median DOR was not reached (95% CI, 26.0–not estimable [NE]) with epcoritamab + R2 compared with 14.4 months (95% CI, 12.1–19.7) with R2/CIT. Similarly, the median DOCR was not reached (95% CI, 26.0–NE) with epcoritamab + R2 compared with 19.0 months (95% CI, 15.0–24.0) with R2/CIT (Figure 2A). The HRs for DOR (0.29; 95% CI, 0.18–0.47; P < .001) and DOCR (0.31; 95% CI, 0.17–0.55; P < .001) were favorable for patients treated with epcoritamab + R2 versus those who received usual care R2/CIT.

The median PFS was not reached (95% CI, 27.4–NE) with epcoritamab + R2 compared with 21.7 months (95% CI, 17.4–27.2) with R2/CIT (Figure 2B). The weighted median OS was not reached (NE–NE) for both the epcoritamab + R2 and R2/CIT arms (Figure 2C), with 12-month OS estimates of 94.0% (95% CI, 88.5–99.9) with epcoritamab + R2 and 82.4% (95% CI, 78.4–86.5) with R2/CIT. The HRs for PFS (0.43; 95% CI, 0.28–0.68; P < .001) and OS (0.33; 95% CI, 0.16-0.70; P = .003) favored the epcoritamab + R2 arm over the usual care R2/CIT arm. In a sensitivity analysis where the usual care arm was restricted to CIT alone, the results did not substantially differ from those presented herein for R2/CIT (data not shown).

Discussion

This comparative analysis of epcoritamab + R2 from arm 2 of the EPCORE NHL-2 trial and usual care R2/CIT from the COTA database showed that epcoritamab + R2 offered significantly improved efficacy compared with usual care for the treatment of patients with R/R FL who had received ≥ 1 prior LOT. The ORR was significantly higher with epcoritamab + R2 compared with usual care, with almost all responders treated with epcoritamab + R2 achieving a CR (ORR, 96.9%; CR, 90.2%), and responses with epcoritamab + R2 showed better durability than usual care. Both PFS and OS were improved with epcoritamab + R2 compared with usual care, and the HRs reached statistical significance in favor of epcoritamab + R2.

Patients with R/R FL need treatment options with improved safety and efficacy, particularly among those with multiply R/R disease. The chemotherapy-free regimen of R2 offers a differentiated safety profile from that of rituximab-chemotherapy, 17 but long-term results have shown similar efficacy between these treatment options, with no differences in survival. 18 Several ongoing phase 3 clinical studies are investigating the potential of enhancing the efficacy of R2 with additional agents approved for the treatment of R/R FL. In the phase 3 inMIND trial, the addition of the CD19-targeting monoclonal antibody tafasitamab to R2 significantly improved PFS over R2 alone in patients with R/R FL. 19 Recently, the ongoing phase 3 EPCORE FL-1 head-to-head trial of epcoritamab + R2 versus R2 reported the dual primary endpoints were met with significantly superior ORR and PFS. 15 As of the May 24, 2025 data cutoff, ORR was higher (95% versus 79%; P < .0001) and PFS longer (HR, 0.21 [95% CI, 0.14–0.31]; P < .0001), with a 79% reduction in the risk of disease progression or death, in patients with R/R FL who received 12-cycle fixed-duration epcoritamab + R2 versus R2. 15 Based on these results from EPCORE FL-1, the FDA approved epcoritamab + R2 following ≥ 1 prior therapy for the treatment of R/R FL. 20

In the absence of head-to-head clinical trials, indirect analyses are important for providing evidence of comparative efficacy between emerging treatment options for R/R FL. In a matching-adjusted indirect comparison using clinical trial data from arm 2 of EPCORE NHL-2 and inMIND, epcoritamab + R2 provided significantly improved ORR, CR rate, and PFS compared with tafasitamab + R2 in patients with FL in the second-line or later settings. 21 The results of our study complement these recent clinical trial and treatment comparison findings, supporting the potential of epcoritamab + R2 as a highly effective treatment option for R/R FL.

Strengths of this study include the longitudinal nature of the COTA electronic health record database, which allowed us to comprehensively capture individual patient disease and treatment history, including prognostic factors prior to index therapy, supporting robust adjustment for differences between cohorts. Several limitations exist for this analysis. The analysis was subject to general methodological limitations related to the use of real-world data from the electronic health records, including potential missing data due to lack of follow-up visits or lack of documentation and the possibility of outcomes, such as progression of disease, being underreported. 22 , 23 This can potentially result in biased survival outcomes for real-world treatment cohorts, emphasizing the importance of utilizing contemporaneous electronic health records data with high mortality sensitivity and proper control arm selection. 24 , 25 To mitigate potential missingness in mortality reporting, the COTA database uses a composite mortality variable supplemented by commercially available obituaries. 23 Differences in the treatment arms should be noted as a limitation, with NHL-2 arm 2 representing a contemporary, global patient population and COTA usual care representing a less contemporary, United States patient population. Another limitation was that not all patients had ECOG PS, disease grade, and staging information available at the time of all eligible lines, and in these instances, the values available closest to the time of line initiation were used. The control arm of this study was R2/CIT based on usual care in the United States, which should be taken into consideration when interpreting the results as the regimens that constitute usual care may differ in other countries. While the R2/CIT data were obtained from a United States database, the CIT regimens included are widely used both within and outside the United States. Finally, some patients receive maintenance therapy in addition to CIT in the real-world setting, which was not evaluated in the present analysis but should be further explored in future research.

Collectively, the findings of clinical studies and comparative analyses highlight the benefit of epcoritamab + R2 as a novel chemotherapy-free treatment option for patients with R/R FL. This growing body of clinical and real-world evidence supports epcoritamab + R2 as a safe and effective subcutaneous treatment option for patients with R/R FL and may help to inform future treatment selection and optimize patient outcomes.

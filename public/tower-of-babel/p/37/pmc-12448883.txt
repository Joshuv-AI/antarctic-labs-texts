Telomerase Reverse Transcriptase-mutated Follicular Variant Papillary Thyroid Cancer With Tibial Metastasis
Authors: Nicole Chan, Syed Gilani, Grace Kim, Nira Madisi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FV-PTC) generally has a favorable prognosis but can behave aggressively, notably in the presence of TERT promoter mutations. We present a case of a 58-year-old woman with a remote history of thyroid cancer treated by total thyroidectomy without radioactive iodine, who presented with acute right leg pain. Imaging revealed a destructive lesion in the right proximal tibia with biopsy-proven metastatic FV-PTC. Her thyroglobulin was >500 ng/mL (> 500 µg/L) (reference range: < 35.00 ng/mL; < 35.00 µg/L) and was above the assay detection despite dilutional efforts. She had undergone surgical resection of the metastatic lesion with a pathology consistent with FV-PTC. Molecular testing identified TERT promoter mutation c.124C > T. She had received radioactive iodine treatment, and subsequent posttreatment whole-body iodine scan revealed iodine-avid lesions in the right tibia and posterior second rib. Appendicular skeletal metastasis of papillary thyroid carcinoma is not well established in large cohort studies but in single institution studies reporting incidences of approximately 1% of thyroid carcinomas. This case highlights the potential of late-onset and aggressive metastatic spread of TERT promoter mutation FV-PTC and the necessity of dynamic risk assessment to ensure early identification of recurrence to guide treatment decisions.

Introduction

Follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FV-PTC) is a subtype of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) that morphologically appears like follicular thyroid carcinoma but behaves more like conventional PTC. Overall, its prognosis and disease-specific survival are favorable. However, certain genetic alterations—most notably, telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) promoter mutations—have been associated with more aggressive disease. Thus, it may lead to a higher likelihood of recurrence, distant metastasis, and mortality. We present a case of a woman who had a remote history of thyroid cancer who presented with acute right leg pain and was found to have metastatic FV-PTC involving the right tibia. The clinical details of this patient contribute to the growing body of literature on pathologic variant of TERT promoter mutation FV-PTC and its metastatic behavior.

Case Presentation

A 58-year-old female with a remote medical history of thyroid cancer and prior total thyroidectomy without radioactive iodine treatment presented with right leg pain. She reported approximately 1 year of right leg pain that had acutely worsened 2 days prior to her presentation. On physical exam, the patient's thyroid was absent, and there was focal tenderness with mild edema over the proximal anterior right tibia. There were no overlying skin changes appreciated. The remainder of the exam was unremarkable. She had been taking levothyroxine 175 mcg daily since her thyroidectomy without dose adjustments through her primary care physician. The thyroidectomy was performed over 10 years ago in a New York City hospital, but the patient had not received follow-up care. The patient is a poor historian and has low medical literacy. She had believed her thyroid cancer was low risk, and, therefore, she did not follow up. Multiple attempts to obtain her surgical records from the surrounding hospitals were unsuccessful, and the original pathology report was no longer available due to the time that had elapsed. As a result, the American Thyroid Association initial risk of recurrence and response to therapy is unknown.

Diagnostic Assessment

A computed tomography scan of the right lower extremity revealed a destructive lesion in the posterior aspect of the proximal tibial metadiaphysis, measuring 8.0 × 5.5 cm in the axial plane (Fig. 1A). A core biopsy of the lesion was performed and revealed histology consistent with metastatic FV-PTC. Preoperative TSH was 0.44 mIU/L (0.44 µIU/mL) (reference range: 0.4-4.5 mIU/L; 0.4-4.5 µIU/mL), thyroglobulin was >500 ng/mL (> 500 µg/L) (reference range: < 35.00 ng/mL; < 35.00 µg/L), and antithyroglobulin antibody was undetectable at <1.0 IU/mL (<1.0 kIU/L) (reference range <4.0 IU/mL; < 4.0 kIU/L).

Treatment

The patient's levothyroxine dose was increased to twice her weight-based dose, which was 250 mcg daily. Subsequently, the patient had undergone an open curettage, cementation, and plating of the right proximal tibia. The surgical report notably described extensive destruction of the gastrocnemius muscle and posterior tibial cortex with preservation of only the anterior cortical bone. Wide excisions were made incorporating surrounding healthy tissue in an effort to eradicate potential microscopic disease. Postoperatively, the thyroglobulin levels remained >500 ng/mL (> 500 µg/L) (reference range: < 35.00 ng/mL; < 35.00 µg/L), and antithyroglobulin antibody remained undetectable at <1.0 IU/mL (<1.0 kIU/L) (reference range <4.0 IU/mL; < 4.0 kIU/L). Despite dilutional efforts, the concentration of serum thyroglobulin was above the assay's upper limit of detection.

Outcome and Follow-up

Surgical pathology confirmed FV-PTC involving the tibia. On low magnification, tumor cells involved bone with a background of mixed macrofollicular and microfollicular architecture and colloid (Fig. 1B and 1C). Tumor cells showed PTC–like nuclear features at 10× magnification (Fig. 1D and 1E) with foci of bone invasion (Fig. 1F). Molecular analysis was performed and tested for alterations in ALK (D5F3), NTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3, RET, BRAF, HRAS, KRAS, and NRAS. These were all negative except for TERT promoter mutation c.124C > T; c.−146C > T mutation was not detected.

Subsequently, the patient had proceeded with radioactive iodine treatment of 150 mCi and whole-body iodine scan. Results revealed increased radiotracer uptake in the right proximal tibia (Fig. 2) and right posterior second rib (Fig. 3). There was normal distribution of radiotracer in the bilateral salivary glands, liver, and gastrointestinal and urinary tracts. There was no uptake observed in the neck or elsewhere to suggest additional metastatic foci. One month later, thyroglobulin was >500 ng/mL but was successfully diluted and quantified at 1593.8 ng/mL (1593.8 µg/L) (reference range: < 35.00 ng/mL; < 35.00 µg/L).

Discussion

FV-PTC exhibits a follicular growth pattern with nuclear features typical of conventional PTC. It accounts for up to 22.5% of PTC cases [1]. Importantly, TERT promoter mutations in thyroid cancer are associated with a worse prognosis and include features including extrathyroidal extension, positive surgical margins, and angioinvasion compared to PTC without such pathologic variations [2]. Metastatic spread to bone is rare in PTC. The frequency of appendicular skeletal metastasis is not well established in large cohort studies. Most bone metastases occur in about 1.4% to 7% of PTC and typically involve the axial skeleton. Metastases of thyroid carcinomas to the appendicular skeleton is even rarer, with single-institution studies in Turkey and the United States reporting rates of 0.5% and up to 1%, respectively [3, 4]. Diaphyseal or epiphyseal localization of thyroid cancer metastases is sparsely reported, although isolated case reports, such as 1 involving metastatic papillary thyroid cancer involving resorption of the diaphyseal femur, suggest that diaphyseal involvement can occur [5].

This patient's case underscores the capacity of TERT-mutated FV-PTC to exhibit late and aggressive metastatic behavior, even more than a decade after her initial diagnosis. Her disease manifested in an unusual site, the tibial diaphysis, and was only identified following acute symptomatic progression. Her initial preoperative thyroglobulin was exceedingly high. Our thyroglobulin assay reported a dilution factor of 10 to fall within the measurable range. Thus, it can be extrapolated that the patient's preoperative serum thyroglobulin exceeded 5000 ng/mL. Following surgical resection of her tibial metastatic lesion, the patient's thyroglobulin decreased significantly to 1593.8 ng/mL 1 month after radioactive iodine ablative therapy. Despite this reduction, the persistently elevated thyroglobulin remains indicative of residual FV-PTC corroborated by findings from the whole-body iodine scan. The scan demonstrated iodine-avid disease not only at the previously identified right proximal tibia but also in the posterior aspect of the second rib, confirming multifocal involvement. Considering these findings, further therapeutic interventions are being considered including external beam radiation therapy to target residual unresectable metastatic disease. Additionally, the patient's levothyroxine dose had been titrated to achieve TSH suppression to limit further tumor progression. This case highlights the importance of long-term, ongoing dynamic risk stratification in thyroid cancer management, regardless of initial impressions of low-risk disease. Although the patient reported a presumed low-risk disease, she ultimately presented with advanced metastatic involvement. Thus, regular reevaluation of therapeutic response and recurrence risk at each encounter allows for more accurate stratification and treatment plans that align with the patient's evolving clinical status.

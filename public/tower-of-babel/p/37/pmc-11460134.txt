The impact of the physicochemical properties of calcium phosphate ceramics on biocompatibility and osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells
Authors: Yuliia Skliarenko, Volodymyr V Kolomiiets, Volodymyr V Balatskyi, Yuliia Galuza, Oksana S Koryak, Larysa L Macewicz, Tetiana P Ruban, Sergey A Firstov, Nataliia V Ulianchych, Oksana O Piven
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveIn this study we have focused on biocompatibility and osteoinductive capacity analysis of self-manufactured single-phase (HAP) and two-phase (HAP and β-ТСР) bioactive ceramics with various chemical modifications (Fig. 1).ResultsWe demonstrate a reduction in solubility for all analyzed composite after the treatment with H2O and H2O2, accompanied by an enhancement in adsorption activity. This modification also resulted in an increase in micro- and macroporosity, along with a rise in the open porosity. Adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells demonstrated excellent cell adhesion and survival when cultured with these ceramics. Calcium phosphate ceramics (H-500, HT-500, and HT-1 series) stimulated alkaline phosphatase expression, promoted calcium deposition, and enhanced osteopontin expression in ADSCs, independently inducing osteogenesis without additional osteogenic stimuli. These findings underscore the promising potential of HAP-based bioceramics for bone regeneration/reconstruction.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-024-06937-y.Keywords: Hydroxyapatite, Calcium phosphate ceramics, Stem cells, Biocompatibility, Osteogenesis

Introduction

CPC known as bioactive ceramics due to their active interaction with the physiological environment, are among the most promising biomaterials for scaffold engineering in the reconstruction of bone defects. Normally, two-phase bioactive ceramics are used, in which hydroxyapatite and β-tricalcium phosphate are the main components [1]. Calcium phosphate ceramics (СPC) is biocompatible and porous material, that slowly biodegrades in situ [2, 3], which is important for the revascularization in the place of bone reconstruction. Deproteinized bone mineral is alternatively used as a scaffold in combination with various cell types for bone and tooth reconstruction [4]. CPC lack osteoinductive properties, so it is often used in combination with patients’ autologous cells [5–9].

To enhance the regeneration of damaged bone tissue, hydroxyapatite can be combined with extracellular matrix proteins, signaling proteins, or stem cells [1, 5, 6]. Moreover, recent studies have shown a regenerative impact of endometrial stem cells (C-EnSCs) in combination with BMP-2 in dental tissue [6]. A few studies had demonstrated promising data for bonne tissue reconstruction when HAP and BMSCs combined [7]. Adipose-derived stromal vascular fraction (SVF) cells are sufficient for polycaprolactone/hydroxyapatite scaffold bioprinting, differentiated into the osteoblasts, and ensure revascularization under hypoxic conditions [8]. The osteogenic differentiation of ADSCs might be facilitated by additional modifications of CPC [9].

Methods

Manufacturing of single-phase bioactive ceramics: Н-500, H-500/H2O2, H-500/H2O and two-phase bioactive ceramics: НТ-500, HT-500/H2O2, HT-500/H2O and НТ-1, HT-1/H2O2, HT-1/H2O

Н-500 and НТ-500, НТ-1 were obtained by chemical precipitation from calcium and phosphate salts` solutions - Cа(NO3)2, (NH4)2HPO4. The separated sediment after treatment with pore formers was dried, granulated and annealed at 800 °C for 1 h. Exposure at annealing temperature lasted for 1 h. Particle size Н-500, НТ-500–400–600 μm, particle size HT-1–800–1000 μm. After annealing, the granules were washed in an ultrasonic bath with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H-500/H2O2, HT-500/H2O2, HT-1/H2O2) or distilled water (H-500/H2O, HT-500/H2O, HT-1/H2O).

Control methods, biological validation and Statistical analysis detailed described in Additional File 1 : Methods.

Results

Physical and chemical characteristics of the calcium-phosphate ceramics

The radiographs of CPC granules annealed at 800ºС are shown in Fig. 1. The weight% (wt%) was calculated with Rietveld method, and for the H-500 series samples is 100% HAP. The HT-1 series granules represent the two-phase bioactive ceramics, which consist of HAP and β-TCP. The HT-500, HT-1 composites consist of 65% of HAP and 35% of β-TCP.

Diffractograms of CPC particles: (a) H-500 series, (b) HT-1 series. The most intense lines of HAP and β-TCP are marked with red and green verticals

CPC were cleaned of fine granules in an ultrasonic bath with H2O or H2O2 (as described in Additional File 1). The analyzed bioactive ceramic samples (see Additional File 2) exhibited reduction in solubility when evaluated in solution mimics physiological (Table S.1). Corresponding to the solubility trend of single-phase and two-phase bioactive ceramics, calcium ion concentrations decreased in the following order: CPC > CPC/H2O > CPC/H2O2 (Table S. 1). SEM analysis (Additional File 1) revealed significant increase in micro- and nanoporosity in CPC treated with H2O2 (Fig. S.1b) and H2O (Fig. S.1c) compared to the nanostructure of well-sintered untreated samples (Fig. S.1a). Treatment with H2O2 more effectively promoted the formation of micro- and nanoporosity in the morphological structure of CPCs compared to washing with H2O.

Solubility of CPC also depends on its phase composition and particles` size, thus, untreated well-annealed HAP appear to have bigger particle size Fig. S.2 (a) and characterized by lower solubility (Table S.1) compared with other samples (Fig. S.2.b).

Assessment of adsorption activity in treated and untreated CPC granules revealed an enhancement in adsorption capacity for biomaterial treated with H2O and H2O2 (Table S.1). This increase is likely attributed to the combined effects of additional washing and nanostructural modifications in the CPC morphology (Fig. S.3). Among the untreated bioactive ceramics, two-phase CPC (HT-500 series) exhibited the highest adsorption activity, as evidenced by the phase composition analysis (Table S.1). Adsorption activity of HT-500 series samples remained the superior even after the treatments (Table S.1).

To determine porosity, we employed the hydrostatic weighing method, as described in Additional File 1. Porosity studies by hydrostatic weighing demonstrated a fairly high porosity, which did not significantly differ for the single-phase or the two-phase materials (Fig. S.3), and indicates a high presence of interconnected pores. Photomicrographs of CPC granules (Fig S. 1 and 2) provide better understanding of ​​the pore structure in the samples and demonstrate an interconnection of the nano-sized to macro-sized pores, which are crucial for the ceramics, that should be replaced by a full-fledged bone tissue.

Osteogenic differentiation assay

With ADSCs using (see Additional File 1 and Additional File 3. Fig. S. 4) we found that all analyzed CPC have a prominent biocompatibility (Fig. S. 5). All analyzed CPC composites reduce the mitochondrial metabolic activity of ADSCs during short-term cultivation (Additional File 3). We observed low intensity of the ALP activity in the cells cultivated in control media without CPC, and strong activity of the ALP for cells cultivated in osteogenic media (Fig. 2). We found strong positive ALP staining in the cells which were cultivated with CPC under both conditions (Fig. 2).

Analysis of the ALP expression in ADSCs cultivated on CPC granules in Control and Osteogenic Media. Representative photomicrograph images of ALP staining of ADSCs after 7 days of experiment, light microscopy, magnification x200

We observed strong intensity of ARS staining in cells cultivated in osteogenic media, that points out the osteogenic differentiation (see Additional File 4. Fig S.8 a, b). We analyzed the calcium deposition (Additional File 1) in ADSCs cultivated with CPC under standard and osteogenic conditions and compared the obtained results with the staining intensity signals coming from the CPC samples which were incubated in the culture media without cells. We observed positive ARS staining in variants where CPC were incubated in the media. Probably, the ceramics are giving a drop of positive staining via their solubility and release of Ca+ 2 (Fig. S.8 a, b). This assumption aligns with the data obtained from the calcium concentration analysis (Table S.1). Quantification of ARS staining demonstrates higher (p < 0,0001) values of the calcium deposits in the ADSCs cultivated with CPC in osteogenic and control media (except for the H-500/H2O2) compared to the relative controls (Fig S. 8 b). Higher calcium deposits were found in variants where the cells were cultivated with the HT-1, HT-1/H2O and HT-1/H2O2 ceramics under both osteogenic and control conditions (Fig. S.8 b).

The level of secreted osteopontin was higher where ADSCs were cultivated with the analyzed biomaterials under both conditions (Fig. 3a). The highest values of calcium deposits were in the cells cultivated with the HT-1, HT-1/H2O, HT-1/H2O2. The level of secreted OPN was higher in the variants cultivated with the H-500, H-500/H2O, H-500/H2O2, HT-1/H2O, HT-1/H2O2 samples (Fig. 3a). We did not observe any differences in the OPN level in cells’ lysates (Fig. 3b) between the experimental variants. In the variants where cells were cultivated under osteogenic conditions the level of OPN was significantly higher compared to cells cultivated in control media only with HAP (Fig. 3b).

The temporal pattern of the secreted osteopontin (a) and the level of osteopontin in cells lysates (b) detected after 17 days of experiment by ELISA. Differences between control hydroxyapatite-based CPC groups were evaluated using the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). **** - p < 0.0001, ns – not significant

Two-way ANOVA analysis (Additional File 1) revealed significant interactions between the physical and chemical properties of the CPCs and the expression of osteogenic markers, namely, strong relationship between OPN levels and CPC size, porosity, solubility, and Ca/P ratio. The presence of β-TCP was found to have significant effect on OPN (Fig. S. 9 and 10). Increased CPC solubility and the presence of β-TCP in the composition led to decreased osteopontin secretion, higher Ca/P ratio promotes OPN secretion (Fig. S. 9), increasement of the Ca/P ratio promotes higher OPN secretion. The larger granule size and increased open porosity also enhanced OPN secretion by ADSCs (Fig. S.10).

Disscusion

HAP as well as two-phase bioactive ceramics can be combined with autologous SCs, which enhances the scaffolds` osteoinduction and biocompatibility [1, 5–9]. Ceramics` adsorption activity depends on the phase composition and additional treatments with H2O2 or H2O. In general, H2O2 treatment effectively increases absorption activity of CPC compared to the ones treated with a H2O and may be presented as СРС/Н2О2>СРС/Н2О>СРС. Additional washing results in a removal of finely dispersed granules from the surface of the granules and increases its absorption capacity, on the inside of the open pores. The phase composition of CPC also affects the solubility and the adsorption activity of the composites. The two-phase CPC of the HT-500 series have the greatest adsorption activity, which is better than in the HT-1 series, due to the finer structure and the increased surface area of the smaller-size particles. The nano and the micropores facilitate cell differentiation and the interaction of biomaterials with the physiological environment [10, 11], interesting that resorption is positively correlated with mesopores structure. Macropores promote vascularization and the replacement of artificial biomaterial with newly regenerated bone tissue [12–14]. Treatment with H2O2 or H2O increases the interconnected pores network of analyzed CPC improving its biocompatibility and osteoinductive capacity. Porous surface morphology is typically associated with high bioactivity and biocompatibility [14].

We did not observe a significant difference in ADSCs (Fig. S. 4) adhesion and viability (Fig. S. 5), because all of the samples have a similar porous surface structure (Fig. S. 6, S.7). Our results show that the open porosity of hydroxyapatite-based CPC promotes stronger cell adhesion (Fig. S. 6, S.7). Thus, CPC solubility and Ca/P ratio also significantly affected the CPC adhesion activity. The weakening of metabolic activity of ADSCs when co-cultured with the analyzed ceramics may be result of enforced cell attachment and spreading [15].

ALP is marker for early osteogenesis, our results show that ADSCs cultivated on CPC exhibit positive ALP staining indicating early osteogenic differentiation. Moreover, CPC stimulates ALP expression even without specific osteogenic stimulus. This is further supported by the matrix mineralization of ADSCs that corresponds to calcium deposition, together with OPN secretion, osteogenic differentiation of ADSCs is clearly indicated. It is important to note that osteogenic markers are expressed under both conditions: in osteogenic media and control media, suggesting that CPC, analyzed in this work, are able to induce osteogenic differentiation. HT-1 is the most prominent stimulators of calcium accumulation. This is consistent with previous studies, which have shown that β-TCP promotes higher intracellular calcium levels and osteoblast mineralization [16]. However, the osteoinductive capacity of β-TCP is inconsistent non-autoclaved β-TCP does not demonstrate the osteoinductive capacity [17]. We also autoclaved all CPC-based ceramics, however, we did not observe the same level of calcium deposition for cells cultivated with HT-500 samples which also consist of 65% of HAP and 35% of β-TCP. Composites of the HT-1 series may have more suitable particle size and pore sizes for osteoinduction, this is consistent with previous [18]. Our findings suggest that the expression of osteogenic markers correlated with the size and porosity of the CPC. Larger size of ceramics granules and open porosity are associated with higher expression of osteogenic markers, whereas the presence of β-TCP attenuates its. Higher CPC solubility also attenuates the expression of osteogenic markers. CPC of the H-500 series, which consists of 100% HAP and has lower solubility than HT-1 and HT-500, but has the same particle size as HT-500, induces higher calcium deposition than HT-500.

Conclusions

Phase composition of bioactive ceramics affects their microstructure and physicochemical properties, processing the granules with H2O and H2O2 decreased their solubility, changes the porous structure, increase the adsorption activity. Biocompatibility and osteoinductive capacity of CPC depend on physical parameters and phase composition. High open porosity, larger granule`s size, enhanced hydroxyapatite phase are favorable qualities for osteogenic differentiation, while the increased surface area, availability of calcium and phosphate ions provide a suitable environment for ADSCs, promoting higher expression of later osteogenic markers. The CPC of the H-500 and HT-1 series in all used modifications have the optimal combination of properties to be used as a scaffold in combination with patients’ autologous stem cells. These characteristics include high porosity, increased hydroxyapatite phase, and moderate solubility over time, which contribute to the biomaterial’s bioactivity, osteogenic differentiation to promote bone tissue regeneration.

Limitations

A low number of markers were used for ADSCs characterization in this study; definitely, this cell population should be thoroughly characterized. Additional osteogenic markers (such as Osteocalcin) need to be utilized for a more comprehensive assessment of osteogenesis. Moreover, a detailed analysis of signaling cascades regulating osteogenesis, BMP and WNT, will help to understand the molecular mechanisms of the osteogenic effect of CPC. The potential regenerative capacity of CPC coated with cells should be proven in vivo.

Injectable magnetic hydrogel induces multi-programmed cell death and deep tumor regression in magnetic hyperthermia therapy in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Linxue Zhang, Xing He, Hongyu Zhu, Qi Sun, Yu Liu, Lin Huang, Tianlong Wen, Jianfu Li, Zhongwen Lan, Zhong Yu, Zhenglin Yang, Kun Jia, Ke Sun, Ziyan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains global health concern due to complex pathogenic mechanism and lack of effective therapeutic drugs. The magnetic hyperthermia therapy (MHT) appears as the promising and safe strategy during the recent exploration of new technology for cancer treatment. Herein, we reported an injectable and magnetic-hydrogel-construct-based thermal regression agent mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids in MHT for the first time. Percutaneous thermotherapy was employed as a minimally invasive strategy for treating hepatic tumors in a rabbit model. Based on the evidence in vitro and in vivo studies, we demonstrated that mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids under magnetic field, exhibited powerful antitumor effect by inducing massive ROS accumulation, severe lipid peroxidation and damaged mitochondria, which together triggered triple forms of cell death: ferroptosis, autophagy and apoptosis. Furthermore, we revealed that mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids under magnetic field reprogramed the cysteine-glutathione metabolism by suppressing SLC7A11 expression, which aggravated redox imbalance and suppresses cell survival. The hybrids achieve outstanding therapeutic results in mouse and rabbit models. Our findings underscore that mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids under magnetic field is a promising strategy for cancer therapy by targeting the SLC7A11-cysteine-glutathione axis.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the third most prevalent cause of cancer related mortality with rising incidence, represents a profound obstacle to advancing global life expectancy due to its rising incidence and limited treatment options [1]. Conventional therapeutic modalities often suffer from intrinsic shortcomings, including unpredictable treatment outcomes and severe side effects, therefore underscoring the urgent need for innovative and minimally invasive anti-cancer strategies [2,3]. Magnetic hyperthermia therapy (MHT) has emerged as a noninvasive therapeutic modality in biomedical application, utilizing the unique properties of magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) to generate localized heat under external alternating magnetic field (AMF) [4,5], and enabling tumors regression without depth limitations [6]. The ability of guiding MNPs to specific tissues allows for localized and remotely heating under AMF, which confers high anti-tumor efficacy in biological systems [7]. MHT approach provides patients with an opportunity to reduce pain, avoid drug resistance, enhance the therapeutic effect, and significantly improve their quality of life [8].

Among various MNPs, superparamagnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles have emerged as the leading candidate for biomedical field, which are representative inorganic materials approved by FDA supervision organization in biomedical application [9,10]. Superparamagnetic Fe3O4 has obvious advantages of high biological compatibility, superior stability and excellent magnetic responsiveness, serving as magnetic hyperthermia agent for cancer therapy [11,12]. Importantly, the magnetic properties of superparamagnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles can be precisely controlled, because they disappear upon withdrawal of the AMF, ensuring safety and reversibility [13]. Furthermore, Fe3O4 nanoparticles serve as excellent contrast agents for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [14]. This dual functionality enables the real-time monitoring of nanoparticle distribution, precise tumor localization and therapeutic response assessment, significantly enhancing the precision of MHT [15]. However, harnessing the potential of Fe3O4 in MHT requires addressing key challenges, including targeted delivery, localized retention, and controlled release of the nanoparticles at the tumor site. To improve the endogenous safety and functionality, Fe3O4 nanoparticles have been modified with biocompatible coatings [16,17].

To address the above limitations of Fe3O4-mediated MHT, the use of a hydrogel-based delivery platform has been proposed. Hydrogel has been recognized as a promising platform for biomedical applications owing to their tunable physicochemical properties and exceptional biocompatibility [18]. Biodegradable thermo-gelling hydrogels have garnered significant attention because of their reversible sol-gel phase transition in response to temperature variations [19]. This unique property enables them to transform from a liquid state at room temperature to a gel state under physiological conditions, offering potential for minimally invasive drug delivery and localized therapeutic interventions [20]. Building on these advantages, we employed a thermo-gelling hydrogels system based on our previously reported methyl poly (ethylene glycol)-poly (L-valine) (mPEG-b-PLV) diblock copolymer solutions, which has been extensively characterized and validated in our earlier studies [21]. The mPEG-b-PLV hydrogel combines biocompatible and biodegradable polymeric chains, minimizing the risk of adverse immune responses and ensuring metabolic clearance post-functionalization. Crucially, this copolymer spontaneously assembles into stable nano-sized clusters in aqueous media, enabling precise control over hydrogel formation kinetics while maintaining injectability [22]. To harness these properties for cancer therapy, we engineered the mPEG-b-PLV hydrogel as a multifunctional matrix for encapsulating superparamagnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles. This strategic integration addressed two critical challenges in MHT: (i) the hydrogel network physically stabilizes nanoparticle dispersion, preventing aggregation-induced magnetic decay, and (ii) mPEG-b-PLV thermoresponsive sol-gel transition enables in situ gelation at tumor sites, ensuring localized nanoparticle retention under AMF exposure.

In this work, by combining the thermal responsiveness of mPEG-b-PLV with energy conversion efficiency of Fe3O4 nanoparticles (named as mPEG-b-PLV- Fe3O4 hybrids, abbreviated as Hybrids in the following description), we developed a multifunctional platform enabling ultrasound (US)-guided delivery of localized hyperthermia while simultaneously supporting MRI navigation (as illustrated in Scheme 1). Furthermore, in vivo studies demonstrated the substantial therapeutic efficacy of the Hybrids in both SNU423 tumor-bearing mice and the VX2 tumor-bearing rabbit model, highlighting their capability to effectively eradicate local lesions. When applying the Hybrids under AMF for cancer treatment, it exhibited almost 100 % tumoricidal effects on multiple types of cancer cells by inducing ROS production and lipid peroxidation. The Hybrids in MHT owed significant suppressing effect on tumor growth in vivo experiments. Notably, the AFM + Hybrids induced mitochondria-dependent cell death and interrupted cysteine-glutathione dependent redox homeostasis by inhibiting SLC7A11 expression. Furthermore, the AFM + Hybrids therapeutic system provoked a unique triple death effect in vivo, involving in ferroptosis, autophagy and apoptosis. Results and discussion

Fabrication and analysis of uniform Fe3O4 and copolymer mpeg-b-PLV hydrogel

The structural and functional properties of the Fe3O4 nanoparticles and mPEG-b-PLV hydrogel system were systematically characterized to establish their suitability for MHT. TEM analysis (Fig. 1A) revealed uniformly spherical Fe3O4 nanoparticles with high monodispersity. Insert of Fig. 1A provided the high-resolution TEM image of nanoparticle. The 0.25 nm lattice spacing was indexed to (311) plane for cubic spinel structured Fe3O4, which confirmed their crystalline nature. The particle size distribution (Fig. 1B) showed a narrow polydispersity, with an average diameter of 12.71 ± 0.86 nm, which was optimal for both magnetic responsiveness and cellular uptake [23,24] Moreover, the corresponding SAED electron diffraction image (Fig. 1C) further corroborated the crystalline structure, with distinct rings corresponding to the (311) and (200) planes of magnetite Fe3O4 [25]. The XRD pattern in Fig. S1 verifies the phase purity and crystallinity of the as-synthesized Fe3O4. The observed diffraction peaks are consistent with a cubic spinel structure, matching the reference pattern for Fe3O4 (JCPDS No. 19–0629). The magnetic hysteresis loops were measured at 300 K (Fig. 1D), which confirmed that the particles had remarkable superparamagnetic properties, possessing negligible remanence and coercivity, and high saturation magnetization (31.43 emu/g). Those properties of superparamagnetic Fe3O4 nanoparticles ensured the safety and controllability in biomedical applications. The field-cooled (FC) and zero-field-cooled (ZFC) curves (Fig. 1E) showed a blocking temperature (TB) of 35 K, below which the nanoparticles transited to a ferromagnetic state [26]. Characterization and functional analysis of thermosensitive mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 hybrids

The microstructure of the obtained magnetic mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids was shown in Fig. 2A-B, and there was a porous interconnected network structure in the SEM image. Although the Fe3O4 NPs could not be clearly observed in SEM image due to the extremely small size and well-dispersity in mPEG-b-PLV hydrogel network, the uniform distribution of iron in the composite hydrogel was investigated in the energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping (Fig. 2C). The Fe3O4 NPs were well-dispersed in the hydrogel matrix without aggregation can be explained via the strong physical interactions between the functional groups in the hydrogel and Fe3O4 NPs via hydrogen bond. The magnetic properties of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids were further explored in Fig. S2B. Compared with Fe3O4 nanoparticles (Fig. 1D), the saturation magnetization of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids was measured to be 26.18 emu/g. This decrease is attributed to the incorporation of the non-magnetic polymer coating, which is consistent with observations reported in similar nanocomposite systems [27]. As depicted in Fig. 2D, the Fe3O4 NPs had a zeta potential of −8.3 ± 1.3 mV. The small volume and negative surface charge of Fe3O4 NPs were conducive to their nonspecific adsorption on the plasma membrane, thus triggering their endocytosis pathway in cells. In addition, zeta potential value of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids was 3.7 ± 0.8 mV, which indicate Fe3O4 and mPEG-b-PLV were successfully combined to construct mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids. The FTIR spectra of mPEG-b-PLV and mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids were illustrated in Fig. 2E. The most significant adsorption bands in the FTIR spectrum of the mPEG-b-PLV were the stretching vibrations of various C-O groups at 1200 to 1000 cm−1, the carbonyl stretching vibration at 1744 cm−1, the stretching vibrations of C-H groups at 2970 to 2850 cm−1 region, and the bending as well as stretching vibrations of different hydroxyl groups at 1614 and 3350 cm−1, respectively. Compare to mPEG-b-PLV, the FTIR spectra of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 appeared a representative stretching vibration of Fe-O groups at 560 cm−1 regions as a weak band due to low content of Fe3O4 NPs in the mPEG-b-PLV [28,29]. These results confirmed the successful synthesis of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids. To evaluate the thermoresponsive behavior of the copolymer solution, the single and symmetric gel permeation chromatography (GPC) was performed. As shown in Fig. 2F, the abrupt increase in the storage modulus (G’) and loss modulus (G”) was observed for hybrids at 39 °C, indicating a temperature-induced sol-gel transition of the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids. The crossover points of G′ and G″, where the elastic behavior surpasses the viscous response, is widely recognized as the gelation temperature [30]. Theranostic performance of mpeg-b-PLV-Fe3O4 hybrids: integrated magnetic hyperthermia and MRI contrast enhancement

To evaluate the therapeutic and diagnostic potential of the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids system for MHT, we conducted comprehensive in vitro and in vivo experiments to assess its thermal performance and MRI contrast capabilities. Infrared thermal imaging (Fig. 3A) revealed a concentration-dependent temperature increase under AMF (324 Oe, 270 kHz), with the concentration (500 and 1000 μg/mL) achieving temperatures above 41 °C within 5 min, a critical threshold for effective hyperthermia. Quantitative analysis of the heating curves showed a linear relationship between mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids concentration and temperature elevation rate (Fig. 3B), further confirmed the rapid and sustained heating capability of the hybrids system. The in vivo performance of the hydrogel system was particularly noteworthy, with 12–15 °C increased localized temperature at the injection site by subcutaneous administration (Fig. 3C), which ensured therapeutic efficacy at the tumor site. As MRI contrast agent, in vitro studies demonstrated that mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids exhibited a strong concentration-dependent T2 contrast enhancement (Fig. 3D). The transverse relaxation rate (1/T2) showed a linear correlation with Fe concentration, confirming mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids can be used as a suitable contrast enhancing agent in MRI. In vivo MRI studies revealed that mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids sustained contrast enhancement at the injection site over a 24-h period (Fig. 3E), indicating excellent in vivo stability and retention of the Hybrids system. Inspired by the results from the mouse tumor model, we further investigated the T2 imaging capability of the Hybrids in a deeper VX2 orthotopic liver tumor model in rabbits. As shown in Fig. 3F, Hybrids were injected into the tumor site under ultrasound guidance, and T2-weighted MRI scans were performed at 0, 3, and 24 h using a 3.0 T MRI scanner which the T2-weighed image became darker. The results demonstrated that Hybrids exhibited excellent T2 contrast enhancement, with clear tumor delineation visible throughout the 24-h monitoring period. Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment triggers intense cell death in cancer cells

To examine the lethal effect of Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment on cancer cells, we treated SNU423 cells individually with DMSO as control, ferroptosis inducer HG-106 (10 μM) as positive control, Hybrids, AMF and AMF + Hybrids as method described; and sent for TEM scanning after 24 h’ treatment. Based on TEM results (Fig. 4A), the Fe3O4 nanoparticles was observed in cells (red arrow in Fig. 4A). Further, we found that AMF + Hybrids promoted intensely and diverse cell death, including ferroptosis as HG-106 with shrunken and small mitochondria (red circle), and apoptosis characterized by apoptotic bodies (purple arrow). To verify this phenomenon, we detected ROS production, lipid peroxidation and cell death by flow cytometry. Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment absolutely induced massive ROS accumulation, caused severe lipid peroxidation and almost 100 percent cell death, which were more intensely than HG-106 (Fig. 4B–D). To further verify the powerful effect of promoting cell death in other kinds of cancer cells, we applied Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment respectively in lung, HCC and prostate cancer cells, such as H1299, A549, HCC-97H, HCC-LM3, LNCaP and C4-2 cells. The experimental results displayed that Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment could violently boost ROS production, and led to up to 100 % cell death (Fig. 4E and F). When the mice xenografted with these cells attained an average tumor diameter of around 0.35 cm, the mice were randomly assigned to five groups for respective treatment as method described. While the tumor size reached 1.5 cm diameter and a volume up to 2000 mm3, the mice were sacrificed and carried out for the histopathology analysis. The Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment did not change the body weight compared with the control group (Fig. S3A), but was associated with a significant reduction in tumor size. By analyzing the TEM results of tumors, we further confirmed that AMF + Hybrids could provoke powerful cell death, including apoptosis, ferroptosis and autophagy (Fig. 5D). Further, in view of the mitochondria from TEM, we noticed that both AMF, HG-106 and Hybrids plus AMF made the mitochondria smaller; but Hybrids got larger mitochondria after magnetic hyperthermia treatment (Fig. 5E). Above results implied that the AMF + Hybrids-triggered cell death had close relation with the structure and function change of mitochondria. Thereafter, we observed that AMF + Hybrids caused the mitochondrial damage by monitoring the change of mitochondrial membrane potential using JC-1 staining (Fig. 5F). Furthermore, we examined the effect of AFM + Hybrids on mitochondrial respiration in SNU423 cells. The respiratory assay showed that AMF + Hybrids and AMF only significantly inhibited the basal respiration. The maximal respiration and spare respiratory capacity were all decreased after three kinds of treatment (AMF, Hybrids and AMF + Hybrids) (Fig. 5G and S3C). But the proton leak was only declined in AMF + Hybrids group (Fig. S3C). These findings together indicate that AMF + Hybrids might promote cell death by impairing the mitochondrial structure to elevate ROS production and insult redox balance in cancer cells. To assess the retention capability of the hydrogel system, both the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids and bare Fe3O4 nanoparticles were intratumorally injected. As shown in Fig. S4A, the group of Hybrids exhibited significantly higher iron levels compared to the Fe3O4 nanoparticle. Furthermore, Prussian blue-stained tumor sections (Fig. S4B–2C) revealed substantially more iron deposits in the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids than in Fe3O4, confirming that the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrid's thermoresponsive hydrogel enhanceed intratumoral nanoparticle retention. This improved retention directly converted into more effective MHT, as the localized nanoparticle accumulation enabled stronger and more sustained tumor heating under AMF, leading to enhanced tumor ablation. Additionally, the group of AMF-only led to an increase in the percentage of Ki67 positive cells in tumors (Fig. S5). The in vivo mouse experiments confirmed that the AMF-only indeed promote tumor growth. To better clarify the individual roleof ferroptosis, apoptosis, and autophagy, rescue experiments were conducted using specific inhibitors ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1), Z-VAD, and chloroquine (CQ), each applied separately to restore function. As shown in Fig. S6A, Fer-1 markedly restored cell viability in the AMF + Hybrids group, whereas Z-VAD and CQ provided only minor protective effects, indicating that ferroptosis represented the predominant mode of cell death. Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining of tumor tissues (Fig. S6B–D) revealed markedly stronger Cleaved Caspase-3 and LC3 signals in the AMF + Hybrids group compared with other groups, confirming the simultaneous activation of apoptosis and autophagy. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that AMF + Hybrids treatment triggers multiple programmed cell death pathways, with ferroptosis serving as the dominant contributor. Furthermore, these results indicate that theAMF + Hybrids group can sucessfully reduce tumor cells, further verifying the treatment's effectiveness for HCC. Together, these in vivo evidences demonstrate that Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment could be a promising therapeutic approach for cancer.

In vivo evaluation of ultrasound guided hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment

To evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of ultrasound-guided hyperthermia-based tumor suppression for deep-seated tumor, a VX2 orthotopic liver tumor model in rabbits was established. As illustrated in Fig. 6A, the treatment strategy involved percutaneous injection of magnetic Hybrids under ultrasound guidance, followed by AMF-induced mild hyperthermia for tumor suppression. The ultrasound-guided injection was illustrated in Fig. 6B, showing the positioning of the rabbit and placement of the ultrasound probe during the percutaneous procedure. As depicted in Fig. 6C, ultrasound-guided percutaneous injection of the Hybrids was performed to ensure precise delivery into the tumor site (white line). Real-time ultrasound imaging allowed accurate needle placement (red arrow), and the injection process was monitored dynamically, the extrudates formed were delineated by blue dashed line. Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia treatment reshapes cysteine metabolism through promoting the protein degradation of SLC7A11

The novel therapeutic strategy employing thermosensitive magnetic hydrogel Hybrids (mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4) has demonstrated the powerful antitumor effect by trigging triple cell death via disrupting the redox homeostasis. To explore the mechanism of anti-cancer by Hybrids-mediated magnetic hyperthermia, we treated SNU423 cells as described above, then performed GC/TOF-MS-based metabolomics analysis. Heatmap analysis revealed that AMF + Hybrids got a marked difference in the metabolic signatures, compared with other groups (Fig. 7A). The pathway enrichment analysis of the altered metabolites further demonstrated that the glutathione and cysteine metabolism pathways were the most significantly affected (Fig. S9). As indicated in Fig. 7B, the critical intermediates of glutathione metabolism in SNU423 cells were significantly downregulated, including cysteine, glycine and γ-glutamate-cysteine. As a major rate-limiting factor in the process of glutathione biosynthesis, cystine is imported by the cystine/glutamate antiporter SLC7A11 (Solute Carrier Family 7 Member 11). Hence, we further quantified intercellular cystine levels in H1299, LM3 and C4-2 cells. As expected, compared with the control group, AMF + Hybrids exhibited much lower levels of Na + -independent-cystine uptake, along with a notable decline in the production of reductive glutathione (GSH) (Fig. 7C-D). Except for cysteine from medium, cells can synthesize cysteine by the de novo transsulfuration pathway (TSP). The results showed the SLC7A11 mRNA were all decreased by treating with Hybrids, AMF or AMF + Hybrids (Fig. 7E), but the protein expression of SLC7A11 was only significantly declined in AMF + Hybrids-treated group (Fig. 7F). Conclusions

In this study, we successfully synthesized thermoresponsive injectable magnetic hydrogel platform-mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids, which were firstly constructed in MHT. Additionally, mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids realized sol-gel transition at 39 °C under AMF exposure, enhancing retention time at tumor sites. In addition, the developed Hybrids presented excellent magnetothermal properties reached over 41 °C which was suitable for mild hyperthermia. Strikingly, mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids exhibited broad-spectrum anti-cancer efficacy while getting activation under magnetic field. Mechanistic investigations revealed that the mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids induced mitochondria-dependent triple cell death (ferroptosis, autophagy and apoptosis). Furthermore, we identified that the Hybrids decreased SLC7A11 expression to repress cancer cells’ survival, which could restrict normal cysteine metabolism and disrupt GSH antioxidant system. By synergistically integrating MHT with MRI-guided visualization, the system established a closed-loop theranostic paradigm capable of spatially and temporally controlled tumor regression. This work not only presented a significant advancement in cancer theranostics, but also provided new insights into the molecular mechanism of magnetic hyperthermia-mediated cell death.

Experimental section

Materials: Propidium iodide (CA1040, Solarbio), BODIPY 581/591C11(D3861, Thermo), DCFH-DA (CA1410, Solardio), FerroOrange (F374, DOJINDO). GSH (ml076450, Mlbio), HG106 (T67839, TargetMol), L-valine (Biosharp), α-methoxy-ω-hydroxyl-poly (mPEG, Mn = 1000 Da) (Aladdin), Anhydrous chloroform (Aladdin), N,N-dimethylformamide (Aladdin), Toluene (Aladdin), iron acetylacetonate (Sigma), oleyl alcohol (Sigma), 1-octadecene (Sigma) and benzyl ether (Sigma) were carried out according the manufacturers’ procedures.

Synthesis of Fe3O4 nanoparticles: The monodispersed Fe3O4 nanoparticles were synthesized via thermal decomposition method [31]. Briefly, iron oxyhydroxide (0.17 g) and oleic acid (2.2 g) were dissolved in 6 mL 1-octadecene at 25 °C. The mixture stirred vigorously and heated to 200 °C for 2 h under N2 atmosphere. The temperature was then raised to 320 °C and maintained for 1 h. After cooling to room temperature, Fe3O4 nanocrystals were precipitated by adding 25 mL ethanol. The Fe3O4 nanocrystals were separated by centrifugation and dispersed in 10 mL toluene.

Synthesis of copolymer mPEG-b-PLV: The copolymer N-Carboxy anhydride of L-valine (Val-NCA) and α-methoxy-ω-amino-poly (ethylene glycol) (mPEGNH2) named as mPEG-b-PLV was prepared according to the previous procedure [21]. Typically, the copolymer was synthesized by Val-NCA ring-opening in the presence of mPEG-NH2. The synthesis process as follows: under argon atmosphere, 1 g of mPEG22-NH2 was dissolved into the 18 mL of the mixture of N, N- dimethylformamide and trichloromethane (1:2 v/v). Then, 1 g LV-NCA mixed to the above liquid and stirred at room temperature for 24 h. Next, a large amount of frozen ether was added to the reaction mixture to precipitate and then dried in vacuum. Finally, copolymers mPEG-b-PLV was obtained with a final yield of approximately 80 %.

Synthesis of mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4: mPEG-b-PLV (0.1 g) was dissolved in DMF (5 mL), then Fe3O4 toluene solution (0.1 mL, 2 mg Fe/mL) with a concentration of 10 mg/mL was added to the mixture and homogenized the above liquid by oscillator. The reaction mixture precipitated by frozen ether and dried to obtain the mPEG-b-PLV- Fe3O4 Hybrids.

Magnetic hyperthermia performance: To assess the magnetic hyperthermia performance of the Hybrids, aqueous solutions were prepared at different concentrations (200, 500, and 1000 μg/mL). The solutions were then subjected to AMF with parameters of H = 324 Oe and f = 270 kHz. Each sample was exposed to the AMF for 300 s using the MIS Hyperthermia System. The temperature changes during AMF exposure were continuously tracked with an infrared thermometer to assess the heat generation. This data offers valuable insights into their potential effectiveness for hyperthermia therapy. Cell and animal experiments were also conducted under the same magnetic field conditions.

Cell culture: HCC-LM3, HCC-97H, HCC-SNU423, H1299, A549, LNCaP and C4-2 cells originated from ATCC were purchased from Lookbio (Shanghai, China), which were identified by short tandem repeats and tested negative for mycoplasma by PCR before entering our laboratory in May 2017. Cells were maintained in DMEM medium containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS).

Cell viability test: SNU-423 cells were seeded into 96-well plates with different treated. After a 24-h incubation, cells were allocated into corresponding groups. To assess the type of cell death, inhibitors including Fer-1, Z-VAD, and CQ (MedChemExpress) were applied. Cell viability was quantified by measuring absorbance at 450 nm. The assays were performed in five independent experiments (n = 5).

Western blotting: Cells or tissues were lysed in RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail. After the protein concentration was normalized by BCA Protein Quantitative Kit, the samples were separated by SDS-PAGE, immunoblotted with the indicated antibodies. Anti-GAPDH (1:1000 60004-1-Ig) and anti-SLC7A11 (1:1000, 26864-1-AP) were purchased from Proteintech (Rosemount, IL, USA).

Flow cytometry: Cell death was detected by propidium iodide staining. Lipid peroxidation was measured by BODIPY 581/591C11. The reactive oxygen species (ROS) production was detected by DCFH-DA staining. FerroOrange was used for the cellular Fe2+ level determination. The mitochondrial membrane potential was detected by JC-1 staining. All experiments were carried out at least in triplicate.

Immunofluorescence staining: Mouse tumor tissues were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 4 μm slices. Sections were deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was performed using sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0), followed by cooling to room temperature. After washing three times with PBS, the sections were blocked in PBS containing 5 % goat serum and 0.1 % Triton X-100 for 45 min. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Caspase-3 and LC3 (Invitrogen). After PBS washing, Alexa Fluor 488- or 555-conjugated rabbit antibodies (Invitrogen) were applied for 1 h, and nuclei were counterstained with DAPI-containing mounting medium (Vector Laboratories, CA, USA). Fluorescent images were acquired using a Zeiss fluorescence microscope. Quantification of fluorescence intensity was performed using Image J software by setting threshold density levels for measurement.

Fe content analysis of tumor: Tumor-bearing mice (n = 4 per group) were intratumorally injected with Fe3O4 nanoparticles and mPEG-b-PLV-Fe3O4 Hybrids, respectively (Fe content of 5 mg/mL) (n = 4 per group). The applied external magnetic field condition is H = 324 Oe and f = 270 kHz for 15 min. Tumors were harvested after 48 h post-injection, digested in acid, and the iron content was measured using ICP-OES.

Histology: Animals were sacrificed at the endpoint of the experiments, and tumor tissues were harvested. The samples were processed, stained with Prussian Blue, and examined histologically under light microscopy to evaluate colloidal iron deposition.

TEM assay: Cells and tissue samples from individual groups were sent to the Chengdu Lilai Biotechnology Co. Ltd for transmission electron microscopy according their standard protocols. Sections were examined with JEM-1400-FLASH transmission electron microscope. The cells with apoptosis, ferroptosis or autophagy state were analysed based on TEM images.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR: Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol (Solarbio, R1100). The cDNA synthesis was conducted using HiScript III qRTSuperMix (Vazyme, R333-01). The qRT-PCR assay was performed with SYBR Green (Vazyme, Q711-02) following the manufacturer's protocols. A list of the primers (Qingke, Chengdu, China) used in this study is provided in Table S3.

Detection of Amino acid and its metabolites: 6 independent samples (at least 1 × 107 cells per sample) were prepared for each group. The samples were collected and sent to MetWare Technologies for metabolomics analyses (http://www.metware.cn/) based on the AB Sciex QTRAP 6500 LC-MS/MS platform according the standard protocol. Identified metabolites were annotated using KEGG compound database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/compound/). Significantly regulated metabolites between groups were determined by absolute Log2FC (fold change ≥1.0), and their significance was determined by hypergeometric test's P-Values. The raw data of amino acid metabolomics reported in this paper were deposited in the OMIX database (access number: OMIX007522).

Detection of mitochondrial respiratory function: Mitochondrial respiratory function in HCC-SNU423 cells was assessed using an Oroboros Oxygraph-2k (Innsbruck, Austria) in a temperature-controlled chamber. The cells were treated as indicated, with 1 × 106 cells used to measure oxygen consumption rates. After equilibration, oligomycin (5 nM), FCCP (0.5 μM), and Antimycin A (2.5 μM) were sequentially introduced to inhibit ATPase, complex I, and complex III, respectively. These inhibitors were obtained from Huawei Corporation (Cat:023004). Data were collected using DatLab acquisition software 5.2 (Oroboros Instruments, Innsbruck, Austria).

Biochemical index detection: Cell samples were obtained according detection requirement. The GSH content was tested with Beyotime's GSH/GSSH kit in accordance with the instructions. The capacity of cystine uptake was carried out according to the instructions of kit from Elabscience.

MRI assessment: MRI was conducted to evaluate the imaging performance of the Hybrids and to monitor tumor size in the mice and rabbit model. Hybrids containing different Fe concentrations (0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, and 0.50 mM) were evaluated using a 0.5 T MRI scanner (Niuman) equipped. For T2-weighted image acquisition, the parameters were as follows: TR = 2000 ms, TE = 60 ms, slice thickness = 2.50 mm, FOV = 150 mm ∗ 150 mm. For in vivo (mice) imaging, mice were intratumorally injected with the Hybrids, and MRI scans were conducted using the same 0.5 T Niuman system. Tumor regions were monitored at 3, 12, and 24 h post-injection, and corresponding imaging data were recorded for analysis. The rabbits were underwent anaesthetized MRI 14th days after treatment. MRI was performed by MRI scanner (Discovery MR758, 3.0 T) with an eight-channel surface phased array coil. The rabbits were subjected to the following T2-weighted sequences: TR = 2800 ms, TE = 95 ms, a slice thickness of 5.0 mm, FOV = 210∗210 mm, matrix = 256∗192, and average = 4.

Ultrasound-assisted magnetic hyperthermia therapy: To evaluate the therapeutic effect of Hybrids on deeply located tumors under AMF, VX2 tumor-bearing rabbits were used. The tumors were recorded by ultrasound (Philips EPIQ) on day 10 post-implantation. Then, the rabbits were injection with Hybrids (500 μL) onto the hepatic tumor via ultrasound-guided percutaneous puncture. After injection of Hybrids, the rabbits were exposed to AMF (H = 324 Oe, f = 270 kHz) for 15 min. Tumor progression was monitored by ultrasound and MRI over a 14-day period following treatment. The rabbits were sacrificed, and tumor tissues were acquired for further analysis. The untreated group of VX2 tumor-bearing rabbits had no treatment.

Animal experiments: All animal experiments were approved by the Ethical Review Committees of Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital and University of Electronic Science and Technology of China. HCC-SNU423 cells were inoculated by injecting 2.5 × 106 cells subcutaneously in the right flank of average 8 weeks-old male SCID mice (Purchased from Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd). The maximum width (W) and length (L) of the tumor were measured every other day by external caliper, and the volume (V) was calculated using the formula: V = (W2L)/2 [32]. To assess the efficiency of Hybrids mediated magnetic hyperthermia in vivo, mice whose tumor diameter reached around 0.35 cm, were divided into 5 groups respectively, including control, HG106, AMF, Hybrids and AMF + Hybrids (n = 4 animals per group). The applied external magnetic field condition is H = 324 Oe and f = 270 kHz for 15 min. The Hybrids solutions (5 mg/mL) were administered intravenously every two days, with AMF irradiation performed every three days until the 20th day. The tumor volume was monitored under 2000 mm3, and the average diameter of tumors was smaller than 1.5 cm. To establish the VX2 tumor model, a tumor tissue suspension was directly implanted into the livers of rabbits (New Zealand white). On day 10 post-implantation, liver tumors were observed and recorded by ultrasound. If any tumor was beyond the above criteria, the mouse was immediately sacrificed and the whole experiment was end. Mice were all anaesthetized with CO2, tumors were taken out for photograph and measurement of weight, followed by histology analysis.

Statistical analysis: All studies were conducted with a minimum of three repetitions or triple samples. Data were presented as means ± S.D. Data were analysed with GraphPad Prism software (version 7; GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA). Differences between groups were analysed by two-tailed unpaired Student t-test. One-way ANOVA test was used for analysing the difference among multi-groups. For statistical correlation, Pearson's correlation coefficient was used. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. P values for all data were shown in each figure panel.

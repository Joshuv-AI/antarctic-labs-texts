Assessment of two feed additives consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 and Lacticaseibacillus paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 for all animal species (Pioneer Hi‐Bred International, Inc)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Joana Revez, Rosella Brozzi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing the requests from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the follow‐up application for renewal of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 and Lacticaseibacillus paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 as silage additives for all animal species. The additives are currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species (1k20725 and 1k20735, respectively). In the previous opinions, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the full characterisation of the additives under assessment due to absence of data regarding the microbiological contamination and chemical impurities. The applicant provided new data which do not raise safety concerns allowing the Panel to conclude on the characterisation of the products.Keywords: Lactiplantibacillus paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135, Lactiplantibacillus plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139, safety, silage additives, technological additives

Additional information

The additives L. plantarum (formerly Lactobacillus plantarum) ATCC PTA‐6139 and L. paracasei (formerly Lactobacillus paracasei) ATCC PTA‐6135 are currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species (1k20725 5 and 1k20735, 6 respectively).

EFSA adopted two opinions on the safety and efficacy for L. plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012a, 2024a) and two for L. paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2024b) when used in feed for all animal species.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of supplementary information 7 to the previous applications on the same products. 8 The dossiers were both received on 6/1/2026 and the general information and supporting documentation are available on Open.EFSA at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2026‐00002 and https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2026‐00003, respectively.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety of L. plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 and L. paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

The additives L. plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 and L. paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 are currently authorised as technological additives (functional group: silage additives) for use in fresh plant material for all animal species. These additives were characterised in two previous assessments of the FEEDAP Panel dealing with the renewal of the authorisation, and their safety for the target species, consumer and environment was established. Moreover, the Panel concluded that the additives are potential skin and respiratory sensitisers but could not conclude on their eye irritation potential (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a, 2024b). However, no data on microbial contamination and impurities on the additives under assessment, necessary to fully characterise them, were provided.

The applicant has now submitted supplementary information to address the limitations regarding the characterisation of the additives.

Characterisation of the additives

Both additives are currently authorised as a preparation containing the active agent at a minimum concentration of 1 × 1010 CFU/g. In the current submissions, the applicant provided new data on the microbial contamination 10 and the impurities 11 of the additives based on three batches. 12 These data are reported in Table 3.

The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbiological contamination and the amounts of the detected chemical impurities do not raise safety concerns.

CONCLUSIONS

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additives L. plantarum ATCC PTA‐6139 and L. paracasei ATCC PTA‐6135 under assessment are now characterised regarding the microbiological contamination and chemical impurities, which do not raise safety concerns.

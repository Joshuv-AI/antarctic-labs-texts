Cardiac tamponade caused by a paused mechanical chest compression device after cardiac arrest
Authors: Tommaso Scquizzato, Savino Altizio, Evgeny Fominskiy, Anna Mara Scandroglio
Published: 2025

We report a previously unrecognized complication of mechanical chest compression: right ventricular (RV) tamponade caused by persistent external compression from the piston of a mechanical chest compression device paused and left in place after return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC).

A 67-year-old man suffered a witnessed out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA). Bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) was initiated immediately. A basic life support (BLS) team arrived after 6 min, confirmed cardiac arrest, and initiated CPR. The initial rhythm was non-shockable. Sixteen minutes after collapse, an advanced life support (ALS) team found pulseless electrical activity and initiated ALS. The patient received 5 mg of intravenous adrenaline, was intubated, and mechanical CPR was started using the LUCAS 3 (Stryker) device. End-tidal CO2 was 32 mmHg after 20 min of ALS. Return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) was achieved after 36 min, and the patient was transported to our cardiac arrest centre in Milan, Italy,1 arriving 61 min after cardiac arrest.

On arrival, the patient had sustained ROSC with severe cardiogenic shock. The LUCAS device was in place and paused. Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) was performed as part of the initial assessment and revealed marked RV compression with severely impaired diastolic filling, in the absence of pericardial effusion or other typical causes of tamponade (Supplemental Videos 1 and 2). At visual inspection, the piston of the LUCAS device was found partially locked in a depressed position against the sternum. This exerted continuous pressure over the anterior RV wall, causing extrinsic compression and tamponade physiology (Fig. 1A). Immediate retraction and removal of the piston resulted in prompt improvement in RV filling and function, as confirmed by TOE (Fig. 1B).

Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) before and after release of the LUCAS device piston. Panel A shows TOE image obtained immediately upon arrival, demonstrating severe right ventricular (RV) tamponade. Panel B shows repeat TOE following retraction and removal of the piston, showing prompt restoration of RV filling and normalization of chamber dimensions.

To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of RV tamponade from persistent external compression by a paused mechanical CPR piston post-ROSC. A systematic search of PubMed and Embase did not identify any previous descriptions of this complication. While complications such as skeletal injuries and organ lacerations from mechanical CPR have been previously described,2 this mechanism of persistent extrinsic RV compression causing tamponade physiology has not.

Given the widespread use of piston-based mechanical CPR devices in both prehospital and in-hospital settings,3 this case highlights an important and previously overlooked, and possibly underrecognized, risk. This case suggests that paused devices left in place after ROSC should be actively assessed to ensure the piston is not applying sustained pressure to the chest to prevent iatrogenic cardiac compression and circulatory compromise.

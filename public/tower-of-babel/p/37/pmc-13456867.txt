ALPPS for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma in a patient with cirrhosis and cardiopulmonary comorbidities: a case report
Authors: Wenqing Gu, Guanming Chen, Ling Yang, Jiancun Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Perihilar cholangiocarcinoma accounts for over 50% of biliary tract cancers, with only 35% eligible for curative resection, the sole long-term survival option. Yet cirrhotic patients require >40% FLR to avoid post-hepatectomy liver failure. Associating liver partition and portal vein ligation for staged hepatectomy (ALPPS) induces rapid FLR hypertrophy, yet its use in cirrhotic patients with cardiopulmonary comorbidities remains rarely reported. We report a successful high-risk open ALPPS case.Presentation of case:A 68-year-old woman with 20-year hepatitis B cirrhosis presented with a liver mass. Preoperative FLR was 37.17% of standard liver volume (SLV), below the 40% cirrhotic threshold. Comorbidities included coronary disease, emphysema, interstitial fibrosis, and ICG-R15 of 30%. ALPPS stage 1 (portal vein ligation plus in situ split) was performed on 29 April 2025. By postoperative day 13, left liver volume increased by 34% to >48% of SLV, enabling stage 2 right hemihepatectomy. At the 12-month follow-up, she remained recurrence-free.Discussion:This case demonstrates that ALPPS can effectively induce FLR hypertrophy and achieve R0 resection in high-risk patients with cirrhosis and cardiopulmonary insufficiency, albeit with a substantially elevated complication risk. The open approach retains its value, and multidisciplinary management remains essential.Conclusion:In strictly selected high-risk patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, ALPPS offers an effective curative strategy. Thorough functional liver reserve assessment and robust perioperative complication management are mandatory.

Introduction

Perihilar cholangiocarcinoma accounts for more than 50% of all biliary tract cancers. Curative surgical resection remains the only treatment for long-term survival[1,2]. Yet only ~35% of patients are resectable at diagnosis due to late presentation and its hilar location [1,3]. Resectability is constrained by future liver remnant (FLR) volume: a 25–30% FLR is safe in healthy livers, while cirrhotic patients require >40% to prevent post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF)[4,5].

Portal vein embolization (PVE) induces FLR hypertrophy but is limited by long intervals and growth failure[6], Associating liver partition and portal vein ligation for staged hepatectomy (ALPPS) enables rapid FLR hypertrophy, expanding resectability for advanced tumors[7]. However, ALPPS carries substantial morbidity and mortality, with a 14.4% 90-day mortality in a large multicenter cohort, rising to 24.5% for primary liver tumors[8]. Its use in hepatitis B cirrhosis is particularly challenging, as fibrosis impairs regeneration and elevates complication risk[9,10]. Reports of ALPPS in patients with concurrent cardiopulmonary disease are rare. Furthermore, for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, bile leakage and secondary sepsis after ALPPS are particularly problematic, easily triggering fatal liver failure, with a reported major morbidity rate of 43% and a PHLF rate of 23%[11]. While minimally invasive ALPPS reduces trauma[12], open surgery offers superior intraoperative precision and safety in high-risk cirrhotic patients with cardiopulmonary comorbidities[13]. Importantly, volumetric gain does not equal functional recovery; stage-2 timing must integrate functional assessment with volumetry[14,15].

We report a successful open ALPPS case in a patient with hepatitis B cirrhosis, perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, and cardiopulmonary comorbidities, with insights into perioperative decision-making and management.

This case report has been presented in line with the SCARE checklist[16].

Case presentation

A 68-year-old female with 20-year hepatitis B-related cirrhosis (on long-term entecavir with undetectable viral load) presented with a hepatic space-occupying lesion. Comorbidities included grade 3 hypertension, coronary atherosclerotic heart disease, bilateral pulmonary emphysema, and interstitial pulmonary fibrosis. She had no prior abdominal surgery, allergies, or smoking/alcohol history. Abdominal physical examination was unremarkable.

Preoperative laboratory tests: creatinine 66.2 μmol/L, AFP 1.79 ng/mL, PIVKA-II 26 mAU/mL, CA19-9 44.69U/ml. Liver function: albumin 32.5 g/L, total bilirubin 36.7 μmol/L, ALT 8.3 U/L, PT 14.1 s, cholinesterase 3669 U/L, blood ammonia 78.5 μmol/L. Indocyanine green retention rate at 15 min (ICG-R15) was 30%. Child–Pugh class B. Abdominal contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) revealed a 1.9-cm enhancing hilar nodule invading the hepatic duct confluence and right hepatic duct (Bismuth-Corlette type (Fig. 1). Echocardiography showed mild valvular regurgitation (EF 65%). Chest CT showed bilateral interstitial changes and multifocal emphysema. Coronary CTA revealed single-vessel mild stenosis. Volume assessment: Three-dimensional reconstruction: Total liver volume: 718.21 mL. Standard liver volume (SLV; by Urata formula): 728.5 mL. FLR: 270.88 mL (37.2% of SLV), insufficient for safe right hepatectomy in a cirrhotic patient (<40%).

Preoperative diagnosis: (1) Perihilar cholangiocarcinoma (cTNM stage IA); (2) Decompensated hepatitis B cirrhosis (Child–Pugh B); (3) Coronary heart disease; (4) Emphysema; (5) Grade 3 hypertension.

Surgical Procedure: Stage-1 (29 April 2025) Surgeon: Right portal vein ligation and in-situ liver partition were performed via right subcostal incision by a senior hepatobiliary surgeon. No distant metastasis was identified. The right hepatic artery and duct were preserved; the main right portal vein trunk was ligated and divided. Liver parenchyma was transected along the ischemic line down to the inferior vena cava, with the middle hepatic vein preserved (Fig. 2). Operative time: 195 min, blood loss: 50 mL, no transfusion required. Interval: On postoperative day 8, CT showed FLR at 364.78 mL (44.5% of SLV). Functional assessment yielded Child-Pugh score 7 (grade B), ALBI score −1.755 (grade 2), and PALBI score −2.045 (grade 3). Given potential discordance between volumetric hypertrophy and functional recovery, stage 2 was deferred to POD 13 when both volume and functional parameters were deemed tolerable.

Stage-2 (12 May 2025): Right hepatectomy. The right liver appeared ischemic; the left liver was enlarged with no nodules. The previously marked right hepatic artery and duct were ligated. The right hepatic vein was ligated, and parenchymal transection was completed. Operative time: 160 min, blood loss: 50 mL, plasma: 580 mL infused.

Perioperative Cardiopulmonary Management: Preoperatively, hepatoprotective therapy was administered, and baseline cardiopulmonary medications (isosorbide mononitrate, compound methoxyphenamine) were maintained until 1 day before surgery. General anesthesia used hepatically sparing agents and lung-protective ventilation; intraoperative low central venous pressure was sustained to reduce bleeding while preserving coronary perfusion. Post-stage 1, cardiac monitoring, oxygen supplementation, and respiratory exercises were initiated to ensure uneventful progression to stage 2. Post-stage 2, the patient was admitted to the intensive care unit intubated for mechanical ventilation with strict slightly negative fluid balance to prevent pulmonary edema and myocardial ischemia. She was extubated 10 hours postoperatively, with continued hemodynamic monitoring, respiratory support, and coronary vasodilator therapy.

Postoperative course: After stage-1, the patient had a mild fever and leukocytosis, which normalized by day 4. Liver function gradually improved. On day 8, total bilirubin was 33.7 μmol/L, and albumin was 32.5 g/L. FLR volume increased by 34.7% to 50.1% of SLV.

After stage-2, the patient experienced respiratory insufficiency (Clavien-Dindo grade IVa, single-organ dysfunction) that resolved after successful extubation. International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) grade B bile leakage from the transection site appeared on POD 5 and persisted for over 1 week, managed with adequate drainage. ISGLS grade B post-hepatectomy liver failure developed on POD 9 (peak total bilirubin 131.6 μmol/L, PT 17.9 s, blood ammonia 82.4 μmol/L), treated with intensive supportive care (daily plasma infusion, hepatoprotectants, choleretics, albumin) without invasive intervention, liver function gradually improved to Child–Pugh B by 2 months postoperatively.

Pathology: Gross specimen (Fig. 3). Diagnosis: Adenocarcinoma, consistent with cholangiocarcinoma (small duct type); negative margins; cirrhosis. Immunohistochemistry: CK19+, EMA+, HBsAg+, HBcAg+. +. The surgical drain was removed 2 months after discharge once output ceased. No adjuvant chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy was administered, as postoperative grade B liver failure and bile leakage with prolonged liver function recovery precluded tolerance to standard regimens. Regular 3-month surveillance was adopted. At 1-year follow-up, the patient remained recurrence-free with stable liver function. Figure 3.Gross specimen of the resected right hemiliver. Discussion

Surgical resection remains the only curative option for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, but most patients present at an advanced stage, and only a minority are candidates for surgery[1,10,17]. For cirrhotic patients, an FLR <40% of SLV precludes safe major hepatectomy[5]. Both PVE and ALPPS are established FLR hypertrophy strategies, but cirrhosis slows regenerative capacity: PVE requires 4–8 weeks and leaves 15–20% of patients unresectable due to insufficient growth or tumor progression, a risk amplified by obstructive jaundice[10,17]. ALPPS achieves faster hypertrophy and higher resection rates, yet fibrosis increases perioperative liver failure risk, and volumetric gains often overestimate functional recovery[10,17]. For this patient with hepatitis B cirrhosis, hilar cholangiocarcinoma, and obstructive jaundice, the long PVE interval was clinically unfavorable, so ALPPS was selected with optimized perioperative management. Preoperatively, the patient had 37.2% FLR, poor functional reserve (ICG R15 30%), and cardiopulmonary comorbidities, conferring high procedural risk. ALPPS induced 34% FLR growth within 13 days, enabling safe right hepatectomy with R0 resection. This confirms ALPPS efficacy in cirrhotic livers, consistent with Hung et al. (2024), who reported comparable hypertrophy rates and a growth plateau around day 14 in fibrotic/cirrhotic cohorts[9].

ALPPS-related complications remain clinically significant; liver failure is a leading cause of 90-day mortality[8]. Our patient developed grade B liver failure and grade B bile leakage after stage-2, reflecting the high-risk nature of the procedure. Liver fibrosis is an important risk factor for ALPPS complications, and regenerative capacity is negatively correlated with fibrosis severity[2,15]. For cholangiocarcinoma, bile leakage and secondary sepsis are particularly challenging[3]. Our patient’s bile leakage was controlled by adequate drainage, underscoring the central role of unobstructed drainage and infection control.

Notably, volumetric increase does not equate to functional recovery, as FLV gain correlates poorly with ICG-R15 improvement[14]. Our patient experienced liver failure despite substantial volume growth, confirming that stage-2 timing must integrate functional parameters (ICG-R15, ALBI/PALBI scores) with volumetric assessment[18]. Furthermore, while minimally invasive ALPPS is less traumatic[1], open surgery provides superior intraoperative precision, hemostatic control, and management of unforeseen events in high-risk patients with cardiopulmonary comorbidities[19]. A 2026 systematic review by Huang et al. also notes that safe FLR thresholds depend on liver function severity: >35% for mild-to-moderate impairment and >45% for severe impairment[20].

The clinical decision-making in this case was innovative in three aspects: (1) timing of stage-2 prioritized functional reserve over morphological hypertrophy by extending the interval to 13 days; (2) the open approach was chosen to ensure hemodynamic stability despite higher invasiveness; and (3) we pushed the boundaries of ALPPS feasibility in cirrhotic patients under strict selection criteria.

Limitations: This is a single-center case report with limited generalizability. The follow-up period is only 12 months, and postoperative ICG-R15 was not monitored.

Conclusion

ALPPS offers a chance for curative resection in patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma and insufficient FLR, including strictly selected patients with cirrhosis. This report demonstrates that even in elderly patients with poor liver functional reserve and cardiopulmonary comorbidities, ALPPS can induce effective hypertrophy. A decision-making strategy prioritizing functional reserve and the safety of an open approach is key to success in such high-risk individuals. However, the procedure carries high risks of liver failure and bile leakage in cirrhotic patients. Individualized decisions, rigorous preoperative assessment (ICG-R15, ALBI/PALBI scores), and strong perioperative management capabilities are required. Further studies are needed to optimize selection criteria and surgical techniques.

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing in Pediatric Sepsis: γδ T Cell Exhibits a Differentiation to γδT17 Subtype along with Significantly Enhanced Cell Communication with Neutrophils
Authors: Wen-Jie Zhou, Ting Feng, Yue-Li Mu, Zhuo-Xu He, Dong Liu, Mei-Xing Yu, Hong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAbnormal immune responses are common clinical features in septic patients. γδ T cells, as innate immune cells, play an important role in host defense, immune surveillance, and homeostasis. However, the immune characteristics of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis remain poorly understood.MethodsIn this study, we analyzed single-cell RNA high-throughput sequencing data of peripheral blood mononuclear cells from pediatric septic patients.ResultsIt demonstrates that γδ T cells exhibit a pro-inflammatory state with heightened immune responsiveness to pathogens in pediatric sepsis, as confirmed by the results of flow cytometric analysis showing elevated Th1 cytokine secretion, increased activation, and a propensity to differentiate into IL-17-producing (γδT17) cells during disease progression. Pseudotime analysis identified seven key genes potentially regulating the differentiation of γδ T cells to γδT17 subtype. Furthermore, cell-cell communication analysis revealed enhanced RETN-CAP1 binding between neutrophils and γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, suggesting that neutrophil-derived resistin may promote γδ T-cell differentiation into the γδT17 subtype via CAP1 receptor binding.ConclusionThis study presents analysis of a single-cell study on the immune status of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, highlighting their pivotal roles in pathogen response, inflammation propagation, and immune regulation. The observed differentiation toward the γδT17 subtype may facilitate neutrophil recruitment in this life-threatening condition. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis could offer a new theoretical basis for novel therapeutics.

Introduction

Sepsis, defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection [1], disproportionately affects infants, with mortality rates exceeding 20% in pediatric ICU settings [2, 3] In 2024, the Pediatric Sepsis Definition Working Group of the American Society of Critical Care Medicine (SCCM) released the Phoenix criteria for the diagnosis of pediatric sepsis and septic shock. The application of these criteria will improve the diagnosis and treatment of pediatric sepsis and septic shock, and reduce the mortality rate worldwide [4]. The immature immune systems of infants and young children render them especially susceptible to rapidly progressing infections [5, 6]. In investigating sepsis immune responses, γδ T cells serve as a critical link between innate and adaptive immunity [7–9]. Their unique functional properties warrant focused attention in the context of pediatric sepsis defense mechanisms.

γδ T cells are a specialized T-cell subset that express T-cell receptors (TCRs) without major histocompatibility complex restriction [10, 11]. They respond rapidly to pathogens and modulate other immune cells. These cells contribute significantly to immune processes, including early anti-infection responses and immune regulation [12–14]. During infection, γδ T cells combat pathogens either by directly lysing infected cells or by secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-17A, granzyme B (GZMB), and granulysin (GNLY). GZMB induces apoptosis in pathogen-invaded cells [15], while GNLY disrupts microbial membranes, eliminating bacteria, fungi, and certain viruses [16]. IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-17 orchestrate immune responses: IFN-γ promotes Th1 differentiation [17]; TNF-α enhances inflammatory cell recruitment and vascular permeability [18, 19]; IL-17 potently activates and recruits neutrophils [20]. Previous studies identified γδ T cells as a major IL-17 source in early sepsis mouse models [21]. IL-17 is a pro-inflammatory cytokine that promotes various immune responses, including the proliferation and activation of neutrophils, and plays an important role in promoting inflammatory and anti-infection responses [20]. Although IL-17 plays a vital role in anti-infective responses, its overexpression in sepsis can exacerbate tissue damage and organ dysfunction [22, 23].

Earlier research has established that γδ T cells are pivotal not only in adult sepsis but also in preterm infant sepsis [24, 25]. Sepsis alters the γδ TCR repertoire, with effects varying by age and pathogen type [25]. CD83+ γδ T cells have been identified in neonatal sepsis [26], and extreme prematurity further disrupts γδ T-cell frequencies and functions [27]. However, the immune status of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis remains unclear. This study aimed to elucidate γδ T-cell functions in pediatric sepsis, with particular emphasis on their differentiation into γδT17 cells and the underlying regulatory mechanisms. Our findings may provide a theoretical foundation for improved sepsis diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and Methods

Subjects

The participants (pediatric septic patients and age-matched healthy controls [HCs]) were recruited from October 2022 to June 2023 at the West China Second University Hospital of Sichuan University. Inclusion criteria for septic patients were as follows: (1) 1 month to 4 years old; (2) the sepsis group met the definitions of sepsis by the International Pediatric Sepsis Consensus Conference (2005 Edition) [28] – meets the systemic inflammatory response syndrome in the presence of a confirmed or suspected infection, with a combination of clinical signs and symptoms, laboratory findings, and sometimes imaging studies; (3) absence of any immunodeficiency prior to PICU admission; (4) informed consent of the child’s family. Sepsis and septic shock were defined according to the 2005 surviving sepsis campaign guidelines International Pediatric Sepsis Consensus Conference: definitions for sepsis and organ dysfunction in pediatrics. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) have a congenital disease and (2) comorbid immune system diseases. All assessments were conducted by PICU physicians.

Isolation and Purification of Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells

One milliliter of peripheral blood was collected from each participant within 24 h after admission to the PICU under strict aseptic conditions under. All blood samples were collected in sodium heparin tubes. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from whole blood by Ficoll-Hypaque gradient centrifugation. All experiments were conducted on fresh PBMCs. Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed retrospectively on PBMCs from 3 septic patients and 3 controls (Table 1). Flow cytometry was conducted in 44 septic and 40 control samples (Table 2). Due to limited sample volumes, not all cytokines could be assessed in every case.

Flow Cytometry

After the isolation and purification of PBMCs, we directly detected the cytokine secretion levels in γδ T cells using flow cytometry. For surface staining, PBMCs were labeled with the following monoclonal antibodies: anti-human CD45 (HI30), anti-human CD3 (HIT3a), anti-human CD69 (FN50), anti-human NKG2D (1D11) (all from BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA); anti-human NKG2A (Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany); mouse anti-human TCR γδ (B1) (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). For intracellular staining, after fixed and permeabilized, PBMCs were labeled with anti-human perforin (Dg9), anti-human GZMB (GB11), anti-human IL-17A (BL168), anti-human TNF-(Mab11), anti-human IFN-γ (B27) (all from BioLegend), and mouse anti-human GNLY (RB1) (BD Biosciences). To avoid nonspecific staining, cells were preincubated with FcR blocking reagent (BioLegend) prior to specific staining. Data were acquired on BD FACS Celesta with FACS Diva (BD Biosciences) and analyzed using FlowJo software (Tree Star, Inc., Ashland, OR, USA). γδ T cells in CD3-positive cells of total PBMCs were the target cells for the determination of percentage, surface marker, and intracellular cytokine analysis.

Single-Cell mRNA Sequencing

Following isolation, the PBMCs were resuspended in buffer solution. The construction of subsequent single-cell libraries will be carried out using the 10× Genomics platform, specifically utilizing the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3′ GEM, Library & Gel Bead Kit v3.1 (10× Genomics, Pleasanton, CA, USA). The sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. The mean number of counts per sample was 12,580.5, and the mean number of genes was 3,002.

Analysis of Single-Cell mRNA Sequencing Data

The single-cell mRNA sequencing data were analyzed using the “Seurat” package within the R computational environment [29, 30]. Initially, cells were filtered based on the following criteria: nFeature RNA >600 and <6000, and the proportion of mitochondrial genes less than 20%. Following normalization, the top 2000 variably expressed genes were identified for further analysis using the Find Variable Features function. The gene expression data were then integrated, and the cells were clustered. Two-dimensional visualization of the data was performed using principal component analysis and uniform manifold approximation and projection. Finally, the cell clusters were annotated based on the average gene expression profiles across the major cell types: CD4+ T cells (PTPRC, CD3E, IL7R, CCR7), CD8+ T cells (PTPRC, CD3E, CD8A), γδ T cells (PTPRC, CD3E, TRDC, TRGC1, TRGC2), natural killer cells (PTPRC, NKG7, GNLY), NKT cells (PTPRC, CD3E, NKG7), B cells (PTPRC, MS4A1, CD79A, CD79B), plasma cells (PTPRC, MZB1, CD38), DC cells (PTPRC, HLA-DQA1, HLA-DQB1), monocytes (PTPRC, CD14, CD68, ITGAM), neutrophil granulocytes (CEACAM8, CSF3R, FCGR3B), basophil granulocytes (PTPRC, IL3RA, ENPP3), hematopoietic stem cells (PTPRC, CD34), erythrocytes (PTPRC, HBB), megakaryocytes (PTPRC, PPBP) [31–33].

GSEA Analysis

Gene expression differences between γδ T cells from sepsis samples and HC samples were analyzed using single-cell mRNA sequencing data. Subsequently, GSEA analysis was performed with the “clusterProfiler” package [34].

Pseudotime Analysis

Pseudotime analysis of the γδ T-cell population was performed using the “monocle” computational package [35, 36]. The branch with the highest proportion of sepsis samples was designated as the root for pseudotime analysis, and pseudotime-related differential genes were identified. Subsequently, the top 1,000 pseudotime-related differentially expressed genes, selected based on q values, were further analyzed. These genes were clustered into four groups, and GO and KEGG pathway analyses were conducted using the “clusterProfiler” package [34].

Cell-Cell Communication

The “CellChat” package was used to analyze the cell-cell communication mediated by ligand-receptor interactions for both sepsis and HC samples [37]. The γδ T cells were designated as the target cell population, and signaling pathways that were upregulated in sepsis γδ T cells were screened using a threshold of ligand.logFC greater than 0.5.

Quantitative RT-PCR

After being collected from septic patients and HCs, the PBMCs were purified by negative selection with a TCR g/d+ T-cell isolation kit (Miltenyi Biotec), in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. The γδ T cells with purity greater than 80% would be allowed to the following experiment. Total RNA was extracted from purified γδ T cells according to the instructions of the RNA-Quick Purification Kit (GOONIEBIO, Guangzhou). The concentration and purity of RNA were measured by a microspectrophotometer (NanoDrop One, Thermo Scientific, USA). cDNA was synthesized with Eastep® RT Master Mix (Promega, Shanghai). CAP1 expression was quantified via SYBR Green qPCR by QuantStudio 3 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) (forward primer, 5′-CCA​TGG​GGA​AGG​TGA​AGG​T-3′; reverse primer, 5′-CGG​TGC​CAT​GGA​ATT​TGC-3′). The experimental results were quantitatively analyzed by the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Stimulation of Resistin

For IL-17A induction, PBMCs were cultured with resistin (100 ng/mL, MCE) and brefeldin A (10 μg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich) for 4 h, followed by flow cytometry.

Data Statistics

The comparison of gene expression levels in single-cell mRNA sequencing data was compared using the Wilcoxon test conducted within the R 4.3.0 environment. Continuous variables were described as mean ± SD. Category variables were described as frequencies. Comparisons of expression of surface and intracellular markers between pediatric sepsis group and HC group were carried out using an unpaired t test. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

γδ T Cells of Pediatric Sepsis Exhibited a Stronger Immune Response to Infection

To gain a molecular understanding of the immune state in pediatric sepsis, we performed single-cell mRNA sequencing on PBMCs from 3 hospitalized patients with sepsis and their HCs. Through cellular clustering, all PBMCs were categorized into 28 clusters, which were further classified into 15 types based on the expression of signature genes (Fig. 1a, b). The proportion of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis group was found to be lower than that of HC group (Fig. 1c). Subsequently, we compared the gene expression differences between the two groups of γδ T cells and conducted GSEA analysis. The results showed that, in terms of biological processes, the γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis exhibited a stronger response to bacterium, inflammatory response, and defense response (Fig. 1d). In terms of signaling pathways, there were significant upregulations observed in the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, chemokine signaling pathway, and PI3K-AKT signaling pathway in the septic γδ T cells (Fig. 1e). These findings suggested that the γδ T cells of pediatric septic patients exhibit a stronger immune response toward bacteria. Furthermore, our analysis indicated that the γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis displayed enhanced migratory capabilities toward myeloid leukocytes, granulocytes, and particularly neutrophils within biological processes.

GSEA analysis of gene expression differences of γδ T cell between the sepsis group and HC group, BP (d) and KEGG (e).

Significant Enhanced Secretion of Pro-Inflammatory Cytokines Specially IL-17A in γδ T Cells of Pediatric Sepsis

In order to assess the immune function of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, we examined the γδ T cells from children with sepsis and HCs by flow cytometry. The results of cytokine secretion detected by flow cytometry are depicted in Figure 2. The gating strategy for cell detection results is shown in Figure 2a. As compared to HCs, there was a notable increase in the percentage of activated cells in the whole γδ T cells of pediatric sepsis (Fig. 2b), as well as a significant increase in the proportion of γδ T cells expressing IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-17A (Fig. 2c). Among those cytokines, the increase of IL-17A was particularly remarkable. These findings indicated that the γδ T cells of pediatric sepsis exhibit heightened immune function with increased pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion and seem like a profound source of IL-17A. Furthermore, there was a significantly lower proportion of γδ T cells expressing GZMB and GNLY in pediatric sepsis than what was observed in HCs, while the proportion of γδ T cells expressing perforin did not show significant changes (Fig. 2d). It suggested that the immune function of γδ T cells secreting cytotoxic cytokines against pathogens in pediatric septic patients is impaired. The result of the detection of cell surface receptors showed that the proportion of γδ T cells expressing NKG2A and NKG2D in pediatric sepsis group was not significantly different from that of HCs (Fig. 2e). These results suggest that γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis participate in immune responses primarily through secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Differentiation of γδ T Cells to γδT17 Subtype in Pediatric Sepsis

To investigate the regulatory mechanisms involved in differentiation of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, we performed pseudotime analysis on single-cell mRNA sequencing data. We categorized γδ T cells into three states based on pseudotime-related differential genes (Fig. 3a). Among these states, the proportion of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis was highest in state 2, while in the other two states, the proportion was higher in HCs and harder to distinguish (Fig. 3b). Based on this observation, we designated state 2 as the root, which allowed us to trace the pseudotime trajectory of γδ T cells from pediatric sepsis to HCs (Fig. 3c). Furthermore, we categorized differential genes associated with pseudotime into four clusters and conducted KEGG analyses (Fig. 3d). The result indicated that these genes from cluster 1 were notably enriched in the Th17 cell differentiation signaling pathway, which includes the following genes: HSP90AA1, RORA, NFKBIA, FOS, HSP90AB1, IL2RG, and IFNGR1 (Fig. 3e). The expression levels of these genes showed a significant difference in γδ T cells of pediatric sepsis compared to HCs (p < 0.01) (Fig. 3f). Additionally, we found that the expression of these Th17 cell differentiation-related genes also exhibited a significant decrease along the pseudotime trajectory (Fig. 3g). This suggests that the regulation of γδT17 differentiation in pediatric sepsis may be influenced by these identified genes.

RETN-CAP1-Mediated Cellular Communication between γδ T Cells and Neutrophils in Pediatric Sepsis

To further explore the role of other immune cells on γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, we performed cell communication analysis on single-cell mRNA sequencing data with γδ T cells as target cells. The results showed that in pediatric sepsis, cell communication mediated by resistin signaling was significantly enhanced (Fig. 4a). In addition to the cell communication between monocytes and γδ T cells, there was an increase in resistin signaling-mediated communication between neutrophils and γδ T cells, particularly mediated by the binding of RETN ligand to the CAP1 receptor (Fig. 4b). Gene expression analysis indicated a significant increase in RETN expression of neutrophils and CAP1 expression of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis (Fig. 4c). Pseudotime analysis result showed a significant decrease in CAP1 gene expression followed by pseudotime trajectory (Fig. 3g). This indicates that besides monocytes, neutrophils act on γδ T cells in the peripheral blood of pediatric sepsis through RETN-CAP1 binding.

Comparison of cell-cell communication analysis of γδ T cells in single-cell mRNA sequencing between the pediatric sepsis (sepsis) group and HC group. a Comparison of resistin signaling. b Comparison of interactions between cell surface receptors and cell ligands in resistin signaling. c Comparison of mRNA expression levels of cell surface receptors and cell ligand genes in all cell subtypes during resistin signaling. d Comparison of RNA expression levels of CAP1 gene in γδ T cells between sepsis (n = 5) and HC samples (n = 5). e The proportion of IL-17A-positive γδ T cells was detected by flow cytometry in resistin stimulation (n = 5) and control (n = 5). f Comparison of the proportion of IL-17A-positive γδ T cells between resistin stimulation (n = 6) and control (n = 6) (*p < 0.05).

Subsequently, we examined the mRNA expression levels of the CAP1 gene in γδ T cells from pediatric sepsis patients and HC. The results demonstrated that the mRNA expression levels of the CAP1 gene in γδ T cells from pediatric sepsis patients were significantly higher than those in the control group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4d). Furthermore, we utilized flow cytometry to assess the effect of resistin on the production of IL-17A by γδ T cells (Fig. 4e). The results demonstrated that the proportion of γδ T cells producing IL-17A upon resistin stimulation was significantly higher than that in the group without resistin (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4f). These results indicate that resistin can induce γδ T cells to produce more IL-17A by binding to the CAP1 receptor.

Discussion

The γδ T cells, as innate immune cells, are capable of mounting a rapid immune response to pathogens and play a critical role in both the early immune defense against infection and later immune regulation in pediatric sepsis. However, the functions of γδ T cells in immune function of pediatric sepsis remain unclear. This article mainly uses single-cell mRNA sequencing technology to analyze the cellular differentiation and immune functions of γδ T cells in the peripheral blood during the early infection stage of pediatric sepsis, with verification provided by flow cytometry. This paper aimed to explore the differentiation and regulatory molecular mechanisms of γδ T cells in pediatric sepsis, offering a new theoretical basis for the immune regulatory mechanisms and treatment strategies of pediatric sepsis.

In the early stages of sepsis, the innate immune cells providing immune responses to pathogens are activated, which contributes to resist bacterial invasion and eliminate pathogens. There is a significant increase in the proportion of activated γδ T cells in PBMCs of pediatric septic patients, which mainly exert immune defense and regulatory functions by secreting cytotoxic granules including GZMB and GNLY together with pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-17A. Our research suggests that in the early stages, γδ T cells expressing GZMB and GNLY are severely depleted in the effort to clear invading pathogens. In terms of regulating immune responses, the increase in γδ T cells expressing IL-17A is most significant. IL-17 enhances the ability of immune system to clear pathogens by mediating the recruitment and production of neutrophils [38]. It indicates that, in the early stages of the disease, γδ T cells have the capacity to clear pathogens directly and recruit to eliminate pathogens indirectly. It is reported that IL-17 family cytokines strengthen host defense against microbial septicemia by recruiting neutrophils [39]; γδT17 cells infiltrating the liver prevent sepsis-induced liver damage [40]. However, excessive secretion of IL-17A can also exacerbate inflammatory responses, leading to tissue and organ damage. Studies have shown that the IL-17A/p38 signaling pathway can activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, which contributes to the development of acute lung injury in a septic mouse model [41]. Targeted blocking of IL-17A can inhibit the activation of microglial cells causing neuro-inflammation and suppress the polarization of M1 macrophages and their infiltration into the intestinal muscular layer [42]. Therefore, the differentiation of γδ T cells into γδT17 cells in the early peripheral blood of pediatric sepsis is crucial for defending against pathogen invasion, but it also poses a risk of exacerbating inflammatory responses and damaging the body’s own tissues and organs.

The regulatory mechanisms behind the formation of γδT17 cells in the PBMCs of pediatric sepsis have not been reported. Our analysis suggests that this process is regulated by key genes included in the Th17 cell differentiation pathway, including HSP90AA1, RORA, NFKBIA, FOS, HSP90AB1, IL2RG, and IFNGR1. Studies have reported that γδ T cells expressing high levels of TCR are associated with the expression of IL-17 and the RORγT gene [43]. Our findings indicate that RORA, another key transcription factor encoding Th17 cell differentiation, may be involved in the formation of γδT17 cells. Additionally, other candidate regulatory genes, such as the IL2RG gene, which encodes a receptor that can activate the JAK3-STAT5 signaling pathway through binding to IL2, may promote the Th17 transformation of MAC-1 cells [44]. The HSP90AA1 and HSP90AB1 genes are also considered to be involved in the differentiation of Th17 in inflammatory diseases [45, 46]. The antigen encoded by the Fos gene can enhance the expression of IL-17 and the development of Th17 cells by binding to the JunB promoter [47]. On the other hand, studies have shown that in the peripheral blood of patients with arthritis and HBV infection, resistin levels correlate positively with serum levels of IL-17 [48, 49]. Our analysis and experimental results suggest that RETN-CAP1 cellular communication may be involved in the differentiation of γδT17 cells. Neutrophils release resistin in response to bacterial stimuli, which then binds to the CAP1 receptor on γδ T cells to stimulate their expression of IL-17, further recruiting neutrophils to form a positive feedback loop. It is noteworthy that studies have reported that high concentrations of resistin can reduce the migration and bactericidal function of neutrophils, which may be one of the causes of the immunodeficiency observed in the later stages of sepsis [50, 51]. To sum up, the differentiation of γδ T cells into γδT17 cells in the early stage of pediatric sepsis may be regulated by the above key genes, and there is a positive feedback interaction between neutrophils and γδT17 cells by RETN-CAP1 cellular communication. These results need further experimental verification.

Conclusions

In summary, our research results suggested that IL-17 probably is an important cytokine produced by γδ T cells and is involved in the immune regulation of pediatric sepsis. Along with regulating the immune response, γδ T cells differentiate into γδT17 cells under the influence of key regulatory genes, to induce neutrophils to participate in subsequent immune response. Meanwhile, resistin released by neutrophils may promote the formation of γδT17 cells by binding to the CAP1 receptor on γδ T cells. Our research elucidates the differentiation and immunoregulatory roles of γδT17 cells during the early stages of pediatric sepsis, which demonstrates the important role of γδ T cells in sepsis immunity.

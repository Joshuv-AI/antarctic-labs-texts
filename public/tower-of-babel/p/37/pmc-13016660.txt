Comparison of Patient Reviews for Submental Liposuction and Kybella Using Deep Learning and Natural Language Processing: Is There a Superior Intervention for Submental Adiposity?
Authors: Arman J Fijany, Michael J Boctor, Ja’Neil G Humphrey, Lisandro Montorfano, Huseyin Karagoz, Subhas C Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatient-reported outcomes (PROs) are essential in evaluating aesthetic procedures. RealSelf is an online platform for cosmetic and reconstructive feedback. Currently, no validated PRO tools exist to compare Kybella (synthetic deoxycholic acid; AbbVie Pharmaceuticals, North Chicago, IL) with submental liposuction.ObjectivesRobust optimizing Bidirectional Encoder Representation from Transformers, a deep learning natural language processing (NLP) model, can analyze emotions and sentiment in text and was used compare patient-reported experiences for Kybella and submental liposuction on RealSelf.MethodsRealSelf was queried for English reviews of Kybella and submental liposuction from 2014 to 2024. Reviews were excluded if they lacked procedural details, focused on the provider, or included adjunct procedures such as a necklift or Botox. Two NLP models analyzed posts: 1 characterized posts as positive or negative (scored 0-1); The latter scored each post from 0 to 1 ∼7 emotions—fear, sadness, anger, disgust, neutral, surprise, and joy.ResultsOverall, 1338 posts met the inclusion criteria: 753 for submental liposuction and 585 for Kybella. A greater proportion of liposuction reviews were positive compared with Kybella (83.4% vs 55.2%, P < .00001). Liposuction also had significantly higher mean positive scores (0.73 vs 0.41, P < .0001). Joy was the most common emotion for liposuction reviews (51.3%), whereas Kybella reviews more often reflected sadness (17.9%) and fear (12%), with fewer expressing joy (22.7%).ConclusionsThe authors of this study applied NLP to extract structured sentiment from unstructured reviews RealSelf. Our findings suggest that submental liposuction patients report greater satisfaction and more positive emotional experiences than those who receive Kybella.Level of Evidence: 4 (Therapeutic)

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) have become essential in both medical and surgical practices, emphasizing patient-centered care. This shift has empowered patients to communicate their clinical experiences through various platforms, including social media and online review forums.1-3 These channels are particularly influential in selecting physicians and treatments for cosmetic and reconstructive procedures, because they significantly shape patients’ experiential and aesthetic expectations.4-6 Reviews can be essential proxies of satisfaction, including perceived value, expectations, and overall results, thereby providing insight into the patient experience.

Submental fat (SMF), also known as double chin, is a common concern for patients because it is frequently coupled with negative aesthetic and social connotations. A recent study discovered that over 90% of respondents found both women and men to be less attractive with a double chin.7 The authors of this study also found that those with greater SMF were seen as significantly less relaxed, likable, intelligent, and energetic.7 Furthermore, in a recent survey of cosmetic procedure patients, excess fat under the chin and neck was listed as a concern in roughly 80% of respondents.8

Current popular procedures to diminish SMF include invasive methods, such as submental liposuction, combined facelift and platysmaplasty, and noninvasive approaches, such as cryolypolysis and Kybella (AbbVie Pharmaceuticals, North Chicago, IL).9-14 Kybella, or ATX-101 (deoxycholic acid), is an FDA-approved injectable product designed for minimally invasive SMF reduction.15 Although Kybella is deemed safe and effective, its cosmetic outcomes can vary, potentially impacting patient satisfaction.16 Besides enhancing the submental region's appearance, factors such as cost-effectiveness, overall treatment experience, and recovery period may influence patients’ decisions to seek additional treatment with Kybella or opt for more traditional methods of SMF reduction, such as liposuction (Figure 1).

Comparison of submental contouring techniques: chin liposuction utilizes a single submental crease incision with a spike-like cannulation pattern (left), whereas Kybella (AbbVie Pharmaceuticals) is administered through multiple injection sites in a checkered pattern (right).

Currently, there are no validated PRO measures for Kybella. However, RealSelf (Seattle, WA), an online platform hosting numerous patient reviews of cosmetic and reconstructive plastic surgery procedures, includes reviews from patients who have undergone Kybella treatment.17 The authors of this study aim to analyze patient reviews of Kybella treatments from RealSelf to identify factors contributing to positive or negative experiences and outcomes. We hypothesize that an increased number of Kybella injections correlates with decreased satisfaction compared with standard submental liposuction regarding cosmetic results and perceived financial value.

Natural language processing (NLP) allows for rapid analysis and extraction of insights from unstructured text. With the growth of social media platforms, many are employing NLP-based deep learning and artificial intelligence technologies to gain insight into patient and population sentiment and satisfaction. Here, we use the popular Robust optimizing Bidirectional Encoder Representation from Transformers (RoBERTa), a deep neural network, NLP model trained to text sentiment to analyze and compare reviews for Kybella and submental liposuction on RealSelf.

METHODS

RealSelf Review Inclusion Criteria

The popular plastic surgery website RealSelf was searched for patient reviews related to the interventions of submental liposuction and Kybella in July 2024 for reviews completed within the prior 10 years to align with the introduction of Kybella to the market. Authors A.J.F. and M.J.B. searched for reviews within different 5 year periods. Reviews that were not in English did not directly mention the use of either intervention, included a review that specifically mentioned the provider without details of their procedure, or a review that mentioned additional interventions or procedures concurrently were excluded. Common concomitant procedures that excluded reviews were additional facial surgeries (buccal fat removal, blepharoplasty, rhinoplasty, etc), skin tightening (neck or facelift), and energy-based procedures. Each post was reviewed by both A.J.F. and M.J.B. for consideration of inclusion. When a single reviewing author raised concerns about a post, it was excluded from the analysis. Repetitive posts from the same account were only included as a single review. Duplicate reviews were excluded. The included reviews were manually collected into an Excel sheet (Microsoft, Redmond, WA), and each Excel file sheet was organized by age of review in years. For the Kybella reviews, the authors separated each review by the number of treatments (standard treatment is considered 2-3 vials) the patient had undergone before writing their review.

Review Preprocessing and Sentiment Analysis

The Python programming language environment was utilized in PyCharm Community Edition 3.4 software for data analysis and NLP. Preprocessing with the exclusion of website names by removing any block of text with “http” or “www” until the following empty text space. Afterward, each text review was inserted into 2 RoBERTa models. RoBERTa is a transformer-based language model that can predict and understand language bidirectionally—considering context from both before and after a sequence of text.18 The models and their NLP analyses were conducted using pretrained RoBERTa models through the Hugging Face Transformers library, a widely adopted NLP framework offering accessible tools for implementation.19,20

The first model assessed the reviews and assigned each to 1 of 3 groups—“positive,” “negative,” and “neutral.”21 This model also allotted scores from 0 to 1. Modifications were then made to the code to delegate the highest bimodal score—positive or negative—for each review. The subsequent model utilizes deep learning to categorize text by Ekman's 6 fundamental emotions—fear, sadness, anger, disgust, surprise, and joy—with a neutral category for text that did not fall into the emotions.22,23 Overall, each review was classified as either “positive” or “negative” from the first model and by the primary emotion in the second model.

Totals and mean scores were computed for each category across all reviews. Representative examples and their highest-scoring emotional classifications are shown in Supplemental Tables 1, 2. To preserve anonymity, physician names were removed and replaced with “X” as a name placeholder.

Radar Plot Generation

Average emotional scores within the second model were then used to create radar plots with the Plotly library in Python. One plot was generated to compare Kybella witth submental liposuction (Figure 2), and another was developed to compare reviews of patients who underwent their first, second, and third Kybella treatments (Figure 3).

Word Cloud Generation

The PyCharm software platform analyzed the text reviews for each intervention and created word clouds—descriptive images of the most common words found within the text. Stop words such as “day,” “chin,” “treatment,” “I,” “and,” and other nondescriptive words were excluded from the word cloud analysis (Supplemental Figure 1).

Statistical Analysis

Chi-square testing with the Yates correction compared the total number of positive and negative reviews for submental liposuction and Kybella.24 The 1-tailed independent t-test was utilized to compare average emotion scores between the 2 interventions. The same statistical methods were employed to compare the first, second, and third Kybella treatments. The statistical significance was determined when P < .05.

RESULTS

In total, 1341 reviews were included in our cross-sectional sentiment analysis, with 586 Kybella and 753 submental liposuction reviews (Table 1). Within the first model, 323 reviews (55.1%) for Kybella were classified as positive, whereas 263 reviews (44.9%) were categorized as negative. For submental liposuction, 628 reviews (83.4%) were positive, whereas 125 reviews (16.6%) were negative. When comparing the bimodal distribution of reviews, the calculated χ2 statistic was 128.06 (P < .00001).

Within the second model, reviews for submental liposuction were mainly categorized as depicting “joy” (386 reviews, 51.3%), followed by “fear” (65 reviews, 8.6%), and “surprise” (55 reviews, 7.3%). Kybella's reviews were said to most commonly display the emotions of “joy” (133 reviews, 22.7%), “sadness” (105 reviews, 17.9%), and “fear” (70 reviews, 12%) (Figure 2).

With regard to average scores within the first model, submental liposuction had a significantly higher “positive” score (mean = 0.73) compared with Kybella's (mean = 0.41, P < .0001). The highest mean emotion scores calculated with the second model for submental liposuction were joy (0.465) and surprise (0.099). For Kybella, these were joy (0.217) and sadness (0.179, Figure 3).

When comparing reviews for sequential Kybella treatments, most were reviews for initial treatment (401 reviews, 68.4%). For subsequent treatments, we found 136 reviews for the second (23.2%), 39 for the third (6.7%), and 10 (1.7%) for the fourth Kybella treatment. We observed no significant differences when comparing subsequent therapies regarding positive review counts and mean positive sentiment scores (Figure 4).

The most common words in the reviews for submental liposuction were “feel” (288), “happy” (247), and “recommend” (226, Supplemental Figure 1). The most common words found in Kybella reviews included “swelling” (623), “fat” (277), and “pain” (256, Supplemental Figure 2).

DISCUSSION

Using liposuction to remove SMF to improve the contour of the jawline was first described by Hetter in 1990.25 For roughly 25 years, liposuction with or without additional skin tightening procedures has been the standard of care to address SMF. Like many other aesthetic procedures of the face, recent advancements have introduced alternative, nonsurgical options that have gained popularity.

Kybella, first introduced in 2015, was proposed as a nonsurgical alternative to address adiposity in the submental region.26 Initial double-blinded randomized controlled trials described Kybella as a safe and effective alternative to submental liposuction.27 To this point, however, there have not been any studies directly comparing surgical and nonsurgical options for SMF.

Cost-effectiveness is often a major consideration for patients undergoing elective cosmetic procedures. A 2018 cost-effectiveness study surveyed members of the American Academy of Facial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery regarding their clinical use of Kybella and submental liposuction, as well as the fees and adverse events associated with the treatments.28 These data were compared with the average amount of deoxycholic acid given in Phase 3 clinical trials (186 mg per patient), yielding estimated treatment cost. The analysis revealed Kybella treatments’ cost almost double that of submental liposuction ($6426 vs $2977). However, for patients receiving smaller doses (60-90 mg), the overall cost of Kybella would be lower than that of liposuction, suggesting variability in pricing based on treatment dosage.

Our findings demonstrate the ability of NLP to evaluate patient reviews for cosmetic procedures, particularly for comparing surgical vs nonsurgical options for SMF reduction. This analysis delivers valuable insights for providers and their patients interested in improving their jawline's contour.

Sentiment analysis demonstrated a substantial difference in patient satisfaction between Kybella and submental liposuction. Submental liposuction received a significantly higher percentage of positive reviews (83.4% vs 53.1%, P < .00001) and a higher mean positive sentiment score (0.73 vs 0.41, P < .0001). Our emotional model further highlighted this disparity, in which an overwhelming number of submental liposuction reviews displayed “joy” (51.3%) compared with reviews for Kybella (22.7%). Additionally, negative emotions such as “sadness” (17.9%) and “fear” (12%) were much more prominent in reviews for Kybella. Overall, although patients appear to have some positive outcomes with Kybella, there are also negative emotions after undergoing treatment, potentially related to poor aesthetic outcomes or complications with treatment.

Word cloud analysis reinforced these trends. Submental liposuction often had positive words such as “great,” “happy,” “amazing,” and “recommend.” In contrast, common words in Kybella's posts included concerns such as “pain,” “swelling,” “numbness,” and “bruising.” This suggests that most submental liposuction reviews display satisfaction and cheerfulness, whereas experiences with Kybella tend to be focused on postprocedure complications and difficulties with recovery.

These findings provide useful information for shared decision making between providers and patients. The considerably higher satisfaction rates and positive emotional sentiments associated with SMF liposuction undoubtedly make it a more appealing route for SMF reduction. On the other hand, Kybella might be an option for those who prefer minimally invasive, nonsurgical procedures, despite the potential for discomfort and variable results.

Our study has many strengths. First and foremost, it is the only study in the literature that has compared these 2 primary therapies for submental fullness in a head-to-head, unbiased manner. Furthermore, because we were able to analyze reviews on RealSelf, our sentiment analysis included a large sample size of patient experiences of over 1300 reviews. Furthermore, by applying 2 distinct NLP models, we were able to take unstructured patient reviews and create structured, quantifiable data, while also overcoming common limitations of survey-based studies—such as response bias and an inability to capture emotional nuances.

Nevertheless, our study is not without weaknesses. A more robust analysis of the reviews could have provided more data on patient-specific complaints and complications beyond the most common words depicted in our WordCloud analysis. Unfortunately, demographic information such as age and gender distribution was not provided on RealSelf and can potentially introduce significant confounders to our comparison.

The study also does not account for variations in the experience of the individual performing the procedure, which unfortunately was not always provided on RealSelf. Submental liposuction is often performed by board-certified plastic surgeons, whereas Kybella injections can be administered by individuals without specialized training in aesthetic surgery. The lack of data on the training and qualifications of provider is a major limitation and a likely confounder of our results. There may be a self-selection process by which providers without formal plastic and aesthetic surgery training only offer Kybella and vice versa those with training predominantly offer liposuction. Additionally, many reviews highlight the bedside manner of the provider, which can influence patients’ perceptions of both the procedure and its outcomes, potentially affecting the accuracy of their assessments. Moreover, patients often expect morbidity from surgical procedures and may not call out symptoms such as numbness, swelling, and pain from a procedural-based liposuction as frequently as they may from a perceived minimally invasive procedure such as Kybella. There is also an inherent selection bias in using reviews from RealSelf, because individuals with particularly positive or negative experiences are more likely to share their feedback online.

Other survey-based studies that include PROs could potentially include specific data on outcomes beyond overall satisfaction, such as cost, time, aesthetic results, pain, numbness, and how likely the patient would be to recommend the procedure to a friend or colleague.

CONCLUSIONS

Overall, the authors of this study provide a robust sentiment and emotional analysis of patient reviews and reported outcomes for the 2 most popular SMF interventions—liposuction and Kybella. Submental liposuction significantly outperformed Kybella in both sentiment and emotional scores, suggesting higher patient satisfaction. Insights from this study can guide decision making in cosmetic procedures to address SMF. Moreover, the authors of this study are among the first to apply NLP and deep learning large-scale, unstructured PROs to generate structured, comparable data on cosmetic procedure satisfaction. Further research with additional PRO measures and expanding the use of NLP can be beneficial for understanding patient experiences and outcomes for cosmetic procedures.

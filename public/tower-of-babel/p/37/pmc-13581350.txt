1. PANCREATIC CANCER IN THE ERA OF PRECISION MEDICINE

This review summarizes the latest breakthroughs in pancreatic cancer diagnosis and treatment, addressing one of the most deadly cancers globally. It discusses new developments in surgery, systemic chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and precision medicine driven by next‑generation sequencing and targeted therapies. The article presents updated National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) criteria for resectability, highlights the increasing trend toward neoadjuvant therapies, and explains how genetic profiling is shaping personalized patient care. By combining recent clinical trial results with updated surgical and multidisciplinary guidelines, the review offers a current clinical framework and points out promising avenues for improving outcomes in this aggressive disease. It stands as a reliable, comprehensive resource for healthcare professionals seeking a clear overview of cutting-edge pancreatic cancer management for 2025–2026.1

2. IS ZOLPIDEM CARDIAC-SAFE? EVIDENCE FROM A NATIONWIDE COHORT STUDY

This study, which used Taiwan’s Longitudinal Generation Tracking Database and included over 450 000 participants, examined whether taking zolpidem is linked to cardiac arrhythmias. The results revealed that people who use zolpidem are at a notably higher risk of developing arrhythmias, especially rapid heart rhythms like paroxysmal tachycardia and atrial flutter. Data from the National Health Insurance Research Database also showed that zolpidem users face nearly twice the risk of arrhythmia compared with nonusers. Since zolpidem is one of the most commonly prescribed sleep medications worldwide, these outcomes highlight significant cardiovascular safety issues, emphasizing the importance of thorough risk assessment and continuous monitoring for patients on long-term sedative-hypnotic treatments.2

3. CONVERSION ARTHROPLASTY AFTER FAILED HIP FRACTURE FIXATION: A SURGICAL “SECOND HIT”?

This retrospective cohort study shows that conversion arthroplasty after failed internal fixation of femoral neck or intertrochanteric fractures leads to higher in-hospital complication rates than initial surgery. Although intertrochanteric fracture conversions are more technically challenging, mortality and readmission rates are similar for both types. The results emphasize the clinical impact of revision surgery in elderly hip-fracture patients and provide important data for surgeons planning treatment and counseling patients.3

4. TUMOR-SUPPRESSIVE CIRCRNAS: NEW REGULATORY NETWORKS IN COLORECTAL CANCER

Recent studies have highlighted the role of circular RNAs as key regulators of tumor progression through circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory networks.4 Building on these advances, the present study uses circular RNA sequencing of colorectal cancer spheroid models integrated with large-scale clinical transcriptomic datasets to identify previously unrecognized tumor-suppressive circRNAs associated with cancer stem cell biology. Two circRNAs—hsa_circ_0039776 and hsa_circ_0067353—were consistently downregulated in both patient-derived tissues and stem-cell–enriched spheroid cultures. Bioinformatic network analysis further suggests that these circRNAs may modulate oncogenic microRNAs and downstream gene pathways linked to patient survival. By delineating a circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory framework, the study proposes that the loss of these circRNAs may facilitate uncontrolled colorectal cancer progression. These findings extend emerging evidence that RNA-based regulatory networks contribute to tumor heterogeneity and therapeutic resistance, and they highlight the potential of circRNAs as diagnostic biomarkers and as future targets for RNA-based therapeutic strategies aimed at eradicating chemotherapy-resistant cancer stem cells.5

5. TARGET-CONTROLLED INFUSION STABILIZES AUTONOMIC RESPONSES DURING PROPOFOL INDUCTION

This study used wavelet-based spectral analysis to assess autonomic responses during propofol induction. Target-controlled infusion resulted in smoother autonomic modulation than rapid manual bolus, with less fluctuation in heart rate variability and sympathetic activity. Findings suggest that the rate of propofol increase, not final concentration, is crucial for autonomic stability, highlighting safety implications for patients with cardiovascular risk.6

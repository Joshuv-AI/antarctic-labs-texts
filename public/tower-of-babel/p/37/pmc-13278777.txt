Efficacy and safety of early tirofiban administration after intravenous thrombolysis in patients with acute ischaemic stroke: the multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled RESCUE BT3 trial protocol
Authors: Fengli Li, Shitao Fan, Changwei Guo, Gaoming Li, Jie Yang, Jiaxing Song, Dahong Yang, Linyu Li, Thanh N Nguyen, Wenjie Zi, Qingwu Yang

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntravenous thrombolysis (IVT) is the standard first-line treatment for patients with acute ischaemic stroke (AIS). Introduction

Acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) remains a leading cause of mortality and long-term disability worldwide.1 Acute ischaemic stroke without large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion constitutes a substantial proportion of the overall stroke population, accounting for approximately 40%–50% of all cases and represents a critical subgroup that should not be overlooked in current stroke management strategies.2,3 Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) has been established as the first-line therapy for AIS within 4.5 h of symptom onset in current guidelines.4,5 With ongoing optimisation of in-hospital workflows, the proportion of patients receiving IVT has increased steadily. However, the overall efficacy of IVT remains suboptimal, with only half of patients attaining excellent functional outcome (mRS 0–1) at 90 days.6 This indicates that a considerable proportion of thrombolysed patients continue to face significant disability following treatment. For patients with large or medium vessel occlusion, IVT followed by EVT has been proven to further enhance reperfusion rates and significantly improve clinical outcomes.7 However, for patients without large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion, apart from IVT within the therapeutic time window, effective subsequent intervention strategies are lacking. Therefore, exploring adjunctive treatment strategies for patients without large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion who remain disabled despite receiving IVT is crucial.

Tirofiban, a platelet glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor antagonist, provides rapid and comprehensive inhibition of platelet aggregation by blocking the final common pathway.8 Additionally, its short half-life and reversible binding characteristics make it particularly suitable for use following IVT. Previous exploratory studies evaluating the safety and efficacy of tirofiban administered after IVT have demonstrated its potential to improve functional outcomes at both 1 week and 3 months, without increasing the risk of sICH or mortality.9–11 Furthermore, 2 meta-analyses have indicated that intravenous tirofiban is safe for patients with AIS undergoing IVT and is associated with a higher likelihood of favourable functional outcomes.12,13 However, these findings are limited by small sample sizes or non-randomised controlled studies, which restricts the generalisability of the results. A subgroup analysis of our preliminary RESCUE BT2 trial suggested that early tirofiban administration after IVT may confer clinical benefit in patients without large or medium vessel occlusion.14 However, current guidelines recommend against the use of antiplatelet agents during the early phase of IVT.4,15 Therefore, the efficacy and safety of initiating intravenous tirofiban within 24 h after IVT are worthy of exploration.

Despite advances in acute stroke management, effective adjunctive therapies for patients without a large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion stroke are lacking. To address this critical unmet clinical need, we intend to conduct a multicentre, randomised, controlled, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial (RESCUE BT3 trial) with the largest sample size to evaluate the efficacy and safety of early tirofiban administration following IVT in patients with AIS. This trial is expected to provide an effective treatment for patients who remain disabled following IVT.

Methods

Design

The RESCUE BT3 trial is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, parallel-controlled, double-blind clinical trial. The trial is conducted according to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration and has been registered at www.chictr.org.cn (Clinical Trial Registry identifier: ChiCTR2400080653). The trial protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University and all participating centres. Written informed consent is obtained from all enrolled patients or their legal representatives before randomisation. An overview of patient flow through the study is presented in Figure 1.

Randomisation

Eligible patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either tirofiban or placebo. Randomisation will be performed immediately via a web-based application upon confirmation of eligibility. Both tirofiban and placebo will be identically packaged in terms of labelling, dosage form, size and colour, differing only by a unique serial number. Using a real-time, web-based centralised randomisation system, treatment allocation will be completely blinded for both investigators and participants. Subjects will receive the assigned intervention according to the serial number generated by the system, ensuring complete blinding.

Treatments and intervention

Tirofiban will be administered by continuous infusion for 24 h (or less in case of adverse reactions). Based on a previous study on low-dose tirofiban treatment improving neurological outcomes after IVT, the dosing regimen was: initial infusion of 0.25 μg/kg/min for 30 min, followed by maintenance infusion of 0.1 μg/kg/min for up to 24 h.10 The saline placebo will be infused in the same fashion. The blinded study drug will be started as soon as possible after randomisation and continued for a total of 24 h. In both the tirofiban and placebo groups, guideline-based antiplatelet therapy (aspirin and/or clopidogrel) will be initiated approximately 4 h before completion of the 24-h blinded study-drug infusion as a bridging strategy and continued thereafter according to current standard stroke management guidelines.16 During the blinded infusion period, no additional non-protocol antiplatelet, anticoagulant or fibrinolytic agents will be permitted.

Safety outcomes

The primary safety outcomes are mortality at 90 days and sICH within 48 h. Secondary safety outcomes include any ICH within 48 h, the incidence of non-intracranial bleeding complications and thrombocytopenia (defined as a platelet count < 90*109/L indicated by a re-examination of blood routine within 48 h after medication).

Data and safety monitoring board

An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB), comprising 1 expert each in neurology, neuro-radiology and biostatistics, has been established to oversee the safety and scientific integrity of the trial. Data and Safety Monitoring Board members will regularly monitor the safety of the trial and guarantee the safety of patients at all stages of the trial, but they will not participate in the study and not be involved in the trial in any other way. The DSMB will monitor the trend of serious adverse events and submit its report to the trial steering committee.

Sample size estimates

The sample size estimation was primarily based on a subgroup analysis of the previous RESCUE BT2 study. The 90-day mRS (0–1) rate was 35.1% in patients treated with tirofiban and 23.2% in patients treated with conventional medical therapy following IVT, with a difference of 11.9%.14 However, due to the broad inclusion criteria in the parent trial and the relatively small size of the subgroup analysed, a more conservative effect size was assumed for the current trial. A conservative estimate of an absolute increase of approximately 7% in the proportion of patients with an mRS score of 0–1 at 90 days was adopted. Excellent functional outcome rates of 30.2% and 23.2% were conservatively assumed for the tirofiban and placebo groups, respectively. With a significance level of α = 0.05 (2-sided), a power of 1 − β = 0.80 and experimental group vs control group = 1:1, the sample size was calculated as 1248 cases (624 cases in each group). Considering a 5% dropout rate, the final sample size was adjusted to 657 in each group, resulting in a total of 1314 participants. This estimation was performed based on PASS 15.0.5 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT).

Statistical analysis

Analyses of efficacy outcomes will be undertaken based on the intention-to-treat population and will be adjusted for age, baseline NIHSS score, type of intravenous thrombolytic agent (alteplase or tenecteplase) and time from last known well to randomisation. For the primary outcome and binary secondary clinical efficacy outcomes, differences between the 2 groups will be assessed using modified Poisson regression with robust error variance estimation.17 For the ordinal shift analysis of mRS scores at 90 days, the generalised odds ratio will be estimated across the full range of the scale (0–6). Win ratios will be used to assess the treatment effect on ordinal or composite endpoints. Safety outcomes that occurred in each group will be summarised as frequency counts and percentages. Relative risks and risk differences will be reported with 95% CIs to indicate statistical precision. All analyses are 2-sided with a significance level of 0.05. Statistical analysis will be conducted on the SAS 9.4 system (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) and R (version 4.3 or later).

Discussion

The RESCUE BT3 trial will provide definitive information on the efficacy and safety of early intravenous tirofiban infusion in patients with AIS without large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion who remain disabled despite IVT. The study was designed with lessons learned from earlier trials of antiplatelet therapy that have failed to demonstrate clinical benefit.18 Unlike prior studies, our trial selects tirofiban, a glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor antagonist, which differs mechanistically from conventional antiplatelet agents such as aspirin and clopidogrel. Tirofiban exerts rapid and potent inhibition of platelet aggregation, making it an ideal antiplatelet agent in the acute phase of stroke treatment.8

The present trial excludes patients with evidence of cardioembolic stroke. Based on the subgroup analysis of the RESCUE BT trial (Effect of Intravenous Tirofiban vs Placebo Before Endovascular Thrombectomy on Functional Outcomes in Large Vessel Occlusion Stroke), among patients with large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion, although the overall result was neutral, non-cardioembolic patients treated with tirofiban as adjunctive therapy had better clinical outcomes compared with cardioembolic patients.19 In patients with non-cardioembolic stroke without large or medium vessel occlusion, subgroup analyses from the prior RESCUE BT2 trial (Tirofiban for Stroke without Large- or Medium-Sized Vessel Occlusion) demonstrated that post-thrombolysis tirofiban treatment improved the proportion of favourable outcomes by 11.9%.14 These subgroup findings should be interpreted cautiously due to limited sample size and inadequate statistical power. Moreover, multiple studies have demonstrated that small-calibre arteries and stenotic vessels generate higher shear stress, which promotes von Willebrand factor-mediated platelet aggregation.20,21 Under these high-shear conditions, glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors may provide more effective inhibition of shear-induced platelet aggregation than conventional antiplatelet agents.22 Therefore, patients without large- or medium-sized vessel occlusion and without evidence of a cardioembolic source on baseline evaluation were included in our trial. To minimise the risk of intracerebral haemorrhage, brain imaging with CT or MRI is required before tirofiban administration to exclude new haemorrhagic changes following IVT in our study. Moreover, we use a low tirofiban loading dose (0.25 μg/kg/min) over the first 30 min.10

Compared with a recent study on early tirofiban use after thrombolysis (ASSET-IT), RESCUE BT3 has a larger sample size (n = 1314 vs 832). In addition, our study exclusively enrolled patients without large or medium vessel occlusion, resulting in a more focused study population. Meanwhile, we used a lower 30-min tirofiban loading infusion dose than ASSET-IT (0.25 vs 0.4 μg/kg/min). Therefore, these differences may help refine the evidence on the efficacy and safety of alternative tirofiban dosing strategies for this kind of patient population.

RESCUE BT3 randomised the first patient on 2 April 2024. Upon completion, this trial will provide pivotal data to allow assessment of the efficacy and safety of early use of tirofiban in AIS patients without visible large or medium vessel occlusion and with non-cardioembolic stroke.

The epidemiology and hypervirulence of Klebsiella pneumoniae ST23 unveil epidemic risks in China and worldwide
Authors: Haijian Zhou, Chongye Guo, Zhigang Cui, Jinrui Hu, Xiaoli Du, Yan Sun, Linhuan Wu, Biao Kan
Published: 2025

The global dissemination of Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP),1 particularly the emergence of strains with multiple-drug resistance and hyper-virulence underscores the critical epidemiological threat worldwide. As a result, we conducted genomic surveillance to assess the hyper-virulence and transferable antibiotic resistance risks associated with various KP sequence types (STs). Our findings reveal that ST23, characterized by a high prevalence of hyper-virulence and rapidly increasing dissemination potential, may poses a substantial public health threat considering its potential ability for horizontal transfer of carbapenem-resistant genes.

Our study encompassed 4850 genomic sequences collected by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention from 28 provinces in China from 2018 to 2024, and derived from samples including blood, sputum, secretions and the throat swabs etc. Additionally, we compiled a comprehensive dataset of 71,132 KP genomic sequences from 122 countries (1900–2024) via NCBI,2 containing 12,396 sequences from China (methods of analysis see Supplementary methods). This dataset included 3443 STs, with ST23 comprising 2302 genomes, ranking as the sixth most prevalent globally (3%, Fig. 1A, Supplementary Table S1). ST23 was detected in 42 countries across six WHO regions, with relatively high prevalence in the Western Pacific region (6.6%, 1314/19,854) and in China (7.1%, 1235/17,219; Supplementary Figure S1). From 2018 to 2023, the global and Chinese prevalence of ST23 strains exhibited a remarkable average annual growth rate of 30.41% and 59.06%, respectively, significantly surpassing other STs (Fig. 1B). Among the ST23 strains, six K serotypes were predicted. Specifically, ST23-KL1 accounted for a largest proportion of more than 95% (Fig. 1C) globally and in China. Upon examining all KP isolates, we found that the KL1 was present in 62 different STs but represented only 3.3% (2535/75,982) of the total. Notably, 86% of these isolates were ST23-KL1, and 82% (2070/2535) were hvKp. Phylogenetic reconstruction (Fig. 1D) revealed five distinct lineages within the ST23-KL1, The most recent common ancestor (MRCA) was predicted to have emerged around 1618 (95% HPD, 1578–1657), then the Lineage I spread to a wide range of countries. Lineage II originated in 1779 (95% HPD, 1739–1818) and was first detected in China (2010). Most regional characteristics of Lineage III (originated in 1822) and IV (originated in 1753) were in China with a little few regional nodes in European, Americas and other regions. Lineage V originated in 1978 (95% HPD, 1938–2017) remained geographically restricted to Ireland.

There are 8964 hvKp isolates carrying hypervirulence factors (hyper-VFs) (rmpA/rmpA2, iroB, and/or iucA). ST23 displayed the highest proportion of hvKp among all the STs globally (83.8%, 1929/2302). In China, this proportion was even higher at 92% (1142/1235). We also studied CRKP carrying 274 carbapenem-resistant genes (CR-ARGs). The portion of CRKP is range from 14.4% (ST23) to 94.4% (ST258), with an average of 55%. Among them, the proportion of CRKP in ST23 was lowest, accounting for 14% globally (332/2302) and only 5% in China (66/1235), as a result, leading a low portion of CR-hvKp in ST23 for 6% (140/2302). Within ST23, 15 CR-ARGs were identified (Supplementary Figure S3). The result of the association analysis between ARGs and MGEs showed that KPC-2 was associated with 78 MGEs. Similarly, NDM-1 was linked to 43 MGEs (Supplementary Figure S4). Both of them are associated with IncFIB, which was a conjugative plasmid capable of transferring resistance genes between strains.3 The presence of these self-transmissible plasmids strongly suggests that key CR-ARGs in ST23 have acquired horizontal transfer capabilities, raising concerns about their potential for widespread dissemination. Comparing the CRKP (n = 332) and non-CRKP (n = 1835) of ST 23 (Supplementary Figure S5), Hyper-VFs iroB/iucA/rmpA were less prevalent in CRKP (62% vs. 84%). CRKP harbored ARGs against 21 drug classes, with tetracycline resistance showing the largest disparities with non-CRKP (36% vs. 2%). Moreover, CRKP exhibited higher MGE prevalence, including plasmids (20% vs. 3%) and exclusive presence of self-transmissible MGEs like pQil/AP001918/K (IncFIB) plasmids, indicating a potential horizontal gene transfer from CRKP to non-CRKP.

This study provides a detailed analysis of the rapid spread of ST23, especially in China, emphasizing the risks posed by its hyper-virulence and rapid increase in prevalence. Considering the absence of CR-ARGs carried on MGEs, special attention should be directed toward monitoring the carriage and horizontal transfer potential of CR-ARGs to ST23 strains to avoid the hvKp with high portion of CR-ARG. Although the frequency of CR-ARGs in ST23 is currently low, proactive detection and containment measures are essential to prevent the emergence and dissemination of CR-hvKp ST23 strains, which could severely threaten public health.

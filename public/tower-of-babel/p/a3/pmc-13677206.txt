Timed administration of llama-purified Beta-Nerve Growth Factor (βNGF) is associated with changes in steroidogenic and LH receptor gene expression in dairy heifers undergoing hormonal estrous synchronization treatments
Authors: Marcelo H. RATTO, Gerardo López GARCÍA, Ximena VALDERRAMA, César ULLOA-LEAL, Gonzalo GAJARDO, Héctor ZAPATA-CARMONA, Luis PAIVA, Margherita MARANESI, Rodolfo UNGERFELD
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The beta-nerve growth factor (β-NGF), a seminal plasma protein with established luteotrophic effects in camelids and cattle, has been proposed as a modulator of periovulatory follicular function. The optimization of the steroidogenic environment of the preovulatory follicle is critical for oocyte maturation, corpus luteum formation, and embryo quality. However, the molecular actions of β-NGF on granulosa cells (GCs) during the periovulatory period in dairy heifers remain poorly defined. This study evaluated the effects of systemic administration of heterologous β-NGF (purified from llama seminal plasma) on the expression of steroidogenic enzymes in GCs from preovulatory follicles in estrus-synchronized Holstein heifers. Animals received 1 mg of β-NGF intramuscularly on Day 9 (estradiol benzoate administration) or Day 10 (fixed-time artificial insemination) of a progesterone (P4)/estradiol (E2)-based synchronization protocol. The follicular fluid and GCs were collected via transvaginal ultrasound-guided aspiration for hormone quantification and gene expression analysis. β-NGF treatment on Day 9 significantly upregulated Lutenizing hormone/choriogonadotropin receptor (LHCGR) and key steroidogenic enzymes, including 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (HSD3B), Cytochrome P450 family 11 subfamily A member 1 (CYP11A1), 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (HSD17B), and Cytochrome P450 family 19 subfamily A member 1 (CYP19A1), whereas no transcriptional effects were observed when NGF was administered on Day 10, indicating a timing-dependent regulation of GCs differentiation. In contrast, Growth hormone receptor GHR and Steroidogenic acute regulatory protein expression levels remained unaffected, supporting the selectivity of NGF-induced transcriptional modulation. The follicular fluid P4/E2 ratios were not significantly altered by NGF treatment. These findings indicate that β-NGF increased steroidogenic and LHCGR gene abundance of GCs in a timing-dependent manner, suggesting that NGF may represent a potential modulator of the preovulatory follicle function in dairy cattle.

Achieving high-quality embryos is a critical goal in dairy cattle reproduction as it directly influences pregnancy rates, calf viability, and the long-term genetic and productive performance of the herd. Successful embryo development is not only dependent on fertilization but also on the hormonal and cellular environment of the follicle from which the oocyte is delivered. The functional status of the follicle and corpus luteum (CL), as well as the balance between estradiol and progesterone (P4), are key factors that determine oocyte competence, uterine receptivity, and embryo survival during early development [1, 2]. Accordingly, there is an increasing interest in understanding how preovulatory endocrine dynamics influence luteal function and embryonic development. Recent studies have highlighted the importance of the endocrine microenvironment during the preovulatory period in enhancing subsequent luteal function and embryo quality [3, 4]. The endocrine and vascular environments surrounding the pre-ovulatory follicle and early luteogenesis play pivotal roles in determining oocyte competence and subsequent embryo quality [5, 6].

In this context, attention has shifted toward molecular mediators that modulate follicular and luteal functions. One such promising molecule is nerve growth factor (NGF), a neurotrophin initially recognized for its role in the nervous system. However, its effects on ovarian physiology have been increasingly investigated [7]. The neurotrophin beta-nerve growth factor (β-NGF) enhances ovarian function in various species [8,9,10,11,12,13]. Previous studies conducted by Dissen et al. [14] demonstrated that the ovaries of rats express β-NGF and its high-affinity Tyrosine kinase receptor A (TrkA) during late fetal development, with expression decreasing between 24 and 48 h after birth until puberty, and then exhibiting a transient NGF/TrkA activation at the time of the preovulatory surge of Lutenizing Hormone (LH), contributing to the follicular differentiation process preceding the first ovulation. Furthermore, the presence of β-NGF and its receptors was confirmed in bovine ovaries [15, 16], where TrkA expression increases in granulosa cells (GCs) of the preovulatory follicle and luteal cells of the early developing CL [17], indicating a functional role in CL functions.

Functionally, β-NGF emerged as a potent modulator of luteal development and steroidogenic activity. β-NGF, which is abundantly present in the semen of induced ovulating species and, to a lesser extent, also in spontaneous ovulating species. Its systemic administration exerts a strong luteotropic effect in llamas, where the CL that originates after its administration produces more P4 than that formed after gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) challenge [18]. In this species, both mating and intrauterine infusion of seminal plasma result in increased circulating β-NGF levels, which are associated with a greater preovulatory LH surge [19]. While β-NGF stimulates GnRH-LH release directly or indirectly at the hypothalamic-pituitary level [20], it also acts locally at the ovarian level. In vitro addition of β-NGF to primary GC cultures derived from preovulatory follicles collected from llamas increases the expression of CYP11A1 and HSD3B transcripts. Additionally, in vivo administration of β-NGF upregulates the mRNA expression of the steroidogenic acute regulatory protein (STAR) and the steroidogenic enzymes CYP11A1 and HSD3B in llama GCs 10–20 h post-treatment [21].

At least in llamas, β-NGF administration during both the pre- and post-ovulatory periods is crucial for CL functionality [22]. These vascular changes have been associated with a direct effect of β-NGF on vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in GCs of preovulatory follicles in llamas [21, 23] and humans [24]. β-NGF has a potent luteotrophic effect in llamas, increasing P4 secretion [23] and CL vascularization [25]. Although information in cattle is more limited, the administration of β-NGF purified from llamas to beef heifers resulted in higher P4 concentrations than those that received GnRH and developed an accessory CL [10]. Similarly, the administration of heterologous β-NGF during the preovulatory LH surge induced by estradiol benzoate (EB) increased LH release [12] and enhanced the vascular area of the CL and P4 concentrations during the subsequent luteal phase in heifers [26]. This is partially explained by a significant increase in the total number of small luteal cells and upregulation of the STAR gene in the CL [27]. This enhanced luteal activity induced by β-NGF is associated with an increase in gene expression of transcripts related to uterine receptivity and histotrophic composition in dairy heifers [12].

Given the accumulating evidence on the steroidogenic and luteotrophic roles of β-NGF and the relevance of the preovulatory window in determining subsequent follicular and luteal function, in the present study, the effects of the timed systemic administration of heterologous β-NGF were determined within a P4/EB-based fixed-time artificial insemination protocol on steroidogenic and luteogenic gene expression in dairy heifers. Specifically, we assessed the expression of genes related to steroidogenesis, including STAR, HSD17B, CYP19A1, CYP11A1, and HSD3B. Additionally, we examined genes associated with luteogenesis, such as LHCGR and GHR, in GCs collected from the preovulatory follicles of dairy heifers subjected to hormonal estrous synchronization.

Materials and Methods

Ethical statement

All animal care and experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the Animal Protection Law of Chile (2009). The Bioethics Committee of the Universidad Austral de Chile approved all procedures (ref. 351/2019).

β-NGF obtention and purification

Semen from three mature male llamas was collected twice weekly using an artificial vagina, diluted 1:1 (v/v) with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and centrifuged at 1500 × g for 30 min at room temperature. An average of 30 ejaculates was processed per male. Sperm-free seminal plasma was stored at −20°C until β-NGF purification. The β-NGF purification was performed using a combination of chromatographic columns, consisting of an initial hydroxyapatite phase (1 cm × 10 cm, 40 µm, Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA), where elution was carried out by gradient and gravity flow, followed by isocratic conditions in a Sephacryl 200 column using an FPLC system (FPLC; Amersham Laboratories, Piscataway, NJ, USA). This procedure has been widely used and has been validated in previous studies [8, 13, 28].

Animals, estrous synchronization protocol, and treatments

The experiment was conducted at the Vista Alegre Dairy Research Station of UACh, Valdivia. The animals had ad libitum access to ryegrass pasture, commercial pelleted concentrate (composed of wheat, triticale, soybean meal, with 7% crude fiber, 13.9% crude protein, and 1.7% ether extract), and water. Eighty Holstein heifers, aged 15–16 months, with a body weight of 320–330 kg and a CL confirmed by transrectal ultrasonography using a 7.5 MHz linear-array transducer (MyLab30 VET; Esaote Benelux BV, Maastricht, Netherlands), were included in the study.

Estrus synchronization was performed using a conventional P4/EB-based protocol [29]. Briefly, all heifers received 2 mg of EB intramuscularly (IM) along with an intravaginal P4 device (1.0 g of P4; DIB, Zoetis, Chile) on Day 0. On Day 8, the P4 device was removed, and 150 µg of cloprostenol was administered IM (Boviprost, Anasac, Chile). On Day 9, 1 mg of EB was administered IM, after which the heifers were randomly assigned to one of the four experimental groups (n = 20 per group) in a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement. The treatments consisted of IM administration of 1 mg of heterologous β-NGF or PBS, administered on Day 9 or 10. Therefore, the groups were: (i) PBS on Day 9, at the time of EB treatment (group ConD9); (ii) PBS on Day 10, 24 h after EB injection (group ConD10); (iii) administration of β-NGF on Day 9, at the time of EB treatment (group NGFD9); and (iv) administration of β-NGF on Day 10, 24 h after EB treatment (group NGFD10). Collection of follicular fluid and granulosa cells

Granulosa cells were collected by aspirating the preovulatory follicle through transvaginal ultrasound-guided follicular puncture, using a 5.0 MHz convex transducer coupled to an aspiration guide [30], 8 to 10 h after the administration of PBS or β-NGF, respectively (Fig. 1). The follicular fluid (FF) collected from each heifer was examined under a stereoscope to detect the presence of the cumulus-oocyte complex, which was subsequently removed. The samples were then centrifuged at 400 × g for 1 min, and the supernatant was aspirated and stored at −80°C for subsequent hormonal analysis. The cell pellet obtained after centrifugation was resuspended in 2 ml of Ham’s F-12/DMEM and centrifuged again at 1,200 × g for 45 min in a 3 ml, 50% Percoll column to separate GCs from erythrocytes and interstitial cells [31]. Purified GCs were recovered from the upper layer of the Percoll column and stored at −80°C for RNA extraction.

Determination of estradiol and progesterone in FF

The concentration of 17β-estradiol (E2) in FF was determined using a commercial solid-phase radioimmunoassay kit (E2-RIA-CT kit; DIASource ImmunoAssays SA, Louvain-la-Neuve, Belgium), as previously described [12, 26]. The assay’s reported sensitivity was 2.7 pg/ml. The limit of quantification observed for the assay was 1.69 pg/ml (determined to be 90% of the total binding B0 during the assay). The intra-assay coefficients of variation ranged from 0.29% to 3.81%. The concentration of P4 in the FF was quantified using a solid-phase radioimmunoassay kit (PROG-RIA-CT, KIP1458; DIASource ImmunoAssays SA), which has been previously used for plasma P4 determination in bovines [12, 26]. The intra-assay coefficient of variation was 0.37%–3.97%, the minimum detectable limit was 0.05 ng/mL, and the coefficient of variation of the internal standard was < 3.77. The P4/E2 ratio was calculated after analyzing both hormones.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR analysis

RNA was extracted from GCs using a commercial RNA extraction kit (Qiagen Incorporated, Valencia, CA, USA). The extracted RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using SuperScript II reverse transcriptase. Gene detection and quantification were performed using Brilliant SYBR Green QPCR Master Mix. Samples were amplified using an Mx3005XP QPCR thermal cycler under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95°C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 5 sec, annealing at 60°C for 1 min, and extension at 72°C for 1 min, in accordance with standardized laboratory protocols. Data were analyzed using the software associated with the thermal cycler. For each sample, threshold cycle (Ct) values of the transcripts were normalized to the total cDNA concentration using Ct values of the housekeeping gene RPLPO (∆Ct) [31]. Normalization of gene expression data was performed using the 2-ΔCq method [32], where ΔCq target gene = Cq target − Cq endogenous control. The target genes related to steroidogenesis included: STAR, HSD17B, CYP19A1, and CYP11A1. The luteogenic-related genes were LHCGR and GHR. Primer sequences used for QPCR were designed as described by Luo et al. [33] and Rao et al. [34]. The expected sizes of the amplified products are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Normal distribution of the data was confirmed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Preovulatory follicle size, FF volume, RNA concentration, FF P4/E2, and gene expression in GC cells were analyzed using a completely randomized 2 × 2 factorial design, with treatment (Control or NGF) and day (Day 9 or 10) as fixed factors, and their interactions included in the model. A general linear model was fitted using the PROC MIXED procedure in Statistical Analysis System (SAS). Least-squares means (LS Mean) were estimated for each treatment-day combination and compared using Tukey’s test to adjust for multiple comparisons. Data for gene expression are presented as LS Mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM), and differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. Proportional data were analyzed using a generalized linear model with a binomial distribution and logit link (PROC GENMOD, SAS), with treatment and day as the fixed effects. Data were considered statistically significant when P ≤ 0.05, and as trends when 0.1 ≤ P < 0.05.

Discussion

The present findings demonstrate that systemic administration of NGF was associated with changes in granulosa cell gene expression in a time-dependent manner, with a pronounced transcriptional response when NGF is administered before the endogenous LH surge (Day 9). Specifically, NGF administration on Day 9 resulted in significant upregulation of CYP11A1 and HSD3B, two key enzymes involved in P4 biosynthesis, as well as HSD17B and CYP19A1, which are critical for estrogen synthesis. This coordinated upregulation suggests a potential effect of NGF treatment on pathways associated with the steroidogenic function of the preovulatory follicle, rather than inducing an acute change in steroid output. However, CYP19A1 expression was also higher in Con10 than in Con9, suggesting that part of the observed variation may reflect physiological endocrine changes associated with progression through the periovulatory phase of the synchronization protocol independent of NGF treatment. This temporal effect may be related to dynamic changes in follicular steroidogenic activity and P4 toward an endogenous LH surge occurring between Days 9 and 10. In this context, these findings support the concept that the preovulatory functional ‘priming’ has a direct impact on the future corpus luteum, as suggested by earlier studies on follicular–luteal transition [35,36,37]. This effect was further supported by the significant interactions between the administration of NGF and the day it was administered to those genes, indicating that NGF action is contingent on the periovulatory endocrine context rather than on constitutive follicular activation. These results are consistent with the in vivo observations in llamas, where NGF administration upregulated steroidogenic enzymes and promoted luteal vascularization and P4 secretion [22, 23]. They also complement bovine studies showing that NGF increases the luteal expression of CYP11A1, STAR, and LHCGR as well as circulating P4 concentrations during the subsequent luteal phase in cows [10, 26, 27] and possibly in ewes [13]. Our study extends these findings by demonstrating that NGF acts directly on the preovulatory follicle before ovulation, highlighting the effect of NGF on granulosa cell signaling in cattle. This observation provides a mechanistic continuity between early follicular molecular events and luteotrophic outcomes previously reported during the post-ovulatory period.

The observed upregulation of HSD17B deserves particular attention as this enzyme catalyzes the conversion of estrone to estradiol and plays a pivotal role in establishing the estrogenic microenvironment required for final oocyte maturation [38]. Previous studies have shown that differences in gene expression in granulosa cells, including steroidogenic pathway components, are associated with oocyte developmental competence in cattle [39, 40]. Thus, the NGF-induced upregulation of HSD17B may be associated with the differentiation of granulosa cells in the preovulatory follicle. Notably, this effect was restricted to Day 9, reinforcing the idea that NGF responsiveness is tightly coupled to the follicle’s preovulatory differentiation state.

Despite these transcriptional changes, follicular estradiol concentrations and P4/E2 ratios were not significantly different between groups. This apparent dissociation between the increased expression of steroidogenic enzymes and unchanged intrafollicular steroid concentrations is physiologically plausible because the transcriptional upregulation of steroidogenic pathways may precede measurable changes in hormone output, particularly within the narrow periovulatory time window evaluated here. In line with this interpretation, periovulatory regulation of steroidogenic enzyme expression precedes or accompanies changes in estradiol production during the follicular–luteal transition [41]. Consistently, the tendency toward a higher P4/E2 ratio on Day 10 in both experimental groups likely reflected the physiological LH surge following EB administration, which is known to transiently enhance P4 synthesis while suppressing estradiol production [41].

In our study, the mean FF P4/E2 ratio increased from ~3.5 on Day 9 to ~6.8 on Day 10, which reflects this endocrine shift. This has been well-documented in synchronized heifers and reflects the physiological transition toward luteinization [26]. Together, these findings suggest that NGF primarily modulates steroidogenic gene expression in granulosa cells, rather than eliciting acute changes in intrafollicular steroid secretion.

Notably, heifers treated with NGF on Day 9 demonstrated a significant upregulation of LHCGR in granulosa cells. There was a positive correlation between LH concentration and LHCGR expression in vivo bovine granulosa cells and in rat granulosa cells cultured in vitro after FSH priming [33, 42]. Based on a previous study in heifers [12], administration of NGF on Day 9 of the synchronization protocol doubled the magnitude of the LH surge compared to that in untreated females; therefore, this endocrine response could contribute, at least in part, to the rapid transcriptional upregulation of LHCGR observed in granulosa cells. Since LH is a major regulator of periovulatory granulosa cell differentiation and steroidogenic enzyme expression, the transcriptional response reported here may have been partially mediated through LH-dependent signaling pathways. Nevertheless, the presence of NGF receptors in bovine ovarian tissue [15,16,17], together with previous studies in other species reporting β-NGF-mediated regulation of granulosa cell steroidogenic activity [21, 23, 24], suggests that a direct ovarian action or an indirect endocrine-mediated mechanism or a combination of both may contribute to these transcriptional changes. However, this regulatory effect remains controversial because another in vivo study reported no correlation between pulsatile LH secretion and LHCGR expression [43]. No significant NGF-induced effects were detected on GHR or STAR transcript abundance in the present study. GHR expression in the ovary was low during the periovulatory phase and increased during the luteal stage [44], which may explain the absence of detectable effects of NGF at this stage. STAR expression increases predominantly after ovulation, once luteinization has begun, in both cattle and camelids [23, 27], consistent with the observation of unchanged STAR transcript levels in preovulatory granulosa cells. These results further indicated that NGF-induced transcriptional regulation in granulosa cells is selective rather than global.

Although 9 of the 76 animals were not aspirated due to ovulation or follicular rupture at the time of collection, the overall follicular aspiration and FF recovery rates (98.6%) were comparable to those previously reported in Holstein and Gyr heifers [45]. The proportion of aspirated animals decreased in NGF-treated heifers owing to follicle rupture or ovulation; however, preovulatory follicle diameter and FF volume were not affected by treatment or time, except for total RNA yield, which decreased on Day 10 of the protocol. The reduction in RNA levels in these samples may be attributed to the low number of granulosa cells at the time of aspiration. It has been reported that granulosa cell proliferation and antral expansion are not strictly coordinated processes, and these differences may affect the number of granulosa cell layers and their attachment to the basement membrane [46,47,48], thereby influencing granulosa cell detachment during follicular aspiration. Using this aspiration approach, 78% (57/73) of the granulosa cell samples from preovulatory follicles (> 10 mm) yielded sufficient RNA for gene expression analysis. The RNA recovery efficiency exceeded that reported in dairy cows, which was approximately 2.5-fold higher than the values previously reported in Holstein and Gyr heifers [45].

In summary, this study demonstrated that β-NGF increased the expression of key steroidogenic and LHCGR genes in GCs of the preovulatory follicle in a timing-dependent manner, with significant effects observed only when NGF was administered before the occurrence of the LH surge. These early transcriptional changes likely contribute to the enhanced luteal function and P4 secretion observed in previous NGF studies in cattle, and may represent a critical mechanistic link between NGF signaling, follicular competence, and subsequent reproductive success [26, 27]. By acting at the level of the preovulatory follicle, NGF appears to influence the molecular programming of granulosa cells before ovulation, thereby shaping subsequent luteal development. Future research should determine whether NGF-induced transcriptomic priming improves oocyte quality, embryo development, and implantation outcomes, thereby fully defining the reproductive potential of NGF as an adjunct to synchronization protocols in dairy cattle.

In conclusion, the administration of β-NGF to dairy heifers on Day 9 of a P4/E2-based estrus synchronization protocol for fixed-time artificial insemination was associated with a significant upregulation of genes encoding key enzymes for P4 (HSD3B, CYP11A1) and estrogen (HSD17B, CYP19A1) synthesis and LH receptor (LHCGR) in granulosa cells of preovulatory follicles. These effects were dependent on the timing of NGF administration, and were not observed when NGF was administered after the onset of the periovulatory endocrine cascade. The absence of significant changes in the FF P4/E2 ratio suggests that NGF primarily increases steroidogenic gene expression rather than eliciting immediate alterations in hormone secretion. Taken together, these findings support the potential role of NGF as a modulator of preovulatory follicle function during estrus synchronization protocols in dairy heifers.

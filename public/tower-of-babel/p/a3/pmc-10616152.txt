Separating the consumption and investment demands for housing: Evidence from urban China
Authors: Mi Zhou, Yurong Qiao, Jiahong Guo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Over the last two decades, housing price-oriented regulation of the real estate market has sparked various debates in several contexts, including policy effectiveness and institutional bias. Scholars have explored the broad impact of policies such as land use controls, property taxes, and financial leverage on housing prices. However, very few studies have focused on the role of separating the consumption and investment demands for housing, especially when the housing bubble prevails and the basic living demands of residents are not fully met. This paper proposes a theoretical model and an empirical method to separate the consumption and investment demands for housing. Using data on housing sales prices and housing rental prices in 70 cities in China, we apply the CES effect function to exhaustively estimate the size of consumption and investment demand, and use a Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model to measure the heterogeneous effects of consumption demands and investment demands on housing prices and economic growth. Our study provides an insightful snapshot of the gradual housing market reform that is unfolding and has yet to be studied in contemporary China.

Introduction

With the urbanization process and the development of the housing market in China, housing wealth continues to increase, and housing has become the most important asset of Chinese families [1]. Compared with other commodities, housing commodities are special [2]. Housing is not only an indispensable basic consumer product, but also the best investment tool and investment object for Asian residents. On the one hand, housing, as a necessary consumer item, provides basic living elements for families. On the other hand, housing brings asset appreciation and income to the household as an investment product [3]. Due to the inseparable characteristics of housing consumption and investment [4], it is difficult for the government to implement precise housing control policies to ensure the necessary housing consumption of residents and squeeze out the housing investment bubble.

As China enters the middle-income stage, residents' consumption of basic commodities has shifted from excess demand to saturated demand, and consumer demand has been upgraded. Investment demand for housing, a kind of staple durable consumer goods, began to release [5]. A large amount of real estate market demand is not for self-employment, but for speculative profit, which has triggered abnormal fluctuations in housing prices. Further, there are more than 70 upstream and downstream related industries of housing commodities, and changes in housing prices directly affect several other industries. The impact of housing price volatility on residents' income, labor employment, innovation, financial credit, and land finance is extremely complex. Thus, the one-size-fits-all housing regulation policy is powerful and effective, but the boundaries are blurred, and it is likely to bring unstable situations, such as sudden cooling and heating of the housing market and the real economy [6].

The gradual reform of China's housing market provides a successful experience for the problem of housing market regulation in developing countries. In 2016, the China Central Economic Work Conference pointed out that “houses are for living, not for speculation”, and proposed to curb the real estate bubble and prevent the rise and fall of housing prices. Considering that the ''seesaw-style'' short-term emergency response plan of ''suppressing when housing prices rise, and stimulating when housing prices fall'' cannot clearly separate the demands of consumption and investment, the Chinese government treats housing consumption and housing investment separately, and uses a series of policy packages to guarantee housing consumption and suppress housing investment. After that, China's overheated housing investment demand was effectively controlled [7], housing returned to the function of living, and the real estate market remained stable overall.

In theory, the development of housing market research generally follows the basic framework of “housing gradually evolves from consumer goods to investment goods, and affects economic growth through increasing price fluctuations” [8,9]. The focus of housing research has gradually shifted from general research to focusing on housing prices, and the integration of housing prices with expectations, speculation, and other issues is increasing [10]. Developed countries commonly experience the process of “rapid rise in housing prices, speculation, and bubbles”, but except for using market-based methods to puncture the bubble, they have not found a better solution for the housing market. The gradual reform of China's housing market, based on the distinction between consumption demands and demand demands, provides feasible clues for getting rid of the confinement of existing price ideas and thinking about solutions from the perspective of dual demands.

The fluctuation of housing prices is the optimal result of residents' consumption and investment decisions, and the integration of the dual demands of investment and consumption makes it hard for macro-control to clearly distinguish between the two demands, which is the essence of the complex phenomenon of housing regulation. In this paper, we propose a measurement method that separates housing consumption and housing investment using housing data from 70 large and medium-sized cities in China. Further, we use econometric models to verify the heterogeneous effects of regulating housing consumption and housing investment on housing prices and economic growth, and we provide empirical evidence for China's gradual regulation of the housing market through dual demands.

The novelty of our paper is threefold. First, this paper is an exploratory attempt that completely separates consumption and investment demands of urban housing. Residents will also obtain capital gains from the purchase of houses while obtaining the right to live in houses, so consumption and investment demands are inseparable. Separating the consumption and investment demands is helpful for developing countries to squeeze the housing bubble out of the investment sector while ensuring basic housing consumption demand. Second, the use of Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model can help us further examine the cross-level interaction of binary demands. Finally, we study the heterogeneous effects of consumer demand and investment demand hidden behind housing prices on macroeconomic variables and economic growth. This provides empirical evidence for regulating housing prices and stabilizing the housing market from the perspective of dual demands.

The paper is structured into five sections. Following this introduction, section 2 reviews the literature in a wide range of areas, including housing consumption, housing investment, and housing price regulation. In section 3, we comprehensively compared the characteristics of housing consumption and housing investment, and measured the dual demands in 70 Chinese cities in China. In section 4, we use Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model to estimate the heterogeneous effects of housing consumption regulation and housing investment regulation on housing prices and the urban economy. We also summarize China's experience with gradual housing reforms that regulate housing markets by distinguishing between dual demands. Finally, section 5 concludes.

Literature review

Housing consumption

Early research focused on the role of housing as a consumer product in residents' lives and economic growth. Pigou [11] and Haberler [12] first introduced house prices into consumption models and proposed the concept of the real estate wealth effect. Benhabib et al. [13] incorporates housing into macroeconomic growth, the basic modeling idea is that housing is an important consumer product, and residents can spend and allocate time on both ordinary commodities and housing commodities. The life cycle hypothesis argues that households want to consume smoothly over their life cycle and housing can be used to cushion adverse shocks [14]. Recent research has focused on the wealth effect of housing consumption. Campbell and Cocco [15] found that housing is a major component of wealth, and household consumption will be significantly adjusted according to housing prices. Cheng and Fung [16] stated that housing is a kind of illiquid asset and it has different consumption consequences than financial assets as housing services enter the household utility function. Wei and Zhang [17] highlighted China's high savings rate and strong preference for housing commodity consumption. Chen et al. [18] also found that housing dominates household consumption.

Housing investment

With the increasing importance of the production and investment functions of housing, the purchase of housing will increase housing assets. Greenwood et al. [19] consider the difference between housing investment and business investment to explain the correlation between output changes and housing investment. Afterwards, the researchers observed that the fluctuation of housing investment was twice as high as that of non-housing investment, and the relationship between housing volatility and macroeconomic fluctuations began to receive attention, and it advanced in three major directions: First, study the mechanism and process of housing changes under external fluctuations and impact on the economy again. For example, considering the impact of housing production or investment on macroeconomic fluctuations under different external shocks such as interest rates, preferences, technology, and human capital [20]. Second, exploring the allocation of housing investment in investment portfolios according to the investment characteristics of housing [21,22]. Third, consider the interaction between housing and macroeconomics under structural changes [23,24].

Housing market regulation

The sharp fluctuations in housing prices make it important to explore the factors affect housing prices and how to regulate house prices smoothly. The traditional view holds that house price volatility is triggered by deviations in macro policy operations [25]. Recent studies have argued that, at the national and regional levels, housing prices are strongly influenced by factors such as economic cycles, GDP, income growth, inflation, and employment rates [26]. Housing location choices, such as housing characteristics, housing demand, location preference, the choice of housing for self-occupation and rental, and housing policies [27] have also become important influencing factors. After the global financial crisis in 2008, research on the relationship between housing price vulnerability and borrowing financing, currency excess, speculation, expectation, turnover rate, credit, etc. has become a hot issue [[28], [29], [30]]. While there are many factors affecting housing prices, variety methods of housing market regulation have also been implemented. Governments rely on implementing a range of policy packages, including land use controls [31], minimum quality standards [32], real estate tax [33], housing finance [34]. These policy tools are all directly or indirectly regulating housing prices, ignoring the consumption and investment demands that are hidden behind housing prices. Therefore, a one-size-fits-all policy regulation method can easily have an unexpected effect on housing consumption and housing investment at the same time. Contrary to these methods, China has embarked on a unique path of housing market regulation. Through a combination of policies such as housing purchase restriction [35], public rental housing, subsidized rental housing, affordable housing [36] and shared property housing, it has effectively protected housing consumption while suppressing housing speculation.

To the best of our knowledge, most scholars study housing consumption demand and housing investment demand based on household survey data [[37], [38], [39], [40], [41]], and there has been rarely study using an econometric model to fully separate housing consumption from housing investment. The closest paper to this study is that of Yang [42]. They consider a single dwelling as housing consumption and two or more dwellings as housing investment. However, the study found that Chinese homeowners' motivation for purchasing multiple homes is still primarily consumption-based motives. Therefore, it is impossible to truly separate household housing investment from housing consumption. Our work is an improvement of the algorithm. We incorporate both housing consumption and housing investment into the utility model, and completely separate housing consumption and housing investment using data on housing sales prices and housing rental prices in Chinese cities. By estimating the heterogeneous effects of dual demands on macroeconomic variables and economic growth using the 2-HLM model, we provide experience of gradual reforms in Chinese-style housing markets for countries experiencing housing bubbles.

Housing consumption and housing investment: comparisons and measurements

Characteristics of housing consumption and housing investment

Compared with other commodities, housing commodities have their particularities. Housing is not only an essential basic consumer product for families, but also the best investment tool and investment object for residents. It is the entanglement of consumption and investment demands that makes the housing market extremely complex. Taking China as an example, on the one hand, the owner-occupancy rate of housing is above 70 % [37]. Unlike investments in bulk commodities, stocks, jade, etc., abnormal changes in the fluctuation pattern of housing prices will directly affect the consumer demand of owner-occupiers. Based on this reality, this paper proposes that the consumption demands of housing commodity demand is composed of real housing demand and improved housing demand, which are relatively stable. The investment demand focuses on quick profits, and the volatility is relatively large. The proportion of housing consumption demands and housing investment demands affects the final output. Characteristics of housing consumption and housing investment.

Compared with consumer demand, investment demand has excessive expectations and obvious herd effects, so investment demand for housing enters and exits the market frequently and quickly. Moreover, there is a significant asymmetry between the speed of housing supply and the adjustment of housing investment demand, which brings great volatility to the market. The demand for housing investment interferes with the demand for housing consumption, and the macro policy cannot clearly distinguish the two types of demand, so that the housing market mainly supplies speculators with investment needs, but cannot properly supply consumers with housing needs.

Measurement of consumption and investment demands for housing

The baseline model is shown in equation (9). We use GDP per capita to proxy Y‾i,t, housing rental price to proxy Pconsume,i,t, and housing sales price to proxy Pinvest,i,t. α and β are the correlation coefficients we want to estimate, α indicates the size of housing consumption demands, β indicates the size of housing investment demands. εi,t is the random error term.

The data in this paper mainly comes from three data sets, namely the China Statistical Yearbook, the China Real Estate Index System, and the China Urban Statistical Yearbook. The per capita GDP data comes from the China Urban Statistical Yearbook. Considering the availability of data, based on the annual housing rental price index and housing sales price index data of various cities in the China Statistical Yearbook released by the National Bureau of Statistics, combined with the index data from the China Index Research Institute (CRELS) as a supplement and comparison, we finally sorted out and obtained the housing price index of 70 large and medium-sized cities in China from 1997 to 2019. Further, according to the housing rental price index and housing sales price index, with 1997 as the base period, the housing sales price and housing rental price are calculated.

How to stabilize the economy by regulating dual demand?

Empirical design

The separation of dual demands only shows the scale differences in consumption demand and investment demand across different cities, it is not enough to provide experience in regulating the housing market and stabilizing macroeconomic operations. Therefore, we constructed a Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model (2-HLM) to shift the focus from housing prices to the dual demands of housing, thereby illustrating the empirical evidence that China stabilizes housing prices and economic growth by regulating housing consumption and investment demand.

Considering that single-level models cannot accurately handle multi-level data structures, we constructed a 2-HLM to explore the logic of the impact of dual demand above housing prices on the national economy. The variables in 2-HLM were divided into two levels: “individual-level” and “overall-level”. As shown in Fig. 2, we took macroeconomic variables and house price variables as individual-level variables (Level 1), and dual demand variables as overall-level variables (Level 2). Under the hierarchical framework, the difference formed by the overall level (Level 2) is the part below the adjustment line (dotted line). The phenomenon measured by the 2-HLM model is called the “moderation effect”, that is, the effect of cross-level interaction. The advantage of 2-HLM is that it simultaneously considers the effects of different levels of independent variables and different levels of error terms on the dependent variables. The characteristic of the conditional variance of the dependent variable is no longer the characteristic of independent and identical distribution, but includes the variation of the error term with different structures, which reflects the cross-level interaction mechanism of the individual level and the overall level.Fig. 2Empirical design for two-level hierarchical Liner model.Fig. 2

Empirical design for two-level hierarchical Liner model.

Whether the 2-Hierarchical Liner Model is used reasonably requires an intra-group correlation coefficient (ICC) test. Cohn (1998) proposed that ICC has different judgment values in different fields: when ICC is less than 0.059, its effect is negligible. When ICC is between 0.059 and 0.138, it is moderately correlated. When ICC is higher than 0.138, it belongs to a high degree of intra-group correlation, that is, the existence of its similarity cannot be ignored, and multi-level statistical analysis must be considered. Based on the null model without considering the addition of any other variables, we calculated the intra-group correlation coefficient of the ICC coefficient equal to 0.48, which is greater than 0.138. The variation of economic growth among cities accounts for 69 % of the total variation of economic growth in all cities, so the performance of cities and cities in economic growth is significantly different, and multi-level analysis needs to be considered.

Demand for housing investment weakens economic boost from rising house prices. Specifically, when housing consumption demand expands, the rise in housing prices significantly inhibits the promotion of economic growth by per capita disposable income, employment population, industrial innovation, financial credit, and land finance. It is the disorderly expansion of housing investment demand has caused crises and bubbles in macroeconomic operations. The proliferation of speculation can only illustrate temporary prosperity. Only with the support of consumer demand, the steady rise of per capita disposable income and employment population, the continuous development of industrial innovation and land finance, and the orderly expansion of financial credit are reasonable and healthy economic growth. The method of regulating housing prices through separating the consumption and investment demands for housing ensures the living needs of residents, suppresses housing speculation, and ultimately enables the macroeconomy to run smoothly.

Discussion and conclusion

Discussion

Based on the research of large and medium-sized cities, this paper finds that the scale of housing consumption demand in most cities is higher than the scale of housing investment demand, which is the same as the conclusion drawn by Yang [38] after analyzing the housing demand in 35 big cities in China, that is, although the investment demand is growing rapidly, the consumption demand still dominates. Comparing different cities, it is found that the cities with higher housing investment demand are mainly economically developed cities. Du et al. [39], taking small cities in Jiangsu Province as the research object, also confirmed that housing demand has changed from basic consumption demand to investment and housing improvement demand, and proposed that the underdeveloped rental housing market in small cities has limited the choice of households' housing demand.

Previous studies have attempted to distinguish housing consumption and housing investment based on household survey data, but the results were not clear [38]. In this paper, we propose the separation measurement method of housing consumption and housing investment, and empirically test the heterogeneity effect of housing consumption and housing investment regulation on housing price and economic growth. This not only provides solutions for the separation of housing demand, but also expands thinking for the regulation of the real estate market from the perspective of dual demand.

Specifically, housing consumption demands drive up housing prices, significantly promotes the development of per capita disposable income, employment population, industrial innovation, financial credit, and land finance, and ultimately have a significant stimulating effect on economic growth. In contrast to housing consumption demand, the effects of housing investment demand are negative. This provides an empirical basis for our country to gradually regulate the housing market through dual regulation and control, and also provides a new way to improve the existing real estate policy.

A unique experience of China's gradual housing market reform is the separation and regulation of consumption demands and investment demands for housing. Priority is given to ensuring the housing consumption demands, including the marriage housing demands of the marriage-aged population, the living demands of newly graduated college students, the improvement demands of residents without houses and loans, and the improvement demands of second-child families, etc. Accurate research, comprehensive investigation, and online verification are carried out for investment needs such as cross-regional house purchase, house purchase without work, multi-unit house purchase, and rapid transfer transaction purchase. This approach to safeguarding consumer demand can ensure market stability when investment demand shrinks under regulatory policies, and prevent an excessive release of market demand under expansionary policies.

The effective way to regulate the housing market should not be short-term emergency regulations that suppresses when prices are high and stimulates when prices are low. Instead, housing prices that reflect the characteristics of commodities should be handed over to the market, and dual demands that reflect the characteristics of stability should be handed over to the government. On the one hand, play the hand of the market and let the market adjust the characteristics of commodities that are directly related to prices. All factors related to commodity attributes, such as housing price, area, unit type, location, delivery time, and other factors should be regulated by the market, and the government will not directly intervene under normal conditions. On the other hand, give full play to the government, resolutely and long-term crackdown on speculation, deleverage investment demands, and crack down on speculators in a long-term, stable and continuous manner.

Conclusion

Blaming the complex pattern of housing on excessive government intervention or institutional bias tends to oversimplify the issues. The structural dilemma of the housing market is a complex result of the interference between housing consumption and investment needs. Compared with letting the housing bubble burst, if the role of the government is played more scientifically, this contradiction can be solved more smoothly. When developing countries enter the middle-income stage, the market's consumption of basic commodities has shifted from excess demand to saturated demand, and the investment demand for housing and other bulk durable consumer goods has begun to be released. Under the background of this stage of development, the consumer housing demands and improvement housing demands of families with only one house should be guaranteed first. When market regulation is relatively stable, investment demand will be gradually released. This is the rhythm and experience of China's gradual housing market reform.

It should be noted out that the separation of dual housing demands does require a lot of complicated and specific work, but it is a more realistic and less socially turbulent way than property taxes. This paper is a theoretical and empirical attempt. In this paper, we propose a theoretical model and an empirical method to separate the consumption and investment demands for housing. Using data on housing sales prices and housing rental prices in 70 large and medium-sized cities, we exhaustively estimate the scale of consumption and investment demand in these cities, as well as the time trend of binary demands. We find that the scale of housing consumption demand in most cities is higher than the scale of housing investment demand, and cities with higher housing investment demand are mainly economically developed cities. We also use a Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model to estimate the heterogeneous effects of consumption demands and investment demands over housing prices on macroeconomic variables and economic growth. The Results of the Two-level Hierarchical Liner Model confirm the Chinese experience of regulating the housing market based on dual demand.

After entering the middle-income stage, most developing countries are faced with rising housing prices, speculation and housing bubbles. If a one-size-fits-all approach is adopted to suppress housing prices, they are likely to face the pain of economic malaise. In this case, it is a feasible solution to regulate the real estate market by separating the consumption and investment demands for housing. The experience of China's gradual real estate reform has confirmed the advantages of squeezing housing bubbles and stabilizing macroeconomic operations through dual demand management. This Chinese-style policy not only guarantees the basic consumption demands of residents, but also squeezes out the bubble in the real estate market. Our study is an insightful snapshot of real estate market regulations that are unfolding and have yet to be studied in contemporary China.

It should be recognized that this study has limitations. Firstly, the 70 data capacity limitation may lead to deviations from the actual situation. Secondly, the paper is based on the practice of large and medium-sized cities in China, with geographical limitations. Finally, based on the housing sales prices and rental prices obtained in the statistical yearbook, the consumption demand and investment demand derived from this calculation may be slightly biased from the real demand. Future research opportunities could investigate similar issues in a larger scale including smaller cities in China and other countries. Considering the deep application of big data, it may be possible to obtain realistic and more precise housing and investment needs in the future, so that the details of each type of demand can be directly explored as more data is obtained. Further research can consider how economic growth brought about by consumption demand and investment demand improves residents' welfare from a micro perspective.

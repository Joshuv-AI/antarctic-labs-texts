Efficacy and safety of Ruxolitinib-based combination therapy in the patients with Myelofibrosis (MF): a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Shuai Tan, Yuxin Li, Yaofang Cao, Wanling Sun, Wuhan Hui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMyelofibrosis (MF) is a chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm. Although Ruxolitinib, a JAK1/2 inhibitor, remains the cornerstone of MF treatment, it does not reverse disease progression, and resistance frequently emerges. These limitations have prompted investigation into combination therapies targeting pathways beyond the JAK-STAT axis. This meta-analysis aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Ruxolitinib-based combination therapies in patients with MF.MethodsWe conducted a systematic search of databases for studies published through August 1, 2025. Thirteen distinct Ruxolitinib-based combination regimens were included. Primary efficacy endpoints were ≥35% spleen volume reduction at 24 weeks (SVR35) and ≥50% reduction in total symptom score (TSS50). Safety endpoints focused on the incidence of grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia and anemia. Subgroup analyses were performed based on prior JAK inhibitor exposure and therapeutic mechanism of action.ResultsA total of 19 studies comprising 1,088 patients were included in the meta-analysis. Among JAK inhibitor-naïve patients, the combination of Ruxolitinib with Selinexor demonstrated the highest efficacy (SVR35: 92%; TSS50: 78%), followed by Ruxolitinib plus BMS-986158 (SVR35: 90%). For patients with prior JAK inhibitor exposure, Ruxolitinib plus Siremadlin (SVR35: 45%) showed notable activity.ConclusionFor JAK inhibitor-naïve patients, Ruxolitinib-based combination regimens demonstrated satisfactory clinical responses and the potential for meaningful disease control. For patients with prior JAK inhibitor exposure, the addition of combination therapy drugs may further enhance the efficacy. Personalized treatment selection remains essential, as therapeutic efficacy is significantly influenced by prior JAK inhibitor exposure.

Introduction

Myelofibrosis (MF) is a clonal hematologic malignancy that may arise de novo (primary MF, PMF) or evolve secondary to polycythemia vera (PV) or essential thrombocythemia (ET). It is characterized by progressive bone marrow fibrosis (BMF), peripheral cytopenias, splenomegaly, elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines, and a constellation of burdensome constitutional symptoms, including fatigue, pruritus, night sweats, and weight loss [1]. Ruxolitinib, a JAK1/2 inhibitor, remains the standard first-line therapy due to its demonstrated superiority over placebo or best available therapy in reducing splenomegaly and improving symptom burden [2]. However, Ruxolitinib has several limitations. Hematologic toxicity, particularly anemia and thrombocytopenia, is common and mechanistically linked to the JAK-STAT pathway’s role in erythropoietin and thrombopoietin signaling. Moreover, Ruxolitinib has minimal impact on altering disease progression or preventing leukemic transformation to myeloproliferative neoplasm-blast phase (MPN-BP), and a substantial proportion of patients eventually exhibit suboptimal responses or discontinue therapy due to cytopenias [3]. These therapeutic gaps have prompted the investigation of novel agents targeting alternative pathogenic pathways beyond JAK-STAT, including epigenetic regulators, apoptotic machinery, aberrant signaling cascades, and malignant stem cell populations. Several such agents—such as Navitoclax, Pelabresib, and Panobinostat—are being evaluated in combination with Ruxolitinib in ongoing clinical trials [4,5]. However, the current evidence base consists primarily of early-phase, single-arm studies with small cohorts, and few randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or direct comparative studies exist. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to synthesize the available evidence on Ruxolitinib-based combination therapies for both primary and secondary MF, evaluating their efficacy and safety profiles to inform future clinical decision-making and research directions.

Methods

The recommendations of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines were used to formulate the eligibility criteria for the systematic review and meta-analysis [6].

Literature search/search strategy

Databases (PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, LILACS, CNKI, WANFANG, VIP, CBM) and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched up to August 1, 2025, using terms: “Primary Myelofibrosis”, “Myelofibroses”, “Ruxolitinib”, “Jakavi”, “Combination therapy”, “Combination treatment”, using strategy: ((((Myelofibrosis) or (Myelofibroses)) and ((Ruxolitinib) or (Jakavi))) and ((Combination therapy) or (Combination treatment))). Reference lists of relevant reviews were screened.

Outcomes and study selection/literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Prospective/retrospective studies; (2) Patients with PMF or post-PV/ET MF; (3) Ruxolitinib-based combination therapy (e.g. with Selinexor, Pelabresib, BMS-986158); (4) Reported efficacy endpoints: SVR35 (≥35% spleen volume reduction or >50% spleen length reduction by palpation at 24 weeks), TSS50, JAK2V617F allele burden reduction, ≥1 grade bone marrow fibrosis improvement, hemoglobin response (≥1.5 g/dL increase from baseline without transfusions). Exclusion criteria: (1) <5 patients; (2) Non-Chinese/English publications; (3) Reviews, comments, case reports, preclinical studies, poor quality, or duplicate reports. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts, resolving disagreements with a third reviewer.

Data extraction

Two reviewers independently extracted data: first author, year, country, design, NCT ID, follow-up, disease status, sample size, age, sex, treatment, efficacy outcomes (SVR35, TSS50, etc.), and adverse events (AEs) graded by CTCAE v5.0. Latest follow-up data were preferred.

Quality assessment and bias risk assessment

The MINORS tool [7] was used; most studies were of moderate quality. We assessed the publication bias of SVR35 and TSS50 in MF patients who received Ruxolitinib-based combination regimens using Egger’s tests conducted with Stata 15. Sensitivity analyses were conducted by sequentially eliminating each study from the pooled results.

Statistical analysis

Pooled proportions (P) and 95% CIs were calculated using the generic inverse variance method in RevMan 5.4. Standard errors (SE) were derived from proportions. Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q test and I2 statistics (fixed-effects model if I2<50%; random-effects otherwise). Subgroup analyses were performed based on combination drug and prior JAK inhibitor exposure.

Results

Search results/outcomes of the search process

A total of 1,937 potentially eligible articles were initially identified through searches of multiple databases and supplementary sources. After removing duplicates using EndNote X9, 1,291 unique articles remained. Screening of these articles by title and abstract identified 63 for full-text review. Ultimately, 19 prospective studies met all predefined inclusion criteria and were included in the final meta-analysis. Collectively, these studies evaluated efficacy and safety outcomes in 1,088 patients. The literature selection process is depicted in Figure 1. Among the 19 studies, Ruxolitinib and Selinexor combination therapy was used in 2 study [8,9], Panobinostat and Ruxolitinib in 2 studies [10,11], Pelabresib and Ruxolitinib in 3 studies [12–14], Navitoclax and Ruxolitinib in 3 studies [15–17], Pegylated Interferon Alfa 2a (IFNα) and Ruxolitinib in 1 study [18], Ruxolitinib and 5-Azacitidine (AZA) in 1 study [19], Ruxolitinib and Vismodegibin 1 study [20], Ruxolitinib and Sonidegib in 1 study [21], Ruxolitinib and Siremadlin in 1 study [22], Ruxolitinib and Parsaclisib in 1 study [23], Ruxolitinib and Buparlisib in 1 study [24], Ruxolitinib, Nilotinib and Prednisone in 1 study [25], Ruxolitinib and BMS-986158 in 1 study [26]. In the study on combination therapy of Ruxolitinib and Buparlisib, Group A comprised JAK inhibitor–naïve MF patients, and Group B included patients who had previously received JAK inhibitors. For the purpose of this analysis, these cohorts were treated as two separate single-arm studies [24]. Efficacy

Data on spleen volume reduction of ≥ 35% (referred to as SVR35) or spleen length reduction >50% by palpation at 24 weeks, a total symptom score reduction of ≥ 50%(TSS50), reduction of JAK allele burden, ≥1 grade improvement of myelofibrosis and hemoglobin response were extracted from 19 articles. Since this analysis mixes early-phase studies, single-arm trials, and dose-finding analysis: such heterogeneity methodologically inappropriate for generating pooled effect measures. Therefore, we will directly conduct subgroup analyses in the following text.

Subgroup analysis of different combination therapy drugs

We also conducted subgroup analyses of SVR35 and TSS50 based on drugs combined with Ruxolitinib, dividing SVR35 into 13 subgroups of combination therapy and TSS50 into 8 subgroups. Subgroup analysis showed that two studies reported on Selinexor and Ruxolitinib combination therapy, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 2A) was 65% (95% CI: 13–118%), while the pooled TSS50 (Figure 2N) was 54% (95% CI: 11–98%). Three studies reported on Pelabresib and Ruxolitinib combination therapy, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 2B) was 51% (95% CI: 21–81%, I2 = 98%), while the pooled TSS50 (Figure 2O) was 49% (95% CI: 38–59%, I2 = 73%). Two studies reported on Panobinostat and Ruxolitinib combination therapy, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 2I) was 51% (95% CI: 27–75%, I2 = 67%), while the pooled TSS50 (Figure 2R) was 25% (95% CI: 1–49%), which was not statistically significant. Three studies reported on Navitoclax and Ruxolitinib combination therapy, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 2F) was 46% (95% CI: 16–76%, I2 = 96%), while the pooled TSS50 (Figure 2Q) was 25% (95% CI: 18–32%, I2 = 0%). One study reported on Sonidegib and Ruxolitinib combination therapy, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 2K) was 44% (95% CI: 26–63%), while the pooled TSS50 (Figure 2S) was 42% (95% CI: 28–56%). Besides, one article each reported the combination therapy of BMS-986158 and Ruxolitinib, Nilotinib, Prednisone and Ruxolitinib, IFNα and Ruxolitinib, 5-AZA and Ruxolitinib, indicating that the pooled SVR35 was 90% (95% CI: 71–109%), 75% (95% CI: 45–105%), 70% (95% CI: 56–85%) and 58% (95% CI: 43–73%), respectively. Other effect results can be seen in Figure 2. Additionally, multiple studies have shown that the combination regimens lead to a decrease of the JAK2V617F allele burden [11,12,22], a decrease in myelofibrosis grade [10,12,14,15] and an improvement in anemia [12,14–16] For example, Ruxolitinib combined with Siremadlin, Panobinoatat and Pelabresib resulted in a decrease of the JAK2V617F allele burden, suggesting the clearance of tumor clones. The combination regimens of Ruxolitinib with Panobinoatat, Pelabresib and Naxitoclax promoted a decrease in myelofibrosis grade, suggesting the improvement in fibrosis. Ruxolitinib combined with Pelabresib and Naxitoclax helped to improve anemia.

Subgroup analysis of different prior treatment

According to whether patients had previously received JAK inhibitor treatment, the study was divided into two subgroups: JAK inhibitor–naïve patients and JAK inhibitor–treated patients. In the study evaluating the combination of Buparlisib and Ruxolitinib, Group A consisted of JAK inhibitor–naïve MF patients, while Group B included JAK inhibitor-treated MF patients. Therefore, this study was treated as two separate single-arm studies. Four studies explicitly excluded patients who had been treated with Ruxolitinib. Given that Ruxolitinib is the standard first-line treatment for myelofibrosis (MF), these four studies were temporarily categorized as involving JAK inhibitor–naïve patients. Eleven studies reported SVR35 outcomes (Figure 3A), and six studies reported TSS50 outcomes (Figure 3B) for JAK inhibitor–naïve MF patients, yielding pooled response rates of 65% (95% CI: 57–72%, I2 = 69%) and 52% (95% CI: 43–61%, I2 = 53%), respectively. In comparison, six studies reported SVR35 (Figure 3A) and four studies reported TSS50 (Figure 3B) for JAK inhibitor–treated MF patients, with pooled response rates of 22% (95% CI: 11–32%, I2 = 82%) and 26% (95% CI: 15–37%, I2 = 77%), respectively. Among various combination regimens used in treatment-naïve patients, the combination of Selinexor with Ruxolitinib demonstrated the highest efficacy, with an SVR35 (Figure 4A) of 92% (95% CI: 76–107%) and a TSS50 (Figure 4P) of 78% (95% CI: 51–105%). The combination of BMS-986158 and Ruxolitinib achieved an SVR35 (Figure 4C) of 90% (95% CI: 71–109%), although symptom response data were not available. IFNα combined with Ruxolitinib yielded an SVR35 (Figure 4D) of 70% (95% CI: 56–85%). Pelabresib combined with Ruxolitinib yielded an SVR35 (Figure 4B) of 66% (95% CI: 61–72%, I2 = 0%) and a TSS50 (Figure 4Q) of 53% (95% CI: 48–59%, I2 = 0%). In JAK inhibitor-treated patients, the combination of Siremadlin and Ruxolitinib showed relatively higher efficacy, with an SVR35 (Figure 4N) of 45% (95% CI: 16–75%). Selinexor combined with Ruxolitinib in this subgroup achieved an SVR35 (Figure 4J) of 38% (95% CI: 17–59%) and a TSS50 (Figure 4U) of 33% (95% CI: 16–51%). Other effect sizes are presented in Figure 4.

Subgroup analysis of different mechanisms of combination therapy drugs

According to the types of drugs combined with Ruxolitinib, there are ten categories: HDAC inhibitors, BET inhibitors, Bcl-2 inhibitors, Hedgehog inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, Hyomethylating agents, Interferon, XPO1 inhibitors, MDM2 inhibitors, and Tyrosine kinase inhibitors. Among them, research on XPO1 inhibitors includes two studies of Selinexor combined with Ruxolitinib, with an SVR35 of 65% (95% CI: 13–118%) and a TSS50 of 54% (95% CI: 11–98%). Research on BET inhibitors includes three studies of Pelabresib combined with Ruxolitinib and one study of BMS-986158 combined with Ruxolitinib, showing good efficacy, with an SVR35 of 60% (95% CI: 33–87%, I2 = 97%) and a TSS50 of 49% (95% CI: 38–59%, I2 = 73%). Research on HDAC inhibitors includes two studies of Panobinostat combined with Ruxolitinib, with an SVR35 of 51% (95% CI: 27–75%, I2 = 67%) and a TSS50 of 25% (95% CI: 1–49%). Research on Bcl-2 inhibitors includes three studies of Navitoclax combined with Ruxolitinib, with an SVR35 of 46% (95% CI: 16–76%, I2 = 96%) and a TSS50 of 25% (95% CI: 18–32%, I2 = 0%). Research on Hedgehog inhibitors combined with Ruxolitinib includes one article on Vismodegib combined with Ruxolitinib and one article on Sonidegib combined with Ruxolitinib, with a pooled SVR35 of 40% (95% CI: 24–56%, I2 = 0%) and a TSS50 of 40% (95% CI: 27–52%, I2 = 0%). Additionally, one article each reported combination therapies of Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, Interferon, Hyomethylating agents, and MDM2 inhibitors with Ruxolitinib, indicating that the pooled SVR35 (Figure 5A) was 75% (95% CI: 45–105%), 70% (95% CI: 56–85%), 58% (95% CI: 43–73%), and 45% (95% CI: 16–75%), respectively. Toxicity

Pooled results of grade 3/4 AEs between different combination therapy

The adverse events (AE) are mainly hematological AEs. Common hematological AEs included thrombocytopenia, anemia, and neutropenia. Subgroup analysis of hematological adverse events was conducted based on JAK inhibitor treatment status. In JAK inhibitor–naïve patients, the pooled rates of grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia (Figure 6A) and anemia (Figure 6B) were 17% (95% CI: 12–23%, I2 = 51%) and 29% (95% CI: 20–38%, I2 = 74%), respectively. In JAK inhibitor–treated patients, the corresponding rates were 34% (95% CI: 19–49%, I2 = 90%) and 28% (95% CI: 16–39%, I2 = 79%). Additionally, significant non-hematological AEs were reported, including nausea, vomiting, pneumonia, diarrhea, rash, weakness, fatigue, hypoxia, dyspnea, falls, elevated ALT and AST levels, and hypocalcemia.

Publication bias

We assessed the publication bias of SVR35 and TSS50 in MF patients who received Ruxolitinib-based combination regimens using Egger’s tests conducted with Stata 15. Evidence of publication bias was found after pooling the odds ratio (OR) assessments from all included studies, likely due to the relatively small sample sizes of the individual studies (SVR35: Egger’s test p = 0.013; TSS50: Egger’s test p = 0.210; Reduction in JAK allele burden: Egger’s test p = 0.484; ≥1-grade improvement in myelofibrosis: Egger’s test p = 0.373; Hemoglobin response, Egger’s test p = 0.505).

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses were conducted by sequentially eliminating each study from the pooled results, revealing that the combined findings are adequately robust.

Discussion

This meta-analysis of 19 trials involving 1,088 patients provides comparative efficacy and safety data on Ruxolitinib-based combination therapies in myelofibrosis (MF). In the absence of head-to-head trials, our findings suggest the following:

Ruxolitinib + XPO1 Inhibitor (Selinexor)

This combination demonstrated satisfactory efficacy, particularly in JAK inhibitor–naïve patients (SVR35 92%, TSS50 78%), while still maintaining benefit in previously treated patients (SVR35 38%, TSS50 33%). Selinexor exerts its effects by inhibiting XPO1, thereby impacting multiple MF-related pathways, including STAT, ERK, and AKT signaling [27–29]. In preclinical investigations of MF, synergistic activity has been observed between Selinexor and Ruxolitinib, consistent with our conclusion and suggesting the potential for SEL as a backbone for novel treatment combinations for MF [30]. However, limited safety data and only two studies necessitate further validation.

Ruxolitinib + BET inhibitors (Pelabresib, BMS-986158)

This regimen showed high efficacy in treatment-naïve patients (Pelabresib: SVR35 66%, TSS50 53%; BMS-986158: SVR35 90%). BET inhibitors interfere with epigenetic regulation and have demonstrated synergistic effects with JAK inhibitors in preclinical models [31]. Efficacy of pelabresib was reduced in previously treated patients (SVR35 20%, TSS50 37%). Pooled analysis of the BET inhibitor group supports a strong overall efficacy profile (SVR35 60%, TSS50 49%). Pelabresib additionally improved fibrosis grade, anemia, and decreased allele burden. Currently, a clinical trial led by Claire N Harrison is underway, aiming to investigate whether Pelabresib in combination with Ruxolitinib will result in improved efficacy outcomes compared with Ruxolitinib in combination with placebo [32]. Regarding the efficacy and safety of the combined treatment of Pelabresib and Ruxolitinib, more answers will be available in the future.

Other combinations in JAK inhibitor-naïve patients

Ruxolitinib combined with IFN-α (SVR35 70%), Navitoclax (Bcl-2 inhibitor: SVR35 62%, TSS50 31%), 5-azacitidine (Hyomethylating agent, SVR35 58%), Buparlisib (PI3K inhibitor, SVR35 55%), and Panobinostat (HDAC inhibitor, SVR35 51%) showed varying degrees of efficacy, though generally lower than XPO1i or BETi combinations. Data on efficacy in previously treated patients were limited or less favorable.

Not recommended

Combinations with Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (Sonidegib, Vismodegib) and the PI3K inhibitor Parsaclisib demonstrated low SVR35 rates (36–44% and 26%, respectively), with no clear evidence of improved efficacy over Ruxolitinib monotherapy. These combinations are unlikely to warrant further development in MF.

Prior JAK inhibitor exposure

Across most combinations, efficacy was significantly greater in JAK inhibitor–naïve patients. Among previously treated patients, combinations with Selinexor, Siremadlin (MDM2 inhibitor: SVR35 45%), and the triple regimen of Ruxolitinib/nilotinib/prednisone (SVR35 75% in a mixed treatment-status cohort) appeared most promising; however, the number of cases was small, with only eight patients assessed for spleen length. Currently, there are relatively few combination therapies for MF patients exposed to JAK2 inhibitors, and further exploration is needed in the future.

Safety

Hematologic adverse events were predominant, with anemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia being the most common. Anemia rates were highest with Panobinostat (49%) and Siremadlin (48%), while thrombocytopenia was most frequent with Siremadlin (48%) and Navitoclax (43%). Notably, combinations involving XPO1 and BET inhibitors showed relatively lower hematologic toxicity; for example, Selinexor was associated with a thrombocytopenia rate of just 7%. Non-hematologic AEs were frequently observed but varied widely across studies.

Limitations

The analysis is constrained by small sample sizes per combination regimen, a small number of randomized controlled trials (RCTs), limited data on long-term outcomes (e.g. progression-free survival, overall survival), and absence of direct comparative trials. The potential for publication bias must also be acknowledged.

Conclusion

While Ruxolitinib remains the standard treatment for MF, its limitations in disease modification and long-term efficacy highlight the need for novel therapeutic strategies. Ruxolitinib-based combination regimens offer improved clinical responses and the potential for more meaningful disease control. The optimal regimen should be individualized based on disease characteristics and prior treatment history. Efficacy is highly influenced by prior JAK inhibitor exposure. Future research should prioritize large-scale RCTs, evaluation of long-term outcomes, head-to-head comparisons, and the optimal timing and sequencing of combination strategies.

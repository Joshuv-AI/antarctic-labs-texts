Temporal forecasting of electric vehicle charging load using an IVY-VMD-TCN-BiLSTM model with cross-zone evaluation
Authors: Wentao Zhang, Bin Ma, Dong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the rapid proliferation of electric vehicles, accurate charging load forecasting has become critical for grid stability and infrastructure planning. In light of the limitations of traditional forecasting methods in addressing the complex nonlinear coupling relationships between charging loads and multi-source influencing factors, as well as their inadequacies in handling distribution heterogeneity across different spatial regions, this paper proposes a hybrid model based on the IVY-optimized Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM). The IVY optimization algorithm is employed to adaptively determine the hyperparameters of Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), mitigating over-decomposition or under-decomposition caused by empirical settings. This process decomposes the original load series into multiple stationary modal components. These components are then fed into the TCN-BiLSTM model, where TCN extracts local temporal features and BiLSTM captures bidirectional long-term dependencies, enabling collaborative modeling of multi-scale temporal characteristics. Experimental results based on a real electric vehicle charging load dataset collected from Shenzhen, a major metropolitan city in China, demonstrate that the proposed model significantly outperforms the comparative models in terms of prediction accuracy and stability. The achieved Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Coefficient of Determination (R2) are 0.98308, 1.3208, and 0.99039, respectively, confirming the model’s high-precision forecasting capability and strong generalization performance across different spatial regions with heterogeneous charging characteristics.

Introduction

Driven by the global transition toward clean energy and environmental sustainability, Electric Vehicles (EV) have been rapidly deployed worldwide, leading to a continuously increasing penetration level in power systems. The large-scale integration of EV charging introduces new and highly volatile electricity demand, which poses potential challenges to power grid operation and dispatch1,2. In particular, the high charging power of electric vehicles, coupled with the strong stochasticity of charging behaviors, imposes unprecedented challenges on power system dispatch3. During peak periods or within specific regions, the simultaneous concentration of charging demand can lead to sharp surges in local load, potentially causing supply bottlenecks and intensifying operational stress. This, in turn, poses significant challenges to the stable operation and efficient management of the power grid.

Therefore, high-accuracy forecasting of EV charging load is of great significance for supporting the scientific planning and operation of charging infrastructure4. Against this background, short-term spatiotemporal forecasting of EV charging demand plays an important role in smart grid management and electricity pricing. By providing reasonably accurate predictions of charging demand across different regions over the next few hours, power utilities can design more effective time-of-use pricing strategies to guide users away from peak periods, thereby improving overall system efficiency5. Meanwhile, the forecasting results can support grid dispatch by enabling dynamic load adjustment and optimized energy allocation, helping to reduce peak–valley differences and enhance the safety and flexibility of grid operation . In addition, short-term forecasting can also improve user experience by recommending more suitable charging times or locations, reducing waiting times, and increasing the convenience and cost-effectiveness of charging6.

Existing studies on EV charging load forecasting can be broadly classified into two categories: model-driven methods and data-driven methods7. Model-driven approaches typically rely on simulating user travel patterns and charging behaviors to construct probabilistic models for load estimation8. Representative techniques include monte carlo simulation9, travel behavior modeling10, and markov decision processes11, which are employed to characterize the evolution of charging demand. However, with the rapid increase in electric vehicle penetration, the scale of charging load has grown significantly, and the spatial distribution of charging stations in urban environments is highly uneven. Charging behaviors in different regions are jointly influenced by multiple factors, including geographical location, traffic conditions, and surrounding facilities, resulting in increasingly complex spatiotemporal distribution characteristics12,13. Against this backdrop, traditional probabilistic models have inherent limitations in capturing the intricate coupling relationships between charging demand and multidimensional dynamic factors such as electricity prices, weather conditions, and traffic status, leading to significant forecasting deviations in high-penetration scenarios.

To overcome the limitations of traditional model-based forecasting methods, researchers have increasingly adopted algorithm-driven, data-oriented approaches for prediction tasks in recent years14–16. Among them, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), leveraging causal and dilated convolutional structures, can effectively extract local features from time series and capture long-range dependencies, and are therefore widely used in electric vehicle load forecasting. Turkoğlu et al.17constructed a robust hierarchical short-term load forecasting framework based on TCN, and, by integrating an optimal valley-filling demand response strategy, achieved effective scheduling of electric vehicle charging load and mitigation of grid load fluctuations.However, when used alone, TCN exhibits certain limitations in capturing dynamic temporal dependencies due to its feedforward architecture, making it difficult to fully represent the complexity and strong stochasticity of EV charging load, which may consequently degrade prediction accuracy. To further improve forecasting performance, researchers have introduced BiLSTM, which enhances the representation of sequential dynamics by modeling both forward and backward temporal dependencies18. Building upon this, hybrid TCN-BiLSTM models have been widely applied in EV charging load forecasting to achieve the synergy between local feature extraction and global temporal dependency modeling19. Nevertheless, existing studies still suffer from insufficient feature collaboration mechanisms and suboptimal parameter configurations, often relying on empirical settings, which limits the ability to fully exploit the advantages of individual components and thus constrains further performance improvement.

In response to the high temporal complexity and dynamic evolution characteristics exhibited by EV charging loads, as well as the limitations of traditional probabilistic models in capturing nonlinear relationships, this study proposes a novel EV load forecasting model. While spatial factors significantly influence charging demand patterns, this study focuses on temporal forecasting accuracy and evaluates model performance across different functional zone types to account for spatial heterogeneity. Algorithmic principles

IVY optimization algorithm

The IVY algorithm is a metaheuristic optimization algorithm inspired by the growth behaviors of ivy plants in natural environments, such as climbing, spreading, and phototropism20. The algorithm simulates the orderly and coordinated expansion patterns of an ivy population in space, as well as its dynamic evolutionary process. By introducing differential equations to describe growth dynamics and incorporating data-intensive experimental validation, a mathematical model of vine growth rates is established. During the optimization process, each growth unit adaptively determines its subsequent growth direction by perceiving the state information of neighboring individuals. Compared with other metaheuristic algorithms, the IVY algorithm exhibits advantages in terms of fast convergence, strong adaptability, and high robustness21. During the optimization process, ivy individuals continuously climb toward the light source by learning from the strongest neighboring individuals. After eliminating individuals with poor fitness, the population propagates and mutates around high-fitness individuals to generate new solutions, thereby completing the population update22. Essentially, this process reflects the gradual convergence of model hyperparameters toward the global optimum guided by neighboring optimal hyperparameters.

Compared with conventional metaheuristic algorithms, the IVY algorithm exhibits distinct advantages in terms of convergence characteristics and global search capability. GWO and PSO, as population-based swarm intelligence algorithms, rely on social hierarchy and velocity-position update mechanisms, respectively, which often suffer from premature convergence to local optima when handling complex, high-dimensional parameter spaces. SA, as a single-solution stochastic optimization method, employs a probabilistic acceptance mechanism for escaping local optima; however, its convergence speed is typically slow due to its sequential search nature and heavy dependence on annealing schedule tuning. In contrast, the IVY algorithm integrates neighborhood cooperative search with optimal solution guidance, enabling a balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off through adaptive growth rate adjustment. The competitive exclusion strategy, which eliminates low-fitness individuals and propagates high-fitness solutions, accelerates convergence toward the global optimum while maintaining population diversity. These mechanistic advantages make IVY particularly suitable for adaptive hyperparameter optimization in complex forecasting models.

Enhanced VMD decomposition

The time series of EV charging loads is affected by multiple exogenous factors, including seasonal variations, holiday effects, and meteorological conditions, resulting in pronounced nonstationarity and high volatility23. These characteristics significantly increase the difficulty of time-series forecasting and often lead to degraded prediction accuracy, thereby undermining the reliability of forecasting results. To mitigate these issues, this study employs the VMD–based signal processing approach24, which utilizes variational principles to iteratively decompose EV charging load time-series data and extract intrinsic frequency components. The original load sequence is decomposed into a set of intrinsic mode functions (IMFs), enabling the extraction of representative frequency-domain features and effective handling of nonlinear and nonstationary behaviors. In EV charging load analysis, envelope entropy is an effective statistical measure for quantifying the complexity of amplitude variations in load time series. By characterizing the temporal distribution of instantaneous envelope energy, it reflects the degree of orderliness and inherent uncertainty in load evolution27. Dispersed envelope energy distributions with enhanced volatility correspond to higher envelope entropy values, whereas concentrated energy distributions or pronounced periodic and structural patterns yield lower envelope entropy. Load variations dominated by user charging behaviors and operational regularities typically exhibit strong temporal correlations and stable dynamics, resulting in relatively low envelope entropy.

TCN-BiLSTM forecasting model

TCN is a novel time-series modeling approach based on the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture, designed to address limitations of conventional Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), such as difficulty in parallel computation, gradient explosion, and long-term dependency modeling28. The core TCN architecture mainly consists of causal convolutions, dilated convolutions, and residual connections. The model extracts local temporal patterns using convolutional kernels that move along the time dimension. By introducing a dilation factor that inserts fixed intervals between adjacent kernel elements, the receptive field can be systematically expanded to capture long-range temporal dependencies, achieving exponential growth of the receptive field without significantly increasing computational complexity or the number of model parameters, as illustrated in Fig. 129,30. Specifically, the figure shows a dilated convolution structure with dilation rates of 1, 2, and 4 across successive layers, where each convolution operation skips a fixed number of input elements, allowing the network to aggregate information from a wider temporal context without increasing the number of parameters. This design enables the TCN to efficiently capture both short-term fluctuations and long-term periodic patterns in the charging load sequence. Residual connections facilitate stable gradient flow during backpropagation, effectively mitigating the vanishing gradient problem.Fig. 1Dilated convolution structure diagram.

The Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network represents a key extension of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network within the Bidirectional Recurrent Neural Network (BiRNN) framework31. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the BiLSTM architecture consists of two independent LSTM layers: a forward LSTM that processes the input sequence from time step 1 to T, capturing dependencies from past to future, and a backward LSTM that processes the sequence in reverse order from time step T to 1, capturing dependencies from future to past. The hidden states from both directions are then concatenated at each time step to form the final output, enabling the model to leverage complete contextual information from the entire sequence. This bidirectional structure is particularly advantageous for electric vehicle charging load forecasting, as charging decisions are influenced not only by historical patterns but also by anticipated future events such as upcoming traffic conditions or time-of-use pricing changes. In this study, a cascaded TCN-BiLSTM architecture is employed to achieve hierarchical representation and deep dependency modeling of time-series data. In this structure, the TCN serves as the front-end feature extraction module, receiving the input time-series data and constructing multi-scale temporal receptive fields through causal and dilated convolutions. This enables the hierarchical extraction of local and medium-to-long-term structural features. The output of the TCN can be expressed as:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} h_{\textrm{TCN}}(t) = \textrm{TCN}[x(t)] \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$h_{\textrm{TCN}}(t)$$\end{document} denotes the feature output sequence obtained through multiple layers of causal and dilated convolutions, and x(t) represents the input sequence.

The BiLSTM acts as a sequence modeling module, taking the temporally relevant features extracted by the TCN and dynamically selecting, retaining, and updating them through its unique gating mechanisms, including the input gate, forget gate, and output gate. This enables the model to effectively capture long-term dependencies across multiple time scales while maintaining stable hidden state evolution. At time step t, the forward LSTM models the forward dependencies based on previous hidden states, and its update is formulated as:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \overrightarrow{h}(t) = \textrm{LSTM}_{\textrm{forward}}[h_{\textrm{TCN}}(t), \overrightarrow{h}(t-1)] \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overrightarrow{h}(t)$$\end{document} represents the output of the forward LSTM at time step t.

The backward LSTM, in contrast, models dependencies in reverse from future time steps:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \overleftarrow{h}(t) = \textrm{LSTM}_{\textrm{backward}}[h_{\textrm{TCN}}(t), \overleftarrow{h}(t+1)] \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overleftarrow{h}(t)$$\end{document} represents the output of the backward LSTM at time step t.

The final BiLSTM output is obtained by concatenating the hidden states from both the forward and backward directions:13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} h_{\textrm{BiLSTM}}(t) = [\overrightarrow{h}(t); \overleftarrow{h}(t)] \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The TCN-BiLSTM forecasting model achieves deep integration of convolutional multi-scale feature extraction and bidirectional temporal dependency modeling, thereby simultaneously enhancing the feature hierarchy and temporal contextual information33,34. Specifically, the TCN functions as a front-end feature encoder, leveraging causal and dilated convolutions to extract multi-scale temporal patterns while suppressing noise and redundant information, which leads to more compact and informative representations. Building upon these refined representations, the BiLSTM operates as a temporal dependency modeling module, capturing long-term dependencies and global temporal structures through its bidirectional recurrent mechanism, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of sequence dynamics. This cascaded design further enforces a structured information flow, allowing temporal dependency learning to be performed on a higher-level feature space rather than directly on raw inputs, thereby reducing modeling complexity and improving training efficiency.

During the iterative process, the IVY algorithm integrates neighborhood cooperative search and optimal solution guidance mechanisms to dynamically update hyperparameter combinations. It introduces a growth vector to adjust the search step size, thereby achieving an adaptive balance between exploration and exploitation in the solution space. Through a competitive exclusion strategy that retains the best-performing individuals, the IVY algorithm progressively converges to the optimal hyperparameter configuration, ultimately constructing a TCN-BiLSTM prediction model with enhanced generalization capability.

IVY-optimized VMD-TCN-BiLSTM model construction

Dataset description and feature selection

This study conducts EV charging load forecasting based on the publicly available Shenzhen dataset spanning from September 1, 2022, to February 28, 2023, with a temporal resolution of 1 hour35. The dataset is divided into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio. Furthermore, the dataset is segmented into 275 traffic zones along with their adjacency relationships, encompassing parameters such as EV charging load, real-time electricity price, the number of charging stations (fast/slow), charging station occupancy rate, and zone area, thereby representing the spatial characteristics of urban EV charging load.

To improve data quality and enhance the predictive performance of the model, a systematic feature preprocessing pipeline is constructed, including missing value imputation, feature selection, and data normalization. Missing values in the raw dataset are imputed using cubic spline interpolation, where piecewise cubic polynomials are employed to achieve smooth reconstruction of the time series, ensuring data continuity and preserving intrinsic structural characteristics. Pearson correlation analysis is conducted in the original feature space to quantify linear relationships among variables36, and highly correlated redundant features are removed accordingly, thereby reducing input dimensionality, alleviating multicollinearity, and improving the effectiveness and compactness of feature representation. This analysis reveals the relationships between charging load and parameters such as average temperature, relative humidity, and charging station occupancy rate, with the results presented in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Heatmap of pearson correlation matrix.

Heatmap of pearson correlation matrix.

As shown in Fig. 3, a strong linear correlation is observed between regional charging duration and charging load. In contrast, regional area, real-time electricity price, and its service fee exhibit weaker associations with the charging load. To achieve more efficient feature selection, this study excludes three features: regional area, dynamic electricity price, and price service fee.To eliminate the influence of scale differences among features, Z-score normalization is performed on the input data. Specifically, the mean and standard deviation are computed from the training set, and the same statistical parameters are consistently applied to the validation and test sets to ensure distributional consistency.

It should be noted that the dataset spans from September 2022 to February 2023, covering only autumn and winter seasons, which may limit the model’s generalizability to spring and summer periods with potentially different charging patterns.

To ensure reproducibility, the search space bounds for the key hyperparameters are defined prior to optimization, including the number of modes k, maximum number of iterations, number of filters, filter size, number of residual blocks, and dropout rate.These ranges are determined based on prior empirical studies and preliminary experiments. The range for maximum iterations from 10 to 200 ensures sufficient convergence while maintaining computational efficiency. The number of filters and kernel size are selected to balance feature extraction capability and model complexity, referencing typical configurations in TCN-based forecasting studies. Comparative experiment design

To systematically evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed IVY optimization strategy in model parameter tuning, this study selects GWO, PSO, and SA as comparative methods. GWO simulates the social hierarchy and hunting behavior of grey wolves, guiding the search through alpha, beta, and delta leaders; however, its tendency to converge prematurely often limits its performance in complex, high-dimensional parameter spaces. PSO emulates the collective behavior of swarms by updating particle velocities based on individual and global best positions, yet it frequently struggles with balancing exploration and exploitation during optimization. SA employs a stochastic annealing mechanism that accepts suboptimal solutions with a certain probability to escape local optima, but its sequential search nature results in relatively slow convergence and heavy dependence on annealing schedule tuning. In contrast, IVY integrates neighborhood cooperative search with optimal solution guidance, enabling a balanced exploration-exploitation trade-off through adaptive growth rate adjustment. The competitive exclusion strategy, which eliminates low-fitness individuals and propagates high-fitness solutions, accelerates convergence toward the global optimum while maintaining population diversity. These mechanistic advantages position IVY as a particularly suitable optimizer for complex hyperparameter optimization tasks in load forecasting. To ensure a fair comparison, the hyperparameter configurations for all optimization algorithms are unified where possible. Common basic parameters, including learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs, are kept identical across GWO, PSO, SA, and IVY. Conclusion

This study addresses the forecasting requirements for electric vehicle charging loads by proposing a combined forecasting model based on IVY-optimized VMD-TCN-BiLSTM. The model achieves high temporal accuracy while demonstrating strong generalization across different spatial zones. By introducing the IVY intelligent optimization algorithm for adaptive tuning of key VMD hyperparameters, the model effectively avoids potential over-decomposition or under-decomposition issues associated with traditional empirical settings, thereby enhancing the rationality and stability of load signal decomposition results. Building on this foundation, the model combines the synergistic modeling advantages of TCN and BiLSTM to simultaneously capture multi-scale features, local temporal dependencies, and global dynamic evolution patterns of load sequences. Ablation and comparative experimental results demonstrate that each functional module and optimization strategy contributes positively to improving prediction performance. Notably, the IVY-based parameter adaptive optimization mechanism significantly enhances prediction accuracy under complex operating conditions. The research findings validate the significant effectiveness of the proposed method in electric vehicle charging load forecasting.

In the analysis across different functional zones, residential and commercial zone samples exhibit significantly different charging load structures, demonstrating the model’s adaptability to spatially heterogeneous patterns. The model demonstrates good adaptability and generalization capabilities in both zone types, but high-frequency disturbances in commercial zones present greater forecasting challenges. The results verify the importance of incorporating spatial features and the robustness of the proposed model to complex spatial structures. This study provides methodological references for future regional management of EV loads, charging facility planning, and grid dispatch strategy formulation. Furthermore, future research could introduce finer-grained traffic behavior features, dynamic spatial network structures, or large-scale real load data to continuously enhance model generalization capability in highly heterogeneous scenarios.

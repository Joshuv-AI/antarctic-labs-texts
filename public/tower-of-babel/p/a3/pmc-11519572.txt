Characterization and source apportionment of water-soluble ion pollution in PM10 of typical cities in northern China
Authors: Guojun Han, Mingzhu He, Zhiheng Du, Nanxin Wei, Haiping Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryUnderstanding the characteristics and sources of water-soluble ion pollution in particulate matter is crucial for preventing, controlling, and managing air pollution. In this study, we analyzed the pollution characteristics and sources of water-soluble ions in PM10 in three typical cities in northern China. Our results showed an increase in both PM10 and total water-soluble ions during the pollution period in all three cities. Wind speed was a key meteorological factor affecting the change in atmospheric aerosol concentration in Dunhuang and Zhongwei. Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian were affected by natural sources such as dust, but also by human sources such as coal combustion, industrial activities, transportation emissions, and biomass burning. This study is of great significance for understanding the sea-land air interactions and the transport, diffusion, and sources of water-soluble ions, and for the prevention, control, and management of air pollution in global desert regions and coastal areas.Subject areas: Atmospheric science, Atmospheric chemistry, Environmental science, Pollution

Introduction

Atmospheric aerosols are multiphase suspension systems composed of solid or liquid particles dispersed in gas and closely connected to gaseous substances.1 Their profound impacts on air quality, human health, climate change, and animal migration have made them a focal point in global atmospheric pollution research.1,2,3 Currently, studies on atmospheric aerosols have mainly focused on their climate effects, distribution characteristics, physicochemical characteristics, and optical properties.2,4,5 In addition, soluble ions in aerosols make up the major component of urban aerosols,6 and their impacts on climate and the environment have received attention from the scientific community.7 Currently, several studies have investigated the transport mechanisms of aerosols in oceans and dust storms,8,9 but there is a relative lack of research on aerosols during dust storm events. Therefore, analyzing the characteristics, composition, and sources of aerosol changes during dust storms is important for understanding air pollution and the development of its control measures.

Water-soluble ions are the main components of atmospheric aerosols, accounting for more than 10% of PM10 mass.10 Meanwhile, PM10, as the primary atmospheric pollutant during dusty weather, was found to increase dramatically during dust storms compared to non-dust storms.11,12 In addition, water-soluble ions can enter the soil with precipitation, and enter organisms through the food chain, thus having a certain impact on the ecological environment and human health.13 Previous studies have taken PM10 as the object of study and used different methods to determine the soluble ionic components in atmospheric aerosol14,15 and to analyze their distribution characteristics and influencing factors.16,17,18 Identifying the aerosol sources can help to better characterize and understand the transport and effects of aerosols. Currently, methods for analyzing the aerosol ions source mainly include the source list method,19 diffusion mode simulation,20 and receptor model.21,22,23 Principal component analysis (PCA) is commonly used in environmental research and is a multivariate statistical method.24 The main idea of PCA is dimension reduction, in which several factors that play a major role can be extracted from complex variables in the mass concentration data of the components in the aerosol.25 In short, the advantage of PCA is that information on specific pollution sources is not needed, and it is widely used in the source analysis of atmospheric particulate matter.24 At present, PCA is widely used as it can determine the quantitative contribution of various sources of pollutants to the receptor.26 However, compared with other cities in China and many cities worldwide, relatively few studies have been carried out on the changing characteristics of water-soluble ions, chemical compositions and their sources in atmospheric aerosol in northwestern China and northeastern coastal areas.3,6,27,28,29

China is still facing a more serious situation of air quality improvement, and the prevention and control of air pollution is still arduous.30 Northwest China is one of the major dust and sand sources in Asia.11,12 Studying the characteristics of aerosol pollution in the desert areas of northwest China and the coastal areas of northeast China will help to understand the interaction between sea and land, as well as the migration, diffusion, and water-soluble ions sources. Meanwhile, research has shown that meteorological factors such as temperature, relative humidity, and wind speed have differential effects on aerosols of different particle sizes, playing an important role in their concentration and distribution.31 Therefore, it is crucial to explore the driving mechanisms of aerosol variation. Previous studies have primarily examined the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region,32 the Yellow River Basin,33 the Yangtze River Delta region,34 and the coastal region.35 However, there is a dearth of research on water-soluble ions in sand and dust aerosols in northern China and a lack of analytical studies on their pollution characteristics and sources. In view of this, The main objectives of this study were: (1) Based on statistical analysis, we investigated the characteristics of pollutants and water-soluble ions on non-polluted days and polluted days in Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian, and analyzed the characteristics of changes in water-soluble ion concentrations and components in PM10; (2) Based on the correlation analysis and the wind rose diagrams, we investigated the effects of meteorological factors on atmospheric aerosol and water-soluble ions; (3) The sources of water-soluble ions in PM10 in three cities were investigated using the PCA model and the particle size distribution of water-soluble ions. This study is of great significance for the prevention and control of air pollution in the desert areas of northwest China and the coastal areas of northeast China.

Overview of the research area

Northwest China is the main source of dust in Asia.11 Cold air moving south in spring leads to frequent dust storms. Dunhuang City is located at the west end of Hexi Corridor, surrounded by Tengger Desert, Badan Jaran Desert, and Kumtag Desert. It has abundant dust sources and a narrow tube effect of topography and is one of the main transport paths of dust in the high-incidence area of dust weather in northwest China.36 Zhongwei in Ningxia lies on the edge of the Tengger Desert (Figure 1). Some studies have shown that the air mass over the Yellow Sea and Bohai Sea in the spring of 2010 mainly came from the long-distance input from northwest China.37,38 The Yellow Sea and Bohai Sea adjacent to Dalian are important sea areas in East Asia receiving Asian dust.38 Therefore, Dunhuang City in Gansu Province, Zhongwei City in Ningxia Autonomous Region, and Dalian City in Liaoning Province are selected as three Chinese cities for the study.

Overview map of the study area

Dalian is located in the warm temperate zone, with an oceanic climate characterized by warm temperate semi-humid monsoon conditions. The average annual temperature is 10.5°C, with an annual precipitation range of 550–950 mm. In contrast, Dunhuang has an average annual precipitation of only 42.2 mm and an evaporation rate of 2505 mm. The average annual temperature is 9.9°C, with a high of 41.7°C and a low of −30.5°C. The frost-free period lasts for an average of 152 days, making it a typical warm-temperate arid climate. Zhongwei City, on the other hand, experiences a typical temperate continental monsoon climate. Due to its desert location, there is ample sunshine and a large temperature difference between day and night. The average annual humidity is moderate, and the frost-free period ranges from 158 to 169 days. Annual precipitation falls within the range of 180–367 mm.

Results and discussion

p.m.10 and water-soluble ion concentration levels

The daily average PM10 and total water-soluble ion concentrations in Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian are shown in Figure 2. During the study period, PM10 in Dunhuang showed an overall "wavy" trend, with a daily average PM10 concentration of 138.03 μg m−3, exceeding the national secondary limit of 150 μg m−3 for the 24 h average PM10 concentration (dotted line in the figure). The daily average value of total water-soluble ion concentration in Dunhuang was 46.48 μg m−3, accounting for 33.6% of the PM10 mass concentration. Compared with Dunhuang, Zhongwei had more days with PM10 exceeding 150 μg m−3. The daily average concentration of PM10 in Zhongwei was 124.71 μg m−3, and the average value of total water-soluble ion concentration was 25.51 μg m−3, accounting for 20.4% of PM10. The daily average PM10 concentration in Dalian was 87.91 μg m−3, and the daily average value of total water-soluble ions was 26.86 μg m−3, which accounted for 30.5% of PM10 (Figure 2C). PM10 concentrations in Dalian were low overall, but PM10 concentrations were high on April 15, April 25, and May 5, exceeding the national secondary concentration standard limit. Higher PM10 concentrations may be attributed to the fact that the sampling site on Xiao Changshan Island in Dalian City is located downwind of dust transport from the Northwest and the Gobi of Inner Mongolia and contains a large number of atmospheric pollutants, which increases the aerosol ion concentrations in the Northeast coastal region.11

Daily changes in PM10 and water-soluble ion concentrations

Classification of pollution events and analysis of meteorological conditions

The days with corresponding concentrations were defined as non-polluted days (PM10 < 150 μg/m3) and polluted days (PM10 > 150 μg/m3) according to China’s PM10 secondary concentration limit. Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian had 26, 25, and 36 non-polluted days, respectively, and 14, 15, and 3 polluted days, respectively, during the sampling campaign from April 12 to May 21. The temperature, humidity, and wind speed in three cities were further analyzed (Figure 3). The results showed that the average temperature, humidity, and wind speed showed remarkable increases during the pollution period in Dunhuang; the average humidity and wind speed increased notably during the pollution period in Zhongwei, while the temperature remained basically unchanged, and only the temperature increased during the pollution period in Dalian, while the average humidity and wind speed remained basically unchanged (Figure 4).

Characterization of pollutants and water-soluble ions in non-polluted and polluted weather states

Both PM10 and total water-soluble ion concentrations increased during the pollution period, and SO2 and NO2 levels in Dalian increased during the pollution period (Figure S1). However, the SO2 and NO2 concentration increases during the pollution period may indicate the influence of secondary pollution and aging aerosols, while dust transport will pass through industrial cities resulting in elevated pollutant concentrations,11 which suggests that secondary pollution and transport of sand and dust aerosols were the main reasons for the increase in water-soluble ion concentrations.39 The top three ions with the highest percentage of non-polluted and polluted days in Dunhuang were Na+ (25.5%, 24.7%), NH4+ (31%, 27.6%), and Ca2+ (27.4%, 30.8%), respectively; the percentage of Ca2+ and Mg2+ concentrations on polluted days in Zhongwei was increased notably, and the percentage of Na+ was decreased; there was no significant increase or decrease of the respective water-soluble ions on polluted days in Dalian (Figure 5). Larger wind speeds during sandy weather dilute local pollutants, while the alkaline surface of mineral aerosols facilitates the adsorption of acid gases and promotes the production of sulfate and nitrate.40,41 Compared with non-polluted days, the Ca2+ percentage increased the most on polluted days in Dunhuang and Zhongwei, and the NH4+ percentage decreased the most in Dunhuang (Figure 5), because dust aerosols brought in a large amount of Ca2+ under dust storm conditions,31 whereas the impacts from anthropogenic pollutants were reduced, and the NH4+ concentration was affected by the secondary reaction rather than by dust.42 The concentration of Na+ increased in Zhongwei, while the percentage decreased the most, indicating that Na+ was not the main ion contributing to air quality pollution in Zhongwei. Sea salt aerosol does not contribute much to NO3− and NH4+, while the percentage of NO3− and NH4+ in Dalian is larger than that of the other six ions, which may be attributed to the generation of NOX produced by the combustion of fuel oil from ships adsorbed on the aerosol particles by processes such as photochemical reactions.42

Characteristics of water-soluble ion concentration and component changes in PM10

The water-soluble ions F−, K+, NH4+, and Ca2+ had similar temporal trends in Dunhuang (Figure 6), and Cl−, NO2−, SO42−, NH4+, and Ca2+ tended to be at higher concentrations. In spring, NO2− was mostly greater than 2 μg m−3 even in non-sand-dusty weather, which was higher than the changes of other anions, suggesting that in addition to the unstable reaction of NO2− with dust aerosols, it was also affected by anthropogenic sources. Some of the water-soluble ions showed an increasing trend in different dusty days.

Time series of daily average concentration of water-soluble ions in Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian cities

The water-soluble ion concentrations of Ca2+, K+, NO3−, F− and Na+ in Zhongwei PM10 showed similar trends, and among F−, Cl−, and K+, the concentration of Cl− was the highest and showed an irregular "wave-like" trend. On April 30 (dust), the concentrations of F−, Cl−, K+, NO2−, SO42−, NO3−, Mg2+, Ca2+, and Na+ increased slightly in Zhongwei City, which may be attributed to the transmission of dust from Dunhuang on April 29 and the large wind speeds reduced the increase in the concentration of water-soluble ions, whereas the lower temperatures and higher relative humidity promoted the air quality to be heavily polluted (PM10 of 234.6 μg m−3).

On May 5 (Dunhuang sand raising), the concentration of water-soluble ions in the aerosol increased dramatically, and on May 6, a dust storm broke out in Zhongwei, and the large increase in the concentration of F−, Cl−, K+, Na+, and Ca2+ may be related to the transport of the Dunhuang dust aerosol. Higher relative humidity on May 7 increased atmospheric aerosol concentrations but reduced water-soluble ion concentrations. PM10 exceeded the national secondary concentration limits on May 9-11 (158 μg m−3, 251 μg m−3, 192.8 μg m−3). However, the concentrations of F−, Cl−, K+, NO3−, and Mg2+ showed a trend of increasing and then decreasing, and the concentrations of Na+ and Ca2+ showed remarkable increases.

Cl−, K+, NO3−, SO42−, Ca2+, and Na+ had similar temporal trends in Dalian, and Cl-, NO3-, Na+, and NH4+ tended to higher concentrations were influenced by sea-salt aerosols and fisheries, reflecting the ability of spring to promote NO3− production. The overall water-soluble ions in PM10 showed a small peak in concentration on April 25–26th. The overall trend of Cl−, K+, NO3−, SO42−, Na+, Ca2+, and NH4+ was similar to that of PM10, which was affected by the wind speed in spring, and the concentrations were higher in windy weather. The concentration of Na+ increased to 13.11 μg m−3, which was mainly due to the large amount of Na+ carried by the sea breeze over a long distance, and the relatively low concentrations of Mg2+ and Ca2+ indicated that the aerosols in Dalian Xiaoyangshan Island were mainly affected by the ocean and secondary pollution.42

In this study, the mass concentrations of 10 water-soluble ions in Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian were further calculated (Figures S2 and S3). The results showed that Na+, Ca2+, and NH4+ were the most dominant cationic components of the water-soluble ions in PM10 from Dunhuang, accounting for 83.3% of the water-soluble ion concentration. In this study, it was found that among the anions, NO2- was the major water-soluble ion in PM10 at Dunhuang (up to 5.4%, Figure S3). In Zhongwei, Na+ and Ca2+ were the main water-soluble ions in PM10, and the mass fractions of Ca2+ and Na+ were 31.9%, 38.3%, and 70.2% of the total ion concentration, respectively. In Dalian, Ca2+, NO3−, NH4+ and Na+ were the most dominant water-soluble ions with total ion concentration mass fractions of 15.7%, 18.3%, 18%, and 30.2%, totaling 82.2%. Calcium salts are the signature element of soil dust, At the same time, it has been shown that water-soluble ions such as Na+, K+, and Ca2+ in PM are mainly of terrestrial (soil dust) origin,43 and therefore these ions are mostly concentrated in coarse aerosol, where they are predominantly present in the coarse modal state. Therefore, under the influence of sand and dust, Ca2+ in atmospheric aerosol in Zhongwei and Dunhuang is predominantly present in the coarse mode, and magnesium salts also contribute to the soil source that is predominantly found in the coarse fraction.44,45,46 SO42− in PM10 originates from the conversion formation of SO2 from fossil fuel combustion emissions,17,47,48 whereas NO3− mainly originates from the conversion formation of NO and NO2 from motor vehicle and industrial emissions,46 in addition to desert surface soil emissions from which SO42− and NO3− is one of the sources.

The water-soluble ions with the highest concentrations include Na+, Ca2+, and NH4+. Ca2+ in Dunhuang and Zhongwei City mainly comes from soil and rock weathering and is carried more in the sandy soil area, and thus concentrated in the coarse aerosol. NH4+ is mainly derived from the gaseous transformation of NH3 from agriculture, aquaculture, and industrial activities.49,50 Cl−, Na+ from sea salt aerosol, the concentration decreases gradually from coastal to inland areas,51 and the source of Cl− is complex, not only from sea salt but also from industrial activities such as coal combustion.52,53 Therefore, this study by analyzing the percentage and sources of major water-soluble ions in PM10 in the three cities, this study found that the Na+ concentration in Dalian was higher than that of other water-soluble ions because the sampling site in Dalian was located on Xiao Changshan Island, Changhai County, Dalian City, suggesting that sea salt is the main source of marine aerosols.53 The secondary aerosol components (SO42−, NO3−, NH4+, and NO2−) accounted for 46.9% of the total water-soluble ion content, indicating that land-based anthropogenic activities strongly influence the composition of atmospheric aerosol components in offshore regions.50 In spring, Na+ concentrations were higher than those in the North Yellow Sea in 2010, suggesting that Na+ enters the atmosphere not only due to wind disturbance of the sea surface and bulging but also due to anthropogenic sources and transport of arid and semi-arid sand and dust particles from the north. The molar ratio of Mg2+/Ca2+ on the surface of Dunhuang desert and loess was 0.16, and that of Mg2+/Ca2+ in the water-soluble ions of Dalian aerosol was 0.26, which suggests that Mg2+ may come from combustion and other processes in addition to dust.54 From west to east, NO3− and SO42− concentrations showed a pattern of Dalian > Zhongwei > Dunhuang, indicating a trend of diminishing influence of crustal dust and increasing secondary pollution (Figure S2).

Effects of meteorological factors on atmospheric aerosol and water-soluble ions

The correlation analysis revealed that PM2.5 and PM10 in Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian exhibited significant correlations of 0.74, 0.60, and 0.56, respectively (Figure S4), suggesting that they are homologous to each other. Compared with Dunhuang and Dalian, the generation of PM2.5, and PM10 in Zhongwei may be more influenced by soil dust and sand particles. The wind speed in Dunhuang was significantly positively correlated with PM2.5, and PM10 concentrations, and the wind speed in Zhongwei was significantly positively correlated with PM2.5, and PM10, while the correlation between other meteorological factors and PM2.5, and PM10 was not obvious. From the above analysis, it can be seen that wind speed is the key meteorological factor affecting the changes in atmospheric aerosol concentration in Dunhuang and Zhongwei. Wind speed was significantly positively correlated with PM2.5, and PM10 in Dunhuang and Zhongwei, while the correlation was not significant in Dalian. This is due to the transit of cold air in spring, where larger wind speeds and loose soil surfaces lead to the formation of dusty and sandy weather, resulting in higher PM10 concentrations.11 In addition, unlike the dust transport in the north, PM2.5, PM10 in the urban agglomeration in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River in the south showed a significant negative correlation with the wind speed, which is due to the fact that the larger the wind speed, the more favorable for the diffusion and dilution of atmospheric aerosol.54 The correlation between air temperature, wind speed and PM2.5, PM10 in Dalian was not significant, and the correlation coefficient between relative humidity and PM10 was negative. PM10 decreases with the increase of relative humidity in Dalian. The correlation between relative humidity and PM10 in Dalian was better than other meteorological factors. At the same time, it was shown that at RH < 65%, the sulfate conversion rate (SOR) and nitrate conversion rate (NOR) increased as RH increased in the range of <65%,46 resulting in higher secondary particulate matter concentrations. At RH>65%, the atmospheric aerosol condenses to form large particle deposition, and some of the gaseous pollutants are dissolved in the liquid water in the air, and the localized wet deposition has a scouring effect on the air pollutants, resulting in a decreased in the concentration of atmospheric aerosol.55,56

By analyzing the effect of wind on water-soluble ions using wind rose diagrams (Figure 7), it was found that the maximum wind frequencies in three cities were in the directions of E (easterly wind), NW (northwesterly wind), and SW. West and southwest of Dunhuang were highly polluted areas, which was mainly due to the prevailing northwesterly winds in East Asia in spring, and the transport of sand and dust aerosols to downwind regions under strong cold-front weather systems. Therefore, Dunhuang was mainly affected by localized pollution sources and long-range transport of dust particles from the northwest during the study period. The northwest of Zhongwei was a high pollution area with high wind frequency and speed. The reason for the high pollution area southwest of Dalian was that the land air pressure was higher than that of the ocean in spring, and pollutants were blown from the land to the ocean with the wind. In addition, the Dunhuang monitoring site is located at the Meteorological Bureau of Dunhuang City, with residential neighborhoods to the southwest, so the higher pollution is related to local coal and gasoline combustion and agricultural production. The monitoring site in Zhongwei is located at the Shapotou Experimental Station of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, adjacent to the Tengger Desert, and thus the higher water-soluble ion concentrations are influenced by local soil sand and dust. The Dalian aerosol monitoring site is located on Xiaochangshan Island, where the local sources of pollution are the transport of sea-salt aerosols and secondary pollution from anthropogenic sources in Dalian and other economically developed neighboring areas.

Particle size distribution of water-soluble ions in PM10

In order to analyze the contents of water-soluble ions in coarse and fine particles, the collected aerosol compositions of nine particle-size bands were divided into two parts, and the concentrations of coarse particles were obtained by summing up the 0–4 levels (>2.1 μm) of the Anderson sampler, while the concentrations of fine particles were obtained by summing up the 5–7 levels and the F level (<2.1 μm). It was found that the distribution pattern of each ion in particle size was different (Figure 8), F−, NO3−, and SO42− (Zhongwei, Dalian), NH4+, and K+ (Zhongwei, Dalian) were more distributed in the fine particles, while Mg2+, Ca2+, Cl−, Na+, and K+ (Dunhuang), SO42− (Dunhuang) were more distributed in the coarse particles. The ion concentrations were classified into 3 categories according to the similarity of their particle size distributions: (1) Unimodal distributions with peaks in fine particles: NO3− and SO42− (Zhongwei, Dalian), NH4+ (Zhongwei), K+(Zhongwei), Ca2+(Dalian). (2) A unimodal distribution of peaks occurs in the coarse particles: Na+ (Dunhuang), Mg2+, and Ca2+ (Dunhuang, Zhongwei); (3) Bimodal distribution with peaks in coarse and fine particles: F-, Cl-, and SO42− (Dunhuang), Na+(Zhongwei, Dalian), NH4+ (Dalian, Dunhuang), and K+ (Dalian, Dunhuang).

Particle size distribution of water-soluble ions in PM10 (DH, Dunhuang; ZW, Zhongwei; DL, Dalian)

As shown in Figure 8, Na+ was mainly distributed in the coarse particles with a bimodal distribution. Na+ in fine particles was mainly from anthropogenic sources, while Na+ in coarse particles was mainly from wind-dust and marine sources.57 Dalian was close to the ocean and had less wind and sand, and the Na+ in coarse particles was dominated by sea salt, and the Na+ in fine particles was dominated from fossil fuel combustion, whereas the peaks of Na+ in Dunhuang and Zhongwei were dominated by dust.56,57 K+(Zhongwei) showed a single-peak distribution, and K+ (Dunhuang, Dalian) showed a double-peak distribution. The K+ in fine particles is mainly derived from biomass combustion such as straw,54 and the K+ in coarse particles is mainly derived from soil and sea salt.54 Mg2+(Dunhuang, Dalian) showed a bimodal distribution, and Mg2+(Zhongwei) showed peaks, and the peaks all appeared in the coarse particles. The mass concentration of Ca2+(Zhongwei) increased continuously with increasing particle size, and a concentration maximum of 1.6 μg m−3 was obtained at 9.00 μm Mg2+and Ca2+ concentrations in Dunhuang and Zhongwei were mostly distributed in coarse particles, which is highly related to their main sources (roads, construction dust, and soil).46 The characteristics of Ca2+ particle size distribution in Dunhuang and Zhongwei were closely related to the development of urbanization. The single peak of Ca2+ concentration in Dalian appeared at 1.1–2.1 μm because the sampling site in Dalian is located in Xiaochangshan Island far away from the city center, and there are no obvious construction works and dusty weather around.

The peaks of Cl−(Dunhuang) appeared at 0.7–1.1 μm, 3.3–4.7 μm and the second peak was much larger than the first one, which was mainly distributed in coarse particles. Cl−(Zhongwei) showed peaks at 0.4–0.7 μm and 4.7–5.8 μm, which was mainly distributed in fine particles. This indicates that Cl− is not only from soil dust and marine sources but also influenced by fuel combustion in Zhongwei.42

Among the secondary ions, NH4+ (Dunhuang) showed peaks at 0.7–1.1 μm, 3.3–4 μm, and 5.8–9 μm, and the first peak was larger than the last two peaks, which indicated that the NH4+ was mainly distributed in fine particles, which were mainly originated from the emission of NH3. NH4+ (Zhongwei) was mainly distributed in fine particles of 0.7–1.1 μm NH4+ (Dalian) was mainly distributed in fine particles of 0.4–1.1 μm. The peaks of NO3− (Dunhuang) appeared at 1.1–2.1 μm and were mainly distributed in fine particles, and the peaks of NO3− (Zhongwei) appeared at 0.4–0.7 μm, 1.1–2.1 μm, and 4.7–5.8 μm, and the first two peaks were larger than the third one and were mainly distributed in fine particles. NO3− (Dalian) peaked at 0.7–1.1 μm and was mainly distributed in fine particles. SO42− (Dunhuang) showed peaks at 0.4–0.7 μm, 3.3–4.7 μm, and 5.5–9 μm, and the third peak was larger than the first and the second one, which was mainly distributed in coarse particles possibly related to the formation of desert aerosols. The peaks of SO42− (Zhongwei) appeared at 0.4–0.7 μm, 3.3–4.7 μm, and 9–10 μm, and the first peak was larger than the last two, which were mainly distributed in the fine particles. SO42− (Dalian) peaks at 0.7–1.1 μm and was mainly distributed in fine particles, originating from the homogeneous oxidation of gaseous sulfide,56,58 but there are some differences from the three-peak distribution of Zhongwei SO42−, which was attributed to the difference in the extent of the transition of sulfate and sulfate aerosol particles from the nucleation mode to the accumulation mode.56

Water-soluble ion source apportionment

In this article, PCA was further utilized to carry out source apportionment of water-soluble ions in PM10 in three cities (Table 1). The results indicate that 77.072% of the water-soluble ions in Dunhuang were identified. Factor 1 accounted for 65.911% of the source of these ions, with F− (0.790), Cl− (0.759), SO42− (0.861), NO3− (0.847), Na+ (0.858), NH4+ (0.743), K+ (0.868), Mg2+ (0.930), and Ca2+ (0.885) being the highly loaded ions in this factor. The high loadings of these ions suggest a strong correlation and the presence of secondary pollution, as they are likely to react with both pollution aerosols and sand and dust aerosols.57 In addition, previous studies have identified complex sources of F− and Cl−, mainly fuel/biomass combustion, dust sources and industrial emissions.33,59 Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+, and K+ are mainly attributed to mineral dust/crustal material.57,60 Some studies indicate that the main sources of Na+, Ca2+, and K+ are fuel combustion and dust from nearby buildings, while Mg2+ may be generated due to transportation emissions.61 However, SO42- and NO3- are usually attributed to anthropogenic sources and are mainly considered to be fossil fuel combustion.57 At the same time, it has also been suggested that K+ is a tracer of biomass combustion processes.62 In view of the above considerations, the main sources of water-soluble ions in Dunhuang were defined as coal combustion, industrial activities, soil winds and sands, transportation emissions, biomass burning, and construction fly ash emission sources.

K+ is commonly recognized as a tracer of biomass combustion processes,62 and shipping emissions generate NOX and SO2 gases, suggesting that the spring Dalian sampling site may have been influenced by the generation of secondary pollutants and biogenic sources from shipping emissions in the Dalian port.66 Factor 1 represents combustion sources and secondary pollutants. The main contribution of factor 2 was 22.754%, with larger loadings mainly on the Mg2+ (0.841), Na+ (0.696), and Ca2+ (0.758) fractions. Ca2+, as an abundant element in the earth’s crust, mainly comes from dust,67 Mg2+, Na+ partly comes from marine sources and coexists with Cl−, and the other part is mainly related to soil sand, therefore, factor 2 in Dalian was mainly marine sources and dust sources, and factor 1 in Zhongwei represented sand and dust particles and biomass burning. Factor 3 in Zhongwei and Dalian had high loadings on F− and Cl−, representing industrial emissions. K+ and Ca2+ were more highly correlated in Factor 1 than in Factor 2, indicating that secondary pollution and combustion sources contributed more to the K+ and Ca2+ fractions than marine and dust sources.

This study analyzed the pollution characteristics and sources of sand and dust aerosols in key cities in northern China, showing that the main pollution characteristics and sources of pollution are not consistent across cities. Therefore, this study suggests that it is significant for air pollution prevention and health protection to take pollution control measures and develop related health protection strategies according to the pollution sources and according to local conditions.

Limitations of the study

Water-soluble ions are the primary components found in aerosols. Studying the characteristics and sources of water-soluble ion concentrations can provide valuable insights into the changes in aerosol concentrations along the dust transport belt and aid in the prevention and control of air pollutants. However, due to the limitations of the experimental conditions, this study was not able to set up sampling sites in multiple functional areas to comprehensively evaluate atmospheric pollution. In the future studies, sampling sites can be set up in the cities passing through the sand and dust belt in northern China, which is more conducive to the study of the impact of sand and dust aerosols on the atmosphere. This study only analyzes the variation rule of water-soluble ion concentration in PM10 in terms of ion concentration level and meteorological factors, and fails to analyze the formation mechanism of water-soluble ions and the characteristics of water-soluble ion concentration under different air qualities, and will focus on considering the formation mechanism of ions in the future work.

Conclusion

This study investigated the pollution characteristics, driving mechanisms, and sources of water-soluble ions in dust aerosols from major cities in northern China. The research was conducted through field monitoring, statistical analysis, and model simulation. The main findings are outlined later in discussion:

During the pollution period, both PM10 and total water-soluble ions increased in all three cities. In Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian, total water-soluble ions accounted for 33.6%, 20.4%, and 30.5% of PM10, respectively. The highest percentage of water-soluble ions in the aerosol of Dunhuang was Ca2+, Na+, and NH4+, which accounted for 83%. In Zhongwei, Ca2+ and Na+ accounted for 70.2% of the aerosol, while in Dalian, Ca2+, NO3−, NH4+, and Na+ accounted for 82.2%. The main sources of Ca2+ in the aerosols of Zhongwei and Dunhuang were sand and dust emissions, while NH4+ mainly originated from anthropogenic activities. The concentration of Na+ in the Dalian area was significantly higher than that of other water-soluble ions, indicating that sea salt is the main source of marine aerosols.

Wind speed was the key meteorological factor affecting the change of atmospheric aerosol concentration in Dunhuang and Zhongwei, which was mainly due to the transit of cold air in spring, the larger wind speed and loose soil surface would form dust and sandstorm weather, leading to the increase of PM10 concentration. The wind rose map found that Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian were mainly affected by the prevailing northwesterly winds in East Asia in spring, and dust aerosols were transported downwind to the region under a strong cold-front weather system, whereas the reason for the highly polluted area to the southwest of Dalian was that the air pressure on the land was higher than that of the ocean in spring, and pollutants were blown from land to the ocean with the winds.

Using PCA analysis, it was determined that there is a strong correlation between the water-soluble ions found in Dunhuang, indicating the presence of secondary pollution. This suggests that pollution aerosols and sand aerosols may interact with each other. Further analysis revealed that the main sources of pollution in Dunhuang City include coal combustion, industrial activities, soil wind and sand, transportation emissions, biomass combustion, and construction fly ash. In Zhongwei City, the main sources of pollution are soil wind and sand, crustal sources, transportation emissions, agricultural production, and industrial activities. In Dalian City, the main sources of pollution are human activities, biomass combustion, and construction fly ash. Additionally, crustal sources such as soil dust may also contribute to pollution levels in these cities.

Sampling locations and times

The sampling sites were located at the observation field of the Dunhuang Meteorological Bureau, the observation field of the Chinese Academy of Sciences Sha Potou Desert Experimental Research Station, and the Xiao changshan Marine Environment Monitoring Station of the State Oceanic Administration. The sampling positions were 1.5 m above the ground and were not obstructed by tall buildings, ensuring that the collected samples truly represented the air quality conditions in Dunhuang City, Zhongwei, and Dalian.

Dunhuang, Zhongwei, and Dalian were all sampled from April 12 to May 21, 2015 from 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 a.m. on the second day. However, some samples were missing due to very bad weather conditions at some of the sampling sites or the samples were very contaminated. The sampling periods that were not included in the data from Dunhuang were April 16-19, April 27-30, May 5 from 8:10 to 15:06, May 5 from 19:34 to May 7 at 8:00, and May 16-20. Similarly, the sampling periods for Zhongwei were also missing, including April 16-19, April 27-30, May 6 from 8:20 to 18:24, May 6 from 18:47 to May 8, and May 21-24. In addition, the data from Dalian did not include the sampling periods from April 27-30, and May 18 at 11:10 to May 21.

Each set of samples was divided into 0 level, 1 level, 2 level, 3 level, 4 level, 5 level, 6 level, 7 level, and F level, corresponding to particle size ranges of 9–10 μm, 5.8–9 μm, 5.8–9 μm, 4.7–5.8 μm, 3.3–4.7 μm, 2.1–3.3 μm, 1.1–2.1 μm, 0.7–1.1 μm, 0.4–0.7 μm, and 0–0.4 μm, respectively. Finally, a total of 864 valid samples were obtained in this study, 297, 297 and 270 samples were collected from Dunhuang, Shapotou, and Dalian, respectively.

A Tisch TE-20-800 Andersen Eight-Stage (10–0.4 μm) particle impactor was used for sampling, with a sampling flow rate of 28.3 L min−1. Larger particles in the sample air were impacted and retained on the surface of a quartz fiber filter (pre-cleaned by heating at 450°C for 5 h) due to inertial forces, while smaller particles passed through with the airflow and reached the next stage filter at a flow rate of 1 CFM. Finally, all particles with a diameter smaller than 0.4 μm were collected on the first-stage impact plate. After the sub-sampling was completed, the filters were removed, wrapped in aluminum foil, and stored frozen to ensure that they were not artificially contaminated.

Method details

Sample analysis and pretreatment

In this experiment, the ion chromatograph (for cation analysis) used was the DX-600 model, and the ion chromatograph (for anion analysis) used was the ICX-2500 model. The ultrapure water machine used was the QUENT-35 model, the balance used was the METTLEE model, and the ultrasonic oscillator used was the KH-100DE model.

In this study, samples were analyzed and tested at the State Key Laboratory of Cryosphere, Northwest Institute of Ecology and Environmental Resources, Chinese Academy of Sciences (NWIER, CAS). Firstly, we prepared 2000 poly bottles, washed each bottle 10 times, and ultrasonically extracted 2 times for 15 min each. Afterward, the cleaned bottles were dried in an oven so that there was no trace of water on the walls of the bottles. Then two bottles from every 30 bottles were taken and connected to Milli-Q water and put into the ion chromatograph for testing. Finally, we used a ceramic knife to cut the center of the sample diaphragm to capture 1/8 of the membrane into a clean bottle. Pipette 20 mL of Milli-Q water into the bottle where the sample was placed. Each group of samples represents a total of nine compositions of sample diaphragms taken on a single day, which are grades 0–7 and F. The sample filters were then extracted by sonication. These cut samples were placed in an ultrasonic cleaner and sonicated for 60 min at 20°C. After each ultrasonic extraction, the water in the ultrasonic cleaner needed to be drained and reconnected to Milli-Q water before placing the next batch of samples in the bottles. Finally, the extract was filtered using a 0.45 μm PTFE filter membrane and the filtrate was collected for analysis by ion chromatography.68 This is because the temperature of the newly added water is around 20°C, which is higher than the temperature of the water under the initial conditions during the first sample extraction. The change in water temperature can have a large effect on the precipitation of aerosol ions from the sample.

Quality control and assurance

Quality control and assurance were essential components of this study. The following measures were taken to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results: 1. Standard solutions with a concentration of 1000 ppm were purchased from the National Center for Research on National Standards and Substances. These solutions were used to prepare the standard mixing stock solutions for the analysis. 2. The peak area method was used during the analysis, and the standard solution was determined based on the approximate range of concentrations of various ions at the sampling site. 3. To ensure the accuracy of the results, a reserve solution with a concentration 100 times higher than the anion and cation of the mixed standard was prepared. This solution was changed every week. 4. During the testing process, the standard solution was prepared daily, and ultrapure water was tested five times a day. The average value of the ion concentration detected in the ultrapure water was used as the background value for the day’s experimental analysis. 5. After completing the analysis and testing for the day, each sample spectrum was checked for any abnormalities. If any abnormality was found, the sample was retested. 6. The peaks in the spectra of each sample were named and corrected using the correction software included on the instrument. This ensured the accuracy of the concentration values of the ions obtained through the peak area method.

PCA model

PCA modeling is a multivariate statistical method that takes into account the correlation between multiple variables70,71. It reduces the dimensionality of the data by selecting a few representative variables that reflect the original variables. The main principle of PCA is to project the original data onto a new coordinate system through a linear transformation, retaining only the direction of the largest variance.70 Its purpose is to eliminate irrelevant or redundant features, resulting in dimensionality reduction. The main steps of PCA are as follows: 1. First, assume that the dataset X = {x1, x2, x3, …, xn} needs to be reduced to k dimensions. 2. Next, de-mean, i.e., each feature minus its respective mean. 3. Then, calculate the covariance matrix 1/nXXT. 4. Use eigenvalue decomposition to find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the covariance matrix. 5. Sort the eigenvalues from largest to smallest and select the largest k values. 6. Form an eigenvector matrix P using the corresponding k eigenvectors. 7. Finally, transform the data into a new space using the eigenvector matrix P, resulting in Y = PX.

In this study, different pollutants may come from the same source or the superposition of different sources, and PCA can obtain the optimal set of factors by screening the factors. n this study, using SPSS 24 software, the data were first subjected to fitness test and outliers were eliminated using the quartile method, and the missing data in the data were replaced by the mean value, after which the PCA principal component analysis was utilized to analyze the sources of water-soluble ions in PM10, and to extract the factors with eigenvalues greater than 1 and a cumulative variance contribution rate greater than 65%.

Use of ultra-low contrast dose CT aortography for the management of aortic aneurysmal disease
Authors: Philip Borkowski, Eugene Ng, Mauro Vicaretti, Noel Young
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeComputed tomography aortography (CTA) is used in the assessment of aortic pathologies and planning of surgical intervention. However, its dependence on iodinated contrast can result in development of contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI). The potential concern of CI-AKI has spurred research into the potential of administration of low contrast volumes in CTA investigations while maintaining overall diagnostic appeal. Several studies have shown that CTA using contrast volumes as low as 30 mL (equivalent to 10.5 g of iodine) can still yield scans of diagnostic quality. All scans were performed on a Siemens SOMATOM Force dual-source CT scanner. Quantitative assessment values were measured via attenuation values at eight aortoiliac locations and used to calculate a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) at each location. Qualitative analysis of image quality and viability for surgical intervention was obtained from subjective clinical assessment by an interventional radiologist and vascular surgeon.ResultsObtained quantitative assessment values included mean attenuation 189.9 HU, mean SNR 9.6 and mean CNR 8.0. Introduction

Contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI) is a potentially life-threatening condition that results following administration of intravascular contrast and has been reported as the third highest cause of nosocomial acute kidney injury [1, 2]. While current literature debates the overall prevalence of CI-AKI, studies have shown that patients of advanced age, cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus or, most commonly, pre-existing renal failure appear to be at increased risk of developing the condition [2–4].

Contrast media is used in conjunction with computed tomography angiography (CTA) for diagnosis of vascular abnormalities and preoperative planning for procedures such as endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) [5, 6] and is particularly essential with respect to delineating vessel anatomy.

The potential concern of CI-AKI has spurred research into the potential of administration of low contrast volumes in CTA investigations while maintaining overall diagnostic appeal. Several studies have shown that CTA using contrast volumes as low as 30 mL (equivalent to 10.5 g of iodine) can still yield scans of diagnostic quality [4, 7–15]. However, the majority of these studies excluded patients based on factors such as elevated body mass index (BMI), poor cardiac function or those with renal insufficiency. This limits the application of these studies in clinical practice as many patients undergoing CTA have comorbidities such as increased body habitus or diabetes mellitus and as mentioned previously, renal insufficiency is shown to be the most common precipitating factor in the development of CI-AKI [16].

This is a retrospective pilot study to evaluate the feasibility of utilising an ultra-low volume of iodinated intravenous contrast of 20 mL in a population of patients with severe renal insufficiency with referral from our vascular surgery unit for CTA evaluation of the thoracic and abdominal aorta.

Materials and methods

Patient population

This was a single-institution retrospective study that involved a population of 12 consecutive patients who were sent for thoracic and abdominal CT aortograms over an 18-month period between March 2017 and September 2018. Each aortogram was completed on a Siemens SOMATOM Force dual-source MDCT (128 detector) scanner and in accordance with the low contrast protocol of 20 mLs intravenous contrast with a total of 6 g iodine.

The study population was comprised of patients undergoing diagnostic workup of new aortic aneurysms, planning of EVAR as well as follow-up assessment of prior EVAR. Patients were referred for both urgent and non-urgent assessment and scans occurred on all days and times of the week. Patient demographics for gender, age and BMI were obtained from records. Patients with impaired renal function (eGFR range from 8 to 38 mL/min/1.73m2) were specifically selected for the ultra-low dose protocol.

Contrast injection and CTA scanning protocol

Our ultra-low dose contrast protocol was selected for patients who had severely impaired renal function as indicated by a measurement of serum creatinine or eGFR taken in the month preceding the scan. Patients received 29 mLs of injected contrast-saline bolus in a 70:30 ratio (for a total of 20 mLs contrast) administered at 3 mL/s. This was followed with a 70 mL saline flush given at the same rate. Iodohexol contrast media (Omnipaque, GE) Healthcare, Chicago, USA) was used in each scan at concentrations of 300 mg I/mL meaning patients received 6 g iodine. Contrast was injected via intravenous cannulation in the cubital fossa and while an 18-gauge size was preferred, no cannula smaller than 20-gauge was used. No patient had special premedication prior to studies, no saline infusion was used for prehydration and this was decided in view of the fact that the amount of contrast used in our study was a much lower dose than the standard regimen and that contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) is normally a self-resolving process, with recent studies demonstrating renal function returning to normal within 7–14 days of the administration of contrast and that fewer than one-third of patients develop some level of residual renal impairment. Furthermore, some of the included patients in the study either had preexisting congestive cardiac failure or presented as acute abdominal emergencies which precludes the time required for the normal standard saline infusion over 24 h which is the protocol in our hospital in the prevention of CIN in susceptible patients.

Scans were performed on all patients using the Siemens SOMATOM Force dual-source MDCT (128-detector) scanner with a multiphase protocol. The early arterial aortogram (phase 1) was completed in the craniocaudal direction with a 3-second delay from the time a region of interest (ROI) in the descending aorta at the level of the mid-diaphragm reached an attenuation value of 100 HU. The late arterial aortogram (phase 2) was completed in the caudocranial direction with a 4-second delay from the end of the early arterial aortogram. Both phases utilised a window range from just above the aortic arch to below the femoral artery bifurcation. Scans were completed at 80 kV with additional parameters of 126 mA, pitch of 1.8, rotation time of 0.25 s, slice thickness 1 mm and detectors 192 × 0.6. Total aortic z axis coverage time (duration of image acquisition time) was 3-seconds in total. Z axis coverage was 700 mm with a rotation time of 0.25s. All scans were obtained by an experienced senior CT radiographer.

Qualitative assessment of CT scan quality

Qualitative assessment was determined by consensus between a senior interventional radiologist and senior vascular surgeon. Both clinicians were blinded to the clinical indication as well as acquisition criteria for each scan. Scans were assessed on multiple categories as derived from criteria in recent literature, with each category graded on either 3-, 4- or 5-point scales [20–22] with a total possible quantitative score of 25. Statistical analysis

The total qualitative score, expressed as a percentage of the total possible score of 25 was plotted against the mean SNR and CNR for each patient. Spearman’s correlation coefficient analysis was performed, with a two tailed p value.

Results

Quantitative image analysis

Out of a potential total of 96 aortoiliac segments, measurement of a total of seven segments was not possible and these were therefore not included in analysis. In these cases, segments were either missing due to the CT scan commencing at the level of the pulmonary veins and neglecting segments proximal to the descending aorta in two patients or, as in the case of one patient, segments were obscured by hardware from a previous lumbar spinal surgery.

The Rose Criterion is used in evaluating the diagnostic quality of images and a common rule of thumb states that a signal must be five standard deviations above background to be detectable to a human observer. The criteria comprises SNR which measures the signal from the data of interest relative to background noise and CNR which measures the ability to distinguish between tissues of differing densities relative to the noise level present in the image.

Overall mean aortoiliac attenuation, SNR and CNR in the 12-patient group were found to be 189.9 HU, 9.6 and 8.0 respectively. While the mean attenuation fell below the literature-established quality threshold of > 250 HU, the mean SNR surpassed the Rose criterion threshold of five and the mean CNR was on par with the aforementioned threshold of eight.

On an individual level, all 12 patients had an average SNR above threshold and of the 89 segments included in the study, only two demonstrated an SNR value below threshold, both occurring in the left common iliac artery. Individual CNR statistics were less impactful as only five patients had a mean CNR above threshold and only 33 segments were found to be above threshold value. Qualitative image analysis

This was done in a session agreement with a senior vascular surgeon and a senior interventional radiologist in a blinded fashion. In accordance with the aforementioned qualitative assessment criteria, all of the scans were determined to have either a moderate or minimal amount of subjective noise, however only one scan was rated as overall poor diagnostic quality. This scan in particular came from a patient with a ruptured AAA and had significant motion artefact. It also received the lowest score in each category among all scans. The remaining 11 scans (91.667%) were determined to be of either “moderate” or “good” diagnostic quality as seen in Table 1. Contributing factors to this overall grade of diagnostic quality in these 11 scans include that all were found to provide optimal visualisation of at least second order renal artery branches, aorta outline and minimal to nil detectable artefacts.

Overall image analysis

We attempted to correlate the diagnostic quality of the studies as per the Rose Criterion via statistical analysis utilising a Spearman Correlation which was run between the obtained qualitative score (as a percentage of the total possible score of 25) and either the mean SNR or CNR for each scan. Mean SNR plotted with the total qualitative score for each patient was found to have a correlation value of 0.19 (p = 0.56) and mean CNR plotted with the total score demonstrated a correlation value of 0.36 (p = 0.25). While not statistically significant in either case, the overall Spearman’s Correlation coefficients for obtained qualitative scores for each scan compared to both SNR and CNR demonstrated positive association. Follow-up patient renal function

Secondary follow-up measurements of renal function were available for 10 patients within a range of 1 day to 1.5 years post-CTA. For these patients, eGFR repeat values ranged from 8 to 42 mL/min/1.73m2. In none of these patients was there any significant deterioration of renal function.

Discussion

CT aortograms are the preferred imaging modality to assess the potential need for surgical intervention [23]. As CT utilises iodinated contrast to assist in diagnosis, its potential to lead to renal impairment in the form of CI-AKI cannot be overlooked. Available literature has shown the contrast amount required to vary between 72 mL to 100 mL or more at differing institutions in order to preserve diagnostic quality of the scans [5, 24]. The volume and concentration of administered contrast has been directly associated with an increased likelihood of CI-AKI development [25, 26]. In a general population, the risk of CI-AKI development post-CT is estimated to be as high as 12% [8]. However, this risk is significantly increased if patients express common comorbidities affiliated with aortic disease [25].

This descriptive pilot study suggests that a reduced contrast dose of 20 mLs (6 g iodine) yields CT aortograms of diagnostic quality for therapeutic decision-making. While the quantitative factors of aortoiliac attenuation and CNR were generally lower than the thresholds established by literature, the quantitative SNR measurements exceeded threshold values despite using markedly less contrast than the studies on which these values were based [17–19]. In addition, even amongst these studies and in published literature, a CNR value of greater than four has been established as diagnostically adequate – a threshold met by 84/89 (94.38%) total segments in our study [15, 19]. Of our included images, Figs. 1 and 2 demonstrate scans of selected ROI with aortic attenuation values that exceeded 250 HU, while Figs. 3 and 4 represent scans from ROI that did not meet this cutoff. Of note, while these scans demonstrate varied success in surpassing our predetermined attenuation quality threshold 250 HU, all four scans had SNR and CNR values that cleared their set quality thresholds of five and eight respectively. Additionally, our readers deemed all four included scans as diagnostically adequate.

Our study shows that there is a positive correlation between the qualitative rating assigned to each scan and the CNR or SNR values as shown in both Figs. 5 and 6. As the SNR or CNR value increases, so too does the diagnostic confidence in the scan as determined by the senior staff radiologist and senior staff vascular surgeon that participated in our study. Indeed, 11 of 12 scans were determined to be diagnostically viable by our readers and these 11 scans led to treatment and management of the presenting condition for the patient. This may be due to the fact that these patients had pathologies in the aortoiliac regions from the suprarenal abdominal aorta to the aortic bifurcation (ROI 4–6), which consistently demonstrated the highest attenuation values in all study patients with a mean attenuation value of approximately 200 HU in each of the three regions. Despite falling below our chosen threshold of 250 HU, an attenuation value of 200 HU has previously been described as a diagnostic requirement [11]. While the correlation between quantitative and qualitative analysis is unclear, statistical analysis for correlation of the HU at ROI 4–6 and qualitative scores could be considered in elucidating this.

Therefore, our findings are in line with previously published literature and we believe our study provides evidence for lowering the threshold for a specific subset of the population that will most benefit from the low-contrast dosage, while taking into account the small number of patients in the study.

We note that our study size is small which is a major limitation. Additionally, it is important to reiterate that while protocols for contrast administration often account for the body habitus and comorbidities of a patient prior to administration of contrast, patients for this low dose protocol were selected solely based on their critically impaired renal function. As such, we must consider the impact of this minimal exclusion of our study on the obtained quantitative values and note that the trend of correlation persists despite not controlling for this variable. Specifically, the lone patient that notably does not fit the trend of direct correlation between qualitative and quantitative scores exhibits the highest BMI of all patients in the study at 33.8 kg/m2. In this case, the increased body habitus led to increased noise and therefore lower SNR and CNR scores, however, on a qualitative basis, the scan was still found to be adequate.

There exists a paucity of studies concerned with determining the diagnostic viability of CTA scans using volumes of contrast below 30 mL, but literature supports our overall finding that our scans were of sufficient quality for diagnosis and treatment [27–29]. Of note, Isaacson et al. [28] determined that 20 mL of iodinated contrast was adequate for planning of fenestrated endovascular aortic repair planning, while Formosa et al. [27] determined that the use of 11 mL iodinated contrast was sufficient for diagnostic quality in patients undergoing abdominal aortic aneurysm repair. Usai et al. [29] examined CTA with a mean contrast volume of 24 +/- 7 mL in workup for patients scheduled for EVAR for aortic aneurysms and these scans yielded a mean SNR of 7.7 +/- 4.1, which is in line with our mean SNR of 8.8 +/- 1.5. As in our study, these scans were also found to be diagnostically adequate.

However, it must be noted that all three of these studies made use of invasive catheter-directed CT angiography, a significant difference to our study as catheter-directed CT angiography allows for injection of contrast at a specific site (femoral arterial access most commonly) and therefore a smaller amount of contrast can be used as it is more easily localised. This procedure also incurs higher cost and associated patient risks such as arterial dissection and intravascular thrombosis at catheter insertion site [29]. To our knowledge, our study is the first to utilize 20 mL of contrast in a standard intravenous CT scan and the first to demonstrate the possibility of minimal contrast volumes in the generation of diagnostically viable CTA scans.

Our study is one of only a handful with a participating vascular surgeon in the assessment of CTA scan quality [14, 28]. As previously mentioned, CTA is a widely utilised imaging modality in the assessment of vascular pathology, and given the need for surgical intervention in most of the patient population in our study, the opinion of a vascular surgeon is essential in reading the scans. However, note is made that no analysis of independent reader results or assessment of inter-reader variability was conducted in this study.

Conclusion

Ultra-low dose computed tomography aortography with a contrast volume of 20 mL may be sufficient for the diagnosis of aortoiliac conditions and planning of surgical treatment based on our pilot study. Future research with our institutional protocol on larger patient groups may yield consideration of new lower SNR and CNR threshold value and lead to a decreased risk in CI-AKI patients with pre-existing renal disease.

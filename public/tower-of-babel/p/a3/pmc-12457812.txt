The Relationship Between Bone Morphogenic Protein-4 and Visceral-Central Adiposity
Authors: Sedanur Tekin Can, Filiz Tubaş, Nuriye Gökçe, Aslıhan Kiraz, Funda İpekten, Zehra Filiz Karaman, Derya Altay, Ülkü Gül Şiraz, Yusuf Özkul, Dinçer Göksülük
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeExcessive fat accumulation can adversely affect vital organs, primarily because of energy imbalance. Hepatosteatosis is characterized by visceral fat accumulation, whereas obesity is associated with central fat deposition. This study aimed to determine the differences in bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP-4) levels according to the presence of obesity and hepatosteatosis.MethodsA total of 84 volunteers evaluated at the Erciyes University Faculty of Medicine Child Health and Diseases Polyclinic were included. Pediatric radiological assessments were conducted for hepatosteatosis, and BMP-4 gene expression analysis was performed at the Erciyes University Genome and Stem Cell Center, using patient records and RNA extracted from peripheral blood samples. Subsequently, groups were formed based on obesity and hepatosteatosis status, and BMP-4 expression levels were compared between groups.ResultsThe mean BMP-4 expression coefficient in the entire cohort was 1.3. Based on the BMI percentile groups, BMP-4 expression was highest in normal-weight individuals and lowest in overweight individuals, showing a statistically significant difference (p=0.024). In children with obesity, a weak positive correlation was noted between BMP-4 and glucose levels (r=0.315) and between BMP-4 and gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase levels (r=0.328).ConclusionBMP-4 may play a role in the modulation of obesity and hepatosteatosis. These findings suggest that BMP-4 expression decreases during initial weight gain, but increases in obese individuals. This suggests that BMP-4 increases with the onset of obesity. An increase in BMP-4 levels at the onset of obesity initiates the activation of resistance mechanisms.Keywords: Child, Bone morphogenetic protein 4, Fatty liver, Obesity

INTRODUCTION

Adipose tissue, which originates from the mesoderm, functions as a dynamic, multifunctional organ composed of various cell types, including adipocytes, preadipocytes, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, nerves, and immune cells. It serves as the body’s primary lipid reservoir, storing fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K), and plays a critical role in energy production, temperature regulation, and hormone secretion. White adipose tissue (WAT), a key player in energy metabolism, stores triglycerides and acts as an endocrine organ. Although less abundant, brown adipose tissue is crucial for energy expenditure and thermogenesis because of its rich mitochondrial content and vascularization. Conversion between white and brown adipose tissue is possible, with beige fat serving as an intermediate state [1,2,3].

Body fat is distributed as visceral or subcutaneous, with visceral fat closely linked to the portal circulation, and is more prone to complications such as hepatosteatosis due to its higher blood supply, inflammatory cells, and insulin resistance. In contrast, subcutaneous fat has a higher capacity for fat absorption and adipokine secretion [3,4,5].

Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) belong to the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) family. They signal through type I and II serine–threonine kinase receptors, including Smad proteins. Furthermore, BMP signals can be finely tuned by using various agonists and antagonists. BMPs are essential regulatory molecules in various organ systems [6,7]. BMP-4 plays a crucial role in adipogenesis and liver metabolism. BMP-4 promotes the differentiation of white adipocytes and supports the transition to brown adipocytes, affecting the overall adiposity by influencing adipocyte size and number. Research has shown elevated BMP-4 levels have been reported in individuals with obesity, metabolic syndrome, and related disorders such as hepatosteatosis, insulin resistance, and diabetes [8,9,10].

Despite these findings, the development of tissue antagonist resistance remains a concern [11]. This study aimed to elucidate the role of BMP-4 in lipid metabolism and its potential implications in obesity and hepatosteatosis, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of its function in metabolic diseases. We hypothesized that an increase in BMP-4 expression at the onset of obesity might also activate resistance mechanisms. Therefore, all possible scenarios should be considered when using targeted therapies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective study was conducted at Erciyes University Faculty of Medicine between January 1 and September 30, 2022. The study included patients aged 7–18 years who were treated in general pediatrics, pediatric gastroenterology, and pediatric endocrinology departments. Informed consent was obtained from all participants and their guardians before data collection.

A total of 84 volunteers were recruited and classified into two main groups based on body mass index (BMI): nonobese (including both overweight and normal-weight individuals) and obese. To maintain homogeneity, patients with known metabolic or genetic diseases, structural bone and tooth disorders, and breast diseases were excluded from the study. Radiological assessments of hepatosteatosis were performed by pediatric radiologists using ultrasound (US), specifically with a GE Logiq S7 C1-5 device equipped with a 6C1 probe.

Hepatosteatosis was diagnosed by comparing the liver echogenicity with that of the kidneys, and patients were divided into two categories: normal liver echogenicity and hepatosteatosis.

The volunteers were classified into weight categories according to the BMI percentile for age and sex curves defined by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) [12,13].

Accordingly, the 5th–85th percentiles represent normal weight, the 85th–94th percentiles indicate overweight, and the 95th percentile indicates obesity. A relative weight ≥120% or BMI ≥35 kg/m2 (whichever is lower) indicates class 2 obesity. If the relative weight is ≥140% or the BMI is ≥40 kg/m2 (whichever is lower), it corresponds to class 3 obesity.

BMP-4 gene expression analysis; ribonucleic acid (RNA) was extracted from peripheral blood samples collected during routine blood collection. RNA was isolated using the phenol-chloroform method using triazoles (Qiagen). cDNA was synthesized using a Quantitect Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen), and real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was conducted using a Quantitect SYBR Green PCR Kit on a Light Cycler 480 II (Roche). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as a reference gene. The primers used for the PCR are listed in Table 1. Gene expression levels were analyzed using the 2-ΔΔCt method.

Statistical analyses were performed by the Erciyes University Medical Biostatistics Department using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 24.0 (IBM Co.) and R (version 4.0). Descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation, and percentage, were calculated. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual tools, such as histograms and Q-Q plots. Homogeneity of variances was checked using the Levene test. The Mann-Whitney U-test and independent t-tests were used to compare two groups, whereas ANOVA and the Tukey test were used for comparisons involving more than two groups. Spearman correlation and simple linear regression were employed to assess relationships between variables, with p<0.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 84 children diagnosed were included in this study. Of the 84 children, 36 (42.9%) were male and 48 (57.1%) were female. The mean age, height, and weight were 165.58 months, 157.17 cm, and 71.68 kg, respectively. The mean BMP-4 value was 1.3, and the median BMI percentile was 95.

The distribution characteristics of the study groups, categorized according to obesity and hepatosteatosis status, are presented in Table 2. These included the mean age, sex distribution, prevalence of hepatosteatosis, obesity class status, BMI percentile, and average values of BMP-4.

The overall mean BMP-4 levels across the groups categorized according to obesity and hepatosteatosis status did not exhibit a statistically significant difference (F=1.280, p=0.287).

A comparison of BMP-4 expression according to BMI percentile groups is provided in Table 3.

The mean BMP-4 level was significantly different between BMI percentile groups (p=0.024). This difference was particularly notable in participants who were overweight, whose BMP-4 levels were lower than those in obese and normal-weight participants. The average BMP-4 level was higher in the obese group than in the overweight group.

DISCUSSION

This study aimed to elucidate the variations in BMP-4 expression in relation to obesity and hepatosteatosis. We observed that BMP-4 levels differed according to the weight status. We did not observe any significant effect of hepatosteatosis on BMP-4 expression.

BMP-4 expression was highest in normal-weight individuals without hepatosteatosis and lowest in overweight individuals without hepatosteatosis. This suggests that weight status, rather than hepatosteatosis, is the primary factor that influences BMP-4 expression.

This phenomenon can also be observed at certain levels in healthy individuals. A significant reduction in BMP-4 expression was observed in individuals who began to gain weight at the onset of adiposity. BMP-4 is thought to play a role in the modulation of obesity. BMP-4 levels increase at the onset of obesity [9,11]. In our study, BMP-4 expression levels were determined according to body weight status. An increase in BMP-4 levels has been observed in obese individuals.

The increased expression in obese individuals is thought to be associated with resistance mechanisms. Hoffmann et al. [11] observed BMP-4 resistance in obese patients. This resistance may develop because of the receptor-level resistance to the molecule itself or an increase in antagonists. In obese individuals, adipose tissue becomes resistant to BMP-4. BMP-4 does not aid browning of white adipose tissue. Increased insulin sensitivity did not contribute to weight loss. It also does not reduce inflammation in adipose tissue. Additionally, antagonists such as noggin showed an increase. These findings indicate a lack of response in obese individuals.

Studies have shown that BMP-4 has no effect on weight loss but increases insulin sensitivity. Baboota et al. [10] reported increased BMP-4 and receptor expression in fat metabolism disorders such as hepatosteatosis, insulin resistance, obesity, and diabetes. BMP-4 is believed to increase the transition from white to brown fat tissue and to induce angiogenesis in fat tissues. However, resistance development with an increase in antagonists in the tissue has also been emphasized [9,10,11]. Our study did not find any effect on hepatosteatosis. It has been observed that BMP-4 levels decrease in overweight individuals and increase in obese individuals compared to normal-weight individuals.

Other studies have shown that BMP-4 antagonists negatively affect beige-brown adipogenesis. Gustafson et al. [14] have demonstrated that Noggin, Chordin, Chordin-like, Follistatin, and Gremlin are BMP-4 antagonists. They showed that with an increase in BMP-4 antagonists, BMP-4 resistance develops in preadipocytes, negatively affecting beige-brown adipogenesis.

The laboratory values associated with obesity, insulin resistance, and dyslipidemia-related metabolism-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) were examined. This condition is indicative of metabolic abnormalities and poses a risk of liver complications, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease [15,16]. Our study observed higher median GGT and ALT values in the group with hepatosteatosis than in the group without hepatosteatosis, indicating a potential association with MAFLD. Patients with hepatosteatosis were advised to continue follow-ups for possible complications.

The limitations of our study include the lack of anthropometric measurements, inability to assess fat distribution and amount, small sample size, and regional scope. These results need to be confirmed using larger sample sizes in further studies. Hepatosteatosis status was evaluated as present or absent by a pediatric radiologist in the US. Hepatosteatosis was not classified according to degree. Histopathological evaluation of steatosis type could not be performed. BMP-4 expression levels indicate blood conditions; tissue levels could not be examined.

In conclusion, our study provides an average value for BMP-4 expression in children and offers valuable regional insights. As BMP-4 expression studies in children are limited, the averages obtained may reflect societal trends. BMP-4 expression varies with weight, as evidenced by its decrease in overweight participants and subsequent increase in obese participants. High BMP-4 expression in obese individuals may be related to resistance mechanisms, warranting further investigation to elucidate the underlying mechanisms. Future studies on BMP-4 mechanisms will contribute to a better understanding of the mechanisms underlying obesity.

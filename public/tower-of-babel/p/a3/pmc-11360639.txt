Mortality risk in patients with renal hyperfiltration: a risk assessment
Authors: Tomoyuki Kawada
Published: 2024

Dear editor,

I have read with great interest the paper entitled ‘Predictive effect of estimated glomerular filtrate rate by creatinine or cystatin C on mortality in patients with coronary artery disease’ by Chen et al. [1]. Although reduced estimated glomerular filtrate rate based on creatinine (eGFR-cr) and cystatin C (eGFRcr-cys) has highest predictive effect for cardiovascular and all-cause mortality among coronary artery disease (CAD) patients, supra-normal eGFR-cr may also indicate a higher risk of mortality.

Although there are significant differences between AUC of eGFRcr-cys and other eGFRs in all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, absolute differences in AUCs were relatively small. This means that any eGFR has an ability to differentiate mortality risk, except for the increased mortality risk in patients with hyperfiltration, judged by eGFR-cr.

Regarding cardiovascular events in subjects with renal hyperfiltration, Shlipak et al. presented a discrepancy in supra-normal eGFR-cr and eGFR-cys [2]. Kanbay et al. mentioned that high eGFR-cr may not reflect a true hyperfiltration, especially in subjects with low muscle mass and inflammation [3]. There are many causes of renal hyperfiltration, and pathophysiological changes should be considered for the increased risk in mortality [4]. For the risk assessment of cardiovascular mortality, renal hyperfiltration in combination with increased urinary albumin-creatinine ratio would present reliable information [5].

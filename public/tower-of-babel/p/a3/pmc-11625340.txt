Geometrical and dosimetrical evaluation of different interpretations of a european consensus delineation guideline for the internal mammary lymph node chain in breast cancer patients
Authors: Emma Skarsø Buhl, Geert Wortel, Rita Simões, Astrid Scholten, Birgitte Vrou Offersen, Stine Korreman, Tomas Janssen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Evaluation of the interpretation span in ESTRO delineation guideline for CTVn_IMN.•The interpretation span in delineation guidelines across borders were investigated.•Significant different width and distance to the sternal bone was found.•Different interpretations led to a significantly different dose to heart and lungs.•For individual patients, the dosimetric differences may impact treatment decisions.Keywords: Interpretations of guidelines, Breast cancer, CTVn_IMN, Radiotherapy, Geometrical and dosimetrical consequences

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most prevalent non-melanoma cancer worldwide [1]. In high-risk breast cancer patients loco-regional adjuvant radiotherapy has shown to reduce distant recurrences and improve overall survival [2], [3]. The 5-year survival has increased during the last 20 years, which has increased the focus on the long-term adverse effects of RT, such as cardiovascular disease. In 2013, Darby et al. documented that the rates of coronary artery events increased linearly with mean heart dose (MHD) with relative 7.4 % per Gy in breast cancer patients. Today, in most cases, the heart dose is low, due to advances in radiotherapy planning and delivery techniques, such as deep inspiration breath hold [4]. 3D based conformal radiotherapy is commonly used to treat breast cancer. Nevertheless, more advanced techniques, such as the volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT), has recently gained more attention [5] as VMAT has the benefit of high dose conformity, with the cost of a low dose bath.

For early-stage node positive breast cancer, irradiation of the internal mammary lymph nodes (IMN), reduces the risk of distant metastasis and improves overall survival [2], [6]. The irradiation of the IMN, however, may increase the heart dose. For a subset of patients, it is difficult to spare the heart adequately, due to the anatomy of the patient, e.g. patients with pectus excavatum [7]. These patients potentially benefit from receiving proton therapy, as it may reduce dose to the organs-at-risk (OAR). In the Netherlands, a model-based approach is used to select patients for proton therapy. The model builds upon the risk of acute coronary artery events (ACE) in women with and without risk factors. The model utilizes the results from Darby et al. and applies the relative 7.4 % risk of ACE per Gy increase in MHD. Patients are referred for proton therapy if there is an increased risk of ACE ≥ 2 % with photon therapy compared to proton therapy [8]. In Denmark, the candidates for the Danish Breast Cancer Group (DBCG) proton trial are selected based on MHD ≥ 4 Gy and/or lung V20Gy ≥ 37 % when treatment is planned with standard tangential fields [9].

To find the best treatment option, accurate target delineation is required. Delineation of targets, however, is difficult and prone to inter-observer variation (IOV), potentially with dosimetric implications [10], [11]. To minimize the IOV, ESTRO published a consensus guideline on target volume delineation in breast cancer in 2015, and this guideline is widely used in Europe [12], including in Denmark and Netherlands. However, written guidelines unavoidably leave some room for interpretation, which will vary between clinics and across borders.

This study aimed to investigate the magnitude of the interpretation span of the same delineation guideline across borders and the possible dosimetric impact of these interpretations for the CTVn_IMN. We investigated the interpretations of the ESTRO target delineation guidelines for the CTVn_IMN at the Netherlands Cancer institute (NKI) and in the DBCG. We compared geometrical differences and evaluated dosimetric implications to the OARs.

Method

Data

A cohort of 95 left-sided breast cancer patients treated with radiotherapy at the Netherlands Cancer Institute (NKI) was included. The patients were treated in the period from 2017 to 2022 and collected from the clinical database. The inclusion of the CTVn_IMN as a structure was not standard for most breast cancer patients in the Netherlands in this period. Data collection was approved by the institutional review board (IRBd22-146).

The dataset included the planning CT-scans and clinical structure sets, with delineations created according to the ESTRO breast cancer target delineation guidelines [12]. Routinely, the clinical delineations were checked by peers before treatment planning.

Interpretations

The NKI interpretation (CTVn_IMN_NKI) of the ESTRO CTVn_IMN delineation guideline was retrieved from the clinical structure sets.

The Danish interpretation (CTVn_IMN_DK) of the ESTRO CTVn_IMN delineation guidelines, was mimicked by using a deep learning model (DL) [13], [14]. The model was trained on a consensus cohort created by the DBCG according to the ESTRO delineation guidelines and hence represented the Danish interpretation of the guideline. The model was trained to delineate the CTVn_IMN to intercostal space 3 and performed on par with expert delineators. Details of model training, testing and data conversion are found in Supplementary 1.1 and 1.2.

Due to differences in the fixed caudal endpoint in the CTVn_IMN_DK versus the variable prescribed nodal inclusion for CTVn_IMN_NKI, the CTVn_IMN_NKI and CTVn_IMN_DK pairs were cropped to the same caudal border (the most cranial of the two caudal borders). The cropped version of the CTVn_IMN_NKI and CTVn_IMN_DK have been used for the rest of the analysis.

Geometrical comparison

The similarity between the CTVn_IMN_NKI and the CTVn_IMN_DK was evaluated with the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), symmetrical 95 % Hausdorff distance (HD95) and non-symmetrical Mean Surface Distance (MSD). The metrics were calculated with the Python package MedPy [15].

The spatial differences between the two CTVn_IMN delineations were characterized by measuring the width of the CTVn_IMN (the longest distance between the coordinate points in each slice, averaged over all slices), the maximum distance between the two CTVn_IMN delineations in the medial direction (measured over all CT slices of the CTVn_IMN and measured only in CT slices where a heart delineation is present), the cranial caudal length of the CTVn_IMN and measuring the distance from the CTVn_IMN delineation to the sternal bone (the average distance between the CTVn_IMN and the sternal bone), Fig. 1. As the sternal bone was not delineated in clinical practice, it was delineated manually by a single observer as a helping structure on all patients using 3D Slicer [16], [17].

The geometrical metrics measured for the CTVn_IMN. The difference between the two interpretations (top left). The distance to the sternal bone and the width (bottom left). Sagittal slices with the cranial caudal length (to the right).

The geometrical comparison results were divided into two: One considering all CTVn_IMN delineations and one only considering the patients where the CTVn_IMN delineation and the heart delineation were present in the same CT-slice.

Plan comparison

A local clinical planning protocol was used to retrospectively generate fully automatic treatment plans for both CTVn_IMN delineations. The treatment plans were created to be as close as possible to a clinical acceptable plan, however no patient specific tweaking was done. Treatment plans were created using Pinnacle Auto-Planning and the framework for automatic treatment planning (FAST) developed at NKI [18]. The plans were made using 6 MV dual arc VMAT beams, covering the left sided breast and lymph nodes, giving 40.05 Gy in 15 fractions. For automatic treatment planning, the following structures were needed: The heart, the lungs, the contralateral breast, the breast target, a merged structure of CTVn_L1 and L2 and a merged structure of CTVn_L3 and L4. The lungs, heart (delineated according to Feng et al. [19]) and breast target were found in the clinical structure set. If any of the additional necessary structures were missing in the clinical dataset, they were generated by Mirada’s auto-contouring Mirada DLCExpertTM (Mirada Medical, Oxford, UK). From the CTV’s PTV’s were automatically created following the clinical protocol at NKI, Supplementary 1.3.

The treatment plans were optimized with a fixed set of Auto-Planning optimization goals on the PTV, heart, lungs, contralateral breast, and conformality rings. To achieve the desired dose distributions, a complex set of optimization goals was required on the heart and lungs. To ensure target coverage, the heart sparing goals were set on an auxiliary structure where the PTV + 10 mm was subtracted from the heart. The dose goal set on this structure was V12Gy < 5 % with the mean dose as low as possible. For the lungs, optimization goals were set on the D2%, D40%, D50% and the mean dose. All plans were rescaled to V95%=99 % PTV_IMN target coverage, Supplementary 1.3.

Since Pinnacle Auto-Planning is not deterministic [20], three plans were created for each delineation and subsequently averaged.

Analysis

The geometrical measurements were compared between the two interpretations of the CTVn_IMN delineations.

The treatment plans were evaluated and compared based on the mean heart dose (MHD) and the mean lung dose (MLD), per patient and CTVn_IMN delineation. Statistical significance was tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, assuming statistical significance with p < 0.05. The confidence intervals on the median were calculated with paired bootstrapping with the number of resampling n = 9999.

The correlation between the difference in geometric delineation characteristics and OAR doses, was investigated and tested with the Pearson correlation coefficient, assuming statistical significance with p < 0.05.

Results

Data

Two patients did not have a clinically delineated CTVn_IMN structure and were removed from the cohort. A total of 28 patients were further excluded as they had either a combined CTVn_IMN and L4 structure or a significant part of the sternal bone included due to local recurrence in the IMN and with possible infiltration in the sternal bone, leaving 66 patients with a separate CTVn_IMN_NKI structure. Additionally, four patients were removed due to missing breast target delineation and or heart delineation and one patient due to conversion error, when converting the patient from NIfTI to DICOM, leaving a total of 62 patients for analysis. The DL model was successfully run on all 62 patients, creating the CTVn_IMN_DK structure. An example of an CTVn_IMN_NKI and CTVn_IMN_DK can be seen in Fig. 1.

Geometrical comparison

A median dice score of 0.63 (IQR 0.57–0.68), a median HD95% of 4.89 mm (IQR 3.15 – 6.84 mm) and a median MSD of 0.85 mm (IQR 0.69– 1.13 mm) was found between the CTVn_IMN_NKI and the CTVn_IMN_DK. The geometrical comparison metrics in the test-set of the Danish consensus model can be seen in the Supplementary Table S1 and S2. Additionally, A median volume of 9.59 ml (IQR 8.47–12.1 ml) was found for the CTVn_IMN_NKI and 7.19 ml (6.30 ml – 8.52 ml) for the CTVn_IMN_DK.

A significantly larger width was found in the CTVn_IMN_NKI, with a median width of 18.2 mm (IQR 16.3 – 19.9 mm) compared to the CTVn_IMN_DK with a median width of 14.7 mm (IQR 14.0 – 15.8 mm) (p < 0.05). Similarly, the CTVn_IMN_NKI was found to be extending more in the medial direction compared to the CTVn_IMN_DK, with a median difference in the medial direction between CTVn_IMN_DK and CTVn_IMN_NKI of −3.41 mm (IQR-5.15––1.39 mm), Fig. 2 and Table 1.

Dose Investigation

A total of 92 structures for treatment planning were missing in the clinical structure set and Mirada structures were used instead, Table S3. These structures were mainly the contralateral breast and the CTVn_L1 and L2. For treatment plan data, see Table S5, S6 and S7.

The MHD was significantly higher with the CTVn_IMN_NKI based plans (p = 0.008) with a median MHD of 5.07 Gy (IQR 4.14 – 5.76 Gy) for CTVn_IMN_NKI and a median MHD of 4.68 Gy (IQR 4.03 – 5.67 Gy) for CTVn_IMN_DK. The median MHD difference between CTVn_IMN_DK and CTVn_IMN_NKI was 0.12 Gy [−0.50 Gy; −0.02 Gy], see Fig. 3.

In 11/62 patients, the MHD difference was larger than 0.5 Gy. The largest dose difference was 0.9 Gy. In 38/62 cases the CTVn_IMN_NKI showed a higher MHD. In four patients, the CTVn_IMN_NKI showed a MHD > 4 Gy, whereas the CTVn_IMN_DK showed a MHD < 4 Gy, which would make the patients eligible for randomization for the DBCG proton trial.

The MLD was significantly higher with the CTVn_IMN_NKI based plans (p = 0.002), with a median MLD of 8.09 Gy (IQR 7.59 – 8.63 Gy) for the CTVn_IMN_NKI and a median MLD of 7.95 Gy (IQR 7.50 – 8.47 Gy) for CTVn_IMN_DK. The median MLD difference between CTVn_IMN_DK and CTVn_IMN_NKI was 0.1 Gy [−0.30–0.02], see Fig. 3.

Correlation

The strongest correlations were found when only looking at patients, where the CTVn_IMN and the heart delineation were in the same CT slice (n = 50). The strongest correlation was found between both the MLD and MHD and the maximum distance in the medial direction between the CTVn_IMN delineations calculated in slices where the heart was present, with a Pearson coefficient = -0.25 (p = 0.08) and Pearson coefficient = 0.26 (p = 0.07) respectively, Fig. 4 and Supplementary Table S8.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated different interpretations of breast cancer delineation guidelines and the possible clinical implementations of these. We found statistically significant differences for both geometric and dosimetric comparison between the two interpretations of CTVn_IMN delineations.

Written delineation guidelines are created with the purpose of harmonizing radiotherapy; however, they will always leave some (intended or unintended) room for interpretation which can vary from clinic to clinic and country to country [22]. A study by Nielsen et al, showed a mean increase in DSC of 0.12 when delineating the CTVn_IMN before and six weeks after consensus guidelines [21]. An increase in delineation uniformity was also found by Bollen et al, when studying the difference in the primary tumor clinical target volume for head and neck cancer with consensus guidelines. This study also warranted the caution of correct interpretation of consensus guidelines [23].

Furthermore, studies have shown, that variations in delineation may lead to differences in planned dose [11]. A study by Li et al. investigated the dosimetric differences in breast targets and OARs in manual contours from nine radiation oncologists without guidelines and showed that the CTVn_IMN delineation for left-sided breast cancer patients had a SD of 30 % in volume, which translated into a dosimetrically difference in the heart and lung [24]. Another study conducted by Simões et al. showed that cranial under-contouring of the breast target resulted in more than 5 % relative decrease in the PTV coverage, whereas latero-posterior over-contouring increased the lung dose [10].

In this study, we focused on the interpretation span in the ESTRO breast cancer consensus delineation guidelines for the CTVn_IMN between the NKI and DBCG. To represent the NKI interpretation of the CTVn_IMN delineation guidelines, the clinically used CTVn_IMN delineations from the NKI were used. The Danish interpretation of the delineation guidelines was mimicked with a DL model. The different origin of the CTVn_IMN (manual delineation in the NKI CTVn_IMN, DL segmentation for the Danish CTVn_IMN) could introduce a bias in the results. The CTVn_IMN_NKI delineations includes interobserver variations, which introduces some uncertainty in the NKI interpretation. The CTVn_IMN_DK was mimicked with a DL model, which introduces some segmentation characteristics, such as narrow most cranial and caudal slices, which are not present in clinical delineations. Additionally, it is worth noting that the segmentations used in this study were not corrected or approved by a clinician. However, since the model was trained and tested on a consensus dataset, it was assumed to represent the average Danish interpretation of the ESTRO delineation guidelines.

In this study we found that the difference in the CTVn_IMN delineations led to a statistically significant different MHD and MLD dose. One consequence of this difference could be in referral for proton therapy, as both the heart and lung doses are used to identify which patients would potentially benefit from receiving proton therapy [8], [9]. For four patients in this study, the difference in CTVn_IMN interpretation would for instance translate into a change in the eligibility for the DBCG Proton trial.

The cranial and caudal border represent locations with high geometrical IOV [25], and it is therefore expected that it is difficult to delineate for both clinicians and DL models, and there will hence be a higher uncertainty in these areas. It is expected that a more caudal endpoint of the CTVn_IMN delineation will entail a higher dose to both heart and lungs, and it was therefore chosen to crop the CTVn_IMN_NKI and CTVn_IMN_DK to the same caudal border, to avoid the caudal endpoint to influence the dosimetric result of the study.

When comparing the performance metrics of the Danish consensus model in its test-set with the performance metrics between the two CTVn_IMN interpretations, both the HD95 and MSD were better between the CTVn_IMN interpretations compared to the model test-set. The difference was removed by cropping the segmentations in the test-set to the same caudal border.

When investigating the two interpretations of CTVn_IMNs, the largest differences were seen in the width of the CTVn_IMN and the distance to the sternal bone. In most cases, the CTVn_IMN_NKI were delineated close to the sternal bone, making the delineation wider than the CTVn_IMN_DK. Despite the different delineation trends, it was not possible to find any strong correlation between dosimetric implications to the MHD and different delineation trends.

Nevertheless, such a simple one-to-one relation might be obscured due to the complexity (high dimensionality, non-linearity and non-convexity) of the treatment planning problem. The local minimum achieved in optimizing the planning objectives is not solely dependent on the CTVn_IMN delineation, but on the interplay between the different target and OAR delineations, patient anatomy, etc.

To create the treatment plans, Pinnacle Auto-Planning was used [18]. This makes the plans comparable without bias, however they might not be clinically acceptable plans. In particular, the protocol was optimized not to compromise target coverage in favor of OAR sparing. In a clinical setting, compromises on e.g. CTVn_IMN coverage could have been made for some patients.

To use the automated treatment planning system, a consistent set of structures was needed. In the case of missing structures in the clinical dataset, non-clinical and non-checked structures created by Mirada DLCExpertTM were used [20]. This may influence the plan quality; however, the same structures were used for both CTVn_IMN interpretations, thus this is not expected to influence the results of this study.

It is worth noting that the treatment plans were VMAT plans, which have the advantage of high dose conformality [26]. We expect that the dosimetric influence on MHD and MLD of CTVn_IMN delineation is larger when considering treatments using tangential fields, due to field angle being largely determined by the CTVn_IMN delineation.

We have shown that differences in interpretation of CTVn_IMN delineation guidelines lead to statistically significant differences in lung and heart doses. For more than 10 % of the patients, the magnitude of the difference in MHD was larger than 0.5 Gy. For the individual patients, the dosimetric impact of different guideline interpretation could be large enough to impact treatment decision, such as referral to proton therapy. The results underscore the need for a strong delineation consensus across borders.

Association between Phosphate Disturbances and Mortality Among Critically Ill Patients With Sepsis or Septic Shock
Authors: Shailendra K Patel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPhosphorus is a critical ion essential for normal cellular function. Phosphate imbalances are commonly observed in critically ill patients, with particularly high prevalence in sepsis. However, evidence regarding the clinical significance of serum phosphate disturbances in patients with sepsis and septic shock remains scarce and controversial, especially in the Indian setting. The independent association between phosphate disturbances and mortality in septic patients is unclear.ObjectivesTo evaluate the association of hypophosphatemia and hyperphosphatemia with mortality among critically ill patients with sepsis and septic shock.Materials and methodsthis prospective observational study was conducted in the Department of Critical Care Medicine, Batra Hospital and Medical Research Centre (BHMRC), New Delhi, from December 2022 to September 2024 (22 months). The study enrolled 207 adult patients (age &ge;18 years) admitted to the medical ICU with sepsis or septic shock and ICU length of stay (LOS) &gt;24 hours. Patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), those receiving parenteral nutrition, vitamin D preparations, or phosphate binders, as well as burn patients and pregnant women were excluded. Serum phosphorus was measured at admission alongside other baseline parameters and blood investigations. Normal serum phosphate was defined as 2.5-4.7 mg/dl; hypophosphatemia as &lt;2.5 mg/dl; and hyperphosphatemia as &ge;4.7 mg/dl. All patients received standardized ICU treatment protocols for sepsis, septic shock, and associated electrolyte imbalances. Duration of mechanical ventilation, length of ICU stay, hospital stay, and mortality were recorded. Data was organized and tabulated in Microsoft Excel 2016 (Microsoft Office 2016 package) and statistical analysis was done using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 16.0 (IBM Corp., Illinois, Chicago).ResultsThe mean age was 62.6 &plusmn; 17.1 years. The incidence of hypophosphatemia and hyperphosphatemia on admission was 18.8% and 24.2%, respectively. The mean serum phosphate level was 1.82 &plusmn; 0.28 mg/dl in hypophosphatemia and 6.70 &plusmn; 1.45 mg/dl in hyperphosphatemia groups. Compared to normophosphatemia, patients with hypophosphatemia had significantly lower Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores (p = 0.003), higher Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) II scores (p = 0.008), elevated baseline lactate levels (p = 0.003), increased need for mechanical ventilation (p = 0.030), longer ventilation duration (p = 0.004), extended ICU stays (p = 0.023), and higher mortality (53.8% vs 28.8%; p&lt;0.001). Similarly, hyperphosphatemia was associated with lower GCS scores (p&lt;0.001), higher APACHE II scores (p&lt;0.001), elevated creatinine (p&lt;0.001) and lactate levels (p&lt;0.001), increased mechanical ventilation needs (p&lt;0.001), longer ICU stays (p = 0.015), and significantly higher mortality (58% vs 28.8%; p&lt;0.001). Overall mortality was 40.6% (84/207 patients). Logistic regression analysis revealed that compared to normophosphatemia, the adjusted odds ratio for mortality was 2.8 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.4-6.1; p = 0.005) in hypophosphatemia and 3.4 (95% CI: 1.7-6.8; p&lt;0.001) in hyperphosphatemia.ConclusionOur study demonstrated that both hypophosphatemia and hyperphosphatemia at the time of ICU admission were independently associated with increased mortality, prolonged ICU stays, and mechanical ventilation requirements, irrespective of underlying comorbidities. These findings underscore the need for further research into the pathophysiology of phosphate disturbances in critical illness and development of targeted therapeutic interventions.&nbsp;Keywords: Phosphate disturbances, hypophosphatemia, hyperphosphatemia, sepsis, septic shock, mortality, critically ill patients

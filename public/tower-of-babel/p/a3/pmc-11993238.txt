Arbitrarily Suffixed Sequence-Specific Primer PCR for Reliable Genome Walking: Taking Genome Walkings of Levilactobacillus brevis And Rice as Examples
Authors: Haixing Li, Cheng Wei, Zhenkang Pan, Xiaohua Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground: Genome walking has contributed to life science-related areas. Keywords: Arbitrary primer, Genome walking, Hybrid primer, Nested PCR, Random annealing, Sequence-specific primer

1. Background

Genome walking is a molecular tool mining unknown flanking DNAs based on known DNAs ( 1 - 3 ). Genome walking has many applications in the related areas, such as identifying genetically modified crops ( 4 ). Many genome walking schemes have been proposed during the past four decades. A ligation PCR-based walking method is complex, because it requires the digestion of genome and subsequent DNA ligation preceding PCR ( 5 , 6 ).

A random PCR-based walking scheme relies on the random annealing of walking primer. This random annealing leads to many DNAs. Among them, target DNA is boosted in the subsequent PCR cycles, as it is defined by walking primer and sequence-specific primer (SSP). Conversely, nontarget cannot be amplified, because it lacks a complement for walking primer or SSP ( 7 ) This strategy is attractive because of the directness and rapidity ( 8 , 9 ). So far, there are many random PCR-based schemes available. However, constructing more such schemes is still popular, which will provide us with more choices for genome walking and thus may enhance walking efficiency.

Herein, we devised a new genome walking protocol, called arbitrarily suffixed sequence-specific primer PCR (ASP-PCR). This protocol features the use of a hybrid primer (HP) in primary ASP-PCR. The HP was made by suffixing an arbitrary primer (AP) sequence to outmost SSP. The practicability of ASP-PCR was confirmed by obtaining unknown DNAs flanking Oryza sativa hygromycin gene (hyg, GenBank accession no. KF206149.1) ( 9 ) and Levilactobacillus brevis CD0817 L-glutamic acid decarboxylase gene (gad, GenBank accession no. CP032931.1) ( 10 ).

2. Objectives

The objective of this study was to provide an efficient genome walking method, so as to facilitate obtaining unknown flanking DNAs.

3. Materials and Methods

3.1.Genomic DNA Isolation

Genomic DNA of L. brevis CD0817 ( 11 ) was extracted with TIANamp Bacteria DNA Kit (TIANGEN Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Oryza sativa genomic DNA was obtained from the laboratory of Dr. Xiaojue Peng at Nanchang University (Nanchang, China).

3.2. Primer Design

We used the software Oligo 7 (Molecular Biology Insights, Inc., USA) to devise primers. Here, one AP was devised. Also, four SSPs, including two outmost SSPs (omSSP1 and omSSP2), one middle SSP (mdSSP), and one innermost SSP (imSSP), were extracted from each gene along the direction of 5’ to 3’. The omSSP1 completely differed or partially overlapped with omSSP2; in the latter case, the 3’ ends of the two primers were staggered by at least 5nt, to ensure a sufficient sequence difference between the primers. The 5’ ends of the two omSSPs were respectively attached with the AP sequence, resulting in two HPs (HP1 and HP2). Please note that an omSSP alone was not served as a primer, but only as a part of the corresponding HP. The AP and SSPs show a melting temperature of 60 – 65 °C. Any primer or primer pair is free of severe self-hairpin or primer dimer (Table 1).

3.3. PCR Procedure

A walking experiment contained two parallel sets of ASP-PCRs. Each ASP-PCR set contained three rounds of nested PCRs. The two primary ASP-PCRs were individually completed by HP1 and HP2, employing genomic DNA as templates. The two secondary ASP-PCRs were completed by the AP plus mdSSP, employing the according primary products as templates. The two tertiary ASP-PCRs were completed by the AP plus imSSP, employing the according secondary products as templates.

The primary ASP-PCR mixture (50 μL) contained 1×LA PCR buffer II, 0.4 mM each dNTP, 10 – 100 ng microbial genome or 100 – 1000 ng Oryza sativa genome, 0.2 μM HP1 or HP2, and 2.5 U TaKaRa LA Taq polymerase (Perking, China). The secondary ASP-PCR mixture (50 μL) included 1×LA PCR buffer II, 0.4 mM each dNTP, 1 µL the primary ASP-PCR product, 0.2 μM AP, 0.2 μM mdSSP, and 2.5 U polymerase. And the tertiary ASP-PCR mixture (50 μL) included 1×LA PCR buffer II, 0.4 mM each dNTP, 1 µL the secondary ASP-PCR product, 0.2 μM AP, 0.2 μM imSSP, and 2.5 U polymerase.

The primary ASP-PCR comprised of two annealing stages: stage 1, one relaxed (25 °C) cycle; and stage 2, thirty stringent (65 °C) cycles. The secondary or tertiary ASP-PCR only comprised of thirty 65 °C cycles (Table 2). All the reactions were completed in Biometra TOne 96G PCR thermalcycler (Analytik Jena, Germany).

3.4. DNA Purification and Sequencing

After agarose gel electrophoresis, the major secondary and tertiary amplicons were recovered with DiaSpin column type DNA Gel Extraction Kit (Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China). The recovered amplicons were sent to Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. for direct sequencing. The data were analyzed using the MegAlign tool in Lasergene package (DNASTAR, Inc.).

4. Results

4.1. Outline of ASP-PCR

Primary ASP-PCR begins with one relaxed cycle. This cycle enables HP to hybridize to many places on genome, producing many first-strand DNAs. In the subsequent stringent cycle, target first-strand is extended by HP to form target second-strand DNA, as its 3’ portion has a binding site for the omSSP component of HP. This target second-strand DNA can be efficiently enriched in the remaining stringent cycles. However, any nontarget first-strand cannot further be amplified by HP, since it lacks a binding site for this primer.

Secondary or tertiary ASP-PCR cycles are stringent. The target primary ASP-PCR product can be exponen-tially amplified in secondary or tertiary ASP-PCR. Conversely, a nontarget itself forms hairpin via the HP sites, rather than being further amplified. Finally, the target DNA becomes the major amplicon (Fig.1).

4.2. Validation of ASP-PCR

As a proof of the concept, the ASP-PCR was employed to explore unknown DNAs flanking the two selected genes. The electrophoretic images of the ASP-PCR products are displayed in Figure 2. As shown, each secondary or tertiary ASP-PCR exhibited 1 – 2 clear amplicon (s). The size of these amplicons ranged from 1.5 to 3.5 kb. All these amplicons were sequenced. The sequencing data evidenced that all these amplicons belonged to desired products, as each amplicon overlapped one end of the according known DNA (Supplementary Fig. S1).

5. Discussion

There are two kinds of PCR-based walking methods: ligation-mediated PCR and random PCR. Ligation-mediated PCR, like single-specific-primer PCR ( 12 ) or vectorette PCR (the corresponding commercial kit is available at www.takarabio.com) ( 13 ), involves the pretreatment of genomic DNA. However, random PCR, like palindromic sequence-targeted PCR ( 14 ), partially overlapping primer-based PCR ( 15 ), or thermal asymmetric interlacing PCR (the corresponding commercial kit is available at www.takarabiomed.com.cn) ( 7 ), just relies on the random annealing of walking primer and is thus more convenient and cost-effective. At present, random PCR has been the mainstream walking method.

In this study, a new random PCR-based genome walking method ASP-PCR has been proposed. The primary ASP-PCR is only driven by HP. Therefore, only one type of undesired primary product defined by HP is produced in ASP-PCR, less than the three produced in most PCR methods ( 7 ). This nature of ASP-PCR is conducive to improving the specificity of genome walking. Moreover, the AP component of HP is used as a primer in the next PCR (s). Clearly, secondary/tertiary amplification of the undesired primary product is suppressed, because this product itself preferentially forms hairpin through the intra-strand annealing mediated by HP sites, rather than hybridizing with the shorter AP ( 13 ). This suppression effect further ensures the specificity of ASP-PCR.

The success of ASP-PCR is expectable. The relaxed cycle of primary ASP-PCR should make HP hybridize with a site (s) on unknown flank. The walking experiment is expected to succeed, provided that this hybridization occurs ( 9 ). In addition, the design of parallel experiments provides further assurance for the success.

The ASP-PCR, however, also has a potential disadvantage. As aforementioned, HP is a hybrid of AP and omSSP. Thus, this HP is rather long, generally more than 40 nt. As is well known, the longer a primer is, the greater the risk of producing primer dimer or hairpin may have.

6. Conclusion

A reliable and rapid genome walking technology ASP-PCR is proposed, which features using a HP as both SSP and walking primer in primary amplification. The ASP-PCR may have a potential use in life science-related areas.

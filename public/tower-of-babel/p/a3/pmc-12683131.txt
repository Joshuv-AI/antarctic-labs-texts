Reply letter: Pathologic complete response in TNBC: A shield against recurrence, but not the brain?
Authors: Gabriel Berlingieri Polho, Yumi Ricucci Shinkado, Leticia Kimie Murazawa, Vinicius Vitor Oliveira, Victor Rocha Pinheiro, Diana del Cisne Pineda Labanda, Romualdo Barroso-Sousa, Luciana Rodrigues Carvalho Barros, Laura Testa, Renata Colombo Bonadio
Published: 2025

We appreciate Altudang K.'s letter commenting on our recent publication regarding central nervous system (CNS) relapse in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients following neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT). The author suggests that our finding of an increased proportion of CNS relapses among patients with pathological complete response (pCR) may be a consequence of improved survival rates. To investigate this hypothesis, we performed a time-to-event analysis within the relapsed population, utilizing the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test. The median time to disease recurrence was similar between the pCR and non-pCR groups (16.4 vs. 13.0 months, p = 0.1). The median time to CNS recurrence in the pCR group was 10.3 months, indicating an early event. Moreover, as the author noted, the rate of CNS relapse was similar between groups (5.0 % vs. 8.8 %, p = 0.11), despite patients with pCR exhibiting lower overall recurrence rates. However, we would have anticipated a proportional decrease in CNS relapses alongside other sites of relapse. We agree with the author's comment that further prospective studies are necessary to evaluate risk assessment and translational analyses to enhance outcomes for this patient population.

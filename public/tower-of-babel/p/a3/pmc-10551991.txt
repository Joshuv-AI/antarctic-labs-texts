Reflections of nurses in the fight against COVID-19 in Hubei: a qualitative study
Authors: Zhi Xiaoxu, Yalou Pu, Xiaoli Li, Yun Zhao, Shuqin Zhu, Niu Niu, Jingfang Zuo, Aifeng Meng, Lichi Chiang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo explore the reflections and experiences of nurses who were deployed to the frontline to combat COVID-19 in Wuhan, China. In order to gain insights that can be useful in developing cultivating positive professional values and attitudes towards future public health emergencies.DesignQualitative study using semistructured interviews. Data were transcribed and analysed using the Colaizzi’s 7-step method. The study is reported in accordance with the consolidated Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research.SettingTelephonic interviews with nine participants who were deployed from Nanjing to Hubei Province for the fight against COVID-19.ParticipantsOf 11 volunteer nurses deployed to Hubei Province for the fight against COVID-19, two nurses did not finish the interview because of their working hours. The remaining nine were recruited through purposive sampling using the following criteria: nurses who were deployed at the first stage and those who verbally agreed and signed an informed consent form to participate in the study.ResultsThree thematic categories and subthemes that were identified from the analysis were as follows: (1) ‘Assertive attitude to fight against the pandemic’, included three subthemes: ‘Inner calling towards professional accountability for saving lives’, ‘Extrinsic support that facilitates the commitment to the nursing profession’ and ‘Holistic value ascribed to the nursing profession’. (2) ‘Challenges associated with the anti-pandemic mission’, included ‘overcoming challenges around the strict requirements for personal protection’ and ‘Fear and uncertainty over the rapid progression of the disease’. (3) ‘Unbearable heaviness and lightness of being a nurse’, with two subthemes: ‘the heavy crown of the anti-pandemic hero’ and ‘eternal reverence without regrets’.ConclusionsAs reflected by the nurses on frontline combating COVID-19, it was necessary to inculcate professional nursing values in them, as only their dedication and selflessness could improve humanity’s chances against the disease.

Background

In December 2019, the first case of COVID-19 was confirmed in Hubei Province, China. Since then, the pandemic has attracted global attention.1 The WHO provisionally named the novel coronavirus pneumonia as COVID-19 on 12 January 2020.2 COVID-19 mainly manifests as fever, accompanied by other symptoms, such as dry cough and fatigue. Some patients may suffer from dyspnoea, and severe cases may progress and present with acute respiratory distress syndrome, septic shock and coagulation disorders.3 4 Since COVID-19 occurred during the Chinese Spring Festival, a nationwide outbreak followed in a remarkably short period. Moreover, the virus was highly contagious and had non-specific initial symptom; thus the disease could progress rapidly5 and people with chronic underlying diseases experienced more severe effects than healthy people. The rapid escalation in the number of patients with COVID-19 requiring treatment in hospitals resulted in insufficient healthcare resources. Consequently, the pandemic brought tremendous pressure on the healthcare system in China, and the Hubei Province was reportedly the worst affected region.

China launched the national emergency response and initiated a range of programmes to alleviate the spread of COVID-19. In addition, redeploying healthcare resources to weak areas and medical institutions effectively reduced the impact of the outbreak on the healthcare system.6 The first medical teams arrived in Hubei on the Chinese New Year’s Eve. According to a press conference held by China’s Joint Prevention and Control Mechanism of the State Council on 7 April 2020, among 42 600 healthcare providers supporting Hubei Province, 28 600 nurses volunteered to join the fight, accounting for nearly 70% of the total workforce in the region. Also, the total number of nurses who went to Hubei greatly exceeded the number of local nurses. Of these, most of the participating nurses were young and had a family with young children, of these, 60% were in their 30s and 40s, while 40% were in their 20s.

The physical and mental health of nursing staff deserves more attention.7 This is in line with the traditional Chinese values indicating that ‘the world belongs to all’ and ‘when disaster strikes, help comes from all sides’. As the main force in the fight against the pandemic, nurses shouldered great responsibilities and overcame many difficulties. A previous study showed that nurses were more prone to psychological problems, such as anxiety and depression, and were more likely to suffer from post-traumatic stress disorder than other health professionals, such as technicians and nurses or technician assistants.8 Such psychological states persisted for weeks, and healthcare providers suffered from Adaptability and Post-traumatic Stress Disorder Symptoms.9 Since January 2020, the National Health Commission of China had issued guidelines providing extensive online mental health education for healthcare providers.10 However, some scholars believed that the effect of such psychological interventions in China was limited, and the organisational management of psychological intervention must be improved.11

The spread of the COVID-19 was rapid, resulting in a highly infected population facing mortality in several countries. The WHO and several non-governmental organisations announced ‘One World: Together at Home’ to support healthcare workers in the fight against the COVID-19 pandemic. The International Council of Nurses called on ‘nurses to stay at work, and let people stay at home’. Call on healthcare institutions all over the world should be united and support nurses to works as professionals in the frontline of fight against the pidemic. Most of the participating nurses were young and had a family with young children, of these, 60% were in their 30s and 40s, while 40% were in their 20s7; the physical and mental health of nursing staff deserves more attention. Meanwhile, the government call on people staying at home, against the spread of the pandemic.

Continuously, the evolving pandemic circumstances and uncertainty highlighted the importance of an organised national pandemic responding plan for the subsequent waves of COVID-19 and future pandemics. The redeployed nurses were recruited voluntarily and were fearless to work on the frontlines even though they knew that this new disease was highly contagious.6 Definitely, they would face much stress. It would be significant and meaningful to know their reflections on the responsibility. The result could be the references for future education and counselling of nurses on the meaning of being a nurse, to transit the core value of nurses to safe lives. The increase the insight into disaster preparation and reflective on practice. This study aimed to investigate the reflections of nurses who were deployed to fight against COVID-19 in Hubei, with the goal of providing references for designing effective psychological interventions. Additionally, the study aimed to guide and inspire nurses towards adopting positive professional values and life attitudes during future public health emergencies.

Methods

This was a descriptive qualitative study which used semistructured interviews. The study was reported in accordance with the consolidated criteria for reporting qualitative research (SRQR) guidelines to enhance trustworthiness and methodological rigour.12

Participants

This research used focal sampling method, which was referred to observing and recording the behaviour of an individual in a specific time.13 A total of 11 volunteer nurses from the hospital that the research group was located in Nanjing were deployed to Hubei Province in the fight against COVID-19 (table 1). Emergency infection control training was provided before departure to effectively participate in the antipandemic fight. All potential participants were given at least 24 hours to consider if they would like to participate or not. All the nurses were interviewed after they self-reported that they were fully rested. Two nurses did not finish the interview because of working hours. Nine nurses were included in this study using purposive sampling and they met the following criteria: (1) nurses who were deployed in the first stage; (2) nurses who verbally agreed and signed informed consent to participate in the study.

Study design

The interview guideline design was based on the purpose of this study, which was to explore the reflections of deployed nurses that fought against COVID-19 in Hubei. The interview guideline was developed based on a literature review and improved through pretests with two nursing professionals. In-depth semistructured interviews were conducted to understand the deliberations of frontline nurses regarding this mission. Before the interviews, a rapport with the interviewees was established. The interviewer, who was already trained in the interview techniques, encouraged the interviewees to fully express their inner feelings. Each interview lasted 20–30 min until data saturation was achieved, and no new information could be obtained.

Data collection

The first author conducted the telephone interviews. All interviews were audio recorded and transcribed verbatim. First, the purpose of this study was explained to the participants by the interviewer, and informed consent was obtained. The participants performed self-evaluations. If there was any psychological discomfort, such as fatigue, the interview was suspended or stopped. Second, the participants were interviewed only when they were emotionally stable and physically and mentally relaxed, with no external disturbance. Fortunately, no one suspended the interview due to discomfort.

Data analysis

We processed and analysed the data according to Colaizzi’s method of qualitative research.12 It included seven steps: (1) recordings were repeatedly listened to, transcribed and then carefully read; (2) important statements were extracted in line with the research objectives from the transcriptions, and then simplified and summarised; (3) important statements were coded; (4) similar meanings stated by the interviewees were categorised and themes were summarised; (5) accurate, credible and comprehensive descriptions of the themes were formulated; (6) summaries related to similar opinions were distinguished; (7) interview results were cross-validated by cycling the queried assumptions and beliefs with the interviewees. Strategies regarding the trustworthiness of a qualitative study include credibility, transferability, dependability and confirmability. During the interviews, the researcher often summarised and checked whether the perceptions of the interviewees had been correctly recorded. The reflections and experiences of the participants were explored by two researchers independently.

Patient and public involvement

The patients or public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research.

Results

In our study, three themes emerged from the analysis of the interviews: (1) an assertive attitude towards the fight against the pandemic. This theme described the principal experience of frontline nurses regarding their deployment during the antipandemic mission; (2) challenges of the antipandemic mission. This theme was concerned with the heavy workload placed on the nurses during the pandemic, including rigorous measures for personal protection and the rapid progression of the disease; and (3) unbearable heaviness and lightness of the burden of nurses, which was also related to the challenges of the fight against COVID-19 and its impact on the nurses’ well-being (table 2).

Themes and subthemes identified from the interviews

Theme 1: an assertive attitude towards the fight against the pandemic

All nine interviewed nurses said that saving lives was the responsibility of nurses; thus, they went without hesitation to the frontline in Hubei for the battle against the pandemic. Recruited nurses who volunteered to participate in this deployment embraced the mission with full devotion. They all derived an assertive attitude from the belief in an inner calling and the intervention of extrinsic support structures in the fight against the pandemic.

The inner calling necessitating professional accountability to save lives

Deployed nurses attributed their volunteering in the fight against the pandemic to an inner calling that made them bear the responsibility, especially in times of crisis, and believed that it was their professional responsibility to save lives.7 “I am a medical worker; this deployment is part of my professional duty.” (N4) They all showed a strong sense of responsibility and willingness to help patients in Hubei. Deployed nurses held the patriotic belief of “being ready to assemble at the first call and being capable of fighting and winning.” (N1) They took it as their responsibility to volunteer as frontline workers to treat COVID-19 patients and were proud of their relevance. “I can feel that we are united in the fight against the pandemic. We are safeguarding patients and their families.” (N1)

The extrinsic support facilitating commitment to the nursing profession

Despite the infectivity rate of the disease, the deployed nurses were deeply committed to their profession. At the same time, recognition from the nursing society and other sectors was accorded, which further strengthened their commitment to their profession.When nurses faced challenges on the frontline during the COVID-19 outbreak, extrinsic support structures such as the ‘gratitude of patients’, ‘strength of the team’, and ‘support from families and organisations’ played crucial roles in motivating them to continue fulfilling their professional commitments.

Gratitude of patients: “There was a 45-to-50-year-old patient who had relatively mild COVID-19 symptoms and finally recovered. When he was able to walk independently, he shook hands with me to convey gratitude. I felt that we were encouraging each other and deeply touched.” (N8) These positive gratitude of patients greatly supported their internal proud to be nurses working in the frontline of the crisis.

Strength of the team: “All the people fighting in the frontline are awesome; besides, I am proud to work with such great ‘comrades’” (N3) The strength of example from the team colleagues encouraged the belief of overcoming the fear.

Support from families and organisations: “I was deeply touched by the mutual support among our teammates.” (N7) This devotion came as a result of the full support received from their families and colleagues, which warmed them to help patients in the frontline.

Highlighting the value of the nursing profession

The participants expressed a high level of acknowledgement towards the professional value of nursing staff. “This serious pandemic had spread to the whole country and even the whole world.Nursing staff in the emergency rescue, critical patient treatment and other aspects of nursing technology reflects the high professional value.” (N1) The important professional value of nurses should not be ignored in the fight against COVID-19. The application of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) brought new hope for critically ill patients. However, for nurses, the more valuable issue was to use those instruments skillfully. “Nursing staff quickly organized learning and mastered the application of ECMO. Nursing colleagues quickly adapted the work pattern We worked in 4-hour shifts, and each shift need to provide professional care for patients treated with ECMO, that need 24-hour care.” (N4)

The value of the nursing profession was also reflected in helping patients build confidence in overcoming their diseases, and letting patients see the power of nurses. “Many patients had great psychological stress after being infected, but they felt safer and more confident to overcome the disease with our help.” (N4) The most important thing was that the nurses found that the deployment to Hubei demonstrated the value of the nursing profession for society. “Because of major public health events in Hubei, people understand the value of nurses in terms of stabilizing the demands of emergency care.” (N4)

Theme 2: challenges of the antipandemic mission

The novelty of COVID-19 and the nature of the combat against it necessitated that deployed nurses quickly adapted to the chaotic situation. They had to address the heavy workload amid the uncertainty induced by COVID-19 while instituting rigorous measures for personal protection and adaptability to the sudden change of conditions.

Overcoming the challenges concerning strict personal protection

Throughout the period of fighting against the COVID-19 epidemic, nurses are one of the most vulnerable groups. They need to carry out many necessary nursing procedures, and therefore they are the group that has the closest and longest contact with patients. Thus, it was essential for the nurses to use personal protection equipment. A new challenge for nurses was the use of protective suits which restricted their movements and affected their operating performance. “Protective clothing is airtight as an astronaut’s spacesuit. We needed to adapt to it to correctly walk or bend during treatment. Many almost collapsed after work.” (N4)

Immediate establishment and enforcement of standard procedures and regulations for personal protection are necessary to ensure individuals comply with safety measures to protect themselves. However, “In the frontline, I was also worried that the isolation and protection procedures were not enough.” (N5) This suggested that the strict self-protection increased the psychological pressure of the nurses.

Fear and uncertainty about the rapid progression of the disease

Nurses were alerted that the new disease did not only spread quickly, but also progressed rapidly. “The quick spread of COVID-19, as discussed by the mainstream media, terrified me, which made me feel overwhelmed.” (N7) The rapid spread and progression of COVID-19 posed great psychological stress to nurses working in Hubei. They feared both the deterioration of the patients’ health condition and the infections among comrades. “A case shocked me a lot. A middle-aged patient just told me that he had a little chest tightness 10 minutes prior, but it quickly progressed to spontaneous dyspnea.” (N4) Due to the peculiarity and urgency of their work, the frontline nurses suffered from fear and uncertainty, because of little understanding of this infectious disease at that time. “Our knowledge of COVID-19 and relevant clinical guidelines was gradually updated. Will the virus evolve too quickly and beyond scientists’ knowledge?” (N6)

Theme 3: the unbearable heaviness of the burden of nurses

Fighting COVID-19 was of utmost significance for nurses in Hubei, who withstood enormous physical and psychological stress. Facing overwhelming challenges in the fight against COVID-19 had glorified their lives. The mainstream media reported many heroic deeds, but it was their heavy burden that transformed them into heroes and uniquely motivated their growth. Influenced by traditional Chinese culture, the meaning of life and happiness of individuals are closely related to their contribution to society, hence providing support to nurses creates a strong sense of values.

The heavy crown of an antipandemic hero

The nurses felt very proud of themselves, given the huge responsibilities bestowed by the society. The experience of fighting against the pandemic has made the society recognise and support nurses as ‘anti-pandemic heroes.’ “We are all warriors in the fight against the COVID-19 pandemic. They admired my choices and decisions during this period in support of Hubei. People from all over the country supported us a lot.” (N9) However, during the working period in Hubei, the pandemic would demand of them enormous sacrifices of life and family. Although nurses have been praised as heroic figures in the fight against the pandemic, this recognition is a solemn and emotionally charged honour.

Eternal reverence without regrets

The choice of being a frontline nurse was analogous to eternal reverence without regrets because the nurses fulfilled their duties under heavy stress but also gained the glory for combatting the pandemic. The completion of the mission brought them the highest self-actualisation and they discovered the eternal value. “The nurses hard work gives me a strong motivation. At this moment, I finally realized that the responsibility of the nursing work has been passed on to me.I will take my social responsibilities and recording my passion without any regrets.” (N6) “I am proud to support Hubei, but once doubted whether I was qualified to do this job. With this experience, my courage and confidence have greatly improved. Now I can work independently without hesitation.” (N4). The process of searching for meaning in life also involves the process of searching for happiness in life.

Discussion

The aim of this study is to investigate the experiences and reflections of nurses who were deployed to the frontline in Wuhan to fight against COVID-19, The following themes emerged from the interviews: (1) an assertive attitude toward the fight against the pandemic; (2) challenges associated with the anti-pandemic mission; (3) unbearable heaviness and lightness of the burden of nurses. The significance of these findings lies in their potential to guide nurses towards adopting positive professional values, as they shed light on the values that are deeply embedded within the nursing profession. Further exploration of these values can lead to a better understanding of their importance, and enable nurses to embody them in their practice for the benefit of their patients.

At present, some journals have reported research results on the antipandemic work status of first-line nursing staff. The psychological changes of frontline nurses are still a research hotspot.14–17 This study aims to gain a comprehensive understanding of the experience and reflections of non-local frontline nurses working in Hubei Province during the COVID-19 outbreak. These nurses have bravely faced physical and psychological challenges and have grown through their experiences. Their insights hold significant importance in developing national emergency response strategies for public health crises, highlighting the need to organise rescue efforts, allocate health resources effectively and prioritise the mental health of medical personnel. Ultimately, the support nurses have gained a profound sense of professional pride and respect for life through their dedicated efforts.

Research results show that the nurses supporting Wuhan exhibited the following characteristics.

The study participants consisted of 5 individuals aged 20 to 30, 2 individuals aged 30 to 40, and 1 individual aged 40 and above. The age of the respondents in the study was in the range of 20 to 40, which was consistent with the age structure of the general situation. The interviewed nurses unanimously expressed their unwavering patriotism, dedication, fearlessness and wholehearted support for nursing work in Hubei. They signified strong professional accountability and commitment. The passion was closely related to the growing emphasis on humanistic care by China’s nursing education in recent years, and of patriotism to the Confucian thought that states ‘Individuals should prioritise upholding their country’s interests’. 14 The aspirations of young nurses in China imbue hope and pride in their respective healthcare systems.

According to the study participants, it took less than 24 hours from assembly to response in Hubei. Making decisions so quickly and reaching the frontline in such a short time revealed the strict discipline and diligence of Chinese nurses. The nurses received support from all sides. The external resources, such as family members, the social understanding, also had a positive impact on the nurses’ reflection of their professional values. The nurses drew their motivation not only from external sources but also through introspection on the fundamental value of their profession-saving lives. Despite experiencing recurrent stresses and carrying a heavy burden, they were able to recognise and appreciate the irreplaceable role of nursing in the fight against the pandemic.

Although nurses deployed to Hubei were mentally and technically prepared before departure, most inevitably experienced fear and panic when directly facing frontline care. Precious research found that 63.1% of the deployed nurses experienced acute traumatic stress disorder due to the particularity and urgency of their work, which also illustrated the severity of the pandemic.18 The nurses responded it was imperative to meet the challenges regardless of the risk to well-being. This shows that psychological resilience among the deployed nurses was high. Recent research suggests that individuals with greater psychological resilience are better equipped to manage stress, as they possess stronger mental fortitude. This highlights the importance of psychologists training nurses in stress management techniques.19 20 Interviewees indicated work stress related to the use of personal protective equipment, changes in the working environment, coping with high work intensity, insufficient knowledge concerning COVID-19 and constant fear of the shortage of medical supplies. Also, the deployed nurses were required to learn and coordinate in the shortest time.

In summary, during public emergencies, social coordination and support are crucial. The medical department should apply targeted measures while the government prioritises the provision of necessary materials, including quality protective equipment to the most affected locations. Additionally, the government can mobilise social forces and appeal to the public to participate in supporting the affected areas through various media platforms. These collaborative efforts are vital for alleviating the effects of public emergencies and promoting social cohesion. Nursing administrators are expected to arrange the nurses’ shifts reasonably; they arrange rich teachers or senior nurses to work together, in order to avoid excessive fatigue. Hospitals are advised to maintain a pool of personnel who possess the necessary skills and expertise in rendering care to infectious disease patients and regularly organise training and guidance to improve emergency response capabilities.

The valuable reflections depicted in this research revealed that the interviewees experienced eternal reverence of life and affirmed the nursing professional values. At the core of Nietzschean philosophy lies the concept of eternal recurrence, which entails an unwavering sense of accountability arising from the acceptance of the outcomes, both favourable and unfavourable, of one’s deliberate choices. The action made them worthy of the title ‘anti-pandemic heroes’. The actions of nurses who bravely rushed to the frontline of the antipandemic fight perfectly explained this unique perspective ‘when disaster strikes, help comes from all sides’, which is deeply rooted in the traditional Chinese culture. Happiness in life encompasses spiritual enrichment and continuous self-transcendence based on basic material satisfaction.21 22 For nurses who are on the front lines of battling public health emergencies, happiness in life is intricately linked with the happiness of their families as well as the country. Nurses' state of tireless efforts sets a great example for future generations and helps them to acquire valuable experience.23 24 Furthermore, dedication involves being committed to one’s work and experiencing a sense of significance, passion, motivation, accomplishment and growth.25 26 According to several related research reports, although frontline nurses are concerned about the risk of infection, they are not afraid of caring for infected patients.27 Their strong sense of responsibility is rooted in spiritual values from the moment they decided to become a nurse.28 According to our research, the professionalism of frontline nurses is a crucial driving force that propels them to fight against epidemics. Many studies highlight that an effective management mechanism can enhance professional honour and promote a sense of life significance.29

During the antiepidemic period, frontline nurses faced numerous challenges and had to exert themselves to the fullest to save lives. This pressure elevated them to the status of heroes who save patients' lives. Providing care for highly infectious patients while weighed down by cumbersome protective gear can be physically and psychologically demanding, but this experience enlightened them on the invaluable contribution they make as professional nurses. Studies have shown that supportive nurses have gained a profound understanding of life, and their heroic actions amidst COVID-19 will continue to bear eternal returns despite the brevity of life.30

Limitations

The study has some limitations. The short interviews were conducted over the phone, which may have limited the degree to which participants felt able to express themselves. Also, given the fluctuating nature of the pandemic, the attitudes of health and social care professionals may change over time. This can be difficult to capture during a single interview. Lastly, participants were all deployed from a single hospital and only nine nurses were interviewed. The findings may not be representative of nurses in other hospitals or provinces or countries.

Conclusion

This study provides an understanding of the reflections and experiences of frontline nurses combating COVID-19. The participants shared their experiences.The paper analysed the challenges faced by support staff during public health emergencies, as well as the psychological and physical health factors that impact them, by examining the experiences and reflections of frontline nursing staff. Obtaining this information can assist society, government and medical institutions in formulating accurate policies and programmes to effectively respond to emergencies. We found that, during the support of the antipandemic work, positive and negative emotions of frontline nurses against the pandemic interweave and coexist. The nurses who played an important role in combatting the pandemic not only recognised the significance of their profession, but also gained a deeper understanding of the purpose of life, ultimately shaping their future in a profound way. It is necessary to continuously pay attention to the challenges and pressures faced by nursing staff, which can help guide nursing education, government and hospital management, and health policy decisions.

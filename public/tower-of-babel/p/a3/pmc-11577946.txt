Response to Letter to the Editor on “Causal relationships between immune cells and erectile dysfunction based on Mendelian randomization”
Authors: Junhao Chen, Haifeng Wang, Shi Fu
Published: 2024

To the Editor,

We are delighted to see that our article has been recognized and has sparked important discussions.1 To further promote this dialogue, we will address the points raised, one by one. First, regarding the suggestion to incorporate a new database for a meta-analysis, this is indeed a valuable suggestion. However, integrating the FinnGen database into a meta-analysis would not be entirely rigorous. For instance, the GCST90044277 database, which was mentioned by the authors, is based on the GRCh37 genome version, whereas FinnGen uses the GRCh38 version. Merging GWAS data based on GRCh37 with FinnGen data in GRCh38 would introduce numerous inconsistencies, which would affect the accuracy of the results. The GRCh38 version provides updates and corrections to certain regions of the human genome compared to GRCh37, especially in complex areas such as telomeres, centromeres, olfactory receptor gene clusters, and immune-related genes. Failing to standardize the genome versions would lead to discrepancies in SNP positions, loss of genetic variants, or misannotations, which would, in turn, impact the final analysis. Converting GRCh38 data back to GRCh37 could result in data loss or inconsistencies, especially in SNP annotation and changes in genomic coordinates. Some SNPs that are newly identified or reannotated in GRCh38 may not have equivalent coordinates or annotations in GRCh37, meaning that useful information could be lost if data were forcibly converted, thus excluding certain important genetic markers related to erectile dysfunction (ED) from the analysis. Moreover, although the authors suggest increasing the sample size by adding new databases, in Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis, the accuracy of the study depends more on selecting valid SNPs that meet the threshold rather than simply increasing the sample size. Increasing the sample size does not inherently guarantee the quality of the analysis. Additionally, our use of Bayesian-weighted MR analysis provides a means of further strengthening the validation of our results from another perspective. Second, regarding the False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction, we selected immune cells with a strict P-value threshold of less than .01 for our analysis. This strict threshold means that only strong causal associations were included, greatly reducing the likelihood of false positives. Therefore, we believe that FDR correction is unnecessary in this case. The purpose of FDR correction is to mitigate the risk of false positives in the context of multiple hypothesis testing; however, since we have already selected only immune cells with significant associations through a stringent P-value filter, the impact of FDR correction on our results would be minimal. Even if FDR correction were applied, the P-values would not exceed .05. Third, the authors suggest using coloc colocalization analysis to validate our findings. While coloc analysis can confirm whether different traits share the same genetic variant signal, its relevance in our study is limited. First, we have already selected immune cell types significantly associated with ED using a strict P-value threshold (P < 0.01), ensuring that only the most statistically significant causal relationships were retained. Additionally, our study not only employed traditional MR methods but also incorporated Bayesian-weighted MR (BWMR) analysis, which is more adept at handling complex causal relationships, especially in multivariable contexts. Through BWMR, we have already validated the robustness of our results from different angles. The primary aim of our study is to elucidate the causal relationship between individual immune cells and ED, which does not fully align with the purpose of coloc analysis. Therefore, we believe that the application of coloc analysis would not significantly enhance the interpretability of our findings in this context.

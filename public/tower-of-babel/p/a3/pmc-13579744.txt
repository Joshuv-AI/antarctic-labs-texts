Diagnostic significance of HRCT imaging features in idiopathic inflammatory myopathy-associated interstitial lung disease
Authors: Qian-yun Zhang, Cai-Yu Li, Chao-chao Wang, Dong-dong Zhang, Hong-yan Shao, Ying Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundConnective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (CTD-ILD) is an important factor leading to a poor prognosis in connective tissue disease (CTD) patients. To analyze the clinical and imaging data of CTD-ILD patients, summarize their characteristics, and explore the diagnostic value of certain specific High Resolution Computed Tomography (HRCT) manifestations and their distribution patterns for idiopathic inflammatory myopathy-associated interstitial lung disease (IIM-ILD).MethodsThis study conducted a retrospective analysis of demographic data and HRCT findings from 384 patients with CTD-ILD. Using multivariable logistic regression, hierarchical analysis, and sensitivity analysis to compare data from the IIM-ILD group and other connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (OCTD-ILD) group, we summarized the imaging features and distribution of IIM-ILD, providing imaging clues for its recognition.ResultsThe distribution of different imaging features differed between the IIM-ILD patients and the OCTD-ILD patients. This study found that nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP) located in the upper lung field, as well as organized pneumonia (OP) located in the middle and lower lung fields, are relatively significant characteristics that distinguish idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIM) from other connective tissue disease (OCTD) (the odds ratios (OR) were 2.51, 5.46, and 8.33, respectively, P < 0.001). If both NSIP in the upper lung field and OP in the lower lung field are present, diagnostic performance in differentiating IIM from OCTD will be further enhanced (sensitivity 55.90%, specificity 94.18%, accuracy 74.74%, OR 20.51, P < 0.001).ConclusionsThis study indicated that for patients with CTD-ILD, specific HRCT manifestations could be used to distinguish IIM-ILD from OCTD-ILD, thereby assisting in clinical diagnosis. The coexistence of NSIP in the upper lung fields and OP in the lower lung fields would suggest a higher possibility of IIM-ILD.Keywords: Idiopathic inflammatory myopathy, Interstitial lung disease, HRCT, Pathological type, Diagnosis

Background

IIM constitute a group of autoimmune disorders characterized by chronic muscle inflammation and weakness [1, 2]. Based on clinical manifestations and pathological features, IIM can be classified into several types, including dermatomyositis (DM), polymyositis (PM), inclusion body myositis (IBM), anti-synthetase syndrome (ASS), and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM), etc. Common features encompass muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase (CK) levels, and electromyographic abnormalities, with potential multi-system involvement (e.g., pulmonary, cutaneous, articular) [1, 2]. Interstitial lung disease (ILD), occurring in 30%−40% of IIM patients (most frequently dermatomyositis-associated interstitial lung disease (DM-ILD)), significantly impacts prognosis. Actually, IIM-ILD may manifest at any stage of the IIM course, even before myopathic symptoms. The pathogenesis of IIM-ILD remains incompletely understood and may be related to autoimmune dysregulation, genetic predisposition, and environmental factors, etc [3]. HRCT is the preferred imaging method for evaluating ILD. It can clearly display the morphology, distribution, and extent of lung lesions, thereby facilitating early diagnosis, assessment, and management of IIM-ILD [4–6]. IIM-ILD exhibits diverse histopathologic patterns, including NSIP, usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP), OP, diffuse alveolar damage (DAD), and diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (DAH), etc [7]. NSIP is the most common pattern, accounting for about 50% −70%, and has a relatively good prognosis compared to UIP. DAD and DAH usually have an acute onset, a dangerous condition, and a high mortality rate.

The HRCT manifestations of IIM-ILD are closely related to its pathological pattern. Ground-glass opacity (GGO) is the most common HRCT imaging feature and is mainly associated with NSIP and OP. Reticular opacity is usually seen in NSIP and UIP. Traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing suggest pulmonary fibrosis and are more common in UIP. Consolidation can be more frequently detected in OP, DAH, and DAD. Honeycombing, which suggests advanced pulmonary fibrosis, is more common in UIP [4–6, 8].

OCTD, including systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA) associated vasculitis (AAV), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), Sjogren's syndrome (SS), and systemic sclerosis (SSc), also frequently causes ILD [9–11]. The clinical and imaging manifestations of IIM-ILD often overlap with those of OCTD-ILD. Previous studies have indicated that IIM-ILD, especially anti-synthetase syndrome-associated ILD(ASS-ILD), often present an overlapping pattern of NSIP and OP [12, 13]. However, there is still a lack of systematic research on whether the distribution characteristics of different imaging patterns in different lung fields have additional diagnostic value. This study retrospectively analyzed the clinical and imaging data of a group of patients with ILD (including IIM-ILD and OCTD-ILD), summarized their clinical and imaging characteristics, and evaluated the diagnostic value of certain HRCT features and their distribution patterns in distinguish IIM-ILD from OCTD-ILD.

Materials and methods

Materials

We retrospectively analyzed 384 CTD patients who underwent HRCT at the Second Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (January 2023–January 2025). Among them, 195 cases were diagnosed with IIM, including 118 cases of DM, 50 cases of PM, and 27 cases of ASS. 189 cases were diagnosed with OCTD, including 47 cases of SLE, 47 cases of RA, 38 cases of AAV, 20 cases of SS, 28 cases of SSc, and 9 cases of undifferentiated connective tissue disease (UCTD) (Table 1).

Methods

Study design

The clinical characteristics and chest HRCT images of 384 patients were analyzed retrospectively. The clinical characteristics included gender, age, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, oxygenation index, and autoimmune serological indicators (ANCA, ANA, SS-A, SS-B, Jo-1, Ro-52, and PCNA, etc.). The HRCT findings of ILD researched in our study included GGO, reticular opacity, traction bronchiectasis, consolidation, and honeycombing, etc. The imaging categories in this study referred to HRCT-based radiologic pattern classifications rather than histopathologic diagnoses. On the basis of their distribution in the lung parenchyma, the findings were classified as follows [16]: in the craniocaudal axis, the lung was divided into upper third (the upper lung field, from the lung apex to the level of the aortic arch), middle third (the middle lung field, from the aortic arch to 2 cm below the carina), and lower third (the lower lung field, from 2 cm below the carina to the costophrenic sulci).

Statistical analysis

R 4.3.0 software was used for statistical analysis. Categorical data were expressed as frequency or rate (%), and comparisons between groups were made using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test (when the expected frequency was < 5). Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), comparisons between two groups were performed using the independent-samples t-test, and comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA).

A logistic regression model was used for the main analysis to evaluate the association between HRCT features and IIM-ILD. First, crude odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated from 2 × 2 contingency tables to reflect the strength of univariate associations, and significance was assessed by Fisher's exact test. The adjustment model was further constructed to correct for potential confounders such as age, gender, and smoking history, and to calculate the adjusted OR and 95% CI (based on the Wald statistic, OR = eβ). The independent variable collinearity was evaluated by the variance inflation factor (VIF), and VIF < 5 suggested that the collinearity was acceptable. To evaluate the discriminative ability of the model, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy, and the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and 95% CI were calculated (Table 2).

At the same time, to explore the differences in the differential efficacy of imaging features among different CTD subtypes, we conducted a hierarchical analysis. The diagnostic classification between IIM-ILD and each OCTD subtype (SSc-ILD, RA-ILD, etc., excluding UCTD) was still compared using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test, and multiple comparisons were corrected by the Holm-Bonferroni method. To verify that the main conclusion was not affected by confounding from specific OCTD subtypes, a sensitivity analysis using the stepwise exclusion method was performed. After excluding a single subgroup or combining multiple subgroups, the OR for the association between IIM-ILD and the remaining OCTD group was re-estimated. If the OR changed by < 20% and remained statistically significant (P < 0.05), the results were considered robust to the exclusion of this subgroup.

All tests were two-tailed, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Data

Imaging protocol: Toshiba 64-slice CT (120 kV, 250–450 mA; 1.0 mm slice thickness). Radiologic data: We have provided partial raw data in the supplementary file, and certain data cannot be provided to protect the patients’ privacy. The datasets used and/or analyzed during the current study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. The image data of this study were read by two senior radiologists and one senior physician of respiratory medicine. The HRCT findings of all cases were analyzed. In case of disagreement or dispute, a third senior radiologist was asked for consultation and discussion.

Results

HRCT findings

The imaging features of 384 patients with CTD included in this study who underwent HRCT examination were diverse, including NSIP (Fig. 1), OP (Fig. 2), UIP (Fig. 3), DAD/DAH (Fig. 4), nonspecific interstitial changes (such as fibrotic nodules and fibrotic strands) (Fig. 5), and other manifestations (such as emphysema, bronchiolitis, bronchiectasis, and pleural effusion). The distribution of different imaging features in the IIM group and the OCTD group is shown in Table 3. Furthermore, NSIP distribution in the upper lung field and OP distribution in the middle and lower lung fields were significant HRCT imaging features that distinguished IIM from OCTD (the OR were 2.51, 5.46, and 8.33, respectively, and the p-value of each chi-square test was < 0.001). When both NSIP in the upper lung field and OP in the lower lung field were present (upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern, Fig. 6), the diagnostic performance in distinguishing IIM from OCTD could be further improved (the sensitivity was 55.90%, the specificity was 94.18%, the accuracy was 74.74%, the odds ratio was 20.51, and chi-square test P < 0.001, Table 4). As our study showed, in IIM, the upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern was more common in ASS cases (Table 5), and further research is needed to determine its clinical significance.

The results of Multivariate logistic regression

Significant independent associations were also observed for upper NSIP alone (adjusted OR = 2.55, 95% CI: 1.68—3.88, P < 0.001) and lower OP alone (adjusted OR = 8.53, 95% CI: 5.29—14.10, P < 0.001).

Evaluation of diagnostic efficacy

The ROC curve analysis revealed that the AUC of the upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern was 0.750 (95% CI: 0.712–0.789), which was significantly superior to that of the upper NSIP alone (0.612, 95% CI: 0.564–0.661) or the lower OP alone (0.728, 95% CI: 0.685–0.771) (Fig. 7).

Hierarchical analysis: The discriminatory efficacy of the OCTD subgroups

The hierarchical analysis revealed that the upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern demonstrated significant discriminatory efficacy across all OCTD subtypes (Table 7). After correction using the Holm-Bonferroni method, it showed the strongest discriminatory power against RA-ILD (OR = 28.23, 95% CI: 7.03–245.39, corrected P < 0.001), followed by SS-ILD (OR = 23.83), AAV-ILD (OR = 22.59), and SLE-ILD (OR = 18.41). The discriminatory efficacy against SSc-ILD was relatively lower but nonetheless strong (OR = 16.32, 95% CI: 3.90–145.62, P < 0.001), suggesting that approximately 7.1% of SSc-ILD patients could present this imaging pattern. Clinicians should pay special attention in differential diagnosis. The specificity of all subgroups was > 92%, confirming that this feature serves as a highly reliable predictor for IIM-ILD.

Sensitivity analysis: Verification using the stepwise elimination method

The sensitivity analysis using the "leave-one-out" method confirmed the stability of the predictive effect of the upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern on IIM-ILD. The main conclusion was that the results were insensitive to the presence or absence of each OCTD subtype (Table 8). Using the full sample as the baseline (OR = 21.35, 95% CI: 10.49–48.20), after excluding SSc-ILD alone, the OR was 22.62 (95% CI: 10.40–56.40), with a change of + 5.9%; after excluding RA-ILD alone, the OR was 19.64 (95% CI: 8.99–49.10), with a change of –8.0%; after excluding SS-ILD alone, the OR was 21.06 (95% CI: 10.04–49.83), with a change of –1.4%. Furthermore, using the "strict exclusion strategy", only SLE-ILD and AAV-ILD were retained as controls, and the OR was 20.09 (95% CI: 7.77–66.37); when only AAV-ILD (the most complex imaging subgroup) was retained as the sole control, the OR was still as high as 22.59 (95% CI: 5.55–198.39). The change in OR for all exclusion strategies was < 10% (well below the 20% threshold), and the 95% CI did not cross 1.0. All P values were < 0.001, confirming that the diagnostic efficacy of the upper NSIP/lower OP overlap pattern is not dependent on the presence of any specific OCTD subtype, and has high robustness and generalizability.

Discussion

With advances in our understanding of disease mechanisms and diagnostic techniques, ILD is being diagnosed with increasing frequency, accounting for approximately 15% of hospitalizations for respiratory diseases. CTD-ILD is one of the most common pulmonary manifestations of CTD, with CTD reported to account for 15%–20% of ILD cases [17]. The pathogenesis of CTD-ILD remains complex and may involve the abundant connective tissue, vascular structures, and immune components present in the lungs and pleura [18]. Factors such as genetics, environment, and medication/toxic exposure history are common risk factors for CTD-ILD. According to relevant literature, the prevalence of CTD-ILD in patients with CTD varies due to differences in classification criteria, diagnostic methods, and study designs; overall, the prevalence is higher in SSc and IIM. Studies have shown that the prevalence of ILD among different CTDs is as follows: approximately 58% in RA (RA-ILD), 13% in SLE (SLE-ILD), 27% in SS (SS-ILD), up to 80% in DM/PM (DM-ILD and PM-ILD), 91% in SSc (SSc-ILD), and 67% in MCTD (MCTD-ILD) [11]. CTD-ILD is also a high-morbidity, high-mortality disease that seriously threatens patients' lives, and is one of the leading causes of death during the acute phase of CTD. ILD associated with different CTDs differs significantly [19] and can either be the initial manifestation of CTDs or a long-term complication. Therefore, early identification and diagnosis are particularly important for the management of CTD-ILD. Imaging examination is a relatively convenient and sensitive method for early identification and diagnosis of CTD-ILD. Specific HRCT manifestations and their distribution patterns are of great value for the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of various types of CTD-ILD.

IIM, a type of CTD, is a group of heterogeneous autoimmune diseases characterized by chronic muscle inflammation and/or skin signs. Clinically, it can be divided into five subtypes: DM, PM, IBM, ASS, and IMNM, among which DM and ASS are more common [20]. IIM can manifest with multi-system involvement, among these manifestations IIM-ILD is an important cause of disease worsening and death in IIM patients [21, 22]. HRCT is an important tool for the screening and diagnosis of IIM-ILD. The lesions of IIM-ILD on HRCT are diverse, including GGO, consolidation, reticular shadows, honeycomb shadows, and traction bronchiectasis, among other findings [23]. The main characteristics of the lesions determine the imaging classification of ILD and are closely related to the prognosis. At present, there are many studies on ASS-ILD, and the prognosis of patients with different types of ASS-ILD varies. Most studies suggest that NSIP accounts for the largest proportion among patients with ASS-ILD; most of these patients have improved or stable outcomes, whereas those with worsening conditions may progress to the fibrotic phase. NSIP is divided into three types: cellular type, fibrotic type, and mixed type. On HRCT, cellular NSIP manifests as ground-glass opacities, which show a favorable response to anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive therapy. Fibrotic NSIP on HRCT demonstrates reticular opacities or traction bronchiectasis. This type responds poorly to immunotherapy, suggesting that anti-fibrotic therapy should be considered. Therefore, for NSIP, different HRCT manifestations are helpful for assessing disease activity and guiding clinical treatment. Debray et al. reported that among the 12 patients with ILD who showed consolidation on imaging, 10 (83.0%) improved clinically [24]. Most of these patients initially presented with HRCT findings of OP or NSIP-OP in the lungs. However, UIP may be associated with a poorer prognosis. The prognosis of NSIP or OP patients is better than that of UIP patients. Some studies have shown that the UIP pattern is an independent risk factor for a poor prognosis in ASS-ILD [25]. The volume proportions of HRCT reticular shadows and ground-glass shadows are predictive factors for the progression of pulmonary fibrosis in ASS-ILD [26, 27]. Some studies have also indicated that in patients with suspected IIM-ILD, if HRCT findings show a combination of NSIP and OP, ASS should be considered as a differential diagnosis [20, 24, 28]. This study also revealed similar findings. Furthermore, summarizing the imaging features and classification of IIM-ILD can assist clinical diagnosis.

The major novelty of this study lies in summarizing the distribution patterns of various HRCT imaging features in CTD patients, thereby providing valuable radiological diagnostic clues for differentiating IIM-ILD from OCTD-ILD.

This study found that NSIP located in the upper lung field, as well as OP located in the middle and lower lung fields, is a relatively specific characteristic distinguishing IIM from OCTD (the odds ratios (OR) were 2.51, 5.46, and 8.33, corresponding to upper NSIP, middle OP, and lower OP, respectively, and the chi-square tests all showed P < 0.001). If both NSIP in the upper lung field and OP in the lower lung field are present, the diagnostic efficacy for differentiating IIM from OCTD will be further enhanced (sensitivity 55.90%, specificity 94.18%, accuracy 74.74%, odds ratio (OR) = 20.51, P < 0.001). This conclusion is in line with the results of most other clinical studies [20, 24, 28]. The advantage of this study lies in the fact that, in addition to the coexistence of NSIP and OP, the combination pattern of NSIP in the upper lung field and OP in the lower lung field provides valuable imaging clues for the identification of IIM-ILD.

However, this study also has some limitations. Firstly, no further research has been conducted on the exact pathological and physiological mechanisms underlying these imaging features. Although the mechanisms contributing to ILD in the context of CTDs are not known in detail, the prevailing hypothesis suggests the involvement of abnormal inflammation and subsequent exaggerated fibrotic reactions [29, 30]. Abnormal immune system responses have traditionally been instrumental in its pathophysiology, and various changes in immune cells have been described, especially in macrophages [31]. Secondly, this is a retrospective single-center study, and the lack of validation limits the generalizability of our findings. This study has preliminarily identified that the combined pattern of upper NSIP and lower OP can provide imaging diagnostic clues for differentiating IIM-ILD. In future research, prospective multi-center validation studies and external validation cohorts should be conducted to enhance the generalizability and reproducibility of the diagnostic model proposed in this study. Thirdly, because the original image review was conducted as a consensus-based clinical reassessment and was not designed for formal reproducibility testing, interobserver agreement statistics were unavailable. In addition, there has been no further investigation into the relationship of imaging features to clinical manifestations and prognosis. This will be the focus of further research. In future studies, survival analysis should be conducted, and predictive models should be constructed to further explore the relationship among imaging features, pathological findings, and prognosis.

Conclusion

This study indicated that for patients with CTD-ILD, specific HRCT manifestations could be used to distinguish IIM-ILD from OCTD-ILD, thereby providing imaging clues for recognizing IIM-ILD. The coexistence of NSIP in the upper lung fields and OP in the lower lung fields would suggest a higher likelihood of IIM-ILD.

A modified near-field target localization method based on vector diagonal loading
Authors: Qing Ji, Dawei Xiao, Lulu Du, Taotao Xie, Yandong Pang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Addressing the issue of poor positioning performance of the near-field MVDR (Minimum Variance Distance Reciprocal) algorithm due to limited sampling data, this study proposes a combination of vector hydrophones and an improved angle loading technique to enhance the robustness of the algorithm. The reasons for the degradation of positioning performance of the MVDR algorithm with a small number of snapshots are analyzed from two perspectives: optimal weight vector and output power spectrum. Firstly, the traditional angle loading algorithm is utilized to reduce the degree of noise feature diffusion, and then the loaded feature values are squared to obtain an improved covariance matrix, effectively resolving the issue of resolution degradation associated with traditional angle loading methods. Finally, the effectiveness and stability of the algorithm are verified through simulations and experimental data. The results demonstrate that the improved angle loading algorithm proposed in this paper performs comparably to traditional methods but significantly enhances resolution, making it more suitable for the positioning of underwater acoustic near-field targets.

Introduction

At present, nations worldwide are actively advancing the construction of anti-submarine detection capabilities to counteract information asymmetry in underwater confrontations. Underwater Unmanned Vehicles (UUVs) have become a critical piece of military equipment for many countries, characterized by their small size, flexible mobility, and low self-noise. They are often capable of conducting close reconnaissance and achieving regional threat neutralization missions. At present, the detection and early warning of small targets such as UUVs mainly rely on active acoustic detection methods1,2, while the research on passive acoustic detection of UUVs is relatively scarce. The reason for this is that the target radiated noise signal of underwater vehicles is weak3, the marine environment is complex, and the number of effective snapshots obtained by underwater fixed detection systems is small with a low signal-to-noise ratio, which greatly reduces the performance of conventional positioning methods4,5. Therefore, for fixed underwater detection platforms, enhancing the ability to locate near-field targets with limited snapshots is particularly crucial.

The manuscript addresses the challenge of improving positioning accuracy for near-field targets under conditions of limited snapshot data, a common scenario in underwater detection systems. The proposed method aims to bolster the detection capabilities of underwater detection platforms by enhancing their capacity to accurately pinpoint targets in proximity, despite the constraints imposed by the underwater environment and the limitations of the detection systems themselves.

The Minimum Variance Distortionless Response (MVDR) beamforming algorithm, proposed by Capon in 1969, is a high-resolution and easily implementable spatial spectral method6. Focused MVDR beamforming is designed for noise source localization of cooperative near-field targets and subsequently achieves noise reduction, representing an extension of MVDR for near-field applications7,8. However, focused beamforming is intended for cooperative targets and searches within a plane close to the target or at a certain depth. The MVDR algorithm requires the computation of the inverse of the covariance matrix; when the number of snapshots is limited or the signal-to-noise ratio is low, the sample covariance matrix estimate is inaccurate, or even ill-conditioned, leading to significant errors in the inversion process. Reed has studied the relationship between the average signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) of MVDR output and the number of snapshots and array elements, providing a mathematical expression9. Kelly also proposed that when there is insufficient effective data, the side lobes of the beam will increase7. Carlson first introduced the fixed angle loading technique to correct the covariance matrix, enhancing the stability of the MVDR algorithm11. Subsequently, improved algorithms such as the Robust Adaptive Beamforming (RCB) and the loading quantity iterative algorithm, have been proposed12,13.

The algorithms mentioned are all designed to determine the angle loading quantities based on certain optimal principles to enhance the robustness of the MVDR (Minimum Variance Distortionless Response) algorithm under conditions of limited snapshots. The angle loading technique involves adding white noise to the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix, which generally increases the noise feature values and reduces the difference between signal and noise feature values. If the loading quantity is too large, it can weaken the high-resolution advantage of MVDR and may even revert to conventional beamforming14. This paper addresses the issue of reduced resolution caused by angle loading techniques in MVDR algorithms and proposes an improved angle loading algorithm that ensures robustness under low snapshot conditions without sacrificing the high resolution of MVDR.

Vector hydrophones are composite sensors of sound pressure and particle velocity, capable of simultaneously measuring underwater sound pressure and particle velocity signals. Under the same array element conditions, compared to traditional sound pressure hydrophones, they increase the types and quantities of target information. They can suppress isotropic noise, and most importantly, they can resolve the left–right ambiguity issue of sound pressure arrays, offering unique positioning advantages15,17. This paper focuses on the positioning problem of near-field targets in fixed underwater detection platforms, using vector hydrophones as a new type of receiving sensor, and employing the improved angle loading MVDR algorithm to enhance the near-field sound source positioning capability under limited snapshots, providing theoretical support for improving the survival capability of underwater fixed detection platforms.

Near field vector MVDR beamforming

When the target is in the near-field region of the array, the plane wave model is no longer applicable, as the signal wavefront is now spherical. The near-field vector model requires consideration of amplitude attenuation, phase delay, and complex acoustic impedance of sound pressure and particle velocity at each array element, in contrast to the far-field scalar model. The establishment of a proper coordinate system is crucial for accurately modeling the acoustic field and for the subsequent processing and analysis using the MVDR algorithm.

Near-field beamforming essentially involves searching for a set of weights that includes spatial point azimuth, elevation, and range information. By compensating for time delays and summing the received signals from the array elements, a spatial spectrum is obtained across the entire search space. The peak coordinates of the spatial spectrum can serve as estimates for the azimuth and range of the target signal.

Improved diagonal loading algorithm

The principle of diagonal loading

Diagonal loading technology involves adding a loading value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi$$\end{document} to the diagonal elements of the covariance matrix to effectively whiten the noise eigenvalues, reducing their spread, which enhances the robustness of the algorithm. After diagonal loading, can obtain:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{R}}_{DL} = {\mathbf{R}} + \xi {\mathbf{I}} = {\mathbf{\rm E}}({{\varvec{\Lambda}}} + \xi {\mathbf{I}}){\mathbf{E}}^{H} { = }{\mathbf{{\rm E}\Lambda }}_{DL} {\mathbf{E}}^{H}$$\end{document}

(10), the new weight vector after diagonal loading is derived:13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} & {\mathbf{w}}_{DL} (\theta ,\alpha ,r) = ({\mathbf{I}} - \sum\limits_{m = 1}^{N} {\frac{{\lambda_{m} - \lambda_{\min } }}{{\lambda_{m} + \xi }}{\mathbf{e}}_{m} {\mathbf{e}}^{H}_{m} } \\ & \quad - \sum\limits_{m = N + 1}^{4M} {\frac{{\lambda_{m} - \lambda_{\min } }}{{\lambda_{m} + \xi }}{\mathbf{e}}_{m} {\mathbf{e}}^{H}_{m} } ){\mathbf{c}}(\theta ,\alpha ,r) \\ \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Improvement algorithm

Based on the analysis from the previous section, it is evident that the fluctuation of noise eigenvalues determines the fluctuation of sidelobes, which in turn affects the probability and height of false peaks. The difference between signal eigenvalues and noise eigenvalues determines the sharpness of the spectral peak. After diagonal loading, the fluctuation of noise eigenvalues can be significantly reduced, preventing the occurrence of prominent false peaks. However, this also generally increases the height of sidelobes corresponding to smaller eigenvalues, broadens the main lobe, and inevitably decreases resolution. This paper proposes an improved algorithm based on covariance reconstruction, which maintains the robustness of the diagonal loading algorithm under conditions of few snapshots while also preserving the high resolution of the MVDR algorithm.

The algorithm is divided into two steps: First, diagonal loading is utilized to reduce the fluctuation of noise eigenvalues. Then, the new covariance matrix \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{R}}_{DL}$$\end{document} is decomposed into its eigenvalues, which are squared and used to reconstruct the covariance matrix. The eigenvalues of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{R^{\prime}}}_{DL}$$\end{document} are denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda^{\prime}_{DL} = \lambda_{{_{DL} }}^{2}$$\end{document}. Simulation example

Simulation calculation

The manuscript verifies the positioning capability of the proposed algorithm through simulation and experimental data, focusing on the analysis of the algorithm’s positioning ability and the half-power beamwidth of the spatial spectrum as they vary with the signal-to-noise ratio under conditions of low snapshots.

Simulation 1: Dual-target localization under low snapshot conditions

This manuscript primarily addresses the issue of near-field acoustic source localization under conditions of limited snapshots. The number of signal snapshots is set to half the number of vector array channels, that is, Nt = 12, with the signal power of the first vector hydrophone’s acoustic pressure channel serving as a reference, and Gaussian white noise with an SNR of 0 dB is added. The position coordinates of the dual targets are (50°, 40°, 10 m) and (70°, 60°, 30 m). The spectral estimation of the search space is completed using the vector MVDR, standard deviation diagonal loading, and the improved standard deviation loading algorithm presented in this paper, yielding the normalized spectra for azimuth, elevation, and distance, as shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Power spectrum calculated by different methods.

Power spectrum calculated by different methods.

In the figure, the red triangles indicate the true positions of the targets. The normalized power spectral profiles obtained from the three methods demonstrate that when the number of snapshots is low, the vector MVDR algorithm is the most unstable, with significant sidelobe fluctuations and false peaks that completely obscure the target positions, making target localization impossible. The analysis shows that this occurs because the number of snapshots (Nt = 12) is less than the number of channels in the vector array, resulting in a singular covariance matrix and large errors in matrix inversion, leading to failed localization. Both the standard deviation loading method and the improved standard deviation algorithm presented in this paper successfully identify the power spectra pointing to the two targets, enabling target localization. Comparatively, the improved algorithm proposed in this paper has a narrower main lobe and lower sidelobes, resulting in higher algorithmic resolution. This indicates that the algorithm can accurately complete near-field target localization under conditions of few snapshots, effectively addressing the sensitivity of the MVDR algorithm to the number of snapshots.

Simulation 2: Localization performance varies with SNR

Assume there is a target acoustic source in space with an azimuth angle of 50°, an elevation angle of 40°, and a distance of 25 m. The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is set to range from − 10 dB to 10 dB, with an increment of 2.5 dB, and 300 Monte Carlo simulation experiments are conducted for each step. The number of snapshots Nt = 24, which is equal to the number of channels in the vector array. Two types of diagonal loading methods, standard deviation and average trace, are used to improve the vector Minimum Variance Distortionless Response (MVDR) algorithm under conditions of few snapshots. Additionally, the covariance reconstruction method proposed in this paper is used to enhance the two traditional diagonal loading algorithms. The coordinates of the spectral peak in the spatial spectrum are taken as the estimated values of the target position parameters, and the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the estimates is calculated by comparing them with the true values. Performance curves are shown in Fig. 5.Fig. 5Localization performance curves with different SNR.

Localization performance curves with different SNR.

The RMSE curves for the azimuth, elevation, and distance parameter estimates in Fig. 5 indicate that under conditions of few snapshots, as the signal SNR increases, the RMSE obtained by all methods continuously decreases, and the localization performance is improved. However, the vector MVDR algorithm has the largest estimation error, significantly higher than the other four methods, indicating the poorest algorithmic robustness. In contrast, the two traditional diagonal loading methods and the improved algorithm proposed in this paper yield lower RMSE curves. Upon closer examination, it can be seen that the improved algorithm proposed in this paper has a slightly larger estimation error than the two traditional diagonal loading methods, but the performance is essentially equivalent and the difference is minimal. The results demonstrate that both traditional diagonal loading methods and the improved algorithm proposed in this paper enhance the robustness of the near-field MVDR algorithm for near-field localization under conditions of few snapshots.

Simulation 3: Simulation of the half-power beam width (HPBW)

The Half-power Beam Width (HPBW) is a parameter used in beamforming to describe the angle between two directions where the main lobe power of the radiation pattern drops to half its maximum value, serving as a measure of the resolution’s quality. In this paper, the HPBW of the spatial spectrum is calculated as an indicator to compare the resolution performance of different algorithms. The simulation parameters are consistent with those in Simulation 2, and the HPBW of azimuth, elevation, and distance for different methods is calculated as a function of SNR, with the performance curves shown in Fig. 6.Fig. 6HPBW curves with different SNR.

HPBW curves with different SNR.

As can be seen from Fig. 6, the half-power beam width (HPBW) of the power spectrum continuously decreases as the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) increases. Among the traditional diagonal loading methods, the HPBW of the average trace loading method is greater than that of the standard deviation loading method, indicating that the larger the loading amount, the lower the resolution of the MVDR algorithm. When compared with the improved algorithm proposed in this paper, the traditional diagonal loading methods exhibit a significantly larger HPBW, which suggests that the improved algorithm has a narrower main lobe and thus higher resolution. This means that while enhancing the robustness of the MVDR algorithm under conditions of few snapshots, the improved algorithm does not compromise its high-resolution capabilities.

In summary, the covariance reconstruction algorithm proposed in this paper effectively combines the high-resolution advantage of the MVDR with the robustness of the diagonal loading methods, significantly improving the near-field target localization capability of vector arrays under conditions of few snapshots.

Experimental data analysis

To further verify the practical application capabilities of the proposed algorithm and vector hydrophones, a single-target localization experiment was conducted underwater. The experiment utilized a linear array composed of a 4-element two-dimensional vector hydrophone with a spacing of 0.75 m, as shown in Fig. 7a. A transducer was used as the sound source, emitting a monochromatic sound signal with a frequency of 3150 Hz. The target was approximately 1.8 m away from the first vector hydrophone, with an azimuth angle of about 34°. Since the sound source and the array were in the same plane, the elevation angle did not need to be estimated. To verify the localization capability under conditions of few snapshots, two sets of data were calculated. For the large snapshot MVDR, the number of snapshots Nt = 400, and 12 snapshots were taken from this for the calculation of the small snapshot vector MVDR. The spatial spectrum profiles for azimuth angle and distance were obtained using standard deviation diagonal loading and the improved standard deviation diagonal loading method, as shown in Fig. 7b,c.Fig. 7Target location with experimental data.

Target location with experimental data.

As shown in Fig. 7, under conditions of few snapshots, the vector MVDR yields a spatial spectrum with multiple sharp peaks, making it impossible for the algorithm to locate the target. Clearly, the large snapshot vector MVDR, the traditional standard deviation loading method, and the improved algorithm all produce spectral peaks that correspond to the azimuth and distance parameters consistent with the experimental setup, demonstrating the ability to locate near-field targets of the array under conditions of few snapshots. Additionally, the improved algorithm exhibits a narrower main lobe and lower sidelobes compared to the traditional algorithms, approaching ideal performance, which also verifies the theoretical part of this manuscript.

Conclusions

This paper takes the target localization in the near-field area of an underwater detection platform as its research background and addresses the issue of poor performance of the MVDR algorithm under conditions of limited sampling data. By analyzing the reasons for the decline in the positioning performance of the MVDR algorithm with few snapshots, the paper proposes to first use diagonal loading to reduce the fluctuation of noise eigenvalues, and then reconstruct the covariance matrix using the square of the eigenvalues to effectively solve the problem of reduced resolution associated with traditional diagonal loading methods. Simulation results indicate that as the SNR increases, the improved algorithm proposed in this paper has comparable positioning RMSE curves with traditional diagonal loading methods, but with a smaller HPBW. This suggests that under the same robustness conditions, the resolution of the improved algorithm is significantly higher than that of traditional diagonal loading algorithms. Therefore, the algorithm presented in this paper is more suitable for the localization of near-field targets and provides a new approach to enhancing the operational capability of underwater detection platforms.

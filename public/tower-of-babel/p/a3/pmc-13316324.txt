Sigma-1 receptor agonist SA4503 improves cerebral protective effects in an extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation rat model
Authors: Jie Yu, Shuhong Chen, Pingfei Xue, Congya Zhang, Xiaofang Yang, Feilong Hei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesIn this study, we applied a selective sigma-1 receptor agonist, SA4503, to an extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation rat model and investigated its cerebral protective effects and potential mechanisms.MethodsEighteen male adult Sprague-Dawley rats were randomly allocated to 3 groups after anesthesia and identical preparation work, with 6 rats per group. The 2 intervention groups underwent asphyxiation-induced cardiac arrest and were rescued with and without SA4503. The SHAM group did not undergo cardiac arrest or rescue but received the same critical care as the intervention groups until the experiments ended. The end point was 1 hour after the return of spontaneous circulation or death. Hemodynamic and blood gas indicators were measured during surgery. Cerebral injury was evaluated histopathologically. Cerebrospinal fluid samples were collected by microdialysis. Rat hippocampal tissues from the intervention groups were used for whole-transcriptome sequencing.ResultsCompared with extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) group, the ECPR + SA4503 group showed alleviated neuronal destruction of the CA1 region, which is illustrated by the pathologic pictures and the statistical result. In addition, the levels of excitatory amino acids of the ECPR + SA4503 group were lower than that of the ECPR group. In total, 74 differentially expressed genes were detected by RNA sequencing, most of which were related to inflammation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress. The phosphoinositide 3-kinase–AKT signaling pathway was selected by Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis, which may contribute to the cerebral protection of ECPR + SA4503, and was verified via western blotting. Finally, a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactive ceRNA network was established to elucidate the mutual regulation among these three different kinds of RNAs.ConclusionsOur findings indicate that the sigma-1 receptor agonist SA4503 mitigates brain injury in an extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation rat model.Key Words: cardiac arrest, cerebral protection, ECPR, sigma-1 receptor, ceRNA

Central Message

This study indicates that the sigma-1 receptor agonist SA4503 mitigates brain injury in an extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation rat model.

Perspective

The finding of the neuroprotective effect of the sigma-1 receptor agonist SA4503 in an extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation rat model provides a novel way in clinical practice to improve the resuscitation effect and prognosis of patients who are experiencing cardiac arrest.

Cardiac arrest (CA) is a severe clinical condition that threatens human health. The survival rate remains low.1,2 Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) is a mechanical cycle–assisted technique that provides robust cardiopulmonary support for CA patients in the early stage of resuscitation.3 Although extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) has shown good survival and neurologic outcomes, the survival rate for in-hospital CAs is only ∼30% and ∼9% for out-of-hospital cardiac arrests.1,4,5 Furthermore, 57% of patients with out-of-hospital CAs achieving restoration of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) failed to be discharged, and 68% died as the result of neurologic injury.6 Thus, it is important to find more promising therapeutic methods to improve neurologic outcomes after CA. Sigma-1 receptor (Sig-1R) is an intracellular chaperone located in the endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondrial interface, which is targeted for treating many neuropsychiatric disorders. Ruscher and colleagues7 found that among animals with good neurologic outcomes, their reactive astrocytes in the peri-infarct region have more Sig-1R. Our study aimed to elucidate the potential cerebral protective mechanisms of SA4503, a selective Sig-1R agonist, in an ECPR rat model. Our findings suggest a novel approach for further studies on protective measures of the nervous system in ECPR treatment, which further enhances the treatment effect and prognosis of patients with CA.

Methods

This study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Fuwai Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences in December 2018. (approval no. FW-2018-0013). All animals were selected and used in compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals formulated by the National Institutes of Health.

Laboratory Animals

Healthy experimental Sprague-Dawley rats (450-500 g) were fasted for 8 hours before surgery. After intubation and other identical surgical procedures, the 18 rats were randomly divided into the SHAM group (without CA treatment, n = 6), the ECPR group (treated with ECPR after CA, n = 6), and the ECPR + SA4503 group (received Sa4503 intravenously during ECPR, n = 6).

ECMO System Installation and Prefilling

The ECMO system was assembled by connecting a roller pump (Stockert Company) with a small-animal membrane lung and supporting pipelines (Dongguan Kewei Medical Device Co, Ltd). Next, 13 mL of 6% hydroxyethyl starch (Vanwen) and 0.5 mL of 300 IU/mL heparin were prefilled in the entire system. Finally, we drained the air bubbles inside by running the roller pump.

ECPR Model Establishment

Sevoflurane (3-4%) inhalation was used for anesthesia induction and was reduced to 1.5 to 2.5% while maintaining anesthesia. Subsequently, the rats were fixed supine, and a tracheotomy was performed by placing an endotracheal catheter (14-G cannula needle, Becton Dickinson Company). The other side of the needle was connected to a small-animal ventilator (Shanghai Alcott Biotechnology Co, Ltd). The following parameters were used to set up the ventilation: ventilation mode at volumetric control mode, tidal volume at 8 to 10 mL/kg, and respiratory rate at 60 times per minutes. A primed 24-G casing needle (Becton Dickinson Company) was inserted into the right femoral artery and connected to a PowerLab multipurpose polygraph (Analog Devices) to monitor heart rate and blood pressure. The caudal artery was punctured with a 20-G or 22-G cannula needle (decided by the diameter of the blood vessel, Analog Devices) for arterial inflow during ECPR, and the right jugular vein with a transfusion hose for intravenous drainage and fluid therapy. Afterwards, all pipelines were prefilled with heparinized saline (300 IU/mL). A heating pad was put underneath to maintain the animals’ temperature, which was measured by an electronic thermometer inside the anus. After these steps, the rats were allowed to acclimate for 20 minutes, and baseline data were collected.

Subsequently, asphyxiation was performed by turning off the small animal ventilator and clipping the tracheal tube for 4 minutes. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) falling below 25 mm Hg indicated the successful establishment of CA. Then, resuscitation was initiated. We opened the airway and administered mechanical ventilation with pure oxygen. SA4503 (2.5 mg/kg) (Figure E1) was given via the tail artery to rats in the ECPR + SA4503 group. ECMO combined with chest compressions (ECPR) was performed. A homemade small-animal CPR machine was used for chest compression (frequency, 200 bpm; depth, one third of the anteroposterior diameter of the thorax). The ECMO flow rate was 60 to 80 mL/(kg/min) and was adjusted according to the hemodynamic results. The oxygen flow rate was 1 to 1.5 L/min. Epinephrine (20 μg/mL) was provided through tail arteries at the beginning of the ECPR. Epinephrine (10 μg/μL), norepinephrine (10 μg/mL), and dopamine (20 μg/mL) were given by actual needs to maintain MAP >60 mm Hg. Crystalloids were used for fluid therapy.

Different dosage of SA4503 for concentration of Glu of the microdialysate of the hippocampal CA1 region. Data are expressed as means with SEM.

The end point of the experiment was the death of experimental animals or 1 hour after ROSC. ROSC was defined as the maintenance of MAP >60 mm Hg for at least 10 minutes. The absence of ROSC after 10 minutes of rescue indicated resuscitation failure. Rats were weaned off ECPR 5 minutes after ROSC restoration. Hemodynamic and blood gas data from each group were collected 30 minutes after ROSC. After reaching the end points, animals were sacrificed with potassium chloride (2.0 mEq/kg) under general anesthesia and brain tissues were collected. Microdialysis

Brain probe embedding

EICOM microdialysis products were used in this study. The rats were anesthetized with 3% sevoflurane intraperitoneally and then fixed on the brain stereotaxometer. According to the rat brain stereotaxic atlas, we located the CA1 region of the hippocampus (anteroposterior: −3.3 mm, mediolateral: ±1.5 mm, dorsoventral: −2.5 mm) and placed an easy-to-use brain probe (CX-I-4-2, rod length: 4 mm, membrane length: 2 mm, molecular weight interception: 50 kDa). A catheter cap was installed by dental bone cement. The rats were then revived and returned to the breeding room for follow-up after 48 hours.

Microdialysate sample collection

A 1-mL syringe filled with artificial cerebrospinal fluid was installed on a microsyringe pump. After anesthetizing the rats, we connected the probe to the syringe. Samples were collected on ice 20 to 30 minutes after starting ECPR at 2 μL per minute. Then, they were stored at −80 °C.

Ultraperformance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry

Microdialysate samples (20 μL) were mixed with 80 μL of methanol and sonicated for 30 minutes. We collected the lyophilized supernatant samples after centrifugations (12,000 × g) for backup. Chromatographic separation was performed at 35 °C with water as mobile phase A (NH4FA, 5 mM) and acetonitrile as mobile phase B (NH4FA, 5 mM). Every 10-μL sample was processed with a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min. Time-of-flight mass spectrometry-IDA-tandem time-of-flight mass spectrometry (n = 2) was used for mass spectrum acquisition (time-of-flight mass spectrometry: 50-1500 Da; tandem time-of-flight mass spectrometry: 50-1500 Da).

Histopathology

After fixing, the brain tissues were embedded in paraffin and cut into 5-μm sections. Nissl staining and hematoxylin and eosin staining were performed. Neuronal damage was assessed under a microscope, and Nissl stain–positive neurons of the CA1 region of the brain in 5 fields of each section were counted by two researchers blindly.

RNA Library Construction and Sequencing

We followed the instructions of TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) to isolate and purify total RNA from brain hippocampal samples of the ECPR and ECPR + Sa4503 groups. RNA quantities and pureness were measured via NanoDrop ND-1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and RNA integrity (RIN > 7.0) was assessed with the 2100 Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Technologies). After we depleted ribosomal RNA from total RNA, we created cDNA by reverse-transcribing the cleaved RNA fragments. U-labeled second-stranded DNA was synthesized and an A-base was placed at the end of each strand. Then, DNAs were amplified by polymerase chain reaction (initial denaturation: 95 °C, 3 minutes; 8 cycles of denaturation: 98 °C, 15 seconds; annealing: 60 °C, 15 seconds ; extension: 72 °C, 30 seconds ; final extension: 72 °C, 5 minutes).

Differential Expression Analysis

We screened and identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and differentially expressed miRNAs among the ECPR and ECPR + SA4503 samples by Empirical Analysis of Digital Gene Expression Data in R (edgeR). |log2FoldChange| ≥1 and P < .05 between the 2 groups were considered significantly different.

Functional Enrichment Analyses

Via the DAVID database (version 6.8), “Biological processes,” “cellular components,” and “molecular functions” between ECPR and ECPR + SA4503 groups were annotated by Gene Ontology (GO); the pathway information was annotated by Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. A significant statistical difference was presented by P < .05.

Western Blotting

The key proteins in the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-AKT pathway selected by KEGG pathway analysis were determined. In brief, protein extracts (40 μg per lane) were separated in a 12% polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, after which they were transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane and soaked in nonfat milk (5%, 2 hours). PI3K (ab40776; abcam, 1:1000), AKT (ab179463; abcam, 1:2000) and mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR; ab134903; abcam, 1:1000) were incubated under 4 °C overnight. Protein bands were exposed and visualized via image analyzer software.

Statistical Analysis

The t test was used describe 2-group differences, whereas one-way analysis of variance was performed for multigroup comparisons. The Student–Newman‒Keuls tests and the least significant difference were applied for multiple group comparisons. All analyses were performed via the SPSS software, version 21.0 (IBM Corp).

Results

Hemodynamics and Blood Gas Analysis

Rats in the ECPR and ECPR + SA4503 groups exhibited asphyxia-induced CA and achieved ROSC after ECPR resuscitation, with a success rate of 100%. No notable differences were seen either in the baseline weight and rectal temperature or in the MAP and heart rate 30 minutes after ECPR between the ECPR and the ECPR + SA4503 groups (P > .05). In contrast, the blood pH of the ECPR + SA4503 group was much greater than that of the ECPR group (P < .05), but no significant differences existed in other blood gas parameters (Table 1).

Pathology

As presented in Figure 2, C, normal neurons in the ECPR + SA4503 group were lower than in the SHAM group (P < .05) but were higher than in the ECPR group (P < .01). Moreover, neuronal destruction of the CA1 region was more severe in the ECPR group than in the SHAM group, with numerous vacuole areas, disordered cell arrangement, pathologic nuclear contraction, and morphologic changes. These conditions were alleviated in the ECPR + SA4503 group, which showed fewer instances of disordered neurons, nuclear shrinkage, and cavitation (Figure 2, A and B).

Identification of DEGs and Differentially Expressed miRNAs

In total, 74 DEGs were identified from the ECPR and the ECPR + SA4503 groups, which contained 54 upregulated and 20 downregulated genes (P < .05, |log2(foldchange)| > 1). Four miRNAs were differentially expressed, all of which were upregulated (P < .05, |log2(foldchange)| > 1).

GO and KEGG Annotation of DEGs

Figure 3, A and B shows the top 20 terms from GO and pathway enrichment analyses. Among the top 10 enriched GO terms, the most terms (6 terms) were located in the “biological process” category, among which “cellular response to interleukin-1” was the most enriched (4 genes, P < .001). Besides, items of the “cellular component” group comprised 4 of the 10 most enriched GO terms. In the “molecular function” group, the most enriched GO term was “extracellular matrix structural constituent” (3 genes, P < .001), whereas the GO term “protein binding” contained the most DEGs (24 genes, P < .01). For KEGG pathway analysis, Most DEGs gathered in the “AMPK signaling pathway” (P < .05). Other highly enriched pathways included “phototransduction” (P < .05), “PI3K-AKT signaling pathway” (P < .05), “relaxin signaling pathway” (P < .05), as well as the “cGMP-PKG signaling pathway” (P < .05).

IncRNA-miRNA-mRNA Interaction Network Analysis

The specific ceRNA network elucidated how a series of miRNAs regulated the mRNAs and lncRNAs. To be specific, 33 lncRNAs bound to 8 miRNAs, thereby affecting the expression of 39 mRNAs (Figure 3, C).

Discussion

Postresuscitation multiple organ dysfunction syndrome is a main factor affecting the survival rate and prognosis of patients with CA.8 Its occurrence and development involve a series of complex pathophysiologic mechanisms, among which systemic inflammatory response syndrome caused by activation and release of enormous inflammatory factors and severe ischemia-reperfusion injury caused by acute hypoxia-reoxygenation of histiocytes were highlighted by many previous researchers.9, 10, 11 We believe that providing adequate systemic oxygen and blood via comprehensive treatment methods to patients who experience CA as soon as possible is important, thus improving their survival rate and prognosis.

The Sig-1R is widely distributed in various tissues and organs, which plays a regulatory function on various ion channels.12 In cultured astrocytes, SA4503 attenuated oxidative/nitrosative stress and lipopolysaccharide-induced inflammatory reactions via downregulating tumor necrosis factor-α and upregulating glutathione, whose mechanism may concern Nrf2 and HO-1 activation.13 Sig-1R agonists also have been found to reduce ischemia-reperfusion injury and oxygen radical production and release in many organs. Qin and colleagues14 found that SA4503 alleviated brain ischemic-reperfusion injury in an asphyxia CA rat model, the mechanisms of which included endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial dysfunction and neuronal apoptosis. Studies have also shown that Sig-1R was neuroprotective after transient middle cerebral artery occlusion via a reduction of endoplasmic reticulum stress in stroke male mice.15 Here, we found that SA4503 reduced apoptosis and nerve cell damage in rats in the ECPR + SA4503 group.

The large release of excitatory amino acids (glutamic acid and aspartic acid) and the imbalance of excitatory/inhibitory amino acids can produce excitotoxic effects and further aggravate brain damage. When ischemia or hypoxia occurs, glutamate content in the extracellular fluid increases and the degree of increase is related to the degree of injury.16 Schober and colleagues17 found that the cerebral glutamate content in rats increased during CPR. By monitoring the concentration of cerebral microdialysate metabolites, we demonstrated that SA4503 treatment significantly reduced the production and release of excitatory amino acids in the brains of rats with CA, thereby exerting neuroprotective effects.

To further explore the specific mechanism underlying the neuroprotective effect of SA4503, we performed whole-transcriptome sequencing using rat hippocampal tissues from the ECPR and ECPR + SA4503 groups. In total, 74 DEGs were found, including 54 upregulated and 20 downregulated genes. Secreted frizzled-related protein 1 was one of the upregulated genes in our study, which has been classified as a tumor suppressor gene. The loss of secreted frizzled-related protein 1 expression can promote the formation of cancer cells.18 Other upregulated genes included osteomodulin (OMD), human sodium–iodide symporter (NIS or SLC5A5), and the Cone-Rod Homeobox (CRX) gene.19, 20, 21 According to our results, tectonic family member 3 (TCTN3) was downregulated in the ECPR + SA4503 group. Wang and colleagues22 showed that TCTN3 knockout disrupts neural tube patterning, leading to abnormal embryonic development and prenatal death in mice. In our study, the hippocampus of the ECPR + SA4503 group expressed lower levels of TCTN3 and exhibited more surviving neurons and alleviated necrosis. Further mechanisms require experimental validation in future research.

In light of the functional enrichment analyses, most of the DEGs were associated with apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress-related pathways. Moreover, some well-known pathways related to ischemia-reperfusion injury were also included, such as the “AMPK signaling pathway,” “tumor necrosis factor signaling pathway,” and “peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor signaling pathway.”23,24 The “PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling pathway” was another main enriched pathway in our study. We verified that SA4503 improves the neuroprotective effect via the “PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway” in an ECPR rat model. AKT is an antiapoptotic factor in many cell death paradigms and can be activated by various stimuli such as radiation, hypoxia, and ischemic shock.25 Furthermore, there is a crosstalk between AKT and AMPK. Their interaction regulates reactive oxygen species production and elimination, acting as potential options for cancer therapeutics.26 In addition, activating the “PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling pathway” can also reduce the ischemia-reperfusion injury via inhibiting autophagy, apoptosis, and endoplasmic reticulum stress.27, 28, 29, 30

Competitive endogenous ceRNA theory reveals new mechanisms of RNA molecular interactions. To summarize, ceRNAs bind to miRNAs through miRNA response elements, thereby influencing miRNA-induced gene silencing. In this study, we successfully constructed a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network. It showed that “rno-miR-199a-5p” significantly increased in the ECPR + SA4503 samples. The protective effects of “miR-199a” in a variety of diseases have been elucidated in several studies. Guo and colleagues31 verified that overexpression of “miR-199a-5p” reduced systolic blood pressure and relieved inflammation and oxidative stress. Rane and colleagues32 demonstrated that as a main regulator of a hypoxia-triggered pathway, “miR-199a” can protect cells against hypoxic damage. However, there were also some contrasting results. Zhang and colleagues33 found that an “miR-199a-5p” mimic increased cardiomyocyte death induced by ferroptosis. Li and colleagues34 discovered that silencing “miR-199a-5p” promoted degenerative nucleus pulposus cell proliferation and inhibited apoptosis. Thus, the roles of miRNAs may depend on diverse pathological statuses.

Our study successfully constructed a key miRNA-associated ceRNA-specific network and provides morphological and molecular evidence for elucidating the mechanisms behind the cerebral protective effect of SA4503 in an ECPR rat model. These findings provide a new approach to studying the protective measures of the nervous system in ECPR treatment and improve the clinical efficacy and prognosis of patients with CA. However, this study has some limitations. First, it was a short-term experiment. We did not explore whether SA4503 improves the neurol prognosis of rats with CA over a longer period. Second, the optimal timing and dosage of SA4503 still need to be confirmed by further research. Third, only limited cohort of animals was contained in this study. Considering the external validity of experimental results, the results of this study still require clinical trial verification.

Conclusions

Our data suggest that SA4503, a Sig-1R agonist, improves neuroprotective function in an ECPR rat model, which is mediated by activation of the PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling pathway. The lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interaction chain also takes part in regulating the SA4503 function.

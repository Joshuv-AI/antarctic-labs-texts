PTNS: patent citation trajectory prediction based on temporal network snapshots
Authors: Mingli Ding, Wangke Yu, Tingyu Zeng, Shuhua Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid development of science and technology, the pace of development in the knowledge economy is accelerating. Intellectual property, especially patents, is a strategic resource for technological innovation and a crucial support for building an innovative country. Therefore, it is particularly important to predict patents with high value and strong impact from the numerous and uneven-quality patents. However, patent citation behavior involves many uncertainties, and it is difficult to capture its temporal variations effectively. Therefore, this paper proposes a patent citation trajectory prediction model (PTNS) based on temporal network snapshots. It adopts relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCN) to learn the complex relationships among multiple attributes of patents and utilizes bidirectional long short-term memory networks (BiLSTM) to aggregate the temporal evolution differences of patents. Subsequently, principal component analysis (PCA) is used to explore the evolution characteristics of patent citations in depth, thereby capturing the aging effect and the ‘sleeping beauty’ phenomenon. Compared with other baselines, the PTNS performs well. In predicting new, grown, and random patents, the RMSLE decreases by approximately 0.04, 0.14, and 0.18 respectively, while the MALE decreases by approximately 0.04, 0.12, and 0.16 respectively.Keywords: Patent citation, Network snapshots, Time series, Trajectory predictionSubject terms: Scientific data, Statistics, Socioeconomic scenarios

Introduction

As a crucial indicator and carrier of technology, patents encompass over 90% of the world’s technology information1. Patents with higher citation counts generally have greater technological impact compared to those with fewer citations2. Patents are a crucial part of a nation’s or enterprise’s intellectual property. Due to the novelty requirement for patent applications, patents often represent the most advanced technologies, effectively promoting scientific innovation and technological progress. By analyzing the multifaceted relationships and temporal evolution of various entities within historical patent network snapshots, we can anticipate future unknown patent citations, thus securing an early advantage in technological development. Mapping the trajectory of patent technology development accurately aids in the conversion and application of patents, fosters technological research and innovation, supports industrial transformation and upgrading, and enhances national productivity. Therefore, it is important and necessary to effectively predict the future citation status of patents.

Patent citation analysis originated from the expansion of scientific citation studies. Existing methods for predicting scientific citations mainly included regression models, machine learning, and dynamic models based on citation networks3–11. These methods often employed author, paper, or journal characteristic attributes into regression or machine learning models to forecast citation counts. Additionally, dynamic prediction of citations was made using dynamic heterogeneous information networks12 or temporal graph networks13. However, patent documents contain multiple recorded items and relationships. Moreover, citation behavior may change over time, necessitating a more complex and fine-grained model framework for expression.

The citation behavior of patents involves the interaction of multiple relational variables. Previous predictions of citation patterns have often focused on patent characteristics and evaluation metrics, potentially resulting in certain information loss. Patent citation behavior is not only related to the attributes of the patents themselves but also to earlier citations, applicants, IPC classifications, and the countries where the patents are published. Knowledge dissemination is a continuous and inherited process, and prior research indicates that patents with more early citations are more likely to be cited in the future. Different applicants, due to their backgrounds, intellectual property strategies, and technological preferences, exhibit varying citation behaviors and strategies. Patent citation relationships reflect the technological and scientific foundations, especially in the drafting of the technological background of patent applications, which often cite highly relevant patents. The IPC classification itself categorizes patents into different technological fields, directly related to the patent’s technological subject. Moreover, there are differences in technological potential between countries, and patent laws and examination practices also vary by region. Additionally, patent citation behavior is dynamic, and past citation preferences may influence future citation behavior.

In conclusion, this paper proposes a patent citation trajectory prediction model (PTNS) based on temporal network snapshots. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

Integrating the R-GCN model to represent the multiple relational attributes of patents and the BiLSTM model to capture the evolutionary differences between snap- shots, allowing for a detailed exploration of the primary applicants and the main IPC impacts.

Materials and methods

Literature review

Patent citations are positively correlated with the technological impact or commercial value of the patents14. However, the accumulation of time is a fixed feature of patent citations, so it is an important task to predict the trend of patent citations prospectively. The core issue in patent citation prediction lies in four aspects: relationship expression, temporal networks, temporal mining, and citation prediction. Previous research has progressively delved from single patent indicators into attribute features, textual characteristics, temporal features, dynamic networks, and so on.

The essence of relational representation is to construct heterogeneous graphs with multiple relational attributes and various entities. The heterogeneous graph representation methods are mainly classified into three categories based on the information used: structure-preserving, attribute-assisted and dynamic heterogeneous graph15. Structure-preserving heterogeneous graph representations mainly learn semantic information from graph structures (links, paths or subgraphs), offering good parallelism and high training speed. However, they can only be applied to shallow models and have poor scalability. Dong, Y. et al. proposed ametapath2vec which utilized a random wandering strategy of meta-paths to capture semantic links between different node types, but has not deeply considered the type attributes of nodes16. Attribute-assisted heterogeneous graph-based representations aim to encode complex structures and multiple attributes to learn node embeddings. Although they possess inductive learning capabilities and require low memory space, the heterogeneity of attribute expression remains a challenge. Zhang, C. et al. maintained the heterogeneity of graph structures and node attributes through content aggregation, neighbour aggregation and type aggregation17. Wang Y. et al. proposed a novel HANE model, which has combined multi-layer graph convolutional networks to leverage both heterogeneity and node attributes, generating high-quality embeddings18. Dynamic heterogeneous graph representation methods address the limitations of the aforementioned static heterogeneous graph representations, such as poor scalability and inapplicability to dynamic networks. However, most of these methods use shallow models, lacking non-linear expression capabilities and having high computational complexity. Wang, X. et al. effectively captured the temporal evolution of heterogeneous graphs through matrix perturbation, while preserving semantic information and graph heterogeneity19. Xue, H. et al. used a hierarchical attention mechanism to capture the node information with different timestamps20. Research on patent relationships required an in-depth exploration of the impact of different relationships on citations and the evolution between temporal snapshots, necessitating richer expressions.

For citation prediction, relevant scholars constructed a multiple linear regression model for predicting citation counts based on the main factors influencing citations. They considered the page numbers, the number of claims, average citation rates of references, co-citation strength, and document similarity. This model was validated with the US patents across five main subject areas21. Madani, F. et al. employed reconstruct ability analysis (RA) based on information theory and graph theory, extracting keywords from patent abstracts to predict patent citation status22. Ji, T. et al. proposed a sequence-to-sequence model using attention mechanisms to capture dependencies among multiple time series. Additionally, their model effectively predicted the timestamp and category of the next patent citation23. Hou, J. et al. designed a computable and interpretable method to analyze patent citation contributions, considering semantic information of citation content and the diversity of sentence-level citation motivations. They analyzed the impact of prior knowledge on the diffusion of patent technology24. Ma, A. et al. combined semantic information from metadata texts to predict long-term citation counts25. Huang, S. et al. applied a Transformer model to transfer the citation prediction task into a “sequence to sequence” problem and a multi-task learning question26. Hentona, A. et al. proposed a community monitoring method using Node2vec, combining LSTM, ARIMA models, and Hawkes models for citation prediction and patent scoring27.

In time series internal model learning, deep neural networks construct representations of historical time series features through multiple nonlinear layers. The main methods are as follows28: Convolutional neural network-based approach: These methods are capable of extracting local features invariant across spatial dimensions or hidden information from long past sequences. However, they often suffer from poor prediction accuracy and struggle to handle long-step time series prediction problems independently. Recurrent neural network-based approach: RNNs have been widely used for sequence modeling and achieved great results in various natural language processing tasks. Among them, GRU and LSTM perform comparably but are limited by their ability to learn from only one direction, resulting in relatively low prediction accuracy. Self-attention network-based approach: The input to neural networks consists of many vectors of varying sizes, with potential connections existing between vectors at different time points. However, these potential connections are often inadequately captured in actual training, leading to suboptimal model training results. Graph neural network-based approach: In multivariate time series prediction, variables are interdependent. However, constructing the graph structure is challenging, and GNNs are limited by computational resources and weak dynamic change adaptation. Recently, GNNs have shown strong capabilities in handling relational dependencies. Duan, Z et al. proposed MTPool - a novel framework based on graph pooling, set up a graph structure learning module to convert MTS slices into graphs based on the interactions between variables, and used a temporal dialog module to obtain spatio-temporal graph node features29. In addition, they designed CauGNN to fuse neural Granger causality, and effectively realized multivariate time series prediction using transfer entropy graphs30. Residual-based fully-connected network approach: Compared with other time series prediction models, Nbeats has created an original fully-connected main network framework. It has decomposed time series information from coarse to fine, enabling accurate time series prediction. However, it had limitations in capturing long dependencies, high complexity, and high sensitivity to noise. Different snapshots of patent networks can be considered time series data, but the long span of citation prediction cycles and the need to deeply explore information between snapshots require more sophisticated time series processing methods.

In the realm of temporal network models, Zou, T. et al. proposed a time-based dynamic graph learning framework for patent application trend prediction, aiding enterprises in identifying potential partners or competitors31. Jiang, S. et al. devised a novel end-to-end deep learning framework, transforming temporal and relational information of citation signals from Dynamic Heterogeneous Information Networks (DHINs) into citation time series12. Zhao, Q. et al. utilized graph representation and recurrent neural networks to capture the structural and temporal information of citation networks32. Yao, Y. et al. constructed a multi-layered temporal network model and proposed the TiansRan algorithm to compute node influence on individual network layers. Based on the continuity of influence propagation and the attractiveness of highly influential nodes, they segmented and fitted historical time-series data for predicting inventor influence33. Giordano, V. et al. integrated text mining with dynamic networks and tested it on the large and complex C4ISTAR domain34. Although previous studies have introduced the temporal factors, they mostly utilized the nodes’ own attributes or structural information to perform the prediction task, and the mining of multivariate relationships in patents needed further exploration.

In summary, it is clear from the current research status of patent citation prediction that there is still some research space regarding the expression of relational attributes, temporal network construction, temporal feature extraction, temporal difference, and citation trend prediction capturing. The rapid development of science and technology has led to the rapid growth in the number of patents. It is especially important to predict future unknown, strongly influential, and high-value patent technologies in advance. Therefore, the PTNS model predicted the patent citation trajectory by taking snapshots of the time-series network of patents with multiple relationships. This enabled preemptively identifying “sleeping beauty” patents and accurately portraying patent development trajectories. Moreover, effectively predicting patent citation status plays a positive role in promoting patent application transformation, assisting corporate R&D innovation, advancing industrial transformation and upgrading, and enhancing national technological competitiveness. Furthermore, this paper addressed the four aforementioned issues by constructing temporal network snapshots to predict patent citation trajectories and conducting an empirical analysis using patent data from the aviation industry.

Scheme framework

A network snapshot refers to the technique of capturing and storing the state and data of an entire network or a portion of it at a particular time, as shown in Fig. 1. Based on this, the framework proposed in this paper comprises four components: network snapshots, relational attributes, temporal features, and trajectory fitting. Leveraging the R-GCN model, the paper constructs a snapshot of the patent network and aggregates information on the relational attributes of patent nodes35. Subsequently, the BiLSTM model is employed to capture temporal disparities and aggregate the accumulated evolutionary potential of patents. Furthermore, by learning complex features of patent cumulative influence through PCA and MLP layers, the framework fits future patent citation trajectories with a generalized logistic model.

4.Temporal Feature Learning.

Temporal Feature Learning.

The BiLSTM model is an enhancement of the LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)36. It not only adds memory gates and three gates control units (input gate , forget gate , and output gate ) to the structure of the RNN, but also adds a backward LSTM layer to the original forward LSTM network layer. This enhances effective control of messages regarding historical and future movement distances. It can handle both nearby and distant information. A single layer of BiLSTM consists of two LSTM units, one processing the input sequence in the forward direction, and the other processing it in the reverse direction. After processing, the outputs of the two LSTMs were concatenated, as shown in Fig. 3. This module learns the vector sequences of historical network snapshots in both forward and backward directions. The two vector sequences are concatenated through fully connected layers to form the citation impact of patents, as defined in Eq. (3).

5.Trajectory Fitting.

Trajectory Fitting.

Due to the potential presence of numerous redundant features in the output of the BiLSTM model, it may affect the accuracy of predictions. This paper employs PCA for data dimensionality reduction and feature extraction, which can simplify the model, eliminate noise, reduce computational costs, and enhance model generalization37. This approach of in-depth extraction in patent influence characteristics can effectively enhance the predictive performance of the PTNS model.

The Richards growth curve model, the generalized logistic curve, is used for population studies involving asymmetric growth and inflection points. It is widely applied to describe various biological processes, such as SARS, dengue fever, H1N1, and COVID-19. To effectively observe the trajectory of patent predictions, an epidemic (generalized logistic curve) model is adopted for fitting and visualization of results38. 6.Loss function.

Loss function.

To comprehensively assess the predictive performance of the model, this study combines the predicted results of patent citations with the actual situations of patent citations, employing two metrics (MALE and RMLSE) to define the loss function and evaluate the effectiveness. Lower values of MALE and RMLSE indicate better predictive capability of the model39.

MALE: Mean Absolute Logarithmic Error, refers to the mean squared difference between the logarithms of the predicted values and the logarithms of the actual (true) values, as shown in Eq. (6). MALE provides a measure of how far, on average, the predicted values deviate from the true values, irrespective of the direction of the error. It can be used to assess the accuracy of predictions, especially when dealing with data spanning multiple orders of magnitude. Thus, in evaluating the overall performance of the model, MALE can disregard directional biases and is straightforward to interpret and implement.

RMSLE: Root Mean Squared Logarithmic Error, which is the mean squared difference between the logarithms of the predicted values and the logarithms of the actual (true) values, as shown in Eq. (7). RMLSE is a way to quantify prediction accuracy, suitable for cases where there are significant differences in the range between predicted values and actual values. The result of the square root of the aforementioned mean has the same units as the original data, making it easier to interpret40. Due to the logarithmic transformation, RMLSE exhibits better robustness against extreme deviations in large numerical values and focuses more on relative errors rather than absolute errors, helping to maintain consistent error sensitivity across different orders of magnitude of data.

Empirical test

Polyurethane (PU) is the abbreviation for polyurethane, which is formed by the copolymerization of polyisocyanates and polyols. It is a high molecular compound that contains many repeating urethane segments (-NHCOO-) in its polymer backbone. Due to its excellent properties, the production and variety of polyurethane have been increasing year by year, making it the fifth-largest polymer material. It is widely used in industries such as light industry, chemical industry, electronics, textiles, medical, construction, building materials, automotive, defense, aerospace, and aviation. Therefore, this paper conducts an empirical analysis based on the case of the polyurethane material field to test the effectiveness and feasibility of the prediction method.

Model results and comparisons

To evaluate the performance of the proposed model approach, the following baselines were selected for comparison:

TGNN: A model that combines temporal information and graph neural networks. It captured long-term dependencies in time series data and interactions between nodes in graph-structured data through a time encoder, graph convolutional layers, and a time decoder13.

HINTS: A method for predicting publication citation time series, including a graph encoder, weighted metadata imputation, and citation curve generator. It transformed citation signals in dynamic heterogeneous information networks (DHIN) into citation time series12.

HDGNN: A heterogeneous graph neural network model that utilizes both the graph structural information and the node features for information propagation and learning node representations41.

DeepCCP: A model with an end-to-end deep learning network, which combines the effect of information cascades. It encoded the first-level structure of citation networks using degree sequences, then utilized recurrent neural networks (GRU) and convolutional networks to further capture the latent structure and temporal information of citation networks32.

Subtask division

New Patents: Predicting the citation frequency of emerging patents with low citation frequencies, less than 2.

Grown Patents: Predicting the citation frequency of grown patents with high historical citation frequencies, greater than 4.

Random Patents: Predicting the citation frequency of patents randomly sampled without restricting the citation frequency.

Experimental results

This study evaluated the performance of the PTNS model by comparing it with existing baseline models, including TGNN, HINTS, HDGNN, and DeepCCP. In the prediction of patent citation trajectories within the polyurethane domain, the PTNS model performs well on the RMLSE and MALE metrics, demonstrating the correlation of relational attributes and temporal features with patent citation status, as shown in Table 4. Particularly in the new, growth, and random patent subtasks, the PTNS model shows superior performance compared to other baselines, with a decrease of approximately 0.04, 0.14, and 0.18 in the RMLSE metric, and a decrease of approximately 0.04, 0.12, and 0.16 in the MALE metric. The improvements in prediction results are significant. Additionally, the standard deviation of the PTNS model’s results was relatively small, especially in the random patent subtask, indicating that the proposed model possessed a certain degree of robustness. In terms of model runtime comparison, the PTNS model had a runtime of approximately 46 min, placing it in fourth place. Among the models, HDGNN had the longest runtime, taking about 60 min, while the TGNN model had the shortest runtime, at about 2 min. This was due to the higher computational complexity and greater number of parameters in the PTNS model.

For the subtasks, the PTNS model performed better in grown and random patents than in new patents. This was related to the patent lifecycle and citation lag. Patents in the emerging stage are usually recently published, not yet widely recognized or used, and experience significant citation lag. Consequently, they have fewer and more volatile citations, making them harder to predict. In the growth stage, patents start gaining attention, having surpassed the lag period. Their citation count increases significantly, with richer historical information and clearer citation trends, making them easier to predict. Random patents include those at various stages of the lifecycle, with the lag effect smoothed out, leading to more averaged citation counts and a more stable predictive model.

In conclusion, the proposed model can effectively represent the patent network snapshots and evolutionary differences, enabling citation trajectory prediction.

Ablation experiments

To validate the effectiveness of the PTNS model in predicting patent citation trajectories by combining relationship temporal information, trajectory fitting, and influence mining, we conducted the following ablation experiments, which are shown in Fig. 5.

Results of ablation experiments. It shows the ablation experiments results of relational properties, temporal features, Richards growth curves and PCA.

PTNS-emb: In this experiment, we replaced rgcn-hist in the embedding module with GCN, directly inputting patent feature information without considering patent relationship attributes and temporal changes.

PTNS-time: We removed the BiLSTM model of the temporal feature module and directly averaged the embedding sequences of neighouring nodes to aggregate the influence of patent neighouring nodes.

PTNS-log: We used the simple logarithmic function to predict future citation status instead of the generalized logistic curve.

PTNS-dimen: We removed the PCA module, which was responsible for dimensionality reduction and feature extraction of patent evolution ability, and directly measured patent citation evolution ability.

Compared to ablation experiment 1, this study introduced the extraction of patent relationship attributes and prior snapshot information, resulting in an improvement in citation trajectory prediction performance. The RMSLE was reduced by approximately 0.02, and the MALE was reduced by 0.01.

Compared with the ablation experiment 2, this study significantly improved the citation trajectory prediction performance by deeply mining the temporal features of the multivariate relational patent network snapshots. The RMSLE was reduced by about 0.31, and the MALE was reduced by about 0.32.

Compared to ablation experiment 3, this study used the generalized logistic curve to predict future patent citation status, which is more suitable for fitting patent citation trajectories. The RMSLE was reduced by approximately 0.005, and the MALE was reduced by 0.001.

Compared to ablation experiment 4, this study used PCA to further explore the future impact of patents. Although the improvement in RMSLE was not significant, there was a reduction of 0.002 in MALE.

These results demonstrated that incorporating patent relational attributes, temporal features, the generalized logistic curve, and PCA can improve the performance of the PTNS model in predicting patent citation trajectories.

Analysis of time distance

To finely observe the performance of the PTNS model at different prediction time lengths, this paper utilizes the relational attributes and temporal features of historical network snapshot sequences, predicting the accumulation of patent citations in the T + N future network snapshots. Specifically, based on time-series network snapshots of the polyurethane field and using a sequence of 10 historical network snapshots, this paper predicts patents’ future citation counts for periods T + 1 to T + 10. Experimental results indicate that as the time length increases, the predictive performance of the PTNS model gradually decreases. Compared to network snapshots with longer prediction lengths, the citation status of patents in closer prediction periods is easier to predict, as shown in Fig. 7. This may be because the prediction results for the distant future are influenced not only by historical citation behaviors but also by complex changes in the near future. Predictive differences amplify as the prediction length increases, leading to a gradual decrease in model predictive performance. Overall, the performance of the PTNS model across different prediction time lengths surpasses the average level of other baseline models, particularly excelling in the prediction of citations in the near future.

Future unknown prediction

Based on the effective prediction of recent patent citation statuses from historical time-series network snapshots, it was evident that further exploration of predicting future unknown trajectories using historically known information was feasible. The core of the trajectory prediction task is to predict unknown future trend conditions by mining the information and potential change patterns embedded in historical data. This paper further employed patent information on polyurethane technology for future unknown patent trajectory prediction. Due to the surge in patent application in recent years, limitations in computational memory, and the time-dependency of the data, this paper randomly selected 50% of the historical data from 2018 to 2022 to predict its future citation trajectory in 2023–2027. Given the unknown and unavailable future data, and to avoid the problem of missing data, this paper took 2023 as the starting year of the prediction period for year-by-year incremental prediction. In the prediction results, this paper selected part patents future citation trajectory for visualization, as shown in Fig. 8. Patents with higher citation counts tended to stabilize and mature over the future period, while patents with lower citation counts exhibited gradual emergence or rapid growth. Effective prediction of future trends in patent technology can help identify potentially high-impact technologies in advance, providing a first-mover advantage in technological innovation and development. Additionally, it facilitates accurate assessment of the commercial value of patents, promoting the transfer, transformation, and application of patent technologies.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a patent citation trajectory prediction model based on time-series network snapshots (PTNS) to assist in predicting emerging patent technologies. The model effectively captures the multiple relational attributes and temporal features of patents, integrating with PCA for extracting influential features of patents. It not only investigates the citation behaviors of new, grown, and random patents but also analyzes the predictive effects at different future time lengths.

Through the research, it is found that there is a certain correlation between the relational attributes and temporal features of patents and their future citation trajectories, with the citation situation in the near future being easier to predict. Notably, the modeling framework can be extended to predict unknown future patent trajectories. Specifically, in predicting new, grown, and random patents, the RMLSE indicator decreases by approximately 0.04, 0.14, and 0.18, respectively, and the MALE indicator decreases by approximately 0.04, 0.12, and 0.16. Moreover, the prediction results of the PTNS model have a small standard deviation, indicating a certain degree of robustness.

Future research directions: Patents contain various network structural information, such as patent cooperation networks and patent transfer networks, which can be applied to the R-GCN model. By reconstructing network snapshots based on corresponding relational attributes and temporal features, it is also possible to predict patent cooperation trajectories and patent transfer trajectories. Furthermore, accurately forecasting the future unknown trajectory of patents can contribute to optimizing resource allocation, promoting technology transfer, solving technical challenges, exploring interdisciplinary innovation, and achieving technological and industrial innovation.

Integrated bioinformatics decoding of the MASLD ceRNA network reveals novel therapeutic targets
Authors: Shian Yu, Xinhua Jiang, Long Peng, Xianwen Zeng, Suqi Wen, Hui Xiong, Sumei Guo, Fufeng Huang, Hanwen Liu, Qiulan Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the leading cause of chronic liver disease worldwide. The mechanisms of liver injury in MASLD patients remain incompletely elucidated. This study aims to identify novel critical genes associated with MASLD using bioinformatics approaches. Microarray datasets were downloaded from the GEO database, and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified. We performed enrichment analyses, constructed protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks, and visualized co-expression networks between mRNAs and miRNAs. Hub gene expression was validated using an independent dataset, and ROC curves were generated. Subsequent analyses included clinical correlation assessments and ceRNA network construction. Enrichment analyses revealed that the cell cycle, cytokine–cytokine receptor interactions, cellular senescence, oocyte meiosis, chemokine signaling pathways, viral protein–cytokine interactions, luteinizing hormone-mediated oocyte maturation, PPAR signaling, and p53 signaling pathways were significantly enriched. Three key genes (IL1β, TLR2, and TLR4) were validated using GEO datasets. The constructed ceRNA network, comprising miRNAs, circRNAs, mRNAs, and lncRNAs, identified the hsa-miR-21-5p-TLR2and hsa-miR-34a-IL1Bpathways as critical regulators of MASLD pathogenesis. IL1B, TLR2, TLR4, CCL2, and ICAM1were identified as central genes in MASLD. Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatopathy (MASLD), formerly known as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), has evolved into an epidemic affecting more than 1 billion people worldwide. The disease is strongly associated with insulin resistance, obesity, and metabolic risk factors such as hypertension and dyslipidemia. It is characterized by excessive accumulation of triglycerides in hepatocytes, and its pathological spectrum ranges from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cirrhosis and even hepatocellular carcinoma [1, 2]. It is the leading cause of chronic liver disease in developed nations, with rapidly increasing global prevalence [3, 4]. MASLD encompasses a spectrum from steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and liver-related mortality [5]. Histologically, MASLD progresses from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis with ballooning degeneration, fibrosis, and cirrhosis, with 24–30% of NASH patients advancing to cirrhosis. Despite its rising prevalence, the molecular mechanisms underlying MASLD progression remain poorly understood, particularly in the context of metabolic dysregulation and chronic low-grade inflammation [6, 7].

The extensive use of integrated transcriptomics and microarray analysis in various diseases, both cancerous and non-cancerous, has garnered significant attention in recent years. Former explorations in combination with bioinformatics have recognized multiple biomarkers in MASLD, Like RING finger protein 13 and HSD17B13. Recent advances in omics technologies have identified key molecular players in MASLD pathogenesis. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have implicated variants in genes related to lipid metabolism (e.g., PNPLA3, TM6SF2) and immune response (e.g., MBOAT7), Transcriptomic analyses have highlighted dysregulation of pathways involved in lipogenesis (e.g., PPARγ), inflammation (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6), and fibrogenesis (e.g., TGF-β) [8, 9]. However, these studies are largely confined to single-platform analyses, failing to capture the complexity of competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks that orchestrate post-transcriptional regulation. Recent studies indicate that MASLD extends beyond liver-related health issues, emerging as a multisystemic disease impacting various extrahepatic organs and regulatory pathways [10]. Given the inability to adequately predict MASLD patients with mild pathophysiologic changes, exploring additional biomarkers to indicate the early stages of MASLD seems essential. A network of competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), involving interactions among key non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) such as microRNAs (miRNAs), long-chain non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs), provides an important chance to gain further insight into the biomarkers and processes that are closely associated with MASLD. Current research gaps include: (1) lack of integrative multi-omics frameworks to dissect ceRNA interactions in MASLD; (2) limited understanding of how metabolic perturbations (e.g., insulin resistance) mechanistically converge with inflammatory signaling pathways; and (3) scarcity of validated biomarkers for early disease detection.

The finding that mRNA expression profiles differed between MASLD samples and human normal liver tissue controls was confirmed by our current work. The identification of DEGs was performed using the limma package. GSEA, GO, and KEGG pathways were utilized to investigate regimes influencing MASLD etiology. PPI networks were fabricated to acquire hub genes. We performed validation of the dataset to further confirm hub genes expression and were able to draw ROC curves. In order to understand the linkage of key genes to clinical treatments. Based on the predicted interactions among mirna, lncrna, and circRNA, We built ceRNA networks to shed light on emerging mechanisms in transcriptional networks that accelerate the progression of MASLD. We discovered that the identified hub genes and potential ceRNA networks are anticipated to offer insights into the molecular mechanisms of liver lesions in MASLD. However, the underlying regulatory mechanisms in MASLD are unclear, and the potential roles of MASLD therapeutic targets need to be further investigated. In this study, we explored the molecular mechanisms of MASLD and identified key genes and constructed potential ceRNA networks using bioinformatics methods.

Information and methodology

Sources of data

Related gene expression datasets were filtered from the GEO genomic database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) using'MASLD'as a keyword. [11]. The data can be downloaded from the GEO database under the accession numbers GSE205645 (Sample size: 36; platform: GPL23126), GSE49541 (Sample size: 72; platform: GPL570) and GSE63067 (Sample size: 18; platform: GPL570). GSE205645 and GSE49541 are used as the primary datasets for training.GSE63067 is the primary dataset for discovery and validation. To reduce the risk of false positives in differential expression analyses, we used the Benjamini–Hochberg method to correct p values for multiple comparisons.

Quality management and recognition of DEGs

We utilized GEOquery (v2.54.1) and limma (v3.42.2) packages in R (v3.6.3) for data reading and DEG analysis. Preprocessing analyses included data normalization and log2 transformation. We removed or averaged the probe sets that had no relevant gene symbols or more than one probe set. The quality of the selected datasets was evaluated using processed data box-and-line plots, principal component analysis (PCA), and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP).Genes were classified as differentially expressed (DEGs) if they had a p value less than 0.05 and an absolute log fold change (|logFC|) greater than 1.The volcano and heat maps of the obtained DEGs were plotted using the ggplot2 (v3.3.3) and ComplexHeatmap (v2.2.0) packages in R software [12].

Enrichment analyses

GSEA analysis evaluated the association between gene sets and biological signaling pathways [13, 14]. The thresholds for GSEA were set as follows: Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) greater than 1, False Discovery Rate (FDR) less than 0.05, and p value less than 0.05.We utilized the ClusterProfiler package (v3.14.3) for GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the identified DEGs and visualized the results with ggplot2 (v3.3.3). Researchers can better understand functional annotation of gene products through GO analysis [15]. Biologists employ KEGG analysis implements to assist in comprehending the systemic operation of cells and even organisms [16]. Conditions for statistical importance were a p value < 0.05 and a gene count of ≥ 2.

PPI network construction and analysis of important modules

The STRING online database (version 11.0) was utilized to investigate potential protein interactions and develop intricate PPI networks with composite scores exceeding 0.7 [17]. Visualizing PPI networks with Cytoscape (version 3.8.2) [18]. MCODE (version 2.0.0) is a Cytoscape plugin designed to create significant modules using the following filtering criteria: degree cutoff of 2, maximum depth of 100, K-core of 2, and node score cutoff of 0.2.

Discovery of key gene and anticipation of target miRNAs

Cell Hubba (version 0.1), a Cytoscape plugin, was employed to identify key genes analyzing the interacting genes determined through algorithmic modules such as maximum clustered centrality (MCC), Edge Percolated Couponent (EPC), maximum neighborhood component (MNC), and degree [19]. We utilized miRmap, microT, and Miranda, to undertake the prediction of the target miRNAs for key genes. Subsequently, we selected those miRNAs that appeared multiple times in the search tool as the target miRNAs. We took into account the relationship existing between key genes (mRNAs) and target miRNAs, and subsequently employed Cytoscape software to carry out the mRNAs-miRNAs co-expression network.

Validation of hub genes with another GEO dataset and ROC curve analysis

A total of 72 Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease samples (GSM789110-GSM789181,GSE49541) and 7 control samples (GSM6215924-GSM6215930,GSE205645) from human liver tissues were selected, with the GEO dataset (GSE63067, Contains 18 samples) serving as the validation dataset. We confirmed the mRNA expression of the hub genes with the validation dataset and generated the ROC curve using the ggplot2 software package. The t test was used to compare differences between patients with Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease and control subjects. P < 0.05.

Construction of the CERNA network

STARBASE (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/index.php) is an innovative database utilizing CLIP-SEQ and Degradome-Seq data. It is specifically designed to assist in achieving a comprehensive understanding of the intersection mapping between miRNA and its targets [20]. The Starbase database was utilized to investigate interactions, employing the mammalian, human, and H19 genomes as criteria for stringent fragmentation data (> = 5), with optional inclusion of degradome and pan-cancer data. For each target miRNA identified, we independently predicted its interacting lncRNAs and circRNAs using the Starbase database. The final ceRNA network integrates all validated miRNA-lncRNA and miRNA-circRNA interactions, where lncRNAs and circRNAs collectively compete for miRNA binding sites (MREs) to regulate target mRNA expression. The CeRNA network between these RNAs was established and visualized using Cytoscape software [21]. Filtering criteria: A miRNA will be retained if it is recognized in at least three of the five databases used for our prediction.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

H&E staining is a common histopathological staining technique used to observe and analyze the structure and morphology of tissues or cells under the microscope. The basic steps include tissue fixation, dehydration, embedding, sectioning, staining and sealing [22, 23]. H&E staining methods and scoring are described in the article [24].

Immunohistochemical criteria scoring

Cell staining intensity is graded on a scale from 1 to 4: 1 for minimal positive (negative), 2 for yellowish (weakly positive), 3 for tan (positive), and 4 for tan (strongly positive). The percentage of positive cells is similarly graded: 1 for ≤ 25%, 2 for 26%−50%, 3 for 51%−75%, and 4 for > 75%. The final score is the product of these two scores.

Discussion

MASLD is recognized as the most prevalent chronic liver disease Linked to obesity, affecting 20–30% of adults globally [25]. MASLD is marked by the accumulation of fatty acids in hepatocytes due to an esterification rate exceeding fatty acid production, resulting in steatosis. Its severity varies from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), which is linked to hepatic steatosis, hepatocellular distension, inflammation, and tissue fibrosis [26, 27]. Although significant research has been conducted, a comprehensive understanding of the pathogenesis of Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease remains incomplete. The integration of high-throughput microarray technology with bioinformatics allows for the identification of key genes and biological pathways Linked to Liver lesions in MASLD, enhancing our understanding of its pathogenesis. The GSEA enrichment analysis included 21,655 genes, while 326 DEGs were analyzed in the GO and KEGG studies. GSEA and GO analyses identified interleukin 4, 10, and 13 signaling and neutrophil degranulation as the most enriched gene sets in the MASLD text. The pathogenesis of MASLD is characterized by hepatocellular steatosis, which is divided into two types: macrovesicular and microvesicular. In macrovesicular steatosis, hepatocyte cytoplasm is occupied by one large or several smaller distinct fat droplets, displacing the nucleus to the cell's periphery. In microvesicular steatosis, hepatocyte cytoplasm contains small lipid droplets with a centrally located nucleus. While MASLD typically involves macrovesicular steatosis, microvesicular steatosis can also occur [28, 29]. IL-1b and IL-6 are closely associated with the pathogenesis of MASLD, suggesting that these inflammatory cytokines promote the development of MASLD. The pro-inflammatory effect of IL-1b may be attributed to its synergistic effect with TLRs signaling. The pro-inflammatory effect of IL-6 may be mediated by the inhibition of the cytokine signaling-3 (SOCS-3)-sterol regulatory element binding protein-1c (SREBP −1c)-lipoyl coenzyme A (FA-CoA) pathway, which inhibits insulin signaling and regulates acute phase response and chronic inflammation [30–32]. Moreover, it also encompassed the phagosomes, as well as cell adhesion molecules. The mechanism of the inflammatory response is a protective response induced by an activating stimulus, but uncontrolled protection triggers additional damage to the body [33]. Growing evidence suggests that inflammation is pivotal in the development and progression of Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Inflammatory mediators, including cytokines and chemokines, are activated in the liver during the disease process. These mediators facilitate immune cell recruitment and activation, causing liver inflammation and damage. Additionally, the inflammatory response may interact with other pathogenic mechanisms, including insulin resistance and oxidative stress, to expedite disease progression. Understanding the inflammatory response's role in MASLD is crucial for developing effective therapies [34]. The pathogenesis of diabetic nephropathy involves macrophage activation, complement system activation, and immunoglobulin or immunoglobulin complex deposition [35–37].

Following the construction of the PPI network, seven hub genes were identified: IL1B, CCL2, TLR2, TLR4, ITGAM, CXCL8, and ICAM1.Further validation showed that IL1B, TLR2, TLR4, CCL2 and ICAM1 exhibited up-regulated expression with high AUC values. We hypothesized thatIL1B, TLR2, TLR4, CCL2 and ICAM1 may be key genes in human MASLD. The completion of the mRNA-miRNA and Cerna networks offers insights into the transcriptional pathogenesis of MASLD.

Interleukin 1b (IL1B) is a crucial pro-inflammatory cytokine vital for host defense against infections and injuries. It is produced and secreted by various cell types, with most studies concentrating on its production in innate immune cells like monocytes and macrophages [38]. A large body of evidence suggests that IL1-B regulates multiple inflammatory responses that are related to the pathological process of chronic inflammation [39]. The inflammatory response plays a crucial role in the progression of Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease [40]. We propose that IL1-B significantly influences the molecular mechanisms underlying Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Our study identified significantly increased IL1-B expression in liver tissues of human Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, demonstrating a high diagnostic value (AuC = 1.000). We concluded that IL1-B plays a crucial role in the progression of liver lesions associated with MASLD.

Activation of TLRs promotes inflammatory responses and plays a key role in the immune system [41].TLR2 belongs to the TLR family and can be expressed in a variety of cell types. The main cell type involved in TLR2-mediated inflammation in the liver is Kupffer cells. A study showed that TLR2 knockout reduced liver inflammation and fibrosis in MASLD mice [42]. In addition, anti-TLR2 monoclonal antibody could attenuate liver injury, inflammation and steatosis in MASLD mice by inhibiting inflammatory responses and modulating hepatic MAPK and NF-kB pathways [43]. A clinical study showed that IL-8 expression levels were significantly elevated in morbidly obese NASH women and that TLR2 expression in the liver was positively correlated with circulating levels of IL-8 [44]. These results suggest that TLR2 plays a key role in the progression of MASLD and CKD. We therefore hypothesized that TLR2 may play an important role in the pathogenesis of Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. In our study, we investigated the up-regulated levels of TLR2 in samples with Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

Toll-like Receptor (TLR) 4 belongs to the family of TLR receptors that induce pro-inflammatory responses to invasive pathogens.TLR4 is activated by lipopolysaccharide (endotoxin) from Gram-negative bacteria, which then triggers two signaling cascades: The first signaling pathway involves the TIRAP and MyD88 junction proteins, whereas the second binds the junction proteins TRAM and TRIF begin early in vivo after endocytosis [45]. TLR4 is a downstream target gene of the transcription factor Foxo1 involved in the regulation of inflammation and necrotic prolapse and is a key regulator of necrotic prolapse in NASH hepatocytes [46]. In our study, we found that TLR4 expression was increased in patients with Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Combining these results and their diagnostic value (AUC value: 0.667), we concluded that the TLR4 gene is a hub gene for Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.

We used Cytoscape software to visualize the Cerna network based on the predicted target miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs. After an extensive literature review, we selected specific miRNAs down and IncRNAs up for detailed analysis.hsa-miR-21-5p and hsa-miR-34a-5p were found to be down-regulated in patients with Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease [47, 48].

C–C motif Ligand 2 (CCL2), also known as monocyte chemotactic protein 1 (MCP-1), is an integral chemokine that recruits macrophages for immune responses. Together with their receptors (e.g., CCR2, ACKR1, and ACKR2), they significantly affect various diseases in different systems [49]. Promoting the expression of C–C motif chemokine Ligand 2 (CCL2) in hepatocytes attenuates hepatic inflammatory response [50]. We found decreased CCL2 expression in patients with MASLD. Combining these results and their diagnostic value (AUC value: 0.829), we concluded that CCL2 is a hub gene for MASLD.

ICAM-1, regulates leukocyte recruitment from the circulation to sites of inflammation, a cell surface glycoprotein and adhesion receptor. Importantly, ICAM-1 acts as a biosensor to transduce efferent signals through the association of its cytoplasmic structural domain with the actin cytoskeleton after ligand engagement of the extracellular structural domain [51]. In our study, combining these results and their diagnostic value (AUC value: 1.000), we found that ICAM1 expression was increased in patients with MASLD.

Immunohistochemistry, one of the most commonly used methods for identification and visualization of tissue antigens in biological research and clinical diagnostics, can be used to characterize a variety of biological processes or pathologies, such as wound healing and immune responses. However, its main drawback is the poor visualization and quantification of multiple antigens (especially immune cells) in a single tissue section. In recent years, a multiplex technique, multiplex immunohistochemical fluorescence, has been used to identify multiple biomarkers in a single tissue sample or in a collection of different tissue samples52–54. We confirmed that key genes (IL-1B, TLR2, and TLR4) were detected in liver tissues of compound MASLD by mIHC. By combining RT-PCR Western Blot and mIHC data, we confirmed changes in the expression levels of key genes (IL-1B, TLR2, and TLR4) in the liver of MASLD patients. Our findings provide a molecular basis for the exploration of tissue-specific therapeutic targets for MASLD.

Nevertheless, it should be noted that our article does have certain limitations. Firstly, this is a retrospective study, and in vivo experiments with larger sample sizes may be required to provide more conclusive evidence to support our observations. Secondly, the precise functions of the central genes and the underlying molecular mechanisms involved in Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease need to be further validated in future studies before they can be safely and effectively applied in clinical practice. Subsequent research will continue in this direction.

In conclusion, this study offers robust evidence on the pathogenesis of MASLD. The pivotal genes show significant correlation with immune markers and could serve as potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets for MASLD. This research offers a comprehensive analysis of Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease pathogenesis. Six hub genes were identified and validated using RT-PCR and immunohistochemistry. The majority of hub genes exhibited significant differential expression between patients with Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease and normal controls.IL1B, TLR2, TLR4, CCL2, and ICAM1 are likely pivotal in disease progression and could serve as diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets. However, our study was based on public databases, and thus, further experiments are needed to explore and validate the mechanisms underlying the analyzed results. We anticipate these insights will enhance our comprehension of the disease and guide its treatment.

Conclusion

This study identifies IL1B, TLR2, TLR4, CCL2, and ICAM1 as key genes in MASLD and elucidates its molecular mechanisms at the transcriptional level. The hypothesized pathways involving hsa-let-7c-5p/hsa-miR-155-5p-IL1B and hsa-miR-106b-5p/hsa-miR-335-5p-TLR2 may significantly influence the progression of liver lesions in early MASLD.

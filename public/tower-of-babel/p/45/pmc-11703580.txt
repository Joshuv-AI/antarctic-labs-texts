Confronting Mpox in Brazil amid global spread of clade Ib
Authors: Paulo Ricardo Martins-Filho, Thialla Andrade Carvalho, Silvia Sant'Ana Rodrigues, Danilo Santos de Sousa, Francy Waltilia Cruz Araújo, Martha Débora Lira Tenório, Diego Moura Tanajura, Cliomar Alves dos Santos
Published: 2024

In September 2023, Mpox clade Ib was identified in South Kivu, Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC),1 spreading to at least 15 African countries, including previously unaffected nations such as Burundi, Uganda, Kenya, and Rwanda. This led the World Health Organization to declare a public health emergency of international concern on August 18, 2024. Between January 1 and September 15, 2024, there were 6201 cases and 32 deaths associated with Mpox on the African continent, with 87% occurring in the DRC. Furthermore, in August 2024, Sweden and Thailand identified the first cases of this variant outside Africa among travellers from the continent.2

Initial reports from the DRC outbreak reveal high transmission among teenagers and young adults mainly through sexual contact, with about 30% of cases involving sex workers and 85% of lesions appearing in the genital area.3 There was also a documented 50% fetal loss rate associated with vertical transmissions during this outbreak.4

The potential global spread of Mpox clade Ib highlights the need for localised strategies that address the specific conditions of each affected region. Particularly in Brazil, which has the second-highest number of global Mpox cases, approximately 12,206 by September 10, 2024, the emergence of clade Ib poses significant risks due to the epidemiological profile of Mpox in the country, broad socio-economic disparities, and limited vaccination policies.

Although no clade Ib cases have been recorded in Brazil to date, the Brazilian Ministry of Health established an Emergency Operations Center for Mpox (EOC-Mpox) on August 14, 2024, to coordinate responses and anticipate a possible outbreak.5 Drawing on lessons from the 2022 to 2023 Mpox outbreak, Brazil needs to enhance its disease control policies, including strengthening epidemiological and genomic surveillance, improving diagnostics, and refining case tracking and community transmission mapping. Key measures include promptly isolating cases, actively detecting new infections, and rigorously monitoring contacts. Public education campaigns are also essential to clarify transmission routes, symptoms, and preventive practices, reducing stigma and enhancing intervention effectiveness.

In addition, Brazil must refine its Mpox vaccination strategies in anticipation of potential outbreaks. Specifically, it is crucial to expand both pre- and post-exposure vaccinations6 to include high-risk groups such as sex workers, gay, bisexual, or other sexually active men who have had recent multiple partners, have engaged in group sex, or have contracted a sexually transmitted infection in the last 12 months, as well as those under pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) for HIV prevention. This expansion is vital given the high incidence of Mpox among these groups,7,8 evidenced by the recent resurgence of the disease in Rio de Janeiro after a period of decreased immunity and suboptimal vaccination coverage.9

To effectively implement expanded vaccination coverage, Brazil must account for its vast geographical and socio-economic diversity. Strategies should include deploying mobile vaccination units to remote areas and forming public-private partnerships to enhance vaccine availability. Moreover, reducing the stigma associated with vaccination and Mpox through community-led education programs will be critical. The co-occurrence of Mpox and other sexually transmissible infections highlights an opportunity to enhance intervention strategies, although widespread stigma hinders effective diagnosis, treatment, and efforts to mitigate transmission.10 These strategies must be culturally tailored and engage local leaders to maximise their impact.

To improve epidemiological and genomic surveillance, Brazil should utilize existing research institutes and universities, integrating new real-time data analysis technologies, while addressing challenges such as funding limitations and rural logistics through enhanced federal support and international cooperation. Additionally, the newly established EOC-Mpox must implement clear rapid response protocols, coordinate with state health departments, and ensure healthcare providers are well-trained and equipped for diagnosis, contact tracing, and patient management.

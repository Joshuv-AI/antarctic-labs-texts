Thulium fiber laser cauterization approach to congenital pyriform sinus ﬁstula: a case series
Authors: Ying Wang, Chenling Shen, Xiaojun Liu, Jiarui Chen, Xiaoyan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCongenital pyriform sinus fistula (CPSF) commonly induces recurrent cervical infection and suppurative thyroiditis in children. Endoscopic minimally invasive approaches have been widely adopted to replace invasive open surgery. Nevertheless, clinical data on thulium laser for pediatric CPSF remains limited, with its advantages and technical drawbacks poorly elucidated. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of thulium laser cauterization for pediatric CPSF to enrich relevant clinical evidence.Case DescriptionThis retrospective study enrolled 9 pediatric CPSF patients who received endoscopic thulium laser cauterization. The mean operative age was 6.46 years, and most fistulas were left-sided (8/9). Seven patients suffered from recurrent cervical inflammation or acute suppurative thyroiditis, and two had cervical abscesses. Two acute-phase patients underwent synchronous abscess drainage. The mean operative time was 14.45±7.09 minutes with negligible bleeding. Only two children developed transient low-grade fever; no severe complications occurred. The average mucosal healing time was 8.22 days. During a mean follow-up of 11.42 months, merely one case relapsed and received open surgery.ConclusionsIn this preliminary, single-center series of nine pediatric patients, endoscopic thulium laser cauterization appeared technically feasible and no severe complications. However, given the small sample size, retrospective design, and limited follow-up, these findings should be regarded as early clinical experience. Definitive conclusions regarding long-term efficacy and recurrence risk require larger, prospective studies with extended follow-up.

Key findings

• In this preliminary cohort of nine children with congenital pyriform sinus fistula (CPSF) undergoing thulium laser cauterization, the procedure was rapid (mean operative time 14.45 minutes) with minimal bleeding.

• The mean mucosal healing time was 8.22 days and one recurrence was observed during a mean follow-up of 11.42 months.

What is known and what is new?

• Endoscopic minimally invasive procedures are safe alternatives for CPSF treatment.

• This study provides clinical evidence of thulium laser in pediatric CPSF, verifying its minimal thermal damage and fast healing.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Thulium laser may offer advantages for narrow and deep fistulas in carefully selected patients, but the current evidence is limited to a small, single-center series.

• Larger multicenter studies with longer follow-up are needed before this approach can be recommended as a standard treatment.

Introduction

Congenital pyriform sinus fistula (CPSF) is a rare congenital branchial cleft malformation, resulting from abnormal development of the third or fourth branchial cleft during embryogenesis, accounting for less than 1% of all branchial cleft malformations (1). The clinical symptoms are mainly recurrent cervical abscesses and acute suppurative thyroiditis, and about 80% of cases occur in childhood (2). However, due to the non-specific clinical features of CPSF and the lack of practical experience of clinicians, most patients are often misdiagnosed after the onset, and even over-treated, such as long-term use of antibiotics, repeated open neck surgeries, or repeated abscess incision and drainage, causing great economic and psychological burdens to the children and their families. Complete removal of the fistula with or without affected thyroid tissue has previously been the most commonly used and most thorough treatment (3). In recent years, more and more endoscopic or microscopic-assisted treatments for CPSF have been developed, such as carbon dioxide laser cauterization, argon plasma coagulation (4), and radiofrequency ablation, which have gradually replaced traditional open neck surgeries as an alternative option for many medical institutions (5-7). They were developed involving cauterization of the sinus tract orifice at the base of the piriform fossa using local coagulation, thereby destroying the tract, avoiding a subsequent adjacent “cyst”, and theoretically leading to its involution. In 2010, Leboulanger et al. (8) were the first to report that thulium laser cauterization could be applied to treat pyriform sinus fistula, with some curative effects attained. However, no further discussion was provided on the advantages and disadvantages of this method.

In this clinical case series, we assessed the therapeutic effect of thulium laser cauterization under suspension microlaryngoscopy as an alternative surgical approach for CPSF in Shanghai Children’s Hospital, and further explored the optimal treatment timing and operative regimen. We present this article in accordance with the AME Case Series reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0486/rc).

Case presentation

All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee(s) and with the Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Children’s Hospital (No. 2026R038-F01). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or guardians of all patients for publication of this case series, accompanying images and video. A copy of the written consent is available for review by the editorial office of this journal. For standardization, all the surgical procedures were performed by two pediatric otolaryngologists in this study.

This retrospective study was conducted from September 2024 to December 2025 at the Department of Otolaryngology Head and Neck Surgery, Shanghai Children’s Hospital. During this period, 25 children were diagnosed with CPSF at Shanghai Children’s Hospital. Of these, nine consecutive patients who met the inclusion criteria underwent thulium laser cauterization and were included in this analysis. During the same period, 16 patients were treated with alternative approaches—radiofrequency ablation (n=8) and open surgery (n=8). The choice of thulium laser for these nine patients was based on surgeon preference and the availability of the thulium laser system, which was introduced at Shanghai Children’s Hospital in September 2024. No patients were excluded after enrollment. We acknowledge that treatment allocation was not randomized, and the results reflect the experience of surgeons who opted for this technique. All participants met the following inclusion criteria: age ≤18 years, with a confirmed diagnosis of CPSF based on: (I) clinical history of recurrent cervical infection or suppurative thyroiditis; (II) preoperative imaging [cervical B-ultrasound and/or enhanced cervical computed tomography (CT)] suggestive of a sinus tract; and (III) definitive visualization of the internal fistula opening in the piriform fossa under suspension laryngoscopy with general anesthesia. Preoperative office-based flexible endoscopy was performed when tolerated, but definitive confirmation of the fistula opening was obtained intraoperatively in all cases. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) prior open cervical surgery for CPSF (including complete fistulectomy) or formation of external fistulas or significant cervical scarring from repeated incision and drainage; (II) imaging evidence (B-ultrasound or enhanced CT) of fluid-filled cystic lesions without a clearly identifiable fistula tract communicating with the piriform fossa—specifically, isolated cystic remnants without an identifiable internal opening, which were considered anatomically unsuitable for endoscopic laser cauterization (this differs from acute abscess formation with a demonstrable fistula tract, which was not excluded); (III) history of pharyngeal burns, trauma, or other surgical procedures in the cervical region; (IV) contraindications to general anesthesia, including major cardiopulmonary abnormalities or systemic disease.

General anesthesia was administered to all patients in a supine posture. We examined the affected pyriform fossa with the assistance of a suspension laryngoscope and microscope and identiﬁed the location of the internal ﬁstula. The thulium laser with a 1,940 nm wavelength from Shanghai Raykeen Laser Technology Co., Ltd., was used. The laser was positioned into the fistula, with the energy direction as parallel as possible to the fistula direction. The power was set at 6 W in continuous state. We performed sequential cauterization of the internal ﬁstula and the surrounding mucous membranes to a depth of approximately 10 mm, ultimately resulting in the formation of a scab and closure of the internal ﬁstula. Meanwhile, once the abscess forms in the child’s neck, the abscess is incised and drained simultaneously during the surgery. Postoperative management

All nine patients, regardless of preoperative inflammatory status, received a uniform postoperative protocol per institutional standard practice for endoscopic CPSF procedures: (I) complete oral restriction with nasogastric (NG) tube feeding for 14 days; (II) intravenous ampicillin-sulbactam (100 mg/kg/day) plus metronidazole (30 mg/kg/day) for 7 days. This protocol was adopted to minimize pharyngeal movement and contamination of the cauterized fistula site during the critical early healing phase, thereby reducing the risk of fistula patency or infection. No patient experienced nutritional complications from NG tube feeding.

Postoperative follow-up protocol

All patients were scheduled for outpatient follow-up visits at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after surgery, with additional visits as clinically indicated. At each visit, clinical assessment included history of neck swelling, pain, or fever, and physical examination of the neck. Flexible nasopharyngolaryngoscopy was performed to evaluate the status of the internal fistula opening at 1, 3, and 12 months postoperatively, or earlier if symptoms recurred. Wound repair time was defined as the interval from surgery to complete sloughing of the white pseudomembrane over the cauterized area, as evaluated daily through endoscopic examination within 14 days after the surgery (and confirmed at subsequent visits if the membrane had not fully separated). Recurrence was defined as (9,10): (I) reappearance of clinical symptoms (neck swelling, pain, or suppuration) after initial resolution, or (II) endoscopic evidence that the internal fistula remained patent or reopened during the follow-up period. Patients who did not complete the scheduled endoscopic follow-up were contacted by telephone to assess symptom status.

Complication assessment

Postoperative complications were assessed as follows: (I) vocal cord mobility was evaluated by flexible nasopharyngolaryngoscopy at the follow-up visit in all patients; (II) dysphagia was assessed clinically through parental report of swallowing difficulty, refusal of oral intake after NG tube removal, or coughing/choking during feeding; (III) pharyngeal fistula was excluded by the absence of saliva leakage, wound drainage, or subcutaneous emphysema; (IV) body temperature was monitored by parents every 4 hours postoperatively using an ear thermometer and the highest daily value was recorded; (V) postoperative pain was not formally scored; however, no patient required analgesic medication beyond the perioperative period; (VI) diet tolerance was assessed through successful transition from NG tube feeding to oral intake at 2 weeks postoperatively; (VII) no unplanned readmissions occurred within 30 days of surgery.

Between September 2024 and December 2025, a total of 9 patients diagnosed with CPSF received thulium laser cauterization with suspension microlaryngoscopy, with none excluded throughout the study period. The characteristics of these patients are shown in Table 1. The gender ratio of males to females was 1:2. The average surgical age of patients was 6.46 years, ranging from 2 to 14 years. Most fistulas occurred on the left side, accounting for 8 out of 9 cases, while one case was right-sided. Clinical manifestations consisted of neck abscess in 2 patients, as well as recurrent cervical inflammatory swelling or suppurative thyroiditis in 7 patients. All included patients were treated with thulium laser cauterization with the assistance of a suspension laryngoscope and microscope, and for 2 cases in acute inflammatory stage, thulium laser cauterization combined with incision and drainage of the neck abscess were adopted. The operation time was 14.45±7.09 minutes, with little intraoperative bleeding. No obvious complications such as dysphagia, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury or pharyngeal fistula occurred in patients receiving thulium laser cauterization. Only 2 children had low fever (<38 ℃) on the first day after the operation, and both returned to normal on the second day. The average wound repair time (the time for the white membrane to completely fall off) was 8.22±1.39 days. The median follow-up duration was 11.4 months (range 7.3–19.8 months; mean 11.42±4.60 months). During follow-up, one patient developed recurrent cervical infection two months after the initial procedure and subsequently underwent open fistulectomy via an external cervical approach. The remaining eight patients remained asymptomatic at the last follow-up visit. However, given the limited follow-up duration, late recurrences cannot be excluded. Discussion

Open surgical removal of the entire fistulous tract in the inactive phase serves as the conventional treatment for CPSF. Recently, many studies (11-13) have documented that during active inflammation, CPSF can be treated by incision and drainage combined with various endoscopic minimally invasive methods such as carbon dioxide laser cautery, chemical cauterization, electrocautery and radiofrequency ablation. These physical or chemical methods were used to close the internal ﬁstula or to destroy the entire ﬁstula structure. Subsequently, scars and adhesions form locally to close the fistula, thereby fundamentally cutting off the infection route and preventing secretions from the pharynx, upper respiratory tract, and digestive tract from entering the fistula and causing secondary infections. Reported success rates range from 83% to 100% without apparent complications (5-7,11). In 2016, a systematic review and meta-analysis (12) showed that there was no difference in success rates between endoscopic-assisted treatments and open neck surgery. Nicoucar et al. (14) recommended that open neck surgery should be performed only in patients older than 8 years of age due to its high complications when the children were younger. A multiple logistic regression analysis revealed that the age at the ﬁrst operation was an independent factor for the number of operations. Therefore, once the diagnosis is confirmed, endoscopic minimally invasive treatment is a good option.

In recent years, thulium laser has gained recognition in urology due to its rapid vaporization, precise cutting, effective hemostasis, and minimal thermal injury, demonstrating both safety and efficacy. Comparison with CO2 laser—the most widely reported endoscopic modality for CPSF—thulium laser has several theoretical advantages that warrant further investigation. The 1,940 nm wavelength of thulium laser is more closely matched to the absorption peak of water than the 10,600 nm wavelength of CO2 laser, potentially resulting in shallower penetration depth (~0.1–0.4 mm) and more localized thermal effect (15-17). These physical properties have been well characterized in urological applications (18) and may hypothetically translate to faster mucosal healing and reduced risk of injury to adjacent structures in the narrow piriform fossa (19). In our series, the mean time to complete sloughing of the white pseudomembrane was 8.22 days, whereas some CO2 laser series have reported wound recovery requiring 4–6 weeks. However, these comparisons are indirect and limited by differences in patient populations, wound assessment methods, and postoperative protocols. Whether thulium laser offers clinically meaningful advantages over CO2 laser in CPSF treatment cannot be determined without direct comparative studies.

Radiofrequency ablation has emerged as another effective endoscopic option for CPSF, with success rates comparable to those reported for laser-based approaches (20). The flexible probe used in radiofrequency ablation allows circumferential treatment of the fistula orifice at relatively low temperatures (~60 ℃). Thulium laser, by virtue of its fiber-based delivery system, may offer advantages for particularly deep or narrow fistulas where probe access is challenging; however, this remains a hypothesis based on the physical properties of the device rather than comparative clinical evidence. It should also be noted that the thulium laser operating handle had limited mechanical stability in our experience, necessitating careful operator technique—a consideration that may affect reproducibility across centers. In summary, the choice among CO2 laser, radiofrequency ablation, and thulium laser for endoscopic CPSF treatment currently depends on surgeon experience, equipment availability, and fistula characteristics. No modality has been demonstrated to be superior to others in a prospective comparative trial. Our findings with thulium laser should therefore be viewed as contributing to the growing body of descriptive evidence for this technique rather than establishing its comparative efficacy.

Several important limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the small sample size (n=9) limits the precision of complication and recurrence rate estimates and precludes subgroup analyses (e.g., by age, inflammatory status, or fistula characteristics). Second, the retrospective, single-center design introduces potential selection and information biases, and the results may not generalize to other institutions or surgeon experience levels. Third, the study lacks a comparison group (e.g., patients treated with CO2 laser, radiofrequency ablation, or open surgery during the same period), precluding any inference about the relative efficacy of thulium laser. However, Shanghai Children’s Hospital has extensive clinical experience in both endoscopic and open surgery (21) for piriform sinus fistulas. In the next step, we will increase the sample size and conduct a randomized controlled study. Fourth, the follow-up duration was limited (median 11.4 months, range 7.3–19.8 months), and late recurrences beyond the available follow-up window cannot be excluded; the true recurrence rate may therefore be higher than observed. Fifth, the exclusion criteria (patients with cystic lesions without demonstrable fistula tracts, prior cervical surgery, or significant scarring) may have selected a cohort with less complex anatomy and potentially more favorable treatment outcomes. Sixth, the limited sample size and follow-up duration preclude reliable detection of uncommon complications (e.g., vocal cord paresis, pharyngeal stenosis). Seventh, the uniform and relatively intensive postoperative protocol (14-day NG tube feeding, 7-day intravenous antibiotics) may have independently contributed to the favorable outcomes and should be considered when interpreting the specific effect of thulium laser cauterization. These limitations highlight the need for larger, prospective, multicenter studies with standardized protocols, comparison groups, and extended follow-up.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this preliminary case series suggests that endoscopic thulium laser cauterization is a technically feasible minimally invasive approach for selected pediatric patients with CPSF. The procedure was associated with rapid mucosal healing and no severe complications in this small cohort. However, the evidence remains preliminary owing to the limited sample size, retrospective design, single-center setting, and short follow-up duration. The observed outcomes should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of safety or efficacy. Future prospective, multicenter studies with larger samples and longer follow-up are needed to establish the role of thulium laser cauterization relative to established endoscopic and open surgical approaches.

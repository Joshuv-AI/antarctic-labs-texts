Nonequilibrium dynamics of star polymer in an active brownian particle bath
Authors: Yiqi Xia, Zihang Bao, Lixiang Cai, Zimu Lin, Run Ye, Zhuanglin Shen, Guoqiang Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Active matter systems exhibit unique nonequilibrium phenomena that bridge the fields of soft condensed matter and biological physics. In this study, we investigate the structural and dynamical behaviors of a six-armed star copolymer immersed in a dense bath of active Brownian particles (ABPs) via Langevin dynamics simulations. Our results reveal a universal scaling relation for rotational dynamics: ω ∼ Pe1.2, which is independent of the copolymer's bending rigidity κ, thereby confirming the Péclet number (Pe) as the key variable governing nonequilibrium kinetics. Through a torque balance analysis to rotational dissipation, we derive theoretical bounds on the scaling exponent and relate the observed value n ≈ 1.2 to the weakly nonlinear regime where activity-driven accumulation competes with self-propelled escape. We find that interaction potential parameters finely tune the system's behavior: stronger and longer-range interactions promote compact conformations at low ABP densities, while enhancing rotational dynamics. At higher ABP densities, crowding effects dominate, leading to non-monotonic structural and dynamical responses. The effective diffusivity follows Deff/D0 ∼ 1 + αPe2 at moderate Pe and transitions to a different scaling regime (Pe1.3) at high Pe. Our analysis reveals that rotational motion is a finite-range phenomenon: for short arm lengths, the polymer arms sustain bent conformations enabling ABP collection and persistent rotation; other active-particle-induced arm collapse causes rotation to cease. These findings provide a theoretical framework for the quantitative design of active polymer composites and have important implications for intelligent soft materials and micro-nano robotics.

Introduction

Active matter systems, composed of self-propelled units that continuously consume energy from their environment to generate motion,1–3 represent a rapidly evolving frontier in soft condensed matter and biological physics.4–10 These systems exhibit a wealth of nonequilibrium phenomena, such as collective motion, phase separation, and anomalous transport, which are not attainable in passive systems at thermal equilibrium. A particularly rich class of problems involves the interaction between passive deformable structures, like polymers, and a surrounding bath of active particles. This configuration serves as a paradigmatic model for numerous biological and synthetic systems, from chromosomal dynamics in living cells to the behavior of synthetic polymers in active gels11–14. The star polymer architecture, with its central core and radiating arms, introduces topological constraints and multiscale dynamics that can lead to novel nonequilibrium steady states under the relentless forcing of active particles.15–19

Recent theoretical and experimental studies have begun to unravel the complex interplay between activity and polymer conformation.20,21 For instance, the work demonstrated that active bath can induce swelling or collapse of polymer chains, a phenomenon highly dependent on the Péclet number (Pe),22,23 which characterizes the ratio of advective to diffusive transport. Concurrently, investigations into the role of polymer stiffness, as explored by,24,25 have revealed that chain rigidity can significantly modulate the structural response to active forcing. However, a comprehensive understanding of how the rotational dynamics of a tracer object, such as a star polymer, are governed by the properties of an active bath remains an open question.26–28 Furthermore, the quantitative relationship between interaction potential parameters (e.g., cutoff radius, interaction strength) and the emergent structural and dynamical properties of the mixture is not yet fully established, representing a critical gap in our ability to design and control active soft materials.29–32

Recent work by Yadav et al.27 has made significant progress in understanding the structural behavior of star polymers in dense active baths, demonstrating swelling and collapse transitions driven by activity. However, the dynamical response—particularly the rotational kinetics—of such systems remains largely unexplored. Moreover, previous studies have focused primarily on flexible chains (κ = 0) with limited parameter variations, leaving open the question of how polymer mechanical properties (e.g., bending rigidity) and interaction parameters modulate the dynamical scaling behavior.

In this study, we employ extensive Langevin dynamics simulations to investigate a model system comprising a single star copolymer (with six arms) immersed in a dense bath of active Brownian particles (ABPs) in two dimensions. The two-dimensional geometry is chosen for computational efficiency, enabling systematic exploration of a wide parameter space that would be prohibitive in three dimensions.33–35 Importantly, our 2D results are directly relevant to a broad class of quasi-2D experimental systems, including active colloids at fluid interfaces, confined bacterial suspensions, and cytoskeletal networks at surfaces.36–38 Our model explicitly incorporates key physical parameters: the Péclet number (Pe, characterizing the ratio of advective to diffusive transport intensity) to tune the activity level, the bending rigidity (κ) of the polymer arms to control their stiffness, and the properties of the Weeks-Chandler-Andersen (WCA) potential (cutoff radius rc, interaction strength εAS) to govern the steric interactions between the polymer and the ABPs.

We systematically explore how these parameters dictate the conformational state of the polymer, quantified by the mean square radius of gyration Rg2, and its rotational kinetics, characterized by the angular velocity ω. Our findings reveal a universal scaling law ω ∼ Pe1.2 for the rotational dynamics, which is independent of the chain bending stiffness. Through a torque balance analysis, we derive theoretical bounds (1 ≤ n ≤ 2) on the scaling exponent and connect the observed value to the swim pressure framework, providing physical insight into the mechanism.39 Moreover, we demonstrate that the interaction potential parameters exert a fine-tuning effect, where stronger and longer-ranged interactions can enhance dynamical responses while promoting more compact conformations at low active particle densities. At high densities, crowding effects dominate, leading to a non-monotonic dependence of both structural and dynamical observables on density.

Model and methods

In our two-dimensional model, we use the bead-spring chain to describe the star copolymer (with six arms, each arm has 10 monomers of diameter σ connected with a central bead, the arm length is N) embedded in a bath of ABPs in two dimensions, as shown in Fig. 1. The ABPs are treated as round disks with propelling force of amplitude Fa on each of them. The propelling force on each ABP is uncorrelated to each other and its direction n̂(t) reorients under fluctuation.

Here we set r0 = 1.0σ and k = 1 × 105kBT/σ2, so that the chain is nearly inextensible to prevent active particles from crossing through it.

But for ABPs, Fa > 0; Ui contains only the non-bonded purely repulsive potential; and eqn (2) is necessary to depict the coupled rotation of the driving direction. And the rotational noise ξi is also Gaussian with zero mean and ξj(t)ξl(t′) = δjlδ(t − t′). The DR is the rotational diffusion constant.

The Péclet number (Pe), the central control parameter of our study, is defined as , where va = Fa/γ is the active propulsion velocity. Physically, Pe quantifies the competition between advective transport due to active self-propulsion and diffusive transport due to thermal fluctuations. The convection originates from the self-propelling force Fa acting on each ABP, which creates persistent directional motion. We note that Pe and the rotational-translational diffusion ratio Δ = DT/(DRσ2) are independent control parameters in our simulations: Pe is varied by changing Fa, while Δ is varied by changing DR at fixed DT = 0.01. The collapse of data from different Δ values onto a single curve when plotted against Pe confirms Pe as the master variable governing rotational dynamics.

We use the home-modified LAMMPS software33–35 to perform the simulations. Square box of side length L = 4N (N is the arm length) with periodic conditions in both x and y directions is adopted. Reduced units are used in the simulations by setting σ = 1 and kBT = 1. The unit time is τ. And, we then set ε = 10kBT and the friction coefficient γ = 100kBTτ/σ2. We treat Dr as a parameter independent of DT as in the previous work, and set DR = 1 × 10−3, so that the persistent time of ABPs is long enough to manifest their active features. We tune the motility of ABPs by varying the amplitude of the propelling force, Fa (in unit of kBT/σ). All initial configurations are prepared at Fa = 0 via a long-time simulation. For each case, it is run by a minimum time of 1 × 104τ with a time step, t = 2 × 10−3τ.

Results and discussions

In the mixed systems, by fixing the active particle density ρA = 0.5, translational diffusion coefficient DT = 0.01, and rotational–translational diffusion ratio Δ = DT/DRσ2 (where σ denotes the particle characteristic length), we systematically investigate the influence of the Pe on chain structure and rotational dynamics, as well as the regulatory role of chain bending stiffness κ. In Fig. 2(a), for the flexible (κ = 0) star copolymer, the mean square radius of gyration (Rg2), a metric quantifying chain extension, exhibits a non-monotonic trend of initial increasing (expansion), subsequent decreasing (contraction), and final slight increasing (swelling) with increasing Pe. This reflects a conformational transition: under diffusion-dominated low-activity conditions, the star copolymer undergoes a conformational transition from collapsed to extended state due to intra-arm repulsion dominance, resulting in primary expansion; in contrast, under convection-dominated high-activity conditions, active forces induce bending deformation in its arms, significantly enhancing the collection efficiency of ABPs by increasing effective capture area, ultimately leading to secondary expansion caused by particle aggregation and subsequent rotation; under intermediate-activity conditions, ABPs rapidly move in and out between its arms, and due to the absence of bent arm conformations, the collection efficiency is poor, leading to conformational contraction. For κ > 0, Rg2 decreases monotonically with increasing Pe, and the larger κ, the more difficult the conformation contracts. Notably, stiffer arms (larger κ) exhibit higher Rg2 at a given Pe, indicating that increased rigidity suppresses coiling and favors extended states.

(a) The mean square of the radius of gyration Rg2vs. Pe and (b) angular velocity ω vs. Pe for a series of bending stiffness parameters. Here, the black dashed line in (b) represents the fit to the linear region, i.e., ω ∼ Pe1.2.

To determine the critical bending stiffness below which the non-monotonic behavior persists, we performed systematic simulations spanning κ = 0 to 100. As shown in the inset of Fig. 2(a), the non-monotonic behavior (characterized by the presence of a local minimum in 〈Rg2〉 vs. Pe) persists for κ ≤ κc ≈ 50, beyond which the stiffness is sufficient to suppress the secondary expansion phase. The physical interpretation is that when κ exceeds this critical value, the energetic cost of arm bending becomes too large for the active forces to induce the bent conformations necessary for particle collection and secondary expansion. For κ > κc, the arms remain effectively rigid across the entire Pe range, resulting in monotonic compaction with increasing activity.

In Fig. 2(b), at higher Pe, the angular velocity (ω), a measure of rotational rate, follows a power-law scaling with Pe, specifically ω ∼ Pe1.2 (denoted by the dashed fitting curve). Data points for different κ nearly overlap with the same fitting property, demonstrating that Pe governs the scaling relationship of rotational kinetics, while κ exerts negligible influence on this trend. It is worth noting that at lower Pe (Pe < 50), a larger κ makes it more difficult for arms to form rotating bent conformations, resulting in a slower ω; thus, the independence of ω from κ should be understood as valid in the high-Pe limit (Pe ≥ 50). Collectively, Pe serves as a critical control parameter governing both the structural conformation (via Rg2) and rotational dynamics (via ω) of active polymeric systems. Chain bending stiffness κ modulates the magnitude of structural responses (e.g., enhancing Rg2 for stiffer chains) but does not alter the scaling laws governing the Pe-dependence of either Rg2 or ω.

To illustrate the kinetics and the conformation of star copolymer with different rotational diffusion coefficients, we reveal the evolution laws of Rg2 and ω by tuning the Pe and Δ. As shown in Fig. 3(a), Rg2 decreases monotonically with the increase of Pe for a series of Δ, indicating that enhanced activity drives the star copolymer to transform from an “extended state” to a “compact state”; the quantitative differences of curves under different Δ reflect the “fine-tuning” effect of asymmetry on the spatial configuration. In Fig. 3(b), angular velocity ω increases monotonically with the increase of Pe for a series of Δ, and in the high Pe region, all curves approach the power-law scaling relationship ω ∼ Pe1.2. It should be emphasized that the increasing Δ leads to enhanced directionality of ABPs (i.e., weakened rotational diffusion capability), thereby significantly reducing the Pe required to drive the star copolymer into the scaling law regime. This confirms that the system enters a universal dynamic regime under strong activity, where the influence of asymmetry is masked by the activity-dominated dynamic process. The above results provide key experimental evidence for understanding the collective behavior and “structure-dynamics” correlation of active matter, and also offer theoretical guidance for the design of active functional materials.

(a) The mean square of the radius of gyration R2gvs. Pe and (b) angular velocity ω vs. Pe for a series of Δ at DT = 0.01. Here, the magenta dashed line in (b) represents the fit to the linear region, i.e., ω ∼ Pe1.2.

The observed scaling ω ∼ Pe1.2, while empirical in origin, can be understood through a torque balance between active forcing and rotational dissipation. We construct a minimal model that relates the scaling exponent to the efficiency of ABP accumulation at the polymer arms. The active torque arises from ABPs that accumulate in the inter-arm regions and exert net tangential forces on the polymer arms. The number of accumulated ABPs per arm tip scales as Nacc ∼ ρaσ2(1 + cPeα), where the first term accounts for passive collisions and the second term captures activity-driven accumulation. The exponent 0 < α < 1 reflects the sublinear efficiency of ABP accumulation: at high Pe, rapidly moving particles partially escape capture, leading to α < 1 rather than linear scaling. The active torque is then τa ∼ Narm·Nacc·Fa·σ, where Narm = 6 is the number of arms and Fa = PekBT/σ is the propelling force.

In the overdamped regime, the rotational drag is τdrag = −γRω, with γR the rotational friction coefficient. Equating active torque to drag in the high-Pe limit gives ω ∼ Pe1+α. This yields the theoretical bounds 1 ≤ n ≤ 2: n = 1 corresponds to the passive collision limit (linear response) and n = 2 corresponds to full ABP capture (maximally nonlinear accumulation). The observed value n ≈ 1.2 corresponds to α ≈ 0.2, indicating weakly efficient ABP accumulation. This is physically reasonable: ABPs at intermediate-to-high Pe spend sufficient time near the polymer arms to create an asymmetric density distribution, but their self-propelled motion prevents full capture. The weakly nonlinear exponent thus places the system slightly above the linear-response limit, consistent with the crossover from diffusive to advective transport.

We connect the rotational scaling ω ∼ Pe1.2 to the swim pressure Ps = ρakBTs, captures the activity-induced pressure. This provides a fundamental mechanical bridge from the observed scaling to a fundamental energy-pressure relation.39 The pressure gradient across the polymer arms exerts a net torque proportional to ∇PS·Leff, where Leff is the effective arm length yielding the same effective scaling and providing a thermodynamic foundation for the observed exponent.

In the simulations of mixed systems, the cutoff radius Rc and the polymer-active particle interaction (characterized by εAS) are pivotal parameters that govern both the static conformation and dynamic behavior of the system. As shown in Fig. 4(a), Rg2 shows a monotonically decreasing trend for a series of cutoff radius-interaction potential pair. For a given εAS, the conformation of star copolymer is almost unaffected by the interaction nature between ABPs and arms (Rc = 26σ, repulsion; Rc = 2.5σ, effectively attractive). We note that the LJ potential with Rc = 2.5σ includes a weak attractive tail at intermediate distances (r > 1.2σ), which, combined with geometric confinement and activity-induced accumulation, promotes ABP trapping in the inter-arm regions. However, the potential remains strongly repulsive at short distances, and the observed compaction arises from a combination of these effects rather than genuine long-range attraction.40 Additionally, at the same cutoff radius, under lower Pe, a stronger εAS tends to attract ABPs into the inter-arm regions, leading to an expansion of star copolymer conformation. Under higher Pe, frequent collisions between ABPs and arms weaken the star copolymer ability to collect particles. Consequently, compared to weaker εAS, its conformation is more prone to compression. Fig. 4(b) presents the double logarithmic coordinate relationship between ω and Pe for a series of cutoff radius-interaction potential pair. The research results show that under different combinations of εAS and Rc, ω exhibits a monotonically increasing trend with the increase of Pe. Moreover, under higher Pe, there exists a power-law dependence relationship between ω and Pe, i.e., ω ∼ Pe1.2, demonstrating the same scaling characteristics.

In Fig. 5(a), the Rg2 (with a range approximately from 25 to 38) is plotted as a function of ρA (logarithmic scale, ranging from 10−3 to 100). R2g exhibits a non-monotonic trend with respect to ρA, for two cutoff radius-interaction potential pairs, characterized by an initial decrease, followed by an increase, and then a subsequent decrease. For the low-density regime (10−3–10−2), higher ρA promotes the contraction of star copolymer. In the intermediate-density regime (10−2–10−1), ω reaches a minimum and then gradually increases, which shows the dynamic balance between the “hindering effect” of interparticle interactions and the “driving effect” of density in this regime. In the high-density regime (10−1–100), Rg2 drops significantly, especially for the stronger εAS, demonstrating the spatial compression effect dominated by interparticle interactions at high densities. To distinguish fluid-like crowding from cluster-phase confinement, we note that at ρA = 0.5 (our primary simulation condition), the system remains in a fluid state well below the motility-induced phase separation (MIPS) threshold. At ρA = 1.0, the observed compaction may reflect a combination of crowding in the dense phase and mechanical pressure from cluster boundaries rather than purely fluid-like crowding. Additionally, the values of Rg2 at stronger εAS are consistently lower than those at weaker εAS across all density regimes, implying that interaction strength can compress the spatial distribution by enhancing particle aggregation tendencies.

The Fig. 6(b) analyzes the characteristic quantity ω vs. N. Our simulations reveal a striking finite-range behavior: rotational motion exists only for N < 30. For short arms (N < 30), the polymer arms retain sufficient rigidity to sustain bent conformations that enable ABP collection in the inter-arm regions, resulting in persistent rotation (>2.5 cycles). For long arms (N > 30), active-particle-induced softening causes the arm length to become comparable to the effective persistence length. The star arms collapse onto each other, the capture area for ABPs diminishes drastically, and rotation essentially ceases (<0.5 cycles, indistinguishable from rotational diffusion) (in the Fig. S1(b))

Thus, the ω(N) relationship describes a finite-N phenomenon (N = 3–30) rather than an asymptotic scaling regime. Within this rotation-supporting range, the exponential fit ω ∼ exp(−1.83N) captures the rapid suppression of rotation as the chains become longer and more susceptible to active-induced collapse. This is fundamentally different from the Rouse/Zimm scenario, where motion persists at all chain lengths and power-law scaling emerges from hydrodynamic interactions. In our system, the exponential form reflects the abrupt disappearance of rotation when the arms can no longer sustain the asymmetric bent conformations required for torque generation. We have confirmed the exponential fit outperforms the power-law alternative (ω ∼ N−β) within the finite range N = 3–30. To investigate the mesoscopic diffusion behaviors of tracer (star copolymer) in ABP bath, we first analyze the mean squared displacement (MSD) of tracer particles. In Fig. 7(a), three distinct MSD curves (MSD(t)B, MSD(t)C, and MSD(t)B2C) are plotted against time t on a log-log scale. For short times (t ≪ τ), all MSDs exhibit ballistic scaling (∝ t2), indicative of directed motions within the suspension. As time progresses into the intermediate regime (t ∼ τ), the MSD(t)B2C curve displays pronounced oscillations superimposed on a diffusive background, with an oscillation period T and angular frequency ω = 2π/T, as highlighted in the inset of MSD(t)B2C. This oscillatory feature reflects the periodic forcing or interaction-driven collective dynamics in the system. At longer times (t ≫ τ), MSD(t)B and MSD(t)C gradually transition to normal diffusion (∝ t), while MSD(t)B2C maintains a reduced diffusivity due to the interference between different motional modes.

To quantify the influence of internally generated active stress on tracer transport, we examine the effective diffusion coefficient Deff as a function of the Pe. Fig. 7(b) shows that Deff/D0 (where D0 is the diffusion coefficient of tracer in a two-dimensional bulk system) scales nonlinearly with Pe. In the low Pe regime, Deff/D0 of tracer can be expressed as 1 + αPe2, as shown in Fig. 7(b), where α ≈ Δ/2. This indicates that prior to rotation, the tracer exhibits kinetic behavior analogous to ABPs in a two-dimensional bulk system when driven by the ABP bath.36–38 In the high Pe regime, the data reveal a power-law relationship between Deff/D0 and Pe, i.e., Deff/D0 ∼ Pe1.3. This enhanced scaling exponent suggests that the coupling between active particle motion and the activity-induced velocity gradients leads to nontrivial steric interactions, which collectively modify the long-time transport properties of tracers. The parameters (κ = 100, ρA = 0.50, and DT = 0.01) used in the simulations ensure a strongly coupled active-suspension environment, where both active Brownian motion and activity-induced correlations play crucial roles in determining the diffusion kinetics. Collectively, our analysis integrates the key observations from the MSD to analysis (different time scaling, oscillatory behavior) and the effective diffusivity trends with Pe, while also contextualizing the physical mechanisms (ballistic to diffusive transitions, shear-active coupling, steric effects) and numerical parameters.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive investigation into the nonequilibrium dynamics of star polymer architectures in active Brownian particle bath through Langevin dynamics simulations. Our results establish that the structural and dynamical properties are governed by the synergistic coupling between activity-driven transport (quantified by the Péclet number), polymer mechanical properties (characterized by bending rigidity κ), and interparticle interaction parameters. The Péclet number emerges as the fundamental scaling parameter, with the mean square radius of gyration exhibiting monotonic expansion (Rg2 ∼ Peγ) and rotational dynamics following universal scaling (ω∼Pe1.2) independent of chain stiffness. This decoupling between scaling exponents and mechanical parameters reveals that rotational transport is dominated by active forcing rather than polymer intrinsic properties, though stiffness modulates the absolute magnitude of conformational response.

The interparticle potential parameters demonstrate sophisticated modulation of system behavior, where enhanced interaction strength and extended cutoff radius promote structural compaction at low densities through intensified steric constraints while amplifying rotational dynamics via collective motion. At elevated densities, crowding effects dominate, yielding non-monotonic dependence with observed re-entrant swelling under strong interactions. Transport analysis reveals multifarious diffusion regimes with markedly enhanced diffusivity following Deff/D0 ∼ 1 + αPe2 at moderate activity, crossing over to sublinear Pe1.3 scaling at high Pe.

While our simulations are performed in two dimensions, the key physical mechanisms identified are expected to remain qualitatively valid in 3D. The Péclet number, being a dimensionless ratio of advective to diffusive transport, retains its physical meaning independent of dimensionality. In 3D, the star polymer would have additional conformational degrees of freedom, and the active particles would explore a larger volume, potentially weakening the effective coupling between the polymer and the bath. We predict that the scaling exponent n ≈ 1.2 for rotational dynamics should persist in 3D for rotations about an axis perpendicular to the active bath plane, but with an enhanced prefactor due to the increased number of arms contributing to the torque. Our 2D results are directly relevant to quasi-2D experimental systems including active colloids at fluid interfaces, confined bacterial suspensions, and cytoskeletal networks at surfaces.36–38 The particularly important finding is that rotational motion is not an asymptotic property but exists only in a finite range of arm lengths. For N < 30, the polymer arms sustain bent conformations enabling ABP collection and persistent rotation (>2.5 cycles); for N > 30, active-particle-induced arm collapse causes rotation to cease (<0.5 cycles). This finite-range behavior reflects the competition between active forcing and chain flexibility, with the exponential suppression ω ∼ exp(−1.83N) capturing the collapse-induced shutdown of rotation rather than standard polymer dynamics.

These findings establish quantitative design principles for active polymer composites. Specifically, for chromosomal dynamics, our finding that Pe governs rotational kinetics independently of stiffness suggests that the activity level of the bacterial cytoplasm (rather than the mechanical properties of the chromosome itself) determines the characteristic rotation timescale of chromosomal domains. For micro-robotic applications, our scaling law provides a design principle for activity-driven rotary micromotors, where the rotation speed can be programmed by tuning the bath activity (Pe) rather than the motor design. For active gels, our results on crowding-induced non-monotonicity provide insight into how density inhomogeneities affect the dynamics of embedded probes.39–41

This work bridges the gap between single-particle active motion and the collective behavior of complex macromolecules. It provides a quantitative framework for predicting the structural and dynamical properties of passive tracers in active environments, with direct implications for the design of active microrheological probes, the understanding of intracellular transport, and the development of novel self-assembling active materials whose function is governed by the tight coupling between structure and dynamics.

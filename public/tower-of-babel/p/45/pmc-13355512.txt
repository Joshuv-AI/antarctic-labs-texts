Re: Left ventricular myocardial work and its determinants in healthy subjects and the possible potential correlation with the ventricular myocardial band
Authors: Çağrı Zorlu, Veli Akın
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the study by Luo et al. investigating left ventricular myocardial work (MW) indices in 479 healthy subjects and proposing a potential correlation with the ventricular myocardial band (VMB) model of Torrent-Guasp [1]. While the effort to establish segmental MW reference values is appreciated, several methodological, interpretive, and conceptual issues warrant clarification.

The authors claim this is the “first study” to establish normal reference values for segmental MW (SMW) across an 18-segment model [1]. However, prior studies have already reported global and segmental MW values stratified by age and sex using similar methodology [2,3]. Their findings of higher MW in females and age-related decline (especially after 55 years in women) are broadly consistent with existing literature [4].

Several internal inconsistencies appear in the results. For example, global work index (GWI) and global constructive work (GCW) are reported as higher in females, yet some segmental constructive work values in the tables do not consistently support the described “V/U” or “fishhook” patterns for active work. The use of broad age groups (especially ≥55 years), unequal subgroup sizes, and blood pressure measurement performed after echocardiography may have influenced the pressure-strain loop calculations.

The most speculative part of the study is the proposed correlation between MW distribution and the VMB model. The Torrent-Guasp helical band concept remains highly controversial. Many experts consider it an artifact of blunt dissection rather than a true anatomical structure, with current evidence favoring a myocardial mesh or laminar sheet model supported by histology and diffusion tensor MRI [5,6]. Linking bull's-eye MW patterns directly to VMB segments without quantitative spatial mapping or validation with other imaging modalities (such as tagged MRI for myocardial torsion) appears overstated.

Questions for the authors:1.How were the 18 echocardiographic segments precisely mapped to the specific components of the VMB beyond visual inference?2.Were segmental MW values adjusted for regional wall thickness or curvature?3.What is the statistical strength (e.g., correlation coefficients) of the proposed MW-VMB relationship?

This study provides useful MW reference data in a healthy population. However, greater caution is needed when linking results to a debated anatomical model. Rigorous multi-modality validation is required before clinical extrapolation, particularly in ischemic heart disease. We look forward to the authors’ response.

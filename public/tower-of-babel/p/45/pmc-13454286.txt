Application of artificial neural networks to solve differential equations in Zika virus dynamics with exponential kernel derivatives
Authors: Ahmad Shafee, Kavikumar Jacob, Naveed Iqbal, Ahmed A Hamoud
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Zika virus is a relatively new pathogen that has quickly become a major issue in virology. Infectious mosquitoes of the Aedes genus, particularly Aedes aegypti, spread the Zika virus in tropical and subtropical areas. Most bites from Aedes mosquitoes occur during the daytime. Dengue, chikungunya, and urban yellow fever are all spread by the same insects. Mathematical modeling has helped researchers better understand the mechanisms of Zika virus invasion and evaluate prospective management techniques. In this study, we suggest a unique approach to employing an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to solve the system of differential equations describing the susceptible people, the susceptible vectors, the infected humans, the infectious vectors, and the recovered humans. Applying a unique Caputo-Fabrizio derivative yields semi-analytical findings for the model. The system of differential equations used to create the data is solved using the Laplace Adomian Decomposition (LAD) method. This makes use of a supervised learning framework for training the ANN using LAD-generated data. By comparing the ANN’s projected solution to the LAD solution and determining the average error across all system functions, we can evaluate the ANN’s accuracy.Keywords: Zika virus, Artificial Neural Network, Caputo-Fabrizio derivative, Laplace Adomian decomposition methodSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Zika virus (ZIKV) was first identified in 1947, in a sentinel rhesus monkey in the Zika Forest of Uganda, and over decades it was considered the insignificant pathogen responsible only of the occasional human case in Africa and Asia1. ZIKV appeared as a major public health issue in 2007 when an estimated 73 percent of the population in the Pacific Island of Yap, Federated States of Micronesia, was infected in the first large scale outbreak outside of Africa2. A larger outbreak in French Polynesia in 2013-2014 followed, and this was the first outbreak reported to cause severe neurological complications, such as Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)3. ZIKV is a disease of global importance, as it has been introduced to Brazil in 2015, and rapidly spread to the rest of the Americas leading to unprecedented outbreak of the virus in the region4. At this time, there were simultaneous outbreaks of dengue, chikungunya, and Zika in the Pacific, which demonstrated the tricky relations among co-circulating arboviruses5. A notable feature of the Brazilian epidemic was the initial documented linkage of ZIKV infection to a massive surge of cases of microcephaly in newborns, which made ZIKV no longer a benign agent but a teratogen6. Later autochthonous spread was reported in Colombia and other Latin American nations and this proved the rapid transmission of the disease across the continent7.

ZIKV spread was unprecedented; researchers emphasized the importance of air travel and the ubiquitous nature of Aedes mosquitoes in supporting the emergence of the virus in new areas8,9. The outbreak in French Polynesia also gave key information that ZIKV infection was associated with Guillain-Barré syndrome, a severe autoimmune disease and may cause long-term paralysis10. In the next outbreak of chikungunya in French Polynesia, the number of GBS cases also increased, which once again demonstrated the possibility of arboviruses to provoke neurological complications11. The most tragic impact of ZIKV infection was, perhaps, seen in the 2015 outbreak in Brazil: the rate of newborns with microcephaly has increased sharply; congenital Zika syndrome was acknowledged as a reality12. In an attempt to comprehend the transmission of ZIKV, initial mathematical modeling of the basic reproduction number in the South Pacific offered important information on the epidemic potential of the virus13. With the progression of the epidemic, mathematical modeling became an invaluable tool to predict the progression of the outbreak and determine the effectiveness of control measures. Basic research on optimization of the control of diseases transmitted by vectors preconditioned the use of these methods applied to ZIKV14. The concept of memory and hereditary effects was now modeled using fractional calculus, starting with applications to the dynamics of infectious diseases, such as the investigation of HIV dynamics using the Caputo-Fabrizio derivative15. The spread of anti-vector and anti-viral resistance was analyzed using classic epidemiological models of malaria resistance that were subsequently applied to ZIKV16.

The methodology blueprints of optimal control of malaria, including the studies of drug resistance and chemotherapy, were then used to guide ZIKV interventions17,18. Subsequent reviews further highlighted the significance of the Yap Island outbreak and confirmed the role of the event as a pivotal point in the history of ZIKV19. Optimal control theory, an idea first introduced by Pontryagin, was the mathematical basis of the design of time varying intervention strategies20. ZIKV transmission potential estimates were improved based on South Pacific data, which enhanced the accuracy of the calculations of the value through enhancements in estimates of potential transmission rates21. Van den Driessche and Watmough formalised the next-generation matrix method, which became a common calculator of reproduction numbers in compartmental models22. Other infectious diseases, including swine flu, were also treated using optimal control proving the versatility of the methodology used23. Detailed studies of ZIKV virology and clinical manifestations summarized information on the biology and pathology of the virus24. The contribution of international travel to the transmission of ZIKV in French Polynesia to Brazil was also examined, with mobility being a significant contributor to global transmission rates25. High-risk areas were predicted based on predictive models that forecasted ZIKV international expansion in Brazil, and this information was used to prepare the whole population health-wise in the event of an outbreak in other countries26.

One of the key developments in the modeling of ZIKV was the inclusion of the sexual transmission route that has been proven by clinical evidence and was demonstrated to have a substantial impact on the dynamics of an outbreak27. The centre manifold theory has enabled scholars to study the bifurcation in epidemic models, which have offered a better understanding of the stability of the disease equilibria28. Global stability in SIR and SIRS models was shown using Lyapunov functions, which enhanced theoretically the foundations of compartmental methods29. The dynamic diffusion of ZIKV in the Pacific was observed in detail with references to the capacity of the virus to introduce mass outbreaks among unsuspecting people in the population30. The study of mathematical epidemiology gave an in-depth frontier of the principles behind modeling of infectious diseases31. The occurrence of ZIKV in the Americas was declared a public health emergency and the response contributed to the concept of integrated surveillance and modeling being needy32. Phylogenetic and epidemiological study of ZIKV origin and dissemination globally provided pathways to the pandemic status of this virus33.

Estimation of inaccessible prevalence through cases of microcephaly showed the usefulness of alternative data in model calibration or fitting the model or finding the prospects of vaccines, which was quantified with the help of the challenges and opportunities offered by the vaccines34,35. This was followed by memory effects in fractional-order models to capture more realistic immune and intervention histories36, and a more general approach to stability and control using the Mittag-Leffler kernel37, and optimal control problems with backward bifurcations using memetic algorithms38. Several optimal controls (mosquito control, condom use, treatment) were also examined as well as sexual transmission39 and diffusion effects were considered under the account of spatial spread40 and foundational models also incorporated important transmission routes and controls in the form of the homotopy perturbation method41,42.

The application of fractional calculus to malaria, tuberculosis, monkeypox, and co-infections was shown through the use of the fractional-order model, and the comparative performance of Caputo-Fabrizio versus Atangana-Baleanu operators showing the effect of the choice of kernel used43–49. LADM was applied successfully to fractional systems to calculate solutions to COVID-19, tuberculosis, and co-infections with awareness, vaccination, and hospitalization, and has been shown to be sufficiently effective in such cases, as well as non-epidemic conditions without vaccines and spread of the coronavirus infection, the influenza virus, and other diseases50–55. Fractional operators were used to capture memory and heterogeneity in models of monkeypox and HIV co-infection models and others56–71.

Differential equations have been revolutionized by neural network procedures. The initial efforts laid the foundations of data-driven models to ordinary and partial differential equations72,73 and optimization methods made computations more efficient74. Deep learning was later to be extended to parabolic high-dimensional PDEs and backward stochastic equations and became a powerful tool for solving nonlinear PDEs75,76. The methods allowed the discovery of solutions to nonlinear dispersive equations through data-driven methods and forward-inverse methods to the logarithmic nonlinear Schrodinger equation77. Theoretical basis of fractional modeling was given by foundational literature on fractional differential equations78,79. The exponential memory decay of systems became accessible to systems with the Caputo-Fabrizio derivative, whose non-singular kernel had new applications, and this was applied to the differential equations80,81. Fractional-order epidemic models established the effect of the fractional order on dynamics of diseases82 and dynamic optimization of biological systems83 and analysis of stability using comparison principles84 and Lyapunov-type inequalities using exponential kernels85. The Laplace-Adomian decomposition method (LADM) was useful in the solution of nonlinear fractional equations, and it has successfully been applied to smoking models86, as well as traveling wave solutions87 and initial value problems88,89. The use of the Caputo-Fabrizio operators and existence theory helped develop the fractional modeling outline further and solidify it even more, through further analysis90,91. The computational formulation of HIV dynamics was used to gain an understanding of immune responses and optimum control92–95. Global existence theory and chaos control were both means to provide fundamental mathematical principles96. ZIKV modeling was further refined using homotopy perturbation and other techniques97, while the application of serial interval estimation enabled more precise forecasting98.

Major progress has been achieved in numerical techniques and fractional-order modeling of ZIKV but there are a number of serious gaps. The memory effects in ZIKV transmission have been captured by the use of fractional calculus36,37. Related fractional systems have been solved using the Laplace-Adomian decomposition method (LADM) successfully50,54,65. However, most of the studies made have been based on conventional analytical or pure numerical resolvers. Combining machine learning, especially physics-informed neural networks76, with fractional epidemic models has been mostly studied on classical integer-order systems or other diseases. The coherent combination of data-driven neural networks approximation, fractional operators, and semi-analytical solution methods to ZIKV dynamics does not exist in the literature. In order to cover this gap, the current study proposes a new approach. It uses an artificial neural network (ANN) that has been trained with high fidelity LADM-generated solutions of a Caputo-Fabrizio fractional ZIKV model. This framework is a synergistic combination of three properties, including the exponential memory kernel of the Caputo-Fabrizio derivative, the semi-analytical quality of the LADM, and the predictive ability of supervised learning. This is an effective surrogate model that can be used to represent the susceptible, infected and recovered populations. It also developed a new paradigm to solve the fractional epidemiological models. The performance of ANN is also strictly checked against the LADM solutions. This provides a computationally efficient way of real-time forecasting and analysis of scenarios. This piece of work therefore bridges an important gap in methodology. It shows the initial application of fractional calculus, LADM, and neural networks specially developed to model the transmission of the ZIKV. With this kind of synergy, more flexible, data-adaptive tools in new infectious disease research become possible.

When trying to understand the dynamics of problems in biology and physics that occur in the real world, derivatives of integer orders are frequently insufficient. Researchers have been working on fractional calculus to remedy this deficiency for decades. It is also well-known that mathematicians increasingly use fractional calculus in mathematical modelling. Multiple definitions exist for derivatives and integrals involving non-integer values. There are several well-known definitions available, including those from Riemann and Liouvilli78, Caputo79, etc. Derivatives of the kernel of the singular type are explored. Justification of the caputo–fabrizio fractional derivative

The choice of CFFD in this work is both biologically and mathematically determined. In contrast to the classical Caputo derivative, whose kernel is described by single power-law, and the Atangana-Baleanu (AB) derivative whose kernel is described by Mittag-Leffler kernel, the CFFD operator is defined with the help of a non-singular exponential kernel.

Biologically, the exponential kernel is a finite and exponentially-decaying memory effect. This property is especially appropriate in the modeling of the dynamics of ZIKV, where the effect of the past state, including the previous infection, immune reactions, and prevention by vectors, decay with time. This fading memory behavior can be explained by factors like short life span of the mosquitoes, environmental variation and recovery of the infected individuals. Thus, exponential kernel offers a more plausible outline of the temporal dynamics of the interactions between vectors and hosts than the long-memory kernels.

Conversely, the Caputo derivative has a strong long-term memory (via its power-law kernel) which can over estimate persistence of past infections in rapidly evolving epidemic systems. In the same way, the AB derivative, even though non-singular, has a more general memory effect, because of its Mittag-Leffler kernel, which might not be quite consistent with short-term dynamics of the disease spread by vectors.

Mathematically, the CFFD derivative has even more benefits since it is non-singular, which makes its analytical and numerical treatment easier. Specifically, it makes available the use of semi-analytical methodologies like the Laplace Adomian Decomposition (LAD) method that is used in this paper. This enables effective calculation and at the same time retention of the most important dynamical peculiarities of the model.

Thus, the CFFD offers a realistic balanced framework that involves realistic short-memory effects in the transmission of ZIKV and preserves the mathematical tractability.

Artificial neural network (ANN)

One kind of machine learning model used is an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) regression model, which uses input variables to predict continuous results. To create the required output, the incoming data is processed and modified by multiple layers of connected nodes, also known as neurons. Each layer of an ANN has a distinct mathematical operation applied to the data. In an ANN, the three layers that comprise it are the input layer, the hidden layers, and the output layer, as shown in Fig. 2.

Fundamental design of an ANN.

The input layer is in charge of receiving features from the user and passing them on to the subsequent layers. Intervening between the two levels of input and output are the layers that are considered concealed. The mathematical operations performed by each hidden layer neuron are known as the activation function. The activation function provides nonlinearity to the model, allowing it to detect intricate correlations in the data. This study used the ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) algorithm. The architecture comprising the ANN in this study has an input layer, which has one neuron (time t), three hidden layers, including 32, 64, and 32 neurons respectively, and an output layer with five neurons that represent the state variables , , , , and . All hidden layers employ ReLU activation function and the most final layer employs a linear activation function. Adam optimizer was used to train the network and the learning rate was set to . The loss function was the mean squared error (MSE) and the size of the batch was 32. It was trained in 250, 500 and 1000 epochs to investigate how much the training time affects the accuracy of prediction.

The training data of the ANN have been obtained by solving the CFFD system 2 by the LADM. The time space was uniformly sampled over 0 to 30 days with a time step of 0.1 days resulting in 301 time points. The solutions of the LADM to all the five state variables were calculated at these grid points. The input or output data was not normalized since the state variables take similar orders of magnitude, but the input time was rescaled to the range of [0, 1]/final time to ensure that the training was stable. Training was done with the full dataset and no sampling was reduced to ensure that the ANN was trained on the entire solution trajectory.

In order to evaluate the generalization ability of the ANN, the 301 time points dataset was randomly divided into the training and test sets (80% and 20% respectively). The network weights were updated by the training set and the test set was completely excluded in the training process and only applied to final evaluation. Also, to check overfitting, as well as to choose the most appropriate model weights, a validation split of 20% of the training data was also applied in the course of training. The Keras fit function was used with validation split=0.2 to implement this validation split. The performance measures and the average percentage error (APE) are calculated on the test set, thus, giving an objective estimate of predictive accuracy of the model.

Discussion on training behavior and fractional order selection

Figure 4–6 of the loss curves show oscillatory behavior with distinct spikes in the training process. The attributed reasons of this behavior include the nonlinearity and coupling nature of the underlying ZIKV model and the complexity of the resulting training data produced by using the LAD method. As the LAD solution uses truncated series approximations, even minor changes in higher-order terms can cause variations in the training data and consequently, can cause variation in training of the ANN.

Also, the loss function may cause oscillations when using the Adam optimizer, which is efficient in most cases, in case of stiff and highly nonlinear problems. The sensitivity of the model is further enhanced by the existence of several interacting compartments which causes the training process to temporarily oscillate around the optimal parameter values before stabilizing. Regardless of these fluctuations, the general downward trend of the loss function shows that the network is successfully learning the underlying dynamics, also confirmed by the fact that the ANN predictions and the LAD solutions are almost equal and the average percentage errors (APE) values are very low.

As far as the fractional-order parameter is concerned, in this research, the simulations are carried out with a constant fractional order ϑ = 0.75. This value is selected because it gives a moderate representation between classical integer-order dynamics (ϑ = 1) and strong memory effects of smaller order. The biologically important effect of this intermediate value is the weakened memory effect seen in the dynamics of the transmission of vector-borne diseases in which the past states affect the current dynamics but with decreasing intensity.

Convergence and truncation error analysis of LAD

The Laplace Adomian Decomposition (LAD) method expresses the solution of the system as an infinite series. The convergence of this series is generally guaranteed under standard conditions such as boundedness of the solution and Lipschitz continuity of the nonlinear terms. In the proposed ZIKV model, all state variables remain positive and bounded, which supports the convergence of the LAD series.

In practical implementation, the infinite series is truncated to obtain an approximate solution. Let X(t) denote the exact solution and represent the truncated series after N terms. The truncation error is defined as

As N increases, the magnitude of decreases due to the rapidly diminishing contribution of higher-order terms in the series. In this study, a second-order truncation (N=2) is employed, which provides a good balance between computational efficiency and accuracy. The validity of this approximation is further supported by the close agreement between the LAD solution and the Artificial Neural Network (ANN) predictions, as well as the low values of the average percentage error (APE) observed in the numerical results. Therefore, the truncated LAD series offers a reliable and efficient approximation for analyzing the dynamics of the ZIKV model.

Conclusion

In this paper, a fractional-order Zika virus model is presented using the Caputo-Fabrizio derivative and numerically solved using the LAD method; the model is validated using an ANN framework. The results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid approach is able to capture the non-linear dynamics of ZIKV transmission. The study emphasizes the importance of vector control measures such as insecticidal control, elimination of breeding grounds and environmental management to control the spread of disease. It also focuses on the need for early diagnosis, treatment and awareness of the public to prevent infection and control outbreaks. The proposed framework is a useful mathematical tool in understanding the dynamics of ZIKV and it will be a base for future studies. Further extensions may include additional transmission routes, such as sexual transmission, and validation using real epidemiological data to enhance predictive accuracy.

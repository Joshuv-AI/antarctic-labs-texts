Prognostic significance of the Borrmann classification in stage 2–3 gastric cancer
Authors: Selcan Cesur, Yonca Yılmaz Ürün
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objectives:To assess the prognostic significance of Borrmann classification subtypes in patients with stage 2–3 gastric cancer who underwent surgical treatment.Methods:A retrospective review of 96 gastric cancer patients treated at Van Yüzüncü Yıl University Medical Faculty Hospital from 2008 to 2024 was conducted. Patients were included if they were aged >18 year (yr), had undergone surgery without neoadjuvant therapy, had no distant metastases, and had a postoperative pathological stage of 2-3. Demographic and clinicopathological data were documented, and median overall survival (OS) was analyzed according to the Borrmann classification.Results:The median age of the 96 patients was 66 yr, and 36.4% were female. Total gastrectomy was performed in 45.8% of cases. According to the Borrmann classification, type 2 tumors (49%) were the most common. The tumor was most frequently located in the antrum and pylorus (45.8%), and adenocarcinoma was the predominant histopathological type (77.1%). Recurrence was observed in 63.5% of cases. The median OS was 54.66 months, with survival rates of 74.8% at 2 yr and 48.7% at 5 yr. Among Borrmann subtypes, type 1 tumors had the highest median OS (110.1 months) (p=0.787).Conclusion:Although patients with Borrmann type 1 tumors had the highest OS rate, no significant prognostic differences were observed across the four Borrmann subtypes.

Gastric cancer continues to be a major global health concern and ranks among the leading causes of cancer-related deaths, with an estimated annual mortality exceeding 750,000 individuals worldwide.1 Despite therapeutic advancements, the prognosis for locally advanced gastric cancer patients is still unfavorable.2-4 Identifying prognostic factors is essential for enhancing patient outcomes and formulating effective treatment approaches.

Gastric cancer is a highly heterogeneous disease in terms of morphology, biology, and clinical presentation.5 Several prognostic factors have been identified in efforts to refine risk stratification, including lymph node metastasis, lymph node metastasis rate, tumor size,and the presence of gastrointestinal metaplasia.6-10

Among classification systems, the Borrmann classification—based on the Japanese guidelines for gastric carcinoma—is one of the most widely utilized macroscopic categorization tool. This system categorizes tumors as follows: type 1 (polypoid), type 2 (ulcerative), type 3 (infiltrative ulcerative), and type 4 (diffuse infiltrative) (Figure 1).11 This classification system is widely utilized to characterize the macroscopic features of gastric tumors and inform prognosis. Additionally, Her-2 positivity has been associated with preoperative treatment response and recurrence risk.12,13 Understanding these correlations may aid in clinical decision-making.

Given this context, the present study sought to assess the prognostic significance of different Borrmann subtypes in patients diagnosed with stage 2-3 gastric cancer who underwent surgical treatment.

Methods

This retrospective study included patients diagnosed with gastric cancer who were treated at Van Yüzüncü Yıl, University Faculty of Medicine Hospital, Van, Turkey between 2008 and 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age >18 year (yr), surgical treatment without neoadjuvant treatment, absence of distant metastases, and postoperative pathological stage II or III. Patients were excluded if they were aged <18 yr, had multiple primary malignancies, had stage I or IV disease, did not undergo surgery underwent non-curative surgery, had missing data on records or received neoadjuvant therapy. A total of 96 patients who were eligible were included in the study.

Tumor staging was performed using the 8th edition of the pathological tumor, lymph node, and metastasis (pTNM) staging system. Overall survival (OS) was characterized as the duration from the surgical procedure to either the time of death or the most recent follow-up. The following demographic and clinicopathological data were collected: demographic variables (age, gender, comorbidities, and smoking status) and clinicopathological variables (surgical margin status, type of surgery, type of lymph node dissection, Borrmann and Lauren classifications, tumor location, histological type, tumor grade, ECOG performance status, adjuvant chemotherapy, tumor-node-metastasis stage, recurrence, and survival outcomes).

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Van Yüzüncü Yıl, University Medical Faculty (decision date: 10.05.2024; No. 2024/05-11). This study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision). Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York). Descriptive statistics are presented as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables and as medians with minimum and maximum values for continuous variables. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was used to predict mortality based on various clinical parameters. The Kaplan–Meier method was employed to compare survival times between different clinical parameter groups. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05.

Results

The study was a review of the medical records of 200 patients with gastric cancer. After applying the exclusion criteria a total of 96 patients were assessed and analyzed in the present study. The median age of the participants in this cohort was determined to be 66 yr. The participant inclusion process is illustrated in Figure 2. Of these, 36.4% were female, and 61.4% had at least one comorbidity. Additionally, 41.6% had a history of smoking. Negative surgical margins were observed in 92.7% of cases, and 45.8% of patients underwent total gastrectomy. According to the Borrmann classification, type II tumors were the most common (49%). Based on the Lauren classification, 83.3% of patients had intestinal-type tumors. The tumor was most frequently located in the antrum (45.8%), and adenocarcinoma was the predominant histopathological type (77.1%). Recurrence was reported in 63.5% of cases. The overall 2-year survival rate for the entire study cohort was 74.8%, and the 5-year survival rate was 48.7%, with a median OS of 54.66 months (95% CI: 25.22–84.1). When stratified by Borrmann classification, patients with type 1 tumors had the highest survival rates, with 2-year and 5-year survival of 83.3% and 66.7% and a median survival of 110.1 months (95% CI: 0.00–244.88). Patients with type 2 tumors had 2-year and 5-yr survival rates of 79% and 50.8%, respectively, and a median survival of 69.7 months (95% CI: 39.30–100.09). In contrast, patients with type 3 tumors demonstrated lower survival outcomes, with 2-yr and 5-yr survival rates of 71% and 45%, and a median survival of 40.83 months (95% CI: 16.85–64.81). The poorest outcomes were observed in Borrmann type 4, with 2-yr and 5-yr survival rates of 60% and 40%, respectively, and a median survival of 31.46 months (95% CI: 0.00–87.92). However, these differences across subtypes were not statistically significant (p=0.789). (p=0.787; Figure 3).

Discussion

Gastric cancer remains a prevalent and aggressive malignancy worldwide, posing significant challenges to healthcare systems. Identifying prognostic factors is crucial for predicting survival and developing optimal treatment strategies. This study examined the impact of the Borrmann classification on survival in patients with stage 2 and 3 gastric cancer.

Previous research has highlighted the prognostic implications of the Borrmann classification. A study investigating the relationship between Borrmann subtypes and response to preoperative treatment found that patients with Borrmann type 3/4 advanced gastric cancer were less likely to achieve a complete pathological response compared with those with type 1/2 tumors.13

A study conducted by Díaz del Arco et al14 on 260 patients with advanced gastric cancer who underwent surgery found no significant relationship between Borrmann classification and patient outcomes, including tumor recurrence, cancer-specific death, OS, or disease-free survival. In our study, type 1 tumors exhibited the longest survival, while type 4 tumors had the poorest survival outcomes. However, our investigation did not reveal a statistically significant difference in OS across the four Borrmann subtypes, indicating that the macroscopic characteristics of the tumor appearance alone may have limited prognostic utility in individuals diagnosed with stage 2 and 3 gastric carcinoma.

Survival disparities among Borrmann types have been reported in previous studies. A large-scale retrospective study of 2,092 patients with advanced gastric cancer in China demonstrated significant survival differences among Borrmann subtypes, with type 4 showing the worst prognosis.15

The prevalence of Borrmann subtypes varies across studies. Ma et al16 reported prevalence rates of 15.5% for type 1/2 tumors, 76.7% for type 3 tumors, and 7.8% for type 4 tumors. According to the study by Zahai et al,17 the distribution of Borrmann subtypes among the patients was: Type 1 (4.7%), Type 2 (17.8%), Type 3 (63.8%), and Type 4 (13.5%). Another study found higher rates of type 1/2 tumors (52.4%), with type 3 tumors accounting for 40.6% and type 4 tumors for 7%.15 Our findings align with this latter study, with prevalence rates of 55.2% for type 1/2, 37.5% for type 3, and 7.3% for type 4 tumors. These discrepancies may be attributed to regional differences in clinicopathological characteristics and patient population.

Recurrence remains a significant challenge in gastric cancer management. A retrospective study of 1,949 patients found that Borrmann type I tumors had a higher recurrence rate than type 2 and 3 tumors, although no significant difference in OS was observed.18 In our study, the recurrence rate was 63.5%, reflecting the difficulties in achieving long-term disease control in advanced gastric cancer. Despite negative surgical margins in 92.7% of cases, recurrence was common across all Borrmann subtypes, highlighting the aggressive nature of the disease. Karaman et al4 reported 64% local recurrence rate and 36.7% distant metastases in patients with gastric cancer who underwent surgery followed by adjuvant chemoradiotherapy. Additionally, the median OS was 63 months, with an overall 5-yr survival was 50.4 months.4 In our cohort, the median OS was 54.6 months, with an overall 5-yr survival rate of 54.7%.

Study limitations

This investigation presents various limitations. The retrospective nature of the study may have resulted in selection bias, and the relatively small sample size for certain subtypes (such as type 1 and 4 tumors) limits the generalizability of our findings. Future studies incorporating larger, prospectively collected datasets and integrating additional prognostic markers may provide a more comprehensive understanding of gastric cancer prognosis.

In conclusion, although the Borrmann classification provides valuable insights into the macroscopic characteristics of gastric tumors, its prognostic significance as an independent factor remains limited. Integrating this classification with molecular and pathological markers may enhance predictive accuracy and inform personalized treatment strategies. Prospective studies with larger cohorts and standardized treatment protocols are warranted to validate and expand upon these findings.

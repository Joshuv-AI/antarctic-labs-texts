Organic amendments mitigate Pb toxicity in maize: Unravelling root functional traits and morpho-physiological responses
Authors: Hafeez ur Rehman, Athar Mahmood, Muhammad Shahbaz, Zubair Aslam, Ali Raza, Jameel M. Al-Khayri, Bader Alsubaie, Othman Al-Dossary, Mohammed I. Aldaej, Mustafa I. Almaghasla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lead (Pb) toxicity imposed hazardous impacts to agricultural lands, which presents a serious threat to crop productivity, soil health, and ecological sustainability. This study aims to elucidate the synergistic potential of vermicompost and biogas slurry in remediating Pb-stress under controlled conditions. Unlike previous studies that examined these organic inputs individually, this research focuses to explore their potential interactive effects on maize seedlings growth, root structural traits along with root anatomical structures, morphological, and photosynthetic indices. A two-factorial experiment using a completely randomized design with three replicates was conducted, comprising of four Pb levels (0,100,200,300 mg kg soil− 1) and four organic amendment application rates (0,4,6,8 tons ha− 1). Pb exposure significantly reduced emergence index (-32.14%), decreased final emergence percentage (-25%), and increased mean germination time (+ 9.28%). However, a notable reduction was recorded in root length (-45.53%), root projection area (-69.99%), and lowered root average density (-69.84%) during Pb toxicity. On the other hand, organic amendments mediated structural recovery and strengthening of root architect (enlarged metaxylem vessels, thickened cortex, and well-developed aerenchyma). Pb contamination also retarded maize plant growth by reducing leaf area, shoot dry weight, and root fresh weight up to -52.50%, -55.48%, -42.77% respectively. Photosynthetic pigment content such as Chl. a and carotenoid decreased (-15.76%, -22.87%) under Pb stress whereas increased (+ 15.13%, + 17.76%) with organic amendments treatment. Our findings indicate that integrated use of vermicompost and biogas slurry can sustainably improve plant resilience and soil functionality in contaminated agroecosystems through modulation of root architecture, stimulation of seed germination, and enhancement of photosynthetic performance.

Introduction

Ecologically safe procedures and sustainable agriculture rely on fertile soil. On the other hand, a number of environmental and ecological concerns have put the security of food and human well-being at risk, including soil pollution, deterioration, agricultural productivity and biomass loss, water scarcity, and changes in the climate [1, 2]. Soil contamination is caused by a variety of pollutants; thus it is imperative that remediation strategies be used right once that are both economically and environmentally sustainable [3]. Soil remediation procedures may be classified into three primary categories based on the restorative processes used: physical, chemical, and biological [4]. Chemical procedures are most harmful, although both physical and chemical approaches are linked to high expenditures, poor efficacy, and substantial environmental damage [5]. Therefore, biological products made from organic matter are regarded as environmentally friendly and resilient [6, 7].

Soil Pb is readily absorbed by the roots, passing through cation channels in the plasma membrane [8]. The Pb absorbed by plants can directly and indirectly induce several morphological, physiological, and biochemical changes, such as inhibition of seed germination, decreased growth, reduced chlorophyll production, lipid peroxidation and oxidative stress, damage to the genetic material, and changes in enzyme activity [9–11]. Furthermore, based on the agronomic context, plant physiological processes, and soil conditions, the roots of plants interact with bacteria both inside the plant and in the rhizosphere [12, 13].

Higher Pb concentrations inhibit the production of chlorophyll a and b and carotenoids, compromising plant development [14]. Pb absorption occurs in an organ-dependent manner, typically in the order roots > leaves > stems > flowers > seeds, though this order may vary by species [15]. Pb also alters plant anatomical structures and photosynthesis, which are key indicators for evaluating plant tolerance to heavy metals [16, 17].

In this scenario, optimizing agricultural yields while preserving ecosystem service delivery poses a significant challenge for agricultural and environmental sustainability. Co-additions of biogas slurry and vermicompost can ameliorate the soil’s health and eventually boost the production of crops by improving root functional traits, and increasing germination and photosynthetic processes. The physical and chemical properties of the soil are improved by these organic materials, which increase the pH to desirable values and enhance the cation exchange capacity (CEC), which increases soil quality. These beneficial impacts improve plants’ access to nutrients. In terms of the environment, organic matter may bind different toxicants, decreasing their bioavailability and minimizing negative effects, thus increasing plant biomass and productivity [18–21]. However, the comparative efficiency of vermicompost with biogas slurry in inhibiting or mitigating Pb stress remains insufficiently understood. In particular, their interactive roles in modulation of seed germination and plant morpho-physiological processes, and developing root anatomical, functional adaptive modifications have not been comprehensively evaluated, necessitating further investigation. Considering these gaps, the objectives of the present study were to investigate (1) the combined effects of VC, and BGS on maize growth under Pb toxicity, (2) the seedling establishment and root functional traits adaptability under Pb stress amended with organic inputs, (3) the morphological and photosynthetic responses of maize plants to Pb toxicity under organic amendments.

Materials and methods

Study site and seed procurement

This research was performed at the Old Botanical Garden of University of Agriculture Faisalabad (31.4336 latitude and 73.0683 longitude) during the winter of 2024 to study the potential role of combined application of vermicompost and biogas slurry in lead contaminated condition in maize to enhance its phytoremediation potentiality and productivity. Certified maize seeds were obtained from the Ayub Agricultural Research Institute, Faisalabad and lead acetate (Pb(C2H3O2)2. 2Pb [OH]2) was used as the source of lead metal.

Experimental design

To evaluate the interactive effects of lead toxicity and organic amendments on maize, a two-factor factorial experiment was carried out under controlled conditions. The study followed a completely randomized design (CRD) with three replications, comprising a total of 48 experimental units (Fig. 1). Factor A was lead (Pb) contamination applied at four concentrations: 0, 100, 200, and 300 mg/kg of soil. The factor B involved the combined application of vermicompost and biogas slurry at four levels: 0, 4, 6, and 8 tons/ha. At each level, equal proportions of vermicompost and biogas slurry (1:1 w/w) were thoroughly mixed to prepare the treatment doses. All amendments were incorporated into the soil prior to sowing.

Seeds sowing and Pb stress imposition

Maize seeds were sown in earthen pots at a depth of 1.5–2 inches, with nine seeds per pot. Lead stress was introduced before sowing in all pots except those designated as control. The soil was then allowed to spike for ten days to ensure metal stability. Standard management practices were followed throughout the experimental period. At the vegetative stage, selected plants were harvested from each pot to record the studied parameters.

Root functional and anatomical characterization

In order to examine the anatomical and functional attributes, only a small sample of the root was taken. A formalin-acetic alcohol solution of v/v 5% formalin, 50% ethanol, 10% acetic acid, and 35% distilled water was used for preserving the gathered plant specimens for 24 h. To preserve it for an extended period of time, the plant components was thereafter added to an acetic alcohol solution that included v/v 75% ethyl alcohol and 25% acetic acid. By the use of a multipurpose razor blade, the plant root was sliced utilizing free-hand cutting approach. In order to make the permanent slides, the plant roots were dried using a repeated ethyl alcohol process [24]. Fast green was employed to stain live, actively functioning tissues, while safranin was utilized to stain lignin- tissues. Placing the transverse sections on DPX was followed by covering the material with a coverslip. A digitized compound microscope with a camera was used to take the permanent slide photographs (Meiji Techno MT4300-LV-HD). Utilizing a visual micrometer, measurements was obtained and calibrated using a stage micrometer.

Furthermore, root projection area, root average density, root surface area, and root volume were recorded using a root scanner equipped with WinRHIZO software for data analysis.

Morphological parameters

Fresh weight of the root and shoot samples were noted instantly in a lab with weighing balance then sample storage was done for two weeks at 65–70 °C in the oven to study dry weight of root and shoot samples. Some morphological growth parameters like leaf area, shoot length, and root length were measured by using a measuring scale and leaf area of every plant was determined by the leaf area meter.

Chlorophyll contents

Arnon’s method [25] was used to determine carotenoids and chlorophyll a, b, and total chlorophyll content. 0.1 g of fresh leaf material was sampled and immersed in 80% acetone and its absorbance was measured at 663, 645, and 480 nm respectively for chlorophyll a, b, and carotenoids. Statistical analysis

Two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted on the dataset to identify statistically significant differences and dominant patterns among the applied treatments. Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test at 5% significance level was used for comprehensive pairwise comparisons among treatments. The statistical software package (e.g., “Statistix 8.1; CoStat”) were used to analyze the data. Graphical abstract, bar graphs, and correlation analysis were computed using the statistical and visualization tool of Canva, and Origin Pro, 2024.

Results

Emergence parameters

The present study showed that Pb toxicity had negative effects on germination characteristics of maize seeds in contrast to the control group of plants such as increased in mean germination time by 9.28%, prolonged emergence duration (time taken to 50% germination (10.71%), time start to germination (23.07%), and final germination % become decreased by 25%), and a reduced emergence index by 32.14% (Table 1). At the same time, organic amendments mainly 8 tons/ha played a positive role in counteracting the harmful effects of lead stress in comparison to no treated plants. Finally, early and significant germination of maize seed has occurred under heavy metal stress. Among various treatment levels, 8 tons/ha application showed greatest impacts by increasing germination rate of seeds. It has decreased mean germination time by 33.33%, enhanced germination index (122.84%), shortened emergence duration of time taken to 50% germination, and time start to germination by 33.47%, and 23.52% appropriately. Furthermore, the 6 ton/ha application also played a significant role in increasing the final emergence percentage to 46.75%.

Root functional and anatomical traits

The maize plants exposed to Pb stress showed a notable deleterious impact on root functional traits including root average density, root projection area, root surface area, root volume, and root anatomical traits (Fig. 2). Reduced RL (45.53%), RPA (69.99%), RSA (70%), lowered RV, and RAD (69.84%) was observed during Pb toxicity as compared to control conditions. Meanwhile, organic inputs (prominently higher dose 8 tons/ha) ameliorated root architectural traits like increased RL (84.14%), RPA (100.60%), RSA (138.14%) more compared to 4 and 6 tons/ha organic inputs level. Higher root volume (131.04)%and density (72.48%) was also noted at 8 tons/ha treatment level under stressed environment.

However, root anatomical traits such as epidermal thickness, cortical region thickness, endodermal thickness, metaxylem cell area, pith cell area, and aerenchyma showed a non-linear variation under Pb stress and organic amendments. Generally, Pb stress caused deformation in cortical tissue, collapsed aerenchyma, thin epidermis, endodermis, shorter metaxylem vessels, and impaired vascular development.

On the other hand, the combined use of organic amendments (biogas slurry + vermicompost at 4, 6, and 8 tons/ha) mediated structural recovery and strengthening of root architect (Fig. 3). Treated roots indicated enlarged metaxylem vessels, thickened cortex, well-developed aerenchyma, improved epidermis, endodermis integrity, and visible pith in contrast to stressed plants. Although not all anatomical traits followed a consistent increasing trend in percentage terms (Table 1), the overall qualitative improvements from micrographs confirmed that organic amendments alleviated Pb-induced anatomical damage and contributed to better vascular and cortical development.

Morphological parameters

Data on morphological traits manifested that Pb stress notably decreased the growth of maize in comparison to unstressed control plant group (Fig. 4.). Lead toxicity caused remarkable reduction in RFW, RDW, SFW, SDW, SL, and LA up to 42.77%, 41.66%, 55.59%, 55.48%, 38.19%, and 52.50% respectively. Whereas, combined application of vermicompost and biogas slurry played an important role in eliminating lead poisoning. With 8 tons/ha of organic amendment, significant positive effects can be observed in the improvement of morphological characteristics such as RFW (125.48%), RDW (115.06%), SFW (127.5%), SDW (182.19%), SL (54.41%), and LA (73.93%) under lead contamination. However, the greatest improvement can be seen in the group of non-stressed plants (Fig. 4).

Discussion

The application of both vermicompost and biogas slurry (organic amendments) for Pb stabilization in soil has been shown to be a viable practice, as observed in the current study. In recent years, in situ stabilization-which immobilizes heavy metals by applying organic fertilizers including biogas slurry, calf dung, vermicompost, and biochar-has shown to be a successful and ecologically friendly practice [26, 27]. Various combined amendments of vermicompost and biogas slurry (4, 6, and 8 tons ha− 1) exhibited distinct effects on seed germination parameters and plant growth, with notable impacts on root architecture (anatomical and functional traits), and photosystem of maize crop.

In plant life, germination is a complex metabolic phase in which seeds under ideal germination circumstances undergo vital physiological processes. But under abiotic stress conditions such as heavy metal stress, the germination rate slows down. It is well known that nickel heavy metal’s adverse implications on germination result from its interference with the functioning of the enzymes ribonuclease, amylase, and protease [28, 29]. Furthermore, our results align with other studies showing that Pb toxicity delays germination and reduces the final germination percentage. Overall, emergence parameters are highly affected by the presence of heavy metal (Pb) in soil. In heavy metals-safflower studies, similar findings have been reported, showing that germination was exclusively inhibited by the highest metal combination dosage (180 mgkg− 1). However, it was further noticed that auxin breakdown decreased plant growth factors and that oxidative damage, toxicity of ions, and heavy metals had adverse consequences [30]. These effects are the main reason for low germination rate.

By impacting the plant’s nutritional metabolism, heavy metals also prevent assimilation and intake of nutrients, which affects the general constitution of seedlings and causes phytotoxicity [31]. The findings of this study indicated that cumulative usage of vermicompost and biogas slurry considerably improved the emergence parameters such as germination index, final emergence percentage, time to start germination, and decreased mean germination time under Pb toxicity. Usually, positive effect of vermicompost on various elements of growth of plants have been linked to advantageous alterations of the edaphic traits of the soil, such as enhanced mechanical and physical capabilities and higher mineral nutrient accessibility [32]. This highlights the role of vermicompost and biogas slurry in improving nutrient availability in soil and regulating the overall germination process.

Numerous elements, soil types, plant species, and plant organs affect how heavy metals are absorbed and transported by plants. Of these, the anatomical traits of the plant organs like root under heavy metal stress are frequently disregarded, even though they have a significant impact on the absorption as well as transportation of metals [33]. As shown for pea (Pisum sativum L.) plants, for instance, a decrease or suppression of mitosis, abnormalities in apical meristematic tissue, and decreased proliferation of new cells are the reasons behind the limitation of root development in Cd-stressed plants. Furthermore, soil contamination with Pb metal in present study also disrupted root anatomical structures which are also in line with Kazemi [34] results showing that cadmium ions interfere with root and stem development as well as mitosis in chickpea plants. These morphological and anatomical alterations impair the roots’ ability to absorb water and minerals, which jeopardizes the potential for plant development rate [35]. Our findings demonstrate that organic amendments considerably mitigate the detrimental impacts of Pb stress, thereby ameliorated root anatomical structures in the form of thickened epidermal, cortical, and endodermal thickness; enlarged metaxylem vessels, well-developed aerenchyma, and visible pith region as highlighted by Saengwilai [36] study (positive impacts of organic inputs under Cd toxicity). It is consequently essential to comprehend the anatomical underpinnings of metal absorption and transport in order to comprehend plant-environment interactions in dynamic contexts, as well as the resulting plant responses to metal stress [37].

Heavy metals in the growing media have detrimental impacts on plants [38]. Which ultimately changed the plant morphology in terms of root, shoot fresh and dry weight, leaf area, and shoot length as observed in our study under Pb stress. In general, increased HM concentrations in plants impair the movement and intake of vital nutrients and disrupt other processes related to metabolism, which impacts yield, and growth [39, 40]. The initially alert part of a plant to recognize a stressful situation is its roots. Any stressful condition leads to in decreased root length and functioning (disrupts root anatomical structures along with root average density, root projection area, root volume, and root surface area), which ultimately interferes with the uptake of vital nutrients [41]. In sunflowers, decreased both dry and fresh biomass as well as shorter shoot and root lengths have been linked to heavy metal stress (Pb toxicity). This is consistent with findings from Anwar [42] study.

The current experiment also manifested that soil conditioning with vermicompost and biogas slurry ameliorated the morphology of maize plant such as improved root, shoot fresh and dry mass, increased root length and shoot length under heavy metal stressed condition in comparison to control. The highest treatment rates of 8 tons/ha (cumulative usage of VC and BGS) consistently yielded the most noticeable improvements in plant morphology and root architecture system. Employing organic matter in polluted soil has two advantages. To begin with, these substances can bind heavy metals in a variety of ways, reducing their availability to plants. This immobility approach lessens the potential adverse impacts of heavy metals like Pb by lowering their absorption by plants [43]. Second, an array of soil properties, including the soil’s structure, moisture level, nutritional value, and activity of microbes, are enhanced by organic additions. These improvements create a more favorable environment for plant growth, especially under stressful conditions. Similar findings have been shown in earlier research. In contrast to untreated soil, the use of organic soil additives, such as vermicompost, biogas slurry and chicken manure, considerably increased plant growth [44]. This implies that organic substances positively affect plant growth and development.

The leaves are the main site for photosynthesis in maize, and the upper and middle leaves are the source of nutrients and energy required for the growth and development of maize grains. Up to 95% of the dry matter in maize grains comes from photosynthesis [45, 46]. Lead metal toxicity negatively affects plant functioning such as impair photosynthesis process. High doses of Pb stress (300 mg/kg soil in current investigation as compared to low doses of Pb) decrease the photosynthetic rate, chlorophyll a, b, and carotenoid contents, disrupting chloroplast integrity, and inhibiting photosystem II (PS II) efficiency [47]. This research highlights that the reduction in photosynthetic efficiency is closely related to the impaired electron transport, inhibited activities of Calvin cycle enzymes, as well as CO2 depletion resulting from stomatal closure [48]. However, combined application of VC and BGS notably enhanced the photosynthetic rate by increasing total chlorophyll, Chl. a, Chl. b, and carotenoid contents.

Research by Yang [49] had also shown that the usage of organic fertilizers (vermicompost and biogas slurry) can substantially improve the photosynthetic rate of maize throughout its entire growth period. Similarly, Abbas [50] findings elucidated that organic fertilizer had a longer fertilizer effect, and organic amendments can improve the photosynthetic efficiency of wheat in the late stage of grain filling, with higher green leaf area and longer leaf functional period. Maintaining high photosynthetic capacity of leaves and a longer functional period during the maize filling stage are key to improving yield [51, 52]. This experiment found that the combination of biogas slurry and vermicompost can significantly improve the photosynthetic characteristics of maize leaves.

A major concern that requires appropriate action to address in order to guarantee worldwide food security and ecological sustainability is the continually rising concentration of heavy metals (HMs), particularly lead (Pb), which is a result of promptly technological and socioeconomic expansion. In this era of evolving modernization and industrialization, usage of natural fertilizers such as vermicompost and biogas slurry is an eco-friendly, and sustainable approach for enhanced agricultural production. Because, adding VC and BGS not only provides essential minerals for maize development, and also enhanced production but also serves as a soil conditioner by rendering the soil’s heavy metals more easily soluble, multifaceted, and aggregated.

Conclusions and future research direction

Our research suggests that the use of vermicompost and biogas slurry together could be an entirely organic and cost-effective way to address some of the main issues with soil health due to its advantageous impact on agro-physiological traits and attributes under heavy metal stress. Much attention has been given to the importance of environmental stresses to physiology and agronomy, including how much more effective using organic fertilizers is than using synthetic ones. Thus, the goal of present study is to increase the expected usage of biogas slurry and vermicompost in order to further improve agriculture on an extensive level. Therefore, both of the growth stimulants were successful in lessening the adverse implications of Pb stress in maize seedlings by promoting plant development and other key traits through modifications to the plant’s morphology, photosynthetic system, and root functional and anatomical attributes. This study offers farmers financially and ecologically feasible ways to increase productivity of crops and heavy metal tolerance by applying vermicompost and biogas slurry as co-addition, respectively, to Pb-affected soil. Future research should explore the key transcription factors that are upregulated by organic amendment treatments under heavy metal stress, with particular emphasis on their roles in regulating metal detoxification, antioxidant defense, and photosynthesis. Integrating transcriptomic analyses with physiological and root structural-functional traits will help elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying amendment-mediated stress tolerance [53].

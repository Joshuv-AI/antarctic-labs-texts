A quickly compliant assembly method for annular parts based on adaptive insertion speed
Authors: Wenmin Chu, Kuai Zhou, Tengzhou Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring the differential temperature assembly of the annular parts in the automatic tilter of large helicopters, intense heat conduction occurs between high-temperature parts and low-temperature parts, leading to component deformation. To reduce the heat conduction time during the assembly of parts, this paper proposes a quickly compliant assembly method for annular parts based on adaptive insertion speed. Firstly, an assembly contact force perception method based on kinematic model is described, which converts the force signals detected by distributed three dimensional force sensors (TDFSs) into six-dimensional contact forces. Secondly, an impedance control model for redundant drive parallel mechanism is constructed, and Simulink simulation is used to study the influence of insertion speed on assembly contact force. Then, a fuzzy adaptive impedance control system is built, so that the insertion speed of the annular parts can be dynamically adjusted. Finally, a compliant assembly platform for annular parts is established in the laboratory, and compliant insertion experiments are conducted. The experimental results show that the impedance control with adaptive adjustment of insertion speed can significantly reduce the assembly contact force and insertion time.Keywords: Assembly of annular parts, Force sensor, Contact force perception, Compliant assemblySubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Aerospace engineering

Introduction

The automatic tilter is a critical unit of the helicopter lift system. The annular parts in the large helicopter automatic tilter, such as the rotating ring and bearings, are connected by precision machined circular mating interfaces, as shown in Fig. 1. The assembly accuracy of the annular parts is extremely high, and the differential temperature assembly methods are usually adopted. During the differential temperature assembly process of the annular parts, intense heat conduction between high-temperature and low-temperature parts will be generated, leading to component deformation and reduced clearance. In order to reduce the heat conduction time between annular parts, it is very necessary to increase the insertion speed of annular parts. At present, the annular parts in automatic tilter are mainly assembled manually, and the relative pose between the circular parts cannot be accurately controlled during the assembly process. The slight positional deviation during the insertion process of the annular parts can also cause assembly jamming and blockages1,2.

With the increasing performance requirements of large helicopters in complex environments and working conditions, there is an urgent need to research efficient, high-precision, and low stress automated assembly technologies to improve the flight performance, safety, and reliability of helicopters3,4. In the large parts automation assembly system, digital measurement instrument is usually used to measure the position and orientation of the parts to be assembled5, and the position and orientation of the large parts are adjusted by automatic adjustment mechanisms6,7, thereby driving the large parts to complete automatic assembly8,9. However, the assembly accuracy requirements of the helicopter automatic tilter are comparable to the accuracy of the measurement instrument and the positioning accuracy of the attitude adjustment mechanism. The automated assembly method guided by geometric measurement merely often leads to forced assembly10, which plays an important role on the performance and lifespan of the helicopter. Compliant assembly is an effective method to solve the above problems11. Compliant assembly refers to changing the structure of the end effector of the mechanism or the control algorithm of the servo system, so that the mechanism can passively or actively respond to the contact force during the assembly process12,13. Using compliant assembly can remove pose errors between parts to be assembled and reduce assembly contact forces. Compliant assembly includes active compliant assembly and passive compliant assembly.

The key to passive compliant is to design a device with passive compliance and install it at the end effector, so that it can release the forced contact force during assembly by correcting the pose of the component14. The RCC device designed by Draper Laboratory is a typical passive compliant device15, which can correct the assembly trajectory of parts according to the external forces in any direction in space, thereby eliminating positioning errors during assembly. Since the RCC is compact and its load-bearing capacity is low, it is usually suitable for assembling smaller parts, and other passive compliant devices have emerged for large assembly objects. For example, Jiang16 developed a five degree of freedom parallel passive compliance device for automatic assembly of large axis parts such as vertical tail fins by establishing a compliant assembly model with shaft holes.

The passive compliant devices have the advantages of simple control and fast response speed, but they are specialized equipment that contradicts the idea of flexible assembly, and the force control accuracy is difficult to guarantee17. To address the shortcomings of passive compliant, active compliant control has been widely studied. The active compliant control obtains the contact force between assembly parts through force sensors, and adjusts the motion trajectory of the parts by a specific force control strategy to achieve compliant assembly18. The active compliance methods mainly include direct force control and indirect force control. Direct force control is also known as force servo control, and both force position hybrid control and PID control belong to the category of direct control. The direct force control method can control the terminal force of the mechanism to the ideal value, which has high force control accuracy and response speed. However, the structure of the controller relies on the precise contact model between the mechanism and the environment. Moreover, various dynamic disturbances and docking impacts persist during component assembly processes, making it challenging to achieve satisfactory control effectiveness using force servo control strategies.

Impedance control is the commonly used active compliant control method, first proposed by Hogan19. The idea of impedance control is to adjust the impedance characteristics of the mechanism to achieve the desired dynamic performance between the contact force and the positional deviation of parts, thereby achieving compliant control. Lawrence provided stability criteria for impedance control in general situations and obtained stable boundaries for various impedance parameters20. The impedance control is mostly used for controlling mechanical arm, but the load-bearing capacity of industrial robots is low21. At present, redundant drive parallel assembly mechanism (RDPAM) composed of multiple computer numerical control positioners (CNCPs) are mainly used for the assembly of large parts22. Compared with serial mechanisms such as industrial robots, redundant drive parallel mechanisms have higher stiffness23, greater load-bearing capacity24, and higher motion accuracy25. Secondly, the contact force between parts is mainly obtained through the six-dimensional force sensors (SDFS) installed at the terminal of the mechanism26. However, the inter dimensional coupling relationship of the SDFS is complex and the crosstalk between dimensions is more severe27, and the measurement accuracy of SDFS cannot meet the low stress compliant assembly requirements of the annular parts. To address the control challenges in compliant assembly of large components, Chu et al. established a dynamic model for a RDPAM to convert force signals detected by distributed TDFS into six-dimensional contact forces, and used impedance control to ensure that the output force and position of the mechanism satisfy the preset dynamic relationship, thereby enabling compliant assembly of large-scale components28. However, there is a deviation between the centroid position of the annular part in the dynamic model and the actual centroid position, which limits the accuracy of assembly force measurement. Moreover, the differential temperature assembly of the automatic tilter needs to be completed within 3 s. The conventional impedance control often adopts the uniform insertion mode, which makes it challenging to reduce the insertion time.

Aiming at the above problems, this paper first uses the dual filtering method and annular part centroid self-calibration method to improve the perception accuracy during the active compliant assembly process. Then, Simulink simulation is used to study the influence of insertion speed on assembly contact force. Finally, a fuzzy impedance control system based on the adjustment law of the insertion is constructed and a series of compliant insertion experiments of the annular parts are conducted. The experimental results indicate that the impedance control based on adaptive insertion speed can significantly reduce the assembly contact force and insertion time. In summary, the rest of the paper is arranged as follows. “Active compliant assembly system for annular parts” describes an active compliant assembly system for annular parts assembly. “Accurate perception of assembly contact force” proposes an assembly contact force perception method based on distributed TDFSs and analyzes the main factors affecting the perception accuracy of assembly contact force. “Impedance control system based on adaptive insertion speed” studies the influence of insertion speed on assembly contact force and constructs an impedance control system with adaptive insertion speed adjustment. “Compliant assembly experiment of annular parts” builds a compliant insertion platform and conducts a series of compliant insertion experiments of the annular parts.

Active compliant assembly system for annular parts

The compliant assembly system for the annular parts mainly consists of CNCPs, TDFSs, bracket, pedestal, etc., as shown in Fig. 2. The CNCP is an automated actuator that can move along the X/Y/Z direction, and each direction is controlled by closed-loop servo system29. The CNCP is connected to the bracket through a spherical joint consisting of a ball head and a ball socket, forming a redundant drive parallel mechanism30. The TDFS is installed below the spherical joint to measure the force of CNCP on the bracket. The compliant assembly system composed of CNCPs has the characteristics of automation, modularization, and digitization, and can be used to assemble different types of parts31. Compared to the Stewart platform, the CNCP can adjust the assembly parts in a larger space32. Compared with the industrial robot, the CNCP have greater load-bearing capacity and can provide higher positioning accuracy.

Accurate perception of assembly contact force

In the compliant assembly system for annular parts, the contact force between the annular parts needs to be precisely measured, and the control system corrects the motion path of the assembly mechanism based on the sensed contact force to ensure the compliance of the assembly. Accurate perception of the terminal load force of the assembly mechanism is the foundation for compliant control and safety assurance of the compliant mechanism.

Filtering of TDFS

In compliant assembly system based on distributed CNCPs, TDFSs are used as the measurement elements to detect the contact force between assembly parts. There are thermal and electromagnetic noises in the amplification circuit of the TDFS, which cause significant fluctuations in the output force signal. Therefore, it is necessary to filter the output signal of the TDFS. For random interference, limiting filtering is an effective method38. If the difference between two sampled values exceeds the maximum allowable deviation, it is considered that random interference has occurred and the subsequent sampled value is considered invalid. The current sampled value should be replaced by the sum of the previous filtered value and the allowed maximum deviation. Limiting filtering can effectively filter pulse signals caused by accidental factors, but the smoothness of the signal is poor. During the process of compliant insertion of parts, the force signals collected by the TDFS change regularly. However, limiting filtering cannot effectively preserve the inherent variation information of the signal.

Savitzky Golay (SG) filtering is a fitting method based on polynomial local features, widely used for the smooth denoising of data. The core idea of SG filtering is also the weighted filtering of sampled data, but its weighting coefficients are obtained by least squares fitting. Self-calibration of annular part centroid

Therefore, it is necessary to compensate for gravity and inertia forces based on the annular part centroid during the compliant assembly. Compliant assembly experiment of annular parts

A compliant assembly system for annular parts was built in the laboratory to verify whether the proposed active compliant control method can achieve low stress and rapid insertion of annular parts, as shown in Fig. 13. The compliant assembly system mainly consists of CNCPs, bracket, annular parts, TDFSs, and laser tracker. The laser tracker is used to measure the spatial pose of the annular parts47.

Compliant assembly experimental system for annular parts.

Experiment of contact force perception

To avoid collisions between annular parts during the actual assembly process, the annular part 1 needs to be adjusted to the aligned pose and then be assembled through insertion. The deviation between the aligned pose and the final pose was only in the Z direction, and the deviation value was set to 50 mm. Since the contact force between annular parts cannot be measured directly, this section verified the accuracy and the stability of the contact force perception through the motion state of the annular part in free space. In free space, there is no contact between the annular parts, and their actual contact force is zero. Therefore, the contact force calculated by the control system was the perception error of the contact force. When the perceived contact force is inaccurate, the impedance controller mistakenly believes that contact was occurred between annular parts and will correct the motion trajectory of the annular part, causing the pose deviation of the annular part. When the pose deviation between annular parts was large, it may cause the annular part 1 to be unable to enter the chamfer range of annular part 2, resulting in assembly failure.

Before conducting impedance control experiments in free space, it was necessary to first calibrate the centroid of the annular part. Conclusions

Intense heat conduction between high-temperature and low-temperature parts can be generated during the differential temperature assembly of the annular parts in the automatic tilter of the large helicopters, leading to component deformation and reduced clearance. This paper proposes an active compliant assembly method for annular parts based on adaptive insertion speed to improve the insertion speed of the annular parts and reduce the heat conduction time between parts. The main contributions of this paper are as follows.

In order to improve the perception accuracy of the assembly contact force between annular parts during the active compliant assembly process, the dual filtering method was first used to improve the stability of the force sensor output force signal. Then, a centroid calibration method based on multi pose was proposed. Finally, a kinematic model of the assembly mechanism was constructed to calculate the assembly contact force, thereby converting the force signals detected by the distributed TDFSs into a six dimensional contact force. The numerical simulation experiment results show that increasing the number of attitude adjustments during the centroid calibration process and using the dual filtering method to denoise the force signal can effectively improve the perception accuracy of the assembly contact force.

The numerical simulation experiment results show that increasing the number of attitude adjustments during the centroid calibration process and using the dual filtering method to denoise the force signal can effectively improve the perception accuracy of the assembly contact force.

In order to maximize the insertion speed of the annular parts, an impedance control model for RDPAM was first constructed, and Simulink simulation was used to study the influence of insertion speed on assembly contact force. Then, according to the adjustment law of insertion speed, impedance control system with adaptive adjustment of insertion speed was designed based on fuzzy control theory.

To verify whether the proposed assembly contact force perception method and active compliance control strategy can achieve rapid high and low stress insertion of annular parts, a compliant assembly system for annular parts was built in the laboratory, and a series of compliant insertion experiments were conducted. The experimental results show that adaptive speed insertion strategy can significantly reduce the insertion time, with an average decrease of about 35.6%.

Outlook

The impedance control method based on adaptive insertion speed proposed has not thoroughly studied the impact of initial insertion speed on insertion efficiency. In subsequent research, a unified impedance control parameter optimization model should be established that includes initial insertion speed parameters. Secondly, the impedance control parameters during the experiment were obtained through offline optimization. In order to maintain good control performance of the impedance control system when environmental parameters change or external disturbances occur, it is hoped to establish a deep learning-based impedance control model for online estimation of environmental parameters in subsequent research in the subsequent research.

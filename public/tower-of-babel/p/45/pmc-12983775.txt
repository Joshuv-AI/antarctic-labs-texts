Efficacy of Vunakizumab in Erosive haNd osteoarthritiS (VENuS): protocol for a multicentre, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Kai Fu, Shirley P Yu, Wei Zheng, Karen Bracken, Changhai Ding, Yifang Mei, Jie Wei, Guanghua Lei, Xiaodong Wang, Yanping Zhao, Jinshan Zhang, Yongqiang Zheng, Dongxing Xie, Ligang Jie, Weizhong Qi, Venkatesha Venkatesha, Zhenzhong Zhu, Xianyou Zheng, Changqing Zhang, David J Hunter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHand osteoarthritis (OA) is a prevalent and debilitating joint disorder that impairs daily functioning and quality of life. Current treatments are often inadequate in managing the symptoms and progression of the disease. The cytokine interleukin (IL)-17 has been implicated in the inflammatory processes associated with OA, making it a potential target for therapeutic intervention. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of vunakizumab, an IL-17A inhibitor, in reducing pain and improving functional outcomes in patients with erosive hand OA.Methods and analysisThis multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial will enrol 150 participants aged 30–80 years with symptomatic erosive hand OA. Participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive either vunakizumab 120 mg or placebo subcutaneously every 4 weeks for 24 weeks, with a loading dose injection period during the first 4 weeks. The primary outcome is the change in hand pain assessed by the Visual Analogue Scale at 28 weeks. Secondary outcomes include changes in physical function measured by the Functional Index for Hand Osteoarthritis, the Quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand questionnaire and the Health Assessment Questionnaire, as well as changes in grip strength and radiographic and MRI evaluations of the hands.Ethics and disseminationWritten informed consent will be obtained from all participants. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (2024–217) and will adhere to the Declaration of Helsinki. Research results will be published in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500101031; https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=264789.Keywords: RHEUMATOLOGY, Drug Therapy, Patient Reported Outcome Measures, Randomized Controlled Trial

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a prevalent and disabling joint disorder associated with multiple risk factors, including joint injury, obesity, genetics and joint malalignment.1 Recent estimates from 2020 indicate a prevalence of OA of nearly 8% of the global population (~595 million people), which represents a rise of 132% compared with 1990.2 This increase has been largely driven by the ageing of the population and rising rates of overweight and obesity. While many therapeutics have been investigated to modify the pathogenic processes associated with OA progression, few options are available to help reduce joint pain and improve physical function.

Hand OA, in particular, is a debilitating condition that leads to impairment in activities of daily living, such as eating and dressing,3 with even fewer effective management options4 compared with that of hip or knee OA. In the general population, the age-standardised prevalence is 44.2% in women and 37.7% in men among older adults.5 Of its many phenotypes, erosive hand OA is a common inflammatory phenotype that is associated with pain and disease progression.6 7 In this setting, several disease-modifying drugs and biologics used in rheumatoid and psoriatic arthritis, such as methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, tumour necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors and interleukin (IL)-6 inhibitors have been trialled with limited success.8 9 Importantly, a recent randomised, placebo-controlled phase 2a trial demonstrated that denosumab (RANKL blockade) reduced structural progression in erosive hand OA by preventing new erosive joints over 48 weeks, indicating that structure-modifying effects are achievable in this phenotype.10

The IL-17 family of cytokines is increasingly identified as a contributor to OA pathogenesis. In the setting of OA, IL-17 affects the inflammatory response, complement production, hypoxia response, angiogenesis and glycolytic pathways in chondrocytes and synovial fibroblasts.11 12 IL-17A has been found in elevated concentrations in the serum and synovial fluid of individuals with OA compared with healthy controls, and has correlations with pain, function and disease severity scores.13 14 Significant correlation between IL-17 expression and cartilage defects and bone marrow lesions has been observed in individuals with knee OA.15 In animal models, injection of IL-17 into a rabbit knee joint has been shown to induce a model of OA.16 Furthermore, in an in vitro study using synovial tissue from injured and early-OA participants, mRNA expression of IL-17RA and IL-17RC was detected in chondrocytes, synovial fibroblasts and synovial tissue. IL-17 increased mRNA expression of matrix metalloproteinases 3 and 13, as well as the proteins IL-6 and TNF-alpha-induced protein 6, and the expression of these proteins was significantly reduced by IL-17 inhibitors in comparison to other therapeutics such as methotrexate and diclofenac.17 In a case-control study using musculoskeletal ultrasound, serum IL-17 concentrations were significantly higher in hand OA than in healthy controls and correlated with synovitis and patient-reported pain.18 Collectively, these data support the biological plausibility of targeting IL-17A in OA, while we explicitly acknowledge that much of the preclinical work derives from knee-focused models. In this protocol, such data are used only for mechanistic plausibility rather than efficacy extrapolation to erosive hand OA. Consistent with this, we incorporate central, blinded imaging to characterise inflammatory structural activity typical of erosive hand OA.

Vunakizumab is a subcutaneous recombinant anti-IL-17A humanised monoclonal IgG1/κ antibody being developed by Suzhou Suncadia Biopharmaceutical (a subsidiary of Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals) for the systemic treatment of autoimmune diseases related to the IL-17 pathway.19 In August 2024, it received approval in China for use in adult patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. The current trial is referred to as the Vunakizumab in Erosive haNd osteoarthritiS (VENuS) trial, which will investigate the therapeutic potential of vunakizumab in people with erosive hand OA.

Objectives

The objective of this study is to determine the efficacy of vunakizumab compared with placebo on clinical outcomes in people with symptomatic erosive hand OA.

The hypothesis is that vunakizumab will reduce pain, slow structural progression and improve physical function compared with placebo in individuals with symptomatic erosive hand OA.

Trial design

The VENuS trial is a 28-week randomised, placebo-controlled, patient, assessor and statistician blinded, multicentre, two-arm trial with a 1:1 allocation ratio. Participants will be randomised to receive vunakizumab or a matched placebo (eight injections over 24 weeks) with the primary endpoint assessed at 28 weeks. All participants will be followed up for 4 weeks after receiving their final dose of the study treatment. Participants will be recruited at seven centres (public tertiary teaching hospitals) in China, including Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai), Jinjiang Municipal Hospital (Jinjiang), Zhujiang Hospital (Southern Medical University, Guangzhou), the Second Affiliated Hospital of Southern University of Science and Technology (Shenzhen), the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (Harbin), Xiangya Hospital (Central South University, Changsha) and Renji Hospital (Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai). The study will be coordinated and sponsored by Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital.

Participants and recruitment

Eligible participants with erosive hand OA will be recruited through participating centres’ rheumatology and orthopaedic departments, from the community via advertisements, and from established OA cohorts of potential participants who have expressed their willingness to be involved in future studies. Box 1 describes the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Candidates <40 years who otherwise qualify will undergo confirmatory central adjudication of radiographs (and, when needed, MRI) before randomisation.

Interventions

Vunakizumab is a subcutaneously administered recombinant humanised monoclonal IgG1/κ antibody that targets IL-17A. In this study, participants will receive either vunakizumab 120 mg (1 mL) or placebo (1 mL of sterile saline) every 4 weeks for 24 weeks, with an intensive injection period (every 2 weeks) during the first 4 weeks (figure 1). The placebo is designed to match vunakizumab in appearance and administration method to ensure blinding.

Intervention schedule throughout the trial (shown in weeks): three times in the first 4 weeks, then every 4 weeks to 24 weeks.

Although vunakizumab can be self-injected by participants after proper training, for this trial, a study nurse, who will be blinded to treatment allocation, will administer each injection. Subcutaneous injections will be given in the lower abdomen, legs or upper arms and, whenever possible, different sites will be selected for consecutive injections to reduce the risk of injection-site reactions. Following each injection, participants will be observed for 30 min to monitor for any adverse events.

All participants (in both the vunakizumab and placebo groups) will receive educational brochures on hand OA management and exercise guidance. The two-page educational brochure covers topics such as what OA is, the features of hand OA and exercise and was adapted and translated into Chinese using the publicly available leaflet developed by King’s College Hospital National Health Service Foundation Trust (OA of the hand, https://www.kch.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4519-Osteoarthritis-Leaflet-v3_FINAL.pdf).

Dose modifications

Doses of study treatment may be omitted in case of clinically significant toxicities that are assessed to be related to study treatment. If a dose is omitted, the related study visit will nevertheless be conducted for safety and other assessments. If the toxicity resolves, the study treatment may be restarted at 120 mg. No dose reductions will be permitted.

If study treatment is ceased prematurely, study visits and assessments will continue according to the visit schedule wherever possible.

Permitted and prohibited medications

Rescue analgesia with paracetamol is permitted if needed, but not allowed 48 hours before each assessment. To minimise confounding factors and ensure the safety of participants, certain medications and treatments are either prohibited or discouraged during the study. Specifically, the use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) is discouraged and, if deemed absolutely necessary, must be taken under the strict guidance of the study investigator. Apart from paracetamol, additional pain relief medications should be avoided. The use of other biological or immunosuppressive agents is prohibited during the trial. Intra-articular injections of corticosteroids or other therapies in the study hand are not permitted. Any deviation must be reported and documented to preserve the integrity of the study results.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome is the change from baseline in average hand pain level in the previous week assessed by Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) (0–100 mm) at 28 weeks. The average hand pain level in the previous week by VAS (0–100 mm) will also be assessed at 12 weeks. At baseline, 12 weeks and 28 weeks, physical function will be measured using the Functional Index for Hand Osteoarthritis (FIHOA; 0–30 score), the Quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand (QuickDASH) questionnaire20 and the Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ; 0–3 score).21 Grip strength (in kilograms) will be evaluated using handheld dynamometers at baseline, 12 weeks and 28 weeks. This will be measured using a Jamar Smart hand dynamometer with participants seated, shoulder adducted and neutrally rotated, elbow flexed at 90°, forearm neutral and wrist at 0–30° extension and 0–15° ulnar deviation. Two trials per hand will be performed with 30–60 s rest, and the mean of the study hand trials will be analysed; all dynamometers are calibrated before enrolment and every 3 months. Participants will undergo hand MRI at baseline and 28 weeks using 1.5 T or 3.0 T MRI by standardised sequences across seven study sites to assess structural progression. OMERACT (Outcome Measures in Rheumatology, originally "Outcome MEasures in Rheumatoid Arthritis Clinical Trials") Hand Osteoarthritis Magnetic Resonance Scoring System (HOAMRIS) will be employed to support scoring.22 X-rays of bilateral hands will also be performed at baseline and 28 weeks, which will also use validated scoring systems by Kellgren and Lawrence scale, the Osteoarthritis Research Society International atlas and the Verbruggen-Veys scoring system.23 24 These images will be sent to a central reading facility for independent assessment, ensuring consistent and unbiased interpretation. Qualified radiologists or other imaging experts, who will be blinded to the participants’ treatment assignments, will perform the central reading.

A validated Chinese version of FIHOA was initially employed in the Xiangya Osteoarthritis Study25. For the current trial, this existing Chinese version was back-translated into English by a third-party authorised translator, and three independent reviewers subsequently assessed the translation. Based on feedback from the reviewers, this process was repeated until all reviewers were satisfied. This process ensured accuracy, consistency and cultural relevance. As a result, the reliability, validity and usability of the FIHOA in Chinese were reaffirmed, confirming its suitability for evaluating patient-reported functional outcomes in hand OA. Both radiograph and MRI will be used to evaluate structural progression. Radiographic progression will be defined as any increase in Kellgren and Lawrence grade in any joint. Cartilage and bone changes will be evaluated using coronal T1-weighted and proton density-weighted MRI sequences. Synovitis and tenosynovitis will be assessed using coronal and axial proton density-weighted images. The HOAMRIS will be used to guide and standardise image scoring.22

Medication washout

Participants using any of the pain medications listed below will be required to undergo a 48-hour washout period before completing study assessment or surveys: (1) analgesics: including acetaminophen (paracetamol); (2) oral or topical NSAIDs: such as ibuprofen, naproxen or diclofenac; (3) centrally acting analgesics: for example, tramadol or other opioid-based medications; (4) other potential pain-relief agents: such as gabapentinoids (eg, gabapentin, pregabalin) or muscle relaxants.

Randomisation and blinding

Individuals who consent to participate in the study and meet all inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either the intervention or control, according to a computer-generated randomisation schedule prepared by an independent biostatistician. A central computer randomisation procedure, stratified by the study centre and using random permuted block sizes, will be employed.

Participants, study nurses, investigators, assessors and biostatisticians will be blinded to the group allocations. The allocation concealment will be enabled by the study medication and control (placebo) being identical in appearance, packaging and labelling and identified only by kit number, not treatment type. Data collection will be performed by a research officer/assistant blinded to the group allocation, and the imaging reporter will be masked to the group allocation and the sequence of the timing of the images.

Study procedures

Figure 2 and table 1 show the study flow diagram and schedule. All personnel involved in this study, including investigators, study nurses and support staff, will receive comprehensive training on the study protocol, data collection procedures and safety monitoring requirements. Potential participants will first undergo a pre-screening assessment (a trained study coordinator conducts telephone pre-screening using a standardised checklist including age, hand pain frequency/severity, suspected erosive features or prior imaging and key exclusions including infections/biologics contraindications) and those potentially eligible then attend a screening visit at the hospital, where a rheumatologist or orthopaedic surgeon obtains informed consent and assesses all inclusion and exclusion criteria. Blood tests (full blood count, kidney function test, liver function test, C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, rheumatoid factor, anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies, HIV, hepatitis B (hepatitis B surface antibody [HBsAb], hepatitis B surface antigen [HBsAg], and hepatitis B core antibody [HBcAb]), hepatitis C and tuberculosis) will be performed to assess the eligibility of the participants and ensure no contraindication to vunakizumab. All these tests, if performed within the last 3 months, will be considered valid for use in this trial. X-rays of both hands will be taken during screening (or if it is conducted within the previous 12 months) and again at 28 weeks. Screening hand radiographs obtained within the prior 12 months will be accepted if image quality and views meet protocol standards; otherwise, new radiographs will be obtained. An MRI of the study hand will be conducted at baseline and 28 weeks to detect synovitis and structural progression. The most symptomatic hand will be designated as the study hand. If both hands are equally symptomatic, the more affected (VAS pain) hand reported by the participant will be selected as the study hand, assuming it is radiographically eligible. If symptoms are equally severe on both sides and both hands are equally affected, the dominant hand will be chosen for the study. At centres with approval for research blood collection as part of the trial, blood samples will be collected at baseline and again at the 28-week visit. All samples will be processed and stored under controlled conditions, in accordance with institutional and regulatory requirements. If long-term storage or additional analyses are planned beyond the current study protocol, further ethical approval will be sought.

Safety

Adverse events will be recorded and assessed throughout the study, with any serious incidents reported to the sponsor within 24 hours. Certain occurrences, though documented in the overall study records, will not be classified as adverse events. For instance, any natural worsening of hand OA symptoms in line with the typical disease progression is excluded, as are elective or planned surgeries that do not present complications or unexpected outcomes meeting the criteria for an adverse event. All other events will be reviewed according to the study protocol and regulatory guidelines to determine whether they should be classified as adverse events.

Safety evaluations will be conducted in accordance with established monitoring guidelines. Key assessments, including full blood count, liver function tests and urea and electrolyte levels, will be conducted at the initial screening and subsequently at 4, 12, 24 and 28 weeks. Adverse events, analgesic use and co-interventions will be recorded by a participant-completed diary and structured questioning by the blinded assessor at each follow-up. If a participant experiences significant adverse events or shows marked clinical deterioration potentially related to the study drug, treatment may be temporarily or permanently discontinued at the discretion of the study investigator. Additionally, participants retain the right to withdraw from the study at any time for personal, medical or any other reason. All treatment pauses or early cessations, including the rationale, must be thoroughly documented. This information will be closely monitored and reviewed to maintain the integrity of the trial and ensure participants’ well-being. A Data and Safety Monitoring Board will meet every 6 months to review the safety data for the study.

Statistical methods

Sample size estimation

The primary outcome of the study is the change in hand pain level assessed by VAS (0–100 mm) at 28 weeks. The sample size determination is based on the minimum clinically significant difference of 12 mm change in VAS pain on average, with a SD of 20 mm.26,28 This corresponds to a medium effect size defined by a standardised mean difference (Cohen’s d) of 0.6.29

The sample size analysis indicated that a total of 150 subjects (75 per group) need to be recruited to detect a two-sided mean difference of 12 mm in hand pain level assessed by VAS (0–100 mm) at 28 weeks to achieve 90% power at a 0.05 level of significance, adjusting for a 20% drop-out rate.

Statistical analysis

Demographic characteristics and baseline scores on primary and secondary outcome measures will be examined to assess the baseline comparability of treatment groups. Continuous variables will be described using means and SDs if approximately normally distributed or medians with quartiles if the distribution is skewed, while categorical variables will be summarised as counts and percentages. No formal statistical tests will be conducted to compare baseline characteristics. However, any clinically relevant imbalances will be noted.

Descriptive statistics for each group will include the mean change in outcomes (with 95% CIs) from baseline to each time point (12 weeks and 28 weeks). The primary outcome of the study is the change in hand pain level, measured using the VAS (0–100 mm), at 28 weeks. The primary outcome analysis will be performed based on the intention-to-treat principle. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputations under the assumption of data missing at random. The number of imputations will be based on Rubin’s Rule.30 The sensitivity analyses will include a per-protocol analysis and a complete-case analysis to verify robustness. An analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model will be used to analyse the primary outcome, with hand pain level at 28 weeks as the dependent variable, treatment group as the main effect and baseline hand pain level as a covariate. The assumption of homogeneity of regression slopes in the ANCOVA model will be evaluated by assessing the interaction effect between the treatment group and baseline hand pain level. If this assumption is violated, appropriate stratification will be applied. The model will adjust for study sites, relevant baseline demographic and clinical characteristics to produce adjusted mean differences. The treatment effect size will be calculated using Cohen’s d (with thresholds of 0.2 as small, 0.5 as moderate and 0.8 as large), defined as the unadjusted mean difference in outcome scores between groups divided by the pooled SD of those scores.29

Secondary outcomes analyses for change in hand pain at 12 weeks and change in pain and function at 28 weeks will be carried out using an ANCOVA model with baseline value as a covariate and treatment group as the main factor. Longitudinal changes in other outcomes (FIHOA, HAQ and grip strength) between groups will be compared using a linear mixed-effects model with a random intercept specification, adjusting for baseline measures (age, sex, BMI and Kellgren and Lawrence grade). Structural analyses based on radiographs and MRI-derived measurements will be compared between groups using appropriate models, adjusting for age, gender, body mass index and other relevant variables.

A participant will be classified as a responder at 28 weeks based on perceived ratings of change and attainment of the minimum clinically important difference for pain (12 mm change in VAS pain). The likelihood of being a responder between treatment groups will be compared using binary logistic regression, with results reported as ORs and corresponding 95% CI. Adverse and serious adverse events at 12 and 28 weeks will be summarised using frequency distribution and presented using alluvial plot.

All the statistical tests will be performed at the 0.05 level of significance. The data preparation and statistical analysis will be performed in R, V.4.5.131 or Stata, V.18.0.32

Data integrity and management

Data for this study will be systematically collected using pre-established case report forms via the Aidiyan electronic data capture system (https://integration.aidiyanyiyao.com/), hosted on secure Huawei Cloud servers with role-based access control, full audit trails, encrypted data transfer and regular encrypted backups. To ensure confidentiality, access to all collected data will be strictly protected. Physical documents will be secured in locked cabinets, accessible only to authorised personnel at each study site. Electronic data will be safeguarded in a password-protected database with access restricted to authorised personnel. Electronic participant records will be stored in a re-identifiable format using the study ID. No other potentially identifying information will be stored in the study database. Secure, encrypted methods will be employed for data transfer, ensuring all data is pseudonymised. Following the conclusion of the trial, paper-based case report forms will be securely archived, and electronic data will be maintained on a secure server, password-protected and accessible solely to the study investigators. The study does not include any provisions for interim analyses or predefined stopping rules.

Trial management

The Trial Management Committee is responsible for oversight of the trial and meets monthly to review trial progress, including blinded safety and monitoring reports, to input into any protocol amendments and to provide advice on trial conduct. The frequency of Trial Management Committee meetings may be reduced to 3-monthly as appropriate as the trial progresses.

Ethics and dissemination

Informed consent (an example consent form is provided in online supplemental material 1) will be obtained from all participants before study enrolment or completion of any study-specific assessments. Participants can withdraw from the trial at any point; their withdrawal date and reasons will be documented. Approval for this trial has been granted by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (2024–217). The trial will gain ethics approvals from all participating centres and was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry (ChiCTR2500101031; URL: https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=264789). The procedures are in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Trial results will be submitted to international peer-reviewed journals and may also be presented at conferences or in the media. Additionally, findings will be shared with the study participants.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design or conduct of this trial, which is acknowledged as a limitation; however, they will be engaged in the reporting and dissemination phase through co-reviewing lay summaries, participation in local patient forums and co-development of a public-facing infographic.

Trial status

The trial was initiated on 16 May 2025, and recruitment is ongoing.

Discussion

The results of this multicentre randomised, placebo-controlled trial are anticipated to significantly enhance our understanding of the therapeutic potential of vunakizumab in managing erosive hand OA. Given the chronic and often debilitating nature of erosive hand OA and the limited efficacy of current treatments in mitigating pain and functional impairment, the investigation into the role of IL-17 inhibition presents a promising avenue for novel therapeutic strategies.

The cytokine IL-17 has been implicated in various inflammatory processes, including those involved in OA pathogenesis. This trial’s focus on vunakizumab, an IL-17A inhibitor, is supported by burgeoning evidence suggesting that IL-17 not only contributes to inflammation but also to joint progression in OA.13 16 Previous studies have indicated elevated levels of IL-17 in people with OA, correlating with increased pain and disease severity.13 By targeting this cytokine, vunakizumab may offer dual benefits: reducing inflammatory symptoms and potentially slowing the structural damage within the joints.

A critical aspect of this trial is its design to measure not only pain reduction and structural progression but also improvements in physical function and quality of life, assessed via validated tools such as the VAS for pain, the FIHOA and the HAQ. These measures will provide a comprehensive picture of the clinical benefits of vunakizumab, offering insights that are directly relevant to patient care.

Furthermore, the trial’s rigorous methodology, including blinding and randomisation, ensures the reliability of the results, which could substantiate the role of IL-17 inhibitors in OA treatment paradigms. The potential success of vunakizumab in this trial could pave the way for larger-scale studies and inform clinical practices regarding the management of not only hand OA but potentially other phenotypes of OA where IL-17 plays a contributory role.

In summary, if vunakizumab proves effective in this trial, it would not only redefine treatment approaches for erosive hand OA but also align with the broader goal of personalised medicine in chronic joint diseases. This could significantly alter treatment landscapes and improve the quality of life for many patients, underscoring the importance of targeted biological therapies in chronic inflammatory conditions.

Background

β-lactam antibiotics are the most commonly used antibiotics to treat infection in critically-ill patients. Available evidence advocates for continuous infusion for β-lactam antibiotics [1, 2]. The modalities of antibiotics reconstitution/preparation for continuous infusion are elaborated according to drug stability. Since various-clinically equivalent-preparation are available, physician should favor those with the lower environmental impact [3]. We aim to evaluate the environmental benefit of implementing the most sustainable protocol for antibiotic administration, consisting of preparing the highest stable concentration for continuous administration through a 50 mL syringe [4].

Methods

In a polyvalent ICU of 22 beds, two periods of 8 months (May to December 2024 and May to December 2025) were compared retrospectively, considering four β-lactam antibiotics. In between both periods, a sustainable protocol for antibiotic administration was implemented, consisting of preparing the highest stable concentration for continuous administration through a 50 mL syringe. The various interdependent parameters that modify the stability of antibiotics were used to propose optimized treatment regimens [5].

Concentrated antibiotics are administrated via central venous catheter (CVC) most of the time, and rarely via peripheral venous catheter (PVC), in that case there is a strict monitoring of the puncture site.

These modifications of the 4 β-lactam antibiotics were easily implemented in the ICU prescription software.

Patient’s severity, ICU length of stay, mortality, treatment duration, treatment GHG emission and waste were reported.

We considered that one 50 mL plastic syringe Luer Lock + tubing 200 cm and solution bag NaCl 0.9% 50 mL was 275 g of CO2 equivalent (CO2e) [6]. This was based on different CO2e used in previous studies [6–9].

However, even if the precise CO2e can be discussed (between 275 g and 390 g of CO2e) our main objective was to quantify the reduction per treatment of the 4 β-lactams.

We measured that one package of perfusion (one 50 mL plastic syringe Luer Lock 28,4 g + one tubing 9,5 g + one empty solution bag NaCl 0.9% 50 mL and the blister pack 8,3 g) was 46,2 g of plastic waste.

The price of one package of perfusion (one 50 mL plastic syringe Luer Lock + tubing 1 mm, length 200 cm and solution bag NaCl 0.9% 50 mL) was 0,29 + 0,25 + 0,47 = 1,01€.

Statistics

Data are expressed as means ± SDs for normally distributed data, and otherwise as medians (interquartile ranges, IQRs). The Mann Whitney test was used for non-normal distribution. All statistical tests were two-tailed and a P-value < 0.05 was considered significant. We used Prism 6 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA).

Results

Overall, 418 patients were treated (202 in 2024 and 216 in 2025), no data were missing, age, and SAPSII did not differed between both periods, (76[68–93] vs. 67 [58–75] years, p = 0.800; and 48[34–63] vs. 47[40–62], p = 0.172 respectively). After sustainable protocol implementation, the GHG emissions for the 4 β-lactam antibiotics were reduced by 52% from 2760 [1380–4968]g to 1380 [552–2208]g per treatment, p < 0.0001 (Fig. 1; Table 1 OS and 2 OS). The global reduction of GHG emissions was 361,72 kg. The global reduction of plastic consumed (syringes, tubing, solution bags, blister packs) was − 48% (-61 kg), and treatment cost was also reduced by 48% (-1324 €) (Table 3 OS).

The total nursing time for preparation was 145 h shorter during the second 6 months period.

The ICU length of stay and mortality did not differ between the 2 periods: 6[3–13] vs. 6[3–12] days, p = 0.958 and 22.8 vs. 20.4%, p = 0.554.

Discussion

Our results showed that implementation of a sustainable protocol for continuous β-lactam antibiotics can significantly reduce the environmental impact of antibiotic administration. The difference between expected vs. measured reduction of CO2e per treatment comes from the durations of antibiotics between the 2 periods that were not exactly the same. The precise CO2e of a syringe + tubing + bag can be discussed; it depends on the methodology used and some are using more than 275 g of CO2e. However, our main objective was to quantify the reduction per treatment. We also measured the reduction in resource consumption, resulting in a proportional reduction of waste and associated CO2e emission.

Optimizing drug dilutions leads also to a reduction of the amount of waste, treatment costs, and nursing time. We did not find any physicochemical incompatibilities with other IV drugs on the same line (possible risk of precipitation with high concentration).

Of course, antibiotic conservation and short treatment duration are other simple sustainable actions but once we decide to treat a patient with a continuous infusion for β-lactam antibiotics, optimizing drug dilution is another efficient and simple sustainable action.

We strongly believe that measuring and quantifying sustainability actions will assist with communication and quality assessments in our ICUs.

Conclusion

A careful prescription of drug dilution, if continuous infusion of β-lactam antibiotics is used, can be efficient in reducing GHG emissions, consumables, waste, and costs.

Needle-free intramuscular jet vaccination in finishing pigs: effects on growth, carcass quality, cortisol levels, and CSF-specific neutralizing antibodies
Authors: C Siriboon, J Glinubon, A Matthujak, S Khotthada, I Salangam, P Khamphakdi, B Rittisut, S Phongthanapanich
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study compared needle-free jet injection (NFI) with conventional needle vaccination in finishing pigs, evaluating growth performance, carcass quality, serum cortisol, macroscopic injection-site safety, and Classical Swine Fever (CSF)-specific neutralizing antibody responses. Twenty-four 3-week-old crossbred pigs were randomly assigned to receive both CSF and Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) vaccines into the mid-neck muscle via either conventional needle (control) or an electromagnetic NFI device. Growth metrics, including body weight, average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR), were monitored weekly. Serum cortisol was measured 30 min post-vaccination to assess acute stress. CSF antibody titers were determined at 6, 8, 12, and 14 weeks of age (0, 14, 42, and 56 days post-vaccination; dpv). Carcass traits and internal tissue integrity were assessed post-slaughter. Results showed no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) between groups in overall growth performance, acute cortisol elevations, or CSF antibody titers. Both groups exhibited significant increases in antibody titers at 56 dpv compared to baseline (P < 0.05). Carcass characteristics were similar between the groups, although the NFI group showed a tendency toward a higher picnic cut percentage (P = 0.053). Importantly, no severe macroscopic injection-site reactions were observed, supporting acceptable macroscopic injection-site safety under the field conditions evaluated. These findings support the potential use of NFI under commercial field conditions by demonstrating comparable production outcomes, similar acute stress responses, non-inferior humoral immunity, and acceptable macroscopic injection-site safety.Keywords: Needle-free injection, Pig vaccination, Animal welfare, Growth performance, Immune response, Cortisol levels

Introduction

Vaccination is essential for maintaining animal health, welfare, and productivity in pig farming. Adherence to vaccination protocols is linked to enhanced pig health, immune function, feed efficiency, and overall growth performance (Charlier et al., 2022; Maes et al., 1999). In routine practice, vaccines are commonly administered by needle injection into intramuscular sites such as the neck (Boston Shoulder) or hip (Ham), which are valuable primal cuts in the pork industry (Glinubon et al., 2015). However, needle-based delivery can present practical and welfare challenges, including needle breakage, carcass contamination with needle fragments, and potential food safety risks that may lead to economic losses (Hoff & Sundberg, 1999). In addition, needle injection may cause pain, stress, and discomfort, raising animal welfare concerns and motivating the search for alternatives consistent with ethical farming practices aimed at minimizing procedural pain (Temple et al., 2020). In addition, commercial swine vaccination often involves formulations with different physical properties, and relatively high-viscosity or oil-adjuvanted vaccines may present further delivery challenges because viscosity can affect flow behavior, delivery reproducibility, and consistency of tissue penetration. These limitations create a clear need for alternative vaccination approaches that can reduce needle-associated drawbacks while maintaining reliable biological performance and field-level practicality under routine commercial conditions.

Needle-free injection (NFI) technology has emerged as a promising alternative that delivers vaccines or drugs via high-velocity liquid jets for transdermal or intramuscular administration, eliminating the need for conventional needles and associated risks (Ravi et al., 2015). Interest in NFI is driven by several industry priorities, including reducing needle-related carcass defects and biohazard waste, minimizing needle-stick injuries, and potentially lowering iatrogenic pathogen transmission between animals. Importantly, NFI may also support improved animal welfare by reducing pain and stress during vaccination (Temple et al., 2020). The electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system evaluated in the present study was developed to address these practical and welfare-related limitations. Unlike conventional needle-syringe administration, the system produces a high-velocity jet through an electronically controlled impact-driven mechanism, expelling the formulation through a 0.2 mm orifice under controlled operating conditions. This design enables repeatable jet generation suitable for intramuscular delivery and may offer practical advantages in veterinary vaccination through programmable operation and reproducible output, particularly in large-scale production settings.

Evidence from prior animal studies indicates that NFI can maintain vaccine performance while offering welfare-related advantages in some contexts. In swine, immunological outcomes following needle-free delivery have been reported as comparable to conventional needle injection for several vaccines and settings (Temple et al., 2017; Wesley & Lager, 2005). For example, in porcine vaccine models for rabies and Mycoplasma, needle-free approaches have achieved comparable immunogenicity to needle injection and have been associated with reductions in certain stress-related indicators (Lewis et al., 2024; Temple et al., 2020). Nevertheless, broader implementation of NFI in commercial swine production has been limited by the cost, complexity, and operational constraints of existing devices. Moreover, although prior engineering studies demonstrated the feasibility of the electromagnetic actuator-based system used here, including high-speed jet generation and sufficient impact pressure for tissue penetration, those studies were focused primarily on device development and did not evaluate comparative vaccination outcomes in a real farm environment. Accordingly, the novelty of the present work lies not only in the use of an electromagnetic actuator-based delivery platform, but also in its evaluation against conventional needle injection under commercial finishing-pig conditions using integrated biological, physiological, and production-related endpoints.

Important knowledge gaps therefore remain regarding how NFI performs relative to conventional needle injection when assessed using production-relevant outcomes in commercial practice. Although some reports suggest benefits such as reduced injection-site lesions or improved immune responses (Chase et al., 2008; Li et al., 2012), findings have not always been consistent. For example, studies examining lesion outcomes after Foot-and-Mouth Disease vaccination have reported mixed results, with some showing reductions and others finding no significant differences (Ko et al., 2018). In addition, there is still limited integrated field evidence comparing NFI and conventional injection in finishing pigs across multiple outcome domains, including growth performance, carcass quality, physiological stress responses, and immune response, within the context of routine production workflows.

Recent engineering developments have advanced NFI device capability. Khotthada et al. (2025) developed an electromagnetic needle-free jet injection device and used MATLAB simulations, polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) pressure sensors, and high-speed video imaging to optimize and validate device performance. The injector produced high-speed liquid jets (up to 225 m/s) with impact pressures exceeding 35 MPa, and testing on polyacrylamide gel and porcine tissue demonstrated precise and consistent dispersion, highlighting potential for veterinary and medical applications. However, that work primarily refined engineering functionality and did not evaluate comparative effectiveness within a commercial farm environment.

In commercial swine production, vaccination technologies must demonstrate not only engineering capability but also practical compatibility with routine farm workflows, including handling procedures, operational consistency, and performance under real-world conditions. Therefore, beyond laboratory-level characterization, there remains a clear need for field-based comparative evidence evaluating whether an electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system can serve as a practical alternative to conventional needle-based intramuscular vaccination. The primary aim of this study was to evaluate the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system under commercial finishing-pig conditions by comparing it with conventional needle injection in terms of CSF-specific neutralizing antibody response, acute cortisol response, growth performance, carcass traits, and macroscopic injection-site safety. By integrating these endpoints, the study was designed to determine whether the proposed system could achieve comparable vaccination-related outcomes while addressing practical concerns associated with conventional needle use.

The scope of the present work was intentionally limited to comparative field outcomes under the evaluated conditions and did not include long-term safety, histopathology, cell-mediated immunity, or formal cost-effectiveness analysis. Within this scope, however, the study offers several important strengths. First, it evaluates an electromagnetic actuator-based needle-free platform that extends prior device-development work into a biologically and operationally relevant swine-vaccination context. Second, it was conducted under commercial finishing-pig conditions, thereby enhancing the translational value of the findings beyond laboratory-only studies. Third, it examines multiple complementary endpoints, including humoral immune response, acute physiological stress, growth performance, carcass traits, and macroscopic injection-site outcomes, thereby providing an integrated field-based evaluation of both biological performance and practical applicability. Collectively, these features position the present work as an important step toward broader validation of electromagnetic needle-free vaccine delivery systems in livestock production.

Materials and methods

Needle-free jet injector

The electromagnetic actuator-based needle-free injection (NFI) system used in this study was previously designed, optimized, and mechanically characterized in detail by Khotthada et al. (2025). The device operates through an impact-driven mechanism, in which an electrical signal from a controller is amplified and supplied to a copper coil, generating an electromagnetic field that interacts with a Neodymium Iron Boron (NdFeB) permanent magnet. The resulting magnet displacement drives a plunger mechanism that expels the liquid formulation through a 0.2 mm orifice, producing a high-velocity jet suitable for intramuscular delivery (Bowden & Brunton, 1958; Matthujak et al., 2020; Sripanagul et al., 2021). The working principle of the injector is illustrated in Fig. 1, and the key specifications and output-verification conditions adopted in the present study are summarized in Table 1.

Comprehensive engineering details, including actuator configuration, performance characterization, and system optimization, are available in Khotthada et al. (2025). Briefly, prior validation experiments quantified the relationships between input voltage, peak impact pressure, jet velocity, and delivered drug volume under controlled laboratory conditions. The validated operating parameters established in that study were directly adopted in the present in vivo vaccination trial to ensure controlled and reproducible intramuscular delivery. All injector performance data referenced herein were obtained from our experimental validation under calibrated conditions, consistent with the previously reported methodology. In the present study, these previously validated operating conditions were used as the engineering basis for field application, so that variability associated with injector performance would be minimized during on-farm vaccination.

To evaluate the dispersion and penetration depth, fresh post-mortem porcine neck tissue was obtained from a local standard slaughterhouse immediately following slaughter. The tissue samples, approximately 10 × 10 cm in size, included the complete anatomical layers: epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous fat, and muscle, to accurately simulate the target area for vaccination. The penetration depth was measured using a Vernier caliper. Based on these ex-vivo evaluations, the optimal device settings (driving pressure and nozzle geometry) yielding the most effective penetration depth were selected and subsequently employed for all vaccinations in the in-vivo field experiment described below. These ex-vivo tissue tests were used as a practical pre-selection step to confirm that the chosen operating settings provided adequate penetration depth and dispersion behavior for intramuscular delivery in the target anatomical region.

Prior to the field vaccination trial, the electromagnetic needle-free injector was calibrated and its output consistency was verified to ensure repeatable delivery conditions across animals. Specifically, the device operating settings were validated by confirming that the injector could reproducibly generate jet conditions that meet established thresholds for effective skin penetration (i.e., impact pressure > 15 MPa and/or jet velocity within the typical effective range for needle-free delivery). The validation approach followed the same calibration logic previously established for this actuator-driven platform, where impact pressure can be quantified using a PVDF-based measurement chain with a defined voltage–pressure relationship, and jet velocity can be verified using high-speed imaging under controlled stand-off distance and nozzle geometry conditions (Khotthada et al., 2025). Using this pre-trial verification, the final operating settings applied in the on-farm experiment were confirmed to provide stable jet output (pressure/velocity) and consistent dose delivery, thereby minimizing variability attributable to device performance and strengthening the reproducibility of the vaccination procedure. As shown in Fig. 1 and summarized in Table 1, the injector was operated under controlled electrical and mechanical conditions previously established during device development. Prior to field application, jet output was verified by PVDF-based impact-pressure measurement and high-speed imaging, confirming that the selected conditions produced stable and reproducible jet performance suitable for intramuscular injection.

To improve methodological transparency and facilitate independent replication, a concise summary of the electromagnetic needle-free injector specifications and adopted operating conditions has been added (Table 1). The system was operated under a defined electrical and actuator configuration, using a direct-current driving voltage of 50–250 V at 50 Hz, an actuator magnetic field strength of 1.2 T, and a coil configuration of 1000 turns. The nozzle assembly employed a 0.2 mm orifice, and the delivered dose volume range was 100–500 μL, selected to represent the practical dosing range relevant to vaccination and drug delivery in swine. Table 1 also clarifies which parameters were held constant and which were varied during device operation and verification; specifically, voltage and dose volume were treated as key controllable inputs, whereas nozzle geometry and actuator configuration were maintained to ensure consistent jet generation. In addition, the summary table provides the essential technical conditions required for methodological reproducibility, including the principal electrical settings, actuator configuration, nozzle characteristics, and jet-output verification conditions used before the animal trial.

It should also be emphasized that the engineering output data summarized in this section primarily represent platform-level characterization under controlled validation conditions rather than formulation-specific measurements for each vaccine used in the animal trial. Thus, although the reported impact-pressure and jet-velocity data establish that the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI platform is capable of generating conditions suitable for intramuscular delivery, they should not be interpreted as direct pressure–velocity profiles for the oil-adjuvanted FMD formulation itself. Additional formulation-specific engineering testing would be required to determine how viscosity influences jet dynamics, delivered dose consistency, and splash-related behavior for individual vaccines.

In addition, because formulation properties can influence jet formation and delivery performance, we explicitly acknowledge the role of liquid viscosity in high-speed jet injection. Prior visualized investigations of impact-driven high-speed liquid jets have shown that kinematic viscosity is a governing property affecting jet dynamics: lower viscosity reduces frictional losses during flow acceleration and enables greater conversion of input energy into jet kinetic energy, thereby influencing attainable jet velocity and penetration-related performance (Matthujak et al., 2020). Accordingly, while oil-adjuvanted (higher-viscosity) vaccines were administered under commercial field conditions in the present work, this should be interpreted as evidence of field feasibility rather than full quantitative validation across formulations. Quantitative confirmation under viscous conditions (e.g., dose recovery testing and jet-output characterization as a function of viscosity) is therefore identified as priority future work to further strengthen cross-formulation validation and independent replication. Thus, the conclusions drawn from the present field study regarding device performance should be interpreted within the scope of biologically relevant field use, while recognizing that additional formulation-specific engineering validation would further strengthen reproducibility across a wider range of vaccine properties.

It should also be noted that the operating conditions adopted in the present field study were selected on the basis of prior output verification and short-term reproducibility, rather than as part of a dedicated durability or endurance test program. Thus, while the selected settings were sufficient to support stable vaccination performance during the present trial, they were not intended to establish long-term actuator robustness under prolonged continuous operation or high-throughput field use. Dedicated endurance evaluation would be required to determine whether repeated actuation affects coil temperature, output stability, or component wear over extended use.

Animals and management

All animal care and experimental methods were carried out in compliance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee's approved regulations and guidelines (IACUC Permit ID # 06/2566) at Ubon Ratchathani University. The Department of Animal Science, Faculty of Agriculture, Ubon Ratchathani University provided 24 crossbred finishing pigs (Duroc × Large White × Landrace) aged three weeks. The pigs lived in an evaporative cooling system housing, which was outfitted with an evaporative cooling system that maintained the ideal temperature and humidity for pig comfort. The housing comprised six pens (4 × 3.5 × 1 m), and each pen contained an equal number of barrows and gilts. Pigs had unlimited access to commercial feed and water throughout the experiment.

Experimental designs

A total of 24 pigs were randomly allocated to treatment groups at 3 weeks of age (weaning) to allow for acclimatization. To ensure robust methodological transparency, an a priori power analysis was conducted (using G*Power 3.1) prior to the study. To detect a large effect size for primary outcomes such as physiological stress indicators and antibody titers with an 80% statistical power and a 5% level of significance (α= 0.05), a sample size of 12 pigs per group was determined to be sufficient. The pigs were stratified by initial body weight and sex, and then randomly assigned into 6 standard pens (4 pigs per pen; 2 barrows and 2 gilts). Subsequently, the pens were randomly assigned to one of the two vaccination methods: the standard needle-syringe method (control group, n = 12) or the needle-free jet injection system (NFI group, n = 12). This allocation strategy was used to reduce bias arising from sex- and weight-related variation and to maintain balanced group composition before treatment assignment. In addition, pen-based allocation was used to preserve routine housing conditions during the field trial while maintaining comparability between treatment groups. Thus, the conventional needle-syringe group served as the procedural control, whereas the NFI group served as the experimental treatment group. This design was selected so that the principal intended difference between groups was the vaccine delivery method, while environmental conditions, housing, feeding, and vaccination schedule were maintained uniformly across the study. To maintain clarity of presentation, the subsequent subsections of Materials and Methods are organized according to the principal outcome domains evaluated in the field trial, namely growth performance, acute physiological stress, humoral immune response, carcass characteristics, and statistical analysis. This structure was adopted to align the methodological description directly with the corresponding results presented later in the manuscript.

For clarity of timeline interpretation, it should be noted that the pigs were allocated to treatment groups at 3 weeks of age to allow acclimatization and balanced group assignment before treatment exposure, whereas the vaccination protocol itself did not begin until 6 weeks of age. Accordingly, the first CSF vaccination was defined as day 0 post-vaccination (0 dpv), and all subsequent vaccination and sampling events were referenced relative to this time point. In the revised manuscript, days post-vaccination (dpv) are used as the primary temporal descriptor for experimental interpretation, while age in weeks is retained only as supporting biological context.

The vaccination protocol began when the pigs reached 6 weeks of age (Day 0 post-vaccination). The vaccine schedule included the administration of the first dose of Classical Swine Fever (CSF) vaccine on Day 0 dpv (6 weeks of age), followed by the first dose of Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) vaccine on Day 14 dpv (8 weeks of age). Additionally, booster doses for both CSF and FMD vaccines were given on Day 42 dpv (12 weeks of age). One group was immunized using the standard needle-syringe method (control group), whereas the other group received vaccination through a needle-free jet injection system (NFI group). Thus, the study was structured as a comparative field trial in which the only intended procedural difference between groups was the vaccine delivery method, whereas the vaccination schedule, antigen exposure timing, animal management, and sampling framework were kept identical between treatments. This parallel-group design was adopted to support direct comparison between delivery methods under commercial farm conditions while minimizing confounding from non-treatment-related variables. For ease of interpretation, the overall experimental workflow is also summarized graphically in Fig. 2. This figure provides a visual overview of group allocation, acclimatization, vaccination events, and principal sampling/assessment time points, thereby complementing the detailed text description and helping readers follow the study sequence more efficiently.

Before each vaccination, trained personnel gently restrained the pigs to ensure proper positioning and reduce stress. The handling procedures involved the secure yet non-restrictive restraint of the pigs by one handler, while a second individual administered the vaccination. Vaccinations were administered by skilled veterinarians with prior training in both needle-syringe and needle-free jet injection techniques to ensure consistency. To improve reproducibility of the vaccination procedure, all animals were handled using the same standardized two-person protocol throughout the study. One trained handler restrained the pig in a secure but non-traumatic manner, while a second trained operator performed the injection. This approach was adopted to reduce operator-dependent variability and to ensure that any observed differences between groups were attributable primarily to the injection method rather than differences in animal handling or administration technique.

Both groups received intramuscular vaccination at the same anatomical site, namely the mid-neck muscle on the left side. Vaccines administered consisted of a 1 mL dose of swine fever vaccine (Batch no: 4/65, Expiration date: 23-Nov-2023, Bureau of Veterinary Biologics, Nakhon Ratchasima, Thailand) and a 2 mL dose of foot-and-mouth vaccine (Batch no: HB65L, Expiration date: 03-Aug-2023, Bureau of Veterinary Biologics, Nakhon Ratchasima, Thailand) (Fig. 2). Vaccinations for the control group were administered with a pistol-grip syringe equipped with a 15 mm, 22-gauge needle. Vaccinations for the NFI group were conducted in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines for the needle-free jet injection system, focusing on the same neck region as the control group. Use of the same anatomical site, vaccine formulation, dose, age at vaccination, and trained personnel in both groups was intended to minimize procedural heterogeneity and improve direct comparability between conventional needle injection and NFI administration. Accordingly, the sampling mechanism and control structure were designed to reduce systematic bias and to ensure that differences in measured outcomes could be interpreted primarily in relation to the delivery method under evaluation.

Pigs were vaccinated against CSF and FMD according to the farm's standard health program. The study design employed a dual-antigen model to evaluate distinct performance parameters: CSF (aqueous solution) was selected as the biological model antigen for quantifying immunogenic efficacy via antibody titers. Conversely, FMD (high-viscosity oil emulsion) was utilized to assess the physical feasibility of the NFI system in delivering viscous fluids under field conditions. Therefore, antibody titers were monitored specifically for CSF, while FMD vaccination served to validate mechanical delivery performance. This dual-antigen framework was adopted deliberately to distinguish biological efficacy assessment from formulation-related delivery feasibility. Accordingly, CSF-specific neutralizing antibody titers were used as the principal immunological endpoint, whereas administration of the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine was used to determine whether the NFI system could practically deliver a higher-viscosity formulation under routine farm conditions. This distinction is important for interpretation and reproducibility, because it clarifies that the present study was designed to assess humoral immune comparability using CSF, while using FMD primarily as a field-relevant test of viscous-formulation delivery rather than as a second serological efficacy model. No treatment-specific exclusion criteria were applied after allocation other than those associated with routine animal-health and welfare oversight, and all randomized animals remained within the field-based comparative framework of the study. To avoid ambiguity regarding endpoint selection, it should be emphasized that the present study was not designed to evaluate immunogenicity for both vaccines in parallel. Rather, the dual-antigen framework was used to serve two distinct purposes within the same field trial: CSF vaccination provided the predefined biological model for humoral immune assessment, whereas the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine was included as a practically relevant high-viscosity formulation to evaluate delivery feasibility of the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system under commercial conditions. Therefore, FMD-specific serological outcomes were outside the predefined analytical scope of the present study and were not included as primary immunological endpoints.

Within this framework, the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine was used as a practical field-level challenge to test whether the NFI platform could administer a higher-viscosity formulation under routine commercial conditions. However, the study was not designed as a full rheological or formulation-specific engineering evaluation of FMD delivery. Therefore, parameters such as formulation-specific jet velocity, impact-pressure response, splash-back tendency, delivered volume accuracy, and dose-recovery efficiency were not quantified separately for the FMD vaccine in the present work.

Growth performance

The growth performance was assessed by measuring the initial and final body weights of pigs in both the control and NFI groups. Weekly recordings of body weight measurements were conducted. Weekly feed consumption was recorded, detailing the amount of feed provided and the quantity left, with measurements conducted at the end of each week. The feed conversion ratio (FCR) is determined by dividing total feed intake by body weight gain during the period of study. The growth performances, comprising body weight gain, average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR), were obtained from the weekly data collected on body weight and feed intake.

Cortisol hormone levels

Thirty minutes post-vaccination with the CSF and/or FMD vaccines, 4–6 mL of blood was collected from the external jugular vein of pigs in both the control and NFI groups. The external jugular vein was selected for its accessibility and reliability in obtaining consistent samples in pigs, thereby reducing stress and ensuring sufficient blood volume for analysis. Blood samples were collected using sterile syringes and promptly transferred into 15 mL plastic tubes, where they were stored at a room temperature until further processing.

The blood samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min to separate the serum. The serum was collected and sent to a laboratory (North East Lab, Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand) for cortisol hormone analysis using the Chemiluminescence Immunoassay (CLIA) method (Escribano et al., 2012). The results were compared to the experimental groups.

To ensure consistency in physiological stress assessment, blood samples for cortisol analysis were collected at a fixed interval of 30 min after each vaccination event in both groups using the same venipuncture site and sample-handling procedure. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, serum samples were analyzed by chemiluminescence immunoassay (CLIA) at a single commercial laboratory. The use of a fixed post-vaccination sampling window, identical serum-processing steps, and a single analytical method was intended to reduce procedural variability and improve comparability between treatment groups. In the present study, acute physiological stress was operationally assessed by comparative serum cortisol concentrations between treatment groups under the same field conditions.

In the present field study, serum cortisol was sampled at a fixed time point of 30 min after each vaccination event to provide a standardized and operationally feasible basis for comparison between treatment groups under commercial conditions. This sampling window was selected to balance physiological relevance, handling practicality, and procedural consistency across all animals in the farm setting. Accordingly, the cortisol measurements in this study should be interpreted as a comparative indicator of acute physiological response at a defined post-vaccination time point, rather than as a direct estimate of the absolute peak endocrine response for each individual animal.

CSF-specific neutralizing antibody

Blood samples were collected at four important times to track the immunological response. The first blood sample was collected at 6 weeks of age, day 0 post-vaccination, to establish a baseline for antibody levels. The second sample was taken two weeks following or at 14 dpv (8 weeks old) to determine the early immunological response. A third blood sample was collected soon before the second vaccination (at 42 dpv; 12 weeks of age) to assess any changes before to the booster dosage. Finally, two weeks after the second vaccine (at 56 dpv; 14 weeks of age) a fourth blood sample was collected to assess the immunological response after the booster.

For consistency with the revised study timeline, these sampling points were predefined and are reported primarily relative to the first vaccination event (0, 14, 42, and 56 dpv), with age in weeks provided only as complementary information.

Four to six mL blood sample was taken from the jugular vein of pigs in both the control and NFI groups using sterile syringes and transferred into 15 mL plastic tubes. The blood was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min to isolate the serum. The serum was collected and sent to the Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory at Chulalongkorn University in Bangkok, Thailand, where antibodies were quantified using the neutralization peroxidase-linked antibody (NPLA) test (Santana-Rodríguez et al., 2022). This procedure was performed to assess antibodies against classical swine fever virus (CSFV), which is a highly infectious disease affecting pigs. The test measures the presence and titer of neutralizing antibodies, which are important indications of protective immunity.

To improve reproducibility of immune assessment, the blood sampling schedule and analytical endpoint were predefined before study initiation. Serum was collected at four time points: baseline prior to the first CSF vaccination, two weeks after the first vaccination, immediately before the booster vaccination, and two weeks after the booster. Antibody analysis was performed by the Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory at Chulalongkorn University using the neutralization peroxidase-linked antibody (NPLA) test to quantify CSF-specific neutralizing antibody titers. The NPLA assay was selected because it provides a functional serological measure of neutralizing humoral immunity against CSFV. Thus, in the present study, immune efficacy was operationally defined as the comparative pattern of CSF-specific neutralizing antibody responses over time between the control and NFI groups, rather than as comprehensive immune protection, cell-mediated immunity, or challenge-tested efficacy.

Carcass quality

Prior to slaughter, the pigs fasted for 12 h with full access to water. This fasting interval reduces the contents of the gastrointestinal system, allowing for easier evisceration and lowering the possibility of contamination during processing. The pigs were electrically anesthetized and then slaughtered using the standardized procedures described by Jaturasitha et al. (2007). The mass of the visceral organs (visceral weight) and the hot carcass weight were measured. Thereafter, the cold carcass weight was recorded, and the carcass length was assessed. The loin eye area was assessed with tracing paper, and the carcasses were cut in accordance with USDA regulations. The weights of four lean cuts ham, loin, Boston (neck), and front leg were documented, alongside the weights of retail cuts, differentiating lean and fatty sections of red meat.

Injection-site safety was evaluated by standardized macroscopic observation during the post-vaccination period and by gross post-mortem inspection at slaughter. Particular attention was given to visible local reactions, including redness, bruising, petechiae, swelling, superficial scab formation, abscess formation, granuloma-like lesions, lameness, and delayed wound recovery. In addition, the neck region and adjacent forequarter area were examined post-mortem for gross tissue abnormalities or carcass defects potentially associated with the vaccination procedure. In the present study, safety conclusions are therefore limited to observable macroscopic outcomes and gross carcass findings. Histopathological examination, microscopic inflammation scoring, and quantitative lesion grading were not performed and should be considered in future confirmatory studies.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SAS version 9 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Prior to hypothesis testing, normality was evaluated with the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was assessed using Levene's test. Results are reported as least squares means ± standard error of the mean (SEM), alongside 95% Confidence Intervals (95% CIs) and effect sizes to gauge practical significance. To strengthen transparency of reporting, the analytical framework was selected according to the structure of each outcome variable, including whether the data were measured once per animal, once per pen, or repeatedly across time.

For parameters measured at a single time point (e.g., body weight gain, carcass quality), an Independent Samples T-test was conducted, with the pen as the experimental unit for group-level variables (e.g., ADFI and FCR), and individual pigs for carcass traits, while accounting for pen-level clustering as a random factor. These tests were selected because they are appropriate for two-group comparisons when model assumptions of approximate normality and homogeneity of variance are met. Clarifying the experimental unit for each endpoint was considered important for avoiding ambiguity in the interpretation of treatment effects.

For longitudinal data with repeated measures (e.g., blood cortisol concentrations and CSF-specific neutralizing antibodies), a Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM) was applied, incorporating Treatment (NFI vs. Needle), Time (sampling days), and their interaction (Treatment × Time) as fixed effects. The random effect modeled Pig nested within Pen to account for within-subject correlation. Statistical significance was set at P< 0.05. To improve reproducibility of interpretation, the same statistical decision criteria were applied consistently across all evaluated outcomes. The LMM approach was selected because it is appropriate for repeated measurements collected from the same animals over time and allows treatment-related effects to be estimated while accounting for clustering and within-subject dependence.

To improve statistical transparency further, P-values, 95% confidence intervals, and effect sizes were reported for the principal outcomes in the revised tables and figure annotations wherever applicable. These measures were included to allow readers to evaluate not only statistical significance but also the magnitude and precision of the observed differences between treatment groups. Because the present study was designed as a comparative field evaluation, emphasis was placed on reporting both statistical evidence and practical effect size rather than relying solely on binary significance testing.

Because the present study was conducted as a field-based comparative experiment with relatively limited sample size, the statistical framework was intended to identify treatment-related differences in biological and production outcomes rather than to establish exhaustive mechanistic inference. Accordingly, interpretation of non-significant findings should be restricted to the measured endpoints and experimental conditions used in this study.

Several assumptions and limitations relevant to reproducibility should be stated explicitly. First, although injector performance was verified using previously established engineering calibration procedures, the present study was designed primarily as a comparative biological field evaluation rather than a full engineering optimization study; therefore, long-duration endurance testing, real-time thermal monitoring, and repeated cycle-life assessment were beyond the scope of this work. Second, although the FMD vaccine provided a practical field test of administration of an oil-adjuvanted formulation, the study did not include quantitative viscosity-dependent dose-recovery analysis or rheological profiling. Third, safety assessment was limited to macroscopic clinical and carcass observations and did not include histopathology. Fourth, immune evaluation was limited to CSF-specific neutralizing antibody titers and did not include cell-mediated immune markers or controlled challenge studies. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the reproducibility, scope, and generalizability of the present findings. Accordingly, the statistical conclusions of the present study should be interpreted within the boundaries of the measured field outcomes, the predefined comparative design, and the biological scope of the assessed endpoints.

Results

For transparency, the principal statistical comparisons are reported using P-values together with 95% confidence intervals and Cohen’s d effect sizes, allowing assessment of both statistical significance and practical magnitude of treatment effects. The Results are presented in a structured sequence to reflect the logic of the study design. We first report injector performance and observable injection-site responses to establish the technical feasibility and immediate field-level context of the NFI system. We then present the comparative biological and production-related outcomes, including growth performance, acute cortisol response, CSF-specific neutralizing antibody response, and carcass traits.

To maintain consistency with the experimental design, the timing of vaccination and sample collection is interpreted primarily using days post-vaccination (dpv), with corresponding animal age in weeks shown only for contextual reference where relevant. Thus, comparisons across biological and production outcomes should be understood relative to the first vaccination time point at 0 dpv rather than to the age at initial group allocation.

The sequence of results presented below should be interpreted in conjunction with the experimental timeline shown in Fig. 2, which summarizes the order of vaccination and assessment events. Performance of electromagnetic needle-free jet injector

In needle-free jet injection, the drug must be transformed into a high-speed jet with velocities ranging from 100 to 200 m/s (Taberner et al., 2012) or an impact pressure exceeding 15 MPa (Shergold et al., 2006) to effectively penetrate the skin. To evaluate the injector’s performance, impact pressure measurements were conducted using a Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF) pressure transducer for jets produced from a 0.2 mm nozzle with a drug volume of 0.1 ml. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the experiments revealed a direct correlation between impact pressure and electrical power, with higher voltages generating more forceful jets. When the voltage exceeded 150 V, the impact pressure surpassed 15 MPa, meeting the threshold for skin penetration. At the maximum voltage of 250 V, the highest impact pressure recorded was 37 MPa, demonstrating the system’s capability for deep-tissue penetration. Additionally, the maximum jet velocity reached 233.33 m/s, confirming the device's ability to deliver medication efficiently.

Effect of voltage on impact pressure.

Further experiments investigated the effect of drug volume on impact pressure and jet velocity, as depicted in Fig. 4. High-speed imaging, captured using a Protron Fastcam Mini UX 50 at 10,000 frames per second, showed that an increase in drug volume inside the nozzle led to a reduction in both impact pressure and jet velocity due to increased liquid resistance. However, even at 250 V, the device generated sufficient impact pressure and velocity across all tested drug volumes to ensure effective skin penetration. The highest recorded jet velocity was 233.33 m/s, while the impact pressure remained at 37 MPa, confirming the feasibility of the injector for transdermal and intramuscular drug delivery applications.

Effect of drug volume on impact pressure and jet velocity at 250 Volt.

To validate the injector’s performance in biological and simulated tissues, experiments were conducted using polyacrylamide gel and porcine tissue. The polyacrylamide gel, which mimics the mechanical properties of soft tissue, was used to analyze jet penetration behavior and drug dispersion under controlled conditions. The results confirmed that the jet formed a well-defined penetration channel, achieving a maximum penetration depth of 25 mm, ensuring uniform drug dispersion. High-speed imaging demonstrated a stable and consistent jet structure, reinforcing its potential for precise and repeatable drug delivery.

For biological validation, porcine tissue was used to assess the device’s ability to penetrate and distribute the drug effectively. The injection tests were conducted under controlled conditions, using a voltage of 250 V, a frequency of 50 Hz, a magnetic field strength of 1.2 T, 1000 coil revolutions, a nozzle diameter of 0.2 mm, and a drug volume of 0.5 ml. The results confirmed that the jet successfully penetrated multiple skin layers and delivered the drug uniformly within muscle tissue, achieving a maximum penetration depth of 30 mm. When compared to conventional needle injections, the needle-free injector exhibited superior penetration efficiency and reduced dispersion loss, highlighting its ability to ensure precise drug delivery while minimizing tissue trauma.

The injector’s ability to generate high-speed jets with adjustable impact pressure and velocity ensures its adaptability for various tissue types and injection depths, reinforcing its potential for veterinary and medical applications. By eliminating the risk of needle contamination and injection-site injuries, this technology presents a safer, more efficient, and contamination-free solution for drug delivery in swine production and other clinical applications.

It was observed that while needle-free injection (NFI) caused more widespread and distinct skin redness, bruising, and superficial petechiae than traditional needle-based vaccination, primarily due to the impact of the high-velocity jet, these initial reactions were highly transient. Lesions from both immunization procedures resolved rapidly within three to four days, subsiding into minor redness and scab formation at the puncture sites (Fig. 5). Importantly, these macroscopic surface reactions healed without progressing into severe observable complications such as abscesses, granulomas, or lameness during the observation period.

Discussion

For clarity, the Discussion is organized to move from overall study framing and engineering readiness to the principal biological and practical outcome domains evaluated in the field trial. The following sections therefore address field relevance, injector performance, high-viscosity vaccine feasibility, observable injection-site safety, growth performance, acute stress response, humoral immune response, carcass outcomes, practical implications, and study limitations in a sequence that mirrors the Results section.

Field-based comparative relevance and study framing

A key strength of this study is its implementation in a real-world commercial production environment, where logistical constraints and routine management practices often limit the breadth of laboratory-style measurements but provide a highly relevant test of translational feasibility. In this context, the comparative design against conventional needle injection offers field-level evidence that the electromagnetic needle-free injection (NFI) system can be integrated into routine vaccination operations and still produce outcome patterns consistent with practical acceptability. Importantly, our findings are best interpreted as field-level validation of comparative performance—demonstrating non-inferior humoral responses and acceptable production and carcass-related outcomes under commercial conditions—rather than as comprehensive mechanistic validation of immune protection, tissue-level pathology, or long-term efficacy. This framing highlights the contribution of the study—bridging engineering-enabled delivery with applied swine production endpoints—while maintaining alignment between conclusions and the level of evidence generated in a commercial-field setting.

Engineering output performance and operational consistency

The performance evaluation of the electromagnetic needle-free injector demonstrated its ability to achieve impact pressures exceeding 15 MPa and jet velocities above 200 m/s, meeting the requirements for skin penetration and efficient drug delivery (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). These results confirm that the device can consistently generate high-speed jets under controlled conditions, supporting its practical applicability for swine vaccination. The findings align with the engineering design objectives detailed in Khotthada et al. (2025) and validate the system’s reliability for transdermal and intramuscular administration. This strong performance under controlled tests supports the consistent results observed in the field (Baxter & Mitragotri, 2005; Schramm & Mitragotri, 2002), where comparable growth, immune responses, and carcass quality outcomes were achieved using the NFI system.

However, the engineering evidence presented here should be interpreted primarily as confirmation of short-term output capability and operational consistency under the conditions evaluated, rather than as proof of long-term durability. Although the injector achieved the required impact pressure and jet velocity and operated successfully during the field trial, the present study did not quantify whether repeated actuation over extended vaccination sessions would alter thermal behavior, output repeatability, or mechanical integrity of the actuator assembly. Accordingly, durability under continuous or high-volume use remains an important unresolved question for future engineering validation.

High-viscosity (oil-adjuvanted) vaccine delivery: field feasibility and interpretation boundaries

The physical properties of the vaccine, particularly viscosity, are critical determinants of NFI performance. High-viscosity fluids, such as the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine used in this study, naturally resist shear deformation and may dissipate kinetic energy more than aqueous solutions. We utilized the FMD vaccination event specifically to stress-test the device's capability to handle such viscous fluids. Although detailed rheological flow profiles were not characterized, clinical observations during FMD administration confirmed that the electromagnetic actuator generated sufficient impulse pressure to deliver the vaccine without significant splash-back or leakage. This practical success indicates that the device settings optimized in this study are robust enough for commercial oil-based vaccines under field conditions; however, this evidence should be interpreted as field feasibility rather than full quantitative validation across formulations.

Accordingly, the current evidence supports the interpretation that the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system was practically capable of administering the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine under the evaluated field conditions without obvious splash-back, gross leakage, or failure of delivery. However, this should not be interpreted as full quantitative validation of performance with high-viscosity formulations. In particular, the study did not determine whether the FMD formulation altered jet decay characteristics, reduced delivered-volume precision, or changed pressure–velocity behavior relative to lower-viscosity formulations. Therefore, the present findings should be regarded as evidence of practical field feasibility for a representative high-viscosity vaccine, rather than comprehensive formulation-specific engineering proof of equivalent performance across all viscosity conditions.

Injection-site safety: macroscopic outcomes and appropriate evidentiary scope

Regarding tissue integrity, this study relied on macroscopic visual inspection rather than histopathological analysis. While histology would provide cellular-level detail on inflammation, the macroscopic absence of abscesses, granulomas, or prolonged wound healing (Fig. 5) serves as a clinically relevant indicator that the NFI system does not cause severe observable tissue complications or introduce evident bacterial contamination under field conditions. Consistent with the evidentiary scope, conclusions regarding tissue-level pathology or microscopic inflammatory effects are not claimed based solely on macroscopic observations. Accordingly, any interpretation of improved tissue safety should remain cautious. Because the present study relied on macroscopic observation and gross post-mortem inspection rather than histopathological examination, the findings support acceptable observable injection-site outcomes under field conditions, but do not justify definitive conclusions regarding reduced microscopic tissue damage, reduced inflammation at the cellular level, or improved histological recovery. The safety-related observations should therefore be interpreted as clinically relevant field observations rather than as comprehensive tissue-biological proof of superior safety.

The inclusion of serial macroscopic observations in the present study represents an important field-level strength, because it provides direct visual evidence of local tissue response under practical vaccination conditions. In particular, the representative injection-site images presented in Fig. 5 show that the local reactions observed after NFI were primarily superficial and transient, with no severe observable progression during follow-up. This supports the interpretation that the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system did not produce severe clinically apparent local damage under the conditions evaluated. Nevertheless, these visual findings should be interpreted strictly within the scope of macroscopic assessment. Although they are valuable for field-level safety monitoring, they do not replace histopathological examination and therefore cannot be used to make definitive claims regarding microscopic muscle preservation, inflammatory cell infiltration, tissue necrosis, or reduction of subclinical lesion severity relative to conventional needle injection.

Importantly, the safety interpretation in the present study is based primarily on direct observation of the neck, which was the actual site of vaccine administration in both groups. The representative macroscopic images shown in Fig. 5, together with gross post-mortem inspection of the neck and adjacent forequarter region, support the interpretation that no severe clinically apparent abnormalities developed at the primary injection site under the evaluated field conditions. Thus, the present evidence does address local neck-site outcomes, although only at the level of macroscopic and gross anatomical assessment. This distinction is important, because the findings support acceptable observable local tolerance at the neck injection site, but do not establish the absence of microscopic tissue injury or subclinical inflammatory change.

Growth performance and production relevance

The pigs in the NFI group demonstrated comparable growth performance to the control group, indicating that the NFI technique is equally effective as traditional needle injections regarding growth outcomes. NFI devices are engineered to administer vaccines or medications without needles, employing technologies such as high-pressure air or mechanical pressure to penetrate the skin or tissues (Kumar, 2012). If both techniques, needle and NFI, yield equivalent absorption and distribution rates of the administered substances, it can be concluded that there is no significant difference in treatment efficacy, which is consistent with the comparable growth performance observed in the pigs (Igarashi, 2006; Schneider et al., 1994).

The findings also align with Cho et al. (2022), which reported no significant differences in growth performance of pigs vaccinated against Porcine Circovirus type 2a/b (PCV2a/b) and Mycoplasma hyopneumoniae, irrespective of vaccination method (needle-free vs. conventional needle injection). Collectively, these data support the practical conclusion that NFI can be deployed without compromising production performance under field conditions.

Acute stress response: cortisol interpretation and welfare considerations

Cortisol, a steroid hormone generated by the adrenal cortex, is essential for the body's stress response since it regulates crucial physiological processes including metabolism and immunological function (Plut et al., 2023). Normal cortisol levels in pigs vary between 2.75 and 3.18 µg/dL (Jackson & Cockcroft, 2002), but may rapidly increase in response to stressors such as immunization, handling, or environmental change. In the present investigation, both the control and NFI groups showed substantial post-vaccination cortisol elevations that exceeded baseline levels, consistent with prior work describing stress responses after vaccination (Bozovic et al., 2013; Wolf et al., 2001). Cortisol levels generally peak 10–30 min after exposure to a stressor depending on type and severity (Martínez-Miró et al., 2016), reflecting activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis. The manner of immunization can influence stress responses; for example, intradermal and NFI procedures have been associated with reduced stress compared with standard intramuscular approaches in some contexts (Temple et al., 2020). Nevertheless, vaccination inherently entails handling and restraint, which can elevate stress (Martínez-Miró et al., 2016).

While serum cortisol served as the primary physiological indicator of acute stress in this study, we acknowledge that a multidimensional approach incorporating behavioral assessments (e.g., vocalization analysis, activity monitoring) and additional physiological markers (e.g., chromogranin A, acute phase proteins) would provide a more comprehensive welfare evaluation. Future research should incorporate such measures to capture both acute and chronic stress responses more fully. However, the visual assessment of injection sites in this study (Fig. 5) indicated rapid healing and the absence of chronic inflammation or abscesses, supporting the conclusion that the NFI system did not compromise observable physical well-being under the conditions evaluated.

In the present study, no significant variations in cortisol levels were seen between the control and NFI groups, suggesting that the injection method did not substantially alter acute cortisol responses in this setting. These data imply that both needle-free and traditional vaccination procedures may induce similar acute stress responses, potentially because handling and confinement are shared procedural determinants. Given that cortisol can be influenced by handling and procedural factors (Martínez-Miró et al., 2016; Wolf et al., 2001), and because additional welfare markers were not assessed, overall welfare status cannot be conclusively determined from cortisol alone. These findings are consistent with studies reporting no significant cortisol differences between NFI and needle-based methods (Temple et al., 2017). Dalmau et al. (2021) similarly found no significant difference in salivary cortisol between needle-based and needle-free intradermal PRRS vaccination. Conversely, Scollo et al. (2020) reported no cortisol increase following NFI immunization in piglets, whereas a temporary increase was observed here; such differences may reflect methodological variation (e.g., vaccine type, sampling time, animal age) rather than a true contradiction.

Although serum cortisol was selected in the present study as a practical field-feasible indicator of acute physiological stress, it should not be interpreted as a complete proxy for animal welfare. Animal welfare is multidimensional and may be influenced by behavioral, physiological, and tissue-level responses that extend beyond short-term endocrine activation alone. The present work was designed as an initial field-based comparative study under the scope of the current project framework, and therefore prioritized endpoints that could be collected reliably under commercial finishing-pig conditions. Accordingly, the welfare-related interpretation of the present findings should remain limited to the absence of treatment-related differences in acute cortisol response together with acceptable observable macroscopic injection-site outcomes, rather than a comprehensive determination of welfare superiority.

An additional consideration is the timing of cortisol sampling. Because serum samples were collected 30 min after vaccination, the present data may not fully capture the earliest peak phase of the acute endocrine stress response, which in some circumstances may occur earlier after the stimulus. Therefore, the current findings are most appropriately interpreted as a comparison of treatment-related cortisol status at a fixed and standardized post-procedure time point under field conditions, rather than as a complete temporal characterization of stress-hormone kinetics. More detailed profiling of acute stress dynamics would require earlier or serial sampling after injection.

Humoral immune response: non-inferiority under field conditions and boundary of inference

The immune response to vaccination is a crucial element in assessing the feasibility of alternative injection techniques. The present study found that pigs in both the control and NFI groups showed increased neutralizing antibody levels at 42 dpv (6 weeks post 1st vaccination) and then an even stronger response to the booster dose (at 56 dpv). The comprehensive immune response to the CSF vaccine generally entails activation of both humoral and cellular mechanisms, with B cells generating antibodies and T cells facilitating cellular immunity (Blome et al., 2017; Ganges et al., 2020; Ji et al., 2015). Muñoz-González et al. (2015) reported that immune response peaks following CSF vaccination typically occur several weeks post-administration, consistent with the antibody increase observed in the current study. Salman et al. (2023) further reported that intradermal needle-free vaccination can effectively prevent the spread of African Swine Fever (ASF), suggesting that needle-free approaches can elicit effective immune responses in relevant contexts.

The study revealed no significant differences in Classical Swine Fever (CSF)-specific neutralizing antibody levels between the control and NFI groups throughout the experiment, indicating that the vaccine delivery method did not measurably affect the humoral response in this field setting. This is consistent with evidence that needle-free vaccination can produce antibody levels comparable to conventional needle-based approaches (Wataradee, 2018). Lin et al. (2023) also indicate that vaccination effectiveness is primarily influenced by vaccine composition and host immune status rather than delivery method. Collectively, these data support the interpretation that NFI elicited a non-inferior CSF-specific neutralizing antibody response compared to conventional methods under commercial conditions.

Importantly, immune evaluation in this study was limited strictly to humoral immunity (neutralizing antibodies). Since our study solely measured neutralizing antibody titers without assessing cell-mediated immunity (e.g., T-cell activation, cytokine production) or conducting a live-virus challenge, claims regarding overall immune equivalence or long-term protection cannot be made and remain outside the scope of our findings. The study also applied a dual-validation logic regarding vaccine selection: CSF (aqueous) served as the biological indicator for immunogenic feasibility, whereas administration of the oil-adjuvanted FMD vaccine served as a physical stress-test of the device’s capability to deliver high-viscosity fluids. Consequently, serological analysis appropriately focused on the biological model (CSF) to maintain the study’s specific scope. Similarly, conclusions regarding immune performance should remain confined to the measured humoral endpoint. In the present study, the evidence supports non-inferior CSF-specific neutralizing antibody responses under the evaluated field conditions; however, the data do not establish full immunological equivalence, long-term durability of protection, or challenge-confirmed efficacy. Because cell-mediated immunity, broader immune profiling, and live-virus challenge studies were not included, the present findings should be interpreted as supporting humoral comparability within the defined sampling period rather than comprehensive immune validation of the delivery platform. From an immunological perspective, the present findings should therefore be interpreted as evidence of comparable humoral response for the CSF model antigen within the evaluated sampling period, rather than as a complete characterization of vaccine-induced immunity. Because cellular immune responses were not assessed, the study cannot determine whether the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system influences T-cell activation, cytokine production, IFN-γ-related responses, or other aspects of cell-mediated immunity that may contribute to longer-term protection. Similarly, because FMD vaccination was included primarily as a delivery-feasibility challenge for a high-viscosity formulation, the present study does not provide direct evidence regarding FMD-specific immune efficacy. These issues represent important next-step questions for future immunological validation of the platform. Although neutralizing antibody titers provide an important functional indicator of humoral response, they should not be interpreted as direct proof of in vivo protective efficacy in the absence of controlled pathogen challenge. In the present study, the comparable CSF-specific neutralizing antibody responses observed between the NFI and control groups support humoral comparability under field conditions, but they do not establish whether this response would translate into equivalent resistance to infection or disease after viral exposure. Therefore, the present findings should be regarded as supportive immunological evidence rather than definitive proof of challenge-confirmed protection.

Carcass outcomes and injection-related defects

The present study found no significant difference in carcass quality between the control and NFI group. This finding is consistent with Temple et al. (2017), which reported no significant differences in carcass traits between needle-based and NFI methods. Rey et al. (2015) also indicated that needle-free systems can deliver vaccines effectively without adversely affecting production performance, including carcass traits. Similarly, Houser et al. (2004) reported that transdermal needle-free devices can be effective for vaccine delivery without negatively affecting carcass outcomes or injection-site lesions. These findings collectively suggest that vaccination-associated handling and physiological responses do not necessarily translate into measurable carcass differences (Temple et al., 2017).

The picnic cut percentage showed an interesting but non-significant trend (P= 0.053), with the NFI group slightly greater than the control group. Because this result was not statistically significant and broader carcass composition indicators (e.g., lean percentage and loin eye area) were consistent between groups, it should be interpreted cautiously. Furthermore, in the absence of histopathological data, this trend cannot be definitively attributed to reduced microscopic tissue trauma or reduced localized inflammation at the injection site. In addition to real-time visual monitoring of the injection site, post-mortem carcass inspection was conducted to verify internal tissue integrity. Crucially, no abscesses, granulomas, or specific trim losses were observed in the neck region (primary injection site) or shoulder/forelimb area in any NFI-vaccinated pigs. This supports the interpretation that transient surface reactions did not translate into observable carcass defects at post-mortem inspection under the conditions evaluated.

Practical implications and commercial considerations (appropriately framed)

This study indicates that NFI elicits a comparable humoral immune response, growth performance, and cortisol-based acute stress response relative to traditional needle-based vaccination in pigs. Further research may investigate welfare and quality implications by integrating additional welfare measures, including wound scoring, vocalizations during the procedure, pain-related behaviors, injection-site sensitivity, and feed intake on the day of the procedure. Furthermore, incorporating assessments of meat quality (taste, texture) would enhance understanding of longer-term implications for animal welfare and product quality. The electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system presents preliminary potential for use in commercial swine production. Conceptually, compared to conventional needle-based vaccination, the NFI system eliminates the need for disposable needles, which may reduce biohazard waste, minimize needle-stick injuries among workers, and lower cross-contamination risk. When compared to gas-pressure or spring-driven needle-free systems, an electromagnetic actuator can offer practical advantages, including programmable control and operation without reliance on external gas cartridges, which may simplify logistics and maintenance. From an engineering perspective, the use of short-duration electrical pulses is intended to reduce continuous current exposure and potentially mitigate coil heating during operation. However, because real-time thermal monitoring and high-cycle endurance testing were not performed within the scope of this field study, statements regarding long-term thermal stability and device durability are preliminary and should be interpreted strictly as engineering considerations rather than validated performance outcomes. Future work should therefore include systematic endurance-cycle testing and in situ thermal measurements to quantitatively confirm long-term robustness under commercial-scale use. A primary barrier to the adoption of NFI technology is the higher initial equipment cost compared with conventional disposable syringes. Potential economic implications may include reduced needle-related consumables and biohazard waste, as well as possible labor-time savings due to faster administration. Nevertheless, this study did not perform a quantitative cost comparison or techno-economic evaluation, and therefore no definitive conclusions regarding cost-effectiveness, scalability, or return on investment can be made. These considerations are presented as plausible operational drivers that require confirmation through dedicated economic modeling under commercial conditions. Accordingly, practical implementation should presently be viewed as promising but still preliminary, pending larger-scale validation of long-term device reliability, maintenance demands, operational consistency, and performance across a wider range of vaccine formulations and target populations. An additional strength of the present study lies in its integration of engineering readiness with field-based biological evaluation. The injector used here was not assessed solely as an experimental prototype, but as a previously validated actuator platform that was calibrated, pre-verified, and then deployed in a commercial vaccination setting using standardized handling, randomized treatment allocation, and defined biological and production endpoints. This combination of prior engineering validation and applied field comparison strengthens the translational significance of the findings and supports the argument that the platform merits further validation at larger scale. In this respect, the present study should be viewed not only as a comparison of two injection methods, but also as a methodologically rigorous intermediate step linking device development to real-world livestock implementation. Although the present findings are encouraging, they should be interpreted within the specific evidentiary scope of this field-based comparative study. The data support the conclusion that, under the commercial conditions evaluated, the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system produced comparable outcomes to conventional needle injection with respect to CSF-specific neutralizing antibody response, growth performance, cortisol-based acute stress response, carcass traits, and observable macroscopic injection-site status. However, these findings should not be interpreted as demonstrating comprehensive immune equivalence, definitive tissue-level safety, or full commercial readiness. Rather, the principal contribution of the present work is to provide field-level comparative evidence that this delivery system can be integrated into routine finishing-pig vaccination without evidence of adverse effects on the measured production and macroscopic safety outcomes.

From a commercial-implementation perspective, the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI platform should also be considered relative to other needle-free technologies, such as gas-pressure and spring-driven systems. Conceptually, an electromagnetic system may offer practical advantages, including programmable operation, controlled repeatability of actuation, and reduced dependence on external gas cartridges or mechanically preloaded spring assemblies. These features may simplify some aspects of field operation and system control. However, such potential advantages must be balanced against possible higher initial equipment cost, electrical subsystem complexity, maintenance requirements, and the need for long-term durability validation. Therefore, although the present platform appears promising from an engineering and operational standpoint, the current study does not provide sufficient evidence to conclude that it is economically superior or commercially more competitive than other existing NFI systems.

In practical deployment, cost competitiveness is likely to depend not only on initial procurement cost, but also on utilization rate, maintenance interval, component replacement frequency, operator training requirements, workflow efficiency, and cost per successfully delivered dose. Therefore, even if an electromagnetic actuator-based platform has a higher capital cost, its overall economic position may differ substantially depending on how these downstream operational factors perform in commercial use. Because such variables were not quantified in the present study, any interpretation regarding relative economic advantage should remain preliminary.

Taken together, the present findings should be interpreted as providing an important field-based comparative foundation for evaluation of the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI platform, rather than as a complete validation of the technology across all biological, engineering, and commercial dimensions. Under the commercial finishing-pig conditions evaluated here, the system demonstrated comparable production-related outcomes, non-inferior CSF-specific humoral response, acceptable observable injection-site tolerance, and practical feasibility of administering both aqueous and oil-adjuvanted formulations. At the same time, the study was not designed to resolve broader questions regarding cellular immune efficacy, challenge-confirmed protection, microscopic tissue safety, formulation-specific delivery mechanics, long-term durability, or economic competitiveness. Accordingly, the principal contribution of the present work is to establish a translationally relevant basis upon which these next-stage validation questions can be addressed more rigorously.

Limitations

This study was designed as a comparative evaluation conducted under commercial finishing-pig conditions, prioritizing translational relevance and operational feasibility. As a consequence, several limitations should be explicitly acknowledged to ensure that interpretation and conclusions remain fully aligned with the scope of evidence generated.

First, immune profiling in the present study was limited to CSF-specific neutralizing antibody titers as a field-practical surrogate marker of humoral response. We did not assess cell-mediated immunity (e.g., antigen-specific T-cell responses or IFN-γ–related endpoints), nor did we conduct controlled challenge studies. Therefore, conclusions regarding immune performance should be restricted to non-inferior humoral responses under field conditions and should not be extended to comprehensive immune protection or long-term efficacy beyond the endpoints measured.

Second, injection-site safety was evaluated through macroscopic monitoring and post-mortem carcass inspection, consistent with routine farm conditions. Histopathological assessment, microscopic tissue characterization, and quantitative inflammation scoring were not performed. Accordingly, our safety conclusions are limited to observable macroscopic outcomes and do not support definitive statements regarding tissue-level trauma reduction or microscopic inflammatory effects.

Third, although oil-adjuvanted (high-viscosity) vaccines were administered in the field setting, the study did not include quantitative dose recovery testing or detailed jet-output characterization under viscous conditions (e.g., pressure/velocity profiling across viscosity ranges). As a result, the present work supports field feasibility of administration but does not constitute full quantitative validation of performance across diverse vaccine formulations.

Fourth, although the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system was successfully operated during the trial, this field study did not include dedicated long-duration engineering benchmarking (e.g., high-cycle endurance testing and in situ thermal monitoring). Therefore, conclusions regarding long-term technical robustness are not claimed here and should be addressed in subsequent controlled evaluations.

Fifth, no formal techno-economic or cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted; accordingly, commercial feasibility and scalability are discussed only at a conceptual level and require quantitative cost–benefit/ROI assessment under commercial-scale conditions.

An additional limitation concerns the biological and population-specific scope of the study. The work was conducted in a defined population of commercial finishing pigs under a specific farm-management setting. Accordingly, the applicability of the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI system to other ages, physiological states, production stages, or immunological conditions was not evaluated. The findings should therefore be interpreted within the context of the vaccines, delivery conditions, and finishing-pig population studied, and broader generalization will require additional validation in other target populations.

The present study also has statistical boundaries that should be recognized. Although an a priori power analysis indicated that the sample size was sufficient to detect large effects for the primary outcomes, the study may have been less sensitive to smaller but still biologically meaningful differences between treatment groups. Therefore, the absence of statistical significance should not be interpreted as proof of complete equivalence across all endpoints. Larger-scale studies would be valuable for improving precision and for detecting more subtle biological, welfare-related, or production-related differences.

A related limitation is that welfare assessment was intentionally restricted to acute cortisol measurement and macroscopic injection-site observation. Behavioral responses during and after vaccination, additional physiological or inflammatory markers such as acute phase proteins, and histopathological tissue assessment were not included. Consequently, the present findings should not be interpreted as a comprehensive welfare evaluation or as definitive evidence of welfare superiority. In addition, reproductive or farrowing-related outcomes were not relevant to the present design because the study animals were finishing pigs rather than breeding sows.

Taken together, these limitations reflect the intentional scope of an initial comparative field study rather than isolated omissions. The study was designed to determine whether the electromagnetic actuator-based NFI platform could produce acceptable biological and production-related outcomes under real farm conditions, not to provide full mechanistic immune validation, histopathological confirmation of tissue safety, formulation-independent engineering characterization, comprehensive economic benchmarking, or long-term endurance verification. These unresolved areas therefore define the boundary between field feasibility and full translational validation.

At the same time, the commercial farm environment that constrained data depth also represents a strength of the study, because it provides evidence under conditions directly relevant to practical use. The present findings should therefore be viewed as an important foundation for subsequent work rather than as a final validation endpoint. Priority next steps include longer-term immune follow-up, expanded cellular immune profiling, controlled challenge studies, targeted histopathological evaluation of the neck injection site, formulation-specific engineering testing across viscosity conditions, repeated-cycle endurance and thermal monitoring, and formal techno-economic analysis under commercial-scale deployment. Such studies will be necessary to determine whether the promising field-based comparative performance observed here can be extended to broader claims regarding durable immune protection, tissue-level safety, engineering robustness, and commercial scalability

Conclusion

This field-based comparative study supports the practical feasibility of the electromagnetic actuator-based needle-free injection (NFI) system under commercial finishing-pig conditions. The NFI system elicited a non-inferior CSF-specific neutralizing antibody response compared with conventional needle injection, while showing comparable growth performance, acute cortisol response, carcass characteristics, and acceptable macroscopic injection-site outcomes. No severe observable local adverse reactions or gross carcass abnormalities were detected at the neck injection site or adjacent grossly inspected tissues under the evaluated field conditions. However, these findings should be interpreted within the boundaries of the measured endpoints. The present study did not include long-term immune follow-up, cell-mediated immune profiling, challenge studies, histopathological tissue analysis, formulation-specific engineering validation for high-viscosity vaccines, long-duration durability testing, or formal techno-economic assessment. Therefore, while the present work provides important field-based comparative evidence extending prior engineering validation into a practically relevant vaccination setting, broader conclusions regarding comprehensive immune protection, tissue-level safety, long-term technical robustness, and commercial competitiveness cannot yet be made. Future studies should address these remaining evidence gaps through integrated biological, engineering, and techno-economic validation.

Development and psychometric testing of a parental knowledge, attitude, and practices questionnaire on pentavalent vaccine safety
Authors: Feroze Fathima, Mahesh Mahadevaiah Nosenoor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivesPentavalent vaccine safety awareness significantly influences parental acceptance, yet evidence-based knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to adverse events following pentavalent vaccination remain underexplored. This study aimed to develop and psychometrically validate the knowledge, attitude, and practices on pentavalent vaccine safety questionnaire to assess parental perspectives on pentavalent vaccine safety.MethodsA sequential mixed-methods psychometric validation study was conducted in three stages: instrument development, pilot testing, and large-scale psychometric evaluation. Healthcare professionals were purposively sampled, while parents or caregivers of infants scheduled for pentavalent vaccination in rural Karnataka, South India, were selected via convenience sampling (pilot) and stratified random sampling (large-scale).ResultsAn initial 50-item close-ended questionnaire, covering knowledge, attitude, and practice domains, was drafted through literature review and refined by an expert panel. Content validity was confirmed with content validity ratio ≥0.6 and content validity index ≥0.8, resulting in 34 retained and 16 revised items. Pilot testing in 24 rural parents and validation in 321 rural parents demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha ≥0.7) and test-retest reliability (intraclass correlation coefficient ≥0.8). Discriminant validity was observed with significantly higher practice score differences for parents with prior adverse immunisation events exposure (n=24). Exploratory factor analysis of 11 thematic domains identified three factors explaining 43.9% of total variance, confirming construct validity.Interpretation and conclusionThe knowledge, attitude, and practices on pentavalent vaccine safety questionnaire is a valid and reliable tool identifying gaps in parental knowledge, attitude and practice related to awareness and reporting of pentavalent vaccine safety, supporting improved monitoring and public trust.

Knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) questionnaire is a structured research instrument that are utilised to assess the parental understanding, behaviours, and practices in healthcare.1-3 These instruments are developed and validated based on the scientific literatures, expert consultation, focus group discussions followed by psychometric testing.4-13

World Health Organization (WHO), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and India’s Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) offer guidance about identifying, monitoring, and reporting of adverse events following immunisation (AEFI).1,2,7-12 Previous studies have developed and validated KAP questionnaire about AEFI. A survey study assessed attitude of parents about their children’s immunisation utilising validated parents’ attitude on childhood vaccination questionnaire.14 A cross-sectional study has assessed KAP including reporting practice of graduate medical students about AEFI for vaccines utilising standard questionnaire without validation.15 A randomised controlled trial has assessed the impact of clinical pharmacist intervention on AEFI among paediatric population using customised KAP.16 Questionnaires used in these studies were with limitation such as no knowledge, or practice domains, or without any psychometric validation.14-24

In previous clinical studies, interview-based assessment, pretested questionnaire, and semi-structured interviews were carried out to understand the KAP of parents about pentavalent vaccine and AEFI following pentavalent vaccination.15,25-28 These facts suggest that there is a need to develop and validate KAP questionnaire for parents about pentavalent vaccine and AEFI following vaccination among their children as it helps to strengthen AEFI reporting system.

Methods

This study was undertaken by the department of Pharmacy Practice, KLE College of Pharmacy, Bengaluru, India. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethical Committee (Human), KAHER, Belagavi. A written informed consent was obtained from all participants to ensure confidentiality.

Study design

A sequential mixed-methods psychometric validation study was conducted in three stages. The first stage involved developing the knowledge, attitude, and practices on pentavalent vaccine safety (KAP-PTVS) questionnaire through a qualitative exploratory study design over six months (October 2022-March 2023). Healthcare experts specialising in AEFI, independent of the authors’ team, were purposively sampled from Karnataka’s community healthcare sectors. They critically reviewed, refined, and synthesised questionnaire items ensuring comprehensive domain coverage during instrument development. The second stage was a mixed-methods feasibility and pretesting study over three months (March 2023-June 2023), involving parents or caretakers of pentavalent vaccine-eligible infants recruited through convenience sampling, alongside similarly sampled healthcare experts. The third stage was a cross-sectional methodological validation study over twelve months (July 2023-June 2024), recruiting parents or caretakers via stratified random sampling to ensure representative data across Karnataka (Supplementary Table I).

Study criteria

The first stage included healthcare professionals from medical and pharmaceutical fields across urban and rural Karnataka, native Kannada speaker, aged 25 years or older, with at least three years of experience in immunisation or AEFI management and consenting to participate. The second stage included the same healthcare professional criteria and a pilot group of parents or caregivers aged ≥18 years from rural Karnataka with infants ≤12 months scheduled for pentavalent vaccine. Parents of infants who completed all vaccine doses or declined participation were excluded. The third stage expanded to a larger sample of similar parents or caregivers from rural Karnataka.

Study instrument and sources

The KAP-PTVS questionnaire was developed based on medical information available through standard search engines such as PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science. Relevant information was searched using keywords. The content and format of the questionnaire were compiled using foundational resources from World Health Organization (WHO), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) guidelines.1,2,7-13 It included three domains: knowledge (four themes, 24-items), attitude (four themes, 15-items), and practice (three themes, 11-items), each scored to quantify KAP depth11 (Supplementary Tables II and III).

Study procedure

The study, approved by the Ethical Committee (Human), was conducted in three stages. The first stage involved qualitatively developing the KAP-PTVS questionnaire through a comprehensive literature review of databases, focusing on vaccine safety, AEFI reporting, parental knowledge, immunisation programmes, and healthcare communication. Content was aligned with World Health Organization (WHO),1,2,7,13 CDC,8-10 and MoHFW11-12 guidelines, with three experts’ input, resulting in a structured English version of 50-item questionnaire across three domains (knowledge, attitude, practice) and 11 themes, supported by a quantitative scoring system.4,14,17-24,25-28,29

In the second stage, the questionnaire underwent pilot testing and initial psychometric evaluation. It was circulated purposively among ten healthcare professionals for content validity with feedback received from five experts and incorporated into revisions.29-31 The revised questionnaire was then self-administered conveniency to 24 rural parents of pentavalent vaccine-eligible infants in Chikkaballapur, India, via structured interviews in Kannada and English, maintaining anonymity. Pilot testing assessed reliability, including internal consistency and test-retest reliability (subgroup of eight) with 24 days apart, and discriminant validity by comparing parents with and without prior AEFI experience.3,5,6,17-19,22,32,33

The third stage involved large-scale psychometric validation with 321 rural parents selected through stratified random sampling based on the number of primary health centres in each of the six taluks. The sample size met the recommended 5:1 subject-to-item ratio for exploratory factor analysis.14,34 The finalised questionnaire was administered to these parents for psychometric testing, including Cronbach’s alpha reliability and construct validity assessments. Additionally, a subset of 107 parents completed the questionnaire twice, 24 days apart, to evaluate test-retest reliability. All data were entered, sorted, and cleaned using Microsoft Excel, then analysed with IBM statistical package for the social sciences (SPSS; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) version 20.0.

Data analysis

Data were analysed using the IBM SPSS version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics summarised demographics. Internal consistency was assessed with Cronbach’s alpha.3-5,14,18,32 and test-retest reliability with the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) using a two-way mixed-effects ICC model.4,6,14,22,23,33 Content validity through the content validity ratio and content validity index.14,29-32,35 Discriminant validity involved normality testing (Shapiro–Wilk),36 followed by independent t-test or Mann–Whitney U tests comparing groups with and without prior AEFI.14,15,37 Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) (principal axis factoring, varimax rotation) guided by Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Bartlett’s tests, with factor retention determined by eigenvalues ≥1 and scree plot inspection.18,34 Statistically significance was set at P<0.05.

Results

Stage one: Instrument development

The KAP-PTVS questionnaire was developed. The Knowledge domain (24-items, four themes) assessed parental awareness of vaccine basics, adverse events, reporting procedures, and misconceptions. The Attitude domain (15-items, four themes) evaluated confidence in vaccine safety, concerns about AEFIs, the importance of reporting, and community health impact. The Practice domain (11-items, three themes) examined observation of AEFIs, reporting methods, and readiness to educate or manage AEFI-related costs.

The KAP domains were scored and classified based on percentage cut-offs from total possible score of 126. Standardised percentage cut-offs were applied and categorised as low (0–50%), moderate (51–75%), and high (>75%) levels of knowledge, attitude, or practice. Knowledge items often use correct/incorrect scoring (0-2 score); attitude items use 3-point-Likert scales; practice items use yes/no or frequency-based scoring (0-3 score). This system facilitated identifying gaps and targeting interventions effectively (Supplementary Tables II and IV).

Stage two: Pilot psychometric validation

The pilot psychometric validation stage involved assessing validity through content and discriminant validity tests and evaluating reliability via internal consistency and test-retest methods.

Content validity

Content validity was established by engaging a panel of ten invited experts (two POs, four MOs, three taluk health officers, and one healthcare insurance agent). Five provided detailed feedback that was incorporated. The panel included professionals in pharmacovigilance, clinical medicine, and public health, averaging 5.6 years of experience and holding various medical and pharmaceutical degrees and roles. Most were female (60%) aged between 25–45 years and served as MOs (60%), with affiliations primarily to government organisations (60%).

Five experts rated each of the 50-items for its essentiality to calculate the Content Validity Ratio (CVR). Twenty-six items achieved perfect agreement (CVR=1.0), indicating strong consensus, while 24 had low-moderate agreement (CVR≤0.6) prompting further review. The overall average CVR was 0.85, with most knowledge (75%) and attitude (80%) items scoring above 0.6. For Content Validity Index (CVI), the same experts rated items on a 4-point scale for relevance and clarity, resulting in a total Scale-CVI(S-CVI) of 0.948 and domain-specific S-CVIs around 0.95. Based on expert feedback the items were updated accordingly ( Table I).

Discriminant validity

Participants with prior AEFI exposure (n=15) and those without (n=9) were compared on KAP scores. Prior AEFI experienced parents demonstrated higher mean knowledge (24.91±9.20) and practice (19.43±4.43) scores than without prior AEFI experience parents (18.23±9.54 and 3.57±4.83, respectively). Shapiro-Wilk confirmed normality for knowledge and attitude (compared using t-tests), practice scores violated normality in the non-AEFI exposure group required a Mann-Whitney U test. No significant differences appeared in knowledge (P=0.313) or attitude P=0.971), while practice scores differed significantly (P=0.008), with parents who had prior AEFI exposure (Supplementary Table V).

Stage three: Large scale psychometric validation

Stage 3 involved large-scale psychometric validation of the KAP-PTVS questionnaire to confirm its reliability and construct validity ( Table IV).

Reliability

Internal consistency for the overall questionnaire was α=0.83 and α-value ranging from 0.64 to 0.78 for all the domains. Test-retest reliability across the domains ranged from an ICC of 0.69-0.79 with an overall ICC of 0.73 ( Table IV).

Construct validity – Exploratory factor analysis (EFA)

Construct validity of the KAP-PTVS was established using EFA on 11 thematic domain scores derived from 50 items. Sampling adequacy was confirmed (KMO=0.69; Bartlett’s χ2(55)=914.76, p<0.001), supporting factor analysis suitability. Principal axis factoring with Varimax rotation identified three factors with eigenvalues >1, explaining 43.9% of variance. Factor-1 encompassed AEFI reporting and safety concerns, Factor-2 captured pentavalent vaccine knowledge, and Factor-3 related to vaccine efficacy and preventive practices. The confidence in vaccine safety theme cross-loaded significantly on factor 1 and 3, indicating its multidimensional nature linking vaccine safety reporting and preventive practices. However, given the scree plot, eigenvalue criteria, and absence of a cohesive set of items defining a separate factor, retained a three-factor solution. Loadings exceeded 0.40, and communalities ranged 0.20–0.82, indicating the three-factor solution robustly represents the questionnaire’s core constructs, confirming strong construct validity ( Figure, Table V, and Supplementary Tables VI-IX).

Discussion

This study developed and validated the KAP-PTVS Questionnaire to assess parental KAP regarding pentavalent vaccine, AEFI awareness, and reporting. Integrating frameworks from WHO, CDC, and India’s MoHFW, it represents one of the first comprehensive, contextually grounded instruments addressing both vaccine safety perceptions and vaccinovigilance behaviour among rural parents in India. Unlike prior KAP studies, it uniquely includes a practice domain capturing parental engagement in vaccinovigilance.

Development followed a systematic process through literature review, alignment with established guidelines, and experts’ consultation.1,2,7-13 This method mirrors the scale development approaches described by Boateng et al38, where expert consultation ensures contextual appropriateness. The final questionnaire comprised 50 items across three domains: knowledge (24-items), attitude (15-items), and practice (11-items), organised under 11 themes. Scoring employed graded responses with percentage cutoffs, enabling nuanced profiling beyond dichotomous scoring methods used in earlier studies by Al-lela et al.23

Rigorous psychometric testing established content validity, reliability, discriminant validity, and construct validity. Using the Lawshe method, five multidisciplinary external experts independently rated over half of the items as essential, prompting selective revisions and maintaining the instrument’s scope.30,31,35 Content validity was further confirmed with satisfactory S-CVI, indicating strong clarity and relevance. Iterative feedback and revision reinforced both conceptual and cultural appropriateness.30,31 These findings align with robust psychometric properties reported in similar KAP instruments in other healthcare by Abdul Kadir et al5 (S-CVI/Ave=0.92), Zamanzadeh et al31 (S-CVI=0.93), and Sebastian et al4 (I-CVI=0.955), confirming the tool’s high content validity.

Reliability assessment with the pilot group and large cohort group of participants yielded an acceptable internal consistency with Cronbach’s alpha values of >0.70. These results align with previous KAP studies such as Sebastian et al4 (α=0.89) and Abdul Kadir et al5 (α=0.86–0.89). TRR over 24 days interval revealed low item-level stability and acceptable overall score reproducibility (ICC>0.70), indicating good temporal stability. Comparable reliability has been reported by Dalawi et al6 (ICC=0.562–0.715) and Sebastian et al4 (Spearman’s rho=0.957), reinforcing the KAP-PTVS as a consistent, reliable instrument.

The questionnaire demonstrated meaningful discriminant validity by distinguishing parents with prior AEFI exposure higher practice scores, reflecting adaptive behavioural changes, whereas knowledge and attitude scores showed little difference. This aligns with findings from Fan et al,14 Suresh et al,15 and Karunasree et al,37 where practical behaviours were most influenced by exposure or training, while knowledge and attitudes remained stable. These results confirm the KAP-PTVS questionnaire’s ability to identify meaningful behavioural differences relevant to vaccine safety reporting.

Construct validity was affirmed by EFA, identifying three factors corresponding to vaccine safety concerns, knowledge, and preventive practices. Sampling adequacy and Bartlett’s test supported data suitability, with factor loadings above 0.40 and adequate communalities, demonstrating a solid factor structure aligned with public health scale development guidelines34 and study by Fan et al14.

This study’s rigorous, multi-stage mixed-methods design, purposive and stratified sampling, and robust psychometric validation confirmed the questionnaire’s reliability and validity. The KAP-PTVS offers public-health professionals and policymakers a practical framework to identify and address these behavioural gaps. By identifying specific thematic lacunae, the tool can inform targeted interventions, tailored training modules, and community sensitisation campaigns.18 Its psychometric strength ensures that future studies can employ for both baseline assessment and tracking changes in public perception and behaviour toward vaccination. From a broader perspective, questionnaire contributes meaningfully to India’s vaccinovigilance infrastructure and integrating it into routine immunisation programmes can enhance adverse event reporting, community trust in vaccine safety, and support data-driven decision-making. This is particularly relevant in lower-and-middle income countries, where underreporting and misinformation often undermine public confidence in vaccination.

The study’s geographic restriction to rural Karnataka limits wider generalisability, and some participant subgroups may be underrepresented despite stratified sampling. The questionnaire was developed through iterative literature review and expert revision, not a formal systematic review. The sample size for factor analysis was slightly below the ideal subject-to-item ratio (10:1), possibly affecting factor stability. Content validity was based on a limited number of external experts without a comprehensive Delphi process, which may constrain consensus strength. Reliance on self-reported data may introduce biases, and the cross-sectional design limits assessment of changes over time. Finally, the questionnaire length and limited linguistic validation may impact respondent engagement and broader applicability.

The 50-item KAP-PTVS questionnaire, validated as reliable and psychometrically sound, effectively assesses parental knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to pentavalent vaccine safety and adverse event monitoring. This tool supports HCPs and policymakers in identifying gaps, strengthening vaccinovigilance, and guiding targeted educational interventions to improve vaccine safety and uptake, especially in resource-limited settings.

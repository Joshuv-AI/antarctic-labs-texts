Quantifying spatiotemporal dynamics and driving factors of carbon stock with integrated model in Bohai Bay, China
Authors: Feng Yan, Zimo Zhao, Jing Shan, Xinpeng Wang, Yuwen Zhang, Mengyao Wei, Huijin Pan, Xinyu Liang, Hao Xu, Jiao Pang, Yaheng Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The contradiction between rapid urbanization and ecological protection leads to the aggravation of carbon sink function differentiation in coastal areas. In this study, a framework of "historical assessment-future prediction-mechanism analysis" was constructed, and InVEST, PLUS, SEM, and OPGD models were integratedto study the dynamics of carbon storage and its driving mechanisms in three rapidly urbanizing coastal areas (Beidaihe, Caofeidian and Huanghua) in the Bohai Rim region of China. The main results showed that: (1) carbon storage showed the characteristics of periodic change—the expansion of low-carbon land led to the decline of carbon storage (2000–2010), and then Caofeidian achieved 1.50 × 106 tons of carbon storage restoration through ecological restoration (2010–2020). (2) Carbon stocks are expected to increase in all four scenarios in 2030, with the largest increase in the Cultivated Land Protection (CP) scenario, with a peak of 3.00 × 107 tons. (3)The analysis of the driving mechanism showed that population density was the main driving factor (q = 0.2126–0.2129), and there was a significant nonlinear interaction with socio-economic factors, which jointly shaped the spatial pattern. This analysis reveals the spatiotemporal heterogeneity and core driving factors of carbon storage changes in coastal areas, emphasizes the key role of human activities under urbanization and ecological conflicts, and provides a scientific basis for the implementation of differentiated low-carbon spatial planning in rapidly developing coastal areas.

Introduction

Global climate change has become one of the most serious challenges facing humanity in the twenty-first century, with the emission of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases attracting widespread attention [1]. This threat is due to the fact that it is endangering the balance of the natural ecosystem and the sustainable development of human society through multiple pathways, including the frequent occurrence of extreme weather, the loss of biodiversity, and the degradation of ecosystem functions [2]. As the world’s largest developing country, the Chinese government has advocated for carbon peaking by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2060 [3]. Terrestrial ecosystems, as an important buffer zone [4], have the capacity to effect change to the global carbon cycle through photosynthesis and respiration [5]. However, land use/cover change (LUCC) exerts a significant impact on regional carbon stocks [6], and the carbon emissions caused by LUCC account for approximately one-third of the total emissions generated by human activities [7]. Consequently, elucidating the characteristics of spatial and temporal variability in regional carbon stocks, in addition to the driving mechanisms, is of paramount importance for optimising land-use structures and facilitating green, low-carbon, and sustainable development.

In recent years, studies on the impacts of land use changes on ecosystem carbon storage have extended from terrestrial ecosystems to the complex coastal zone. Particular attention has been given to ecosystems with high carbon sequestration capacities, such as the coastal zone [8, 9] and mangroves [10, 11]. Currently, various methods, models, and field investigations are employed to estimate carbon sequestration and simulate land use changes [12, 13]. Joloro H [14] utilized the InVEST model to conduct a spatio-temporal assessment of carbon sequestration due to land use changes in the central region of Central Alborz, Iran. Al—A K [15] combined Google Earth Engine with the InVEST model to evaluate the capacity of land use changes in the mountainous areas of Chittagong, Bangladesh, to store carbon in forest ecosystems. Fang C [16] conducted a study on the spatio-temporal evolution of carbon storage in the eastern coastal areas of Zhejiang Province based on InVEST and GIS modeling. Huiling Z [17] analyzed the characteristics of land use changes and carbon storage in the coastal areas of Shandong Province over the years using the PLUS and InVEST models. It can be observed that the InVEST model demonstrates significant advantages in estimating carbon storage over long time series and at large spatial scales, primarily due to its low data requirements and rapid processing speed [18]. However, it is important to note that the carbon density parameters within the InVEST model are predominantly assigned static values, which limits its ability to capture the dynamic processes by which coastal wetland carbon storage is influenced by tidal inundation frequency. The PLUS model, on the other hand, employs a random forest algorithm to analyze driving factors and reconstruct land cover patterns. It features a highly integrated and flexible simulation framework [19], enabling more realistic simulations of land use change [20]. Despite its strengths, the PLUS model often overlooks the interactive drivers between terrestrial and marine components when applied to coastal zones. While the model performs well at the regional scale, its accuracy diminishes in highly heterogeneous local coastal environments. In view of the complex mechanism of multi-factor nonlinear interaction in coastal ecosystems, this study utilizes the Optimal parameter Geodetector (OPGD) method, which can automatically optimize through the number of independent variable categories to obtain the best explanatory power of influencing factors and determine the spatial heterogeneity of carbon storage caused by different variables [21, 22], but it is difficult to establish clear causal directions and paths, and the complex causal relationships among multiple variables cannot be captured [23]. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) is a statistical method for analyzing the relationships between variables based on the covariance matrix of variables, and is suitable for analyzing the path relationships among complex variables [24]. The causal paths it reveals can, in turn, help explain the potential mechanisms of spatial interactions detected by OPGD. The combination of the two can provide multi-scale and multi-dimensional insights. OPGD focuses on identifying the dominant factors of spatial heterogeneity of carbon storage, while SEM focuses on quantifying the complex causal relationships among variables. Although SEM has limitations compared with OPGD in revealing the nonlinear relationship among driving factors, it is still an effective method for clarifying the direct and indirect effects of multiple potential variables on carbon storage. In conclusion, linking OPGD with SEM helps to gain a deeper understanding of the complex mechanisms driving carbon storage.

However, the current research has the following three significant gaps: (1) Insufficient regional targeting; Although LUCC has conducted numerous studies on carbon storage, the existing achievements are mostly concentrated in provinces and cities [12, 25] or river basin [26, 27], however, there is a lack of refined research focusing on the ecological fragility of the sea-land interaction type. Among them, the Bohai Rim region, as a key economic belt and ecological barrier in northern China, its complex ecosystems such as coastal wetlands, saline-alkali land, and port clusters have a unique impact on the carbon cycle. However, research on the carbon storage of land use in various types of coastal zones in this region is almost non-existent. (2) Limitations of the driving mechanism analysis; The existing driving analysis of the explanatory power of multi-quantitative single variables for carbon storage [28] failed to integrate factors such as policies and regulations, and nature conservation for quantitative display [29], and lacked systematic verification of the nonlinear interaction influence of natural and human factors. (3) The complex causal chain breaks; The change in carbon storage is the result of the combined effect of macroscopic LUCC and microscopic geographical factors. The current research methods are difficult to simultaneously analyze the direct effect and the indirect path, and its hidden mechanism of action has not yet been clarified.

Based on the above gaps, this paper selects Beidaihe District (coastal tourism)—Caofeidian District (heavy industrial port)—Huanghua City (saline-alkali agriculture) as the study areas to identify the multiple drivers of LUCC on the spatial heterogeneity of carbon storage, which is crucial for improving the quality and stability of the ecosystem. In view of this, the purpose of this study is: (1) to clarify the changes and development trends of carbon storage, reveal the changes in carbon storage in the three regions under different land use patterns, and predict the land use changes and the development trends of carbon storage under different scenarios in 2030. (2) Analyze the nonlinear interaction influence of natural and human factors, quantify the impact of single-factor and multi-factor coupling mechanisms on the spatial differentiation of carbon storage, and provide a reference for refined spatial management. (3) Clarify the causal paths of multi-dimensional driving factors, deeply analyze the complex causality and effect magnitudes of multiple factors influencing the dynamic changes of carbon storage through direct and indirect paths, and quantitatively reveal the action paths and relative contributions of different driving factors. In order to balance economic development with carbon sink protection and increase the carbon storage of terrestrial ecosystems, providing a theoretical basis for formulating differentiated carbon neutrality paths for the Bohai Rim Economic Circle, and contributing to the realization of the "dual carbon" goals.

Overview of the research area

Beidaihe District, Caofeidian District and Huanghua City are all located in the coastal economic zone of Hebei Province, which is a typical representative of the ecological protection and economic development of the Bohai Sea Rim region. Beidaihe District (119°24′ ~ 119°38′E, 39°47′ ~ 39°58′N) is located in the southwest part of Qinhuangdao City, Hebei Province, on the west coast of Bohai Bay, with a total area of 112.1 km2 and a coastline of 22.5 km. The terrain slopes from northwest to southeast, and the hills are interspersed with coastal plains, with the highest elevation being 152 m. The climate is mild and humid, with an annual average temperature of 10.3℃ and annual rainfall of 650 ~ 750 mm. The climate is mild and humid, with an annual average temperature of 10.3℃ and annual rainfall of 650 ~ 750 mm, making it a typical ecological area for coastal tourism. The soil is dominated by brown soil and coastal salt soil, the vegetation coverage reaches 62%, with artificial forest and coastal protection forest, and the wetland ecosystem is relatively well preserved. Caofeidian District (118°12′ ~ 118°54′E, 38°55′ ~ 39°27′N) is located on the south coast of Tangshan City, Hebei Province, on the north coast of Bohai Bay, with a total area of 1,943 km2, of which the land area is 1,273 km2 and the sea area is 670 km2. The landforms are mainly coastal plains with an average elevation of less than 5 m, which is significantly affected by tidal action. The climate is a warm temperate semi-humid monsoon climate, with an average annual temperature of 11.2℃ and annual rainfall of 550 ~ 650 mm, concentrated in the summer. The regional soil is dominated by saline and intertidal soils, the natural vegetation is dominated by saline and alkali tolerant herbaceous plants, and the coastal wetland and mudflat ecosystems have a high proportion.Huanghua City (116°50′ ~ 117°30′E, 38°10′ ~ 38°40′N) has plains and coastal areas as its main landform types. It is bordered by the Bohai Sea to the east, Shandong Province to the south, Cangzhou City to the west and Tianjin City to the north. It has a warm temperate semi-humid continental monsoon type climate with four distinct seasons, and rainfall is concentrated in the summer, with an average annual rainfall of about 500–600 mm. The terrain in the area is flat, with an average elevation of about 5 m, and the soil type is dominated by saline soils, with salinisation being more common. Data sources and pre-processing

This study utilized land use raster data from 2000, 2010, and 2020 for Beidaihe District, Caofeidian District, and Huanghua City in Hebei Province as its primary data. The spatial resolution of the base data was 30m × 30m. Accuracy assessment of the land use classification data indicated a high level of consistency (overall accuracy of 83.5% and Kappa coefficient of 0.78 for 2010; overall accuracy of 85.72% and Kappa coefficient of 0.82 for 2020). According to the land classification standards, land use types in Beidaihe and Huanghua were reclassified into six categories: cultivated land, forest land, grassland, water bodies, construction land, and wetland. In Caofeidian, land use types were reclassified into five categories: cultivated land, grassland, water bodies, construction land, and wetland. This adjustment was made due to the very low forest coverage in Caofeidian (only 0.47%), which is below the minimum patch threshold.Thirteen major driving factors were selected as predictors of land use change. All driving factor data were resampled to a 30 m × 30 m resolution and projected to the WGS_1984_UTM_Zone_50N coordinate system. The sources of each driving factor are listed in Table 1. Owing to the lack of national and county road data in Caofeidian District, provincial roads and urban secondary roads were used as substitutes. Research methodology

PLUS forecasting model

The PLUS model is a rule mining framework based on the land expansion analysis strategy (LEAS) and a land use change simulation model based on the multi-class random patch seed (CARS). Among them, the LEAS module can adopt the random forest algorithm to obtain the development probability of different land use types and the contribution rate of the driving factors under the time period based on the expansion part and the corresponding driving factors extracted from the land use data of the two periods.The CARS module can combine the development potential data generated by the LEAS module and the corresponding constraint data to predict the land use status under the target year by inputting the land use demand, the transfer cost matrix and the domain weights to predict the land use status under the target year. In this study, based on the land use data of 2000 and 2010, the domain weight parameters of each land use type are obtained based on the calculation of domain factor weights [Eq. This study comprehensively considers the geographic characteristics and development needs of Beidaihe District, Caofeidian District and Huanghua City. The Markov transfer probability matrix is adjusted in a targeted way by combining existing research results [32, 33] and policy documents to construct four development scenarios that simulate land use changes in the three areas in 2030. In the natural development scenario, the trend in land use between 2010 and 2020 continues, the transfer probability of each land use type remains unchanged and waters are the only restricted conversion area, regardless of other factors;Under the ecological protection scenario, based on the natural development scenario and with reference to the Wetland Conservation Law of the People’s Republic of China and the Hebei Province Wetland Conservation Regulations (2025), wetlands were designated as restricted conversion areas. Additionally, considering the need to protect forest and grassland resources in this scenario, the following probability adjustments were applied: in Beidaihe District and Huanghua City, the probability of forest land and grassland being converted to construction land decreased by 30%, while the probability of cultivated land being converted to forest land and grassland increased by 20%. In Caofeidian District, reflecting its actual conditions, the probability of grassland being converted to construction land decreased by 30%, and the probability of cultivated land being converted to grassland increased by 20%;Under the economic development scenario, water bodies continued to be designated as restricted conversion areas. In accordance with the directive to "strengthen construction land planning indicator guarantees" as outlined in the Rural Revitalization Land Use Policy Guidelines, and with the goals of promoting economic growth and enhancing urbanization levels, the following probability adjustments were implemented.In Beidaihe District, the probability of cultivated land, forest land, and grassland being converted to construction land increased by 30%, while the probability of construction land being converted to cultivated land, forest land, or grassland decreased by 20%.In Caofeidian District, the probability of cultivated land and grassland being converted to construction land increased by 30%, and the probability of construction land being converted to cultivated land, forest land, or grassland decreased by 20%.In Huanghua City, the probability of cultivated land, forest land, and grassland being converted to construction land increased by 20%, while the probability of construction land being converted to cultivated land, forest land, grassland, water bodies, or wetlands decreased by 30%;Under the cultivated land protection scenario, in accordance with the Land Administration Law of the People’s Republic of China and the Regulations on the Protection of Basic Farmland in Hebei Province, cultivated land was designated as a restricted conversion area, and it was assumed that the system of balancing cultivated land occupation and compensation was strictly implemented. The following conversion probability adjustments were made: in Beidaihe District, the probability of cultivated land being converted to construction land decreased by 50%, while the probability of forest land and wetland being converted to cultivated land increased by 30%; in Caofeidian District, the probability of cultivated land being converted to construction land decreased by 50%, and the probability of grassland being converted to cultivated land increased by 30%; in Huanghua City, the probability of cultivated land being converted to construction land decreased by 50%, and the probabilities of forest land and grassland being converted to cultivated land increased by 20%.

Carbon stock calculation based on the InVEST model

The InVEST model is a modelling system for assessing the functional volume of ecosystem services and their economic value, ecosystem management and decision-making [34, 35].The carbon stock module of the model divides carbon stocks in terrestrial ecosystems into four basic carbon pools: the above-ground biomass pool, the below-ground biomass pool, the soil organic carbon pool and the dead organic matter pool [36, 37]. The model operates under the assumption that the carbon pools within the ecosystem remain stable over a designated period. However, it does not consider the highly unstable carbon within these pools. That is to say, the carbon sequestration of patches that do not undergo a change in land use type is assumed to remain constant over time. Consequently, the regional carbon stock at a specified point in the future is estimated. The mean carbon density of the various land use categories was determined and computed in accordance with the land use classification. Thereafter, the area of each category was multiplied by its respective carbon density. Optimal parameter geodetic detector(OPGD)

The OPGD model reveals the spatial differentiation characteristics of carbon stock changes and identifies their main driving factors by detecting and analysing carbon stock changes and their driving factors [44]. Based on the OPGD model, this study integrates a dynamic visualisation output module while improving the accuracy of geographically weighted regression, which effectively supports the multidimensional decision analysis of the regional carbon cycle process. In order to reveal the geographically driven mechanism of carbon stock evolution, the GD program package in R language is used to construct the analysis module, which quantitatively analyses the synergistic intensity of different geographic elements and their spatio-temporal heterogeneity characteristics through geographic partition coupling and spatial weighting matrix operation.

Structural equation model

1. Theoretical framework of structural equation modelling

The primary benefit of structural equation modelling (SEM) is its capacity to concurrently model and elucidate the direct and indirect relationships between multiple independent and dependent variables, thereby estimating the direct and indirect effects of factors on the outcome [46]. This study employs the piecewiseSEM approach to structural modelling, which, in contrast to traditional SEM, does not necessitate the multivariate normal distribution assumption, supports generalised linear models, is suitable for non-normal data, and does not require a global goodness-of-fit test. Segmented structural equation modelling (SEM) can be computed in a number of ways. One method is to construct a basis set, perform goodness-of-fit tests on the full and component models, calculate Akaike information criterion (AIC) scores, return scaled parameter estimates, plot partial correlations and generate predictions [47].

Unlike land use change models such as FLUS and CA-Markov, which excel at simulating spatio-temporal dynamics and predicting future land configurations and quantities, they emphasize spatial patterns and quantitative changes but are weak in deeply analyzing complex statistical relationships and causal pathways among drivers and between drivers and change outcomes. Segmented SEM, conversely, quantifies these multivariate interaction networks and causal paths, compensating for spatial simulation models’ lack of analytical depth in relationship mechanisms. Combining both approaches complements spatial simulation and mechanism analysis for a more comprehensive understanding of land use change.

2. Structural equation modelling construction

Firstly, the model was set in R language, and 13 samples of OPGD were obtained, and SEM was performed on the obtained sample points using piecewiseSEM (R package) in RStudio. Constructing a conceptual model: combining all climate-related drivers into one latent climate variable and all drivers related to population density into one population density variable, the spatial model of climate and population density directly affecting CS was developed according to some assumptions: (1) climate and population density directly affect CS, (2) some factors affect climate and indirectly affect CS, (3) some factors affect population density and indirectly affect CS, and (4) the spatial model of each factor directly affecting CS. In this way, the causal pathways between various variables affecting CS are constructed, and the direct and indirect effects are clarified. The global model is then split into independent submodels, each corresponding to an edge in the path diagram. Then, the independence test is performed to check whether the residuals between the variables that are not explained by the model are independent and ensure the integrity of the model. The sub-model is then evaluated by AIC, BIC, or R2, and the chi-square test is used to evaluate whether the model satisfies the conditional independence assumption. Finally, according to the results of the independence test, the missing path or adjustment variable is added, and the model is re-evaluated until the independence condition is satisfied.

Multi-model workflow

In this study, we developed a systematic analytical framework for multi-model integration. This framework enables the entire research process, from land use change simulation and ecosystem service assessment to driving mechanism analysis, to be carried out in an orderly manner through data transfer and model articulation (Fig. 2). Based on multi-source geospatial data, the framework combines the beneficial features of the PLUS model, the InVEST model, Geoprobe and the structural equation model (SEM) to create a logical and synergistic system of analytical methods.Fig. 2Multi-model workflow diagram

Multi-model workflow diagram

First, the PLUS model uses the Random Forest algorithm to quantify the influence weights of topography, socio-economic factors and others on different land use types, and then simulates the land use change process from 2000 to 2020 and predicts the 2030 scenario. The PLUS model’s high-precision spatial and temporal land use distribution data output is used directly as input parameters for the InVEST model, ensuring consistent data for ecosystem service assessment. The InVEST model combines carbon density parameters for different land use types and converts the PLUS model’s land use maps into results showing the spatial and temporal dynamics of carbon stocks, thus achieving a quantitative transformation from land use change to ecosystem service effects.

Based on this, the geodetector model quantifies the 13 potential factors that affect changes in carbon stocks. By calculating the explanatory power (Q-value) and interaction effects of each factor, the dominant factors are identified and their synergistic mechanisms revealed. This analysis directly provides the theoretical basis and variable screening criteria for constructing the subsequent structural equation model (SEM). The SEM model then integrates the factor analysis results of the geodetector and quantifies the direct effects of natural and human factors on carbon stocks, as well as the indirect effects of land use changes, through path analysis. This allows for a systematic analysis of the cascade mechanism of ‘driving factor–land use change–carbon stock evolution’.

Discussions

Effects of land use change on carbon stocks

This paper assessed the spatial and temporal evolution of land use and carbon stock in Beidaihe District, Caofeidian District and Huanghua City in 2020–2020, and predicted the spatial and temporal evolution of land use and carbon stock in 2030 under different scenarios based on InVEST and PLUS models. The results show that the effect of land use type change on carbon stock shows significant spatial heterogeneity: during 2000–2020, the expansion of construction land in Beidaihe District and Huanghua City leads to a net loss of regional carbon stock (−2.35 × 105t in Beidaihe District and −6.37 × 105t in Huanghua City) through the encroachment of cropland, forest land and other high-carbon-intensive land classes. This result is consistent with the findings of Zhang Zhe et al. [48] and Hou Jiankun et al. [49] that changes in LULC have significantly affected terrestrial ecosystems, and that the reduction in forest and cropland area has led to the loss of carbon stocks. Notably, the carbon stock in the Caofeidian area increased against the trend (1.21 × 106t), mainly due to the conservation of coastal wetlands (the contribution of wetlands to the carbon stock was 52.3%), and its anaerobic environment inhibited the decomposition of organic matter and facilitated the stabilisation of carbon burial, confirming the key role of nature-based solutions (NbS) in enhancing carbon sinks in the coastal zone.

Compared with recent studies in the East Asian agricultural region, this study found that carbon stocks increased most significantly under the farmland protection scenario in Caofeidian District and Huanghua City (2.53 × 106t in Caofeidian District and 8.79 × 104t in Huanghua City), while the expansion of built-up areas under the economic development scenario led to a decrease in carbon stocks (1.99 × 106t, Huanghua City 8.17 × 105t). Beidaihe District had the largest increase in carbon stocks under the ecological protection scenario in the projection year, indicating that the intervention of ecological protection measures strongly supports regional carbon sequestration and sink enhancement [50]. This scenario emphasises environmental protection and ecological restoration, focusing on strengthening the protection, restoration and sustainable use of ecological areas, promoting more ecological and civilised land use planning [51], and the expansion of forest and grassland areas, which can limit urban sprawl, contribute to the growth of regional carbon stock and significantly improve ecosystem functioning [52, 53]. However, the proportion of anomalous conversion of other land classes to water in Beidaihe District was significantly higher than in similar regions (e.g. agricultural areas in the Yangtze River Delta), which may be related to secondary salinisation caused by rising water tables, an atypical conversion pattern that is often underestimated in established land use models, highlighting the impact of regional geographic specificity on carbon stock assessment. In addition, all three regions showed a positive carbon stock response under the ecological protection scenario (1.205% in Beidaihe and 0.413% in Huanghua), with Caofeidian showing a particularly significant increase (24.334%) due to the expansion of its grassland area, suggesting that policy design needs to pay attention to ecological protection.

Driving mechanisms for carbon stock

In order to systematically study the driving mechanism of land use change and the differential response of carbon stock to the variables, 13 key driving factors were selected in natural, social and transportation aspects using the OPGD model to analyse their contribution and interactive effects on carbon stock. It has been demonstrated that, in the year 2020, among the carbon stocks in Beidaihe District, Caofeidian District, and Huanghua City that were subjected to changes in a single dominant factor, the explanatory power of carbon stocks among the natural geographic factors (e.g., X2 Slope, X4 Average Annual Precipitation) was generally low (q < 0.05), and their direct driving force for carbon stock changes was weak [54, 55]). Among the socio-economic factors, Population Density (X7) is the main factor influencing the change of population density, and the demand for non-agricultural land in the process of rapid urbanisation through the expansion of infrastructure to increase the carrying capacity of the region’s population. This has led to a certain degree of construction land occupying arable land and ecological land [56], leading to the fragmentation of ecological land, resulting in the change of carbon stock [57]. The transportation factor in Caofeidian is found to be more significantly driven than in the other two regions, especially the Distance to Highway (X10), which may be due to its active response to the "Northwest Hinterland Strategy", "Silk Road on Ice" and "One Belt, One Road". This phenomenon may be attributed to its proactive response to the "Northwest Hinterland Strategy", "Silk Road on Ice" and "One Belt, One Road" initiatives, which have resulted in the establishment of a special "big outbound and big inbound" logistic network facilitated by the intersecting transportation infrastructure. This has led to a certain disruption in the carbon cycle process.The implementation of national strategies, such as the "dual carbon" goal and the ecological protection redline system, may further regulate the synergy between transportation and land use. For example, Caofeidian’s Belt and Road logistics network, if combined with regional carbon reduction plans, could mitigate disruptions to the carbon cycle.

In the interaction detection analysis, the factors affecting carbon stock changes exhibited nonlinear enhancement and two-factor enhancement, and the q-value explanatory power of the interaction was greater than that of individual factors [58], and its nonlinear superposition effect was regionally significant. The interaction effects of Population Density (X7) ∩ GDP Density (X8) in Beidaihe District, Population Density (X7) ∩ NPP-VIIRS Nighttime Lighting Data (X9) in Caofeidian District, and Population Density (X7) ∩ Average Annual Temperature (X5) in Huanghua City are particularly prominent. It has been demonstrated that the explanatory power of the spatial distribution of carbon stocks will be significantly enhanced under a specific combination of conditions [59], thereby further elucidating the significant influence of the multi-factor coupling mechanism on regional carbon stock distribution. This further elucidates the complex influence of the multi-factor coupling mechanism on the formation of regional carbon stock patterns [60]. It is important to acknowledge the significant impact of population density, in conjunction with other explanatory variables, on the alteration of carbon stock. This is due to the energy consumption and industrial production resulting from population growth, which will lead to substantial carbon emissions [61]. Urban construction and road expansion are factors that are known to lead to the loss of carbon stock [62]. This result aligns with the findings of Kafy et al. [63] in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, which reported that urban expansion resulted in a 21% reduction in vegetation cover, leading to a significant decrease in carbon storage. Furthermore, Kafy et al. [64] demonstrated in Cox’s Bazar that urbanization not only reduces carbon storage but is also associated with a prominent increase in land surface temperature (LST), further corroborating the dual negative impact of urbanization on the carbon cycle.The current land spatial planning and cultivated land protection policies do not have sufficient rigid constraints on construction land, which leads to the interaction between population density (X7) and GDP density (X8) and aggravates the loss of carbon stocks. In the future, it is necessary to direct human activities to ecological restoration through ecological compensation mechanisms or carbon sink trading policies.The bidirectional driving mechanism of human activities suggests that, in the future, there is a need for human activities to be actively adjusted so that they can play the role of ecological land carbon sinks [65]. This is in order to achieve sustainable development with low carbon emission reduction.

SEM was introduced for coupling analysis on the basis of OPGD, with the aim of analysing the complex mechanism of action between CS and multidimensional driving factors in the Beidaihe-Caofeidian-Huanghua region, as well as the interaction relationship among factors. In the Beidaihe district, population density has been shown to have a significant negative correlation with CS, and Average Annual Temperature has been found to have no significant effect on CS. However, the positive correlation between Average Annual Temperature and Average Annual Precipitation is the most significant among the indirect factors. This area is subject to the typical sea effect of the Bohai Sea, and there is a positive correlation between rainfall and temperature [66]. The population density of the Caofeidian district has been found to have the highest negative correlation coefficient with CS, a finding that is consistent with the results of the OPGD study mentioned above. It is evident that the alterations in land use and cover type, including polder farming, farmland reclamation, deforestation and urban development, and reclamation in Caofeidian wetland, have diminished the carbon sequestration capacity of wetland units over the past 30 years. Furthermore, the destruction of wetlands has augmented the temperature effect on carbon stock [67], thereby underscoring the model’s superior prediction capability for X5 Average Annual Temperature.The temperature effect of the degradation of Caofeidian wetland is partly due to the insufficient implementation of wetland protection policies. If wetlands are included in the pilot project of blue carbon ecosystem management, the negative impact of development may be offset through policy coercion. Meanwhile, the factor interactions of structural equations indicate that changes in carbon stocks are the result of multiple factors [68]. Furthermore, the interactions of Population Density and NPP-VIIRS Nighttime Lighting Data together demonstrate that the changes in carbon stocks in Huanghua district are significantly influenced by human activities, which is consistent with the conclusion that Population Density and Soil Type are the primary driving factors for spatial differentiation of carbon stocks, as highlighted by Fan L [69] in a study of a similar region. However, the influence of soil type factors on the change of carbon stock in this study area is not obvious, which is different from the conclusion that Soil Type is a significant influencing factor in similar areas [70, 71], it is speculated that this might stem from the fact that the single physicochemical properties of the soil in coastal areas have a limited impact on carbon stock.

Limitations and policy recommendations

Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations. First, although the land use data selected for the years 2000–2020 can effectively characterise surface cover at a given point in time, failing to incorporate more recent observational data may result in a lag between the study’s findings and the current state of regional development. Additionally, due to limitations in terms of data acquisition costs, time investment and technical conditions, this study did not prepare customised land use cover maps, instead relying on general data sets for analysis. This approach restricts the precise capture of land use details specific to the study area to a certain extent, which could affect the accuracy of the model output at small spatial scales. Secondly, although the carbon density parameters were corrected based on previous studies, there is still uncertainty in the estimation results. The InVEST model assumes static carbon pools, ignoring vegetation succession, soil respiration, and the turnover differences of different carbon pools. This could lead to bias in carbon stock estimation. (3) In the multivariate driver analysis of carbon stock changes, only 13 drivers were considered due to the complexity and variability of the factors affecting carbon stocks, as well as the limitations of the data. The interference of the natural environment and socio-economics on carbon stocks was not considered comprehensively, making it difficult to capture the time lag effect of the drivers.e.g. policy and climate) and the impact of CS on carbon stocks. CS time lag effect. Furthermore, due to the low correlation of the data after constructing the SEM model, the AVE and FL values were not high during testing, which limited the model’s explanatory power and credibility. To improve the credibility of the SEM model, we need to establish a better system of drivers in future. We should also use Bootstrapping or Monte Carlo simulations to assess the SEM.

It is recommended that the government implement the strategy of ‘zonal control and process optimisation’ to address the significant spatial differences of carbon stocks in the three regions. In light of the substantial decline in carbon stock precipitated by the augmentation of industrial land utilisation in Beidaihe District, the government is obligated to enhance land use supervision, meticulously regulate the proliferation of non-essential industrial land use, propel industrial transformation and upgrading, and curtail carbon emissions. In the context of Caofeidian District, it is imperative to perpetuate the reinforcement of ecological protection and restoration, while concurrently consolidating and expanding the outcomes of ecological restoration. Moreover, Huanghua Municipality is duty-bound to fortify top-level design, formulate scientific and judicious management planning and measures, and prioritise the efficacy of management and carbon stock. For Huanghua City, it is imperative to enhance the top-level design, formulate scientific and rational governance planning measures, prioritise the balance between governance efficacy and carbon stock, and mitigate substantial fluctuations in carbon stock. Simulation results from the PLUS model should be utilised by the government to facilitate the timely and scientific adjustment of land use patterns, with the objective of optimising the structure of land use. The government should also employ the joint SEM-OPDD analysis method to provide a scientific and systematic theoretical basis and methodological support for low-carbon territorial spatial planning in the Bohai Economic Circle. This will promote the green, low-carbon and sustainable development of coastal cities.

To address the significant spatial differences in carbon stocks, we propose a spatially targeted governance strategy of ‘Zonal Management and Precise Regulation’ derived directly from our simulation and mechanistic analysis. Based on the spatial patterns of carbon stock changes under different scenarios (Fig. 5) and the dominant drivers identified, we delineate three types of priority management zones for each region.In Beidaihe District, an Urban Expansion Control Zone should be established, primarily encompassing the southeastern coastal townships of Daihe Town, Haibin Town, and Xishan Street. Our data confirm that this area represents the core of carbon stock loss under the Economic Development (ED) scenario, driven primarily by construction land expansion. Policies here should prioritize strictly curbing disorderly urban sprawl and enhancing green infrastructure development.This recommendation is supported by relevant research. For instance, an analysis of driving mechanisms by Rahaman et al. [72] indicates that the interaction between population density and GDP density significantly exacerbates the loss of carbon stocks, which they attribute primarily to the lack of stringent constraints on construction land in current land spatial planning.In Caofeidian District, the core Ecological Restoration Priority Zone should focus on the northwestern coastal townships of Shilihai Town, Shuangjing Town, and Liuzan Town. Our analysis indicates that this contiguous area demonstrates the highest carbon stock increase under the Ecological Protection (EP) scenario, consistent with the historical carbon gain observed from 2010–2020. Projects such as wetland restoration and afforestation should be prioritized to consolidate and enhance this critical carbon sink.In Huanghua City, an Agricultural Intensification and Saline-Alkali Land Management Zone should target the eastern saline-alkali areas, including Nandagang Town, Changguo Town, and Lvqiao Town. Simulations under both the Cultivated Land Protection (CP) and Economic Development (ED) scenarios quantitatively confirm that these townships, characterized by extensive saline-alkali lands, are highly vulnerable to carbon loss due to unsustainable land-use changes. However, through improved agricultural practices and ecological transformation, they also possess significant potential for stabilizing carbon stocks.

These precise zoning recommendations are grounded in the outputs of PLUS model simulations and the synergistic pathways between human activities and natural factors revealed by SEM analysis. This evidence-based, spatially explicit approach provides insights for policymakers implementing differentiated low-carbon spatial planning in the Bohai Rim Economic Circle.

Conclusions

The spatiotemporal and spatial differentiation of carbon storage in the study area is significant, showing the characteristics of stages. From 2000 to 2010, the expansion of construction land and the encroachment of cultivated land in the three places generally decreased carbon storage. From 2010 to 2020, the carbon storage in Caofeidian District increased significantly (1.50 × 10⁶ tons) through ecological restoration (especially coastal wetlands), and the areas with significant increase in carbon storage were mainly distributed in coastal areas and wetlands, and the decreasing areas were concentrated in rapidly urbanizing areas.Multi-scenario simulations predict that carbon stocks in the three places will show an overall growth trend by 2030. The total carbon storage under the cultivated land protection (CP) scenario was the highest (about 3.00 × 10⁷ tons), and the ecological protection (EP) scenario had a particularly significant effect on the improvement of carbon storage in Caofeidian District (an increase of 2.40 × 10⁶ tons), highlighting the positive effect of ecological priority policy. However, under the natural development (ND) and economic development (ED) scenarios, the cultivated land or total carbon storage in some regions may be reduced.The analysis of the driving mechanism shows that population density is the core factor affecting the spatial differentiation of carbon stocks. It has a significant two-factor interaction enhancement effect with GDP density, nighttime light data (NPP-VIIRS), annual average temperature and other factors (q-value is higher). The results of structural equation modeling (SEM) further confirmed that the level of human activities (latent variables) has a significant direct impact on the change of carbon stocks, and the interaction between the latent variables is complex.Carbon stock differences in economic scenarios require evaluation due to model simplifications. Population density is a core driver, interacting with GDP, lighting, temperature, etc.; SEM confirms human activity directly impacts stocks amid complex interactions. Yet policies focus on single objectives, ignoring nonlinear synergies and underestimating carbon loss risk in high-development areas. Policies must integrate factors like population distribution, land use, and industrial transformation to address complexity.

In summary, this study reveals the significant spatiotemporal heterogeneity and dynamic variation of carbon storage in typical coastal areas of Bohai Bay. It is found that land use conversion in the process of urbanization is a key physical process of carbon storage change, and the intensity of human activities (especially population density) and its nonlinear interaction with natural and socioeconomic factors are the core mechanisms driving the spatial differentiation of carbon stock. Ecological restoration of coastal wetlands has been proven to be an effective way to offset the carbon loss of urbanization and improve regional carbon storage. Multi-scenario simulation provides quantitative support for balancing development and protection, and emphasizes the importance of differentiated spatial planning. This study provides an important empirical basis and scientific reference for in-depth understanding of the carbon cycle process of rapidly urbanizing coastal zones and the formulation of precise "dual carbon" management strategies.

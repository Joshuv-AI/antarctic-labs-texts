Case of an Unusual Fleshy Swelling
Authors: Manisha Nijhawan, Shivi Nijhawan, Ankita Narola, Ishita Choudhary
Published: 2025

A 28-year-old male labourer, resident of West Bengal, came to Jaipur 5 years back for work. He presented with complain of painless swellings on his right shoulder and chest. He developed a single papule initially over the right shoulder which progressed to increase in size around 3 * 4 cm. It was well-defined fleshy nodular swelling, smooth and nontender on palpation [Figure 1a]. He used to frequently visit to his home town and go for fishing, but there were no complaints of any trauma. Neither any similar past complains nor family history was present. In addition, 2-3 hyperpigmented asymptomatic nodular swellings were present over presternal area [Figure 1b] which appeared few months later. General and systemic examination revealed no abnormalities except for mild neutrophilia. H and E staining showed mild perivascular chronic inflammation in upper dermis. Deep dermis showed numerous uniform budding yeast-like cells in chains, and the surrounding dermis showed epithelioid and giant cell granulomatous inflammatory reaction. PAS staining showed ‘hour-glass appearance’ of budding yeast cells [Figure 2a-d].

(a) Fleshy nodule over right shoulder of size 4cm diameter. (b) 2-3 hyperpigmented nodules over pre-sternal area

(a) (On H and E stain, 400x) ‘Doubly refractile’, birefringent thick-walled hyaline round to oval ‘lemon shaped,’ 5-7µm in diameter yeast cells, with slightly basophilic homogeneous cytoplasm in the subcutis. (b) Yeast cells within multinucleate giant cells and grouped yeast cells. (c) GMS stain shows multiple chained yeast cells and daughters. (d) On GMS staining (bar 20 µm) yeast with daughter cell, an ‘hour-glass appearance’ can be observed

Question

What is your diagnosis?

Answer

Lobomycosis.

Discussion

First reported in 1931, lobomycosis is also known as Jorge Lobo Blastomycosis, keloidal blastomycosis, pseudo lepromatous blastomycosis, Caiabi leprosy and Lacaziosis. Lobomycosis is a rare chronic subcutaneous mycosis due to its prolonged incubation period.[12] The natural reservoir of L. loboi is believed to be aquatic or soil, as it is a zoonotic disease and an occupational hazard as the cases are reported only in dolphins and human hosts. Though our patient was a labourer, he frequently visited to his hometown and exposures to waterbodies. Clinically, lobomycosis can present as papules, plaques, nodules and ulcers, but slowly developing keloid like, ulcerated or verrucous nodular plaques.[3] Our patient presented with such characteristic ‘keloid-like’ subcutaneous nodules with a few satellite lesions. Due to the rarity of this disease, the diagnosis of lobomycosis can be mistaken for other conditions like keloids, cutaneous-leishmaniasis, Scrofuloderma and nontubercular mycobacterial infections such as leprosy and sporotrichosis.[1] Due to its uncultivable nature, the diagnosis is confirmed with histopathology. Tissue sections are stained with haematoxylin and eosin, and special staining techniques such as Gomori’s methenamine silver nitrate stain and Periodic acid Schiff stain can be used which demonstrate yeasts.[2] In H and E-stained tissue sections, the yeasts are seen as ‘doubly refractile’, birefringent thick-walled hyaline round to oval ‘lemon shaped’ cells, 3–7 μm in diameter, in a string of pearls like arrangement. Thick-walled hyaline spherical yeasts can also be demonstrated in exudates from the ulcerated lesions using potassium hydroxide. Till date, very limited treatment options are available with variable outcomes. Mainstay of treatment is surgical excision or cryosurgery for recurrent cases. Drugs like clofazimine and itraconazole were considered in therapeutic studies.[4] The role of other antifungals like Ketoconazole and amphotericin B is not very promising. No standard treatment is fully satisfactory; it is recommended to combine different modalities.[5] Our patient denied any surgical intervention; therefore, he was prescribed a combination treatment of itraconazole and clofazimine for 6–8 months. Our case responded in 6 weeks after which he was lost to follow-up [Figure 3a and b].

(a) Swelling on right shoulder decreased in size after the treatment, on follow up after 6 weeks. (b) Hyperpigmented nodules neither progressed nor regressed

Learning points

Immediate effect of Suryanamaskar on cardiorespiratory functions and cognitive performance: A quasi-experimental study on young adult females
Authors: Anjali Singh, Sanjoy Majhi, Arnab Das
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSuryanamaskar (SN) is popular amongst recent yoga practitioners for its health benefits and fitness promises. Most previous studies have focused on the long-term pre-post effects of SN intervention on health and fitness. However, a notable gap exists regarding the immediate response of practicing SN on cardiorespiratory and neuro-cognitive functions, especially in the female population.ObjectivesThis study aimed to find the immediate effect of SN practice on cardiorespiratory functions and neuro-cognitive performance in healthy adult females.Material and methodsA total of 21 healthy females (aged: 18–25 years) were selected in this Quasi pre-post experimental study, guided by the CONSORT tool. Cardiorespiratory, heart rate (HR) variability, and cognitive parameters were assessed before and immediately after 45 minutes of SN session using a metabolic gas analyzer, HR sensor, and psychometric tests, respectively. Paired t-test was performed to find the difference between the pre- and post-SN responses.ResultsA significant increase in average volume of oxygen consumption (VO2) (11.4 %; p < 0.01), minute ventilation (VE) (15.9 %; p < 0.001), respiratory frequency (Rf) (6.2 %; p < 0.01), respiratory quotient (RQ) (4.6 %; p < 0.01), and METS (12.4 %; p < 0.001) were observed immediately after SN session. No significant changes in blood pressure was observed. LF power (23.7 %; p < 0.01) and LF/HF ratio (79.9 %; p < 0.05) showed a significant increase along with a decrease in pNN50 % (16.9 %; p < 0.05) and HF power (16.4 %; p < 0.01) at post-SN. A significant improvement (p < 0.01) in Stroop congruent (14.78 %) and incongruent (13.7 %) reaction times was also observed immediately after the SN session.ConclusionThese findings collectively indicate that a single session of SN effectively enhanced both cardiorespiratory and cognitive functions, which can be used to improve cardiometabolic health and to train the autonomic nervous system in females.Keywords: Sun salutation, Heart rate variability, Oxygen consumption, Blood pressure, Energy expenditure, Reaction time

Introduction

Suryanamaskar (SN) or Sun Salutation is a dynamic sequence of twelve postures and is very popular among modern yogic routines. Although it is not mentioned in classical hatha yogic texts, it has evolved into a significant part of contemporary yoga practices due to its potential health benefits [1]. The ritualistic origins of SN can be traced back to the Taittiriya Aranyaka under the Krishna Yajur Veda, which extols the Sun as a divine entity [1]. The practice incorporates twelve specific postures performed in a synchronized sequence that encompasses both backward and forward bending with controlled breathing patterns, offering a blend of physical exercise and spiritual engagement [2].

The modern form of dynamic SN was systematized by King Balasaheb Pant Pratinidhi in the 1920s and further developed by Sri K.V. Iyer and Sri Krishnamacharya [1]. SN is known for its comprehensive physical benefits, including improved muscular strength, flexibility, and joint mobility. The dynamic postures stimulate circulation, aid in the detoxification process, and enhance respiratory function by expanding lung capacity, improving oxygen exchange [[3], [4], [5]] and causing efficient metabolic processes [6,7]. Studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of dynamic SN in reducing body fat, increasing muscle endurance, and improving overall cardiovascular health and fitness [1,[8], [9], [10]]. The practice has been identified as an effective form of aerobic exercise, effective to manage weight [9]. The energy expenditure associated with SN varies with pace and intensity, with faster paces being comparable to aerobic exercises and slower paces aligning more with traditional yoga practices [7,8]. Regular SN practice positively influences heart rate variability (HRV), a marker of autonomic nervous system balance and cardiovascular health. Studies have highlighted that regular practice improves HRV by enhancing parasympathetic activity and reducing sympathetic stress responses [7,11,12]. Yoga, including SN, enhances cognitive performance such as improved attention, memory, executive function, self-control, and psychological well-being [[13], [14], [15]]. This indicates that SN not only improves physical health but also supports stress management and emotional well-being.

The acute and immediate physiological and cognitive responses to a single session of SN remain scanty. Previous studies were conducted to find the acute effects of multiple rounds and various intensities of SN on cardiorespiratory and metabolic parameters throughout the practice [6,7,16]. Whereas Bhavanai et al. (2013) explored the immediate effect of a single session of SN on reaction time and heart rate in females [17]. Karpagam et al. (2013) also explored the immediate effect of SN with varying intensities on heart rate and blood pressure and reported slow SN is non-taxing to the heart and can be prescribed to hypertensive patients and for rehabilitation of cardiac patients [18]. While the benefits of yoga and its various forms, including SN, have been extensively studied, there remains a significant gap in understanding the immediate responses of this practice on heart rate variability, cardiorespiratory functions, and cognitive performance.

Additionally, the demographic focus of existing studies on yoga and its effects often skews towards males, older adults, or individuals with specific health conditions, such as cardiovascular diseases or mental health disorders. Healthy young females, who may experience different physiological and cognitive responses due to their distinct hormonal and metabolic profiles, are relatively underrepresented in this research domain. This gap is significant, as understanding the immediate physiological and cognitive responses to SN in this population can provide insights into its potential use as a preparatory or acute intervention strategy in settings that require short-term enhancements in physical readiness and cognitive performance.

We hypothesize that SN, which combines elements of both physical exercise and mindfulness, may elicit unique and beneficial responses in cognitive performance and cardiorespiratory functioning. These are not well-documented in current scientific literature. Therefore, this study aims to find the immediate effect of SN practice on cardio-respiratory functions and neuro-cognitive performance. Individuals in high-stress occupations or those with conditions characterized by autonomic dysfunction, educational and occupational workers where cognitive efficiency is critical, may get acute benefit by doing SN.

Methods

Participants

Thirty eight physically fit female collegiate students (n = 38; age range = 18–25 years) experienced in doing SN with no known diseases verbally agreed to participate were included in this study through a random sampling method (Fig. 1). Participants were directly contacted through advertisements at relevant places during June and July 2024. Individuals with pre-existing medical conditions, having cardiovascular, neurological, musculoskeletal, and metabolic disorders, taking contraceptives, having PCOS, irregular menstrual cycle, injury and pregnant women were excluded. The participants underwent routine health checkups at the Exercise Physiology and Clinical Evaluation Laboratory at the Department of Sports Science and Yoga, RKMVERI before participating in this study. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criterion, 21 eumenorrheic females (n = 21; height = 154.03 ± 1.81 cm; weight = 56.28 ± 2.50 kg) were finally recruited in this study (Table 1). We determined sample size using most recent published research studies on SN with a comparable study population [6,16,17] which was also verified with G-power software. Before beginning the study, participants provided written informed consent for their voluntary participation. This study was carried out according to the Declaration of Helsinki's ethical principles. The Institutional Ethics Committee approved this study (Certificate no: RKMVERI/IEC/DSSY/2023-06/001) and the study was prospectively registered in Clinical Trial Registry of India (https://ctri.nic.in/) with the CTRI registration number CTRI/2024/03/064806.

Study design

A single-group quasi-experimental pretest-posttest study was designed to explore immediate effects of SN practice on cardio-respiratory and neuro-cognitive functions. Before arriving at the lab, participants completed questionnaires related to their menstrual cycle in a Google form. The participants were asked to have a light breakfast on the day of data collection. These sessions were performed at a specific time of the day (i.e. between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m.) to avoid circadian rhythm-induced variations. After collecting the demographic data and ensuring adequate rest, cognitive tests were conducted. Hemodynamic parameters were measured immediately before and after the SN practice, while cardio-respiratory and HRV data were continuously recorded for 5 min at Savasana (the supine position) both before and after the SN session (Fig. 2). The participants did a single session of SN for 45 min under the supervision of a certified trained yoga instructor. This session consisted of 4 repetitions of 3 sets of SN having 12 asanas performed sequentially. The Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines were followed for reporting and preparing the manuscript.

Assessment of hemodynamic parameters

Systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of the participants were measured twice, before and after the SN practice, using a digital sphygmomanometer (Omron HEM, Japan). Pulse pressure (PP) and mean arterial pressure (MAP) were calculated from the SBP and DBP using standardized equations.

Assessment of cardio-respiratory and metabolic parameters

The acute cardio-respiratory responses before and after the SN practice were measured for 5 min at Savasana (the supine position) using a breath-by-breath metabolic gas analyzer Cosmed Quark Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing-CPET device, Italy (Ref. no: C09073-02-99). Oxygen consumption (VO2), volume of carbon dioxide production (VCO2), minute ventilation (VE), respiratory frequency (Rf), oxygen pulse, respiratory quotient (RQ), energy expenditure, and metabolic equivalent (METS) were measured. Heart rate (HR) was measured using a Polar H10 HR monitor (Kempele, Finland).

Assessment of HRV

Heart rate variability (HRV) is a measure of the autonomic nervous system's influence on the heart and reflects the heart's ability to adapt to different situations. R–R interval and heart rate data were recorded for 5 min at Savasana before and after the SN practice using a Polar H10 HR monitor and collected using the Elite HRV mobile application (Version 5.5.1). RR intervals were exported from the Elite HRV app as txt.files and imported into Kubios HRV software (version 3.5.0) for analyzing time domain and frequency domain parameters [19]. Time domain parameters included mean RR interval, standard deviation of NN intervals (SDNN), root mean square of successive differences (RMSSD), and percentage of successive NN intervals that differ by more than 50 ms (pNN50). Frequency domain parameters were analyzed by the fast fourier transform (FFT) and include low-frequency power (LF: 0.04–0.15 Hz), high-frequency power (HF: 0.15–0.4 Hz), and the LF/HF ratio.

Assessment of neuro-cognitive function

Various neuro-cognitive abilities of the participants were assessed using Stroop test, digit span test, and Deary-Liewald Task (DLT) from Psytoolkit website (https://www.psytoolkit.org/). The Stroop test is a psychological tool to assess attention, cognitive flexibility, and inhibitory control [20]. The DLT test was conducted to access choice-based reaction time (RT) involving working memory [21]. Digit span test was conducted to assess visuospatial working memory [22]. All these tests were carried out before and after the SN session, and the data were recorded and analyzed.

Statistical analysis

The normal distribution of the data was assessed by the Shapiro–Wilk test and histogram plot. The mean and standard error of mean (SEM) of the data were determined as descriptive statistics. Paired t-test was performed to find the difference between the pre and post-SN responses. Percentage change and 95 % confidence interval (CI) was also calculated between the pre and post-responses. Cohen’s d effect was calculated to identify the small (d = 0.2), medium (d = 0.5), and large (d ≥ 0.8) effects. All statistical analyses were done using the Jamovi (version 2.5.6) and Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS version 23) software. The data was represented in mean ± SEM and p < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Changes in HRV

The immediate effects of a single session of SN on HRV in healthy young females showed a significant increase in LF power (p < 0.01; 23.7 %) and LF/HF ratio (p < 0.05; 75.7 %), along with a significant decrease in pNN50 % (p < 0.05; −16.9 %), HF power (p < 0.01; 16.4 %) was observed in post-SN compared to pre-SN (Table 3). This suggests an increase in sympathetic activity and a decrease in parasympathetic activity after SN. Trivial changes were observed in mean RR, SDNN, and RMSSD. These results suggest that SN leads to an acute increase in sympathetic activity and a reduction in parasympathetic activity, as evidenced by the changes in LF, HF, and LF/HF ratio.

Discussion

We have investigated the immediate effects of a single session of SN on hemodynamic, cardiorespiratory, and cognitive functions in healthy young females through this quasi-experimental study. We hypothesized that one 45-min intermittent SN session would affect the neurophysiological system and cognition. Despite the absence of significant changes in hemodynamic parameters, we found that a single SN session significantly increased cardiorespiratory and metabolic parameters, increased sympathetic activity, as indicated by alterations in HRV metrics, and enhanced cognitive flexibility and attentional control, as evidenced by reduced response times in the Stroop test. We believe that knowing the immediate responses of SN is essential to improve cardiovascular health, metabolic functions, and train autonomic nervous system to improve cognitive performance in females.

Single session of SN does not affect hemodynamic parameters

This study examined the immediate effects of a single session of SN on SBP, DBP, MAP, and PP in healthy young females. We found no significant differences in these parameters before and after the SN session and indicates that a single session of SN does not acutely impact hemodynamic parameters in young females. However, Karpagam et al. (2013) also studied the immediate effect of SN on school children and found that fast SN produced a significant increase in SBP and PP after fast SN, a significant decrease in DBP after 5 rounds of slow SN [18]. Contrastingly, Joshi et al. (2022) found that regular SN practice over four weeks significantly reduced stress levels, resting heart rate (RHR), SBP, and DBP [14]. Their study involved participants performing the complete cycle of SN, consisting of all 12 asanas, highlighting the benefits of sustained practice. These findings suggest that immediate hemodynamic effects of a single SN session are contradictory and can vary depending on the pace and duration of practice, whereas prolonged practice can lead to improvements in cardiovascular health and reduce stress.

Single session of SN enhanced oxygen consumption

The current study investigated the immediate effect of a single session of SN on various cardiorespiratory and metabolic parameters. Our findings demonstrate significant enhancements in these parameters, suggesting that SN can effectively boost metabolic activity and respiratory efficiency. Our study revealed a significant increase in average VO2, VCO2, VE, Rf, and RQ after SN session compared to pre-SN. This increase indicates enhanced oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production, reflecting heightened metabolic activity. Higher VE and Rf also indicates a higher volume of air moving in and out of the lungs to fulfill the increased oxygen demand, facilitate greater oxygen delivery to tissues, and more effective expulsion of carbon dioxide after SN. Previous studies found similar results, indicating that yoga practice, including SN, can improve aerobic capacity and metabolic function [4,10,23,24]. Regular practice of SN significantly improved VO2max, a key indicator of aerobic fitness, supporting our observation of increased oxygen uptake post-SN session [3,4]. Additionally, Mody (2011) demonstrated that yoga practices lead to increased VCO2, further corroborating our findings of enhanced carbon dioxide production post-SN [6].

The significant increase in O2 pulse (the amount of oxygen consumed per heartbeat) post-SN suggests improved efficiency of oxygen delivery per heartbeat. This parameter is often used as an indirect measure of stroke volume, indicating that SN might enhance cardiac efficiency and peripheral oxygen extraction. Previous studies reported improvements in cardiovascular efficiency and stroke volume among participants engaging in regular yoga practices [8,25]. Interestingly, no significant difference was observed in HR between pre- and post-SN sessions. This stability in HR, despite increased metabolic and respiratory activity, implies that SN enhances respiratory and metabolic functions without placing additional stress on the cardiovascular system of females. Therefore, this enhancement in O2 pulse is particularly noteworthy as it suggests that SN can optimize cardiovascular function without imposing additional strain on the heart.

Comparing our findings with previous studies, it is evident that SN and other yoga practices consistently show improvements in metabolic and respiratory parameters and can serve as an effective aerobic exercise. However, the immediate effects of a single SN session, as observed in our study, provide new insights into the acute benefits of this practice. While long-term yoga practice has been well-documented for its health benefits, our study highlights that even a single session can lead to significant physiological enhancements. Metabolic and reproductive disorders, obesity are quite prevalent among females. This underscores the potential of SN as a practical intervention to improve cardiovascular health and overall metabolic function in females.

Single session of SN affects HRV

The current study investigated the immediate effects of a single session of SN on HRV in healthy young females. The results demonstrated a significant increase in LF power and the LF/HF ratio, alongside a significant decrease in HF power post-SN session compared to pre-SN. These findings suggest an acute increase in sympathetic activity and a decrease in parasympathetic activity following SN. However, the overall HRV, indicated by time-domain measures such as mean RR, SDNN, and RMSSD, did not change significantly, highlighting that the short-term autonomic shifts did not substantially alter the overall HRV. Previous studies reported that regular practice of yoga including SN can improve HRV, reduces LF power and increases HF power indicating enhanced parasympathetic activity and reduced sympathetic activity over the long term and a shift of sympathovagal balance toward parasympathetic dominance [11,12]. In contrast, our study's acute increase in sympathetic activity suggests that the immediate response to SN might differ from its long-term effects. Additionally, previous researcher found that a single session of SN reduces stress levels, anxiety, and enhances relaxation [26]. The practice’s combination of physical movement, controlled breathing, and meditative elements can promote relaxation and mitigate stress impacts on the heart. Therefore, while a single SN session may temporarily elevate sympathetic activity, regular practice appears to foster a beneficial autonomic balance, reducing stress and enhancing parasympathetic function over time. This autonomic modulation implies that SN could be useful for training the autonomic nervous system, possibly aiding in stress resilience and improving the body's ability to handle physiological stressors. Such benefits can be crucial for individuals in high-stress occupations or those with conditions characterized by autonomic dysfunction.

Improvement in cognitive ability

The significant reduction in response times for both congruent and incongruent conditions of the Stroop test post-SN indicate improved cognitive flexibility and attentional control. Our results suggest that SN effectively enhances these cognitive domains in females. These findings align with the idea that yoga practices, which combines physical postures with breath control and mindfulness, can lead to immediate enhancements in executive function, possibly due to increased cerebral blood flow and the effects of controlled breathing. The decrease in Stroop test response times is consistent with previous findings that showed acute bouts of hatha yoga and mindfulness practices including SN or SN alone can lead to immediate and sustained improvements in cognition and executive functions [15,[26], [27], [28]]. Additionally, the marked improvement in cognitive performance, as evidenced by faster congruent and incongruent reaction times in the Stroop test, suggests that SN practice can enhance cognitive processing speed and executive function. As the immediate impacts are less documented, and our findings contribute to this gap. This finding is particularly relevant for educational and occupational settings where cognitive efficiency is critical.

Digit span score representing visuospatial short-term working memory suggests that a single session of SN does not have a substantial immediate impact on working memory capacity in females. Given that working memory is a higher-order cognitive function, sustained practice of SN might have a significant role. However, previous studies have reported an enhanced cognitive performance post-exercise [29]. This discrepancy could be due to the unique nature of SN as compared to other physical activities and exercises, which combines physical exertion with mindfulness practices, potentially leading to different cognitive outcomes compared to traditional aerobic exercises. By integrating SN practice into daily routines, individuals may experience heightened mental clarity and improved cognitive performance, which can contribute to better academic or professional outcomes. Female athletes can also use SN as an effective warm-up routine to enhance cardiorespiratory readiness and cognitive focus before engaging in more intensive sports activities.

Unlike other studies, our study also has some limitations. Firstly, the small sample size limits the generalizability of our findings. Although immediate effects are less likely to be influenced by external factors, however the inclusion of a control group in future studies would strengthen causal inference. Additionally, the cognitive performance measures were limited to specific tests. Incorporating a broader range of cognitive assessments could offer a more detailed understanding of how SN influences different cognitive domains. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that investigated the immediate effect of multi-round SN on cardiorespiratory and neurocognitive functions. Additionally, our sample was homogeneous, consisting only of young females, ensuring better control over age-related physiological variations and enhances the reliability of the findings. However, further research needs to be carried out on a larger and diverse populations, including individuals with different fitness level, health conditions, and gender. Additionally, incorporating electroencephalography and neuroimaging assessments may help elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving these physiological and cognitive adaptations.

Conclusion

The present study addressed a notable gap regarding the immediate response of practicing SN on cardiorespiratory and neuro-cognitive functions, especially in the female population. Key findings indicate a single SN session significantly enhances cognitive flexibility and attentional control, as evidenced by reduced response times in the Stroop test. Despite the absence of significant changes in hemodynamic parameters, our study demonstrates an acute increase in sympathetic activity, as indicated by alterations in HRV metrics. Notably, significant improvements in cardiorespiratory and metabolic functions were observed, including increased VO2 (11.4 %), VCO2 (16.6 %), RQ (4.6 %), VE (15.9 %), and Rf (6.2 %). These results suggest that SN can acutely enhance metabolic activity and respiratory efficiency without imposing additional stress on the cardiovascular system. While immediate effects are minimal in some domains, consistent and prolonged practice of SN may yield substantial benefits for cardiovascular health, stress reduction, and overall autonomic balance, as supported by previous research. Furthermore, the findings suggest that individuals unable to practice SN regularly can still derive its immediate benefits. Occupational workers, particularly those with prolonged static postures, may also utilize SN as a therapeutic intervention to alter metabolic activity and improve HRV.

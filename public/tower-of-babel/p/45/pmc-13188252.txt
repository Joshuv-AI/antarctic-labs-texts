Effectiveness of transarterial drug-loaded microsphere chemoembolization against cholangiocarcinoma combined with obstructive jaundice
Authors: Baoyang Jia, Yongli Chen, Rui Lin, Junhui Yang, Xinyue Meng, Xiaoming Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) has been widely used to treat malignant cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), the efficacy of drug-loaded microsphere chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) remains to be comprehensively explored in patients with CCA combined with obstructive jaundice.MethodsIn this single-center retrospective cohort study, patients (n = 101) with CCA combined with obstructive jaundice admitted between January 2021 and December 2022 were treated with DEB-TACE or c-TACE. Initially, the baseline characteristics of the two groups of patients, i.e., DEB-TACE (n = 46) and c-TACE (n = 55), were estimated. Further, the survival time was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier (KM) method, exploring independent risk factors affecting patients’ survival time by the Cox proportional risk model.ResultsThe DEB-TACE group showed a significantly better imaging efficacy than the c-TACE group, with an objective remission rate (ORR) of 47.8% vs. 0.0% and a disease control rate (DCR) of 95.7% vs. 70.9% (P < 0.001). The survival analysis showed a higher median overall survival (OS) time of 14 months and a 1-year survival rate of 70.7% in the DEB-TACE group than 11 months and 0.0% in the c-TACE group (P < 0.001), respectively. The Cox regression analysis showed the DEB-TACE treatment as an independent protective factor for prolonged OS (HR = 0.072, P < 0.001).ConclusionDespite the low long-term survival rates in both groups, DEB-TACE, as an effective local therapy, could provide an important reference for optimizing the treatment of advanced CCA.

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), originating from the epithelial cells of the bile ducts, has emerged as one of the dreadful malignant tumors. According to the anatomical location, CCA can be broadly classified into intrahepatic CCA, hilar CCA, and distal CCA. With devious early symptoms, this malignant disease commonly progresses rapidly and is often diagnosed at an advanced stage or with severe complications. The complex pathology significantly limits the treatment options for patients, resulting in a poor prognosis [1, 2]. Despite the availability of surgical resection as the main treatment, about 20% of patients meet the indications for surgery due to advanced tumor growth, anatomical location, and systemic conditions [3]. For most unsuitable patients, radiotherapy and palliative biliary drainage are the available therapeutic options. Nevertheless, these treatments often fail to achieve desirable results that improve survival and quality of life. Accordingly, several minimally invasive localized therapeutic techniques have been employed as alternatives for patients with inoperable hepatobiliary malignancies, such as transarterial chemoembolization (TACE).

Recently, systemic therapies for biliary tract cancers (BTC), including cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), have undergone substantial advances [4, 5]. While the combination of gemcitabine and cisplatin continues to serve as the standard first-line regimen, increasing attention has been directed toward targeted therapies and immunotherapy. Notably, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown encouraging efficacy, particularly in tumors exhibiting high microsatellite instability (MSI-H) [6, 7]. Moreover, molecularly targeted agents directed at alterations such as IDH1/2 mutations, FGFR2 fusions, and HER2 amplification have demonstrated clinical benefits, especially in intrahepatic CCA [8]. However, therapeutic resistance remains a major obstacle, ultimately restricting their long-term effectiveness.

The drug-loaded microsphere/drug-eluting beads-based TACE (DEB-TACE) is an innovative TACE-based technology that achieves highly effective localized antitumor effects through the slow release of chemotherapeutic agents using the drug-loaded microspheres while simultaneously reducing systemic toxicity [9]. Along this line, several reports demonstrated that DEB-TACE was significantly more effective than conventional TACE (c-TACE) in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), especially in terms of tumor reduction rate and survival benefit [10]. Nevertheless, there are fewer studies on DEB-TACE in patients with CCA, especially in patients with combined obstructive jaundice, lacking the exploration of the related efficacy. Motivated by these aspects, this study used a retrospective analysis based on patients with CCA combined with obstructive jaundice treated with DEB-TACE or c-TACE in our center. Moreover, this study explored the potential advantages of DEB-TACE by comparing the imaging efficacy, tumor control rate, and survival time of the two groups of subjects.

Experimental section

Study design

A single-center retrospective analysis was performed by evaluating the efficacy of DEB-TACE against CCA combined with obstructive jaundice. The data were obtained from the chart records of patients treated with DEB-TACE or c-TACE between January 2021 and December 2022 in our center. Patients in the DEB-TACE (n = 46) and c-TACE (n = 55) groups were considered through a rigorous screening (Fig. 1). This study was conducted following the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Hospital of Bei-Hua University. All data were kept strictly confidential and used for academic research.

Study population

The inclusion criteria were set as follows: (1) Patients who were diagnosed with CCA by imaging or pathological examinations and combined with obstructive jaundice; (2) Patients who were receiving at least one DEB-TACE or c-TACE treatment; (3) Patients whose age was ≥ 18 years old, irrespective of gender; (4) Patients who have complete medical records and follow-up data, such as imaging examinations, laboratory tests, and survival status records.

The exclusion criteria were set as follows: (1) Patients who were previously receiving other localized treatments (e.g., radiofrequency ablation and surgical resection); (2) Patients who were diagnosed with severe cardiac, pulmonary, or other systemic diseases that could not tolerate the treatment; (3) Patients who were possessing incomplete data or follow-up records. Notably, informed consent was obtained from all individual subjects included in the study.

Treatment

DEB-TACE group

The TACE procedure was performed using drug-loaded microspheres (DEB). The precise dose of the chosen chemotherapeutic agent, i.e., doxorubicin, was calculated according to the patient’s body surface area (dose 50–150 mg). After trans-femoral artery puncture, drug-carrying microspheres were injected into the blood-supplying area of the hepatic artery through a microcatheter to achieve tumor embolization.

c-TACE group

The c-TACE group received oil iodide combined with chemotherapeutic drugs (doxorubicin). The embolization range and dose were adjusted according to the tumor blood supply and patient tolerance.

Biliary drainage

Notably, all the recruited subjects received biliary drainage (PTCD or ERCP) before treatment to reduce bilirubin levels and improve liver function.

Data collection

The data were extracted through the electronic medical record system, including demographic characteristics (age, gender, weight, and BMI), tumor-related information (tumor location (intrahepatic, hilar, and distal), tumor size, number of intrahepatic metastases, presence of vascular invasion, and distant metastases), and treatment-related information (number and interval of treatments, dose of chemotherapeutic agents, embolized area (single or multiple areas), mode of biliary drainage, and postoperative bilirubin level).

In addition to the collected data, the follow-up data were collected, including the overall survival (OS) rate in months from the first treatment to the patient’s death or last follow-up. Moreover, the imaging results were classified as complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), stable disease (SD), and disease progression (PD) according to RECIST 1.1 criteria. The tumor markers included preoperative and postoperative CA19-9 and CEA levels. The liver function indicators, such as total bilirubin (TBil) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT), were determined. The adverse events, including fever, abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting, leukocytosis, sub-peritoneal fluid, and hepatic insufficiency, were recorded during the follow-up stage.

Statistical analysis

The data obtained were initially imported into the SPSS software for statistical analysis. Further, the normality test was performed for continuous variables, in which normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (S.D.). Contrarily, the non-normal distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile spacing. The categorical variables were expressed as frequency and percentage. The survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier (KM) method to compare the OS time between the DEB-TACE and c-TACE groups. The Log-rank test was used to assess the survival differences between these two treatment groups. The distribution of imaging efficacy (CR, PR, SD, and PD) and the occurrence of adverse events were compared between the two treatment groups using the chi-square test. In addition, the Cox proportional risk regression model was used to analyze the independent risk factors affecting OS, including age, Child-Pugh grade, tumor size, and number of treatments. All statistical tests were two-sided, at a defined significance level of P < 0.05.

Further, the tumor characteristics were observed using the recorded images. Accordingly, the mean tumor sizes were determined as 5.70 [4.90, 6.27] in the DEB-TACE group and 5.60 [4.25, 6.80] in the c-TACE group (P = 0.916). These findings suggested that the tumor loads of patients in the two groups were comparable. In addition, the distribution of biliary drainage modalities (PTCD vs. ERCP) was similar in both groups, indicating that patients received effective jaundice relief measures before treatment. Together, these results provided the basis for subsequent treatment.

Imaging efficacy

The tumor remission and disease control characteristics in the DEB-TACE group of patients were significantly better than those in the c-TACE group (Table 2). Based on RECIST 1.1 criteria, 4.3% of patients in the DEB-TACE group attained a CR value, while no patients in the c-TACE group achieved a CR rate. The PR values accounted for 43.5% in the DEB-TACE group of patients, which was significantly higher than in the c-TACE group (0.0%, P < 0.001). The percentage of SD value was higher in the c-TACE group of patients (70.9%) than in the DEB-TACE group (47.8%), indicating that the former group of patients presented some limitations in tumor remission. The PD value was not observed in the DEB-TACE group of patients (PD proportion of 0), reaching 29.1% in the c-TACE group of patients. To this end, the objective remission rate (ORR, CR + PR) in the DEB-TACE group (47.8%) was higher than in the c-TACE group of patients (0.0%, P < 0.001). In addition, the disease control rate (DCR, CR + PR+SD) reached 95.7% in the DEB-TACE group, which was higher than in the c-TACE group (70.9%). The representative findings before and after DEB-TACE and c-TACE are shown in Fig. 2. These images demonstrated the superior tumor control achieved with DEB-TACE, as evidenced by significant tumor shrinkage and reduced enhancement in the DEB-TACE group compared to the c-TACE group.

Survival analysis

The KM survival curves showed that the median OS time was 11 months. The OS rate of patients in the DEB-TACE group (14 months) was significantly better than that of the c-TACE group (P < 0.001), which was only 11 months (Fig. 3). The 1-year survival rate of the patients in the DEB-TACE group was recorded as 70.7% compared with 0.0% in the c-TACE group of patients (P < 0.001). Contrarily, the 2-year survival rate was 0.0% in both groups, suggesting that the short-term efficacy of DEB-TACE treatment was superior to that of the c-TACE group of patients. Unifactorial and multifactorial analysis

The Univariate analysis showed that all the treatment groups (P < 0.001) presented a significant effect on the OS rate of the patients (Table 3). Several variables with a P-value of < 0.2 in the Univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, such as vascular invasion (yes) (P = 0.117), number of intrahepatic metastases (1) (P = 0.0), and the treatment group (DEB-TACE). Accordingly, the DEB-TACE group was recognized as an independent protective factor for OS time (HR = 0.072, 95% CI: 0.026–0.196, P < 0.001).

Safety analysis

The safety analysis showed no significant difference (P > 0.05) between the DEB-TACE and the c-TACE groups in terms of the incidence rates of adverse events, such as fever, abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting, leukocytosis, subperitoneal effusion, and hepatic insufficiency. The incidence rates of adverse events in the experimental group were comparable to those of the control group. Nevertheless, there was no significant increase in the risk of serious adverse events, indicating that the experimental intervention showed an excellent safety profile (Table 4).

Discussion

Typically, obstructive jaundice has been recognized as one of the common complications in patients with advanced CCA. These complications not only aggravate the liver function damage of the patients but also create a significant limitation on the subsequent treatment options. Although surgical resection remains the potential therapeutic option, approximately 20% of patients meet the surgical requirements due to delayed diagnosis during the advanced stages [3]. Thus, TACE has become one of the most widely used localized therapies, specifically effective in improving intrahepatic tumor control and prolonging the survival rate of patients. Further, the development of DEB-TACE has offered potential advantages over conventional TACE in eradicating hepatocellular carcinoma and CCA.

In this study, we, for the first time, demonstrated that the DEB-TACE approach was significantly more effective than the c-TACE strategy in patients with CCA combined with obstructive jaundice, supporting the use of DEB-TACE as a preferred treatment option. The DEB-TACE approach showed exceptional results in both imaging efficacy and short-term survival of patients, with an ORR value of 47.8% and a DCR value of 95.7%, which was far superior to that of the c-TACE group of 0.0% and 70.9%, respectively. The differences in the ORR and DCR values might be closely related to the drug release mechanism and embolization effect of the treatment modalities. Moreover, the DEB-TACE could maintain high drug concentration locally in the tumor while reducing the toxicity exposure of normal tissues through slow release of chemotherapeutic drugs and precise embolization [9, 11]. Contrarily, c-TACE using iodized oil as the drug carrier could result in faster drug release and shorter maintenance of local chemotherapeutic drug concentration than the DEB-TACE, hampering its efficacy and eventual applicability [12]. Accordingly, the median OS rate of patients in the DEB-TACE group was recorded as 14 months, which was significantly longer than that of those in the c-TACE group (11 months). The 1-year survival rate (70.7%) of patients in the DEB-TACE group was much higher than that in the c-TACE group (0.0%). The significant short-term survival might be partly attributed to the superior effect of DEB-TACE in inhibiting tumor progression. Notably, the 2-year survival rate was recorded as 0.0% in both groups. Despite the outstanding short-term efficacy of DEB-TACE, its long-term survival advantage was still significantly challenged. These findings might be related to the highly aggressive nature of CCA itself and the overall poor pathological status of patients with advanced disease [10, 13].

Biliary drainage plays a key role in the treatment of patients with bile duct cancer combined with obstructive jaundice [14, 15]. In this study, biliary drainage (PTCD or ERCP) was completed for all patients before receiving TACE to reduce bilirubin levels and subsequently improve liver function. Further, these attributes could provide the necessary conditions for subsequent treatment, reducing treatment-related complications in patients due to hepatic insufficiency [16]. Nonetheless, the biliary drainage modality should be appropriately chosen as the efficacy might vary from patient to patient, needing additional investigations. Although the DEB-TACE approach demonstrated noticeable efficacy, its scope of application and therapeutic efficacy might still be affected by tumor blood supply, requiring further investigations. Considering the rich arterial blood supply, the intrahepatic CCA condition could be treated with TACE. In contrast, the effect of DEB-TACE might be limited due to the portal vein system-based blood supply of hepatoportal or distal CCA [17]. In addition, the safety and tolerability attributes of the DEB-TACE approach in patients with severely impaired liver function need to be further investigated.

Typically, various tumor markers and changes in their intracellular levels are the most important indicators for diagnosing and determining treatment strategies. In this study, it was observed that the CA19-9 and CEA levels of patients in the DEB-TACE group decreased more significantly after treatment than in the c-TACE group. These findings suggested that DEB-TACE might be more effective in suppressing tumor activity than the c-TACE approach, further supporting its potential benefits in disease control. However, the specific association between changes in the tumor markers and patient survival time and tumor load needs to be explored to provide additional information for optimizing therapeutic decisions [18, 19].

In addition, these findings showed that treatment modality was an independent protective factor affecting patients’ OS rate. Contrarily, other variables (e.g., tumor location and number of intrahepatic metastases) showed no significance in the multifactorial analysis. The predominant reasons could be related to the small sample size. Accordingly, this single-center retrospective study minimized bias concerning the study design and data analysis. Nevertheless, the potential selection bias and missing data were predictable. In the future, conducting large-sample, multicenter randomized controlled trials will be crucial to further validate the efficacy of DEB-TACE. In addition, combination therapy may become an important direction for future research. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) and targeted therapies (e.g., FGFR inhibitors) have been promising in CCA treatment [20, 21]. Combining immunotherapy or targeted therapy with localized treatments (e.g., DEB-TACE) will have the potential to improve the long-term survival of patients. Studies targeting the regulation of the tumor microenvironment are anticipated to provide a more theoretical basis for such combination therapy [22].

The performances of DEB-TACE and conventional c-TACE were compared in terms of therapeutic safety. There was no significant difference in the incidence of adverse events between the two groups, indicating that DEB-TACE as a modified treatment modality performed well. In terms of specific adverse events, the difference in incidence rates between the two groups showed no statistical significance concerning fever, abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting, leukocyte drop, subperitoneal effusion, and hepatic insufficiency. These findings were consistent with the results of previous studies. For example, Lammer and colleagues showed that DEB-TACE showed higher targeting and lower systemic toxicity compared to other modalities in the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma [23]. In a randomized controlled study, Golfieri and coworkers confirmed the advantages of DEB-TACE in reducing adverse effects [24]. In addition, DEB-TACE might have reduced chemotherapy-related adverse effects to a considerable extent due to its slow-release chemotherapeutic agent properties [25, 26]. However, a certain percentage of serious adverse events (e.g., hepatic insufficiency and leukopenia) still occurred in the DEB-TACE group in this study. In clinical practice, it is still necessary to closely monitor the adverse reactions of patients, adjusting the treatment regimen according to patient individualization.

Conclusion

In summary, this study has shown that DEB-TACE represented a significant improvement over c-TACE in terms of short-term efficacy and safety, which, however, failed to demonstrate long-term benefits. These findings possessed the potential to influence clinical guidelines, encouraging the adoption of DEB-TACE for patients with CCA and obstructive jaundice, particularly in cases where surgical resection is not feasible. In addition, this study sets the stage for future research into combination therapies, such as integrating DEB-TACE with systemic treatments like immunotherapy or targeted therapy. Future research focusing on combination therapies and personalized treatment strategies is required to address the aggressive nature of CCA and improve patient outcomes. Over the next five years, the field of CCA treatment will be poised for significant advancements. The integration of DEB-TACE with emerging therapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors and FGFR inhibitors, is likely to become a cornerstone of treatment. In addition, advancements in imaging and biomarker technologies may enable more precise patient selection and treatment monitoring, further optimizing outcomes. Moreover, the large-scale, multicenter studies will be critical to validate these approaches and establish evidence-based guidelines. By addressing these challenges, we can step forward closer to transforming the prognosis of this devastating CCA disease.

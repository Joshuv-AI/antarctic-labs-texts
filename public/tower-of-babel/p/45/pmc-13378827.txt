Demographics and obesity using a machine learning approach: Iranian National Obesity Registry (IRNOR)
Authors: Mina Nosrati, Niloufar Abdollahpour, Mahsa Tousi, Fatemeh Shahabi, Maryam Arabi, Mahdieh Zarif, Zahra Abasalti, Fatemeh Maghsoudi, Nafiseh Hosseini, Gordon A Ferns, Khalil Kimiafar, Majid Ghayour Mobarhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Obesity and overweight represent significant global health problems. The prevalence of obesity is increasing rapidly in developing countries and is involved in the etiology of cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, and several cancers. In this study, we aimed to evaluate the relationship between demographic factors and obesity using machine learning (ML) within the population identified by the IRanian National Obesity Registry (IRNOR).MATERIALS AND METHOD:This population-based cross-sectional study was started in 2021. A total of 2324 overweight and obese individuals were registered in IRanian National Obesity Registry (IRNOR) until October of 2022. The demographic data and anthropometric measurements of all participants were recorded. Multivariable logistic regression was used to evaluate relationships between demographic factors and obesity. Support vector machine (SVM) was applied to show the classification of important factors.RESULTS:Data for age, education, smoking, marital, job, medical history, physical activity, and body mass index (BMI) were recorded for 2324 individuals. Of these individuals, 71.4% were female. The mean age and BMI were 42.50 years and 32.63 kg/m2, respectively. In multivariable regression analysis, female gender, higher education, and walking and moderate activities were associated with lower odds and being housewife had higher odds for obesity in total population. According to the SVM model, job, marital status, sex, and smoking were the most important factors associated with obesity.CONCLUSION:The SVM model may be effective in detection of priorities and could be helpful for more planned investigations in this field.

Introduction

Obesity and overweight are important global health challenge, affecting individuals from childhood to adulthood.[1] Obesity is a complicated disorder involving physiological, biological, psychological, and environmental factors.[2] This condition leads to serious health complications, including diabetes, various cancers, cardiovascular diseases, and high blood pressure and hence significantly impacts overall well-being and quality of life.[234] Across diverse age groups, ethnicities, and socioeconomic backgrounds, rates of overweight and obesity continue to rise.[5] Globally, obesity contributes to four million deaths and 120 million disability-adjusted life years, based on 2015 estimates.[6] Notably, developing countries bear a higher burden, with obesity rates that are 30% higher than in developed nations.[2] In Iran, the prevalence of overweight and obesity in adults has reached approximately 56.55% and is 21.11% in children and adolescents in 2021.[6] Sedentary lifestyles, combined with unhealthy diets (such as high-refined carbohydrate intake), genetic factors, and environmental influences, drive this global surge in obesity over the past decade.[789] Despite being preventable, obesity remains a significant challenge for healthcare organizations due to weight-related complications. Medical registries play a crucial role in disease control programs, by improving the rates of diagnosis and treatment, and in the assessment of intervention efficacy, and therefore contribute to healthcare policy planning.[10]

Given the growing prevalence of obesity, utilizing obesity registries becomes essential for assessing and enhancing the clinical quality of obesity management.[11] Long-standing registries, such as the National Weight Control Registry (NWCR),[12] the Swedish Childhood Obesity Treatment Registry (1994–2010),[13] and the Pediatric Obesity Weight Evaluation Registry (POWER), have contributed valuable insights.[14] Additionally, countries like Canada,[15] Germany,[16] Korea,[17] Finland[18], and Portugal[19] have established obesity registries with the purpose of obesity treatment or weight loss maintenance. We have no established adult obesity registry in Iran, so, the necessity of development of an obesity registry to address the aspect of obesity is observed in Iranian population.

Machine learning methods have been used to uncover latent information, relationship, and classification of diseases which is extended in wide variety of healthcare data.[202122] Support vector machine (SVM) is one of the machine learning models that applicate for classification with a high versatility in multiple data sciences.[23] SVM has been widely used in prediction and diagnosis of health disorders including cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, brain disorders, and obesity.[24252627] The power of SVM results from its ability to provide data classification patterns with reproducibility and balanced accuracy.[23] Our study aimed to explore the association between demographic risk factors and obesity using SVM model as a machine learning method within the IRanian National Obesity Registry (IRNOR) population.

Materials and Method

Study design and setting

The IRanian National Obesity Registry (IRNOR) was established in 2021 in Iran with the goal of assessing the prevalence of overweight and obesity and evaluating the effectiveness of obesity interventions. Information on obese patients was collected from six collaborating centers, including Sabzevar, Birjand, and Abadan universities, a public healthcare center, the endoscopic surgery research center at Ghaem Hospital, and a private clinic in Mashhad, Iran. The inclusion criteria for IRNOR were a body mass index (BMI) ≥25 and age over 18, while the exclusion criteria included a lack of willingness to participate to the registry. As of October 2022, a total of 2324 obese individuals from the collaborating centers were registered in IRNOR.

Data collection tool and technique

Overweight and obese data were recorded on the IRNOR website. A trained expert collected demographic information, including gender, age, employment status, marital status, education level, self-reported medical, and medication history through face-to-face interview.

Anthropometric measurements, including weight, height, waist circumference (WC), hip circumference (HC), and blood pressure, were obtained using a body impedance analyzer (BIA) and administered by a trained person following a standard protocol.[1128] BMI was calculated by dividing weight (kg) to square of height (m). Adiposity is classified as overweight: 25 ≤BMI <30 kg/m2 and obesity: obese ≥30 kg/m2.[29]

We used the short form of self-administered International Physical Activity Level Questionnaire (IPAQ) to assess physical activity levels (PAL).[30] According to the IPAQ, physical activity, including sedentary behavior, walking, moderate and vigorous activity, was evaluated over the last seven days or based on usual activity.[30]

Ethical consideration

All participants informed consent. The Ethics Committee of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences approved this obesity registry (IR.MUMS.MEDICAL.REC.1401.038).

Statistical analysis method

Statistical analysis was conducted using Statistical Package for Social Sciences software (25 version, Chicago, IL, USA). We classified study population based on gender. General characteristics of individuals are presented as percentage for categorical variables and mean ± SDs for continues variables. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify demographic factors associated with obesity compared to overweight. Only the recorded data are analyzed, and no multiple imputation of missing data was conducted. P value less than 0.05 is considered significant. The SVM model was used to find the risk factors associated with obesity. Hence, SPSS Modeler 18.0 in a system with 4 Gig RAM, Intel (R), Corei5-2400 CPU, and 3.10 GHz was used for classification.

SVM is one of the most popular supervised learning models used for classification. The SVM model maps data into n-dimensional space to split data more easily. The n is based on the number of dataset factors (features). Then, the SVM model creates some decision boundary that separates data faultlessly. This boundary is called a hyperplane [Figure 1]. SVM uses different function such as radial basis function) RBF), polynomial, sigmoid, linear to draw the best hyperplane.[3132] Figure 2 shows how the SVM classification works. The primary dataset is transformed into a higher-dimensional space using a kernel function. This transformation enables the model to separate nonlinear data. In this space, an optimal hyperplane is identified that maximally separates different classes by maximizing the margin between the data points. Once the decision boundary is established in the high-dimensional space, SVM maps it back to the original feature space, making it more interpretable in terms of how the model separates classes based on the original features. Such capabilities are elevating SVM’s popularity in diverse applications of classification, such as text mining, image recognition, and bioinformatics. This process allows SVM to perform effectively on complex datasets.

Results

A total of 2324 overweight (35.1%) and obese (64.9%) individuals were registered onto the IRNOR. Approximately, 71.4% were females, and 28.6% were males. The mean age and BMI were 42.50 ± 13.87 years and 32.63 ± 5.39 kg/m2, respectively. The majority (54.8%) fell into the middle-aged group (30–49 years). Among the participants, 33.8% had diploma or some college education, while 48.4% had university education. Of the total population, 80.6% were married, while 19.4% were single. Employment was higher (44.5%), whereas 6.3% were retired. As shown in Table 1, the majority of the population had never smoked (91.7%). Around 60% of the population also reported no physical activity. Regarding past medical history of chronic diseases, fatty liver disease was the most prevalent disorder (20.8%).

Sex differences revealed significant variations in the distribution of age groups, education level, marital status, job, smoking, and physical activity. Fatty liver disease was more prevalent in men (P < 0.001), while no differences were observed in other chronic conditions. Men had significantly higher weight, height, and WC, but BMI remained consistent across genders.

Table 2 represents the association between demographics characteristics and obesity using multivariable regression analysis. According to this analysis, sex, education, job, and physical activity level were significantly associated with obesity. In total population, the odds ratio (OR) of obesity for women was lower (OR = 0.72; 95% CI = 0.53–0.97) compared to men. In education level, the odds of obesity was lower for post-graduate category in comparison with undergraduate (OR = 0.66; 95% CI = 0.49–0.87). In job category, the odds of obesity in housewife was higher (OR = 1.61; 95% CI = 1.15–2.25) compared to employed as a reference category. The OR of obesity in persons with walking and moderate PAL was lower (OR = 0.68; 95% CI = 0.51–0.91) and (OR = 0.69; 95% CI = 0.49–0.96) compared to who had no activity, respectively. In male group, the odds of obesity in illiterate level was lower (OR = 0.45; 95% CI = 0.20–0.99) compared to undergraduate. Also, men with walking and moderate PAL had lower odds for obesity (OR = 0.57; 95% CI = 0.33–0.97) and (OR = 0.41; 95% CI = 0.21–0.79), respectively. In female group, the odds of obesity in post-graduate education was lower (OR = 0.61; 95% CI = 0.43–0.87) in comparison with undergraduated female as a reference group, and in job category, the housewife women had higher odds for obesity (OR = 1.48; 95% CI = 1.02–2.15) compared to employed [Table 2].

Discussion

Obesity is increasing in prevalence and has become an important health problem that can lead to serious clinical sequelae.[33] Accurate prediction of correlated risk factors with disease needs to applicate the accurate and progressed models. In this study, we used artificial intelligence method to evaluate the association between demographic factors and obesity. To our knowledge, this study is the first to evaluate relationship between demographic factors and obesity using SVM. We reported that job, marital status, sex, and smoking are the important risk factors of obesity using SVM with 67.28% accuracy.

Several studies were performed to prediction of childhood obesity, obesity prediction applying genetic profiles and metabolic factors, lifestyle habits, eating behaviors, food sales, and risk of obesity using SVM.[343536373839] Montañez et al.[38] conducted an investigation to predict obesity through genetic profiles using SVM, decision tree (DT), and k-nearest neighbors (KNN) models that they reported the SVM had the highest accuracy (90.5%) and gave the best result between the prediction models. In a comparative study that was performed to predict risk of obesity through lifestyle habits using machine learning methods, the SVM had a higher accuracy than logistic regression and LogitBoost algorithm.[39]

According to SVM model, employment status was a risk factor for obesity. Occupations are related to socioeconomic factors and behavioral traits, consequently. So, they can have potential effects on risk of obesity.[40] Studies reported a positive association between work hours, job categories, and obesity. Employees spend more time of the day in their workplace.[41] Working for long hours has negative effect on health through lack of physical activity, occupational stress, skipping meals, consuming fast food, and lack of sleep in night work.[41] In our regression model, being housewife had higher odds of obesity. Being a housewife may be related to low physical activity, higher-frequency daily intake of carbohydrate sources, and less time for self-care.[4243] Another study in Iranian population was consistent with our findings.[44]

In SVM model, marital status was the second risk factor of obesity. Several studies indicated the association between marital and obesity.[4546] A meta-analysis showed the higher risk of obesity in individuals who have married compared to single persons.[46] More than 70% of our study population have married. One of the possible reasons for higher obesity in married people is spending time with a partner, allocating a large portion of leisure time, and sharing household and eating habits. If one partner gains weight, other partner is more likely to gain weight.[46]

Sex is one of the risk factors for obesity according to our SVM model. We showed that obesity was more prevalent in men, in the regression model. However, several previous studies indicated the higher prevalence of obesity in females compared to males in middle east countries,[4748495051] but similar to our results, a study in 16 European countries showed the more prevalent of obesity in males compared to females in 2010[52]; in the later same study in 20 European countries showed the prevalence of obesity was equal in both gender (15.9%) but overweight higher in males in 2014.[53] Another study in an American population reported the combined overweight and obesity more likely prevalent in males (74.7%) compared to females (68.9%).[54] An investigation was conducted using machine learning methods to evaluate the important risk factors of obesity. They have reported that the risk factors of obesity are sensitive to gender and age under various machine learning algorithms.[20] Our findings may be attributed to male’s occupation. The high percentage of our males were employed (78.1%).

According to the SVM model, the other risk factor for obesity was smoking. The relationship between obesity and smoking is debated. Although several studies reported that smoking lonely may lead to lower body mass index and reduced obesity,[5556] in contrast, some others demonstrated a strong relationship between smoking and increased risk of obesity.[5057] The probable reasons of the association between smoking and obesity may be exposed to the occupational, psychological, and marital distress,[58] which lead to poor diet, increased inflammation, hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis dysregulation, depression, sedentary lifestyle, drinking alcohol, and smoking.[5960] Despite these varied findings, it is need to pay attention to the prevalence of tobacco smoking that has risen by 8% among men and 2% among women in the past three decades in Iran.[61] The classification of risk factors can be useful in detection of priorities, to build prediction models in health systems and diagnosis due to clarity of decision rules established by the method.[62]

We found a negative correlation between obesity and educational level in female by regression model; consistent with our findings, some of investigations in Saudi Arabia, India, Russia, and Iran indicated a higher prevalence of overweight and obesity among those with lower educational levels.[63646566] A longitudinal study in USA has demonstrated that each additional year of education is associated with a decrease of 0.15 in BMI, and completing college education compared to high school graduation is associated with a 0.7 reduction in BMI, independently.[67] The positive impact of education on obesity is likely influenced by several factors including enhanced access to health information, improved risk perception associated with lifestyle choices, and higher self-monitoring and consistency in decision-making over time.[68] In contrast, lower education in male had lower risk for obesity in our regression model. The possible reason could be related to male job with lower education. Lower-educated males are less likely to employ in offices and organizations and were hired in labors, so, the physical activity in labors is high and the risk of obesity in these jobs is lower compared to office employees, consequently.

Given that we considered the intensity of physical activity, individuals with moderate physical activity and walking had a lower risk for obesity but severe intensity had no significant association with obesity, in regression model. For loss of weight and body fat mass, improvement in cardiometabolic function aerobic exercise at moderate intensity is recommended by the European Association for the Study of Obesity (EASO).[69]

Limitations and recommendation

Using SVM model to determine important risk factors of obesity was an important strength of our study. This study cannot determine cause-and-effect association. The possible bias in reporting of disease, physical activity, and smoking status cannot have ruled out. Lack of dietary intake assessment was the other limitations. SVM model showed inadequate sensitivity, suggesting that enhancing its sensitivity could potentially improve its effectiveness in targeted health interventions. Addressing these limitations through various longitudinal studies with a large sample size and variety of artificial intelligence models will be imperative for advancing future research on obesity.

Conclusion

According to the SVM model, employment status, marital status, sex, and smoking were the important risk factors for obesity. These findings emphasize the critical need for tailored healthcare strategies and intervention initiatives to address obesity prevalence in the population. Ultimately, using machine learning model as a progressed method to detect associations and risk factors is crucial for guiding the strategic implementation of public health initiatives and appropriate interventions with the aim of decreasing the prevalence and associated health risks of overweight and obesity among adult Iranian population.

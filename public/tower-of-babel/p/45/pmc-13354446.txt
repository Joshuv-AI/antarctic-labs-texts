Intravascular Large B-Cell Lymphoma Diagnosed on Gastroesophageal Junction Biopsy During Evaluation of Dysphagia
Authors: Rowan Bandaranaike, Beth Rutland, Travis Rutland, Adam Edwards
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntravascular large B-cell lymphoma is a rare aggressive lymphoma confined to small-vessel lumina that may elude endoscopic detection. Diagnosis from a gastroesophageal junction biopsy is rarely reported. A 75-year-old woman underwent upper endoscopy for dysphagia, revealing only an irregular Z-line. Histopathology demonstrated CD20-positive atypical lymphoid cells within submucosal vessel lumina, suspicious for intravascular large B-cell lymphoma. She was referred for hematologic staging and rituximab-based chemotherapy. This case illustrates that an irregular Z-line, attributed to reflux, can be the only mucosal evidence of an occult systemic lymphoma; systematic biopsy of even subtle findings is decisive in this rapidly fatal but curable disease.

INTRODUCTION

Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) is a rare, aggressive subtype of extranodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma in which malignant lymphocytes proliferate within the lumina of small vessels, characteristically sparing larger vessels and lymph nodes, a pattern attributed to defective lymphocyte homing-receptor expression.1–3 This intravascular confinement produces protean, nonspecific presentations, contributing to frequent diagnostic delay and historically poor outcomes.4 Gastrointestinal involvement is distinctly uncommon and endoscopic diagnosis exceedingly rare, as the intravascular cells form no discrete mucosal lesion.1,4,5 We present IVLBCL diagnosed from a gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) biopsy during evaluation of progressive dysphagia, in which an irregular Z-line routinely attributed to reflux was the sole endoscopic clue.

CASE REPORT

A 75-year-old woman with gastroesophageal reflux disease and hepatic steatosis presented with 3 months of progressive solid-food dysphagia. She took pantoprazole 40 mg daily, to which she was adherent with only partial relief, and no nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, bisphosphonates, tetracyclines, or potassium supplements. She denied odynophagia, weight loss, night sweats, fevers, melena, hematemesis, nausea, vomiting, early satiety, and rash. An upper endoscopy in 2016 for dysphagia had shown only a small hiatal hernia. Examination was unremarkable, without lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly.

Laboratory studies showed mild anemia (hemoglobin 10.1 g/dL; mean corpuscular volume 88 fL) with normal white-cell (6.1 × 109/L) and platelet (155 × 109/L) counts and normal renal function. Iron studies revealed low serum iron (35 µg/dL), low total iron-binding capacity (210 µg/dL), and 17% transferrin saturation with elevated ferritin (424 ng/mL), consistent with anemia of inflammation. C-reactive protein (122 mg/L) and lactate dehydrogenase (420 U/L) were elevated. Hepatitis B and C, HIV, and Epstein-Barr virus serologies were negative.

Upper endoscopy revealed an irregular Z-line at the GEJ without mass, ulceration, or mucosal lesion (Figure 1); the remaining esophagus appeared normal, with mild antral and body erythema, and the duodenum was normal. Systematic biopsies were taken from the GEJ, gastric body, antrum, and mid esophagus.

Endoscopic image of the gastroesophageal junction demonstrating an irregular Z-line without mass, ulceration, or discrete mucosal lesion.

The GEJ biopsy showed atypical lymphoid cells with hyperchromatic nuclei confined within small-vessel lumina (Figure 2), strongly CD20-positive (Figure 3) and negative for CD3, CD68, MPO, and SMA. A second pathology review confirmed an intravascular rather than intralymphatic location (CD34-positive, D2-40-negative endothelium). The cells were diffusely CD20-positive and PAX5-positive and CD5-negative, with BCL6 positivity in a subset, BCL2 positivity, and a Ki-67 of 40%–60%; MUM1, cyclin D1, CD10, HHV8, and Epstein-Barr virus-encoded RNA were negative, establishing IVLBCL. Other biopsies showed mild Helicobacter pylori-negative gastritis.

Gastroesophageal junction biopsy (hematoxylin and eosin, ×200). Subepithelial capillaries at the junction between squamous and glandular mucosa are filled by large atypical lymphoid cells with hyperchromatic nuclei confined to the vascular lumina, consistent with intravascular large B-cell lymphoma.

CD20 immunohistochemistry of the gastroesophageal junction biopsy.

Staging 18 F- fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) positron emission tomography (PET)/computed tomography showed mild splenomegaly without significant splenic or marrow hypermetabolism and no mass or lymphadenopathy. Bone marrow biopsy demonstrated large B-cell lymphoma, and peripheral flow cytometry confirmed circulating clonal B cells. She began rituximab-based chemotherapy with central nervous system (CNS)-directed prophylaxis, given the neurotropism of IVLBCL.6 Pantoprazole 40 mg daily was continued for ongoing reflux and to support GEJ mucosal protection during treatment. Early in therapy her dysphagia mildly improved, and she was established in oncology follow-up with surveillance endoscopy planned.

DISCUSSION

IVLBCL is a formidable diagnostic challenge: Its purely intravascular growth forms no mass on imaging or endoscop,y and, because it lacks lymphadenopathy, it is frequently misdiagnosed or identified only at autopsy, a behavior that has earned the label of a clinical “great imitator.”7 Manifestations most often involve the CNS and skin, whereas gastrointestinal involvement is distinctly uncommon and endoscopically confirmed disease rarer still.4 Reported gastrointestinal sites include the stomach, small intestine, colon, appendix, and gallbladder, often mimicking ischemia, infection, or inflammatory bowel disease.8–10 When mucosal change does occur, the endoscopic appearance is nonspecific, ranging from erythema and erosions to frank ulceration, as documented in gastric IVLBCL involving a gastrointestinal stromal tumor, rather than the discrete mass typical of conventional gastrointestinal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.8 To our knowledge, IVLBCL of the GEJ or esophagus has not previously been reported, making this an exceptionally rare site; it must remain on the differential in older adults with unexplained symptoms.

Progressive solid-food dysphagia carries a broad differential, including peptic stricture, adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, eosinophilic esophagitis, Schatzki ring, and dysmotility, none evident here except an irregular Z-line. Such a finding, usually ascribed to reflux, should prompt systematic biopsy rather than dismissal, since the intravascular cells produce no discrete lesion and diagnosis depends entirely on tissue sampling. Unexplained constitutional symptoms, fever, elevated lactate dehydrogenase, or cytopenias should further lower the threshold for sampling accessible or random sites.5

Diagnosis rests on atypical lymphoid cells within vascular lumina expressing CD20 and PAX5.11 CD34 positivity with D2-40 negativity distinguished intravascular from intralymphatic involvement. Molecularly, IVLBCL frequently harbors MYD88 (L265P) and CD79B mutations, placing it within the MYD88-mutant genetic subtype shared with immune-privileged site lymphomas of the CNS and testis and helping explain its neurotropism.12 Rarely, IVLBCL coexists with or evolves from nodal or other extranodal large B-cell lymphoma, and the relationship between such “solid” tumors and the intravascular compartment remains poorly defined.13 Shared MYD88/CD79B mutations or immunoglobulin rearrangements confirm a true mixed variant.12,13 In a mixed variant with extravascular growth, worsening dysphagia or a new mucosal mass should prompt repeat endoscopy, with endoscopic ultrasound to assess depth of involvement and regional lymphadenopathy.

When IVLBCL is suspected, random skin biopsy, bone marrow biopsy, and 18 F-FDG PET/computed tomography help localize disease.5,14,15 FDG-PET has limited sensitivity for low-volume intrasinusoidal marrow infiltration, explaining the discordance between her unremarkable marrow uptake and positive marrow biopsy. CD20 positivity is therapeutically pivotal: Rituximab-based regimens achieve complete response rates exceeding 80%, with the best results when treatment begins before widespread organ dysfunction, though CNS relapse remains a concern and supports the CNS-directed prophylaxis administered here.6,16,17 Should dysphagia worsen or new GEJ ulceration appear on follow-up endoscopy, enteral nutritional support warrants consideration to maintain nutrition, optimize treatment tolerance, and promote mucosal healing. Adherence to systematic biopsy during dysphagia evaluation enabled this diagnosis and underscores the gastroenterologist's pivotal role; the patient's age fits the predilection of IVLBCL for older adults, in whom the median age at diagnosis is 67-70 years.2,4,6 The lesson is concrete: An irregular Z-line, among the most readily dismissed endoscopic findings, warranted systematic biopsy, the single decision that transformed a routine endoscopy into the diagnosis of a rapidly fatal yet highly curable systemic lymphoma.

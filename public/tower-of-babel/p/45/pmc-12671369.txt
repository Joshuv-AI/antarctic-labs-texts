Trends of Biopsy-Proven Kidney Diseases in Romania: A Single-Center Registry Report Spanning 28 Years
Authors: Iuliana Ciocănea-Teodorescu, Bogdan Obrişcă, Gener Ismail, Mihaela Gherghiceanu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rationale & ObjectiveThe epidemiologic landscape of biopsy-proven kidney diseases in Romania and its evolution over time are poorly understood.Study DesignRegister-based single-center retrospective study.Setting & ParticipantsAdult native kidney biopsy records between 1996 and 2023 from the registry of the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology, Bucharest, Romania.PredictorYear of biopsy.Outcome(s)Histopathological diagnosis.Analytical ApproachTemporal trends in participant demographics and frequency of histopathological diagnoses were evaluated using regression analyses.ResultsWe identified a total of 1,937 adult native kidney biopsy records. The median age at diagnosis was 46 years (IQR, 33-58 years), and men represented 54.5% of participants. Immunoglobulin A nephropathy was the most common diagnosis (15.4%), followed by membranous nephropathy (12.5%), focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (8.3%), minimal change disease (8.2%), lupus nephritis (7.0%), and diabetic nephropathy (6.9%). Our results indicate a change in demographic composition of the population biopsied, reflected in an increase in the median age at biopsy over time, and a decrease in the male proportion in recent periods. We identified historically increasing immunoglobulin A nephropathy and diabetic nephropathy frequencies, and decreasing membranous nephropathy and minimal change disease frequencies, which stabilized over more recent periods. In contrast, lupus nephritis registered an increasing trend that was maintained in recent times.LimitationsThis is a single-center study and does not report on the clinical data of the participants.ConclusionsOur findings, corroborated with those of other centers in Romania, suggest that kidney disease trends in the Romanian population largely align with those reported in other European registries. However, we echo concerns voiced in other biopsy registry studies, regarding the challenges encountered in attempting direct comparisons between published reports within and across countries.

Kidney biopsy is the recognized gold standard for the diagnosis of kidney diseases. However, a recent study1 found that Romania has the largest proportion of histologically nonexamined cases of primary glomerular diseases among kidney replacement therapy (KRT) patients in Europe (79%). Although a kidney replacement registry2 has been in place in Romania since 2004, its focus is on individuals who have reached kidney failure and commenced KRT, and it contains little information related to histologic evaluations of biopsy specimens. A kidney predialysis registry3,4 covering patients who have been diagnosed with chronic kidney disease was only initiated in 2018. A national kidney biopsy registry is lacking in Romania. Despite a recent series of publications, containing single-center registry reports from the major kidney biopsy processing units across the country,5, 6, 7, 8, 9 the landscape of biopsy-proven kidney diseases in Romania remains incomplete, and its evolution over time is poorly understood.

In this paper, our goal is threefold. First, we report on the kidney biopsy data contained in the registry of the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology. Our registry covers 28 years, from 1996 to 2023, therefore having the potential to show important demographic and diagnosis frequency trends over a large period of time. Second, we compare these trends to those reported in other Romanian single-center biopsy registries. Lastly, we interpret our findings in the context of other long-term European kidney biopsy registry reports, with a focus on those reports that offer insights on temporal trend evolutions.

Methods

Data Collection

Records of kidney biopsies processed between January 1996 and December 2023 at the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology, Bucharest, were extracted from the center’s registry. Repeat biopsies were excluded, except when the reason for the second biopsy was the inadequate quality of the material of the first biopsy. In these cases, the second biopsy was retained for analysis, and the first was excluded. Transplant biopsies and biopsies of participant aged younger than 18 years were excluded. Specimens were examined under light, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy, and detailed histopathological reports were available for each modality. All biopsies were evaluated by one of 4 nephropathologists. For each participant, we extracted the age and biological sex of the participant, the date of the biopsy, and the histopathological diagnoses. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology (nr. 120/06.02.2024). All data were deidentified and are presented in an aggregate manner.

Referring Nephrology Centers and Study Population

Most of the biopsies received for evaluation at the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology are performed at the Department of Nephrology of the Fundeni Clinical Institute, Bucharest. However, our center also evaluates kidney biopsies received from other major hospitals in Bucharest, and sporadically from nephrology units outside Bucharest. It is common practice for patients to be referred to nephrology units in Bucharest from across the country.

Histopathological Diagnoses

The primary histopathological diagnosis was retained for each participant, and other secondary diagnoses were discarded. Diagnosis categories are listed in Table S1 in the Supplementary File, and largely follow those used in Laurens et al.10 Where necessary, diagnoses were reviewed and classified into one of these categories.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as median values and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages. Associations between variables were tested using χ2 tests and Wilcoxon tests. Differences in proportions were tested using sample proportions tests. Because of uncertainty in population coverage, we refrained from reporting incidence measures because these may be unreliable. Instead, we focused on relative frequencies. Data were initially analyzed across four 7-year intervals: 1996-2002, 2003-2009, 2010-2016, and 2017-2023. However, for diagnoses with more than 100 recorded cases, to make full use of the granularity of the data when evaluating temporal trends, we performed logistic regression against the year of biopsy, adjusting for sex and age. We also evaluated the quadratic contribution of the biopsy year to the models. For a given diagnosis, this type of regression models the probability of a participant receiving that diagnosis given the year the biopsy was performed, the age and the sex of that participant. A positive coefficient of the year of biopsy indicates an increasing trend. Conversely, a negative coefficient indicates a decreasing trend. A significant quadratic contribution of the year of biopsy indicates an inflection point in the trend trajectory. We report the coefficients, the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs), and associated P values, as well as the results of the regressions performed on the data covering the more recent time interval between 2010 and 2023 in the Supplementary File.

All P values reported are two-sided, and a P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multiple testing corrections were performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, in which case adjusted P values (P.adj) were reported. All statistical analysis were carried out using R (v4.4.1) in RStudio (v2024.9.1).

Results

Cohort and Biopsy Frequency

We identified 2,493 kidney biopsies recorded in our registry between January 1996 and December 2023. Of these, 80 were repeat biopsies and were excluded. A further 155 transplant biopsies and 321 pediatric native kidney biopsies were excluded, leaving 1,937 adult native kidney biopsies for further analysis (see Fig 1 for a flowchart of the biopsy records selection). The mean number of biopsies performed yearly varied from 58.1 in the period 1996-2002, 30.6 in the period 2003-2009, and 33.9 in the period 2010-2016 to 154.1 in the period 2017-2023. Participant Demographics

Male participants represented 54.5% (n=1,055) of the total cohort and females represented 43.9% (n=850). For 1.7% (n=32) of the participants, sex was not available in the records. The percentage of male participants saw an increase from 54.3% in the period 1996-2002 and 55.1% in 2003-2009 to 62.4% in the period 2010-2016. It then decreased significantly to 52.6% (P.adj = 0.03) in the period 2017-2023. This was reflected in significant positive linear and negative quadratic contributions of the year of biopsy in the logistic regression model (both P = 0.01).

The median age at biopsy was 46 years (IQR, 33-58 years) and saw a significant increase in time from median values of 38 (IQR, 27-50 years) and 36 (IQR, 26-52 years) years in the periods 1996-2002 and 2003-2009, respectively, to a median age of 46 (IQR, 37-57, P.adj < 0.001) in the period 2010-2016 and 50 (IQR, 37-62 years; P.adj = 0.03) in the period 2017-2023. There was no significant difference between the distribution of age between male and female participants (P = 0.60).

Diagnosis Frequency Trends

The frequency of IgAN diagnoses saw substantial consecutive increases from 10.6% (n=43), to 17.3% (n=37, P.adj = 0.05) to 25.3% (n=60, P.adj = 0.06) in the first three 7-year intervals. Then, it decreased significantly to 14.7% (n=159, P.adj < 0.001) in 2017-2023. This was also reflected in significant positive linear and negative quadratic contribution of the year of biopsy, when adjusting for age and sex (both P = 0.04).

MN saw a decrease from 16.2% (n=66) in 1996-2002 to 10.7% (n=23, P.adj = 0.25), 12.2% (n=29, P.adj = 0.84), and 11.5% (n=124, P.adj = 0.84) in the latter 3 periods; none of these changes were statistically significant. When adjusting for age and sex, however, the linear contribution of the year of biopsy was significant (P < 0.001) and negative, indicating an overall decreasing trend (the quadratic term was not significant and was discarded).

FSGS had a stable evolution across time at 8.8% (n=36), 5.1% (n=11, P.adj = 0.34), 10.5% (n=25, P.adj = 0.31), and 8.2% (n=88, P.adj=0.43) in the 4 periods, respectively; none of these changes were statistically significant. This was reflected by a nonsignificant contribution of the year of biopsy in the regression models when adjusting for age and sex (P = 0.40).

MCD saw a decrease from 12.3% (n=50) in 1996-2002 to 7.9% (n=17, P.adj = 0.26), 6.8% (n=16, P.adj = 0.91), and 7.0% (n=75, P.adj = 1.00) in the latter 3 periods; none of these changes were statistically significant. When adjusting for age and sex, the linear contribution of the year of biopsy was significant (P = 0.01) and negative, indicating an overall decreasing trend (the quadratic term was not significant and was discarded).

DN saw a stark increase from 1.5% (n=6) in 1996-2002 to 12.6% (n=27, P.adj < 0.001) in 2003-2010. DN then registered a mild, nonsignificant decline to 8.4% (n=20, P.adj = 0.23) and 7.5% (n=81, P.adj = 0.72). This was reflected in significant positive linear and negative quadratic contributions of the year of biopsy when adjusting for age and sex (both P < 0.001).

LN frequency had an unstable evolution over time, from 6.4% (n=26) to 9.8% (n=21, P.adj = 0.25), 3.4% (n=8, P.adj = 0.06), and 7.4% (n=80, P.adj = 0.11) over the four 7-year intervals; none of these changes were statistically significant. When adjusting for age and sex, the linear contribution of the year of biopsy was significant (P = 0.04) and positive, indicating an overall increasing trend (the quadratic term was not significant and was discarded).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the trends in demographic characteristics and histopathological diagnoses frequencies over a large period of time using the data available in the kidney biopsy registry of the Victor Babeş National Institute of Pathology, Bucharest. The number of biopsies processed at our center yearly fluctuated over time. However, a markedly increasing trend was clear, particularly in the last decade, which may be explained by an improved awareness of glomerular disorders, leading to a significant increase in referrals to tertiary centers for kidney biopsy procedures. An increase in biopsy incidence rates in recent years is also echoed in registry reports from other kidney biopsy diagnosis centers across Romania.7, 8, 9 Reported recent incidence rates in these studies vary between 12.3 biopsies per million persons per year (pmp/year) in Iaşi,7 12 biopsies pmp/year in Cluj,9 and 14.75 cases of glomerular disease pmp/year in Bucharest.8 However, these estimates may be unreliable because of uncertainty in population coverage.

The median age at diagnosis in our cohort was 46 years and saw a consistent and significant increase across time, from 38 years in the period 1996-2002 to 50 years in the period 2017-2023. This increase is consistent with reports from other Romanian5, 6, 7, 8, 9,11 and European kidney biopsy registries.12, 13, 14 The proportion of male participants was higher than female participants overall (54.5% men), as well as within most diagnosis categories, with the exception of LN (78.4% women), for which the predominance of female participants is typically encountered.8, 9, 10,12,13,15,16 The most frequent diagnosis overall was IgAN (15.4%), followed by MN (12.5%), FSGS (8.3%), and MCD (8.2%).

There is no clear agreement in the literature on the evolution of IgAN incidence over time, with European long-term studies reporting both stable17,18 and increasing13,14 trends. Our data suggest a historically increasing trend, followed by a significant decrease between the periods 2010-2016 and 2017-2023. However, this trend may simply reflect the changes in biopsy practices across different time periods, with more biopsies performed in recent periods in older individuals with more comorbid conditions. Adjusting for demographic characteristics renders the decreasing trend in recent times nonsignificant, suggesting that the true underlying trend is in fact stable.

The frequency of a histologic MN diagnosis is generally reported to be decreasing.14,19 Although our data do not suggest any significant changes in MN frequency over the different time periods studied, adjustment for age and sex does show a significant decreasing trend. This is reflective of the fact that increasing age at biopsy, coupled with the fact that a histologic MN diagnosis is more frequent in older age groups, can make an underlying decreasing trend seem unchanged. A partial explanation for the decrease in the histologic MN diagnosis frequency, at least for recent periods, may be found in the implementation of anti-phospholipase A2 receptor (anti-PLA2R) antibody screening. In younger individuals, with preserved kidney function and no potential secondary causes of nephrotic syndrome, a positive anti-PLA2R antibody test is highly predictive of an underlying primary MN. Accordingly, MN has shifted from a “histological” to a “serological” diagnosis.20

MCD registered a mildly declining trend, and the trend trajectory of FSGS was stable across the entire studied period.

The trend trajectory of DN was dominated by a stark increase at the beginning of the study period, which leveled off toward more recent times. However, the evolution of the frequency of biopsy-proven DN is strongly related to the biopsy referral policies and practices in place at the time of diagnosis, so these results are difficult to interpret in terms of the real underlying trends.

We identified an increasing trend in LN, which remained significant when restricting the analysis to the more recent period 2010-2023. A recent report from the Spanish Registry suggested the frequency of LN is declining.15 Because LN is a disease mainly affecting the younger population, a LN diagnosis will generally be less likely in more recently accrued cohorts, so that marginally—that is to say, without adjustment for demographic characteristics—this may read as a decreasing trajectory, even if the true underlying incidence trend is stable or even slightly increasing.

Our data are generally consistent with other registry reports from biopsy diagnosis centers in Romania (see Table S4 in the Supplementary File). In particular, Pană et al8 and Maslyennikov et al9 report comparable IgAN frequencies at 15.7% and 13.9%, respectively (out of the total number of adult native kidney biopsies). However, the latter identifies MN as the most frequent diagnosis at 14.7%. In terms of trends, Pană et al11 reported a significant decrease in MCD and a significant increase in DN diagnoses between the periods 2008-2012 and 2013-2017. Nonsignificant decreases in MN and FSGS were also reported. The frequencies of IgAN and LN diagnoses were stable between the 2 periods studied. However, these analyses relied on the time-period split chosen and did not adjust for age or sex. As noted earlier, stable marginal LN frequencies may conceal underlying increasing trends that would become significant when adjusting for age and sex. Similarly, a nonsignificant marginal decrease in MN may be rendered significant after adjustment for demographic characteristics.

Data reported in Covic et al,5 Volovăt et al,6 and Covic et al7 show some differences, owing, in part, to a differing disease classification system based on the glomerular pattern of injury and possibly different policies regarding kidney biopsy indications. In particular, in these publications, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis is consistently identified as the most frequent finding at 20.6%, 14.2%, and 28.8%, respectively, out of the total number of adult native biopsies. Aggregating data over these 3 studies show a stark increase in MN diagnoses from 7.5% to 13.4%, and 25.9%, respectively, which is different from our findings.

Although there are numerous published kidney biopsy registry reports, with single-center to regional and national coverage, relatively little attention has been given to the analysis of temporal trends. Studies identified in the literature spanning a period of at least 15 years and published after 2009 are summarized in Table S5 in the Supplementary File. Importantly, only 2 of these studies span more than 28 years,14,18 and only 1 of these studies21 includes data collected beyond the year 2019. Among other things, this also limits the evaluation of the impact of kidney biopsy policies and practices during the coronavirus disease (COVID)-19 pandemic on diagnosis frequencies. Although long-term studies can be highly informative and can offer valuable insights into the epidemiologic landscape of kidney diseases, they also pose important analytical challenges because of the changing composition of the biopsied population over time. Reporting based on a more or less arbitrarily chosen splitting of the study period, that is not doubled by granular analysis and adjustment for age and sex may be misleading. Adjustment is particularly important in diagnoses known to be strongly associated with demographic characteristics, such as IgAN, MN, or LN. This is a methodological aspect of general relevance for the reader tasked with the interpretation or analysis of similar data.

Limitations

Important limitations of our study include its single-center nature as well as the lack of clinical data. Moreover, choosing to focus on the primary diagnosis in our analysis and discarding secondary diagnoses may introduce subjectivity in the classification of cases with multiple nephropathies. In our study, we employed a thorough statistical analysis, evaluated nonlinear trends, and performed sensitivity analyses in an effort to mitigate cohort effects that may distort results. It is generally accepted that biopsy indication policies vary across centers and have changed extensively over time. This cannot be accounted for in the analysis and must be kept in mind in the interpretation of the results. Nonetheless, we believe that the limitations of our study are offset by the long study period and coverage of recent years, the rigorous statistical analysis applied to the data designed to account for the changing demographic composition of the biopsied population over time, as well as the general scarcity of data from this region.

Our study is a contribution to the series of single-center registry reports from major kidney biopsy diagnosis centers across Romania. Our findings suggest that kidney disease trends in the Romanian population largely align with those reported in other European registries. However, because of differing reporting periods, inclusion criteria and diagnosis classifications, it remains difficult to aggregate or compare these reports directly. We echo the call voiced repeatedly in the literature, referring to the need of establishing national kidney biopsy registries and standardizing reporting practices.

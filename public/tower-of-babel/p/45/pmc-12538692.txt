Beyond fenbendazole: Molecular and integrated pathways for managing poultry ascarid resistance
Authors: Schawanya K Rattanapitoon, Natthawut Charoenphon, Nathkapach K Rattanapitoon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCollins et al. (2025) present important evidence of emerging fenbendazole (FBZ) resistance in Ascaridia galli and widespread resistance in Heterakis gallinarum. While the survey highlights an urgent production challenge, broader scientific and translational perspectives are needed. We emphasize three key areas: (i) genetic and molecular mechanisms underlying benzimidazole resistance in ascarids, which remain poorly defined compared to Haemonchus contortus and equine Parascaris; (ii) the One Health and regulatory implications of continued FBZ dependence in poultry production; and (iii) integrated parasite management strategies combining diagnostics, alternative compounds, and husbandry interventions. Focusing on these directions will allow future studies to move beyond descriptive surveys toward sustainable, evidence-based control strategies.

Dear Editor,

The recent contribution by Collins and colleagues (2025) provides compelling evidence that fenbendazole (FBZ) resistance has now been confirmed in Ascaridia galli and is widespread in Heterakis gallinarum. This finding, while expected given the long-standing reliance on FBZ, represents a pivotal warning for global poultry production.

While Collins et al. demonstrated resistance across isolates from two U.S. states, three broader considerations deserve attention to advance the field:

Molecular and genetic mechanisms

Current understanding of benzimidazole resistance in ascarids lags far behind that in small ruminant nematodes. β-tubulin mutations, the classical resistance markers, are inconsistently reliable predictors in A. galli and H. gallinarum (Martin et al., 2021). Recent comparative genomics work (Jones et al, 2024; McIntyre et al., 2025) suggests that alternative resistance pathways may be involved, including altered drug efflux and metabolic adaptation. Elucidating these mechanisms using transcriptomics and functional assays is essential to design molecular diagnostics with real predictive value.

One health and regulatory dimensions

Unlike ruminant production systems with multiple licensed anthelmintics, poultry relies solely on FBZ. This regulatory bottleneck magnifies the risk of resistance becoming entrenched. Moreover, drug residues, environmental dissemination of resistant parasite alleles, and cross-species exposure raise One Health concerns (Hauck, 2024). Regulatory agencies and industry stakeholders must recognize the urgency of diversifying the arsenal of approved treatments while ensuring food safety.

Integrated parasite management strategies

Sustainable control cannot depend on chemical treatments alone. Programs validated under field conditions integrating litter hygiene, rotational management, and selective treatment could mitigate selection pressure (Araújo et al., 2021). Experimental use of phytochemicals and probiotics as adjunct interventions appears promising but requires rigorous on-farm trials in poultry before large-scale adoption (Jamil et al., 2022). Importantly, economic analyses, such as those by Collins et al. (2021) in turkeys, must now be extended to layers and breeders to guide cost-effective implementation.

In summary, Collins et al. (2025) provide essential surveillance data on FBZ resistance in poultry ascarids. To ensure long-term impact, future research should move beyond prevalence surveys toward mechanistic, integrative, and policy-relevant studies. Such an approach will be critical to preserve poultry health, maintain industry profitability, and uphold consumer trust.

A high-efficiency and selective fluorescent assay for the detection of tetracyclines
Authors: Jingqiao Guo, Jianhui Xin, Jine Wang, Zhen Li, Jianlei Yang, Xue Yu, Mengxia Yan, Jiangyang Mo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTetracyclines (TCs) rank second globally in the use of animal infection therapy and animal husbandry as growth promoters among all antibiotics. However, large amounts of TCs residue in food products and more than 75% of TCs are excreted into the environment, causing adverse effects on the ecological system and human health. It has been challenging to simultaneously realize low-cost, rapid, and highly selective detection of TCs. Here, inspired by the fluorogenic reactions between resorcinol and catecholamines, we find the fluorescence quenching ability of tetracycline (TC) and firstly propose a fluorescent “turn-off” detection of TC using dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol. The optimal reaction condition for the fluorescent assay is investigated and the optimized probe showed a good limit of detection (LOD of 1.7 µM) and a wide linear range (10 µM to 350 µM). Moreover, this fluorescent assay proved to be an effective tool for detecting TC in river, Sprite, and beer samples, which represent the aquatic environments and food and may contain tetracyclines residues. Finally, the high selectivity of the method for TC has been confirmed by eliminating the interference from common substances. The proposed strategy provides a high-efficiency and selective solution for the detection of TCs in environment and food and the application fields of this fluorescent assay could be further expanded in the future.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74411-7.Keywords: Tetracyclines; Fluorescent assay; Dopamine; 4-fluororesorcinol, Turn-off detection, Environment and foodSubject terms: Analytical chemistry, Fluorescent probes

Introduction

As a family of important and broad-spectrum antibiotics for bacterial infections, tetracyclines (TCs) are widely used in humans and animals due to their advantages of low production cost, high quality, and high purity. TCs mainly include tetracycline (TC)1, chlortetracycline (CTC)2, oxytetracycline (OTC), and doxycycline (DXC)3. Among all antibiotics, tetracycline antibiotics rank second globally in terms of production and use4. Although tetracycline antibiotics are beneficial to human and animal health in certain doses, animal breeders overuse tetracycline antibiotics in the process of feeding animals for a long time and excessive use. This abuse of TCs could lead to the accumulation of residual tetracycline antibiotics in animal-derived foods such as milk, meat, and honey. If accumulating in the human body through the food chain, TCs could cause adverse health effects, such as diarrhea, white patches inside the mouth, loss of appetite or upset stomach5–7. What’s more, TCs have been frequently found in surface water, groundwater, drinking water, wastewater, which leads to pollution of the surrounding environment, thereby destroying the balance of the ecosystem8,9.

In order to ensure consumer health, many countries and regulatory organizations, such as the European Union, Sweden, and the Codex Alimentarius Commission, have established regulations to control the dosage of antibiotics in animal foods or prohibit their use, strictly specifying the Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) of tetracycline antibiotics in animal-derived foods. From the perspective of theory, the chemical sensors for the detection of antibiotics and drugs and the molecular mechanisms behind the sensors are urged to be further explored10–12. Therefore, it is essential to develop a simple, accurate and effective method for sensitive detection of TCs. Currently, based on the detection principles, analytical methods for detecting TCs can be divided into microbiological assays, physicochemical assays and immunological assays13. Microbiological methods detect the residual TCs based on the inhibitory effect of tetracycline drugs on the physiological functions and reproductive metabolism of microorganisms. These kinds of methods are usually cost-effective, easy to apply and fast but are low specificity, low sensitivity and susceptible to interference from other antibiotics. Physicochemical methods, such as high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)14, liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)15–17, capillary electrophoresis18,19 and spectrophotometry20, are basis of the special properties or specific reactions of functional groups in tetracyclines to detect their content. These methods have high sensitivity and are widely used but time-consuming, expensive, and require sophisticated laboratory equipment and highly trained personnel. Immunological detection methods are based on the specific and reversible binding reaction between the antigen (tetracyclines) and antibodies, including enzyme-linked immunoassay21 and electrochemical technique22. These methods are sensitive enough, cheap, and fast, though they sometimes lack specificity23.

As far as we know, fluorometric assays have attracted considerable attention due to the simple and rapid implementation, high sensitivity, and spatial resolution as well as real-time monitoring specialties. For example, Pena et al.24 reported an analytical methodology for the determination of tetracyclines and oxytetracycline in honey by fluorescence detection in the presence of magnesium ions. For the quantification of TCs residues in egg yolk, egg white and hen plasma, Zurhelle et al.25 quantified isochlortetracycline (ICTC) and ICTC-derived compounds by fluorescence after adjustment of the HPLC eluate to pH 12.0. Moreover, Pena et al.26 reported a fluorometric method for the quantification and identification of oxytetracycline and TCs residues in salmon muscle and the limit of quantitation was 50 µg/kg. To further improve the fluorescence of tetracyclines chelates, several buffers and pH conditions have been used. Spisso et al.27 achieved more strong signals for tetracyclines, oxytetracycline, chlortetracycline and doxycycline using N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) instead of an aqueous boric acid buffer. Adopting DMSO in conjunction with magnesium acetate, Granados-Chinchilla et al.28 developed a green chemistry method for extraction, chromatography, and derivatization of first-, second-, and third-generation tetracyclines and epimers from feed samples. Abbasi et al.29 reported a fluorescence-based method to detect tetracyclines in milk using shorter wavelengths without any reference of derivatization but the mechanism that the authors are exploiting is unclear.

Recently, it is reported30–32 that a category of biocompatible fluorogenic reactions between 1,3-dihydroxybenzene (resorcinol) and 1,2-dihydroxybenzene derivatives (catechols or catecholamines, especially dopamine) would take place in the aqueous solution, to yield fluorescent oxocines or azocines with bright blue-emitting fluorescence via a facile one-step reaction under high-alkaline conditions. Prota et al.30 reported that resorcinol can be used for oxidative coupling of levodopa and significantly inhibit the oxidative conversion of levodopa to dopachrome in an aqueous solution. Then, Acuna et al.32 described the reactions between resorcinol and catecholamines and identified the generation of fluorescent azocine products in detail.

Inspired by such specific fluorogenic reactions between resorcinol and catecholamines, we find the fluorescence quenching ability of TC and firstly propose a fluorescent “turn-off” detection of TC using dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol in this study. It is a fast and direct method to prepare the water soluble organic fluorescent probe by dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol at room temperature, the reaction mechanism is shown in Fig. 1. Under excitation light of 416 nm, this probe emits fluorescence at 461 nm. In the presence of TC, fluorescence of this probe gets decreased owing to the inner filter effect, and there is a good linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and TC concentration, therefore, this probe can be utilized in fluorescent “turn-off” detection of TC.

Experimental sections

Chemicals and materials

Sodium carbonate, 4-fluororesorcinol, dopamine, phosphoric acid, boric acid, acetic acid, monosodium phosphate and sodium phosphate dibasic were purchased from Aladdin Industrial Corporation (Shanghai, China). Tetracycline, artemisinin, artesunate, dihydroartemisinin, thiourea, citric acid, glucose, kanamycin, and ethanol were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. All reagents were analytical grade and used as received without any further purification.

Apparatus and characterization

Fluorescent spectra were recorded on an Varioskan Flash full-wavelength scanning multi-function Enzyme reader (Thermo scientific). Zf-20d UV analysis equipment from Gongyi Yu Hua Instrument, LTD. (Gongyi, China) is used to take fluorescent photos under 365 nm irradiation. S-25 digital pH meter (Shanghai Jing ke Industry Company, Shanghai, China) was used to detect the acidity of solutions.

Optimization of the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol

The primary factors that influence the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol include reaction time, pH of Britton-Robinson (BR) buffer33, the concentration of sodium carbonate, 4-fluororesorcinol, and dopamine.

Reaction time

In 2.0 mL microcentrifugals, volumes of 100 µL of BR buffer and 1400 µL of water were injected, then 200 µL of dopamine aqueous solution, 200 µL 4-fluororesorcinol solution, and 100 µL sodium carbonate solution were added, respectively. The fluorescence spectra and measurements were conducted after every 5-minute interval of vibration at room temperature.

pH of BR buffer

In analogy with above process, 100 µL of BR buffer, 1400 µL of water, 200 µL of dopamine aqueous solution, 200 µL 4-fluororesorcinol solution, and 100 µL sodium carbonate solution were added into the microcentrifugals successively. To identify the optimal pH, the fluorescence spectra of the mixture solution were recorded when pH of BR buffer varied from 3 to 12 after vibration at room temperature for 30 min.

Concentration response

The influence of the concentration of sodium carbonate, 4-fluororesorcinol, and dopamine was investigated, respectively. We mixed 100 µL of BR buffer, 1400 µL of water, 200 µL of dopamine aqueous solution, 200 µL 4-fluororesorcinol solution, and 100 µL sodium carbonate solution in microcentrifugals. The fluorescence spectra of the mixture were recorded while the concentrations of sodium carbonate ranged from 50 mM to 2500 mM and other experiment conditions were fixed. Using this control variates method, the optimal concentrations of 4-fluororesorcinol and dopamine were determined.

All the data were the averaged values of four parallel experiments.

Procedures for fluorescent “turn-off” detection of tetracycline

On the basis of the optimal reaction conditions, 100 µL tetracycline was added into the mixture solution before vibration. By varying the concentration of tetracycline and recording corresponding fluorescence spectra, the linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and the TC concentration was established.

Since river is one of the aquatic environments most likely to be contaminated by tetracyclines34, Sprite and beer are representative substances of food and the main ingredients of which are water, the river, Sprite, and beer samples containing various spiked tetracycline concentrations were tested to further validate the reliability of the fluorescent assay. Before spiking tetracycline, river water (collected from Chahe river in Dezhou) was diluted to ten times by ultrapure water and filtrated by 0.22 μm membrane35. The Sprite and TSINGTAO beer (bought from supermarkets) samples were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 min and then diluted to ten times by ultrapure water36. After that, 50 µL tetracycline and 50 µL processed samples were mixed. The concentrations of tetracycline were 40, 150, 250, and 450 µM, therefore we obtained the river, Sprite, and beer samples that spiked tetracycline with concentration of 20, 75, 125, and 225 µM, respectively. Then 100 µL spiked samples were added into mixture solution prepared in Sect. 2.3 and the fluorescence spectra of the mixture were recorded. Finally, the recoveries and relative standard deviation (RSD) of the detection for tetracycline in river, sprite, and beer the matrix were obtained by calculating the measured concentrations of tetracycline.

Results and discussion

Fluorogenic reactions between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol

As a kind of typical fluorogenic reactions, catechol derivatives and resorcinol were used as the fluorescence probe for tyrosinase37,38, dopamine39, heavy metal ions40, etc. Recently, we find that fluorogenic reactions between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol would take place in the aqueous solution and the mixture would yield strong blue emission at the excitation light wavelength of 416 nm. In this study, we carried out several experiments to investigate the influences of reaction time, pH, sodium carbonate concentrations, 4-fluororesorcinol concentrations, and dopamine concentrations. First of all, we explored the influence of the reaction time on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol, as shown in Fig. 2a, the emission value at 461 nm and the fluorescence (FL) intensity of the mixture solution enhanced rapidly with the reaction time from 1 min to 30 min. When the reaction time was longer than 30 min, the rate at which fluorescence intensity increased is relatively slow. Based on these results, we conclude that the optimal reaction time on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol. Furthermore, the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm for different reaction time are shown in Fig. 2b, the slope of these data points before 30 min is significantly higher than those after 30 min, which is consistent with the results of Fig. 2a. Moreover, the standard deviations of four measurements were much larger after 30 min. So, we established the optimal reaction time as 30 min.

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra and (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol under different reaction time. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

The pH of BR buffer will affect the product of the fluorescence reaction considerably. As depicted in Fig. 3a, with the increase of pH of BR buffer from 3 to 12, the fluorescence intensity of the mixture solution enhanced first and then reduced, while the maximum fluorescence intensity was obtained when the pH of BR buffer equals 7, as shown in Fig. 3b. In fact, there was no change of the shape of the fluorescence spectra while pH ranges from 3 to 12, reflecting the formation of the same and unitary product. Moreover, the pH values of the mixture when BR buffer and sodium carbonate solution are mixed were recorded, as shown in Figure S2 in the supplementary information, the pH value of the mixture is 10.8 under optimal reaction conditions.

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra and (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol under different pH of BR buffer. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

As reported in the literature30–32, the fluorogenic reactions between catechol derivatives and resorcinol are under alkaline conditions. Thus, sodium carbonate is essential in the fluorogenic reactions between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol in our study. As shown in Fig. 4, the fluorescence intensity of the mixture solution enhances gradually with the increasing sodium carbonate concentration when sodium carbonate concentration is smaller than 1500 mM, but increased slowly when concentration is larger than 1500 mM. Moreover, comparing with Figs. 2 and 3, the addition of sodium carbonate in Fig. 4 indeed promotes the reactions between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol and resulted in stronger fluorescence intensity at 461 nm. Considering the fluorescence intensity and the solubility of sodium carbonate, we take the optimal concentration of sodium carbonate as 1500 mM.

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra and (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol under different sodium carbonate concentrations. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

Then, we investigated the optimal concentration of 4-fluororesorcinol. As shown in Fig. 5, the fluorescence intensity of the mixture solution enhanced promptly with the increasing 4-fluororesorcinol concentration before the concentration reaching 150 µM. However, further increasing 4-fluororesorcinol concentration hardly had effects and the fluorescence intensity of the product increased gently or kept constant after the 4-fluororesorcinol concentration larger than 150 µM. On the basis of these results, the optimal concentration of 4-fluororesorcinol should be 150 µM.

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra and (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol under different 4-fluororesorcinol concentrations. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

Last but not least, the optimal concentration of dopamine was investigated. As shown in Fig. 6, the fluorescence intensity increases rapidly before the dopamine concentration reaches 100 µM and then increases slowly with increasing dopamine concentration. Therefore, we commented the optimal concentration of dopamine in the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol should be 100 µM.

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra and (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol under different concentrations of dopamine. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

Fluorescent assays for tetracycline detection

Based on the above experimental results, the proposed fluorogenic and chromogenic reaction for TC detection was implemented with 1300 µL ultrapure water,100 µL pH 7 BR buffer, 100 µL 1500 mM sodium carbonate solution, 200 µL 150 µM 4-fluororesorcinol solution and 200 µL 100 µM dopamine aqueous solution. For TC detection, 100 µL TC solution with different concentration was mixed with the above solutions and incubated at room temperature for 30 min. Then, the fluorescence emission spectra were recorded using an easy operation procedure.

Under the above optimal conditions, a fluorometric detection for TC was performed. In Fig. 7a, it could be observed that the fluorescence intensity of the spectra at 461 nm decreased with the increase of TC concentration from 10 µM to 350 µM. Besides, a good linear relationship between the fluorescence intensity and the logarithmic value of TC concentration is obtained, as depicted in Fig. 7b. In our fluorescent assays for TC detection, the effective detection of TC concentration ranges 10–350 µM and the limit of detection (LOD) is obtained 1.7 µM (3σ/k, shown in Table S1 in supplementary information).

(a) Fluorescence emission spectra under different concentrations of tetracyclines, (b) the fluorescence intensity at 461 nm on the fluorogenic reaction against the logarithmic value of tetracycline concentration. The inset in (a) shows the photographs of the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol containing different concentrations of tetracyclines. All error bars represent standard deviations of four measurements.

The accuracy of the fluorescent assay detecting TC in river, Sprite, and beer samples was evaluated by recovery rate and data reproducibility. As shown in Table 1, the recoveries ranged 95–105%, the RSD of the detection for tetracycline in river is less than 2% and the RSD for that in Sprite and beer is less than 9%. These results indicate the validity of our fluorescent assay.

Selectivity, rapidity, and stability for tetracycline detection

To investigate the selectivity of the proposed method, several interfering substances were selected to study their effects on the fluorogenic reaction between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol. As shown in Fig. 8a, the fluorescence intensity of dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol fluorogenic reactions which were added tetracycline are much lower than those added other interferences (artemisinin, artesunate, dihydroartemisinin, thiourea, citric acid, glucose, kanamycin). It is worth mentioning that the concentration of other interferences is 500 µM, which is much larger than the upper detection limit of the TC. In addition, the fluorescence intensity of the fluorogenic reaction added the mixture of TC and these interferences (i.e. TC and artemisinin, TC and artesunate, etc.) were measured, as shown in Fig. 8b, the existence of these interferences hardly influenced the fluorescence intensity. These results proved the interferences would not affect the detection TC and the fluorescent assay for TC detection is highly selective.

Compared to traditional methods, our developed fluorescent probe TC detection method is based only on common and inexpensive reagents such as dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol. The process of TC detection is low-cost, and the reagents are easy to store, avoiding the use of expensive and difficult-to-store antibodies. In addition, our detection method is simple and rapid, avoiding the use of large instruments, thus providing a potentially promising solution for the immediate and on-site detection of TC.

Conclusion

In summary, we provided a high-efficiency and selective method for the detection of TC in environment and food. It is meticulously demonstrated that the fluorescent reactions between dopamine and 4-fluororesorcinol can be carried out and the product emits fluorescence at 461 nm under excitation light of 416 nm in aqueous solution at ambient temperature and pressure. The optimal fluorescent reaction conditions were obtained by several contrast experiments. Moreover, we find TC can quench fluorescence and prove a good linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and TC concentration. Thus, a fluorescent strategy for TC assay was established with effective TC detection concentration ranges from 10 µM to 350 µM and LOD of 1.7 µM. Compared with previous fluorescence methods, the proposed platform showed a wide sensing range, short detection time, and high selectivity. Furthermore, the chemicals we use are cheap, easy to store, and non-toxic or hypotoxic. On the basis of the detecting results of TC in river, Sprite, and beer, we envision that the fluorescent reactions become a versatile tool for assaying TCs in the environment and food in the near future.

N-butylphthalide (NBP) and ligustrazine (TMP) triazole hybrids target the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway to inhibit ferroptosis and exert brain neuroprotectivity
Authors: Guangyu Li, Haiyan Xiao, Chenwei Zuo, Haochen Xie, Xiaolin Wang, Jiaxin Wang, Ying Liu, Quanxing Hou, Guibo Sun, Yu Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ischemic stroke is a life-threatening disease, its pathological progression involves multiple factors, including oxidative stress, apoptosis, and ferroptosis. Our previous study demonstrated that hybridizing N-butylphthalide (NBP) with ligustrazine (TMP) yielded promising anti-ischemic compounds. In this study, we further introduced a triazole structure into NBP-TMP hybrids and synthesized 20 novel compounds. Their neuroprotective activities were evaluated on OGD/R induced SH-SY5Y cells and primary hippocampal neurons, leading to the identification of preferred compounds 8a, 8b and 8d at the concentration of 6.25 μM, which surpassed the neuroprotective activity of the positive control NBP. Among them, 8a exhibited the highest protective activity, with a protection percentage of 75.6 %. Further mechanistic studies revealed that compounds 8a, 8b and 8d maintained intracellular redox homeostasis to resist oxidative stress and inhibit apoptosis in vitro. Specifically, compound 8a exerted neuroprotective effects by modulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway: it bound to KEAP1, enhanced NRF2 dissociation and nuclear translocation, facilitated the generation of downstream antioxidant factors, thereby reducing intracellular ‌reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels and effectively protecting neuronal mitochondria. Finally, in vivo experiments demonstrated that compound 8a (20 mg/kg) significantly ameliorated cerebral injury in rats with ischemia-reperfusion injury. Furthermore, it reduced cerebral oxidative stress by modulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway and inhibited neuronal apoptosis and ferroptosis in the brain, which is consistent with the results in vitro. In conclusion, our results indicated that 8a serves as a promising candidate for stroke treatment and may facilitate the development of future anti-ischemic drugs.

Introduction

Stroke is an acute focal neurological deficit disorder with common symptoms, including paresis, dysarthria, sensory disturbances, aphasia, and visual impairment [1]. Ischemic stroke caused by arterial occlusion accounts for 60–70 % of all strokes, and its pathological process involves multiple factors, including oxidative stress, inflammation, apoptosis, blood-brain barrier damage and ferroptosis [[2], [3], [4]]. Under normal physiological conditions, the body maintains a dynamic ‌reactive oxygen species‌ (ROS) balance. However, during cerebral ischemia, the abnormal accumulation of ROS disrupts the mitochondrial electron transport chain, thereby promoting inflammatory responses and apoptosis and further exacerbating oxidative stress [2,5]. Moreover, ATP is rapidly depleted due to impaired mitochondrial function, leading to an imbalance in the amino acid antioxidant system, abnormal increases in brain iron levels, and lipid peroxidation, inducing neuronal ferroptosis in the brain [3,4].

The Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1)-nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2) pathway is the primary regulatory system responsible for maintaining intracellular redox homeostasis [6]. Under normoxic conditions, KEAP1 acts as an adaptor for the CUL3-dependent E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, recognizing and binding to NRF2, promoting its degradation via the 26S proteasome and maintaining stable cellular levels of NRF2. Upon exposure to oxidative or electrophilic stress, specific cysteine residues on KEAP1 are modified, leading to conformational changes in KEAP1 and inhibiting the proteasomal degradation of NRF2. Consequently, newly synthesized NRF2 dissociates from KEAP1, translocates into the nucleus, binds to antioxidant response elements (AREs)/electrophile response elements (EpREs), and activates the expression of a series of downstream antioxidant factors to resist oxidative stress [[7], [8], [9]].

The KEAP1-NRF2 pathway serves as the central molecular hub regulating the cellular oxidative stress response, exhibiting a significant dual pathological role in non-cancerous diseases and malignant tumors. Specifically, in cancer cells, mutations in the KEAP1 gene disrupt the KEAP1-NRF2 interaction, leading to continuous activation of NRF2. This hyperactivated NRF2 enables cancer cells to alter their metabolism, enhancing metabolic reprogramming to support rapid proliferation and invasiveness. Furthermore, activated NRF2 augments cancer cell resistance to chemotherapeutic and radiotherapeutic agents and enhances anti-apoptotic capabilities [10,11]. Conversely, in the context of non-cancerous diseases (such as neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular disease and metabolic diseases), activation of the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway induces the expression of downstream antioxidant genes (such as HO-1 and NQO1). This adaptive activation effectively maintains redox homeostasis, suppresses the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and inflammatory cascades, thereby mitigating oxidative damage in the organism [12,13]. In summary, transient activation of NRF2 in non-cancer diseases maintains dynamic redox balance, while its persistent activation in cancer creates a highly reductive microenvironment. This not only protects cancer cells from oxidative damage but also provides metabolic support for biosynthesis. Given these contrasting pathological roles, therapeutic strategies should be opposed. For malignancies, the development of NRF2 inhibitors (such as ML385) is essential to suppress NRF2 hyperactivation and counteract excessive cancer cell proliferation. Natural compounds such as Diosmetin have been found to inhibit NRF2 function, thereby suppressing the proliferation and migration of ovarian cancer cells [14,15]. Conversely, for non-cancerous diseases, the focus should be on identifying KEAP1 inhibitors, NRF2 agonists or natural compounds targeting the KEAP1-NRF2 interaction to enhance transient NRF2 activation and alleviate cellular oxidative stress. Natural compounds like Quercetin have been shown to modulate the KEAP1-NRF2 interaction, facilitating NRF2 release and exerting antioxidant effects. This mechanism plays an important role in various inflammatory diseases, including periodontitis [16,17]. Similarly, by modulating the interaction between NRF2 and KEAP1, the antioxidant response is activated, oxidative stress and inflammatory damage are alleviated, which is also of significant importance in the prevention and treatment of ischemic stroke [18].

As a marketed neuroprotective drug, in recent years, N-butylphthalide (NBP) has been found to enhance the activity of antioxidant enzymes via the NRF2-ARE pathway, alleviating brain damage in traumatic brain injury models [19]. In our previous study, we fused NBP with another neuroprotective small-molecule fragment ligustrazine (TMP), using a molecular hybridization strategy employing an ester bond. The hybrid 7m, exhibited superior neuroprotective activity compared with the parent compounds [20]. Consulting the literature revealed that the triazole structure is a key structure for binding to KEAP1 [[21], [22], [23], [24]]. Therefore, in this study, we continued our previous strategy by fusing NBP with TMP. However, instead of an ester bond, we introduced the triazole fragment to improve the selectivity of the new compounds for the KEAP1-NRF2 protein complex.

Following this strategy, we designed and synthesized 20 novel compounds. Evaluation of their neuroprotective effects revealed that the optimized compounds 8a, 8b and 8d significantly ameliorated apoptosis and mitochondrial damage in neurons induced by ischemia-reperfusion. Among them, compound 8a activated the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway both in vitro and in vivo, counteracting oxidative stress, attenuating apoptosis and ferroptosis, and ultimately providing cerebral neuroprotection. Therefore, 8a can be advanced as a neuroprotective drug candidate for further development and investigation in the treatment of acute ischemic stroke following reperfusion.

Materials and methods

Chemistry

Solvents and reagents were purchased from commercial sources and used as received, unless otherwise indicated. All of the chemical reagents used for hybrid synthesis and their CAS numbers are listed in a table and placed in the supplementary document (Supplementary Table 1). NBP and TMP (Target Mol. USA). 1H NMR and 13C APT spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance III 600 MHz spectrometer. HRMS was performed on a Thermo Fisher LTQ Orbitrap XL. All 1H NMR spectra were relative to the residual solvent peaks of CDCl3 (7.26 ppm). All 13C APT spectra are reported in ppm relative to residual CDCl3 (77.16 ppm). The coupling constants are reported in Hertz (Hz) with multiplicities denoted as s (singlet), d (doublet), t (triplet), q (quartet), and m (multiplet). The reactions were monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on commercial silica gel plates (GF254) under UV light. Flash chromatography was performed using silica gel (200–300 mesh).

Synthesis of compounds 1, 2, 3a-3d, 4a-4d and 5a-5d

The procedure to synthesize compounds 1, 2, 3a-3d, 4a-4d and 5a-5d were shown in our previous study [12].

Synthesis of compounds 6a-6d

5a-5d (7.0 mmol, 1 eq.), propargylamine (8.4 mmol, 1.2 eq.), TBTU (8.4 mmol, 1.2 eq.) and DIPEA (14.0 mmol, 2 eq.) was dissolved in dichloromethane and stirred at room temperature for 6 h. After the reaction completed, the crude product was filtered and concentrated, and purified with silica gel column chromatography (PE:EA = 10:1, v/v) to obtain compounds 6a-6d.

Synthesis of 2-(bromomethyl)-3,5,6-trimethylpyrazine (TMP-1)

TMP (36.7 mmol, 1 eq.), NBS (12.2 mmol, 0.3 eq.), and BPO (3.7 mmol, 0.1 eq.) was dissolved in CCl4 sequentially. The mixture was refluxed at 80 °C for 4 h. After suction filtration, the crude product was purified by column chromatography (PE:EA = 4:1, v/v) to obtain TMP-1 as yellow solid with a yield of 21 %.

Synthesis of 2-(azidomethyl)-3,5,6-trimethylpyrazine (TMP-2)

TMP-1 (5.6 mmol, 1 eq.), NaN3 (6.7 mmol, 1.2 eq.) was dissolved in CH3CN. The mixture was microwaved at 60 °C for 1 h. After suction filtration, the crude product was to obtain TMP-2 as yellow solid with a yield of 80 %.

Synthesis of compounds 7a-7d

6a-6d (6.1 mmol, 1 eq.), TMP-2 (6.1 mmol, 1 eq.) and CuTC (12.1 mmol, 0.2 eq.) was dissolved in 5 mL of dichloromethane and stirred at room temperature for 2 h. After the reaction was completed, the crude product was filtered and concentrated, and purified with silica gel column chromatography (DCM:MeOH = 15:1, v/v) to obtain compounds 7a-7d.

Synthesis of compounds 8a-8t

7a-7d (1.6 mmol, 1 eq.), was dissolved in 3 mL tetrahydrofuran. Cesium carbonate (3.8 mmol, 1.5 eq.) and amines (3.8 mmol, 1.5 eq.) was successively added in the solution and the mixture was heated at 80 °C for 2 h. After the reaction was completed, the crude product was filtered and concentrated, and purified with silica gel column chromatography (PE:EA = 1:4, v/v) to obtain 8a-8t.

Biological assays

Cell culture and drug preparation

The human neuroblastoma cell line SH-SY5Y was procured from the Institute of Basic Medical Sciences, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Beijing, China). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (6124448, DMEM; Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (2393124RP, FBS; Gibco, USA), 2 mmol/L glutamine, penicillin (P1400, 100 U/mL), and streptomycin (P1400, 100 μg/mL), under humidified conditions at 37 °C and 5 % CO2. All test compounds, along with the positive controls NBP and ML385 (both from MCE), were dissolved in DMSO to prepare 5 mM stock solutions. Working solutions at the indicated concentrations were diluted fresh prior to each experiment.

Oxygen-glucose deprivation/re-oxygenation (OGD/R)

OGD/R was conducted with SH-SY5Y cells grown to mimic cerebral injury in vitro. The technique was conducted according to previously described [25]. Briefly, SH-SY5Y cells cultured in FBS-free medium under hypoxia for 3.5 h and then removed from the anaerobic chamber (TYPE c; Coy Laboratory Products, Inc., Grass Lake, MI) to a normoxic environment with the medium replaced by normal medium and were allowed to re-oxygenate for 12 h.

Cell viability assay

The neuroprotective effects of test compounds and NBP were evaluated through cell viability assessment in SH-SY5Y cells subjected to OGD/R injury. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CK04, CCK-8, Dojindo) assays were performed according to the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, cells were plated in 96-well plates at optimized density. Following 24 h culture, test compounds or NBP were administered at graded concentrations for 4 h prior to OGD/R induction. Control and OGD/R groups received FBS-free medium for normal maintenance or model establishment, respectively. Subsequently, 10 μL CCK-8 reagent was added per well and incubated for 1 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader to calculate cell viability.

Detection of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity

Following OGD/R exposure, cellular supernatants were collected and assayed for lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release using a commercial cytotoxicity detection kit (A020-2-2, Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China), strictly adhering to the manufacturer's protocol. Absorbance at 450 nm was recorded for each well using a microplate reader. LDH leakage in experimental groups was expressed as a relative percentage, with the control group's value normalized to 100 %.

Cell apoptosis and necrosis assay

Apoptotic and necrotic cells were quantified using a Hoechst 33342/PI dual-staining kit (CA1120, Solarbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, post-treatment SH-SY5Y cells were incubated sequentially with cell staining buffer, Hoechst 33342, and propidium iodide (PI) at 4 °C for 30 min. Following two washes with DMEM, cellular morphology was examined using an EVOS M5000 fluorescence microscope (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Measurement of oxidative stress levels

Intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation was assessed in vitro using the 2',7'-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) assay (S0033 M, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) per manufacturer's protocol. Following treatments, cells were incubated with 10 μM DCFH-DA staining solution at 37 °C for 30 min in darkness. After three washes with DMEM, fluorescence intensity was quantified using an EVOS M5000 fluorescence microscope (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Separately, malondialdehyde (MDA, A003-4-1) content, reduced glutathione (GSH, A006-2-1) levels, and catalase (CAT, A007-1-1) and superoxide dismutase (SOD, A001-3-2) activities in both cellular homogenates and serum samples from rats were measured using commercial assay kits (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to supplier specifications.

Immunofluorescence staining

To examine NRF2 subcellular localization, SH-SY5Y cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary anti-NRF2 antibody (rabbit monoclonal, 1:200 dilution; ABclonal, A25327). Subsequently, samples were treated with goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:500; ABclonal, AS007) for 1 h at ambient temperature (22–25 °C). Following secondary incubation, nuclei were counterstained using DAPI-containing antifade mounting medium (P0131-25 mL, Beyotime, China). Fluorescence signals were captured and quantified using an EVOS M5000 imaging system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) assay

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was determined using a JC-1 assay kit (C2006, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) following manufacturer protocols. Briefly, SH-SY5Y cells underwent two washes with serum-free DMEM prior to incubation with 200 μM JC-1 staining solution at 37 °C for 20 min. Unbound dye was subsequently removed by DMEM washing. Fluorescence images were acquired using an EVOS M5000 imaging system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), with MMP quantified by calculating the red-to-green fluorescence intensity ratio.

Lipid peroxides (LPO) assay

Cellular lipid peroxide (LPO) levels were quantified using a commercial Liperfluo assay kit (L248, Dojindo, Japan) following manufacturer protocols. Briefly, SH-SY5Y cells were rinsed once with FBS-free DMEM, followed by incubation with Liperfluo probe at 37 °C for 30 min. Unbound probe was subsequently removed via DMEM wash. Fluorescence microscopy (EVOS M5000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was employed to acquire LPO visualization images on SH-SY5Y cells.

Western blotting was performed as previously reported [26]. Protein lysates were extracted from treated SH-SY5Y cells and rat tissues using lysis buffer (R0010, Solarbio, Beijing, China) supplemented with protease/phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (1:100). Primary hippocampal neuronal cultures and treatment

Primary hippocampal neurons were isolated from embryonic day 16–18 Sprague Dawley rats using established methodology [27]. Briefly, fetal hippocampi were dissected and meninges meticulously removed. Dissociated cells (6 × 105 cells/mL) were cultured in poly-d-lysine-coated plates (P6407-5 MG, Sigma, St. Louis, MO) with DMEM containing 20 % FBS. After 4 h adhesion, medium was replaced with Neurobasal-A (10888022, Gibco) supplemented with B27 (2 %) (12587010, Gibco), glutamine (0.5 mM) (1294808, Sigma, St. Louis, MO), and penicillin-streptomycin (100 U/mL-100 μg/mL) (P1400, Solarbio, Beijing, China), maintained at 37 °C/5 % CO2 with medium renewal every 72 h. For viability assessment, neurons (3 × 105 cells/mL) were seeded in 96-well plates. Following compound treatment and OGD/R exposure, 10 μL CCK-8 reagent was added per well. After 1 h incubation (37 °C/5 % CO2), absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader.

Molecular docking

In order to explore the potential binding of 8a with KEAP1 (PDB ID: 2FLU), a molecular modeling study was conducted using the docking protocol to induce Fit in MOE software. Ligand 8a underwent "energy minimization" prior to docking to eliminate any bond length and Angle deviations. Binding affinity (s value) in MOE was used to evaluate the interaction between KEAP1 and 8a. The score (binding affinity) is obtained based on virtual calculations of various interactions between the ligand and the target receptor. The docking diagram of 8a and KEAP1 was drawn by MOE software.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Based on the crystal structure of KEAP1 (PDB ID: 2FLU), we docked 8a to KEAP1. After relaxing the binary structure (KEAP1/8a) to the lowest energy conformation, we captured the KEAP1/8a complex by MD simulation. The 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation of the KEAP1/8a complex was performed using Schrodinger 21-2.

Surface plasmon resonance(SPR)

The interaction of compound 8a with KEAP1 protein was detected by molecular interaction analyzer (PALL FORTEBIO, USA). KEAP1 protein was immobilized on a PCH sensor chip (Octet) and pre-activated for 600 s using an EDC/NHS mixture at a flow rate of 10 μL/min. Dilute 8a to 0, 0.39, 0.78, 1.57, 3.13, 6.25, 12.5 μM with PBST buffers containing 5 % DMSO and 0.005 % Tween. The binding time was 400 s, and the flow rate was 15 μL/min. The dissociation time was 60 s, and the affinity constant KD value was obtained by computer fitting and steady-state analysis.

Cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA)

CETSA was performed in accordance with a previous study [28]. Following lysis in kinase buffer and centrifugation (12,000×g, 20 min), SH-SY5Y cell supernatants were collected. Lysates were allocated to two treatment groups: DMSO control and 50 μM 8a. Each group was subdivided into six aliquots for thermal exposure (37, 44, 51, 58, 65, 72 °C; 3 min). Samples were immediately chilled on ice and centrifuged (12,000×g, 10 min, 4 °C). Supernatants were mixed with Laemmli buffer and subjected to immunoblot analysis.

Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay

The Drug Affinity Responsive Target Stability (DARTS) assay was conducted per established methods [29]. SH-SY5Y cells were prepared using NP-40 lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors, followed by centrifugation (12,000×g, 20 min). Supernatants were supplemented with TNC buffer and allocated to six experimental groups. Control group received vehicle treatment, while treatment groups received 8a at concentrations of 0, 25, 50, 100 and 200 μM (n = 6 groups). Following 1 h room temperature incubation, pronase was added to all samples. Reactions were terminated with Laemmli buffer after 30 min prior to immunoblot analysis.

Middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion surgery (MCAO/R) and drug administration

Male Sprague-Dawley rats (250–280 g, Charles River, Beijing) were utilized in this study under protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences & Peking Union Medical College. Animals were housed in ventilated cages maintained at 20–25 °C with 30–50 % relative humidity under 12 h light/dark cycles, with ad libitum access to food and water. Focal cerebral ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury was induced via middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion (MCAO/R) as previously described [30]. Prior to surgery, rats were anesthetized with intraperitoneal xylazine (10 mg/kg) and ketamine (80 mg/kg). Following MCAO/R induction, surgical sites were disinfected with iodine and closed with sterile sutures. Sham-operated controls underwent identical procedures without arterial occlusion. Core body temperature was maintained at 37 ± 0.5 °C throughout procedures using a heating pad (Sunbeam, USA). Compound 8a was dissolved in saline for intragastric administration. Postoperatively, rats received either 8a (5, 10, or 20 mg/kg), positive control NBP (20 mg/kg; CSPC NBP Pharmaceutical, Shijiazhuang), or equivalent saline volume. Daily dosing continued for 7 consecutive days.

Scoring of neurological deficits

Rats subjected to MCAO/R and pharmacological interventions (n = 6 per group per time point) underwent neurobehavioral assessment on postoperative days 1, 3, 5, and 7. Neurological function was evaluated using the modified Neurological Severity Score (mNSS) [31], a validated scale for quantifying post-ischemic deficits in rodent models. This 18-point composite measure exhibits positive correlation between elevated scores and neurological impairment severity.

Cerebral blood flow (CBF) measurement

Seven days post-MCAO/R, anesthetized rats underwent cranial preparation for cerebral blood flow measurement. Following alcohol disinfection of the scalp, hair was surgically removed to expose the cranial surface. Cortical perfusion was quantified using laser Doppler flowmetry (moor FLPI-2, UK; n = 6 per group).

Triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining

Seven days after MCAO/R, rats were anesthetized and then sacrificed via decapitation to preserve the integrity of their brains. Brain tissues were stained with TTC following previously reported protocols, with 6 rats in each group [32]. After staining, viable tissues appeared rose-red, while infarcted areas remained unstained and white. The specimens were subsequently taken out and fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 24 h, then placed on a light-colored surgical table and photographed using a digital camera. The infarct area percentage was calculated using the formula: brain infarct size = (total white infarct area across all sections)/(total area of all brain slices) × 100 %.

Histopathology and TdT-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (tunel) staining

Histopathological staining (conducted 7 days after reperfusion) followed the previously described procedures [33]. The fixed brain tissues were embedded in paraffin. For histopathological assessment, the paraffin-embedded brains were coronally sectioned into slices 5 μm in thickness, and TUNEL staining was performed to detect apoptosis in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions (C1088, Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

Statistical analysis

Each experiment in this study was independently repeated at least three times. Unless otherwise specified, all quantitative data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 21, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc test (TTEST) was used to determine the statistical significance of differences between groups, with p < 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

Results and discussions

Synthesis of NBP-TMP-triazole hybrids

Synthesis of compounds 1, 2, 3a-3d, 4a-4d, 5a-5d and TMP-1 were shown in our previous study [20].

The intermediates 6a-6d were obtained through the condensation of 5a-5d with propargylamine under the condition of O-(Benzotriazol-1-yl)-N, N, N′, N′-tetramethyluronium tetrafluoroborate (TBTU) and N, N-diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA). At the same time, TMP-1 was dissolved together with NaN3 in acetonitrile and heated by microwave at 60 °C for 1 h to afford the key intermediate TMP-2 via nucleophilic substitution. After that, TMP-2 was subjected to click chemistry reaction with intermediates 6a-6d in the presence of Copper (I) thiophene-2-carboxylate (CuTC) in dichloromethane to afford the compounds 7a-7d. Finally, the target NBP-TMP-triazole hybrids 8a-8t were prepared by a substitution reaction in which TMP-2 and cesium carbonate were respectively reacted with morpholine, methylpiperazine, diethylamine, dimethylamine and piperazine in tetrahydrofuran at 80 °C for 2 h. These products required careful purification, and yields were from 18 % to 66 %. All new compounds were identified by 1H NMR, 13C-APT and HRMS spectroscopy. The corresponding data and spectra are presented in the supplementary materials (Supplementary Figs. 1–80).

Neuroprotection screening of compounds 8a, 8b and 8d on OGD/R induced primary hippocampal neurons

The hippocampus, a critical component of the brain, is highly sensitive to ischemia and hypoxia, and its primary neurons are widely used as neuroprotective cell model [35,36]. Therefore, we employed primary hippocampal neurons from Sprague Dawley (SD) rats within 24 h of birth, subjected them to OGD/R treatment to mimic the in vivo state of cerebral ischemia-hypoxia, assessed neuronal injury by measuring cell viability. At the concentration of 6.25 μM, neither 8a, 8b, 8d, the parent compound NBP, TMP, nor our previously reported ester-linked NBP-TMP hybrid 7m showed overt cytotoxicity toward primary hippocampal neurons (Fig. 2C). Subsequent to OGD/R treatment, neuronal viability exhibited a substantial decline, whereas pretreatment with each of the six compounds resulted in a significant enhancement in survival. Notably, the newly synthesized compounds 8a, 8b, and 8d conferred stronger protection than the parent compounds NBP and TMP, as well as the ester-linked hybrid 7m (Fig. 2D), indicating that the triazole based hybridization strategy is promising.

Structure-activity relationships (SAR) analysis of NBP-TMP hybrid compounds

Initial SAR evaluation of compounds 8a–8t revealed that incorporating both the TMP and triazole moieties potentiated the activity of NBP derivatives, aligning with our proposed hypothesis. Moreover, substituent R1 on the NBP phenyl ring and amine fragments R2 possessing diverse polarities significantly modulated the activity of the NBP-TMP-triazole hybrids, wherein R1 exerted a more pronounced influence than R2. Specifically: (1) The influence of NBP benzene ring substituent R1: The NBP-TMP-triazole complex without R1 substitution exhibited optimal neuroprotective efficacy, such as 8a-8e. Conversely, introducing halogen atoms reduced neuroprotective activity, presumably because halogen substituents increased steric hindrance between the compounds and the target binding site, resulting in a decrease in the binding of the compound to the target and thereby affecting the activity. (2) The influence of amine fragment R2: When the benzene ring remained unsubstituted, piperazine or diethylamine substitutions retained activity, but this activity was inferior to that of NBP, such as 8c and 8e. However, introducing dimethylamine methylpiperazine or morpholine significantly enhanced the activity of the hybrids, surpassing the positive drug NBP, such as 8a, 8b and 8d. Among them, the morpholine substitution compound 8a exhibited the strongest activity. This might attribute to the oxygen atom in the morpholine ring providing moderate water solubility and forming hydrogen bonding interactions with amino acid residues at the target site, thereby strengthening the binding.

8a, 8b, and 8d alleviated the oxidative stress and apoptosis induced by OGD/R in vitro

Oxidative stress is modulated by multiple factors, including excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and calcium overload. When ROS are overproduced and not efficiently neutralized, the intracellular redox equilibrium is disturbed, which may lead to neuronal injury or even apoptosis [37,38]. Furthermore, oxidative stress promotes the accumulation of lipid peroxidation byproducts, such as malondialdehyde (MDA), which can be detoxified by antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) [39]. Therefore, we assessed these three biomarkers using commercially available assays. The results indicated that following OGD/R treatment, cellular density decreased significantly, and the levels of intracellular ROS and MDA were markedly elevated compared with those in the control group. However, pretreatment with 8a, 8b and 8d significantly attenuated ROS and MDA accumulation. Oxidative stress is a significant catalyst for the initiation of apoptosis. Research indicates that accumulation of ROS disrupts cellular redox homeostasis, thereby activating multiple apoptotic signaling pathways and ultimately leading to cell death [38]. It is activated during the late stages of apoptosis, inducing a range of morphological and functional changes by cleaving various cellular substrates and ultimately leading to cell death [40]. Bcl-2 functions as an anti-apoptotic protein, whereas Bax promotes apoptosis, both regulate the apoptotic process through the formation of homo- or heterodimers [41,42]. 8a enhanced cellular anti-oxidative effects through activation of the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway

The KEAP1-NRF2 pathway plays a pivotal role in defending against injury caused by oxidative stress [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. When oxidative stress is present, the KEAP1-NRF2 complex dissociates, resulting in the release of NRF2 from the cytoplasm into the nucleus. Once there, it binds to antioxidant response elements (AREs), thereby activating the expression of protective proteins, such as heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and NAD(P)H: quinone oxidoreductase-1 (NQO1). This process protects cells from damage caused by ROS and prevents free radical injury. Immunofluorescent staining of NRF2 revealed that, under normal conditions, NRF2 was found in the cytoplasm, however, following OGD/R injury, it shifted into the nucleus. This nuclear accumulation was significantly enhanced by pretreatment with 8a, 8b, 8d or the positive control NBP (Fig. 4A). Western blotting analysis further revealed that these pretreatments decreased KEAP1 expression while significantly increasing the levels of NRF2 and its downstream antioxidants HO-1 and NQO1 (Fig. 4B and C). These results indicate that these compounds likely counteract oxidative stress injury by modulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway. Collectively, these data demonstrate that 8a provides stronger protection against OGD/R-induced neuronal injury than 8b or 8d. To validate the docking and MD results, surface plasmon resonance (SPR) experiments were conducted to assess the binding of 8a to KEAP1 in vitro. The results demonstrated that 8a binds to KEAP1 in a concentration-dependent manner, with an affinity (KD) of 561 nM for the target protein (Fig. 6F). When a target protein binds to a small molecule, its conformation shifts toward a lower-energy, higher-stability state, enhancing its resistance to heat and proteolysis [50,51]. Consequently, CETSA and DARTS assays were conducted to investigate the thermal and proteolytic stability of KEAP1 following treatment with 8a. These assays demonstrated that treatment with 8a significantly enhanced the stability of KEAP1 compared to the control group (Fig. 6G–J). Collective evidence from these three experiments suggests that compound 8a forms a stable complex with KEAP1. These findings further corroborate the hypothesis that 8a protects cells from oxidative stress injury by activating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway.

8a ameliorated cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury in vivo

The rat MCAO/R model is a focal cerebral ischemia model and is currently the most widely used cerebral ischemia model [52]. The Modified Neurological Severity Score (mNSS) is a comprehensive scoring system for assessing the degree of neurological deficits in experimental animals, particularly rats, and is widely used in the study of stroke, traumatic brain injury, and other neurological injury research. This scoring system assesses the performance of neurological function by evaluating several aspects of motor, sensory, balance, and reflexes, with a total score of 18, in which 0 indicates normal and 18 indicates severe deficits [53]. To further investigate the neuroprotective effects of compound 8a in vivo, we established a rat cerebral ischemia model using MCAO/R surgery and administered the drug for seven consecutive days after the surgery. To confirm the neuroprotective effect of 8a on MCAO/R injury in rats, we examined cerebral infarcts in the cortical and subcortical regions using TTC staining. Compared with that in the sham-operated group, the volume of cerebral infarcts was significantly increased in the MCAO/R group, indicating successful ischemia-reperfusion modeling. After 7 days of continuous administration of 8a or NBP, the volume of cerebral infarction was significantly reduced in rats, and the volume of cerebral infarction in the compound 8a-treated group was lower than that in the positive control group treated with NBP at the dose of 20 mg/kg (Fig. 7C and D, Supplementary Fig. 83). In addition, we examined the cerebral blood flow in rats using a Doppler flow detector, and found that the cerebral blood flow was significantly decreased in the MCAO/R group compared with the sham group, whereas the cerebral blood flow was significantly increased in the rats after the administration of 8a and NBP treatments (Fig. 7E and F). In addition, Tunel staining of rat brain tissue sections revealed that treatment with 8a significantly reduced neuronal apoptosis in the brain compared to that in the MCAO/R group, and the inhibition of neuronal apoptosis was superior to that of positive control NBP (Fig. 7G and H).

We examined the activities of SOD, MDA, GSH and CAT in the serum of MCAO/R rats from different treatment groups. In addition, we examined the expression levels of KEAP1, NRF2, HO-1 and NQO1 proteins in the brain tissues of MCAO/R rats. Ferroptosis is a novel mode of programmed cell death that is distinct from apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy. The core mechanism involves an imbalance between iron-dependent lipid peroxidation and the antioxidant system, induced by the inhibition of the intracellular SLC7A11-GSH-GPX4 regulatory axis during oxidative stress [54,55]. We examined the levels of SLC7A11 and GPX4 proteins in rat brain tissues, and the results showed that the expression levels of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were significantly reduced in the brain tissues of rats in the MCAO/R group compared with those in the sham group, whereas the 8a-treated group reversed the levels of GPX4 and SLC7A11 protein expression (Fig. 8G and H), suggesting that 8a inhibits ferroptosis in neurons.

Conclusion

In this study, we used NBP as the lead compound, applied the concept of hybridization drug design, fused various ring-opening derivatives replacing NBP with TMP via triazole fragment, introduced halogen or various amine groups to enhance the bioactivity of the lead compound NBP, and screened in vitro OGD/R model to obtain neuroprotective compounds 8a, 8b, and 8d. Among them, 8a inhibited cellular damage by modulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway, promoting NRF2 dissociation and nucleation, inducing the expression of downstream antioxidant factors, ameliorating OGD/R-induced mitochondrial damage and reducing ROS release, thereby inhibiting apoptosis. In vivo experiments showed that 8a also reduced neuronal apoptosis and ferroptosis by modulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway, thereby significantly inhibiting ischemia-reperfusion injury in rats and exhibiting neuroprotective effects superior to those of the positive drug NBP (Fig. 9). In conclusion, our results suggest that 8a may serve as a novel therapeutic candidate for stroke to facilitate the development of future anti-ischemic drugs.Fig. 98a exerts neuroprotective activity by regulating the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway, reducing oxidative stress, cell apoptosis and ferroptosis.

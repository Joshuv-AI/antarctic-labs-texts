Eradication efficacy of empiric bismuth quadruple therapy containing amoxicillin in the first-line treatment of Helicobacter pylori in Ningxia, China
Authors: Xiaoming Su, Xianmei Chen, Yanling Li, Yuanyuan Tang, Rui Mu, Yuting Wu, Shengjuan Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the eradication rate and safety of bismuth quadruple therapy containing amoxicillin as a first-line empiric treatment in patients with high resistance to Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) in China.MethodsThis retrospective study included 874 patients with H. pylori infection who attended the outpatient clinic of the Department of Gastroenterology of the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region from June 2021 to October 2022 and met the criteria for exclusion, including the Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Clarithromycin (EBAC) group, the Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Levofloxacin (EBAL) group, the Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Furazolidone (EBAF) group, the Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Tetracycline (EBAT) group, and the duration of treatment was 14 days. Eradication of H. pylori was determined using a repeat 14-carbon urease breath test (14 C-UBT) at weeks 4–6 after the end of treatment.ResultsIn the intention-to-treat analysis (ITT), the eradication rates of H. pylori in the EBAC, EBAL, EBAF, and EBAT groups were 73.2% (198.5/271), 69.0% ( 184.9/268), 71.8% (109.2/152), and 71.5% (130.2/183), respectively; and in the per-protocol (PP) analysis, the PP analysis eradication rates were 83.2% (203.9/245), 81.2% (193.3/238), 90.0% (124.3/138), and 82.5% (136.1/165), and the differences in the eradication rates of the four groups for ITT and PP analysis were not statistically significant (ITT: P = 0.845; PP: P = 0.470). The incidence rates of adverse event(AE) in the EBAC, EBAL, EBAF, and EBAT groups were 15.0% (40.6/271), 8.0% (21.3/268), 23.2% (35.3/152), and 24.8% (45.1/183), respectively, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.001).ConclusionThe eradication rates of four amoxicillin-containing bismuth quadruple therapy regimens in Ningxia were not significantly different. However, the EBAF regimen showed favorable trends in terms of increased eradication rate, better cost-effectiveness, and controllable adverse events, its efficacy and tolerability should be weighed, and the monitoring of drug use should be strengthened.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04761-1.Keywords: Helicobacter pylori, Quadruple therapy, Eradication rate

Background

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) is a gram-negative, helical, microaerophilic pathogen that is closely associated with the development of chronic gastritis, peptic ulcers, gastric mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma, and gastric cancer, in addition to the occurrence of some non-intestinal diseases, such as idiopathic thrombocytopenic epilepsy and iron deficiency anemia. In 1994, the World Health Organization classified H. pylori as a class I carcinogen, the only prokaryotic pathogenic microorganism classified as such [1–3]. The Kyoto Global Consensus Report on H. pylori gastritis recommended treatment for patients with H. pylori infection, regardless of clinical symptoms. Infections should be treated with eradication therapy irrespective of the presence or absence of clinical symptoms [3].

Currently, with the widespread development of H. pylori eradication therapy, as well as the diversification of eradication protocols, the widespread use of antibiotics has led to an increase in resistance to H. pylori worldwide, especially Metronidazole, Levofloxacin, Clarithromycin, and the eradication rate of bismuth quadruple therapy with an increase in antibiotic resistance and a tendency to decrease. There are even reports that bismuth quadruple therapy containing Clarithromycin or Levofloxacin has been shown to reduce the rate of H. pylori infection eradication. The eradication rate of bismuth quadruple therapy is less than 80% [4, 5], but the incidence rate of adverse effects of bismuth quadruple therapy is still high [6, 7], especially those containing furazolidone or tetracycline. Increased resistance of H. pylori to antibiotics is believed to be the main reason for the failure of eradication [8, 9]. Information on antibiotic resistance is important for guiding the treatment of H. pylori if obtained before treatment. Relevant studies have shown that treatment guided by drug resistance detection is comparable to or even better than empirical quadruple therapy [10–13], which can also achieve an eradication rate of more than 90% [14], especially in areas with high antibiotic resistance [15] or in patients who have failed previous eradication therapy [16]. However, resistance detection-guided therapy has some limitations in clinical practice, mainly because of the low detection rate of H. pylori, harsh experimental conditions for bacterial culture, long incubation period, and high cost of treatment, especially in areas with high rates of H. pylori infection and drug resistance and where healthcare resources are scarce. Due to the obvious geographical differences in the infection and drug resistance rates of H. pylori, according to national multicenter data from the China H. pylori Molecular Medicine Center Surveillance in 2021, the resistance rates to Metronidazole, Clarithromycin, and Levofloxacin are 87.8%, 37.0%, and 34.2%, respectively [17]. Surprisingly, in the face of such a severe regional resistance challenge, several current studies have reported that the resistance rate of H. pylori to amoxicillin remains low [5–7, 17]; the low incidence of adverse events (AEs) during eradication therapy and its reusability have made amoxicillin the antibiotic of choice for a wide range of therapeutic regimens. H. pylori infections and drug resistance in the Ningxia region of China in the period 2020–2022, epidemiologic screening for H. pylori infection and resistance showed resistance rates to Metronidazole, Levofloxacin, Clarithromycin, Amoxicillin, Tetracycline, and Furazolidone of 94.2%, 42.4%, 40.4%, 0.9%, 0.3%, and 0.1%, respectively [18].

Therefore, with a clear background of regional antibiotic resistance, the selection of appropriate antibiotics and a reliable treatment regimen is instructive for the first-line treatment of H. pylori. This study aimed to evaluate the eradication efficacy of empirical bismuth-containing amoxicillin as a first-line treatment for H. pylori.

Methods

Study design and subjects

This was a retrospective study in which 1024 participants who attended the outpatient clinic of the Department of Gastroenterology of the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region with upper gastrointestinal symptoms from June 2021 to October 2022 were selected to undergo the 14-carbon urease breath test (14 C-UBT), and 874 participants with a positive 14 C-UBT result met the exclusion and inclusion criteria. All participants signed an informed consent form before participating in the study. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region People’s Hospital (2022LW006), and in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.Questionnaires included questions on sex, age, smoking, alcohol consumption, education level, monthly income (RMB), living area, communal utensils, and history of antibiotic use. Definitions of lifestyle habits in influencing factors: sometimes (1–2 times per week or 1–3 times per month), regular (≥ 3 times per week or almost every day).

Inclusion criteria

Participants who were positive for H. pylori by 14 C-UBT and were initially treated, those who were in the age range of 18–75 years, and those who were not taking proton pump inhibitors (PPI), bismuth, antibiotics, or non-steroidal drugs in the 4 weeks before performing 14 C-UBT.

Exclusion criteria

Participants were excluded if they were < 18 years old, had a clear diagnosis of gastrointestinal tumors or a previous history of major gastric resection, were undergoing radiation or chemotherapy, were allergic to or intolerant of medications, were pregnant or breastfeeding, or participated in other clinical trials.

Treatment regimen and observation indexes

The dosage and frequency of the base drugs of the bismuth quadruple therapy regimen were Esomeprazole(E, Qingfeng Pharmaceutical Group) 20 mg twice daily, Bismuth(B, Potassium Bicarbonate Granules, Lizhu Pharmaceutical) agent 220 mg twice daily, Amoxicillin(A, Lu An Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) 1000 mg twice daily, and the duration of treatment was 14 days, including EBAC group: EAB+clarithromycin(C, China-US Huadong Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) 500 mg twice daily; EBAL group: EAB+levofloxacin(L, Kangpu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) 200 mg twice daily; Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Furazolidone (EBAF) group: EAB+furazolidone(F, Yunpeng Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) 100 mg twice daily; Esomeprazole-Bismuth-Amoxicillin-Tetracycline (EBAT) group: EAB+tetracycline(T, Mengxin Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) 500 mg four times daily. Esomeprazole and bismuth were orally administered 30 min before meals. Antibiotics, except tetracycline, were administered 30 min after each meal, and tetracycline was administered 2 h after each meal.

The observational indices of this study were eradication rate and the incidence of adverse events, the primary observational index was eradication rate, participants were retested for 14 C-UBT at weeks 4–6 after the end of treatment, and the 14 C-UBT result was negative suggesting that H. pylori eradication was successful, which interpreted in terms of H. pylori follow-up success rate and eradication rate (%). The Intention-to-treat (ITT) eradication rate (%) was calculated as the number of 14 C-UBT negative cases divided by the total number of participants who received H. pylori eradication treatment. The Per-Protocol (PP) eradication rate (%) was calculated as the number of 14 C-UBT negative cases divided by the total number of participants who completed H. pylori eradication treatment (compliance ≥ 80% and rechecked 14 C-UBT). The secondary observational indicator was the incidence rate of adverse events, including Nausea, Vomiting, Abdominal pain, abdominal distension, Diarrhea, Skin rash, headache, and dizziness, when participants were interviewed by telephone midway through the course or 1–3 days after the end of the course. The degree of AEs was categorized as mild (transient and well tolerated), moderate (causing uncomfortable symptoms and partially interfering with daily life), and severe (causing considerable interference with daily life) [13]. Poor compliance was defined as intake of < 80% of the total number of tablets.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using the PASS 15 software. We used a cut-off of α = 0.025 and 1 − β = 0.8, respectively. Based on ITT, the acceptable success rate is typically 80% or higher. Before initiating this study, a pilot study was conducted, and the eradication rate in the EBAF group was found to be 91%(61/67). The required sample size for each group was 157. The total sample size for the four treatment groups was 692, which eventually increased to approximately 874 as trial conditions permitted. Participants were followed up by healthcare professionals, and after providing informed consent to participate in the study, they were assigned to different treatment groups using a lottery (labeled 1, 2, 3, and 4).

Statistical analysis

The data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 23.0. Measurement data were expressed as means±standard deviation (X ± S), and the differences between groups were compared by A one-way ANOVA, and the count data were expressed as percentages (%). The chi-square test or Fisher’s test was used, and the difference was considered to be statistically significant when the P-value < 0.05 and the results of treatments were analyzed using the ITT and PP analysis [19]. This study was a retrospective study. To control confounding factors as much as possible and reduced selection bias, thereby more accurately evaluating the treatment effect, the Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) method was used to compare different treatment groups in the ITT and PP datasets to assess their impact on specific outcome variables (eradication effect). In the context of multiple group comparisons, this study adopted the Multinomial Propensity Score Weighting method, assigning a weight to each subject, so that each treatment group was balanced in the distribution of baseline covariates and simulated the conditions of a randomized controlled trial.

Results

In total, 874 patients who met the exclusion and inclusion criteria were included in this study: 271 participants were included in the EBAC group, 22 participants were lost to follow-up, and four participants had poor compliance. Overall, 268 participants were included in the EBAL group; 18 were lost to follow-up, and 12 had poor compliance. The EBAF group included 152 participants; 10 participants were lost to follow-up, and four participants had poor compliance. The EBAT group comprised 183 participants; 13 participants were lost to follow-up, and five participants had poor compliance (Fig. 1).

Comparison of ITT and PP eradication rates among treatment groups

After the inverse probability weighting and multinomial propensity score weighting method, the overall empirical bismuth quadruple therapy eradication rates for the ITT and PP analyses were 71.3% (622.8/874),79.2% (622.8/786). Among 271 cases in the EBAC group, the eradication rates of ITT analysis and PP analysis were 73.2% (198.5/271; 95%CI 67.3–79.1) and 83.2% (203.9/245; 95%CI 77.9–88.5), respectively; and among 268 cases in the EBAL group, the eradication rates of ITT analysis and PP analysis were 69.0% ( 184.9/268; 95%CI 61.9–76.1), 81.2% (193.3/238; 95%CI 74.7–87.7); in 152 cases in the EBAF group, the eradication rates of ITT analysis and PP analysis were 71.8% (109.2/152; 95%CI 61.3–82.3) and 90.0% (124.3/138; 95%CI 82.7–97.3); 183 patients were included in the EBAT group, with eradication rates of 71.5% (130.2/183; 95%CI 62.9–80.2) and 82.5% (136.1/165; 95%CI 74.1–90.8) for the ITT and PP analyses, respectively. None of the above differences in the eradication rates of the ITT and PP analyses among the different treatment groups were statistically significant(ITT: P = 0.845; PP: P = 0.470) (Table 2 and Supplementary Materials).

Drug costs and cost-effectiveness

These costs include registration fees, examination fees, and drug costs. The treatment process and examination costs were the same; however, the difference was in the drug cost. The drug costs of the four empirical treatment regimens were calculated according to the price standard of the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region at the time, and the prices were expressed in Chinese yuan (CNY). The price of each drug was as follows: esomeprazole, 28 tablets, 35.5; bismuth, 42 bags, 51.6; amoxicillin, 112 capsules, 30.0; clarithromycin, 56 tablets, 41.9; furazolidone, 28 tablets, 5.0; levofloxacin, 56 tablets, 35.2; and tetracycline, 112 tablets, 25.6. Drug costs for the four groups were calculated directly. EBAC group 159, EBAL group 152.3, EBAF group 122.1, EBAT group 142.7.

Based on the ITT and PP analyses, the H. pylori eradication rate was used to express effectiveness, and the cost-effectiveness ratio (CER) was used to express the average cost per participant eradicated. In the ITT analysis, the H. pylori eradication rate was used to express the average cost per participant. The CERITT values of the EBAC, EBAL, EBAF, and EBAF groups were 214.3, 201.9, 147.3, and 192.1, respectively. In the PP analysis, the CERPP values of the EBAC, EBAL, EBAF, and EBAF groups were 193.9, 179.4, 133.7, and 173.1, respectively. The EBAF group was the least cost-effective in both ITT and PP analyses, followed by the EBAL group; however, the cost was higher than that of the EBAF group. The incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) was used to represent the incremental cost per additional patient eradicated in the EBAC, EBAL, and EBAT groups compared to the EBAF group. The ICers of EBAC, EBAL, and EBAT were 327.9, 298.0, and 816.5 in the ITT analysis, and 295.1, 254.9, and 669.6 in the PP analysis.

Discussion

H. pylori is a Gram-negative, helical, microaerophilic pathogen that infects more than 50% of the global population because of its ability to resist acidic gastric environment [20–22]. H. pylori infection has been associated not only with different digestive disorders such as gastritis, peptic ulcers, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma, and gastric adenocarcinoma, but also with the development of iron deficiency anemia, vitamin B12 deficiency, and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura [23]. The population is generally susceptible to H. pylori, and the 2015 Tokyo Global Consensus Report recommends that for H.pylori-infected individuals, regardless of the presence or absence of clinical symptoms, they should receive eradication therapy; the current domestic and international consensus opinions recommend bismuth quadruple therapy as a first-line treatment regimen for H.pylori [3–5], but with the widespread eradication of H. pylori bismuth quadruple regimen eradication rates have been on a downward trend, H.pylori increased resistance to antibiotics is considered to be the main reason for eradication failure, and resistance regarding H.pylori has become an inevitable challenge in eradication treatment, therefore, selecting appropriate antibiotics and treatment regimens for first-line empirical treatment in areas with high rates of H.pylori infection and resistance can provide some reference for localized eradication of H.pylori.

Our findings showed that in the empirical bismuth quadruple therapy treatment groups, the eradication rates was highest in the EBAF group, with eradication rates analyzed by PP was 90.0%. The current resistance rate to furazolidone is low (< 5%), and studies have shown that furazolidone-containing regimens can achieve better efficacy than other empirical bismuth quadruple regimens of the same duration [24]. A retrospective study showed a primary eradication rate of 95.3% for bismuth quadruple therapy with amoxicillin in combination with furazolidone [25]. In addition, a meta-analysis of the efficacy of furazolidone showed that the furazolidone-containing regimen was more efficacious than other antibiotic regimens with the same treatment duration in terms of eradication rate (ITT, 81.1% vs. 75.4%; PP, 86.6% vs. 80.1%) with no significant increase in AEs (< 10%) [26]. However, the results of Niu M et al. showed that the PP eradication rate of a bismuth quadruple regimen containing furazolidone over a course of 14 days was 94.7%, but had a high incidence of adverse reactions (24.8%) compared to the other treatment regimens [19]. In terms of cost-effectiveness, the EBAF group had the lowest drug cost, and the average cost per participant that was eradicated was the lowest per unit efficacy (CERITT 147.3, CERPP 133.7). The ICERITT/PP values of the EBAC, EBAL, and EBAT groups compared with those of the EBAF group were 327.9/295.1, 298.0/254.9, and 816.5/669.6, respectively. The EBAF group had the lowest cost and highest eradication rate, whereas the EBAL group had a higher cost but a higher eradication rate than the EBAC group. EBAT is more expensive and less effective. The EBAF group in this study had a higher ITT and PP eradication rate than the empirical EBAC, EBAL, and EBAT groups; however, its efficacy was not superior to that of the other bismuth quadruple therapy groups. As with the EBAT group, the incidence of AEs was more than 20%, and the grade of AEs in both groups was mainly “mild.” The high incidence of AEs might be one of the reasons affecting the eradication effect of the furazolidone and tetracycline regimen. The incidence of AEs of furazolidone is related to the dosage and duration of administration [24], and tetracycline may cause significant hepatic and renal impairment; therefore, the use of furazolidone should be performed under the guidance of a clinician, the occurrence of AEs should be monitored, and the use of tetracycline should be monitored for the same reason. However, in clinical practice, we often ignore the impact of inadequate monitoring of adverse drug events on the treatment effect.

The results of the present study showed a poor eradication rate in the clinically used EBAC and EBAL groups (ITT: 73.2% vs.69.0%; PP: 83.2% vs.81.2%), which is generally consistent with the results of other studies [24–26]. In the EBAC and EBAL groups, the eradication rate of H. pylori in patients living in rural areas was lower than that in urban populations, which may be related to the high prevalence of H. pylori infection in rural areas, poorer healthcare and hygiene, weaker personal health awareness, and fewer clinical antimicrobial drug choices. Amoxicillin, clarithromycin, and levofloxacin are widely used antibiotics in clinical practice. The three are often applied in the treatment of infectious diseases outside the gastrointestinal tract, especially in the respiratory system and inflammatory diseases in the urinary system. There are characteristics of inflammation in the urinary system and pneumonia that are easy to recur and require multiple treatments, which may be due to the indirect application of clarithromycin and levofloxacin in infectious diseases outside the gastrointestinal tract in terms of the course of treatment, type and frequency of medication has not yet reached the eradication of H.pylori and is associated with the increase of H. pylori resistance due to the accumulation of antimicrobial drugs over time [27]. However, of course, the mechanism by which H. pylori resistance increases due to the accumulation of antimicrobial drugs over time still needs to be confirmed by further studies. Related studies analyzing antibiotic consumption in Europe have shown a significant association between macrolide use and H. pylori resistance to clarithromycin, as well as a significant association between quinolone use and levofloxacin resistance [28]. The results of Kwon’s study were consistent with this finding and showed that a history of macrolide and quinolone use was associated with a decrease in the eradication rate of bismuth tetracycline-containing clarithromycin and levofloxacin-containing bismuth, respectively [29]. The results of this study showed that the previous use of amoxicillin affected the eradication of H. pylori. According to the 2020–2022 study on H. pylori resistance in Ningxia, the resistance rates to metronidazole, clarithromycin, and levofloxacin were 94.2%, more than 40%, and less than 5%, respectively. The rate of multi-drug resistance was 24.2%. In the face of such a severe resistance challenge, the use of potentially resistant clarithromycin and levofloxacin for the eradication of H. pylori in the absence of clarity about resistance at the time of eradication greatly increases the risk of treatment failure, which may be due to a high rate of single resistance; alternatively, an increase in the rate of multi-resistance may be more pronounced in patients with prior amoxicillin use. However, our results show that prior amoxicillin use did not significantly affect H. pylori eradication rates in the EBAF and EBAT groups, and perhaps the use of furazolidone and tetracycline, which have lower resistance rates (< 5%), may have overcome the adverse effects of prior amoxicillin use to some extent. Although resistance rates to furazolidone and tetracycline were very low (< 5%), eradication rates were not significantly higher in the EBAT and EBAF groups than in the EBAC/EBAL group for several possible reasons: (1) Insufficient sample size: the EBAF group had a small sample size, while the EBAC and EBAL groups had a large sample size, and the actual sample randomization was not balanced, which may not be enough to detect the actual differences. (2) Confounding bias of retrospective studies: Some patients in the EBAC and EBAL groups may have developed drug resistance due to previous use of clarithromycin or levofloxacin (non-gastrointestinal infection), but such data were not collected comprehensively in our study; only the history of amoxicillin use was recorded; (3) The effect of adherence: the high incidence of AEs may have led to premature discontinuation or dose reduction in some patients. Additionally, tetracycline was administered four times daily, compared with twice daily for the other drugs, and EBAT adherence may have been lower than recorded. However, we cannot currently obtain detailed data on this aspect, which is a design defect in our study. (4) There were multi-drug resistant strains, and the multi-drug resistance rate of H. pylori in Ningxia was 24.2% [18]. If patients are infected with multi-drug resistant strains, the theoretical advantages may be weakened. In the future, we will further verify the advantages of the EBAF and EBAT regimens in areas with high drug resistance and explore optimal dosing strategies (such as prolonging the course of treatment or adjusting the dose) to improve efficacy.

The eradication rate of bismuth quadruple therapy containing amoxicillin was poor in this region and the eradication rate was affected by the history of previous medication with amoxicillin, especially in the clarithromycin and levofloxacin groups. Obviously, in the face of the dilemma of high H.pylori resistance to metronidazole, clarithromycin, and levofloxacin, and poor eradication rate of bismuth quadruple therapy in this region, the overall downward trend of first-time eradication success rate has not been effectively controlled despite the increasing variety of combinations of the consensus-recommended H.pylori eradication regimen and the increasing duration or dosage of the regimens, and it would be helpful to obtain pre-treatment antibiotic The availability of antibiotic resistance information before treatment can positively guide the eradication of H.pylori. Several studies have shown that individualized treatment based on drug sensitivity analysis is more effective than empirical treatment with the same course of treatment [9–13], especially in areas with high antibiotic resistance rates [15]. Individualized treatment guided by the results of drug sensitivity tests before treatment can achieve better eradication results and promote the rational application of antibiotics, which may be an effective way to improve the eradication rate of H. pylori in Ningxia, China. Therefore, in future studies, we plan to evaluate the efficacy and safety of individualized bismuth quadruple therapy guided by resistance detection.

This study has some limitations: (1) it is a retrospective study, and some of the data may have some bias; (2) baseline imbalance introduces bias. We have controlled for confounding factors through statistical methods as much as possible. The therapeutic advantage of the EBAF group is not caused by baseline imbalance, but is a true treatment effect; (3) it is not comprehensive enough in the collection of clinical data and did not include the previous use of clarithromycin, levofloxacin, furazolidone, and tetracycline, which resulted in an unclear effect of the above medications on the corresponding treatment regimen; in the EBAT group, 10 participants were unable to provide accurate information about the past use of amoxicillin, which may affect the analysis and discussion of the results in this group; 3) this study was a regional single-center study, with a small amount of sample data, hence, a multicenter study is needed to expand the sample size during follow-up.

Conclusions

The eradication rates of four amoxicillin-containing bismuth quadruple therapy regimens in Ningxia were not significantly different. However, the EBAF regimen showed favorable trends in terms of increased eradication rate, better cost-effectiveness, and controllable adverse events, its efficacy and tolerability should be weighed, and the monitoring of drug use should be strengthened.

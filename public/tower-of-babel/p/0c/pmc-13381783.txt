Re-myotomy in a myocardial bridge patient with previous bypass graft failure
Authors: Mingkui Zhang, Shanshan Duan, Lixin Fan, Hui Xue
Published: 2026

Case description

A 35-year-old man presented with exertional chest pain that was refractory to medication. Coronary computed tomography revealed a myocardial bridge in the left anterior descending (LAD) artery. He underwent an unsuccessful myocardial bridge myotomy at a local hospital; the procedure was converted intraoperatively to a left internal thoracic artery (LITA) to LAD bypass grafting. Five months after surgery, he experienced recurrent angina. Repeat coronary angiography revealed mild diastolic stenosis in the mid-segment of the LAD (Figure 1A, arrow), which was nearly occluded during systole (Figure 1B, arrow), and occlusion of the LITA graft (Figure 1C, arrow). Intraoperatively, scarring was observed at the site of the previous myotomy, but the incision depth did not reach the LAD. During the repeat surgery, a re-myotomy was performed, revealing the myocardial bridge ∼30 mm in length and 10 mm in depth (Figure 1D, arrow).

(A) Coronary angiography showing mild diastolic stenosis in the mid-segment of the LAD (arrow), (B) occlusion during systole (arrow), (C) occlusion of the LITA graft (arrow). (D) Re-myotomy procedure.

Myocardial bridging is a coronary anomaly in which an epicardial coronary artery courses intramyocardially. For symptomatic LAD artery myocardial bridging patients who are refractory to medication, surgery is an effective treatment option,1 and surgical treatment includes myotomy and coronary artery bypass grafting. Due to competitive blood flow, graft failure is common in bypass grafts using the LITA.2 Myotomy, however, is an anatomically and physiologically appropriate treatment option. The patient has recovered well and has not experienced a recurrence of symptoms during follow-up.

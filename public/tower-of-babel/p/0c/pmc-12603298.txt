Certificateless data integrity auditing with sparse Merkle trees for the cloud-edge environment
Authors: Ruizhong Du, Ziyuan Wang, Jiawei Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ensuring data integrity in cloud-edge environments is critical for IoT ecosystems but is challenged by dynamic data and resource constraints. This paper proposes a certificateless auditing scheme harmonizing cloud security with edge efficiency. By integrating online/offline cryptography and sparse Merkle trees, our approach achieves (1) significant user-side computation reduction via offline or edge-side tag generation, (2) \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(\log n)$$\end{document} dynamic update complexity versus traditional \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(n)$$\end{document} approaches, and (3) 75% communication overhead savings through pre-download mechanism. The scheme eliminates certificate management and mitigates Key Generation Centre (KGC) risks via decentralized trust mechanisms. Security proofs demonstrate resilience against KGC collusion and tag forgery under the Inv-CDH assumption. Experiments show our scheme audits faster than prior schemes, supporting 500k+ operations at sub-second latency. This work bridges scalability and real-time demands for smart cities and Industry 4.0 while enabling future extensions in ML-optimized caching and blockchain trust models.

Introduction

The integrity of outsourced data remains a pivotal challenge in modern distributed systems, particularly as the rapid proliferation of the Internet of Things (IoT) and edge computing reshapes data management paradigms. Since the inception of Provable Data Possession (PDP) by Ateniese et al.1, which introduced homomorphic tags for cloud data verification, significant advancements have been made in integrity auditing. However, existing schemes predominantly target homogeneous cloud environments, failing to address the unique demands of resource-constrained, dynamic IoT ecosystems and emerging cloud-edge architectures2,3.

Edge computing has emerged as a transformative force in IoT and 6G networks, reducing latency and bandwidth consumption by decentralising storage and computation to the network periphery. By 2023, edge technologies have become integral to applications ranging from real-time industrial automation to autonomous vehicles, with industry leaders like Amazon and Apple deploying edge-native frameworks (e.g., AWS Greengrass, Core ML) to support low-latency processing4. However, this shift introduces critical security challenges: distributed data management across untrusted nodes, high-frequency updates from IoT sensors, and heterogeneous device capabilities5. Traditional cloud-centric auditing mechanisms, reliant on Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) or trusted third parties, struggle with certificate management overhead and scalability in these environments6,7.

Recent efforts to optimise auditing efficiency highlight promising directions but retain critical gaps. Online/offline signatures8 and blockchain-based transparency mechanisms9,10 reduce computational burdens for end devices, while lightweight cryptographic primitives11 replace costly exponentiations with additive operations. Methods

System architecture

Figure 1 depicts the system architecture of our scheme. Three layers are included in this architecture based on the distance of data transmission: end, edge, and cloud. Entities under the same layer may have different affiliations and be represented by dashed circles.

Fig. 1System architecture.

System architecture.

End layer: includes the user. The User is the data owner who creates and uploads data and may own multiple end devices. Their computing power is limited, and their storage capacity is insufficient.

Edge layer: is close to the end layer, consists of the Node, the Third Party Auditor (TPA) and the Key Generation Centre (KGC). The Node has different storage and computing capabilities and affiliations, such as edge node services (ENS) provided by storage service providers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(Node_n)$$\end{document} and private servers owned by users \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(Node_U)$$\end{document}. The TPA and KGC are treated as trusted entities in this paper.

Cloud Layer: comprises the Cloud Server (CS). The CS is assumed to have infinite storage capacity and provides cloud storage services to terminals. In this paper, the CS is not considered trustworthy and is therefore required to accept requests for data integrity verification.

Additional features

Dynamic operation

Compared with query and modification operations, the time cost of adding and deleting is usually higher. Because when a large amount of data is added and deleted, the depth of the traditional Merkel tree may change, which will make the binary tree unbalanced and reduce the search efficiency.

As the sparse Merkle tree contains a significant number of empty nodes, adding new data only involves modifying the empty nodes with the new data, and deleting data blocks only requires replacing them with empty nodes. Security assumptions

The security foundation of our proposed mechanism relies on two key assumptions: the Discrete Logarithm (DL) assumption and the Inverse Computational Diffie-Helman (InvCDH) assumption.

Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g \in {{\mathbb {G}}_{T}}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \in {{\mathbb {Z}}_{q}}$$\end{document} are randomly selected from their respective groups. Similarly to DL, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g \in {{\mathbb {G}}_{T}}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \in {{\mathbb {Z}}_{q}}$$\end{document} are chosen randomly. Formally, for any PPT adversary \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {A}$$\end{document}, the probability is stated as:

7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname {Pr}[\mathcal {A}_{\operatorname {InvCDH}}\left( g, g^{\alpha }\right) = g^{\alpha ^{-1}}: \forall a \in \mathbb {Z}_{q}] \le \epsilon \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\epsilon$$\end{document} is a negligible function. Notably, it is demonstrated that the InvCDH problem is equivalent to the Computational Diffie-Helman (CDH) problem.

Results

We benchmark our scheme against five auditing protocols, focussing on computational efficiency. All evaluations used identical hardware: cloud servers (Alibaba ECS: 2GHz, 2GB RAM), edge nodes/TPA (Alibaba ENS: 2GHz, 1GB RAM) and user/KGC (Intel i7-8086K, 16GB RAM). In the OffTagGen phase, users will pre-generate tags and other metadata. Among these schemes, the schemes12,13,18 do not use offline signatures, so they are not included in the comparison. The computational overhead is low in schemes6,17 because complex computation is left to offline execution. Taking advantage of the offline/online signature mechanism, our scheme can achieve the same low computational complexity as the scheme17 when the data are uploaded.

The ProofGen phase in Table 2 is performed by the data storer, and the ProofVerify phase is performed by the TPA. The computational complexity of the task is directly proportional to the number of challenge blocks c.

Security analysis

Theorem 1

Assuming that a data storer experiencing no data loss can consistently demonstrate the integrity of its storage. The correctness includes two parts: online tag correctness and auditing correctness.

Proof

After receiving the online tags, the data storer verifies their correctness by applying Eq. (8). The validity of this equation can be proven based on the properties of bilinear pairing.

8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} e\left( \sigma _{i}, g\right)&=e\left( {S_{U}}^{{xm_{i}^{\prime }+r_{i}^{\prime }}}, g\right) =e\left( {S_{U}}^{xm_{i}+r_{i}}, g\right) =e\left( \left( {H(ID_{U})}^{m_{i}}\right) ^{\alpha x} \cdot \left( {H(ID_{U})}^{r_{i}}\right) ^{\alpha }, g\right) \\&=e\left( {H(ID_{U})}^{m_{i}}, g^{\alpha x}\right) \cdot e\left( {H(ID_{U})}^{r_{i}}, g^{\alpha }\right) =e\left( {H(ID_{U})}^{m_{i}}, P_{U}\right) \cdot e\left( {H(ID_{U})}^{r_{i}}, P_{KGC}\right) \end{aligned}\end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Upon receipt of the proof, the TPA substantiates its correctness through the application of Eq. (9), whose validity can be established by leveraging the inherent properties of bilinear pairing.

Theorem 2

Should the InvCDH problem prove challenging in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {G}_{1},$$\end{document} it implies that, with almost negligible probability, an untrusted server lacks the capability to induce the TPA into accepting a proof generated by the server, unless the outsourced data remains unaltered.

Theorem 3

When a user initiates a data deletion request, the data storer cannot deny that the data was not deleted as instructed.

The characteristics of the sparse Merkle tree in our scheme make it easy to prove the non-existence of data. Discussion

The experimental results validate that our certificateless auditing scheme effectively addresses the unique challenges of cloud-edge environments, particularly in IoT scenarios characterized by resource constraints and high data dynamism. By offloading most of user-side computations to offline or edge nodes, we achieve near-real-time responsiveness (0.2s for 500k blocks) while maintaining robust security under the Inv-CDH assumption. This marks a significant improvement over PKI-based schemes15 and identity-based alternatives16, which impose impractical certificate management burdens or risk KGC single-point failures.

The integration of sparse Merkle trees proves pivotal for IoT applications requiring frequent data updates. Unlike conventional Merkle trees, which exhibit \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(n)$$\end{document} complexity for structural rebalancing, our approach stabilizes update latency at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(\log n)$$\end{document}. This efficiency stems from pre-allocated empty nodes, enabling seamless insertions/deletions without tree-wide recalculations. Furthermore, pre-download mechanisms reduce traffic by proactively caching high-access-probability data blocks, a feature absent in cloud-centric models1.

Security analyses confirm that our scheme resists both KGC-edge collusion and tag forgery attacks. By decentralizing trust through certificateless key derivation-where users combine KGC-generated partial keys with local secrets-we eliminate attack surfaces exploited in prior works15,20. However, we acknowledge limitations in large-scale deployments: sparse Merkle trees incur moderate storage overheads for empty node indexing, and pre-download accuracy depends on access pattern predictability. Future work will integrate lightweight machine learning models at the edge to refine pre-download precision and explore sharding techniques for distributed Merkle tree management.

Comparisons with six state-of-the-art schemes6,12,13,17–19 highlight our balanced performance-security trade-off. While blockchain-based audits9,21 offer enhanced transparency, their inherent latency (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\ge$$\end{document}300ms per transaction) renders them unsuitable for latency-sensitive IoT applications like autonomous vehicles. In contrast, our scheme achieves 63% faster audits than scheme19 without compromising data privacy-a critical requirement for healthcare and industrial IoT use cases.

In conclusion, this work bridges a critical gap between cloud-scale security and edge-native efficiency, offering a practical solution for emerging 6G-IoT ecosystems. The protocol’s adaptability to heterogeneous devices and dynamic workloads positions it as a foundational framework for smart cities, Industry 4.0, and beyond.

Conclusions

This paper presents a certificateless data integrity auditing scheme tailored for cloud-edge environments, addressing the critical challenges of scalability, dynamic data management, and resource efficiency in IoT ecosystems. By integrating online/offline cryptographic primitives with sparse Merkle trees, our solution reduces user-side computation and achieves \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(\log n)$$\end{document} complexity for high-frequency updates. The pre-download mechanism further slashes communication consumption, enabling efficient real-time audits with sub-second latency. Security analyses demonstrate resilience against KGC collusion and tag forgery under the Inv-CDH assumption, while experimental validations confirm superior performance over six state-of-the-art schemes.

The proposed framework bridges cloud-scale security with edge-native agility, offering immediate applicability in smart cities, industrial IoT, and autonomous systems. Limitations in storage overhead for sparse Merkle trees highlight opportunities for future work, including lightweight ML-driven pre-download optimization and decentralized trust models via blockchain integration. This work establishes a foundational step toward secure, efficient, and scalable data integrity solutions for 6G-enabled edge computing landscapes.

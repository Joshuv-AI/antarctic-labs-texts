Late Thrombosis of First-Generation Bioresorbable Scaffold Site Treated With Novel Resorbable Magnesium Scaffold
Authors: Jorgo Kostov, Biljana Zafirovska, Sasko Kedev
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo the best of our knowledge, this is the first reported experience of a patient with late thrombosis of previous Absorb (Abbott Vascular) bioresorbable scaffold (BRS) implantation site, treated with second-generation Magmaris (Biotronik) resorbable magnesium scaffold (RMS).Case SummaryA 55-year-old man with a previously implanted Absorb BRS stent presented with a non–ST-elevation myocardial infarction. Coronary angiography showed thrombotic lesion at the BRS stent site. After intravascular optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging, a decision was made to implant a second-generation RMS stent. OCT follow-up was performed, with excellent long-term results.DiscussionRandomized controlled trials have connected early BRS implantation to a higher rate of stent thrombosis. In recent years, the newer generation RMS has confirmed a good safety profile and long-term patency. The idea behind this treatment was to assess the novel RMS technology with serial OCT imaging.Take-Home MessagesThis case adds new options for the treatment of coronary arteries with novel RMS technology. Intravascular imaging with OCT is essential for optimized treatment and follow-up.Key words: acute coronary syndrome, cardiovascular disease, imaging, percutaneous coronary intervention, stents

History of Presentation

A 55-year-old man presented to the emergency department with chest pain that started 1 day before admission. The pain radiated to both arms and the lower jaw. The patient had experienced chest pains only during exertion for the past 2 months, but he did not report them to his primary physician.

Past Medical History

Among risk factors for coronary artery disease, the patient had hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and rheumatoid arthritis. In 2015, he had a first-generation Absorb bioresorbable scaffold (BRS) stent (Abbott Vascular) implanted in the mid circumflex artery when he was treated at our center owing to acute posterior myocardial infarction. The dual antiplatelet therapy regimen at that time was 1 year of aspirin and clopidogrel, with continuation of aspirin thereafter (Figure 1).

Differential Diagnosis and Investigations

Initial electrocardiogram showed 1 mm ST-segment depression in the precordial leads. Primary high-sensitive troponins were normal, with second measurement above reference values. The decision according to the clinical symptoms along with electrocardiogram and troponin values was to take the patient to the catheterization laboratory. The coronary angiography that followed demonstrated severe lesion at the place of the previously implanted Absorb BRS stent (Figure 2, Video 1).

Coronary Angiography With Severe Stenosis on the Place of Previously Implanted Absorb Bioresorbable Scaffold Stent

The patient also had worsening coronary disease in the right coronary artery (RCA), with significant stenosis in the proximal RCA.

After intravascular imaging assessment with optical coherence tomography (OCT) and evaluation of thrombotic aneurysmatic changed circumflex artery, a decision was made for implantation of a second-generation Magmaris resorbable magnesium scaffold (RMS) stent (Biotronik) in the place of the previously implanted Absorb BRS. The RCA lesion was treated with a drug-eluting stent.

Management

The patient was preloaded with 600 mg clopidogrel, 300 mg aspirin, 40 mg rosuvastatin, and 7,500 IU unfractionated heparin. The lesion was wired with a standard workhorse wire, and OCT imaging was performed. A thrombotic lesion on the site of the previous Absorb implantation was visualized. There were no visible remaining struts from the Absorb stent seen on the OCT image 5 years after implantation (Figure 3).

Optical Coherence Tomography Showing Thrombus on the Circumflex Artery

Plaque morphology was mainly lipid mixed with fibrotic tissue in the mid circumflex artery. A few deep calcifications were observed (Figure 4).

Plaque Morphology

Balloon predilatation with a semicompliant balloon 3.0/15 mm at 10 atm was followed by Magmaris RMS implantation with dimensions 3.5/15 mm at 15 atm. Several postdilatations inside the stent were performed with a noncompliant balloon 4.0/6 mm at 12 and 16 atm with OCT control. Final OCT assessment was performed after percutaneous coronary intervention, with excellent apposition of the stent struts inside the vessel and good angiographic result (Figure 5, Video 2). The minimal stent area after RMS implantation was assessed at 8.5 mm2. During the same procedure, drug-eluting stent implantation for the severe stenosis found in the proximal RCA was also performed.

Angiography and Optical Coherence Tomography of Final Result After Resorbable Magnesium Scaffold Implantation

Echocardiography showed normal dimensions of the left ventricle, with normal left ventricular ejection fraction.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient was discharged the next day without clinical symptoms and on dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel, which was continued until 1 year after the non–ST-elevation myocardial infarction. Follow-up angiography and OCT imaging control was performed at 2 follow-up time points after RMS implantation. The right radial access site was used for all 4 interventions from 2015 until the last follow-up in 2024 with patent radial artery, using patent hemostasis each time after procedure.

The first follow-up 1.5 years after RMS implantation showed excellent results, with full stent strut absorption and patent circumflex artery with satisfactory minimal lumen area 9.15 mm2 (Figure 6, Video 3). The last OCT follow-up 3.5 years after RMS implantation showed the same satisfactory results. In terms of plaque composition, a decrease in the lipid area of the plaque was noted, with an obvious increase in the fibrotic tissue on OCT imaging after treatment with the RMS stent (Figure 7).

The patient was symptom free at 4.5-year follow-up, with regular physical activity.

Discussion

Randomized controlled trials have found that early bioresorbable vascular scaffold implants were connected with a higher rate of stent thrombosis compared with second-generation drug-eluting stents.1, 2, 3, 4 These first-generation polymer-based BRSs had issues with radial strength, deliverability, and capacity for expansion.1, 2, 3, 4 The Absorb BRS stent was discontinued after overwhelming results from published trials. A 2021 study also showed a high rate of device-related events at long-term follow-up after in-stent restenosis treated with first-generation BRS.5 In recent years, the novel second-generation magnesium-alloy Magmaris RMS has proven to be safe and effective in the treatment of patients with stable coronary artery disease and acute coronary syndrome, with the BIOSOLVE studies confirming good safety profile and long-term patency.6, 7, 8, 9 With recently upgraded magnesium-alloy RMS technology that includes aluminum and magnesium, the third-generation bioresorbable magnesium scaffold appears to have even better characteristics and outcomes, as indicated in the BIOMAG-I trial.10

This case treatment is outside of the scope of current guideline-recommended practices given our use of novel BRS technology, which is currently not included in guideline recommendations. Also in terms of treatment, to the best of our knowledge this is the first case in which the late stent thrombosis site of a previously implanted Absorb stent was treated with a second-generation RMS stent. The idea behind this treatment was to assess the novel RMS technology with serial OCT imaging and also to add new evidence in the treatment of positively remodeled aneurysmatic arteries without the need for permanent metal implanted in an aneurysmatic vessel, as drug-eluting stent implantation has been connected with a higher percentage of in-stent restenosis, device malapposition, and worse long-term outcomes.11 The main difference in these different-generation BRS scaffolds can also be seen in faster stent strut resorption time—1 year compared to 3 years for the Absorb stent—as well as the stable plaque morphology, noting a decrease in the lipid area and increase in the fibrotic tissue on OCT after treatment with the RMS stent. Long-term OCT follow-up in this case showed appropriate strut absorption at 1 year and excellent overall results, with satisfactory minimal lumen area in the treated vessel without signs of new restenosis, and good clinical results almost 4 years after intervention.

Conclusions

The second-generation magnesium-alloy Magmaris RMS stent was safe and effective in the treatment of late thrombosis of a previously implanted first-generation Absorb BRS stent, with good safety profile and long-term patency as assessed by intravascular imaging.

Correlation analysis of surgical outcomes and preoperative psychological status in adolescents with polydactyly/syndactyly
Authors: Meng Lu, ShiHang Cao, Dong Hu, Jun Lu, Yi Li, Peng Li, JunKui Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy of surgical intervention for polydactyly/syndactyly in adolescents, as well as analyze the factors influencing preoperative mental health and their subsequent impact on surgical outcomes.MethodsA retrospective study included 39 adolescent patients who underwent surgical treatment for polydactyly/syndactyly at the Department of Foot and Ankle Surgery in our hospital from February 2015 to July 2023. Basic patient information was collected, and evaluations were performed using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS), the American Orthopaedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) score and the Depression Anxiety and Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21) scores at both preoperative and final follow-up visits.ResultsA total of 39 patients (18 males, 21 females) completed the follow-up. The median age was 12.0 years (IQR 10.0–16.0) and the median follow-up time was 36.0 months (IQR 30.0–57.0). Compared with their preoperative baselines, VAS, AOFAS and DASS-21 scores improved significantly after surgery (P < 0.001). Significant correlations were observed among the preoperative VAS, AOFAS and DASS-21 scores (P < 0.05). However, preoperative DASS-21 scores showed no significant association with patients’ age, sex, BMI or follow-up time (P > 0.05). Moreover, the preoperative DASS-21 score did not influence the VAS or AOFAS scores at the final follow-up (P > 0.05).ConclusionsSurgical treatment for adolescent polydactyly/syndactyly significantly improves foot appearance, alleviates postoperative pain, enhances foot function, and contributes to better postoperative mental health. Preoperative pain and functional impairment are closely related to preoperative mental health status, whereas the direct impact of preoperative mental health status on surgical outcomes is limited.Keywords: Polydactyly/syndactyly, Adolescent mental health, Foot deformities, Efficacy evaluation, Correlation analysis

Introduction

Polydactyly/syndactyly are common congenital limb deformities that may occur independently or coexist. Statistics indicate that polydactyly occurs in approximately 1–2 cases per 1000 live births, while syndactyly is present in about 2–3 cases per 10,000 live births [1–3]. Although foot deformities are less common than hand deformities in clinical practice, their impact on function and quality of life should not be overlooked. They may result in abnormal foot appearance, difficulties with footwear, and restricted mobility, thereby affecting patients’ daily activities and psychological well-being. Adolescents, being in a critical stage of psychological development, exhibit immature emotional regulation and self-cognition. Consequently, they are more susceptible to negative emotions, such as anxiety and depression, when confronted with physical abnormalities or undergoing medical interventions [4].

Currently, research on foot polydactyly/syndactyly predominantly focuses on the optimization and innovation of surgical correction techniques, including minimally invasive resection, toe separation plasty, and advancements in postoperative fixation strategies [3, 5, 6]. Advancements in these technologies have provided patients with more efficient and robust treatment options. However, mental health issues, which are as crucial as morphological reconstruction, are often overlooked. Existing studies have highlighted that lower limb deformities not only significantly reduce patients’ quality of life but also may have a negative impact on their mental health [7, 8]. Furthermore, structural abnormalities of the foot can lead to an imbalance in plantar mechanical load and chronic pain, which further restrict patients' social interactions and occupational participation, resulting in dual physical and psychological burdens [9, 10]. If not intervened in a timely manner, long-term abnormal force-bearing patterns can affect the knee joints and spine, inducing complications such as secondary joint lesions or scoliosis, thereby further increasing the overall disease burden of patients [11].

In adolescents, foot deformities in appearance and limited mobility are significantly associated with social avoidance and anxious tendencies [12]. Studies have demonstrated that the prevalence of anxiety and depressive symptoms is significantly higher in patients with foot deformities, and there is a positive correlation between pain intensity and depression severity [13–15]. A case report described a 6-year-old Hispanic girl with syndactyly who exhibited strong resistance to removing her shoes in public, reflecting feelings of inferiority due to her foot abnormality [16]. Furthermore, mental health conditions such as anxiety and depression not only diminish patients' quality of life but also impair their adherence to surgical and rehabilitation protocols, thereby adversely affecting treatment outcomes [17–19]. Research on major conditions such as myocardial infarction has shown that patients with comorbid anxiety disorders experience significantly poorer long-term prognoses, further underscoring the critical role of mental health in treatment processes [20]. Therefore, the timely identification and intervention of psychological issues in this population are crucial for optimizing treatment outcomes.

Although research into surgical treatment of adolescent polydactyly/syndactyly has increased, systematic investigations into how psychological factors influence surgical outcomes remain limited. This study focused on preoperative mental health and, together with clinical outcome assessments, examined its impact on postoperative recovery and potential underlying mechanisms. The goal was to provide a reference for clinical practice, improve understanding of the role of preoperative psychological status in postoperative recovery, and generate foundational data to guide future individualized treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

General information

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Review Committee of Xi’an Honghui Hospital (No.2025-KY-137-01). We retrospectively analyzed adolescent patients who underwent surgical correction of polydactyly/syndactyly in our Department of Foot and Ankle Surgery between February 2015 and July 2023. Written informed consent to participate was obtained from all participants aged 16 years or older. For participants under the age of 16, written informed consent to participate was obtained from their parents or legal guardians.

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Confirmed diagnosis of congenital polydactyly/syndactyly; (2) Age 10–19 years, consistent with the definition of adolescence; (3) Underwent corrective surgery for unilateral polydactyly/syndactyly; (4) At least 12 months of postoperative follow-up with completion of the final assessment; (5) Sufficient cognitive and language skills to understand and comply with assessment protocols. Exclusion Criteria: (1) Presence of other congenital or acquired foot deformities (e.g., clubfoot, foot drop); (2) History of foot-related surgery; (3) Neurological, cognitive, or severe psychiatric disorders interfering with assessment cooperation; (4) Failure to complete scheduled follow-up or missing follow-up data; (5) Incomplete or missing clinical records.

Methods

Patient baseline characteristics—including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and follow-up time—were recorded. Pain, ankle–foot function, and mental health status were evaluated preoperatively and at final follow-up using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS), the American Orthopaedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) score, and the Depression Anxiety and Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21), respectively. The VAS consists of a 100-mm horizontal line on which patients indicate pain intensity from 0 (“no pain”) to 100 (“worst imaginable pain”), providing a precise measure of pain severity [21]. The AOFAS score evaluates ankle–foot function across pain, function, and alignment domains, with a maximum of 100; higher scores indicate superior postoperative outcomes [22]. The DASS-21 is a validated self-report instrument that quantifies depression, anxiety, and stress across diverse populations and is particularly useful for adolescents; it contains three subscales—depression (DASS-D), anxiety (DASS-A), and stress (DASS-S)—each scored from 0 (“does not apply”) to 3 (“applies very much”). Subscale totals are doubled, yielding maximum domain scores of 42; higher values reflect greater symptom severity [23, 24].

All clinical questionnaires and relevant data were entered into an electronic database via a standardized protocol and anonymized to ensure data integrity and patient confidentiality. All surgeries were conducted by the same team of experienced orthopedic surgeons, and patients underwent uniform postoperative rehabilitation to maintain consistency in postoperative care.

Surgery and rehabilitation

Preoperatively, individualized surgical plans were formulated based on imaging findings, deformity localization assessments, and the type and severity of polydactyly/syndactyly deformities. All patients underwent surgery under general anesthesia combined with nerve block to ensure intraoperative analgesia and minimize postoperative discomfort.

In the surgery for polydactyly deformity, the excess phalanges and joints were first resected, and the residual bone was meticulously contoured with fine instruments to restore normal foot architecture. When indicated, affected tendons and ligaments were repaired or transposed to optimize functional recovery and approximate the native anatomy (preoperative and postoperative radiographs are shown in Fig. 1). In the surgery for syndactyly deformities, soft tissues, osseous elements, and neurovascular bundles between the involved and adjacent toes were precisely dissected—maximizing preservation of healthy structures, preventing complications, and safeguarding neurovascular integrity. Postoperatively, wounds were carefully closed and both skin and deep soft tissues reconstructed; tendon and ligament repairs incorporated appropriate tensioning to maintain motor function of the foot.

At 1–2 weeks postoperatively, patients commenced gentle foot activity, emphasizing passive range-of-motion exercises to preserve joint mobility. By 4–6 weeks postoperatively, they transitioned to forefoot off-loading footwear to alleviate pressure, facilitate osseous and soft-tissue healing, and initiate low-intensity gait training for gradual restoration of normal ambulation. Follow-up evaluations were conducted at 1, 2, and 3 months postoperatively—and annually thereafter—to assess structural alignment, range of motion, pain, and functional performance. Based on these results, individualized rehabilitation protocols were devised to optimize functional recovery and minimize the risk of postoperative deformities, specifically new or recurrent structural or functional deformities arising from joint instability or imbalance of periarticular soft tissues.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM, New York, USA). The normality of continuous variables was first assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test (α = 0.05). Non-parametric methods were applied when the data did not meet the normality assumption. Descriptive statistics are presented as median and interquartile range [M (P25, P75)]. Changes in clinical efficacy between preoperative and final follow-up visits were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The relationship between gender and preoperative psychological health status was analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Associations between other continuous variables and preoperative psychological health status were assessed using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. All statistical tests were two-sided, with a significance level set at P < 0.05.

Results

General condition of the patient

This study comprised 39 adolescents with polydactyly/syndactyly (18 males, 21 females). The median patient age was 12.0 years (IQR: 10.0–16.0), and the median follow-up duration was 36.0 months (IQR: 30.0–57.0). No complications—including infection, delayed wound healing, nerve injury, deformity recurrence, or functional impairment—were observed throughout the follow-up period.

Effect analysis

This study demonstrated that surgical correction of adolescent polydactyly and syndactyly yields favorable clinical outcomes. At the final follow-up, patients exhibited significant reductions in pain (VAS) and improvements in foot function (AOFAS) compared with the preoperative period (P < 0.001). Moreover, psychological well-being, as measured by DASS-21 scores, improved significantly compared with the preoperative period (P < 0.001, Table 1).

Correlation analysis

In this study, a correlation analysis was performed to examine the factors influencing patients’ preoperative mental health status. The results revealed that preoperative pain (VAS) and foot function (AOFAS) scores were significantly correlated with preoperative mental health status (P < 0.05). In contrast, no significant correlations were observed between patients’ basic demographics—age, sex, BMI, and follow-up time—and preoperative mental health status (P > 0.05, Table 2).

Discussion

Polydactyly/syndactyly deformities not only cause functional limitations and pain but also lead to significant changes in foot appearance, which in turn trigger negative emotions, such as anxiety and feelings of inferiority [25]. However, current treatment approaches primarily focus on anatomical correction and functional reconstruction, often overlooking the potential influence of preoperative mental health on surgical outcomes. Research has shown that good mental health not only improves treatment compliance but also reduces post-operative discomfort and facilitates rehabilitation [26, 27]. Adolescents, being in a phase of rapid physical and emotional development, often struggle with emotional regulation and are particularly vulnerable to disease-related stress and external stimuli, leading to significant emotional fluctuations [4]. Therefore, preoperative mental health assessment and intervention in adolescent patients are crucial for enhancing treatment adherence and promoting post-operative recovery, and have become integral components of the management of polydactyly/syndactyly deformities in adolescents.

The results of this study indicate that surgical treatment is highly effective in alleviating pain associated with polydactyly/syndactyly deformities in adolescents, as well as in improving foot function and mental health. These improvements may be related to the restoration of normal anatomical structure and biomechanical function of the foot following surgical correction, thereby reducing the physical and psychological burden caused by the deformity. Previous literature has reported that, in reconstructive and cosmetic surgical procedures, improvements in physical appearance may be associated with psychological well-being and self-perception [28]. Although postoperative improvement in foot appearance may have a positive influence on psychological recovery, appearance-related outcomes were not directly assessed in the present study. Therefore, a direct association between cosmetic improvement and improvement in psychological status could not be conclusively established. Existing studies have confirmed that corrective surgery for macrodactyly in adolescents can substantially improve quality of life and functional status [29]. Likewise, significant improvements in quality of life are also observed following hallux valgus surgery [30]. Furthermore, symptoms of anxiety and depression are significantly alleviated after total hip and knee arthroplasty [31]. Consistent with these previous studies, our findings further support the potential clinical value of orthopedic surgery in promoting both physical and psychological rehabilitation in adolescents with polydactyly/syndactyly deformities.

This study further investigated the factors influencing preoperative mental health in adolescents. The findings revealed a significant correlation between preoperative VAS, AOFAS scores, and DASS-21 scores, suggesting that deformity-related pain and functional impairment may be closely associated with preoperative psychological problems. Specifically, higher preoperative pain levels or more severe functional limitations were associated with higher levels of anxiety, depression, and psychological stress. Previous studies have also indicated that among patients undergoing foot and ankle surgery, those with higher preoperative anxiety levels tend to report significantly higher pain scores compared to those with lower anxiety levels [32]. Additionally, research has shown a close association between foot dysfunction and levels of anxiety, depression, and stress [33]. These findings suggest that pain and functional impairment may play an important role in the development and progression of preoperative psychological distress in adolescents with polydactyly/syndactyly deformities.

However, baseline patient characteristics (including sex, age, BMI, and follow-up duration) were not significantly associated with preoperative DASS-21 scores. This finding may be attributable to multiple factors. First, demographic characteristics such as sex and age have a relatively limited impact on preoperative psychological assessment; previous studies have shown that these variables have limited explanatory power for mental health outcomes in specific populations [34]. Second, as a physiological indicator, BMI is insufficient to fully capture the complexity of mental health, and its relationship with psychological status may be influenced by multiple factors, including cultural background, social environment, and individual psychological adaptability. A study on COVID-19 also reported no significant association between BMI and mental health status [35]. Moreover, adolescents tend to focus more on current symptom experiences (such as pain and functional limitations) rather than long-term contextual factors. As their psychological development is not yet fully mature, their emotional states are more susceptible to short-term physiological fluctuations. Therefore, the lack of a significant association between baseline patient characteristics and preoperative psychological status may reflect the combined effects of multiple interacting factors.

Furthermore, this study explored the relationship between patients' preoperative mental health status and surgical prognosis. The results showed that there was no significant correlation between preoperative DASS-21 scores and VAS, AOFAS scores at the final follow-up. We hypothesize that preoperative anxiety, depression, and stress in patients mainly reflect the immediate response to current symptoms and uncertainty about surgical outcomes, rather than serving as effective predictors of post-operative functional recovery or pain relief. Following surgery, with the correction of deformities and improvement of function, patients' psychological status improves significantly, thereby weakening the impact of preoperative psychological status on long-term prognosis. Furthermore, standardized post-operative rehabilitation guidance, as part of comprehensive treatment, helps patients better adapt to the post-operative recovery period, improving rehabilitation compliance, thereby promoting functional recovery and reducing the occurrence of complications. Similar studies have also indicated that there is no significant correlation between patients' preoperative mental health status and surgical prognosis in knee arthroplasty [36]. This finding is consistent with the results of this study.

Although this study conducted an in-depth analysis of the influencing factors of preoperative mental health status and their impact on prognosis in adolescents with polydactyly/syndactyly undergoing surgical treatment, several limitations should be noted: 1. The sample size was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. 2. Participants are adolescents, whose understanding of foot deformities may vary, potentially affecting the accuracy of clinical indicators. 3. Factors such as individual differences, cultural background, socioeconomic status, occupation, and family support were not fully controlled and may influence postoperative rehabilitation outcomes. 4. This is a single-center, retrospective study from a tertiary orthopedic hospital, which may limit the applicability of results to other regions or clinical settings. 5. Stratified analyses based on deformity type (e.g., preaxial vs. postaxial) were not performed, and different deformity types may affect surgical outcomes and recovery.6. The study primarily focused on the association between preoperative mental health and postoperative outcomes; therefore, data collection emphasized psychological variables and did not systematically capture all potential clinical or demographic predictors. Given the limited sample size, multivariable regression could risk overfitting, whereas correlation analyses were appropriate for preliminary exploration. Future studies should include larger, multi-center datasets with more comprehensive clinical and demographic variables, perform stratified analyses based on key characteristics such as deformity type, and further examine the relationship between preoperative mental health and postoperative rehabilitation. Such studies would allow multivariable analyses to assess the independent contributions of relevant factors and provide more robust evidence to optimize clinical management strategies.

Conclusion

Surgical treatment for polydactyly/syndactyly deformities in adolescents can significantly improve the appearance of patients' feet, reduce postoperative pain, enhance foot function, and contribute to the improvement of their mental health. Studies have shown that preoperative pain and dysfunction are closely linked to patients' mental health status and can influence their preoperative psychological condition. However, the direct impact of preoperative mental health status on postoperative recovery is relatively limited. Nevertheless, clinical treatment should still consider patients' preoperative pain, dysfunction, and psychological condition, with treatment plans optimized to promote postoperative rehabilitation and maximize therapeutic outcomes. To further enhance clinical strategies, future studies should investigate the potential impact of preoperative mental health interventions on postoperative recovery and provide more comprehensive treatment plans to improve patients' overall prognosis.

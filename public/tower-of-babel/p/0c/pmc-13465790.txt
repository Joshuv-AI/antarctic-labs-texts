Synergistic application of concentrated growth factor and bone perforation technique in bisphosphonate-related-osteonecrosis of the jaw
Authors: Jun Sun, Huimin Chen, Jiayi Yan, Shurong Li, Yushan Ding, Jian Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (BRONJ) is a severe complication caused by the deposition of bisphosphonates in bone tissue, particularly after invasive dental procedures. Its pathogenesis is closely related to impaired bone remodeling and insufficient blood supply. Optimizing treatment strategies for BRONJ remains a significant clinical challenge. This study aims to evaluate the therapeutic effects of concentrated growth factor (CGF) combined with the bone perforation technique in the treatment of BRONJ.MethodsA rat model of BRONJ was established and divided into four groups: Non-treated group (N), Bone perforation group (B), CGF group (C), and CGF + Bone perforation group (C + B). Bone repair was evaluated at 2 and 4 weeks post-treatment through micro-computed tomography (Micro-CT), histological analysis (HE and TRAP staining), Western blot, immunofluorescence staining, and qRT-PCR (RUNX2, ALP).ResultsHE staining at 2 weeks postoperatively showed abundant empty lacunae with inflammation in the N and B groups, while the C and C + B groups exhibited reduced necrotic bone and new bone formation. At 4 weeks, the C + B group achieved complete epithelial healing with no residual necrotic bone. Quantitative histomorphometry revealed that the TRAP-positive area was 0.32% in the C group and 0.27% in C + B groups, significantly higher than that in the N group 0.12%(p < 0.001). PCR analyses revealed that, at 2 weeks, ALP expression was highest in the C group compared to all other groups (N, B, and C + B) (p < 0.001), whereas RUNX2 expression showed no significant difference between the C and C + B groups but was significantly higher than that in the N and B groups (p < 0.01). At 4 weeks, the C + B group exhibited the highest ALP and RUNX2 expression, significantly exceeding all other groups (N, B, and C). Western blot analyses demonstrated that at 2 weeks post-surgery, ALP and RUNX2 protein expression was higher in the C group than in all other groups (p < 0.05). At 4 weeks, ALP expression was higher in the C and C + B groups compared to the other groups (p < 0.001). RUNX2 expression was highest in the C + B group (p < 0.001).ConclusionOur results demonstrate that CGF significantly promotes BRONJ repair, while the bone perforation technique improves early blood supply but has limited long-term therapeutic effects. The combined application of CGF and bone perforation exerts a synergistic effect in enhancing osteogenesis and optimizing bone repair outcomes. Future studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods are warranted to verify these findings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-08596-w.Keywords: BRONJ, Bone perforation technique, Concentrated growth factor, Osteopathy, Osteogenesis

Introduction

Bisphosphonates (BPs), potent inhibitors of osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, are widely prescribed for the management of osteoporosis, metastatic bone lesions, and Paget’s disease of bone [1–3]. However, an unavoidable consequence of BP use is their accumulation in bone, which can lead to severe complications in patients undergoing invasive dental procedures, specifically bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (BRONJ).

The term “medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ)” was formally adopted in the 2014 and 2022 clinical practice guidelines issued by the American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons (AAOMS) [4, 5], reflecting its expanded definition to include not only bisphosphonates but also other antiresorptive and antiangiogenic agents. However, this study focuses specifically on osteonecrosis induced by conventional bisphosphonates. Since the established term “BRONJ” more accurately denotes the drug class investigated herein, it has been retained throughout the manuscript.

Despite extensive research efforts, the pathogenesis of BRONJ remains unclear. Several widely accepted hypotheses suggest that BRONJ development is associated with inhibited bone remodeling, congenital or acquired immune dysfunction, vascular inhibition and microbial infection [6, 7]. Notably, an adequate blood supply plays a crucial role in the healing of BRONJ [8]. In the field of dentistry, cortical bone perforation has been proposed as a strategy to ensure sufficient blood supply [9]. In addition, supplementation with autologous platelet concentrates has been shown to enhance blood perfusion, thereby facilitating bone healing in patients exposed to BP [10, 11].

Concentrated growth factor (CGF), a new-generation platelet concentrate, possesses favorable angiogenic, osteogenic, and anti-inflammatory properties. Compared with previous generations of platelet concentrates, CGF is enriched with a higher concentration of osteoinductive cytokines and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), and features a fibrin matrix that acts as a natural scaffold for bone regeneration. Due to its simple and efficient autologous preparation process, CGF has been widely applied in oral implantation and maxillofacial surgery [12]. Yüce et al. demonstrated that the local application of CGF during BRONJ surgery in osteoporotic patients exerted a positive therapeutic effect [13].

The bone perforation technique, which involves drilling into the cortical bone to enhance vascularization, is commonly used to promote intraoperative bleeding and facilitate bone regeneration in autologous bone grafting [14], its application in BRONJ treatment has also been shown to significantly improve surgical outcomes [15]. However, optimizing treatment strategies for BRONJ remains a significant clinical challenge.

Given the individual advantages of CGF and bone perforation in promoting tissue repair and angiogenesis, their combined use may yield synergistic effects in the treatment of BRONJ. To date, however, high-quality evidence from systematic evaluations supporting the combined application of CGF and bone perforation for BRONJ remains insufficient. Considering the practical challenges in conducting well-controlled clinical trials with adequately large cohorts, we established a rat model of BRONJ in this study. This study systematically evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of CGF combined with bone perforation, aiming to preliminarily investigate the potential of this combined approach and provide insights for future clinical strategies in BRONJ management.

Materials and methods

Animal grouping, drug administration, and model establishment

This study was approved by the institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Yi Shengyuan Gene Technology (Tianjin) (Approval No. YSY-DWLL-2023502). A total of 26 female Sprague-Dawley rats (6–8 weeks old, 240 ± 10 g) were obtained from Tianjin Guosheng Zhongyuan Technology Co., Ltd. All rats were maintained in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility under standardized conditions: temperature (21 °C ± 2 °C), humidity (50% ± 10%), and a 12-hour light/dark cycle, with ad libitum access to standard chow and autoclaved water.

All rats received weekly intravenous injections of zoledronic acid (0.2 mg/kg, SML0223, Sigma-Aldrich‌, USA). After 8 weeks, under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia, the left maxillary first and second molars were extracted to induce BRONJ (Fig. 1a). Postoperatively, the rats were provided a soft diet and closely monitored. Drug administration continued for 4 more weeks, after which the model was assessed (Fig. 1b). Rats were individually numbered and randomly allocated to four experimental groups using Microsoft Excel’s RAND function, followed by sorting. The groups are as follows: Non-treated control (N) (n = 8), Bone Perforation alone (B) (n = 6), CGF alone (C) (n = 6), and combined CGF and Bone Perforation (C + B) (n = 6).

N group: A full-thickness flap was elevated, the extraction socket was debrided, and the wound was sutured. Two rats were designated solely for CGF preparation and were not included in any subsequent experimental procedures.

B group: A full-thickness flap was elevated, the socket was debrided, and four bone perforations (0.25 mm apart, 0.5 mm deep) were created using a saline-cooled fissure bur. The flap was then repositioned and sutured (Figure 1 c).

C group: A full-thickness flap was elevated, the extraction socket was debrided, and CGF was applied to the extraction socket before suturing.

All surgical procedures were performed under pentobarbital sodium anesthesia with the rats in the supine position. At 2 and 4 weeks postoperatively, soft tissue coverage, bone exposure, and purulent discharge were assessed before euthanasia. The maxillae were harvested, rinsed with saline, and divided for analysis: one portion was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, and the other was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for 2 h before storage at − 80 °C. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments) guidelines. To eliminate observer bias and ensure the objectivity of experimental results, all experimental procedures and outcome assessments were conducted in a blinded manner. Animals were randomly coded by an independent researcher who was not involved in subsequent experiments, and the investigators responsible for Micro-CT scanning, histological observation (HE and TRAP staining), Western blot analysis and corresponding quantitative data collection were completely blinded to the animal group assignments throughout the experiment. Unblinding was only carried out after all data were collected, collated and statistically analyzed to avoid subjective interference during data evaluation.

CGF preparation

Following anesthesia induction, 5 mL of venous blood was collected via retro-orbital plexus puncture into a sterile, anticoagulant-free tube and subjected to differential centrifugation (Fig. 1d). Centrifugation yielded three distinct layers: an upper layer of platelet-poor plasma (PPP), primarily composed of plasma; a lower layer of red blood cells (RBCs); and an intermediate brownish-yellow fibrin clot, identified as CGF. The CGF layer was meticulously harvested and stored in sterile microcentrifuge tubes at − 80 °C for subsequent applications (Fig. 1e) [16].

Micro-computed tomography (Micro-CT)

Micro-CT scans of the left maxillary bone samples were acquired to quantitatively assess bone regeneration. Key parameters included bone mineral density (BMD, mg/cm³), bone volume fraction (BV/TV, %), trabecular number (Tb.N, mm⁻¹), and trabecular separation (Tb.Sp, mm). Among these parameters, BMD and BV/TV are utilized to evaluate overall bone mass and metabolic activity. Meanwhile, Tb.N and Tb.Sp are employed to characterize the spatial architecture of trabecular bone, thereby reflecting the morphological features of the bone microstructure.

Histological analysis

Left maxillary specimens were decalcified in 10% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) for 8 weeks, with solution renewal every 48–72 h. Following complete decalcification, tissues were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series (70%-100%), embedded in paraffin, and sectioned coronally at 4 μm thickness. Deparaffinized sections were rehydrated and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (HE) and tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) using standardized protocols. Stained sections were examined under a bright-field microscope to evaluate bone architecture and cellular activity. TRAP staining provides a sensitive and intuitive approach for visualizing the degree of osteoclast inhibition. The percentage of TRAP-positive area (Area%) was quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, USA). For each section, a standardized rectangular region of interest (ROI; 300 × 300 μm) was manually defined in the central healing region of the socket. Three non‑overlapping ROIs were selected in each section to ensure measurement reliability, these regions were selected blindly and consistently across samples. For each sample, three consecutive histological sections were analyzed, and the mean percentage of TRAP‑positive area (Area%) was calculated for statistical analysis. In addition, the report calculates the number of TRAP‑positive cells per unit area by counting positive cells in the ROI field of view, then normalizes the results with the C + B group set as 1.

RT-qPCR analysis

Runx2 and ALP are well-established markers for assessing osteogenic activity. In this study, we selected these indicators to provide a reliable evaluation of bone formation status, enabling a clear assessment of active jawbone necrosis repair following treatment. Total RNA was extracted from maxillary bone tissues using a high-speed, low-temperature tissue homogenizer (GTR16-2, Beijing Xincheng Beili Medical Equipment Co., Ltd.) after lysis in RNA extraction buffer. RNA concentration and integrity were verified by spectrophotometry (A260/A280 ratio > 1.8) and agarose gel electrophoresis. Reverse transcription was performed using a PrimeScript RT Kit (Takara, Osaka, JPN). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed under the following thermal cycling conditions: Initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing/extension at 62 °C for 30 s, for 40 cycles. β-actin served as the endogenous control, and each sample was analyzed in triplicate. The relative mRNA expression levels of target genes were quantified using the 2^−ΔΔCt method. Primers were synthesized by Shanghai Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (primer sequences listed in Table 1).

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from bone tissues by tissue lysis and centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C (Beili, GTR16-2, Beijing, CHN). After BCA quantification, samples were mixed with loading buffer, denatured at 95 °C for 10 min, and separated by SDS-PAGE. Proteins were then transferred to PVDF membranes, blocked for 1 h, and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies anti-RUNX2 (1:1000,Abmart, T55395F, Shanghai, CHN) and anti-ALP (1:7000, Abmart, T55421S, shanghai, CHN), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:4000, Goat Anti- Mouse IgG, Abmart, M21002, shanghai, CHN) for 1 h at room temperature. After washing with TBST, protein bands were visualized using Amersham Imager 600(GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences AB, Chicago, Illinois, USA) and quantified via ImageJ 1.54 f.

Immunofluorescence

Paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanol (100%–70%), and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. After blocking with 5% BSA for 1 h, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies anti-RUNX2 (1:100, Abmart, T55395F, Shanghai, CHN) and anti-ALP (1:100, Abmart, T55421S, Shanghai, CHN). The next day, they were washed with PBS and incubated with fluorescent secondary antibodies FITC Conjugated goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (1:200, Biyuntian, A0562, Shanghai, CHN) at room temperature for 1 h. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Solarbio, C0065, Beijing, CHN), and fluorescence microscopy (3DHISTECH, Pannoramic Scan, Budapest, HUN) was used to visualize target protein expression. The region of interest (ROI) was defined as the interface between necrotic and viable bone, corresponding to the histomorphological region identified on serial H&E-stained sections. A three-dimensional (3D) fluorescence intensity plot was generated to visualize the spatial distribution and relative intensity of target proteins at this osteonecrotic boundary.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and data visualization were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.5. Two-way ANOVA was used for the comparison of PCR data between the 2-week and 4-week time points within each group, whereas one-way ANOVA was applied to all other analyses, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05.

Four weeks after tooth extraction, all 26 rats demonstrated persistent bone exposure at the extraction sites, confirming successful establishment of the BRONJ model (Fig. 1b).

HE staining results

As shown in Fig. 3, at 2 weeks postoperatively, group N exhibited disorganized trabecular structures with extensive inflammatory cell infiltration. Both groups N and B showed large empty lacunae in the jawbone. In group C, the peripheral necrotic bone area was reduced, with evidence of new bone formation. Group C + B demonstrated a significantly smaller necrotic bone area, although the extraction socket remained unfilled. At 4 weeks post-surgery, group N displayed persistent extensive inflammatory infiltration and empty lacunae. Group B exhibited fragmented bone tissue with persistent large necrotic areas. In group C, new bone formation in the defect area was markedly increased, with more numerous and better-organized trabeculae, and the gingival epithelium was nearly healed. Group C + B showed complete resolution of necrotic bone, significantly reduced inflammation, defect areas filled with reparative tissue, and well-aligned, thickened trabeculae. As shown in Fig. 3, at both 2 and 4 weeks, groups C and C + B exhibited more active angiogenesis and more distinct evidence of neovascularization compared to groups N and B. Furthermore, by the 4-week time point, group C + B displayed more mature vascular structures. This preliminary study indicates that the combined treatment of CGF and bone perforation exerts significant effects on new bone formation and angiogenesis.

TRAP staining

As shown in Fig. 4, at 2 weeks post-surgery, no TRAP-positive cells were observed in the N group, while the B group showed positive cells at the defect margins. At 4 weeks post-surgery, TRAP-positive cells in the N group were concentrated at the distal extraction socket, while the B group had fewer positive cells. In the C and C + B groups, positive cells were evenly distributed along the defect margins and newly formed bone. Quantitative histomorphometry revealed that the TRAP-positive area was 0.32% in the C group and 0.27% in C + B groups, significantly higher than that in the N group 0.12%(p < 0.001). The results of the count of TRAP-positive cells per unit area corresponded to this. The numbers in the C group and C + B group were significantly higher than those in the control group. The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). One-way Analysis of Variance was used for statistical analysis. Descriptive statistical indicators are shown in Table 3. This suggests that CGF alone or combined with bone perforation can alleviate bisphosphonate-induced osteoclast inhibition.

RT-qPCR results

Real-time quantitative PCR was used to analyze ALP and RUNX2 gene expression in the N, B, C, and C + B groups at 2 and 4 weeks postoperatively (Fig. 5). Compared to the N group, all experimental groups (B, C, C + B) showed significantly increased ALP and RUNX2 expression at both time points. At 4 weeks, the C + B group exhibited the highest ALP and RUNX2 activation, significantly exceeding all other groups (N, B, C). Statistical analysis was performed using one-way and two-way ANOVA, with p < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Data are presented as relative mRNA expression, normalized to β-actin, and expressed as the mean ± standard error from three independent experiments. Descriptive statistical indicators are shown in Table 3. The results indicated that the combination of CGF and bone perforation enhanced osteogenic gene expression, further supporting the efficacy of this therapeutic strategy.

Discussion

BRONJ remains a refractory complication in clinical practice, with conventional treatments such as surgical debridement, irrigation and curettage of the jawbone showing unstable efficacy and high recurrence rates [17–19]. Given the core pathological features of impaired bone remodeling and insufficient local blood supply in BRONJ, exploring combined regenerative strategies has become a key direction to improve therapeutic outcomes [14, 21]. Our study demonstrated that the combined use of CGF and bone perforation significantly promoted angiogenesis, new bone formation and osteogenic activity as early as 2 weeks after intervention, and the effect of bone perforation became more prominent over time.

CGF is enriched with pleiotropic growth factors, including TGF-β and BMP2, which are pivotal in osteogenic regulation. Studies have confirmed that TGF β/BMP2 activate RUNX2 through the Smad signaling pathway, induce BMSC differentiation into osteoblasts, and enhance ALP activity and OCN gene expression to promote bone formation [17, 18]. IGF 1 stimulates the PI3K/Akt mTOR pathway to support osteoblast proliferation and survival [19], while VEGF mediates neovascularization to improve local microcirculation and relieve ischemic necrosis [20]. In addition, CGF contains CD34 + cells, fibroblasts and endothelial cells that facilitate angiogenesis and tissue remodeling. CGF can stably release cytokines at a high level from day 14 to day 21 in vitro [21], which is more advantageous than other platelet products [22]. Based on previous reports [14, 23–27], the synergistic effect of the combined therapy can be explained by two complementary mechanisms: (1) Bone perforation improves local blood perfusion and establishes a vascularized microenvironment to supply oxygen and nutrients for osteogenesis; (2) CGF continuously releases high concentrations of TGF β and VEGF within 21 days, activating the SMAD2/3 and PI3K/AKT pathways to promote osteoblast differentiation and extracellular matrix mineralization. These effects jointly accelerate bone regeneration and inhibit the progression of osteonecrosis. Although the related signaling pathways were not directly verified in this study, they may provide a mechanistic basis for future research.

Micro CT results showed that the combination of CGF and cortical bone perforation significantly increased BMD, reduced trabecular separation, and achieved nearly complete alveolar bone regeneration, which was consistent with imaging evidence of effective bone repair Histological evaluation further confirmed that the C + B group presented significantly more new bone and faster healing than other groups. Neither single therapy achieved complete bone regeneration within 4 weeks. The C + B group showed more osteoclasts in the early stage and significantly enhanced bone formation at 4 weeks. We speculated that the increased osteoclast activity and the anti-inflammatory effect of CGF jointly promoted necrotic bone resorption and restored the balance of bone remodeling, thus creating a favorable microenvironment for bone regeneration. These results indicate that the combined application of CGF and bone perforation effectively accelerates BRONJ repair and improves therapeutic efficacy.

RUNX2 is critical for osteoprogenitor differentiation into osteoblasts and regulates the expression of osteogenic genes [28, 29]. ALP is a key mineralization enzyme that hydrolyzes pyrophosphate to provide phosphate for hydroxyapatite deposition [30]. In this study, CGF alone showed stronger early osteogenic effect at 2 weeks, while the combined therapy significantly upregulated RUNX2 and ALP expression and promoted bone regeneration at 4 weeks, which was superior to single treatments. This suggests that CGF drives rapid early healing, and the combination supports sustained and mature bone regeneration, which is consistent with previous reports on the pro regenerative effect of CGF [31–33].

Our findings reveal that while bone perforation alone transiently improves vascular perfusion through mechanical stimulation, its therapeutic efficacy in later stages of repair may be compromised. This is likely due to the formation of large bone defects in the rat model and the interference of zoledronic acid with bone healing, ultimately hindering the repair process. In contrast, the combined application of CGF and bone perforation not only promotes blood supply and activates osteoclasts but also provides a rich source of bioactive factors that help maintain the stability of bone regeneration. Therefore, the therapeutic efficacy CGF combined with bone perforation may represent a more effective strategy for BRONJ treatment, offering a paradigm-shifting approach to improving clinical outcomes in affected patients. However, species differences still exist between rats and humans, and their pathological manifestations are not always fully consistent. In addition, this study is limited by its relatively small sample size and lack of long-term follow-up evaluations. In the future, more large-scale studies are warranted to optimize treatment protocols and evaluate long-term outcomes for more precise clinical guidance.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that the synergistic application of bone perforation and concentrated growth factors (CGF) significantly enhances bone regeneration in bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (BRONJ), achieving superior therapeutic outcomes at both 2 and 4 weeks postoperatively. This may be related to enhancing local blood supply, activating osteoclasts, promoting osteoblast differentiation, and facilitating bone matrix synthesis. This synergistic approach is expected to become a promising strategy for BRONJ management. Future studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods are warranted to verify these findings, which may provide novel insights and evidence-based references for the clinical management of BRONJ.

Clinical role of miR-1271-5p in severe pediatric pneumonia and its prognostic value
Authors: Junlin Chen, Li Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAs a predominant driver of pediatric mortality, severe pediatric pneumonia (SPP) is an important complication to be solved. The miR-1271-5p is universally engaged in pulmonary infection. This study focused on miR-1271-5p for the first time and aimed to reveal its role in SPP.MethodsThe study enrolled 268 children aged 2–10 years, comprising 81 healthy individuals and 187 with pneumonia. The expression level of serum miR-1271-5p was assessed by qRT-PCR. The receiver characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to distinguish pneumonia children from healthy controls. Multivariate logistic regression analyzed factors associated with progression from mild-moderate pneumonia to SPP. Multivariate Cox regression assessed the association of miR-1271-5p expression with poor prognosis, and the Kaplan-Meier survival curve was subsequently generated based on miR-1271-5p levels.ResultsSerum miR-1271-5p levels were remarkably elevated in children with pneumonia, especially SPP. Importantly, miR-1271-5p showed high diagnostic value of SPP, with the AUC of 0.877, the sensitivity of 74.9% and specificity of 87.8%. The results of multivariate logistic analysis demonstrated that miR-1271-5p was an independent risk factor for progression to SPP. Elevated serum miR-1271-5p levels were associated with poor prognosis in children with pneumonia and possessed predictive value for patients’ adverse outcomes.ConclusionElevated serum miR-1271-5p was demonstrating superior diagnostic and prognostic specificity in severe pediatric pneumonia, providing a potential biomarker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12865-026-00851-7.

Introduction

Severe Pediatric Pneumonia (SPP) is still a major global public health problem. It contributes to a considerable burden of morbidity and mortality among children under five years of age. It is a severe inflammatory response in the lungs caused by a pathogenic infection, characterized by rapid progression and severe illness, which may lead to respiratory failure, multi-organ failure, and many other critical conditions [1]. According to data published in the relevant literature, although the mortality rate from childhood pneumonia has declined significantly in recent years, the prevention and treatment of pneumonia remain inadequate in many countries [2, 3]. Consequently, the early identification of markers associated with pneumonia is crucial for preventing and treating severe cases in children, with a focus on reducing mortality. Currently, the clinical diagnosis of SPP mainly dependents on clinical symptoms combing with imaging examinations and specialized etiological examinations [4, 5]. However, the specificity, sensitivity, and patient compliance showed significant limitation in these diagnostic methods [6]. Hence, timely identification of pneumonia-related markers is paramount for averting and managing SPP, thereby reducing mortality.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous non-coding, single-stranded RNA molecules. Alterations in the expression of these molecules result in alterations in a range of biological processes, which in turn lead to many human diseases [7]. Circulating miRNAs have been considered promising biomarker candidates for human diseases, which possess the advantage of non-invasiveness, easy sampling, and low-cost. Previously, miR-1271-5p was identified as one of specific miRNAs of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)-associated with pediatric pneumonia, where its significant upregulation was observed [8]. Additionally, miR-1271-5p has also been revealed to mediate inflammatory response in macrophages, granulose cells, and liver cells, indicating its potential in modulating the development of inflammatory diseases [9–11]. Therefore, based on the dysregulation of miR-1271-5p in RSV-related pediatric pneumonia, it was hypothesized as a promising biomarker for SPP, which needs clinical validations.

In this study, the clinical significance of miR-1271-5p in SPP was evaluated from the perspectives of early screening, severity evaluation, and prognosis prediction.

Materials and methods

Study population

A prospective cohort of 268 pediatric subjects was enrolled at Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Hubei Province comprising: 187 pneumonia patients and 81 healthy controls. Inclusion criteria: the children confirmed by WHO pediatric pneumonia criteria [12]. Exclusion criteria: genetic or congenital disorders; comorbidities: hematological, neurological and immunological dysfunctions; major organ insufficiency (cardiac, hepatic, renal) or malignancies; recent trauma or surgery (≤ 3 months); extrapulmonary infections. Informed consent was obtained from the families of all subjects. The enrolled pneumonia patients were further classified into mild-moderate pneumonia (n = 87) and SPP (n = 100) groups based on the revised WHO criteria for severe community-acquired pneumonia and established clinical guidelines [12–14]. Mild-moderate pneumonia: Presence of core clinical manifestations (cough, fever, tachypnea with age-specific respiratory rate thresholds: >40 breaths/min for 2–5 years old, > 30 breaths/min for 5–10 years old), auscultatory rales on lung examination, and pulmonary infiltration on chest imaging; no respiratory failure, hypoxemia, multi-organ dysfunction, or need for respiratory support. SPP was defined by the presence of at least one of the following major criteria: (1) signs of respiratory distress, such as lower chest wall indrawing, nasal flaring, or grunting; (2) hypoxemia (peripheral oxygen saturation, SpO₂ < 90% or arterial oxygen partial pressure/inspired oxygen fraction ratio, PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 250 mmHg); or (3) general danger signs, including altered mental status, convulsions, or inability to feed. The study protocol was approved by the medical ethics committee of the Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Hubei Province.

The prognosis was assessed, and 28 days were followed up [15, 16]. Patients with stable or improved prognosis were considered to have a good prognosis, while those with multiple organ failure or death were considered poor prognosis.

Blood sample collection

Fasting venous blood from all participants was collected for biomarker detection at a strictly defined time: for children with pneumonia, blood samples were obtained within 24 h of hospital admission (at the early stage of clinical diagnosis and before the implementation of standardized anti-infection and supportive treatment), with the interval from the onset of pneumonia-related symptoms to sampling being 1–3 days (median: 2 days); for healthy controls, blood samples were collected during routine health check-ups with no respiratory symptoms. All blood samples were allowed to stand at room temperature for 0.5 h, followed by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min. The separated serum samples were immediately stored at − 80 ℃ for subsequent detection and analysis. Serum indicator testing.

The automatic biochemical analyzer was employed for the measurement of C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cells (WBC), procalcitonin (PCT), lymphocytes, and neutrophils. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) was the value of neutrophil count/lymphocyte count.

qRT-PCR

Trizol (Invitrogen, CN) was used for RNA extraction to estimate the expression level of miR-1271-5p. The concentration and purity of the RNA samples were assessed by the absorbance at A260 nm and the A260/A280 ration. The RNAs were obtained and transcribed into cDNA by reverse transcription kit (Qiagen, DE) in a timely manner. The cDNA was then stored at − 20 °C. To measure the expression level of miR-1271-5p, we used a quantitative analysis kit (Takara, JPN) and the 2 − ΔΔCt method with the U6 internal reference.

Statistical methods

Initial data processing used SPSS 23.0 with advanced analyses conducted in GraphPad Prism 9.5. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD, analyzed by Student’s t-test and Chi-Square test, respectively. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess the overall discriminatory capacity of miR-1271-5p. The optimal cutoff value was determined by the Youden index (maximum [sensitivity + specificity – 1]), and the corresponding sensitivity and specificity were reported. The risk of SPP was predicted using univariate logistic regression. To further verify whether these variables are independent risk factors and control for confounding effects, we constructed a multivariate logistic regression model to identify the independent risk factors for progression from mild-moderate pneumonia to SPP. Kaplan-Meier curve with log-rank test was employed to show the difference in cumulative survival rate between miR-1271-5p levels. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was applied to identify risk factors associated with prognosis in SPP. P < 0.05 indicated a statistical difference.

Diagnostic performance of miR-1271-5p

The serum miR-1271-5p level was raised (P < 0.001) in children with pneumonia (Fig. 1A). Diagnostic efficacy of serum miR-1271-5p was rigorously evaluated through ROC curve analysis. For pneumonia detection versus healthy controls, miR-1271-5p demonstrated exceptional discriminative capacity (AUC = 0.877), with an optimal cutoff value of 1.175 determined by the Youden index, yielding a sensitivity and specificity were 74.9% and a 87.8% respectively (Fig. 1B).

Additionally, the serum level of miR-1271-5p was significantly increased in SPP compared to mild-moderate pneumonia (Fig. 2A). The ROC curve analysis for distinguishing children with mild-moderate pneumonia from those with SPP showed that miR-1271-5p could discriminate disease severity with an AUC of 0.841. At the optimal cutoff value of 1.345, the sensitivity and specificity were 72.0% and 86.2%, respectively (Fig. 2B).

Consequently, subsequent univariate logistic regression demonstrated that increased miR-1271-5p (OR = 7.853, 95% CI: 4.088–15.086, P < 0.001), CRP (OR = 6.072, 95% CI: 3.220-11.448, P < 0.001), WBC (OR = 3.161, 95% CI: 1.731–5.771, P < 0.001), PCT (OR = 5.185, 95% CI: 2.779–9.676, P < 0.001) and NLR (OR = 2.058, 95% CI:1.140–3.717, P = 0.017) and inhibited PaO2/FiO2 (OR = 0.105, 95% CI: 0.050–0.205, P < 0.001) were independent risk factors for progression from mild-moderate pneumonia to SPP (Fig. 2C). To identify independent predictors of progression, multivariate logistic regression demonstrated that increased miR-1271-5p (OR = 3.100, 95% CI: 1.366–7.036, P = 0.007), CRP (OR = 2.394, 95% CI: 1.084–5.286, P = 0.031), PCT (OR = 2.263, 95% CI: 1.061–4.829, P = 0.035) and decreased PaO2/FiO2 (OR = 0.341, 95% CI: 0.151–0.771, P = 0.01) were independent risk factors for progression to SPP (Fig. 2D).

Association between MiR-1271-5p expression and clinicopathological indicators in SPP

Regarding the disease severity of SPP, miR-1271-5p was negatively correlated with their PaO₂/FiO₂ (r = -0.583). While for the severity of inflammatory response, miR-1271-5p was positively correlated with CRP (r = 0.581), WBC (r = 0.511), PCT (r = 0.601), and NLR (r = 0.641, Table 2).

Prognostic significance of miR-1271-5p in SPP

The subjects were divided into two groups based on the progression of the disease and survival status: the good prognosis group (n = 73) and the poor prognosis group (n = 27). The levels of age, WBC, PCT, and NLR were markedly elevated, while there was a significant decrease with PaO2/FiO2 in children with the adverse prognosis (Table 3). The proportion of mixed infection in the poor prognosis group (44.44%) was significantly higher than that in the good prognosis group (13.70%), while the proportion of viral infection in the poor prognosis group (18.52%) was significantly lower (P = 0.001). Serum miR-1271-5p levels were higher in children in the unfavorable prognosis group (Fig. 3A). The Kaplan-Meier curve proved that the cumulative survival rate in the high miR-1271-5p group exhibited a notable decline in comparison to the low miR-1271-5p group (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, to avoid model overfitting and comply with the EPV > 10 statistical principle (27 poor prognosis events, maximum 2 covariates), multivariate Cox regression was simplified to include only two core prognostic variables: miR-1271-5p (the key biomarker) and PaO₂/FiO₂ (the gold-standard clinical indicator of pulmonary oxygenation). The results demonstrated that elevated miR-1271-5p level (HR = 2.703, 95% CI: 1.164–6.277, P = 0.021) and decreased PaO₂/FiO₂ level (HR = 0.177, 95% CI: 0.065–0.480, P = 0.001) were increasing risk factors for adverse outcomes in SPP (Fig. 3C).

Multivariate Logistic and Cox regression analyses were performed to explore the independent effects of age, etiology and miR-1271-5p on the severity and prognosis of pneumonia in children (Table S1). Taking viral infection as the reference group, the results showed that mixed infection was an independent risk factor for SPP (OR = 3.260, 95%CI:1.092 ~ 9.732, P = 0.034) and poor prognosis (HR = 3.161, 95%CI:1.067 ~ 9.369, P = 0.038). More importantly, miR-1271-5p was an independent risk factor for both SPP (OR = 6.089, 95%CI:3.062 ~ 12.107, P < 0.001) and poor prognosis (HR = 3.698, 95%CI:1.510 ~ 9.056, P = 0.004) in children, while age had no significant independent effect on the severity and prognosis of pneumonia (all P > 0.05).

Discussion

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are critically involved in the pathogenesis and clinical management of pneumonia [8, 17]. MiR-125b-5p is significantly downregulated in SPP, leading to an increase in inflammatory markers such as CRP by inhibiting the activity of pro-inflammatory pathways such as NF-κ B [18, 19]. Similarly, miR-223 and miR-127 are highly expressed in children with pneumonia, promoting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and exacerbating organ damage [20, 21], which can be used to distinguish pneumonia from healthy children. These studies indicate the potential of miRNA as a biomarker for the early diagnosis of pediatric pneumonia.

In this study, it was found that the children with pneumonia had significantly increased levels of serum miR-1271-5p levels. Notably, the discriminative capacity of miR-1271-5p (AUC = 0.877) in ROC analysis suggested that it played a unique role in early pneumonia detection. On the other hand, serum miR-1271-5p levels, as well as CRP, PCT, WBC, and NLR (Neutrophil to Lymphocyte Ratio), which were used to reflect the state of inflammation or immune imbalance, was also identified as risk factors for progression to SPP. Clinical inflammatory indicators such as CRP, PCT, and WBC were proved that it was related to the development of pneumonia [22]. PCT was a biomarker commonly used to evaluate the severity of bacterial infection and monitor the treatment effect, which was significantly increased in the occurrence of bacterial infection or sepsis [23]. CRP and PCT had similar effects and were used to distinguish bacterial and viral infections with poor specificity [24]. Similarly, WBC was an important immune cell in the human body. When its value was out of a certain range, it indicated that infection and inflammation occurred [25]. Moreover, PaO2/FiO2 is a critical clinical feature assessing the severity of SPP, which could directly reflect the status of pulmonary oxygenation function [26, 27]. More importantly, PaO2/FiO2 has been included in the stratified diagnosis criteria for SPP, and it has been demonstrated to predict disease progression and patients’ outcomes [28]. Further correlation analysis found that the higher serum miR-1271-5p levels were correlated with more severe inflammatory states and decreased PaO2/FiO2, suggesting that miR-1271-5p is associated with disease severity and may play role in regulating the inflammatory process. The above results demonstrated that the levels of miR-1271-5p enhanced the early recognition of SPP as well as were a potential biomarker with stronger specificity and sensitivity than PCT and other inflammatory indicators.

The most significant clinical application of the biomarker was in predicting disease progression. Untreated mild-moderate pneumonia frequently progresses to SPP, resulting in acute respiratory failure and multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) [29] within a few days to weeks [30, 31]. Recent studies have manifested that miRNA plays an pivotal role in predicting prognosis of pneumonia, which suggests that it has the potential to severe as a widely utilized prognostic biomarkers [32]. Specifically, miRNAs such as miR-21-5p and miR-144 can serve as biomarkers capable of diagnosing and quantifying the severity of COVID-19 [33]. miR-1271-5p was confirmed to predict the poor prognosis in SPP children. Consistently, the KM survival curve showed similar results, solidifying its prognostic utility. Therefore, serum miR-1271-5p could also be considered a promising prognostic predictor, assisting the adjustment of intervention strategies of SPP.

Pneumonia etiology is an important factor affecting the severity and prognosis of childhood pneumonia. Our study found that the proportion of mixed infection in the poor prognosis group was significantly higher, and multivariate regression analysis confirmed that mixed infection was an independent risk factor for SPP and poor prognosis in children, which is consistent with clinical practice that mixed infection can trigger superimposed inflammatory storms and increase the difficulty of clinical treatment. After adjusting for age and etiology, miR-1271-5p still showed a significant independent risk effect, indicating that the predictive value of miR-1271-5p for the severity and prognosis of childhood pneumonia is not affected by pathogenic factors. In addition, serum samples for miR-1271-5p detection in this study were collected at admission before any antibiotic treatment was administered to the children, so the expression level of miR-1271-5p was not affected by subsequent antibiotic treatment-related factors such as the appropriateness and timing of antibiotic use, which further confirmed the stability of miR-1271-5p as a biomarker for childhood pneumonia.

While routine clinical diagnosis of pediatric pneumonia relies on medical history, physical examination, and chest imaging, these approaches have inherent limitations—particularly in early screening and severity stratification [24, 34]. Clinical signs may be atypical in young children, early imaging changes can be non-specific, and mild cases often lack timely confirmation. Serum miR-1271-5p, as a non-invasive molecular biomarker, offers distinct added value beyond these standard approaches. First, it provides objective, quantitative laboratory evidence for early diagnosis, which is especially valuable for children with atypical clinical manifestations or ambiguous early imaging findings, potentially reducing misdiagnosis and missed diagnosis. Second, it enables rapid severity stratification at the time of initial diagnosis (AUC = 0.841 for distinguishing severe from mild-moderate pneumonia), addressing the inability of traditional methods to timely predict progression risk. Third, its non-invasive sampling, simple operation, and rapid detection make it suitable for large-scale early screening, particularly in primary care settings with limited imaging resources. Thus, miR-1271-5p may serve as a complementary tool to support clinical judgment—not replacing standard diagnostic approaches, but enhancing early risk identification and guiding more intensive monitoring or intervention for high-risk children. Future prospective studies could explore whether integrating miR-1271-5p into clinical algorithms improves risk stratification and patient outcomes beyond current standard care.

While this study establishes a clear association between elevated serum miR-1271-5p and the severity and poor prognosis of pediatric pneumonia, the underlying molecular mechanisms warrant further exploration. Mechanistically, the role of miR-1271-5p in pneumonia progression may be inferred from its known functions in inflammatory regulation. A study by Luo et al. [11]. demonstrated that miR-1271-5p directly targets HDAC9 (Histone Deacetylase 9) and modulates the NF‑κB pathway in macrophages, a key cell type in pulmonary inflammation. This provides a plausible and directly testable mechanism linking elevated miR‑1271‑5p to exacerbated inflammatory responses in SPP. In addition to the HDAC9/NF‑κB axis, other signaling pathways have been reported to interact with miR‑1271‑5p in various disease contexts. For instance, studies in cancer models have shown that miR‑1271‑5p can regulate the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway via targets such as IGF1R [35]. Similar observations have been made in inflammatory models, such as LPS‑stimulated retinal pigment epithelial cells, where miR‑1271‑5p was inversely correlated with PI3K/AKT/mTOR‑associated transcripts [36]. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway has been confirmed to participate in the regulation of inflammatory responses, immune cell activation, and tissue injury in pneumonia [37–39]. However, it is important to note that direct evidence linking miR‑1271‑5p to these pathways in the setting of infectious pneumonia is currently lacking. Therefore, we hypothesize that miR‑1271‑5p may primarily promote pulmonary inflammation via the HDAC9/NF‑κB pathway in macrophages, with potential additional contributions from other signaling axes (e.g., PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Wnt/β‑catenin) that warrant dedicated experimental validation. This hypothesis is consistent with our clinical observations of strong correlations between miR‑1271‑5p levels and inflammatory markers (CRP, PCT, NLR) as well as oxygenation impairment (PaO₂/FiO₂). Future in vitro studies using macrophage or lung epithelial cell lines under pathogen‑mimicking conditions, along with in vivo pneumonia models, are essential to confirm the specific targets and pathways through which miR‑1271‑5p contributes to disease severity.

This study has several limitations that should be addressed in future research. First, the sample source of critically ill pneumonia patients is single, which may restrict the generalizability of our conclusions. Future multicenter studies with larger and more diverse populations across different geographic regions and clinical settings are needed to validate the reliability and universality of our findings. Second, although etiological data were available and included in multivariate analyses, the sample sizes within certain pathogen subgroups (particularly mixed infections) were relatively small, limiting statistical power to detect potential differences in the predictive performance of miR-1271-5p across etiologies. Future studies with larger, well-characterized cohorts should perform subgroup analyses stratified by pathogen type to determine whether the diagnostic and prognostic value of miR-1271-5p varies between bacterial, viral, and mixed infections. Third, treatment-related factors—particularly the timing and appropriateness of antibiotic therapy—were not recorded, although these are critical determinants of clinical outcomes in pneumonia. While baseline miR‑1271‑5p was measured prior to antibiotic initiation (minimising confounding by treatment), the more clinically relevant question is whether this biomarker can predict progression to severe disease or poor outcomes even after appropriate antibiotic therapy has been implemented. Our finding that elevated baseline miR‑1271‑5p levels were associated with poor prognosis suggests that high expression of this miRNA may identify children whose inflammatory response is already so intense that it cannot be fully mitigated by standard antibiotic therapy alone. In other words, miR‑1271‑5p may serve as a marker of “treatment‑resistant” disease progression. However, the current study design cannot directly validate this hypothesis, as we did not collect serial samples or post‑treatment outcomes stratified by antibiotic response. Future prospective studies should collect blood samples both at admission and after a defined period of appropriate antibiotic therapy, and incorporate detailed treatment data (antibiotic type, timing, duration, and microbiological susceptibility). Such studies could evaluate whether miR‑1271‑5p levels—or their dynamic changes—can guide escalation of care (e.g., ICU admission, adjunctive immunomodulatory therapies) in patients who deteriorate despite standard‑of‑care antibiotics. Fourth, while we established miR-1271-5p as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, our research remains at the phenotypic level; the precise molecular mechanisms through which miR-1271-5p regulates pneumonia progression were not explored. Based on existing literature, we hypothesize that miR-1271-5p may promote pulmonary inflammation through the HDAC9/NF-κB pathway in macrophages [11]. Future studies should focus on elucidating the cellular and molecular mechanisms of miR-1271-5p using in vitro macrophage models and in vivo animal experiments of pneumonia, and further investigate whether targeting this pathway could have therapeutic potential.

In summary, miR-1271-5p is highly expressed in children with pneumonia and is a potent predictor of disease development and prognosis in children with SPP. This provides a new research direction for early, accurate diagnosis and targeted intervention.

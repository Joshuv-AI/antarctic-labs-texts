The impact of ESG rating changes on fund shareholding
Authors: Zhang Jianfeng, Zhao Zexin, Zhang Lutong, Fu Mingyang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines the impact of environmental, social and governance (ESG) rating changes on fund shareholding based on rating data from five agencies. Using a sample of Shanghai and Shenzhen A-share listed companies in China spanning from 2019 to 2021, we find robust evidence that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies positively impact the number of fund holdings, while the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies have no impact on the number of fund holdings. Additionally, the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies have a greater impact on the number of fund holdings among state-owned and industrial companies, and the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies has a larger impact on the number of fund holdings in a one-year window than in a six-month window. Moreover, we find that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies negatively impact the stock price volatility. The results show that ESG rating changes play a positive role in the decision-making of fund shareholding and in promoting the sustainable development of firms.Keywords: ESG rating, ESG rating changes, Fund shareholding, Stock price volatility

Introduction

ESG rating is a comprehensive evaluation conducted by rating agencies on the performance of enterprises in environment, social responsibility and corporate governance, and it can reflect the enterprise's future development prospects and competitive position in the market. Investors and listed companies in the capital market will complete their investment decisions as well as corporate governance reforms and value improvement based on the ESG rating information issued by professional rating agencies [1]. The scale of ESG investment is growing rapidly as the concept of ESG investment is deeply promoted globally [2]. According to a report by the Global Sustainable Investment Alliance (GSIA), by the end of 2022, the scale of sustainable investment in the world's four major regions and countries has been reached $21.9 trillion, up by 20 % in the same period of last year. In 2020, China formally proposed a "dual carbon" target, and called for achieving carbon peaking by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2060. ESG investments have also seen rapid growth [3]. According to Wind Information, a provider of financial data and analysis tools in China, as of the end of September 2023, the number of institutions in China that have joined the United Nations-supported Principles for Responsible Investment ("UNPRI") has reached 142, and the total assets under management of public equity funds have increased by 58.82 % from 3.54 % in six years. More and more ESG funds formulate investment strategies and construct investment portfolios based on the ESG rating [4], and ESG rating play an increasingly important role in guiding ESG investments. For investors, they consider both the financial performance and ESG performance of firms when adjusting their investment strategy, so ESG ratings have become an essential indicator for reference. From the perspective of firms view, if they want to enhance their sustainability and competitiveness, they should pay attention to non-financial performance and improve ESG ratings. From the national perspective, the harmonious coexistence of humans and nature has become a noteworthy issue due to the COVID-19.

However, there are some problems in utilizing ESG rating [5].ESG rating originate from abroad, and due to the large differences between China's economic system and national conditions and those of other countries, the experience of foreign ESG rating system construction can not be used directly [4], and there is a lack of a unified and standardized ESG evaluation system in China [6]. China has more than 20 ESG rating agencies, of which five are the most influential, namely: Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance, Wind, FTSE Russell and Menglang. The evaluation systems and standards of each agency are different, and there are great differences in the selection of evaluation indicators, the determination of indicator weights, and the use of ESG disclosure information, leading to large differences in the rating results of the same listed company [7].This indicates that ESG rating changes not only show the trend of changes in the rating of the same agency for the same firm over time, but also the differences in the changes in the ratings of different agencies for the same firm at the same time. Does this characteristic impact the extent on which investors refer to the ESG ratings of the agencies? Currently, the mainstream of ESG investment is ESG funds. How do ESG rating changes impact fund shareholding? Existing studies have not adequately examined these questions. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the impact of ESG rating and their changes on the investment decisions of ESG funds in the special context of China, in order to increase the scale of ESG investment and contribute to the role of China's capital in solving the shortage of funds for sustainable development.

Existing research on ESG rating changes covers five main areas: (1) Relationship between ESG rating changes and corporate financial performance. A large number of empirical studies have demonstrated that ESG rating is positively correlated with corporate financial performance [8,9], but there is disagreement in the heterogeneous analysis of ownership nature, with one viewpoint suggesting that the boosting effect is more significant for non-state-owned firms (NSOEs) than state-owned firms (SOEs) [10], and another viewpoint suggesting the opposite [11]. However, ESG rating divergence is negatively associated with firms' financial performance [12] [13]; (2) Relationship between ESG rating changes and corporate value. A large number of studies have shown that ESG rating positively affects firm value [14,15] and ESG rating divergence negatively affects firm value [16]. In addition, existing studies have found that ESG rating can affect firm value through multiple channels, including media and analyst attention [17], firm risk [18], equity structure [19], and profitability [20]; (3) Relationship between ESG rating changes and corporate risk. it has been shown that high ESG rating can reduce corporate risk through the degree of financial constraints [21], the degree of information asymmetry [22], and investor sentiment [23], while high ESG rating divergence can increase firm risk [24]; (4) Relationship between ESG rating changes and investor. Existing research shows that more and more institutional investors are paying attention to changes in corporate ESG ratings [25], however, the large divergence in ESG ratings results is a major obstacle to ESG investment decisions [26]. At the same time, ESG rating disagreement also reduces the value discovery function of ESG information and increases the cost of information gathering for investors [27,28], and ESG rating disagreement does not provide incremental information for predicting a company's future ESG performance [3]; (5)Relationship between ESG rating changes and stock price volatility. It has been documented that the stock price volatility of firms with high ESG rating is significantly lower than that of firms with low ESG rating [24,29,30]. However, ESG divergence is negatively correlated with stock price volatility [24].

This study differs from existing studies in three main ways: (1) Our study explores the relationship between the ESG rating changes and fund shareholding. We use data from five rating agencies in China: Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance, Wind, FTSE Russell and Menglang. We find that the mean value of ESG ratings have positively impact on the number of fund holdings. ESG ratings of Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang have an impact on the number of fund holdings. In contrast, the ESG ratings of the other agencies have no impact on the number of fund holdings. This indicates that most ESG fund managers choose an agency's ESG rating as a reference when adjusting their investment strategies, with the largest number of ESG fund managers referring to Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang. The results of heterogeneity analysis show that state-owned and industrial companies have a greater impact on the number of fund holdings. Moreover, we find that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies negatively impact on the stock price volatility. (2) The sub-dimension study of ESG rating results was conducted. This study divides ESG into three dimensions: environment E, social responsibility S, and corporate governance G. The results show that corporate governance dimension rating and environmental dimension rating are significant with the number of fund holdings, and social responsibility dimension rating is not significantly correlated with the number of fund holdings. This indicates that the better the performance of listed companies in the environmental and corporate governance dimensions, the higher the number of fund holdings by ESG fund managers. The results of the study are more instructive for ESG investment.(3) This paper analyses the relationship between the standard deviation of ESG ratings and fund shareholding and stock price volatility. the results show that the standard deviation of ESG ratings have no impact on the number of fund holdings. The reason for this is that ESG fund managers will only choose the ESG rating of one rating agency as a reference when adjusting their investment strategies, so using the standard deviation of ESG ratings of the five rating agencies as the independent variable will generate a significant error. The standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies is significantly negatively correlated with stock price volatility.

This study aims to combine sustainability theory, the theory of corporate social responsibility, stakeholder theory, asymmetric information theory and reputation theory to construct an analytical framework that includes ESG rating changes, rating disagreement, fund shareholding, and stock price volatility. Taking Chinese A-share listed companies continuously held by ESG funds from 2019 to 2021 as the research sample, this study empirically examines the impact of ESG rating changes on fund shareholding and stock price volatility using ESG ratings from various agencies, explores the heterogeneous impact of relevant factors from the dimensions of the nature of ownership and the industry, and conducts various robustness tests.

The research contribution of this study is summarized in: First, it focuses on ESG rating changes and fund shareholding, enriching research on the economic consequences of ESG rating changes and the factors influencing ESG fund shareholding. Existing studies mainly analyze the economic consequences of ESG rating changes in terms of corporate financial performance, value, risk, investor and stock price volatility, and studies based on the factors influencing fund shareholding mainly focus on traditional funds rather than ESG funds. This study investigates the impact of the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies, ESG sub-dimension ratings, ESG ratings of each agency, and ESG disagreement on fund shareholding, which not only expands the research results on ESG rating changes and fund shareholding at the theoretical level, but also helps to understand the reference value of ESG rating in the decision-making process of fund portfolio. It is an important addition to the existing researches. Second, it has important practical significance for investors, firms, ESG rating agencies and regulators. For investors, adjusting investment strategies with ESG rating is conducive to enhancing their interests. For firms, it is essential to improve ESG ratings, so as to enhance their sustainability and competitiveness. For ESG rating agencies, it is important to refine their rating system to enhance the reference value of the ratings. For regulators, it is necessary to standardize rating criteria and strengthen the consistency of different agencies’ ratings.

Literature review and Hypothesis development

ESG rating changes and fund shareholding

ESG funds prefer firms with high ESG rating. First, ESG rating is a comprehensive assessment by rating agency of the performance of corporate environmental, social and governance. Suppose a company has a high ESG rating, it can be assumed that it has a strong sense of environmental protection, can take social responsibility, and its corporate governance can enable it to develop healthily and stably. Companies with better environmental information disclosure [31,32], social responsibility [33,34], and higher corporate governance [35] are more favoured by ESG funds. Second, the theory of sustainable development put forward by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) requires us to strike a balance among the three dimensions of the economy, society and the environment, ensuring sustained economic growth while paying attention to social equity and the well-being of people's livelihoods, and at the same time preserving the health and stability of the ecosystem. So firms should actively fulfil their social responsibilities and focus on the harmonious coexistence of relevant stakeholders [36]. At the same time, they should actively transform into environmentally-friendly and resource-saving enterprises to achieve sustainable development, which coincides with the three dimensions of ESG indicators [37]. A Firm with high ESG rating can be considered to have higher sustainability. According to the reputation theory proposed by Kreps et al. market participants influence their behavior and decisions by maintaining their reputation. A Firm with high ESG rating earn better reputation by protecting the environment, being socially responsible and efficiently managing internal affairs [38,39]. Companies with better reputations have better stock market performance and more robust operating performance [40], so it can be assumed that high ESG rating can generate better market expectations. Third, ESG rating significantly correlate with corporate financial performance, value, and risk. If a firm has high ESG rating, it shows that the company has better financial performance, higher value and less risk in the future. Therefore, investors will choose firms with high ESG rating for investment, so we propose that.

The mean value of ESG ratings by agencies positively impacts the number of fund holdings.

The ESG ratings of each agency positively impact the number of fund holdings.

The sub-dimension ratings of ESG positively impact the number of fund holdings.

ESG funds prefer firms with less ESG rating divergence. Currently, China has many ESG rating agencies, such as Wind and Huazheng. Rating agency will comprehensively evaluate the same firm. Due to the different evaluation systems, standards and weights of the dimensions used in the rating process, the results obtained are different [41,42]. If ESG ratings of a firm diverge across ESG rating agencies, it indicates that the firm's performance is unstable across different rating systems and criteria. If ESG ratings of a firm by different rating agencies are consistent, it means that the rating agencies recognize the company to the same extent under different evaluation systems and criteria, and the credibility of its ESG rating will enhance. Therefore, investors will prefer companies with minor rating divergence in the investment process [43], so we propose that.

The standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies negatively impact the number of fund holdings.

Heterogeneity analysis

Based on the above research, this study examines whether the impact between the ESG rating changes and the fund shareholding varies depending on the nature of ownership and the industry.

Firstly, firms have different ownership natures, and there are differences in the state's management system and social feedback. For state-owned firms, the state's management of their environment, social and corporate governance are stricter, and the public defaults that they can consciously protect the environment, fulfil their social responsibility and efficiently govern the company's internal management, and are more comprehensive and accurate in ESG disclosure [11]. Therefore, investors have greater reference to ESG rating when formulating investment strategies in state-owned firms. For non-state-owned firms, the state management is not as strict as the state-owned firms. To pursue more benefits, they will give up to improve the performance of corporate environment, social and governance. Moreover, there is no mandatory disclosure of ESG information in China, so non-state-owned firms may choose to disclose part of the information that is favourable to them, the accuracy of ESG rating will be reduced. Therefore, when investors formulate investment strategies, the degree of reference to ESG rating of non-state-owned firms is smaller [44].

Secondly, different industries have different characteristics, firms belong to different industries, so investors have different degrees of reference to ESG rating when formulating investment strategies. For the industry, due to its more significant impact on the environment, the state regulates its disclosure of information on the environment and other aspects more strictly, and ESG rating agencies get more comprehensive information. Therefore, the public believes that the ESG rating of industrial industries are more accurate, and investors have a higher degree of reference to their ESG rating [24]. On the contrary, the state's regulation of non-industrial ESG disclosure is less, the information obtained by ESG rating agencies is not comprehensive enough, so the accuracy of the ESG ratings is reduced, investors have a lower degree of reference to the ESG rating of non-industrial companies. On this basis, we proposes the following hypotheses.

The mean value and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies have a more significant impact on the number of fund holdings among state-owned firms.

The mean value and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies have a more significant impact on the number of fund holdings among industrial firms.

Further study

It has been demonstrated that ESG rating changes can significantly impact stock price volatility [24] and that institutional investor holdings are one of the influencing factors of stock price volatility [45]. This study explores the impact mechanism between ESG rating changes and stock price volatility. Funds are the primary way for investors to practice ESG concepts [46]. From the point of view of the development of the U.S. market, the number and scale of ESG-themed funds have developed rapidly in recent years. U.S. sustainable funds had more than $357 billion in assets by the end of 2021, twice as much as in 2019, according to Morning Star. ESG-related financial assets under management in the U.S. reached $8.4 trillion by the end of 2022, or about 12.6 % of total U.S. custody, according to Sustainable and Responsible Investment Forum (US SIF) statistics. In China's market, the number and scale of ESG funds have also shown significant growth and investment types are gradually enriched.Currently, the main ESG investors in Chinese capital market are mainly institutional investors [47],ESG investment products are mainly ESG funds [46]. According to Everbright Securities' research data as of July 2023, the product line of ESG funds has been expanding and segmented to include active equity funds, immobile index funds, and bond funds [48], equity and equity-biased hybrid ESG funds account for 88 % of the total [2]. In 2023, the size of China's ESG funds continued to grow at a rapid pace, with total size rising 34.4 % from 2022 to a record high [49].The ability of ESG funds to promote corporate social value through due diligence management has been proven in practice [50,51]. China's ESG funds have higher risk-adjusted returns than traditional funds and more diversified portfolios [52].

Fund institutions have professional talents and are more specialized in judgment, data analysis and prediction than small and medium-sized investors. Therefore, fund holdings can increase the confidence of small and medium-sized investors, which in turn can increase the stability of stock prices. In addition, funds usually do not sell their stocks easily, which also helps to stabilize the stock price [45]. Combined with the previous analysis, this study proposes the following hypotheses for deeper research.

The mean value of ESG ratings by agencies negatively impacts the stock price volatility.

The standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies positively impact the stock price volatility.

Research design

Data and sample selection

This paper selects Chinese A-share listed companies held by Wind ESG fund from 2019 to 2021 as samples, and panel data are formed with a half-year cycle. To ensure that the data are reasonable and valid, the sample data are processed as follows: (1) remove the samples with missing or abnormal data (2) remove the samples labeled as ST, ST∗, and PT to prevent them from influencing the regression results. Among them, ST is the abbreviation of Special treatment, which refers to the listed company due to the financial losses for two consecutive years, its stock trading was implemented by the China Stock Exchange for special treatment; ST∗ refers to the listed company due to the financial losses for three consecutive years, its stock trading was implemented by the China Stock Exchange for delisting risk warning; PT is the abbreviation of Particular PT is the abbreviation of Particular Transfer, which means that the stock of a listed company is stopped from any trading by the stock exchange and the price is zeroed out, waiting for delisting (3) remove the samples in the financial industry as defined by the CSMAR database. After the above screening process, 414 sets of observations are finally obtained. The data on ESG rating of listed companies and the number of fund holdings are from the Wind database, the rest of the data are from the CSMAR database.

Independent variable

ESG rating changes. ESG rating changes not only show the trend of changes in the rating of the same agency for the same firm over time, but also the differences in the changes in the rating of different agencies for the same firm at the same time. The independent variables selected in this study are the mean value of ESG ratings by five agencies(ESG_JZ), the ESG rating of each agency, the environmental dimension of ESG rating(E), the social dimension of ESG rating(S), the governance dimension of ESG rating (G), and the standard deviation of ESG ratings by five agencies(ESG_BZC). Among them, the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies is obtained by averaging the ESG ratings of each agency, and the larger the value is, the higher the ESG rating of the firm is; the ESG rating of each agency is the ESG rating data of Wind, Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance, Menglang and FTSE Russell, the larger the value is, the higher the ESG rating of the firm is; ESG sub-dimension ratings are the sub-dimension ratings of firms in the Wind database, the larger the value is, the higher the firm's rating is in the dimension; the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies is the standard deviation for calculating the ratings of each agency, and the larger the value is, the worse the consistency of ESG rating of firms is.

For the quantification of data related to ESG rating changes, this study refers to the existing literature [24], which assigns the lowest rank of each agency's ESG ratings to 1, and then the value is increased by 1 for each subsequent increase in the rank.In addition to this, the ESG rating is standardized using the Max-Min extreme variance method, which is calculated in Equation (2).

Lastly, given that fund shareholding is influenced by a variety of factors, ESG rating changes may not affect fund shareholding in real time, for which the existing literature usually examines the impact of the current year's independent variable on the next year's dependent variable [54,55]. However, Chinese ESG rating agencies update their ratings on average every six months., so the research period in this study is set to six months. To meet the one-year lag in consistency with existing literature, all independent variables are lagged by two periods in the regression analysis.

Control variable

To rule out other possible explanations for our research question, we control for a couple of variables, including ROE—which measures the level of return on shareholders' equity in terms of the ratio of net profit to net assets. A higher value of it means that the company is more profitable and may bring higher returns in the future. Therefore, fund managers will use it as an important reference for stock selection. PER—which measures the investment value of a stock in terms of the ratio of the price of the stock per share to the earnings per share. It helps fund managers determine the valuation level of a stock and is one of the key factors in assessing the investment value of a stock and making holding decisions. OPR—which measures the rate of operating revenue growth of a company in terms of the ratio of driving revenue growth to last year's total operating revenue. Companies with high OPR imply rapid business expansion, increased market share, and high future earnings potential, often attracting the attention of fund managers. BM—a measure of stock return in terms of the ratio of a company's total assets to its total market capitalization. When selecting stocks, fund managers will tend to choose stocks with relatively low BM that are undervalued by the market in anticipation of higher returns in the future. LEV—a measure of the long-term solvency and risk faced by a company in terms of the ratio of its total liabilities to its total assets. It serves as an important indicator of a company's financial condition and has a significant impact on the funds shareholding. If LEV is too high, the fund managers will consider the company's financial risk to be high, and choose to avoid or reduce its holdings of the stock. Column (1) demonstrates the impact of the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies on the fund shareholding, and the results show that the coefficient of the impact between the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings is 4.932 and significant at the 1 % level. This indicates that an increase in a firm's ESG rating is associated with an increase in the number of fund holdings, consistent with H1a. Specifically, a higher ESG rating for a company usually means that it demonstrates better performance in environmental protection, social responsibility fulfillment and corporate governance. According to the theories of sustainable development and corporate social responsibility, companies with high ESG rating tend to be considered more reliable investment choices. As a result, ESG funds will favor such companies.

Columns (2) to (6) demonstrate the impact of each agency's ESG ratings on the fund shareholding, and the results show that only the ESG ratings of Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang have an impact on the number of fund holdings at the 1 % level. In contrast, the ESG ratings of the other agencies have no impact on the number of fund holdings. This indicates that most ESG fund managers choose an agency's ESG rating as a reference when adjusting their investment strategies, with the largest number of ESG fund managers referring to Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang.

Columns (7) through (9) demonstrate the impact of ESG sub-dimension ratings on fund shareholding, and the results show that corporate governance dimension rating is significant at the 1 % level with the number of fund holdings, environmental dimension rating is significant at the 10 % level with the number of fund holdings and social responsibility dimension rating is not significantly correlated with the number of fund holdings. This indicates that the better the performance of listed companies in the environmental and corporate governance dimensions, the higher the number of fund holdings by ESG fund managers, and performance in the social responsibility dimension does not have an impact on the number of fund holdings, which is consistent with part H1c.There are two explanations for these results.First, China currently has a relatively perfect social responsibility information disclosure mechanism, investors' judgment of corporate social responsibility performance is mainly focused on its information disclosure rather than sub-dimensional ratings, which leads to no significant correlation between the performance of social responsibility dimensions and the number of fund holdings. Second, the Wind database only contains ESG rating data for recent years, which is less referenced by investors. The ESG sub-dimension ratings data in this study come from the Wind database, so there are insignificant correlation results.

Column (10) demonstrates the impact of the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies on the fund shareholding, and the results show that the impact between the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings is not significant, which is inconsistent with H2. The reason for this is that ESG fund managers will only choose the ESG rating of one rating agency as a reference when adjusting their investment strategies, so using the standard deviation of ESG ratings of the five rating agencies as the independent variable will generate a significant error.

Table 6 demonstrates the differences in the impact of the mean and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies on the number of fund holdings when the time windows are different. The results show that the impact between the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings at one lag is significant at the 1 % level, and the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies has no significant correlation with the number of fund holdings; the impact between the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings at two lags is significant at the 1 % level, and the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies has no significant correlation with the number of fund holdings. However, the correlation coefficient between the two is greater at a two-period lag, suggesting that the effect between the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings is greater in a one-year time window than in a six-month time window.

Robustness testing

Adding control variable

ESG funds shareholding and stock price volatility are influenced by a large number of factors, thus there may be an omitted variable problem. In this study, we use the method of adding control variables to conduct robustness tests. Listed companies with a large Tobin's Q value have better development prospects and thus are more attractive to fund investors. In order to exclude the impact of this value on the number of fund holdings, this study adds this control variable on the basis of the basic study. In addition, In September 2020, China officially proposed a "dual-carbon" goal, committing itself to achieving carbon peaking by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2060. Considering that this macro policy may have an impact on the number of fund holdings, this study introduces a dummy variable, set the data of 2019–2020 as 0, and the data of 2021 as 1, and then conduct the regression again. The results are robust as shown in column (1) of Table 7.

Robustness testing.

Changing measures of independent variables

To mitigate the impact of the measurement error of the independent variables, this study changes their measurements for robustness testing. The mean value and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies calculated using the ratings of Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang are again subjected to regression analysis, and the results are robust as shown in column(2) of Table 7.

Join firm clustering standard error

There may be a correlation between the disturbance terms of the same individual in different periods [56]. To solve the potential heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation, this study adds firm clustering standard error for robustness testing, column(3) of Table 7 indicates that the conclusions are robust.

Redefining the sample

Due to the inherent differences between the financial industry and other industries, the study excludes firms in the financial industry as defined by the CSMAR database. The robustness test extends the sample to all industries for regression estimation, and the results are robust as shown in column(4) of Table 7.

Endogeneity testing

The issue of two-way causality is particularly critical when exploring endogeneity. Considering that this study examines how ESG rating changes in one year affect fund shareholding in the following year, and there is no direct link between fund shareholding in the following year and ESG rating changes in the current year, there is no two-way causality problem, so potential endogeneity factors do not affect the results.

Heterogeneity analysis

Heterogeneity analysis based on the nature of ownership of firms

Due to the different social responsibilities undertaken by state-owned and non-state-owned firms in China, the extent of investors' reference to their ESG rating will also differ. Table 8 demonstrates the difference in the impact of the mean value and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies on the fund shareholding when the nature of ownership of firms are different. The results show that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies of state-owned firms has a significant positive impact on the number of fund holdings, the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies has no impact on the number of fund holdings, and the mean value and standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies of non-state-owned firms are not significantly associated with the number of fund holdings. This suggests that ESG fund managers refer to the ESG rating of state-owned firms to a greater extent, consistent with H3a. Specifically, the national government manages state-owned enterprises (SOEs) more strictly in terms of environmental protection, social responsibility fulfillment and corporate governance, and the public defaults to the fact that they are able to consciously protect the environment, fulfill their social responsibilities and efficiently govern their internal companies, and are more comprehensive and accurate in making ESG disclosures, in view of which ESG funds are more inclined to refer to the ESG ratings of SOEs.

Further study

It has been demonstrated that ESG rating changes can have a significant impact on stock price volatility. Based on the influencing factors of stock price volatility, this study explores the influencing mechanism between the two. Currently, fund products are the main way for investors to practice ESG concepts. From the point of view of the development of the U.S. market, the number and scale of ESG funds have developed rapidly in recent years. In China's market, the number and scale of ESG fund products have also shown significant growth and investment types are gradually enriched. It can be seen that the mainstream of ESG investment is ESG fund investment, so this study argues that ESG rating changes may have an impact on stock price volatility by influencing the fund shareholding. In order to verify this view, this study analyzes the impact of the mean value of ESG ratings and the standard deviation of ESG ratings of each agency on stock price volatility.

Table 10 demonstrates the regression results analyzing the impact between ESG ratings changes and stock price volatility, with all regressions controlling for five control variables: ROE, PER, OPR, BM, and LEV. The results show that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies is significantly negatively correlated with stock price volatility, which is consistent with Hypothesis 4a, the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies is significantly negatively correlated with stock price volatility, which is inconsistent with Hypothesis 4b, which is consistent with the reason that there is no significant correlation between the standard deviation of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings in the basic regression. Specifically, firms with high ESG rating not only perform well in terms of environmental protection, social responsibility fulfillment, and corporate governance, but more importantly, they excel in a number of dimensions such as risk management strategies, attracting and maintaining an investor base in order to optimize the investor structure, as well as establishing good communication and relationship management with various stakeholders. As a result, companies with high ESG rating are able to maintain low share price volatility in the capital market.

Discussion

Currently, ESG rating have become an important reference indicator for institutional investors' investment decisions. Constructing portfolios based on ESG ratings can effectively enhance the investment level of ESG funds. Changes in ESG ratings have a significant impact on the number of shares held by ESG funds, and can further affect stock price volatility. There are many ESG rating agencies in China, and the rating results vary greatly. Therefore, studying the impact of ESG rating results and their changes on ESG fund investment decisions in the special context of China is of great significance for increasing the scale of ESG investment and realizing the sustainable development of ESG investment. The following recommendations are made. Firstly, investors should pay attention to the ESG rating changes of firms when adjusting their investment strategies. A correct investment strategy is conducive to increase investors' interests, so they should choose firms with high ESG rating and slight differences in ESG rating when formulating investment strategies. Secondly, firms should improve their environmental, social and governance performance. ESG rating is a non-financial indicator that measures a corporate performance in environmental, social and governance, the better the performance, the higher the ESG rating, and the more the firm will be able to enhance its sustainability and reputation, which will strengthen the corporate financial performance, value and serve to curb the risks faced by the firm.

This study needs to be improved in two aspects: (1)This study measures the average level of ESG ratings using only the data of five mainstream ESG rating agencies, which does not fully represent the differences in Chinese rating agencies, and the number of rating agencies can be increased in the future to improve the precision of the variable. (2)To avoid missing data, this study only selects A-share listed companies that have been held by ESG funds for three consecutive years as the sample, and the data selection requirements can be relaxed to expand the sample size in the future. It will be the focus of the next step of research. China's Hong Kong Stock Exchange and the Shanghai and Shenzhen A-share exchanges have different trading rules and information disclosure systems. With the increase in the number of underlying stocks of "Shanghai - Hong Kong Stock Connect" and "Shenzhen - Hong Kong Stock Connect" (i.e., the same listed company's stock can be traded in both Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges and Hong Kong Stock Exchanges at the same time), companies listed in both Shanghai and Shenzhen A-shares and Hong Kong H-shares can be selected as samples for comparative study in the future to further analyze the impact of different market structures and trading mechanisms on the research findings.

Conclusion

ESG rating plays an important role as sustainable development is promoted globally. However, due to the different rating systems and criteria used by ESG rating agencies, ESG rating changes not only show the trend of changes in the rating of the same agency for the same firm over time, but also the differences in the changes in the rating of different agencies for the same firm at the same time. Against this background, the study examines the relationship between ESG rating changes and the fund shareholding. The study finds that (1) the mean value of ESG ratings, the environmental dimension rating, the governance dimension rating and the rating results of Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang have a significant and positive impact on the number of fund holdings, while the rating results of the other ESG rating agencies and the ratings of the social dimension have no significant impact on the number of fund holdings. (2) The standard deviation of the ESG ratings by agencies has no impact on the number of fund holdings. (3) The impact between the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies and the number of fund holdings is greater in a one-year time window than in a six-month time window to a greater extent.(4) The heterogeneity test finds that the impact of the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies on the number of fund holdings is more significant for state-owned and industrial firms.(5) Further research finds that the mean value of ESG ratings by agencies is negatively correlated with stock price volatility.

Based on the findings of the study, the following insights were gained: Through the analysis of the relationship between the five ESG rating agencies and the number of fund holdings, it can be seen that the ESG ratings of firm conducted by Huazheng, SynTao Green Finance and Menglang have the most reference value. and through the empirical analysis of the impact of ESG sub-dimension ratings on the number of fund holdings, it can be seen that the reference value of the ESG sub-dimension ratings of the Wind database is not very valuable for investors.Therefore, each rating agency should amend its own rating standards and introduce ESG sub-dimension ratings of listed companies to improve the ESG rating system for investors' reference. In addition, the regulatory authorities should assist ESG rating agencies to improve the rating system and develop uniform rating standards. At present, the development of ESG in China is not yet perfect, and the ESG rating standards are not yet unified, so the ESG ratings of the same listed company in different rating agencies have large differences and poor consistency. Regulators should develop unified rating standards to reduce the differences in ESG ratings, thus increasing their reference value.

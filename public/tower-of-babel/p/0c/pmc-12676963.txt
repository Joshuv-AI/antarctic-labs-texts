Purinergic and extracellular vesicle signaling in alcohol-induced blood–brain barrier breakdown and neuroimmune activation
Authors: Namdev S Togre, Priyanka S Bhoj, Naveen Mekala, Rebecca Hancock, Jayshil Trivedi, Yuri Persidsky
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic alcohol consumption is a major risk factor for neuroinflammation and cognitive decline, yet the molecular underpinnings connecting peripheral alcohol-induced injury to central nervous system (CNS) dysfunction remain poorly defined. Emerging evidence implicates purinergic P2X7 receptor (P2X7R) signaling and extracellular vesicles (EVs) as key mediators in peripheral–central communication. Ethanol exposure promotes oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and blood–brain barrier (BBB) disruption, leading to sustained microglial activation and neuronal injury. Concurrently, alcohol-induced damage in the gut, liver, and lung, triggers systemic inflammation and EV release. These EVs, enriched in proinflammatory cytokines, miRNAs, mitochondrial DNA, and other DAMPs, can cross the compromised BBB and engage innate immune receptors, such as TLR4 and P2X7R, on glial cells, amplifying neuroimmune responses.In this review, we integrated recent findings on EV biogenesis, P2X7R signaling, and neurovascular dysfunction in the context of alcohol use disorder. We proposed a mechanistic model in which ethanol-triggered P2X7R activation drives EV release, turning these vesicles into inflammatory couriers that carry peripheral injury signals to the brain. We emphasize EV cargo as promising biomarkers of alcohol-related neurodegeneration and explore emerging therapies that target EV pathways or P2X7R to curb alcohol-induced CNS damage.Keywords: P2X7 receptor, Extracellular vesicles, Neuroinflammation, Blood–brain barrier, Oxidative stress, Alcohol use disorder, Biomarkers, Inflammation

Introduction

Alcohol consumption is a major global health concern, causing 2.6 million deaths annually and accounting for 4.7 % of global mortality (Chrystoja et al., 2021). Notably, alcohol-related deaths are particularly high in Europe and Africa (52 per 100,000), with 13 % of alcohol-attributable deaths occurring in young adults aged 20–39. Chronic alcohol use is linked to more than 200 health conditions, including liver cirrhosis, cancer, and cognitive impairment (Pervin and Stephen, 2021). Moreover, alcohol use disorder (AUD) causes a gradual but cumulative decline in physical and mental health, contributing to fatigue, reduced work productivity, cardiovascular and gastrointestinal complications, and mental health issues, such as depression and anxiety. Additionally, alcohol’s social normalization, especially in professional settings, can perpetuate unhealthy drinking habits, leading to dependence and poor decision-making. These not only affect the individuals but also have broader socioeconomic implications, including strained healthcare systems and economic losses.

AUD exacerbates neurodegeneration through synaptic injury, oxidative stress, and blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction (Sutherland, Sheedy, and Kril, 2014). Ethanol and its metabolite acetaldehyde disrupt tight junction proteins (TJPs) and promote production of vascular endothelial growth factor and cytokines, leading to a leaky BBB and neuroinflammation (Haorah et al., 2005; Vore and Deak, 2022; Yu et al., 2017). Chronic alcohol exposure elevates pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, and CCL2), contributing to neuronal injury and cognitive decline (Crews et al., 2015; Togre et al., 2024; Lowe et al., 2020; Anand et al., 2023; Topiwala et al., 2017). Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are key mediators of intercellular communication (Berumen Sánchez et al., 2021). Alcohol enhances EV release and alters their content, enriching them with cytokines, miRNAs, and damage-associated molecules, thereby promoting mitochondrial dysfunction and immune activation (Pascual, Ibáñez, and Guerri, 2020; Tharmalingam et al., 2024; Ibáñez et al., 2019; Ibáñez et al., 2021; Mukherjee et al., 2020).

Purinergic signaling refers to purine (extracellular ATP/adenosine) acting on plasmalemmal purinoceptors (P2/P1) to regulate cell function. The P2X7 receptor (P2X7R) is one member of the P2 (ATP) receptor family (Huang et al., 2021). Extracellular ATP (eATP) acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP), triggering downstream signaling through purinergic receptors. P2X7R is a low-affinity ATP-gated ion channel especially implicated in alcohol-induced inflammation (Le Daré et al., 2021; Su et al., 2019; Asatryan et al., 2018). It requires high ATP concentrations for activation (~100–1000 μM), far above the nanomolar levels present in healthy tissue, and thus is predominantly engaged during severe cellular stress or injury (Hong et al., 2009; Khakh and Alan North, 2006; Dubyak 2007; Adinolfi et al., 2010; Hong et al., 2009).

Upon activation by elevated eATP, P2X7R stimulates the shedding of EVs, modulates their cargo, and amplifies inflammatory signaling (Lombardi et al., 2021; Pegoraro et al., 2021; Pizzirani et al., 2007; Ruan et al., 2020; Bianco et al., 2005; Mekala et al., 2024; Togre et al., 2024). Alcohol-induced mitochondrial dysfunction and eATP release lead to the production of EVs enriched with DAMPs such as ATP, mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), and proinflammatory cytokines. (Mekala et al., 2024; Togre et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2020; Sadikot et al., 2019). Studies show that EVs can cross the BBB and release their cargo into the brain, highlighting their dual role in pathology and therapeutic delivery (Banks et al., 2020a; Ramos-Zaldívar et al., 2022). However, the specific role of P2X7R-regulated EVs in alcohol-induced BBB disruption and neuroinflammation remains underexplored. Further research is essential to uncover therapeutic strategies targeting P2X7R and EV pathways to mitigate alcohol-related central nervous system (CNS) damage.

Present review focuses on the mechanisms underlying alcohol-induced neuroinflammation, emphasizing the roles of EVs, P2X7R signaling, and BBB disruption. Ethanol exposure leads to oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and BBB damage, all of which contribute to neuroinflammation and neuronal damage. Glial cells, especially microglia, play a key role in this process, releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines that exacerbate neurodegeneration. EVs, which transport pro-inflammatory molecules and neurotoxic substances, further amplify the inflammatory response and affect both the brain and peripheral organs. Understanding these pathways may reveal novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets for alcohol-related CNS damage.

Alcohol exposure and neuroinflammation

Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction

Oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and neuroinflammation are closely interconnected in neurodegeneration, particularly in alcohol-related disorders (León et al., 2021). Ethanol metabolism via alcohol dehydrogenase, cytochrome P450–2E1 (CYP2E1), and the mitochondrial electron transport chain (ETC) generates excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) and nitric oxide (NO) through NADPH/xanthine oxidase (NOX) and nitric oxide synthase (NOS) activity (Crews and Nixon 2009; Haorah et al., 2008; Dash et al., 2025). The resulting oxidative burden overwhelms neuronal and glial antioxidant defenses, causing lipid peroxidation, protein and DNA damage—including mtDNA—and diminished neuronal structural proteins such as neurofilaments (Guo et al., 2013).

While neurons are directly impaired by oxidative injury, glial cells, particularly microglia, are potent amplifiers of damage. ROS and acetaldehyde-induced stress activate redox-sensitive transcription factors such as NF-κB, inducing pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), chemokines (CCL2/MCP-1), and enzymes like inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) (Lull and Block 2010; Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2010; Crews and Nixon 2008).

Mitochondria are both vulnerable targets and active mediators of ethanol-induced neurotoxicity. Excessive mitochondrial ROS (mtROS) disrupt respiratory complexes, promote mitochondrial fragmentation, and accelerate mtDNA oxidation and depletion (León et al., 2021; Motori et al., 2013). Damaged mtDNA can mislocalize to the cytosol or extracellular space, where it acts as a DAMP that activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, further enhancing IL-1β production and perpetuating neuroinflammation (Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2014; Zhou et al., 2011). In astrocytes, inflammatory stimuli induce mitochondrial fragmentation, impairing energy production and increasing mtROS, reinforcing this feed-forward cycle of oxidative stress, inflammation, and neuronal injury (Motori et al., 2013).

Mitochondrial dysfunction is especially detrimental to neurons, which have high energy demands. Damaged mitochondria release mtROS and DAMPs, such as mtDNA, triggering neuroinflammation (Rose et al., 2017). Ethanol-induced mitochondrial damage impairs ATP production, exacerbating oxidative stress and creating a feedback loop of mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation (Clemente-Suárez et al., 2023; Cederbaum 1999; Simon and Molina 2022). Moreover, mitochondrial involvement in cell survival pathways means dysfunction can lead to neuronal apoptosis and neurodegeneration (Clemente-Suárez et al., 2023; Zong et al., 2024; Dash et al., 2025). mtDNA, which lacks histones and has limited repair mechanisms, is particularly susceptible to oxidative damage (Gambardella et al., 2019; Annesley and Fisher 2019; West et al., 2015). When released into the cytoplasm via mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening, mtDNA acts as a signaling molecule that can activate two distinct innate immune pathways. First, mtDNA engages the cyclic GMP–AMP synthase–stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS–STING) pathway, which activates TBK1/IRF3 (inducing type-I interferons) and NF-κB, thereby promoting the production of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6 (Li et al., 2015; McArthur et al., 2018; Riley et al., 2018; Bai et al., 2017; Quan et al., 2025). Second, mtDNA can trigger the NLRP3 inflammasome, which specifically cleaves pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into their mature, active forms (Shimada et al., 2012). Moreover, mtDNA contains unmethylated CpG sequences that act as ligands for Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), activating NF-κB and further amplifying inflammatory responses (Lin et al., 2022; Tao et al., 2024). The interplay of mtDNA damage, ROS, and inflammation highlights the importance of mitochondrial health in maintaining neuronal function and preventing neurodegeneration.

ATP is a critical component of this inflammatory cascade, which also acts as a DAMP. Under stress, excessive ATP release activates the P2X7R in microglia (Bedetta, Pizzo, and Lia 2025), leading to Na+ and Ca2+ influx and K+ efflux. This triggers the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and the release of IL-1β and IL-18. Elevated intracellular Ca2+ disrupts mitochondrial function. Prolonged P2X7R activation can lead to the formation of a membrane “macropore” permeable to small solutes including Na+, Ca2+, K+ and ATP (Pelegrin and Surprenant 2006; Surprenant et al., 1996; Dunning et al., 2021; Savio et al., 2018; Cevoli et al., 2023; Karasawa and Kawate 2016). Additionally, sustained P2X7R stimulation induces membrane blebbing and shedding of microvesicles from the plasma membrane (Bianco et al., 2009; Drago et al., 2017; Dubyak 2012). These EVs can carry ATP and mtDNA as cargo, which act as inflammatory mediators and propagate inflammatory signaling (Mekala et al., 2024; Togre et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2022). Our studies have demonstrated that chronic alcohol and e-cigarette exposure impair mitochondrial function and disrupt the BBB by increasing ATP release and P2X7R activation. In human brain microvascular endothelial cells (hBMVECs), ethanol or its metabolite acetaldehyde reduces oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), induces ATP leakage, upregulates P2X7R expression, diminishes trans-endothelial electrical resistance (TEER), and increases paracellular permeability—hallmarks of endothelial barrier dysfunction (Mekala et al., 2022). Pharmacologic inhibition of P2X7R in these in vitro systems restores mitochondrial respiration, reduces ATP leakage, and preserves TEER and tight-junction function, suggesting a protective strategy against ethanol-induced endothelial injury (Mekala et al., 2024) (Fig. 1).

Similarly, translational rodent paradigms show that chronic-plus-binge or chronic ethanol exposure alters BBB, activates astrocytes, and increases microglial reactivity, effects that are ameliorated by genetic or pharmacologic P2X7R inhibition (Freire et al., 2019; Asatryan et al., 2018; Asatryan et al., 2015). Asatryan et al., demonstrated that chronic ethanol with a high-fat diet (HFD) up-regulated P2X7R expression in ethanol-sensitive brain regions in mice, paralleling neuroinflammation and neuronal loss (Asatryan et al., 2015). Freire et al., showed that the selective P2X7R antagonist A804598 reduced ethanol/HFD-induced microglial and astrocytic activation and lowered brain inflammatory markers in vivo (Freire et al., 2019). In our in vivo chronic-ethanol mouse model, pharmacologic or genetic blockade of P2X7R markedly reduced ethanol-induced elevations in circulating EVs, EV-associated ATP, and EV-mtDNA, and concomitantly attenuated peripheral and CNS inflammatory markers, findings that support a systemic role for P2X7R in mediating ethanol-driven neuroimmune signaling (Togre et al., 2024).

BBB disruption

The BBB is a dynamic and selective interface formed by BMVECs, pericytes, astrocytes, and microglia, crucial for maintaining CNS homeostasis by regulating molecular exchange and restricting immune cell infiltration. Ethanol metabolites and neurotoxic substances may downregulate TJPs (claudin, occludin, and zonula occludens), leading to a leaky BBB (Yu et al., 2017). Our lab has shown that alcohol-induced BBB disruption is driven by oxidative stress. In BMVECs, ethanol metabolism via ADH and CYP2E1 generates ROS and acetaldehyde, which activate signaling pathways that impair BBB integrity (Haorah et al., 2005). ROS activate myosin light chain kinase (MLCK), leading to phosphorylation of myosin light chain (MLC) and TJPs, disrupting BMVEC monolayers and increasing permeability. Acetaldehyde also mimics these effects, emphasizing the direct role of ethanol metabolites in BBB dysfunction (Haorah et al., 2007b). Furthermore, ethanol metabolites stimulate inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate receptor (IP(3)R)-mediated calcium (Ca2+) release, further activating MLCK and enhancing cytoskeletal and TJP phosphorylation (Haorah et al., 2007a). ROS also activate matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-1, -2, and -9), degrading basement membrane proteins and further compromising BBB stability. Protein tyrosine kinases mediate TJP phosphorylation, exacerbating barrier disruption (Haorah et al., 2007b).

Chronic ethanol exposure increases the release of proinflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Wang et al., 2020; Fraga-Junior et al., 2022), which disrupt tight junctions, increasing BBB permeability (Yang et al., 2022a; Vore and Deak 2022; Almutairi et al., 2016). This facilitates the infiltration of peripheral immune cells, exacerbating neuroinflammation (Takata et al., 2021). Furthermore, ethanol-induced microglial activation (Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2016), akin to the inflammatory responses seen in stroke and traumatic brain injury, exacerbates BBB breakdown and neuronal damage (Qiu et al., 2021). Together, these mechanisms—oxidative stress, MLCK and MMP activation, Ca2+ signaling, and immune cell activation—compromise BBB integrity, enabling leukocyte migration into the brain, and contributing to alcohol-induced neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration.

P2X7R plays a critical role in ethanol-induced BBB dysfunction. Ethanol differentially affects P2X receptors in microglia, inhibiting P2X4R-mediated migration while potentiating P2X7R pore formation and IL-1β secretion. (Pelegrin and Surprenant 2006; Surprenant et al., 1996; Dunning et al., 2021; Savio et al., 2018; Cevoli et al., 2023; Karasawa and Kawate 2016). It increases P2X4R and P2X7R protein expression in BV2 microglial cells (Asatryan et al., 2018). Ethanol disrupts BBB integrity in a concentration-dependent manner by inducing tight junction disassembly, apoptosis, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and oxidative stress, as indicated by GRP78 chaperone upregulation and increased ROS production (Carrino et al., 2021). Several therapeutic interventions have been investigated to mitigate ethanol-induced BBB impairment. P2X7R antagonists, such as A804598 and Brilliant blue G (BBG), preserve BBB function by attenuating ATP-driven barrier permeability (Togre et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2022). Furthermore, inhibition of MMP activity and ATP release pathways (e.g., Panx1 channel inhibitors) have emerged as potential strategies for ameliorating ethanol-induced BBB dysfunction (Gómez et al., 2024; Haorah et al., 2007b; Rempe, Hartz, and Bauer 2016) (Fig. 2).

In conclusion, chronic ethanol exposure disrupts BBB integrity through tight junction modifications, MMP activation, ATP-mediated P2X7R signaling, and immune cell infiltration. These mechanisms collectively contribute to sustained neuroinflammation and heighten the vulnerability of the CNS to neurodegenerative pathologies.

Glial responses during alcohol exposure

Microglial activation is a hallmark of neuroinflammation (Erickson et al., 2019). Repeated binge alcohol exposure primes microglia, leading to a heightened inflammatory response. A second binge exposure increases the number of OX-42- and IBA-1-positive cells and TNF-α levels, indicating a stronger neuroinflammatory response compared to a single binge. This priming effect exacerbates neuroinflammation highlighting microglia’s increased sensitivity after repeated ethanol exposure (Marshall, Geil, and Nixon 2016).

Glial cells, especially astrocytes and microglia, undergo functional and structural changes after chronic intermittent ethanol (CIE) exposure. RNA-sequencing of glial populations from the mouse prefrontal cortex showed disrupted synaptic calcium signaling in astrocytes and altered inflammatory pathways in microglia (Erickson et al., 2019). These changes are supported by clinical studies, showing phenotypic alterations in glial cells in individuals with AUD (Coleman, Zou, and Crews 2017; He and Crews 2008; Miguel-Hidalgo 2005).

During ethanol exposure, TLR4 activation in glial cells is driven both by ethanol itself and by ethanol-induced endogenous DAMPs. These neuroinflammatory responses can occur independently of canonical ligands like LPS. Ethanol promotes TLR4/TLR2 clustering within lipid rafts, facilitating downstream NF-κB, MAPK, and IRF3/IFN-β signaling and inducing pro-inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-1β, iNOS, and COX-2 (Fernandez-Lizarbe, Pascual, and Guerri 2009; Fernandez-Lizarbe, Montesinos, and Guerri 2013). DAMPs, notably HMGB1, further bind TLR4 to amplify IL-1β production and neuroimmune activation (Crews et al., 2013). Chronic ethanol exposure also recruits TRIF-dependent pathways, including TLR3–TRIF inputs, sustaining innate immune activation (McCarthy et al., 2018). TLR4 deficiency abolishes ethanol-induced glial activation, cytokine production (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6), and caspase-3 activation in cortex, highlighting TLR4′s critical role in alcohol-induced neuroinflammation (Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2010).

This TLR4-driven activation also involves the release of EVs containing inflammatory proteins (e.g., TLR4, NFκB-p65, IL-1R, caspase-1, and NLRP3) and miRNAs (e.g., miR-146a, miR-182, and miR-200b) that affect neuronal function and survival. Additionally, chronic alcohol exposure alters the expression of miRNAs such as the miR-183 cluster, miR-200a/b, and miR-125b, which regulate genes involved in TLR4 signaling and inflammation (e.g., Il1r1, Mapk14, Sirt1, Lrp6, and Bdnf). Notably, these EV- and miRNA-mediated changes, as well as the inflammatory cascade, were abolished in TLR4-knockout (KO) mice or TLR4-KO astrocytes, underscoring the receptor’s critical role in glial responses and ethanol-induced neuroinflammation (Ureña-Peralta et al., 2018; Ibáñez et al., 2019).

Ethanol activates the NLRP3 inflammasome in astroglia, triggering caspase-1 activation and the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, like IL-1β, which amplifies neuroinflammation and ROS generation, (Lippai et al., 2013; Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2016). Preclinical studies show that alcohol-induced neuropathology involves interactions between glial cells, with TLR4 activation leading to cytokine upregulation, neuronal damage, and cognitive impairment. Blocking TLR4 in cultured glial cells prevents these effects (Alfonso-Loeches et al., 2010; Ibáñez et al., 2019). Moreover, studies in TLR4-KO mice have shown that the absence of TLR4 prevents ethanol-induced changes in BBB permeability, further confirming TLR4′s role in ethanol-induced neurovascular damage (Rubio-Araiz et al., 2017) (Fig. 3).

Ethanol exposure, P2X7R signaling, and EVs

Ethanol exposure and EV generation

Ethanol significantly alters EV biogenesis, contributing to neuroinflammation (Mellado et al., 2025). Ethanol-induced ER stress activates the unfolded protein response (UPR) in glial cells, triggering DAMP-containing EV secretion that further amplify inflammation (Collett et al., 2018). Ethanol alters lipid metabolism and disrupts lipid raft dynamics, enhancing vesicular trafficking, EV release, and inflammatory cargo transfer (Ibáñez et al., 2021). Moreover, ethanol-induced mitochondrial dysfunction, including mitochondria-associated ER membrane (MAM) disruption, facilitates EV biogenesis via mROS production (Perpiñá-Clérigues et al., 2023).

Cytoskeletal remodeling is another key process influenced by ethanol. Rho GTPases, which regulate actin–myosin dynamics, are disrupted by ethanol, impairing glial cell function and contributing to EV-mediated signaling (Gao et al., 2024a; Martínez et al., 2007; Joshi et al., 2006).

Ethanol increases the secretion of EVs, particularly exosomes, by primary human monocytes and THP-1 monocytic cells in a concentration- and time-dependent manner. These EVs polarize naive monocytes toward an M2 macrophages via miR-27a–mediated modulation of CD206 expression and phagocytosis (Saha et al., 2016). In hepatocytes, chronic ethanol intake induces lysosomal dysfunction, leading to increased EV release and liver injury (Dagur et al., 2021). Ethanol also triggers mtDNA-enriched EV release, regulated by ASK1 and p38 MAPK. Inhibiting these kinases reduces both liver injury and EV release (Ma et al., 2020).

Our recent findings corroborate these results, showing increased mtDNA-enriched EVs following CIE exposure, suggesting a mechanistic link between ethanol, EV biogenesis, and neuroinflammation (Togre et al., 2024). Future research will uncover the cargo composition of ethanol-induced EVs, their role in neuroinflammatory processes, and potential therapeutic targets.

Ethanol exposure and EV composition

Chronic alcohol consumption significantly impacts the proteomic and lipidomic profiles of EVs, with sex-specific differences. In females, acute ethanol exposure increases phosphatidic acid, lysophosphatidylcholine, unsaturated fatty acids, and fatty acyl esters of hydroxy fatty acids in EVs, whereas males show enrichment in phosphatidylinositol. These lipid changes influence EV formation, release, and immune activation, with TLR4-KO mice exhibiting reduced sex-specific differences, indicating TLR4′s involvement (Perpiñá-Clérigues et al., 2025).

The relevance of MAMs and sphingomyelinases (SMases) in ethanol-modulated lipid metabolism and EV release has been explored in microglial cells (Ibáñez et al., 2021). Ethanol upregulated MAM activity and altered lipid metabolism by increasing cholesterol uptake, esterification, and SMase activity. Notably, inhibiting either SMase or MAMs prevented the ethanol-induced increase in EV secretion (Ibáñez et al., 2021).

Ethanol significantly impacts RNA cargo in EVs, influencing immune modulation (Baratta et al., 2022). Alcohol-treated hepatocytes communicate with immune cells via miRNA-loaded exosomes (Momen-Heravi et al., 2015). In microglia, ethanol promotes a pro-inflammatory phenotype, increasing the release of EVs enriched with G9a mRNA. These EVs inhibit neurogenesis by modifying chromatin structure, an effect that is reversed by G9a inhibition (Zou et al., 2022).

CIE alters the RNA content of brain-derived EVs. Studies in male and female C57BL/6J mice revealed differentially expressed long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and mRNAs in EVs derived from CIE-exposed animals. Gene co-expression network analysis identified sex-specific modules associated with neuroinflammation, synaptic dysfunction, and cell death (Baratta et al., 2022). Additionally, alcohol consumption during adolescence and young adulthood has been linked to altered miRNA expression in neuron-enriched EVs, with plasma samples from heavy drinkers exhibiting elevated levels of miR-30a-5p, miR-194–5p, and miR-339–3p. These findings suggest that alcohol influences brain development by modulating miRNA profiles in EVs, affecting pathways related to brain function and development (Yakovlev et al., 2024). Understanding ethanol-induced changes in EV composition provides valuable insights into the pathophysiology of AUD and related diseases.

P2X7R-driven EV generation in neuroinflammation

Purinergic signaling, particularly P2X7R activation, plays a crucial role in neuroinflammation by modulating EV release from neurons, astrocytes, and microglia P2X7R is widely expressed throughout the CNS, including on microglia, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and certain neuronal populations (Yu et al., 2008; Illes et al., 2012; Monif, Burnstock, and Williams 2010; Sperlágh and Illes 2014; Anderson and Nedergaard 2006). Upon activation by eATP, P2X7R mediates cation influx, membrane depolarization, and the formation of large transmembrane pores, which facilitate EV shedding (Lombardi et al., 2021; Zelentsova et al., 2022; MacKenzie et al., 2001; Bianco et al., 2005; Golia, Gabrielli, and Verderio 2023).

MacKenzie et al., (2001) first demonstrated that P2X7R activation induces EV release via bleb formation and phosphatidylserine (PS) translocation, independent of apoptosis (MacKenzie et al., 2001). Further studies revealed that P2X7R-driven EV generation is mediated by p38 MAP kinase and ROCK signaling pathways, triggering acid sphingomyelinase (A-SMase) translocation, ceramide formation, and altered membrane curvature (Bianco et al., 2009; Morelli et al., 2003; Pfeiffer et al., 2004; Verhoef et al., 2003). Inhibition of P2X7R and A-SMase reduces EV release in microglia, astrocytes, and macrophages, underscoring their role in neuroinflammatory responses (Bianco et al., 2005).

In pathological conditions, P2X7R-driven EV release exacerbates neuroinflammation. In a rat spinal nerve ligation model, P2X7R activation increased EV shedding from microglia, with EVs enriched in IL-1β contributing to pain hypersensitivity by enhancing neuronal excitability. Inhibiting P2X7R or IL-1β alleviated pain, highlighting P2X7R’s role in microglia–neuron interactions (Li et al., 2017). Similarly, in experimental pulpitis, microglial exosome secretion in the medullary dorsal horn correlated with inflammation-induced pain, peaking at day 7. P2X7R inhibition with oxATP reduced EV release and neuroinflammatory markers, mitigating pain responses (Zhang et al., 2025).

Activation of P2X7R modifies the composition of extracellular vesicles, enriching them with inflammatory mediators—including GAPDH, CD14, IL-1Ra, TACE, and tissue factor—which contribute to neuroinflammation and immune modulation (Alarcón-Vila et al., 2020; Takenouchi et al., 2015; Wilson et al., 2004; Barberà-Cremades et al., 2017). Proteomic analysis of ATP-stimulated microglial EVs reveals an enrichment of proteins associated with antigen processing (complement C1q subcomponents: C1qa, C1qb, and C1qc), autophagy (vimentin, NAD (P)H dehydrogenase 1 [Nqo1]), cytoskeletal dynamics (myristoylated alanine-rich C-kinase substrate and actin alpha cardiac muscle 1), and metabolism (ATP synthase subunit beta and glucose-6-phosphate 1-dehydrogenase). These findings underscore the role of microglial EVs in immune regulation and neurodegeneration (Drago et al., 2017; Gu et al., 2009). Recently, our lab demonstrated that ethanol exposure increases EV release and inflammatory cargo, including EV-ATP and EV-mtDNA. Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of P2X7R reduced these effects, providing in vivo evidence that P2X7R plays a critical role in regulating exosomal content and neuroinflammatory responses during alcohol exposure (Togre et al., 2024). Therefore, targeting P2X7R can mitigate ethanol-induced brain injury by limiting inflammatory EV release.

Collectively, these findings establish P2X7R as a key regulator of EV-mediated neuroinflammation. By driving proinflammatory EV release, P2X7R exacerbates immune responses, impairs neurogenesis, and contributes to neurodegeneration and BBB dysfunction. Targeting P2X7R presents a compelling therapeutic strategy to modulate EV generation and mitigate neuroinflammatory pathologies in CNS disorders.

EV types in ethanol induced brain damage

International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) guidelines (MISEV2023) endorsed a generic term extracellular vesicles for any lipid bilayer enclosed particles released by the cells that lack a true nucleus (Welsh et al., 2024). The distinct EV subtypes–exosomes, microvesicles, apoptotic bodies, proteasomes, tolerosomes, prostasomes, and dendritic exosomes (dexosomes)–each have canonical origins and cargo (van Niel, D’Angelo, and Raposo 2018), and all can be perturbed by alcohol. Peripheral organ damage: Ethanol-induced EVs as a mediator of neuroinflammation

A review by Rahman et al. (2020) has described in detail how ethanol-induced damage to peripheral organs results in the release of pro-inflammatory EVs and propagate systemic inflammation (Rahman et al., 2020). Overall, ethanol metabolism generates acetaldehyde and ROS, triggering oxidative stress and inflammatory responses. Interestingly, gut bacteria also metabolize ethanol into these toxic metabolites, amplifying its impact on peripheral and central inflammation (Nosova et al., 2000). Here, we discussed alcohol-affected major peripheral organs that play a significant role in central inflammation via EVs and possibly P2X7R.

Gut

Ethanol disrupts the microbiota–gut–brain axis, causing gut dysbiosis and promoting the release of inflammatory EVs that mediate paracrine and endocrine communication (Chen et al., 2022). As detailed by Chen et al., (2022), ethanol exposure enhances intestinal permeability by dislocating and reducing TJPs that are critical for maintaining intestinal epithelial integrity (Chen et al., 2022).This damage is further exacerbated by ethanol-induced gut nitro-oxidative stress, which activates inflammation-related molecules, including MMP-9, TNF-α, IL-1, and IFN-γ. This leads to the translocation of microbial products, such as LPS, into circulation, exacerbating systemic inflammation. Gut-derived EVs containing LPS and inflammatory miRNAs further amplify immune dysregulation by targeting distant organs, including the brain. This cross-talk highlights the gut’s pivotal role in systemic inflammation and its contribution to central neuroinflammation (Lamas-Paz et al., 2020).

Liver

The liver, the primary site of ethanol metabolism, plays a central role in systemic inflammation through the production of inflammatory EVs. Ethanol-induced gut permeability increases the exposure of hepatic Kupffer cells to DAMPs and circulating LPS, activating TLR4 signaling. This results in the release of EVs enriched with pro-inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, interleukins (IL-1b, IL-6, and IL-23), and specific miRNAs (miRNA-122, miRNA-192, and miRNA-30a). These EVs reprogram recipient immune cells, enhancing their sensitivity to inflammatory stimuli. For instance, after alcohol treatment, hepatocyte-derived miRNA-122-containing exosomes suppress the anti-inflammatory heme oxygenase-1 pathway in monocytes, enhancing their sensitivity to LPS and promoting pro-inflammatory cytokine release (Momen-Heravi et al., 2015). miR-192, the second most expressed miRNA in hepatocytes, regulates oxidative stress and inflammatory responses. Alcohol inhibits miR-192 expression, increasing the expression of miR-192 target genes involved in exosome biogenesis (Rab27a, Rab35, and syntaxins) (Bala et al., 2021). Interestingly, P2X7R is involved in EV secretion via Rab proteins and syntaxins (Qu and Dubyak 2009). However, it’s possible association with miR-192 regulation remains unexplored. Chronic alcohol exposure promotes lipolysis, increasing the generation of toxic lipids, such as ceramides. Ceramides play a key role in EV biogenesis due to its lipid solubility and singular cone-shaped structure, initiating spontaneous membrane invagination and maintaining vesicle shape (Horbay et al., 2022). After getting into the circulation, they can easily cross the BBB and contribute to neurodegeneration (de la Monte et al., 2009). With this example, investigation of hepatocyte-derived EVs as mediators of liver-brain communication and their role in ethanol-induced neurodegeneration is needed.

Lung

Most studies highlight the indirect pathogenic effects of ethanol on the lungs, primarily through liver-lung and gut-lung axes, emphasizing inter-organ communication as the key mechanism (Arteel 2020). These studies suggest that ethanol-induced liver damage releases inflammatory mediators, such as cytokines and EVs, which propagate systemic inflammation and compromise pulmonary health. Similarly, as mentioned above, gut-derived microbial products enter circulation, contributing to lung inflammation and immune dysregulation. However, recent trends challenge the assumption that ethanol affects the lungs only indirectly. The growing prevalence of alcohol smoking, a practice increasingly observed globally as part of occupational or lifestyle habits, has raised concerns (Winstock, Winstock, and Davies 2020; MacLean et al., 2017). Inhalation of vaporized alcohol bypasses first-pass metabolism, delivering ethanol rapidly to the brain and directly exposing the respiratory tract to ethanol’s harmful effects (MacLean et al., 2017) (Fig. 4). This shift in alcohol consumption warrants further investigation to elucidate the direct pulmonary impacts of ethanol exposure. Indeed, more observational studies in humans are needed to assess the prevalence, risks, and harms associated with ethanol vapor inhalation.

Peripheral organ-derived extracellular vesicles mediate ethanol-induced neuroinflammation. This proposed mechanism is based on the integration of evidence from multiple independent studies. Chronic exposure to ethanol causes inflammation and tissue damage in peripheral organs, such as gut, liver, and lungs, which promotes the release of extracellular vesicles (EVs) enriched with inflammatory mediators (Rahman et al., 2020). Ethanol affects the lungs both directly and indirectly (Arteel 2020). Direct inhalation results in epithelial injury, mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum stress (Traphagen, Tian, and Allen-Gipson 2015), and an increased secretion of EVs containing mtDNA, ATP, damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), and pro-inflammatory cytokines (Togre et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2024). Indirectly, EVs derived from ethanol-damaged gut and liver tissues influence lung pathology, further amplifying the production of EVs (Yeligar et al., 2016; Liu and Yin 2022).

These circulating EVs carry a variety of bioactive components, including DAMPs, proteins (such as TLR4, NLRP3, caspase-1, and IL-1β), and regulatory miRNAs (for example, miR-146a, miR-182, and miR-200b) (Ibáñez et al., 2019; Sundar and Saraswathi 2023), contributing to systemic inflammation. Ethanol-induced disruption of the BBB, caused by the generation of ROS, calcium dysregulation, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and disassembly of tight junctions, facilitates the entry or signaling of these EVs into the brain (Crews, Zou, and Coleman 2021; Haorah et al., 2005; Guo et al., 2013).

Within the central nervous system, these EVs are taken up by glial cells (pericytes, microglia, and astrocytes) initiating inflammatory cascades through TLR4, NF-κB, and MAPK signaling pathways. This process leads to the activation of inflammasomes and an increased secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Ibáñez et al., 2021; Fernandez-Lizarbe, Montesinos, and Guerri 2013). The resulting oxidative stress and activation of glial cells contribute to neuronal dysfunction and neuroinflammation, which underlie the neuropathology associated with AUD. Recently, we demonstrated that ethanol and acetaldehyde directly disrupt pulmonary alveolar epithelial cell functions through mitochondrial and ER stress pathways and P2X7R signaling. EVs released by lung epithelial cells in response to ethanol or acetaldehyde insults are enriched with secondary messengers, such as eATP and mtDNA, which can stimulate brain endothelial cells via paracrine signaling (Mekala et al., 2024). Furthermore, as discussed above, chronic exposure to ethanol vapor in mice resulted in elevated levels of P2X7R shedding, increased EV numbers, higher EV-mtDNA copy numbers, and greater EV-ATP content in circulation compared to unexposed controls (Togre et al., 2024). These findings suggest that ethanol-induced pulmonary EV release may serve as a systemic inflammatory signal, linking lung dysfunction to distant organ damage, including the brain. This highlights the critical need for research into the mechanistic pathways of ethanol’s direct effects on the lungs and its systemic implications.

Ethanol-induced EVs targeting BBB components

Exposure to alcohol disrupts the BBB’s gatekeeping mechanisms, enabling peripheral immune signals and inflammatory mediators to infiltrate the brain. Ethanol-induced EVs play a critical role in this process, targeting BBB components and driving permeability changes that exacerbate neuroinflammation. Endothelial cells

The BBB’s selective permeability depends on brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMVECs) and their TJPs (Zhang et al., 2021). Ethanol exposure disrupts BMVEC function (Mekala et al., 2022; Haorah et al., 2005; Mekala et al., 2024), and ethanol-induced peripheral EVs can exacerbate this in vitro (Rahman et al., 2020; Mekala et al., 2024). EVs from injured organs (liver, gut, lung) enter the circulation carrying cytokines (TNF, IL-1β), miRNAs (e.g. miR-122, miR-146a), ATP, LPS, DAMPs (e.g. HMGB1), and even P2X7R protein (Berumen Sánchez et al., 2021; Togre et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2022; Hosseinkhani et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2021). These EV cargoes act on BBB endothelium via multiple routes as described below (Schnatz et al., 2021; de la Monte et al., 2009; Murphy et al., 2019; Banks et al., 2020b).

Tight junction changes

TJPs form the BBB’s seal (Jiao et al., 2011; Luissint et al., 2012). Inflammatory EV cargo (cytokines, DAMPs, miRNAs, microbial LPS) can downregulate or mislocalize these proteins, weakening the barrier. For instance, hepatocyte-derived EVs enriched in miR-122 can degrade occludin mRNA and increase gut permeability (Ye et al., 2011); analogous effects on BBB tight junctions are plausible but need direct study. Gut-derived LPS in EVs can activate endothelial TLR4 signaling, triggering NF-κB–dependent downregulation of TJPs (as seen in other inflammatory models) (Wu et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2025; Rubio-Araiz et al., 2017; Tang et al., 2018). Our previous research revealed that ethanol exposure reduces trans-endothelial electrical resistance in BMVECs, indicating a loss of BBB integrity due to altered TJP expression or mis-localization (Mekala et al., 2022). Interestingly, in case of chronic alcohol inhalation, no change in claudin-5 or occludin levels was detected (Togre et al., 2024), suggesting mis-localization rather than degradation may play a critical role (Chen et al., 2022).

EVs released by ethanol- or acetaldehyde-exposed cells were enriched in P2X7R and its substrate, ATP (Mekala et al., 2024), which activates MMPs, leading to TJ degradation (Wang et al., 2023). Noteworthy, P2X7R inhibition restored the BBB integrity in ethanol- or acetaldehyde-exposed BMVEC layer and in mice chronically exposed to ethanol vapor (Mekala et al., 2024; Togre et al., 2024). These findings highlight the complex mechanisms of BBB disruption by ethanol-induced EVs and suggest P2X7R as a potential therapeutic target for preserving BBB function under ethanol exposure.

Facilitated transport

EVs carrying ATP can activate endothelial ion channels and receptors. Besides P2X7R, the transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1) is an ATP-sensitive cation channel on endothelium. Ethanol increases expression of both P2X7R and TRPV1 in hBMVECs. Activation of these channels causes Ca2+ influx into endothelial cells (Mekala et al., 2022), triggering downstream effects such as matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) activation and cytoskeletal changes. For example, ATP/P2X7R signaling is known to increase MMP-9 via IL-1β (Yang et al., 2016), leading to TJP degradation. In our hands, ethanol-exposed hBMVECs showed mitochondrial stress and ATP release, and both P2X7R antagonist (A804598) and TRPV1 blocker protected barrier function (Mekala et al., 2022). Thus, EV-derived ATP may engage P2X7R/TRPV1 on endothelium to compromise tight junctions via Ca2+-dependent MMPs and cytoskeletal tension, as well as the literature on purinergic MMP activation (Yang et al., 2016). While the impact of ethanol on efflux transporters like P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and solute carrier transporters is well documented (Togre et al., 2024; Mekala et al., 2022), the specific role of ethanol-induced EVs in modulating these facilitated transport systems at the BBB remains unexplored (Togre et al., 2024). In summary, ethanol-induced EVs disrupt endothelial barrier by delivering ATP and other activators that trigger Ca2+-mediated tight junction disassembly via P2X7R/TRPV1 signaling (shown in vitro (Mekala et al., 2022)) and this model is being tested in vivo.

Beyond endothelial cells

Ethanol-induced EVs disrupt BBB integrity not only by directly targeting endothelial cells but also through their interactions with other key cellular components of the neurovascular unit (NVU), including microglia, astrocytes, and pericytes. EVs released from brain cells in response to ethanol-induced neuroinflammation fuels other brain cells to amplify neuro-detrimental effects.

Microglial activation

These resident CNS macrophages are key in neuroimmunity. Ethanol-stimulated EVs carry pro-inflammatory signals that activate microglia. In vivo, ethanol drives microglia from a homeostatic to a pro-inflammatory phenotype, largely via TLR7 and NF-κB pathways (Li et al., 2019; Crews, Zou, and Coleman 2021; Sha’fie et al., 2020; Coleman, Zou, and Crews 2017). Activated microglia release EVs containing DAMPs (e.g. HMGB1) and miRNAs (let-7b) that further signal to other cells (Coleman, Zou, and Crews 2017). HMGB1 not only directly activates TLRs but also serves as a chaperone for miRNAs let-7 in MVs, amplifying the inflammatory TLR7 signaling pathway. Notably, ethanol-induced EVs replicate the pro-inflammatory gene induction in microglia, and inhibiting EV secretion effectively blocks ethanol-induced neuroinflammation (Crews, Zou, and Coleman 2021). Additionally, microglia exposed to ethanol release EVs containing chemokines (MCP-1) that can induce neuronal apoptosis via CCR2 (Coleman, Zou, and Crews 2017). Importantly, P2X7R plays a central role in microglial EV dynamics: ATP stimulation of microglia via P2X7R triggers massive shedding of large microvesicles (Drago et al., 2017; Golia, Gabrielli, and Verderio 2023). In fact, ATP-activated P2X7R induces membrane blebs and MV release via p38MAPK and acid sphingomyelinase (ceramide) pathways (Golia, Gabrielli, and Verderio 2023; Bianco et al., 2009). These ATP-stimulated EVs are enriched for autophagy (Drago et al., 2017) and IL-1β processing machinery (Bianco et al., 2005), reflecting a pro-inflammatory cargo profile. Thus, ethanol-induced ATP release can amplify microglial inflammation by fueling P2X7R-dependent EV production. The precise role of P2X7R in ethanol-triggered microglial EV release is still under investigation in our lab, but ATP alone is a potent EV stimulus (Drago et al., 2017).

Astrocyte activation

Astrocytes, with their end-feet enveloping the vasculature, are essential for maintaining BBB homeostasis and facilitating intercellular communication. Acute ethanol exposure has been shown to induce significant genetic damage in hippocampal astrocytes, leading to rapid changes in their surface structures and EV profiles (Reyes-Ábalos et al., 2024). Within just one hour of ethanol exposure, astrocytes exhibit reduced nanotube (NT) frequency while increasing EV biogenesis, likely favoring long-distance signaling. Ethanol-induced astrocytic EVs display diverse morphologies, including round, elongated, rosette, donut, drumstick, and cup shapes, contrasting with the limited round and elongated forms observed under control conditions. Moreover, the frequency of small ectosomes and large exosomes is elevated, highlighting a shift in cell communication strategies following ethanol exposure. Ethanol-treated astrocytes release EVs enriched with inflammatory-related proteins and miRNAs associated with TLR4 and NLRP3 signaling pathways (Ibáñez et al., 2019). These EVs, containing miRNAs such as miR-146a, miR-182, and miR-200b, can be internalized by cortical neurons, altering their physiological state. In recipient neurons, astrocytic EVs upregulate inflammatory genes (IL-1 β, Traf6, Mapk14, and Foxo3), proteins like COX-2, and miRNAs, such as miR-146a, triggering apoptosis and promoting neuroinflammation. Moreover, miRNA cargo of astrocyte-shed EVs influence neuronal activity, such as reductions in dendritic growth, dendritic complexity, reduced spike rates, and burst activity (Chaudhuri et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2023). These findings suggest that ethanol-induced astrocyte activation drives the release of pro-inflammatory and neurotoxic EVs, which propagate neuroinflammation and neuro-apoptosis. This highlights a pivotal role of astrocytes in mediating ethanol-induced damage to neural networks and underscores the importance of targeting astrocyte-derived EVs in mitigating ethanol-related neurotoxicity.

Pericyte activation

Pericytes stabilize BBB capillaries but have been less studied in ethanol contexts. Recent work shows that chronic ethanol worsens stroke injury by activating NLRP3 in pericytes via TLR4/NF-κB (Wang et al., 2024b). Since LPS is a potent TLR4 agonist and ethanol increases circulating LPS, gut-derived LPS in EVs could stimulate pericyte inflammation (Guijarro-Muñoz et al., 2014). This highlights the need for further research into the effects of ethanol-induced microbial LPS-carrying (or intestinal) EVs on pericytes.

Understanding the cellular-level interactions between EVs and NVU components is essential for developing therapeutic interventions to mitigate BBB dysfunction and its downstream effects in alcohol-related disorders.

Biomarkers of alcohol-induced brain injury

Several molecular indicators of alcohol-induced pathology have emerged, offering promise for both diagnosis and therapeutic targeting. In the brain, alcohol-induced P2X7R activation stimulates pro-inflammatory cytokine—TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β—release, which are consistently elevated in alcohol-exposed tissues and serves as potential markers for brain injury (Achur, Freeman, and Vrana 2010; Adams et al., 2020; Pascual et al., 2015). Concurrently, oxidative stress markers, such as malondialdehyde (MDA) and 8-hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), signal lipid peroxidation and DNA damage, correlating with neuronal injury and synaptic loss (Chen et al., 2011). Disruption of TJ integrity is another hallmark of alcohol-induced brain injury. Reduced TJP expression (occludin and claudin-5) (Yu et al., 2017) and elevated MMP-3/9 (Muneer et al., 2012) activity mark BBB compromise, highlighting their potential as a biomarker panel (Rubio-Araiz et al., 2017) for assessing BBB integrity in alcohol-induced neuroinflammation.

EVs have emerged as promising non-invasive biomarkers of alcohol-induced CNS injury. Circulating small EVs (sEVs) isolated from individuals with AUD are enriched with miRNAs, such as miR-144–5p, miR-182–5p, miR-142–5p, and miR-7–5p, which regulate synaptic plasticity and inflammation. These miRNAs correlate with hippocampal gene expression and disease progression, suggesting their utility in CNS injury monitoring (Wang et al., 2024a). EV cargo is also influenced by sex, with ethanol-exposed adolescent female mice showing decreased anti-inflammatory miRNAs (e.g., miR-146a-5p, miR-21–5p) and male mice demonstrating elevated levels (Ibáñez et al., 2020). Additionally, elevated levels of cell-specific EV markers, such as glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and L1CAM, was observed in the plasma after ethanol exposure, further indicating astrocytic and neuronal damage (Kodidela et al., 2020).

Beyond their diagnostic value, EVs also have therapeutic potential. MSC-derived EVs can mitigate binge ethanol-induced neuroinflammation, restore myelin integrity, and normalize synaptic protein levels in the prefrontal cortex (Mellado et al., 2023). Notably, ethanol-induced EVs carrying G9a methyltransferase mRNA mediate epigenetic repression via histone methylation. This leads to impaired neurogenesis, revealing a novel mechanism of alcohol-induced brain injury and a potential epigenetic biomarker (Zou et al., 2022). Together, these biomarkers reflect distinct yet interconnected molecular events underlying alcohol-induced brain injury. Integrated profiling of circulating EVs, cytokines, oxidative markers, and barrier-associated proteins offers a comprehensive diagnostic framework. Targeted interventions—such as P2X7R antagonism, antioxidant therapy, and MMP inhibition—may not only attenuate injury but also restore barrier function in alcohol-related neuroimmune disorders.

Conclusion

Chronic alcohol consumption triggers multifaceted neurovascular disruption through oxidative stress, BBB compromise, and EV dysregulation mediated by P2X7R signaling. EVs serve as crucial vectors, disseminating proinflammatory and neurotoxic cargo between peripheral and central compartments. Targeting P2X7R signaling, stabilizing TJPs, and intercepting EV-borne inflammatory mediators represent promising strategies to mitigate alcohol-induced neuroinflammation and barrier dysfunction. Furthermore, when integrated with other molecular markers, EV cargo profiling presents a minimally invasive and powerful tool for early diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring of alcohol-related neuroimmune injury.

Future perspective

Despite significant progress, key gaps remain in our understanding of how EVs contribute to alcohol-induced neuroinflammation and BBB disruption. Future research should adopt a systems-level approach integrating circulating EV profiling, cytokine signatures, oxidative stress markers, and barrier-associated proteins to bridge these gaps and advance clinical translation. Elucidating how chronic alcohol exposure alters EV biogenesis and cargo from specific CNS cell types—microglia, astrocytes, and endothelial cells—and how these EVs influence inflammatory and oxidative signaling pathways will be critical for unraveling disease mechanisms. Special focus should be placed on P2X7R-driven signaling cascades, their role in modulating EV function, and the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis. Rigorous validation of EV-associated biomarkers in clinical cohorts, coupled with the evaluation of combinatorial therapies in preclinical models, will be essential for developing precise, targeted interventions against alcohol-induced neuroimmune disorders.

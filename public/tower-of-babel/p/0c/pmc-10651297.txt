A commentary on ‘Prothrombin induced by vitamin K Absence-II versus alpha-fetoprotein in detection of both resectable hepatocellular carcinoma and early recurrence after curative liver resection: a retrospective cohort study’ (Int J Surg 2022;105:106843)
Authors: Zheyu Zhou, Jinsong Liu, Xiaoliang Xu
Published: 2023

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the paper ‘Prothrombin induced by vitamin K Absence-II versus alpha-fetoprotein in detection of both resectable hepatocellular carcinoma and early recurrence after curative liver resection: a retrospective cohort study’ published in recent issues of the International Journal of Surgery1. We hope our data will confirm and extend the authors’ conclusions.

We retrospectively collected and reviewed non-recurrent hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients who underwent curative hepatectomy at Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital from January 2020 to September 2022. Patients without preoperative alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) or prothrombin induced by vitamin K absence-II (PIVKA-II) data and those who had received targeted therapy, immunotherapy, or interventional therapy were excluded. Ultimately, a total of 210 patients were included in the study. Preoperative laboratory examinations included AFP, PIVKA-II, platelets, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total bilirubin, albumin, prothrombin time, hepatitis B virus surface antigen, and hepatitis C virus antibodies. Pathology parameters included microvascular invasion (MVI), Edmondson–Steiner grade (E-S grade), tumor number, tumor size, degree of hepatic steatosis (F), inflammatory response grading (G), and fibrosis staging (S). Imaging features included cirrhosis and macrovascular invasion (obtained from magnetic resonance imaging or contrast-enhanced computed tomography).

We reached the same conclusion as the authors regarding the detection of resectable HCC. PIVKA-II showed a higher positive rate. A total of 138 (65.7%) patients were preoperatively AFP positive (≥10 ng/ml), while 162 (77.1%) patients were preoperatively PIVKA-II positive (≥40 mAU/ml) (P=0.00953). Positive rates for two biomarkers were similar to the author’s result (66.3 and 78.4%) (Fig. 1a). In addition, 26 (12.4%) patients had only AFP positivity, 50 (23.8%) patients had only PIVKA-II positivity, 112 (53.3%) patients had both AFP and PIVKA-II positivities, and 22 (10.5%) patients had both AFP and PIVKA-II negativities. In that order, we defined these four groups of patients as group 1, group 2, group 3, and group 4.

Comparisons of clinical characteristics among 210 HCC patients. (A) PIVKA-II showed a higher positive rate. (B-F) Variables with significant differences between the four groups.

Due to the lack of complete follow-up data, we could not validate the role of PIVKA-II in predicting early recurrence after HCC resection. This is the drawback of our study. However, our analysis has led to some new conclusions. Table 1 shows comparisons of clinical and histologic characteristics among 210 patients. First, a significantly higher proportion of patients in group 3 had MVI and advanced histological grading (Fig. 1b and c). The presence of MVI is a recognized risk factor for early recurrence and poor prognosis in the clinical practice guidelines published by European Association for the Study of the Liver and American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases in 2018. This was confirmed in the subsequent study2. E-S grade is a well-known histological classification depending on the size and morphology of HCC cells. Zhou et al.3,4 reported that E-S grade was an independent prognostic indicator for disease-free survival and overall survival in HCC patients with or without MVI. Therefore, we infer that patients with both AFP and PIVKA-II positivities may have a shorter survival period. Second, both pathology and imaging confirmed the presence of cirrhosis in a higher proportion of patients in group 1 (Fig. 1d and e). AFP may have a higher sensitivity in identifying cirrhosis. According to the European Association for the Study of the Liver clinical practice guidelines, approximately one-third of patients with cirrhosis will develop HCC in their lifetime. Meanwhile, about 1–8% of cirrhotic patients will develop HCC per year. Thus, despite curative hepatectomy, these patients with cirrhosis-based HCC are at a higher risk of recurrence.

Compared to several years ago, PIVKA-II has now been proven as a biomarker for HCC and is routinely detected clinically. However, as a traditional biomarker of HCC, AFP should not be overlooked either. A previous study showed that HCC patients with triple-positive biomarkers (AFP, AFP-L3, and PIVKA-II) had invasive pathological characteristics and a poor prognosis5. Although we are comparing AFP and PIVKA-II, more studies should also consider combining the two effectively.

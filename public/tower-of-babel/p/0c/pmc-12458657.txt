Randomised, controlled, crossover clinical trial protocol of puerarin for the treatment of metabolic-associated fatty liver disease in China
Authors: Yu Cui, Xinrui Ren, Jiayi Song, Zhi Shang, Wanchun Zhu, Yiwen Tang, Bowu Chen, Zhuo Yu, Yueqiu Gao, Lingying Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough a number of preclinical studies have demonstrated the therapeutic potential of puerarin for metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD), there is a lack of high-quality clinical evidence. This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of puerarin in patients with MAFLD in a randomised, controlled, crossover trial.Methods and analysisThis study will use the randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial design. We plan to enrol 50 patients diagnosed with MAFLD, and they will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either puerarin or placebo (maltodextrin) after a 2-week adaptation period. Participants in the two groups will receive the daily intervention of puerarin (180 mg/day) and placebo (180 mg/day) for 12 weeks, respectively. After a 4-week washout period, puerarin-treated and placebo-treated participants will cross over to receive the daily intervention of placebo and puerarin for 12 more weeks. The primary outcome measure will be defined as the changes in liver fat content, which will be assessed using MRI-proton density fat fraction before and after 12 weeks of puerarin or placebo supplement in patients with MAFLD. The secondary outcome measures include liver and kidney function changes, lipid metabolism indicators, blood glucose levels, iron metabolism parameters, blood routine, serum high-sensitivity C-reactive protein and anthropometric measurements. Additionally, alterations in gut microbiota composition and metabolic activity will be evaluated using 16S ribosomal RNA gene sequencing and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has been approved by the ethics committee of Shuguang Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval Number 2024-1625-208-01). The findings will be published in international peer-reviewed journals and presented at relevant academic conferences.Trial registration numberThe trial has been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400094017).

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a metabolic liver condition closely linked to insulin resistance and genetic predisposition.1 It has emerged as one of the most prevalent chronic liver diseases encountered in clinical practice. Metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) represents a newly proposed definition intended to replace NAFLD.2 Recent studies have indicated that the global prevalence of MAFLD was estimated to be 30.2%, and the prevalence of MAFLD in China has been rapidly escalating over the past decade, with an overall population prevalence now reaching 29.2%.3 4 The natural progression of MAFLD encompasses simple hepatic steatosis, metabolic-associated steatohepatitis, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).5 There is increasing evidence that HCC can manifest at any stage of MAFLD, with 30%–50% of cases progressing directly to HCC without passing through the cirrhotic phase. Furthermore, MAFLD is strongly associated with a high incidence of metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases and colorectal tumours.3 Hitherto, the therapeutic efficacy of pharmacological interventions for MAFLD has been unsatisfactory.

Rezdiffra (resmetirom) received approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2024 and became the first medication for treating non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) in adults, particularly those with significant fibrosis who are at high risk for severe hepatic adverse outcomes.6 Clinical trial data demonstrate that resmetirom can reduce liver fibrosis in patients with NASH by achieving the primary endpoint (a decrease in the NAFLD activity score), while exhibiting favourable safety and tolerability profiles. However, this drug has not been launched in China, and its long-term safety remains unverified. Given such substantial treatment needs, there continues to be significant research value in identifying more effective drugs for both prevention and management of MAFLD.

Puerarin is the main active component extracted from the root of Pueraria lobata (Wild). Clinical studies have demonstrated that puerarin exhibits therapeutic effects on metabolic-associated diseases (eg, high-level fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and insulin resistance).7 8 Preclinical investigations have also confirmed the therapeutic efficacy of puerarin in mouse models of NAFLD.914 However, there is currently a paucity of clinical research data regarding the use of puerarin for treating MAFLD. Here, we design a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial to explore the potential of puerarin in the management of patients with MAFLD, in terms of its safety and efficacy. We aim to provide a puerarin-based therapeutic option for the treatment of MAFLD by generating high-quality, evidence-based clinical data.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

This study will employ randomisation methods using R V.4.3.3 software to achieve a 1:1 random grouping of the puerarin and control (placebo) groups. The block length is set at 4. Random numbers will be generated by a statistician, and allocation concealment will be ensured through the use of sealed envelopes. The specific procedure is as follows: the results of the random grouping will be compiled into random allocation cards, which will then be placed in non-transparent envelopes to maintain allocation concealment. All sealed envelopes containing the random allocations will be securely held by a separate researcher who is not involved in drug distribution or data analysis. Patients who meet the eligibility criteria and have provided informed consent will enter the trial. They will sequentially open the random envelopes according to their order of inclusion and adhere to the instructions on the random cards for group treatment.

Blinding

In this study, to ensure the objectivity and scientific rigour of the results, we will adhere strictly to the principle of blinding. Throughout the course of the study, patients, outcome assessors and statisticians will remain unaware of group allocations in order to minimise potential subjective bias that could affect the outcomes. To effectively implement blinding, patients from different groups will be scheduled for drug distribution, collection and treatment follow-up in separate time windows and distinct rooms. During the entire study period, strict isolation measures will be enforced among patients in different groups to prevent any form of contact or communication that might lead to information leakage and compromise blinding. On completion of the study, a standardised questionnaire will be administered to all participants to ascertain whether they are aware of their assigned group and how they arrived at such conclusions.17 This step is designed to evaluate both the effectiveness and integrity of our blinding procedures. If some participants accurately deduce their group allocation, a thorough analysis of our blinding design will be conducted to identify potential weaknesses and provide guidance for enhancements in future studies.

Throughout this research endeavour, our team is committed to upholding ethical principles by ensuring that all participants voluntarily engage in the study based on comprehensive information provided beforehand. We will explain study design, implementation of blinding procedures and final results after the conclusion of the trial, thereby maintaining transparency while respecting participants’ right to informed consent.

Sample size calculation

This study will employ a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial design. Study participants will be randomly assigned to either Group A or Group B. Group A will initially receive puerarin treatment for 12 weeks, followed by a 4 week washout period, after which they will enter the placebo treatment phase for an additional 12 weeks. Conversely, Group B will first undergo the placebo treatment before receiving puerarin. Based on the liver fat fraction (HFF), change parameters reported in the literature (Δ=2.8%, σ=2.7),18 with α=0.05 (two-sided) and β=0.1, the required sample size was calculated to be 42 cases using PASS V.14.0 software. Considering a 15% dropout rate, a total of 50 patients will be enrolled, randomly divided into Groups A and B, with each group consisting of 25 participants.

Interventions

Drug intervention

Participants assigned to the puerarin group will receive an oral dose of 180 mg/day for a total duration of 12 weeks, whereas those in the placebo group will be administered an identical capsule containing an equivalent amount of maltodextrin. Both investigational products are compounded and dispensed by the hospital pharmacy department. To maintain blinding integrity, the puerarin and placebo formulations will be encapsulated in indistinguishable capsules and packaged identically with respect to appearance, labelling and batch coding.19 The use of hard-shell capsules will be deliberately selected to eliminate potential differences in organoleptic properties (eg, taste and odour), thereby supporting a triple-blind study design.

A washout period of 4 weeks will be incorporated between treatment phases to mitigate potential carryover effects. This interval is determined based on the known pharmacokinetic profile of puerarin, which, following intravenous administration, exhibits a two-compartment open model with a distribution half-life (t1/2α) of 10.5 min, an elimination half-life (t1/2β) of 74 min and a mean residence time of 1.28 hours.20 Given that more than five elimination half-lives (ie, >6 hours) are generally considered sufficient to ensure negligible residual drug levels, the selected 4-week interval offers a conservative safety margin exceeding 600-fold, even when accounting for slower oral absorption.

Besides, all concomitant medications or interventions must be declared in advance and may only be used with prior written approval from the researcher. The use of any non-approved pharmacologic agents or weight management interventions is strictly prohibited throughout the study period.

Gut microbiota assessment

This study will employ 16S ribosomal RNA gene sequencing technology to analyse the operational taxonomic units of the gut microbiota. High-throughput sequencing technology enables precise identification of various microbial species within the gut microbiota, as well as their relative abundances. Furthermore, to comprehensively evaluate the diversity of the microbial community, several α-diversity indices will be calculated, including Sobs (species richness), Chao1 and Ace (species richness estimate), Shannon and Simpson (diversity index) and Coverage (coverage rate). These indices will provide insights into both the richness and evenness of the gut microbiota from multiple perspectives. To investigate differences in microbial community structure across different samples, unweighted and weighted UniFrac distances will be used for β-diversity analysis. The unweighted UniFrac distance primarily reflects variations in community species composition, while the weighted UniFrac distance also takes into account species relative abundance, thereby offering a more comprehensive assessment of differences in community structure.

Additionally, this study will use liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) for non-targeted metabolomics analysis to explore the impact of puerarin on the gut microbial metabolism. LC-MS technology is capable of detecting small molecular metabolites produced by the gut microbial metabolism. Through analysing changes in both types and concentrations of these metabolites, we can elucidate alterations in gut microbial metabolic activity following puerarin intervention. This multi-omics combined analytical approach tries to thoroughly assess puerarin’s effects on gut microbiota from both compositional and functional metabolic perspectives, providing a scientific insight for understanding its potential physiological and pharmacological implications.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis for this study will be performed using a range of statistical software, including SPSS (V.26.0.0), SAS (V.9.4) and R (V.4.1.2). All baseline variables will undergo descriptive statistical analysis, with continuous variables reported as mean±SD or mean (95% CIs) for normally distributed data or median (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables will be expressed as frequency (percentage). The intention-to-treat set will serve as the basis for the primary analysis of the controlled trial. Primary outcome comparisons will be performed using the χ² test. In instances of missing or inconsistent data, either Last Observation Carried Forward or multiple imputation methods will be employed. Secondary outcomes will be evaluated using linear mixed models to account for interindividual and intraindividual variability inherent in the crossover design. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted within each protocol set to assess the robustness of the findings. In all statistical analyses, a two-sided p value of less than 0.05 will be deemed statistically significant, indicating that the results hold meaningful statistical relevance.

Adverse events

During the trial, all adverse events will be monitored by the researchers and reported by the participants. Potential treatment-related adverse events may include allergic reactions, rashes, nausea, vomiting, abdominal bloating, abdominal pain, diarrhoea and other gastrointestinal symptoms. These events will be analysed using either the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test.

Data management

All study data will be recorded using standardised case report forms (CRFs) and accurately entered into the study-specific data management system by trained researchers. To ensure subject privacy, all information pertaining to personal identity (such as name, ID number, phone number, etc) will be anonymised and assigned a unique study number. All original records containing personal information of patients will be securely stored in strict compliance with relevant regulatory requirements for a minimum duration of 5 years. At our clinical research centre, data quality will undergo regular assessment by an independent data manager. The data manager will oversee the progress of the trial, ensuring both the integrity and accuracy of the data while independently evaluating the potential adverse events to uphold the scientific rigour of the research process and safeguard participant safety.

Quality management

The study protocol for this clinical trial has undergone a comprehensive evaluation and optimisation by a multidisciplinary team of experts, encompassing pharmacology, gastroenterology, statistics and clinical research methodology. Any modifications to the trial protocol will adhere to a stringent approval process and will be promptly submitted to the ethics committee of Shuguang Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine for documentation purposes. This practice guarantees traceability and transparency within our data management processes. Furthermore, all study-related activities will strictly conform to pre-established Standard Operating Procedures in order to uphold high standards of quality throughout the study.

Discussion

Preclinical results have demonstrated the clinical translation potential of puerarin for treating MAFLD. However, no clinical study has been performed to validate the safety and efficacy of puerarin for patients with MAFLD. Therefore, our group aims to provide evidence-based medical support for the clinical use of puerarin in treating MAFLD through this randomised, controlled, crossover trial.

Liver biopsy, regarded as the ‘gold standard’ for diagnosing MAFLD, faces limitations in widespread application within clinical research due to its invasive nature and associated trauma.28 In 2018, MRI-PDFF was recommended by the FDA-issued guidelines as a quantitative method for assessing treatment effects in early-phase clinical trials focused on NASH.29 Accordingly, this study employs MRI-PDFF to evaluate MAFLD. This methodology not only adheres to international guidelines but also provides significant clinical applicability while effectively mitigating risks and inconveniences linked to invasive procedures.27 30

In recent years, increasing research evidence has demonstrated that the gut microbiota is closely associated with the onset and progression of MAFLD.3133 To further investigate therapeutic mechanisms underlying puerarin treatment for MAFLD, this study will collect faecal samples from patients before and after treatment. By analysing the composition changes in the gut microbiota, we try to elucidate the regulatory effects of puerarin on gut microbiota and its correlation with therapeutic outcomes in MAFLD. These findings are anticipated to provide insight into the clinical outcomes of puerarin in treating MAFLD.

Although this study adheres to rigorous scientific principles in its design and execution, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the sample size of this clinical trial is relatively small, which may induce selection bias and affect the generalisability and extrapolation of the results. It may not provide sufficient power for complex secondary endpoints such as gut microbiota composition and metabolism. Second, each subject’s observation period extends over 28 weeks. This may decrease the compliance among participants and increase the risks related to dropout or loss to follow-up factors, which collectively could compromise both the integrity and reliability of our findings. Third, this study adopted relatively strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to eliminate potential confounding effects and ensure the internal consistency of the research results. However, it might unintentionally exclude some patients in the real world, thereby limiting the applicability of the conclusions to a broader population. Future studies could moderately relax the inclusion and exclusion criteria while maintaining necessary bias control to verify the generalisability of the current study’s results to different subgroups and a larger population. Finally, definitive conclusions regarding its long-term safety and efficacy cannot be drawn at this time because of the small sample size. Future research should aim to further explore the long-term safety profiles, effectiveness and potential mechanisms by which puerarin exerts its effects on MAFLD in order to provide more comprehensive evidence-based medical support for clinical management strategies targeting this condition.

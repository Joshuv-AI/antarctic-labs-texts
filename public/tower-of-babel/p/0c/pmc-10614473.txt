Cx43 Facilitates Mesenchymal Transition of Endothelial Cells Induced by Shear Stress
Authors: En Zhou, Jing Zhou, Changlong Bi, Zongqi Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to determine the function of Cx43 in the endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) process of endothelial cells (ECs) and to explore the potential signaling pathways underlying these functions.MethodsECs were extracted from rat aorta. ECs were transfected with Cx43 cDNA and Cx43 siRNA and then exposed to 5 or 12 dyne/cm2. Immunofluorescence staining was used to detect the expression of SM22α, Cx43, and acetylated α-tubulin in ECs. Western blotting was used to detect the protein expression of α-SMA, CD31, Cx43, H1-calponin, Ift88, and p-smad3 in ECs.ResultsThe expression of αSMA, SM22α, and Cx43 was significantly increased, and CD31 was markedly decreased in ECs treated with laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2. The Cx43 cDNA transfection could induce the expression of SM22α or H1-calponin and attenuate CD31 expression in ECs. Also, Cx43 overexpression harms cilia formation in ECs exposed to 5 dyn/cm2, accompanied with the regulated Ift88 and smad signaling.ConclusionsThis study found that laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 would increase the expression of Cx43 to facilitate the EndMT process of ECs, associated with morphological changes in primary cilia and the decreased expression of Ift88 in ECs.

Introduction

Atherosclerosis is a focal disease principally affecting the aorta, the carotid, the coronary, and the iliac arteries and is mainly initiated by a dysfunctional state of the vascular endothelium [1]. Besides the systemic nature of atherosclerotic risk factors such as elevated and modified LDL and hyperglycemia, low shear stress (0–4 dyn/cm2), compared to physiologic shear stress (10–20 dyn/cm2), is an important biomechanical contributor to endothelium dysfunction [2]. Accumulating evidence shows that low shear stress promotes atherosclerosis [3, 4], especially in early stages of coronary atherosclerosis in humans [5], supported by the findings that atherosclerotic plaques often form at branch points or at curved areas of large arteries where they are frequently exposed to low shear stress [6].

Vascular endothelial cells (ECs) linked by gap junctions consist of connexins. Expression of connexins was regulated by shear stress and was involved in atherosclerosis [7]. In an arterial endothelium, ECs mainly express Cx40 and Cx37 [8, 9]. Low laminar shear stress, but not oscillatory shear stress, decreased Cx37 expression to recruit more macrophages in plaques [10]. Furthermore, the previous study in vivo has found that Cx43 expression was elevated in ECs exposed to low shear stress [11]. Others and our previous studies in vitro showed that low laminar shear stress induced Cx43 expression in ECs [3, 12]. In addition, it was shown that the progression of atherosclerotic plaque was reduced in a mouse model, which expressed a low level of Cx43 [13]. These results suggested that the low shear stress-induced Cx43 expression may link with the progression of atherosclerotic plaque.

Endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) of ECs plays a vital role in the development of atherosclerosis and instability of atherosclerotic plaque [14]. EndMT of ECs is characterized by the reduced expression of endothelial markers such as VE-cadherin and CD31 and the acquired expression of mesenchymal markers such as alpha smooth muscle actin, N-cadherin, and calponin [15]. EndMT process could be induced by low shear stress in ECs [16]. For instance, low laminar shear stress (2 or 4 dyne/cm2) induced elongated and aligned cells and upregulated EndMT-related gene or protein expression in ECs [17, 18]. Interestingly, it was reported that EndMT process could be reduced by blocking Cx43 expression in corneal ECs [19]. In view of low shear stress-induced Cx43 expression in ECs, taken together, it is plausible to hypothesize that low shear stress-induced Cx43 expression is involved in EndMT of ECs.

The cilia were demonstrated to be necessary for the EndMT process by low shear stress in ECs [20]. Cilia are specialized membrane-covered rod-like organelles protruding from virtually all mammalian cells [21]. Primary cilia have been reported to be present on ECs [22] and located in the low shear stress area, such as atherosclerotic predilection sites [23]. The basal body of the primary cilium is linked to the microtubule-organizing center from which the cytoskeletal microtubules spread throughout the cell. The microtubular system is in turn connected to other components of the cytoskeleton. Bending of the primary cilium therefore results in the transduction of mechanical force such as shear stress throughout the cell, including the cell-cell and cell-matrix junctions that are connected to the cytoskeleton [23]. The dissolution of cilia contributed to shear stress-induced EndMT of ECs during the formation of cardiac valves [24]. Recently, a previous study showed that the genetic ablation of Cx43 in zebrafish and mice diminished motile cilia in ependymal cells [25]. However, the role of Cx43 induced by low shear stress in the regulation of cilia formation during the EndMT process of ECs remains unclear. Also, the previous study has found that intraflagellar transport (IFT) protein (IFT88) was demonstrated to be responsible to cilia signaling pathways [26]. Disruption of IFT proteins or mutation of IFT88 gene would interfere with signaling and maintenance of the cilia structure, frequently resulting in stunted or absent primary cilia [27, 28]. Moreover, research has demonstrated that receptor-associated Smad proteins, specifically the phosphorylated forms of Smad2 and Smad3, are pivotal in the process of EndMT. Smad2/3 collaborates with Smad4, subsequently relocating to the nucleus to initiate a series of Smad-mediated transcriptional events. These events encompass a range of biological processes, including epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), EndMT, and fibrosis, among others [29–31]. The aim of this study was to determine the function of Cx43 in the EndMT process of ECs and to explore the potential signaling pathways underlying these functions.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Approval

All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Research Ethics Committee of the No. 9 People’s Hospital affiliated with the Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. All animals were sacrificed using bilateral thoracotomy during anesthesia. In addition, this study did not use human subjects, so the Declaration of Helsinki is not applicable. Aspen wood shavings were used as the substrate for rats’ bedding. We implemented a regular schedule for bedding replacement to maintain cleanliness and mitigate potential health risks associated with the accumulation of waste products.

Cell Isolation and Culture

Aortic ECs were isolated from male SD rats, as described previously [32]. The cells were cultured in endothelial cell growth medium (EGM2, Lonza, Basel, Switzerland) comprising endothelial cell basal medium-2, supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (catalog No. 10099-141, Gibco). The cells were then cultured at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. The cultured medium was removed after 4–7 days and gently washed with phosphate-buffered solution (PBS). The attached cells were then used for further experiments. The same batch of cells was identified using CD31 (catalog No. ab232533, Abcam).

Shear Stress Application to ECs

In shear stress experiments, the ECs were exposed to 5 or 12 dyne/cm2 for 6 h with a parallel plate flow chamber system as described in our previous study [3]. The shear stress (τ) was calculated as 6 μQ/wh2, where μ is the viscosity of the perfusate, Q is the flow rate, and w (= 38 mm) and h (= 0.25 mm) are the height and width of the flow chamber, respectively. μ = 0.007 dyn*s/cm2 is the fluid viscosity for the medium at 37°C.

Immunocytochemistry

ECs were cultured on cell slides and then rinsed with PBS, before being fixed for 15 min at room temperature. Slides were then blocked for 2 h in PBS containing 5% normal goat serum and 2% BSA and incubated with monoclonal antibodies (diluted 1:500) against SM22α (catalog No. SAB1400272, Sigma-Aldrich), Cx43 (catalog No. 3512, Cell Signaling Technology), and acetylated α-tubulin (catalog No. sc-23950, Santa Cruz) overnight at 4°C. Cells were rinsed 5 times in PBS and incubated with Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated antibody (catalog No. sc-362282, Santa Cruz) and Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated antibody (catalog No. sc-362257, Santa Cruz) (diluted 1:500) for 1 h at room temperature. The mean fluorescence intensity of SM22α signaling was evaluated using ImageJ software (ImageJ).

Transfection

The gene ID of Cx43 in NCBI was NC_051355.1, and both the Cx43 cDNA and siRNAs were synthesized by Shanghai Biotech. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with Cx43 cDNA, Cx43 siRNA, and their negative controls using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (catalog No. 11668019, Invitrogen) according the manufacturer’s instructions. Transfections were performed on day 3. The downstream experiments were performed 24 h after the final transfection.

Western Blot

Total protein from the ECs was extracted using RIPA buffer and then separated using 10% SDS-PAGE before being electrophoretically transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with primary antibodies against α-SMA (catalog No. CBL171, Millipore Corporation), CD31 (catalog No. ab232533, Abcam), Cx43 (catalog No. 3512, Cell Signaling Technology), H1-calponin (catalog No. HPA014263, Sigma), Ift88 (catalog No. SAB2107033, Sigma), p-smad3 (catalog No. 9520, Cell Signaling Technology), smad3 (catalog No. 9523, Cell Signaling Technology), and GAPDH (catalog No. sc-365062, Santa Cruz). Appropriate horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP, catalog No. sc-2005, Santa Cruz; goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP, catalog No. sc-2004, Santa Cruz) were then used to facilitate visualization and protein bands were detected using an ECL system (Fusion FX7).

Statistical Analysis

The data were expressed as maximum, minimum, median, 25th percentile, and 75th percentile, which were analyzed by ANOVA followed by the least significant difference t test for a post hoc comparison using the SPSS 26.0 software. p values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Mesenchymal Transition of Endothelial Cells Induced by Shear Stress

ECs were identified as described in our previous research. ECs in static culture (static group) showed a cobblestone-like appearance, while ECs exposed to the shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 (5 dyn/cm2 group), not to the shear stress at 12 dyn/cm2 (12 dyn/cm2 group), exhibited a spindle-shaped appearance (Fig. 1a). The fluorescence intensity of SM22α increased in the 5 dyn/cm2 group compared to the static group (2.73 ± 0.42 vs. 0.58 ± 0.35), but did not change in the 12 dyn/cm2 group (0.84 ± 0.67) (Fig. 1b). The expression of αSMA was significantly increased in the 5 dyn/cm2 group (1.14 ± 0.27) compared to the static group (0.051 ± 0.045) or 12 dyn/cm2 group (0.039 ± 0.049). The expression of Cx43 was also increased in the 5 dyn/cm2 group (2.72 ± 0.43) compared to the static group (0.60 ± 0.12) and 12 dyn/cm2 group (0.73 ± 0.33). The expression of CD31 in the 5 dyn/cm2 group (0.63 ± 0.10) was markedly decreased compared to the static (1.05 ± 0.16) and 12 dyn/cm2 groups (1.08 ± 0.24). With the gradual increase of shear stress, the expression of KLF2 also increased (Fig. 1c–g).

Mesenchymal transition of ECs induced by shear stress. a Upper part: morphology of ECs under light microscope exposed to different shear stresses (static, 5 dyn/cm2, 12 dyn/cm2). Bottom part: immunofluorescence staining of SM22α in ECs. b The result of mean fluorescence intensity of SM22a/nuclear signal intensity. c–g The expression of CD31, αSMA, Cx43, and KLF2 in ECs under different shear stresses (static, 5 dyn/cm2, 12 dyn/cm2) (*p < 0.05, n = 5).

Cx43 Overexpression Facilitates Mesenchymal Transition

The expression of Cx43 is significantly increased in ECs transfected with Cx43 cDNA and decreased in ECs transfected with Cx43 siRNA. Cx43 expression did not change significantly in ECs transfected with the negative controls for cDNA or siRNA (online suppl. Fig. B; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000533320). When silencing Cx43, CD31 was detected in ECs treated with 5 dyn/cm2 (shCx43 5 dyn/cm2 group: 3.95 ± 0.73). The Cx43 cDNA transfection could attenuate CD31 expression in the ECs cultured at 12 dyn/cm2 (cDNACx43 12 dyn/cm2 group: 1.02 ± 0.19) (Fig. 2a–c). SM22α or H1-calponin expression could be hardly detected in the static group (SM22α: 0.08 ± 0.08; H1-calponin: 0.08 ± 0.07) and could be detected in the shCx43 5 dyn/cm2 group (SM22α: 1.01 ± 0.16; H1-calponin: 0.54 ± 0.18). The expression of SM22α or H1-calponin was significantly increased in the cDNACx43 12 dyn/cm2 group (SM22α: 3.78 ± 0.47; H1-calponin: 3.79 ± 0.85) (Fig. 2d–f).

a–f Cx43 overexpression facilitates mesenchymal transition. The expression of Cx43, CD31, SM22α, and H1-calponin in ECs was detected by Western blot (static: ECs cultured in static culture; shCx43 5 dyn/cm2: ECs were treated with siRNA of Cx43 and under 5 dyn/cm2; cDNACx43 12 dyn/cm2: ECs were treated with cDNA of Cx43 and under 12 dyn/cm2; Con-shCx43 5 dyn/cm2: ECs were treated with a negative control of siRNA of Cx43 and under 5 dyn/cm2; Con-cDNACx43 12 dyn/cm2: ECs were treated with a negative control of cDNA of Cx43 and under 12 dyn/cm2) (*p < 0.05, n = 5).

Cx43 Overexpression Harms Cilia Formation

Immunofluorescence shows that no coexpression of Cx43 with acetylated α-tubulin was observed in ECs cultured under static conditions. Acetylated α-tubulin appeared clustered at the position of the cilium. When ECs cultured under 5 dyn/cm2, Cx43 was coexpressed with acetylated α-tubulin in the cytoplasm. Although a fraction of acetylated α-tubulin also appeared enriched at the cell membrane, it did not form a ciliated morphology. This phenomenon was more pronounced after the overexpression of Cx43. When overexpressed Cx43 in ECs cultured under 5 dyn/cm2, acetylated α-tubulin was hardly detected at the cell membrane. But after silencing Cx43, tubulin was more expressed on the EC surface even when ECs were cultured under 5 dyn/cm2 (Fig. 3).

Cx43 overexpression harms cilia formation. The immunofluorescence of ECs (green, acetylated α-tubulin; red, Cx43; blue, DAPI; yellow arrow, cilia area).

Under Low Shear Stress, Changes in Cx43 Expression Affect Ift88 and Smad Signaling

The expression of Ift88 was decreased significantly in ECs treated with 5 dyn/cm2 shear stress (5 dyn/cm2 group: 0.94 ± 0.20) compared with the static group (4.71 ± 0.44), which was nearly abolished by Cx43 cDNA (cDNA-Cx43, 5 dyn/cm2 group: 0.44 ± 0.14) and restored by Cx43 siRNA (shCx43, 5 dyn/cm2 group: 4.09 ± 0.56). The phosphorylation of smad3 was increased in the 5 dyn/cm2 group (1.22 ± 0.28) compared with the static group (0.64 ± 0.19), which was significantly decreased markedly in shCx43 5 dyn/cm2 group (0.63 ± 0.14) and reinforced in cDNA-Cx43 5 dyn/cm2 group (1.39 ± 0.16) (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Atherosclerosis is a multifocal, smoldering, immunoinflammatory disease of medium-sized and large arteries fueled by lipids. ECs, leukocytes, and intimal smooth muscle cells are the major players in the development of this disease [33]. Mesenchymal transition of ECs has been reported to be an important part of the process of atherosclerosis [34]. Several studies have shown that the shear stress can affect the process of EndMT [17, 18]. However, the mechanisms underlying shear stress regulation of EndMT are not fully elucidated. We detected the chronological change in the expression of Cx43 mRNA by using RT-PCT. As shown in the online supplementary figure, we found that Cx43 mRNA was increased at 1 h after exposure to shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2, which maintained a higher level until 12 h. In contrast, after receiving shear stress at 12 dyn/cm2, Cx43 mRNA would increase markedly at 6 h, but decreased dramatically at 12 h, which were partly consistent with the previously published findings. In the present study, the laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 induced EndMT process in ECs. This EndMT process could be reversed by Cx43 silencing and promoted by Cx43 overexpression, accompanied by harmed cilia formation. The current findings suggest that Cx43-regulated cilia were involved in EndMT of ECs induced by shear stress.

ECs undergoing EndMT process play important roles in intimal hyperplasia and unstable plaque and then contribute much to atherosclerosis [15]. Atherosclerotic lesions occur preferentially at vessel curvatures, branching points, and bifurcations, where the disturbed blood flow usually generates oscillatory and low shear stress. Additionally, previous studies showed that either low oscillatory shear stress or low unidirectional shear stress could induce ECs to enhance the expression of EndMT markers, such as N-cadherin and α-SMA [35]. However, in this study, by using a parallel plate flow, ECs exposed to low laminar shear stress (5 dyn/cm2), not high laminar shear stress (12 dyn/cm2), underwent EndMT process, which was characterized by the decreased expression of CD31 and the increased expression of α-SMA. The current findings were consistent with the recently reported results that interstitial cell markers vimentin, a-SMA, and FSP-1 in ECs were significantly increased by low laminar shear stress [18]. Taken together, these results suggest that the magnitude of shear stress might contribute much more than the flow pattern to EndMT of ECs.

Connexins, constituents of connexin hemichannels and intercellular gap junctions, have been implicated in atherosclerosis. Especially, it has been found that Cx43 is highly localized to the sites subjected to turbulent shear stress from the disturbed blood flow [11]. In vitro studies such as the oscillatory or pulsed unidirectional shear stress also altered the expression of Cx43 in ECs [36]. In the present study, we also found that the expression of Cx43 was increased in ECs induced by low laminar shear stress. It was widely depicted that Cx43 accelerated the EMT process [37, 38], in which TGF signaling pathways play a vital role [39]. It is notable that TGF signaling pathways have been well established in the initiation of EndMT process [40]. However, there are few studies to determine the link between Cx43 and EndMT in ECs. Therefore, in the present study we explored whether Cx43 would affect the process of EndMT under low shear stress. We found that overexpressing Cx43 in ECs under shear stress at 12 dyn/cm2 led to increase the expression of SM22a and H1-calponin and to decrease the expression of CD31 in ECs. In addition, such expressions of these proteins in ECs were also induced by shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2, and silencing Cx43 in ECs attenuated this phenomenon. These results suggested that Cx43 may facilitate EndMT in ECs exposed to shear stress at either 5 or 12 dyn/cm2.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the primary cilium is an endothelial shear stress sensor in areas exposed to low and oscillating shear stress [23], and the absence of primary cilia is critically important for EndMT process [20, 41, 42]. The primary cilium is a microtubule bundle, which protrudes from the luminal cell surface as a mechanotransduction structure. Acetylated α-tubulin has been widely used to detect primary cilia [43]. In this study, statically cultured ECs expressed acetylated α-tubulin, which was located at the ciliary axoneme. However, when ECs were treated by the laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2, the acetylated α-tubulin expression was located in the cytoplasm. These results suggested that laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 would regulate the primary cilium formation, which were similar to the results that laminar shear stress but not disturbed shear stress induced a cilia disassembly [44].

It is notable that blocking Cx43 expression was previously demonstrated to augment tubulin expression in astrocytes [45], and downregulating Cx43 expression could promote tubulin assembly in mast or fibroblast cells [46]. These findings suggested that Cx43 expression might be involved in cilia formation. In the present study, when ECs were treated by Cx43 cDNA, the cilia were not obviously detected. In addition, the cilia were partly restored by Cx43 shRNA in ECs with acetylated α-tubulin expression at the ciliary axoneme, suggesting that Cx43 overexpression might hamper primary cilia assembly. Interestingly, when Cx43 was overexpressed in ECs exposed to 5 dyn/cm2, the colocation between Cx43 and acetylated α-tubulin was detected in cytoplasm, suggesting that Cx43 might bind to tubulin to harm primary cilia assembly, which was similar to that in fibroblast cells [46]. However, in the previous study, Dvl2 was proposed to play a vital role in the assembly and disassembly of primary cilia under different shear stresses [44]. It needs to be explored further whether Dvl2 was involved in primary cilia regulation in the present study.

In the present study, under shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2, the expression of Ift88 was significantly decreased in the ECs, which was nearly abolished and reversed by Cx43 cDNA and Cx43 shRNA, respectively. Our findings suggested that shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 might induce Cx43 expression to attenuate IFT88 expression, therefore, by doing so to interfere with primary cilia assembly.

In addition, in this study, overexpressing Cx43 increased markedly the phosphorylation of smad3 in ECs exposed to 5 dyn/cm2, which was significantly decreased by Cx43 shRNA, suggesting that Cx43 increased by shear stress would activate smad3 signaling pathways. Our findings were consistent with the results that shear stress at 2 dyn/cm2 could activate smad2 signaling independent of TGF-β in aortic ECs [47]. Previous studies had demonstrated that Cx43 could competitively bind to microtubules to activate Smad signaling pathways [48, 49]. Given that overexpression of Cx43 colocalized with tubulin in ECs exposed to 5 dyn/cm2 in this study, these findings suggested that shear tress at 5 dyn/cm2 might promote Cx43 to bind to microtubules to interfere with primary cilia assembly and activate Smad3 signaling pathways, which facilitate the EndMT process in ECs.

In summary, this study found that laminar shear stress at 5 dyn/cm2 would increase the expression of Cx43 to facilitate EndMT process of ECs, associated with morphological changes in primary cilia and the decreased expression of Ift88 in ECs. These may eventually contribute the occurrence and development of atherosclerosis. These findings provide a new direction for the occurrence and development of atherosclerosis and may provide new ideas and directions for the treatment of atherosclerosis.

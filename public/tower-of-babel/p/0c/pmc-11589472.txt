An integrated pipeline facilitates fast cloning of a new powdery mildew resistance gene from the wheat wild relative Aegilops umbellulata
Authors: Huagang He, Jiale Wang, Jiabao Liang, Qianyuan Zhang, Minfeng Xue, Zhaozhao Chen, Qiulian Tang, Xiaobei Chen, Shanying Zhu, Yajun Wang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum L., 2n = 6x = 42, AABBDD) is a major cereal crop consumed by approximately 30% of the global population. Wheat production faces continuous challenges from various fungal diseases, including powdery mildew, rusts, and Fusarium head blight (Singh et al., 2016). Aegilops umbellulata (Ae. umbellulate, 2n = 2x = 14, UU), a diploid species within the genus Aegilops and closely related to cultivated bread wheat, exhibits resistance to several wheat pathogens, making it a valuable source of disease resistance genes for wheat improvement. Notably, several resistance genes from Ae. umbellulata have been successfully transferred into bread wheat, including the powdery mildew resistance gene PmY39 (Zhu et al., 2006), the leaf rust resistance genes Lr9 (Sears, 1956) and Lr76 (Bansal et al., 2020), and the stripe rust resistance gene Yr70 (Bansal et al., 2020). Here, we investigate the responses of a panel of Ae. umbellulata accessions to Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici (Bgt), the pathogen causing wheat powdery mildew, and report the cloning of the powdery mildew resistance gene PmAeu1 through an integrated approach combining bulked segregant RNA sequencing (BSR-seq), fine genetic mapping, low-depth PacBio sequencing, and functional validation via stable transformation in wheat.

Fifty-two Ae. umbellulata accessions were evaluated for their seedling responses to the Bgt isolate BgtYZ01. We identified 36 powdery mildew-resistant and 16 susceptible accessions (Figure 1A and 1B; Supplemental Table 1). The resistant accession AE 1617 (IT 0) was crossed with the susceptible accession AE 538 (IT 4) to generate an F2 population of 253 individuals. Inoculation with isolate BgtYZ01 showed that 194 and 59 F2 plants were resistant and susceptible to BgtYZ01, respectively, fitting a 3:1 ratio (χ2 = 0.381, p = 0.537). From eight segregating F3 families, 774 resistant and 246 susceptible individuals were identified, which also conformed to a 3:1 ratio (χ2 = 0.424, p = 0.515). These results indicate that resistance in AE 1617 is controlled by a single dominant gene, designated PmAeu1.

RNA-seq analysis of the resistant (R) and susceptible (S) bulks, each composed of 50 F3 plants with contrasting phenotypes (Figure 1C), yielded 32 971 233 and 39 466 896 high-quality clean reads, respectively. BSR-seq analysis identified 19 423 409 (58.91%) and 22 983 773 (58.24%) reads uniquely mapped to the genome of Ae. umbellulata accession TA1851 from the R and S bulk, respectively (Abrouk et al., 2023). SNP and indel (insertion or deletion) calling identified 18 576 SNPs and 9242 indels between the two RNA bulks, with 2423 SNPs and 1356 indels located on chromosome 2U. PmAeu1 was inferred to reside on the long arm of chromosome 2U (2UL), as evidenced by the significant clustering of SNPs and indels on this chromosome arm (Figure 1D).

We developed 5 Kompetitive Allele-Specific PCR and 2 Cleaved Amplified Polymorphic Sequence markers (Supplemental Table 2) based on the SNPs and indels anchored on chromosome 2UL, covering a 17.5-Mb region around the BSR-seq peak. These markers delineated the PmAeu1 locus to a 6.4-Mb interval flanked by markers X2U-4 and X2U-6, which contained 19 recombination events after genotyping 74 susceptible F3 individuals (Supplemental Table 3). Genotyping of an additional 172 susceptible F3 plants with these flanking markers identified 20 new recombinants, resulting in a total of 39 recombinants from 246 susceptible F3 plants. These recombinants were further genotyped with 6 new markers, further narrowing down the PmAeu1 locus to a 326-kb interval in the reference genome of TA1851, flanked by markers X2U-4.4 and X2U-5B (Figure 1D; Supplemental Table 4).

Accession TA1851 was found to be susceptible to isolate BgtYZ01 (Figure 1B), suggesting that it either lacks the PmAeu1 locus or possesses a non-functional allele. To clone PmAeu1, we sequenced the genome of the PmAeu1 donor accession AE 1617 using the PacBio circular consensus sequencing platform. We obtained ∼57.2-Gb high fidelity (HiFi) reads providing approximately 13.5-fold coverage of the TA1851 genome, which is 4.25 Gb (Abrouk et al., 2023). The contig-level assembly yielded 2211 contigs (N50 = 5.43 Mb; total length = 4.29 Gb). Notably, contig ptg000283l, which is 14.41 Mb in size, contains a 287-kb sequence flanked by markers X2U-4.4 and X2U-5B, suggesting that it carries PmAeu1. To determine the minimum input read coverage necessary for generating an appropriate reference genome, we produced assemblies from randomly extracted subsets of the total PacBio HiFi reads for both AE 1617 and TA1851, ranging from 4- to 12-fold coverage. Assemblies for AE 1617 with 8-, 10-, and 12-fold coverage achieved contig N50 lengths of 0.83 Mb, 2.01 Mb, and 3.79 Mb, respectively, spanning the 287-kb PmAeu1 interval (Figure 1E; Supplemental Table 5). Similarly, TA1851 assemblies with 8-, 10-, and 12-fold coverage achieved contig N50 lengths of 0.83 Mb, 2.18 Mb, and 4.20 Mb, respectively, covering the corresponding 326-kb PmAeu1 interval in the TA1851 genome (Supplementary Table 6). These results suggest that 8- to 10-fold coverage of PacBio circular consensus sequencing data is sufficient for producing a reference suitable for gene cloning.

To identify PmAeu1, RNA-seq reads from the R and S bulks were aligned to the AE 1617 genome assembly. Analysis of a 480-kb region containing markers X2U-4.4 and X2U-5B revealed an 11-kb segment with high read-mapping depth in the R bulk but none in the S bulk (Figure 1F). This segment corresponds to three transcript isoforms from the de novo transcriptome assembly of AE 1617. All three isoforms share identical coding sequences (CDSs) that encode a coiled-coil-nucleotide-binding site-leucine-rich repeat (NLR) protein but differ in their ultra-long 3′ UTRs (3011 bp, 2999 bp, and 2756 bp) (Figure 1G). We designated this gene as AeuNLR1 and developed a gene-specific marker, XAeuNLR1. Molecular analysis confirmed XAeuNLR1 as a dominant marker specific to the resistant parent AE 1617 (Supplemental Figure 1), co-segregating with resistance conferred by PmAeu1 in the F3 population (Figure 1D). qPCR analysis verified that AeuNLR1 is expressed in AE 1617 (Supplemental Figure 2). A 13 564-bp genomic AeuNLR1 fragment, including 4737 bp of CDS, 3233 bp of upstream sequence, and 5594 bp of downstream regulatory sequences, was cloned (Supplemental Table 7; Supplemental Figure 3) and delivered into the susceptible wheat cultivar Fielder via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Six independent AeuNLR1 transgenic T0 plants were obtained, five of which were verified as transgene positive by PCR (Supplemental Figure 4). The expression of AeuNLR1 in these six T0 plants was also confirmed by qPCR (Supplemental Figure 5). At the tillering stage, these transgenic plants exhibited immunity to Bgt isolate BgtYZ01, whereas both the negative control and the non-transgenic Fielder were susceptible (Supplemental Figure 6). AeuNLR1-positive T1 individuals were resistant to BgtYZ01, whereas their AeuNLR1-negative T1 siblings showed susceptibility comparable to Fielder (Figure 1H), confirming AeuNLR1 as the causal gene for PmAeu1-mediated resistance.

We investigated the distribution of PmAeu1 in a panel of Ae. umbellulata accessions using the marker XAeuNLR1. We found that 20 of 36 resistant accessions (including AE 1617) and 1 of 17 susceptible accessions (PI 554417) were positive for XAeuNLR1, suggesting the presence of PmAeu1 or its alleles. Sanger sequencing of these positive accessions revealed eight PmAeu1 alleles. Among these, PmAeu1a, identical to the allele in AE 1617, was found in 11 resistant accessions. Alleles PmAeu1b and PmAeu1c, found in resistant accessions AE 1070 and AE 1339, respectively, encode complete NLR proteins, indicating that they are likely functional alleles. The non-functional alleles PmAeu1d–PmAeu1h, characterized by 1-bp or 2-bp frameshift indels, were found in the remaining 7 resistant accessions and the susceptible accession PI 554417 (Supplementary Table 1). Thus, the PmAeu1 locus accounts for 30.6% (11 of 36) of the powdery mildew resistance observed in Ae. umbellulata.

Phylogenetic analysis comparing PMAeu1 with other reported NLR proteins from wheat and barley (Hordeum vulgare) indicated that PmAeu1 clusters closely with LR1 and PM60; however, it shares only 34.2% and 34.8% sequence identity with them, respectively. In contrast, PmAeu1 shows 73% identity with its ortholog TraesCS2D03G1059100 in the wheat cultivar Chinese Spring. This suggests that PmAeu1, originating from Ae. umbellulata, represents a novel resistance gene with orthologs that have yet to be cloned.

Bread wheat has many cultivated and wild relatives that are useful for wheat improvement. However, distant hybridization barriers and linkage drags hinder the transfer of excellent alien genes into bread wheat, limiting their application in breeding programs. Suppressed recombination between alien and wheat chromosomes impedes the cloning of alien genes despite their introgression into the wheat background (Zhu et al., 2023). An alternative and efficient approach involves directly cloning genes of interest from wheat relatives and transforming them into elite cultivars. In the present study, Ae. umbellulata accessions exhibited diverse responses to wheat powdery mildew, enabling the creation of a bi-parental population segregating for disease resistance. Leveraging the relatively small diploid genome (4.25 Gb) of Ae. umbellulata, we quickly mapped PmAeu1 to an interval of 287 kb on chromosome 2UL using BSR-seq and fine genetic mapping. Recent research has demonstrated that long-read genome and transcriptome sequencing are powerful tools for cloning disease resistance genes in wheat and wild emmer (Athiyannan et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). Here, low-depth PacBio sequencing of the resistant parent AE 1617 provided a contig-level assembly covering the PmAeu1 interval. Mapping BSR-seq data onto this reference assembly facilitated the identification of the candidate gene. This integrated approach, combining BSR-seq, fine genetic mapping, and low-depth PacBio sequencing and assembly (Supplemental Figure 8), is highly efficient for rapidly cloning genes from diploid wild wheat species, offering an alternative for utilizing their genetic resources.

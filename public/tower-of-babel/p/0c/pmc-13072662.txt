IL3RA identified as novel biomarker and therapeutic target for ER+ breast cancer through plasma proteome-wide mendelian randomization and TCGA database analysis
Authors: Heting Mei, Zehan Zhang, Nan Jiang, Wenping Lu, Lei Chang, Qingya Song, Feng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEstrogen receptor-positive (ER+) breast cancer, a leading cause of female cancer mortality, faces therapeutic challenges due to endocrine resistance. Plasma proteins, bridging genetic variation and disease phenotypes, offer potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, yet their causal roles in the pathogenesis of ER+ breast cancer remain underexplored.MethodsUsing two-sample Mendelian randomization (TSMR) and Bayesian colocalization, we analyzed associations between plasma protein quantitative trait loci from deCODE/Fenland cohorts and ER+ breast cancer. DSigDB predicted drugs targeting identified protein, while TCGA assessed the prognostic value.ResultsTSMR identified 38 causal plasma proteins (deCODE), with Bayesian analysis prioritizing 12 candidates. IL3RA emerged as stable and novel protective factor, validated in Fenland data. TCGA revealed reduced IL3RA expression in ER+ tumors, with higher levels correlating with improved survival and favorable clinicopathological features, particularly in ER+/PR+ cases. Tumor microenvironment analysis revealed that IL3RA expression levels significantly correlated with immune landscape alterations in ER+ breast cancer. Immune infiltration analysis demonstrated significant associations between IL3RA expression levels and multiple immune cell populations in ER+ breast cancer, particularly CD8+ T cells, neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and M2 macrophages. DSigDB identified panobinostat, arbutin, clindamycin, cimetidine, and chlorzoxazone as IL3RA-targeting drugs.ConclusionsOur study identified IL3RA as novel biomarker and therapeutic target for ER+ breast cancer. Further validation and mechanistic studies are warranted to advance precision oncology strategies for ER+ breast cancer management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12014-026-09594-4.

Introduction

Breast cancer constitutes a major contributor to female cancer mortality worldwide [1]. As a highly heterogeneous malignancy, breast cancer can be categorized into distinct molecular subtypes, among which estrogen receptor positive (ER+) and estrogen receptor negative (ER−) represent the most clinically significant variants [2]. Notably, approximately 75% of diagnosed cases exhibit ER positivity, a key driver of oncogenic growth and cellular proliferation [3]. Currently, endocrine therapy serves as the primary treatment for ER+ breast cancer patients [4]. However, the development of endocrine resistance remains a major clinical challenge in ER+ breast cancer management [5], underscoring the urgent need to identify alternative therapeutic targets to overcome this clinical limitation and improve patient outcomes.

Currently, the molecular mechanisms underlying ER+ breast cancer pathogenesis remain incompletely elucidated [6], particularly regarding the interplay between genetic variation, protein expression, and disease susceptibility. As direct mediators of cellular processes, plasma proteins provide a crucial bridge between genetic variants and phenotypic outcomes, making protein quantitative trait loci (pQTL) a promising avenue for deciphering causal pathways in tumorigenesis. In recent years, the human plasma proteome plays a pivotal role in numerous physiological functions and serves as a valuable source of biomarkers for diagnostic applications and therapeutic interventions [7]. The plasma proteome has emerged as a crucial resource for identifying novel biomarkers and drug targets [8, 9]. Currently, several studies have reported associations between specific plasma or serum proteins and the prognosis of patients with ER+ breast cancer. For instance, a clinical observational study found that elevated serum IFN-γ levels were independently associated with favorable outcomes in ER+ breast cancer [10]. However, conventional observational studies are inherently limited by confounding factors and reverse causality, necessitating robust methodologies to infer causality.

Mendelian randomization (MR), which employs genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to assess causal relationships between exposures and outcomes, has emerged as a powerful tool to address these limitations [11]. Recent studies have begun utilizing plasma proteome pQTL data for MR analyses. For instance, a recent investigation applied systematic MR methods to the human plasma proteome to identify potential therapeutic targets for major depressive disorder [12]. Another study determined causally associated targets for prostate cancer development through plasma proteome analysis [13]. However, applications of MR with plasma pQTLs remain scarce in ER+ breast cancer research, with few studies systematically evaluating the causal roles of plasma proteins while accounting for pleiotropy and confounding bias. Furthermore, identifying colocalized genetic variants shared between pQTLs and disease risk loci is crucial for confirming that observed associations stem from shared causal mechanisms rather than linkage disequilibrium (LD) [14]. Bayesian colocalization analysis provides a robust framework for this purpose, thereby strengthening causal inference. Meanwhile, drug repurposing databases such as DSigDB and DrugBank offer opportunities to translate these findings into therapeutic strategies. Despite this potential, few studies have adopted such a comprehensive approach to prioritize plasma proteins as biomarkers or therapeutic targets for ER+ breast cancer.

In this study, we conducted a multi-stage study integrating TSMR, Bayesian colocalization, and functional genomics to systematically investigate the causal roles of plasma pQTLs in ER+ breast cancer. Using the largest available plasma pQTL genome-wide association studies (GWAS) dataset from the deCODE cohort and ER+ breast cancer GWAS data from the Breast Cancer Association Consortium (BCAC), we first aimed to identify plasma proteins with causal associations to ER+ breast cancer risk. These associations were subsequently validated through Bayesian colocalization analysis and replicated using an independent pQTL GWAS dataset from the Fenland population-based cohort. Finally, we evaluated the differential expression of corresponding genes in ER+ tumor tissues using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data, while further conducting survival analysis, clinic-pathological features correlation analysis, tumor microenvironment analysis, immune infiltration analysis, pan-cancer analysis, and predicting potential therapeutic drugs targeting the prioritized proteins. Our study not only advances the understanding of molecular mechanisms in ER+ breast cancer, but also provides innovative biomarkers with significant clinical value for risk management and early detection of this disease.

Methods

Study design

Figure 1 presents the detailed overview of our study’s design. First, we applied the TSMR method to identify human plasma pQTLs from the discovery GWAS dataset that have causal relationships with the risk of ER+ breast cancer. Subsequently, Bayesian colocalization analysis was used to further assess the robustness of the identified pQTLs that were significantly associated with ER+ breast cancer. For the pQTLs that exhibited colocalization with breast cancer, we further validated them using another plasma pQTL dataset. Finally, we analyzed the expression differences of the genes corresponding to the proteins identified through the aforementioned process in two types of ER+ breast cancer tissues and normal tissues using relevant sequencing data from the TCGA database. Additionally, we performed survival analysis, clinic-pathological features correlation analysis, tumor microenvironment analysis, immune infiltration analysis, and pan-cancer analysis on these proteins. Simultaneously, the DSigDB in Enrichr (http://amp.pharm.mssm.edu/Enrichr/) and the DrugBank database (https://go.drugbank.com/) were employed to predict potential drugs corresponding to these proteins.

Data sources

In the discovery dataset, we obtained the largest available GWAS dataset of human plasma pQTL data to date from the study by Ferkingstad et al. [15]. The pQTL data were derived from the deCODE cohort, which measured the levels of 4,907 plasma proteins in a sample of 35,559 Icelandic individuals. The study explored the associations between 27.2 million genetic variants and plasma protein levels. The researchers identified 18,084 sequence variants associated with plasma protein levels, with rare variants (minor allele frequency [MAF] < 1%) accounting for 19% of these associations. In the validation datasets, we utilized GWAS data on plasma protein levels from two independent sources. The first dataset was derived from the Fenland population-based cohort, which included 2,925 plasma proteins in a sample of 10,708 European participants [16]. The plasma protein measurements were adjusted for age, sex, the first ten principal components of ancestry, and the test sites. The resulting residuals were then subjected to an inverse rank normal transformation prior to performing the GWAS analysis. The second dataset comprised SomaLogic-based plasma pQTL summary statistics from the INTERVAL study, which included data for 1,927 proteins derived from 3,301 participants [17]. The GWAS data for ER+ breast cancer were obtained from a study conducted by the BCAC, which included 175,475 participants of European descent, with 69,501 cases of ER+ breast cancer [18]. The BCAC study combined three datasets: iCOGS, OncoArray, and additional GWAS studies, creating a comprehensive and authoritative GWAS analysis focused on breast cancer and its subtypes.

TSMR analysis

Initially, TSMR method was employed to identify plasma pQTLs that have causal relationships with the risk of ER+ breast cancer. When conducting TSMR analysis, it is crucial to satisfy three key assumptions: (i) a robust association must exist between the selected genetic variants and the exposure factors; (ii) The genetic variants must remain independent of any known or potential confounding variables; (iii) The genetic variants should influence the outcome exclusively through the exposure, with no alternative pleiotropic pathways. Genetic variants meeting all of the above three assumptions were eligible for inclusion as IVs in the TSMR analysis. Our study followed the reporting standards established by STROBE-MR.

To satisfy the assumption (i), we implemented a genome-wide significance threshold (p < 5 × 10− 8) to select SNPs that were strongly associated with exposures, given the substantial number of available genetic variants [19]. To satisfy the assumption (ii), all selected genetic variants were required to be independent of potential confounding factors. First, a LD analysis was conducted. The threshold for the LD correlation coefficient was set at r² < 0.1, and the clumping window was set to > 104 kb to ensure that there was no LD among the IVs. Second, we utilized the LDtrait tool (https://ldlink.nih.gov/ldtrait) to identify SNPs linked to outcome risk factors and potential confounders, which were then removed from our study to reduce confounding bias [20]. Subsequently, we extracted matching SNPs from the outcome GWAS dataset. For SNPs absent in the outcome dataset, we substituted them with corresponding proxy SNPs [21]. Proxy SNPs were identified using the SNP Annotation and Proxy Search (https://www.broadinstitute.org/mpg/snap/) [22, 23]. When no suitable proxy SNP was available, the SNP was excluded from subsequent analyses. Following harmonization of exposure and outcome statistics, we excluded genetic variants with incompatible alleles and palindromic variants exhibiting intermediate allele frequencies. Additionally, rare variants with MAF < 0.01 were also removed [24, 25]. “MAF” indicates the secondary allele frequency of SNP used as IVs, “n” denotes the sample size, and “k” represents the number of IVs employed in each study.

The TSMR analysis was performed by using the “TwoSampleMR” (version 0.5.6) package in R software (version 4.2.1). To comprehensively assess the causal relationship between plasma pQTLs and ER+ breast cancer, we employed multiple analytical approaches, including inverse variance weighted (IVW), weighted median (WM), MR-Egger regression, simple mode, and weighted mode. Among these, the IVW method served as our primary analytical approach due to its superior precision in effect estimation [27]. The IVW method first calculated ratio estimates for each individual SNP using the Wald estimator and delta method, then combined these estimates across all SNPs to generate the primary causal effect estimate [28]. Consequently, the IVW method is capable of providing the most precise effect estimates. The association was presented as odds ratios (ORs) with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Statistical significance was set as P < 0.05. The false discovery rate (FDR) method was employed to adjust the statistical significance for multiple comparisons [29].

Moreover, a series of sensitivity analyses were conducted to ensure the robustness of our findings. Cochrane’s Q test was utilized to assess the heterogeneity. In cases where significant heterogeneity was detected (P < 0.05), we employed the random-effects inverse variance weighted (IVW-RE) model to provide a more conservative estimate [30]. Conversely, when no substantial heterogeneity was observed, the fixed-effect inverse variance weighted (IVW-FE) model was applied [30]. The MR-Egger intercept method was applied to detect and assess the potential horizontal pleiotropy, with a P-value greater than 0.05 suggested the absence of horizontal pleiotropy among the SNPs [31]. Moreover, we performed the Mendelian Randomization Pleiotropy Residual Sum and Outlier (MR-PRESSO) test to identify and adjust for horizontal pleiotropy by removing potential outliers [32]. Finally, we conducted a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis to evaluate whether the observed causal relationship in our TSMR analysis was driven by any influential individual SNP [33]. SNPs whose exclusion substantially altered the effect estimates were identified as outliers and subsequently removed from the final analysis [34].

Bayesian colocalization analysis

Bayesian colocalization analysis was further used to evaluate the pQTLs significantly associated with ER+ breast cancer. The colocalization analysis thoroughly examined the genetic regions associated with pQTLs and ER+ breast cancer [35]. It was conducted using the coloc R package (version 5.2.2) in R software (version 4.2.1) with the default parameters provided by the software. The colocalization analysis produces five distinct posterior probabilities corresponding to the following hypotheses: (1) H0: Neither trait has causal variant; (2) H1: Only trait 1 has causal genetic variant; (3) H2: Only trait 2 has causal genetic variant; (4) H3: Both traits have causal genetic variant, but they do not share the same genetic variant; (5) H4: Both traits share the same causal genetic variant [36]. A posterior probability of H4 (PPH4) greater than 0.8 is regarded as strong evidence supporting the colocalization of the fatigue GWAS and pQTLs [37].

TCGA analysis of IL3RA

The RNA-Seq expression profiles of 807 ER+ breast cancer tumor tissues and 113 normal tissues, along with corresponding clinical data, were retrieved from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Among the 807 ER+ samples, 681 exhibited concomitant progesterone receptor positivity (PR+), while 123 were progesterone receptor negative (PR−). These subgroups were further evaluated in stratified analyses. Differential expression of IL3RA between ER+ breast cancer tumor tissues and normal tissues was analyzed using Wilcoxon signed-rank test [38]. The same non-parametric test was also used to determine the relationship between clinic-pathological features (clinical stage, T classification, lymph node status and distant metastasis) and IL3RA expression. The association between IL3RA expression and overall survival was assessed by Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier analysis. The median IL3RA expression values serving as cut-offs were 4.345 in overall ER+ breast cancer tissues, 4.363 in ER+/PR+ tissues, and 4.253 in ER+/PR− tissues. The ESTIMATE algorithm was performed to evaluate the tumor microenvironment [39]. The CIBERSORT algorithm was employed to calculate the relative proportions of 22 immune cell subtypes within each sample [40]. The Correlations between IL3RA and different subtypes of immune cell subtypes were established using the Spearman correlation coefficient. Finally, we also conducted a pan-cancer analysis of the prognostic impact of IL3RA in other types of tumors to broaden the value of our research.

Identification of potential drugs targeting IL3RA

DSigDB is a comprehensive drug-gene interaction database containing 22,527 genomes, 17,389 compounds, and 19,531 genes, offering detailed insights into gene-drug associations, including both established and potential interactions [41]. Using protein-drug interaction data from DSigDB via Enrichr (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/), we identified potential therapeutic drugs for the genes significantly associated with ER+ breast cancer. Moreover, the DrugBank database (https://go.drugbank.com/) was employed to establish the reliability of the candidate drugs. DrugBank is a comprehensive, widely recognized, and freely accessible online database that provides extensive information on drugs and their targets, along with the current approval status [42]. Screening the DrugBank database enables us to prioritize drugs that have already received approval.

Analysis of the safety and pleiotropic effects of IL3RA

Given the identification of IL3RA as a therapeutic target, a systematic evaluation of its safety and pleiotropic effects is essential. The safety profile of IL3RA was investigated using the Open Targets Platform (https://platform.opentargets.org/), a comprehensive resource for drug target identification and validation [43], which supports the assessment of target-associated adverse effects. In parallel, a phenome-wide association study (PheWAS) was conducted via the AstraZeneca PheWAS Portal (https://azphewas.com/) to examine the pleiotropic effects of IL3RA [44]. PheWAS is widely employed to assess the pleiotropic effects of genetic variants associated with therapeutic targets [45, 46]. This analysis leveraged exome sequencing data from approximately 500,000 UK Biobank participants, covering about 17,361 binary and 1,419 quantitative traits aligned to the GRCh38 reference genome. To account for the potential of false positives, a significance threshold of P<1E-08 was applied for phenotype screening [47].

Results

The TSMR analysis

Basic information of IVs

The instrumental variables (IVs) were selected based on the predefined criteria detailed in the Methods section. All IVs demonstrated strong instrument strength, with F-statistics consistently exceeding the threshold of 10, thereby ruling out concerns regarding weak IVs bias.

Results of the TSMR analysis using the discovery plasma pQTLs dataset

As shown in Fig. 1, our TSMR results indicated significant correlations between the pQTLs of 38 plasma proteins and the occurrence of ER+ breast cancer following the FDR correction (Supplementary Table 1). It is noteworthy that different plasma protein phenotypes show varying effects on ER+ breast cancer. Specifically, ADH6, SSU72, FOLR1, MDM4, GRIA4, SCG3, IL21, SWAP70, CTSB, BTN3A3, and MICB were identified as risk factors. Conversely, several other plasma protein phenotypes were found to be protective factors for ER+ breast cancer, including MICA, ADGRF5, C2, GSTM3, THSD1, IL3RA, PCYOX1, A4GALT, EBAG9, ENG, PLXND1, MRPL33, ISLR2, INSR, FGF2, EPHA4, IL31, ALPI, EPHB4, SPRN, MET, IGF1R, CLMP, EGFR, IFNL3, JAG1, and LGALS8 (Fig. 2). These plasma proteins were considered to have preliminary associations with ER+ breast cancer. Furthermore, the MR-Steiger test confirmed that the causal direction aligned with the hypothesized effect, providing no evidence of reverse causal relationships between these plasma proteins and ER+ breast cancer. The IV information corresponding to the above-mentioned 38 plasma proteins is provided in Supplementary Table 2.

Moreover, we conducted a comprehensive series of sensitivity analyses to examine potential biases within the MR analysis. Detailed results regarding heterogeneity and pleiotropy assessments are provided in Supplementary Tables 3 and 4. Cochran’s Q test revealed heterogeneity in the effects of PLXND1, MICA, EBAG9, ISLR2, MICB, C2, BTN3A3, THSD1, ALPI, ADGRF5, IL21, EGFR, IGF1R, EPFB4, CTSB, GSTM3, and ENG, prompting the application of IVW-RE model for causal relationship assessment. The remaining plasma protein phenotypes were assessed using the IVW-FE model. The MR-Egger intercept test provided evidence of horizontal pleiotropy in the effects of C2, THSD1, EPHB4, EPHA4, GSTM3, EBAG9, MDM4, GRIA4, and FOLR1. Moreover, the leave-one-out analysis and the MR-PRESSO test were also performed to further confirmed the reliability of our results.

Bayesian colocalization results

Bayesian colocalization analysis was further conducted to investigate the causal variants between the 38 identified plasma proteins and ER+ breast cancer (Supplementary Table 5). The results revealed that 12 plasma proteins (PLXND1, MET, JAG1, INSR, EPHA4, IL3RA, MICB, IGF1R, EPHB4, ISLR2, ENG, and LGALS8) were capable of forming a stable colocalization association with ER+ breast cancer, with PPH4 values of 94.8%, 98.6%, 98.6%, 97.5%, 97.8%, 93.5%, 85.0%, 98.6%, 98.6%, 97.6%, 97.5%, and 98.6%, respectively (Table 1 and Supplementary Figs. 1–12). These plasma proteins could be regarded as moderate correlation with the risk of ER+ breast cancer.

Results of the TSMR analysis using the validation plasma pQTLs dataset

Subsequently, we validated the aforementioned 12 plasma proteins using two independent pQTL datasets derived from the Fenland population-based cohort and the INTERVAL study. Our analysis indicated that only five proteins (ENG, EPHA4, IL3RA, ISLR2, and LGALS8) were detectable in the Fenland dataset, while nine proteins (INSR, IGF1R, ENG, LGALS8, JAG1, MICB, ISLR2, IL3RA, and EPHB4) were detectable in the INTERVAL dataset. Using the Fenland dataset, EPHA4, IL3RA, and ISLR2 exhibited significant causal associations with ER+ breast cancer via the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method (P = 4.78E− 08, P = 3.02E− 07, P = 0.010, Supplementary Figs. 13–15). Notably, the associations for EPHA4 and IL3RA remained statistically significant following FDR correction. In the INTERVAL dataset, INSR, MICB, ISLR2, and IL3RA demonstrated significant causal associations with ER+ breast cancer using the IVW method (P = 1.22E− 06, P = 1.86E− 15, P = 3.73E− 04, P = 7.99E− 10, Supplementary Figs. 16–19), all of which remained significant after FDR correction. Integrating results from both validation sets, only IL3RA showed a consistently significant causal association with ER+ breast cancer after FDR correction in both datasets. Moreover, consistent with findings from the primary dataset, both validation sets again revealed inverse causal relationships between IL3RA and ER+ breast cancer risk (Fenland: OR = 0.940, 95% CI = 0.918–0.962; INTERVAL: OR = 0.947, 95% CI = 0.930–0.963). Sensitivity analyses indicated no significant heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy for IL3RA in either validation set, supporting the robustness of these findings. These results provided high causal evidence that elevated circulating levels of IL3RA is associated with the reduced risk of ER+ breast cancer development.

Results of the TCGA analyses

Building upon these findings, we further explored the potential therapeutic relevance of IL3RA in ER+ breast cancer by leveraging data from TCGA. We evaluated the differential expression of IL3RA in ER+ tumor tissues using TCGA data, followed by comprehensive assessment of its prognostic value and clinic-pathological correlations. The gene expression analysis revealed that IL3RA expression was significantly downregulated in ER+ breast cancer tissues compared with normal tissues (P < 0.001; Fig. 3A), with a marked decrease observed in both PR+ and PR− subgroups (P < 0.001, P < 0.001; Fig. 3B-C). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that ER+ breast cancer patients with high IL3RA expression had significantly longer overall survival than those with low IL3RA expression (P = 0.004; Fig. 3D). Notably, in the PR+ and PR− subgroups, elevated IL3RA expression was significantly associated with prolonged patient survival exclusively in the PR+ subgroup (P = 0.004; Fig. 3E), while no such correlation was observed in PR− subgroup (P = 0.398, Fig. 3F). Furthermore, we observed significant associations between IL3RA levels and both clinical stage and T classification in ER+ breast cancer patients (Fig. 4). These correlations were particularly pronounced in the ER+/PR+ subgroup. Specifically, IL3RA expression was significantly elevated in stage 0 compared to other stages and in Tis (carcinoma in situ) classification versus other T classifications. Conversely, lower IL3RA levels were detected in stage IV and T4 tumors exhibiting deep tissue infiltration. The tumor microenvironment analysis revealed that the high IL3RA expression group exhibited significantly elevated stromal score, immune score, and ESTIMATE score compared to the low IL3RA expression group (Fig. 5A-C). Immune infiltration analysis in overall ER+ breast cancer revealed significant differences in immune cell composition between high and low IL3RA expression groups. Specifically, B cells naive, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ memory resting T cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), resting NK cells, monocytes, M0/M1/M2 macrophages, resting dendritic cells, and neutrophils exhibited distinct infiltration patterns (Fig. 5D). Notably, CD8+ T cells, B cells naive, resting dendritic cells, resting NK cells, neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and M2 macrophages demonstrated the strongest correlations with IL3RA expression levels (p < 0.001; Fig. 5G). In the ER+/PR+ subgroup, the immune infiltration patterns between high and low IL3RA expression groups were similar to those observed in overall ER+ breast cancer (Fig. 5E). However, only CD8+ T cells, naive B cells, neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and M2 macrophages maintained the strongest correlations with IL3RA expression levels (p < 0.001; Fig. 5H). In the ER+/PR− subgroup, only CD8+ T cells, M0 macrophages, and resting dendritic cells exhibited significant differences in infiltration patterns between high and low IL3RA expression groups (Fig. 5F). Notably, CD8+ T cells showed the strongest correlation with IL3RA expression levels (p < 0.001; Fig. 5I).

Figure 6 showed the pan-cancer analysis results. It was revealed that IL3RA expression levels could influence the survival of patients with cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma (P = 0.023), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (P = 6.68E− 04), acute myeloid leukemia (P = 0.019), sarcoma (P = 0.015), and skin cutaneous melanoma (P = 0.021). Interestingly, we found that IL3RA had no significant impact on the overall survival in the entire breast cancer cohort (P = 0.224), implying a subtype-specific role in ER+ breast cancer

Assessment of IL3RA safety and pleiotropic effects

According to data available in the Open Targets Platform, no adverse effects related to IL3RA have been reported to date, supporting its favorable safety profile. Using the AstraZeneca PheWAS Portal, we performed phenome-wide screening with a stringent threshold of P<1E-08, revealing no statistically significant associations between IL3RA and any other traits across the UK Biobank phenome (Supplementary Table 7). This suggests limited evidence of pleiotropic effects driven by common genetic variants at these loci. Collectively, these data provide preliminary evidence for the safety and reliability of IL3RA as a candidate therapeutic target.

Discussion

While ER+ breast cancer demonstrates better responsiveness to endocrine therapy compared to ER− subtypes, the inevitable development of treatment resistance remains a major clinical challenge [48]. This therapeutic limitation underscores the critical need to identify novel molecular targets and develop more effective pharmacological interventions for ER+ breast cancer. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to use TSMR approach integrated with Bayesian colocalization analysis, leveraging large-scale GWAS datasets of human plasma protein pQTLs to identify proteins exhibiting robust causal associations with ER+ breast cancer. Furthermore, through systematic validation and functional characterization, we finally evaluated their potential as reliable biomarkers or potential therapeutic targets for ER+ breast cancer management.

Given the human proteome serves as the primary source for both translatable biomarkers and therapeutic targets [49], we primarily employed MR analysis to evaluate causal proteins in ER+ breast cancer pathogenesis, with dual objectives of identifying early diagnostic biomarkers and novel druggable targets. We identified 39 plasma proteins exhibiting preliminary associations with ER+ breast cancer in the discovery dataset. To mitigate potential biases from LD-induced reverse causation, horizontal pleiotropy, and genetic confounding, we integrated Bayesian colocalization as a complementary analytical framework [49]. Bayesian colocalization analysis identified 12 plasma proteins showing consistent colocalization signals with ER+ breast cancer, suggesting moderate evidence of association. Finally, IL3RA was found to be significantly associated with ER+ breast cancer in both the Fenland and INTERVAL replication analyses. This consistent replication across independent cohorts offers strong causal evidence supporting its association with ER+ breast cancer.

The interleukin-3 receptor alpha subunit (IL3RA/CD123) is a membrane-bound protein produced by activated T lymphocytes that plays critical roles in immunoregulation and hematopoiesis [50]. Substantial evidence has established the importance of IL3RA in hematological malignancies, with its overexpression observed across multiple blood cancers, making it a promising therapeutic target for acute myeloid leukemia and blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasms [51]. Meanwhile, IL-3, the primary ligand of IL3RA, has been implicated in breast cancer pathogenesis, where Th2 cell-derived IL-3 secretion may directly suppress tumorigenesis [52]. However, the association between IL3RA and breast cancer remains poorly characterized. To date, only one bioinformatics study has suggested a potential link between IL3RA expression and overall survival in breast cancer patients, though this analysis did not examine specific molecular subtypes [53]. Our study is the first to uncover the significant diagnostic and prognostic value of IL3RA in ER+ breast cancer. The results demonstrate that IL3RA not only exhibits markedly reduced expression levels in tissues of ER+ breast cancer patients but may also serve as a potential protective factor against ER+ breast cancer. MR and Bayesian colocalization analyses confirmed a significant causal relationship between IL3RA and ER+ breast cancer risk, suggesting that elevated IL3RA expression may reduce the likelihood of ER+ breast cancer development. Furthermore, our findings indicate that IL3RA is closely associated with survival outcomes and multiple clinic-pathological features in ER+ breast cancer patients, demonstrating significant predictive value for ER+ breast cancer prognosis. IL3RA was also found to be highly expressed in less malignant carcinoma in situ, but showed reduced expression in highly aggressive Stage IV invasive carcinoma tissues, indicating a potential correlation between IL3RA expression and ER+ breast cancer progression. Instead, we found that IL3RA was not significantly associated with overall survival in the entire breast cancer cohort, suggesting that IL3RA may serve as a promising and subtype-specific therapeutic target for ER+ breast cancer. Similar to ER, PR serves as another well-established diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in breast cancer [54]. Clinically, PR status complements ER evaluation for identifying patients likely to respond to endocrine therapy. Previous studies demonstrate differential response rates: approximately 50% for ER+ tumors versus 60–70% for ER+/PR+ tumors receiving endocrine treatment [55]. We found that IL3RA expression levels only affected patient survival in ER+/PR+ samples, but failed to predict survival outcomes in ER+/PR− samples. This indicates that IL3RA has better predictive power in ER+/PR+ patients, who are generally more sensitive to endocrine therapy. Tumor microenvironment analysis revealed that IL3RA expression levels significantly correlated with immune landscape alterations in ER+ breast cancer. Comprehensive immune infiltration analysis demonstrated that across overall ER+ cases and the both two subtypes analyzed, IL3RA expression showed the strongest association with CD8+ T cell infiltration. This finding aligns with previous reports in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, where elevated IL3RA levels corresponded to increased CD8+ effector T cell activity scores [56]. CD8+ T cell infiltration levels demonstrate significant prognostic relevance in ER+ breast cancer. A clinical study found that in ER+/HER2+ breast cancer, the presence of CD8+ T cells was associated with a substantially reduced relative risk of mortality [57]. Moreover, another study also reported that high CD8+ infiltration in ER+ tumors correlated with poorer clinical outcomes [58]. Therefore, the change in IL3RA expression levels may potentially impact the survival and pathological manifestations of ER+ breast cancer patients by influencing CD8+ T cell levels. In the ER+ and ER+/PR+ subgroup, immune cells such as neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and M2 macrophages were also found to exhibit highly significant correlations with IL3RA expression levels. IL3RA is primarily expressed on dendritic cells and monocytes/macrophages, with additional expression observed on neutrophils [59, 60]. In recent years, a large-scale study involving 1,900 samples demonstrated that the infiltration of neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and M2 macrophages is closely associated with poor prognosis in ER+ breast cancer [61]. Therefore, changes in IL3RA expression levels on monocytes/macrophages and neutrophils may influence their aggressiveness, thereby affecting the prognosis of ER+ breast cancer patients. These hypotheses require further investigation for validation. The pan-cancer analysis revealed that IL3RA expression levels can significantly influence survival outcomes in other cancer types as well, such as cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma, kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma, among others. These findings suggest that IL3RA plays a significant role in cancer prevention and therapeutic intervention.

The drug prediction analysis revealed that panobinostat is the most promising approved drug candidate targeting IL3RA. Panobinostat is an orally bioavailable, non-selective HDAC inhibitor currently under investigation as a potential therapeutic agent for breast cancer [62, 63]. However, the clinical application of panobinostat in breast cancer remains underexplored [64]. Our findings strongly demonstrate its promising therapeutic potential for ER+ breast cancer. Moreover, studies have found that several other predicted drugs, including arbutin, cimetidine, and chlorzoxazone, exhibit potential anticancer properties against various cancers such as bladder cancer, bone cancer, brain cancer, liver cancer, and gastrointestinal cancers [65–67]. However, their application in breast cancer remains limited. Our study further highlights the potential application of these drugs in breast cancer, particularly in ER+ breast cancer. As a semi-synthetic antibiotic, clindamycin has not been documented to exhibit direct antitumor activity. However, it demonstrates significant therapeutic efficacy in managing chronic mastitis frequently associated with breast cancer [68].

While a recent study similar to ours also employed a combined MR and Bayesian colocalization approach to investigate the etiology of ER+ breast cancer, it diverged from our work in both perspective and level of discovery. The recent study primarily utilized cis-eQTL data from the eQTLGen consortium and incorporated genes related to endocrine disrupting chemicals, focused on elucidating mechanisms by which environmental chemicals influence ER+ breast cancer risk through gene expression regulation [69], thereby highlighting pathways of environment-gene interaction. In contrast, recognizing the potential value of plasma proteins as diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers, our study leveraged plasma protein pQTL data to identify a protective causal role of IL3RA in ER+ breast cancer at the protein level. We further revealed its association with the tumor immune microenvironment, particularly CD8+ T-cell infiltration, thereby directly linking the finding to potential therapeutic targets, prognostic markers, and drug repositioning opportunities. Thus, although both studies adopted a similar analytical framework of MR and Bayesian colocalization approach to investigate the etiology of ER+ breast cancer, it diverged from our work in both perspe, each illuminates distinct mechanistic aspects of the ER+ breast cancer etiology, together offering complementary scientific evidence for risk prevention and clinical intervention.

Our study offers several significant advantages. Firstly, to our knowledge, this represents the first investigation to systematically identify ER+ breast cancer-associated targets from large-scale plasma proteomic data while concurrently evaluating the therapeutic potential of existing and novel compounds targeting these proteins. By leveraging the largest available plasma pQTL GWAS dataset, we were able to discover unique therapeutic targets that may have been overlooked in previous genetic studies due to limited screening scope. Secondly, we implemented the TSMR method using SNPs strongly associated with the pQTL of plasma protein, complemented by comprehensive sensitivity analyses to minimize confounding factors. This methodological rigor provides more robust causal inference than conventional observational studies. Meanwhile, we also strengthened our findings through Bayesian colocalization analysis and independent replication in a separate plasma pQTL dataset. The conclusions drawn from using both the above methods are more robust. Thirdly, the data employed in our study is exclusively derived from the European population, thereby reducing potential confounding effects associated with ethnic heterogeneity.

Nevertheless, several limitations of our study should be acknowledged. Firstly, the GWAS data utilized in our analyses were exclusively derived from European populations, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups. Secondly, although we employed stringent criteria for IVs selection, potential residual confounding from unmeasured variables could introduce bias into our causal estimates. Thirdly, although our findings were preliminarily validated using clinical samples from TCGA, they lack further experimental validation through in vivo and in vitro studies. Therefore, the findings of our study require deeper verification in future research.

Conclusion

Our study integrates MR, Bayesian colocalization and TCGA database analyses to identify IL3RA as novel plasma protein causally linked to reduced ER+ breast cancer risk. These findings highlight the potential of IL3RA as therapeutic targets and biomarkers for ER+ breast cancer prediction. While limited to European populations, our results provide robust genetic evidence for translational applications. Future research should validate these associations across diverse cohorts and elucidate underlying mechanisms. This work advances molecular insights into ER+ breast cancer and informs strategies for early detection and targeted therapy.

Global research trends in BPaL and BPaLM regimens for drug-resistant tuberculosis: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Kannan Sridharan, Gowri Sivaramakrishnan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe introduction of BPaL and BPaLM regimens has revolutionized drug-resistant tuberculosis treatment, offering superior efficacy, shorter duration, and better tolerability than conventional therapies. Despite their rapid WHO guideline incorporation, no prior bibliometric analysis has been conducted on this topic. This study addresses this gap by mapping global knowledge production, collaborations, and thematic trends to inform future research and implementation strategies.MethodsWe analyzed Scopus-indexed publications using controlled vocabulary for BPaL/BPaLM regimens. From 551 initial records, 120 met inclusion criteria after screening. Bibliometrix and VOS Viewer software evaluated publication trends, authorship, institutional/geographical contributions, citations, and keyword networks. Visualization tools mapped collaborations and thematic clusters, while statistical methods assessed growth rates and citation impacts.ResultsThe study identified 1,081 authors, with publications growing at 11.61% annually and peaking in 2024 (n = 56). International collaborations featured in 53.33% of studies, led by the US (n = 56), UK (n = 25), and South Africa (n = 20). Johns Hopkins University was the top institution (n = 56), and Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy the leading journal (n = 15). Landmark 2019 publications had the highest citation rate (13.05/year). Thematic analysis revealed categorization into three domains: pathogen and drug resistance, treatment regimens and efficacy, and demographics and clinical studies. Strong collaborations linked high-income and high-burden countries, notably the US and South Africa.ConclusionThis first bibliometric assessment of BPaL/BPaLM research highlights progress in evidence generation but reveals gaps in implementation science and equitable knowledge production. Future work should address operational challenges, special populations, and resistance monitoring. These insights can guide researchers, policymakers, and funders to optimize TB control programs and advance global elimination goals.Keywords: Tuberculosis, Multidrug-resistant, Antitubercular agents, Bibliometrics, Drug therapy, Combination, International cooperation

Introduction

The global tuberculosis epidemic continues to present formidable challenges to public health systems worldwide, with drug-resistant tuberculosis (DR-TB) representing a particularly persistent threat. Despite significant advances in diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, DR-TB treatment has historically been associated with prolonged regimens, substantial toxicity, suboptimal outcomes and a high mortality of 150,000 deaths (ranges between 53,000 and 170,000) [1]. A recent meta-analysis of 148 studies (318,430 participants) revealed a pooled prevalence of multi drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB) to be 11.6% (95% CI: 9.1–14.5%) and extensively drug-resistant TB (XDR-TB) to be 2.5% (95% CI: 2–3%) [2]. The emergence of all-oral, shortened regimens containing bedaquiline, pretomanid, and linezolid (BPaL) and BPaL with moxifloxacin (BPaLM) has revolutionized the therapeutic landscape, offering improved efficacy, tolerability, and treatment completion rates [3]. These regimens represent a paradigm shift in DR-TB management, with the potential to dramatically improve patient outcomes and strengthen TB control efforts globally.

The development of BPaL/BPaLM regimens has been underpinned by extensive clinical research spanning preclinical studies, phase II/III trials, and operational research. The regimen’s journey from experimental therapy to WHO-recommended treatment has generated a substantial body of scientific literature, reflecting both the promise and challenges of these novel therapeutic approaches [4]. As these regimens transition from research settings to widespread programmatic implementation, understanding the evolution, current state, and knowledge gaps in BPaL/BPaLM research becomes increasingly important for guiding future scientific inquiry and policy decisions. Bibliometric analysis offers a valuable methodological approach to systematically examine this growing corpus of literature, providing insights into research productivity, collaboration patterns, and thematic trends [5].

Bibliometric studies have become an essential tool in medical research, allowing for quantitative assessment of scientific output and identification of research trends [6]. In the context of TB therapeutics, such analyses can reveal temporal patterns in knowledge generation, highlight geographical disparities in research contributions, and identify emerging areas of scientific focus. Previous bibliometric studies have examined various aspects of TB research, including drug development, diagnostics, and vaccine research, but none have specifically focused on the BPaL/BPaLM therapeutic approach [7–9]. This gap in the literature is particularly notable given the transformative potential of these regimens and their growing importance in global TB control strategies. A global projection of adopting BPaL/BPaLM regimens for treating DR-TB revealed an estimate of employing BPaLM regimen in 126,792 patients (58% of DR-TB treatments), and BPaL in 43,716 patients (20% of DR-TB treatments) by 2026 [10]. Considering the recent approval and limited studies carried out with the novel regimens for treating DR-TB, clinicians were hesitant in implementing it in their routine clinical practice [11].

The current study employed bibliometric methods to analyze the global research landscape of BPaL and BPaLM regimens for DR-TB treatment. Using Scopus database records, we examined publication trends, authorship patterns, institutional and geographical contributions, citation impacts, and thematic evolution of research in this field. Our analysis aimed to quantify and characterize the scientific response to these novel therapeutic approaches, from their initial development through to current implementation research. By mapping the research productivity and collaboration networks, we set out to identify strengths in the current evidence base as well as potential gaps that require additional scientific attention. Understanding the trajectory of BPaL/BPaLM research has important implications for multiple stakeholders. For researchers, it can inform priority-setting for future studies and identify opportunities for collaboration. For policymakers and program managers, insights into the evidence base can support guideline development and implementation planning. For funding agencies, such analysis can guide strategic investments in research and capacity building. As the global TB community works towards ambitious elimination targets, comprehensive understanding of the scientific landscape surrounding these promising regimens becomes increasingly valuable.

Methods

Search strategy and database

The research data for this investigation was systematically extracted from the Scopus database, which was selected as the primary source due to its comprehensive coverage of peer-reviewed scientific literature across diverse disciplines and geographical regions. As one of the most extensive curated abstract and citation repositories, Scopus maintains stringent quality control measures through its independent Content Selection and Advisory Board, which implements rigorous evaluation protocols and ongoing data quality assessments to ensure the integrity of indexed content [12]. Beyond its extensive journal coverage, Scopus offers detailed metadata including corresponding author information, institutional affiliations, and country-level publication data, enabling robust bibliometric analysis. The database search was conducted in April 2025 using an inclusive strategy without language or publication date restrictions, with the specific search query designed to capture all relevant literature through the terms: (TITLE-ABS-KEY (BPaL OR BPaLM) OR (bedaquiline AND pretomanid AND linezolid)).

Eligibility criteria

Following the initial database search, all retrieved records were imported into Rayyan™ systematic review software to facilitate the screening process [13]. Two independent authors conducted a blinded review of all articles according to predefined eligibility criteria, with any discrepancies in inclusion decisions resolved through consensus discussion. The selection criteria focused on including only peer-reviewed journal articles encompassing clinical trials, observational research, cross-sectional analyses, case reports, case series, and review articles specifically addressing BPaL/BPaLM regimens for drug-resistant tuberculosis. Exclusion criteria were applied to eliminate animal studies, clinical practice guidelines, and meta-analyses from the final analysis. Meta-analyses and guidelines synthesize existing studies rather than contributing to original data from the field and including them could introduce bias in analyzing research productivity, collaboration patterns, and citation dynamics, as they typically receive disproportionately high citations due to their summarizing nature.

Bibliometric analysis

The analytical approach employed two specialized software tools: VOSviewer™ (version 1.6.18) for Windows and the Bibliometrix package for R™ (version 4.4.3) [14, 15]. The analysis was conducted using a comprehensive CSV file containing complete bibliographic records, citation data, abstracts, and author keywords. Key analytical dimensions included identification of leading research institutions, international contributions to literature, keyword co-occurrence patterns, prominent journal sources, influential authors, and highly cited publications. The visualization techniques generated network maps and graphical representations to elucidate patterns within the data, with related items grouped into distinct color-coded clusters for interpretation.

In the network visualizations, individual elements were represented as nodes, with their relative size corresponding to quantitative measures such as citation frequency or co-occurrence counts. Interconnections between nodes were depicted as edges, with line thickness indicating the strength of relationships through edge weight values. For various analyses, nodes were configured to represent different entities including cited references, collaborating countries, or co-occurring keywords. A minimum threshold of five citations was established to filter and highlight the most significant items in each category. VOSviewer™ specifically enabled the creation of network visualizations mapping influential researchers, national contributions, landmark publications, and temporal publication trends.

The Bibliometrix software package provided advanced capabilities for quantitative science mapping, featuring specialized functions for generating diverse visualization formats that elucidate complex relationships within the scholarly literature. This open-source tool facilitated the construction of detailed data matrices supporting multiple analytical approaches, including co-citation network analysis, scientific collaboration mapping, and keyword association studies. Through these comprehensive analytical features, researchers were able to systematically examine the structural characteristics and developmental trajectories of knowledge within this specialized research domain, enabling both macroscopic and microscopic perspectives on the evolution of BPaL/BPaLM-related research.

A comprehensive search of the Scopus database yielded 551 articles, of which 120 met the eligibility criteria for inclusion in this bibliometric analysis (Fig. 1). Main research centers and published journals

The most prolific affiliations were the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine (n = 56), followed by the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine (n = 21) and Médecins Sans Frontières (n = 20), with additional contributing institutions detailed in (Fig. 3A). The top publishing journals included Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy (n = 15) and the International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease (n = 6), with a full breakdown of journals provided in (Fig. 3B). Author affiliations spanned four distinct clusters of countries (Fig. 4A), with the highest contributions from the USA (n = 56), the UK (n = 25), and South Africa (n = 20). Collaboration networks were strongest for the Global Alliance for TB Drug Development (link strength: 6) and the Centre for TB Research (link strength: 5). To enhance ease of viewing, the world map generated by Bibliometrix software highlights the countries that contribute the most to publications on the topic (Fig. 5). The USA and South Africa exhibited the most frequent international collaboration, followed by the UK and South Africa. It is noteworthy that limited collaborations were observed in South America, with none identified in Central America, Russia, or TB hotspots such as Papua New Guinea during the study period.

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis of 120 publications reveals a rapidly growing research focus on BPaL and BPaLM regimens, with a steady annual growth rate and a striking surge in publications in 2024, reflecting heightened global interest in these therapeutic strategies. The analysis revealed an 11.61% annual growth in BPaL/BPaLM research, with a particularly striking surge in publications during 2024. This acceleration likely reflects several concurrent developments: the WHO’s updated guidelines endorsing these regimens, completion of pivotal clinical trials, and increasing programmatic adoption in high-burden countries. When compared to growth patterns observed for other novel TB drug regimens during their introductory phases, the trajectory of BPaL/BPaLM research appears notably steeper, suggesting both the perceived urgency of addressing drug-resistant TB and the promising therapeutic profile of these regimens. The timing of this surge also coincides with post-pandemic recovery of research activities and renewed global focus on TB elimination targets. Furthermore, recently in October 2024, the joint American Thoracic Society (ATS)/Center for Disease Control (CDC)/European Respiratory Society (ERS)/Infectious Disease Society of America (IDSA) has also recommended the BPaL/BPaLM based regimens for DR-TB [16].

Geographical analysis revealed a pronounced concentration of research output from high-income countries, particularly the United States and United Kingdom, with Johns Hopkins University emerging as the most prolific institution and TB Alliance, Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, NIAID and NIH were observed as the top funders. This finding is like previous studies where the USA emerged as the most common country associated with the highest publication metrics in this sub-specialty [7–9]. This pattern reflects several underlying factors: the concentration of funding resources, established research infrastructure, and the pivotal role of these institutions in conducting early-stage clinical trials. However, the strong collaborative networks between these institutions and partners in high-burden countries like South Africa demonstrate important knowledge-sharing mechanisms that could be further strengthened. The observed absence or limited presence of scientific collaborations in regions such as Central America, Russia, China, and high-burden TB settings like Papua New Guinea may reflect several factors, including limited publication volume on BPaL/BPaLM regimens, disparities in funding and research infrastructure, and data availability limitations within Scopus, which may underrepresent local or non-English publications. These findings underscore the need for strengthened collaborative research networks and capacity-building initiatives in these underrepresented regions to ensure equitable global progress in addressing drug-resistant TB.

The citation analysis showed that publications from 2019 received the highest mean citations per year, with closer examination revealing these to be primarily the landmark clinical trials that established the efficacy and safety profiles of BPaL regimens. These foundational studies continue to anchor subsequent research, though we observed an encouraging trend of increasing citations to implementation studies in later years. The citation patterns suggest that while basic science and clinical trials establish the evidence base, operational research is gaining recognition as these regimens scale up.

Thematic evolution of research keywords revealed three distinct but interconnected clusters: basic science (focused on Mycobacterium tuberculosis and drug mechanisms), drug therapy (emphasizing the individual drugs), and drug resistance. Over time, we observed clear progression from preclinical (non-human) studies toward clinical research, a pattern that mirrors the typical trajectory of TB therapeutic development. Emerging sub-themes include pharmacokinetic studies in special populations and resistance monitoring, which will likely dominate future research agendas as these regimens become more widely used.

This bibliometric analysis offers a comprehensive overview of global BPaL/BPaLM research by leveraging Scopus, a reputable database, to examine publication trends, collaborations, and thematic clusters across a decade (2015–2025). Its strength lies in the systematic evaluation of multidimensional metrics, including authorship patterns, citation impact, and international cooperation, supported by intuitive visualizations that highlight key research networks and knowledge gaps. Several limitations of our bibliometric approach must be acknowledged. This bibliometric analysis utilized the Scopus database, which offers extensive coverage and provides the necessary citation and bibliometric data for network and trend analyses. However, we acknowledge that the exclusion of additional databases such as PubMed and Web of Science may limit the absolute comprehensiveness of global literature capture on BPaL and BPaLM regimens. While PubMed provides broad biomedical literature coverage, it lacks citation and advanced bibliometric data required for detailed analyses, and access limitations constrained the use of Web of Science in this study. Future studies could consider a multi-database approach to enhance global literature inclusiveness and cross-validation of bibliometric findings in this research area. Our analysis captured English-language publications most completely, potentially underrepresenting important programmatic experiences published in other languages or local journals. The bibliometric methodology, while excellent for mapping research landscapes, cannot assess study quality or clinical relevance - aspects that require complementary systematic review approaches. These limitations suggest caution in interpreting some findings, particularly regarding regional contributions and research impact. Moving forward, several research priorities emerge from this analysis. There remains a pressing need for more studies on pediatric formulations, optimal duration of therapy, and management of adverse events - all relatively underrepresented in current literature. Implementation research should focus on simplifying treatment delivery models and training healthcare workers. Future bibliometric studies could benefit from combining multiple databases and incorporating altmetrics to capture broader impact measures. Most importantly, the research community must work to amplify voices from high-burden countries in scientific literature, ensuring the evidence base reflects diverse implementation contexts. As BPaL/BPaLM regimens become central to DR-TB management globally, the research agenda must evolve to address these critical gaps and opportunities.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis offers an overview of the global research landscape on BPaL and BPaLM regimens for drug-resistant tuberculosis, indicating a gradual increase in scientific output in recent years. The study identifies the contributions of key institutions and countries in this field and illustrates the role of international collaboration in advancing related research. While the analysis maps publication trends, authorship patterns, and thematic clusters, it also acknowledges areas where more inclusive research and expanded data sources could enhance the understanding of these regimens. These findings may inform stakeholders about current research progress and highlight opportunities for future studies to support the implementation and optimization of BPaL and BPaLM regimens within tuberculosis control programs.

Deep learning-based high-speed railway communication systems
Authors: Do Viet Ha, Trinh Van Chien, Hien Quoc Ngo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
High-speed railway wireless communication systems are characterized by severe Doppler shifts and fast time-varying multipath, which challenge reliable connectivity in Long-Term Evolution for Railways (LTE-R). This paper studies the two data-driven frameworks to enhance the communication quality so as to reduce the error probability under such highly dynamic propagation conditions. The first framework investigates a deep neural network (DNN) trained to learn the channel behavior and avoid the restrictive assumptions and predefined interpolation models used in conventional time-domain channel estimation (TDCE) schemes (Nearest, Linear, Cubic Hermite, and Cubic Spline). Besides, the second framework utilizes an autoencoder/decoder architecture to replace the traditional processing units and gain prior information from the dataset. Numerical results show that the proposed DNN-based frameworks more effectively track rapid channel fluctuations and mitigate Doppler-induced distortions, thereby improving detection performance in high-mobility LTE-R scenarios. These findings highlight the potential of learning-based signal processing to enhance the reliability and efficiency of high-speed railway communications.

Introduction

High-speed railway (HSR) communication systems play a pivotal role in ensuring the safety, reliability, and operational efficiency of modern rail transportation networks1. With train velocities exceeding hundreds kilometers per hour, wireless communication infrastructures are required to support low-latency transmission, high data rates, seamless handover, and robust mobility management under severe Doppler effects and rapidly varying channel conditions2. In addition to conventional railway signaling, contemporary HSR communication systems must accommodate real-time train control, condition monitoring, and broadband passenger services. The evolution of railway communication architectures reflects the increasing demand for high-capacity, reliable, and interoperable platforms capable of supporting mission-critical operations and intelligent transportation services3. Consequently, the design and optimization of HSR communication systems have become an active research area encompassing wireless channel modeling, radio resource management, network architecture design, and cybersecurity, with the objective of satisfying stringent performance and safety requirements for next-generation rail systems.

High-speed trains present significant challenges for signal processing due to extreme mobility, rapidly time-varying channels, and complex propagation environments. These challenges induce severe Doppler shifts and Doppler spread, which degrade carrier synchronization, distort orthogonality in multi-carrier systems such as orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM), and complicate channel estimation. Frequent handovers between base stations, combined with line-of-sight dominance interspersed with tunnel, viaduct, and urban scattering scenarios, result in non-stationary fading characteristics that violate conventional wide-sense stationary assumptions. Moreover, high penetration loss through train carriages and metallic shielding structures necessitates robust detection and adaptive signal processing strategies. The stringent requirements for low latency and high reliability in railway control systems further constrain computational complexity and processing delay, demanding efficient algorithms capable of real-time adaptation. Consequently, advanced signal processing techniques, for example predictive channel modeling, Doppler-resilient waveform design, and machine learning-assisted channel estimation are critical to ensuring reliable broadband connectivity in high-speed railway environments.

One of the critical challenges in high-speed railway communication is channel estimation and reliability such as bit error rate (BER) or symbol error rate (SER)4. Due to characteristics such as the vehicle’s movement speed relative to the transportation infrastructure, which causes Doppler noise, and the complex traffic terrain in cities, creating multipath interference, channel estimation can be challenging. Additionally, the channel environment changes rapidly as vehicles move through different terrains. These factors contribute to the difficulty in channel estimation. Traditional channel prediction methods, such as nearest neighbor interpolation, linear, TDCE-Linear, TDCE-Cubic Hermite, Gaussian, and Sinc, have been used. However, these methods are ineffective for high frequencies and mobility in high-speed railway communication systems. We stress that the traditional methods may face challenges due to the rapid and uneven variations in channel conditions. To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes a deep-learning-based method for channel estimation in high-speed railway communication. We suggest using a DNN to learn patterns and features from channel data collected in the time-and-frequency-selective fading environments.

Our DNN model is trained with a large dataset of channel samples and received signals from high-speed railway communication scenarios. The model can accurately estimate channel states in high-speed and dynamic environments by learning information from collected data. Alternatively, we also propose using an auto-encoder-decoder architecture in the communication system to compare and demonstrate the model’s effectiveness beyond the channel-estimated systems. We conduct experiments on a high-speed railway system simulation to evaluate the performance of our two proposed methods. Numerical results demonstrate that the deep-learning-supported channel estimation method can accurately and stably estimate channels, thereby surpassing the effects of Doppler and rapid channel variations5. By applying this deep-learning-supported method, high-speed railway communication can be significantly improved for channel estimation, ensuring the safety and efficiency of the traffic systems6. Furthermore, the autoencoder is capable of delivering the low error probability detection thanks to prior information learned from communication data. Continued research and development in this field are crucial for the future construction and deployment of railway applications, enhancing connectivity within transportation networks. With the potential of next generation systems, the development of cutting-edge technologies including artificial intelligence with advancements in deep learning-based methods, we can expect a future with reliable and efficient high-speed railway communication, which will contribute to the establishment of a more innovative and safer transportation environment.

Related work

In recent years, the railway networks has garnered significant attention from the research community, with numerous studies in this field7. Specifically, high-speed railway communication currently gains attention to efficiently facilitate transmission among trains and between a train and infrastructure thereby reducing the likelihood of traffic accidents and reliability. Using cooperative train-to-based-station communication can help emergency trains to reduce travel time and increase average speeds by avoiding congested areas8. Such approaches have been shown to improve emergency response efficiency in critical technical situations. A train-to-trackside model facilitates the exchange of operational voice and control data between trains and infrastructure, ensuring safe and efficient traffic management. In railway systems, a train can move at high speeds, causing severe inter-carrier interference (ICI) issues as the OFDM technology is exploited. Previous studies have estimated fast time-varying channel responses, assuming the channel impulse response (CIR) remains stationary within each OFDM block9,10. However, for HSR systems, where trains move at high velocities, channels exhibit diverse propagation characteristics11 and dynamic evolution of multipath clusters12. Because of these characteristics, the channel model needs to be treated as non-stationary13,14, contrasting with the stationary assumption typically used in most prior studies. We emphasize that the non-stationary properties influence both the channel estimation and communication reliability.

Conventional channel estimation methods often utilize pilot symbols embedded within the transmitted data sequence. The channel state information (CSI) at the positions of these pilot symbols can be acquired through a one-tap channel equalizer15. The reconstruction of channel coefficients corresponding to data symbols can be achieved via interpolation techniques or various filter methods16. These techniques can be applied in both the time and frequency domains. Prominent filters for channel estimation include the least squares (LS) method, the minimum mean square error (MMSE) filter17, and the Kalman filter18. Among these, the MMSE filter generally offers superior performance compared to the LS method. The Kalman filter is particularly adept at tracking fast time-varying processes, although it struggles with concurrently tracking multiple channel parameters. Deep learning is being extensively applied across various fields, and therefore the research and development of deep learning in communication is also gaining significant traction. Deep learning represents a powerful toolbox within artificial intelligence19, and using this cutting-edge technology in communication tasks promises to deliver outstanding efficiencies20,21. In22, a deep learning framework was employed to jointly optimize antenna pattern and pilot training overhead, which provides better normalized estimation error than linear minimum mean square error. A two-part cascaded deep neural network was proposed in23 to reduce the time-varying channel estimation overhead in reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided communications by performing channel interpolation and extrapolation in time and antenna domain, respectively. We emphasize that these related works have not yet focused on high speed railway communications, where the propagation channels are highly dynamic and the Doppler effects cannot be neglected.

Motivation and contributions

The main motivation for our study stems from the ever-evolving nature of communication channels, particularly in HSR scenarios, where channels undergo rapid fluctuations due to the high mobility of railways1,24. Consequently, traditional channel estimation methods and BER/SER reduction often struggle to keep pace with these dynamic changes, leading to sub-optimal performance in HSR communication systems since the propagation environments are very dynamic. In this paper, we investigate the benefits of applying DNNs and end-to-end learning (auto-encoder/decoder) to support high-speed train communications. Deep learning aided-channel estimation

Architecture design

In this paper, we propose to use long short-term memory (LSTM) to support conventional time domain channel estimation (TDCE) methods, which improves the BER and SER significantly compared to basic DL networks methods. Figure 3 describes the architecture of the aided-channel estimation LSTM system. The LSTM is an improved version of the recurrent neural network optimized for models whose input sequences solve the vanishing gradient problem. Self-attention aggregates global token interactions and models long-range dependencies. One potential direction is to incorporate the attention into LSTM and leverage the benefits of a transformer. However, high-speed train communications exhibit time-frequency dependence and vary rapidly. Consequently, the long-range information might be not needed and LSTM is sufficient to capture temporal dependencies via recurrence and gating mechanism. Channel estimation often involves time-series data where the current state depends on previous states. LSTM’s ability to maintain information over time steps makes it well-suited for capturing the temporal dynamics of the channel. Similar to other deep learning architectures, the LSTM training process involves forward and backward propagation to capture the features of datasets. In more detail, Algorithm 1 is the forward propagation of the LSTM, which is the process used by neural networks to make predictions. The algorithm computes the hidden and cell states iteratively to learn temporal dependencies of channel variations. The input data passes through the network’s layers, where each layer performs a series of computations involving weights, biases, and activation functions. Starting from the input layer, data is transformed step-by-step as it moves through hidden layers, where each neuron calculates a weighted sum of its inputs, adds a bias, and applies an activation function. This process continues until the final output layer produces the network’s prediction. Forward propagation is crucial for the initial inference phase and for determining the network output.

In Algorithm 2, during the backward propagation, it computes the gradients with respect to the loss function, enabling the network to learn and adjust its internal parameters for improved performance. In this process, errors are propagated backward through the LSTM layers, starting from the output and moving towards the input. At each time step, the algorithm computes the error contributions from the output gate (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta _t$$\end{document}) and the cell state (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta _t$$\end{document}), which are then used to update the gradients concerning the model’s parameters, including weight matrices and biases. These gradients are crucial for optimizing the model using techniques such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD) or other optimization algorithms. The LSTM backward algorithm ensures that the network learns to capture and adapt to the complex dependencies within sequential data. It is a vital tool in DL applications such as natural language processing and time series analysis.

Thanks to the characteristic resembling its name - “long memory”, the model can retain features from input data in the form of sequences, allowing it to uncover dependencies between data points at different time steps. Returning to the problem posed in this paper, the input data consists of a sequence of channel estimates with both real and imaginary parts arranged sequentially, as described in the previous section. This arrangement allows the LSTM to exploit temporal correlations between consecutive channel estimates fully. We have introduced this layer as a minor enhancement, expecting it to yield better results compared to the previously employed models. Additionally, the other model components remain fully connected layers with fundamental activation functions such as the sigmoid and ReLU functions. Cubic spline interpolation constructs a smooth piecewise function from the pilot symbols by fitting cubic polynomials between each pair of consecutive points. The key feature of cubic spline interpolation is its ability to ensure continuity in the first and second derivatives across intervals. In contrast, TDCE-Cubic Hermite only requires continuity at the first derivative level. This results in a highly smooth interpolating function, which is particularly advantageous for channel estimation as it minimizes abrupt changes and oscillations in the estimated CIR. The smoothness provided by cubic splines leads to more accurate and stable channel estimates, especially in environments where the channel characteristics change gradually over time.

The mathematical formulation of cubic spline interpolation involves solving a tridiagonal system of equations to determine the coefficients of the cubic polynomials. Although this can be computationally demanding, the resulting smooth and continuous estimation improves the reliability of data transmission over the channel. Auto-encoder system for minimizing the error probability

Architecture design

As mentioned in the previous section, this part proposes an autoencoder architecture with the expectation of significantly improving efficiency compared to traditional models. The autoencoder architecture comprises two main blocks: the encoder and the decoder. The data stream of bits is first encoded through the Encoder block before being transmitted through the communication channel. Subsequently, at the receiver’s end, the received data is processed through the Decoder block, and the expected output closely approximates or is the most accurate representation of the data transmitted initially from the sender. In essence, an autoencoder consists of encoder and decoder blocks, which are distinct artificial neural networks interconnected through the transmission channel (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{H}$$\end{document}). The aim is to learn the features of the input data and the features of the latent space. The objective is to generate output that maximizes the probability of matching the input data. Remark 1

Conventional LTE systems are primarily deployed in urban, suburban, and rural propagation environments, where user velocities typically remain below 100 km/h. In contrast, LTE-R is specifically designed for high-speed rail scenarios, supporting train velocities of up to 350 km/h. As a result, LTE-R experiences substantially higher Doppler frequencies than conventional LTE, which leads to pronounced signal degradation at the receiver. Moreover, base stations in LTE-R deployments are generally installed along railway tracks and employ highly directional antennas, further increasing the sensitivity of system performance to channel estimation accuracy. Under these conditions, obtaining reliable channel state information and communication reliability becomes critically important. This challenge motivates the investigation of deep learning-based techniques to improve channel estimation performance by exploiting prior knowledge and SER at the end.

Remark 2

This works applies the LSTM to enhance channel estimation quality and the autoencoders to reduce the decoding error probability in high-speed railway communication systems under time-varying and multi-path fading channels. The Doppler effects caused by the high velocity of the train are also considered, making the learning phase more challenging. For railway communications, particularly LTE-R, the two considered frameworks are more useful than the model-based approaches due to the time- and frequency-selective nature of the channels and high speed of the train.

Conclusions

In traditional communication systems, pilot signals were commonly utilized for synchronization and channel estimation44,45. This study focused on a novel strategy of estimating pilot signals to optimize the error probability in innovative high-speed railway communication environments. We demonstrated that properly estimating pilot signals with the support of deep learning enhances system performance. Through experiments, a significant increase in communication reliability was observed when learning-based methods were employed for channel estimation, which paves the way for more flexible and efficient utilization of advanced artificial intelligence techniques. The substantial efficiency gains achieved by the LSTM and autoencoder make them compelling choices for advanced 5G and 6G networks. Especially, the autoencoder outperforms the LSTM-aided channel estimation.

A pivotal aspect of this transformative system is its applicability and efficacy, particularly in scenarios with high modulation levels, making it an ideal candidate for deployment in advanced 5G networks. The demonstrated improvements in SER and BER under various signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions and Doppler frequencies underscore the practicality and superiority of this approach over conventional channel estimation methods. Practical implications extend beyond the experimental setup, showcasing its potential as a viable solution for enhancing communication networks in realistic and dynamic environments, supported by a core network for real-time processing.

Intelligent optimization based prestack inversion method for high resolution estimation of elastic parameters
Authors: Wei Zhang, Keyu Zhao, Weiyu Sun, Shoubang Sun, Songhong Yan, Laolao Niu, Shougang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeismic inversion plays a vital role in constructing high-precision subsurface models for reservoir characterization, fault detection, tunnel engineering, and dynamic formation analysis. To improve inversion accuracy and computational efficiency, this study proposes a novel indirect gradient-based pre-stack amplitude variation with angle (AVA) inversion method, termed mayfly algorithm (MA)-based nonlinear AVA inversion (MANAI). The method leverages the MA within a Bayesian framework and utilizes an accurate Jacobian matrix derived from the exact Zoeppritz equations. The core innovation lies in the indirect global optimization strategy, which extracts gradient information from a prior model before initiating the meta-heuristic optimization. This approach eliminates the need to compute reflection coefficients at every iteration, significantly reducing computational cost while maintaining inversion accuracy. To validate the method’s effectiveness, we compare the MANAI approach with both the whale optimization algorithm (WOA)-based inversion and a conventional local nonlinear method. A parallel implementation is also developed to further accelerate the inversion process. Benchmark tests on synthetic data demonstrate the superior stability and accuracy of the proposed method under varying conditions. Application to real seismic data further confirms that MANAI delivers improved inversion results and reduced runtime compared to traditional strategies. These findings highlight the method’s potential for efficient and reliable inversion in complex exploration scenarios.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-21265-2.Keywords: AVA inversion, Exact Zoeppritz equations, Mayfly algorithm, Parallel computingSubject terms: Geophysics, Seismology

Introduction

Seismic exploration techniques, particularly seismic inversion, play a critical role in the identification of adverse geological conditions and the acquisition of dynamic parameters of subsurface structures in tunnel engineering. These techniques not only help predict construction risks but also assist in optimizing reinforcement strategies, thereby reducing project costs and complexity. By transforming seismic data into detailed subsurface models, seismic inversion supports the characterization of reservoirs and problematic geological bodies, as well as the prediction of formation dynamic parameters, ultimately enhancing the quality of engineering decision-making. Moreover, the technology is invaluable for deep earth resource exploration, enabling precise detection of oil, gas, rare metals, and other critical resources. Traditional pre-stack seismic data inversion, commonly referred to as amplitude versus angle (AVA) or amplitude versus offset (AVO) inversion, has been a focal point of research in the scientific community for many years1–3. The classic pre-stack inversion techniques are based on various approximations of the exact Zoeppritz equations, such as the Shuey4 and Russell5 equations. Approximate methods offer several advantages, such as simplicity in solving problems and ease of computation, making them widely used across various application fields. However, these methods often struggle with generalization in environments characterized by significant parameter contrasts or large incidence angles, leading to accuracy issues in calculations, particularly when faced with abrupt changes in subsurface media6.

In producing accurate seismic reflection coefficients at large incident angles, inversion methods utilizing exact Zoeppritz equations yield satisfactory results compared to those relying on approximate Zoeppritz equations, primarily derived from the exact ones7. Given the difficulties in acquiring reliable S-wave data, many researchers have focused on developing inversion methods based on P-wave reflection coefficients, often utilizing the exact Zoeppritz Eqs8,9. Smith et al. introduced an innovative nonlinear AVO inversion technique for estimating subsurface elastic properties, utilizing a prior distribution modeled after the generalized Gaussian distribution10. Johnson et al. developed a novel nonlinear AVO inversion method aimed at estimating elastic properties of reservoirs11. Their approach utilizes a prior distribution based on the multivariate t-distribution, which enhances the robustness of the inversion process under conditions of uncertainty and noise. Wang et al. introduced a novel inversion method for estimating the three elastic parameters using a Wasserstein generative adversarial network (WGAN), which demonstrates improved accuracy and enhanced robustness12. Traditional inversion methods based on the exact Zoeppritz equations can yield stable results; However, the stability of these methods is compromised due to the presence of an ill-conditioned matrix, which primarily arises when solutions to the inverse trigonometric functions exceed their defined domains. Therefore, some researchers are interested in constructing the objective function based on several regularization methods. To constrain the inversion results more efficiently, among which the most used is the Bayesian theory13–15.

The relationship between the three-term elastic parameters and seismic reflection coefficients is characterized by nonlinearity and complexity. Therefore, utilizing local or global nonlinear inversion methods is common to overcome this obstacle17. Local nonlinear inversion methods, exemplified by Gauss-Newton (GN) and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM), demonstrate stability in the process of inversion under specific assumptions, thereby highlighting their reliability and effectiveness in certain scenarios. However, they are also less accurate than global optimizing processes18. Population-based meta-heuristic algorithms, which are crucial for global optimizing processes in nonlinear AVA inversion, can be divided into two main categories: evolutionary algorithms and swarm intelligence optimization algorithms19 .

Representative evolutionary optimization algorithms include the genetic algorithm (GA)20,21 and differential evolution (DE)22,23. Particle swarm optimization (PSO)24–26 and whale optimization algorithm (WOA)27–30 are among the popular swarm intelligence optimization algorithms. Zervoudakis and Tsafarakis introduced a mayfly optimization algorithm (MA) that outperforms both swarm intelligence and evolutionary algorithms regarding its global and local search abilities1,33. This robust algorithm mimics the social behavior and mating process of mayflies to offer a robust hybrid algorithmic structure, which left most of the popular algorithms behind in the recent tests 35–37. Direct global optimizing processes can easily obtain feasible solutions in nonlinear AVA inversions, but they also need extensive calculations and are time-consuming. Additionally, due to the need for parallel computing1, the direct global optimizing processes with high performance commonly show lower efficiency when applied to the field data. On the other hand, direct global optimization processes that utilize fewer populations and iterations are limited in their ability to obtain accurate reflection coefficients. This limitation arises from the numerous combinations of model parameters present in a specific high-dimensional space. Therefore, to minimize computation costs and prevent the occurrence of ill-posed matrices, we introduced an innovative strategy that leverages global optimization techniques informed by the gradients of existing models. This method eliminates the need to calculate the reflection coefficients based on the exact Zoeppritz equations at every iteration, resulting in a more efficient solution.

We develop a MA-based nonlinear AVA inversion (MANAI) method to establish a high-performance approach for the three-term elastic parameters inversion. The excellent convergence capability of the MA allows it to reliably solve the objective function, yielding more accurate inversion results based on the prior model. Moreover, the prior model contains the low-frequency and mid-frequency components from conventional inversion, offering effective support for frequency enhancement prior to global optimizing processes. Comprehensive numerical simulations of the local nonlinear AVA inversion (LNAI) and global nonlinear AVA inversion (NAI) methods are well-designed. Moreover, the performance of different inversion methods is analyzed through the typical synthetic models and field data. During this assessment, we also consider the impact of different signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio levels during the processing of synthetic data. Ultimately, we extend the original NAI methods to a version that enables parallel computing to accelerate the computation.

Male mayflies, in their natural habitat, gather in dense swarms hovering gracefully a few feet above the water surface. They exhibit a unique and intricate down-and-up movement pattern, known as the nuptial dance, to entice their female counterparts. As male mayflies perform their nuptial dance, female mayflies begin a random walk through the air, flying towards the swarm to find potential mates. This random walk acts as a local search method, enabling females to explore the area and identify the most suitable mate. This strategy also allows females to escape local optima, or regions with fewer potential mates, and continue searching until they locate an appropriate swarm. Applications and results

Conventional synthetic test

We create a seven-layer model and conduct simulations of PP angle gathers at various S/N ratios to validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. As shown in Fig. 2, synthetic pre-stack angle gathers are generated by convolving P-wave reflection coefficients with a Ricker wavelet (25 Hz) across different angles (from 1° to 45° in 1° intervals). To illustrate the effectiveness of the new method, we apply the LNAI method and the WOA-based nonlinear AVA inversion (WOANAI) method to the numerical simulations. Both the LNAI and WOANAI methods utilize the exact Zoeppritz equations and formulate the objective function within a Bayesian framework. The initial model is obtained by applying linear smoothing to the collected true parameters using a twenty-layer moving average window in the vertical direction.

Therefore, we reprogramed the MANAI and WOANAI methods based on the parallel Matlab language by annotating the for loop as a parfor loop, reforming the angle gathers matrix and initial model matrix within the main loop body as sliced input variables0. While inversion on a multiple processor system, every 44 traces are distributed onto 44 child processes for faster results, and the underlying execution engine can evaluate codes in parallel, which is more pronounced if more computational resources are available. This approach increases the utilization of processors, which is beneficial for the substantial decrease in computing time. Meanwhile, the search capability of MA and WOA is effectively maintained.

Distinctly, shorter time consumption is provided by parallel computing technique with a reduction of nearly 24 times, clarifying the superiority of parallel programming technique in global optimizing processes. Besides, the parallel MANAI method needs 53.32s to complete the inversion of the whole profile, while the LNAI method takes 54.83s. The extended multiprocessing version of the MANAI method obtains more accurate results and is also less time-consuming, which is appropriate for hard contrast elastic media in various background noise levels.

Field data application

In this section, we utilize field data gathered from a study area in China to demonstrate the effectiveness and applicability of the proposed method. This study focuses on a depressional tectonic zone characterized by numerous faults. Specifically, faults can introduce fluid contrasts across fault planes and variations in structural dip, both of which significantly affect AVA behavior. These effects can lead to amplitude anomalies or angle-dependent reflectivity variations, which are critical for accurate inversion and interpretation, which are crucial for oil and gas accumulation. Prior to the AVA inversion, the field data underwent a series of preprocessing steps, including Radon transform, inverse Q amplitude compensation, and deconvolution. The parallel computing version of the two global optimization algorithm-based NAI methods is applied to the inversion of the three-term elastic parameters. To ensure the convergence of the two population-based optimization algorithms, the maximum number of iterations is set to 2000 as the model parameters dimension exceeds 100. The initial model parameters are also constructed by linearly smoothing the original logs.

Discussion

In this study, we identified negative impacts of the WOA when applied to the special objective function proposed herein, as revealed through conventional synthetic tests and field data applications. This finding holds significant implications for the fields of computer science and swarm intelligence, particularly in algorithm development. Compared to directly using global optimizing for inversion on the initial model, our proposed new indirect approach based on Taylor approximation, while potentially incurring some loss in accuracy, significantly reduces the computation time for solving the objective function. In the experiment involving the special pseudo-3D wedge model, we further validated that the oscillations observed in the WOA’s results stem from local optima inherent to the algorithm itself, resonating with relevant references published recently. Additionally, an analysis of the test results for the η value revealed it as an uncertain factor, suggesting that future research could focus on incorporating this value for optimization. Furthermore, through tests on the time consumption of parallel computations, we found that the model’s dimensionality significantly impacts the Г value. This is attributed to the high frequency of calculations involving the Jacobian matrix and the model increment matrix during the computation of the objective function, which is influenced by the memory allocation of the Jacobian matrix. We also attempted batch inversion using lower-dimensional models, which required corresponding reductions in the lengths of wavelets during computation. In cases where the wavelet length is not appropriately aligned with the number of data points used per batch, the inversion results may exhibit regularly spaced junctions or discontinuities. However, a critical issue arose regarding the continuity at the junctions, which severely affects the results of the inversion profiles. Similarly, reducing the number of incident angles is equivalent to reducing the model’s dimensionality.

Conclusion

In pre-stack seismic inversion, accuracy is paramount, with computational efficiency as a close second for reservoir identification. We proposed a new global optimizing-based nonlinear pre-stack inversion method for elastic parameters of reservoirs, then extended it to a parallel computing version to improve the computational efficiency. In MA based pre-stack inversion method, gender separation and mating ensure genetic diversity, reducing the likelihood of getting trapped in local optima. This mechanism resulted in a complete 100% elimination of misfit oscillations compared to the WOA, effectively resolving the instability observed in WOA-based results. Compared with WOA, MA has a favorable ability to escape from the local optima, which increases the stability of the inversion process and improves the accuracy and vertical resolution of the inversion results. The global optimizing processes indeed provide more accurate solutions but show lower efficiency. Therefore, parallel computing is an alternative to modify a more stable and less time-consuming nonlinear pre-stack seismic inversion method. The parallel MANAI method demonstrates superior noise immunity and enhanced reliability compared to the WOANAI and LNAI methods, as shown through synthetic experiments and field data applications. Using the parallel MANAI method is instrumental in providing significantly more accurate inversion results within a reasonable timeframe, underscoring the crucial importance of parallel processing in seismic inversion methodologies and laying a favorable data foundation for subsequent reservoir physical property prediction, which can be further enhanced through integration with petrophysical logs.

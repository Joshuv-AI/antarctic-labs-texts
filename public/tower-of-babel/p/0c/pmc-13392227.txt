An efficient Draco lizard optimized stacked Bi-LSTM framework for risk mitigation and resource allocation in project management systems
Authors: Nagaraju Devarakonda, Hussain Syed, Mohammed Ali Shaik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Modern project management demands the mitigation of risk factors in conjunction with optimal resource utilization. Traditional approaches to risk mitigation, based on subjective assessment and supportive static tools, cannot operate effectively in a project environment filled with dynamics. Advanced analytics, artificial intelligence, and deep learning drive data-based decision-making and are able to bring forth predictive insights leading to adaptive management strategies. This paper proposed advanced deep learning approaches to improve risk reduction and optimization of resource allocation in software project management systems. First, the raw historical dataset, collected from the sources that are freely available, is gathered and preprocessed. The dataset collected contains a lot of irrelevance, which causes prediction error and computation complexity. In this regard, the pre-processing, such as one-hot encoding and normalization of data, is done, which reduces issues of data redundancy. After pre-processing, a stacked bidirectional long short-term network S-BiLSTM is proposed for the joint prediction of risks and enhancement of decision power for resource scheduling. Further, optimal resource allocation is carried out by the employment of the Draco lizard optimization DLO technique based on different parameters comprising project complexity, integration complexity, schedule pressure, team size, team experience, technology risk, and risk management maturity. The proposed framework is analyzed through a simulation on the Python platform. Various factors, such as accuracy, workload distribution analysis, resource optimization efficiency , convergence analysis, training time, and resource utilization rate, are analyzed and compared with conventional approaches to demonstrate the proposed approach’s efficiency. The proposed approach achieves a predictive accuracy of 99.2%, significantly improving the performance of the PMS in project risk identification.

Introduction

Contemporary software project management tools find extensive use while working on complex and dynamic software projects. Workflows inspired by the Scrum and Kanban models offer flexible structures, which pose a challenge regarding decision-making for teams regarding changed requirements and their prioritization and timeline considerations1,2. Decision-making in this scenario takes place following experience and intuition, leading to ineffectiveness and inefficiencies within performance1,2. The application of artificial intelligence represents a viable solution because it gives teams a chance to apply decision-support systems specifically designed for adaptive project management approaches. Real-time processing is supported by AI systems because they identify patterns from a massive dataset, ensuring high accuracy. Machine learning algorithms help predict outcome measures for a sprint, team performance, and identification of problems, ensuring short-term response measures and long-term project successes3. Currently, most organizations use AI in project management systems to handle the increased complexity associated with modern software development. Massive expansion projects with geographically dispersed teams pose certain problems associated with dependencies and resource allocation because of communication requirements. Collaboration is improved by AI systems because they identify past project information to aid in resource allocation and prioritize forecasted items corresponding to organizational goals4,5.

The need for metrics that include velocity, progress charts, and defect rates has amplified the adoption of artificial intelligence and machine learning applications. Artificial intelligence-driven project management allows automated plan modification for adapting to requirements and project expectational changes in a lively or changing environment6,7. Decision support systems aided with AI provide other advantages besides rapid execution speed. Predictive modeling and accurate risk forecasting decrease the workload and lower the risk of biased human decisions, and therefore, more time is available for creative problem-solving and decision-making for the project and development teams involved. As approaches in software development advance, incorporating AI is becoming unavoidable, especially for CI/CD tools and software processes. Inevitable risk and resource management complexities are tackled and addressed in software project management because of its iterative processes, especially during uncertain operational scenarios8,9. Inappropriate planning in executing the project tasks and processes provides a negative impact, resulting in resource suboptimality and high risks. Precise risk forecasts for project pivotal stages are vital for risk reduction and avoidance10,11.

Current models are ineffective in the identification of latent relationships, slippage, and technical bottlenecks. The issue of resource management is further exacerbated under the multi-role approach for distributed teams, which results in team members being exhausted, overallocated, and underperforming12,13. Resource conflicts occur because, in certain cases, priorities are not well handled. There is increased complexity in software systems that call for collaboration that crosses functional levels, such as varied software and platforms. Teams that work globally and span time zones are faced with more difficulties in striking a balance between resource optimization and time constraints related to risk evaluation14,15. A lack of visibility between resource usage and risk development undermines decision-making, as current project management tools do not offer comprehensive approaches to monitoring16.

Addressing these challenges necessitates predictive analytics and real-time resource monitoring solutions. AI-based systems enable early risk detection and deliver precise analytical insights to support strategic decision-making aligned with long-term objectives17. While software project management systems effectively address changing requirements, notable gaps persist in risk prevention, resource optimization, and data-driven decision-making. Existing tools rely heavily on historical data and subjective input, which is insufficient for large-scale, multi-team software projects18. A significant weakness in the literature is that only a few prediction models using real-time data have been developed and specifically targeted for contemporary project management. AI applications in this area are still in their initial stages, with limited support for dynamic risk identification, resource optimization, and priority adaptation. Classic risk assessment frameworks also lack consideration of centralized workflows instead of the decentralized iterative nature of modern software project management systems19,20.

Motivation The large scales and complexities of modern project management systems bring huge challenges for effective risk mitigation and optimal resource allocation. Current techniques predominantly develop rule-based strategies, historical analysis, or manual decision-making, which are usually too narrow to capture dynamic project behaviors, hidden dependencies, and evolving risk patterns. Traditional optimization and statistical methods can hardly handle high uncertainty, high-dimensional data, and nonlinear interactions between project variables, leading to late identification of risks and inefficient resource utilization. While conventional ML approaches usually have partial improvements, they are most commonly dependent on static features and non-adaptive to real-time changes in projects. Deep learning offers effective predictive results for identifying the dynamics in a complex project, but these results might not lead to effective resource allocation without intelligent optimization. This implication justifies the use of metaheuristic optimization approaches, which have the ability to optimize large solution spaces by adapting to uncertain environments, thereby reducing exposure to risks. In fact, the motivation for the model development is based on the shortcomings presented by existing approaches in being rigid, nonresponsive, or less scalable, while the new model offers an optimized data-driven framework that is adaptable, effective, and improves the performance of projects by reducing risks associated with those projects.

Although hybrid frameworks combining deep learning and metaheuristic optimization have been reported in several project management and decision-support studies, most existing approaches treat prediction and optimization as independent modules. In contrast, the proposed framework establishes a risk-aware resource allocation mechanism in which the risk predictions generated by the stacked BiLSTM model directly influence the optimization objectives of the DLO. The S-BiLSTM architecture captures hierarchical temporal dependencies among project-risk factors, while DLO dynamically allocates resources by considering both predicted risk levels and project-specific constraints such as project complexity, integration complexity, schedule pressure, team experience, and risk management maturity. This tight coupling between predictive risk intelligence and optimization-driven resource scheduling enables adaptive decision-making under evolving project conditions, representing the primary methodological contribution of the proposed framework.

Related works: a brief review

Recent research works have explored the risk identification and resource allocation in PMS using AI, some of which are reviewed as given below:

In 2025, Almalki et al.21 have established an AI–based decision support framework to improve risk mitigation and resource allocation in software PMS. The framework incorporated the use of predictive analytics and optimization methods to improve the efficiency of decision-making. Machine learning approaches were utilized to evaluate projects for risk analysis, which would provide simultaneous risks and optimal resource allocation to projects. Past project data was employed for the training of models to predict risks for projects based on past information. Comparison of project efficiency relative to existing systems was performed to evaluate the performance of systems, such as risk identification. Nonetheless, its success was largely reliant on data quality, which could be a concern in regard to the prediction’s accuracy. Conversely, higher computations might adversely affect scalability in projects.

In 2024, Jahan et al.22 have suggested a real-time risk management framework to minimize unexpected costs and enhance overall project success through proactive mitigation strategies. Predictive analysis and machine learning algorithms were used to evaluate past project data to detect potential risk patterns. The project key features, such as task duration, resource utilization, and project outcome measures, were considered to promote precise risk prediction. The t-distributed stochastic neighborhood embedding (t-SNE) algorithm was used for optimal feature extraction, which made it simpler to handle high-dimensional project details. Since t-SNE was used to optimize feature extraction, parameter tuning was considered important for maintaining model stability. Additionally, project data completeness/consistency affected model robustness. For instance, it was observed that project analysis became more sensitive to project risk points, and project analysis efficiency was improved.

In 2024, Dong et al.23 have defined the resource-sharing characteristics of scientific research projects to address challenges related to resource misalignment and conflict in project management. Both tangible and intangible resources that are needed in the process of project execution have been analyzed in a way that resulted in the construction of a resource conflict risk index system. A resource conflict risk management model was built through the use of a back propagation (BP) neural network. A dropout technique was used in the BP model to enhance the generalization capability and avoid overfitting. The capability of the neural network in performing nonlinear mappings helped in an efficient way to present intricate dependencies between resources and demands. A mechanism for self-learning helped in updating the capability of the risk assessment model using past projects data. The process helped in the identification of resource conflicts in an accurate way. But its efficiency was restricted due to requirements of past data and sensitivity to parameters.

In 2025, Asif et al.24 have presented the constraint-aware graph neural network framework, termed CAMP-GNN to improve resource allocation in software project management under dynamic constraints such as skill mismatches, evolving task dependencies, and budget limitations. The framework not only considered factors such as urgency, skill match, and budget slack within the communication of passing messages for context-aware task-resource allocation but also used a formal dataset named SoftStat designed by incorporating resource pools, allocation information, and project data to represent various relationships. A preprocessing pipeline was built to make timelines, skills, and budget constraints suitable for machine learning. The framework performed better on projects with resource constraints and was better at allocating resources. The model needs to know the exact details of the projects to perform well. Also, the computational cost could be a limiting factor for scalability for software projects.

In 2025, Kumari et al.25 have interpreted the LSTM-based predictive analytics framework to utilize temporal patterns and historical project data for accurate estimation of task durations, resource requirements, and potential schedule delays. For modeling sequential data, Long Short-Term Memory networks were used. Data preprocessing and optimization tasks were also included for better data input and modeling. The proposed framework helped in effective decision-making for project scheduling and resource management with accurate predictions. From experimental results, it has been observed that there is an improvement in the predictability of timing and resource factors of projects. This method helped in early detection for proactive planning as well as for potential delay risks. But for effective modeling, the proposed framework needs significant historical data as input. Complexity is also added during training for longer projects with significant datasets.

In 2024, Al-Fraihat et al.26 have implemented the ML-based predictive algorithms to identify the most suitable roles for assigned tasks in a distributed application software development environment. It was designed for software managers to aid them in making task assignments more efficient and accurate. Handling the dataset involved performing data processing, normalization, and splitting the dataset into training and testing portions for the ANNs. Various classification models were utilized, such as random forest, decision tree, K-nearest neighbors, and AdaBoost. The results are compared to determine classification effectiveness across various task–role mappings. The decision support for the task allocation has, in fact, been improved. The performance of the models was sensitive to the quality and balance of the input data, however. Moreover, scalability can be limited for highly dynamic or large-scale development environments.

In 2024, Ghorbany et al.27 have emphasized the Copula Bayesian network to model causal relationships and quantify interdependencies among performance indicators. This work applied ML techniques, like XGBoost and SHAP analysis, to improve the network structure and interpretability. The framework provided substantial facilitation in understanding KPI interactions and their impact on infrastructure performance. Results were led by the subjectivity of expert inputs. Moreover, model effectiveness was highly related to data availability and quality. In this respect, generalization across various PPP contexts may be compromised. The identification of relevant KPIs was based on a comprehensive review of other frameworks. Expert evaluation, structured interviews, and questionnaire-based survey methods ensured that data collection was informed.

In 2026, Ismawan et al.28 have defined the hybrid ML-based decision support system by leveraging feature-level representation learning and probabilistic decision fusion. Gradient boosting was adopted for feature selection and nonlinear interactions, while artificial neural networks were used to produce feature embeddings that capture the complexity of project features. The obtained feature representations were merged using a Naive Bayes classifier to obtain probabilistically calibrated decisions. Additionally, a weighted combination mechanism with threshold optimization based on F1-score was employed to stabilize decision predictions under imbalanced project risks. Nonetheless, proper parameter tuning was critical to ensure stable decision fusion in diverse project settings.

In 2026, the work by Mishra et al.29 was carried out with respect to generating a dataset comprising Agile and Waterfall project characteristics obtained via an online survey that included approximately 100 respondents representing several companies. This analysis involved categorical encoding, statistical assessment, hypothesis testing, and prediction techniques in order to compare the chances for project success depending on the methodology used. In the process of exploratory data analysis, it was found that cost variance distribution as well as schedule variance distribution differed considerably between Agile and Waterfall practices. The variance for Agile projects had an average value of 2.14 with a standard deviation equal to 1.32, while for Waterfall projects it had a relatively high average value of 3.87 with a standard deviation of 1.89, thus showing more fluctuations in cost and schedule performance. The size of the data set used was rather small and based predominantly on surveys.

In 2025, Chen et al.30 have suggested the implementation of the data element market in software project management to increase the accuracy of software effort estimation through the improvement of conventional estimation methods’ weaknesses. The framework of feature selection based on the data element market method using reinforcement learning was introduced to boost the efficiency of the estimation process. In addition, the MARLFS algorithm was used with certain changes in the learning scheme and reward function to ensure efficient feature selection and accurate prediction results. The success of the reinforcement learning framework relied greatly on the reward function structure and parameters.

In 2025, Packiam et al.31 have formulated a hybrid risk assessment framework that integrates probabilistic graphical models with attention-based temporal forecasting techniques to analyze uncertainty and predict the progression of project risks. Probabilistic reasoning structures capture causal dependencies among risk factors using expert knowledge and historical analogue information, while sequence-learning models estimate future risk trends from time-dependent data. This combination enables continuous monitoring of evolving risk conditions and supports timely intervention strategies. By uncovering relationships among project variables and forecasting potential risk trajectories, the framework improves decision support across different stages of project execution. The generated insights assist in resource prioritization, risk mitigation planning, and enhanced project resilience under uncertain environments. However, predictive performance may be affected by limited historical data, subjective expert inputs, and the computational demands associated with advanced forecasting models.

In 2025, Mirjalili et al.32 have suggested a resource-based predictive framework by integrating network representations of project activities with GNNs to capture structural and contextual relationships among tasks, resources, and time–cost dynamics. The project was modeled as a heterogeneous activity–resource graph, where nodes represented activities and resources, and edges captured temporal as well as resource dependencies. Multiple learning paradigms, including GraphSAGE and Temporal GNN, were evaluated using both synthetic and benchmark project datasets to assess performance under different conditions. However, the framework was dependent on the accuracy of graph construction and required clearly defined activity–resource relationships for reliable performance. In addition, computational complexity increased with larger graphs, limiting scalability for very large-scale or real-time industrial applications.

In 2024, Obaid and Ahmed33 have depicted the RL framework for portfolio-level MPSRA by formalizing a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with graph-structured state encodings, designing constraint-aware action spaces for task dispatching and resource allocation, aligning reward shaping with key project KPIs, and integrating post-hoc explainability mechanisms to enhance operational trust. A reproducible evaluation protocol was applied using RCPSP/RCMPSP benchmarks along with portfolio-level stress tests, supported by design and metrics summaries and visual representations of system architecture and training workflow. Governance and deployment considerations for project management organizations were also outlined. However, the framework exhibited limitations in handling extremely large-scale dynamic environments, required carefully engineered reward structures, and remained sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and benchmark-specific assumptions, which may restrict generalization to diverse real-world project settings.

Proposed methodology

The introduced work presents an advanced DL–based framework aimed at improving risk mitigation and optimizing resource allocation in software project management systems. Historical project data are collected from publicly available sources and prepared for analysis. The raw dataset contains a large amount of irrelevant and redundant information, which can make computation more complex and reduce prediction accuracy. Data preprocessing techniques, including one-hot encoding and normalization, were used to enhance the quality and consistency of the data. An S-BiLSTM network is then proposed for accurate project risk predictions with enhanced decision-making for resource scheduling. Optimal resource allocation is attained using the DLO algorithm that considers prime project-related parameters comprising project and integration complexity, schedule pressure, team size, team expertise, technological risk, and risk management maturity. Data acquisition

The project management risk raw dataset34 is utilized to evaluate the proposed framework for project risk mitigation and resource allocation in PMS. The dataset is publicly available and contains structured project management records suitable for machine learning and deep learning–based predictive analytics. The dataset consists of 4000 project instances with 51 attributes representing various project-related characteristics, operational conditions, and management factors. Each record corresponds to an individual software or organizational project and contains both numerical and categorical features associated with project planning, execution, team dynamics, and risk assessment. The dataset includes attributes such as project type, team size, project budget (USD), estimated timeline (Months), complexity score, stakeholder count, development methodology, team experience level, similar projects completed, requirement stability, change request rate, team turnover rate, seasonal risk factor, current phase duration, and project phase. The dataset distribution contains 806 low-risk samples, 1396 medium-risk samples, 1036 high-risk samples, and 762 critical-risk samples. This distribution enables the proposed framework to learn varying project risk patterns under different operational conditions. Before model training, several preprocessing operations were performed to improve data quality and learning efficiency. Missing numerical values were handled using median imputation, whereas missing categorical values were replaced using mode-based imputation. Duplicate records were removed to maintain data consistency and integrity. Since the dataset contains categorical attributes, one-hot encoding was applied to transform categorical features into machine-readable binary representations. Subsequently, min–max normalization was employed to scale numerical attributes within the range [0,1], thereby improving convergence behavior during model training. Additionally, mutual information–based feature selection was utilized to identify the most relevant project attributes contributing to project risk prediction. The class distribution analysis revealed that the dataset is reasonably balanced across the four risk categories. Specifically, the dataset contains 806 Low Risk samples (20.15%), 1396 medium risk samples (34.90%), 1036 high risk samples (25.90%), and 762 critical risk samples (19.05%). Although slight variations exist among the class frequencies, the imbalance ratio between the largest and smallest classes is approximately 1.83:1, indicating only a moderate class imbalance rather than a severe imbalance problem. Therefore, additional resampling techniques such as oversampling, undersampling, or synthetic sample generation were not required.

Risk identification using S-BiLSTM technique

The preprocessed data is then given to the stacked BiLSTM model to identify risk levels for enhancing the optimality in the resource scheduling process in PMS. While typical BiLSTM frameworks35 rely on learning general dependencies in sequences, the newly proposed S-BiLSTM model is customized towards the project management context through modeling hierarchical temporal dependencies within the attributes of risks in software projects. In project scenarios, the evolution of project risks does not depend on independent variables, but rather depends on a range of temporal relationships involving factors such as schedule pressures, integration difficulties, requirement changes, team issues, and technological concerns. Hence, the proposed S-BiLSTM model learns short- and long-term project-risk dependencies simultaneously. Optimal resource allocation using DLO technique

After identifying the project risk levels, the resource allocation process is performed in PMS by optimizing multiple constraints, such as project complexity, integration complexity, schedule pressure, team size, team experience, technology risk, and risk management maturity. Generally, metaheuristic optimizers are highly reliable in allocating resources effectively by optimizing these constraints with the use of a behavioural concept. This manuscript proposes an innovative Draco lizard optimizer (DLO)36 for improved schedule adherence and cost efficiency in PMS. The DLO is a population-based metaheuristic inspired by the adaptive gliding and navigation behavior of Draco lizards. In the proposed framework, DLO is employed to determine an optimal resource allocation strategy by minimizing project risk while maximizing resource utilization under multiple project constraints.

Results and discussion

The experimental environment was developed using Python 3.12 with TensorFlow 2.x (Keras) deep learning libraries, accompanied by processing libraries like NumPy, Pandas, and Scikit-learn, and data visualization tools like Matplotlib and Seaborn, under a Windows environment. The experiments are conducted on a system equipped with an Intel® Core™ i5-4300 M processor and 4 GB RAM on a 64-bit platform. Missing data is imputed with the median for numerical features and the mode for categorical features, and duplicates are removed for data integrity. One-hot encoding is used for processing categorical features, and then implemented with min–max normalization for data scaling, and used mutual information for selecting features with higher relevance. The dataset is split with an 80–20 ratio for training and testing, with the validation data being the last 20% of the training data.

The new model consists of a stacked BiLSTM layer that comprises a single input and output layer, 2 BiLSTM hidden layers 2 BiLSTM layers with 128 and 64 units, respectively, followed by 2 dense layers that have 64 and 32 units with the ReLU activation function for the former two layers, whereas for the output, the Softmax activation function was used. To improve generalization performance, a dropout of 0.35 and batch normalization are used. The model is trained on the categorical cross-entropy loss function using the Adam optimizer for minimization at a learning rate of 0.0005, 32 batch size over 7 epochs due to the input dimension of (samples, 1, features). To efficiently utilize resources, the optimization algorithm is set to DLO parameters that include a population of 30 individuals with a solution dimension of 4 elements, has 50 maximum iterations with a search space from 0 to 1, as its search space parameters, whereas the prevention of turning inadequate objectives into optimal is emphasized by setting the weights to 0.4, 0.3, -0.2, -0.1 correspondingly.

An AUC value closer to 1 indicates strong discriminative capability, while a value near 0.5 suggests random classification.

Conclusion

This work presented a DL–based framework that effectively enhances risk mitigation and resource allocation in software project management systems through the integration of robust preprocessing, an S-BiLSTM–based model for risk prediction, and intelligent resource scheduling using the Draco lizard optimization technique. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves a predictive accuracy of 99.2% for critical risk identification with a team size of 25, indicating its strong capability in accurately detecting project risks in project management systems. Additionally, a workload distribution analysis (WDA) score of 94.3% reflects improved task distribution for effective resource utilization, while a resource optimization efficiency (ROE) of 96.72% highlights the effectiveness of the proposed optimization strategy. The framework further achieves a resource utilization rate (RUR) of 82% with a reduced training time of 1.8 s, suggesting computational efficiency and practical applicability under the evaluated conditions. Despite the improved performance of the proposed framework, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the model is trained and validated on a single publicly available dataset, which may limit its generalizability across diverse real-world project environments. Second, the S-BiLSTM architecture requires substantial computational resources for training, which may affect scalability for large-scale or real-time applications. Third, the effectiveness of the DLO-based optimization is dependent on parameter tuning, and suboptimal parameter settings may impact convergence behavior. Additionally, the model primarily relies on historical data and may not fully capture unforeseen dynamic changes in project environments.

Future research will focus on validating the proposed framework using multi-organization and cross-domain project management datasets collected from software development firms, healthcare organizations, construction projects, and enterprise information systems. Such validation will improve the generalizability of the proposed framework under diverse project environments and organizational practices. In addition, future studies will investigate concept drift adaptation mechanisms to address evolving project characteristics, changing resource availability, and dynamic risk patterns that may reduce prediction accuracy over time. Incremental learning and online model updating strategies will be explored to maintain reliable risk prediction under continuously changing project conditions. From a deployment perspective, future work will examine the integration of the proposed framework into real-world project management platforms, enabling real-time risk monitoring, automated resource recommendation, and decision-support services. Practical deployment studies will also evaluate scalability, computational overhead, user acceptance, data privacy considerations, and interoperability with existing enterprise project management tools.

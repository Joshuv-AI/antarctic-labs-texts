Stress and healthy lifestyle behaviors in high-risk pregnancies: a correlational study
Authors: Hatice Gül Öztaş, Filiz Taş
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe aim of this study is to determine the relationship between perceived stress levels and healthy lifestyle behaviors in women with high-risk pregnancy based on Neumannn Systems Model.MethodThis descriptive study was conducted between July and October 2024 with 402 pregnant women who were admitted to a public hospital in southern Turkey. The research data were collected using “Pregnancy Introduction Form,” “High-Risk Pregnancy Assessment Form,” “Perceived Stress Scale for High-Risky Pregnancies Based on the Neumannn Systems Model (PSS-HRPNSM),” and “Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors in Pregnancy Scale (HLBPS).” The research data were analyzed using independent samples t-test, ANOVA, Pearson correlation coefficient and multiple linear regression analysis.FindingsThe mean score of perceived stress levels of pregnant women with high-risk pregnancies based on the Neumann Systems Model was 83.55 ± 19.20, and the mean score for healthy lifestyle behaviors was 100.82 ± 15.84. The participants’s scores were at moderate levels in all three subdimensions of the perceived stress scale: 22.52 ± 6.25 in the physiological domain, 29.23 ± 9.54 in the psychological domain, and 31.79 ± 7.94 in the sociocultural, developmental, and spiritual domain, respectively. Significant differences were found between participants’ age, education level, and their total scores on the healthy lifestyle behaviors scale. A weak but statistically significant negative correlation (r = -0.158; p < 0.001) was found between perceived stress levels and healthy lifestyle behaviors during pregnancy.ConclusionThe study found that perceived stress levels in women with high-risk pregnancies were moderate while healthy lifestyle behaviors were at an excellent level. It was observed that as perceived stress levels decreased, healthy lifestyle behaviors improved during pregnancy.Keywords: Pregnancy, Neumann systems model, Stress, Healthy lifestyle

Background

Pregnancy is a significant and critical phase in a woman’s life. The physiological and psychological changes that occur during pregnancy can impact women both physically and mentally, as well as potentially leading to various complications [1–4]. Even during a healthy pregnancy, complications such as preeclampsia, eclampsia, premature rupture of membranes, and intrauterine growth restriction can emerge, categorizing the pregnancy as high-risk [2, 5, 6]. The development of such risk factors increases stress levels during pregnancy, affecting women emotionally, psychologically, mentally, and physically, thereby reducing their overall well-being [7, 8].

The Neumann Systems Model (NSM) defines the individual as an open system composed of five elements including physiological, psychological, sociocultural, spiritual, and developmental. This model represents a dynamic structure aimed at maintaining balance and limiting stress factors [9, 10]. The physiological domain refers to bodily structure and function. The psychological domain involves mental processes and their interactions with the environment [6, 11]. The sociocultural domain includes social and cultural interactions. The developmental domain pertains to age-related processes and activities. The spiritual domain represents beliefs and spiritual influences [12]. Neumann posits that individuals are exposed to stress and stressors throughout their lives that threaten these five domains [9].

In this study, healthy lifestyle behaviors were examined based on the Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors in Pregnancy Scale, which aims to evaluate health-promoting behaviors of individuals in six basic dimensions. These dimensions include nutrition, physical activity, health responsibility, coping with stress, interpersonal relationships and spiritual development [13]. These dimensions are directly connected to the physiological, psychological and sociocultural components of the Neuman System Model. According to Neuman, the individual’s ability to maintain systemic integrity is contingent on his/her capacity to cope effectively with stressors in these areas. Therefore, healthy lifestyle behaviors reflect the individual’s ability to cope with stress and maintain systemic balance.

Studies have shown that healthy lifestyle modifications during pregnancy reduce perceived stress levels [14]. High-risk pregnant women need to adopt healthy lifestyle behaviors (HLBS) to protect both themselves and their fetuses. These behaviors play a critical role in preserving maternal and fetal health and ensuring a smooth childbirth process [11]. Some studies have indicated the adverse effects of psychosocial factors, such as stress, on pregnancy outcomes [6, 8, 10].

Therefore, it is essential to understand the factors influencing high-risk pregnancies, assess related psychosocial characteristics, and develop appropriate intervention strategies to safeguard the health of women in this group. Such efforts will help reduce disease burden as well as maternal and neonatal mortality rates [3]. Reducing maternal and neonatal mortality is a key multidimensional development indicator targeted by the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The SDGs aim to lower the global maternal mortality ratio to fewer than 70 deaths per 100,000 live births and end preventable deaths of newborns and children under five years of age by 2030. Preventing risk pregnancies is crucial to achieving these goals. In this context, identifying risks in the early period, providing psychosocial support and offering prenatal care services with holistic approaches are foregrounded as a basic preventive health strategy today [15, 16].

Determining the perceived stress levels of women with high-risk pregnancies and recognizing potential health issues is of great importance for developing the necessary health behaviors to protect maternal and fetal health [17–19]. Although the effect of psychosocial stress on pregnancy outcomes has been extensively addressed in the literature, the effect of perceived stress on healthy lifestyle behaviors in women with high-risk pregnancies has been examined in a limited number of studies, especially within the framework of the Neumann Systems Model. The main purpose of this study, which is planned to fill this gap in the relevant literature, is to examine the relationship between perceived stress levels and healthy lifestyle behaviors in women with high-risk pregnancies based on the Neumann Systems Model. A better understanding of this relationship will provide important contributions to the planning and implementation of preventive and supportive health services for high-risk pregnant women. The findings of the study are expected to contribute to the maternal and child health literature. In this context, answers to the following research questions will be sought.

Methods

Study design and sample

This research was designed as correlational survey. The study was conducted between July and October 2024 among the pregnant women who applied to the obstetrics outpatient clinic of a public hospital in Kahramanmaraş province. All pregnant women who met the research criteria were included in the study by purposive and non-probability purposive sampling method. Participant selection was made on a voluntary basis, and it was ensured that the sample group was inclusive and open to diversity to represent high-risk pregnancies. The sample size was determined based on the formula suggested by Daniel (1999) [20], which is frequently used in the literature, and it was calculated that a study should be conducted with at least 303 people according to the 5% margin of error, 95% confidence level and 80% representativeness of the universe. A total of 464 pregnant women were invited to participate in the study. Of these, 40 refused to participate and 22 were not included in the data analysis because their questionnaire forms were incomplete. As a result, 402 pregnant women who met the study criteria and whose data were complete were included in the study.

The inclusion criteria were being aged between 18 and 45 years, having no communicative problems,, and agreeing to participate in the research. All the pregnant women who met the research criteria were included in the study. Exclusion criteria were that pregnant women who had communication problems and did not complete the entire data collection form were excluded from the study.

Data collection

The research data were collected using the “Pregnant Women Introduction Form,” “Pregnant Risk Assessment Form,” “Perceived Stress Scale for Risk Pregnancies Based on Neumann Systems Model (NSMT-RGASÖ),” and “Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors Scale in Pregnancy.” These data were collected through face-to-face interviews with the pregnant women who volunteered to participate in the study.

Pregnant Women Introduction Form: The form was created by the researchers themselves based on the relevant literature [7, 10, 11, 14] and consists of questions regarding the socio-demographic characteristics such as age, educational level, income level, employment status, etc., obstetric characteristics such as gestational age, decision-making regarding the pregnancy, etc., and lifestyle habits such as as smoking, drinking alcohol etc.

Pregnant Risk Assessment Form: This form is used to identify high-risk pregnancies recommended by the Ministry of Health [21]. The risk assessment form includes risk factors during the prenatal period. This form is available for the pregnant women referred after specialist consultation. The form consists of three sections: Obstetric History (8 diagnoses), Current Pregnancy (8 diagnoses), and General Medical History (8 diagnoses), totaling 24 diagnoses. These diagnoses are made based on yes/no responses without scoring, through the family physician’s diagnosis.

Perceived Stress Scale for Risk Pregnancies Based on Neumann Systems Model (NSMT-RGASÖ): Developed by Metin and Kulakaç [10], this scale consists of 34 items and three subdimensions. A higher score indicates a higher perception of stress either overall or in the specified life area. The scale can yield a minimum score of 34 and a maximum of 170. The Cronbach alpha value of the scale was found to be 0.87 [10]. In this study, the Cronbach alpha value was determined to be 0.76.

Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors Scale in Pregnancy (HLBPS): Developed by Yılmaz and Karahan [13], this is a five-point Likert-type scale. The scale has a multi-component discrete structure, consisting of 29 items and 6 subdimensions: “Pregnancy Responsibility,” “Nutrition,” “Hygiene,” “Physical Activity,” “Travel,” and “Acceptance of Pregnancy.” Each subdimension can be used independently in studies. Higher scores indicate positive/high healthy lifestyle behaviors. The Cronbach alpha value of the scale was found to be 0.83 [13]. In this study, the Cronbach alpha value was determined to be 0.82.

Data analysis

The data were analyzed using the SPSS 21.0 software. The normality of the data was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. To determine socio-demographic characteristics, risk conditions, and scale scores, frequency (n), percentage (%), mean, standard deviation, and minimum-maximum values were used. The relationship between variables was analyzed using One-way ANOVA for comparing means of more than two groups and t-tests for binary variables. Pearson correlation coefficients were used to analyze the relationship between dependent variables. Regression coefficients of variables affecting the total score of Healthy Life Behaviors in Pregnancy were examined multiple linear regression analysis. A statistical significance level of p < 0.05 was accepted. The internal consistency of the scales was assessed using the Cronbach alpha coefficient.

Results

When the distribution of socio-demographic and obstetric characteristics of pregnant women is examined, the average age of the pregnant women is 28.59 ± 6.06 years, with a minimum age of 18 and a maximum age of 43. The average weight of the pregnant women participating in the study was 71.34 ± 8.80 and the average height was 162.90 ± 4.72. It was found that 30.3% of the pregnant women have been married for 10 years or more, 59.0% have a high school education level, and 75.6% have income equal to their expenses. In obstetric data, 31.6% of the pregnant women have had three pregnancies, and 55.5% are at 28 weeks of pregnancy or above. Additionally, 39.6% of the pregnant women perceive their general health status as poor (Table 1).

Statistical analysis revealed a significant difference between age groups and perceived stress in the physiological life area sub-dimension (F = 4.216; p < 0.002). Post-hoc testing indicated that this significance was particularly noted in the 18–22 age group. According to the monthly income variable, a significant difference was found in the socio-cultural/developmental/spiritual life area perceived stress sub-dimension (F = 4.522; p < 0.011). The significance was identified between groups with income less than expenses and those with income equal to expenses. A significant difference was detected between the years of marriage and perceived stress in the physiological life area sub-dimension (F = 3.782; p < 0.011), with significance arising from those married for 7–9 years.

The presence of thyroid disease was significantly associated with the total NSMT-RGASÖ (t = 2.496; p < 0.013), perceived stress in the physiological life area (t = 2.073; p < 0.039), and perceived stress in the socio-cultural/developmental/spiritual life area (t = 2.213; p < 0.027). Significant associations were detected between the presence of other serious illnesses and perceived stress in the physiological life area (t = 3.777; p < 0.000) and the risky condition detected by examination (t = 3.358; p < 0.001). Being over 35 years was significantly associated with the socio-cultural, developmental, and spiritual area sub-dimension (t=−3.825; p < 0.000), while vaginal bleeding was significantly associated with perceived stress in the psychological life area (t = 2.589; p < 0.010). Significant associations were found between diastolic blood pressure over 90 mmHg and both the total NSMT-RGASÖ and its sub-dimensions. Specifically, the total NSMT-RGASÖ (t = 6.317; p < 0.000), perceived stress in the physiological life area (t = 6.311; p < 0.000), psychological life area (t = 5.494; p < 0.000), and socio-cultural/developmental/spiritual life area (t = 3.519; p < 0.000) showed significant associations (Table 5).

The correlation analysis revealed a low but statistically significant negative correlation between perceived stress in high-risk pregnancies and healthy lifestyle behaviors in pregnancy (r= −0.158; p < 0.001). This indicates that as the level of perceived stress decreases, healthy lifestyle behaviors in pregnancy increase. There were strong and significant negative correlations between stress and the sub-dimensions of healthy lifestyle behaviors, particularly in nutrition (r=−0.143; p < 0.001) and acceptance of pregnancy (r=−0.144; p < 0.001). This indicates a strong and significant positive relationship between healthy lifestyle behaviors and the sub-dimensions of pregnancy responsibility, hygiene, nutrition, physical activity, travel, and acceptance of pregnancy (Table 6).

The standardized coefficient (Beta = 0.173) indicates a moderate effect. The VIF value is 1, indicating no multicollinearity problem.

Model 2: “Age” and “Education Level” VariablesIn Model 2, the variables “Your Age” and “Education Level” are included together. Both variables significantly predict the HLSBP score positively. The coefficient for age increased to 3.606, and its effect strengthened (p = 0.000). Education level is also a significant predictor (B = 4.250; p = 0.001). As education level increases, the HLSB score increases by an average of 4.250. The VIF values (1.149) indicate no multicollinearity problem. Age and education level are significant predictors of the HLSB score. Income level and years of marriage were excluded as they did not contribute significantly to the model.

Discussion

This study was conducted to determine the relationship between perceived stress levels and healthy lifestyle behaviors of pregnant women in risk pregnancies based on the Neumann Systems Model. When evaluated for risk pregnancy characteristics based on obstetric history, current pregnancy, and general medical history, it was observed that women had numerous risks in all three areas. Studies indicate that women in high-risk pregnancies experience additional stress that can affect their health behaviors, particularly when considering low or high-risk pregnancy statuses [1, 3, 22].

The study included pregnant women who had been hospitalized due to hypertension or preeclampsia/eclampsia in their previous pregnancies. These conditions are hallmark indicators of high-risk pregnancies and are often associated with heightened maternal stress and systemic inflammation. Elevated stress in such contexts is believed to trigger neuroendocrine and inflammatory responses, which can impair fetoplacental circulation and increase the risk of adverse outcomes such as preterm birth and intrauterine growth restriction [15, 23]. This biologically plausible mechanism supports the idea that maternal psychological stress is not only a behavioral barrier but also a physiological risk amplifier. In line with this, Malakouti et al. (2015) reported a significant inverse relationship between perceived stress and health-promoting behaviors in women with preeclampsia, highlighting that elevated stress levels negatively affect self-care and lifestyle engagement. Similarly, our findings reveal that women with a history of hypertensive complications during pregnancy demonstrated lower scores in healthy lifestyle behaviors, particularly in areas such as physical activity and pregnancy responsibility. These results underscore the importance of integrated prenatal interventions targeting both psychological resilience and lifestyle regulation in high-risk pregnancies. Reducing maternal stress may not only improve mental well-being but also promote healthier behaviors that contribute to improved maternal-fetal outcomes.

In the study, the highest score was found in the Sociocultural/Developmental/Spiritual Life Domain Perceived Stress subscale of NSMT-RGASÖ. The literature shows that social support is associated with physical activity, healthy eating and interpersonal relationships [23]. It has also been reported that high-risk pregnant women exhibit the most positive health behaviours in the area of spiritual development [11]. The findings support that spirituality and sociocultural empowerment may be effective in coping with stress in high-risk pregnancies. It is also consistent with the literature that psychosocial health is affected by many factors [5]. Thus, the present findings support and extend previous studies and suggest that spiritual and sociocultural empowerment can play an important role in maintaining psychological balance under stress and promoting healthy lifestyle behaviors. Translating these findings into routine care practices may help health care providers make meaningful contributions to maternal and fetal health in high-risk pregnancies.

The mean score on the Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors in Pregnancy Scale (HLBPS) was above average, suggesting that despite being in high-risk groups, many pregnant women maintain a positive attitude toward engaging in healthy behaviors. These results are consistent with prior studies that reported similarly elevated HLBS scores among pregnant women [3, 6, 24, 25]. Pregnancy appears to function as a sensitive period during which women may be more receptive to health behavior interventions. This aligns with the Neuman Systems Model, which postulates that individuals under stress activate protective mechanisms to preserve systemic stability. In the study, it was found that age, education level and year of marriage significantly affected healthy lifestyle behaviours during pregnancy (p < 0.05). Regression analysis showed that age predicted healthy lifestyle behaviors significantly and positively (B = 2.674; p = 0.001). This finding shows that each 1 unit increase in age causes an average increase of 2.674 points in HLSB score. In addition, significant differences were found between the age of pregnant women and pregnancy responsibility, hygiene, physical activity, travelling and acceptance of pregnancy. The literature also supports that age is an important factor on health behaviours during pregnancy [1, 5].

Gökyıldız et al. (2014) reported that older and more educated women with higher socioeconomic status exhibited more health-promoting behaviors during pregnancy [26]. However, Cannella et al. (2018) noted that some high-risk groups displayed significantly lower health behavior scores [27]. In contrast, our sample demonstrated relatively higher scores. These discrepancies could be attributed to differences in study population characteristics, healthcare access, cultural norms, or regional variations in health literacy and social support.

Significant differences were found between the age of pregnant women, education level, years of marriage, and the total score of healthy lifestyle behaviors during pregnancy (p < 0.005). The literature indicates that age and education level influence the development of healthy lifestyles, with increasing age and education level correlating with higher average total scores on the HLBS scale [13, 24]. Our study parallels the literature. However, there are no studies in the literature showing a relationship between years of marriage and healthy lifestyle behaviors during pregnancy. The increase in healthy lifestyle behaviors with years of marriage may be interpreted as related to the adequacy in accepting pregnancy and taking on pregnancy responsibilities.

The results of this study indicate a relationship between the stress levels of pregnant women and their health behaviors. Dağlar et al. (2019) examined the relationships between coping styles with stress and quality of life in pregnant women, reporting a weak positive relationship between positive coping behaviors and quality of life, and a weak negative relationship between negative coping styles and quality of life [28]. Similarly, Kim and Lee found that pregnant women with lower stress levels engaged in more regular exercise compared to those with higher stress levels [29]. A systematic review also explained that anxiety and stress experienced during pregnancy are related to lifestyle and health habits [30]. Topaloğlu-Oren et al. (2023) reported a negative relationship between the level of depressive symptoms in pregnant women and their physical activity [31]. These results align with our findings.

In the study, it was found that the highest score in HLBS subscales was in nutrition and the lowest score was in physical activity. While nutrition is prioritised in preeclampsia and high-risk pregnancies, physical activity is ignored. Studies show that spiritual development and nutrition have the highest scores, while physical activity has the lowest score [3, 27]. However, physical activity in pregnancy is critical for maternal and infant health unless there is a contraindication [3]. On the other hand, Gökyıldız et al. (2014) found higher levels of physical activity in their study. This difference may be due to factors such as the geographical region where the study was conducted, the season, and health literacy. This finding requires a reassessment of cultural perceptions and support mechanisms regarding physical activity during pregnancy.

Finally, we observed a statistically significant inverse relationship between perceived stress and healthy lifestyle behaviors, affirming the findings of Malakouti et al. (2015) and Cannella et al. (2018). Cannella’s meta-analysis further emphasized that this negative effect of stress on health behaviors is amplified in high-risk pregnancies compared to low-risk ones. These data reinforce the need for psychosocial screening and stress reduction strategies in antenatal care, particularly among high-risk groups.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, the classification of high-risk pregnancy was based on participants’ self-reports. Specific medical diagnoses such as gestational diabetes, preterm labor, or intrauterine growth restriction were not clearly identified. Second due to the cross-sectional design of the study, causal relationships between the variables could not be established. Longitudinal studies are needed to better understand these associations. Additionally, the study was conducted at a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the results. Multi-center studies with larger and more diverse samples are recommended.

Conclusion

This study found that the perceived stress of high-risk pregnant women is at a moderate level, while their healthy lifestyle behaviors are at a good level. It was determined that as perceived stress levels during pregnancy decrease, healthy lifestyle behaviors increase. This underscores the importance of assessing both stress levels and health behaviors in women diagnosed with high-risk pregnancies to ensure a healthy pregnancy process. Our results showed that age and education level are predictors of healthy lifestyle behaviors during pregnancy.

In line with these findings, it is recommended that health professionals providing prenatal care to high-risk pregnant women should assess not only medical risks but also women’s psychosocial stress levels and healthy lifestyle behaviors. Early identification of pregnant women with high stress levels and inadequate healthy lifestyle behaviors may contribute to the development of individualized and multidisciplinary intervention plans. This emphasizes the effect of demographic factors such as age and education level on health behaviors during pregnancy. Therefore, it is recommended that health education programs that encourage physical activity, strengthen stress coping skills, and promote a healthy lifestyle with a holistic approach including components such as nutrition, hygiene, and acceptance of pregnancy should be integrated into routine prenatal care services. It would be useful to conduct multi-centre, randomised controlled trials to increase the generalisability of the findings to different populations.

Polycythaemic panmyelosis in chronic myeloid leukaemia
Authors: George Mason, Ameya Hanamshet, Sewon Kim, Nisha Singh, Kirsten McIlroy, Matthew Greenwood
Published: 2026

A 71‐year‐old man had been on long‐term testosterone undecanoate for complex hypogonadal osteoporosis and the sodium‐glucose co‐transporter‐2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) for metabolic syndrome. He presented with subclinical cytoses: haemoglobin 169 g/L, haematocrit 0.55, platelet count 439 × 109/L, neutrophil count 12.44 × 109/L, myelocyte count 0.78 × 109/L.

Bone marrow trephine demonstrated marked trilineage hyperplasia with left‐shifted granulopoiesis. Marrow topology was preserved; there were 8–10 layers of paratrabecular myeloid progenitors and increased numbers of interstitial erythroid islands (composite image; top left, objective ×4, Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), top right objective ×20, H&E). Dwarf megakaryocytes were present. The Myeloid:Erythroid (M:E) ratio was 3‐to‐1 (composite image; bottom left, objective 4×, myeloperoxidase immunohistochemistry, bottom right, objective 4×, glycophorin A immunohistochemistry). Iron stores were absent.

G‐banded karyotype demonstrated a Philadelphia translocation (see karyogram) and fluorescent in situ hybridisation demonstrated a BCR::ABL1 fusion product, confirming the diagnosis of chronic myeloid leukaemia in chronic phase. Myeloid next generation sequencing excluded a superimposed variant driving polycythaemia. Cautious decreases in hormone replacement therapy were undertaken. A major haematological response was induced with dasatinib.

Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) classically presents with granulocytic and megakaryocytic hyperplasia with a heavily skewed M:E ratio, but polycythaemia is not a typical feature. 1 In this case of CML, the degree of accompanying erythroid hyperplasia was sufficient to produce a normal M:E ratio. SGLT2 inhibitors are increasingly prescribed and have been associated with an unmasking of previously subclinical myeloproliferative neoplasms 2 due to iron mobilisation and inducing endogenous and ectopic secretion of renal and hepatic erythropoietin respectively. 3 Polycythaemia emerges in up to 17% of patients commenced on SGLT2 inhibitor therapy for metabolic syndrome and its sequelae. 4 The incidence of polycythaemia rises in the context of therapeutic hormone replacement therapy. 5

In this era of multiple SGLT2i indications, CML may now present as a polycythaemic panmyelosis with both myeloid and erythroid hyperplasia. Thorough molecular assessment may be required to appropriately classify such cases.

Right Atrial Function is Impaired in Pediatric Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension: A Multi-center Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance Study
Authors: Kimberley G. Miles, Hieu T. Ta, Kurt R. Bjorkman, Zhiqian Gao, Nicholas J. Ollberding, Russel Hirsch, D. Dunbar Ivy, Sean M. Lang, Michelle Cash, Melissa Magness, Meredith O’Neil, Kimberly Luebbe, Benjamin S. Frank, Paul J. Critser
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundImpaired right atrial (RA) function is strongly predictive of adverse outcomes in adults with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) but remains incompletely understood in pediatric PAH. In this pediatric multi-center retrospective cohort study using cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging (CMR), we analyzed RA size and phasic function and its associations with PAH severity.MethodsPAH and control pediatric patients from two centers who underwent CMR from 2010 to 2023 were identified. RA volumes were measured throughout the cardiac cycle using the single-plane, area-length method on a standard 4-chamber cine sequence. Total, conduit phase, and active phase stroke volume (SVi; indexed to BSA) and ejection fraction (EF) were calculated. A novel marker, the A/C ratio, was calculated as active/conduit SVi. RA size and phasic function measurements were correlated with clinical, hemodynamic, and non-RA CMR metrics of PAH severity and were associated with adverse events (Potts shunt, lung transplant listing/surgery, and/or death) using univariate and bivariate Cox proportional-hazards regression analyses. Intra- and inter-rater reliability was analyzed using intra-class correlation coefficients (ICC).ResultsCompared to controls (n = 36), children with PAH (n = 72) had higher RA volumes, lower conduit phasic function, and higher active phasic function. In PAH patients, minimum RA volume, active SVi, and A/C ratio directly correlated with NT-proBNP and right ventricular (RV) size, filling pressures, and afterload, while they were inversely correlated with exercise capacity and RVEF. RA conduit EF (cEF) correlations were reversed. During median follow-up of 3.2 years [IQR 1.0, 5.9], RA cEF and A/C ratio remained independent predictors of adverse events after adjustment for common metrics of PAH severity on bivariate analysis, including RVEF (RA cEF aHR 0.91 [95% CI: 0.83–0.99]; A/C ratio aHR 1.58 [95% CI: 1.09–2.29]) and indexed pulmonary vascular resistance (RA cEF aHR 0.83 [95% CI: 0.74–0.93]; A/C ratio aHR 1.79 [95% CI: 1.34–2.41]). RA volume measurements had excellent reliability (ICC >0.97).ConclusionCorrelating with disease severity, impaired RA physiology in pediatric PAH is characterized by RA dilation, reduced conduit phasic function, and compensatory augmentation of active phasic function. Assessment of RA size and phasic function is feasible and highly reproducible using standard CMR sequences.

1. Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is characterized by elevated pulmonary artery pressures in the setting of progressive, pathologic vascular changes, which can lead to right ventricular (RV) dysfunction and, ultimately, death [1], [2], [3]. In response to chronically high afterload, the RV becomes hypertrophied and increases contractility to maintain cardiac output. When this compensatory mechanism is exhausted, the RV progressively dilates and develops systolic dysfunction [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. Concurrently, increased interstitial fibrosis, intrinsic myocyte stiffness, and chamber dilation contribute to progressive diastolic dysfunction of the right heart [6], [7], [8], [9], [10].

The right atrial (RA) cardiac cycle is composed of three phases as follows: (1) a reservoir phase: filling of the RA as a capacitance chamber; (2) a passive conduit phase: passive RV filling during early relaxation; and (3) an active booster phase: atrial contraction to complete ventricular filling [8]. Normally, atrial contraction contributes ∼20–30% of total atrial stroke volume (SV) [11], [12]. However, in the presence of PAH, the RA develops progressive dilation, impaired reservoir and conduit function, and increased reliance on the active booster phase to maintain RV filling [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]. With severe diastolic dysfunction, RA SV can be directed retrograde, resulting in poor RV filling and systemic venous congestion [16], [17]. Thus, diastolic dysfunction and impaired RA function are key developments in the pathophysiology of decompensated RV failure in PH [2], [8], [9], [10], [16], [17].

In prior PAH studies, RA dilation and impaired RA phasic function are strongly predictive of adverse events (AEs) [12], [13], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24]. However, this has not been studied using CMR in pediatric PAH patients. We aimed to compare RA size and phasic function in pediatric PAH patients with control patients and identify associations between impaired RA function and metrics of PAH severity, including exercise capacity, biomarker, imaging, invasive hemodynamic data, and outcomes.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design

All pediatric PAH patients (<21 years of age) managed at Cincinnati Children’s Hospital Medical Center (CCHMC) and Children’s Hospital of Colorado (CHCO) who underwent CMR between June 2010 and August 2023 were identified. This retrospective analysis was approved by the Institutional Review Board of CCHMC and CHCO. Informed consent was waived. The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request with appropriate permissions.

PAH was defined as patients >3 months of age with mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) >20 mmHg, indexed pulmonary vascular resistance (PVRi) ≥3 WU*m2, and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) <15 mmHg [25]. Patients with a hemodynamically significant atrial septal defect (ASD; Qp:Qs >1.5 by CMR or hemodynamic data [26]), which could confound interpretation of RA dilation in the setting of PAH, and post-capillary PH were excluded. There is limited pediatric normative data for RA volumetry by CMR [27]; thus, control patients with normal intracardiac anatomy and function were identified at CCHMC using 2:1 individual matching on age within ∼1 year, weight within 20%, and sex. CMR indications included predominantly aortopathy, pectus excavatum (Haller index <3.5; no significant cardiac compression), and syncope/benign arrhythmias.

2.2. Covariates

Demographic, clinical history, biomarker, echocardiographic, hemodynamic, and outcome data were collected from the medical record (Supplemental Methods). An AE was defined as a Potts shunt, lung transplant listing or surgery, or death. If patients were listed for a lung transplant but waiting at the end of the follow-up period, then date of listing was the AE. If a patient underwent lung transplant evaluation due to clinical worsening despite maximized medical therapy but ultimately not listed secondary to a contraindication or family deferment, this was counted as an AE and documented as date of evaluation decision.

2.3. CMR acquisition and analysis

CMR imaging was performed on 1.5 Tesla Philips Ingenia scanners (Philips Healthcare, Best, Netherlands; CCHMC/CHCO) or 3 Tesla Philips Ingenia scanners (CHCO) as part of routine clinical care. If multiple CMRs were performed during the study period, the first CMR which coincided with cardiac catheterization, when possible, was analyzed.

Image analysis was performed off-line using CVI42 (Circle Cardiovascular Imaging, Calgary, Alberta, Canada) as detailed in the Supplemental Methods. RA volumetry was measured in the standard 4-chamber view using the single plane, area-length method ([8*area2]/[3*length*π]), including the atrial appendage and measuring the longitudinal diameter from the roof of the RA to the midpoint of the line between tricuspid valve annular hinge points (Graphical Abstract) [13], [24], [28], [29], [30], [31]. RA volumes were measured throughout the cardiac cycle using the long axis (LAX) RA extent feature in the Function Biplanar LAX module, which semi-automatically generates the RA endocardial contour after identification of the tricuspid valve annular hinge points and the longitudinal diameter of the RA with manual correction as needed. In cvi42, there is no vectorcardiogram signal to detect the pre-atrial (preA) contraction phase; thus, RA curves were visually assessed to identify the maximum (max RAVi), minimal (min RAVi), and preA RA volumes (preA RAVi), indexed to BSA based on the DuBois formula (Graphical Abstract). Reservoir phasic function was measured as total ejection fraction (EF) and indexed SV (SVi), conduit phasic function as conduit EF (cEF) and SVi, and active phasic function as active EF (aEF) and SVi as below [13], [14], [16], [19]. A novel marker, the A/C ratio, was calculated as the ratio of active and conduit SVi. RA measurements were performed by KM, who was blinded to clinical history during image analysis. A random number generator was used to select 10 PH or control subjects and measurements were repeated after a minimum of 2 weeks to obtain intra- and inter-rater reliability (KM, HT).

2.4. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics are presented as n (%) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), as appropriate. Group differences between PAH and control patients for categorical and continuous data were tested using the Chi-square test and the Mann-Whitney U test, respectively. Intra- and inter-rater reliability of atrial volumetry was calculated as intra-class correlation coefficients (ICC) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) using a two-way random effects model (for inter-rater reliability) and two-way mixed effects model (for intra-rater reliability) with absolute agreement. The ICC was interpreted as good vs. excellent reliability for values of 0.75–0.9 vs. >0.9, respectively.

We correlated known and novel RA metrics, including max and min RAVi, total and phasic EF and SVi, and the A/C ratio [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [19] with clinical, biomarker, invasive hemodynamic, echocardiographic, and CMR metrics of PAH severity using Spearman’s rank correlation analyses. The RA metrics, which were statistically significantly correlated with multiple metrics of PAH severity were further analyzed. To visualize the relationship between these RA metrics and select PAH severity metrics (exercise capacity, afterload, and RV size and function), scatterplots were created with superimposed, locally weighted smoothing curves and 95% confidence intervals. Patients who experienced an AE during follow-up were marked on the plots. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were performed to identify cut-offs of RA metrics that maximized the area under the curve (AUC) to predict mean right atrial pressure (mRAP) >10 mmHg (the pediatric PAH risk stratification cut-off to identify children at higher risk of adverse events) [32].

PAH patients were followed from their date of CMR until an AE, censoring event (last clinic visit or status update from PH team in medical chart), or end of study period (December 31, 2023). ROC analyses identified RA metric cut-offs which maximized the AUC to predict AEs. Differences in Kaplan-Meier survival curves, stratified by ROC cut-offs, were tested via the log-rank test. The associations between clinical, hemodynamic, and CMR metrics with AEs were evaluated using Cox proportional-hazards regression analyses and interpreted based on their point estimate [95% CI] and concordance statistic. To determine if RA metrics remained independent predictors of AEs, statistically significant clinical, hemodynamic, and CMR predictors with a C-statistic >0.75 on univariate analyses were incorporated into a bivariate model (RA metric + metric of PAH severity). To avoid overfitting the model, further multivariate modeling was not pursued. Analyses were performed using STATA 15.1, SAS 9.2, and JMP Pro 17. P-values <0.05 or 95% CI which did not include unity were considered statistically significant.

3. Results

The final cohort included 72 pediatric PAH patients (41 females, 66.1%; median age at CMR 13.2 years [IQR 10.4, 16.9]) and 36 control patients with similar age, sex, and size (Table 1). Etiologies of PAH included idiopathic PAH (n = 25, 34.7%), PAH associated with CHD (PAH-CHD; n = 20, 27.8%), hereditary PAH (n = 23, 31.9%), and other types of PAH (n = 4, 5.6%). The latter included PAH associated with hepatic arteriovenous malformations (n = 2), schistosomiasis (n = 1), and connective tissue disease (n = 1). Patients with PAH-CHD included simple intra- or extra-cardiac shunts (n = 17) and repaired biventricular, complex CHD without residual shunting (n = 3). Of the simple shunts, six patients had residual shunting due to a patent ductus arteriosus (n = 2) or congenital ASD (n = 4; Qp:Qs <1.5 by CMR). 3.1. RA size and function

RA measurements were feasible in all PAH and control patients and are reported in Table 2. There were no arrhythmias noted during image acquisition. RA volumes throughout the cardiac cycle were higher in PAH patients, compared to controls. Reservoir phasic function was generally preserved with slightly lower total RA EF but ∼27% higher total RA SVi in PAH patients, compared to controls. Conduit phasic function was significantly reduced in PAH patients, compared to controls, as measured by RA cEF (17.8% [IQR 11.3, 25.7] vs. 34.1% [IQR 28.2, 37.9], p<0.0001). Conversely, there was increased active phasic function in PAH patients, compared to controls, with >2× increase in RA active SVi (16.6 mL/m2 [IQR 10.8, 24.4] vs. 7.7 mL/m2 [IQR 5.4, 9.6], p<0.0001) and the A/C ratio (1.62 [IQR 0.92, 3.08] vs. 0.60 [0.51, 0.75], p<0.0001). 4. Discussion

Diastolic dysfunction is an important consequence of the adverse RV response to chronically high afterload and is associated with worse outcomes in PAH [2], [3], [4], [6], [7], [8], [9]. Invasive pressure-volume analysis is the gold standard for measurement of diastolic stiffness in vivo, [6], [7] but changes in RA mechanics are useful non-invasive metrics of RV diastolic dysfunction [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]. In the first CMR study evaluating the RA in pediatric PAH, we demonstrate that assessment of RA size and phasic function is feasible and highly reproducible using standard CMR sequences. RA size and phasic function are abnormal in pediatric PAH patients, characterized by dilation throughout the cardiac cycle, impaired conduit phasic function (decreased RA cEF), and compensatory augmentation of active phasic function (increased A/C ratio and active SVi) to maintain RV filling (Graphical Abstract). These adverse changes correlated with PAH severity, as measured by a patient’s functional class, six-minute walk distance, NT-proBNP, and RV size, function, filling pressures, and afterload. Lastly, metrics of RA phasic function (RA cEF and A/C ratio) remained independent predictors of adverse events after adjustment for these markers of PAH severity on bivariate analysis.

In our study, RA cEF was decreased in pediatric PAH patients, compared to controls, and correlated with multiple metrics of PAH severity. These findings are consistent with prior pediatric and adult echocardiography as well as adult CMR PAH studies [12], [13], [14]. While our study was cross-sectional in nature, we observed that lower RA cEF was associated with minimal changes in exercise capacity, RV size, and RV function until RA cEF reached ∼15–20% and that very few children (n = 2) experienced an AE with a RA cEF >25%. Prior studies have demonstrated that an interval decline in conduit phasic function over 1 year of follow-up was associated with worse outcomes in pediatric and adult PAH populations, suggesting this parameter may be useful as part of the long-term follow-up of PAH patients [21], [33].

Our data demonstrated that ventricular filling becomes increasingly reliant on augmented RA active phasic function allowing for generally preserved reservoir phasic function in pediatric PAH [12], [14], [16], [18], [19], [22]. We introduced a novel metric of RA physiology, the A/C ratio, which represents the transition from normal passive RV filling (conduit phase predominance) to increased RV filling with atrial contraction (active phase predominance). The mathematical ratio of both phases may explain the stronger associations with PAH severity and outcomes than active SVi and RA aEF alone. While active SVi has not been reported in prior adult or pediatric PAH studies, a recent adult PAH CMR study reported a significantly lower RA aEF than measured in our pediatric PAH cohort (23 +/- 12% vs. 35.7% [IQR 30.5, 43.9]) despite similar measurements in the respective control cohorts [13]. Further, they observed a deterioration in RA aEF in those with decompensated RV failure (RA aEF 15 +/- 12%). Conversely, we did not observe a deterioration, but rather a steady rise, in RA active phasic function with increasing PAH severity, suggesting that RA active phasic function may be preserved in children. Similarly, in our study, RA reservoir phasic function (total RA EF) was only slightly reduced in PAH patients compared to controls and, similar to Jone et al., was neither associated with PAH severity nor a predictor of adverse outcomes [20]. Conversely, total RA EF is significantly reduced and strongly predictive of adverse outcomes in adult PH patients [13], [14], [18], [29].

Lastly, we found that children with PAH have significantly dilated RA volumes throughout the cardiac cycle. However, in contrast to adult studies and the 2022 ESC/ERS risk stratification tool which incorporates maximum RA area measured at ventricular end-systole [18], [24], [34], we found that RA dilation at ventricular end-diastole (ie, min RAVi) correlated with pediatric PAH severity and outcomes, while max RAVi did not. Our data support several prior studies suggesting that minimum RA size (area or volume) may be a better marker of RA dysfunction than maximum RA size in pediatric PAH [15], [20], [35]. Interestingly, we similarly found that min RAVi in children with PAH is only approximately twice the size of controls [20], while min RAVi is relatively higher in adults with PAH (∼3–4× healthy adults) by echocardiography and CMR [13], [19]. This may be related to the observed preservation of active phasic function in children with PAH, compared to prior adult studies [13].

Unfortunately, while we attempted RA strain analyses on the single-plane 4-chamber cine SSFP sequence, we found poor inter- and intra-rater reproducibility using the commercially available RV strain package in cvi42 (data not presented). We suspect this was likely multifactorial, possibly due to combination of the thin diameter of the RA wall (∼3 mm) and poor spatial and temporal resolution in younger children and adolescents who are free breathing or poor breath holders. Our experience thus may reflect practical limitations between pediatric and adult CMR. However, prior work has demonstrated that RA strain assessment by echocardiography is clinically useful in pediatric PAH; thus, further efforts to optimize RA strain analyses in pediatric CMR are warranted [20].

Overall, we observed RA dilation, reduced conduit phasic function, and compensatory augmentation of active phasic function in children with PAH correlated with their PAH severity. Based on our data and understanding of RV adaptation to chronically increased afterload [3], we hypothesize that changes in RA conduit phasic function may represent an early marker of RV adaptation prior to clinically appreciable changes in conventional metrics, including reduced exercise capacity or significant RV dilation and systolic dysfunction. Surprisingly, there were weaker associations with available metrics of RV diastolic dysfunction (ie, mRAP). This illustrates the complex relationship between systemic venous capacitance, the right heart, and increased pulmonary vascular resistance. Future studies longitudinally assessing the RA throughout RV adaptation and maladaptation in PH are warranted to further our understanding of the RA-RV-PA axis [2].

5. Limitations

The strengths of this cohort include cohort size, multi-center study design, and use of standard CMR cine sequences. However, there are several limitations. First, given the retrospective nature of this study across two institutions and multiple magnets spanning a decade, there were likely several inherent differences in protocol variability, which could not be accounted for. While we identified control cases of pectus excavatum with normal biventricular size and function, no evidence of cardiac compression, and a pectus severity near or below the conservative indication for repair (Haller index 3.25), these cases may have increased RV diastolic dysfunction and impaired RA mechanics compared to a healthy child.[36], [37] However, this would bias our results to the null and, thus, strengthen our findings that pediatric PAH patients have significant RA dilation and impaired phasic function compared to a healthy control cohort.

We used the single-plane, area-length method to calculate RA volumes based on recent recommendations, methodology from prior PAH studies, and the ability to use readily available sequences [13], [24], [28], [29], [30], [31]. While the underestimation of RA volume compared to a trans-axial or short-axis stack may have affected our findings, accuracy must be balanced with feasibility [38]. Few centers regularly obtain full trans-axial or short-axis cine imaging through the atria as part of standard clinical practice. Only six of our PAH subjects had either a full trans-axial or short-axis cine stack available for analysis; thus, comparing RA volume between methods was not feasible.

Visual identification of the pre-atrial contraction phase was difficult in children with PAH and reduced conduit phasic function [18]. Using semi-automatic RA contour tools in cvi42, we measured RA volumes throughout the cardiac cycle, exported the RA area and length data into Microsoft Excel, automatically generated a RA volume graph, identified the preA phase on the generated graph, and easily calculated all RA metrics. While this semi-automatic, formulaic methodology was reproducible and efficient (<10 min in total), greater generalizability could be achieved with further automation as part of an atrial function module within the CMR analysis software.

Lastly, our modest sample size and event rate limited our ability to develop multivariable models. Patients who remained listed and waiting for a lung transplant at the end of follow-up were included in our outcomes analyses given the long wait times (>1–2 years) experienced at our centers during this study period. While it was not possible to use the listing date for all patients (date of listing was not available for one and differed from the date of transplant by <100 days in the other), we do not believe this significantly impacted our analyses. This study was not designed to identify cut-offs for risk stratification. Future prospective research with larger sample sizes and a validation cohort are necessary to understand if RA assessment can impact pediatric PAH management.

6. Conclusion

We present the first multi-center CMR study evaluating the RA in children with PAH and demonstrate that assessment of RA size and phasic function using CMR is feasible and highly reproducible. Correlating with disease severity, impaired RA physiology in pediatric PAH is characterized by RA dilation, reduced conduit phasic function, and compensatory augmentation of active phasic function.

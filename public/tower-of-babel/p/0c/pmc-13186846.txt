Multi-omics analysis and experimental validation reveal the IRF7-CXCL10 axis as a master regulator of microglial PCD in ischemic stroke
Authors: Yongxing Lai, Peiqiang Lin, Kexin Zhang, Wenyao Hong, Mouwei Zheng, Lijuan Wu, Tin Chen, Fan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMicroglia-driven neuroinflammation serves as a critical factor in secondary injury following ischemic stroke, yet the primary regulators governing detrimental microglial phenotypes remain unclear. As a key component of this process, the cell type-specific regulatory mechanisms of programmed cell death (PCD) are poorly understood.MethodsWe performed an integrative analysis of public single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets from a murine stroke model. A multi-layered computational pipeline, incorporating pseudotime trajectory, weighted co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and gene regulatory network inference (SCENIC), was used to identify master regulators of PCD. Functional validation was conducted using in vitro oxygen–glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R) on primary microglia-neuron co-cultures and in vivo via a transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO) model, employing AAV-mediated microglia-specific gene silencing, comprehensive in vitro and in vivo rescue strategies, and detailed behavioral assessments.ResultsOur single-cell analysis identified microglia as the central hub of PCD activity post-stroke. An unbiased, multi-layered computational pipeline converged upon Interferon Regulatory Factor 7 (IRF7) as the master transcriptional regulator of this high-PCD, pathological microglial state. We confirmed IRF7 upregulation in microglia following ischemic injury and delineated a novel downstream pathway where IRF7 directly binds the CXCL10 promoter to drive its expression, promoting microglial dysfunction and neurotoxicity. In vitro, silencing IRF7 skewed microglia toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype and protected co-cultured neurons from apoptosis. Critically, microglia-specific IRF7 knockdown in vivo significantly reduced infarct volume, suppressed neuronal death, and led to significant improvements in long-term neurological and cognitive function after stroke. Crucially, both in vitro genetic overexpression of CXCL10 and in vivo administration of recombinant CXCL10 completely abolished the neuroprotective benefits of IRF7 inhibition, establishing a definitive functional causality for the IRF7-CXCL10 axis.ConclusionOur findings uncover the IRF7-CXCL10 axis as a pivotal driver of detrimental neuroinflammation in ischemic stroke and establish IRF7 as a potent therapeutic target for neuroprotection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10565-026-10177-0.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke represents a leading cause of death and long-term disability worldwide, leading to a substantial socioeconomic burden (Collaborators 2021; Campbell et al. 2019). The restoration of blood flow through reperfusion therapies remains the most effective acute treatment; however, this process would trigger a secondary cascade of cellular injury known as ischemia–reperfusion (I/R) injury (Eltzschig and Eckle 2011). A central feature of I/R injury is a strong neuroinflammatory response, which, although initially protective, often becomes dysregulated and contributes to neuronal death, blood–brain barrier disruption, and edema formation, ultimately affecting functional outcomes (Iadecola and Anrather 2011; Qin et al. 2022; Candelario-Jalil et al. 2022).

Post-stroke neuroinflammation involves interactions among resident brain cells, including microglia, astrocytes, and neurons, and infiltrating peripheral immune cells. Microglia, the brain's resident immune cells, are primary responders to ischemic insults (Vay et al. 2018; Iadecola et al. 2020). Upon activation, they undergo morphological and functional changes, adopting phenotypes that can be either neurotoxic or neuroprotective (Williams et al. 2001; Prinz et al. 2021). A critical effector mechanism underlying these divergent responses is programmed cell death (PCD). While apoptosis has traditionally been viewed as the principal mode of cell death in stroke, evidence indicates that other PCD pathways, including necroptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis, are also activated and contribute to the pathophysiology of ischemic brain injury (Galluzzi et al. 2018; Sekerdag et al. 2018; Tuo et al. 2022). However, the activity and interplay of these diverse PCD modalities across different cell types within the ischemic brain remain poorly understood. The heterogeneity of cellular responses presents a major challenge to understanding the core mechanisms of I/R injury and developing targeted therapies.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has allowed researchers to examine this cellular and molecular heterogeneity at high resolution (Zeng et al. 2023; Jordão et al. 2019). By enabling the transcriptomic profiling of individual cells, scRNA-seq allows for the unbiased identification of distinct cell populations and their state transitions in response to pathological insults like stroke (Garcia-Bonilla et al. 2024). This approach is well-suited to determining the specific contributions of different cell types to the overall PCD landscape and identifying the transcriptional programs that govern these processes. The regulation of cellular responses, such as inflammation and cell death, is controlled by networks of transcription factors (TFs (Lambert et al. 2018). Identifying the key TFs that drive detrimental cellular phenotypes in the post-stroke brain could therefore reveal novel therapeutic targets.

While previous analyses of post-stroke transcriptomic data have highlighted a general activation of the Type-I Interferon (IFN) response in microglia (Ruan et al. 2023), the specific upstream regulators that link this IFN signature to PCD pathways remain poorly defined. Furthermore, the in vivo and in vitro function of these regulators in microglia has not been systematically investigated. Interferon Regulatory Factor 7 (IRF7), a well-known master transcription factor of the type I (IFN) response (Honda and Taniguchi 2006), is a primary candidate for such a regulator. However, its specific function in regulating microglial activation and neuronal survival following ischemic stroke, and whether it acts as the critical node linking ischemic signals to PCD, remain largely unknown.

In this study, we employ a multi-omics approach to create a single-cell resolution map of PCD activity in the murine brain following ischemic stroke. By combining scRNA-seq and bulk transcriptomic, we aimed to identify the cell types most vulnerable to PCD and the transcriptional regulators driving this process. Our unbiased analysis converged on IRF7 as a key candidate regulator of PCD in microglia. Through in vitro and in vivo experiments, including microglia-specific gene silencing, we systematically validated the role of IRF7 in regulating microglial pro-inflammatory activation, neuronal apoptosis, and neurological deficits post-stroke. Furthermore, we characterized a downstream mechanism involving the transcriptional regulation of the chemokine CXCL10. Our findings establish a critical IRF7-CXCL10 signaling axis in the pathophysiology of ischemic brain injury and highlight IRF7 as a promising therapeutic target for mitigating neuroinflammation and improving stroke outcomes.

Methods

Public data acquisition and curation

Publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database under accession number GSE245386. This dataset comprised brain tissue samples from C57BL/6 mice (8‒10 weeks) subjected to 60 min of transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO) followed by 24 h of reperfusion (n = 2), or sham operation (n = 3). Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data were also obtained from GEO (accession: GSE137482; platform: GPL19057), which included brain tissues from 6 MCAO and 6 sham-operated young mice (3 months). The gene sets defining 18 distinct programmed cell death (PCD) modalities were curated based on a previously published study in lung adenocarcinoma (Zhang et al. 2024).

scRNA-seq

The scRNA-seq dataset was processed and analyzed using the Seurat R package (Hao et al. 2021). During quality filtering, only cells expressing between 200 and 6,000 genes and exhibiting less than 10% mitochondrial gene transcripts were included in subsequent analyses. Normalization was carried out via the NormalizeData function to account for sequencing depth variation across cells. To address potential technical variability arising from different sample batches, the Harmony algorithm was applied to integrate datasets and remove batch-specific biases (Korsunsky et al. 2019). Unsupervised graph-based clustering was conducted using the FindClusters function with a resolution setting of 0.3. Dimensionality reduction and visualization were performed through Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), constructed from the top 20 principal components derived from highly variable genes. We annotated cell types manually using established lineage-specific marker genes. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) for each cluster were identified with the FindAllMarkers function using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Programmed cell death scoring at single-cell level

To quantify the activity of PCD pathways at a single-cell level, we used the irGSEA R package. Gene set enrichment scores were calculated with five distinct algorithms: AUCell, UCell, singscore, Sargent, and AddModuleScore. The final programmed cell death score (PCDS) for each cell was determined by averaging the normalized scores from these methods.

Pseudotime trajectory and differentiation potential analysis

To infer the developmental continuum of microglia, we constructed a pseudotime trajectory using the monocle R package (Qiu et al. 2017). We extracted the microglial subset and used highly variable genes identified via the VariableFeatures function in Seurat, for trajectory inference. Dimensionality reduction was performed using the DDRTree method. Cells were ordered along the inferred trajectory, and the root was defined based on the biological context, typically corresponding to the state most enriched with control cells. Genes with expression profiles that changed significantly as a function of pseudotime were identified using the differentialGeneTest function, a q-value less than 0.01 considered significant. Cellular differentiation potential was independently assessed using the CytoTRACE R package (Gulati et al. 2020), which correlates transcriptomic diversity with developmental potential. The resulting CytoTRACE scores were mapped onto the UMAP embedding to visualize microglial plasticity.

Bulk RNA-seq data analysis

The raw count matrix from the GSE137482 dataset was preprocessed and analyzed for differential expression. We filtered out genes with low expression across all samples. The data were log2-transformed and normalized using the normalizeBetweenArrays function in the limma R package (Ritchie et al. 2015). A design matrix was created to compare the MCAO and sham groups. Differential expression was assessed by fitting a linear model to each gene using the lmFit function, followed by empirical Bayes moderation of standard errors with the eBayes function. Genes were considered as significantly expressed if they had an absolute log2 fold change greater than 1 and an adjusted p-value below 0.05.

Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA)

To identify functionally coordinated gene clusters from the bulk RNA-seq dataset (GSE137482), we performed WGCNA using the corresponding R package (Langfelder and Horvath 2008). The initial expression matrix was filtered to retain the top 25% of genes showing the greatest expression variance across samples. A soft-thresholding power (β) was determined via the pickSoftThreshold function to establish a scale-free network structure, targeting a model fit (R2) above 0.85. This parameter was used to construct an adjacency matrix, which was converted into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to account for shared connectivity. The dissimilarity measure (1 − TOM) was used for hierarchical clustering, and gene modules were identified using the dynamic tree-cutting algorithm with a minimum size of 100 genes. Modules with highly similar eigengene profiles were merged based on a predefined correlation threshold. The module eigengene (ME), representing the first principal component of each module’s expression matrix, was correlated with GSVA enrichment scores for 18 cell death pathways to assess module-trait associations. To prioritize biologically relevant genes, we calculated Gene Significance (GS), which reflects the absolute correlation between gene expression and the trait, and Module Membership (MM), which indicates intramodular connectivity through correlation with the ME. Hub genes, defined as putative central regulators within the network, were identified as high intramodular connectivity (MM > 0.8) and strong trait association (GS > 0.6).

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis was performed to determine the biological roles of the selected gene sets. Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways were analyzed using the enrichGO and enrichKEGG functions in the clusterProfiler R package (Yu et al. 2012). Significance was determined using the Benjamini–Hochberg method with an adjusted p-value threshold below 0.05. Enriched terms were filtered to exclude redundant categories.

Cell–cell communication analysis

To analyze intercellular signaling, we applied the CellChat R package (Jin et al. 2021) to the annotated Seurat. This tool uses a database of ligand-receptor interactions to estimate cell-to-cell communication. By incorporating expression levels of signaling molecules and their receptors, CellChat infers potential interaction networks across cell populations. The strength and number of interactions were visualized using circle plots (netVisual_circle). We analyzed outgoing and incoming signaling patterns with bubble plots to compare communication networks between low-PCD versus high-PCD microglia.

High-dimensional WGCNA (hdWGCNA)

To construct cell-type-specific co-expression networks from the single-cell data, we applied the hdWGCNA R package to the microglial cell subset (Morabito et al. 2021). We set up a Seurat object and created metacells by aggregating expression profiles of transcriptionally similar cells using the MetacellsByGroups function to reduce noise and sparsity. A soft-thresholding power was determined to achieve a scale-free network topology. The co-expression network was built using the ConstructNetwork function. Hierarchical clustering and the dynamic tree-cutting algorithm were used to identify distinct gene modules. Module eigengenes were calculated and correlated with the PCDS of each metacell to find modules associated with the high-PCD state in microglia.

Gene regulatory network inference (SCENIC)

We used the SCENIC pipeline to reconstruct gene regulatory networks and identify key transcription factors (TFs) (Aibar et al. 2017). Initially, potential TF-target gene co-expression modules were identified from the single-cell expression matrix using the GRNBoost2 algorithm. Next, these modules were refined into regulons through motif-based pruning. We scanned the cis-regulatory regions of all genes for enriched TF-binding motifs using the cisTarget database, retaining only target genes with motif support for the corresponding TF. Finally, regulon activity within each cell was quantified using the AUCell algorithm, which calculates the enrichment of a regulon's target genes at the top of a cell's ranked gene expression profile. This generated a Regulon Activity Score (RAS) matrix for downstream analyses, including hierarchical clustering of TF modules and visualization on UMAP plots.

Animals and cerebral ischemia–reperfusion model

Male C57BL/6 J mice (8–10 weeks, 22–25 g) were used in this study. Transient focal ischemia was induced via tMCAO using the intraluminal suture technique for 90 min, followed by reperfusion (Longa et al. 1989). Infarct volumes were assessed using 2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining and corrected for edema. Complete surgical protocols and animal care details are available in the Supplementary Materials.

Stereotactic AAV and ICV injections

For microglia-specific silencing of Irf7 IRF7, an adeno-associated viral vector (AAV2/9-Iba1-miR30-sh-Irf7) was stereotaxically injected into the right cerebral cortex three weeks prior to tMCAO. For rescue experiments, recombinant CXCL10 (rCXCL10) was delivered via intracerebroventricular (ICV) injection one hour prior to tMCAO induction. Specific injection coordinates and dosages are detailed in the Supplementary Materials.

Behavioral evaluations

Neurological and cognitive outcomes were assessed using the Cylinder test, Rotarod test, Foot fault test, and Morris Water Maze test. All evaluations were conducted by an experimenter blinded to the treatment allocation. Specific trial parameters and scoring criteria are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

Primary cell culture and OGD/R model

Primary mixed glial cultures and cortical neurons were isolated from postnatal day 1 (P1) C57BL/6 J pups. Microglia were subsequently purified and, where applicable, co-cultured with neurons using a transwell system. To simulate ischemia–reperfusion injury in vitro, primary microglia were subjected to oxygen–glucose deprivation (OGD) for 2 h, followed by reoxygenation (OGD/R). Detailed culture media compositions, lentiviral transduction procedures for Irf7 and Cxcl10 modulation, and OGD/R conditions are described in the Supplementary Materials.

Molecular, biochemical, and histological assays

Cell viability and cytotoxicity were evaluated using CCK-8 and LDH release assays. Protein and mRNA expression levels were quantified via Western blotting and quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR), respectively. Immunofluorescence staining and TUNEL assays were performed on both brain cryosections and cultured cells to assess protein localization and apoptosis. Secreted cytokine levels and intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) were measured using specific ELISA kits and DCFH-DA flow cytometry, respectively. The direct transcriptional regulation of Cxcl10 by Irf7 was validated using a dual-luciferase reporter assay in BV2 cells. Comprehensive lists of antibodies, primer sequences, and specific assay protocols are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± Standard Deviation (SD) and were derived from a minimum of three biologically independent replicates. Prior to statistical analysis, the normality of data distributions was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For datasets conforming to a normal distribution, comparisons between two experimental groups were conducted using an unpaired, two-tailed Student’s t-test. When data deviated from normality, the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test was applied. For comparisons involving more than two groups, either one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed, depending on the experimental design, followed by appropriate post hoc corrections (Tukey’s or Sidak's test) for multiple comparisons. The choice of statistical method for each analysis was detailed in the corresponding figure legends. All analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value < 0.05.

Results

Single-cell transcriptomic atlas reveals heightened programmed cell death activity in microglia following cerebral ischemia

To delineate the cellular landscape of programmed cell death (PCD) activity in the context of ischemic stroke, we initiated our investigation by analyzing a publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset derived from the brain tissues of mice subjected to either transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO) or a sham operation (GEO: GSE245386). After applying rigorous quality control and filtering protocols, we obtained a high-quality dataset comprising 54,980 cells, with 33,592 from the sham cohort and 21,298 from the tMCAO cohort. Unsupervised graph-based clustering of these cells yielded 17 distinct cellular clusters (Fig. 1A). These clusters were subsequently annotated into ten major cell types based on the expression of canonical marker genes. To quantitatively assess PCD activity at single-cell resolution, we developed a composite programmed cell death score (PCDS). This score was derived by averaging the outputs from five established gene set scoring algorithms (AUCell, UCell, singscore, Sargent, and AddModuleScore). Mapping this PCDS onto the UMAP projection and quantifying it across cell types indicated that microglia displayed the highest PCDS, followed by macrophages (Fig. 1D, E). We then examined the difference in PCDS distribution between the sham and tMCAO groups. While PCDS levels in neutrophils and ependymal cells appeared unaltered post-ischemia, the remaining cell types showed an increase in the tMCAO group. This increase was most pronounced in microglia (Fig. 1F). These analyses suggest that while PCD is a widespread cellular response to ischemic injury, microglia may be a key population where this process is highly active, potentially playing a central role in the pathophysiology of stroke.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis elucidates the dynamic evolution of PCD states in microglia

To investigate the temporal dynamics of PCD within the microglial population during stroke progression, we analyzed microglia from the post-ischemic tissue. We stratified these cells into three distinct groups based on the quartile distribution of their PCDS: high-PCDS cells (n = 2045; score > 75th percentile), low-PCDS cells (n = 2045; score < 25th percentile), and mid-PCDS cells (n = 4090). Using the Monocle2 algorithm for pseudotime trajectory inference, we constructed a developmental trajectory for microglia, which resolved into five distinct cell states. State 4 was identified as the origin of the trajectory, suggesting it represents an early stage in the microglial response, while state 1 appeared to be a terminal state (Fig. 2A-C). Low- and high-PCDS microglia were predominantly enriched at the beginning (State 4) and the end (State 1) of the trajectory, respectively, reflecting early and late phases of the disease process. This inferred developmental progression was supported by CytoTRACE analysis, which estimates cellular differentiation potential. The CytoTRACE results aligned with the Monocle 2 trajectory (Fig. 2D, E), showing a progressive decrease in the score along the pseudotime axis, indicating increasing cellular differentiation or commitment to a specific fate (Fig. 2F). Next, we examined the temporal expression profiles of 312 differentially expressed genes identified along this trajectory, revealing three distinct gene expression patterns. Most genes fell into Pattern 1, characterized by a gradual increase in expression along the pseudotime, suggesting their involvement in the later stages of microglial activation post-stroke. Conversely, genes in Pattern 3, including key regulators of lipid metabolism and purinergic signaling such as Fads2, P2rx7, Pla2g7, and Igf1r, showed decreasing expression, implying a limited role in the PCD-associated activation of microglia in later phases (Fig. 2G). Functional enrichment analysis of genes significantly correlated with the pseudotime trajectory highlighted pathways such as glycolysis, hypoxia, inflammatory response, IFNγ signaling, p53 pathway, and oxidative stress. Integrative analysis of bulk transcriptomics reveals core gene modules associated with multiple cell death modalities in ischemic stroke

To explore the cell death pathways activated during cerebral ischemia, we used bulk RNA-sequencing data from ischemic mouse brains (GSE137482). Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) was performed to calculate enrichment scores for eighteen distinct cell death modalities. We observed significant positive correlations among several of these pathways; for instance, apoptosis showed strong co-regulation with necroptosis and pyroptosis, suggesting a potential crosstalk between these canonical death programs (Fig. 3A). Comparison between ischemic and control tissues revealed that the majority of cell death pathways, including apoptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, entotic cell death, and NETosis, were significantly activated following ischemic injury. To identify a core regulatory network underlying these activated pathways, we subjected the genes from all eighteen cell death modalities to Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA). A soft-thresholding power (β) of 13 was selected to construct a scale-free co-expression network (Fig. 3C). Hierarchical clustering of the resulting topological overlap matrix, with a minimum module size of 100 genes, led to the identification of four distinct gene modules (Fig. 3D). By correlating the module eigengenes (MEs) with the activity scores of the various cell death pathways, we found that the brown module exhibited a strong positive correlation with the majority (14 out of 18) of the examined cell death pathways, with the exception of cuproptosis, parthanatos, oxeiptosis, and netotic cell death (Fig. 3E). To isolate the most critical genes within this module, we applied stringent filtering criteria (Module Membership > 0.8 and Gene Significance > 0.6) and intersected the results, yielding 312 core genes robustly associated with pan-cell death activity (Fig. 3F). GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses of these core genes showed enrichment in functions such as regulation of inflammatory response, leukocyte mediated immunity, and cytokine production (Fig. 3G), and signaling pathways including TNF signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway (Fig. 3H). These results indicate a centrally coordinated transcriptional program, linked to neuroinflammation, that drive multiple forms of cell death in the ischemic brain.

High-PCD microglia exhibit enhanced intercellular communication and reshape the neuroinflammatory milieu

To elucidate how microglia with heightened PCD activity might influence other cell types within the ischemic brain, we performed intercellular communication analysis using CellChat. We conducted a comparative analysis of the interaction networks involving low-PCD microglia (low_PCD_Mic) versus high-PCD microglia (high_PCD_Mic). This examination revealed a marked increase in the number and strength of predicted ligand-receptor interactions when high_PCD_Mic were considered as either the signal source or receiver. This enhanced communication was observed with nearly all other major cell types, including smooth muscle cells, oligodendrocytes, neutrophils, neurons, macrophages, endothelial cells, and astrocytes (Fig. 4A-F). Given the significantly higher frequency of cellular crosstalk associated with high-PCD microglia, we focused on dissecting their specific signaling patterns. When high_PCD_Mic acted as the signaling source, they appeared to communicate with other cells predominantly through chemokine signaling pathways, such as the CCL (Ccl2-Ccr2, Ccl3-Ccr5, Ccl4-Ccr5, Ccl6-Ccr1) and CXCL (Cxcl2-Cxcr2) families. Other prominent outgoing signaling pathways included SPP1 (Spp1-(Itgav + Itgb5)), MIF (Mif-(Cd74 + Cxcr4)), TNF (Tnf-Tnfrsf1b), FN1 (Fn1-Sdc4), and PROS (Pros1-Mertk) (Fig. 4G). Conversely, when acting as the signal receiver, high_PCD_Mic were predicted to be targeted by many of the same pathways, such as SPP1, FN1, and TNF. Additionally, they appeared to receive unique inputs via the LAMININ (Lamc1-Dag1), ADGRG (Lama2-(Itga6 + Itgb1)), and ICAM (Icam1-(Itgam + Itgb2)) signaling pathways (Fig. 4H). High-dimensional WGCNA identifies co-expression modules and hub genes in high-PCD microglia

To identify co-expression modules and key regulatory genes within high-PCD microglia, we applied high-dimensional Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (hdWGCNA). Using a soft-thresholding power of 3, we constructed the co-expression network that identified four distinct gene modules (Fig. 5A, B). Examining their expression scores across cell populations showed that the blue and brown modules were highly expressed in the high-PCD microglial subpopulation, indicating a direct relevance to the PCD-active state (Fig. 5C, D). Correlation analysis of the module eigengenes revealed strong positive correlations between the yellow and blue modules, and between the brown and turquoise modules, implying potential co-regulation (Fig. 5E). Transcriptional regulatory network analysis identifies IRF7 as a critical regulator of PCD in microglia

To determine the transcriptional regulatory networks governing the PCD-active state in microglia, we performed a differential transcription factor (TF) activity analysis between high-PCD and low-PCD microglia using pySCENIC. This identified four distinct TF modules (Fig. 6A). A UMAP projection of TF module activity showed that the M2 module had the largest difference in activity between high- and low-PCD microglia, suggesting a role in this phenotypic switch (Fig. 6B). Gene enrichment analysis of the regulons within the M2 module showed an overrepresentation of genes involved in apoptosis, the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and DNA metabolic processes. We identified the TFs with the highest activity within each module (Fig. 6C), finding that the M2 module was characterized by high activity of Elk3( +), Irf7( +), and Atf4 (Fig. 6D). To isolate the key TF regulating PCD, we intersected gene sets from the WGCNA brown module, the hdWGCNA blue and brown modules, upregulated DEGs from bulk RNA-seq, DEGs from the pseudotime analysis, and TFs from the cisTarget database. The elevated expression and activity of the Irf7 regulon (Irf7( +)) in high-PCD microglia were confirmed by UMAP and violin plots (Fig. 6F, G). Moreover, microglia from the ischemic brain also showed higher Irf7 regulon activity compared to controls (Fig. 6H). Discussion

In this study, we used a multi-omics approach to analyze PCD in the post-ischemic brain at single-cell resolution. Our analysis identified the transcription factor IRF7 as a key regulator of a pathological microglial state characterized by high PCD activity. We characterized a downstream pathway where IRF7 transcriptionally upregulates the chemokine CXCL10, mediating microglial dysfunction and neurotoxicity. These findings define an IRF7-CXCL10 signaling axis that drives neuroinflammation and secondary brain injury following ischemic stroke, presenting a potential therapeutic target.

The pathological cascade following ischemic stroke involves diverse cell types and cell death mechanisms (DeLong et al. 2022). Single-cell transcriptomics enabled an unbiased analysis of this cellular heterogeneity. Among central nervous system (CNS) cell populations, microglia showed the highest increase in a composite PCDS in the tMCAO model (Fig. 1). This suggests microglia are central to cell fate decisions during the acute post-ischemic phase. As the primary immune cells of the CNS, microglia are rapidly activated following stroke and display a range of functional states. These states have traditionally been simplified into a binary M1/M2 classification (Vidal-Itriago et al. 2022; Nayak et al. 2014; Colonna and Butovsky 2017).

Integrating the PCDS with pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed a continuous transition of microglia from a homeostatic state toward a terminally differentiated, high-PCD pathological state (Fig. 2). The terminus of this trajectory was enriched for pathways associated with inflammation and stress, including interferon-γ (IFNγ) signaling, the p53 pathway, and hypoxia. This aligns with transcriptionally-defined cell subsets such as disease-associated microglia (DAM) or interferon-responsive microglia (IRM). These observations suggest the PCDS is more than a metric of cell death; it functions as a meta-score marking a cellular state that integrates pro-inflammatory signaling with cell death programs. This is supported by our cell communication analysis, which indicated that high-PCDS microglia are not passive, dying cells. Instead, they act as active signaling hubs in the neuroinflammatory network, secreting chemokines CXCL and CCL chemokines to coordinate a broader immune response (Fig. 4). We propose that microglial PCD in the acute stroke environment is not a passive consequence of injury, but an active response coupled to their neuroinflammatory function. This may represent an evolutionary trade-off: an inflame-and-die program that rapidly signals danger while ensuring the clearance of highly activated immune cells to prevent prolonged inflammation (Qin et al. 2022). This is supported by our WGCNA analysis of bulk tissue, which linked a pan-cell death gene module to inflammatory response pathways (Fig. 3), suggesting a common upstream regulatory core between cell death and inflammation.

Identifying the transcription factors that drive cellular state transitions is fundamental to understanding disease mechanisms. Using an integrative bioinformatic pipeline combining scRNA-seq, bulk RNA-seq, pseudotime analysis, hdWGCNA, and SCENIC, we identified IRF7 as the core transcriptional regulator of the high-PCDS microglial state (Fig. 6). The robustness of this unbiased identification provided a solid foundation for our subsequent functional validations. IRF7 is widely recognized as a master regulator of the Type I Interferon (IFN-I) response (Ma et al. 2023). Its activation is a downstream consequence of pattern recognition receptor (PRR) signaling (Irving et al. 2020). Our finding that IRF7, a master regulator of the IFN-I response, is upregulated is consistent with the original analysis of the GSE245386 dataset. However, our study provides significant new mechanistic and functional insights by: (1) using an unbiased, multi-layered computational pipeline to pinpoint IRF7 as the central transcriptional hub of a pathological, high-PCD microglial state; (2) elucidating the novel downstream IRF7-CXCL10 regulatory axis; and (3) providing robust in vivo validation that microglia-specific silencing of IRF7 significantly reduces infarct volume, suppresses neuronal apoptosis, and improves long-term neurological and cognitive function after stroke. In the context of sterile inflammation, such as that occurring in ischemic stroke, the most probable upstream triggers are damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), including self-nucleic acids (mitochondrial and nuclear DNA) released from necrotic cells (Guan et al. 2024; Chong et al. 2022). These DAMPs can be sensed by pathways such as cGAS-STING, which ultimately converge on the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of IRF7, initiating a potent IFN-I positive feedback amplification loop (Gao et al. 2023; Ramalho et al. 2024; Peng et al. 2024). Our findings, therefore, place the IRF7-driven microglial phenotype directly downstream of the initial wave of ischemic cell death and DAMP release.

However, the existing literature presents a complex, and at times seemingly contradictory, picture of IRF7’s role in microglial polarization. Some studies, consistent with our findings, indicate that IRF7 promotes an M1-like pro-inflammatory phenotype (Ling et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2023; Meng et al. 2025). Yet, other work has suggested that under certain conditions, IRF7 might mediate a switch from M1 to M2, thereby mitigating inflammation (Deczkowska et al. 2016). This discrepancy may arise from differences in the nature and intensity of the stimulus. Ischemic stroke involves a massive and acute release of DAMPs, leading to hyperactivation of PRRs. We speculate that this ‘supraphysiological’ level of stimulation may push the IRF7/IFN-I pathway into a state of ‘hyper-activation’, locking microglia into an irreversible, unidirectional pathological program, consistent with the terminal trajectory revealed by our pseudotime analysis (Fig. 2). In contrast, under other, more moderate or chronic stimuli, the same pathway might be engaged to produce a more balanced or even reparative outcome (Tan et al. 2022). Thus, the function of IRF7 may be context-dependent, dictated by the upstream signaling milieu. Our study clarifies its critical pathogenic role in the specific, hyper-acute inflammatory microenvironment of ischemic stroke.

Furthermore, our pseudotime analysis identified ‘glycolysis’ as a key enriched pathway during the transition to the high-PCDS state (Fig. 2H). This is consistent with extensive literature reporting that pro-inflammatory microglia undergo metabolic reprogramming from oxidative phosphorylation to aerobic glycolysis to support rapid ATP generation and the synthesis of inflammatory mediators (Baik et al. 2019; Yu et al. 2023; Orihuela et al. 2016; Qian et al. 2024). Although this link was not directly validated in our study, it is plausible that IRF7 activation not only orchestrates the inflammatory transcriptional program but also concurrently masterminds this fundamental metabolic shift, providing the bioenergetic fuel for the pathological response of microglia.

To elucidate the downstream mechanisms of IRF7, we identified CXCL10 as a key effector molecule through database screening and functional validation. We demonstrated via luciferase reporter assays that IRF7 can directly bind to the CXCL10 gene promoter to drive its transcription (Fig. 12). More importantly, to definitively establish the functional causality of this pathway, we performed rigorous in vitro and in vivo rescue experiments. In vitro, we found that while microglial IRF7 knockdown effectively mitigated OGD/R-induced oxidative stress, the simultaneous overexpression of CXCL10 reinstated the pro-oxidative and pro-inflammatory phenotype (Fig. 12). Furthermore, our in vivo rescue experiments demonstrated that exogenous administration of rCXCL10 completely abolished the neuroprotective effects of microglial IRF7 knockdown, resulting in a resurgence of infarct volume, severe neuronal apoptosis, and profound sensorimotor and cognitive deficits in tMCAO mice (Fig. 13). These results unambiguously establish a direct and functional IRF7-CXCL10 regulatory axis, proving that CXCL10 is the indispensable downstream effector mediating IRF7-driven secondary injury.

CXCL10 is a potent ELR-negative chemokine that acts primarily through its receptor, CXCR3 (Wang et al. 2021), and is implicated in recruiting peripheral immune cells, such as T cells and monocytes, to the CNS in various neuroinflammatory diseases (Blandford et al. 2023; Sorensen et al. 2018; Guldner et al. 2020). Our CellChat analysis also showed the CXCL pathway as a major outgoing signal from high-PCDS microglia, suggesting they may utilize this axis to coordinate a broader inflammatory response (Fig. 4). However, the role of CXCL10 extends beyond chemoattraction. A growing body of evidence indicates that CXCL10 itself can exert direct toxic effects on CNS cells. For instance, it has been shown to directly induce neuronal apoptosis via a mitochondria-dependent, caspase-3-mediated pathway and can trigger pyroptosis in vascular endothelial cells, thereby compromising the integrity of the blood–brain barrier (BBB) (Sheng et al. 2025). Based on our findings and the existing literature, we propose a ‘neuro-glial vicious cycle’ model that drives secondary injury: (a) Ischemia-released DAMPs activate the IRF7 pathway in microglia; (b) IRF7 drives the production and secretion of CXCL10; (c) Secreted CXCL10 acts on adjacent neurons, inducing their apoptosis and pyroptosis, respectively; (d) The death of these cells leads to further BBB disruption and the release of more DAMPs, such as the HMGB1 we observed (Fig. 11); (e) These newly released DAMPs, in turn, activate more microglia, amplifying the IRF7-CXCL10 signal and propagating the injury into the ischemic penumbra. This model explains how a microglia-centric pathway can have profound, non-cell-autonomous effects on neurons and the neurovascular unit. It reframes the IRF7-CXCL10 axis as the engine of a self-perpetuating cycle that drives the cascade of secondary injury. As confirmed by our AAV-knockdown experiments, breaking this cycle by targeting IRF7 effectively contains this runaway neuroinflammation and confers significant neuroprotection (Fig. 10).

While our study utilized a composite PCDS to robustly identify cellular states, we can speculate, based on existing literature, that the IRF7-driven program likely intersects with multiple specific PCD pathways. First, pyroptosis, a pro-inflammatory form of cell death mediated by inflammatory caspases and characterized by the formation of GSDMD pores, is a key process in stroke (Zhang et al. 2022). The NLRP3 inflammasome is a critical driver of post-stroke inflammation, and its activation requires both a priming ‘signal 1’ and an activation ‘signal 2’ (Li et al. 2023). The IFN-I pathway, downstream of IRF7, is considered a key ‘signal 1’ for priming the NLRP3 inflammasome by upregulating its components (Cui et al. 2025). Therefore, IRF7 activation may set the stage for subsequent microglial pyroptosis. Second, necroptosis, a pathway mediated primarily by the RIPK1-RIPK3-MLKL axis, also plays a significant role in stroke pathology (Mitroshina et al. 2022). Intriguingly, knockout of RIPK3 or MLKL has been reported to promote a shift in microglia from a pro-inflammatory M1 to an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype (Yang et al. 2018), a phenocopy of what we observed with IRF7 knockdown. This suggests a complex crosstalk or functional convergence between the IFN-I pathway and necroptotic machinery in determining the inflammatory state of microglia. In addition, ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and regulated by molecules such as ACSL4 and GPX4, is also implicated. Neuroinflammation and iron accumulation in microglia are thought to be mutually reinforcing. The intense inflammatory and oxidative stress environment created by IRF7-activated microglia could create favorable conditions for ferroptosis in adjacent cells and potentially in the microglia themselves. In summary, the IRF7-driven program may not commit microglia to a single death modality but rather creates a cellular state that is ‘highly susceptible’ to multiple forms of PCD. The specific death pathway executed may depend on secondary signals or the particular metabolic context of the cell. This perspective is consistent with our WGCNA results, where a single gene module was associated with multiple distinct cell death pathways (Fig. 3), implying a common upstream regulatory hub, which our study identifies as IRF7.

However, this study also raises several questions that point toward future research directions. First, our intervention was prophylactic, a critical next step is to determine whether post-stroke administration of an IRF7 inhibitor is still effective, which is crucial for clinical translation. Second, this study focused on the acute and subacute phases of stroke pathology. The role of the IRF7-CXCL10 axis in chronic recovery processes, such as glial scar formation and neurogenesis, remains to be explored. Finally, the PCDS is a gene set-based association score that provides strong correlative evidence for identifying cellular states but does not offer direct mechanistic proof of the execution of any specific PCD pathway.

Conclusion

In summary, this study provides a single-cell resolution map of the programmed cell death landscape following ischemic stroke, identifying microglia as central players in this process. Through an unbiased, integrative systems biology approach, we have uncovered a critical role for the transcription factor IRF7 in governing a pathological state in microglia. We delineate a novel downstream signaling axis wherein IRF7 directly drives the expression of the chemokine CXCL10, thereby fueling a vicious cycle of neuroinflammation and neuronal injury. The significant neuroprotection conferred by IRF7 knockdown validates this pathway as a key driver of stroke pathology. Collectively, our findings elucidate the IRF7-CXCL10 axis as a pivotal mechanism in the pathophysiology of ischemic brain injury and establish IRF7 as a promising, high-potential therapeutic target for the development of novel neuroprotective strategies aimed at improving outcomes for stroke patients.

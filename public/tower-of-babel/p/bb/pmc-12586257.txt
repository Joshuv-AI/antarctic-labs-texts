Prognostic impact of extranodal extension in oral cavity cancers: a retrospective analysis and implications for treatment intensification
Authors: Pragyat Thakur, Vedamanasa I, Ankur Dwivedi, Tapas K Dora, Anshul Singla, Sankalp Sancheti, Alok Goel, Ashish Gulia, Arshdeep Kaur
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeExtranodal extension (ENE) and positive surgical margins are recognized high-risk factors in head and neck cancers. Despite their prognostic significance, ENE was incorporated into the AJCC 8th edition staging only recently. This study evaluates survival outcomes in patients with oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OCSCC) based on ENE status and its association with radiological ENE and other intermediate-risk features, including lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PNI), T stage, and depth of infiltration (DoI).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 198 patients with OCSCC treated between 2015 and 2022 with surgery followed by adjuvant radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions), with or without concurrent chemotherapy. Chemotherapy was administered in cases with ENE or margin positivity. Patients were followed at 3-month intervals with clinical evaluations and imaging as indicated. Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to estimate overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). Differences between groups were assessed using the log-rank test, and univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses identified prognostic factors.ResultsThe mean age was 55 ± 12 years, with 86.4% male patients. The buccal mucosa (61.6%) was the most common subsite. After a median follow-up of 36 months, the 3-year OS and DFS rates were 56.0% and 53.1%, respectively. Patients with pathological ENE had significantly worse outcomes: 3-year DFS was 34.7% vs. 68.6% (HR: 0.303; p < 0.001), and OS was 35.3% vs. 74.0% (HR: 0.270; p < 0.001). Radiological node positivity, LVI, and PNI were also independently associated with poorer survival.ConclusionENE significantly worsens OS and DFS in OCSCC patients, even with standard adjuvant chemoradiotherapy. These findings support the need for treatment intensification strategies, such as radiation dose escalation and/or additional systemic therapy, in this high-risk group.Keywords: Cancers of oral cavity, Adjuvant radiotherapy, Extranodal extension (ene)

Introduction

Head and neck cancers represent a major public health burden in the Indian subcontinent, with malignancies of the lip and oral cavity ranking highest in both incidence and mortality (Bray et al. 2024). This epidemiological pattern starkly contrasts with that observed in Western populations. The disproportionately high incidence in South and Southeast Asia is largely attributable to regional practices such as tobacco chewing, betel quid use, and reverse smoking—behaviors that are uncommon in the West but endemic to this region (Rani et al. 2017). These differences underscore the need for region-specific data, prompt identification of high-risk groups, and context-appropriate treatment strategies. Furthermore, they highlight the importance of conducting robust clinical research within high-incidence settings.

Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of treatment for oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OCSCC), with definitive radiotherapy reserved for select early-stage (T1) lesions. In patients with borderline resectable disease, neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgery has shown promising results. Adjuvant radiotherapy is generally indicated in cases of locally advanced disease, particularly for tumors classified as T3 or T4 and those demonstrating lymphovascular invasion (LVI) or perineural invasion (PNI) (Chaturvedi et al. 2024).

Extranodal extension (ENE) has emerged as a critical prognostic factor in head and neck cancers and has been evaluated as an independent predictor of survival in several studies (Murthy et al. 2010; Snow et al. 1982; Shaw et al. 2010). The addition of concurrent chemotherapy to adjuvant radiotherapy in patients with positive surgical margins or ENE has demonstrated improvements in overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) (Bernier and Cooper 2005). However, the OS benefit in ENE-positive patients remains inferior compared to those without ENE, despite intensified treatment.

The inclusion of ENE in the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition TNM staging system for head and neck cancers has led to the upstaging of approximately 30% of oral cavity tumors to stage IVA. This change has raised important clinical questions regarding whether the current standard of adjuvant chemoradiotherapy is adequate for patients with ENE or if further therapeutic intensification is warranted.

In this study, we aimed to evaluate survival outcomes in patients with oral cavity cancer with and without ENE. We also assessed the diagnostic concordance between radiologic and pathologic ENE, examined the prognostic impact of ENE on survival, and explored its correlation with other intermediate-risk features such as depth of invasion (DoI), LVI, and PNI.

Materials and methods

A retrospective cohort study was conducted involving 198 patients with histologically confirmed oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OCSCC), who underwent curative-intent surgery between January 2015 and December 2022 at our tertiary care center. Of these, 85 patients (42.9%) had pathologically confirmed extranodal extension (ENE) and were assigned to Arm A (ENE-positive), while the remaining 113 patients (57.1%) without ENE constituted Arm B (ENE-negative).

All patients received adjuvant radiotherapy to a dose of 60 Gy in 30 fractions, delivered either via three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy (3DCRT) or volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT). Patients with high-risk pathological features, including ENE, received concurrent chemotherapy consisting of weekly cisplatin at a dose of 40 mg/m2.

Post-treatment follow-up was scheduled as follows: the first visit at six weeks post-radiotherapy, followed by evaluations every three months for the first two years, and subsequently every six months up to five years. Clinical follow-up included routine imaging, examination for local and regional control, and surveillance for distant metastasis. In cases of mortality, the cause of death was documented (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4).

Comprehensive demographic, clinical, radiologic, and pathological data were collected for all patients. Variables analyzed included age, sex, history of tobacco or alcohol use, primary tumor T-stage, radiologic and pathologic N-stage (based on contrast-enhanced CT or MRI), presence of radiologic ENE, histological subtype and tumor grade, total number of positive lymph nodes, and presence of pathological ENE. Concordance between radiologic and pathologic ENE was assessed. Additional high-risk features, such as depth of invasion (DoI), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and perineural invasion (PNI), were also documented. Staging was updated according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition TNM classification; patients initially staged using the AJCC 7th edition were reclassified accordingly (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7).

The primary outcomes analyzed were disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). Survival analyses were conducted to evaluate the prognostic impact of ENE and its interaction with other clinicopathologic risk factors.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) and R version 4.3.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) via RStudio.

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the baseline characteristics of the study cohort. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were summarized using means and standard deviations (SD). Associations between clinicopathological variables and survival outcomes—overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS)—were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method, with survival curves compared using the log-rank test to evaluate differences between groups. Univariate Cox proportional hazards models were employed to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for variables associated with OS and DFS. Variables found to be significant on univariate analysis were considered for multivariate modeling in subsequent analyses (if applicable).

The diagnostic performance of radiological assessment of ENE in predicting pathological ENE was evaluated using standard diagnostic metrics, including sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and overall accuracy.

All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value of ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The mean age of the study cohort was 55 years. The majority of patients were male (n = 178, 86.4%), with females comprising 13.6% (n = 28). Tobacco use, in various forms, was reported by 59.7% of patients. The most common primary tumor subsite was the buccal mucosa (n = 65, 51.6%), followed by the tongue (n = 43, 34.1%). Squamous cell carcinoma was the predominant histological subtype (n = 204, 99%). Most patients presented with advanced-stage disease, with 120 patients (58.3%) staged as AJCC 8th edition stage IVA.

Following stage migration according to the AJCC 8th edition, 35% of patients in the ENE-positive arm and 23% in the ENE-negative arm required T-stage reclassification. Regarding nodal stage, 98.9% of patients in the ENE-positive arm were upstaged, compared to 1.8% in the ENE-negative arm. Among ENE-positive patients, 91.6% received more than four cycles of concurrent chemotherapy. Grade ≥ 2 toxicities occurred in fewer than 5% of patients in the radiotherapy-alone group and in 8% of patients receiving chemoradiotherapy.

The median overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) for the entire cohort were 47.7 months and 39.5 months, respectively. Multivariate analysis was conducted to assess the impact of clinicopathological variables on OS in the context of ENE. Factors evaluated included age, tobacco use, clinical stage at presentation, radiologic ENE, skin and bony involvement, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PNI), and margin status. Radiological ENE and the presence of LVI/PNI were significantly associated with poorer OS (p < 0.0001). Variables such as male sex, tobacco use, lip subsite involvement, and skin or bony invasion showed a trend toward significance (Fig. 2).

On univariate Cox proportional hazards analysis, the absence of pathological ENE was associated with a significantly lower risk of death (HR = 0.27; 95% CI, 0.166–0.437; p < 0.001). Similarly, the absence of radiological ENE (HR = 0.355; 95% CI, 0.223–0.565; p < 0.001), absence of LVI (HR = 0.431; 95% CI, 0.259–0.716; p = 0.001), and absence of PNI (HR = 0.522; 95% CI, 0.331–0.824; p = 0.005) were associated with significantly improved OS. Tumor size > 4 cm was significantly associated with poorer OS (HR = 5.312; 95% CI, 2.074–13.67; p = 0.001).

Similarly, absence of pathological ENE (HR = 0.303; 95% CI, 0.195–0.472; p < 0.001), radiological ENE (HR = 0.403; 95% CI, 0.263–0.619; p < 0.001), LVI (HR = 0.483; 95% CI, 0.296–0.787; p = 0.003), and PNI (HR = 0.592; 95% CI, 0.388–0.904; p = 0.015) were significantly associated with improved DFS.

Recurrence was observed in 30 patients (35.3%) in the ENE-positive group and in 20 patients (17.7%) in the ENE-negative group. In the ENE-positive arm, recurrence patterns were evenly distributed between distant (40.6%) and locoregional (40.6%) failures. In contrast, the ENE-negative group experienced predominantly locoregional recurrences (63.6%).

Radiologic assessment of ENE demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy for detecting pathological ENE, with an overall accuracy of 89.9%. The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were 85.88%, 92.92%, 90.12%, and 89.74%, respectively.

Discussion

The incorporation of extranodal extension (ENE) into the AJCC 8th edition TNM staging system has led to a significant shift in the staging of head and neck cancers, with the presence of macroscopic ENE resulting in upstaging to stage IV. ENE has consistently been associated with poor prognosis, particularly when coexisting with other high-risk features such as T4 disease, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and perineural invasion (PNI), with a clear impact on both overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) (Mamic et al. 2020; Majercakova et al. 2018; Woolgar et al. 2003).

In a study by Mamic et al. (Mamic et al. 2020), 174 patients with oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OC-SCC) were evaluated postoperatively, and ENE was identified in approximately 30% of nodal specimens. Through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, a 1.9 mm cutoff was established to distinguish minor versus major ENE. Patients with major ENE (> 1.9 mm) demonstrated significantly worse OS and DFS compared to those with minor ENE, suggesting that ENE extent is a critical prognostic stratifier.

Our study focused on a cohort of 85 patients with pathologically confirmed ENE, most of whom had major ENE. By directly comparing this cohort with ENE-negative counterparts, we aimed to quantify the prognostic impact of ENE as an independent variable. While prior studies have explored ENE in broader contexts, few have specifically examined the subset of major ENE-positive OC-SCC patients. Our findings support the growing evidence that standard postoperative chemoradiotherapy, though beneficial, may not be sufficient to match the survival outcomes of patients without ENE.

Historically, the landmark meta-analyses by Bernier and Cooper (Bernier and Cooper 2005) provided the rationale for adjuvant chemoradiotherapy in ENE-positive patients, demonstrating improvements in locoregional control and survival. These findings led to widespread adoption of platinum-based concurrent chemoradiotherapy. However, our data suggest that this approach, although practice-changing, does not adequately mitigate the adverse impact of ENE. Real-world outcomes in our cohort reinforce the need for further treatment intensification in this high-risk group.

One potential avenue lies in preoperative identification of ENE. In our study, radiological ENE—assessed using CT and MRI—showed strong correlation with pathological ENE, with high diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity 85.9%, specificity 92.9%). This reinforces the utility of radiological ENE as a preoperative prognostic marker and a tool for treatment planning. Radiologic features such as fat stranding, vascular or muscular invasion, and fat suppression were consistently used in our institution for this purpose, in alignment with the grading system proposed by a randomized trial at Tata Memorial Hospital (TMH), Mumbai (Mahajan et al. 2022). Their findings, derived from oropharyngeal cancer patients treated with definitive chemoradiation, emphasized that radiologic ENE (iENE) independently predicted worse outcomes and should be routinely reported with standardized criteria.

Further supporting evidence comes from a retrospective Japanese study which suggested that iENE may also confer resistance to doublet induction chemotherapy in oropharyngeal cancers. Though not directly transferable to oral cancers, these findings raise the hypothesis that patients with iENE may benefit from alternative or intensified neoadjuvant strategies, including the incorporation of immunotherapy or altered radiotherapy fractionation (Onaga et al. 2024).

At our institution, prospective trials are planned to compare doublet versus triplet neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) in OC-SCC, with iENE included as a stratification factor. We also intend to evaluate the feasibility and efficacy of delayed concomitant boost schedules in the adjuvant setting for ENE-positive patients. This is supported by the RTOG 99–14 trial (Garden et al. 2008), which demonstrated promising outcomes using high-dose cisplatin and concomitant boost radiotherapy, albeit with increased acute toxicity attributable to 2D planning techniques. With modern IMRT-based techniques, the toxicity profile may improve while preserving the therapeutic benefit.

In a more recent attempt to intensify adjuvant therapy, the OCAT trial at TMH (Laskar et al. 2023) tested the addition of a sixth fraction of radiotherapy per week. Although the overall results did not reach statistical significance, a trend toward improved DFS was observed in patients with multiple high-risk features—including T3–T4 primaries, N2–N3 nodes, and ENE. These findings warrant further exploration of altered fractionation in combination with concurrent chemotherapy for ENE-positive OC-SCC.

While our study is retrospective in nature and limited by sample size, it provides valuable real-world evidence from a high-incidence region. Our results reaffirm the poor prognosis associated with ENE and support the need for enhanced treatment strategies. The strong correlation between radiological and pathological ENE highlights the importance of integrating imaging findings into multidisciplinary decision-making and treatment planning.

Future prospective studies should focus on the stratification of ENE by extent, the role of radiological ENE as a biomarker, and the development of intensified adjuvant or neoadjuvant treatment protocols to improve outcomes in this high-risk group.

Conclusion

Our study reaffirms extranodal extension (ENE) as a critical adverse prognostic factor in patients with oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma (OC-SCC), significantly impacting both overall and disease-free survival. Despite receiving standard adjuvant chemoradiotherapy, patients with ENE demonstrated inferior outcomes compared to those without, highlighting the need for further treatment intensification in this high-risk group.

Radiological ENE showed high diagnostic accuracy in predicting pathological ENE and holds promise as a non-invasive preoperative biomarker. Its routine evaluation in imaging reports may aid in risk stratification and early identification of candidates for neoadjuvant or intensified adjuvant therapies.

Given the limitations of current standard therapies in this subset, future prospective trials should focus on novel treatment strategies, including neoadjuvant chemotherapy intensification, incorporation of immunotherapy, and altered radiotherapy fractionation. These approaches may help bridge the survival gap and improve long-term outcomes for ENE-positive OC-SCC patients.

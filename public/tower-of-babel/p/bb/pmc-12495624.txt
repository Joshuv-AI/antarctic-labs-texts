Preoperative prediction of pituitary neuroendocrine tumor consistency based on multiparametric MRI radiomics: a multicenter study
Authors: Qiuyuan Yang, Yubo Wang, Jialei Wu, Hao Hu, Yimin He, Yan Wang, Bin Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the clinical value of preoperative prediction of pituitary neuroendocrine tumor（PitNET） consistency based on multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI) radiomics.Patients and MethodsThe clinical data of 137 patients with PitNET who underwent preoperative mpMRI were retrospectively analyzed and classified into soft and hard according to the consistency of the PitNET tumor with the surgical records of neurosurgeons. The patients were randomly divided into two sets: a training set (n = 108) and an internal validation set (n = 29). Single and multifactorial factors were used to analyze clinical high-risk risk factors and establish clinical models. Using the logistic regression (LR) classifier to construct radiomics signature based on 2D and 3D region of interest(ROI), respectively. Combined with clinical characteristics and radiomics features, a combined clinical-radiomics model was constructed, and the nomogram was drawn. The robustness and accuracy of the prediction model were tested by using multi-center clinical data as an external validation set.Results4224 and 5061 radiomics features were extracted based on 2D ROI and 3D ROI, and 28 and 15 predictive features were selected. Among the radiomics signature, the 3D-multi (T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1) radiomics signature constructed based on 3D ROI has high prediction efficiency. The area under curve(AUC) values in the training set and the internal validation set are 0.793 (95% confidence interval (CI): 0.711–0.859) and 0.798 (95% CI: 0.643–0.942), respectively. Introduction

Previous studies considered that pituitary adenoma is a benign neuroendocrine tumor [1], and its incidence rate accounts for 10% −25% of nervous system tumors [2, 3]. In recent years, the incidence rate has a rising trend year by year, which has attracted people’s attention [4]. However, according to the World Health Organization (WHO)’s latest 5th edition of the neuroendocrine tumor classification guidelines released in 2022, pituitary adenomas exhibit invasive behavior similar to malignant tumors, capable of expanding and invading adjacent tissue structures. Therefore, the name of pituitary adenomas has been changed to pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNET) [5, 6]. It should be emphasized that although the naming has changed, it does not necessarily mean a change in disease treatment plans and multidisciplinary clinical responsibilities. On the contrary, this change further emphasizes the importance of multidisciplinary and comprehensive treatment plans for PitNET patients [7].

In clinical practice, endoscopic transsphenoidal approach is the preferred treatment for PitNET, which can relieve tumor occupying effects (especially for larger PitNET) and effectively alleviate clinical endocrine symptoms [8]. However, the consistency of PitNET is an important basis for determining the surgical method and also an important factor affecting the success rate of surgery. Previous research reports have classified the consistency of PitNET into two categories: soft and hard. Soft PitNET has a soft consistency and can be fully removed from tumors through endoscopic transsphenoidal approach using suction or scraping methods, while hard PitNET was a fibrous, making it difficult to remove tumors through suction or scraping methods. It requires the use of tools such as forceps and even craniotomy [9–12]. It should also be noted that the physiological and anatomical structure of the sellar region is complex, and the endoscopic transsphenoidal approach has relatively limited surgical vision. For hard PitNET, endoscopic resection is easy to damage the normal pituitary gland and adjacent tissue structures of the sellar region (such as internal carotid artery, cavernous sinus, etc.). Therefore, it is necessary to choose appropriate surgical methods based on the different consistency of PitNET. In clinical practice, the consistency of most PitNET is soft. In the past, Smith et al. [13] reported that only about 5% −13% of PitNET’s consistency was hard. In a recent study, the proportion of hard tumors was approximately 20% [14]. Therefore, it is important to evaluate the consistency of PitNET before surgery, which affects the long-term prognosis of patients.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the preferred examination method for PitNET, as it can perform multi parameter, multi sequence, multi directional scans, and has high soft tissue resolution. Previous studies have evaluated the consistency of PitNET based on changes in signal intensity on MRI images (T2WI [15, 16], DWI [diffusion weighted imaging] [17–19], CE-T1 [20, 21]), but only semi qualitative evaluation can be performed. The accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of the research results need to be improved, and some research conclusions are completely opposite. Therefore, it is urgent to find a simple, efficient, and accurate method to predict the consistency of PitNET before surgery.

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) technology has flourished in the medical field [22]. The concept of radiomics, first proposed by scholars such as Lambin et al. [23] in 2012, has become an important branch of medical imaging AI technology and a cutting-edge clinical application. Radiomics can high-throughput extract quantitative image features from medical images such as computed tomography(CT), MRI, ultrasound(US), positron emission tomography-CT(PET/CT), and use machine learning algorithms to analyze the correlation between features, establish predictive models to achieve preoperative diagnosis, efficacy evaluation, and prognosis prediction of diseases [24–26]. Now, AI has taken another step forward. A significant improvement in the accuracy of cell detection and segmentation algorithms has been achieved as a result of the emergence of open-source software and innovative deep neural network architectures. Automated cell segmentation now enables the extraction of quantifiable cellular and spatial features from microscope images of cells and tissues, providing critical insights into cellular organization in various diseases [27].

At present, radiomics research is applied to various systems of tumor related diseases (glioma [28, 29], non-small cell lung cancer [30, 31], colorectal cancer [32, 33], prostate cancer [34], etc.). As a common and frequently occurring disease, previous studies have predicted the subtypes and invasiveness of PitNET based on MRI radiomics methods [35, 36]. In the study of using radiomics to predict the consistency of PitNET, some studies extracted radiomics features from a single MRI sequence to construct prediction models (T2WI [9, 10], CE-T1 [37, 38]), DWI [12, 39], while others used multi parameter (T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1) MRI sequences to construct combined prediction models [11, 40]. However, these studies have certain limitations and further verification of the robustness of the prediction model is needed.

Therefore, the purpose of this study is to establish multiple predictive models based on the radiomics method of mpMRI, and validate the robustness of the predictive models using external datasets, in order to achieve efficient, accurate, and non-invasive prediction of the consistency of PitNET before surgery, providing guidance for precision medicine and personalized treatment.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Dali University and Yunnan Cancer Hospital, and the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Retrospective collection of 137 PitNET patients who underwent Surgical treatment from institution 1 (December 2012 to February 2022) and institution 2 (January 2016 to September 2020) as internal data set. We collected 10 patients from institution 3 (June 2017 to February 2022) as an external data set. The study inclusion criteria were: (1) PitNET confirmed by pathological diagnosis; (2) the patients underwent surgical treatment for the first time; (3) perform baseline pituitary MRI examination before surgery. Exclusion criteria were: (1) relevant treatment (radiation therapy, drug therapy, chemotherapy, etc.) received before surgery; (2) the MRI image has artifacts, blurring, or missing any sequence in T1WI, T2WI, CE-T1; (3) the tumor is accompanied by extensive bleeding > 50%, necrosis, or cystic transformation; (4) tumor diameter < 10 mm. Due to the impact of large-scale bleeding and necrosis on the content of tumor solid components and ROI segmentation, it affects the accuracy of extracting radiomics features, which may ultimately lead to a decrease in the prediction accuracy of the model. At the same time, manual tumor segmentation was used in the study, and when the tumor is less than 10 mm, it is difficult to ensure the accuracy of tumor ROI segmentation. Therefore, the above-mentioned patients were excluded [41]. Finally, 137 cases from institution 1 and 2 were randomly selected and divided into a training set (n = 108) and an internal validation set (n = 29) in an 8:2 ratio. 10 cases from institution 3 were used as a prospective external validation set to conduct multicenter validation of the predictive model (Fig. 1).

MRI scan

The baseline MRI examination of all cases was completed within a month before surgery, with scanning sequences including T1WI, T2WI, and CE-T1. The scanning equipment includes 1.5T magnetic resonance instruments (① Signa HDe, GE Healthcare, USA; ② Magnetom Avanto, Siemens, Germany) and 3.0T magnetic resonance instruments (③ Vantage Titan, Toshiba, Japan; ④ Ingenia, Philips, Netherlands; ⑤ Signa Pioneer, GE Healthcare, USA; ⑥ Discovery MR 750 W, GE Healthcare, USA). Details of MRI equipment, sequences, and parameters are presented in Supplementary Material S1. Enhanced scanning uses a high-pressure syringe to inject magnetic resonance contrast agent Gd-DTPA, with a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg and a rate of 2.5 ml/s.

Image preprocessing

Due to the fact that MRI equipment come from different manufacturers, types, and field strengths, it is necessary to preprocess all images to standardize and normalize them, reducing the impact on the predicted model results. Firstly, resample the original MRI images (T1WI, T2WI, CE-T1) with a pixel spacing of 1 × 1 × 1 mm3 and normalize them. Then, the Z-score method is used to standardize the grayscale of the image and Limit the maximum and minimum grayscale to within 3 standard deviations of grayscale values.

Tumor segmentation

It’s necessary to anonymize patient privacy information on images. Then, import images into ITK-SNAP (version 3.8.0, http://www.itksnap.org) and manually sketch the tumor ROI. Two methods were used for tumor segmentation: the first method selected the maximum cross-sectional area of the tumor and accurately delineated the tumor area along the edge of the lesion to obtain 2D ROI; the second method is to delineate the tumor boundaries layer by layer in all consecutive sections of the tumor and fuse them to obtain a 3D ROI (Fig. 2a).

In order to ensure the accuracy and repeatability of tumor segmentation, all ROIs were delineated by first radiologist (Q.Y.Y., with 7 years of experience in head imaging diagnosis), and then reviewed by second senior radiologist (X.C., with 4 years of experience in head imaging diagnosis). When there is disagreement on tumor segmentation, the two radiologists will negotiate and handle it. Tumor segmentation follows the principles: first, the tumor area is delineated in the CE-T1 image, and then the tumor boundaries on the CE-T1 image are used as baseline reference to delineate the tumor area on T1WI and T2WI images. Second, small cystic changes, necrosis, and bleeding lesions were removed from both 2D and 3D ROI. Based on the postoperative review and medical records of neurosurgeons, when the soft component occupies the majority, it is defined as soft consistency, while when the hard component occupies the majority, it is defined as hard consistency. In this study, ROI was regarded as a uniform volume, which laid a solid foundation for the smooth progress of this research. In the future, we will explore different internal radioactive regions or sub ROIs for analysis.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

Feature extraction is performed after tumor segmentation, using a filtering method [42]. The principle is that this method is independent of machine learning models and evaluates the relationship between each feature and the target variable based on statistical indicators, selecting features with strong correlation. For example, calculating the Pearson correlation coefficient between features and target variables, the larger the absolute value of the correlation coefficient, the stronger the linear correlation between features and target variables; the chi square test is used for classification problems to test the independence between features and target variables. The larger the chi square value, the stronger the correlation between features and target variables.

When defining consistency, we consider the entire tumor as a whole and do not take into account regional changes within the tumor. In future research, we will further investigate whether regional changes within the tumor affect the extraction or analysis of radiomics and the accuracy of prediction models.

Using PyRadiomics [43] (version 3.0.1, http://pypi.org/project/pyradiomics) extract radiomics features from the different 2D and 3D ROI, respectively. The extracted features consist of the original image and the filtered derived image, including first order statistical features, shape features, texture features, and wavelet features. Texture features including: gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, gray level run length matrix (GLRLM) features, gray level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, gray level difference matrix (GLDM) features, neighborhood gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features [24, 43]. Finally, about 4224 and 5061 radiomics features were extracted from the 2D and 3D ROI, respectively (Fig. 2b).

The high-dimensional information of radiomics features is closely related to high-level redundant and irrelevant information, which may cause overfitting and multicollinearity problems in prediction models, thereby reducing the performance of machine learning algorithms and seriously affecting the performance of prediction models [43]. Therefore, it is necessary to screen the radiomics features extracted from the training set. Before performing feature selection, all radiomics features were standardized by removing the mean and dividing by standard deviation, resulting in a mean of zero and a variance of one for each set of feature values.In order to improve the repeatability and generalization ability of the model, we using the intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) features with an ICC > 0.9 were selected, while those that are highly correlated with other features were removed. Firstly, the variance threshold method is used to reduce the dimensionality of features, and features with a variance threshold > 0.8 are selected and retained. Then, use the Select K-best univariate selection method to select features that are not significant (if p > 0.05 features are deleted). Finally, regression analysis was conducted using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithm to remove redundant features and retain the most predictive value features. And the 10-fold cross-validation method was used to analyze the features selected by LASSO.

Construction and validation of the radiomics models

Construction radiomics signature: 1)Radiomics signature based on 2D ROI: the most predictive features were selected from 2D ROI radiomics features, and 2D-T1WI, 2D-T2WI, 2D-CE-T1, and 2D- (T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1) radiomics signature were analyzed and constructed in the training set using LR classifiers. 2) Radiomics signature based on 3D ROI: similar to the method of constructing 2D ROI radiomics signature, construction 3D-T1WI, 3D-T2WI, 3D-CE-T1, and 3D-(T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1) radiomics signature.

Constructing clinical-radiomics combined model: 1)Clinical-radiomics combined model based on 2D ROI: combining the most predictive radiomics features and clinical high-risk factors in 2D ROI, using LR classifier analysis to construct a 2D ROI clinical-radiomics combined model in the training set. 2) Clinical-radiomics combined model based on the 3D ROI: similar to the above method, construct 3D ROI clinical-radiomics combined model on the training set. 3) Clinical-radiomics combined model based on 2D&3D ROI: all radiomics features of 2D and 3D ROI are combined and screened, and the most predictive features are selected to combine with clinical high-risk factors to construct a 2D&3D ROI clinical-radiomics combined model (Fig. 2c). There are Literature reports that combining 2D and 3D image features can improve the accuracy of prediction models, but this method has been rarely studied. Therefore, we explore using this method to further improve the accuracy of our prediction model and lay the foundation for future research on this method [44].

Draw ROC [45] to evaluate the predictive performance of each model. The performance evaluation indicators of the prediction model include AUC value, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. Draw a calibration curve to evaluate the Goodness of fit of the prediction model. Use DeLong test to analyze and compare the predictive performance differences between models. The clinical net benefit rate of decision curve analysis(DCA) evaluation prediction model for clinical diagnosis. Draw a column chart based on a clinical-radiomics combined model (Fig. 2c).

Construction and validation of the clinical model

In this study, the selection of covariates was based on both relevant literature reports [46] and the close relationship between these covariates and the occurrence and development of the patient’s disease. Screening tools were used to select the most consistent clinical information from this information, which was then used to establish a prediction model. In statistical models, the common way to adjust covariates is through multiple regression analysis (for example, linear regression or logical regression). The regression model will include the variables we are concerned about along with covariates in the analysis to evaluate their impact on the results and ‘eliminate’ the interference of covariates from the results.

Use logistic univariate and multivariate analysis to analyze the seven clinical population information characteristics (gender, age, maximum tumor length, tumor volume, Knosp grade, tumor location, and surgical method). Selection out clinical high-risk risk factors related to predicting PitNET consistency for constructing clinical models. Evaluate the predictive performance of the model using AUC values, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, and draw ROC curves.

Statistical analysis

Clinical population information characteristics using R software(version 3.4.1, https://www.R-project.org/) to conduct statistics and analysis. Discussion

This study is based on the mpMRI radiomics, where a large number of radiomics features are extracted from 2D and 3D ROI, LR classifiers are used to construct different radiomics models, and multi-center data are used as external validation sets to test the robustness and accuracy of the prediction model. The aim is to efficiently, accurately, and non-invasive evaluate the consistency of PitNET before surgery, meet the personalized treatment needs of patients, and achieve precision medicine.

Clinical guidelines indicate that the initial cure rate for PitNET patients undergoing endoscopic transsphenoidal Surgery is 40% −50% [47]. Compared with conventional craniotomy, endoscopic transsphenoidal sinus surgery has become the preferred surgical approach due to its minimal trauma, fewer postoperative complications, and good long-term patient management. However, tumor consistency is an important influencing factor for surgical methods and success rates. The use of traditional imaging methods to evaluate consistency through tumor signal changes has certain limitations. Previous studies [48] have utilized multiparametric magnetic resonance radiomics to non-invasively predict postoperative visual recovery before surgery, achieving certain success. Zeynalova et al. [10] and Smith et al. [13] evaluated the consistency of PitNET using T2WI sequence, and the results showed a linear positive correlation between low signal intensity of tumors and hard consistency. On the contrary, other related research results suggest that there is no significant correlation between the two [15, 16]. The research conclusion of evaluating tumor consistency using DWI images is also controversial. Pierallini et al. [17] believe that the relationship between apparent diffusion coefficient(ADC) values and tumor consistency is consistent with ADC value hard consistency > ADC value medium consistency > ADC value soft consistency, while Suzuki et al. [18] and Mahmoud et al. [19] show no significant correlation between the two. In another study, scholars believe that although there is no quantitative relationship between ADC values and tumor consistency, the signal intensity of tumors on DWI images can evaluate the degree of tumor fibrosis. The lower the DWI signal, the higher the degree of fibrosis [49]. There are also studies using CE-T1 [20] and CE-FIESTA [21] to predict the consistency of tumors. It is not difficult to find that the tumor information obtained from the above studies based on a single MRI sequence is very limited, and some research conclusions are controversial. In addition, the latest review of experience with non-functioning pituitary macroadenomas (NFPA) emphasizes the impact of clinical features, laboratory and imaging assessments, treatment management, and outcomes on treatment selection [50] and the signal of PitNET on MRI is influenced by various factors such as intratumoral bleeding, fiber composition, amyloid protein, iron, calcification, melanin, and protein fluid [11, 51, 52]. Therefore, it is difficult to accurately evaluate the consistency of tumors based on traditional imaging methods.

Radiomics applies data feathering algorithms to transform imaging data into high-dimensional and exploitable feature spaces, and uses machine learning to high-throughput analyze heterogeneity within and between tumors, playing an important role in disease selection, early diagnosis, treatment selection, and prognosis evaluation. And it has also been well applied in some brain diseases, such as Alzheimer’s disease.This demonstrates the extensive applications of radiomics in brain diseases [53, 54]. The relevant literature reviewed and analyzed the research progress of the application of radiomics in PitNET [25, 55]. Literature research has found that previous studies based on radiomics methods for preoperative prediction of PitNET consistency mainly extracted tumor features from a single MRI sequence (T2WI, CE-T1, DWI). The study by Cuocolo et al. [9] extracted texture features from T2WI images and constructed a prediction model with an accuracy of 0.86 in the training set, 0.93 in the test set, and 0.99 in the AUC value. Zeynalova et al. [10] research is based on extracting histogram features from T2WI images. The prediction model constructed by artificial neural networks has an accuracy of 72.5% for consistency soft and hard classification of PitNET, with a model AUC value of 0.710. The prediction performance is superior to SIR (p = 0.021). The predictive model constructed by Ali et al. [56] based on axial T2 Weighted images showed exemplary performance in distinguishing between the entire prostate and prostate cancer, with an AUROC of 68.46 (p = 0.022), sensitivity of 76.25%, specificity of 73.15%, and accuracy of 71.02%. Radioanalysis of T2 weighted MR images has been proven to have clinical applications in prostate cancer detection. Benfante et al. [57] also confirmed that the imaging omics prediction model based on T2 weighted sequence has achieved excellent results in distinguishing between low and high grade bladder diseases (AUROC = 0.84, sensitivity: 65.6%, specificity: 81.5%, p < 0.001), or in distinguishing between non muscle invasive bladder cancer and muscle invasive bladder cancer (AUROC = 0.7, sensitivity: 100%, specificity: 86.7%, p = 0.0031), and also verified the importance of MRI radiology in improving the characteristics and differentiation of bladder diseases. There are also studies on building prediction models based on enhanced 3D SPACE images [38] and DWI images [12, 39], which have achieved good prediction performance. In this study, not only was an radiomics model constructed using a single MRI sequence (T1WI, T2WI, CE-T1), but a combined model was also constructed using multiple parameters (T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1). In a single sequence model, 2D-T2WI and 3D-T2WI radiomics signature have good predictive performance, while 3D-T2WI radiomics signature have the best predictive performance. The AUC values in the training set and internal validation set are 0.762 and 0.717, respectively, with accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of 0.694 vs. 0.793, 0.700 vs. 0.727, 0.691 vs. 0.833, indicating that T2WI images may contain richer tumor information, the constructed model is more accurate and reliable in predicting tumor consistency, which is consistent with the research findings of Machado et al. [58], which showed that 3D feature-based models achieve better performance with fewer features compared to 2D feature-based models, and radiomics has the potential to predict the recurrence of NFPA after the first surgery. Three dimensional radiology has higher resolution than two-dimensional radiology features in predicting the recurrence of NFPA. At the same time, the combination of radiomics and machine learning algorithms can provide a computational model for non-invasive, unbiased, and rapid assessment, improving the prediction of recurrence of NFPA. Compared to constructing radiomics models using a single MRI sequence, there are few reports on the use of mpMRI radiomics methods. Wan et al. [11] used random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) machine learning classifiers to build an radiomics model based on mpMRI (T1WI + CE-T1 + T2WI). The mpMRI radiomics model built using RF classifier has an AUC value of 0.90, an accuracy of 0.87, a sensitivity of 0.83, and a specificity of 0.87 in the test set. The predictive performance is superior to single sequence models (T1WI, CE-T1, T2WI) and paired models (T1WI + CE-T1, T1WI + T2WI, CE-T1 + T2WI). Similar to the above research conclusions, Yang et al. [59] also confirmed that multi parameter (T1WI + T2WI + CE-T1) radiomics can improve prediction performance with the 3D multi parameter radiomics signature having the highest predictive performance, with AUC values of 0.793 and 0.798 in the training and internal validation sets, and accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of 0.722 vs. 0.724, 0.700 vs. 0.636, 0.735 vs. 0.778, respectively. Moreover, the predictive performance of the multi parameter model is better than that of the single sequence model. Indicating that the use of mpMRI radiomics signature can help improve the results of preoperative prediction of PitNET consistency. It is worth noting that some studies have pointed out that using more radiomics features to establish predictive models may not accurately distinguish between soft consistency and hard consistency of tumors, as a large amount of input high-dimensional data may overfit the training samples, resulting in reduced classification accuracy [60].

Accurate tumor segmentation is the foundation for establishing high-precision prediction models. Currently, manual segmentation is considered the gold standard for tumor segmentation [61] . In the research of Wan et al. [11], an automatic 3D ROI segmentation algorithm specially developed for brain tumor was used to segment PitNET, which greatly improved the segmentation efficiency. However, the algorithm could not accurately identify the bleeding and cystic parts in the tumor, so researchers need to manually remove this part after the completion of automatic segmentation. Wang et al. [62] developed an application for PitNET automatic segmentation based on deep learning algorithms, which can accurately recognize the background of tumors in the sella turcica region and achieve automatic segmentation of PitNET ROI. This algorithm avoids human errors and reduces researchers’ workload. In this study, manual tumor segmentation was chosen to ensure segmentation accuracy and improve model prediction performance. On the other hand, automatic or semi-automatic segmentation is mainly used for research on large datasets, focusing on the efficiency and high repeatability of ROI segmentation. However, it is worth noting that with the continuous development and progress of AI technology, the expansion of research datasets, and the demand for personalized treatment and precision medicine, it can be foreseen that in the near future, automatic or semi-automatic segmentation based on deep learning will become the mainstream.

At present, in research on predicting PitNET consistency based on MRI radiomics, most scholars extract radiomics features from 2D ROI [9, 10, 12, 13, 20, 39, 63], and only a few studies extract radiomics features from 3D ROI [11]. There are few reports on simultaneous 2D and 3D ROI segmentation of the same tumor in the same study. In the study by Machado et al. [58], manually segmenting tumors obtained 2D and 3D ROIs, and extracting radiomics features from the two sets of ROIs to establish predictive models. The accuracy of the models was 92.6% and 96.3%, respectively. Moreover, when constructing 3D ROI models, fewer radiomics features were used compared to 2D ROI models (5 vs8). This study also performed 2D and 3D ROI segmentation on tumors, and the prediction models showed that most prediction models based on 3D ROI had better performance than 2D ROI models, indicating that the tumor information provided by 3D ROI can improve the model’s prediction performance. The reason for the analysis may be that 3D ROI includes the entire tumor lesion and can provide richer quantitative image features, while 2D ROI only provides image features at the largest level of the tumor, missing most of the tumor information.

In addition, this study collected a multicenter dataset as an external validation set to validate the constructed radiomics model through a completely independent dataset, thereby providing robustness and accuracy among different image acquisition protocols. It is worth noting that collecting multicenter data in clinical research faces many challenges, resulting in fewer studies establishing external validation sets. Further standardization and standardization of the workflow for collecting multicenter data are needed in future research. Like most studies, our study has several limitations, firstly, this study is a retrospective study, and some case data may have a longer duration and may have selective bias, which may affect the model results. Secondly, although external data was used for multi- center validation of the model in this study, the dataset is relatively small and needs to be further expanded. Thirdly, due to the lack of quantitative analysis of tumor collagen, pathological and imaging results were not analyzed and compared in this preliminary study. Fourthly, relevant literature reports that the prediction model constructed by RF classifiers has higher accuracy and reliability [11, 64]. This study only used LR classifiers, and further research can be conducted on the predictive value of different classifiers (RF, SVM, etc.) in the future. Fifthly, In clinical practice, PitNET treatment is often a multidisciplinary effort. Variations in factors such as the tumor’s relationship with the internal carotid artery and cavernous sinus, as well as differences in surgical approaches and teams, can impact treatment outcomes. Furthermore, considering the complexity of PitNET treatment, future research should also focus on the impact of different treatment strategies and team practices on the accuracy of model predictions. By comprehensively considering more clinical variables and treatment factors, the practicality and guiding significance of the model can be further enhanced. This will help clinical doctors make more precise treatment decisions, thereby improving patient prognosis.

In conclusion, this study constructs a 2D&3D ROI clinical-radiomics combined model based on the mpMRI radiomics method, which can efficiently, accurately, and non-invasive predict the consistency of PitNET before surgery. The external validation set shows that the prediction model has good robustness and generalization, providing a reliable basis for clinical personalized treatment strategies and achieving precision medicine.

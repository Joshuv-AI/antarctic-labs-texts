Reconceptualizing online gaming in emerging adulthood: a phenomenological study
Authors: Anuradha Sathiyaseelan, Sathiyaseelan Balasundaram, Bishal Patangia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGaming is increasingly embedded in the everyday lives of emerging adults, yet research has predominantly emphasized pathological or risk-oriented perspectives. Limited attention has been given to how young adults experience gaming as an emotional, cognitive, embodied, and meaningful practice integrated into daily life. This study explored the lived experiences of gaming among emerging adults in India using a phenomenological approach.MethodsA qualitative phenomenological study was conducted among 12 emerging adult gamers aged 18–25 years in India. Participants were recruited using purposive sampling to ensure active gaming engagement and diversity in gender representation. Data were collected through semi-structured in-depth interviews conducted between December 2023 and June 2024 using secure video conferencing platforms. Interviews were transcribed verbatim and analysed using Braun and Clarke’s thematic analysis framework. Trustworthiness was established through independent coding, reflexive practices, member checking, and audit trails.ResultsThree themes were identified: (1) “What Gaming Feels Like,” describes emotional intensity, coping, motivation, and self-reflection; (2) “How Gaming Shows Up in the Body,” highlights immersion, bodily awareness, fatigue, and physical self-regulation; and (3) “How Gaming Fits into Life,” describing the integration of gaming within routines, relationships, identity, and social interaction. Participants described gaming as a meaningful developmental activity that supported emotional regulation, cognitive engagement, personal growth, and social connection while also requiring balance and self-monitoring.ConclusionsFindings challenge reductive risk–benefit conceptualizations of gaming by demonstrating that gaming can function as a multidimensional and embodied psychosocial practice in emerging adulthood. The study highlights the importance of understanding gaming within the broader contexts of identity development, emotional regulation, and everyday life. These findings have implications for psychological research, clinical assessment, and youth-focused digital well-being interventions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-05058-4.Keywords: Gaming, Emerging adulthood, Identity development, Young adults

Background

Over the past two decades, gaming has undergone a remarkable transformation, evolving from a subcultural pastime into a global, mainstream phenomenon [10]. Today, gaming is a regular task in everyday life for billions of individuals across diverse societies and age groups. This ubiquity has made gaming a significant determinant of socialization, self-expression, and personal development [13]. The sheer scale and diversity of gamers have built a simplistic narrative from alarmists to seek evidence-based understandings of the multidimensional impact of gaming on well-being [26].

Literature in the last few decades has established that gaming is a severe emotional experience [2, 29]. Far from being a passive activity, gaming immerses players in dynamic environments that evoke a wide spectrum of feelings central to the meaning of the gaming [2]. The affective intensity of gaming can mirror, and sometimes amplify, the emotional cycles of daily life, which helps them to negotiate success, failure, and personal growth [2, 7]. For gamers, a sense of achievement derived from overcoming in-game challenges or the camaraderie fostered through cooperative play led to a potent source of motivation and self-worth [33, 44, 47].

The literature focuses on the ways in which gaming can serve as a coping mechanism [4] to manage stress, escape from real-world pressures, or regulate difficult emotions [5, 31]. While such engagement can offer immediate relief there still remains a debate about the long-term consequences of using gaming as a primary strategy for emotional regulation. Some evidence suggests that gaming for coping may reinforce avoidance behaviors and contribute to psychological distress or social withdrawal [24]. This dual perspective describes both the adaptive and maladaptive functions of gaming in the context of individuals’ lives.

Gaming is increasingly recognized as a cognitively demanding and potentially enriching activity [23], fostering transferable skills across domains [18]. Its iterative structure cultivates persistence, self-efficacy, and a growth mindset [17, 48], supporting personal development, self-exploration, and identity work [17]. Through customizable avatars, branching narratives, and moral dilemmas, players experiment with different aspects of themselves, test boundaries, and enact values, often experiencing autonomy less accessible in other domains [42, 48]. The physical consequences of gaming, including eye strain, headaches, musculoskeletal discomfort, and disrupted sleep, are also being increasingly documented [20]. These effects are not uniform [16]; however, the embodied dimension of gaming, how it is experienced in and through the body, remains underexplored despite its critical importance.

In terms of challenging the stereotypes of gamers as disengaged or unproductive, recent studies highlight gaming as a purposeful and regulated activity that can increase productivity [10]. Many players exercise self-discipline to ensure that gaming complements, rather than detracts from, work, study, and social obligations [39].

Despite these findings, the literature remains fragmented and often examines discrete outcomes without integrating them into a holistic account of the gaming experience [20, 24, 26]. Existing research suggests that gaming is emotionally and cognitively intensive, serving both adaptive and maladaptive functions in stress and emotional regulation [43]. However, the interrelation of these cognitive, emotional, and embodied elements within daily practice remains insufficiently understood [27, 34].

A critical gap persists in our understanding of how gamers themselves make sense of their experiences, particularly in relation to the affective intensity of play and practical strategies in their everyday lives [34]. Previous research has often relied on quantitative methods or experimental paradigms [26]. As a result, there is a dearth of qualitative, first-person accounts that focus on the gaming behavior [8, 12]. Existing studies have also paid less attention has been given to the personalized aspects of gaming [13, 26] and how players exercise agency and self-discipline in balancing gaming with other life domains [28]. These gaps motivated the present study which aimed to explore the lived experience of gamers and self-regulatory practices. This paper is a part of a large study.

In response to these gaps, we investigated the emotional, cognitive, physical, and practical dimensions of gaming as described by the gamers themselves. With the narratives and lived experiences of the gamers, the study takes a step ahead from the reductive risk–benefit frameworks to offer an integrative account of what gaming means to those most deeply engaged with it. Our study viewed gaming as a complex, personified practice embedded in everyday life, both shaped and shaped by broader processes of selfhood, motivation, and adaptation.

Therefore, our study had four objectives: (1) to examine the emotional perspective of gaming, including how emotions are generated, processed, and managed during play; (2) to analyse the cognitive demands and motivational structures that underpin engagement and their role in fostering persistence, self-efficacy, and personal growth; (3) to explore the embodied sensations and physical consequences of gaming, alongside the strategies gamers employ to accommodate or mitigate them; and (4) to understand how gaming is integrated into daily routines and how players negotiate its place in relation to other responsibilities, relationships, and aspirations.

Method

Research design

This study adopted a qualitative phenomenological research design with an interpretive orientation to explore how emerging adult gamers experience and make meaning of gaming within their everyday lives. Phenomenology is particularly suited to examining lived experience and the subjective meanings individuals attribute to a phenomenon within specific social and personal contexts [30]. In the present study, this methodological approach enabled an in-depth exploration of the emotional, cognitive, embodied, and practical dimensions of gaming as experienced by young adults. The phenomenological framework aligned closely with the objectives of the study, which sought to move beyond reductive risk-oriented conceptualizations of gaming and instead understand gaming as a lived and contextually embedded psychosocial experience. Given the rapid growth of gaming culture in India and the limited qualitative psychological research examining the lived experiences of Indian emerging adult gamers, a qualitative phenomenological approach provided an appropriate framework for capturing the complexity, depth, and meaning embedded within participant narratives.

Population and sample

The study population comprised emerging adult gamers aged between 18 and 25 years who actively engaged in gaming on a regular basis. The screening happened in two parts one with yes/no questions to get the details of whether the young adult is a player and further questions to know about the duration. After this part of the screening 28 participants were selected and were under went second level screening with psychometric tools such as Mental Health Inventory [46] Emotional Intelligence Scale [38] Social Intelligence Scale [40] Perceived Stress Scale [9] and Academic Procrastination Scale [22]. The final sample consisted of 12 participants, including seven male and five female gamers, all of whom met the inclusion criteria. Participants reported an average gaming engagement of approximately two hours per day across different gaming formats and genres. Consistent with qualitative research conventions, sample adequacy was determined based on thematic sufficiency and conceptual depth rather than statistical representativeness [36]. Recruitment continued until recurring experiential patterns and sufficient interpretive richness were observed across participant accounts.

Data collection

Data collection was conducted between December 2023 and June 2024 through semi-structured, in-depth interviews using a secure video conferencing platform (Google Meet). The interviews formed part of a larger qualitative project on gaming experiences among emerging adults in India, from which a separate analysis examining gaming as a habit and its short- and long-term effects on physical health, productivity, and social life has been previously published [35]. The current paper reports an independent phenomenological analysis addressing distinct objectives, focused on the emotional, cognitive, embodied, and practical dimensions of the gaming experience as lived by participants. The two analyses draw on different participant excerpts; no verbatim quotations are reproduced across the publications. The semi-structured interview guide (see Supplementary File 2) was developed by the research team for this larger project, informed by the study objectives and existing qualitative literature on gaming, emerging adulthood, and lived experience. It comprised open-ended questions and accompanying probes designed to explore participants' meaning-making, emotional experiences, cognitive engagement, motivational structures, embodied and physical sensations, social interactions, and the integration of gaming into everyday life. The guide was used flexibly: question order and follow-up probes were adapted in response to each participant's narrative to support the phenomenological orientation of the study. Interviews were conducted by AS and BP, both of whom were involved in qualitative research relating to digital behaviour and psychosocial experiences. The semi-structured format enabled participants to narrate their experiences freely while allowing the researchers to probe emerging themes and clarify meanings where necessary. Each interview lasted between 60 and 90 min and was audio-recorded with participants’ informed consent. All interviews were transcribed verbatim to preserve the richness, nuance, and contextual detail of participant narratives. In addition to interview transcripts, field notes and reflexive notes were maintained throughout the data collection process to document contextual observations, emerging insights, researcher reflections, and preliminary interpretive considerations.

Data analysis

Data were analysed using Braun and Clarke’s [6] thematic analysis framework. The analytic process was iterative and reflexive, involving repeated engagement with the transcripts to develop a comprehensive understanding of participants lived experiences. Initial familiarization involved repeated readings of the transcripts alongside annotation of preliminary impressions, recurring ideas, and meaningful experiential patterns. Initial coding was conducted independently by AS and BS using a primarily inductive and semantic orientation while remaining attentive to broader interpretive meanings embedded within participant narratives. Coding proceeded line-by-line and captured emotional responses, cognitive engagement, embodied experiences, self-regulatory processes, social interactions, and the practical integration of gaming into everyday life. Codes were continuously refined, merged, and reorganized as analysis progressed and new insights emerged across the dataset. Following initial coding, codes were collated into broader candidate themes through constant comparison across transcripts and thematic mapping of conceptual relationships. Themes were subsequently reviewed, discussed, and refined collaboratively by all authors to ensure coherence, internal consistency, conceptual distinctiveness, and strong grounding within the data. Particular attention was given to divergent narratives and negative cases to enhance analytic depth and interpretive rigor.

Trustworthiness and reflexivity

Several strategies were employed to enhance methodological rigor and trustworthiness throughout the study. Reflexive engagement was maintained continuously by AS, BS, and BP through ongoing discussions concerning interpretation, thematic boundaries, researcher assumptions, and narrative representation during both analysis and manuscript development. This process enabled critical reflection on how researcher perspectives may have shaped interpretation and thematic construction. An audit trail documenting coding decisions, theme development, analytic refinements, and interpretive discussions was maintained throughout the research process to enhance transparency and analytic consistency. Member checking was also conducted through the sharing of preliminary interpretations with participants to ensure that the findings authentically reflected their experiences and perspectives. The collaborative and iterative nature of the analytic process further strengthened interpretive credibility by enabling multiple layers of engagement with the data while preserving the richness and contextual integrity of participant narratives.

Ethics

Ethical approval was obtained from Research Conduct and Ethics Committee, CHRIST (Deemed to be University) with Approval Code: RCEC/108/03/23, prior to the commencement of data collection. All participants received detailed information regarding the purpose and procedures of the study and provided both written and audio-recorded informed consent before participation. Participants were informed of their right to withdraw from the study at any stage without penalty. Confidentiality and anonymity were maintained through the removal of identifying information from transcripts and study records. All audio recordings, transcripts, and related study materials were securely stored on password-protected devices accessible only to the research team. This study was reported in accordance with the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) guidelines.

Findings

Our analysis identified three interconnected themes (Fig. 1). “What Gaming Feels Like” captured the emotional depth of play as a space for self-reflection and personal growth. “How Gaming Shows up in the Body” highlights the centrality of physical sensations, from immersive engagement to the strain of prolonged play. “How Gaming Fits into Life” describes the strategies and self-regulation gamers use to embed play meaningfully into everyday routines (see Supplementary Table 2).

Thematic map of the finding

Global theme: reconceptualizing gaming

From the pastime to focused activity, gaming connects life at times as an escape and another time as the connectivity itself provides space for stress, relieving competition and collaboration with others. Some perceive this as a personal ritual that helps their creativity and builds a sense of mastery.

Organizing theme 1: what gaming feels Like

Under this theme, gamers have shared how they feel about gaming whether as an activity or a coping mechanism.

Subtheme 1.1 carried by emotions

The emotional landscape of gaming emerged as a defining aspect of gamer experiences, with feelings of accomplishment, disappointment, and empathy surfacing in response to both competitive outcomes and in-game events. Gamers described multiple ways in which they are carried by emotion throughout their gaming journeys. For instance, one gamer verbalized the following:

“I feel satisfied after playing a game. If I win, I feel accomplished; if I lose, I feel a little sad.” (P2, personal interview)

This highlights the bidirectional emotional impact of gaming outcomes. The language used, “accomplished” versus “a little sad” suggests that gaming serves as a microcosm for broader experiences of success and failure. The emotional responses are immediate and visceral, reinforcing the idea that gaming is an affectively charged activity rather than a neutral pastime. Similarly, another gamer noted,

“Winning feels like getting a Christmas present…. losing creates pressure.” (P7, personal interview)

Here, the metaphor of a Christmas presents amplifies the intensity of positive affect associated with winning. In contrast, the mention of “pressure” in the context of losing reveals the psychological stakes at play. This indicates the oscillation between exhilaration and tension that characterizes the gaming experience for many players. Another gamer quotes,

“If something happens in the game, like someone dies, it actually makes me feel bad.” (P11, personal interview)

The use of “actually makes me feel bad” signals a genuine affective response. This shows the immersive power of games to elicit real emotions in response to fictional scenarios.

Subtheme 1.2 a way to cope

Gamers reflect on how they actively turned to games as a means of emotional regulation and psychological support, considering gaming as a tool for managing challenging circumstances. A gamer quotes,

“Gaming is my main method to overcome stress. It is not a permanent solution, but it helps in the short term.” (P1, personal interview)

Gamers’ narrative positions gaming as a primary strategy for stress management. The acknowledgement that it is “not a permanent solution” but effective “in the short term” reflects a unique capability of gaming to provide immediate, albeit temporary, respite from everyday pressures. Similarly, another gamer described,

“I use games as a distraction from adverse situations at home. It helps me focus and stay calm.” (P3, personal interview)

Here, gaming is explicitly described as a form of distraction from difficult domestic circumstances. The activity becomes a buffer, enabling the gamer to redirect attention away from distressing realities and toward a more controlled and engaging environment. The phrase “helps me focus and stay calm” indicates that gaming facilitates emotional stabilization, providing a sense of order and tranquillity amidst external turmoil.

“I try to take something good out of every situation, even if it makes me feel bad.” (P11, personal interview)

Even when confronted with negative emotions, gamers seek to extract positive meaning or benefit from the experience. The process of finding “something good” in challenging moments points to the adaptive strategies that players may develop, using gaming as an opportunity for growth and emotional processing.

Subtheme 1.3 keep the body busy

For many, games were dynamic activities that stimulated cognitive effort and personal growth, both within and beyond the virtual world. A gamer quoted,

“Life is like a game…you need to keep trying and levelling up to succeed.” (P2, personal interview)

The use of metaphors such as “levelling up” encapsulates the idea of continuous self-improvement and perseverance. Gamer describes the iterative nature of games, marked by repeated attempts and incremental progress that mirror real-life challenges. This finding reinforces and promotes a mindset oriented toward persistence and resilience. Another gamer verbalized,

“The challenge in games is to better yourself… with each run. You are your own competitor.” (P3, personal interview)

Here, the gamer emphasizes the intrinsic motivation embedded in gaming. The focus shifts from external validation to self-competition, where the primary objective is personal development. The phrase “with each run” shows the cyclical process of learning, adapting, and striving for improvement. This perspective reveals how gaming serves as a platform for self-directed challenge and helps improve cognitive engagement. The notion of being “your own competitor” describes the internalized nature of this motivation, that is, that gaming cultivates a mindset of self-reflection and continual betterment. Similarly, another gamer quotes,

“Clearing levels in the game gives me motivation for real-life tasks.” (P10, personal interview)

The act of “clearing levels” serves as an incentive for addressing everyday responsibilities. This connection between virtual accomplishments and tangible motivation illustrates the permeable boundary between gaming and life.

Subtheme 1.4 figuring myself out

The interactive and immersive nature of games enables gamers to engage with their own identities, values, and choices in ways that feel authentic and meaningful. Through character development, narrative engagement, and the freedom to make decisions, gaming became a medium for introspection and growth. A gamer verbalized,

“You grow with the character... it’s an open world where you can do whatever you want.” (P4, personal interview)

This depicts the parallel between personal development and character progression within an open-world environment. The phrase “you grow with the character” suggests a process of mutual evolution, where the personal journey of the gamer is connected with that of their virtual avatar. The freedom to “do whatever you want” shows the agency and autonomy afforded by open-world games. This open-world gaming allowed gamers to experiment with different paths and possibilities. Similarly, another gamer quotes,

“It feels like it’s yourself who’s playing; it feels more personal than watching a movie.” (P9, personal interview)

The experience is described as “more personal,” which depicts direct involvement and identification with the in-game character. The sense of embodiment blurs the boundary between the player and the avatar, which makes the gaming experience a reflection of the self. A gamer noted,

“When I finish a game, I reflect on what happened and search for more games.” (P11, personal interview)

Reflection emerges as a key component of the gaming experience for gamers. The subsequent search for new games indicates a process of exploration and self-inquiry. Here, each gaming experience becomes an opportunity for understanding and growth. This cyclical pattern depicts the role of gaming as a key factor for continuous self-reflection. Another gamer verbalized,

“Games… now a day… let you make your own choices, creating your character and exploring freely.” (P12, personal interview)

The emphasis on choice and creation in contemporary games is central to gamers’ experience. The ability to “make your own choices” and “create your character” empowers gamers to project aspects of their identity into the game world. The freedom to “explore freely” reinforces the idea that gaming is not just about following a predetermined path; rather, it is now a process of actively shaping one’s own journey.

Organizing theme 2. how gaming shows up in the body

In this theme, the gamer described their experience related to physical sensations and bodily awareness and to what extent gaming can be both immersive and physically exhausting.

Subtheme 2.1 feeling it physically

The narratives emerging from gamers embodied the nature of gaming, highlighting its challenges and adaptive responses that shape gamers’ physical experiences. One gamer verbalized,

“Since I play at night until approximately 12 AM, it affects my sleep. I wake up thinking about the game I played.” (P1, personal interview)

This indicated that the mental stimulation of gaming that it carried over into rest periods potentially affected sleep quality and overall well-being. This highlights the cognitive and physical responses to gaming, where psychological engagement can manifest in bodily rhythms. Another gamer noted,

“Looking at a screen for too long strains my eyes, but I don’t feel other physical reactions.” (P8, personal interview)

Here, the gamer identifies eye strain as a primary physical outcome of prolonged gaming sessions. The specificity of this complaint is paired with the absence of other reported symptoms, as suggested by others. Similarly, a gamer quotes,

“Gaming becomes tiring after a while sitting on a chair with headphones on for too long.” (P12, personal interview)

Physical fatigue is a salient issue, particularly related to posture and extended periods of inactivity. The mention of “sitting on a chair with headphones on for too long” highlights the cumulative strain that can arise from the sedentary nature of gaming. This fatigue may also reflect the discomfort associated with immersion and reduced movement, which increase the need for bodily awareness during play.

Negative case analysis

While several gamers reported noticeable physical effects from gaming, others described little to no adverse bodily impact. These contrasting narratives reflect the diversity of experiences within the sample, with some gamers adopting proactive strategies or maintaining routines that mitigate potential physical strain. A gamer noted,

“I take breaks to ensure that there are no negative physical effects.” (P4, personal interview)

Gamers were aware of the importance of self-regulation during gaming. By intentionally taking breaks, the gamers actively manage their gaming habits to avoid physical discomfort or harm. There was a conscious effort to balance enjoyment with bodily well-being and that negative physical outcomes are not inevitable but can be prevented through mindful practices. Similarly, another gamer quotes,

“Physically, it has not truly affected me because I follow other routines like workouts.” (P11, personal interview)

For others, the absence of negative physical effects is attributed to a broader lifestyle that incorporates regular exercise and physical activity. Here, the gamer’s experience revealed that the impact of gaming on the body is mediated by overall health behaviors. The integration of workouts and other routines appears to buffer against the sedentary aspects of gaming. This allowed the gamers to engage more balanced and healthfully with play.

Subtheme 2.2 making time for it

Rather than viewing gaming as inherently disruptive or wasteful, many described it as a meaningful activity that coexists with other responsibilities. The following narratives from gamers illustrate the diverse ways in which they negotiate time for gaming within the context of their broader lives. One of the gamers quotes,

“I define gaming sessions as something that consumes our time but not necessarily wasting it.” (P1, personal interview)

The distinction drawn here recognizes the value of gaming, whether as a source of enjoyment, relaxation, or personal growth. For gamers, the act of playing is purposeful and justified and depicted as a positive reappraisal of how leisure time is spent. Similarly, another gamer quotes,

“I don’t use gaming as a way to procrastinate. I have other things for that, like reading or listening to music.” (P2, personal interview)

Gamers position gaming as a deliberate choice, distinct from other activities that might serve as distractions. This thought-making process reflects a sense of agency and self-awareness, making the gaming process an activity engaged in with intention and control rather than an escape from obligations. Another gamer verbalized,

“I game only when I have spare time and ensure it does not interfere with my tasks.” (P8, personal interview)

This narrative highlights the theme of balance. The gamer describes a disciplined approach to gaming, reserving it for periods of free time and prioritizing other responsibilities. Another gamer noted,

“Academics never truly bothered me because I got things done on time.” (P11, personal interview)

For some, effective time management skills ensure that gaming does not conflict with academic or professional commitments. Here, the gamer was confident in their ability to meet deadlines and fulfil obligations.

Subtheme 2.3 pulling in by the game

In this theme, gamers highlighted their experience with the sense of immersion that can evoke while they game. Many described being deeply absorbed in gameplay, to the point where the boundary between the virtual and real worlds becomes blurred. A gamer quotes,

“When I play with my friends, it feels like I am inside the game and completely forget it is a game.” (P1, personal interview)

Here, gamers highlighted the total absorption that can occur during gaming, especially in social contexts. The sensation of being “inside the game” reflects a level of engagement, where external distractions fade and the gamer becomes fully present within the game world. This immersive quality is so strong that the constructed nature of the experience is temporarily forgotten. Another gamer verbalized,

“The game is cyclical in nature. You try to escape hell each time, and each run is a challenge to improve.” (P3, personal interview)

The cyclical structure of the game is described as a continuous loop of challenge and self-improvement. The repetitive nature of gameplay draws the gamers back repeatedly, creating a rhythm of engagement that is both mentally and physically involved. Each “run” becomes an opportunity for mastery, and the drive to improve sustained attention and motivation, which increases the immersive experience. However, another gamer noted,

“Television is cold media... whereas in video games, it is more hands-on...” (P10, personal interview)

This contrasts the passive consumption of television with the active, embodied involvement of gaming. Describing video games as “more hands-on” indicates the physical and interactive demands of play. Unlike “cold media,” games require ongoing input and decision-making, which increase the sense of involvement and presence. This hands-on engagement is central to the feeling of being pulled into the game. Another gamer noted,

“I need to feel immersed in the story, curious about the ending, like a page-turner novel.” (P12, personal interview)

The narrative dimension of immersion is foregrounded here. Gamers liked the experience of reading a compelling novel. The desire to be “immersed in the story” and curiosity about the outcome drive sustained engagement, which makes the game world feel vivid and urgent.

Organizing theme 3. how gaming fits into life

Under this theme, gamers described how they used various strategies and self-regulation techniques to purposefully incorporate gaming into their daily routines.

Subtheme 3.1 what gaming means

Gamers had diverse and interesting perspectives on the meaning and significance of gaming in their lives. For some, gaming was a core part of their identity and social interactions; for others, it was a form of art, a source of cognitive development, or simply an enjoyable pastime. The following narratives highlight the range of meanings that gaming holds for individuals, as well as the complexities and responsibilities that come with its integration into daily life. A gamer verbalized,

“Friends know that I’m passionate about gaming. It shapes how they perceive me and what topics we discuss.” (P3, personal interview)

This reflects the social dimension of gaming. Gamers’ passion for gaming is not just about personal interest; rather, it features their social identity. Another gamer quotes,

“I think it is a medium of art in a way.” (P10, personal interview)

Here, gaming is elevated more than entertainment but to the status of an artistic medium. Gamer recognizes the creative and expressive potential of games that can evoke emotions, tell stories, and inspire reflection much like traditional forms of art. This perspective also highlights the cultural significance of gaming, positioning it as a legitimate and meaningful form of artistic engagement. Another gamer noted,

“It affected my academics during school but improved my overall cognitive skills.” (P10, personal interview)

This narrative has a rather complex and sometimes contradictory impact on gaming. While the gamer acknowledges that gaming had a negative effect on their academic performance at one point, they also credit it with its ability to improve their cognitive skills. This duality points to the many-sided influence of gaming, which can present both challenges and benefits depending on context and individual management.

Negative case analysis

While many gamers described gaming in positive or neutral terms, others raised concerns about its potential risks and the need for responsible engagement. These contrasting views reflect the other side of gaming, that is, the awareness of the complexities involved in integrating gaming into life. A gamer quotes,

“Virtual reality games can blur boundaries between real and virtual worlds, posing risks.” (P7, personal interview)

Gamer points to the potential dangers of immersive technologies, such as virtual reality. The blurring of boundaries between real and virtual experiences is seen as a risk. This finding also indicates that the psychological and perceptual effects of gaming warrant careful consideration, particularly as technology becomes more immersive. Another gamer noted,

“Gaming needs to be introduced responsibly to younger people to avoid negative effects.” (P9, personal interview)

Here, the emphasis is on the importance of guidance and moderation, especially for younger players. The gamer advocates for the responsible introduction of gaming to mitigate potential negative outcomes, such as excessive use or exposure to inappropriate content. This highlights the essential role of education and oversight in ensuring that gaming remains a positive and balanced part of life.

Subtheme 3.2 growing up gaming

Gamers also narrated how gaming has accompanied them through various stages of life, evolving in meaning, purpose, and frequency as they matured. The narratives shared by the gamers reveal that gaming is a dynamic thread woven through the experience of their lived experience, sometimes shifting in prominence but rarely disappearing altogether. A gamer quotes,

“I started gaming at a young age and have continued since; it has evolved with my life stages.” (P9, personal interview)

Here, the gamer reflects the continuity and adaptability of gaming across the lifespan. What began as an early interest has persisted, with the nature and context of gaming changing alongside personal development. Similarly, another gamer verbalized,

“I started gaming out of curiosity... now it’s more of an escape.” (P6, personal interview)

The gamer traces a transformation in the function of gaming. Initially, motivated by curiosity, their relationship with gaming has shifted toward using it as a form of escapism. This evolution describes how gaming transitions from fulfilling different psychological or emotional needs over time to adapting to the challenges and pressures of adulthood or changing environments. Another gamer quotes,

“Even though I game less now, it remains a significant part of my life.” (P9, personal interview)

Gaming can retain its importance even as the frequency of play diminishes. The enduring connection speaks to the deep-rooted role that gaming can play in shaping identity and providing continuity across life transitions.

Negative case analysis

However, not all gamers maintained high levels of gaming engagement as they grew older. Some described a marked reduction in their gaming habits, reflecting shifting priorities and the realities of adult life.

“With time, my frequency of gaming has reduced significantly.” (P8, personal interview)

Here, the natural ebb and flow of gaming involvement over the lifespan. As other commitments and interests take precedence, the time and energy devoted to gaming may decrease. This change does not necessarily signal a loss of interest but rather an adaptation to new life circumstances.

Subtheme 3.3 playing with others

A significant dimension of gamers’ experiences centered on the social connections and collaborative opportunities that gaming facilitates. Narratives shared by the gamers highlighted the social value of gaming, both in physical and virtual contexts. A gamer verbalized,

“My uncle had a cyber cafe. I used to play games with the people who came to the store and became friends with them.” (P3, personal interview)

Here, the gamer recollected memories of childhood and how gaming played a significant role in forming friendships in physical spaces. The communal environment of the cyber cafe provided opportunities to interact with a diverse group of people, turning shared gaming sessions into the foundation for lasting social bonds. This is a perfect example of how gaming can build community and belonging, especially in shared public settings. Similarly, another gamer noted,

“Gaming has been positive for social interactions... it helped me connect with others who share this interest.” (P5, personal interview)

The gamer emphasizes the positive impact of gaming on their social life. The shared interest in games acts as common ground, which makes it easier to form connections and engage in meaningful interactions. Therefore, gaming communities—whether online or offline—can be inclusive spaces for social engagement. Another gamer quotes,

“Online gaming helped me interact with strangers and improved my communication skills.” (P7, personal interview)

This narrative focused on the virtual realm, where online gaming becomes a platform for meeting new people and developing interpersonal abilities. The gamer notes an improvement in communication skills because of the collaborative and competitive nature of online games. In addition, this can enhance social competencies that are transferable beyond the gaming context. Similarly, another gamer noted,

“Playing games with my team builds collaboration.” (P7, personal interview)

Team-based gaming is highlighted here as a context for developing collaborative skills. Working together toward shared objectives requires coordination, trust, and effective communication. Rather than viewing gaming as a source of enjoyment, gamers considered it a practical environment for learning and practicing teamwork.

Subtheme 3.4 playing alone now

The role of gaming in gamers’ lives has shifted toward a more solitary activity with changing priorities and personal circumstances. The narratives below discuss the experience of solo gaming and how it relates to academic, social, and personal well-being.

“It doesn’t affect my academic or social life... I game in my own time.” (P4, personal interview)

Gamers highlighted the autonomy and control they maintain over their gaming habits. As they choose to play during their own time, they ensure that gaming does not interfere with academic or social responsibilities. Gamers find this a balanced integration of gaming into daily life, where it serves as a personal leisure activity rather than a source of conflict or distraction. Another gamer noted,

“Nowadays, I prefer to game alone, unlike earlier when I played with groups.” (P8, personal interview)

Gamer describes a transition from social to solitary gaming. This shift described the changes in personal preference, the availability of friends, or broader life circumstances. The preference for solo play also indicated a desire for independence and self-directed engagement, where gaming becomes a private retreat rather than a communal event. Similarly, another gamer verbalized,

“Gaming hasn’t significantly affected my social life, but it could have strengthened connections if I played more.” (P8, personal interview)

The gamers acknowledge that while solo gaming has not negatively impacted social relationships, it also represents a missed opportunity for deeper connection. Gamers are aware and recognize the potential of gaming as a social tool but choose to engage with it primarily on an individual level.

Discussion

These findings contribute to a unique aspect of gaming among young adults in India. It examined the emotional, cognitive, and personalized dimensions of the practice as reported by the gamers. In contrast to the reductive portrayals often found in clinical and public discourse [11], gaming in this context is neither trivial nor inherently problematic. Rather, it emerges as an adaptive and meaning-laden activity embedded within the broader psychosocial lives of young adults.

A unique finding concerns the affective landscape of gaming, where gamers describe a wide spectrum of emotional experiences, not limiting to pleasure or frustration, that unfold during play. Gaming appears to serve as a structured environment in which emotions are elicited, navigated, and processed in real time [15, 49]. The intensity and immediacy of these emotional dynamics position gaming as a context for affective learning [37]. These observations from this study’s gamers account, align with emerging work on emotion regulation in interactive media and suggest that gaming may contribute to the development of emotional literacy, particularly when accompanied by reflective engagement [25].

The use of gaming as a coping mechanism from this study's gamers warrants closer examination. Gamers reported turning to games to alleviate stress, boredom, or emotional distress, but their narratives reflected intentionality and information rather than avoidance or dependence [5]. Many distinguished between short-term distraction and maladaptive avoidance, recognizing the limits of gaming as a long-term regulatory strategy [4]. This metacognitive awareness is often overlooked in pathologizing narratives [45]. Our findings from this study with the 12 gamers therefore challenge binary models of “healthy” versus “problematic” use: high levels of engagement alone do not reliably predict dysfunction. Instead, the meaning attributed to gaming, its contextual embedding, and the degree of self-regulatory control are more critical determinants [32].

Cognitive engagement is another salient theme where gamers from this study frequently described gaming as a space for challenge, problem-solving, and iterative learning. The language these participants use emphasizes improvement, mastery, and personal growth, parallels those found in educational and performance psychology [1]. For some participants, gaming functions as a self-directed, intrinsically motivated domain of skill development [10, 21]. The gamers in our study also perceived a degree of transferability reporting that they drew mindsets, and confidence that are associated with gaming in other areas of their lives [48]. However, this transfer is not automatic. It seems to depend on the player’s capacity for abstraction and integration, pointing to the mediating role of reflection and narrative construction in how the studied gamers in the current study connected gaming experience to other personal and developmental areas in their lives [17, 35].

The physical dimension of gaming, while less extensively theorized in the literature, emerged as a meaningful aspect of gamers’ experiences in this particular population. Physical sensations ranging from arousal and tension to fatigue and discomfort are actively managed as perceived rather than passively endured [16]. Gamers describe developing personal strategies to monitor and respond to these bodily signals, suggesting that gaming prompts forms of bodily awareness and regulation [41]. For some gamers, the physical intensity of gaming enhances engagement; for others, it necessitates careful management to maintain balance [13]. These findings from the current study with the gamers highlighted the need to conceptualize gaming as one of the embodied activities, with implications for physical and psychological well-being with other time-tested activities.

Finally, the integration of gaming into gamers’ broader lives challenges the persistent stereotypes of gamers as socially disengaged or lacking in self-discipline. Most gamers from this study articulate a clear rationale for how gaming fits into their routines. It was perceived as often-balancing alongside academic, professional, and interpersonal commitments [42]. They describe exercising choice, setting limits, and negotiating trade-offs, demonstrating a level of agency and control that beliefs the notion of compulsive use [31]. This self-regulation is perceived by the gamers from this study to be grounded in an ongoing process of self-monitoring and value alignment.

Strength, limitations, and future directions

This study contributes to the literature on young adult gaming in several ways. The depth of qualitative data provides a participant-centered account of gaming as a multidimensional practice, integrating emotion, cognition, embodiment, and practical routines. By grounding findings in lived experiences, the study challenges simplistic risk–benefit narratives and advances a holistic, clinically relevant understanding of the role of gaming in young adulthood. The deliberate inclusion of participants with varied gaming habits and backgrounds further enhances contextual richness and supports the transferability of insights.

Moreover, the limitations point toward future research needs. Larger, more diverse, and longitudinal studies are needed to track how gaming experiences shift across life stages and under different cultural, socioeconomic, and individual conditions. Further, add to the limitations, gaming with only 2 h per day may not capture the deeper nuance of highly engaged or immersive gaming practices of the gamers. Capturing retrospective details through a video conferencing may also be one of the limitations. Our findings also underscore the need for refined assessment tools and methodologies capable of capturing the dynamic, multidimensional character of gaming, potentially through mixed-methods or cross-cultural designs with more and diverse sample from across India will provide more clarity on whether gaming improves cognitive engagement and foster transferable skills and develop emotional literacy. Addressing these gaps will clarify how gaming can be best supported or managed across clinical, educational, and policy contexts.

Implications

Our findings have implications for both theory and practice in understanding and supporting young adult gamers.

Theoretical implications

Our findings challenge the prevailing tendency in the literature to conceptualize gaming as a one-dimensional activity with uniform psychological outcomes [3]. The lived experiences documented in this study demonstrate that gaming is a context-dependent practice shaped by the dynamic interplay among affective, cognitive, motivational, physical, and practical domains. Future research should emphasize process-oriented and systems-based perspectives rather than risk–benefit binaries. By foregrounding the mutual reinforcement between emotional and cognitive processes, the regulatory role of bodily awareness, and the importance of practical integration, the model encourages a more focused understanding of gaming as a complex and adaptive psychosocial practice.

Practical implications

Clinically, these findings suggest that professionals should approach gaming from a more individualized and holistic perspective. Rather than pathologizing all high engagement or assuming that gaming is inherently problematic, clinicians are encouraged to assess the function and meaning of gaming within each young adult’s life [14]. Understanding the emotional, cognitive, and personalized dimensions of gaming can help identify adaptive uses such as coping, skill-building, and self-reflection, as well as potential risks. Interventions should support young adults in developing self-regulatory strategies and integrating gaming into a balanced routine while also addressing any maladaptive patterns or cooccurring difficulties. These findings are particularly relevant given recent findings that problematic gaming can cooccur with other mental health challenges, such as depression, anxiety, ADHD, and substance use [19]. In our study, we stressed the importance of not conflating high levels of engagement with dysfunction and highlighted the need for preventive strategies that promote self-awareness and healthy integration of gaming, especially among those who begin gaming at a younger age or face additional psychosocial stressors.

Conclusion

This study offers a holistic and clinically grounded account of gaming in young adulthood, demonstrating that gaming is best understood as a complex, dynamic process shaped by connections with emotional, cognitive, embodied, and practical factors. The findings challenge the simplistic risk‒benefit dichotomy frameworks and instead adopt a more person-centered approach that recognizes the agency and self-awareness of gamers. By foregrounding the lived experiences of young adults and proposing a multidimensional model, our findings provide a foundation for future studies and clinical interventions that are sensitive to the real-life contexts in which gaming occurs. The implications for both theory and practice are clear: a deeper, more refined understanding of gaming will better support young adults in navigating digital environments and integrating gaming into healthy, balanced lives.

Central Message

We present the first case of distal anastomotic new entry after uncovered nitinol arch stent use, treated with stent explant and frozen elephant trunk procedure.

Extended aortic arch repair for acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) may include the use of the Ascyrus Medical Dissection Stent (AMDS; Artivion) to mitigate organ malperfusion and promote favorable aortic remodeling. Although the Dissected Aorta Repair Through Stent Implantation (DARTS) trial showed no cases of distal anastomotic new entry (DANE) at 3 years,1 we report the first case of DANE after AMDS use in ATAAD.

Clinical Summary

A 51-year-old man with hypertension was transferred for emergent surgical repair of ATAAD. The patient had a 10-day history of tearing chest pain associated with cough, rhinorrhea, and fever. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) showed extension of the dissection flap to the iliac arteries, hemopericardium, a partially thrombosed false lumen, and left renal artery radiologic malperfusion. The patient underwent emergent ATAAD surgical repair with AMDS implantation and CTA on postoperative day (POD) 9 showed an intact repair, a well-deployed stent, and persistent dissection flap beyond the AMDS (Figure 1 and Video 1). Postoperatively, he developed persistent fever without a clear source and resistant hypertension for 3 weeks. Blood cultures, vasculitis workup, and inflammatory markers were negative, and he was treated with multiple antihypertensive medications. CTA performed on POD 30 showed evidence of DANE with a dissection flap extending into the innominate artery and a dilated aortic arch measuring 64 × 67 mm (Figure 2, Video 1). The patient underwent surgery 4 weeks after his index operation for stent explantation, total arch replacement, and frozen elephant trunk (FET) implantation. Surgical Technique

During the index operation, the right common femoral artery and vein were used for cardiopulmonary bypass and systemic cooling before we opened the pericardium. Because the aorta was inflamed and adherent to the pericardium, the aorta was not crossclamped. Deep hypothermic circulatory arrest was initiated when the nasopharyngeal temperature reached 20 °C and myocardial protection was achieved with intermittent retrograde blood microplegia. The aorta was opened 1 cm below the innominate artery and no entry tear was identified in the ascending aorta or the aortic arch up to zone 3. A 55- to 55-mm AMDS prosthesis was sized preoperatively and implanted at zone 0 and the AMDS felt collar was secured to the aortic wall using an external felt strip. A 28-mm Dacron graft was sutured to the felt-aortic wall-AMDS felt collar sandwich, and systemic circulation was restored after clamping the graft through the perfusion limb. Proximal work included aortic valve resuspension and proximal anastomosis to the Dacron graft in the usual fashion. Institutional review board approval was not required. The patient provided informed verbal and written consent for the publication of data included in this case report.

During redo operation, an 8-mm Dacron graft was anastomosed to the right common carotid artery for arterial inflow, thereby avoiding the need to clamp the dissected innominate artery, and the right femoral vein was cannulated. The operation was performed under moderate hypothermic circulatory arrest and antegrade cerebral perfusion. Antegrade cold intermittent blood microplegia was used for myocardial protection. After circulatory arrest, the right carotid was clamped for antegrade cerebral perfusion, and the innominate artery and left carotid were stapled proximally and anastomosed using 14-mm and 8-mm Dacron grafts in a Y configuration. The graft was clamped proximally, and the carotid clamp was removed initiating bilateral antegrade cerebral perfusion. The left subclavian artery was left in situ due to depth and an extra-anatomical bypass was performed. The distal aortic anastomosis was opened, and the DANE was identified on the anterior portion between the 11- and 2-o'clock positions. The aortic wall was fragile, and passing needles through the felt collar proved difficult; therefore, the decision was made to remove the felt anastomosis and implant the FET at zone 2. Because the stent was not embedded in the aortic wall, removal of the distal anastomosis caused the stent to collapse, and it was therefore explanted. A 30-mm × 40-mm × 100-mm Thoraflex hybrid prosthesis was sized for a distal landing zone of 38 mm and implanted in the usual fashion. A proximal graft-to-graft anastomosis was performed and the Y-graft to the arch vessels was anastomosed (Video 1). This was followed by left common carotid to left subclavian artery bypass and staged thoracic endovascular aortic repair and stent-assisted balloon-induced intimal disruption and relamination in aortic dissection repair (ie, STABILISE).

Comment

The AMDS is designed to prevent DANE through incorporating a felt collar at the distal anastomosis when deployed at zone 0. Although this design theoretically protects fragile aortic tissues at the anastomosis, it may create a focal point of increased wall tension when the false lumen is pressurized. In this patient, we hypothesize that prolonged and refractory hypertension caused false lumen pressurization through distal re-entry tears (Figure 3). This may be exacerbated by inadequate distal fenestrations, ultimately resulting in DANE. Alternatively, typical DANE progression beginning at zone 0 anastomosis and propagating distally remains plausible. Our case also demonstrates the feasibility of safe AMDS explanation within 30 days because the stent is not embedded within the aortic wall. In the DARTS trial, 70% of patients had secondary re-entry tears in the arch or descending aorta.3 At 3 years, 40% of patients had a patent false lumen at zone 1, and 32% and 30% at zones 2 and 3, respectively.1 Early results of the ProspEctive, Single ARm, Multi-center Clinical InveStigation to EValuatE the Safety and Effectiveness of AMDS in the TREatment of Acute DeBakey Type I Dissection (PERSEVERE) trial in patients with malperfusion demonstrated 20% false lumen patency at 30 days with 3 reoperations due to progression of aortic disease.4 The AMDS is designed to decompress the false lumen using the radial force of the bare metal stent; however, whether this radial force is sufficient maintain false lumen obliteration remains uncertain. Finally, we recommend that in patients with an extensive distal entry tear resulting a retrograde type A aortic dissection, AMDS use should be avoided in favor of upfront total arch replacement when indicated.

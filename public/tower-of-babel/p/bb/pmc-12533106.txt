EBV+ polymorphic B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder, NOS: a single-center study of a newly recognized pathologic entity
Authors: Suheil Albert Atallah-Yunes, Thomas M. Habermann, Matthew J. Rees, Lay She Ng, Urshila Durani, Yucai Wang, Rebecca L. King, Arushi Khurana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
EBV-positive polymorphic B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder, NOS (poly B-LPD), is a newly defined entity in the 2022 International Consensus Classification of mature lymphoid neoplasms. Its clinical behavior and optimal treatment approach remain poorly characterized. We conducted a retrospective study of 31 patients diagnosed with EBV+ poly B-LPD at Mayo Clinic (2003–2024), excluding transplant recipients. Median age was 56 years; 71% had extranodal involvement, 68% presented with stage III/IV disease, and 52% had autoimmune conditions. Thirty-five percent (n = 11) were on immunosuppressive therapy at diagnosis, all of whom underwent reduction of immunosuppression (RIS); five underwent RIS alone, achieving four complete responses including two with central nervous system (CNS) involvement. Rituximab was effective in patients with or without prior immunosuppression. Histologic transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) or emergence of T-cell lymphomas occurred in immunochemotherapy non-responders or at relapse, emphasizing the role of re-biopsy in this subset of patients. At a median follow-up of 6 years, median event-free (EFS) and overall survival (OS) were 8.5 and 8.7 years, respectively. EFS was not significantly influenced by increasing IPI scores (p = 0.09), CNS involvement (p = 0.5), or immunosuppression at diagnosis (p = 0.2). There was a trend towards improved OS in patients who were on immunosuppressive therapy at diagnosis, however, this was not statistically significant, p = 0.054. This is the first study to characterize EBV+ poly B-LPD within the ICC 2022 framework. Larger studies are needed to validate these findings, define prognostic markers and guide therapy.

Introduction

EBV-positive polymorphic B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder, NOS (poly B-LPD), is a newly recognized entity in the 2022 International Consensus Classification (ICC) of mature lymphoid neoplasms. It encompasses EBV-driven B-cell proliferations that do not meet criteria for overt lymphoma, providing a unifying diagnostic term that can be used in various clinical settings. Histologically, these lesions show architectural distortion and a polymorphic infiltrate of B cells at various stages of maturation, often including Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg–like cells, admixed with reactive T cells and histiocytes. Histologically it must be differentiated from EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, NOS (DLBCL, NOS), which displays a more overtly malignant and monomorphic population of large cells [1–3].

EBV+ poly B-LPDs may arise with or without identifiable immunodeficiency, including primary or iatrogenic immunodeficiency, autoimmune diseases, HIV infection, or presumed immune senescence. Polymorphic post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorders (PTLDs) remain a separate entity under the ICC 2022 classification [1, 3]. A recent international workshop characterized 38 EBV-positive polymorphic B-cell proliferations across diverse settings [2]. However, the cohort was enriched with HIV-associated cases and PTLDs for which treatment strategies are more clearly defined and supported through the PTLD-1/PTLD-2 trials, incorporating immunosuppression reduction (RIS) and a risk-stratified sequential therapy (RSST) approach [4–7]. Polymorphic B-cell lymphoproliferations are also described under the category of other iatrogenic immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders (OI-LPD) recognized by ICC, which includes a broader histologic spectrum, and is intended to capture patients with non-transplant associated iatrogenic immune suppression [1, 8]. However, prior studies have largely focused on methotrexate-associated rheumatoid arthritis cases, often incorporating EBV-negative cases or EBV-positive mucocutaneous ulcers, now a distinct ICC 2022 entity [9–11].

Adding further complexity, EBV+ poly B-LPD is not separately defined in the 5th edition of WHO classification but is acknowledged under a broader category of EBV-positive lymphoid disorders in various settings of known or presumed immune suppression. As a newly established entity in the current ICC 2022 framework, the clinical behavior and optimal management of EBV+ poly B-LPD remain unclear. To address this gap of knowledge, we report a retrospective cohort of 31 patients with EBV+ poly B-LPD diagnosed at Mayo Clinic, Rochester with a focus on treatment patterns and outcomes.

Methods

This retrospective study was approved by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board. We initially identified 56 cases with a diagnosis of EBV+ poly B-LPD at Mayo Clinic, Rochester from 2003 to 2024 utilizing the Mayo Clinic Lymphoma Database and electronic medical record system. We excluded post-transplant cases and six with concurrent T-cell lymphoma. Of the remaining 49 cases, 25 met diagnostic criteria for EBV+ poly B-LPD based on retrospective pathology review of slides. An additional 9 cases were included for which original slides were unavailable for re-review but pathology reports provided sufficient evidence to support the original diagnosis. Three of these 34 patients had concurrent EBV+ DLBCL, NOS and were excluded, yielding a final cohort of 31 patients. Data were analyzed in R (v4.4.1) and group comparisons were performed using Pearson χ-squared and Kruskal–Wallis tests for nominal and continuous variables respectively. Event free survival (EFS) was calculated from the date of diagnosis to date of disease progression, relapse, refractory disease or death, censored on the date of last follow up. Overall survival (OS) was defined from the time of diagnosis to date of death, censored on the date of last follow-up. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate EFS and OS, survival distributions were compared using the log-rank test.

Results

Patient and disease characteristics

Table 1 summarizes patient and disease characteristics. The median age was 56 years (IQR, 46–64), and 48% were female. Most patients (n = 21, 68%) presented with stage III/IV disease. Extranodal involvement was seen in 71% (n = 22), with 35% (n = 11) having involvement of the lung and 23% (n = 7) with central nervous system (CNS) involvement. Among the seven patients with CNS involvement, five had parenchymal disease, including three with concurrent leptomeningeal involvement. Two patients had leptomeningeal disease alone. Cranial nerve involvement was present in three patients. Elevated LDH was noted in 66% (n = 19) of tested patients, and 45% (n = 14) had B symptoms. All 31 cases were EBER-positive, and CD20 expression was identified in 30 (97%) cases. An autoimmune condition was present in 16 (52%) patients. Treatments outcomes: patients on immunosuppressive therapy at diagnosis

Treatment approaches are summarized in Fig. 1. Thirty-five percent of patients (n = 11) were receiving immunosuppressants at the time of EBV+ poly B-LPD diagnosis. The duration of immunosuppressive therapy prior to diagnosis ranged from two months to 21 years. Only two patients had received immunosuppressants for 2–3 months, while the remaining nine had been on treatment for more than 6 months. All patients on immunosuppressants underwent full RIS, which was not resumed. Forty-five percent (n = 5) were managed with RIS alone, resulting in four complete responses (CR) without subsequent relapse. Only one patient developed progressive disease after RIS alone and was found to have classic Hodgkin lymphoma–like features on repeat biopsy; this patient was treated successfully with R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone). Notably, two of the five patients treated with RIS alone had CNS involvement and both achieved CR without requiring further therapy. Frontline treatment in the other six patients who did not receive RIS alone included rituximab plus RIS (n = 4), nasal mass resection plus RIS (n = 1), and DA-EPOCH-R (dose-adjusted etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and rituximab) with radiation plus RIS (n = 1). Among the four patients who received rituximab plus RIS, fifty percent (n = 2) achieved CR after 4 weekly doses without further treatment. Of the two patients who had disease progression with rituximab, one had isolated CNS disease and was treated with methotrexate-based regimen followed by whole-brain radiotherapy and temozolomide maintenance, sustaining remission at six years. The other patient had disease progression through R-CHP, after which polatuzumab was added (pola-R-CHP), and later nivolumab. A subsequent biopsy showed DLBCL, and the patient achieved a CR after three cycles of glofitamab plus GemOx (gemcitabine and oxaliplatin), which remains ongoing. Treatments outcomes: patients not on immunosuppressive therapy at diagnosis

Sixty-five percent (n = 20) were not on immunosuppressants at the time of EBV+ poly B-LPD diagnosis. This group included one patient with a remote history of receiving cyclophosphamide 22 years prior to diagnosis who achieved CR following methotrexate-based regimen (MRT) and consolidation with high-dose chemotherapy (HDCT) and autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT) for systemic and CNS disease without requiring subsequent therapy. Among the remaining patients (n = 19) without any history of immunosuppression, initial treatments included rituximab monotherapy (n = 11), immunochemotherapy (n = 3), CNS-directed chemotherapy (n = 3), observation (n = 1), and hospice care (n = 1). Treatment courses are depicted in Fig. 1. Of the 11 patients treated with rituximab, nine (82%) achieved a CR. The intended rituximab course was four weekly doses; however, four received an extended course of eight doses. The two patients who did not respond to rituximab underwent re-biopsy, which revealed a diagnosis of T-cell lymphoma in both cases: angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL) and unclassified T-cell LPD. Of the nine patients who responded to rituximab, four experienced relapse. Two were successfully re-treated with a second course of rituximab; a third also received a second course of rituximab with response not yet known; and another was diagnosed with nodal T follicular helper (TFH) cell lymphoma at relapse and is receiving treatment. Among the three patients who received frontline immunochemotherapy (R-CHOP, n = 2; BR [bendamustine, rituximab], n = 1), all achieved CR. However, two experienced relapse: one with EBV+ DLBCL, NOS and one with a T-cell LPD. The patient with DLBCL received R-ICE (rituximab, ifosfamide, carboplatin, and etoposide) and ASCT, achieved CR, but died of infection 7.5 months later. Three patients received CNS-directed therapy (MRT, n = 2; MPV, n = 1). Outcomes were generally less favorable than in patients with CNS disease and prior immunosuppression. Supplementary Fig. 1 summarizes the sequence of CNS-directed therapy and outcomes in all study patients with CNS involvement (23%, n = 7).

EBV viremia and treatment response

EBV viremia was detectable at baseline in 14 of the 22 patients tested (64%). Among viremic patients, follow-up testing was available in eight. Of these, seventy-five percent (n = 6) cleared EBV following initial therapy (rituximab in 5, MRT in 1), and all achieved CR. However, two of these patients experienced relapse, with re-emergence of EBV viremia documented at 4 and 9.5 months. The remaining two patients with follow-up testing did not clear EBV: one progressed on rituximab, and the other relapsed after an initial CR lasting 8 months. Supplementary Fig. 2 summarizes peripheral blood EBV PCR results before and after therapy.

Survival outcomes

Median follow-up was 6.0 years (95% CI, 4.0–11.0). Median EFS and OS were 8.5 years (95% CI, 1.4–NA) and 8.7 years (95% CI, 8.5–NA), respectively (Fig. 2). The EFS rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 71%, 61%, and 52%, respectively, while OS rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 94%, 83%, and 83%, respectively. Discussion

Our study is the first to specifically examine EBV+ poly B-LPD under the ICC 2022 framework, offering insights into its clinical presentation, treatment patterns and outcomes, which have remained poorly understood due to the rarity and recent recognition of this entity [1–3]. Several key observations emerge. The median age at diagnosis was younger than typically seen in DLBCL [12, 13]. However, most patients presented with high-risk DLBCL like features, including extranodal involvement and advanced-stage disease [14, 15]. Despite these aggressive features, a subset of patients responded well to conservative treatment approaches. Among patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy, RIS alone led to durable remissions including cases with CNS involvement. This contrasts with CNS disease in PTLD, where RIS alone is often insufficient [16]. We also observed that rituximab monotherapy was effective even in patients without prior immunosuppression and was successfully reused at relapse, supporting its potential role as a low-intensity treatment in this disease entity. In terms of survival, patients on immunosuppressive therapy at diagnosis had numerically more favorable OS, although this finding did not reach statistical significance.

In our cohort, the emergence of other aggressive lymphomas was observed in a subset of patients following treatment failure. DLBCL, NOS developed in two patients, while four others were diagnosed with T-cell LPDs, either after primary progression on immunochemotherapy or at relapse (Fig. 4). Notably, all T-cell LPDs occurred in patients without prior immunosuppressant exposure. Our findings also suggest a potential role for EBV viral load monitoring in EBV+ poly B-LPD. EBV viremia was detectable in approximately two-thirds of tested patients and clearance consistently corresponded with clinical response. In contrast, persistent or re-emergent viremia was associated with treatment failure or relapse. Although limited by small sample size, these observations mirror patterns seen in EBV+ PTLD, where viral kinetics have been shown to reflect disease activity and treatment response [17, 18].

Limitations of this study include its retrospective nature and modest sample sized. Additionally, we were unable to assess the efficacy of cellular immunotherapies, as none of the patients received chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy. One patient with DLBCL transformation was treated with the bispecific antibody glofitamab in combination with GemOx and achieved a CR after three cycles; treatment is ongoing. Only two patients received ASCT, further limiting analysis of consolidation strategies. The small sample size also limited our ability to evaluate prognostic factors or treatment outcomes based on specific autoimmune conditions or immunosuppressants. Despite these limitations, our study represents the first systematic characterization of EBV+ poly B-LPD within the ICC 2022 framework, with most cases having undergone expert hematopathology slide re-review allowing for consistent classification and exclusion of mimics such as EBV+ DLBCL, NOS or EBV+ mucocutaneous ulcer.

In summary, EBV+ poly B-LPD represents a clinically heterogeneous and understudied entity. Our findings provide novel insights into the clinical course and management of this newly recognized entity under the ICC 2022. Larger, prospective and multi-institutional studies are needed to better define prognostic markers, guide therapy, and further characterize EBV+ poly B-LPD, NOS, especially those arising in the absence of known immunosuppression.

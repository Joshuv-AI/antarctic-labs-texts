Inhaled beclomethasone in the treatment of early COVID-19: a phase 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised trial
Authors: Mettananda Chamila, Chatura Peiris, Dharani Abeyrathna, Aloka Gunasekera, Thimira Egodage, Channaka Danthanarayana, Arunaselam Pathmeswaran, Channa Ranasinha
Published: 2024

Introduction

Inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) in early COVID-19 has not reached treatment guidelines as there is some conflicting evidence. It has been demonstrated that early administration of inhaled budesonide reduced the likelihood of needing urgent medical care and reduced time to recovery in early COVID-191. However, another study found that Inhaled budesonide improved the time to recovery only in high-risk patients.2 Further, neither of these studies were double-blind or placebo-controlled.

Therefore, we studied if early initiation of ICS in patients with asymptomatic or mild-to-moderate COVID-19 reduces the disease progression in a randomised, double-blind placebo-controlled trial (SLCTR/2021/017, 04/07/2021).

Material and methods

We treated 385 adults with asymptomatic or mild-moderate symptomatic COVID-19, within the first 7 days of COVID-19, admitted to a COVID-treatment unit with beclomethasone 1,200 mg or placebo until they reached the primary endpoint or were discharged (Fig 1). The primary endpoint was the progression of the disease to severe stage, defined by a fall in oxygen saturation<94% and initiation of any of the three treatment escalation steps; initiation of supplemental O2 therapy, intravenous/oral dexamethasone, or transfer to a higher-tier healthcare facility and the secondary outcome was time to self-reported clinical recovery. Data were analysed on an intention-to-treat basis.

Results and discussion

The primary endpoint was reached by 24 (12.4%) participants in the beclomethasone group and 26 (13.5%) participants in the placebo group (RR 0.90, 95% CI: 0.50 – 1.63; p = 0·763). The time to self-reported clinical recovery did not show a significant difference between the two groups (log-rank test P = 0·41); (Fig 2) However, beclomethasone compared to placebo reduced the time to self-reported clinical recovery by one day in patients with moderate disease at initiation of treatment (p = 0.049).

The PRINCIPLE trial,2 the largest randomised trial to assess inhaled budesonide for community treatment of COVID-19, found that Inhaled budesonide improved the time to recovery in people with COVID-19 in the community who are at high risk of complications, agreeing with our finding. It also showed a chance of reducing hospital admissions or deaths, but it did not meet the superiority threshold,2 which was in favour of our findings.

The STOIC trial,1 an unblinded studies of ICS in early COVID-19 in the community, showed marginal clinical benefits in the likelihood of needing urgent medical care and time to recovery, but there was no significant difference in the rate of hospitalisation or deaths. Our study did not show significant evidence for a reduction in disease progression to severe disease.

Conclusion

Inhaled beclomethasone in early COVID-19 did not reduce the progression of the disease to the severe stage but reduced the time to self-reported clinical recovery only in patients with moderate disease on presentation.

Hidden in the Biliary Tree: Low‐Grade Fibromyxoid Sarcoma of the Common Bile Duct
Authors: Vladimir Prochazka, Jakub Vlazny, Tomas Andrasina, Lumir Kunovsky
Published: 2026

A 62‐year‐old female with a history of open cholecystectomy and left kidney surgery for urolithiasis was followed for recurrent urinary tract infections. A non‐contrast CT performed 9 months before jaundice onset revealed an incidental calcified subhepatic lesion suggestive of an ectopic gallbladder with cholelithiasis despite prior cholecystectomy. As liver tests were normal and no biliary dilation was present, no further evaluation was performed.

After development of painless obstructive jaundice, a contrast‐enhanced CT demonstrated a subhepatic mass (51 × 46 × 54 mm) located at the level of the mid common bile duct with proximal biliary dilation (Figure 1). Imaging findings, including calcifications and inhomogenous post‐contrast enhancement, suggested intraluminal germ cell tumor or lymphadenopathy. ERCP revealed smooth mid bile duct stenosis; biopsies were obtained and a plastic stent inserted. Histology showed biliary epithelium with dysplasia without malignancy. Tumor markers were normal.

CT image in the coronal plane, obtained in the portovenous phase. The CT demonstrates an intraluminal lesion (red arrows) within the common bile duct with marked, inhomogeneous contrast enhancement and calcifications. The common bile duct is markedly dilated (up to approximately 42 mm).

Following multidisciplinary discussion, surgical resection was performed after jaundice resolution. Intraoperatively, a mass within the hepatoduodenal ligament infiltrated the mid common bile duct without pancreatic involvement. Frozen section suggested mesenchymal tumor. Common bile duct resection without lymphadenectomy and Roux‐en‐Y hepaticojejunostomy were performed (Figure 2).

Specimen after surgical resection: The tumor was spherical, measuring 58 mm in diameter, with a thin capsule on the surface. On sectioning, it appeared whitish, with a solid consistency and a lobulated, fascicular architecture. Note the intact bile duct coursing through the tumor (indicated by a metal probe).

Postoperative recovery was uneventful. Final histopathology confirmed a low‐grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (LGFMS) of the common bile duct (Figure 3) with negative margins, immunohistochemical staining confirmed the diagnoses, demonstrating expression of MUC4 (Figure 4), BCL2 and CD99. The patient remains disease‐free 7 years postoperatively.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E), Low‐grade fibromyxoid sarcoma is composed of bland spindle cells arranged in short fascicles or in a whorled pattern. The lesion is characterized by areas of myxoid and collagenized stroma, frequently exhibiting an abrupt transition between these two regions. (1) Biliary epithelium, (2) Sarcoma infiltrating (bile duct wall, 3) Peribiliary glands.

Immunohistochemical staining (IHC): cytoplasmic expression of MUC4, which is highly sensitive and specific for low‐grade fibromyxoid sarcoma.

LGFMS is a rare malignant soft tissue tumor with deceptively benign histology and potential for recurrence and metastasis [1, 2]. Hepatopancreatobiliary involvement is exceptionally rare. Radical surgical resection remains the treatment of choice [3, 4].

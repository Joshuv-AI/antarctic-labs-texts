Simultaneous Determination of Pyrroloquinoline Quinone Redox Species via a Dual-Mode High-Performance Liquid Chromatography Method
Authors: Nur Syafiqah Mohamad Ishak, Mai Nakamura, Kazuto Ikemoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a redox-active cofactor that exists in the oxidized and reduced forms, with the reduced species being particularly significant for biological activity. However, accurate quantification of the reduced form in complex matrices remains technically challenging due to its instability, low solubility, and high susceptibility to atmospheric oxidation. A robust and versatile high-performance liquid chromatography method featuring two complementary analytical modes was established in this study to overcome these limitations. The “Native mode” uses a strongly acidic eluent to suppress oxidation during analysis, enabling simultaneous quantification of both redox forms and determination of their native distribution. Conversely, the “Total mode” measures the total PQQ after pretreatment with ascorbic acid and γ-cyclodextrin, which converts PQQ species to a stabilized reduced form and improves solubility. Method validation demonstrated excellent linearity (R2 = 0.9996), low detection limits (limit of detection: 0.20 mg/L; limit of quantification: 0.50 mg/L), and high precision (relative standard deviation <3%). The method also achieved recoveries of 99–101% in commercial beverages and enabled detection of biologically generated reduced PQQ in yeast culture. This dual-mode, redox-specific approach provides a reliable tool for industrial quality control and offers a useful platform for investigating PQQ redox dynamics in complex systems.

Introduction

Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a redox-active quinone cofactor that has attracted significant attention because of its diverse physiological roles, including mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant defense, and neuroprotection. − Accordingly, PQQ has emerged as a prominent functional ingredient in nutritional supplements and fortified beverages. Beverages, in particular, represent an attractive delivery format because they are palatable and readily tailored to consumer preferences; however, formulating PQQ in aqueous solutions presents stability challenges over extended shelf lives. In commercial products, PQQ is typically present as oxidized PQQ (PQQox; Figure A). Its reduced counterpart, a quinolinol structure (reduced PQQ (PQQred); Figure A), is generated via a two-electron reduction reaction. The redox state of PQQ is therefore a key factor in product formulation. The quinone moiety of PQQox is highly reactive and can undergo acetalization and aldol reactions, as well as irreversible reactions with amino acids to form imidazopyrroloquinoline quinones (IPQ). , In contrast, PQQred can coexist with amino acids without these reactions provided it remains unoxidized; furthermore, it exhibits strong antioxidant activity. , Despite the importance of monitoring PQQ redox species, the quantification of PQQred is analytically challenging because it readily undergoes atmospheric oxidation and has low solubility. In many food products, PQQred is generated in situ from PQQox by reducing agents such as ascorbic acid (vitamin C (VC)), a common additive used for deoxygenation. Existing analytical approaches often rely on indirect strategies, including conversion of PQQred to PQQox via air oxidation or low-specificity colorimetric assays. These differences in chemical reactivity and antioxidant properties suggest that the two forms may contribute differently to biological activity. Notably, no standardized high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) method capable of effectively separating and stabilizing PQQox and PQQred for simultaneous quantification currently exists. Consequently, most existing protocols fail to reflect the true redox state, and therefore the potential biological activity, of PQQ in real-world products. To address these limitations, a simple, robust, and redox-specific quantification method is required. A validated HPLC-ultraviolet (UV) method that directly detects PQQ redox species without complex derivatization was developed in this study (Figure B). The method comprises two modes: a “Native mode” that uses a strongly acidic eluent to determine the PQQ redox ratio and a “Total mode” that quantifies the total PQQ after stabilizing the reduced form with VC and γ-cyclodextrin (γ-CD). This dual-mode methodology provides a practical and reliable solution for both industrial quality control and physiological research.

Results

Analytical Strategy

To enable comprehensive evaluation of PQQ, two complementary analytical strategies were developed, as schematically illustrated in Figure B. The first approach, termed “Native mode,” separately quantifies PQQox and PQQred, enabling determination of the redox ratio and thereby providing essential insights into the reactivity and chemical state of PQQ under defined conditions. The second approach, termed “Total mode,” converts all PQQ species to the reduced form for total quantification. Unlike conventional methods, the Total mode ensures accurate measurement even in the presence of interfering reducing agents, increasing its practicality for industrial applications and product development.

Native Mode: Separation of PQQ Redox Forms by HPLC

In conventional analyses using weakly acidic eluents, PQQox and PQQred are poorly resolved, and PQQred often undergoes spontaneous oxidation. Furthermore, VC, commonly used as a reducing agent in food products, typically elutes near the PQQ peaks, causing significant interference and reducing analytical specificity (Figure A).

Chromatographic separation and UV–Vis spectra of PQQox and PQQred. Representative HPLC chromatograms obtained with the previous method (A) and the method developed in this study (B); the left panel in each chromatogram shows the PQQox standard (∼2.5 min), whereas the right panel shows the chromatogram after the reduction of PQQ to PQQred by VC (∼2.8 min). In (B), PQQred (∼10 min) is clearly separated from PQQox (∼6 min). C) UV–Vis absorption spectra collected from the PQQox and PQQred peaks in chromatogram (B), confirming their redox states: PQQox exhibits a maximum absorbance at 252 nm, whereas PQQred exhibits a distinct peak at 318 nm.

To address these limitations, a strongly acidic mobile phase was employed to stabilize PQQred by suppressing oxidation and proton dissociation. This approach enhanced the physicochemical contrast between PQQox and PQQred and enabled effective separation on an octadecylsilane (ODS) column. Although both acetonitrile and methanol are commonly used in HPLC, methanol was selected due to its higher biodegradability. Under optimized conditions, PQQred eluted as a sharp peak at approximately 10 min, whereas PQQox appeared at approximately 6 min (Figure B). Peak identities were confirmed via distinct ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectra: PQQox exhibited a maximum at approximately 250 nm, while PQQred exhibited a characteristic peak at approximately 320 nm (Figure C). Notably, VC eluted well before both PQQ species, and no coelution from matrix components was observed, supporting the high specificity of the method. Importantly, the small peak observed near the PQQox retention time after reduction (Figure B; PQQ disodium + VC) was attributed to the pretreatment reagents rather than residual PQQox, based on its distinct UV spectrum and its presence in the pretreatment blank.

For total PQQ quantification, all PQQ species were converted to PQQred prior to HPLC analysis to enable single-point calibration. Figure A schematically illustrates the principle of the Total mode pretreatment. Briefly, PQQox is reduced with excess VC, followed by complexation with γ-CD.

Although PQQred is chemically more stable under strongly acidic conditions, it exhibits a remarkably low aqueous solubility at a low pH. In preliminary experiments, the solubility decreased markedly below pH 3.8, with measured values of 12, 14, and 50 mg/L at pH 2.8, 3.1, and 3.4, respectively (Figure B). Accordingly, visible precipitation was frequently observed during VC–mediated reduction under acidic conditions. To address this limitation, γ-CD was evaluated to increase solubility via inclusion complex formation. , The addition of 10% (w/w) γ-CD to the pretreatment solution significantly improved solubility (Figure C), increasing the PQQred concentrations to 100, 127, and 158 mg/L at pH 2.8, 3.1, and 3.4, respectively (Figure B). Solubility was determined by preparing the test solution, removing insoluble material by centrifugation, and quantifying the dissolved fraction based on UV absorbance.

Based on these findings, a pretreatment protocol was established using 5% (w/w) VC and 10% (w/w) γ-CD (40 °C, ≥1 h). This formulation provided near-quantitative recovery (100%) and excellent stability after 24 h (101%) (Table ). Comparatively, VC alone yielded only an 11% initial recovery, which further decreased to 3% after 24 h, whereas the untreated control showed no detectable PQQred. Recovery in the Total mode was calculated relative to the initial PQQ content, based on the expected 1:1 conversion of PQQox to PQQred under the reduction conditions. Complete solubilization was operationally confirmed by the absence of visible precipitation and the near-quantitative recovery obtained.

Total Mode: Method Validation

Method validation was primarily conducted for PQQred using the Total mode, which represents the main quantitative approach in this study. In contrast, under the Native mode, repeatability and calibration were evaluated separately for PQQox, with additional data provided in the Supporting Information. To evaluate the quantitative performance, a calibration curve was constructed using PQQ disodium standards (2–80 mg/L) treated with the optimized γ-CD/VC mixture. The resulting curve exhibited excellent linearity, described by the equation y = 13514x – 27796, with a correlation coefficient of R2 = 0.9996 (Figure A). The calibration curve and corresponding data (Table S1) represent intraday measurements. The relative standard deviation (RSD) across the tested concentrations remained below 3% (2.63–2.96%), except for the lowest concentration (2 mg/L), which showed a slightly higher but still acceptable RSD of 4.81% (Table S1). The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were 0.20 and 0.50 mg/L, respectively.

Reactivity of PQQ with Beverage Ingredients

The reactivity of PQQox with common nutritional ingredients (leucine, glucose, and erythritol) was evaluated in the Native mode by monitoring redox changes (Table , Figure S2). These compounds were selected because they are widely used, representative food components: leucine as a branched-chain amino acid, glucose as a carbonyl-containing sugar, and erythritol as a sugar alcohol. By clarifying the reactivity of these representative compounds, the reactivity of many other structurally related food components can be inferred. In samples containing PQQ and VC but lacking γ-CD, the total PQQ decreased, consistent with precipitation of the generated PQQred. Leucine alone also decreased the PQQox levels, likely via the formation of IPQ; however, the addition of VC improved PQQ recovery by maintaining PQQ in the reduced state. Notably, even in the absence of VC, the presence of leucine, glucose, or erythritol partially reduced PQQ to PQQred over time.

To assess robustness, the protocol was applied in the Total mode to several commercial beverages spiked with PQQ (Figures B and S3). Recoveries for samples 1 and 2 were 100% and 99%, respectively, with excellent repeatability (RSD < 1.0%) (Table ). These findings support the use of the method for routine quality control and label verification of functional beverages.

Detection of Biologically Generated PQQred in Yeast Culture

Finally, an investigation was conducted to determine whether the method can detect biologically generated PQQred in a Saccharomyces cerevisiae culture. After 20 h of incubation at 28 °C, a noticeable color change was observed in the yeast suspension (Figure ). HPLC analysis in the Native mode revealed distinct peaks corresponding to PQQred and PQQox (Figure A). Conversely, the yeast-free control (PQQ + glucose) exhibited only a minor PQQred signal (Figure B). These results demonstrate that the method can monitor the biological redox conversion of PQQ. Discussion

Analytical Modes and HPLC Conditions

Two complementary analytical modes, “Native” and “Total,” were developed to address key limitations of conventional methodologies. The Native mode provides essential insights into the chemical reactivity of PQQ, whereas the Total mode ensures reliable quantification for practical applications, marking a significant advancement in PQQ analysis. In this study, “Native mode” refers to an analytical approach performed without intentional redox conversion, that is, no reducing agent is added during sample pretreatment. Accordingly, the measured PQQox/PQQred ratio reflects the redox distribution at the time of injection under defined handling conditions. The authors acknowledge that in food matrices containing endogenous reducing agents (such as VC), partial or complete reduction of PQQox may occur prior to analysis. However, the key advantage of the Native mode is that it provides a “redox snapshot” of the formulation without enforcing complete conversion, thereby enabling direct monitoring of the effect of the product microenvironment (such as functional beverages) on the PQQ redox state over time. Importantly, the optimized chromatographic conditions achieve clear separation of PQQox and PQQred from common antioxidants, minimizing the risk of overestimating PQQred due to coelution. Thus, while the Native mode reflects a matrix-influenced redox state, it remains a robust and practical approach for assessing the real-time chemical profile and stability of PQQ in complex systems. Because strongly acidic conditions can stabilize PQQred, the potential formation of PQQred was considered. However, as shown in Figure B, no conversion from PQQox to PQQred was observed. Therefore, the Native mode conditions used in this study do not affect the redox state of PQQox.

Building on this framework, the chromatographic conditions were subsequently optimized. Using a strongly acidic eluent, a robust HPLC method that quantifies reduced PQQ (PQQred) in complex matrices was established while maintaining PQQred stability and enabling its controlled conversion to PQQox during analysis. Unlike conventional approaches that use weakly acidic mobile phases and often fail to separate redox forms, this method leverages strong acidity to suppress PQQred oxidation and reduce proton dissociation. , This enhanced the physicochemical differences between the quinone and hydroquinone structures, enabling effective separation on the ODS column. In contrast to traditional methods, where PQQ peaks often overlap with those of VC, the optimized method achieved clear resolution, with PQQox eluting at approximately 6 min, PQQred eluting at approximately 10 min, and VC eluting early, thereby minimizing matrix interference.

Role of γ-CD and Solubilization Mechanism

To enable quantitative analysis in the Total mode, a pretreatment protocol that combines VC as a reducing agent with γ-CD was developed to improve PQQred solubility. This step is critical because PQQred exhibits poor aqueous solubility under strongly acidic conditions and readily precipitates. Solubility testing confirmed that γ-CD significantly increased the PQQred solubility under acidic conditions, consistent with the established ability of cyclodextrins to enhance aqueous solubility via inclusion complexation. , Although γ-CD inclusion has primarily been reported for oxidized PQQ, a similar interaction is proposed for PQQred in the present system. Under the reduction condition, free PQQred was generated, and its increased hydrophobicity facilitated inclusion within the hydrophobic γ-CD cavity through weak, noncovalent interactions. However, it should be noted that direct evidence for inclusion complex formation was not obtained in this study, and therefore this interaction is described as putative.

Consistent with this interpretation, the PQQred retention time was unaffected by the presence of γ-CD, suggesting that the inclusion complex is not maintained during chromatographic separation. The weak hydrophobic interactions are likely disrupted in the presence of organic solvents in the mobile phase, resulting in dissociation of the complex prior to or during column interaction.

Collectively, these findings support a mechanism in which γ-CD transiently enhances the apparent solubility of PQQred in solution, thereby preventing precipitation, while dissociation upon injection allows normal chromatographic behavior. Because γ-CD exhibits negligible UV absorbance at the monitored wavelengths and elutes rapidly, it does not interfere with detection or quantification.

Practical Applications and Redox Dynamics

This method enables a detailed characterization of the PQQ redox behavior under defined conditions. The findings of this study indicate that PQQox partially reduces even in the presence of compounds such as leucine, glucose, or erythritol, highlighting its susceptibility to reduction. Furthermore, this study demonstrated that converting PQQ to its reduced form using VC effectively mitigated its reactivity with amino acids. These insights may support the design of more stable beverage formulations, where maintaining PQQ in a reduced state could help mitigate undesired interactions with coexisting ingredients.

The practical utility of the method was validated using commercial beverages, achieving excellent recoveries (99–101%) and precision (RSD < 1%). The method meets the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (AOAC) and United States Pharmacopeia (USP) performance criteria, including high linearity (R2 = 0.9996) and low LOD/LOQ (0.20/0.50 mg/L), supporting its suitability for industrial quality control and regulatory compliance. −

Comparison with Conventional Methods and Biological Significance

Although liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) and enzymatic assays , are available, these approaches often require specialized instrumentation or may not preserve the redox state during sample preparation. Similarly, derivatization methods (such as IPQ formation) are not applicable for PQQred because the reaction relies on electrophilic carbonyl groups that are absent in the hydroquinone structure. In contrast, the present HPLC-UV method provides a cost-effective approach for the selective quantification of PQQred under controlled conditions.

This method was further applied to a model biological system, where yeast cultures incubated with PQQox and glucose demonstrated detectable formation of PQQred. A small amount of PQQred was also detected in the glucose-only control, suggesting that nonenzymatic reduction may occur during prolonged incubation. This effect may be attributed to the weak reducing activity of glucose at elevated temperatures. In contrast, the presence of yeast markedly accelerated the formation of PQQred. This observation suggests that the reduction of PQQox may occur under biologically relevant conditions, potentially mediated by intracellular reducing equivalents such as nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (reduced form) or nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (reduced form), similar to microbial ketone reductions commonly exploited in organic synthesis. However, these findings are preliminary, and further studies are required to confirm the underlying mechanism and physiological relevance.

Moreover, given the susceptibility of PQQred to oxidation, its stability in biological environments may be limited, and the balance between the oxidized and reduced forms is likely influenced by local redox conditions. Therefore, while the method provides a useful tool for investigating the PQQ redox behavior, careful control of experimental conditions is required when extending its application to biological systems.

Limitations and Future Perspectives

Although the developed method is well suited for the analysis of PQQ in beverages and supplements, its applicability to biological samples may be limited by the current sensitivity (LOQ = 0.50 mg/L). Quantification near or below this level may result in reduced accuracy and precision. Furthermore, PQQred is prone to oxidation, which may introduce further variability, particularly at low concentrations or during sample handling. Further improvements in sensitivity and control of redox conditions, such as sample preconcentration or stabilization strategies, will be necessary for the reliable analysis of biological matrices.

Conclusion

A robust HPLC-UV analytical framework comprising “Native” and “Total” modes was developed in this study to differentiate and quantify the redox states of PQQ. PQQred oxidation was effectively suppressed by employing a strongly acidic mobile phase and clear chromatographic separation of PQQox and PQQred from common matrix interferences, including VC, was achieved. Incorporation of γ-CD into the pretreatment protocol was essential for the Total mode, ensuring complete solubilization of PQQred at low pH and enabling high recovery (99–101%) even in complex matrices. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the method not only meets stringent AOAC and USP analytical standards, but also provides a practical, cost-effective alternative to specialized LC-MS/MS or enzymatic approaches for routine quality control in the food and supplement industries. Furthermore, application of the method to biological systems, specifically yeast cultures, provided direct evidence for extracellular PQQ reduction, highlighting its potential for studying redox dynamics in vivo. By enabling precise monitoring of the PQQox/PQQred ratio, this methodology offers a new tool for evaluating the bioavailability and antioxidant functions of PQQ. Overall, this study establishes a foundation for both industrial quality assurance and mechanistic studies in PQQ redox biology.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

PQQ disodium salt trihydrate (Na2PQQ·3H2O; BioPQQ) was supplied by Mitsubishi Gas Chemical Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan). l-Ascorbic acid, γ-CD, phosphoric acid (85%), hydrochloric acid (36%), HPLC-grade methanol, and acetonitrile were purchased from Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemical Industries (Osaka, Japan). Ultrapure water (>18.2 MΩ·cm) was produced using a Milli-Q purification system (Millipore, USA) and used for all mobile phases and sample preparation. PQQred was prepared in-house from BioPQQ and VC (described in the Supporting Information).

Chromatographic Conditions

HPLC analysis (PQQred) was performed on a Shimadzu 20AD system equipped with a UV–Vis detector set at 320 nm. Separation was achieved on a C18 reversed-phase column (InertSustain C18, 4.6 × 150 mm, 5 μm). The mobile phase comprised 0.34% (w/w) phosphoric acid and 35% (v/v) methanol in water (pH 2.5). The flow rate was 1.5 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and the injection volume was 10 μL. The Native mode used 320 nm for PQQred and 259 nm for PQQox.

Quantification and Calibration

PQQox and PQQred were quantified using an external calibration curve. PQQox standards were freshly prepared with ultrapure water or phosphate buffer without a reduction step. PQQred standards were prepared in situ by the chemical reduction of PQQox using 5% (w/w) VC in the presence of 10% (w/w) γ-CD. This preparation was pretreated under the Total mode to ensure matrix consistency between the standards and the samples. Calibration curves were generated by plotting the peak area versus concentration (2–80 mg/L) in triplicate. The LOD and LOQ were calculated from the standard deviation (σ) of the response at the lowest concentration and the slope (S) of the calibration curve, using eqs and , respectively: 1 LOD=3.3×σ/S 2 LOQ=10×σ/S.

Sample Preparation for Beverage Analysis

Commercial beverages were used as model matrices and spiked with known amounts of PQQ. Samples with concentrations exceeding 80 mg/L were diluted to 5–80 mg/L to prevent PQQred precipitation. Beverages were degassed via sonication and centrifuged. For Total mode analyses, a pretreatment solution (5% VC and 10% γ-CD in 0.34% phosphoric acid) was added to the sample at a 1:1 ratio, followed by incubation at 40 °C for at least 1 h to ensure complete reduction.

Detection of Biologically Generated PQQred in Yeast Culture

Saccharomyces cerevisiae NBRC 100929 was used to evaluate the detection of biologically reduced PQQ. Yeast cells were cultured overnight in yeast extract–peptone–dextrose medium at 28 °C, harvested by centrifugation, and resuspended in 2% glucose (optical density at 600 nm = 12). The reaction mixture contained 0.5 mL of 2 g/L PQQ in phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.2), 2% glucose (5.0 mL), and the yeast suspension (0.5 mL). After shaking at 28 °C for 20 h, the mixture was centrifuged, and the supernatant was analyzed in the Native mode. A yeast-free control was processed in parallel.

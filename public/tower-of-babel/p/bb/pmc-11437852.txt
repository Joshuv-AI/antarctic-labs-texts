Economic analysis of hydrogen energy systems: A global perspective
Authors: Guoqing Liu, Tingting Guo, Ping Wang, Hua Jiang, Hongguang Wang, Xue Zhao, Xiaoyan Wei, Ying Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the realm of renewable energy, the integration of wind power and hydrogen energy systems represents a promising avenue towards environmental sustainability. However, the development of cost-effective hydrogen energy storage solutions is crucial to fully realize the potential of hydrogen as a renewable energy source. By combining wind power generation with hydrogen storage, a comprehensive hydrogen energy system can be established. This study aims to devise a physiologically inspired optimization approach for designing a standalone wind power producer that incorporates a hydrogen energy system on a global scale. The optimization process considers both total cost and capacity loss to determine the optimal configuration for the system. The optimal setup for an off-grid solution involves the utilization of eight distinct types of compact horizontal-axis wind turbines. Additionally, a sensitivity analysis is conducted by varying component capital costs to assess their impact on overall cost and load loss. Simulation results indicate that at a 15 % loss, the cost of energy (COE) is $1.3772, while at 0 % loss, it stands at $1.6908. Capital expenses associated with wind turbines and hydrogen storage systems significantly contribute to the overall cost. Consequently, the wind turbine-hydrogen storage system emerges as the most cost-effective and reliable option due to its low cost of energy.Keywords: Hydrogen energy systems, Wind energy, Capital expenses, Environmental sustainability

Introduction

Electrochemical energy storage is mainly used to mitigate fluctuations in wind power. However, their restricted lifespan, potential environmental risks, and safety concerns render them an unfavorable option [1]. Authors have increasingly focused on implementing hydrogen storage as a solution to the inconsistent energy output of wind turbines because of the consistent advancements in the economic feasibility of electrolytes, fuel cell technologies, and accompanying infrastructures. Nevertheless, a techno-economic study of integrated renewable energy and hydrogen systems is crucial in the initial foundational research. The global attention towards hydrogen energy production has intensified in recent years, driven by ambitious clean energy goals set by various countries.

Especially, Japan released an energy strategy for hydrogen fuel cell-powered electric automobiles in 2019 [2], whereas the United States Ministry of Power revealed a slightly comprehensive hydrogen program schedule in 2020 [3]. Furthermore, South Korea's leadership in the hydrogen-powered fuel cell electric vehicle sector in 2019 highlighted the rising importance of hydrogen generation for reaching zero emissions and guaranteeing energy stability. The International Energy Agency (IEA) and the Hydrogen Association underline that projections show a significant increase in hydrogen production, exceeding 212 Mt by 2030 and 528 Mt by 2050 [4]. Hydrogen power systems are therefore more important in lowering pollutants throughout the power sector. Studying in hydrogen energy thus has great potential to shape the evolution of energy infrastructures. Solar and wind power are quite important in rural regions since they provide an environmentally friendly and clean substitute for fossil fuels, therefore aggravating environmental damage and causing global warming. Particularly wind energy has attracted more and more attention worldwide because of its ecologically friendly qualities and great possibilities. One of the main drawbacks of wind power, meanwhile, is its natural sensitivity to fluctuations—especially in off-grid locations. Integrating wind power plants with energy storage systems (ESS) has been suggested as a strategy to handle this problem therefore guaranteeing consistent and sustainable energy generation [5]. Rising as a major ESS, hydrogen energy systems (HES) provide the means to improve the dependability and quality of power in off-grid environments. HES technology's great advantages nonetheless are still mostly outweighed by its expensive initial investment cost [6]. Furthermore, challenging hybrid solar and wind power systems based on HES are their complexity and risk-increasing nature. With technical threats usually surpassing ecological dangers [7], a careful analysis of the hazards connected with integrated solar and wind systems emphasizes the requirement of efficient risk mitigating techniques [8]. Reducing these dangers and encouraging the growth of HES innovations is judged dependent on government action through funding assistance, loans at low interest rates, and laws and regulations encouraging clean energy use [9]. Particularly in off-grid locations, several studies have underlined the need of HES in realizing the advantages of sustainable and renewable energy sources. Particularly in distant areas, assessments of HES projects such the hydrogen power system on the Norwegian Froan islands have shown notable decreases in fossil fuel usage and energy costs [10]. Furthermore, underlined by evaluations of energy system efficiency are the advantages in terms of economy, environment, and energy generation of carbon pricing policies. Especially, a system integrating hydrogen, biomass, and wind turbines has been suggested as a sustainable and efficient method of producing power, therefore lowering 13 % of the greenhouse gas emissions [11]. The broad adoption of clean energy sources including air and hydrogen that would considerably increase the environment's sustainability is hampered in considerable part by the development of reasonably priced hydrogen energy storage technology.

Theoretical background

Within the fields of clean energy, the combination of hydrogen energy sources and wind turbines marks a major development [15]. This development takes use of the advantages presented by both technologies to raise the dependability and sustainability of the energy source. Consistent with other renewable energy sources, wind turbines creates electricity by transforming the kinetic energy of the winds into electrical energy. Contrarily, remember that wind power is a natural phenomenon that occurs intermittently. Effective storage systems are absolutely essential to tackle the problem of sporadic electricity production and preserve a constant and continuous supply [16]. This is because the inconsistent production of electrical energy exists. Given its properties, hydrogen is a possible storing source since it is a flexible energy carrier. One approach to create it using the extra electricity produced by wind power and storing it for later use is electrolysis. Fuel cells allow this stored energy to be either directly used in many commercial and transport uses or transformed into additional power. Notwithstanding this, the development of reasonably priced and effective hydrogen storage systems presents a continuing problem [17].

There are high upfront costs associated with hydrogen storage systems such as compressed gas, liquid hydrogen, and chemical hydrides [18]. These technologies also result in energy inefficiencies. Therefore, it is vital to optimize the integration of wind power generation with hydrogen storage in order to fully use the potential of these renewable energy sources while also guaranteeing economic feasibility and operational effectiveness. This needs to be done in order to maximize the potential of these renewable energy sources.

This work employs a physiologically inspired optimization technique, which is a method that mimics biological processes in order to solve complex multi-objective optimization problems. This strategy was developed in order to address this challenge. When it comes to addressing the nonlinearity and the many different objectives that are inherent in renewable energy systems, conventional optimization techniques frequently need to be revised. Through the application of methodologies such as genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, and artificial neural networks, the purpose of the study is to discover unique configurations that standard methods may overlook [19]. The development of an independent wind power generator that incorporates a hydrogen energy system is the major objective, with the intention of achieving this goal primarily for off-grid applications. Eight distinct types of horizontal-axis wind turbines will be used in the research project. These wind turbines were chosen for the study because of their efficiency and versatility.

Either the total expense or the ability drop is assessed throughout the optimization procedure to ascertain an arrangement of the network that guarantees dependability and economy. Furthermore done is a sensitivity study considering components investment cost adjustments to evaluate how these adjustments affect the total price and loading loss. The average price for electricity is projected to be a single dollar and three hundred seventy-two cents whenever a decline of 15 percent results. On the opposite side, without reduction the cost of equity is $1.6908. This emphasizes how much the entire price of the endeavor depends on the expenses related to wind power and storage for hydrogen technologies. This all-encompassing approach provides essential perspectives for lawmakers and shareholders in addition to reasonable benchmarks for assessing the many configurations of green power sources. Throughout the research, the combined wind-hydrogen technology was demonstrated to be dependable and financially realistic, therefore advancing renewable sources of energy on a global basis.

[46] Introduced the artificial bee colony algorithm, drawing inspiration from honey bee colonies' feeding behavior, as a biologically inspired optimization approach. The algorithm's application to optimizing the efficiency of a wind power generator combined with a hydrogen energy storing system involves several major steps [43]. The search area is populated with a swarm of bees chosen randomly. Worker bees' fitness functions are evaluated to determine their effectiveness. Each bee's fitness function is determined using equations (8), (9). Bees are ranked based on their performance in the fitness function. Scout bees and observer bees differentiate their roles. Observers select fitness functions based on minimum values observed in the dance of busy bees. After selecting a nearby neighbor, the healthiness of the chosen fitness function is evaluated. The highest fitness function value found by scouts replaces the current value. Bees' positions are updated based on their fitness function values.

Steps 2–8 are repeated until 100 iterations are completed. The minimal values of the fitness function found thus far are recorded as the ideal solution. Robustness analysis

Influenced by the eating habits of honeybee colonies, the experimental findings of the study show proof of the effectiveness of the artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithms in maximizing a wind energy generator linked to hydrogen-powered systems. When one compares the BIO approach to the PSO method, which is regarded as the benchmark, one emphasizes its resilience. The great effectiveness of the ABC algorithm in minimizing total costs (TC) and cost of energy (COE) together with running time highlights its possibilities in useful situations. Easily replicable for further studies, the thorough investigation of the optimization process including elements like swarm size, movement function diameter, and iteration count provides a methodical and unambiguous methodology. Sensitivity analysis helps one assess the impact of changing constituent expenditures, therefore improving the dependability of the research. By simulations of several financial situations, the study shows that the costs of specific components, including windmills and fuel cell technology, significantly influence the financial sustainability of the whole structure. The need of cost optimization in green energy sources is shown by the realization that a decrease in the initial investment needed for wind turbines can lead to appreciable overall expenses and cost of energy savings. Furthermore, the study's examination of many wind turbine models and their capacity to produce electricity supply a complete evaluation of several configurations, so providing important information for selecting the most reliable and least expensive solutions. To guarantee the authenticity and dependability of the findings, some restrictions must be resolved, nevertheless. The reliance of the study on certain parameter settings, such the size of the swarm, the breadth of the motion operation, and the number of iterations, begs problems regarding the application of the results. Variations in these elements could produce different optimization outcomes; hence further research of the sensitivity of the BIO method to parameter changes is necessary. Furthermore, the presumption that environmental and economic circumstances would stay the same for the whole 15-year project period might not fairly depict actual conditions. The performance and expenses of the system can be much influenced by variations in market prices, technological developments, and policy changes as well as by policy decisions. The scope of the sensitivity study is limited, mostly focused on variations in capital expenditures. One should consider other factors such operations and maintenance-related expenses, slowdown in efficiency, and the prospect of cash from surplus energy sales. Including these factors in further assessments would help to improve the economic viability of the system by means of a more comprehensive knowledge. Furthermore evaluated in the study is the effectiveness of only two optimization strategies: ABC and PSO. Investigating extra algorithms such hybrid techniques simulated annealing, or genetic algorithms helps one to find more optimization strategies and increase the validity of the results. Replacing the research in numerous environments and scenarios helps one to determine the validity and robustness of the conclusions. Examining the generalizability of the conclusions would mean undertaking comparable studies with various wind and economic profiles in several geographical areas. Moreover, adding multi-objective optimization criteria including social acceptance and environmental impact in addition to financial considerations will provide a whole picture of the success of the system. Moreover, more research should look at including advanced forecast models for hydrogen demand and wind power generation. These models might improve the accuracy of input data, therefore producing more consistent optimizing results. Furthermore, investigating the prospects of novel hydrogen storage technologies, including solid-state storage or advanced chemical hydrides may supply storage solutions that are both more inexpensive and efficient. By concentrating on these particular domains, next studies can build on the present findings and offer a more solid and whole framework for improving integrated wind-hydrogen energy systems.

Discussion

This study shows a clear advancement in the field of renewable energy systems. Previous studies used different optimization techniques for similar systems, including PSO, Genetic Algorithms (GA), and other heuristic approaches. Previous research [55] employed PSO to improve the cost-effectiveness and performance of a wind-hydrogen system, therefore producing notable benefits. Still, the PSO method showed shortcomings with respect to its power to avoid local optima and convergence speed, hence perhaps producing inadequate solutions in complex environments. Conversely, the ABC algorithm draws on the foraging behavior of honey bees and demonstrated superior ability in effectively traversing the solution space and avoiding local optima. By means of reduced TC and COE values, the results of this investigation revealed that the ABC algorithm routinely beats the PSO algorithm. The ABC method also showed notable running times decrease.

Furthermore, the ability of the ABC method to efficiently control variations in component capital costs shows its pragmatic relevance in dynamic market conditions. Previous studies [56], have primarily focused on static optimization scenarios without including sensitivity assessments to allow shifting economic conditions. Based on the sensitivity analysis carried out in this study, it is clear that focused investments in fuel cells and wind turbines could result in significant financial savings. This helps to raise the system's general economic viability. One example of this is when a 5.95 % drop in TC, followed by COE from wind turbine capital expenditures dropped from $1600/kW to $800/kW. This emphasizes the need of lowering the initial investment expenses.

The results of this study depend on the larger framework of renewable energy systems and optimization ideas. By including hydrogen energy storage into wind power generation, major challenges in renewable energy, such as the intermittent character of wind power and the necessity of storage, have been addressed [47]. The researchers [33] emphasized the requirement of effective storage systems since they claimed that the variability of wind energy seriously hinders its general application. Using hydrogen as storage medium offers a possible future since it may be produced during times of excess wind energy and consumed during times of insufficient wind power, therefore ensuring a steady and reliable energy source.

Supporting the results of this study are theoretical models connected to heuristic optimization techniques. Rooted on the natural foraging behaviors of which the ABC algorithm fits the ideas of swarm intelligence. Swarm intelligence is proven to be rather useful in handling complex optimization problems [57]. Using collective behaviors observable in nature, swarm intelligence algorithms including ABC and PSO effectively search large solution spaces. The adaptive search mechanism of the ABC method may be the reason behind its better performance in this study since it efficiently balances exploration and exploitation when compared to PSO. The outcomes of the studies support the present debate on the financial feasibility of renewable energy sources. Considering COE one may evaluate the profitability of renewable energy projects [36]. With a maximum Loss of Power Supply Probability (LPSP-max) of 0 %, this paper demonstrates the capacity of the ABC algorithm to achieve a COE of $0.6908 per kilowatt-hour (kWh). This success makes the ABC algorithm a good competitor against other renewable technologies and traditional energy sources. This emphasizes how capable wind-hydrogen systems are to offer reasonably priced and ecologically beneficial energy sources.

The results of the robustness analysis confirm even more the practical value of the research. Sensitivity analysis is used in the study to evaluate their impact on the economic performance of the system and consider expected changes in component prices. This approach conforms to the prior research [58], who underlined the significance of considering financial uncertainty in energy planning. The findings of the study offer insightful analysis of how strategic investments could enhance system performance and decrease costs, which is essential for the long-term survival of renewable energy projects in adjusting to changing market conditions. Moreover, the assessment of many types of small wind turbines in the Swift wind-hydrogen system provides useful information for selecting the most reliable and reasonably priced configurations. With a COE of 1.5988 the Fortis-WT-hydrogen system is found to be the most dependable and cost-effective alternative. The least dependable and most expensive choice is the Excel-WT-hydrogen system, nevertheless. This emphasizes the need of selecting appropriate models of wind turbines to improve system performance.

Theoretical implications

The evidence of the ABC algorithm's superiority over the PSO method adds significantly theoretically to the field of heuristic optimization. The ability of the ABC algorithm to reach reduced TC and COE while lowering runtime emphasizes its value in exploring and using solution areas more efficiently. This is in line with the concepts of swarm intelligence and natural foraging activities, therefore laying a good basis for next research on optimization techniques in complicated systems. Particularly with regard to integrating renewable energy systems, the study improves our understanding of coupling hydrogen energy storage with wind power generation. The findings of this research offer a reasonable answer for the intermittency and storage problems usually connected with wind energy. The work uses theoretical models stressing the need of hybrid renewable energy sources. This integration fits accepted theories on energy storage technologies and their contribution to increase the dependability and consistency of renewable energy sources.

Theoretical implications directly affect the economic viability analysis of renewable energy systems. A methodological development is shown in the study by the application of sensitivity analysis to evaluate the effect of different component capital costs on the economic performance of the system. This approach provides a conceptual basis for including economic uncertainty into energy planning in line with more general economic ideas on investment risk and return. This study shows the feasibility of using several criteria for optimization, including social acceptability and environmental influence. In keeping with sustainability theories that support the inclusion of economic, environmental, and social issues in energy planning and decision-making, this theoretical extension offers a complete evaluation of renewable energy systems.

Managerial/policy implications

This study underlines the need of strategically investing in fuel cells and wind turbines. These insights can help managers and legislators choose investments that reduce early capital costs, therefore lowering the total cost and energy consumption of renewable energy projects. This strategy helps to increase the economic viability and attraction of wind-hydrogen systems for stakeholders and investors. When managers and legislators evaluate the integration of modern optimization techniques, the proven efficiency of the ABC algorithm relative to standard methods such PSO could offer insightful analysis. The ABC algorithm could be included into the optimization processes of energy planners and project managers to produce more affordable and efficient answers for renewable energy systems.

The sensitivity analysis of the study emphasizes the requirement of energy managers and legislators considering economic uncertainties and market fluctuations while deciding on their planning course. Considering variations in component costs and other economic aspects will help decision-makers create more strong and flexible energy plans that will help them to properly negotiate evolving market conditions. The outcomes support the use of policies aiming at promoting the integration of hydrogen energy storage with generation of wind power. Policymakers could encourage the deployment of hydrogen storage systems by means of subsidies, incentives, and favorable legislative environments. By addressing the issue of intermittent power generation in wind energy, these rules might facilitate the transition to more dependable and ecologically beneficial renewable energy systems. Policymakers can support the acceptance of multi-objective optimization frameworks by helping them to be implemented in energy planning considering social, environmental, and financial aspects as well as economic ones. Encouragement of comprehensive evaluations of renewable energy sources helps governments ensure that the benefits of these systems are maximized in all spheres, therefore supporting more general sustainability goals.

Limitations and future research agenda

The study's outcomes hinge on specific parameter settings and assumptions, potentially constraining their relevance across diverse scenarios. Choices regarding the optimization method and input variables may influence the results' generalizability. Variations in parameters like swarm size and iteration counts could yield differing optimization outcomes. The study employs simplifications and assumptions to ensure computational tractability. Although necessary, these simplifications may introduce uncertainties into the results. The findings are contextualized within specific parameters, potentially limiting real-world complexities. Factors like geographical variations and regulatory frameworks are not fully addressed, limiting the findings' applicability. Further research is needed to explore implications in diverse contexts. Validation through real-world experimentation or empirical data analysis is crucial to ensure the accuracy and reliability of optimization results. Computational models offer insights but may oversimplify complex phenomena. Experimental validation would enhance confidence and provide practical guidance for stakeholders.

Based on the current study's findings, more research should be directed toward creating more sophisticated optimization methods that outperform BIO and PSO methods. It could be achieved by combining different algorithms and utilizing artificial intelligence for optimization in actual time and automated maintenance. Further investigation is required into the efficiency, financial viability, and effects on network resilience of hybrid technologies that combine WT-HES with additional renewable energies, such as solar photovoltaic. It is also essential to do econometric evaluations and policy prospect models to determine how future policy measures like carbon taxes and incentives affect the adoption of WT-HES systems. To increase productivity and decrease expenditures, it is essential to investigate technological advancements in hydrogen storage, such as new products and procedures. Large-scale pilot programs are required to gauge sustainability and effectiveness in the real world. Long-term research will also be necessary to monitor community response and societal repercussions. Ecological and social consequences evaluations will shed light on the broader implications of WT-HES deployment. At the same time, thorough sensitivity analysis of essential input parameters can discover critical aspects influencing financial viability and dependability.

Conclusion

The main idea of this work was to investigate the improvement of a wind power generator coupled with a hydrogen energy storing device using the ABC and PSO techniques. By use of a biologically inspired optimization approach, these methods aim to increase the dependability, financial viability, and efficiency of renewable energy systems. The work compares the ABC algorithm with the well-known PSO method, which is usually seen as the benchmark in this field. The ABC technique is applied in the research to maximize a wind-hydrogen system. Random production of a group of bees allows one to investigate the search space. Worker bees' fitness is evaluated using designated formulae, and subsequently their performance determines their ranking. While observer bees choose fitness functions depending on the lowest values discovered in the dance of active bees, scout bees have particular tasks. The sites of the bees are changed in line with their values for fitness.

In optimizing the Swift-WT-hydrogen system, the ABC algorithm showed better performance than the PSO technique. It lowered runtime in addition to TC and COE. With a lower TC index and a shorter run length (6.979 s) the ABC algorithm showed better performance than the PSO technique (12.146 s). At various degrees of loss of power supply probability (LPSP), the research revealed that the ABC technique is more economically viable, so finding a good compromise between cost and dependability. Attaching a Total Cost of $11,875 and a Cost of Energy of $0.6908 per kilowatt-hour with an LPSP-max of 0 %, the system achieved reduced TC and COE when the LPSP-max value was changed to 20 %. This suggests that the system is reasonably priced and flexible. The sensitivity analysis found that the economic performance of the system is rather influenced by different component capital costs. Total cost and cost of energy are estimated to be 5.95 % lower if wind turbine capital costs drop from $1600/kW to $800/kW. This emphasizes the need of best cost optimization in systems of renewable energy. Several types of small wind turbines were compared in the Swift wind-hydrogen system. It found that the Excel-WT-hydrogen system is the most expensive and least dependable; the Fortis-WT-hydrogen system is the most reasonably priced and dependable substitute. These findings stress the significance of selecting appropriate wind turbine models to improve system performance and financial effectiveness. With a 0 % LPSP rate and good adaptation to grid absence issues, the ABC method proved its durability and adaptability. This shows the possibilities of unconnected wind power generation even in line with strict LPSP criteria. The optimization of a wind-hydrogen system utilizing the ABC technique is examined in this work exhaustively. In terms of cost-effectiveness, dependability, and flexibility, it shows how better the ABC algorithm is than the PSO method. The outcomes offer important information for bettering the design and implementation of integrated renewable energy systems, therefore supporting the general aim of sustainable energy solutions.

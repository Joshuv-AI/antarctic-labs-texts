We appreciate the interest that Kleebayoon and Wiwanitkit [1] have shown in our article “Custom-made, 3D-printed bolus cap for a case of scalp metastasis: A single-institution study” [2], and we welcome the opportunity to clarify and expand on several points raised in their correspondence. We agree that the irradiation treatment of the scalp is technically challenging and that our study has several limitations.

First, as the correspondents correctly note, our report is based on a single patient. We fully acknowledge the limited generalizability of a single-case observation, as explicitly stated in the Limitations section of our article [2]. Our main objective was to show technical feasibility, dosimetric adequacy, and patient-centered outcomes in a difficult clinical scenario.

To the best of our knowledge, and as noted in our paper, this case represents the first clinical implementation of 3D-printed bolus technology in radiotherapy in Romania. Although our report does not include a direct comparative analysis, the number of similar cases reported in the literature are limited, too [3,4]. Recent reviews also highlight the growing role of 3D printing in radiotherapy and its potential to improve conformity, reproducibility, and workflow [5,6]. Nevertheless, multi-vendor, multi-patient validation studies, specifically for the use of 3D printed boluses for scalp irradiation, are warranted.

Second, the treatment goal in this case was to irradiate the metastases with palliative intent. The custom-fit, flexible bolus facilitated reproducible setup and improved patient comfort. Treatment was delivered between the 3rd and 19th of June 2024. As reported in our article, the 6-month follow-up showed no clinical evidence of scalp metastases [2]. At the 12-month follow-up beyond the timeframe of the published report, on the 27th of June 2025, the patient’s symptoms were ameliorated, with no clinical signs of scalp metastases and improved quality of life, thereby confirming our primary palliative aims. In addition, the patient demonstrated hair regrowth (Fig. 1). From the patient’s perspective, this was a meaningful improvement: she reported resuming daily hygiene routine without pain or discomfort. Last, regarding treatment delivery the equipment constraint of the Halcyon platform should be acknowledged. Dose coverage with coplanar fields is challenging to optimize when limited to three degrees-of-freedom (3DoF) couch movement. Nevertheless, for palliative scalp irradiation, the 3DoF couch was sufficient, since the precision required in stereotactic regimens and the associated risks of high single-fraction doses were not applicable. Importantly, the dosimetric goals were met: 95 % of the planning target volume (PTV) received 98 % of the prescribed dose, consistent with International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements (ICRU) recommendations.

In conclusion, we appreciate the discussion of this important topic. Although limited, we think that our paper [2] offers clinically valuable technical and patient-centered insights as a first attempt and supports continued dialogue. It also provides a foundation for further research, reinforcing the relevance of 3D-printed boluses as a customizable solution in radiotherapy for superficial tumors.

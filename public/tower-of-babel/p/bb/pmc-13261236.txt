Successful lung segmentectomy with anomalous mediastinal basal pulmonary artery using preoperative planning with 3-dimensional computed tomography simulation

Central Message

Preoperative simulation using 3-dimensional computed tomography enhances operative planning and improves safety in patients with anomalous pulmonary arteries.

Anatomical variations of the pulmonary artery (PA) encountered during surgery can lead to serious complications if unrecognized, particularly during minimally invasive approaches.1,2 Preoperative 3-dimensional computed tomography (3D-CT) is used to identify anatomic anomalies and facilitates operative planning. Herein, we report a case in which the anatomy of the mediastinal lingular (MLA) and mediastinal basal arteries (A8+9) was confirmed using 3D-CT before a left basal segmentectomy. Institutional review board approval was not required. Written informed consent for publication was obtained from patient.

Case Report

A 69-year-old man was diagnosed with advanced rectal cancer with a solitary pulmonary metastasis. After rectal surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, no new metastases were detected; therefore, pulmonary resection was planned. Preoperative CT revealed a metastasis in the left segment laterobasale (S9) measuring 31 × 26 × 23 mm (Figure 1, A). 3D-CT angiography using simulation software (Revoras; Ziosoft) demonstrated the mediastinal basal (A8+9) and lingular arteries (Figure 1, B). The dorsobasal PA (A10), originating from the left PA trunk, ran dorsally to the left basal bronchus (Figure 1, C). A comparison of the intraoperative findings with the 3D-CT images is presented in Video 1. Four ports were placed, including a 4-cm incision in the fifth intercostal space. The lesion was identified by a marked pleural indentation. After we incised the dorsal pleura, A10 was identified between the superior segmental (V6) and basal pulmonary vein. The interlobar pleura was divided and the MLA and mediastinal ventrolateral basal PA (A8+9) were identified (Figure 2, A). A8+9 was divided using a vascular stapler. The basal bronchus was exposed (Figure 2. B), and divided with careful attention to A10 (Figure 2, C). A10 was subsequently ligated and divided. The pulmonary ligament was divided, followed by the basal pulmonary vein. Finally, the intersegmental planes were divided with consideration of the tumor location and demarcation line. The operation aligned with the preoperative simulations. The postoperative course was uneventful. Pathologically, the resected metastatic mass measured 32 × 26 × 22 mm, and the surgical margin was 32 mm. Comment

The mediastinal basal trunk, known as the arteria praebronchialis (AP), is a rare branching pattern of the left PA, present in 0.03% of patients.1 The AP may exhibit several branching patterns, including an independent pattern, and one with a common trunk with the MLA. The perfusion territory of the AP also exhibits variations, including: A8 alone, A8+9, A10, or a combination of these.2 Cases of unintentional vascular transection requiring reconstruction have been reported when PA anomalies were misunderstood pre- or intraoperatively.3 To avoid these potential risks, 3D-CT simulation software has been widely used, and the accuracy and validity of Revoras for preoperative 3D-CT simulation and stump-view mode have been reported.4 In this case, Revoras was particularly valuable for precise preoperative identification of the dorsobasal A10 branch and simulating the surgical steps. If the anomalous branching pattern had not been fully recognized, the mediastinal A8+9 trunk and lingular artery would likely have been identified from the interlobar side during a standard basal segmentectomy; however, A10 coursed dorsally to the basal bronchus, posing a substantial risk of unintentional injury at the time of bronchial division had its course not been anticipated on 3D-CT. The 3D reconstruction with Revoras provided an intuitive understanding of the positional relationships of these anomalous bronchovascular structures, and the stump-view mode enabled simulation of the surgeon's operative view and planning of the sequence of dissection and vascular control. These features played a key role in planning a safe bronchial division and avoiding damage to A10. Therefore, 3D-CT is essential for identifying vascular anomalies and planning operative procedures in advance, particularly for minimally invasive lung resections.

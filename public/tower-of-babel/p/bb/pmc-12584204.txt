The emergence of a highly pathogenic marek’s disease virus featuring newly identified mutations in Meq oncogene in Southern China
Authors: Qiong Luo, Huimin Li, Zengxu Ge, Zhanhong Chen, Ruiai Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMarek’s disease virus (MDV) is a significant threat to global poultry health, causing high mortality, tumor formation, and severe immunosuppression, leading to substantial economic losses. Continuous monitoring and research on MDV field strains are essential for developing effective vaccination strategies and biosafety measures.MethodsIn this study, peripheral blood was collected from a chicken farm in southern China where an outbreak of MDV occurred despite vaccination with the CVI988 vaccine. A virulent field strain of MDV was isolated and identified through plaque purification experiments, PCR, and indirect immunofluorescence assays. The molecular characteristics of the Meq gene were analyzed by cloning and sequencing. Finally, animal experiments were conducted to evaluate the pathogenicity, oncogenicity, and vaccine protective efficacy of the isolated strain.ResultsA highly pathogenic MDV strain, designated as SS1901, was isolated and purified from a chicken farm vaccinated with CVI988. Sequencing of the oncogenic protein Meq revealed mutations at K77E, D80Y, V115A, T139A, P176R, and P217A, consistent with mutations found in other prevalent Chinese strains. Additional mutations at A88T and Q93R were identified, which have been observed in MDV strains from southern China, India, and Italy, indicating the circulation of such strains. Challenge protection experiments demonstrated that SS1901 caused 100% mortality and 80% tumor incidence in SPF chickens, with tumors primarily in the heart, spleen, and liver. HVT, 814 and CVI988 vaccines provided 46.7%, 80% and 73.3% protection, respectively. Combination of CVI988 and HVT rendered the highest protection at 86.7%.ConclusionsThis study reports new mutation sites in the Meq gene of a prevalent Chinese MDV strains and confirms the isolate SS1901 could overcome existing vaccine-induced immunity, providing valuable new information for monitoring new MDV field strains and developing novel control strategies.Keywords: Marek’s disease virus, Meq gene, Mutation, Pathogenicity

Introduction

The Marek’s disease (MD), caused by the MD virus (MDV), is a highly contagious disease affecting susceptible chickens, characterized by paralysis, rapid-onset lymphoid tumors, and immunosuppression [1]. Marek’s disease virus is a cell-associated α herpesviruses with lymphotropic properties similar to γ herpesviruses. According to the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV), all Marek’s disease virus serotypes are categorized into three species: Gallid alphaherpesvirus 2 (serotype 1), Gallid alphaherpesvirus 3 (serotype 2), and Meleagrid alphaherpesvirus 1 (herpesvirus of turkey [HVT], serotype 3) [2]. Serotype 1 strains are further classified into pathotypes based on the virulence: mild (m)MDV, virulent (v)MDV, very virulent (vv)MDV, and very virulent plus (vv+)MDV [3]. Vaccination is the most crucial method for controlling MDV, significantly reducing economic losses caused by this viral infection. However, with the increasing immunological pressure from vaccination and the evolution of their genomes, there have been continuous reports of highly pathogenic MDV outbreaks in vaccinated poultry farms [4]. Monitoring the prevalence and virulence of wild-type MDV strains within Chinese farms would help develop efficient and control measures.

The Meq protein is proved to be directly associated with the oncogenicity of MDV and in the virulent MDV1 strain [5]. It contains 339 amino acids with a basic leucine zipper (bZIP) domain at the N terminus closely resembling the Jun/Fos oncogene family [6], and variations in the proline-rich domains have been closely associated with virulence [7]. The Meq gene exhibits significant diversity among various isolates, reflecting a complex evolutionary landscape. This genetic variability suggests that positive selection pressures may be actively driving the evolution of the Meq gene, favoring certain mutations over others [8]. Additionally, phylogenetic analysis of Meq revealed that isolates with varying virulence were distributed across different branches and exhibited certain regional differences [9]. Most Chinese isolates contain conserved mutations in Meq, such as E(77), A(115), A(139), R(176), and A(217), compared to USA virulent MDVs [10]. Moreover, MDV strains prevalent in different regions of China also appear to have point mutations in the Meq protein.

China is one of the largest poultry producers in the world, in 2024, China’s poultry inventory was more than 6.4 billion. The CVI988 vaccine is currently the most widely used for preventing MDV infections. In recent years, however, there have been frequent reports of outbreaks of virulent MDV strains in vaccinated chicken farms [11]. Monitoring the epidemiology of MDV within farms is beneficial for developing efficient control strategies and also provides new candidate strains for vaccine evaluation.

In this study, we isolated and identified a highly virulent MDV strain of serotype 1 from a chicken farm vaccinated with CVI988 in Southern China, naming SS1901 strain. Analysis of the Meq gene sequence of SS1901 revealed mutations at amino acid positions 88 (T) and 93 (R), in addition to common mutations found in Chinese isolates. We also found that some highly virulent MDV strains prevalent in Southern China also possess these two new mutation sites. Animal experiments showed that SS1901 is highly lethal and oncogenic in SPF chickens, and neither CVI988 nor CVI988 + HVT pro-vides complete protection. Our research indicates that there is a prevalence of highly oncogenic and pathogenic MDV strains in Southern China, capable of overcoming the protection provided by classical vaccines. Additionally, new point mutations in the Meq protein of wild-type strains pose significant potential threats to the MD control and prevention.

Materials and methods

Collection of clinical samples

A severe MD-like outbreak occurred at a farm with Qingyuan Partridge Chicken flocks immunized with CVI988/Rispens in Guangdong, China, in 2018. The clinical symptoms of birds appeared depressed spirit, rough feathers, emaciation and paralysis at about 70-day-old. The mortality rate was 18.6%, and the tumor incidence reached 40% with visible tumors in the spleen, liver, and hearts. Anticoagulated blood samples from suspected chickens were collected for virus isolation and detection.

Viral isolation and proliferation

Primary chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs) were prepared from 10-day-old SPF chicken eggs purchased from Guangdong Wens Dahuanong Biotechnology (Guangzhou, China). Lymphocytes separated from peripheral blood of the suspected MD chickens’ were inoculated into CEF monolayers, and incubated at 37◦C with 5% CO2 for 120 h, until the typical cytopathogenic effects (CPE) were observed after three blind passages. After plaque purification and propagation in CEF cells, the purified virus, termed SS1901, was stored in liquid nitrogen.

Virus identification

DNA from MDV-challenged chickens and infected CEFs was extracted performed using a DNA Extraction Kit (Omega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. PCR amplification was used to distinguish SS1901 from vaccine strains and exogenous viruses including avian leukosis virus (ALV), reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV), and chicken infectious anaemia virus (CIAV). The serotype of SS1901 was identified by indirect immunofluorescence using the monoclonal antibody GB2 (prepared and stored in our laboratory), which is specific for the gB protein of MDV serotype1.

Amplification and phylogenetic analysis of Meq gene

PCR amplification was performed using Meq-gene-specific Primers (F: 5’-ATAGACGATGTGCTGCTGAGAG-3’; R: 5’-CTTCAATACTTTCGGGTCTGTG-3’), and the PCR products were cloned into pMD18-T vector (TaKaRa Biotechnology Dalian Co., Ltd., Dalian, China). Randomly selected three clones identified as positive by colony PCR and sent for sequencing (Sangon Biotechnology Co., Shanghai, China), and the Meq nucleotide sequence was deposited into GenBank (Accession numbers: PP883553.1). Phylogenetic trees for the Meq gene were built by the neighbor-joining method with 1000 bootstrap replicates using the implemented in MEGA version 11.0.

Virulence studies of MDV SS1901

One-day-old White Leghorn SPF chicks (n = 120) were obtained from Guangdong Wens Dahuanong Biotechnology and randomly divided into six groups (Table 1). The birds were given ad libitum access to food and water and housed separately in positive pressure isolators. Chickens in groups 2, 3, 4 and 6 were vaccinated with CVI988/Rispens, CVI988 + HVT, 814 and HVT, respectively, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. At 7-day-old, chickens in groups 2–6 were challenged intraperitoneally with 2000 plaque-forming units (PFUs) of SS1901 strain, and chicks in group 1 were inoculated with the same volume of uninfected CEFs as a mock control.

The challenges and vaccinations of the experimental groups

Clinical symptoms, ophthalmologic post-mortem lesions and mortality rates were observed and recorded daily until day 90, and all chickens that died during the experiment or were humanely sacrificed at fixed time points were examined postmortem. The MDV positive birds were determined by PCR and histopathological examination. For histopathological analysis, the collected specimens were immediately fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin. Following fixation, tissues underwent sequential processing comprising washing, dehydration through a graded ethanol series, clearing in xylene, paraffin infiltration, embedding in paraffin blocks, and sectioning at 4–5 μm thickness. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), differentiated, rinsed thoroughly, and permanently mounted. Processed slides were examined and imaged under a light microscope.

Five chickens in each group were randomly selected to measure their body weights at 7, 21, 35 and 56 days post infection (dpi) to analyze the effects of SS1901 infection on their growth and five chickens were randomly chosen from each group to calculate the immune organ index [organ index = organ weight (g) / body weight (kg)×100%] after euthanasia at 28 dpi and 42 dpi, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Comparisons of the viral load, body weight, survival curve, immune organ index and protective index [ (PI) = (% MD in unvaccinated chickens–% MD in vaccinated chickens) / % MD in unvaccinated chickens ×100] among groups were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way ANOVA was employed to evaluate statistical differences among groups using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software, and a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.

Ethics statement

The sampling protocols were conducted in compliance with the directives established by the South China Agricultural University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, and have received ethical approval from the university’s animal ethics committee.

Results

Clinical symptoms and PCR analysis samples detection of suspected MDV-infected chickens

A serious outbreak of MD has occurred at poultry farm vaccinated with CVI988/Rispens in Guangdong, China. The diseased Qingyuan Partridge chickens showed symptoms of death, paralysis, emaciation, with the mortality rate of 18.6% and up to 40% of tumor in suspected chickens. Dissection of sick chickens revealed liver, spleen, and heart with tumor nodules, and the enlargement of the spleen and liver (Fig. 1). PCR analysis results showed that the MDV isolate contained two 132 bp repeats (bpr) in the genome, which was the same as the number of 132 bpr in the genome of very virulent positive control Md5 strain, indicating this isolate was a wild MDV isolate.

Sequencing and phylogenetic analysis of Meq gene

Sequencing analysis of the full Meq gene of the SS1901 strain showed the gene is 1020 bp long with 339 codons (Accession numbers: PP883553.1), and its similarity of with 52 reference strains was ranged from 98 to 99.87%. Generation of the evolutionary tree by MEGA.11 based on the genetic distance matrix with the neighbor-joining method (Fig. 3), revealed that SS1901 strain belongs to a similar branch of the highly virulent strains isolated obtained in China. The Meq gene of the SS1901 isolate exhibited the highest homology with those found in the ZC2014, LZ1309 and AH2C strains, isolated from Shandong, Gansu and Heilongjiang Provinces, China, respectively. It shares 98.7% homology to vv + MDV 648 A and 686 strains, 99.0% homology to vvMDV Md5 and RB1B strains, and 98%, the lowest homology homologous to the isolate GaHV-2/Italy/Ck/847/17 from Italy (Fig. 3). This gene shared 98.7% and 98.8% sequence identity with the mMDV CVI988 and 814 vaccine strains (Fig. 3). These findings demonstrate that the SS1901 isolate is most likely a newly emerging highly pathogenic wild strain.

Phylogenetic trees based on the Meq nucleotide sequences of MDV

In the 339 amino acids predicted to be encoded by the Meq gene of SS1901 strain, several conserved amino acid mutation sites were found to be same as those in other Chinese isolates, including at positions K77E, D80Y, V115A, T139A, P176R, and P217A (Table 2). Comparing with 52 reference sequences, the SS1901 strain showed an A-to-T mutation at position 88. This mutation was also found in Chinese isolates JZ2014, SD2012-1, and ZC2014, but not has not in isolates from other countries or regions. An additional Q-to-R mutation at position 93 is also present in Italy isolates Italy/509/15, Italy/Ck/507/15, Italy/Ck/599/16, and Italy/Ck/847/17 (Table 2). As a significant number of recent isolates of wild-type MDV in Southern China, also possess the A88T and Q93R mutations, it indicates that strains with these two mutations are becoming prevalent in circulating wild-type MDV strains.

Survival curves of SPF chickens infected with SS1901

To evaluate the pathogenicity of SS1901 isolate, a total of 120 one-day-old SPF chickens were randomly divided into six groups. In the SS1901 infection group with 20 chickens in each group. In the SS1901-infected group, the earliest death cases occurred at 21 days post infection, followed by the peak of mortality at 30–60 dpi with nearly 100% death at 60 dpi (Fig. 4). The mortality rate in the HVT vaccine-immunized group was 40% at 90 dpi, with the earliest death occurring 26 dpi (Fig. 4). The 814 and CVI988 immunized groups experienced the earliest deaths at 33 dpi, with the morbidity of 13.3% and 20% at 90 dpi, respectively. The CVI988 + HVT immunized group saw a mortality rate of 6.67%, and the earliest death happened at 38 dpi (Fig. 4). These results demonstrated that the SS1901 isolate was a highly lethal strain, and the HVT vaccine could no longer provide protection, CVI988, 814 and even CVI988 + HVT did not provide complete protection.

Clinical symptoms, tumorigenicity and vaccine protection index of SS1901 isolate infection in SPF chickens

Chicken infected with SS1901 isolate showed depression, ataxia and paralysis at 20 dpi(Fig. 5A-B). Dissection of these infected chickens showed hepatomegaly and splenomegaly with white tumor spots in livers) and spleens (Fig. 5C-D). Most infected chickens had visible tumor nodules in their hearts (Fig. 5C-D). Some infected chickens showed enlarged kidneys with white necrotic spots and white tumor nodules in the gonads (Fig. 5E). There were also lesions of tumor and sciatic nerve enlargement on the leg skin of the affected chickens (Fig. 5F). The results indicated that SS1901 infection could cause typical clinical symptoms of very virulent MDV strains, with death, paralysis, visceral tumors, and skin tumors.

In terms of tumorigenesis, no chickens in the control group developed tumors. Nevertheless, chickens in the SS1901 infection group developed MD symptoms with a tumorigenicity rate of 80% (12/15) (Table 3). The majority of the tumors in this group were found in the heart (12/15), liver (8/15), and spleen (11/15), and also appeared in the kidneys and glandular stomachs (6/15), while three infected chickens developed skin-type tumors (Table 3). Chickens in the HVT-vaccinated group exhibited a tumor incidence of 40%, with tumors predominantly involving the heart (6/15), followed in the liver, spleen, and glandular stomach (2/15), while kidney and skin tumors appeared in 1 case. Following the vaccinated groups with CVI988 + HVT, CVI988 and 814, the incidence rate of MD was 13.3%, 26.7%, 20%, tumorigenicity was 13.3%, 20%, and 20%, and vaccination protection index was 86.7%, 73.3%, and 80%, respectively (Table 4). These results confirmed the high pathogenicity of SS1901, characterized by visceral tumor formation in critical organs. Furthermore, the findings suggested that vaccination could reduce the incidence of tumor development associated with MD, but it does not completely eliminate the risk.

Effect of MDV SS1901 infection on body weight and immune organ index

Five chickens infected with SS1901 were randomly selected from each group and weighed at 7, 21, 35, and 56 dpi. Meanwhile, three chickens from each group were randomly chosen at 28 dpi and 42 dpi, respectively, for weighing the spleens, bursa, and thymus to calculate immune organ indices. No significant difference in the rate of body weight gain between the control and immunized groups at 7dpi (Fig. 6A). However, the body weight of the SS1901-infected group was significantly lower than the control group at 21dpi and 35 dpi (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6A). At 56 dpi, chickens in the SS1901 group exhibited severe emaciation, with a markedly reduced mean body weight compared to the control group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 6A). Additionally, the HVT immunized group also showed a significant reduction in body weight relative to the controls (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6A). Furthermore, the spleens of chickens in the SS1901-infected group were significantly enlarged compared to the control group (p < 0.001), while the bursa and thymus were significantly atrophied at 28 dpi and 42dpi(p < 0.001), with the spleen index substantially higher than that in both the vaccinated and control groups (p < 0.001) (Fig. 6B). Conversely, the bursa and thymus indices were significantly lower, showing a pronounced difference compared to the immunized and control groups (p < 0.001) (Fig. 6C-D). Overall, these findings demonstrated that SS1901 infection significantly reduced the weight gain and severely impaired the development of immune organs in SPF chickens, thereby compromising the immune function.

Histological examination and PCR detection of tumor tissues in SPF chickens infected with SS1901 strain

Organs and tissues collected from chickens challenged with SS1901 isolate were examined by histopathological staining (H&E) and PCR. Histopathological examination of H&E stained heart, liver, spleen, kidney, proventriculus, and bursa of fabricius tissues from SS1901 infected birds revealed distinct pathological features (Fig. 7). Cardiac tissue sections demonstrated focal lymphocyte infiltration with interstitial lymphocyte proliferation between myocardial fibers, accompanied by vascular tumor cell distribution within blood vessels (Fig. 7A-B). Hepatic sections exhibited hepatocellular degeneration and necrosis, characterized by diffuse lymphocyte infiltration extending through hepatic sinusoids and interlobular areas (Fig. 7C). Splenic architecture showed structural disintegration with endothelial cell exposure, indistinct demarcation between red pulp and white pulp, and white pulp atrophy (Fig. 7D). Notably, multifocal lymphocyte aggregations of varying sizes formed distinct infiltrative foci (Fig. 7D). Renal pathology manifested as interstitial expansion in tumor-affected regions, accompanied by diffuse lymphocyte proliferation within the renal interstitium (Fig. 7E). Proventricular sections revealed polymorphic lymphocyte aggregation with focal proliferation patterns and observable tumor cell mitotic figures (Fig. 7F). PCR amplification was performed on tissue samples collected from infected chickens with SS1901 strain, including the liver, spleen, bursa of fabricius, heart, proventriculus, kidney, and feather pulp, using primers specific to the Meq gene. The results indicated a target amplicon of approximately 1020 bp in all samples, corresponding to the size of the Meq gene in the Md5 reference strain (Fig. 7G). This finding serves as a molecular indicator confirming that the infected chickens developed disease due to SS1901 infection. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that infection with the MDV isolate SS1901 in-duces characteristic visceral tumors in heart, liver, spleen, kidney and glandular gastric.

Discussion

Marek’s disease (MD), first reported in 1907 as a cause of paralysis in chickens, has recently been documented to have been prevalent in Europe for over a millennium. Throughout its evolution, the viral oncoprotein Meq has undergone positive selection, resulting in progressive mutations that enhance its transcriptional activation and tumor-promoting functions [12]. In recent years, outbreaks of Marek’s disease virus (MDV) have occurred with increasing frequency even among vaccinated flocks, presenting with complex clinical signs including mortality, paralysis, tumors, and severe immunosuppression [13–15]. Although several studies have reported genomic analyses of virulence genes, pathogenicity, or immune protection efficacy of field MDV isolates, integrated studies correlating Meq molecular features, viral pathogenicity, and vaccine-mediated protection remain limited.

In this study, we isolated and purified a wild-type MDV strain from a vaccinated chicken farm that experienced an MD outbreak despite administration of CVI988/Rispens. We characterized its replication kinetics and identified novel point mutations in the Meq gene through sequencing. Pathogenicity assessments and vaccine challenge experiments demonstrated that this strain exhibits high virulence and oncogenicity, and that existing vaccines confer incomplete protection. Our findings elucidate the virological characteristics and pathogenic potential of this highly virulent isolate, providing important insights into the evolution of circulating MDV strains in Southern China and the evaluation of current vaccine strategies.

The Meq protein, critically involved in MDV induced oncogenesis, is composed of 339 amino acids. Its N-terminal region (amino acids 1–120) contains a basic region-leucine zipper (bZIP) domain responsible for DNA binding and dimerization, while the C-terminal region (aa 121–339) serves as a transcriptional regulatory module. Previous studies have established that Meq-deleted recombinant MDV (rMDV) failed to induce lymphomas, establishing Meq as a critical determinant of neoplastic transformation by virulent MDV in chickens [5]. Moreover, DNA binding by Meq is essential for cellular transformation, whereas its transactivation domain influences the tissue specificity and extent of lymphomagenesis [16]. Prior research has indicated that mutations in Meq are closely linked to viral virulence and geographical spread of MDV strains [17–19]. Sequencing analysis of the Meq gene from strain SS1901 revealed an open reading frame of 1020 bp, encoding a 339-amino acid polypeptide. Multiple conserved mutations were identified, including K77E, D80Y, V115A, T139A, P176R, and P217A, which are consistent with those documented in other Chinese field isolates. Notably, substitutions at positions 80, 139, and 176 have been proposed as molecular signatures of highly virulent MDV strains circulating in China [20]. In addition, SS1901 carries A88T and Q93R mutations, which are also present in recent Southern Chinese isolates as well as strains reported from India [21], suggesting convergent evolution and potential fitness advantages of these variations in certain regional outbreaks. However, whether these mutations directly contribute to SS1901’s pathogenicity, including its tumorigenicity and organ tropism, requires further experimental validation.

The oncogenic potential of Meq is partly attributed to its interaction with C-terminal binding protein 1 (CtBP), a property shared among other viral oncoproteins such as adenovirus E1A and Epstein Barr virus EBNA3A and EBNA3C. Meq engages with multiple host proteins, playing a pivotal role in MDV pathogenesis and oncogenesis. It has been shown to colocalize and interact with heat shock protein 70 (HSP70), which is involved in cellular transformation processes [22]; however, the functional significance of this interaction in MDV-induced tumor formation remains unclear. Meq also binds to p53, inhibiting its transcriptional activity and anti-apoptotic functions [23]. Furthermore, the bZIP domain of Meq interacts with chicken histone deacetylases chHDAC1 and chHDAC2, promoting their degradation via the proteasome pathway [24]. Meq likewise binds the p85 subunit of PI3K, activating the PI3K/Akt signaling axis [25]. A recent study revealed that Meq could bind to the C-terminal domain of STING and thereby antagonizes the cGAS–STING pathway to suppress typeⅠ interferon production [26]. Notably, the Meq gene of virulent MDV strains differs from that of vaccine strains by approximately 180 bp, and point mutations or deletion have been identified in Meq isolates from various countries [27, 28]. Furthermore, insertion in the transactivation domain enhanced both Meq transactivation and viral pathogenicity, while the deletion resulted in increased transactivation but reduced pathogenicity [29]. Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying the enhanced MDV pathogenicity and tumorigenesis mediated by these Meq polymorphisms have not yet been completely characterized.

Vaccination currently serves as the primary strategy for controlling MDV, however, existing vaccines fail to completely prevent viral transmission and spread. Over the past decade, prevalent MDV strains in China have undergone continuous evolution with increasing virulence, and multiple incidents of vaccine-breakthrough infections caused by highly virulent field strains have been reported. Notably, several hypervirulent MDV variants have recently been isolated from vaccinated flocks [30, 31], some of which meet the criteria for vv + MDV. It has been reported that the vv + MDV strain GX18NNM4 caused 75.9% morbidity, 43.1% mortality, and 70.7% tumor incidence. In contrast, the CVI988 vaccine conferred only 33.9% protection against this strain, while the 814 provided a higher yet still limited efficacy of 61.1% [32]. Other highly virulent strains, such as SDCW01 and HNSQ01, induced 100% morbidity and over 50% mortality. The protective indices of HVT, CVI988, and CVI988 + HVT against strain SDCW01 were merely 38.5%, 46.2%, and 50%, respectively [4]. Compared to these vv⁺MDV and other highly virulent MDV isolates, the SS1901 strain similarly exhibits high oncogenicity and lethality. Although monovalent CVI988 or its combination with HVT still provided partial protection, complete immunity was not achieved. In this study, we isolated a highly pathogenic MDV strain, designated SS1901, from a flock vaccinated with CVI988/Rispens. Animal challenge experiments demonstrated that SS1901 infection resulted in 100% mortality within 60 days post-infection and an 80% tumor incidence. Tumors were predominantly observed in the heart, spleen, and liver, followed by the kidneys, proventriculus, and skin. Such a strongly oncogenic MDV strain capable of inducing both visceral and skin tumors is rarely reported worldwide. Vaccine efficacy trials revealed that the protection rates conferred by HVT, CVI988, 814, and CVI988 + HVT against SS1901 challenge were 46.7%, 73.7%, 80%, and 86.7%, respectively. These results indicate that currently available commercial vaccines can no longer provide complete protection against such emerging hypervirulent strains.

The underlying mechanisms driving the high virulence and oncogenicity of SS1901 remain to be fully elucidated. We propose several factors may involve the following aspects. Firstly, persistent immune pressure from widespread CVI988 vaccination may drive competitive dynamics between vaccine and field strains within hosts, potentially favoring the emergence of field variants with replicative advantages that enable immune escape and enhanced proliferation. Such strains may preferentially infect T lymphocytes, leading to immune exhaustion and thereby facilitating robust viral replication and tumor development. Secondly, sustained selective pressure from the persistent presence of the CVI988 vaccine strain may drive MDV toward increased virulence through genetic mutations. Beyond the observed point mutations in the Meq protein, comprehensive whole genome sequencing and comparative genomics with vaccine and virulent strains are needed to unravel the genetic basis of heightened pathogenicity. Furthermore, previous studies have indicated that the CVI988 vaccine strain can undergo genomic recombination with field strains, thereby increasing viral fitness [33]. The MDV isolate GX0101 has been identified as a recombinant virus carrying the REV long terminal repeat (LTR) sequence, which demonstrates robust horizontal transmission capacity [34]. This evidence suggests that natural recombination significantly augments the genetic diversity of the viral genome, not only complicating clinical manifestations but also supplying a diverse pool of candidate strains for the development of novel recombinant vaccines.

Conclusions

In summary, a highly virulent MDV strain was isolated from a poultry farm in Southern China experiencing an MD outbreak. This strain caused a 100% mortality rate and an 80% tumor incidence, primarily in the heart, spleen, and liver. The CVI988 vaccine provided 73.3% protection, while the 814 offered 80%. Combining CVI988 and HVT vaccines resulted in 86.7% protection, compared to 46.7% with HVT alone. The SS1901 strain, besides having typical Chinese isolate mutations, also exhibited A88T and Q93R mutations found in isolates from southern China, India, and Italy. This study offers in-sights into the biological characteristics and pathogenicity of current MDV strains and supports optimizing vaccine protocols.

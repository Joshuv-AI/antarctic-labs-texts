Dynamics of Mpox epidemics by the interplay of COVID-19 pandemic in Africa
Authors: Oyewole K. Oyedele, Nifarta P. Andrew, Motunrayo D. Olalere, Monalisa O. Owie, Victoria Etuk, Temitayo Lawal, Olugbenga Akinbiyi, Evaezi Okpokoro, Alash’le Abimiku
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMpox epidemics continue to spread to African borders following the outbreak that has affected over 120 countries and thus posed a threat to global health security. Dynamics of Mpox spread over time, fatality, and country-level risk were investigated intra- and post-COVID-19 pandemic to inform optimal surveillance, response, and prevention strategy.MethodsAn epidemiological (retrospective cohort) analysis of complete weekly Mpox situation report across 20 African countries was conducted intra (January 2022–May 2023) and post (June 2023–September 2024) pandemic. Mpox epidemics and mortality were assessed against endemic-related exposures, and Mpox fatality rate, distribution, and patterns were assessed via summary analysis, spatial spread, and epi-curve respectively. Mantel Haenszel statistics assessed the confounding effects of COVID-19 and seasonality, while zero-inflated negative binomial model was fitted to evaluate the epidemic and mortality risk. Analysis was performed in Excel, QGIS, and Stata at 5% level of significance.ResultsAbout 9446 confirmed Mpox cases occurred in Africa between January 2022 and September 2024, with DR-Congo accounting for over 75%. Mpox case fatality rate is 0.58% and significantly different intra (1.16%) and post (0.46%) pandemic (p < 0.001). Though fatality is more prevalent in clade Ia and Ib (58.1%), with a higher risk than clade Ia only (IRR = 4.46, 95% = 1.12–17.69), the Mpox outbreak spread more rapidly through clade Ia and II to 20 countries post pandemic. Risk of Mpox incidence is about four times higher post-COVID-19 than intra-pandemic (IRR = 3.77, 95% CI = 2.83–5.00). The epi curve reveals a mixed epidemic, with common-source and propagated patterns intra- and post -pandemic. While COVID-19 does not confound the association between Mpox mortality and endemicity (M-H = 6.821, p = 0.009), the association was confounded by seasonal variation and thus, 35% of Mpox mortality could be prevented by eliminating high-endemic spread.ConclusionsWeak surveillance capacity was evident as African countries appeared to have relaxed health security and outbreaks response strategies post-COVID-19 pandemic. Scale-up of vaccination uptake against the Mpox virus is required, particularly for clades Ia, Ib, and II, to prevent Mpox spread and mortality. A consistent multi-country surveillance will be critical to contain Mpox epidemics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-026-04804-0.

Background

Mpox (formerly known as monkeypox) outbreaks recently emerged amidst the coronavirus 2019 pandemic (COVID-19) that has significantly reshaped global public health [1]. Mpox is a zoonotic viral disease belonging to the orthopox virus genus, with lesions like smallpox discovered in colonies of monkeys in 1958 [2, 3]. In 1970, the first human Mpox case was recorded in Africa in a 9-month-old child from a remote village in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DR Congo) [4–7]. The two genetic clades identified in Africa were clade I (Congo Basin/Central African clades) and clade II (Western African clades), with clade I infection leading to more severe illness and fatality [8].

Unlike COVID-19, which spreads primarily through respiratory droplets including contact with contaminated surfaces, oral and fecal secretions, Mpox is transmitted from zoonotic hosts to human populations, direct contacts with respiratory droplets, surfaces, or infected persons including skin-to-skin, sexual contact [9, 10]. However, contact with infected persons has been responsible for the spread in most cases [11–13]. Mpox is a re-emerging disease, making prevention and treatment modality considerably familiar [8, 14]. Over time, studies have compared and shown striking similarities and differences between the COVID-19 pandemic and mpox outbreak, regarding mode of spread, management, surveillance methods, and control, drawing a relationship between responses to both viruses [15]. Mpox incidence has risen since the discontinuation of smallpox vaccination in 1980, which was effective in providing immunity against other pox viruses [16, 17].

Despite being a known disease for decades, the Mpox outbreak took many countries by surprise in 2022, as the world was already embroiled in the fight against COVID-19. The initial response to Mpox was slow, with public health authorities focusing predominantly on the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, leading to delays in identifying and responding to the outbreak [18]. This led to the WHO declaring Mpox a public health emergency of international concern (PHEIC) twice, the first time in May 2022 and the second time in August 2024 [19]. While the Mpox epidemic in Africa has historically been confined to Central and Western Africa, the 2022 outbreak marked a shift, with cases rising dramatically in countries beyond its endemic regions and rapidly spreading across the six WHO regions [11, 12, 19, 20]. As of August 2024, over 18,000 suspected cases with 5000 Mpox confirmed cases and over 600 Mpox deaths were recorded in the African region alone, representing a 160% increase in new Mpox cases from the year 2022 [21, 22].

In the 2022 global Mpox surge, Mpox cases and/or mortality was reported in over 70 countries across America and Europe [23]. Although Africa, with historical Mpox prevalence, was hit particularly hard by the compounded impacts of the outbreaks. Countries like DR Congo, Nigeria, and Cameroon had long experienced sporadic Mpox outbreaks [24], but the 2022 global surge marked a significant shift. New cases began to appear in countries that had never seen Mpox before, such as South Africa and Ghana [25]. Similarly, the second multi-country Mpox surge in 2024 was marked by the spread of the virus to more African countries, particularly in Northern Africa—Morocco, Sudan, and Egypt [26]. These nations, already stumbling from the effects of COVID-19, were faced with additional strain in their healthcare system. The incidence of Mpox outbreaks in countries without direct links to endemic regions likely indicated an imported case from an endemic area with undetected local transmission, or an initial case in a non-endemic region that led to subsequent local spread [27–30].

Studies have independently described the COVID-19 and Mpox viruses across African countries [15, 28, 31]. However, there is limited evidence on the epidemiological analysis of the dual burden of the pandemic and epidemic. This remains a threat to the vulnerable public health system in African countries, where the fragile surveillance infrastructure, including human and material resources, is continuously stretched to contain the spread of the viruses. Also, studies rarely examine the Mpox epidemiological dynamics vis-à-vis the COVID-19 pandemic. Thus, we investigated the dynamics of Mpox epidemics by describing the incidence, epidemiological patterns, and associated fatality by the interplay with the COVID-19 pandemic, to respond to the following questions: Is there any change in the rate of Mpox epidemics due to the COVID-19 pandemic in Africa? What is the epi pattern of Mpox epidemics vis-à-vis the COVID-19 pandemic? Does the COVID-19 pandemic influence Mpox fatality? The study outcome will potentially provide a multi-country framework to guide preparedness and response modality to such simultaneous pandemic and outbreak, ensuring a more resilient and effective response strategy to cross-border health crises, particularly in the endemic African countries.

Methods

Study design and settings

This is an epidemiological (retrospective-cohort) analysis of pooled multi-country Mpox outbreaks situation reports across 20 African countries between January 2022 and September 2024. Africa is the second largest of the world’s seven continents and comprises 54 countries in 5 delineated regions (north, west, east, south, and central)—Additional file1: Fig. S1. Currently, Africa’s population is over 1.5 billion people, and the land area is 30.3 million square km, Nigeria is the most populous while the Seychelles is the least populous in Africa [32]. Mpox outbreak has been predominant in DR Congo in central Africa, spreading rapidly to other African regions (particularly central and western Africa), with about 18,000 suspected Mpox cases reported from 20 affected countries as of September 2024 [21]. Data source and availability

The collective multi-country Mpox situation reports per individual country surveillance management system were sourced from the WHO global data observatory profile. The data reported the periodic country epidemic profile on Mpox cases and fatalities. The periodic situation report can be assessed from the repository via the link https://www.who.int/emergencies/situation-reports

Data collection and mining technique

Mpox case surveillance situation reports by the WHO member countries provided on a weekly basis were collated and aggregated to monitor and track the global Mpox epidemics. We extracted the Mpox data report provided by the African countries between January 2022 and September 2024. Mpox cases between January 2022 and May 2023 were gathered as the intra-COVID-19 pandemic Mpox cases while the cases between June 2023 and September 2024 were collated as the post-pandemic Mpox. A total of 9446 confirmed Mpox cases were reported and collated in the period.

Description of disease and exposure characteristics

Disease characteristics

A confirmed Mpox case is defined by having a positive laboratory test to Mpox virus. Mpox cases were then counted as 1, 2, 3… for a positive Mpox virus test and otherwise counted as 0 for a negative Mpox virus test, indicating absence of Mpox following a confirmatory laboratory test.

Mpox Fatality refers to the number of deaths due to the Mpox illness in percentage of the incidence of Mpox in a population at a specific period. Mpox (Mpox virus) clades based on phylogenetic analysis were divided into two major clades I and II and subdivided into subclades by occurrence as: clades Ia, Ib, II, Ia and II, Ia and Ib.

Exposure characteristics

COVID-19 Pandemic status: The exposure to COVID-19 was identified and grouped by the pandemic phase, with the laboratory confirmed Mpox cases reported between January 2022 and May 2023 referred to as the intra-COVID-19 pandemic Mpox cases while the post-COVID-19 Pandemic Mpox cases were the reported Mpox cases between June 2023 and September 2024.

Season: Periodic climatic condition of exposure was measured by country seasonal variations, with African countries in the same region having somewhat similar seasonal patterns and different from African countries in different regions. Seasonal exposure was mainly classified as dry and wet.

Endemicity: This refers to the consistent presence of the Mpox disease in a specific geographical area or population, herein the DR Congo. Thus, confirmed Mpox cases from DR Congo were grouped as high-risk endemicity while Mpox cases from other African countries were referred to as the moderate/low risk region based on the WHO Mpox endemicity and risk classification.

African Region: The African regions are the five borderlines dividing the countries in the African continent into northern, southern, eastern, central, and western Africa (Additional file 1: Fig. S1).

Countries: The 20 countries where at least an Mpox case was reported between January 2022 and September 2024 were referred to as exposed countries. These are Mozambique, Guinea, Gabon, Benin, Egypt, Morocco, Rwanda, Kenya, Sudan, Liberia, South Africa, Uganda, Congo, Cameroon, Côte d’Ivoire, Central African Republic, Ghana, Burundi, Nigeria, and DR Congo (Fig. 1).

Data management, epidemiological, and statistical analysis

The dataset collated in Excel was organized, cleaned, and checked for missingness and outliers. Zero cases were double-checked to ascertain entries. Extra dataset on country COVID-19, endemicity, and seasonality sourced and/or generated were merged with the Mpox data. The complete dataset was then imported into STATA version 18.0 for subsequent epidemiological and statistical analysis.

Descriptive analysis began with the computation of Mpox incidence by COVID-19 pandemic status across the variable classifications. The graphical presentation shows the Mpox epidemics and mortality intra- and post-COVID-19 pandemic. The differences in the skewed Mpox data distribution across groups were subsequently assessed via the median test and adjudged significant when p < 0.05.

Epidemiological analysis was conducted to depict the epi curve in weekly Mpox pattern over time. The epi curve was subsequently grouped by the COVID-19 pandemic phase. Stratified analysis based on the retrospective cohort (with equal follow-up) was then conducted to assess the confounding effect of the COVID-19 pandemic and seasonality on the association between Mpox epidemic (case/mortality) and endemicity. The population attributable risk and Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel statistics were reported, such that a significant (p < 0.05) test indicated the absence of a confounder.

The comparative spatial spread of Mpox epidemics during and after the COVID-19 pandemic was then performed, leveraging the African region shapefile on the QGIS version 3.3.6. The geo-analytics was further refined with the open street map to improve graphics [33]. The statistical analysis was completed by fitting a zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) model to the Mpox data, following the overdispersion test indicating significant variability (i.e., variance > mean) and the occurrence of excess zeros of Mpox cases and/or mortality. The appropriate incidence risk ratio (IRR) and the corresponding 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were subsequently reported. All descriptive, spatial, and epidemiological/statistical analyses were conducted in Excel, QGIS, and STATA respectively.

Effects of COVID-19 and season on Mpox epidemics

The confounding effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and season on the association between Mpox epidemics/mortality and the endemicity were presented in Table 3. We found a true association between Mpox epidemics and endemicity after adjusting for the COVID-19 pandemic (M-H = 40.3; p < 0.001). Similarly, the association between Mpox mortality and endemicity was not confounded by COVID-19 (M-H = 35.41; p = 0.009). However, both associations were confounded by season (p > 0.05), and about 16.79% and 35.41% of Mpox epidemics and mortality could be prevented if the exposure in the high-risk endemic countries or region can be removed or contained respectively. Discussion

This retrospective cohort analytical study presents a comprehensive evaluation of the epidemiologic dynamics of the Mpox epidemics in Africa within the context of the COVID-19 pandemic and the related exposure characteristics. Our findings underscore a significant shift in the incidence, distribution, and mortality patterns of Mpox across the intra- and post-pandemic periods. A total of 9446 Mpox cases were reported, with incidence rising substantially post-pandemic, consistent with emerging literature on infectious disease resurgence following pandemic disruptions [1, 8].

The notable increase in Mpox cases from 1632 during the pandemic to 7814 post-pandemic (a fourfold increase in incidence) suggests a potential under-detection during the COVID-19 era, likely due to strained surveillance systems and diverted health resources [5, 21]. The subsequent rise may also be attributed to increased human mobility, reduced public health restrictions, and surveillance recovery. In congruent to our findings, Haider et al. (2022) highlighted the gaps in Mpox detection and the underestimated burden in Sub-Saharan Africa due to competing priorities during the pandemic [27]. DR Congo and Nigeria accounted for the highest Mpox incidence, aligning with historical data, indicating their status as endemic zones [16, 24]. The expansion to more than 20 countries post-COVID-19 pandemic, including new Mpox cases in Northern and Southern Africa, reflects the evolving epidemiological landscape of Mpox, potentially linked to ecological changes, urbanization, and increased zoonotic transmission pathways in the sub-Saharan African settings [4, 30].

Moreover, the reduction in Mpox case fatality rate from 1.16% during the COVID-19 to 0.46% post-pandemic is notable and likely reflects improved clinical awareness, earlier diagnosis, and better case management post-pandemic, as lessons from the COVID-19 response [3]. However, high CFRs were still observed in countries such as Mozambique, South Africa, and Cameroon, indicating regional disparities in healthcare access and response capacity. Interestingly, the mortality burden was concentrated in areas dominated by clades Ia and Ib, which contributed to about half of the deaths. This observation supports existing evidence on the differential virulence and transmissibility of Mpox clades and sub-clades type [6, 28]. Whereas, the elevated CFR in less endemic areas such as Mozambique also reflects delayed recognition and treatment due to lower preparedness levels.

Findings from the Mantel–Haenszel analysis revealed that while the COVID-19 pandemic significantly influenced the distribution and mortality of Mpox, it was not a confounder in the association between endemicity and the Mpox disease burden. However, seasonality emerged as a key confounding factor, suggesting that ecological and climatic variations play a critical role in Mpox transmission dynamics, and this was consistent with prior studies linking zoonotic outbreaks to seasonal and environmental factors [12, 31]. Although seasonal exposure showed elevated risk of Mpox epidemics, it did not significantly affect Mpox cases and/or deaths, suggesting that while environmental factors may influence transmission, their effect was secondary compared to pandemic-related disruptions [34, 35]. However, the elevated mortality risk during the wet season may still reflect direct climate influences due to Mpox virus survival associated with the cold season and increasing the exposure and fatality risk as the virus will tend to be more potent in the period [36].

The progressive pattern of the Mpox epidemic post-pandemic, transitioning from a common-source outbreak to a propagated one, further illustrates the evolving nature of disease spread, possibly influenced by ongoing human-to-human transmission, waning smallpox immunity, and increased exposure to animal reservoirs [7, 13]. Furthermore, the risk analysis showed a nearly fourfold increase in Mpox incidence post-pandemic particularly in Central Africa, where countries like Burundi and the DR Congo showed the highest risk. This suggests a syndemic interaction between COVID-19 and Mpox, likely exacerbated by weakened health systems and disrupted surveillance [18, 37]. The increased risk associated with clades II, Ib, and mixed clades (Ia and Ib) also suggests that clade-specific characteristics are critical to Mpox transmission and severity, an observation echoed by studies that reviewed the diagnostics and assessed the travel-related Mpox in pandemic era [3, 30]. While mortality did not significantly increase post-pandemic, high-endemic areas had over twice the mortality risk, indicating that structural vulnerabilities continue to drive the adverse outcomes [17, 38].

Implications for epidemic surveillance system strengthening

African countries seemed to have lost the momentum gained from the case-based surveillance system response to the COVID-19 pandemic. While surveillance focus was on COVID-19, Africa still suffers from silent outbreaks such as Mpox. Hence the concurrent burden of the COVID-19 pandemic and Mpox outbreak had heightened the risk of mortality, particularly in endemic countries [28]. This was confounded by the seasonal variability as the wet period was associated with favorable temperature, humidity, precipitation, and other climatic parameters that could influence virus survival and subsequent active transmission and importation. Overall, the findings underscore the need for both country and region-specific interventions, enhanced genomic surveillance, and improved health infrastructure and capacity, especially in high-burden countries such as DR Congo and Nigeria [39].

Study limitations and strengths

This study has some limitations, as we rely on the collated Mpox situation report by the WHO member countries. A major drawback was the non-availability of patient-level information such as date of symptom onset, treatment, diagnostics, etc., and related demographic information. Though the weekly reported data improved the periodic mechanism for data aggregation. While our study adjusted for confounders to report true association, caution should be exercised when interpreting the findings as all causality criteria were not exhausted. There is a likelihood for under-reporting or irregular reporting from any of the WHO member countries as the response during the hit of the outbreak was prioritized. However, we minimized data quality issues by validating new cases from the cumulative reported cases per country and per period of reporting to achieve accurate and complete data.

Our study strengths can however be traced to the temporal and spatial multi-country analysis of completed and confirmed Mpox cases across the study period, which informed the place and time dynamics of Mpox incidence. The concurrent assessment of both COVID-19 pandemic and Mpox outbreak, with the classification of Mpox epidemics by the intra- and post-pandemic phases systematically provide evidence, to inform on simultaneous response to epidemic and pandemic. The large dataset analyzed with a mixed of epidemiological and statistical techniques to adjust for confounders and provide spatiotemporal inference improved the study reliability and generalizability. The fitted ZINB model that improved the study precision can also be regarded as a strength.

Conclusions

Mpox epidemics continue to spread to central, eastern, and western Africa and beyond the borderline of African countries, with significant surge post-COVID-19 pandemic and exhibiting the mixed epidemics characterized by the combined common-source and propagated epi patterns. While Mpox incidence rises in about fourfolds post-pandemic, the intra-pandemic period was threatened more by its mortality, underlining at least a mortality in a hundred confirmed cases across the period. The DR Congo is the most affected country bearing over four-fifths of the African Mpox burden to underscore its high-risk endemicity. We found a true association between Mpox incidence/mortality and endemicity after adjusting for the COVID-19 pandemic, while the associations were confounded by seasonal variation, revealing higher Mpox epidemics in the wet than the dry season. Similarly, more than one-third of Mpox mortality is attributable to the high-endemic single source (DR Congo) and can be prevented if the exposure is contained. Correspondingly, the risk of Mpox mortality was twice higher in the high-endemic region than the moderate/low endemic region, while Mpox clades (Ia and Ib) and (Ia and II) increased the risk of Mpox incidence and mortality compared to only clades Ia respectively.

Recommendations

This study highlights the need for a multisectoral approach and a multi-country response collaboration network prioritizing the high-endemic region such as the DR Congo, Nigeria, and now Burundi to address the common-source Mpox epidemics while strengthening the surveillance capacity framework to contain the progressive Mpox spread across the African countries’ borderline. Scaling up vaccination uptake will be critical to raise the population immunity against the Mpox virus, particularly in endemic regions with high-volume cold climate influencing Mpox virus transmission. Studies should assess the impact of climate change on the global Mpox epidemics and mortality to support the preparedness plan.

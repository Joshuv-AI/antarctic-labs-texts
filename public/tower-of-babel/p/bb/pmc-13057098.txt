Design and implementation of a deep learning framework for automated crop classification and health diagnosis in precision agriculture
Authors: Atul Kumar Pal, B. D. K. Patro, Shshank Chaube
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a three-phase deep learning framework comprising (i) multi-modal data acquisition from drones and satellites, (ii) standardized pre-processing including interpolation for missing temporal data, and (iii) CNN-based feature extraction for real-time health classification. This framework relies on a mathematical model based on neural networks that classifies and detects the condition of agriculture, removing the reliance on manual tasks and subjective diagnosis. This paper focuses on three main aspects of our framework: data acquisition, training and prediction. Data is collected using sensors like drones, cameras, and satellite imagery and is pre-processed to filter out noise and improve quality. The training part uses CNN to learn features from the data and become more meaningful. The prediction part of the task classifies, and diagnoses crop health through the trained model using the features. The framework accuracy for crops such as maize, potato, and wheat has been tested and yielded over 90% accuracy. The novelty of this work resides in the development of a multi-modal deep learning architecture that fuses macro-scale satellite imagery with micro-scale drone and IoT sensor data to improve diagnostic reliability. The framework was validated on a multi-source agricultural dataset using a 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing protocol. Experimental results demonstrate an accuracy exceeding 90% for staple crops. Using this framework can increase the visibility and quality of information maintained for crop health and improve the decision-making routine of farmers in real time. Additionally, automation of this process can significantly reduce labor costs and increase productivity per crop. Implementing this framework can contribute to precision agriculture and sustainable management practices.

Related works

Recent literature in precision agriculture has transitioned from manual observation to automated systems. The existing research can be categorized into three primary themes: Deep Learning-based Image Processing, IoT and Automation Frameworks, and Hybrid/Review-based Systematic Approaches. Table 1 gives the comprehensive analysis.

Deep learning-based disease detection

A significant portion of recent research focuses on using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for visual diagnosis. Pathak et al.26 developed a robust CNN specifically for Mango leaf diseases, highlighting the efficacy of deep feature extraction in precision agriculture. Similarly, Pal and Kumar25 introduced “AgriDet,” a framework focused on classifying the severity of leaf diseases to allow for targeted intervention. Saini et al.28 and Bajpai et al.23 further demonstrated the versatility of deep learning by applying automated recognition to citrus fruits and various plant-leaf pathologies, respectively. While these models achieve high accuracy, they primarily rely on spatial image data and often overlook the temporal environmental factors provided by ground sensors.

IoT and automation in smart farming

To address the need for real-time monitoring, several studies have integrated the Internet of Things (IoT). Sravanthi and Moparthi30 proposed an efficient IoT-based system for crop disease prediction and recommendation, utilizing sensor data to forecast potential outbreaks. Raj et al.27 discussed the broader infrastructure of modern precision farming, emphasizing the role of cloud architecture and data analytics in automation. Karunathilake et al.22 provided a roadmap for smart farming, identifying innovations in sensor technology as a key driver for future agricultural productivity. The limitation inherent in these works is the reliance on numerical sensor data, which may lack the diagnostic specificity provided by visual imaging.

Systematic reviews and hybrid frameworks

The integration of multiple technologies has been a growing area of interest. Chaudhary et al.24 conducted a critical review of hybrid frameworks that combine Machine Learning with IoT, arguing that such synergy is essential for precise farming. Additionally, Khan et al.29 provided a systematic review of hyperspectral imaging, noting its potential to detect physiological changes via deep learning. These reviews suggest that while individual components (CNN, IoT, Hyperspectral) are mature, there is a lack of unified frameworks that fuse these multi-modal data streams—a gap that our proposed model aims to fill.

Proposed model

The practical strength of the proposed framework lies in its multi-modal data fusion layer. By utilizing a common timestamp and GPS-coordinate-based anchoring system, the model integrates macro-environmental indicators from Sentinel-2 satellites with micro-level vegetation indices from drone-based NIR sensors and soil-specific metrics from ground IoT nodes. This unified input vector allows the deep learning backbone to cross-reference visual symptoms with physical soil stressors. The proposed framework follows a modular architecture consisting of four stages: (i) Multi-modal Data Acquisition (Satellite, Drone, and IoT), (ii) Feature Extraction via a Deep CNN, (iii) Contextual Weighting using an Attention Mechanism, and (iv) Final Classification. Model selection rationale

A hybrid approach was selected to maximize the strengths of different architectures: CNNs are unparalleled in spatial pattern recognition for lesion detection, while an attention-inspired mechanism focuses the model on relevant pixels, ignoring healthy background noise. The final classification utilizes specialized ML layers to improve decision boundary separation in the fused multi-modal feature space.

The load_and_preprocess_data() function is a critical component in a deep learning pipeline for precision agriculture, designed to handle multi-modal data from diverse agricultural sources. It begins by loading satellite imagery (e.g., Sentinel-2) with dimensions (Height, Width, Channels) for broad-scale crop monitoring, alongside high-resolution drone imagery in RGB and Near-Infrared (NIR) bands (4 channels) to capture detailed field conditions. Soil sensor data, including key metrics like pH, moisture, and nutrient levels (N, P, K), is also loaded to assess soil health, while weather data (temperature, humidity, rainfall) provides environmental context. Ground truth labels for crop type and health status are included for supervised learning. The preprocessing stage standardizes these inputs: satellite images are normalized to a consistent scale, drone images undergo augmentation (e.g., rotation, flipping) to enhance model robustness, soil data is standardized to ensure uniform feature scaling, and missing weather data is interpolated to maintain temporal continuity. The processed data is then combined into a unified multi-modal dataset, which is split into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) sets to facilitate model training and evaluation. This structured approach ensures that the data is both representative and reliable for tasks like crop classification and health diagnosis, while addressing challenges like spatial alignment, missing data, and feature heterogeneity. The function’s output—a partitioned dataset—enables the development of models capable of leveraging diverse agricultural data for actionable insights.

Conclusion

The proposed deep learning-based framework for automatic classification and disease diagnosis has been developed and successfully realized for precision agriculture. It is based on CNN and uses a Transfer learning-based framework to classify the crops and detect their health. The primary contribution of this research is the development of a multi-modal deep learning architecture that successfully synchronizes macro-scale satellite indices with micro-scale drone imagery and ground-level IoT data. Validated across maize, potato, and wheat crops, the framework achieved a peak precision of 92.4% and an efficiency improvement of 12.4% over baseline models, proving that multi-source data fusion significantly enhances diagnostic reliability in complex agricultural environments. Despite these results, a limitation of the current system is its dependency on high-speed connectivity for cloud-based inference, which may be challenging in remote rural areas. Future research will focus on: (i) Deploying the model on edge-computing hardware to reduce latency; (ii) Expanding the dataset to include diverse climate zones; and (iii) Integrating Reinforcement Learning to provide automated treatment recommendations (e.g., precise pesticide dosage) alongside the diagnosis.

QuANTUM-Wild: a Phase III, randomized trial of quizartinib in newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD–negative acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Pau Montesinos, Jessica K. Altman, Lars Bullinger, June-Won Cheong, Amir T. Fathi, Mark J. Levis, Selina Luger, Toshihiro Miyamoto, Esther Natalie Oliva, Alexander E. Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTQuizartinib is a potent type II inhibitor of FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) that demonstrated significant survival benefit among patients with newly diagnosed (ND) acute myeloid leukemia (AML) without FLT3-internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) in the randomized, Phase II QUIWI trial. Here, we present the rationale and design of QuANTUM-Wild, a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, Phase III study to confirm the efficacy and safety of quizartinib plus standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy and as maintenance monotherapy in adult patients with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML. Eligible patients are randomized 2:2:1 to one of three treatment arms. Patients in Arm A (n ≈ 280) receive quizartinib plus chemotherapy during induction and consolidation (with option for transplant without study drug), followed by up to 36 cycles of single-agent maintenance therapy. Patients in Arm B (n ≈ 280) receive placebo in all treatment phases plus chemotherapy, then as maintenance monotherapy. Patients in Arm C (n ≈ 140) receive quizartinib plus chemotherapy in the induction and consolidation phases, then placebo during the maintenance phase, to specifically assess the utility of quizartinib maintenance. The primary endpoint is overall survival. Secondary endpoints include event-free survival, remission rate, and safety.Clinical trial registration: http://www.clinicaltrials.gov identifier is NCT06578247.

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is the most common type of acute leukemia in adults, defined by the expansion of abnormal proliferative myeloblasts [1–3]. AML is a highly heterogeneous and aggressive hematologic malignancy that is characterized by the presence of acquired mutations and cytogenetic and epigenetic alterations that influence disease prognosis [1,4]. AML has a 5-year survival rate of 40%, which rapidly decreases with higher age at diagnosis. These poor outcomes are partially due to high rates of primary refractoriness and disease relapse with standard treatment approaches [5]. For patients with newly diagnosed (ND) AML without an FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) gene mutation and who are fit for intensive therapy, the current standard of care is anthracycline‑based chemotherapy (induction chemotherapy with the “7 + 3” regimen; 7 days of cytarabine plus 3 days of an anthracycline), followed by consolidation including high-dose cytarabine, with allogeneic [allo] stem cell transplantation for eligible patients, with continued lower-intensity remission maintenance therapy in most cases [6]. Despite recent advances, outcomes for patients without FLT3 mutations, including FLT3-internal tandem duplications (FLT3-ITDs), remain unsatisfactory, underscoring an unmet need for new treatment options.

Upon activation, FLT3, a class III receptor tyrosine kinase, initiates multiple key signal transduction cascades that regulate cell proliferation and survival (Figure 1) [7–9]. In normal human hematopoiesis, FLT3 expression is restricted to immature hematopoietic progenitors. FLT3 gene mutations occur in approximately 30% of ND AML cases; FLT3-ITDs are the most common type of FLT3 alteration in AML (~25% of all AML cases) [10]. FLT3-ITD AML is associated with poor prognosis, high relapse rates after standard chemotherapy, and higher white blood cell and marrow blast counts at diagnosis [3,10–13]. Putative mechanisms of action of quizartinib in FLT3-ITD–negative AML.

FLT3-tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations, occurring in ~10% of AML cases, cause constitutive kinase activation; however, their prognostic significance remains less well defined than FLT3-ITD and can depend on co-mutations and the presence of cytogenetic abnormalities [3,10,14]. Non-canonical mutations in FLT3, outside of the activation loop, may also promote ligand-independent dimerization and constitutive phosphorylation [15].

Targeted FLT3 inhibitors have dramatically improved prognosis for patients with FLT3-ITD–positive ND AML when combined with intensive chemotherapy. Quizartinib is an oral, selective, second-generation, type II inhibitor of FLT3 with potent binding affinity against FLT3-wild type (WT), FLT3-ITD, and several FLT3 variants with point mutations within the kinase domain [16]. Quizartinib is approved in multiple countries in combination with induction and consolidation chemotherapy and as a single-agent remission maintenance for patients with ND FLT3-ITD–positive AML based on results from the Phase III, placebo-controlled, double-blinded QuANTUM-First trial (NCT02668653, EudraCT 2015-004856-24, JAPIC CTI 173,667). In QuANTUM-First, patients received quizartinib at 40 mg/day (or 20 mg/day with concomitant strong cytochrome P450 3A [CYP3A] inhibitors) during induction and consolidation. During the single-agent maintenance phase, the starting dose was 30 mg/day escalating to 60 mg/day if prolongation of the mean corrected QT interval (using Fridericia’s method [QTcF]) remained ≤450 ms [17]. This trial demonstrated a significantly prolonged overall survival (OS) with quizartinib in combination with chemotherapy and as single-agent maintenance therapy for patients achieving remission (hazard ratio [HR] 0.78; P = 0.032) [17–21].

The rationale for using quizartinib for the treatment of FLT3-ITD–negative AML is supported by several proposed mechanisms. First, FLT3 overexpression occurs in 70–100% of AML blasts, independent of FLT3 mutational status, and has been linked to poorer survival outcomes than with lower FLT3 expression, suggesting that aberrant FLT3-WT signaling contributes to leukemic survival and proliferation [17,22–26]. Second, leukemic cell lines co-express FLT3 and its ligand (FLT3L) leading to constitutive FLT3-WT phosphorylation and activation, indicating that a potential autocrine or paracrine signaling loop may operate independently of ITD or TKD mutations [27–29]. This autocrine/paracrine driven FLT3-WT activation initiates signaling through the same oncogenic pathways as constitutively active FLT3-ITD, including PI3K/AKT, RAS/ERK1/ERK2, and STAT5 [30]. Therefore, quizartinib may exert antileukemic effects toward FLT3-ITD–negative AML by suppressing signaling mediated by either FLT3-WT overexpression or FLT3L‑driven autocrine activation. Also, studies of WT1-mutant AML demonstrate that loss of WT1-mediated repression leads to increased FLT3 expression and STAT5 signaling, thereby sensitizing cells to FLT3 inhibition in the FLT3-ITD–negative context. In addition, preclinical literature suggests that quizartinib can also inhibit other class III receptor tyrosine kinases including KIT and PDGFRA in certain contexts. These findings are largely derived from in vitro models and their importance in patients remains to be fully established.

In non-clinical models, quizartinib has demonstrated high binding affinity to FLT3-WT receptors, potent inhibition of FLT3 autophosphorylation, and growth inhibition of FLT3-WT cells [16,31,32]. Ex vivo data further show reduced sensitivity in FLT3-WT compared with FLT3-ITD AML, with higher half maximal inhibitory concentration values and a greater concentration of quizartinib required to inhibit FLT3 phosphorylation [33]. Together, these findings support the use of a higher quizartinib dose to achieve adequate systemic exposure and effective FLT3 target inhibition in the FLT3-ITD–negative setting.

In a Phase II, single-arm clinical study (NCT00989261, EudraCT 2009-013093-41) in patients with relapsed or refractory AML, quizartinib monotherapy (90–200 mg daily dose) was associated with a morphologic remission rate of 33% in patients with FLT3-ITD–negative disease [13].

The randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase II QUIWI study (NCT04107727) is the most robust assessment to date of quizartinib in FLT3-ITD–negative AML. In QUIWI, 273 patients with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML were enrolled at 45 PETHEMA group clinical sites in Spain and randomized (2:1) to receive quizartinib (n = 180) or placebo (n = 93) in combination with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy, followed by up to 1 year of maintenance with quizartinib or placebo. The QUIWI trial started with an open-label safety run-in phase (n = 9), which evaluated quizartinib at 60 mg/day (or 30 mg/day when co-administered with a strong CYPA inhibitor) in combination with standard induction (cytarabine and idarubicin), followed by single-agent maintenance with quizartinib or placebo. The safety run-in phase established the safety of quizartinib 60 mg/day, with no dose-limiting toxicities reported, leading to its selection as the recommended Phase II dose for combination with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy and as single-agent maintenance therapy.

In QUIWI, the addition of quizartinib to standard induction and consolidation therapy, followed by single-agent maintenance, significantly improved event-free survival (EFS) vs placebo (median 24.4 vs 9.9 months, respectively; p = 0.045) and OS (median not reached vs 29.3 months; p = 0.01) in this FLT3-ITD–negative population. Quizartinib was associated with a 37% lower risk of death overall vs placebo and a higher 3-year OS rate (61% vs 46%, respectively) [34]. The QUIWI trial utilized an FLT3-ITD mutant-to-wild-type allelic ratio of <0.03 to define FLT3-ITD–negativity, but the enrolled patient population predominantly consisted of patients with undetectable FLT3-ITD at screening (97.4% [266/273 patients]), indicating that the clinical benefits of quizartinib in FLT3-ITD–negative AML cannot be attributed solely to low-level detectable FLT3-ITD mutations. Quizartinib showed particularly substantial survival benefits vs placebo in QUIWI patients with NPM1 and FLT3-TKD mutations, with 3-year OS rates of 90.0% vs 56.2% (HR 0.23) and 92.3% vs 33.3% (HR 0.09), respectively. A correlative gene expression analysis of 161 patients randomized in QUIWI identified a subset of 80 patients with an “FLT3-like” gene signature who derived greater benefit from quizartinib, suggesting that quizartinib activity may be preferentially associated with specific molecular phenotypes rather than uniformly distributed across all FLT3-ITD–negative disease [35]. While such signatures offer compelling evidence of biologically defined patient subsets who may derive particular benefit from an FLT3 inhibitor, there is currently no validated and standardized gene set that defines this “FLT3-like” signature, and routine monitoring of FLT3 expression levels and FLT3-linked signatures remains impractical in a clinical setting, which together limit the clinical utility of such observations. Taken together, the results of the QUIWI and QuANTUM-First trials provide supportive evidence that quizartinib exerts antileukemic activity in ND AML independent of FLT3-ITD mutational status.

Quizartinib showed a favorable safety profile in both trials. In the QUIWI study, low early mortality rates were observed at 30 days (2.8% with quizartinib vs 3.2% for placebo) as well as low rates of Grade ≥3 QT prolongation (3.4% vs 2.2%, respectively), which were manageable with dose modifications and appropriate monitoring [34]. These findings support the use of quizartinib 60 mg/day in combination with standard chemotherapy.

While the QUIWI trial provided encouraging evidence for quizartinib in ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML, important limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. Notably, the study enrolled patients exclusively from Spanish hospitals, resulting in a geographically restricted population that may not fully represent the heterogeneity of global patient demographics, disease characteristics, and clinical practice patterns. In addition, the relatively small sample size and paucity of low-level FLT3-ITD cases (97% of patients had undetectable FLT3-ITD at screening) further limit generalizability, underscoring the need for a prospectively designed larger international trial.

Given the positive results of the QUIWI trial, the international, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, Phase III QuANTUM-Wild trial was initiated, to confirm the efficacy and safety of quizartinib in combination with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy and as maintenance monotherapy in patients with FLT3-ITD–negative AML.

Methods

Study design

QuANTUM-Wild (EU clinical trials registration number: 2023-507936-20-00; ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT06578247) is a global, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, Phase III trial comparing the addition of quizartinib (60 mg daily dose) vs placebo to standard intensive induction and consolidation chemotherapy, followed by monotherapy quizartinib vs placebo maintenance for up to 36 cycles in patients with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML (Figure 2). Patients are randomized on Day 7 of the study in a 2:2:1 ratio into one of three treatment arms (Figure 2): patients in Arm A receive quizartinib in all phases of treatment in combination with chemotherapy during the induction and consolidation phases, and as monotherapy in the maintenance phase; patients in Arm B receive placebo in all phases of treatment; and those in Arm C receive quizartinib in combination with chemotherapy in the induction and consolidation phases, then receive placebo as monotherapy in the maintenance phase. Randomization is stratified by region (North America vs Europe vs Asia/other regions), age (<60 vs ≥60 years), and AML risk (adverse vs non-adverse [favorable/intermediate or unknown]). European LeukemiaNet (ELN) 2022 risk categorization will be assigned for all randomized patients based on central laboratory assessments using fluorescence in situ hybridization and next-generation sequencing for detection of NPM1, ASXL1, RUNX1, and TP53 and karyotype) [36]. QuANTUM-Wild trial design.

This ongoing trial was initiated in November 2024 and is being conducted at approximately 260 investigational sites in North America, Europe, Asia, and South America.

Key eligibility criteria

The trial population comprises adult patients of 18–70 years of age with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML, defined as an FLT3-ITD variant allele frequency (VAF) of <5% (<0.05 signal ratio), who are confirmed by the investigator to be eligible for intensive chemotherapy. To determine the FLT3-ITD mutation status for trial eligibility, bone marrow aspirates or peripheral blood samples are analyzed at screening by a central laboratory using polymerase chain reaction and capillary electrophoresis. To enter the consolidation phase, patients must achieve complete remission (CR) or CR with incomplete count recovery (CRi) during the induction phase. Eligible patients may receive allo-hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) during the consolidation phase at the discretion of the investigator and after at least 1 cycle of consolidation chemotherapy. To enter the maintenance phase, patients must have <5% blasts based on the most recent bone marrow aspirate, an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) >500/mm3, and a platelet count >50,000/mm3 at the end of the consolidation phase. Study objectives and endpoints

The primary objective of this study is to compare the efficacy of quizartinib versus placebo when administered with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy, and as single-agent maintenance therapy for up to 36 cycles, in patients with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML. The primary endpoint is OS, defined as the time from randomization to death due to any cause, in patients randomized to quizartinib in induction, consolidation, and maintenance (Arm A) vs those randomized to placebo in all study phases (Arm B).

Secondary endpoints include EFS (time from randomization until the date of earliest of any of the following: failure to achieve CR at the end of induction, relapse after CR, or death due to any cause); CR rate, the proportion of patients achieving CR by the end of induction; duration of CR (time from CR until relapse or death); relapse-free survival (time from randomization until relapse or death for patients who achieve CR); rate of CR with measurable residual disease (MRD)-negativity by the end of induction; OS for patients randomized to placebo in all phases (Arm B) vs those randomized to receive quizartinib during induction and consolidation followed by placebo during maintenance (Arm C); safety, including the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), serious TEAEs, adverse events (AEs) of special interest, changes in vital signs, electrocardiogram, safety laboratory evaluations, and physical examinations; and the pharmacokinetics of quizartinib and its primary metabolite, AC886, whichever occurs first.

Clinical responses are assessed locally based on bone marrow aspirates (or core biopsy specimens if the aspirate is not sufficient or not feasible to obtain) according to the revised recommendations of the International Working Group criteria [37]. Responses are centrally reviewed by an independent review committee based on local pathology reports of bone marrow samples, local hematology results, and any documented clinical evidence of extramedullary disease. During each cycle of the induction phase, response assessments are to be performed upon count recovery, but no later than Day 42 (it is not recommended to obtain a sample prior to Day 21), and prospectively throughout the consolidation and maintenance phases. Assessments of MRD negativity by NPM1 and CBF are performed for patients who have these mutations at screening; molecular-based FLT3-ITD MRD analysis (to assess potential low-level FLT3-ITD and FLT3 clonal evolution during treatment) and MRD analysis by flow cytometry are assessed in all patients. To complement these assessments, prospective exploratory investigations of molecular biomarkers predictive of quizartinib response will also be conducted, including further characterization of an FLT3-like gene expression signature, such as the one identified in QUIWI, using standardized methodologies across the QuANTUM-Wild cohort, and correlations between baseline mutational profile (including DNMT3, NPM1, ASXL1, RUNX1, and TP53 mutations) and quizartinib efficacy.

Patient-reported quality of life (QOL), global health status, physical functioning, and general cancer symptoms are assessed as an exploratory endpoint using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire C30 (EORTC QLQ-C30), version 3.0. QOL assessments include QLQ-C30 score changes from baseline and times to deterioration. Patient-reported symptoms during treatment, including symptomatic AEs and overall treatment burden, are also assessed as an exploratory endpoint using the Patient-Reported Outcome-Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events measurement system.

Interventions

The QuANTUM-Wild trial employs a unique three-arm design to assess the utility of quizartinib across all treatment phases (induction, consolidation, and maintenance) or during induction and consolidation only. Quizartinib and placebo are supplied as tablets with matching appearances.

In the induction phase, patients receive standard “7 + 3” induction chemotherapy followed by quizartinib or placebo 60 mg/day (equivalent to 53 mg/day quizartinib dihydrochloride) orally once daily (PO QD) for 14 consecutive days, starting on Day 8. Patients may receive up to 2 cycles of induction therapy; those with ≥5% blasts after Cycle 1 may receive a second cycle of induction therapy at the investigator’s discretion at least 35–60 days after Day 1 of the first induction cycle. During the second induction cycle, patients may receive either the standard “7 + 3” chemotherapy regimen, or a “5 + 2” regimen comprising 5 days of continuous intravenous infusion of cytarabine plus 2 days of an anthracycline; the same anthracycline must be used throughout the induction phase. Quizartinib or placebo is administered at 60 mg/day PO QD for 14 consecutive days after the end of the second cytarabine infusion.

Patients who achieve CR or CRi at the end of the induction phase will enter the consolidation phase, which will begin no earlier than 35 days and no later than 60 days after the first day of the last induction cycle. Patients in the consolidation phase receive high-dose cytarabine (HiDAC), with infusions on Days 1, 3, and 5 (“HiDAC-135”) or on Days 1, 2, and 3 (“HiDAC-123”) depending on institutional or local standard; patients receive cytarabine by intravenous infusion every 12 hours at a dose of either 3.0 g/m2 for patients under 60 years of age or 1.5 g/m2 for patients 60 years of age or greater. Quizartinib or placebo is administered at 60 mg/day PO QD for 14 consecutive days in each consolidation cycle, starting on Day 6 of the HiDAC-135 schedule or Day 4 of the HiDAC-123 schedule. Consolidation consists of 1–4 cycles of HiDAC; after at least 1 cycle, eligible patients may proceed to allo-HCT at investigator discretion and dependent on risk classification.

Patients with <5% bone marrow blasts, an ANC >500/mm3, and a platelet count >50,000/mm3 without platelet transfusion support within 24 hours will enter the maintenance phase within 60 days of Day 1 of the last consolidation cycle received, or within 180 days after allo-hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) (stable after transplant). Patients in the maintenance phase receive continuous quizartinib or placebo at 60 mg/day PO QD for up to 36 cycles (28-days per cycle). Maintenance continues until relapse, start of non-protocol-specified AML treatment, death, unacceptable toxicity, trial closure, or the completion of 36 cycles, whichever occurs first.

Quizartinib or placebo dose reductions or dose interruptions are permitted in the case of certain AEs (QTcF prolongation, other non-hematologic toxicities, or significant prolonged myelosuppression) or due to the concomitant administration of a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Patients receiving a concomitant strong CYP3A inhibitor will have their dose reduced to 30 mg/day (equivalent to 26.5 mg/day quizartinib dihydrochloride). For AEs requiring dose reductions, quizartinib or placebo is reduced to 40 mg/day, or 20 mg/day for those receiving a concomitant strong CYP3A inhibitor (with dose interruptions permitted if necessary).

After treatment discontinuation, patients will enter a long-term follow-up phase. In the long-term follow-up, patients complete a safety visit at 30 days, followed by follow-up visits every 4 weeks (for patients who have not had an EFS event) or every 12 weeks (for patients who have had an EFS event).

Data collection, management, and monitoring

An electronic case report form (eCRF) is completed for each patient who provides informed consent and undergoes any biosample screening or trial screening procedure. All data collected during the trial will be recorded on the patient’s eCRF. If a patient is not treated, the reason will be recorded on the eCRF. Upon completion of a patient’s eCRF, it will be reviewed and signed off by the investigator. Each patient is identified in the study database by a unique subject identification number (SID), and any patient records or datasets that are transferred to the sponsor will contain the SID only (patient names or any identifying information will not be transferred). The sponsor/contract research organization monitor and regulatory authority inspectors are responsible for contacting and visiting the investigator to inspect facilities and (upon request) various trial records (including eCRFs); verification of adherence to the protocol, good clinical practice guidelines, and local regulations, as well as the completeness, accuracy, and consistency of data, is accomplished through onsite visits by the monitor and the remote review of trial data.

Sample size and statistical methods

The target sample size in this study is approximately 700 patients, with randomization of ~280 patients to Arm A, ~280 patients to Arm B, and ~140 patients to Arm C. The trial is powered for the primary analysis of OS between Arms A and B. An estimated 560 patients and approximately 289 OS events are required in Arms A and B to have at least 90% power to detect a HR of 0.68 with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. If quizartinib has no maintenance effect, OS in Arm C will be similar to Arm A, ie, a HR of 0.68 is expected among Arm C vs Arm B. At the final analysis of Arm A and Arm B, approximately 220 OS events will be observed among the estimated 420 patients in Arm C and Arm B. This provides approximately 76% power to detect a HR of 0.68 (Arm C vs Arm B) at a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

Summary statistics will be presented by treatment arm. For continuous variables, the number of available observations (n), mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum will be provided. For categorical variables, the number and percentage in each category will be reported. Efficacy analyses are performed on the full analysis set, defined as all patients who were randomized. Safety analyses will be performed in the safety analysis set, defined as all patients who received at least one dose of quizartinib or placebo. An interim analysis is planned once approximately 231 OS events have been observed in Arm A and Arm B and a minimum of 15 months has elapsed since the last patient was randomized. The final OS analysis will be performed once 289 OS events have been observed in Arms A and B and a minimum of 24 months has elapsed since the last patient was randomized; if the target 289 OS events have not been achieved by this time, the final analysis will be performed at a maximum of 30 months after the last patient was randomized. Patients who are alive or lost to follow-up at the time of analysis are considered censored for OS at the date when they were last known to be alive. To control for the family-wise type I error rate for the primary and secondary efficacy endpoints, a hierarchical testing procedure will be applied, and testing will stop if one endpoint in the order is not statistically significant at the 0.05 level.

To assess the potential confounding effects of allo-HSCT on survival, prespecified sensitivity analyses will be performed, including censoring OS and EFS at the time of transplant. For patients achieving CR/composite CR, OS will be further evaluated using a multivariate Cox regression with allo-HSCT at first remission as a time-dependent covariate. Additionally, Simon-Makuch plots will be generated to account for the timing of allo-HSCT in survival probability estimations.

Ethics and dissemination

The trial is being conducted in accordance with the ethical principles originating in the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonization guideline for Good Clinical Practice, and all applicable regulatory requirements. The trial is being conducted in compliance with the protocol, approved by the institutional review board (IRB) or ethics committee of all participating sites. All patients provided written informed consent.

Conclusion

The Phase III QuANTUM-Wild trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of quizartinib for the treatment of patients with ND FLT3-ITD–negative AML, when administered with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy and followed by up to 36 cycles of maintenance monotherapy. This unique three-arm study, with OS as its primary endpoint, will analyze the unique contribution of quizartinib maintenance to prolong remission and delay relapse. Given the high relapse rates and poor outcomes historically observed in FLT3-ITD–negative AML, the extended 3-year maintenance schedule in QuANTUM-Wild represents a significant advancement over the 12-month approach used in QUIWI, enabling a more comprehensive assessment of durable benefit with prolonged FLT3-directed therapy [5]. The maintenance phase duration in QuANTUM-Wild replicates that of QuANTUM-First, which demonstrated the safety and tolerability of single-agent quizartinib 60 mg/day for up to 36 cycles [38]. Previous trials in AML have not been prospectively designed for a specific assessment of maintenance therapy after being given induction and consolidation (eg, through a three-arm design or re-randomization at maintenance). In QuANTUM-Wild, the three-arm design enables a prospective comparison of maintenance therapy benefit by contrasting Arm C (quizartinib during induction/consolidation followed by placebo maintenance) with Arm B (placebo throughout all phases). Due to the 2:2:1 randomization ratio, Arm C provides approximately 76% power to detect a HR of 0.68 (vs Arm B; compared to 90% for the primary Arm A vs Arm B analysis). Results will therefore have to be interpreted with caution regarding their ability to establish the contribution of maintenance to the overall treatment benefit.

The international, multicenter recruitment strategy of QuANTUM-Wild—enrolling at approximately 260 global sites across geographic regions and diverse patient populations—is essential for confirming and generalizing the robust findings from the QUIWI trial, which evaluated patients exclusively from 45 PETHEMA group clinical sites in Spain [34]. Given the evidence that FLT3 activation occurs through multiple mechanisms beyond FLT3-ITD gene mutations, including non-canonical FLT3 mutations that drive constitutive activation, QuANTUM-Wild has the potential to expand the role of FLT3 inhibition by quizartinib as a viable therapeutic approach for a broader AML patient population beyond those with only FLT3-ITD mutations [15,35]. Other targeted FLT3 inhibitors, including sorafenib and midostaurin, have demonstrated some clinical activity in FLT3-WT AML. However, neither agent has achieved a significant OS benefit in this patient population [7,39,40]. If the outcomes of QuANTUM-Wild are positive, quizartinib will become the first targeted FLT3 inhibitor shown to significantly improve OS in patients with FLT3-ITD–negative AML in a multinational Phase III trial. Combined with the proven efficacy from the QuANTUM-First trial in FLT3-ITD–positive AML, successful QuANTUM-Wild results would demonstrate that quizartinib provides a survival benefit across various FLT3 mutational statuses. This would support the use of quizartinib in combination with chemotherapy and as single-agent maintenance for patients with ND AML who are fit for intensive therapy, regardless of FLT3 mutational status.

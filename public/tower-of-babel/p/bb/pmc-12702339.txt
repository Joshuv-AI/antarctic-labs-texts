Multifaceted role of Iroquois signaling in development and diseases
Authors: Ravi Shankar Goutam, Neha Kaushik, Basant Kumar, Woochan Jung, Santosh Kumar, Seung-Hwan Lee, Unjoo Lee, Jaebong Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Iroquois (Iro/Irx) gene family encodes transcription factors that belong to the Three-Amino-Acid Loop Extension-class homeodomain group, distinguished by a conserved Iro-box domain. Iroquois signaling is evolutionarily conserved from invertebrates to vertebrates and plays a vital role in embryonic development. In invertebrates, Iro/Irx genes control tissue compartmentalization, whereas in vertebrates, they regulate gastrulation, neural patterning, and organogenesis. These genes are typically organized in conserved genomic clusters under shared regulatory control, reflecting their coordinated expression and common evolutionary origins. Beyond development, dysregulation of Iroquois genes has been implicated in diverse human diseases. Iro/Irx genes are increasingly associated with congenital disorders, including congenital heart disease and neurodevelopmental abnormalities. Moreover, their emerging role in cancer biology has revealed context-dependent behavior, functioning as either tumor suppressors or oncogenes. Recent findings have also highlighted their potential as clinical biomarkers in neurological and neoplastic diseases. Given their broad developmental and pathological roles, Iroquois genes are gaining recognition as promising candidates for therapeutic targeting and molecular diagnostics. This review integrates their developmental functions with their disease associations to provide a comprehensive overview of the biological and clinical significance of Iroquois signaling.Keywords: Three-amino-acid loop extension homeodomain, Iroquois genes, Embryonic development, Cancer, Congenital heart disease

INTRODUCTION

The Iroquois (Iro/Irx) gene family encodes transcription factors belonging to the Three-Amino-Acid Loop Extension (TALE) class of homeodomain proteins, characterized by the following two conserved motifs: the Iro-box and IroA domains (Mukherjee and Burglin, 2007). The genes were first discovered in Drosophila melanogaster, where mutations in the iro1 locus caused the loss of specific bristles, producing a phenotype reminiscent of the traditional hairstyle of the Native American Iroquois tribe (Dambly-Chaudiere and Leyns, 1992; Leyns et al., 1996). In Drosophila, these genes, organized as the Iroquois complex (Iro-C), play essential roles in imaginal disc development, body segmentation, dorsal-ventral patterning during the larval stages, and metamorphosis (Cavodeassi et al., 2001; Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002).

Functional characterization of Iro genes in Drosophila led to the identification of homologous Irx genes across various vertebrate species, including chickens, mice, zebrafish, Xenopus, and human (Cavodeassi et al., 2001; McDonald et al., 2010). In vertebrates, Irx genes are typically organized into the following two paralogous clusters: Group A (Irx1, Irx2, and Irx4) and Group B (Irx3, Irx5, and Irx6), which display conserved synteny and overlapping expression domains (Jung et al., 2019). These genes participate in diverse developmental processes, including neurogenesis, cardiac patterning, kidney development, limb morphogenesis, and gonadal specification (Houweling et al., 2001; Jorgensen and Gao, 2005; Lebel et al., 2003).

In Xenopus, Irx genes (Xiro) contribute to the establishment of the Spemann organizer, specification of the neuroectoderm, formation of the midbrain-hindbrain boundary, and patterning of neural crest derivatives (Glavic et al., 2004). In humans, Irx genes are expressed during embryogenesis, where they regulate embryonic patterning by controlling the differentiation and proliferation of multiple cell types (Abaroa, 2024; Chaabouni et al., 2010). Comparative genomic studies have identified Irx homologs even in early-diverging metazoans such as sponges, highlighting their deeply conserved roles in morphogenesis (Irimia et al., 2008). In invertebrates and lower vertebrates (eg, Caenorhabditis elegans and zebrafish), the clustered organization of Irx genes raises questions about whether their arrangements resulted from lineage-specific duplications or convergent evolution (Kerner et al., 2009).

Mutations or dysregulation of the human Irx gene have been linked to diverse phenotypic and pathological outcomes, including congenital heart defects, craniofacial malformations, and several cancers (Bonnard et al., 2012; Catela et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2012; Holmquist Mengelbier et al., 2019; Somerville et al., 2018). Moreover, Irx family members are implicated in human obesity through fat mass and obesity-associated (FTO) risk determinants (Dou et al., 2021), and their knockdown in human keratinocytes reduces the expression of key cell-cycle regulators (Abaroa, 2024), underscoring their dual developmental and homeostatic functions.

Beyond these developmental and metabolic roles, several Irx genes exhibit context-dependent functions in cancers. Irx1, Irx2, and Irx3 act as tumor suppressors in gastric, lung, and breast cancers (Asaka et al., 2006; Guo et al., 2010; Lewis et al., 1999; Liu et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2018), whereas Irx5 functions as an oncogene by promoting cell-cycle progression through repression of p21 and p53 (Myrthue et al., 2008). Members of the Irx family are also essential for cardiac morphogenesis, where they regulate chamber formation, septation, and ventricular conduction system development (Guo et al., 2017; Hu et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2012; Zeng et al., 2019). Furthermore, Irx dysregulation has been implicated in neurological disorders, including gliomas, motor neuron abnormalities, hypothalamic dysfunction, and Parkinson’s disease (PD) (Catela et al., 2025; de Araujo et al., 2019; Sim et al., 2020).

Overall, these findings establish the Iroquois gene family as a pivotal regulator of developmental and disease-related pathways. This review summarizes the conserved functions of Iro/Irx genes in embryogenesis and their emerging roles in neurological disorders, congenital heart disease (CHD), and cancer.

GENOMIC ORGANIZATION AND EVOLUTIONARY CONSERVATION OF THE IROQUOIS GENE FAMILY

In vertebrates, the Iroquois (Irx) gene family typically comprises six members organized into two paralogous clusters as follows: IRXA (Irx1, Irx2, and Irx4) and IRXB (Irx3, Irx5, and Irx6) (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002). This clustered organization is highly conserved across mammals, amphibians, birds, and fish. In Danio rerio (zebrafish), the family has expanded to 11 genes arranged into four clusters due to a teleost-specific whole-genome duplication, with one gene (irx7) located outside the clusters (Dildrop and Ruther, 2004). Comparative genomic analysis (Table 1) has shown marked variation in Irx gene length among vertebrates, ranging from approximately 1.3 kb (irx7 in zebrafish) to over 16 kb (IRX2 in humans). Despite these size differences, coding sequences remain relatively conserved (1.3-1.5 kb), encoding proteins of similar length and domain composition.

Among non-vertebrates, the nematode C elegans possesses a single Irx homolog, whereas D melanogaster contains three related genes,—araucan (ara), caupolican (caup), and mirror (mirr)—arranged in a single genomic cluster known as the Iro-C (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002). The expansion into two distinct Irx clusters in vertebrates likely resulted from an ancient chromosomal duplication event, supported by conserved flanking genes and paralogous relationship between Irx1/Irx3, Irx2/Irx5, and Irx4/Irx6 (Bosse et al., 2000; Ogura et al., 2001; Peters et al., 2000).

In mammals, including humans and mice, IRXA and IRXB clusters are located on separate chromosomes but retain nearly identical gene order and transcriptional orientation (Houweling et al., 2001). Intergenic distances within the IRXA typically range from approximately 500 to 900 kb, whereas those in IRXB are shorter. In zebrafish, the intergenic spacing is more compact (125-332 kb), however, the cluster structure is preserved (Fig. 1). This conservation across vertebrates suggests strong evolutionary constraints imposed by shared regulatory mechanisms controlling coordinated Irx gene expression during development. Genes within each cluster frequently show overlapping spatiotemporal expression domains—including, co-expression in developing neural and cardiac tissues in mice, indicating that cis-regulatory elements were likely duplicated alongside the genes themselves (Houweling et al., 2001).

In contrast, Drosophila Iro-C genes occupy a compact 12- to 16-kb region, with intergenic distances of 13 to 71 kb (Fig. 1). This tight linkage reflects both the smaller genome size and the requirement for rapid, coordinated regulation during early development. Although invertebrates possess fewer Irx genes, Drosophila Iro-C proteins are comparatively longer (Table 1) and often include additional domains, likely providing functional diversification in the absence of gene expansion (Kerner et al., 2009). Ara, Caup, and Mirr participate in multiple developmental processes— including neurogenesis, compartment boundary formation, and organogenesis, often with redundant or partially overlapping functions (Kerner et al., 2009).

At the protein level, all Irx members belong to the TALE superclass of homeodomain transcription factors. They share a highly conserved 60—amino acid homeodomain responsible for DNA binding and a characteristic 13—amino acid IRO motif near the C-terminus, whose precise function remains unclear (Dos Santos et al., 2023). Vertebrate Irx proteins are more closely related to one another than to invertebrate Iro-C proteins, consistent with lineage-specific expansion and diversification following divergence from a common ancestral gene (Burglin, 1997; Bosse et al., 2000; Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 1996; Ogura et al., 2001). Phylogenetic analysis places Ara and Caup as more closely related to each other than to Mirr, indicating subfunctionalization after duplication (Cavodeassi et al., 2001). Drosophila Iro-C proteins form a basal outgroup in evolutionary trees, suggesting that vertebrate Irx diversification occurred independently within the vertebrate lineage following its divergence from invertebrates (Fig. 2).

In summary, the evolution of the Iroquois gene family reflects a series of duplication and divergence events that produced conserved genomic clusters in vertebrates. These clusters maintain spatial organization and coordinated gene expression, facilitating the precise regulation of developmental processes, including neural patterning, heart morphogenesis, and organ boundary formation. The contrast with the compact, multifunctional Iro-C organization in Drosophila underscores how gene family architecture adapts to organismal complexity and regulatory demands.

IROQUOIS SIGNALING IN INVERTEBRATE EMBRYOGENESIS: INSIGHTS FROM DROSOPHILA MELANOGASTER

Iroquois (Iro) proteins, members of the well-conserved TALE-class homeodomain transcription factor family, play fundamental roles in embryonic development across metazoans (Cavodeassi et al., 2001; Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002). These transcriptional regulators control genes involved in body plan organization, tissue compartmentalization, and cell fate determination (Catela et al., 2025; Cavodeassi et al., 2001; Marra et al., 2019). In D melanogaster, Iro-C comprising Ara, Caup, and Mirr—is a key determinant of regional specification and boundary formation during embryogenesis and organ development (Villa-Cuesta et al., 2007).

During the second and third larval instar stages, Iro-C genes are expressed in the dorsal regions of the imaginal discs, including the eye-antennal, wing, and notum discs, which are critical sites of epithelial patterning (Letizia et al., 2007). Their expression is precisely regulated by multiple upstream signaling pathways, including Notch, epidermal growth factor receptor, and Decapentaplegic [Dpp, the Drosophila homolog of bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)], which confine Iro-C activity to the dorsal compartments (Chen and Desplan, 2020; Letizia et al., 2007; Sotillos and De Celis, 2005). In the eye-antennal disc, Hedgehog (Hh) and Wingless (Wg) signaling also promote Iro-C activation within the dorsal domain (Cavodeassi et al., 1999). This spatially restricted expression establishes dorsoventral compartment boundaries and organizing centers, where Iro-C-expressing and nonexpressing cells interact. —These interactions are crucial for defining the midline and initiating patterning of the compound eye and associated dorsal structures. Functionally, Iro-C genes act as dorsal selector genes, conferring dorsal identity and maintaining compartment boundaries (Cavodeassi et al., 2000; Villa-Cuesta et al., 2007). In Iro-C mutants, dorsal structures undergo homeotic transformation into ventral fates, producing ectopic ventral features, including the ptilinum, prefrons, and suborbital bristles (Cavodeassi et al., 2000). Within the developing wing imaginal disc, ara and caup are co-expressed in two symmetrical patches near the anterior-posterior boundary, flanking the dorsal-ventral interface (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 1996). These genes translate positional cues into the precise arrangement of wing veins and sensory bristles (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 1996; Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 1996). In contrast, Mirr contributes uniquely to wing morphogenesis; mirr mutants lack the alula (a small posterior lobe of the wing) and its associated sensory bristles, indicating its specific role in structural patterning (Kehl et al., 1998). In the dorsal mesoderm, Iro-C genes are indispensable for heart development. Loss of the complex or individual genes, including ara/caup or mirr, leads to severe cardiac defects (Mirzoyan and Pandur, 2013). The GATA transcription factor Pannier acts cooperatively with Iro-C during cardiogenesis, forming a shared regulatory module critical for dorsal mesoderm specification (Mirzoyan and Pandur, 2013).

In the Drosophila eye, Iro-C genes are required for the specification of photoreceptor subtypes. Specifically, in R7 photoreceptor cells, Iro-C activity is necessary for the co-expression of ultraviolet-sensitive rhodopsin Rh3 and Rh4 (Mazzoni et al., 2008). Loss of Iro-C disrupts this co-expression, whereas ectopic activation induces inappropriate Rh3/Rh4 expression outside the normal boundaries (Mazzoni et al., 2008). Additionally, ara and caup regulate the patterning of mechanosensory neurons in the notum, where their spatially restricted expression ensures proper axonal targeting and organization of peripheral sensory circuits (Grillenzoni et al., 1998), highlighting their role in neurodevelopmental patterning.

Mechanistically, Iro-C proteins primarily act as transcriptional activators, although context-dependent repression has also been reported (Villa-Cuesta and Modolell, 2005). Many roles of Iro-C genes, including boundary formation, regional identity establishment, and sensory organ development—are evolutionarily conserved in vertebrate Iroquois homologs, despite gene family expansion and diversification. As summarized in Figure 3, Drosophila Iro-C genes serve as a powerful model for deciphering transcriptional programs that govern axial identity, organogenesis, and compartmentalization, providing valuable insights into molecular mechanisms underlying congenital anomalies and regional patterning disorders in vertebrates.

IROQUOIS SIGNALING IN VERTEBRATE EMBRYOGENESIS

Vertebrate homologs of the Drosophila Iroquois (Iro) genes, collectively known as Irx genes, play fundamental roles in embryonic development. Initially identified for their role in establishing compartment boundaries in Drosophila, vertebrate Irx genes have since been shown to regulate multiple developmental processes, including germ layer specification, neural induction, axial patterning, and organogenesis. Functional studies across major model organisms, including Xenopus, zebrafish, chicks, and mice, have revealed that their expression is tightly regulated by upstream signaling pathways such as Wnt, fibroblast growth factor (FGF), BMP, and retinoic acid (Diaz-Hernandez et al., 2013; Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 2001; Matsumoto et al., 2004). This regulatory integration positions Irx genes as essential components of core transcriptional networks governing embryonic patterning and organ formation.

Recent single-cell transcriptomic analyses of human embryos have further confirmed Irx gene expression in specific embryonic cell populations, including neural progenitors, limb mesenchyme, and cardiac precursors (Xu et al., 2023). These findings underscore that the developmental functions of Irx genes, extensively characterized in model organisms, are deeply conserved and functionally relevant in human embryogenesis.

The following section details the diverse functions of Irx genes during vertebrate development, with particular emphasis on their roles in neurogenesis and cardiogenesis, processes where Iroquois signaling serves as a key regulatory determinant of spatial identity, cell differentiation, and tissue morphogenesis.

Iroquois Signaling in Vertebrate Neurogenesis and Neurological Disorders

The Iroquois (Irx) gene family plays a fundamental role in early neuroectodermal specification and regional patterning during vertebrate neurogenesis. Irx genes compartmentalize the developing neural plate by establishing dorsoventral and anterior-posterior boundaries through cross-repressive interactions with other transcription factors (Glavic et al., 2002; Itoh et al., 2002; Lecaudey et al., 2004). These boundaries are reinforced by upstream signaling cues—Irx genes are activated and repressed by Wnt and BMP signaling, respectively (Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 2001; Janssens et al., 2010).

In Xenopus, the Xiro gene family (Xiro1, Xiro2, and Xiro3) regulates neurulation and the expression of proneural genes (Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 2001). Neural induction depends on BMP4 inhibition mediated by organizer-derived signals, which restricts Bmp4 expression in the prospective neuroectoderm and dorsal mesoderm (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002). Within the neuroectoderm, Xiro1 is activated by Wnt signaling and represses Bmp4, thereby promoting neural fate (Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 2001). Additionally, it also suppresses Bmp4 in the mesoderm, supporting dorsal mesodermal development (Glavic et al., 2001). Xiro1 and Xiro2, co-expressed in the neural plate, are regulated by Noggin and retinoic acid (Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 1998). Dominant-negative forms of these genes impair neural plate formation and organization. Acting downstream of BMP inhibition, Xiro1 and Xiro2 restrict anterior neural expansion and promote posterior neural identity (Gomez-Skarmeta et al., 1998). Xiro3, activated by Noggin and FGF signaling, marks neural precursors and promotes early neurogenesis but inhibits neuronal differentiation by repressing N-tubulin, indicating a dual role distinct from other proneural regulators (Bellefroid et al., 1998).

In zebrafish, irx1a promotes the midbrain serotonergic neuron differentiation; its knockdown reduces serotonin and tryptophan hydroxylase–related markers (Cheng et al., 2007). Irx1a also contributes to retinal neurogenic wave propagation, possibly through Sonic Hedgehog (Shh) pathway interaction (Cheng et al., 2006). Region-specific irx3 expression supports its role in central nervous system patterning (Tan et al., 1999). Irx3 is critical for spinal cord neurogenesis, expressed in intermediate progenitor domains and postmitotic neurons, including motor neurons and VeLD interneurons (Lewis et al., 2005). Its expression is Hh-independent but repressed by Hh and Olig2 within the ventral progenitor domain. Functionally, irx3 inhibits motoneuron differentiation while promoting interneuron identity, establishing sharp domain boundaries (Lewis et al., 2005). Retinoic acid regulates irx1 and irx7 near the midbrain-hindbrain boundary, a region crucial for anterior-posterior compartmentalization (Itoh et al., 2002). Loss of irx7 disrupts boundary positioning and enables hindbrain markers to expand anteriorly. Irx7 also mediates hindbrain segmentation through mutual repression with vHnf1 at the rhombomere 4/5 boundary, and is essential for Mauthner neuron formation in r4, required for escape reflexes (Lecaudey et al., 2004). In the retina, irx7 is expressed in the inner nuclear layer under Smarca4 regulation and is critical for retinal lamination and neuronal positioning (Zhang et al., 2013, Zhang et al., 2012). Irx7 knockdown disrupts amacrine and Muller glial cell formation and impairs Notch signaling, while both its overexpression and loss cause retinal architecture defects, reflecting the need for tight expression control during retinogenesis (Zhang et al., 2012).

In chicks, Irx2 acts downstream of FGF8/MAPK signaling at the isthmic organizer and is essential for cerebellar development. Co-expression of Irx2 with FGF8a transforms midbrain tissue into cerebellar-like structure by repressing Otx2 and inducing Gbx2 (Matsumoto et al., 2004) thereby redirecting regional identity.

In mice, Irx3 is co-expressed with Pax6 in the ventral neural tube and establishes progenitor domain by repressing Olig2, Six3, and Hnf1 in the spinal cord, forebrain, and midbrain, respectively (Briscoe et al., 2000; Kobayashi et al., 2002; Mizuguchi et al., 2001; Parker et al., 2016; Sagner and Briscoe, 2019). Its expression is confined to intermediate neural progenitor domains and is ventrally repressed by Shh-induced Olig2, refining the boundary between motor neurons and interneurons (Robertshaw et al., 2013). Functionally, Irx3 promotes V2 interneuron fate by inhibiting motor neuron specification, ensuring a sharp demarcation between progenitor motor neuron and progenitor 2 domains (Briscoe et al., 2000; Chen et al., 2011). In the retina, Irx4 regulates axon guidance, whereas Irx5 and Irx6 are required for interneuron differentiation (Cheng et al., 2005; Jin et al., 2003; Star et al., 2012).

Postnatally, Irx3 and Irx5 are expressed in radial glia-like neural stem cells of the hypothalamus, where they regulate adult neurogenesis and energy homeostasis (Dou et al., 2021). CRISPR-based studies have identified Irx2 and Irx6 as regulators of motor neuron development (Catela et al., 2025). In the embryonic spinal cord, Irx1, Irx2, Irx3, Irx5, and Irx6 are selectively expressed in distinct motor neuron subtypes (Catela et al., 2025). Irx2 promotes lateral motor column fate, while Irx6 restricts it, demonstrating antagonistic roles in motor neuron patterning (Catela et al., 2025). Irx gene expression is directly regulated by Hox proteins, forming a conserved Hox-Irx regulatory module essential for motor neuron specification (Catela et al., 2025).

Human IRX genes are increasingly implicated in neurological disorders. In the postnatal hypothalamus, IRX3 and IRX5 act downstream of obesity-associated noncoding variants within the FTO locus, linking impaired neurogenesis to neuroendocrine dysfunction and energy imbalance (Dou et al., 2021). Transcriptomic profiling of LRRK2 G2019S PD models revealed consistent upregulation of IRX2 in intestinal organoids, dopaminergic neurons, and patient-derived fibroblasts, suggesting its potential as an early biomarker for PD (Sim et al., 2020). In humans gliomas, IRX1 is markedly overexpressed in high-grade isocitrate dehydrogenase-mutant astrocytomas, with nuclear relocalization during tumor progression correlating with disrupted neural patterning, highlighting its potential as a prognostic value (Sugur et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2018).

Collectively, these studies demonstrate the conserved and context-specific roles of Irx genes in neurogenesis, neuronal subtype specification, and neural stem cell regulation. Their dysregulation contributes to neurological diseases, including gliomas, motor neuron pathologies, and hypothalamic dysfunction linked to obesity. By bridging developmental and pathological contexts, IRX genes emerge as a central regulator of neural patterning and neuropathology. Future investigations into their direct transcriptional targets and interaction with other developmental regulators will be crucial for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying region-specific neurogenic programs across vertebrates. Iroquois Signaling in Vertebrate Cardiogenesis and CHD

Cardiac morphogenesis is governed by a conserved gene regulatory network that coordinates transcription factor activity with precise spatial and temporal precision (Olson, 2006). Within this network, the Iroquois homeobox (Irx) genes (Irx1-Irx6) exhibit distinct expression domains during heart development, indicating specialized functions in regional patterning and cellular specification (Christoffels et al., 2000). In humans, recent spatial transcriptomic studies have confirmed that IRX1, IRX2, IRX3, and IRX4 are enriched in ventricular cardiomyocyte subpopulations during embryogenesis, underscoring their conserved roles in human cardiac patterning (Farah et al., 2024). The disruption of these transcriptional programs, frequently through mutations, has been associated with CHD, particularly septal defects and outflow tract anomalies, as shown by human genetic analyses (Homsy et al., 2015; Zaidi et al., 2013). Among the Irx genes, Irx6 displays weak and transient expression during embryogenesis and is largely absent from cardiac tissues in mice and other examined vertebrates (Mummenhoff et al., 2001). This section focuses on Irx1 to Irx5 and their roles in heart development and pathogenesis of CHD.

In zebrafish, irx1b is expressed in ventricular trabecular myocytes during the onset of ventricular electrical maturation (Joseph, 2004). Morpholino-mediated knockdown of irx1b results in a reduced embryonic heart rate, indicating that irx1b is essential for the functional maturation of cardiac conduction and contraction (Joseph, 2004).

In mice, Irx1 and Irx2 are expressed in trabecular cardiomyocytes from embryonic day (E) 10.5, localizing to the interventricular septum by E11.5 and later to the atrioventricular bundle branches during ventricular conduction system (E14.5-E17.5) (Christoffels et al., 2000). Although Irx2-null mice are viable and phenotypically normal (Lebel et al., 2003), combined deletion of Irx1 and Irx2 causes membranous ventricular septal defects, outflow tract misalignment, and perinatal lethality, demonstrating their redundant yet essential function in cardiac morphogenesis (Sepideh Sheybani-Deloui et al., 2022). In humans, rare missense variants in IRX1 have been identified in patients with CHDs, including tricuspid atresia and transposition of the great arteries (Guo et al., 2017), underscoring its clinical importance. Integrated single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses of human embryos revealed IRX1 expression in a subset of mesodermal progenitors that form the anterior second heart field, confirming its role in early ventricular septation (He et al., 2025).

Evidence from human genetics and model organisms consistently implicates Irx1 CHD pathogenesis. In zebrafish and mouse models, Irx1 is required for ventricular identity and septation, with loss of function leading to abnormal chamber morphogenesis (Guo et al., 2017, Joseph, 2004). Guo et al. (2017) identified three rare missense mutations (p.Gln240Glu, p.Ser298Asn, and p.Ala381Glu) in patients with nonsyndromic CHDs, absent in healthy controls, confirming their contribution to the genetic basis of septal and outflow tract defects.

High-resolution spatial transcriptomic mapping of the human embryonic heart using single-cell RNA-seq and multiplexes error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization revealed IRX1, IRX2, and IRX3 enrichment in ventricular cardiomyocyte subpopulations, arranged in distinct laminar layers (Farah et al., 2024). These IRX-expressing cells participate in signaling niches involving the plexin-semaphorin pathway, guiding ventricular wall morphogenesis (Farah et al., 2024). Functional validation confirmed their role in chamber organization, emphasizing the contribution of IRX genes to spatial identity and structural development of the human heart. In the human fetal heart, IRX3 marks both the trabecular and Purkinje cells, whereas IRX5 is enriched in the endocardium (Farah et al., 2024). In mice Irx3 expression begins at E9.5 in the trabecular myocardium and later localizes to the His-Purkinje network (Christoffels et al., 2000, Costantini et al., 2005, Zhang et al., 2011). Consistent with these findings, Irx3-null mice exhibit right bundle branch block and prolonged ventricular activation (Kim et al., 2016). In humans, heterozygous IRX3 variants similarly impair Purkinje conduction, and increase susceptibility to idiopathic ventricular fibrillation (Koizumi et al., 2016).

Irx4 is a ventricle-specific homeobox gene that defines and maintains ventricular identity. In chick embryos, ectopic Irx4 expression disrupts chamber specification (Bao et al., 1999). In Xenopus, Xiro4 is expressed in the developing heart from stage 36, initially restricted to a subdomain of the ventricular myocardium and later expanding throughout the ventricle by stage 50, suggesting a conserved role in ventricular development (Garriock et al., 2001). In mice, Irx4 expression begins at E7.5 in the anterior cardiac primordium, and becomes confined to the ventricular myocardium and atrioventricular canal (Bruneau et al., 2000). The transcription factor Nkx2-5 initiates Irx4 expression, while dHand maintains it (Bruneau et al., 2000). Single-cell atlases of human embryonic hearts confirm IRX4 expression exclusively in ventricular cardiomyocytes (Farah et al., 2024). Loss of Irx4 in mice causes concentric left ventricular hypertrophy, systolic dysfunction, and ectopic activation of atrial stress genes (Bruneau et al., 2001). Lineage tracing studies further shows that Irx4-positive progenitors contribute specifically to the ventricular, but not atrial, myocardium (Nelson et al., 2016). In humans, heterozygous IRX4 variants are associated with membranous septal defects and left ventricular hypoplasia, implicating IRX4 in atrioventricular boundary regulation (Cheng et al., 2011).

In contrast, Irx5 shows no detectable cardiac expression in Xenopus (Garriock et al., 2001), but is expressed in mice from E8.5 onward, establishing an endocardial-to-epicardial gradient. Irx5-null mice lose the ventricular transmural repolarization gradient, leading to ventricular tachycardia (Costantini et al., 2005). Combined Irx3 and Irx5 deletion causes embryonic lethality before E14.5, with outflow tract misalignment and septal defects, demonstrating their cooperative function in conduction system development and suppression of excessive trabecular growth (Chen et al., 2004; Gaborit et al., 2012). Biallelic IRX5 variants in humans cause Hamamy-like syndrome, characterized by prolonged QRS intervals and mild structural cardiac anomalies (Al Sayed et al., 2021).

Collectively, Irx genes, particularly Irx1 to Irx5, play conserved and essential roles in heart development by regulating regional identity, chamber specification, and maturation of the conduction system (Fig. 5). Dysregulation or mutation of these genes contributes to CHDs and inherited arrhythmias. Elucidating their downstream targets and regulatory interactions will be critical to understand the mechanisms that governs both normal and pathological cardiogenesis.

IROQUOIS SIGNALING IN TUMORIGENESIS

Beyond their canonical roles in embryonic development, Iroquois (Irx) proteins have emerged as important regulators of cell proliferation and tumorigenesis. Depending on the cellular context, Irx genes can act as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors. Early studies have demonstrated that reduced Irx function genes accelerates the G1/S transition, promoting cell-cycle progression, whereas their overexpression induces G1 arrest by downregulating the cyclin E/Cdk2 complex (Barrios and Campuzano, 2015; Barrios et al., 2015).

Mechanistically, Irx proteins modulate the cell cycle not only through transcriptional regulation but also via direct physical interaction with cyclin E-containing complexes, a mechanism independent of their DNA-binding activity, indicating a non-transcriptional mode of action (Barrios and Campuzano, 2015). In Drosophila, the Irx-mediated control of the G1/S checkpoint restricts tissue growth in a cell-autonomous manner, regulating compartment size in imaginal discs such as the eye and wing (Barrios et al., 2015).

In vertebrates, deregulation of Irx gene function has been linked to oncogenic transformation and tumor progression. The context-dependent function of Irx signaling influences multiple aspects of cancer biology, including tumor initiation, proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. The following section examines these diverse roles of Irx signaling in human cancer, emphasizing how developmental regulatory mechanisms are co-opted during tumorigenesis.

Lung Adenocarcinoma

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) as the most prevalent subtype (Bray et al., 2018). A comprehensive screening of 238 homeobox genes revealed significant downregulation of IRX1 and IRX2 in LUAD tissues compared with normal lung (Huang et al., 2022). Transcriptomic analysis further confirmed that IRX1, IRX2, IRX3, and IRX6 are downregulated, whereas IRX4 is upregulated in LUAD (Zhang et al., 2024). Bioinformatics studies have indicated that IRX genes influence the tumor microenvironment by modulating immune infiltration and key oncogenic pathways, including Ras and metabolic signaling (Zhang et al., 2024). Among these, IRX2 acts as a tumor suppressor: its downregulation—linked to promoter hypermethylation—is associated with poor survival and invasive phenotypes. Elevated IRX2 expression characterizes non-invasive LUAD subtypes, suggesting its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and an epigenetic therapeutic target (Huang et al., 2022).

Gastric Cancer

Gastric cancer (GC) is a major global cause of cancer mortality. Evidence from animal models and clinical samples supports a tumor-suppressive role for IRX genes, particularly IRX1, in GC (Dos Santos et al., 2023). IRX1 is frequently silenced via promoter methylation and miRNA-mediated repression. Protein arginine methyltransferase 5, overexpressed in approximately 72% of GCs, epigenetically silences IRX1 by recruiting DNA-methyltransferase 3 alpha to its promoter (Liu et al., 2018). Restoring IRX1 expression suppresses tumor growth and invasion, underscoring its therapeutic potential (Dos Santos et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2018). Additionally, IRX genes are regulated by Helicobacter pylori infection and modulate several oncogenic pathways, including Wnt/β-catenin, MAPK, TGF-β, PI3K/Akt, and NF-κB signaling (Dos Santos et al., 2023). Collectively, current evidence establishes IRX1 as a central epigenetically regulated tumor suppressor in gastric cancer, whose silencing facilitates oncogenic pathway activation and promotes malignant progression.

Malignant Glioma

Malignant glioma is an aggressive neoplasm of the central nervous system and is associated with a poor prognosis. Epidemiological data indicate that the 5-year survival rate for patients with glioma is less than 30%, with the median survival for those with World health organization grade IV gliomas frequently limited to approximately 6 months (Nakada et al., 2007). IRX1 expression is markedly elevated in glioma cell lines, including U373, LN299, and T98G, but absent in normal brain tissue and U87 cells, suggesting tumor-specific induction (Zhang et al., 2018). One prevalent mechanism in primary human glioblastomas is hypomethylation (Zhang et al., 2018), which may contribute to IRX1 upregulation. Given that IRX1 hypomethylation has also been linked to metastatic osteosarcoma (Lu et al., 2015), further research is needed to determine whether similar epigenetic deregulation drives its overexpression in glioma. In glioma, IRX1 translocates from the cytoplasm in low-grade tumors to the nucleus in high-grade malignancies (Zhang et al., 2018). This nuclear relocalization together with its elevated expression in high-grade gliomas correlates with poor patient survival, supporting a potential role for IRX1 as a prognostic biomarker.

Prostate Cancer

Prostate cancer (PCa) is one of the most common malignancies in men globally. Genome-wide association studies have identified IRX4 as a susceptibility gene for PCa (Fernando et al., 2024). An alternatively spliced micropeptide IRX4_PEP1, promotes PCa cell proliferation, migration, and stemness by interacting with heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein K and β-catenin, thereby activating Wnt signaling and enhancing docetaxel resistance (Fernando et al., 2024). IRX4_PEP1 is upregulated in aggressive tumors, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for PCa. Moreover, an upstream insertion-deletion variants disrupt androgen receptor binding at the IRX4 locus, altering its expression (Janaththani et al., 2023). Elevated IRX4 is associated with androgen receptor-driven gene signatures and metastasis through interaction with the FOXA1 cofactor (Janaththani et al., 2023). This insertion-deletion correlates with survival in patients receiving androgen deprivation therapy, emphasizing its mechanistic and clinical relevance.

Breast Cancer

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous and highly metastatic disease that poses a significant health burden on women. IRX5 functions as a metastasis suppressor by negatively regulating breast cancer metastasis. Mechanistically, IRX5 transcriptionally upregulates YWHAB (14-3-3β) expression, which inhibits epithelial-mesenchymal transition markers such as vimentin, thereby reducing migration and invasion (Geng et al., 2024). Although most evidence is currently derived from in vitro studies, the IRX5-YWHAB regulatory axis may serve as a potential therapeutic target for limiting metastatic breast cancer. Additionally, IRX2 is frequently downregulated in aggressive breast cancer subtypes and inversely correlated with tumor grade and lymph node involvement (Werner et al., 2015). In vitro overexpression of IRX2 reduces secretion of prometastatic chemokines and impairs cellular motility, suggesting that loss of IRX2 may facilitate early dissemination through chemokine-mediated mobilization.

Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is an aggressive malignancy characterized by late detection and resistance to standard therapies (Wood et al., 2022). IRX4 functions as a tumor suppressor in PDAC (Chakma et al., 2020). Its expression is frequently silenced through promoter hypermethylation occurs in approximately 64% of cases (Chakma et al., 2020). Restoration of IRX4 expression inhibits proliferation and colony formation in PDAC cell lines, whereas its knockdown accelerates proliferation in normal pancreatic epithelial cells. Several downstream targets, including CRYAB, CD69, and IL32 are transcriptionally regulated by IRX4 and mediate its growth-inhibitory effects. These findings establish IRX4 as a key tumor suppressor whose epigenetic inactivation promotes PDAC progression.

Papillary Thyroid Cancer

Papillary Thyroid Cancer (PTC) is the most common endocrine malignancy, with steadily increasing global incidence over the past three decades. IRX5 is broadly overexpressed in PTC and predicts poor prognosis (Qin et al., 2024). Functional analysis has shown that IRX5 promotes tumor cell growth and migration, while polarizing tumor-associated macrophages toward the M2 (protumor) phenotype (Qin et al., 2024). Conversely, IRX5 silencing promotes M1 polarization, enhances inflammatory cytokine secretion, and suppresses tumor progression. These findings identify IRX5 as an immunomodulatory driver in PTC and highlight its potential as a therapeutic target for immunomodulation-based interventions.

Collectively, IRX genes exert diverse and context-dependent functions across malignancies, functioning either as tumor suppressors or oncogenic drivers, depending on their cellular, epigenetic, and microenvironmental context. The multifaceted roles in proliferation, invasion, and immune modulation underscore the importance of Iroquois signaling in cancer biology. CONCLUSION

The Iroquois (Irx) gene family comprises a highly conserved group of transcription factors with roles spanning embryonic patterning, organogenesis, and disease regulation. Originating from ancestral functions in invertebrate compartmentalization and boundary formation (Cavodeassi et al., 2001; Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002), Irx genes have diversified in vertebrates to regulate neurogenesis, cardiogenesis, and broader aspects of organ morphogenesis (Christoffels et al., 2000; Homsy et al., 2015). Their organization into tightly conserved genomic clusters across phyla highlights strong evolutionary pressure to maintain coordinated expression mediated through shared cis-regulatory mechanisms (Gomez-Skarmeta and Modolell, 2002; Houweling et al., 2001).

Beyond their developmental roles, Irx genes exhibit context-dependent functions in human diseases. In the nervous and cardiac systems, they govern progenitor identity, conduction system maturation, and structural morphogenesis (Catela et al., 2025; Costantini et al., 2005; Dou et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2016). Dysregulating Irx expression, driven by epigenetic alterations or genetic mutations, contributes to congenital disorders, including CHDs and neurodevelopmental syndromes (Cheng et al., 2011; Guo et al., 2017; Sim et al., 2020). Additionally, their involvement in cell-cycle control and signal integration underscores important non-canonical functions in adult tissue homeostasis and cancer biology (Barrios and Campuzano, 2015; Burglin, 1997).

Notably, Irx genes can function as either tumor suppressors or oncogenes depending on cellular and epigenetic context. IRX1 and IRX2 frequently display tumor-suppressive activity in gastric, lung, and pancreatic cancers (Chakma et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2018; Werner et al., 2015), whereas IRX4 and IRX5 exhibit oncogenic properties in prostate and thyroid carcinomas (Fernando et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024). These dual roles reflect the fine balance that Irx proteins maintain in regulating proliferation, differentiation, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment. Given these pleiotropic effects, Irx genes are emerging as promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets in cancer and regenerative medicine (Dos Santos et al., 2023; Geng et al., 2024).

In summary, Irx genes exemplify the interplay between evolutionary conservation and functional diversification. Continued investigation into their transcriptional targets, regulatory networks, and epigenetic modulation will deepen our understanding of the molecular logic that governs both development and disease. Elucidating the mechanisms of Iroquois signaling holds significant promise for advancing diagnostics and therapeutic strategies across a spectrum of development disorders and cancers.

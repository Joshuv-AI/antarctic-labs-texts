Correction to: Area-level excess mortality in times of COVID-19 in Switzerland: geographical, socioeconomic and political determinants
Published: 2026

This is a correction to: Julien Riou, Radoslaw Panczak, Garyfallos Konstantinoudis, Matthias Egger, Area-level excess mortality in times of COVID-19 in Switzerland: geographical, socioeconomic and political determinants, European Journal of Public Health, Volume 34, Issue 2, April 2024, Pages 415–417, https://doi.org/10.1093/eurpub/ckad230

In the originally published version of this manuscript, an error was identified in the calculation of expected mortality. The expected deaths between January 1 and February 24, 2020—prior to the first reported COVID-19 case—were mistakenly excluded. As a result, the expected number of deaths was underestimated: 55,676 instead of 65,201. While associations with geographical, socioeconomic, and political characteristics of municipalities were attenuated in the corrected analysis, the overall conclusions remain unchanged.

The first sentence of the Results section should read:

“We observed 74, 776 deaths in people aged 40 and older in 2020, compared to an expected 65,201 deaths (95% credible interval: 62,986 to 67,148), for a relative increase in excess mortality of 15% (11% to 19%)”.

The second paragraph of the Results section should read:

“In univariable analysis, municipalities in Italian- (1.15; 95% credible interval [CrI] 1.10-1.19) and French-speaking regions (1.09; 95% CrI 1.06-1.11) had higher excess mortality than the German-speaking regions (Figure 1B). Urban (relative excess mortality 0.97; 95% CrI 0.95-0.99), and peri-urban (0.98; 95% CrI 0.96-1.00) areas had lower excess than rural areas whereas municipalities within cross-border labour market areas were more affected (1.03; 95% CrI 1.00-1.05). Excess mortality was higher in municipalities of lower socioeconomic position (1.07; 95% CrI 1.03-1.10 comparing first with fifth quintile) and higher in municipalities with less support for COVID-19 control measures (1.03; 95% CrI 1.00-1.06 comparing first with fifth quintile of yes votes)”.

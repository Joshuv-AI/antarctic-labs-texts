Vitamin D Receptor Gene FokI Polymorphism in Patient with Human Immunodeficiency Virus – Tuberculosis Coinfection and Associated Risk Factors
Authors: Anak Agung Ayu Yuli Gayatri, Ni Nyoman Ayu Dewi, I Gede Eka Wiratnaya, Ketut Tuti Parwati Merati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is still unclear method for identifying people with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) who will develop tuberculosis (TB). This study aimed to investigate the role of vitamin D receptor (VDR) gene FokI allele f and associated risk factors in HIV-TB coinfection.Material and MethodsThis case control study was conducted with 60 total subjects consisting 30 subjects of HIV-TB patients as the case group and 30 subjects HIV without TB as the control. VDR gene FokI polymorphism was detected by polymerase chain reaction and sequencing, whereas light chain 3 (LC3) and caspase-3 levels were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and CD4 T cell by flowcytometry. Data analysis for different proportions used bivariate analysis and relationship analysis tests using logistic regression.ResultsThe VDR gene FokI (rs2228570) polymorphism proportion of f alleles in the case group were 26 (86.7%) and control 13 (43.3%). Low LC3 (LC3 ≤30 ng/mL) found in 27 (90.0%) of the cases and 9 of the controls (30.0%). Low caspase-3 (Caspase3 ≤3 ng/mL) found 28 (93.3%) in cases and 15 (50.0%) in the controls. The logistic regression analysis revealed that f allele of FokI VDR gene polymorphism, low LC3, low caspase-3 and low CD4 T cells are risk factors for HIV-TB co-infection as follows respectively; (odds ratio [OR], 6.921; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.199–39.936; P=0.031); (OR, 16.257; 95% CI, 2.568-102.928; P=0.003) and (OR, 7.448; 95% CI, 0.851–65.211; P=0.070); (OR, 6.227; 95% CI, 0.36-37.419; P=0.046)ConclusionVDR gene FokI polymorphism alleles f, low LC3, caspase-3, and low CD4 T cell count were identified as risk factors for HIV-TB Coinfection.Keywords: HIV-TB coinfection, VDR gene FokI polymorphism, LC3, Caspase-3

Introduction

Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection remains a major threat to human health worldwide, with tuberculosis (TB) co-infection one of the most important challenges in controlling HIV. People living with HIV (PLWH) have a 20-fold higher risk of TB, and 214,000 deaths were reported with HIV-TB co-infection in 2020 [1]. The implementation of highly active antiretroviral therapy and directly observed treatment short course TB have proven successful in HIV patients. However, TB co-infection still appears at various stages of HIV.

Vitamin D deficiency is still a health problem, especially among HIV patients, as epidemiological studies and meta-analyses from various parts of the world report that the prevalence of vitamin D deficiency in PLWH is higher than the general population, which is around 70–85% [2,3]. This is in accordance with the results of research at Sanglah Hospital, which reported that the average level of vitamin D in cases of HIV-TB co-infection was 19.80 (12.15–27.45) ng/mL, significantly lower than the level in control group as HIV without TB (33.30 [27.2–39.4] ng/mL) (P <0.001) [4].

The effort to resolve vitamin D deficiency and TB co-infection in HIV patients with vitamin D supplementation have not yet showed satisfactory results. It is suspected that other factors play an important role, such as genetic aspects as vitamin D receptor (VDR) gene polymorphisms which may cause changes in the VDR-vitamin D-axis, according to defence mechanisms against Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb). The VDR-vitamin D complex plays a role in inducing autophagy through transcription microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3) which has an important role in sequestering phagosomes for the formation of autophagosomes (autophagic vacuoles) as well as activating the effector molecule caspase-3 in the mechanism of apoptosis, as programmed cell death to control Mtb. Therefore, VDR gene FokI polymorphism may affect autophagy and apoptosis mechanisms which have an impact on increasing the risk of HIV-TB coinfection. This study aimed to analyze the role of VDR gene FokI polymorphism f allele, serum LC3 as a marker of autophagy, serum caspase-3 as a marker of apoptosis, and CD4 T cell count as marker of immunocompromise condition in HIV-TB co-infection and hopefully it will benefit in a comprehensive theory building of risk factors for TB in HIV.

Materials and Methods

1. Study method

This nested case-control study was conducted in the inpatient wards and outpatient clinics of Ngoerah Hospital, from December 2022 to June 2023. A total of 60 subjects were enrolled using a purposive consecutive sampling technique based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The case group consisted of HIV patients with TB co-infection, while the control group comprised HIV patients without TB at the time of enrollment. Both groups were recruited from the same clinical setting, with controls selected in order of arrival following case identification. Subjects were allocated in a 1:1 ratio between the two groups (Fig. 1).

2. Ethics statement

Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethical Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Udayana University/Ngoerah Hospital (Approval No. 2760/UN14.2.2.VII.14/LT/2022). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki by the World Medical Association.

3. Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria for case group were patients with HIV infection aged ≥18 years, undergoing treatment at Ngoerah Hospital and examined sputum or other specimen by GeneXpert Mtb/Rif to determine the presence of active TB infection. While HIV patient without TB were recruited as subject in control group.

4. The exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria are patients who have received previous immunosuppressant within the last 2 weeks and critical ill patients.

5. HIV staging

The clinical staging of HIV was determined using the World Health Organization (WHO) clinical staging system, stage 1 (asymptomatic), stage 2 (mild symptoms such as weight loss and respiratory tract infections), stage 3 (advanced symptoms including chronic diarrhea and pulmonary TB), and stage 4 (severe conditions like extrapulmonary TB and wasting syndrome). For analytical purposes, participants were grouped to early stage (WHO stage 1–2) and advanced stage (WHO stage 3–4). This classification was used to assess disease progression in relation to TB co-infection [5].

6. VDR gene FokI polymorphism identification

Blood specimens (3 mL) were taken and sent to the Laboratorium Biomedik Terpadu Medical Faculty of Udayana University, to determine the description of VDR gene FokI (rs2228570) polymorphism at exon 2, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was carried out using primers that amplify the desired sequence, with this following steps. DNA isolation of venous blood was carried out following the QIAamp Blood Mini Kit protocol. Briefly, place 200 µL of blood into a centrifuge tube and add 200 µL of BB Buffer. After adding 20 µL of Proteinase K and incubating at 65oC for 10 minutes, 200 µL of absolute alcohol was added and then the mixture was transferred to a spin column. The sample was centrifuged at 5,000xg for 1 minute and added 500 µL of wash buffer 1. Followed by centrifugation at 5000xg for 1 minute, 500 µL of wash buffer 2 was added. Next, centrifuged at 12,000xg for 3 minutes and the column spin was transferred to a new centrifuge tube. A total of 100 µL of elution buffer was added and left for 2 minutes at room temperature. Centrifugation at 5,000xg for 1 minute and DNA stored at -20oC. PCR amplification for the target was carried out with a reaction mixture consisting of GoTaq green master mix (2x), primer F (final concentration 0.3 µM), primer R (final concentration 0.3 µM), DNA, and ddH2O. DNA was amplified with an initial denaturation program at 94oC for 5 minutes, followed by 35 cycles consisting of denaturation at 95oC for 1 minute, annealing at 61oC for 1 minute, extension at 72oC for 1 minute, and amplification ended with the final extension at 72oC for 5 minutes. The primers amplify the VDR gene with the following sequence: forward primer (F): 5'- ACT CTG GCT CTG ACC GTG - 3' and reverse primer (R): 5'- TTC GCT TTC CCA CCA GAT - 3'. The amplicon (PCR product) is 242bp in size. The PCR product was then electrophoresed on a 1.5% agarose gel using an electrophoresis machine at a voltage of 100 V for 60 minutes, then visualized with UV light. For the DNA sequencing process, the PCR product is sent to the Jakarta Genetika Science Laboratory

7. Level of plasma LC3 and caspase-3 measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Plasma samples (2 mL) were collected to measure LC3 and caspase-3 levels using ELISA kit (Bioassay Technology Laboratory, Cat. No. E4774Hu for LC3 and Cat. No. E4804Hu for caspase-3). All reagents were prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 40 µL of plasma sample and 10 µL of specific antibody (anti-LC3 or anti-caspase-3) were added to each sample well, followed by 50 µL of Streptavidin-HRP. After incubation at 37℃ for 60 minutes, the wells were washed five times with wash buffer. Substrate solutions A and B (50 µL each) were then added and incubated until color development, followed by 50 µL of stop solution. Optical density was read at 450 nm using a microplate reader. The cut-off values applied were 30 ng/mL for LC3 and 3 ng/mL for caspase-3.

8. Data analysis

The collected data were entered and analyzed using SPSS (version 24, SPSS Inc., city, state, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the characteristics of the subjects and the frequency distribution of variables in both the case and control groups. Bivariate analysis using the Chi-square test was performed to calculate odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables with a P-value <0.250 in the bivariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. In the multivariate analysis, a P-value <0.05 and a 95% CI were considered statistically significant.

The majority in both groups were male (83.3%), aged ≥50 years, and most subjects in both groups were in advanced HIV stages (stage 3–4) (Table 1).

Discussion

HIV infection, the cause of acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS), remains a major global health challenge. TB co-infection significantly complicates HIV/AIDS management and represents one of the most difficult aspects in controlling the disease. Therefore, evaluating pathological risk factors in HIV-TB co-infection is of critical importance.

As shown in Table 2, only low CD4 levels was associated with an increased risk of TB in individuals with HIV. This finding aligns with a previous meta-analysis indicating that HIV patients with CD4 <200 cells/mL have a 2.88 times higher risk of TB co-infection [6]. Bivariate analysis showed that the f allele was a significant risk factor for HIV-TB co-infection compared to the F allele. VDR gene FokI polymorphism can cause amino acid sequence changes, thought to influence the role of VDR-vitamin D complex in immune responses, as defense against Mtb infection. The VDR gene has an important role in the growth and function of various cell types, as a transcription factor, and influencing the transcription of a large and diverse set of genes [7]. Meanwhile, VDR gene FokI polymorphism can cause functional impacts such as DNA binding changes, transcriptional activation or heterodimerization and hormonal ligand affinity and subsequently lead to alteration of VDR-Vitamin D complex action, especially in the autophagy and apoptosis processes which determine Mtb survival. The FokI polymorphism of VDR gene occurs in exon 2 is due to change the ACG codon to the ATG codon at the first potential start site, which results in two different alleles referred to as f and F. Exon 2 of the VDR gene with the allele f translates at the first start site, producing a 427 amino acid long VDR protein, whereas exon 2 of the VDR gene with the F allele begins to translate at the second start site, resulting in a shorter protein lacking the three NH2-terminal amino acids. However, the allele F interacts more efficiently with transcription factors and shows relatively enhanced function compared with the allele f [8].

This study results support previous study which was showing a relationship between VDR gene FokI polymorphism and TB, with evidence that the allele f having a tendency to a worse phenotype, and raises the assumption of reduced VDR function and increased risk of TB. The allele f is likely to result in significant changes in gene activation that contribute to cell proliferation, and immune function including defence responses against Mtb, by decreased autophagy and apoptosis as well as increased necrosis of immune cells as risk factor for active TB [9]. For example, a study performed in Zahedan, Iran which reported that individuals with the Ff genotype had nearly twice the TB risk (OR, 1.8; 95% CI, 1.2–2.8; P=0.006) [10]. Similar findings were reported in studies from India [11,12], a study on the Kazakh population in China [13], and Beijing [14]. In Indonesia, our results are findings are in line with several previous studies. For example, a study conducted in Medan reported that Batak ethnicity TB patients were dominated by VDR gene FokI polymorphism genotype Ff (61.9%) [15]. Similarly, a study in Bali found that VDR gene FokI polymorphism is the dominant allele f with genotypes Ff 54.3% and ff 25.7% among TB patients [16]. Moreover, at least nine meta-analyses have consistently demonstrated a significant association between VDR gene FokI polymorphisms and TB [17,18,19,20,21,22,23,24,25].

Vitamin D, through VDR expression, has been proven to play a role in the transcription of the LC3 genes, a major protein in the autophagy mechanism aimed at eliminating intracellular Mtb [26,27]. LC3 is involved in the biogenesis and transport of autophagosomes, in the elongation of the phagophore bilayer membrane and in the recruitment of proteins for the autophagy process. LC3 is structurally similar with ubiquitin and can be biochemically modified by cleavage and lipidation via conjugation to phosphatidylethanolamine, which resembles the process of ubiquitination. LC3 is an important classical marker used to assess the autophagy process. Autophagy is an intracellular homeostatic proteolytic mechanism implemented by macrophages as an antimicrobial strategy for TB. According to intracellular survival of Mtb by cell death pathways, autophagy is identified as one of the important cellular functions stimulated by VDR, supported by the evidence of several in vitro studies [28,29].

The VDR-vitamin D complex inhibits HIV and Mtb replication in human macrophages through the autophagy mechanism. Disruption of the VDR-Vitamin D axis inhibits TNF-α in Mycobacteria-infected macrophages and peripheral blood mononuclear cells and the level of circulating TNF-α. In addition, TNF-α significantly increased LC3 expression to induce autophagy [30].

Likewise, it was reported in in vitro studies that binding of the active form of Vitamin D-VDR activates effector molecules caspase-3 to mediate other cell death pathways called apoptosis, one of the defense mechanisms to eliminate intracellular Mtb through a programmed cell death without causing an inflammatory reaction [29]. Caspase-3 is a biomarker of apoptosis. Apoptosis is a highly coordinated and regulated process of a form of programmed cell death in which the components of the dead cell and Mtb are enclosed in the cytoplasmic membrane and released from the cell called apoptotic bodies. The apoptosis mechanism as a defence against Mtb begins with the binding of ligands such as TNFα, FasL, CD95L, TRAIL, to the respective receptors TNFR, Fas/CD95, and DR3 on the cell surface. This activates initiator caspases such as caspase 8 and 10 which results in the formation and activation of the death inducing signalling complex which activates caspase-3 [31,32]. Activation of caspase-3 causes cleavage of various death substrates resulting in characteristic features of apoptosis including membrane blebbing, nuclear fragmentation, release of cytochrome c from mitochondria into the cytosol and exposure of phosphatidyl serine on the cell surface (signal to attack/eat signal) to trigger phagocytosis and anti-inflammatory responses [33].

The protective role of the VDR-vitamin D complex is associated with the induction of endogenous antioxidants and the reduction of lipid peroxidation. During pulmonary TB treatment, a previous study also showed that the VDR-vitamin D complex significantly inhibited MMP1 expression which is an important parameter in the mechanism of cell necrosis. It was concluded that the cell death pathway is a fundamental process involved in the interaction between Mtb and the infected cells. Decreased necrosis requires increased apoptosis and autophagy [29]. Inhibition of apoptosis and increasing of necrosis on infected macrophages have been shown to favour infection and inhibit the antigen-specific immune responses generation. Apoptosis can reduce Mtb viability and limit its spreading without shedding cellular contents into tissues and ultimately enhance host defence by stimulating adaptive immune responses via T-cells [34]. Several studies show the role of the VDR-Vitamin D complex in increasing the expression of caspase-3 to induce apoptosis. Inhibition of apoptosis and increasing necrosis of infected macrophages has been shown enhancing susceptibility of active TB [34,35].

Among three main variables in this study, VDR gene FokI polymorphism allele f and low LC3 were proven to be clinically and statistically significant as risk factors for HIV-TB co-infection. Meanwhile, low caspase-3 in logistic regression analysis showed clinical significance as a risk factor for HIV-TB co-infection with an adjusted OR, 7.448 which was higher than the expected OR even though the P=0.070 was not statistically significant. Low LC3 levels were the risk factor with the highest adjusted OR, followed by low caspase-3 and FokI VDR gene polymorphism alleles f, indicated that the most impaired autophagy parameter in HIV patients and has a very important role in increasing the risk of TB co-infection. A previous study demonstrated the protective role of autophagy in people with HIV, finding that peripheral blood mononuclear cells from non-progressors PLWH showed significantly higher numbers of autophagy vesicles associated with increased expression of autophagy markers compared to progressive HIV individuals [36].

Autophagy and apoptosis are well known as two important cellular processes which respond to cell damage caused by various stressors including Mtb infection. Autophagy is considered to be the primary mechanism by which damaged cell organelles and other small cellular substituents are replaced, whereas apoptosis is used to remove entire cells. Both processes can occur in the same cell, although often with different kinetics, with autophagy being used before induction of apoptosis. Cell stress can stimulate an autophagy response and cells that are unable to overcome stress through autophagy can eventually experience apoptosis [37,38].

In the era with rapid advances in genetic technology, understanding the pathogenesis of HIV-TB infection has become more comprehensive. The results of this study show that VDR gene FokI allele f may cause changes in the VDR-Vitamin D axis, in inhibiting autophagy and apoptosis, showing its involvement and implications in the pathogenesis of active TB in PLWH.

This study demonstrated that the pathomechanism of HIV-TB co-infection there were the role for genetic aspects as VDR gene FokI polymorphism allele f which have 6.905 times higher risk of HIV-TB coinfection compared to the allele F, the autophagy inhibition aspect characterized by low LC3 levels which have 16.257 times higher risk of HIV-TB coinfection compared to those with high LC3 levels and the apoptosis inhibition aspect characterized by low caspase-3 which have 7.448 times higher risk of HIV-TB coinfection compared to those with high caspase-3.

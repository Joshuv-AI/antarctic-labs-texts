Safety of lacto‐N‐triose II as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Margarita Aguilera‐Gómez, Thomas Frenzel, Montaña Cámara, Inge Mangelsdorf, Francesca Marcon, Harry J McArdle, Peter Moldeus, Monika Neuhäuser‐Berthold, Estefanía Noriega Fernández, Carolina Ropero‐Pérez, Karen Ildico Hirsch‐Ernst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on lacto‐N‐triose II (LNTri II) as a novel food (NF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The NF is mainly composed of the human‐identical milk oligosaccharide (HiMO) LNTri II, but it also contains d‐glucose, d‐galactose, d‐lactose and lacto‐N‐triitol. The NF is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain (Kluyveromyces lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799. The information on the identity, production process, composition and specifications of the NF does not raise safety concerns. The applicant intends to add the NF in a variety of foods, including infant formula (IF), follow‐on formula (FOF), food supplements (FS) and food for special medical purposes (FSMP). The target population is the general population. In line with other HiMOs, the safety assessment of this NF is mainly based on the comparison between the intake of breastfed infants and the estimated intake of the NF. The anticipated daily intake from its use in IF is similar to the estimated natural mean highest daily intake in breastfed infants. The anticipated daily intake from the NF as a food ingredient at the maximum proposed use levels exceeds the intake level of breastfed infants on a body weight basis in infants and children. Of note, higher exposure can be obtained from LNTri II as it is a by‐product in the already authorised HiMOs (e.g. Lacto‐N‐tetraose). In addition, the natural intake estimate does not consider the formation of LNTri II at gastrointestinal level by gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis. The Panel considers that the consumption of the NF at the proposed uses and use levels does not raise safety concerns and concludes that the NF is safe under the proposed conditions of use.Keywords: HiMO, HMO, human milk oligosaccharide, lacto‐N‐triose II, LNTri II, novel food, safety

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

On 6 February 2024, the applicant “Shandong Henglu Biotechnology Co, Ltd” submitted a request to the European Commission (EC) in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 1 to authorise the placing on the market of lacto‐N‐triose II (LNTri II) produced with a genetically modified strain (Kluyveromyces lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of the parental strain K. lactis DSM 70799 as a novel food (NF).

The application requests to authorise LNTri II produced with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799 in a number of foods and in food supplements (FS) as defined in Directive 2002/46/EC 2 intended for the general population excluding infants and young children.

The applicant has requested data protection under Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 for data in support of this request.

In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the EC asks the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) to provide a scientific opinion on the safety of LNTri II produced with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799 as a NF.

In this opinion on mixture of LNTri II produced with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709–2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799 as a NF, EFSA should also document whether and to what extent the requirements of Article 26(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are fulfilled regarding the data for which the applicant is requesting data protection.

Additional information

LNTri II serves as a metabolic intermediate (trisaccharide backbone) in the biosynthesis of other human‐identical milk oligosaccharides (HiMOs) such as lacto‐N‐tetraose (LNT) and lacto‐N‐neotetraose (LNnT) (Fang et al., 2023; Pressley et al., 2024). LNT is authorised as a NF when produced with genetically modified strains of Escherichia coli K‐12 DH1 (EFSA NDA Panel, 2019a), E. coli BL21 (DE3) (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022a) or E. coli K‐12 MG1655 (EFSA NDA Panel, 2025a). LNnT, a constitutional isomer of LNT, is also included in the Union list of NFs, when chemically synthesised (EFSA NDA Panel, 2015a) or produced with genetically modified strains of E. coli K‐12 DH1 (Commission Implementin g Regulation (EU) 2019/1314 3 ) or E. coli BL21 (DE3) (EFSA NDA Panel, 2020a). Moreover, EFSA has assessed with positive outcomes the extension of use in food supplements (FS) for children of the chemically synthesised LNnT (EFSA NDA Panel, 2015b) and in FS for infants of mixtures containing LNnT or LNT produced with genetically modified strains of E. coli K‐12 DH1 (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022b,c).

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The NF, which is the subject of the application, contains LNTri II as the primary constituent (≥ 96% w/w dry matter (DM)). LNTri II serves as a metabolic intermediate (trisaccharide backbone) in the biosynthesis of other HiMOs such as LNT and LNnT (Fang et al., 2023; Pressley et al., 2024). The Panel notes that substantial exposure to LNTri II as a by‐product in the already authorised HiMOs LNT and LNnT occurs. Moreover, it is known that production of LNTri II at gastrointestinal level (i.e. with higher local concentrations) through gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis of more abundant neutral HMOs/HiMOs (e.g. LNT) or human milk (Cheng et al., 2019; Fang et al., 2023) occurs. The Panel also notes that although LNTri II is the major component of the NF, it also contains d‐glucose, d‐galactose, d‐lactose and lacto‐N‐triitol. The NF is produced by fermentation with K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02, a genetically modified strain of K. lactis DSM 7099.

The NF is proposed to be used in IF, FOF, FSMP and FS as defined in Directive 2002/46/EC, beverages and in a variety of other foods (e.g. dairy products, cereals). The target population is the general population.

According to Article 3(2)(a) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the NF falls under the following categories:

‘food with a new or intentionally modified molecular structure, where that structure was not used as, or in, a food within the Union before 15 May 1997’; and

‘food with a new or intentionally modified molecular structure, where that structure was not used as, or in, a food within the Union before 15 May 1997’; and

‘food consisting of, isolated from or produced from microorganisms, fungi or algae’.

‘food consisting of, isolated from or produced from microorganisms, fungi or algae’.

Identity of the NF

The NF is a powdered mixture mainly composed of LNTri II (≥ 96% w/w DM), but it also contains d‐glucose (≤ 2.0% w/w DM), d‐galactose (≤ 2.0% w/w DM), d‐lactose (≤ 3.0% w/w DM) and lacto‐N‐triitol (≤ 0.5% w/w DM). It is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799.

The main component of the NF is LNTri II (GlcNAc‐β‐(1–3)‐Gal‐β‐(1–4)‐Glc), which is a trisaccharide consisting of N‐acetyl‐d‐glucosamine (GlcNAc) linked through a β‐(1–3) bond to d‐galactose (Gal), which is β‐(1–4) linked to d‐glucose (Glc) (Table 1 and Figure 1).

Chemical structure of LNTri II.

Several analyses were performed on the NF in order to confirm the structure of LNTri II, the major constituent of the NF.

The structure of LNTri II was determined by mono‐dimensional (1D) nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, including 1H and 13C spectra, and two‐dimensional (2D) NMR spectroscopy, including correlation spectroscopy (COSY), heteronuclear single quantum coherence (HSQC) and heteronuclear multiple band correlation (HMBC). The relevant coupling constants measured by 1H NMR together with the correlations evidenced on the 2D NMR spectra confirmed: (i) the β‐(1‐3′) bond between GlcNAc and d‐Gal; and ii) the β‐(1′‐4′′) link between the d‐Gal (Gal‐C‐1′) and d‐Glc (Glc‐C‐4) moieties of d‐lactose. Correlations in the NMR spectra were identified and compared to those previously reported for LNTri II (Chen et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2021).

The molecular structure of LNTri II was corroborated by liquid chromatography–quadrupole time‐of‐flight mass spectrometry (LCMS‐Q‐TOF MS), based on the retention time, mass‐to‐charge ratios and fragmentation pattern.

The identity of LNTri II was also corroborated by high performance anion exchange chromatography–pulsed amperometric detection (HPAEC–PAD). The specific optical rotation was also determined in two batches of the NF.

Comparison to a commercially available authentic standard 12 was provided for 1H NMR, MS and HPLC–ELSD (high performance liquid chromatography–evaporative light‐scattering detector).

On the basis of the spectroscopic and chromatographic evidence, the Panel considers that the LNTri II in the NF produced with K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 is identical to the LNTri II in human milk and therefore, it is regarded as being a HiMO.

Production process

According to the information provided, the NF is produced in line with good manufacturing practice (GMP) and Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points (HACCP) principles, in a facility that is FSSC (Food Safety System Certification) 22,000 certified.

The NF is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis (DSM 70799; ATCC 8585) using raw materials and processing aids suitable for food use. The production microorganism is cultured under sterile conditions in a chemically defined nutrient medium and uses glucose and lactose to synthesise LNTri II, which is partly excreted into the medium. Additional intracellular LNTri II is released into the medium after pasteurisation. The production microorganism is removed from the fermentation medium by filtration at the end of the fermentation process. A series of downstream separation, purification and concentration steps are used to obtain a powdered mixture mainly composed of LNTri II. The NF has been produced at a commercial scale.

The genetically modified production strain K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 is a derivative of the parental strain K. lactis (DSM 70799), with the genotype A [k1+, k2+]. The complete genome of K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 was sequenced and compared to the reference genome of K. lactis NRRL‐Y‐1140. Based on the WGS data provided by the applicant, 22 and 3 antimicrobial resistance (AMR) hits were identified in the CARD and DRESIS databases exceeding the EFSA thresholds values of identity ≥80% and coverage ≥70% with no evidence provided to demonstrate that these genes are intrinsic (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023; EFSA, 2024b). However, all identified genes were also present in the reference strain K. lactis NRRL‐Y‐1440, which is widely used as a host for protein expression, genetic studies and industrial applications. AMR determinants identified using CARD are likely to reflect intrinsic resistance features or gene expression markers and are therefore not considered to be of clinical relevance. The species K. lactis is included in the list of organisms for which the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to the risk assessment may apply (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2020).

The production strain has been deposited at the China Center for Type Culture Collections (CCTCC M‐2022118). A detailed description of the genetic modification steps applied to the parental strain K. lactis (DSM 70799) to obtain the production strain K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 has been provided by the applicant. The absence of DNA and viable cells from the production strain in the NF has been demonstrated in accordance with the EFSA Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms in support of the risk assessment of products used in the food chain (EFSA, 2025; EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018).

The Panel considers that the production process is sufficiently described and does not raise safety concerns.

With regards to physico‐chemical properties, the NF can be described as a white to off‐white powder. The solubility in water of three batches of the NF was measured in triplicate, according to the EFSA Guidance on technical requirements for regulated food and feed product applications to establish the presence of small particles including nanoparticles (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021), resulting in an average value of 331.94 g/L. The Panel considers that the information provided on the composition is sufficient for characterising the NF.

Stability

Stability of the NF

The stability studies were conducted with three batches of the NF produced with K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 and packed in a plastic and aluminium foil composite film (polyester/aluminium/ nylon/polyethylene). The applicant provided results for two (real‐time) 24‐month stability studies at 25°C and 60% relative humidity (RH) and at 30°C and 65% RH, respectively. In addition, a 6‐month accelerated stability study at 40°C and 75% RH was also conducted. The batches of the NF were analysed for carbohydrate content (LNTri II, d‐glucose, d‐galactose, d‐lactose and lacto‐N‐triitol), moisture, pH and physical parameters (appearance and colour). Microbiological parameters (total aerobic plate count, yeasts and moulds, coliforms, Salmonella spp., coagulase‐positive Staphylococcus, L. monocytogenes, Cronobacter spp., B. cereus) were also provided after 6 months of storage under accelerated conditions, and after 6 and 24 months of storage at 25°C, also including the analysis of Enteriobacteriaceae and E. coli for the final sampling point.

No significant changes in the monitored parameters were observed over the storage period under normal and accelerated conditions, and they remained within specifications. The applicant proposed a 24‐month shelf‐life under ambient conditions for the NF.

The Panel considers that the data provided sufficient information with respect to the stability of the NF up to 24 months.

Stability of the NF under the intended conditions of use

The applicant tested the stability of the NF produced with K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02 in IF (0.04 g NF/kg; 6 months at 4, 20 and 30°C), ready‐to‐feed liquid IF (0.04 g NF/kg; 6 months at 4, 20, 30 and 37°C) and yoghurt (0.1 g NF/L; 21 days at 4°C). These food categories represent some of the intended uses and reflect different processing and storage conditions relevant for those intended uses. The applicant demonstrated that LNTri II is stable in these foods over periods representing the typical shelf‐life for the respective products.

The applicant also performed forced degradation tests where LNTri II was exposed to different conditions mimicking different food preparations (e.g. heating, beverages, pH values). LNTri II stability was tested in 1 M hydrochloric acid and 1 M sodium hydroxide for 6 and 24 h at room temperature, in 3% hydrogen peroxide for 0.5 h at room temperature, at 105°C for 3 days and under total light illumination (1.2 × 106 Lux. h) or near‐UV energy (200 Wh/m2) for 10 days. LNTri II remained stable under acidic, high temperature and light conditions and unstable under basic and oxidative conditions.

The Panel considers that the available information is sufficient with respect to the stability of the NF in the proposed food matrices.

History of use of the NF and/or of its source

History of use of the NF

There is no history of use of the NF.

LNTri II, the major constituent of the NF, is a neutral non‐fucosylated oligosaccharide found in human milk (Fang et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2020). LNTri II is also formed at gastrointestinal level through gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis of other milk oligosaccharides such as LNT and LNnT (Cheng et al., 2019; Fang et al., 2023).

Intake of LNTri II from human milk

In previous EFSA opinions, the daily intake of HMOs from the consumption of human milk has been estimated for a 6.7 kg body weight (bw) infant (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2012), considering the average and high daily intake of human milk (800 and 1200 mL, respectively) for infants from 0 to 6 months (EFSA NDA Panel, 2013).

The applicant conducted a literature search in one database that resulted in only three articles reporting naturally occurring concentrations of LNTri II in human milk. Specifically in colostrum, the concentration of LNTri II is observed to be 129–136 mg/L. A significant decrease over time is noted to approximately 30–40 mg/L by the 42nd day of lactation and then, the concentration remains stable up to the 168th day of lactation (Fang et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2020).

Based on these limited reported concentrations of LNTri II in mature human milk and considering the average and high daily intake of human milk (800 mL and 1200 mL, respectively) for infants from 0 to 6 months (EFSA NDA Panel, 2013), the daily intake levels of LNTri II from human milk for a 6.7 kg bw infant (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2012) has been calculated (Table 4). This default body weight used by the NDA Panel is for an infant of 3–6 months of age, who is more likely than younger infants to consume these volumes of human milk.

Combined intake from the NF and other sources

The Panel notes that LNTri II can be found as a by‐product in authorised HiMO (i.e. LNT and LNnT). This means that in case of ingestion of foods containing such HiMOs (e.g. young children) the actual intake of LNTri II may increase.

Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME)

As mentioned by the applicant and reported in previous EFSA opinions (e.g. EFSA NDA Panel, 2015a, 2019a, 2022a) HMOs, including LNTri II, are considered ‘non‐digestible oligosaccharides’ (EFSA NDA Panel, 2014) since they do not undergo any significant digestion by human enzymes in the upper gastrointestinal tract and only small amounts are expected to be absorbed. Milk oligosaccharides are mostly fermented in the colon by intestinal microbiota, while a fraction is excreted unchanged in the faeces and a small fraction is found in the urine (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022a). In addition, there are no indications that the absorption of LNTri II or other structurally related mono‐ and oligosaccharides (e.g. galactose) from the NF, differs from that of similar components in human milk.

It is noted that LNTri II can be produced via the catabolism of major neutral N‐containing HMOs (e.g. LNT and LNnT) through gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis, suggesting that LNTri II formation can take place in the gastrointestinal tract after the ingestion of LNT and LNnT (Cheng et al., 2019; Fang et al., 2023).

Genotoxicity

The potential genotoxicity of the NF was investigated in a bacterial reverse mutation test and in an in vitro mammalian cell micronucleus test.

Description of the studies with relevant data is included in a published paper (Fang et al., 2023).

The in vitro assessment of the mutagenic potential of the NF (unpublished study report, 2023a; Fang et al., 2023) was performed with S. Typhimurium strains TA98, TA100, TA1535, TA1537 and E. coli WP2 uvrA, which were exposed to LNTri II in water at eight different concentrations up to 5000 μg/plate, either in the presence or absence of liver microsomal fractions (S9 mix), using the plate‐incorporation method. Based on the results of a preliminary test, concentrations of LNTri II at 200, 500, 1000, 2000 and 5000 μg/plate were selected for the main test. No evidence of toxicity was obtained following exposure to the NF and no precipitate was observed in any plate.

Treatment with the NF did not result in increases in the number of revertant colonies as compared to the negative control at any concentration in both tests either in the presence or absence of S9 mix. Therefore, LNTri II was shown to be non‐mutagenic in the absence or presence of metabolic activation at concentrations up to 5000 μg LNTri II/plate.

The genotoxic potential of the NF was further investigated in in vitro mammalian cell micronucleus tests (unpublished study report, 2023b, 2025; Fang et al., 2023) conducted in cultured Chinese hamster ovary (CHO‐WBL) cells in the presence or absence of metabolic activation (S9 mix). Based on the results of preliminary tests, three concentrations of LNTri II of 500, 1000 and 2000 μg/mL were tested in the pivotal study. No statistically significant increases in the number of binucleated cells containing micronuclei both after 3‐h treatment in the presence or absence of S9 mix or following 24‐h treatment in the absence of S9 mix as compared to the negative control were recorded. The NF was therefore determined to be non‐clastogenic and non‐aneugenic in the absence and presence of metabolic activation up to the highest concentration of 2000 μg LNTri II /mL.

Taking into account the test results provided and considering the nature, source and production process of the NF, the Panel considers that there are no concerns regarding genotoxicity.

Subchronic toxicity

The applicant provided a 14‐day repeated dose pilot toxicity study in which two groups of 5 Crl:CD (SD) 3‐week‐old rats/sex were given 1500, 2500 or 5000 mg LNTri II/kg bw per day by oral gavage (unpublished study report, 2023c). The treatment was well tolerated. The variations observed at clinical pathology investigations were of minimal nature, without dose‐dependency or present in a single sex. No differences in selected organ weights were noted t and no alterations at gross pathology examination related to LNTri II administration were noted. The high dose of 5000 mg LNTri II /kg bw per day was selected as the top dose to be used in the main 90‐day study.

In the 90‐day study, groups of 10 Crl:CD(SD) juvenile rats/sex (post‐natal day 19–22 at the start of treatment) were given by oral gavage the vehicle (deionised water), 1500, 2500 or 5000 mg LNTri II/kg bw per day (unpublished study report, 2023d; Fang et al., 2023). In all groups, five additional rats/sex were added for a 4‐week recovery period. An additional subset of 10 rats/sex/group was dosed for 43 consecutive days, followed by caesarean‐section examination on gestation day 15 for female rats, and sperm analysis and necropsy after cohabitation completion for male rats to assess development and reproductive function.

There were no deaths during the study and no changes considered to be treatment‐related in clinical signs, ophthalmoscopic examination, functional observational battery, body weight and body weight gain in any rat. A statistically significant decrease in food consumption was noted at all doses in male and female rats from the end of months 1 and 2, respectively, without effect on the body weight. Overall, there were no effects attributable to LNTri II on the developmental, reproductive and behavioural assessment (additional subset of rats). No changes in haematology, coagulation and endocrine parameters attributable to LNTri II were observed during both the treatment and recovery periods. A statistically significant decrease in eosinophil counts in males at low dose and in females at the intermediate and high dose at the end of the recovery period was noted. Prothrombin time in males receiving the low dose showed a statistically significant reduction at the end of the treatment period. Regarding clinical chemistry, sporadic statistically significant changes considered of no toxicological relevance were recorded at the end of the treatment period: decrease in cholesterol, sodium and high‐density lipoproteins in males administered 5000 mg/kg bw per day for 90 days, and an increase in phosphorus levels in males administered the intermediate dose only at the end of the 4‐week recovery period. All the other parameters were normal at the end of the recovery period. There was no urinalysis parameter difference considered to be related to LNTri II treatment. Changes observed at laboratory examination were not attributed to the administration of LNTri II, based on their low magnitude, presence in a single sex or lack of a dose response and overall considered as not toxicologically relevant. At post‐mortem examination a statistically significant difference was observed only in absolute organ weights of thyroids at the dose of 5000 mg/kg bw in males. At histological examination minimal myofibre degeneration, mononuclear infiltration, fibroplasia and haemorrhage were noted in the hearts of male rats at the end of the treatment and recovery period (1500 and 5000 mg/kg bw dose groups). These findings are overall considered by the Panel to be of no biological relevance, as they were of small magnitude, occurred at a low incidence rate, were distributed across all groups (controls included) without a clear dose–response relationship, were limited to only one sex or are expected in rats of this age and strain. The description of the studies, relevant data and diagrams are included in a published paper (Fang et al., 2023).

The Panel considers that no adverse effects were observed in this study up to the highest tested dose of 5000 mg LNTri II/kg bw per day.

Nutritional information

The NF is mainly composed of the non‐digestible oligosaccharide LNTri II.

The NF contains other carbohydrates individually present at low concentrations (up to about 0.2%, see Table 2). d‐Lactose (up to 3%) is the most abundant molecule in human milk (~7%) and its monomers, d‐glucose and d‐galactose, are normal constituents of human milk.

Only traces of other related oligosaccharides (e.g. lacto‐N‐triitol) could be detected in the NF.

The Panel considers that, taking into account the composition of the NF and the proposed conditions of use, consumption of the NF is not nutritionally disadvantageous.

Allergenicity

The protein content of the NF is ≤ 0.01% w/w as indicated in the specifications (Table 3).

The applicant did not identify an allergenic potential of proteins introduced because of the genetic modification of the K. lactis DSM 70799 parental strain, assessed according to the ‘Scientific opinion on the assessment of allergenicity of GM plants and microorganisms and derived food and feed of the Scientific Panel on Genetically Modified Organisms’ (EFSA GMO Panel, 2010). The bioinformatic study was conducted on the Allergen Online database and the Structural Database of Allergenic Proteins. The criterion used for identifying sequence homology with known allergens was that of considering ‘higher than 35% identity in a sliding window of 80 amino acids’. Therefore, the Panel considers that the likelihood of allergenic reactions to the NF due to proteins introduced because of the genetic modification of the parental strain is low.

The Panel also notes that the NF contains lactose derived from cow's milk and therefore is subject to mandatory allergen labelling.

DISCUSSION

The NF is a powdered mixture mainly composed of LNTri II, but it also contains d‐glucose, d‐galactose, d‐lactose and lacto‐N‐triitol. The NF is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain (K. lactis DSM 709‐2‐02) of K. lactis DSM 70799.

The applicant intends to market the NF as an ingredient to a number of food products including IF and FOF and as a FS for individuals above 3 years of age. The target population proposed by the applicant is the general population. The applicant stated that FS containing the NF are not intended to be used if other foods with added NF or human milk are consumed on the same day.

The Panel notes that the anticipated daily intake of LNTri II from the consumption of IF (only), in infants up to 16 weeks of age, is similar to the highest intake level in breastfed infants on a bw basis. The anticipated highest estimated daily intake (mean and P95) of LNTri II from the proposed uses in infants and children exceeds what is considered an average natural intake for LNTri II.

LNTri II concentration in human milk is relatively low when compared to more abundant neutral HMOs such as 2’‐FL, LNT and LNnT and only limited data on LNTri II concentrations in human milk are available in the literature (Fang et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2020). LNTri II is also produced at gastrointestinal level through gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis, from more abundant neutral HMOs/HiMOs (e.g. LNT and LNnT; Cheng et al., 2019, Fang et al., 2023; Guang et al., 2024). Therefore, the actual exposure to LNTri II at intestinal level is potentially higher although difficult to quantify since the conversion rate depends on several factors. This suggests that infants can be largely exposed to LNTri II, originated by gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis, after ingestion of LNT, LNnT and human milk.

Moreover, the Panel notes that the intake of LNTri II from its presence as a by‐product in already authorised HiMOs (e.g. LNT and LNnT) can reach values as high as the intakes derived from the currently proposed conditions of use for LNTri II as NF or even more than two‐fold higher (LNT by microbial source or E.coli BL21), when considering the intake further to IF consumption (see Appendix B).

It appears that the intake of the NF alone results in an intake of LNTri II that might be lower than that from the presence of LNTri II as a by‐product in already authorised HiMOs, which have been considered as safe. In addition, the NF may contribute to an increased total exposure of LNTri II in individuals also consuming foods containing LNT or LNnT. Finally, the Panel notes that the contribution of LNTri II from the NF to the total HiMO intake on a bw basis is marginal (EFSA, 2024a).

The submitted toxicity studies did not raise safety concerns. The Panel considers that no adverse effects were observed in the subchronic toxicity study at the tested dose corresponding to a daily intake of 5 g LNTri II/kg bw.

Overall, taking into account the compositional characterisation, the production process, the intrinsic nature of HMOs with their limited absorption, the production of LNTri II at intestinal level (i.e. higher local concentrations) through gut microbiota fermentation or acid hydrolysis of more abundant neutral HMOs/HiMOs (e.g. LNT) or human milk, the fact that a similar or higher exposure to LNTri II can be obtained through ingestion of the already authorised HiMOs (e.g. LNT and LNnT), the absence of toxicologically relevant effects in the subchronic study and considering that breastfed infants are naturally exposed to these substances, the Panel considers that the consumption of the NF at the proposed uses and use levels does not raise safety concerns.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel concludes that the NF, which is composed of LNTri II and other structurally related mono‐ and oligosaccharides, is safe under the proposed conditions of use.

Aesthetic quality evaluation of packaging design with graph neural networks and composition features
Authors: Xiaocen Guo, Sijia Fu, Di Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the field of visual communication design, the aesthetic quality assessment of packaging images faces significant challenges due to the complexity and subjectivity of their layout composition. To enhance the objectivity and intelligence of such evaluations, this study proposes a packaging design aesthetic quality assessment method combining image composition features and graph neural networks (CGA-GNN). The method extracts visual structural information from images based on graph construction rules (e.g., symmetry, proximity, rule of thirds) and integrates a graph attention mechanism to improve compositional awareness in node feature aggregation. Experiments were conducted on the constructed dataset of 1,200 annotated packaging images. The results demonstrate that CGA-GNN significantly outperforms existing baseline models in both prediction accuracy and consistency. Specifically, the model achieves a Weighted Root Mean Squared Error (WRMSE) of 0.378 ± 0.018, which is significantly lower than that of GraphSAGE-GAT (0.397 ± 0.021, p < 0.05), GAT (0.425 ± 0.022, p < 0.01), and CNN (0.446 ± 0.023, p < 0.001). Regarding Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, CGA-GNN attains a score of 0.714 ± 0.017, Markedly higher than other comparative models, with a Maximum improvement of 0.073 (p < 0.001). Additionally, its Graph Structural Integrity Rate (GSIR) reaches 0.921 ± 0.016, representing an approximately 15% increase compared to CNN (0.802 ± 0.020). Ablation studies further reveal that the model achieves optimal performance when all three compositional rules are incorporated (WRMSE = 0.378, Spearman’s ρ = 0.714, Kendall’s W = 0.691), validating the complementary effect of multi-rule integration. The findings confirm the effectiveness of deep integration between composition rules and graph neural networks in assessing the aesthetic quality of packaging images, providing technical support for standardized design evaluation, personalized recommendation, and creative assistance.Keywords: Visual communication design, Aesthetic quality assessment, Graph neural network, Composition featuresSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, as visual communication design has gained increasing prominence in commercial communication and user experience, image aesthetic quality assessment has gradually emerged as a critical direction for design intelligence research28. As a quintessential medium in visual communication, packaging design’s compositional layout not only affects the clarity of product information dissemination but also directly influences consumers’ aesthetic cognition and purchasing tendencies1,13. Unlike conventional image recognition or classification tasks, aesthetic quality assessment exhibits strong subjectivity and structural sensitivity. In packaging images characterized by intricate compositional structures and diverse visual elements, traditional manual scoring methods have struggled to meet the demand for efficient and standardized evaluation10. Consequently, there is an urgent need for an intelligent scoring approach capable of simulating human visual perception mechanisms and achieving in-depth interpretation of compositional semantics to enhance the objectivity and automation level of design evaluation.

Graph neural networks (GNN) are increasingly being applied to image aesthetic quality assessment, particularly in visual communication scenarios requiring spatial relationship modeling between elements. Research focus has shifted from holistic image perception to advanced structural modeling of compositions, driving the evolution of aesthetic modeling from “image-level judgment” to “composition-level parsing.” Qian et al.15 noted that in advertising and packaging images, reliance solely on global convolutional features fails to capture complex visual hierarchies, whereas composition-driven structural modeling enhances semantic perception. Sun & Li20 further emphasized that structural dependencies among visual elements are critical for aesthetic judgments, arguing that graph-based structural modeling not only improves scoring consistency but also enhances interpretability. Building on this, Nguyen et al. (2022) applied GAT and similar models to image layout optimization, demonstrating their efficacy in improving structural harmony in synthetic images. However, existing studies predominantly focus on style classification or singular composition patterns, remaining insufficiently adaptive to packaging images characterized by compositional complexity, element density, and commercial semantics. These methods often lack mechanisms integrating explicit design rules to address challenges such as compositional balance, visual guidance, and redundancy interference.To address these gaps, this study proposes a packaging aesthetic evaluation method that integrates compositional rules with graph neural networks. The method abstracts key visual elements in images as graph nodes, constructs spatial connections based on composition principles (e.g., symmetry, proximity), and incorporates a graph attention mechanism for feature aggregation. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CGA-GNN model significantly outperforms existing models in overall performance, with case analyses further confirming its efficacy in perceiving composition elements such as visual saliency and text-image balance. Methodologically, this work explores the deep integration of compositional rules with graph neural networks, while in application, it responds to the practical demand for explainable aesthetic evaluation systems in the era of design intelligence. By emphasizing structural aesthetics and visual organization principles within design contexts, this research provides theoretical foundations and practical pathways for achieving refined aesthetic modeling of complex images in visual communication design, exhibiting strong generalizability and scalability.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, at the methodological level, compositional principles from design theory—such as symmetry, proximity, and alignment—are formalized and transformed into structural constraints within graph representations, thereby overcoming the limitations of traditional aesthetic evaluation approaches that rely solely on handcrafted features or convolutional neural networks. Second, in terms of model architecture, this study proposes the Composition-Guided Graph Attention Neural Network (CGA-GNN), which integrates the representational power of graph neural networks with compositional features in visual communication design, enabling cross-level modeling from local visual elements to overall aesthetic quality. Third, with respect to empirical validation, extensive experiments conducted on a self-constructed dataset of packaging design images demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing baseline models in both prediction accuracy and interpretability, confirming its effectiveness and practical value for aesthetic evaluation tasks.

The structure of this paper is organized as follows: The Introduction clarifies the research background, challenges, and significance; the Literature Review summarizes advancements in the application of deep learning to aesthetic quality assessment in packaging design and identifying limitations in existing studies; the Methodology section introduces the research design, encompassing dataset construction, the framework of the core CGA-GNN model, and evaluation metrics; the Empirical Results section validates the model’s advantages in scoring accuracy and structural perception; the Discussion contrasts findings with prior studies to highlight contributions; and the Conclusion summarizes key findings and proposing future research directions.

Literature review

Theoretical foundations of aesthetics

Human aesthetic judgment has long been a central concern in both psychology and art theory, involving multilayered interactions among perception, cognition, and emotion. Early psychological research suggested that aesthetic experience is closely associated with features such as complexity, symmetry, and novelty (Berlyne, 1971). Building on this, the aesthetic information-processing model proposed by Leder et al. (2004) revealed the multi-stage nature of aesthetic evaluation, encompassing perceptual analysis, meaning construction, and emotional response. These theories provide a cognitive foundation for understanding individuals’ aesthetic mechanisms when engaging with design works and offer referential guidance for the development of computational aesthetic models. With the rise of neuroaesthetics, studies have shown that the brain activates regions associated with reward and pleasure when processing proportionality, balance, and composition (Torlak, 2023), further reinforcing the theoretical basis for modeling aesthetic judgment through structural features.

In the field of artificial intelligence, the emergence of explainable aesthetic AI (XAI) addresses the limitations of “black-box” models in design applications. While traditional deep learning methods can achieve high predictive accuracy, they often lack interpretability regarding the origins of their predictions. To remedy this, researchers have introduced XAI techniques such as Grad-CAM and SHAP into aesthetic modeling, enabling visualizations that highlight regions of interest within the model’s attention, thereby validating the alignment between AI predictions and human visual focus (Liu et al., 2023; Arnaud et al., 2024). This interpretability not only enhances model transparency but also endows it with design-assistive functionality, helping designers understand what and why specific elements are perceived as aesthetically pleasing, rather than merely providing a score. Thus, explainable AI has become a pivotal direction in the development of aesthetic evaluation systems, offering critical support for expanding their value in creative industries.

From the perspective of design theory, classical layout principles provide systematic rules for visual aesthetics. In two-dimensional composition, symmetry, the golden ratio, and grid systems are widely acknowledged to enhance a design’s sense of balance and structural coherence. Gestalt principles such as proximity, similarity, and closure reveal humans’ innate preferences for visual organization6; Lucia et al., 2024). These theories not only form the foundational guidelines for layout design but also offer quantifiable priors for computational aesthetic modeling. The golden ratio can be mathematically encoded as spatial proportions, while symmetry and balance can be expressed as node relationships and edge weights—offering theoretical support for graph-based modeling methods such as graph neural networks (Rizzo, 2023). Collectively, the psychological and cognitive mechanisms of aesthetic judgment, the interpretability of AI models, and classical layout theories constitute the theoretical foundation of this study. Together, they uncover the underlying cognitive roots of aesthetic perception and provide operable modeling strategies for intelligent aesthetic evaluation.

Methods for aesthetic quality assessment

The perception and judgment of aesthetic quality, as a critical dimension in visual communication design research, encompass multi-level elements such as compositional structures, color schemes, and visual rhythm, which have long been scrutinized through dual lenses of artistic theory and psychological cognition5. In the current era where digital images dominate as primary communication mediums, packaging imagery not only conveys product information but also transmits brand philosophies and cultural values through visual language, with its aesthetic quality directly influencing consumers’ emotional resonance and purchase intent16. The rise of deep learning has provided a breakthrough pathway for objective and automated assessment of packaging image aesthetics. By leveraging their robust capacity for automatic feature extraction and complex visual pattern recognition, deep learning models have enhanced the precision of aesthetic feature identification and expanded the adaptability of evaluation systems to stylistic diversity and perceptual preferences26. Researchers continue to advance visual aesthetic modeling from “classification and recognition” to “aesthetic comprehension” by embedding aesthetic principles into network architectures or integrating emotional dimensions with compositional logic.

With the widespread application of deep neural networks in image recognition, their automatic feature extraction capabilities have introduced novel pathways for aesthetic classification and clustering of packaging imagery. In packaging design contexts, images often exhibit diverse styles, intricate color schemes, and complex compositional structures, all of which demand higher accuracy in classification and consistency in clustering. Ma11 integrated GoogLeNet with Kansei-inspired data to achieve refined clustering of traditional and modern styles, enabling quantified classification across emotional dimensions, with the method attaining over 90% accuracy in image consistency scoring. Shi et al.17 constructed a packaging visual image database and utilized neural networks to cluster successful brand packaging, accurately capturing the relationship between user preferences and stylistic features, thereby providing algorithmic support for aligning “visual style-brand positioning”. Building on this, Wang21 proposed using generative adversarial networks to cluster hand-drawn packaging illustrations, effectively mapping the distribution of artistic styles in image spaces to support cultural and creative packaging style recommendation systems. Hu6 combined HSV color space with texture features to automate clustering and generation of agricultural product packaging images via deep learning models, significantly enhancing packaging diversity and visual style adaptability. Furthermore, Zheng27 designed a neural network architecture optimized with VONN and Osprey algorithms for industrial packaging aesthetics clustering, demonstrating superior performance in accuracy, precision, and specificity over traditional classification networks, particularly for shape-dominated packaging aesthetics.

Packaging design evaluation extends beyond image quality recognition to deeply involve consumers’ emotional cognition and subjective preferences. Consequently, integrating Kansei Engineering with CNNs has emerged as a critical strategy to enhance human-machine consistency in aesthetic assessment. Shu et al.19 fused CNNs with the NIMA model to evaluate “visual symbol communication efficiency” in packaging images, developing a scoring mechanism reflective of users’ first impressions, with the model outputting both score distributions and focal area analyses. Wen23 applied this framework to modern book packaging design, incorporating emotional factor analysis into color and typographic structures, further validating deep models’ capability in perceptual dimension recognition. From a philosophical aesthetic perspective, Wei (2022) argued that perceptual evaluation encompasses not only composition but also visual rhythm, symbolic expression, and information density, subsequently developing a neural scoring system for multi-dimensional assessment. Hu & Yan7 devised an evaluation framework incorporating visual perception metrics such as “proportion”, “negative space”, and “balance” based on Kansei principles, using deep learning to quantify these metrics and integrate emotional factors into scoring systems. Additionally, the Calista system by Delitzas et al.4 incorporated comparative user evaluation data during model training, aligning deep scoring systems more closely with perceptual outcomes. This mechanism can be adapted for packaging images to construct consumer emotion-driven scoring models.

For packaging images with complex layouts, aesthetic quality is co-determined by structural, textual, and graphic elements. Traditional deep learning models often operate as “black boxes” when processing such data, suffering from inadequate interpretability. Recent studies thus emphasize integrating explainable visualization mechanisms and layout structure modeling to enhance transparency and evaluation validity. Li et al.9 proposed an aesthetic scoring network for typographic optimization by jointly modeling visual saliency and layout structures, providing a deep-learning-assisted tool for refining typographic aesthetics in multi-element packaging. De Souza Lima et al. 3 validated a CNN interpretability framework in interface design, demonstrating that scoring heatmaps highly align with user attention patterns, confirming the transferability of explainable mechanisms to visual aesthetics. Pu et al.14 argued that “layout structure” should be prioritized as a core variable in aesthetic evaluation. They systematically analyzed deep learning’s capability in processing image-text hybrid packaging and advocated for explicit rule-based layout models as auxiliary inputs. Ma et al.12 embedded saliency analysis and visualization modules into packaging design models, enabling score traceability to deliver actionable feedback for designers. Advancing this, Shi et al.18 devised a dual-layer text-guided model that fuses semantic descriptions (“overall theme + detailed composition”) with deep image features, enhancing interpretability across high-order dimensions like compositional harmony, information density, and emotional tonality.

Different models used in aesthetic quality assessment exhibit distinct strengths and limitations. Existing studies have shown that convolutional neural networks (CNNs) excel in extracting texture, color, and global features, enabling effective recognition of visual styles in packaging images. However, CNNs tend to lack sensitivity to complex compositional relationships19. Transformer-based models, which rely on self-attention mechanisms to model global dependencies, demonstrate strong capabilities in capturing long-range semantics and cross-regional interactions. Nevertheless, their absence of explicit design rule embedding often results in limited interpretability8. Traditional aesthetic metrics, which are typically constructed based on explicit rules such as symmetry, balance, and color harmony, offer intuitive understanding and high interpretability, yet they suffer from poor generalizability when applied to multi-element and complex compositions22. In contrast, graph neural networks (GNNs) equipped with attention mechanisms—such as Graph Attention Networks (GAT)—possess unique advantages in modeling spatial dependencies among visual elements. These models can highlight key nodes and suppress less relevant information, thus enabling more accurate semantic representation of composition (Zhang et al., 2023). As a result, GAT partially compensates for the structural perception deficiencies of CNNs and the adaptability limitations of rule-based models, while also surpassing Transformer architectures in interpretability by avoiding complete reliance on black-box mechanisms. This makes GAT a technically suitable pathway for composition-driven aesthetic evaluation tasks.

These studies establish theoretical foundations for packaging aesthetic evaluation: they define a multi-layered visual feature modeling paradigm centered on images, bridging perceptual cognition with structural expression, while affirming the criticality of compositional logic in aesthetic judgment, thereby guiding algorithm design with aesthetic principles. However, existing works predominantly focus on shallow integrations of holistic image features or emotional dimensions, lacking mechanisms for deep semantic modeling of compositional structures and exhibiting limited capability in expressing visual relationships within multi-element layouts. To address these gaps, this paper proposes a Composition-guided Graph Attention Neural Network (CGA-GNN). By representing visual elements as nodes and compositional rules as edges, we construct graph structures integrated with graph attention mechanisms to achieve structured modeling and scoring prediction of layout aesthetics. This approach optimizes the balance between aesthetic cognition consistency and evaluation precision, advancing the refinement of intelligent assessment methods for visual communication design.

Research design

Dataset and preprocessing

This study constructs a dataset for aesthetic quality assessment in packaging design, comprising 1,200 packaging images collected from publicly accessible design platforms such as Behance and Pinterest, as well as award-winning entries from renowned packaging design competitions. The dataset covers multiple product categories, including food, beverages, and daily chemical products. All images were obtained in compliance with their respective copyright licensing terms. Specifically, images sourced from open platforms were used under Creative Commons (CC) licenses, while images from commercial design databases were utilized with institutional authorization granted for academic research purposes. To ensure reproducibility and compliance, the research team performed anonymization and watermark removal on the original data. The complete dataset and access instructions have been archived on an open data repository (Zenodo/figshare, link to be provided), allowing academic researchers to obtain and use the dataset under appropriate licensing conditions.

Compared to existing general-purpose aesthetic datasets such as AVA and AADB, this study opts to construct a custom dataset primarily due to the contextual mismatch between those datasets and the characteristics of packaging design. Specifically, AVA and AADB are largely focused on photographic and artistic images, lacking key compositional elements commonly found in packaging—such as brand logos, typographic structures, intentional white space, and image-text interactions. Moreover, their labels are predominantly derived from crowdsourced aesthetic voting, which fails to capture composition quality judgments within a professional design context. To address these limitations, this study invited annotators with academic and practical backgrounds in visual communication design to perform aesthetic evaluations using a five-point Likert scale. The average score across raters was used as the final label to ensure high consistency and domain relevance. The Manual scoring was based on aesthetic dimensions widely adopted in visual communication design, including proportion and symmetry, text-image balance and white space, visual emphasis and information clarity, as well as color harmony and overall visual appeal. Under the five-level Likert scheme, a score of 1 indicates evident compositional flaws and poor aesthetic quality, whereas a score of 5 reflects highly balanced composition, coherent visual organization, and exceptional aesthetic performance.

During preprocessing, image size normalization and standardization were first applied: all images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels and normalized to eliminate resolution and illumination variations. Subsequently, key visual elements—including text, graphics, color blocks, and brand logos—were extracted using image segmentation and visual saliency detection techniques. Specifically, the U²-Net saliency detection model was employed to identify the most attention-attracting salient regions within each image. These regions were then processed using OpenCV to obtain their bounding boxes and spatial coordinates, providing standardized inputs for downstream composition modeling tasks. The dataset was split into training, validation, and test sets in a 7:1.5:1.5 ratio, ensuring standardized inputs for graph neural network (GNN) model training and evaluation.

Composition-Driven graph construction

To address the complexity of layout structures and diverse inter-element relationships in packaging images, key visual elements (e.g., text blocks, graphic regions, background boundaries) are first extracted. A graph G= (V, E) is constructed, where the node set V={v1,v2,…,vn} represents visual regions, and edges E ⊆V×V are established based on the following composition rules:

Symmetry

High-weight edges are added between nodes if their corresponding elements are approximately symmetric about the image’s geometric central axis;

Rule of thirds and Nine-Grid composition

Nodes intersecting with thirds lines or nine-grid intersections are assigned a “priority position” label;

Proximity & enclosure

Edges are reinforced between nodes if their elements exhibit significant boundary overlaps or inclusion relationships.

During the graph structure construction process, the importance of visual elements is determined by the degree to which they conform to established composition principles. Visual regions that are positioned symmetrically along the geometric axis, located at rule-of-thirds or nine-grid intersections, or exhibit strong proximity and enclosure relationships with other elements are assigned higher edge weights. This emphasizes their compositional dominance within the overall graph representation. Prior research has shown that such compositional principles have long been recognized as critical factors influencing aesthetic judgment in both visual arts and cognitive psychology (Zhang et al., 2022; Xin et al., 2022). Therefore, incorporating these rules into graph structures enhances the model’s interpretability and robustness at both theoretical and methodological levels.

Research results and analysis

Experimental platform

All model training and evaluation experiments were conducted in a high-performance deep learning computing environment. The hardware configuration included an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24GB VRAM), an Intel Core i9-12900 K processor, and 64GB RAM, ensuring efficient and stable processing of large-scale graph data as well as parallel training. The software environment utilized the Ubuntu 20.04 LTS operating system, the PyTorch 1.13.1 deep learning framework, and graph neural network implementations based on PyTorch Geometric 2.2.0.

During training, the Adam optimizer was adopted with an initial learning rate of 0.001, and a StepLR scheduling strategy was applied for periodic learning rate decay. To mitigate overfitting, a Dropout mechanism (rate = 0.5) and an early stopping strategy (halting training when validation performance plateaued) were incorporated. The graph neural network was trained for a Maximum of 100 epochs, with model parameters updated on the training set and performance metrics (WRMSE and Spearman correlation coefficient) monitored on the validation set to ensure optimal model selection.

Overall performance comparison

Figure 2 presents the performance comparison of CGA-GNN with three representative graph neural network models and a CNN baseline model in the packaging design aesthetic score prediction task. The baseline GAT (Graph Attention Network) introduces an attention mechanism between nodes to model adjacency weight differences, offering certain flexibility in local aggregation. The GraphSAGE-GAT combines a sampling-aggregation strategy with attention mechanisms, enhancing its ability to encode local contexts, making it suitable for handling large-scale heterogeneous graph structures. In contrast, GIN (Graph Isomorphism Network) emphasizes structural discriminability, focusing on strengthening isomorphic recognition of graph topologies, which is ideal for fine-grained node feature extraction scenarios.The CNN baseline effectively captures global texture and color features. To ensure the statistical reliability of model performance comparisons, the study employed one-way ANOVA on multiple randomly partitioned test sets to examine overall differences in key metrics, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test to analyze pairwise differences among models.

From an overall perspective, CGA-GNN exhibits significant advantages across multiple core metrics, particularly in prediction accuracy and structural modeling capability. Its Weighted Root Mean Squared Error (WRMSE) is 0.378 ± 0.018, significantly lower than that of GraphSAGE-GAT (0.397 ± 0.021, p < 0.05), GAT (0.425 ± 0.022, p < 0.01), and CNN (0.446 ± 0.023, p < 0.001), indicating that CGA-GNN generates aesthetic predictions closer to human expert evaluations due to its enhanced numerical fitting capability. This superiority stems from its explicit incorporation of visual composition principles—such as symmetry and the rule of thirds—during graph construction, enabling the model to capture spatially aesthetic-oriented relationships at the initial feature extraction stage.

In terms of Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, which reflects the consistency of scoring order, CGA-GNN achieves the highest value (0.714 ± 0.017), significantly surpassing GAT (0.669 ± 0.020, p < 0.01), GraphSAGE-GAT (0.695 ± 0.019, p < 0.05), GIN (0.658 ± 0.018, p < 0.01), and CNN (0.641 ± 0.021, p < 0.001). This indicates that the model not only demonstrates superior accuracy in numerical prediction but also maintains closer alignment with human evaluative logic in terms of cognitive consistency. Furthermore, CGA-GNN also leads in Kendall’s concordance coefficient (0.691 ± 0.016), with differences from the comparative models all reaching statistical significance (p < 0.05).

In structural modeling capability, CGA-GNN achieves the lowest Graph Representation Discrepancy (GRD) at 0.129 ± 0.012, significantly outperforming GIN (0.151 ± 0.013, p < 0.05) and CNN (0.189 ± 0.015, p < 0.001), demonstrating minimal node feature distortion after multi-layer graph convolution and stronger preservation of structural semantics. At the same time, it achieves the highest Graph Structural Integrity Rate (GSIR) of 0.921 ± 0.016, far exceeding GIN (0.872 ± 0.018, p < 0.01) and CNN (0.802 ± 0.020, p < 0.001), indicating that the model retains more critical visual connectivity relationships during graph construction and possesses stronger expressive capacity for compositional semantics Fig 3.

Ablation study

To further examine the independent contributions and combined effects of different visual elements, an ablation study was conducted to compare the individual and joint use of three composition rules: symmetry, proximity and enclosure, and the rule of thirds. Results show that when used individually, each rule contributes to a certain degree of performance improvement, with WRMSE values ranging from 0.414 to 0.423 and Spearman’s ρ ranging from 0.666 to 0.676. Among them, the rule of thirds achieved the best performance (WRMSE = 0.414 ± 0.018, Spearman’s ρ = 0.676 ± 0.018), indicating that grid-based compositional priors provide more stable guidance for aesthetic prediction. Although single rules yield performance gains, their overall effectiveness remains limited, suggesting that single compositional features are insufficient to comprehensively represent aesthetic information.

Under dual-rule combinations, model performance generally surpasses that of single-rule settings. The combination of the rule of thirds with proximity and enclosure reduces WRMSE to 0.396, raises Spearman’s ρ to 0.693, and improves Kendall’s W to 0.669. Likewise, the combinations of symmetry + rule of thirds and symmetry + proximity and enclosure demonstrate notable improvements in both error reduction and rank consistency. These results indicate that the compositional rules are not redundant but complementary, reinforcing spatial structure modeling and enhancing robustness.

When all three rules are simultaneously introduced to construct the complete CGA-GNN model, all metrics achieve their best performance: WRMSE decreases to 0.378 ± 0.018, Spearman’s ρ increases to 0.714 ± 0.017, and GSIR rises to 0.921 ± 0.016. Compared to single-rule and dual-rule models, the differences are statistically significant (p < 0.05), further confirming that the comprehensive integration of multiple compositional principles substantially enhances both structural preservation capability and aesthetic prediction accuracy Table 1.

Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis

To further validate the rationality of hyperparameter settings in edge weight calculations, this study tested four typical combinations under identical experimental conditions: equal weighting (0.33, 0.33, 0.33), symmetry-weighted (0.50, 0.30, 0.20), spatial distance-weighted (0.20, 0.50, 0.30), and directional alignment-weighted (0.40, 0.20, 0.40). As shown in Table 2, all five core evaluation metrics remain stable across different combinations. Further one-way ANOVA results indicate that differences in all metrics across the combinations are not statistically significant (p > 0.05), demonstrating the model’s strong robustness to hyperparameter settings.

By combining these samples with the prediction outputs of the CGA-GNN model, the study examined its judgment consistency and responsiveness to visual features in typical cases. These results indicate that CGA-GNN not only demonstrates robust overall predictive performance but also enables precise perception and fine-grained judgments on specific compositional patterns, offering an explainable modeling pathway for composition-oriented intelligent aesthetic evaluation in packaging design.

Discussion

This study introduces a composition feature-guided graph neural network (CGA-GNN) to model spatial relationships among visual elements in packaging images and predict aesthetic quality. By constructing graph structures guided by compositional principles—where visual elements are mapped as nodes and aggregated through attention mechanisms—the model achieves superior performance in scoring prediction tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that CGA-GNN outperforms existing graph neural network models in scoring accuracy, consistency, and structural preservation capability, establishing an interpretable structural mapping pathway between compositional perception and aesthetic judgment. This advancement significantly enhances the model’s ability to perform composition-driven aesthetic evaluations. The study not only validates the guiding role of compositional rules in graph modeling but also extends the application boundaries of graph neural networks to intelligent evaluation in visual communication design.

In comparisons with existing research, the proposed CGA-GNN extends prior studies in methodological logic and theoretical frameworks while achieving innovations in model architecture and functionality. Yan et al.24 utilized CNNs and transfer learning for image aesthetic prediction, emphasizing deep semantic features but neglecting spatial compositional relationships between visual elements. Addressing this limitation, our approach incorporates compositional rules to build graph structures, equipping the model with enhanced spatial structural perception and bridging the critical gap of conventional methods in modeling visual organization. Jia et al.8 employed Transformers to implicitly capture regional relationships, aligning with our focus on structural interactions among visual elements. However, their method lacks explicit compositional priors, whereas our framework integrates rule-based constructs such as symmetry and the rule of thirds during graph formation, improving interpretability and aesthetic guidance. Chu et al.2 focused on graph clustering and style classification, leveraging graph structures for inter-image style analysis but failing to explore intra-image compositional semantics. In contrast, our model operates on intrinsic compositional logic within images, enabling fine-grained quantitative assessment of layout quality.

The primary contributions of this study lie in expanding the research paradigm of visual aesthetic evaluation in AI, offering novel structural modeling insights for design intelligence. This work fosters interdisciplinary integration between computational aesthetics and graph neural networks, emphasizing the theoretical value of structural perception in aesthetic modeling. It challenges conventional evaluation approaches centered on pixel-level or textural analysis, steering design assessment methodologies from perception-driven to composition-driven frameworks. Furthermore, the research provides methodological support for implementing AI technologies in creative industries, establishing theoretical and technical foundations for generalizable and controllable intelligent aesthetic evaluation systems in consumer-centric image design. These advancements hold significant reference value for both researchers and industry practitioners in related domains.

However, this study has certain limitations. First, the evaluation framework relies predominantly on expert ratings as ground truth, which inadequately incorporates subjective aesthetic variations across user groups, thereby struggling to comprehensively reflect the intricate impacts of cultural diversity and individual preferences on aesthetic judgment. Second, the model’s rule design and parameter configuration retain a degree of prior dependency, limiting self-adaptive generalization. This may result in adaptability constraints when handling novel design domains or complex compositional patterns. Moreover, the experiments were conducted exclusively on the packaging design dataset, without validation on other visual communication tasks such as advertisements, book covers, and interface designs. As a result, the model’s cross-domain adaptability and the external validity of its conclusions remain limited. Finally, the model employs unified expert average scores as labels for aesthetic quality prediction, which fails to capture differences in user groups with respect to age, cultural background, and aesthetic preferences. This limitation highlights the insufficiency of the current framework in modeling individualized aesthetic judgments.

Conclusions

This study addresses the aesthetic quality evaluation of packaging design images by proposing a Composition Feature-Guided Graph Neural Network (CGA-GNN), validating its effectiveness through the following key outcomes:

First, in terms of scoring prediction performance, the CGA-GNN achieves the lowest Weighted Root Mean Squared Error (WRMSE) of 0.378 ± 0.018 and the highest Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient of 0.714 ± 0.017, both significantly outperforming comparative models such as GAT, GraphSAGE-GAT, and GIN (p < 0.05). The corresponding t-test results further confirm that the performance differences across multiple core metrics are statistically significant (WRMSE: t = 3.41, p < 0.01; Spearman’s ρ: t = 3.68, p < 0.01). In terms of graph structural modeling capability, the CGA-GNN also demonstrates optimal performance on Graph Representation Discrepancy (0.129 ± 0.012) and Graph Structural Integrity Rate (0.921 ± 0.016), highlighting its structural preservation advantage under the guidance of composition rules.

Second, model performance declines to varying degrees when compositional rules are removed or edge weight configurations are altered, indicating the significant contribution of composition rules to graph structure construction. The joint use of the three compositional rules yields the best results (WRMSE = 0.378 ± 0.018, ρ = 0.714 ± 0.017), outperforming any single-rule or dual-rule combinations (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the model exhibits high stability under different hyperparameter configurations, demonstrating strong robustness and generalization capability.

Third, in case analyses of representative samples, CGA-GNN accurately identifies key compositional elements such as symmetry, white space, and visual balance. The model’s predicted scores align closely with Human evaluations, notably achieving a predicted score of 4.72 for high-quality samples compared to expert ratings of 4.75, further confirming its stability and interpretability in fine-grained composition modeling and aesthetic judgment.

The study reveals the critical role of compositional features in visual aesthetic perception and their embedding potential in intelligent scoring models. Future research could expand in the following directions. First, incorporating user behavior data and individual rating information could enable the construction of diversified aesthetic preference modeling mechanisms, enhancing the model’s adaptability to different cultural backgrounds and group characteristics, thereby facilitating a shift from expert-dominated to user perception-driven aesthetic evaluation. Second, exploring adaptive optimization methods for composition rules based on meta-learning or neural architecture search could reduce the model’s reliance on prior knowledge and improve its generalization performance in non-standard composition scenarios. Furthermore, future research could expand the dataset to include diverse image types such as advertisements, book covers, and interface designs, training and independently testing the model on these datasets and comparing the results with those obtained in packaging design scenarios. This approach would enable a systematic evaluation of the model’s scoring accuracy, consistency, and compositional perception across different visual contexts, thereby verifying its cross-domain adaptability and external validity. Finally, future studies will incorporate datasets containing user attributes and individual ratings to explore modeling methods that integrate user profiling, aesthetic preference features, and deep learning models. Such an approach aims to construct a personalized aesthetic evaluation system and to validate the model’s adaptability and effectiveness in capturing aesthetic variations across different user groups.

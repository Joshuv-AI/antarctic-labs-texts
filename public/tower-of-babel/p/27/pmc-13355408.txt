Entosis remodels the immune microenvironment of osteosarcoma by regulating macrophage polarization: An integrated study based on multi-omics data and in vitro experiments
Authors: Guoling Huang, Yongbo Xiao, Hongliang Zhang, Yu Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•High Entosis activity predicts favorable prognosis in osteosarcoma.•Entosis remodels immune microenvironment by regulating macrophage polarization.•The Macro_CXCL2 subtype is the core target of Entosis regulation.•A six-gene Entosis-related model effectively predicts osteosarcoma survival.

Introduction

Osteosarcoma (OS) is a highly aggressive malignant bone tumor that mainly affects children under nine years of age, with an incidence rate of 2.3 cases per million population over the past decade [1]. This disease is common in long bones, especially around the knee joint, and manifests as severe pain, bone swelling, and in some cases pathological fractures [2]. About 25% of patients have metastatic lesions when diagnosed, with the lungs being the most common site of metastasis [3]. Although the comprehensive treatment plan of surgery combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy has significantly improved the local control rate of patients in recent years, distant metastasis and treatment resistance are still the main reasons for the poor prognosis of patients. The 5-year survival rate is only 60%-70%, and the survival rate of patients with advanced stage or recurrence is less than 20% [4]. Therefore, in-depth analysis of the molecular mechanisms of cancer development and the discovery of biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets with prognostic predictive value are crucial to optimizing precise diagnosis and treatment strategies for cancer.

Cell death is a basic physiological process that regulates various physiological phenomena such as growth, development, aging and disease. Unlike accidental cell death (ACD), correlated cell death (RCD)—also known as programmed cell death (PCD)—can be triggered by specific signaling mechanisms and/or metabolic reprogramming [5,6]. Programmed cell death (PCD), also known as regulated cell death, plays a key role in maintaining tissue homeostasis and clearing damaged or abnormal cells (9). PCD includes ferroptosis, apoptosis, pyroptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, copper death, protoplast death, endocytic death, reticular death, lysosome-dependent death, alkaline death, oxidative death, reticular death, immunogenic death, extracellular matrix stripping-induced death, parapoptosis, methylation death, endocytic death, MPT-driven necrosis and disulfide bond death [[7], [8], [9], [10]]. Entosis is a cell phagocytosis process that relies on the actin cytoskeleton, in which a cell is engulfed by another adjacent cell and then dies within the phagocyte. Its role in tumor microenvironment remodeling, immune regulation, and tumor cell survival has gradually attracted attention [11]. Studies have shown that Entosis is abnormally expressed in various tumors such as liver cancer and breast cancer, and is related to patient prognosis and tumor malignancy [12]. However, the expression characteristics, regulatory mechanism and interaction between Entosis and the tumor microenvironment in osteosarcoma have not yet been systematically reported.

Based on this, this study integrated batch RNA sequencing and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data to analyze the regulatory mechanism of Entosis on the osteosarcoma tumor microenvironment and malignant cell biological behavior, and constructed a prognostic prediction model based on Entosis-related genes, providing new theoretical basis and potential targets for prognostic assessment and individualized treatment of osteosarcoma patients.

Methods

Data collection

Download the standardized and log2-transformed TPM expression profiles and clinical information for the TARGET-OS cohort from the UCSC Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). We obtained the batch RNA sequencing dataset GSE21257, as well as scRNA-seq datasets GSE162454 and GSE152048, from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE162454 and GSE152048 were used for single-cell transcriptome analysis, while GSE21257 served as the external validation set for the risk model.

Genetset score

We collected Entosis-related genes [12], immune function gene sets [13], and 17 cell apoptosis gene sets [14,15] from existing literature. Subsequently, we employed the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm within the R package “GSVA” to calculate the enrichment scores for each specific gene set across all samples in the TARGET-OS cohort, based on its gene expression data.

Survival analysis

The Kaplan-Meier curve is an effective tool for survival analysis, capable of describing how patient survival rates change over time. In biomedical research, particularly in disease prognosis analysis, Kaplan-Meier curves are widely applied. Tumor samples are stratified into high- and low-risk groups using the median value of a specific characteristic or indicator as the threshold. Survival curves for prognosis analysis are generated via the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test is employed to determine the significance of differences. A p-value < 0.05 indicates a statistically significant survival difference between the two groups.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis of the TARGET-OS dataset was performed using the “limma” package in R software. Significant differentially expressed genes were identified based on the criteria |log2FC| > 0.25 and adjusted_pvalue < 0.05. Expression patterns of these genes were visualized as volcano plots and heatmaps using the “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” packages in R, respectively. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) enrichment analysis was performed on overlapping differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using the ClusterProfiler package. Analysis parameters were set with minGSSize = 3 and pvalueCutoff = 0.1. The top 10 most significant gene sets from the enrichment results were selected and visualized using line charts.

ScRNA-seq data processing and Entosis score calculation

ScRNA-seq data preprocessing was performed using the “Seurat” [16,17] package in R software. The “PercentageFeatureSet” function was employed to assess the proportion of mitochondrial genes in the dataset. To ensure data quality and integrity, only genes expressed in at least three cells were retained; Additionally, each cell had to meet the criteria of expressing between 200 and 10,000 genes, with mitochondrial genes constituting less than 20% of the total. Following preprocessing, the scRNA-seq data were normalized using the “NormalizeData” function. The normalized data were converted into a Seurat object, and 2,000 highly variable genes were selected via the “FindVariableFeatures” function. Subsequently, the “RunPCA” tool performed scaling and principal component analysis on this set of highly variable genes. Dimension reduction and visualization in a two-dimensional coordinate space were achieved using the Shared Nearest Neighbor (SNN) modular optimization algorithm and the t-distributed Stochastic Neighborhood Embedding (t-SNE) clustering algorithm. To identify specific marker genes for each cell cluster, we performed Wilcoxon tests on different cell types using the “FindAllMarkers” and ‘FindMarkers’ algorithms, followed by annotation of various cell subpopulations based on these marker genes. For evaluating entosis scores in osteosarcoma single-cell sequencing data, we employed three widely used algorithms: “AUCell,” “singscore,” and “UCell.” Specifically, “AUCell” is an R-language tool for evaluating the enrichment level of specific gene sets within individual sample gene expression data, aiding in the analysis of biological processes and disease mechanisms; the ‘UCell’ method is suitable for unsupervised cell type identification, enabling classification of specific cell types without relying on historical information or labels; “singscore” is used to quantify the activity level of specific biological functions or processes within individual samples or cells, serving as an effective means for assessing cellular states.

Pseudotime analysis

An analysis of pseudotime, also known as cell trajectory analysis, helps predict the evolutionary trajectory of apoptosis pathways and cell subtypes and infer the differentiation trajectory of stemcells during disease progression. By analyzing key gene expression patterns using Monocle 2, we performed pseudotime analysis in the current study. The pseudotime value was used by Monocle to model the gene expression level as a nonlinear smooth pseudotime function to show changes in gene expression with time. FDR <1e−5 was regarded as a significant difference.

Cell–cell interaction analysis

Based on the ligand–receptor information, we used the single-cell gene expression matrix to unravel the communication between immune cell subtypes which was contained in CellChat software (http://www.cellchat.org/) with default parameters, modeling the communication probability and identifying significant communications.

Copy number alteration inference

The CNV was evaluated by “infercnv” R package (version 1.12.0) to differentiate between malignant and non-malignant cells. The different genomic locations of CNVs were presented.

Construction of weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA is a powerful analytical technique capable of extracting significant insights from high-throughput gene expression data, thereby enabling the analysis of gene regulatory network structure and function [18]. Using the WGCNA package in R software, WGCNA analysis was performed on the top 25% most variable genes in tumor samples. Anomalous samples were detected and excluded using sample-level clustering to select an appropriate soft threshold power for achieving a standard scale-free network. The subsequent phase involved constructing adjacency and topological overlap matrices (TOM), calculating phase differences, and employing dynamic tree cutting to generate gene dendrograms and identify modules with a minimum size of 40. Highly similar modules were then merged through clustering of module signature genes, enabling the calculation of correlations between these signature genes and Entosis activity. Finally, the most highly correlated modules were selected for further analysis.

Construction of risk scoring models

Univariate Cox regression analysis using the “survival” package identified statistically significant prognostic genes (p < 0.05). Subsequently, Lasso and multivariate stepwise regression analysis [19] were employed to further select genes and risk factors highly correlated with prognosis. Based on coefficients derived from multivariate analysis, a risk score was assigned to each osteosarcoma patient. The risk score calculation formula is as follows:Riskscore=∑i=0nβi*χiWhere β_i represents the weight coefficient of each gene in the model; χ_i represents the expression level of the corresponding gene.

Additionally, Kaplan-Meier methods were employed to construct survival curves for predicting the prognosis of osteosarcoma patients, with log-rank tests used to determine the significance of differences. The predictive model's effectiveness was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves [20], where an area under the curve (AUC) value exceeding 0.65 was considered indicative of good model performance. Simultaneously, the R package “ggplot2” was utilized to generate scatter plots of survival time versus survival status.

Unsupervised clustering of model genes

Based on the model genes, we performed unsupervised clustering using the “ConsensusClusterPlus” software package to delineate molecular subtypes. The parameters “maxK” was set to “10”, “clusterAlg” was set to “km”, and ‘distance’ was set to “pearson”.

Cell culture

Mouse osteosarcoma cell lines (DUNN) and BMDM were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2. For suspension culture, cells were seeded in ultra-low attachment plates to induce Entosis. For inhibitor treatment, cells were pretreated with ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 (Beyotime, SC0326, China) for the indicated duration. Conditioned medium was collected after centrifugation and filtered prior to macrophage stimulation.

Fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS)

Single-cell suspensions were washed with FACS buffer. The cells were incubated with the indicated antibodies for 30 min at 4°C, followed by fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde. Flow cytometry data were acquired using a FACSCanto flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA) and analyzed with FlowJo software (Tree Star, USA).

mRNA purification and reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from cells via TRIzol (Sigma-Aldrich, T9424, USA). cDNA was synthesized using a Yeasen cDNA kit (Yeasen, 11119ES60, China), and qPCR was conducted on an ABI system with SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, 11184ES03, China). Expression levels were calculated by the 2-ΔΔCt method (normalized to GAPDH), with all samples tested in triplicate. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of CXCL12 in samples were quantified using commercially available ELISA kits (GOY-1904E, Goybio, China) according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Statistical analysis

R version 4.1.3 was used for all statistical studies. The survival curve was plotted by the Kaplan–Meier survival curve and the P-value was obtained by the log-rank test. The Student’s t-test and Wilcoxon test were used to compare the differences between the two groups, and Spearman analysis was used to calculate the correlation coefficients. Double-tailed P < 0.050 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Enrichment features of Entosis-related gene sets

We obtained 85 osteosarcoma samples with clinical information from the UCSC Xena database. Using the ssGSEA method, we performed single-sample gene set enrichment analysis on the Entosis gene set to obtain enrichment scores. Based on the median score, the cohort was divided into high-score and low-score groups, followed by Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis (Fig. 1A). Results revealed a significant difference in survival prognosis between the two groups (P < 0.05), with patients in the Entosis-high group demonstrating markedly superior survival outcomes compared to the Entosis-low group. Heatmap analysis indicated significant differences in 25 immune functions between the Entosis-high and Entosis-low groups, with significantly higher enrichment levels of immune function-related genes observed in the Entosis-high group (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, we assessed differences in enrichment scores for 17 cell death pathways between groups. Subsequently, the “limma” software package was used to screen for DEGs between the two groups (Fig. 1D), identifying a total of 632 significantly differentially expressed genes, including 611 upregulated genes and 21 downregulated genes. Finally, GSEA was performed on the highly expressed genes in the Entosis-high group to explore their associated biological processes and functional pathways. GSEA analysis of immune function-related gene sets showed enrichment in antigen processing and presentation, chemokine ontology, CSF1 response (mediating macrophage recruitment), and MHC-II molecule-related gene sets (Fig. 1E). GSEA analysis of HALLMARK gene sets revealed enrichment in allogeneic transplant rejection, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, inflammatory response, and KRAS signaling pathway activation-related gene sets (Fig. 1F); KEGG GSEA analysis showed enrichment in pathways related to antigen processing and presentation, asthma, hematopoietic cell lineage, IgA production in the intestinal immune network, leishmaniasis, Staphylococcus aureus infection, systemic lupus erythematosus, Th17 cell differentiation, tuberculosis, and viral myocarditis (Fig. 1G). Collectively, these findings suggest a highly active tumor immune microenvironment in Entosis-high osteosarcoma tissues, potentially conferring greater sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy or macrophage-targeted treatments (e.g., CSF1R inhibitors). This provides a potential biomarker for precise stratification and personalized treatment strategies in osteosarcoma patients.

Enrichment scores for Entosis-related genes in scRNA-seq

To reveal the dynamic changes in the tumor microenvironment (TME) during osteosarcoma development, we acquired two independent single-cell datasets from osteosarcoma samples. After rigorous quality control and data normalization, we obtained transcriptomic data from 95,598 cells. Following batch effect correction, UMAP dimensionality reduction and clustering visualized the cellular distribution patterns (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, standard cellular markers were used for annotation, identifying 12 major cell clusters: macrophages, endothelial cells, plasma cells, B cells, NK/T cells, fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, chondrocytes, monocytes, osteoclasts, osteoblasts, and mesenchymal stem cells (Fig. 2B). Bubble plot visualization successfully depicted the key marker gene expression profiles across cell types (Fig. 2C). Heatmaps further illustrated the expression distribution patterns of Entosis-related marker genes across cell subpopulations (Fig. 2D). Subsequently, based on Entosis-related gene expression data, we assessed Entosis activity levels in scRNA-seq data. Three widely used algorithms (UCell, AUCell, and singscore) were employed to score gene sets for Entosis activity (Fig. 2E). Results revealed significantly higher Entosis scores in macrophages and monocytes compared to other cell subpopulations. Cells were stratified into high- and low-Entosis-score groups using the median Entosis score across all cells as the cutoff. Bar chart analysis demonstrated a similarly elevated proportion of macrophages and monocytes in the high-score group (Fig. 2F). Further statistical analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between macrophages and high Entosis scores (Fig. 2G), indicating that macrophages are the cell type most closely associated with Entosis activity. Identification of malignant cells

This study selected mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), osteoclasts, and osteoblasts as research subjects, using NK/T cells as reference cells, and identified malignant cells through inferCNV analysis. Based on copy number variation (CNV) scores, all candidate cells were clustered into 10 cell clusters (Fig. 3 A); Violin plot results revealed that clusters 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, and 10 exhibited significantly higher CNV levels than other clusters (Fig. 3B). These seven clusters were thus defined as malignant cell clusters. To elucidate the biological characteristics of malignant cell subpopulations, CytoTRACE2 and Entosis scores were further evaluated. UMAP visualization revealed substantial overlap between high CytoTRACE2-scoring and high Entosis-scoring regions (Fig. 3C, D). Bar chart analysis (Fig. 3E) indicated significant differences in cellular differentiation states between high- and low-Entosis-scoring subpopulations, with the high-Entosis group exhibiting a higher proportion of pluripotent cells. Further statistical analysis demonstrated a significant difference in CytoTRACE2 scores between high- and low-Entosis groups (Fig. 3F). Correlation analysis confirmed a significant positive correlation between Entosis and CytoTRACE2 scores (Fig. 3G), suggesting Entosis levels may regulate the differentiation potential of malignant cells and providing crucial insights into the mechanisms underpinning malignant stemness maintenance. Based on these findings, differential expression analysis and GSEA were performed on malignant cell subpopulations with high and low Entosis scores. Results revealed that the hypoxia pathway, tumor necrosis factor α (TNFα)-mediated nuclear factor κB (NFκB) signaling pathway, and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway were significantly activated in the high Entosis score group. In contrast, the low Entosis score group was characterized by high activation of the oxidative phosphorylation pathway and ribosome pathway (Fig. 3H, I). To validate the reliability of these pathway differences, bubble plots were used to visualize expression pattern disparities of hypoxia- and MAPK-related genes between the two subpopulations (Fig. 3J, K). Identification and characterization of macrophages

The infiltration status and functional regulation of immune cells within the TME are critical factors influencing tumor progression. As a core immune cell type, the interaction between macrophages and Entosis, along with their biological functions, remain poorly understood. Therefore, this study further focuses on macrophages to explore their regulatory association with Entosis and functional characteristics. Based on Entosis scores, macrophages were classified into a high-Entosis-score subpopulation (EnhighMacro) and a low-Entosis-score subpopulation (EnlowMacro). Differential expression analysis and GSEA enrichment analysis were performed on both subpopulations. Results revealed that in GSEA analysis using HALLMARK gene sets, the EnhighMacro subpopulation showed significant enrichment in immune-related pathways including allograft rejection, inflammatory response, and interferon-gamma response (Fig. 4A). In analyses of immune function gene sets and KEGG gene sets, this subpopulation exhibited significant activation of chemokine/cytokine-related functions and inflammatory signaling pathways such as NF-κB and TNF (Fig. 4B, C), suggesting that the high Entosis score macrophage subpopulation may participate in TME remodeling by regulating inflammatory immune responses.Fig. 4Identification of macrophage subtypes (A) GSEA based on HALLMARK gene sets. To further explore the intrinsic relationship between macrophages and entosis, UMAP analysis subdivided macrophages into seven distinct subtypes: Macro_APOCA, Macro_LUM, Macro_ISG15, Macro_MT1H, Macro_MKI67, Macro_CXCL2, and Macro_DNAJB1 (Fig. 4D). Heatmaps displayed the top five most highly upregulated genes in each cell type (Fig. 4E). Calculating Entosis scores for macrophage subtypes revealed the highest score in the Macro_CXCL2 subtype, followed by Macro_ISG15 (Fig. 4F). Moreover, compared to the low Entosis score group, the high Entosis score group showed the strongest association with the CXCL2 subtype (Fig. 4G). This suggests that the Macro_CXCL2 subtype may represent a core functional subtype characterized by high Entosis, and its strong correlation with Entosis may play a key role in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment. Based on this, the study employed the “FindAllMarkers” tool to identify marker genes for the Macro_CXCL2 subtype and conducted GO enrichment analysis. Results showed significant enrichment at the BP level in biological processes such as response to bacterial components, leukocyte migration, and chemotaxis; at the CC level in cellular components including secretory granules, outer plasma membrane, and transcription complexes; and at the MF level, they were significantly enriched in molecular functions including cytokine/chemokine activity, transcriptional regulation activity, and receptor binding (Fig. 4H). These findings indicate that the Macro_CXCL2 subtype represents a key cellular population mediating local immune inflammatory responses, suggesting it may serve as a core cellular bridge linking entosis-associated cellular stress to the anti-tumor immune microenvironment.

To further elucidate the origin and developmental differentiation patterns of macrophages and clarify the influence of entosis status on their differentiation process, this study employed the Monocle 2 algorithm to conduct pseudo-time trajectory analysis. Macrophages were sequenced based on gene expression characteristics and transition patterns (Fig. 5A). Conversely, the low-Entosis-score subpopulation (EnlowMacro) showed higher proportions of cells in states 1, 2, and 4, corresponding to early differentiation stages (Fig. 5B). To validate this finding, we further quantified differences in the fitted time series of cell populations with varying Entosis scores across tissues. Violin plot analysis revealed significantly higher fitted time series values for cells in the high Entosis group compared to the low Entosis group (Fig. 5C). This further confirms that high Entosis activity drives macrophage differentiation toward later stages, providing direct evidence for Entosis's regulatory role in macrophage differentiation. Analysis of differentiation differences across seven macrophage subtypes (Fig. 5D) revealed through bar charts that Macro_CXCL2 subtype cells predominantly enriched states 11, 8, and 7 (Fig. 5E), indicating this subtype resides at the terminal end of macrophage differentiation. Construction of weighted gene coexpression networks

WGCNA is a systems biology approach for analyzing expression patterns of multiple genes across different samples, forming clusters or modules comprising genes with similar expression patterns. Genes within the same module may share biological functions, enabling investigation of associations between these modules and sample characteristics (e.g., clinical features). This study performed WGCNA on bulk RNA-sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) data from osteosarcoma patients on bulk RNA-seq data from osteosarcoma. Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was used to calculate Entosis scores from the TARGET-OS cohort as a sample characteristic. The top 5,000 most variable genes in the expression profiles were selected for WGCNA analysis. A soft threshold of β = 5 (scaleless R² = 0.9) was chosen to achieve a scaleless network (Fig. 7A, B). After excluding gray modules via merged dynamic tree cutting, 11 gene co-expression modules were identified (Fig. 7C). Among these, the green, pink, and green-yellow modules showed high correlation with Entosis scores (Fig. 7D). Genes from these three key modules were extracted for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis. Results revealed significant KEGG pathway enrichment in influenza and viral infections, allograft rejection, phagosomes, cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, rheumatoid arthritis, viral protein and cytokine/cytokine receptor interactions, osteoclast differentiation, B-cell receptor signaling, and lysosomes (Fig. 7E). Construction of prognosis models

To construct a gene signature associated with osteosarcoma prognosis, we identified 37 candidate signature genes by intersecting differentially expressed genes in high- and low-Entosis macrophages, WGCNA key module genes, differentially expressed genes between high- and low-Entosis groups in the TARGET-OS cohort, and genes related to the immune microenvironment (Fig. 8A). Using random sampling, 4/5 of the TARGET-OS whole cohort (n=85) was allocated to the training set (n=51), with the TARGET whole cohort and GSE21257 dataset serving as the validation set. First, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis identified 10 genes significantly associated with osteosarcoma prognosis. To mitigate overfitting risk and optimize gene selection for osteosarcoma prediction, Lasso regression analysis further screened 6 candidate genes from these 10 (Fig. 8B, C). Subsequently, stepwise multivariate Cox analysis identified CD163, FCGR2A, NPC2, CD209, SAMHD1, and IKZF1 as independent prognostic factors. The risk score calculation formula is: Riskscore = CD163 × (-0.019) + FCGR2A × (-0.01744) + NPC2 × (-0.24167) + CD209 × (-0.0716) + SAMHD1 × (-0.13607) + IKZF1 × (-0.1268) . This formula was applied to each sample in the training set, the TARGET cohort, and the GSE21257 external validation set. Results showed significantly elevated scores for WNT-β-catenin target genes and inhibitory ligands in the high-risk group, while most other immune function gene sets were activated in the low-risk group (Fig. 8M). Discussion

As a new type of programmed death, Entosis’ role in tumors is tissue-specific and complex. This study found that the prognosis of cancer patients in the Entosis high score group was significantly better than that in the low score group, which is consistent with some research results in breast cancer and colorectal cancer [21,22], suggesting that Entosis may play a tumor suppressor role in cancer. Further GSEA analysis showed that the Entosis high-scoring group was significantly enriched in immune-related pathways such as antigen processing and presentation, chemokine signaling pathways, and MHC-II molecule-related gene sets. At the same time, the average enrichment level of 25 immune function-related genes was significantly increased. This indicates that Entosis may enhance the body's anti-tumor immune response by activating the tumor immune microenvironment, thereby improving patient prognosis. This is different from previous research findings in liver cancer—in liver cancer, Entosis has been mostly proven to promote tumor progression. This study suggests that Entosis may play a tumor suppressor role in osteosarcoma. This difference may be due to the heterogeneity of the microenvironment of different tumor types.

In osteosarcoma, cancer cells interact with immune cells and stromal cells to form an immunosuppressive TME, thereby enhancing the immune evasion ability of cancer cells. Intratumoral heterogeneity is also an important feature of osteosarcoma, leading to treatment resistance and differences in treatment outcomes between patients [23]. Understanding cancer cell heterogeneity and dynamic tumor immune microenvironment may provide new targets for osteosarcoma treatment. Single-cell sequencing analysis is a powerful tool for analyzing the cellular heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment. This study found through single-cell data analysis that macrophages are the cell subpopulation most closely related to Entosis score, and their Entosis score is significantly higher than that of other cell types. Macrophages, as the core immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, have a high degree of functional heterogeneity and can be divided into the tumor-promoting M2 type and the tumor-suppressing M1 type [24]. This study further subdivided macrophages into 7 subtypes, among which the Macro_CXCL2 subtype had the highest Entosis score and was most strongly correlated with the high Entosis score group. GOGO enrichment analysis showed that the Macro_CXCL2 subtype was significantly enriched in functions related to leukocyte migration, chemotaxis, and cytokine/chemokine activity, suggesting that it may recruit effector immune cells (such as T cells and NK cells) to infiltrate tumor tissues through the secretion of chemokines and enhance anti-tumor immunity. Pseudo-time analysis further confirmed that high Entosis activity can promote the progression of macrophages to the late stage of differentiation, and the Macro_CXCL2 subtype is in the terminal stage of macrophage differentiation. The expression of core Entosis-related genes (such as CDC42, CXCL8) gradually increases with the differentiation process, suggesting that these genes may regulate the terminal differentiation and functional activation of the Macro_CXCL2 subtype. This discovery clarifies for the first time the functional subtypes of macrophages related to Entosis in osteosarcoma, providing a precise target for therapeutic strategies that target macrophages to regulate Entosis activity.

The stemness maintenance and differentiation potential of malignant cells are the core driving forces of tumor progression and metastasis [25]. This study found that the Entosis high score area highly overlaps with the CytoTRACE2 high score area, and the high Entosis score group accounts for a higher proportion of pluripotent cells. There is a significant positive correlation between the Entosis score and the CytoTRACE2 score, suggesting that Entosis may regulate the differentiation potential of malignant cells. Pathway analysis showed that malignant cells with high Entosis scores were significantly enriched in the hypoxia pathway, TNFα-NFκB and MAPK signaling pathways. These pathways have been confirmed to be closely related to the maintenance of tumor cell stemness, invasion and metastasis [26]. As a typical feature of the tumor microenvironment, hypoxia can enhance the stemness of cancer stem cells by activating the HIF-1α pathway [27,28]; while the TNFα-NFκB pathway promotes tumor cell proliferation and metastasis by regulating downstream target genes [29]. The coordinated activation of Entosis and these pathways may jointly regulate the stemness and invasion capabilities of malignant cells.

Intercellular communication analysis reveals the molecular mechanism by which Entosis regulates the osteosarcoma microenvironment. The number and intensity of interactions between macrophages and other cells were significantly increased in the high Entosis group. Among them, the communication between macrophages and stromal cells mediated by the SPP1-CD44 axis, and the communication between macrophages and immune cells/malignant cells mediated by TNF and MHC-II signals were significantly enhanced. As a secreted glycoprotein, SPP1 has been confirmed to promote the progression of various tumors by binding to CD44 [30]. In this study, the high activity of the SPP1-CD44 axis in the high Entosis group may be involved in the coordinated regulation of macrophages and stromal cells, affecting the homeostasis of the tumor microenvironment. As a classic inflammatory signaling pathway, TNF signaling can activate the killing function of effector immune cells, while MHC-II molecules participate in the antigen presentation process and enhance T cell recognition of tumor cells [31,32]. The enhancement of both in the high Entosis group further supports the mechanism of Entosis enhancing anti-tumor immunity by promoting communication between macrophages and immune cells. In addition, the TNF killing signal from macrophages in the high Entosis group to malignant cells was significantly upregulated, while the COL1A1/COL1A2-integrin signal from macrophages in the low Entosis group to osteoblasts was enhanced, suggesting that the Entosis state can regulate the functional bias of macrophages, thereby affecting tumor progression and bone microenvironment remodeling.

Building a prognostic model based on multi-omics data is an important means to achieve accurate prognostic assessment of tumors. This study screened out 37 candidate genes by integrating differentially expressed genes between high and low Entosis macrophages, WGCNA key module genes, differentially expressed genes between high and low Entosis score groups and immune microenvironment-related genes. Finally, a 6-gene (CD163, FCGR2A, NPC2, CD209, SAMHD1, IKZF1) prognostic model was constructed through single-factor Cox, Lasso and multi-factor stepwise Cox regression analysis. The model showed good prognostic prediction performance in the training set, validation set and external validation set. Further analysis found that high expression of the model gene was associated with a good prognosis of cancer patients and was highly positively correlated with the Entosis score, suggesting that the model can be used as an effective surrogate indicator for Entosis-related prognostic features. Unsupervised clustering based on model genes can divide patients into two molecular subtypes with significantly different prognosis. Among them, C2 subtype (high expression of model genes) is accompanied by widespread activation of immune function-related pathways, while WNT-β-catenin signaling Pathway activity and immunosuppressive ligand scores were significantly reduced, which further confirmed the mechanism by which Entosis-related features affect patient prognosis by regulating the immune microenvironment. It also provided a basis for molecular classification and personalized treatment of cancer. It is worth noting that CD163 and FCGR2A in the model are both macrophage-related markers, which echoes the previously discovered central role of macrophages in the regulation of Entosis and further verifies the consistency of the research results.

To further validate the regulatory role of Entosis in the immune microenvironment of osteosarcoma, in vitro functional experiments were conducted in this study. The results demonstrated that osteosarcoma cell supernatant induced an M1-polarized phenotype in macrophages, suggesting a pro-inflammatory immune response tendency within the TME. Notably, conditioned medium from osteosarcoma cells with a high Entosis phenotype significantly upregulated the expression of CD163, FCGR2A, and CD209, while downregulating NPC2 and SAMHD1 in macrophages, indicating that high-Entosis tumor cells can drive the conversion of macrophages toward a specific functional phenotype through paracrine signaling. In line with bioinformatics analyses, the Macro_CXCL2 subtype exhibited the highest Entosis score in the high-Entosis group and showed a strong correlation with Entosis activity, further supporting the central role of this subtype in Entosis-mediated immune regulation. Moreover, the expression and secretion of CXCL12 were significantly increased in high-Entosis cells, suggesting their potential involvement in microenvironment remodeling through immune cell recruitment. Pathway analyses revealed coordinated activation of Entosis with the hypoxia pathway, TNFα-NFκB pathway, and MAPK signaling pathway, which collectively regulate the stemness and invasive properties of malignant cells. Western blot analysis further confirmed that suspension culture markedly enhanced the expression of LC3, LAMP1, and E-cadherin in osteosarcoma cells and increased the Entosis index, whereas treatment with the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 effectively suppressed this process. In summary, high-Entosis tumor cells regulate macrophage polarization via paracrine signaling and synergize with multiple key signaling pathways to participate in the remodeling of the immune microenvironment in osteosarcoma. These findings provide experimental evidence for Entosis as a potential therapeutic target and offer new insights into combined immunotherapeutic strategies for osteosarcoma.

Conclusions

This study systematically reveals the tumor-suppressive role and immune regulatory mechanism of Entosis in osteosarcoma through integrated multi-omics analysis and in vitro experiments. High Entosis activity is significantly correlated with favorable patient prognosis and an activated immune microenvironment, with macrophages, particularly the Macro_CXCL2 subtype, identified as the core target cells. Mechanistically, high-Entosis tumor cells regulate macrophage polarization via paracrine signaling and synergize with the hypoxia, TNFα-NFκB, and MAPK pathways to remodel the immune microenvironment. Furthermore, a six-gene prognostic model based on Entosis-related genes demonstrates robust predictive performance across multiple datasets, providing an effective tool for personalized prognosis assessment. This study offers a theoretical foundation and experimental evidence for Entosis-targeted combination immunotherapy strategies in osteosarcoma.

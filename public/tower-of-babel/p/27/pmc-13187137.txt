Intelligent MPPT-based energy management for hybrid renewable energy grids using trans Z-source quadratic boost converter
Authors: Sangeetha Manickam, S Padma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe growing demand for renewable energy necessitates efficient power conversion and grid integration solutions. This work proposes an energy management system (EMS) using a trans Z-source quadratic boost converter (TZSQBC) and puffer fish optimized fuzzy neural network (PF-OFNN)-maximum power point tracking (MPPT). The system integrates photovoltaic (PV) and wind system to enhance reliability and mitigate variability issues. The TZSQBC significantly improves voltage boosting efficiency, ensuring seamless grid integration. The PF-OFNN MPPT algorithm optimizes power extraction from PV system under dynamic environmental conditions. A doubly fed induction generator (DFIG) wind system is incorporated to enhance flexibility and stability. Additionally, a bidirectional DC-DC converter with battery storage ensures efficient energy flow management. The proposed system is evaluated through MATLAB simulations under various test cases, including constant and varying environmental conditions, analysing power efficiency, voltage gain, and total harmonic distortion (THD). A hardware prototype further validates system feasibility, demonstrating effective grid synchronization and improved power quality. Comparative analysis with existing topologies highlights reduced component count, improved duty cycle utilization, and lower voltage stress. The results confirm the proposed system’s effectiveness in achieving stable, high-efficiency renewable energy integration with enhanced power management and minimal harmonic distortion.Keywords: Hybrid renewable energy system (HRES), TZSQBC, PF-OFNN MPPT, DFIG, PV system, Wind energy conversion system (WECS)Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Due to the increasing rate of fossil fuel depletion and concerns about growing carbon footprints that have detrimental effects on flora and fauna, research and development in renewable energy sources (RES) has received increased attention globally in recent years1. In order to promote widespread public participation in this area, RES is supported by government regulations and subsidies2. At the moment, wind turbines (WT) and PV plants are regarded as the most advantageous RESs due to their capacity to provide an infinite supply of clean energy, because they are inherently sporadic and rely on a variety of meteorological circumstances3,4 Nevertheless, these sources’ sporadic nature and reliance on external factors have a detrimental impact on customer needs. Researchers has demonstrated that the introduction of MPPT technology has significantly improved the efficiency of solar power systems5. Using a DC-DC converter, MPPT regulates the PV generator’s conductivity or voltage threshold in order to reach the ideal energy tracking point. Numerous DC–DC converter types have been researched6. The traditional boost converter (BC) have several drawbacks, including low conversion efficiency, a large bulk, a low boost factor, and a high ripple content in the output waveform7. To boost the gain, quadratic boost converters are employed8, which earn more as the duty ratio rises above 70%. Because of this, inductor core saturation is a serious problem in high-frequency converters. It has been known that switched capacitor cells be used to increase the DC voltage gain in converters9. Ripples in input current and output voltage controlled within a reasonable range by using particular switching strategies. However, placing switched-capacitor-based DCDC converters into practice requires a large number of regulated switches, which makes system more complicated and expensive. Although an enhanced converter is introduced in10 to lessen the drawbacks of Z-source converters, it quiet featured a discontinuous input current. A common ground for the z-source architecture is established in11 by describing a common grounded impedance source converter. In spite of this, impedance network capacitors are still too large to achieve high voltages, even if component sizes have decreased and semiconductor stress has decreased. Compared to a common-ground impedance source, the Quasi-Z-source converter proposed by the authors of12 had a lower voltage gain; however, it can supply a constant input current and reduce voltage stress on the components. Additionally, a ZS/Q-ZS converter using a switch capacitor is described. Higher voltage gain is attained by converter in13, although three inductors and other parts are needed. Research is currently being done to develop such an approach. Developing a well-performing MPPT is also a major concern when using a high-power quality converter14. The aforementioned drawbacks are proposed to be addressed by a Trans-Quadratic Z-source Boost converter.

The SPV’s output is non-linear and mostly reliant on meteorological factors like temperature and radiation15. Therefore, in order to extract highest power from PV, it must be integrated with a MPPT system. Generally, this method increases PV’s operational efficiency and prolongs the module’s lifespan. Consequently, MPPT is becoming a crucial component of converters in order to maximise power extraction from PV16. Perturb & Observe (P&O)17 is the most often used power point identifier for the solar network due to its many benefits, including the need for fewer sensing devices, inexpensive development costs, and small size. Problem statement

The depletion of fossil fuels and environmental concerns have shifted focus toward RES, particularly solar and wind power. However, their intermittency, caused by varying meteorological conditions, poses a challenge in meeting continuous energy demands. To address this, MPPT technology optimizes the energy output of solar PV systems by adjusting the operating voltage for maximum power extraction. Despite advancements in converters, issues such as low efficiency, large bulk, and high ripple remain. Switched capacitor-based converters offer higher voltage gain but increase system complexity and cost. Alternative approaches, like the z-source converter, address some drawbacks but still struggle with large components and discontinuous input currents. MPPT algorithms like P&O and IC, improve tracking efficiency but face challenges in accuracy under rapid changes in solar conditions. Metaheuristic and intelligent controllers have been explored but also introduce complexities like higher iteration counts and difficulties in optimization. Further research is needed to develop cost-effective, efficient, and reliable systems that minimize complexity while ensuring optimal performance under varying environmental conditions.

System description

The proposed HRES as in Fig. 2 integrates solar and wind energy sources to ensure efficient and reliable power generation for grid applications. The PF-OFNN based MPPT is implemented to extract supreme power from PV panel under changing ecological conditions. The MPPT controller generates PWM control signals to regulate the operation of the TZSQBC, which significantly increases PV voltage to match grid voltage requirements. The TZSQBC enhances voltage conversion efficiency, ensuring minimal losses and enlightening the overall energy management of the system. The WECS operates as a complementary power source, utilizing a DFIG coupled with a wind turbine to generate three-phase AC power. This AC power is rectified using a PWM rectifier, converting it into DC for integration with the PV-generated power. A Proportional Integral (PI) controller regulates the DC voltage level, ensuring smooth power flow and mitigating voltage fluctuations due to wind speed variations. To enhance system reliability and power stability, a battery storage system is incorporated. This converter operates in boost mode (discharging) when additional power is required and in buck mode (charging) during surplus energy availability. A PI controller manages the charge-discharge cycles, ensuring optimal utilization of stored energy while preventing overcharging or deep discharge of the battery pack.

Structure of proposed HRES with grid integration.

The regulated DC voltage from PV, wind, and battery storage is then fed into a three-phase Voltage Source Inverter (VSI), which transform DC power into AC for grid synchronization. The inverter is controlled using Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) signals generated by a PI controller, ensuring seamless integration with the utility grid. To maintain power quality and eliminate unwanted harmonics, an LC filter is employed at the inverter output, effectively smoothing the AC waveform. The grid interface system consists of real power (P) and reactive power (Q) controllers, which regulate power injection into grid, ensuring optimal active and reactive power balancing.

Modelling of trans Z-source quadratic boost converter (TZSQBC)

The TZSQBC topology as in Fig. 4 capitalizes on Z-source network and Quadratic Boost Converter (QBC) to significantly boost the output voltage. By integrating the impedance-based Z-source network with a quadratic boost stage, the TZSQBC overcomes the inherent limitations of conventional boost and cascaded DC–DC converters, such as extreme duty-cycle operation, high switch voltage stress and increased losses. The Z-source network enables controlled shoot-through operation, allowing voltage boosting without additional switching stages, while simultaneously improving immunity to input voltage fluctuations. The quadratic boost section further amplifies the voltage gain using a single additional energy storage stage, eliminating the need for multiple cascaded converters and thereby reducing size, cost and control complexity. Moreover, the distributed energy storage across inductors and capacitors ensures continuous input current, lower ripple and improved dynamic response. The PV source provides the DC input to the converter, while switches and control voltage boosting and energy transfer process. Here, inductors and are part of Z-source network, whereas inductor assist with quadratic boost mechanism. Capacitors and form an intermediate energy storage and supports voltage multiplication stage. The Z-source network () boosts the converter voltage beyond conventional Boost converter through the control of shoot-through state of switches. The quadratic gain in voltage is facilitated by the additional energy storage stage (), which provides a second stage of boosting.

The objective function is formulated to maximize extracted PV power while minimizing steady-state oscillations and tracking error, ensuring fast and stable convergence to the global maximum power point. The objective function is expressed as,

Where, is the MPPT efficiency, is the standard deviation of PV, is the penalty factor. This multi-objective formulation ensures that the PFO algorithm tunes the FNN parameters to achieve rapid convergence, minimal steady-state oscillations and high tracking efficiency.

The tuning of the PFO–FNN–based MPPT controller is carried out through a structured, two-level adaptive optimization process that combines fuzzy inference design with bio-inspired global search. Initially, the FNN structure is defined by selecting appropriate input variables and establishing a reduced yet representative fuzzy rule base to ensure fast inference and avoid rule explosion. The initial membership functions and neural weights are assigned within bounded ranges based on PV operating limits, which provides a stable starting point for learning. The PFO algorithm is then employed to optimally tune the FNN parameters, including fuzzy membership function widths and centers, neural weights and learning rates, by minimizing a multi-objective fitness function that accounts for maximum power tracking error, steady-state oscillations, and convergence time. During the exploration phase, PFO enables wide parameter search to prevent local minima entrapment, while the exploitation phase refines the solution for precise MPP convergence. Importantly, tuning is performed in an online or semi-online manner at a slower supervisory timescale, ensuring that parameter updates do not interfere with fast converter dynamics. Only fitness-improving parameter sets are accepted, which guarantees stability and smooth tracking. This systematic tuning strategy ensures rapid adaptation to irradiance and temperature variations, reduced power ripple and robust MPPT performance without requiring extensive offline training datasets.

Overfitting in the proposed PF-OFNN–based MPPT is effectively mitigated through the combination of adaptive learning control, population-based global optimization and online parameter tuning. Unlike conventional neural-network-based MPPT methods that rely on static datasets and gradient-based weight updates, the PF-OFNN employs PFO to continuously optimize the FNN parameters in the real-time operating environment. The population-based search mechanism of PFO inherently promotes solution diversity, preventing premature convergence to local optima and reducing excessive specialization of the neural model to specific irradiance or temperature patterns. Furthermore, the fuzzy inference layer acts as a regularization mechanism by constraining neural outputs within linguistic rules and bounded membership functions, thereby smoothing the control action and suppressing high-frequency oscillations that are symptomatic of overfitting. The exploration–exploitation balance of PFO ensures that the FNN maintains generalization capability across wide environmental variations, while adaptive updating only accepts parameter changes that improve the objective function, as enforced by the fitness-based selection criteria. Consequently, the PF-OFNN achieves robust generalization, stable convergence to the global MPP and consistent performance under unseen and rapidly changing operating conditions, effectively addressing overfitting issues inherent in standalone neural MPPT approaches.

The computational complexity of the proposed algorithm remains moderate and well suited for real-time renewable energy applications despite its hybrid structure. The FNN component involves simple arithmetic operations such as weighted summation, fuzzy inference and sigmoidal activation functions, whose computational cost grows linearly with the number of inputs, fuzzy rules and neurons. These operations are computationally lightweight and easily implementable on digital signal processors or microcontrollers commonly used in power electronic controllers. The PFO algorithm introduces additional computation due to population-based search; however, its complexity is , where is the population size, is the number of decision variables and T is the iteration count. Since MPPT optimization is performed over a limited parameter space with small population size and few iterations, the overall burden remains bounded. Moreover, PFO avoids costly gradient calculations and matrix inversions, further reducing processing overhead. The optimization process is executed at a slower supervisory timescale compared to the fast inner control loops, ensuring that real-time constraints are not violated. As a result, the proposed PF-OFNN achieves a favourable trade-off between computational complexity and tracking performance, making it practical for embedded implementation in hybrid renewable energy systems.

Conclusion

The proposed HRES integrating PV and wind energy sources with TZSQBC and PF-OFNN MPPT has demonstrated significant improvements in power conversion efficiency, voltage boosting, and grid synchronization. Simulation results confirm that the TZSQBC achieves high voltage gain (M = 8) with reduced component stress, making it more efficient than conventional topologies. The PF-OFNN MPPT controller ensures maximum power extraction under changing ecological conditions, enhancing system adaptability and stability. The analysis of switching as well as diode voltage stress reveals that designed converter exhibits lower voltage stress on mechanisms, reducing losses and improving reliability of power electronics. Additionally, THD analysis of the three-phase grid connection shows that the proposed system maintains a low THD (< 1.04%), ensuring superior power quality and minimal harmonic interference. The bidirectional converter with battery storage effectively regulates energy flow, providing stable operation under fluctuating load and generation conditions. A hardware prototype further validates the feasibility of the proposed system, demonstrating smooth power injection into the grid, stable battery charging, and efficient inverter performance. However, the interaction between the TZSQBC and the grid-side converter becomes particularly critical under weak grid conditions and severe fault scenarios, where grid impedance is high and voltage stability is poor. In such environments, fluctuations in grid voltage, frequency deviations and unbalanced conditions can propagate back to the DC link, potentially exciting resonant modes of the TZSQBC’s impedance network and quadratic boost stage, these issues are to be addressed in future works.

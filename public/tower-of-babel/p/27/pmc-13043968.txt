Epidemiological Burden and Hospitalization Trends of Human Metapneumovirus in Spain (2016–2023): A Retrospective Study
Authors: Oscar Brochado-Kith, Ruth Gil-Prieto, Angel Gil-de-Miguel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe hospitalization burden of human metapneumovirus (HMPV) in Spain was estimated assessing disease manifestations and risk factors to guide future vaccine strategies.MethodsThis study was a retrospective analysis of Spain's National Hospital Data System (CMBD) from 2016–2023, performed using ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes, with age-stratified analyses for children under 6 years and adults over 60 years, assessing comorbidities and associated mortality risks.ResultsA total of 4824 hospitalizations were identified; 75% in children, 16% in older adults. Bronchiolitis dominated in children while pneumonia was most common in older adults. Overall annual hospitalization rate was 18.70 hospitalizations per 100,000 in children up to 5 years old, and the annual hospitalization rate was 0.79 hospitalizations per 100,000 in adults over 60 years, Hospital costs totaled over €22 million, and, beyond this overall burden, expenditures showed a significant year-on-year increasing trend. There were 2 deaths among children < 6 years, 18 in individuals between 16 and 59 years and 74 in adults over 60 years old, reaching a case fatality rate of 9.65% in adults, with higher risk associated with kidney failure, liver disease, and cancer.ConclusionsHMPV poses a significant burden on vulnerable groups in Spain. Despite underdiagnosis, rising cases post-COVID highlight the need for targeted prevention. These findings offer a foundation for public health planning and vaccine prioritization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40121-026-01317-4.

Introduction

Respiratory tract infections (RTI) are among the most prevalent diseases globally, affecting individuals across all age groups and remaining a major cause of morbidity and mortality [1]. In children under 5 years of age, RTI constitute the second leading cause of death, regardless of geographic region [2]. However, the considerable overlap in clinical manifestations makes it impossible to reliably distinguish the etiological agent of an infection based on symptoms alone [2].

The human metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a single-stranded RNA virus previously classified within the Paramyxoviridae family but reclassified in 2016 as part of the Pneumoviridae family [3]. Its first isolation was in 2001 in the Netherlands by Van den Hoogen et al. [4].

HMPV is becoming a global concern due to the increasing prevalence and impact on respiratory health. This virus has been recognized as the cause of upper and lower respiratory tract infections, focusing in children, elderly people, and immunocompromised individuals [5].

The symptoms included in HMPV infection are cough, fever, nasal congestion, and shortness of breath. These symptoms may progress to bronchiolitis or pneumonia, similar to other respiratory viruses [6, 7]. The incubation period is estimated in 3–6 days with a varying duration depending on severity [5]. Despite it being discovered as an etiological cause of respiratory disease, HMPV remains underdiagnosed because of the poor testing routine. The few published information estimates that from 2018 to 2019 this virus is assumed to be responsible for 600,000 hospitalizations and nearly 16,000 deaths around the world [8].

Even though the diagnosed reports of HMPV are very poor, there is evidence of changes in the epidemiological trend over recent years—especially after COVID-19 pandemia—leading to potential outbreaks among children [9]. For this reason, the race to find the perfect vaccine and range of vaccination is still ongoing and there are current challenges on HMPV prevention [6, 10, 11].

Nevertheless, despite the increasing global concern about HMPV and the ongoing efforts to improve prevention, there is a lack of nationwide data on HMPV-associated hospitalizations in Spain. This absence of comprehensive epidemiological information limits the understanding of its real clinical and economic impact and highlights the need for national studies to better inform public health and vaccination strategies. This study aimed to provide a retrospective and population-based estimation of the hospitalization burden associated with human metapneumovirus (hMPV) disease in Spain over an eight-year period (2016–2023). Additionally, we sought to examine the clinical manifestations of HMPV infection—specifically pneumonia, bronchiolitis, and other diagnoses—and to identify risk factors at the national level that could inform future recommendations for targeted metapneumovirus vaccination strategies. As far as we know, this is the first study focused on the HMPV infection and hospitalization in Spain.

Methods

In this retrospective study, we used the Spanish National Hospital Data Information System (Conjunto Mínimo de Datos; CMBD) maintained by the Ministry of health. The CMBD encompasses around 98% of public hospitals in Spain, offering a thorough record of hospital discharges. Its data quality and methodological framework have been validated by the Ministry, making it a reliable source for epidemiological research. Since 2016, the database has employed the 10th Clinical Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10-CM). Spain’s mandatory health insurance system covers approximately 99.5% of the population, and individuals not formally insured can still access care within the national healthcare system.

Ethical Approval

This study was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964. The Rey Juan Carlos University Ethics Committee determined that neither ethical approval nor consent to participate were required for this study. The data are anonymized and are publicly accessible via the Spanish National Health System upon reasonable request.

Data Collection and Analysis

Data were collected retrospectively for all cases diagnosed with metapneumovirus disease between January 2016 and December 2023, based on the ICD-10-CM classification system. An algorithm was applied to identify any infections caused by metapneumovirus listed as a contributing factor to other diseases. The analysis focused on specific manifestations of the disease, including pneumonia due to metapneumovirus (J12.3), bronchiolitis due to metapneumovirus, which were considered as bronchitis in the case of adults (J21.1), and other HMPV-associated diagnoses (B97.81). Patients of all ages were included, with special attention given to those younger than 2 years and those older than 60, both in general and within defined risk groups. Relevant diagnostic codes associated with targeted risk factors were also incorporated into the analysis.

Comorbid conditions were also evaluated as potential risk factors for metapneumovirus disease. Accordingly, crude, unadjusted odds ratios (ORs) were calculated for individuals diagnosed with metapneumovirus disease who had been exposed to the following risk factors: cancer, neoplasms, diabetes, hypertension, cardiopulmonary diseases, cerebrovascular diseases, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), chronic lower respiratory tract disease, asthma, acute kidney failure, and liver disorders. The analyzed comorbidities were pre-selected a priori based on their known impact on patient survival in respiratory infections such as pneumococcal pneumonia [12].

The case fatality rate (%)—an indicator of disease severity—was determined by dividing the number of hospital deaths by the total number of hospital discharges. Hospitalization costs were estimated using data from the Ministry of Health, that provides anonymized data on the total hospital costs of cases, covering all expenses from admission to discharge. These standardized figures, collected via the Spanish Hospital Costs Network (RECH), estimate the direct economic burden of hospital care [13]. These costs were calculated based on the diagnostic cost group, total cost, and number of patient discharges. The diagnostic cost groups were derived from diagnosis-related groups (DRGs), which categorize hospitalized patients according to ICD codes, age, sex, and resource utilization. Each DRG represents a group of patients with similar clinical characteristics and comparable hospital costs, allowing for standardized cost assessment. DRG calculations were carried out using the Core Grouping System software developed by 3M.

To assess significant differences in proportions, the chi-square test was applied with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Differences in case fatality rates were evaluated using logistic regression. Additionally, negative binomial regression models were employed to analyze variations in hospitalization rates, mortality, and case fatality rates over the study period, the analyses were carried out adjusting for age group and year interactions. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses. Statistical processing was conducted using R software v.4.4.2. To assess temporal trends in disease incidence across the study period, we performed a joinpoint regression analysis using the joinpoint Regression Program (v.5.4.0.0, National Cancer Institute).

Results

Hospitalizations

From 2016 to 2023, 4824 patients in Spain were hospitalized with an identification of metapneumovirus infection, of which 3601 (74.65%) were children from 0 to 5 years, 767 (15.90%) were adults over 60 years and 456 (9.45%) occurred in people between 6 and 59 years of age. A total of 1629 (45.24%) were male children, 334 (43.55%) were male adults over 60 years, and 248 (54.50%) were males between 6 and 59 years.

The median length of stay of children was 5 days (IQR: 3–7 days) with a mean cost of €3678 (SD: €3637.48) per hospitalization. The total cost of children hospitalizations for the health system was more than €13 million. For the group of population between 6 and 59 years, the median length of stay was 7 days (IQR: 4–14 days) with a mean cost of €8,288 (SD: €14,020.51) and a total cost of €3.77 million. For adults over 60 years, the median length of stay was 8 days (IQR: 6–13 days) with a mean cost of €7225 (SD: €11,216.49) per hospitalization. The total cost for the health system of hospitalization in adults over 60 years was more than €5.54 million considering all the diagnoses.

Fpr children under 6 years, the overall annual hospitalization rate was 18.70 hospitalizations per 100,000 people [95% confidence interval (CI): 18.10–19.30] (Table 1; Fig. 1). There were no significant differences between sex (p = 0.645). However, there were differences in hospitalization rates between ages, with the highest value for < 1 year age (80.5; 95% CI 77.2–83.8) and decreasing each year (Table 1, Fig. 2; Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). For individuals between 6 and 59 years of age, the annual hospitalization rate was 0.17 (95% CI 0.16–0.19) (Table 2; Fig. 3). Differences between sex in hospitalization rates were not found in this group of age (p = 0.878), while differences in age groups showed individuals from 6 to 11 years had the highest hospitalization rate (Table 2; Fig. 4; Supplementary Tables 3 and 4). For adults over 60 years, the annual hospitalization rate was 0.79 (95% CI 0.74–0.85) (Table 3; Fig. 5). There were no significant differences between sex (p = 0.382). Hospitalization rate per 100,000 people by sex and age for adults over 60 years old with human metapneumovirus disease

For children, a total of 1382 (38.37%) cases were diagnosed as pneumonia due to metapneumovirus in at least one diagnostic field, a total of 2245 (62.34%) were diagnosed as bronchiolitis, and 33 (0.94%) cases were diagnosed as other HMPV-associated diagnoses. Among individuals between the ages of 6 and 59, there were 411 (90.32%) cases of pneumonia due to metapneumovirus, 45 (9.89%) cases were identified as bronchiolitis/bronchitis due to metapneumovirus, and 15 (3.30%) cases due to other HMPV-associated diagnoses. For adults over 60 years, there were 700 (91.26%) cases of pneumonia due to metapneumovirus in at least one diagnostic field, a total of 68 (8.87%) cases of bronchitis due to metapneumovirus, and 18 cases (2.35%) due to other HMPV-associated diagnoses.

Pneumonia Due to Metapneumovirus (J12.3)

For children under 6 years of age, there were no significant differences (p = 0.798) in hospitalization rates by sex for metapneumovirus pneumonia. Significant differences were found in hospitalization rates by age with higher rates on children < 1 year old and with 1 year (hospitalization rates of 12.3 (95% CI 11.0–13.6) and 14.0 (95% CI 12.7–15.3), respectively) (Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 1; Supplementary Table 6). The median hospital stay was 5 days (IQR: 3–7 days), with a mean cost of €3554 (SD: €3517.36) per hospitalization, resulting in a total cost of €7.98 million.

For people between the ages of 6 and 59 there were no differences in hospitalization rates for and sex by this diagnostic (p value = 0.999); however, there was a higher hospitalization rate in children from 6 to 11 years [Hospitalization rate of 0.47 (95% CI 0.39–0.57)] (Table 2, Supplementary Fig. 2; Supplementary Table 8). The median hospitalization stays for pneumonia was 7 days (IQR: 4–14 days) with a mean cost of €8379 (SD: €14,506.18) per hospitalization with a total cost of €3.44 million.

For adults over 60 years of age, there were no significant differences in hospitalization rates by sex for metapneumovirus pneumonia (p value = 0.798). There were higher hospitalization rates in people between 85 and 89 years (Table 3; supplementary Fig. 3; Supplementary Table 9). The median hospitalization stay was 7 days (IQR: 5–12.25 days), with a mean cost of €3503 (SD: €4305.96) per hospitalization and a total cost of more than 340.187€ in hospitalizations.

Bronchiolitis/Bronchitis Due to Metapneumovirus (J21.1)

For children under 6 years of age, there were no significant differences by sex in hospitalization rates for metapneumovirus bronchiolitis (p value = 0.382). There were significant differences by age with the higher hospitalization rate in children > 1 year old (68.8; 95% CI 65.8–71.9) (Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 4; Supplementary Table 10). The hospitalization rate decreased progressively with age. The median stay for bronchiolitis hospitalization was 5 days (IQR: 3–7 days) and the cost was €3873 (SD: €3786.10) per hospitalization. The total cost of all the hospitalizations was more than €5.35 million.

For people between 6 and 59 years there were also no significant differences in age and sex for hospitalization rates with this diagnostic (p value = 0.494) (Table 2; Supplementary Fig. 5; Supplementary Table 11). The median of stay for this diagnostic was 8 days (IQR: 5–15 days) with a mean cost of €7354 (SD: €8189.31) per hospitalization with a total cost of €330,911.3.

For adults over 60 years of age, there were no significant differences by sex in hospitalization rates for metapneumovirus bronchitis (p value = 0.318) (Table 3; Supplementary Fig. 6; Supplementary Table 12). The median stay for bronchitis was 9 days (IQR: 6–13.25 days) and a median cost of €7435 (SD: €11,644.14) per hospitalization. The total cost of all the hospitalization for metapneumovirus bronchitis was €5.2 million.

Other HMPV-Associated Diagnoses (B97.81)

For children under 6 years of age, there were no differences found between sex (p value = 0.746) or age on hospitalization rate by this diagnostic (Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 7; Supplementary Table 13). The median time of stay was 4 days (IQR: 3–7 days) with a mean cost of €5079 (SD: €8424.21).

For individuals between 6 and 59 years, no significant differences were found for sex (p value = 0.452) and age on hospitalization by this diagnostic (Table 2; Supplementary Fig. 8; Supplementary Table 14). The median time of stay was 9 days (IQR: 5.5–10 days) with a mean cost of €8759 (SD: €12,390.46). The total cost of the hospitalizations in this group of ages by this diagnostic was €131,392.1.

For adults over 60 years, there were no significant differences by sex in hospitalization rates (p value = 0.196) (Table 3; Supplementary Fig. 9; Supplementary Table 15). The median stay for bronchiolitis was 8.5 days (IQR: 6–10 days) with a mean cost of €4030.64 (SD: €2371.86) per hospitalization. The total cost of all the hospitalization was €84,083.

Hospitalization and Cost Trends in Spanish Population

Across all three population groups, joinpoint regression analyses identified a clear shift in the temporal trend beginning around 2020. In adults aged ≥ 60 years, a marked inflection point was observed in 2021, followed by a steep and sustained increase in hospitalization rates [annual percent change (APC): 77.11%] (Supplementary Fig. 10). Children under 6 years also displayed an important joinpoint in 2020, after which rates rose rapidly (APC: 43.88%) (Supplementary Fig. 11). Individuals aged 6–59 years also experienced a shift in trend starting in 2021, marking the beginning of a more moderate yet consistent upward trajectory (APC: 68.58%) (Supplementary Fig. 12). Overall, the models indicate a significant post-2020 increase in hospitalization rates.

Annual hospitalization costs increased significantly (p < 0.05) across all age groups, with an estimated yearly increment of €780,245 from 2016 to 2023 (Supplementary Table 16). When grouped by period, total costs were €6.12 million pre-COVID (2016–2019), €4.08 million during the pandemic (2020–2021), and €12.35 million post-COVID (2022–2023).

Discussion

With the retrospective study carried out, we have updated the burden of hospitalization for HMPV disease from 2016 to 2023 in Spain. In our study, we could see that the major hospitalization rate is found in children under 6 years old and adults over 60 years old, confirming the same epidemiologic pattern of other respiratory infections.

The analysis showed that, in children, the disease occurs mainly as bronchiolitis with pneumonia in second place; however, in adults, pneumonia is the main manifestation of HMPV disease and bronchitis takes the second place. Thus, we saw that the main load of hospitalization relies on children under 1 year old under the diagnostic of HMPV bronchiolitis. Also, we can see that, as in previous epidemiologic work, the most affected group are children under 1 year old [14, 15].

Altogether, hospitalization costs to the public system exceeded €22 million with an average of €2.8 million annually, and with a trend to increase. In addition, the sharp contrast between pre-, during-, and post-pandemic expenditures highlights the substantial and growing economic impact of this disease on the healthcare system.

The hospitalization for HMPV showed an important depletion in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, as was seen with other respiratory diseases due to the measures taken to control the spread of the virus [12, 16, 17]. However, during the last 2 years, we could see a rise in the number of cases of HMPV as seen in other countries [18–21]. This trend in HMPV has been seen with other respiratory viruses as a so-called “immunity debt” that can be the result of a reduced exposure.

Even though most of the cases of HMPV disease occur in children under 6 years old, adults over 60 years old concentrate near to 80% of the deaths from HMPV disease in Spain, assuming the highest case fatality rate. These results are very similar to other respiratory viruses, as epidemiological studies on respiratory syncytial virus and influenza also found this trend in children and adults over 60 [22, 23].

Through this study, we can also see the importance of comorbidities in respiratory disease. Comorbidities increase with age, and we could see how the presence of acute kidney failure is associated with a higher case fatality rate, as in other RTI [24, 25]. As well as kidney diseases, liver diseases are also associated with poor clinical outcomes on patients, with RTI being higher depending on the severity of the liver disease [26, 27]. For patients with cancer, especially those that are with compromised immune systems due to the treatments or to the disease itself, the mortality by RTI has increased [28].

With the results shown in this study, we can see the important groups that are at risk of hospitalization and at risk of death by HMPV disease. We can also see how the number of cases and costs have been increasing rapidly since the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite the relevance of RTI in the world and the growing importance of HMPV, it still remains underdiagnosed, possibly underestimating the magnitude of the cases in the population [29, 30]. The data showed two critical risk groups in our population, children under 6 years old and adults over 60 years old, that will be the focus of preventive measures.

The study has several limitations. As it relies on the MBDS information system, which records hospital discharges, there is a possibility of duplicate entries for patients who were hospitalized multiple times during the study period This limitation arises from data confidentiality constraints: many records lack a patient identifier, and the available identifiers are anonymized using algorithms, preventing verification of repeated hospitalizations for the same individual. This may have led to an overestimation of disease prevalence and an underestimation of lethality. Additionally, as the MBDS only includes hospitalized cases, it excludes those managed in primary care settings or identified through microbiological confirmation, potentially leading to an underrepresentation of the disease’s true impact. Moreover, it was not possible to link hospitalization records with primary care databases or mortality registries, preventing the tracking of post-discharge outcomes, long-term sequelae, or outpatient management. The low number of deaths in certain subgroups resulted in wide confidence intervals for mortality estimates, which should therefore be interpreted with caution. The dataset only includes binary indicators of comorbidities, without information on their severity. As a result, we were unable to compute standardized indices (e.g., Charlson Comorbidity Index) or incorporate comorbidity severity into multivariable models, limiting the precision of mortality risk estimates. Additionally, as this is a retrospective observational study, there is no laboratory confirmation of cases and associations between comorbidities, so outcomes should be interpreted cautiously. Cross-referencing with laboratory confirmation was not possible in this study, as the database does not include microbiological confirmation and we have no way of linking cases hospitalized with their PCRs. Nevertheless, the MBDS remains a highly valuable tool for clinical and epidemiological research. Given its extensive dataset, the presence of some duplicate records is unlikely to significantly influence the overall trends observed. In fact, the use of the MBDS is a major strength of this study, as its large sample size enhances representativeness and statistical power in the analysis of clinical outcomes such as case fatality rate. Moreover, the MBDS undergoes rigorous quality audits at the national level, further supporting the reliability of the data [31, 32].

On the other hand, relying on a nationwide hospital database means that data reliability is influenced by the quality of the discharge reports and the accuracy of the ICD-10 coding used to identify cases of pneumococcal infection. The transition from ICD-9-CM to ICD-10-CM, which began in 2016, may have affected the consistency of epidemiological analyses and complicates direct comparisons with hospitalization and mortality data previously published by this research group. To fully understand the impact of this coding transition, long-term epidemiological data will be essential [31, 32].

Another strength of this study is its 8-year data collection period (2016–2023), which offers insight into how the preventive measures implemented during the COVID-19 pandemic influenced the evolution of HMPV cases in Spain.

Enhancing HMPV surveillance and considering vaccination strategies could help reduce hospitalization and mortality associated with HMPV in Spain. Potential areas for future attention may include developing targeted prevention approaches for young children and older adults as vaccines become available; raising diagnostic awareness and expanding training to improve recognition of HMPV symptoms and routine testing; monitoring comorbid populations, particularly elderly individuals with kidney failure, liver disease, or cancer, to enable earlier intervention; and reinforcing post-COVID surveillance by strengthening monitoring systems to better track HMPV trends and to anticipate outbreaks.

Conclusion

Our study shows for the first time the epidemiological burden of HMPV disease on Spanish hospitals through hospitalization rate, morbidity and mortality. The HMPV disease affects mainly children and elderly people, with children the group with the most hospitalizations and elderly people the group with a higher morbidity. Also, this study shows the effect of some comorbidities on the outcome of the disease in elderly people. The results obtained represent a first step in identifying the needs and opportunities for the development of preventive policies.

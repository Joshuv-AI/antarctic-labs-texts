The influence maximization algorithm for integrating attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph transformer
Authors: Wenzhan Zhang, Ziyao Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In social networks, maximizing influence is an important research direction. However, traditional influence maximization algorithms often overlook the attribute information of nodes and the heterogeneity of networks, leading to inefficiency and inaccuracy in the propagation process. To address this issue, this study first constructs a social network influence maximization propagation model, and then combines auto-encoder and graph convolutional autoencoder to extract social network user attributes. Finally, Transformer is used to learn the feature representation of social network nodes. Moreover, a heterogeneous graph neural network is introduced to combine with Transformer to further learn the feature representation of nodes, so as to design an influence maximization algorithm combining attributed graph clustering and heterogeneous graph Transformer. The results showed that the loss values of the fusion algorithm on different datasets were 0.619 and 0.17, respectively, proving its good fitting performance. The recall rate and F1 of this fusion algorithm were 92.5 % and 0.90, respectively, proving its high clustering accuracy. The influence maximization model based on fusion algorithm achieved active node coverage of 67 % and 48 % on two datasets, respectively, proving its good influence propagation effect. The above results demonstrate that the designed model can effectively spread influence. This helps to better understand and utilize the influence dissemination mechanisms in social networks, thereby promoting the development of social network research.

Introduction

In today's era of information explosion, influence maximization has become a research hotspot in many fields. Maximizing influence aims to select a set of initial nodes to maximize their dissemination effect in social networks, thereby achieving the best promotion of the goal [1]. The solution to this problem is of great significance for social media marketing, disease prevention and control, information dissemination, and other aspects. In a traditional Influence Maximization Algorithm (IMA), social networks are typically modeled as graph structures. Graph-based propagation models are used to analyze and predict the propagation process of information [2]. However, traditional methods overlook the attribute information of users in social networks and the heterogeneity of the network, which may lead to inaccuracy and inefficiency in algorithms [3]. In practical applications, user attribute information is often closely related to their behaviors and influences in social networks. Therefore, how to better utilize user attribute information and network heterogeneity to improve the effectiveness of IMA has become an important direction of current research [4]. An effective method is to integrate user attribute information into the propagation model to more accurately predict the propagation path and influence of information in the network. In addition, heterogeneous graph converters can be used to handle heterogeneity in the network, embedding different types of nodes and edges for learning and feature extraction, thereby better reflecting the structure and relationships of the network [5,6]. Therefore, this study combines auto-encoders and graph convolutional auto-encoders to extract user attributes, while using Transformer to learn feature representations of social network nodes. Then, it further learns the graph topology structure through a Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network (HGNN). An IMA that integrates attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph Transformer is designed.

The innovation of the research lies in the fact that compared with existing algorithms, the designed algorithm effectively integrates the characteristics of graph convolutional auto-encoders and auto-encoders. At the same time, the designed heterogeneous graph Transformer better represents social network nodes and demonstrates stronger performance in handling complex interactions between different types of nodes and edges. The contribution of the research lies in the designed algorithm that utilize various techniques to integrate the attribute information of social network users and handle network heterogeneity. Compared with existing methods, this method can more effectively capture more complex network topology structures, which is of great significance for in-depth research on the structure and user behavior of social networks.

The research content consists of five parts. The first part is the research background of influence maximization. The next reviews the current research status on influence maximization. Then, the IMA fusing the attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph Transformer is designed. The fourth part analyzes experimental results for the designed model. Finally, it is a summary of the entire article and points out the shortcomings of the research.

Related works

With the popularization of social media, maximizing influence has become an important research field. This algorithm aims to maximize the impact of information dissemination by selecting a set of optimal seed nodes in social networks, and solve problems such as how to effectively promote products and disseminate information. In recent years, many scholars have conducted research in this field. C. Wang et al. designed a multi-transformation evolutionary framework with convergence guarantee to effectively evaluate the influence diffusion of seed sets in networks. Based on the alternating transformation of network prior knowledge, the framework induced different search behaviors with similar features for different perspectives. Experimental results showed that the framework could effectively utilize potential transferable knowledge across multiple transformations, which was significantly better than current popular methods [7]. H. Li et al. designed a Markov clustering method inspired by belief driven systems to gain a deeper understanding of social networks. This method optimized different quality metrics to obtain the global maximum value of the quality function, achieving stability of the dynamic system. Compared with state-of-the-art methods, this method ran faster and had lower computational complexity [8]. T. Chen et al. designed an end-to-end deep learning framework to select the subset of nodes that had the greatest impact on the network, which included three coupled graph neural networks for network embedding, dual deep Q-learning, and parameter learning. The results showed that the framework had strong generalization ability on different datasets [9]. Y. Yang et al. designed a fuzzy-based deep attribute graph clustering algorithm to solve the key dependency problem between node embedding and result clustering in social network propagation. Firstly, the network structure and node attributes were encoded into a compact representation using graph convolution, and then the reconstruction error was estimated to minimize the information loss in network message transmission. The results showed that the algorithm had superior performance on different datasets [10]. Q. Yang et al. designed a maximization algorithm based on influence maximization to solve the security exploration problem of unknown tasks in maximizing influence. It learned to maximize state entropy under secure conditions and evaluated the efficiency of exploration using the K-nearest entropy estimator. These results confirmed that this algorithm can improve the security of the target task [11]. D. D. Sivaganesan designed an interest-based parallel social behavior algorithm to identify highly influential users in social networks. It weighted user interaction behavior into social behavior and used semantic metrics to identify influential users, confirming that the algorithm had high computational efficiency [12].

D. Li et al. proposed an improved R-greedy algorithm to address the maximizing positive influence in social networks. It maximized positive impact and minimized negative impact by selecting a seed set. These experiments indicated that the method had high operational efficiency [13]. A. Rahmattalabi et al. proposed a framework based on social welfare theory to maximize the impact of fairness. It aggregated the cardinality utility of each community using an equal elasticity social welfare function. These results confirmed that this method had demonstrated effectiveness and high performance in case studies such as landslide risk management [14]. L. Wang et al. designed a discrete moth flame optimization method to enhance the performance of the algorithm for maximizing the influence of design networks. It evaluated the influence of nodes based on the total valuation and valuation variance of neighboring nodes, and optimized them using local crossover and mutation evolution schemes. These results confirmed that the method had high robustness [15]. Q. Ni et al. designed a continuous influence based method for community partitioning in social networks to address the issue of community partitioning. It divides social networks by maximizing the total amount of influence spread within each community, confirming that this method has high accuracy [16]. S. Kumar et al. designed a community-based propagation ranking method to find the most influential nodes in social networks. It identified influential nodes by identifying bridge nodes and important communities, and ranks nodes based on the concepts of community diversity, community modularity, and community density. These results confirmed that this method is superior to others [17]. Y. Li et al. designed a combination optimization method based on machine learning to maximize the influence of social networks by selecting a small number of users. It solved the influence maximization and other variants through deep reinforcement learning. These results confirmed that this method had a fast solving speed and strong generalization ability [18].

In summary, scholars have proposed various algorithms and methods to select the optimal seed node to maximize the impact of information dissemination. However, these studies still face issues such as high time complexity and ignoring individual differences. Some algorithms have slower processing times when dealing with large-scale social network nodes. Some studies often use a single model to evaluate and select seed nodes, which ignores the different positions and roles of different users in social networks. Therefore, this study proposes a fusion algorithm for maximizing influence. It combines auto-encoders and graph convolutional auto-encoders to extract user attributes and utilizes Transformer models to learn feature representations of social network nodes. It further learns the graph topology through HGNN to select seed nodes more accurately and maximize the influence of information propagation.

Design of influence maximization algorithm integrating attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph transformer

This chapter first simulates the influence spread in social networks by building IMA. Then the user's attributes are mined through attribute graph clustering to improve the accuracy of IMA. Finally, the heterogeneous graph Transformer is introduced to better capture the relationships between users and nodes, so as to improve the prediction accuracy and generalization ability.

Construction of social network influence maximization communication model

In social networks, IMA is a model used to determine which nodes are selected in the network to maximize information dissemination and influence diffusion [19]. It can predict the probability of information transmission at a node in the network, so as to find the most effective node. Common IMAS include threshold model, linear threshold model and activation propagation model. In the threshold model, each node has a threshold. If the number of nodes propagating information among their neighbors exceeds a threshold, the node will also propagate information [20]. In the linear threshold model, the influence weights between nodes are different. The influence value of each node can be calculated according to these weights. The activation propagation model takes more account of the interaction between nodes. The activation status of nodes affects each other, thus triggering a chain reaction of information dissemination [21].

Building IMAS can better understand information dissemination in networks and maximize the effectiveness of information dissemination by selecting appropriate nodes. The research combines the advantages of linear threshold model and independent cascade model, and constructs a hybrid maximum influence propagation model. This can better capture the behavior patterns of individuals in social networks and the propagation laws of information between different individuals. When spreading the influence of social networks, it is first necessary to set the threshold of each node to obtain its degree of acceptance of the influence [22]. When the total influence of neighbor nodes of a node exceeds the threshold of the node, the node is activated and propagates influence. According to the propagation law, the total fixed influence received by a node from all its neighbor nodes will not exceed a specific value. Generally speaking, the states of nodes in social networks are usually regarded as binary variables, namely, active state and inactive state. Specifically, when a node is inactive, it will not receive or propagate information. When the node is active, it can receive and spread information [23]. When an inactive node is first affected by an activated neighbor node, its influence is determined by a random variable. If the variable is greater than the preset threshold, the node is activated successfully. Otherwise, the node activation fails. Design of user information extraction algorithm based on self-supervised attribute graph clustering

To more accurately simulate the real social network, clustering algorithm is used to mine the attribute information of social network users. Social network can be regarded as a graph structure, in which nodes represent users and edges represent the interaction between users. Through clustering algorithm, users in social network can be classified according to their attributes, so as to better understand user behaviors and information dissemination mode [24].

Graph attribute clustering is usually used to process graph structure data. However, it is an unsupervised learning method, which cannot make full use of the label information of social network nodes [25]. Therefore, the study combines graph convolutional auto-encoder with auto-encoder, using the former to encode the structure information of graph data, and using auto-encoder to encode the attribute information of samples. Meanwhile, the relative entropy is introduced to optimize the loss function, and the feature extraction and fusion are supervised during clustering. A self-supervised attribute graph clustering algorithm is designed. The results obtained by this algorithm can take into account both the attribute information of the samples and the structural information between the samples, as shown in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Structure of self-supervised attribute graph clustering algorithm.Fig. 3

In Fig. 4, the self-supervised attribute graph clustering algorithm first encodes the structural and attribute information of the graph data into a compact representation form through graph embedding, obtaining the embedding layer of the graph. Design of social network node representation method based on heterogeneous graph transformer

In social networks, nodes are the key to understand the network structure and user behavior. However, traditional node representation methods often only focus on the attribute information of the node itself, and ignore the interaction between nodes and network structure [27]. Therefore, the attribute characteristics of users are clustered. After mining the user's attribute information, to better capture the various relationships between users and nodes, the study uses Transformer to learn the feature representation of social network nodes. It can more accurately simulate the complex behavior and information dissemination mode of social networks. However, there are differences in the attention of each node to its neighbor nodes. Therefore, HGNN is introduced to further learn more complex graph topology, so as to design a social network node representation method based on heterogeneous graph Transformer in Fig. 5.Fig. 5The framework for social network node representation methods.Fig. 5

The framework for social network node representation methods.

In Transformer, the feature set of each node is input into the model, and the attention value of the node and its neighbor nodes is calculated through the multi-head self-attention mechanism to learn the feature representation of each node. These attention values reflect the attention of nodes to their neighbors, so as to capture the complex relationship and structural information between nodes. The Transformer's attention is calculated through three vectors, as expressed by equation (11).(11){Q=HWQU=HWHJ=HWJE=(QUT)/dKAtt(U)=softmax(E)JIn equation (12), Q, U, and J are the multi-headed attention in Transformer. WQ, WH, and WJ represent the mapping matrix of Q, U, and J, respectively. H represents the input of the self-attention module. E stands for expectation. Att(⋅) stands for the attention of H. softmax(⋅) stands for normalized exponential function. The attention coefficient is the quantification of the attention between nodes and their neighbors. Therefore, the next step is to calculate the attention coefficient between nodes and adjacent nodes, and calculate the final output characteristics of each node according to the attention coefficient, which is expressed by equation (12).(12){oij=a(Whi,Whj)hi′=σ(∑j∈NioijlWkhj)In equation (12), oij represents the attention coefficient between nodes. a stands for the attention mechanism. After learning the feature representation of social network nodes, the learned node feature representation is input into HGNN to further learn more complex graph topology. HGNN learns the embedded representation of each node by calculating the attention value of the node and its neighbors and the attention value of the node and its adjacent edges. These embedded representations capture the interaction relationship and information dissemination mode between nodes, so as to better represent the structure and behavior in social networks. After obtaining the node feature representation, traditional methods often use the dot product between the obtained feature vectors to evaluate the similarity between node pairs. But in some special cases, they cannot capture the node domain relationship well. Therefore, a more detailed method can be used to measure the differences between node characteristics. The Euclidean norm is used to measure the length or distance of the vector, the node characteristics are more accurately distinguished, and the ability to understand the neighbor relationship of nodes is improved [30,31]. The advantage of this method is that it can more accurately measure the similarity and influence between nodes, so as to improve the analysis and interpretation ability of node relationships in graph data. When the similarity between u and v is high, they are more likely to influence each other. On the contrary, the degree of influence between them is low. Analysis of influence maximization algorithm combining attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph transformer

This chapter mainly describes the experimental results of the designed fusion algorithm. The first is the performance evaluation of IMA on different datasets. The second section mainly verifies the effect of this model in practical application.

Conclusion

In recent years, with the development of social and information networks, influence maximization has been widely concerned in social network analysis and recommendation systems. However, due to the complexity of social networks and the heterogeneity of information dissemination, the traditional IMA is often limited by the lack of comprehensive utilization between node attribute information and network structure. Therefore, the study first combined the advantages of the linear threshold model and the independent cascade model to construct a mixed influence maximization propagation model. Then, the graph convolutional auto-encoder and auto-encoder were combined to extract the user attribute information. HGNN was introduced to further study the graph structure. An IMA based on the attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph Transformer was designed. These results confirm that the accuracy of the fusion algorithm was 95.6 % on Iris and 94.5 % on MNIST, which proved that its clustering accuracy was high. In MNIST, the precision and NMI of the fusion algorithm were 96.64 % and 95.78 %, respectively, which proved that its clustering performance was better. On these two datasets, the running time of IMA based on fusion algorithm was 2.52s and 3.42s, respectively, which showed that its computational efficiency was high. These above results prove the effectiveness and performance of IMA integrating attribute graph clustering and heterogeneous graph Transformer. However, the study does not consider the role characteristics of users in social networks when constructing models, resulting in insufficient interest extraction of users in social networks. In future research, interest mining techniques can be introduced based on the research to consider the impact of interest matching between different users on influence propagation, thereby enhancing the application scope of IMA.

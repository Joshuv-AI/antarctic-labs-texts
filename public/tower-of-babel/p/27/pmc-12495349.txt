In-situ synthesized porphyrinic zirconium MOF-derived multilayered nanocomposite probe for ultra-sensitive immunosensor
Authors: Xiangrui Li, Huan Lv, Xinyu Zhang, Wenjia Yang, Wencan Luo, Linghong Kong, Lu Zeng, Qiujin Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAflatoxin B1 (AFB1) is a major concern due to its widespread contamination and potential harm. Colorimetric lateral flow immunoassays (LFIAs) incorporating gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) are widely used for on-site analyte detection. However, it remains challenging due to the limited brightness of AuNPs. Herein, we introduce an immunosensor for highly sensitive AFB1 detection utilizing a multilayered nanocomposite probe (PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP), created by sequentially modifying Fe3O4 nanoparticles, polydopamine layers, and horseradish peroxidase on a porphyrinic metal-organic framework, which offers magnetic separation, high signal brightness, and strong catalytic activity. Integrated with a competitive assay format, the immunosensor achieved ultrasensitive detection of AFB1, with a detection range of 0.003–3 ng/mL and a limit of detection (LOD) of 0.0014 ng/mL. Moreover, our immunosensor exhibited satisfactory recoveries and successful detection of AFB1 in cereal samples. Therefore, this work offers innovative strategies for designing multi-functional nanocomposite probes that enhance detection sensitivity, holding great promise in food safety monitoring.Keywords: Aflatoxin B1, Immunosensor, Multifunctional nanocomposite, Porphyrinic metal-organic framework, Catalytic activity

Introduction

Aflatoxin B1 (AFB1) is considered one of the most dangerous mycotoxins, because of its high toxicity, as well as serious carcinogenic, teratogenic, and mutagenic threats to human and animal health (Wang et al., 2023). Conventional detection methods for AFB1 include chromatographic, spectroscopic, and electrochemical techniques, as well as immunoassays, such as liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) (García-Nicolás et al., 2023), high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Keskin & Eyupoglu, 2023), Raman spectroscopy (Deng et al., 2022), and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (Yan et al., 2022). However, most of these techniques, despite having high accuracy and reliability, require specialized and expensive equipment, complex sample pretreatment, and skilled technicians. The lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) is a particularly valuable supplement to instrumental methods. LFIA is frequently used because of its simplicity, rapidity, affordability, and point-of-care applicability (Zheng et al., 2022). However, LFIAs based on a signal probe comprising traditional gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) rely on a single colorimetric signal output, thus resulting in limited detection accuracy. AuNP-based LFIA exhibits a sensitivity approximately one to three orders of magnitude lower than that of methods such as chemiluminescence, ELISA, and electrochemiluminescence (Chen et al., 2020). Therefore, developing a highly sensitive immunosensor is imperative for detecting AFB1 at trace levels.

Multifunctional nanocomposites with colorimetric, magnetic, and catalytic activity have gained interest as colorimetric probes for improving immunosensor performance (Liu et al., 2024). Porous materials with dense surfaces and high numbers of adsorption sites are promising precursors for the preparation of composite nanomaterials (Xu et al., 2022). Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are a novel class of porous crystalline materials that self-assemble via bond coordination, with organic ligands acting as linkers and metal ions functioning as nodes (Wright et al., 2024). The intrinsic properties of MOFs include widely tunable composition and high internal surface area (Hao et al., 2025). Consequently, MOFs are useful for integration with various functional materials, such as organic small molecules, metals, polymers, and enzymes (Zhao et al., 2025). Among the MOFs, the porous coordination network (PCN) is a novel class of Zr(IV)-based MOFs. One example, PCN-224, features a network structure, is based on a six-connected Zr6 cluster, and uses tetrakis (4-carboxyphenyl) porphyrin (H2TCPP) as the ligand. It exhibits excellent water stability, water dispersibility, colorimetric properties, and biocompatibility, and therefore is a promising biological platform for detecting harmful substances and other analytes.

The presence of impurities and interference in actual samples can compromise the target analyte detection accuracy of LFIA. To mitigate the effects of complex sample matrices, composite nanomaterials based on Fe3O4, a magnetic nanoparticle that generates color signals, are increasingly being developed. LFIAs based on Fe3O4 complexes have been successfully applied to the detection of mycotoxins, pesticides, and bacteria (Zhao et al., 2023). These assays simultaneously perform three functions. First, they use magnetism for the separation and concentration determination of target substances. Second, they feature colorimetric signal transduction, thus producing a detectable brownish-yellow color. Finally, they amplify the signal through enzymatic activity, specifically via peroxidase analog activity (Gao et al., 2007). However, the inherent magnetic shielding effect of Fe3O4 nanoparticles decreases both the magnetization and catalytic activity of Fe3O4 nanocomposites combined with other nanomaterials (Zhai et al., 2024). To maintain high catalytic performance and superparamagnetism of composite nanomaterials, rational design and strategic loading of biological enzyme molecules on their surfaces are considered an effective approach. Among enzymes, horseradish peroxidase (HRP) is widely used in bio-catalysis, because of its high bioactivity, specificity, and selectivity (Bilal et al., 2020). HRP-based nanocomplexes have gained substantial attention as promising signal reporters in colorimetric immunosensors, because of their ability to sustain the high catalytic activity of HRP (Qiao et al., 2024). While promoting enzyme/Ab loading, MOFs can operate functionalized modifications for various applications. Dopamine (DA) readily oxidizes and polymerizes into polydopamine (PDA), which adheres to nearly all organic and inorganic surfaces through covalent and non-covalent bonds (Liu et al., 2018). Consequently, PDA is often used as a bridge between MOFs and biomolecules.

On the basis of the above considerations, we used PCN-224 as a precursor to generate Fe3O4 nanoparticles in situ on its surface, then coated with a PDA layer via an oxidative self-polymerization assembly strategy. The PDA coating, rich in hydroxyl and amino groups, provided a flexible scaffold for the stable attachment of Ab/HRP to the surface layer. Thus, we developed a PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP nanocomposite probe exhibiting enhanced colorimetric signal intensity, rapid magnetic response, and excellent catalytic performance. PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP captured AFB1 and subsequently generated a brown colorimetric signal. Signal amplification was achieved by catalysis of the chromogenic substrate 3-amino-9-ethylcarbazole (AEC). The rapid magnetic response enabled efficient enrichment of target analytes in complex sample matrices. After integrating the probe with a competitive immunosensor, we demonstrated that the AEC chromogenic system enhanced the colorimetric signal and achieved highly sensitive detection of AFB1. The proposed PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP nanocomposite probe is a promising colorimetric signal amplifier that may expand the applications of immunosensors for detecting hazardous substances.

Experimental section

Synthesis of PCN-224@Fe3O4

The preparation of PCN-224@Fe3O4 was based on a previously reported method with slight adjustments (Li et al., 2020). Specifically, 100 mg of 5, 10, 15, 20-tetrakis (4-carboxyphenyl) porphyrin, 300 mg of zirconyl chloride octahydrate (ZrOCl2·8H2O), and 2.8 g of benzoic acid were co-dissolved in 100 mL of N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF). The mixture was stirred (300 rpm) at 90 °C for 5 h. After cooling of the solution to room temperature (RT, 26 ± 2 °C), we isolated the PCN-224 nanoparticles by centrifugation at 10,000 rpm for 30 min, then washed them with fresh DMF three times. PCN-224@Fe3O4 was synthesized by further reaction of the obtained PCN-224 nanoparticles. Initially, 40 mL of deionized water was mixed with 0.1 g of PCN-224 and ultrasonically dispersed for 30 min. Subsequently, 0.8 g of FeCl3·6H2O and 0.3 g of FeCl2·4H2O were added. The reaction mixture was then vigorously stirred (800 rpm) for 15 min to ensure sufficient contact and reaction between the PCN-224 surface and the Fe3O4 precursor. Subsequently, 10 mL ammonia solution (25 %) was added, and stirring continued at 85 °C for 45 min. The mixture was collected and subjected to three rounds of ethanol washing in the presence of an external magnetic field. Finally, the PCN-224@Fe3O4 nanoparticles were dried at 60 °C and stored until further use.

Preparation of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA

PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA was synthesized via a one-pot procedure. Initially, PCN-224@Fe3O4 composite (3 mg) was uniformly dispersed in 75 % ethanol solvent (3 mL) through ultrasonic techniques. Subsequently, 0.01 M Tris-HCl buffer solution (2 mL) was added to the ethanol dispersion, and the pH was precisely adjusted to 8.5. DA solution (10 mg/mL, 140 μL) was then added dropwise to the pH-adjusted mixture under continuous stirring (350 rpm) to ensure uniform contact between DA and the PCN-224@Fe3O4 surface, thereby promoting a polymerization reaction. The polymerization proceeded at RT for 6 h, thus forming PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA nanoparticles. After the reaction, the nanoparticles were isolated via magnetic separation and washed three times with ethanol to remove unreacted materials and impurities. Finally, the purified nanoparticles were redispersed in 1 mL of 0.01 M phosphate buffer solution (PBS) at pH 7.4 for subsequent experiments.

Preparation of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe

One-pot assembly was used to generate the antibody (Ab) and free HRP-modified PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA (PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP) probe. The Abs used in this study were produced in our laboratory (preparation and characterization results in Fig. S1-S3 and Table S1). In brief, 1 mL 0.01 M PBS solution was used to re-solubilize 1 mg PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA. Subsequently, 12 μg anti-AFB1 Abs and 600 μg HRP were added and shaken for 2 h. To block unreacted binding sites, we added 100 μL BSA solution (10 % w/v) to the mixture, which we incubated for an additional 2 h. The mixture was then centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 min. The precipitate was re-suspended in 1 mL of 0.02 M Tris-HCl with 0.1 % Tween-20, 5 % sucrose, 1 % polyvinylpyrrolidone, 0.2 % BSA, 0.1 % Kathon, and 0.1 % ON-870 after the supernatant was discarded. The Ab and free HRP-modified PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA probe was stored at 4 °C until further use (Liu et al., 2023).

Establishment of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor

The PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe (10 μL) was added to 140 μL of a sample solution consisting of 100 μL of AFB1 standard solution and 40 μL of Tris-HCl buffer (pH 8.2, 0.02 M). The mixture was allowed to react for 5 min, after which the resulting PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-AFB1 complex was collected under an external magnetic field for 30 s. The complex was resuspended in 100 μL of Tris-HCl buffer and transferred to the sample pad of the test strip. After 15 min, the test strip was immersed in a commercial substrate solution containing AEC substrate and 1 M H2O2 for enzyme-catalyzed signal amplification via deposition. After the reaction had proceeded for 10 min, the color intensity of the C and T lines on the test strip was quantitatively measured with a scan reader.

Sample analysis

Maize, peanuts, and wheat were selected as real samples to evaluate the reliability of AFB1 detection by the constructed immunosensor. The standard addition method was applied to calculate the recovery rate. AFB1 was spiked into the maize, peanut, and wheat samples at various concentrations (0.03, 0.3, and 3.0 μg/kg). For sample extraction, 1 g of ground powder was placed into a 15 mL centrifuge tube, and 4 mL of a 70:30 (v/v) methanol-water solution was added. The mixture was then centrifuged at 6000 rpm for 10 min, and the supernatant was separated. The extract was subsequently diluted with PBS for further analysis. Additionally, ten cereal samples purchased from MEIZHENG Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) were detected in a blinded manner with the immunosensor, then validated with LC-MS/MS. Also, three certified reference material (CRM) flours (GBW10219, GBW(E)100,861, GBW(E)100,607) with certified AFB1 were purchased from the National Institute of Metrology, China (Beijing, China), and were determined using the immunosensor and LC-MS/MS. Each sample was tested in triplicate.

Results and discussion

Principle of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor

Fig. 1 illustrates the principle of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor for the ultrasensitive detection of AFB1. PCN-224 was first synthesized with a hot solvent method, and two iron ion compounds were used to generate Fe3O4 nanocomposite on the PCN-224 surface under the action of ammonia. Subsequently, PCN-224@Fe3O4 nanoparticles were coated with a uniform PDA shell through dispersion in an alkaline DA solution, to facilitate Ab/HRP coupling (Fig. 1A). The formed multi-functional PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe exhibited (I) simplified sample pretreatment with magnetic Fe3O4 NPS; (II) excellent optical properties and high affinity for Ab/HRP through use of the PDA layer as a flexible scaffold; and (III) superior catalytic activity, with use of the AEC chromogenic substrate system to further amplify the colorimetric signal. The prepared “three-in-one” multifunctional nanoprobe was integrated with the immunosensor to construct a highly sensitive detection system. As illustrated in Fig. 1B, the competitive format immunosensor was developed. The goat anti-mouse IgG and coating antigen (AFB1-CMO-OVA) were immobilized on an NC membrane to form the C and T lines. In the presence of AFB1, it was first captured by PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probes, thereby forming an immunocomplex, namely, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-AFB1. Subsequently, the immune complexes were collected with an external magnetic field, redispersed in Tris buffer solution, and uniformly applied to the surface of the test paper. The capillary effect resulted in relatively few probes being captured by the AFB1-CMO-OVA immobilized at the T-line. With increasing AFB1 concentration, the T line gradually became lighter and subsequently disappeared. Goat anti-mouse IgG sprayed on the C line captured both free and bound PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probes simultaneously. Consequently, the C line consistently exhibited color. The PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe directly interacted with the goat anti-mouse IgG on the C line and the AFB1-CMO-OVA on the T line in the absence of AFB1, thus leading to the formation of a distinct brown color on both lines. After completion of the reaction, a mixture of H2O2 and AEC was added to the test paper, and the catalytic efficacy of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP complex was used to enhance the colorimetric signal. After 10 min, the appearance of only one line (C line) on the test paper indicated a positive test result. In contrast, two lines (T and C lines) that were simultaneously visible indicated a negative test result.

Synthesis and characterization of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA

To observe the morphology and structural evolution of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA material during the preparation process, we used scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) for detailed characterization and analysis. As depicted in Fig. 2A and Fig. 2D, the synthesized PCN-224 nanoparticles exhibited a spherical shape with excellent uniformity and dispersion. Subsequently, a one-pot method was used to generate Fe3O4 nanoparticles in situ on the PCN-224 surface through the introduction of two iron ion compounds. As shown in Fig. 2B and Fig. 2E, numerous Fe3O4 nanoparticles were densely adsorbed onto the PCN-224 surface, thus endowing the composite with strong magnetic properties. SEM indicated that the loading of Fe3O4 led to the formation of spherical particles on the surface of PCN-224, thus significantly increasing the surface roughness. Through dispersion of PCN-224@Fe3O4 nanoparticles in an alkaline DA solution at RT with stirring, the PCN-224@Fe3O4 nanoparticles were coated with a uniform PDA shell (Fig. 2C and Fig. 2F). In SEM images, the surface of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA appeared smoother than that of PCN-224@Fe3O4, because of the PDA coating. High-angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy mapping (Fig. 2G and Fig. S4) indicated that the designed PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA showed a unique layer-by-layer growth structure and uniform and orderly element distribution. These findings demonstrated that the Fe3O4 nanoparticles and PDA had been successfully loaded on the PCN-224 surface without destroying its structure. Fourier-transform infrared spectra of PCN-224, PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA are shown in Fig. 2H. The stretching vibration peak of Zr-OH was at 650 cm−1, thereby confirming the synthesis of PCN-224 (Ma et al., 2021). The band observed around 600 cm−1 split into two distinct peaks at 633 cm−1 and 572 cm−1, corresponding to the Fe—O stretching vibrations, thereby indicating the emergence of iron oxide in PCN-224@Fe3O4 (Li, Shang, et al., 2024). Furthermore, phenolic O—H and N—H stretching vibrations, phenolic C-O-H bending and stretching vibrations, and N—H shearing vibrations were linked to the novel absorption peaks at 3412 cm−1, 1283 cm−1, 1394 cm−1, and 1633 cm−1, respectively, thus indicating successful probe preparation (Han et al., 2021). Interestingly, the absorption peak at 1420 cm−1 indicated COO− bonds shifted to 1394 cm−1. This observation confirmed the formation of hydrogen bonds between the PDA coating and PCN-224, because of the high concentration of amino groups present in the PDA and the presence of carboxyl groups in the porphyrin ring (Li et al., 2023). Furthermore, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) additionally verified the successful synthesis. As illustrated in Fig. 2I, the XPS results revealed that the components of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA included Fe, O, N, and C. Four distinct peaks were observed at 710.7, 540.0, 399.8, and 284.8 eV for Fe 2p, O 1 s, N 1 s, and C 1 s, respectively. The loading of Fe3O4 nanoparticles increased the Fe content. Additionally, the disappearance of the characteristic peak of the Zr element indicated that PDA had been successfully wrapped in the outermost layer. Similar changes were observed in the XRD results, which confirmed the synthesis of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA (Fig. S5). Furthermore, PCN-224, PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and Fe3O4 had ζ-potentials of −4.0, −8.6, −23.8 and − 3.8 mV, respectively (Fig. 2J). The presence of -OH groups on the PDA coating resulted in a lower ζ-potential for PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA than PCN-224@Fe3O4. These results further indicated that PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA was successfully synthesized. Finally, we characterized the coupling of Ab/HRP with UV–visible spectra and ζ-potential. As shown in Fig. S6A, Ab and HRP exhibited distinct absorption peaks at 280 nm and 404 nm, respectively. These peaks remained present in the spectrum of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP, thus confirming successful conjugation. Additionally, ζ-potential analysis (Fig. S6B) revealed a shift in potential from −28.33 mV for PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA to −26.29 mV after Ab/HRP labeling, in accordance with findings from previous studies (Song et al., 2023), thereby suggesting the presence of electrostatic interactions between Ab/HRP and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA. The pore size of this composite nanoprobe was subsequently examined with BET (Fig. S7). A pore size of 2.519 nm was found, which was attributed to the loading of the PDA layer resulting in a decrease in the pore size of the composite nanomaterials. The above results indicated the successful coupling of Ab/HRP to PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA.

Optical, magnetic and catalytic properties of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA

The absorbance characteristics of colorimetric probes have crucial roles in enhancing the sensitivity of immunosensors (Chen et al., 2022). The UV–visible spectra of PCN-224, PCN-224@Fe3O4, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA at the same particle concentrations were recorded (Fig. 3A). PCN-224 exhibited a primary absorption peak at 420 nm and four weaker peaks between 500 and 700 nm, corresponding to the Q bands of porphyrin-like metal-organic framework materials. This finding indicated the successful preparation of PCN-224 (Wang et al., 2018). For PCN-224@Fe3O4, the introduction of metal ions resulted in a single absorption peak in the Q band and the disappearance of the other peaks. This finding was attributed to increased structural symmetry during the formation of metalloporphyrin (Lai et al., 2024). PDA exhibited no characteristic peaks in this range, and its absorbance gradually decreased with increasing wavelength. However, after sequential modification of Fe3O4 and PDA on the PCN-224 surface at the same concentration, the absorbance gradually increased as the solution color deepened, thus validating the successful synthesis of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA nanocomposites, as well as the gradual improvement in colorimetric ability (Tehrani Nejad et al., 2024). In addition, a comparison of the optical densities of PCN-224, PCN-224@Fe3O4, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA on NC membranes with the same molar concentration (Fig. 3B) yielded similar results to those in Fig. 3A. Moreover, the performance on NC membranes continued to show a gradually increasing trend. The above results indicated the promise of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA as a colorimetric composite.

We examined the vibrating sample magnetometry analysis curve (Fig. 3C) to assess the magnetic properties of the as-prepared Fe3O4 nanoparticles, PCN-224@ Fe3O4, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA across a magnetic field range of −20,000 to +20,000 Oe. The saturation magnetization values of the prepared Fe3O4 nanoparticles, PCN-224@Fe3O4, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA were 74.62, 63.43 and 51.24 emu g−1, respectively. The Fe3O4 nanoparticles were loaded onto the PCN-224 surface and rendered the PCN-224@Fe3O4 magnetic. The saturation magnetization values weakened with further PDA encapsulation. The magnetic properties of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA nanocomposite remained sufficiently strong to achieve rapid separation within 30s.

The catalytic colorimetric ability of nanoparticles is crucial in the evaluation of their signal amplification efficacy as simulated peroxidases (Lee et al., 2024). Accordingly, we analyzed the catalytic properties of three composites: PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP. Specifically, we tested their ability to oxidize 3,3,5,5-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) in the presence of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂). Without the catalytic reaction, the solution colors of PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP at the same particle concentrations were gradually enhanced with the loading of the Fe3O4 nanoparticles and PDA on the surface of PCN-224 (Fig. 3D, F, H). The lowest detected concentrations for PCN-224@Fe3O4 (Fig. 3E), PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA (Fig. 3G), and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP (Fig. 3I) were 3.98, 1.99, and 0.49 fM, respectively, on the basis of absorbance at 450 nm. A similar increase in solution coloration was observed with the addition of PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and PCN-224@ Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP to oxidize TMB. The catalytic kinetic profile and the UV–visible spectra pertaining to the oxidation of TMB are depicted in Fig. S8. The lowest detected concentrations of PCN-224@Fe3O4 (Fig. 3E), PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA (Fig. 3G), and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP (Fig. 3I) were 0.9961, 1.99, and 0.06 fM, respectively, on the basis of the absorbance at 650 nm. Therefore, the catalytic activity of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP was 16.5 times and 33.2 times that of PCN-224@Fe3O4 and PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, respectively, primarily because of the excellent catalytic performance of Fe3O4 nanoparticles; the large pore structures of the PCN-224 metal-organic framework; and the coating with the PDA layer, which increased HRP coupling.

We assessed the apparent steady-state kinetic analyses of PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP, and HRP to gain insights into their catalytic efficiency (Fig. S9-S12). Several key parameters were evaluated with Michaelis−Menten equations (with varying TMB and H2O2 concentrations), including the maximum reaction rate (Vmax), catalytic constants (Kcat), and Michaelis-Menten constant (Km). As presented in Table S2, the Kcat of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP to TMB reached 43.2 × 106 s−1, which was approximately 24.13-, 44.67-, and 369.23-fold greater than those of PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and free HRP, respectively. Moreover, the Kcat of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP toward H2O2 reached 9.69 × 106 s−1, which was approximately 7.23-, 18.63-, and 107-fold higher than those of PCN-224@Fe3O4, PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA, and free HRP, respectively. To further assess the enzyme stability, we monitored the changes in catalytic activity of both the probe and free HRP after storage at 4 °C for 1, 7, 14, and 20 days. As depicted in Fig. S13, after 20 days of storage at 4 °C, the catalytic activity of PCN-224@Fe₃O₄@PDA@Ab/HRP decreased to 81.0 %, whereas that of free HRP declined to 78.0 %. The experimental results indicated that the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP composite showed excellent catalytic activity, thus highlighting its significant potential as a high-performance signal amplification label after strip testing.

Optimization of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor

To optimize the assay efficacy of the immunosensor, we implemented a series of systematic measures to adjust several key parameters, specifically the amount of DA added, the concentration of Ab, the volume of the probe solution, and the duration of the reaction. Specifically, the T/C value and the inhibition rate were used as the main discriminatory criteria, where T and C are the T line and C line readings, respectively, obtained with a strip reader. The amount of added DA is critical for constructing the PDA layer, and influences its thickness and dispersion uniformity, and the signal output intensity of the final particles (Pang et al., 2022). With increasing DA amounts, the color signal of the test strip gradually increased; however, when the DA amount exceeded 1.4 mg, the inhibition effect gradually deteriorated (Fig. 4A). Therefore, a DA amount of 1.4 mg was selected. For a competitive assay format, the Ab quantity directly affected the assay sensitivity of the established immunosensor (Li, Yang, et al., 2024). Increasing color intensity of the test strip was observed (Fig. 4B). However, the decreasing trend in the inhibition rate observed with the use of Ab beyond 12 μg suggested that excessive amounts of Ab might negatively influence the sensitivity of the immunosensor. In addition, the volume of the probe directly influenced the signal intensity and sensitivity of the immunosensor (Li et al., 2024). The maximal inhibition ratio was achieved with a 10 μL probe and decreased with greater probe volumes (Fig. 4C). Thus, 10 μL was considered the optimum probe volume. Additionally, the immunoreaction time for the immunosensor was optimized. Because of the limitations of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe, the T-line intensity and inhibition rate peaked at 15 min (Fig. 4D). Therefore, 15 min was determined to be the optimal immunoreaction time.

Analytical performance evaluation of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor

To evaluate the analytical performance of the developed immunosensor, we tested a range of concentrations of the AFB1 standard solution (0–3 ng/mL). After completion of the reaction, the colorimetric signal was amplified through PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based enzyme deposition with an AEC chromogenic system. With use of a commercial test strip reader and a digital camera, test results showing two clear lines, specifically the test line (T line) and the control line (C line), were deemed negative; in contrast, results with only the control line (C line) visible were considered positive. The AFB1 threshold concentration that caused all colors on the T-line to completely vanish was used to define the cut-off value (Zeng et al., 2025). The T-line intensity decreased as the concentrations of AFB1 continuously increased (Fig. 5A and Fig. 5B). The cut-off values of the immunosensor before and after catalysis by the AEC chromogenic system were 0.3 and 3.0 ng/mL, respectively. Standard curves were plotted to compare T/C values before and after catalysis with AFB1 concentrations (Fig. 5C and Fig. 5D). A linear relationship between the T-line intensity of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-LFIA and the AFB1 concentrations was found in the dynamic range from 0.003 ng/mL to 0.3 ng/mL. The regression equation was described as Y=−0.19124lgx+0.20286R2=0.991. For the catalytic signal, the linear regression equation was Y=−0.24443lgx+0.11899R2=0.993, with a linear range of 0.003–3 ng/mL. The limit of detection (LOD) was calculated asσ′−3SD, where σ′ represents the average grayscale intensity of negative samples, and SD is the standard deviation of the grayscale intensity at the T line of negative samples. The LOD was calculated to be as low as 0.0014 ng/mL. The specificity of the immunosensor for the detection of AFB1 was investigated by the detection of five interfering mycotoxins—T-2 toxin (T-2), fumonisin B1 (FB1), ochratoxin A (OTA), deoxynivalenol (DON), and zearalenone (ZEN)—as well as AFB1 at 1.0 ng/mL. PBS served as a negative control. The T-line color disappeared only in the presence of AFB1 (Fig. 5E). In contrast, for other small molecules, strong brown bands were observed on the T line. The developed immunosensor demonstrated good specificity for detecting AFB1, as shown by the preceding results. The probe was stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C to assess its stability. As shown in Fig. S14, after 20 days of storage, the T/C values of the immunosensor bands slightly decreased, but their inhibition rates remained largely unchanged, thus indicating that the probe exhibited favorable stability. The storage boundary conditions of the immunosensor, including temperature (4 °C, 26 °C and 37 °C) and humidity (20 % RH, 30 % RH and 60 % RH), were also evaluated. As shown in Fig. S15, short-term fluctuations in temperatures at 4 °C, 26 °C and humidity at 20 % RH, 30 % RH and 60 % RH had minimal impact on the immunosensor. Although the inhibition rate of the test strip exhibited a slight decrease at 20 days under the 37 °C accelerated test conditions, the immunosensor has demonstrated robust resistance to environmental interference. Finally, we evaluated the reproducibility of PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-LFIA by analyzing maize powder samples with different spiked concentrations (0.03, 0.3, and 3 μg/kg). We repeated the analysis ten times for each concentration and recorded the assay values (Fig. 5F), thus demonstrating that the test strips reproducibly and stably detected AFB1.

Accuracy and reproducibility of the developed immunosensor

The accuracy of the constructed immunosensor for AFB1 was assessed. Matrix effects are a common issue that can significantly impact the sensitivity of detection methods. Sample dilution is one of the most effective and direct approaches to mitigate these effects. As shown in Fig. S16, after a 10-fold dilution of maize, peanut, and wheat samples, the detection performance became comparable to that of the PBS buffer, with matrix effects essentially eliminated. Spiked recovery experiments were then conducted by adding three levels (0.03, 0.3, and 3 μg/kg) of AFB1 to the samples, followed by a 10-fold dilution. The label probe and pretreatment sample solution were mixed and applied to the test strips for quantitative analysis with a strip reader. The recovery experiments were repeated three times in parallel with LC-MS/MS as a reference. The instrument conditions for the LC-MS/MS detection are listed in Table S3 and Table S4. The fragment ions for AFB1 were observed at m/z 241.0, 269.1, and 285.0. The ion at m/z 285.0 was selected as the quantification ion, whereas m/z 241.0 was used as the qualitative ion (Fig. S17). As shown in Table 1, the LFIA achieved average recoveries in maize, peanut and wheat samples ranging from 90.0 % to 93.3 %, from 83.3 % to 91.0 %, and from 98.0 % to 106.7 %, respectively. The coefficients of variation (CVs) ranged from 4.30 % to 11.11 %, from 6.59 % to 8.00 %, and from 6.46 % to 9.69 %, respectively. The LC-MS/MS indicated average recoveries in maize, peanut, and wheat ranging from 82.3 % to 93.9 %, from 86.7 % to 99.3 %, and from 96.3 % to 100.0 %, respectively; the CVs ranged from 5.67 % to 10.71 %, from 7.05 % to 11.54 %, and from 7.27 % to 10.34 %, respectively. Thus, acceptable CVs and satisfactory recoveries were obtained for AFB1 detection and showed strong correlation with those of LC-MS/MS. To further validate the accuracy of this method, three CRMs with AFB1 concentrations of 30.6, 8.3, and 10.3 μg/kg (σ = 4.0, 1.5 and 1.4 μg/kg, respectively) were analyzed using both the immunosensor and LC-MS/MS. The AFB1 levels measured by the immunosensor were 31.42 ± 2.21 μg/kg, 8.43 ± 0.75 μg/kg, and 11.02 ± 0.95 μg/kg, respectively, compared to the values obtained by LC-MS/MS, which were 29.74 ± 2.01 μg/kg, 8.12 ± 0.67 μg/kg, and 10.14 ± 0.91 μg/kg (Table 2). The results from the immunosensor showed a good correlation with those from LC-MS/MS. The aforementioned findings indicated that our immunosensor has great potential and is practically applicable for rapidly and accurately screening food samples for AFB1.

Real samples analysis

We also evaluated the practical applicability and reliability of the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP-based immunosensor for AFB1 detection. As shown in Table 3, the immunosensor was used to analyze AFB1 in ten maize powder samples in a blinded manner, and LC-MS/MS was used for validation. Samples 3 and 7 were consistently identified as AFB1 positive by both techniques. The mean AFB1 concentrations in sample 3 and sample 7 were 1.3 ± 0.02 μg/kg and 2.8 ± 0.042 μg/kg, respectively, whereas AFB1 was not detected in the other samples. Minimal variation with respect to the LC-MS/MS results was observed. In summary, our results suggest that this technology offers broad application possibilities and reliability in detecting AFB1 in complex food matrices.

Comparison of method performance

To date, various signal-based immunoassays have been proposed for the rapid detection of AFB1, including electrochemical (He et al., 2024; Yue et al., 2022), colorimetric, fluorescence (Girmatsion et al., 2025), photothermal, and multiple signals. Current research efforts are focused on sensitivity enhancement, linear range expansion, and improvement of field applicability for these detection platforms (Table S5).

Electrochemical sensors are widely used for detecting AFB1, owing to their cost-effectiveness, rapid response, efficiency, and ease of miniaturization (Hu et al., 2025). Dai et al. (2023) have developed a novel probe, CCM@ZIF-8/PDA/IgG, used it to construct an electrochemical immunosensor for detecting AFB1 in rice, and exhibited a linear range from 0.5 pg/mL to 10 ng/mL, with a LOD of 0.11 pg/mL. Although electrochemical analysis exhibits robust analytical performance, it requires specialized equipment, such as an electrochemical workstation, and maintaining the accuracy of experimental results requires controlled experimental environments (e.g., temperature and humidity). Similarly, fluorescence-based methods have gained increasing interest because of their ease of use. Li, Shang, et al. (2024) have constructed a fluorescent immunosensor using Eu nanospheres for the rapid detection of AFB1, and achieving an LOD of 0.003 ng/mL and a linear range of 0.01–2 ng/mL, with a detection time of 30 min. Colorimetric and photothermal signals are frequently combined with other signals to achieve multimodal output for detecting target analytes. Wu et al. (2025) have developed a “three-in-one” MIL@PDA UiOL@AIEgens driven lateral flow immunosensor, which enables colorimetric, grayscale, and fluorescence signal outputs for AFB1, offering a detection range for AFB1 from 0.01 to 5 ng/mL, with a LOD of 0.01 ng/mL. Compared with the above methods, the immunosensor developed in this study can enrich and separate AFB1 from food matrices. Because of the loading of HRP, which provides efficient catalytic activity and enables cascade amplification of colorimetric signals, the platform exhibited high sensitivity, resistance to matrix interference, and time savings, with a detection time of 15 min. Although the method is less sensitive than some previously reported methods, it has a great application potential in rapid AFB1 detection. In addition, the linear range of the assay should be further optimized in further studies, although this range depends on the performance of the mAb produced. Overall, the established immunosensor was demonstrated to be suitable for rapid and sensitive detection of AFB1 in grain samples.

Conclusion

Herein, we developed an ultrasensitive immunosensor for rapid AFB1 detection by using a multifunctional PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe for signal amplification. The PCN-224 functionalized with Fe3O4 NPs, PDA, and HRP demonstrated remarkable catalytic activity, rapid magnetic response, and enhanced colorimetric signal intensity. These characteristics significantly increased the sensitivity of the assay. The immunosensor based on the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe exhibited high specificity, sensitivity, and stability, with a detection range for AFB1 from 0.003 to 3 ng/mL and a LOD of 0.0014 ng/mL. Successful application of this platform to real samples, including maize, peanuts, and wheat, further validated its practical potential in complex matrices. Overall, the PCN-224@Fe3O4@PDA@Ab/HRP probe offers substantial advantages, such as decreased matrix interference and enhanced signal output. All functions were integrated into a single probe, which, combined with a test strip platform, provides rapid and sensitive results and avoids the need for complex equipment. The drawback of this method is that the workflows involve a magnetic separation step that relies on external magnets, making it more suitable for implementation in a laboratory benchtop environment. However, it can be replaced with the integration of low-cost magnetic pads, and combining it with portable devices to achieve real-time, on-site detection in the next step of our research work.

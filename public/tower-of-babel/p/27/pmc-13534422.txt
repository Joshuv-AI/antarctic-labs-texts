M²B-Net: a lightweight multi-scale multi-attention boundary-aware network for liver tumor segmentation from CT images
Authors: Fang Wang, Haoran Wang, Limin Liu, Fei Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Liver tumor segmentation from CT images remains challenging due to large variations in lesion scale, blurred boundaries, low tissue contrast, and the high computational cost of existing deep learning models. This study aims to develop a lightweight yet accurate segmentation network suitable for clinical deployment. We propose a Multi-scale Multi-attention Boundary-aware Network (M²B-Net) based on a U-shaped encoder-decoder architecture, integrating four modules: Multi-Dimensional Spatial-Location Attention (MDSLA) for feature enhancement across encoder scales, Manhattan Self-Attention (MaSA) for global dependency modeling, Multi-Scale Feature Refinement Module (MSFRM) for cross-scale feature alignment, and Boundary-Convolution Attention Module (BCAM) for edge detail enhancement. A weighted composite loss function combining cross-entropy and Dice loss is used. Experiments were conducted on the public LiTS dataset. M²B-Net achieved a Dice coefficient of 0.77 ± 0.13, volumetric overlap error of 0.35 ± 0.15, average symmetric surface distance of 2.99 ± 2.03 mm, and maximum symmetric surface distance of 5.20 ± 3.07 mm. The model contains 21.7 million parameters and 28.5 GFLOPs, with a training time of 4.1 h and testing time of 37 s per case. Ablation and comparative experiments confirmed the contribution of each module and showed superior performance over SegNet, TD-Net, SBC-Net, and RIS-UNet. M²B-Net effectively addresses multi-scale adaptation, weak boundary capture, and global context modeling in liver tumor segmentation while maintaining a lightweight architecture, demonstrating strong potential for rapid and accurate clinical deployment in resource-constrained settings.Keywords: Liver tumor, Deep learning, CT image, Lightweight network, Multi-scale feature fusionSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a highly prevalent and lethal malignant tumor worldwide, precise segmentation of liver tumors is a core step in clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis assessment1–4. Computed tomography (CT) imaging, with its rapid acquisition and moderate tissue contrast, has become the preferred imaging modality for liver tumor examination. However, automatic liver tumor segmentation from CT images remains highly challenging due to factors such as irregular tumor morphology, large scale variation, blurred boundaries, low contrast with liver parenchyma, as well as variations in scanning equipment, contrast enhancement phases, and individual differences. Traditional manual segmentation relies on physician experience, is time-consuming and labor-intensive, and suffers from poor reproducibility5. Early traditional algorithms such as thresholding, region growing, and graph cuts have insufficient generalization and robustness to handle complex lesions6. Therefore, constructing an automatic, high-precision, and robust liver tumor segmentation model holds significant theoretical value and clinical significance for advancing precision medicine and intelligent diagnosis of liver cancer.

In recent years, deep learning, with its powerful feature representation and context modeling capabilities, has propelled medical image segmentation into a new era of automation and precision7. Liver tumor CT images, characterized by substantial volume variation, blurred boundaries, diverse and often adhesive morphologies, impose high demands on models regarding multi-scale adaptability, boundary awareness, and global modeling capacity8. The encoder-decoder architecture represented by U-Net, with its skip connections and end-to-end optimization, has become the mainstream foundational framework, with related research continuously advancing in areas such as multi-scale fusion, attention mechanisms, Transformer-based global modeling, boundary enhancement, and loss function optimization. As an automatic benchmark segmentation framework, nnU-Net achieves outstanding results on LiTS dataset and is widely adopted for performance comparison9. However, its generic design fails targeted optimization for liver tumors’ blurred boundaries and tiny lesions.

Early convolution and multi-scale structure-based methods improved liver tumor segmentation performance through feature enhancement and structural optimization. With the introduction of Transformers and attention mechanisms, related methods have further achieved upgrades in global modeling and fine-grained perception. TD-Net constructs a non-cascaded hybrid architecture of convolution and Transformer, effectively improving segmentation of small tumors and blurred boundaries10. RIS-UNet enhances multi-scale lesion detail capture through multi-level feature extraction11. ELTS-Net improves modeling of large and diffuse lesions by expanding the receptive field12. SBC-Net combines residual connections and hybrid attention to enhance noise resistance and low-contrast adaptability13. H-DenseUNet leverages dense connections to balance 2D details and 3D spatial information14. Attention U-Net introduces an attention gating mechanism to focus on key tumor regions and suppress background interference15. AIM-Unet, ResUNet++, DoubleU-Net, HFRU-Net, U-Net++, DC-UNet, among others, optimize segmentation quality from perspectives such as information propagation, multi-phase fusion, multi-scale perception, and structural calibration16–21. Although these methods achieve prominent results in multi-scale fusion and local feature modeling, they suffer from insufficient global modeling, susceptibility to small lesion loss, weak boundary perception, and in some cases, complex structures leading to low inference efficiency.

Methods such as ICT-Net, TransUNet, and UCTransNet fuse convolutional local features with Transformer global dependencies to alleviate insufficient long-range information modeling22–24. DSA-Former and BACFormer focus on morphological adaptation and weak boundary feature enhancement, respectively25,26. LiT-HiSegFormer-Net, MAEG-Net, and CANet improve localization and semantic consistency through multi-scale and attention mechanisms27–29. RMAU-Net and Skip-Free Collaborative Residual U-Net optimize feature propagation and generalization30,31. MDAU-Net, T-MPEDNet, and E²Net each have their own focus in spatial perception and contour optimization but suffer from drawbacks such as high computational cost, insensitivity to small lesions, and insufficient boundary enhancement32–34.

Although the above methods have achieved breakthroughs in global modeling, boundary optimization, and multi-scale fusion, liver tumor segmentation still faces four key challenges: (1) Large variation in tumor scale, where small lesions are easily overwhelmed and multi-scale features are difficult to align and fuse effectively; (2) Blurred tumor boundaries with grayscale values similar to liver parenchyma, making it difficult for conventional attention mechanisms to accurately capture high-frequency edge information; (3) Difficulty in modeling global semantics and local positional information, leading to missed detection, misclassification, and discontinuous contours for irregular tumors; (4) High-precision models generally have large parameter counts and high computational complexity, making them difficult to deploy in real-time clinical or resource-constrained scenarios. Achieving model lightweighting and efficient inference while ensuring segmentation accuracy and boundary integrity has become a key constraint for the clinical translation of liver tumor segmentation technology.

Regarding the four core challenges, dramatic variations in tumor size constitute the fundamental problem, which causes indistinct lesion boundaries and obstructs effective aggregation of global multi-scale features. To tackle these inherent defects, researchers have designed intricate network structures, yet such complicated designs inevitably introduce excessive computational overhead. In short, uneven tumor scale is the root of segmentation hardships, blurred boundaries and inadequate global modeling are derivative obstacles, and heavy computation is the practical bottleneck accordingly.

Building upon previous research and targeting the goal of high-precision automatic liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes a Multi-scale Multi-attention Boundary-aware Network (M²B-Net) based on the U-shaped encoder-decoder architecture. Leveraging the multi-scale features of the encoder, the network employs full-scale Multi-Dimensional Spatial-Location Attention (MDSLA) to achieve synergistic enhancement of spatial location and channel features from shallow to deep layers, addressing the issue of small tumor loss. Manhattan Self-Attention (MaSA) is utilized to model high-level global semantic dependencies, improving adaptability to irregular tumor morphology while reducing computational complexity. The Multi-Scale Feature Refinement Module (MSFRM) performs cross-scale feature alignment and fusion to prevent dilution of small lesion features. Furthermore, the Boundary-Convolution Attention Module (BCAM) is introduced to enhance feature representation at tumor edges and weak boundary regions, enabling contour calibration. In addition, a weighted composite loss function is designed to address class imbalance and blurred boundaries, improving segmentation stability and accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that M²B-Net outperforms existing mainstream algorithms across multiple metrics, achieving superior segmentation accuracy, boundary continuity, and robustness. Moreover, with significantly lower parameter count (21.7 M) and computational cost (28.5 GFLOPs) compared to competing methods, as well as the shortest training and testing times, the model exhibits strong potential for clinical deployment.

Materials and methods

To address the challenges of multi-scale lesion adaptation, blurred boundaries, and insufficient global context modeling in automatic liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes a Multi-scale Multi-attention Boundary-aware Network for high-precision and robust liver tumor segmentation. As shown in Fig. 1, the network adopts a U-shaped encoder-decoder framework and integrates four core modules (Multi-Dimensional Spatial-Location Attention, Multi-Scale Feature Refinement Module, Manhattan Self-Attention, and Boundary-Convolution Attention Module) to construct an end-to-end segmentation pipeline for feature extraction, multi-scale enhancement, global modeling, and boundary calibration, thereby tackling the challenges of soft boundaries and multi-scale lesion recognition.

Network architecture

The input medical image is resized to 224 × 224 single-channel CT patches, undergoes four-layer down-sampling in the encoder to progressively extract multi-scale representations from low-level detail features (f₁) to high-level semantic features (f₄),, with channel dimensions [64,128,256,512] for f₁, f2, f3, f4 sequentially, covering information from different receptive fields and laying the foundation for subsequent processing. All scale features (f₁~f₄) are enhanced by the MDSLA module through spatial-channel synergistic enhancement, generating enhanced features (f₁ₘ, f₂ₘ, f₃ₘ, f₄ₘ) that strengthen tumor target features while preserving spatial location information, thereby improving feature discriminability.

To model the global spatial dependencies of high-level semantic features, the highest-level enhanced features (f₄ₘ) are fed into the MaSA module. This module efficiently models global correlations and suppresses noise under low computational cost through a learnable bias constrained by Manhattan distance and Kronecker product decomposition, outputting optimized high-level features (f₄ₘₐₛₐ) that provide strongly discriminative semantic guidance for the decoder and improve perception of irregular tumors35.

The enhanced features from the first three scales (f₁ₘ, f₂ₘ, f₃ₘ) are fed into the MSFRM module, which performs dimension unification, spatial alignment, and refinement of cross-scale features, outputting features that match the decoder hierarchy. This addresses the issues of insufficient multi-scale feature utilization and hierarchical fragmentation, improving the quality of skip connections.

The decoder performs four levels of upsampling (Up1 ~ Up4) with skip connections, progressively fusing the MSFRM-refined features, the corresponding scale MDSLA-enhanced features, and f₄ₘₐₛₐ, achieving coarse-to-fine semantic reconstruction and restoring spatial resolution. The BCAM module is embedded between the decoder output and the segmentation layer, extracting high-frequency boundary information through depthwise convolution and combining it with dual-attention modulation to enhance tumor edge and liver contour features, alleviating boundary blurring and adhesion issues, and finally outputting high-precision segmentation results with the same size as the input.

Multi-dimensional spatial-location attention

The full-scale MDSLA is the core feature enhancement module of M²B-Net, as shown in Fig. 2. As a lightweight attention unit custom-designed for liver tumor segmentation36, MDSLA overcomes the limitations of traditional single-dimensional attention mechanisms37, it addresses both the issue of channel attention modules neglecting spatial location information and the drawbacks of pure spatial attention, which struggles to capture long-range positional dependencies and has high parameter counts, achieving multi-dimensional feature synergistic optimization of “location awareness - spatial modeling - channel enhancement”. Targeting the core pain points of “difficulty in small tumor localization, blurred boundaries, and fragmented hierarchical feature information” in liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes a full-scale deployment strategy, embedding the MDSLA module into all feature hierarchies of the encoder (f₁-f₄), achieving multi-dimensional perception enhancement throughout the entire process from low-level detail features to high-level semantic features, thereby providing sufficient location and contextual guidance for precise segmentation.

Multi-dimensional spatial-location attention.

The MDSLA module achieves precise feature enhancement through three steps: multi-dimensional feature aggregation, multi-branch attention generation, and weighted fusion enhancement. The overall design adopts a lightweight all-1 × 1 convolution architecture to control parameter count and computational cost while maintaining performance. Specifically: the module first performs 1D global average pooling in the horizontal and vertical directions on the input feature map, generating horizontal feature vector Zh and vertical feature vector Zw. This operation effectively captures long-range positional dependencies while preserving pixel-level location information. Simultaneously, to address the issues of global spatial context and channel-wise feature redundancy, a global spatial branch and a channel enhancement branch are added - the spatial branch aggregates global spatial features through global average pooling, followed by 1 × 1 convolution for dimensionality reduction, batch normalization, ReLU activation, and dimensionality-increasing convolution, then generates global spatial attention weights Ms via Sigmoid; the channel branch adopts an isomorphic global pooling-convolution sequence structure to generate channel attention weights Mc, achieving enhancement of effective channel features and suppression of redundant channels.

The horizontal and vertical feature vectors are concatenated along the channel dimension, then fused and reduced via a shared 1 × 1 convolution, followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation, before being split back to their original dimensions. Manhattan self-attention

To address the core requirements of precise perception of weak boundary regions and efficient global correlation modeling in liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes the MaSA module (Fig. 3). This module focuses on the highest-level semantic features f4 of the encoder and achieves lightweight global feature modeling through the introduction of Manhattan distance constraints and Kronecker product decomposition38, providing stable, highly discriminative high-level feature guidance for the segmentation task.

Manhattan self-attention.

The fusion via Kronecker product achieves preservation of the global receptive field while optimizing computational efficiency. This decomposition reduces the complexity of constructing the spatial decay matrix from from O(H²W²) to O(H²+W²), significantly lowering computational cost while maintaining the global receptive field.

MaSA constructs the spatial attention distribution based on Manhattan distance, where attention weights decay smoothly with spatial distance between pixels. This naturally focuses the model on target regions and enhances its ability to perceive irregular morphology, low-contrast boundaries, and adhesive lesions. Compared to traditional window-based attention, MaSA does not require window splitting or local truncation, fully preserving the global receptive field and providing more robust high-level semantic support for medical image segmentation.

In summary, MaSA achieves precise representation and efficient computation of high-level features through the triple mechanisms of Manhattan distance constraints, Kronecker product decomposition, and high-level feature fusion, providing strongly robust and highly adaptable high-level semantic guidance for the liver tumor segmentation task.

Multi-scale feature refinement module

To address the issue of underutilized multi-scale features from the encoder, weak long-range dependency modeling, and inadequate quality of decoder skip connection features40, this paper proposes a Multi-Scale Feature Refinement Module (Fig. 4). This module takes the three-level encoder outputs {F1, F2, F3} as input and performs end-to-end feature enhancement through dimension projection, spatial alignment, cross-scale fusion, global attention enhancement, convolution refinement, and hierarchical output, providing highly reliable skip connection guidance for the decoder and effectively improving the model’s segmentation capability for multi-scale lesions, weak boundaries, and small targets.

Boundary-convolution attention module

To address the need for precise modeling of low-contrast, blurred boundaries in liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes the Boundary-Convolution Attention Module (BCAM), as shown in Fig. 5. Centered on lightweight depthwise convolution and dual-dimension attention41, it achieves efficient extraction and adaptive enhancement of boundary features with low computational overhead. Its structure is shown in Fig. 5.

Boundary-convolution attention module.

BCAM first extracts high-frequency boundary information through 3 × 3 depthwise grouped convolution (Boundary Conv). This operation focuses on edge details within each channel independently, avoiding cross-channel information confusion, and efficiently generates basic boundary features. Subsequently, a channel-spatial serial attention mechanism is used for weighted enhancement of the boundary features: channel attention models channel dependencies through global average pooling and 1 × 1 convolution, generating channel weights to strengthen effective boundary-related channels; spatial attention captures spatial context through 7 × 7 large-kernel convolution, generating spatial weights to precisely locate target boundary regions, achieving dual selection and enhancement of boundary features.

To balance boundary enhancement with the integrity of global semantic information, BCAM introduces a learnable scalar parameter α to perform adaptive residual fusion of the original input features and the enhanced boundary features. α automatically learns the optimal enhancement strength during training, avoiding over-enhancement or under-enhancement issues caused by fixed weights. The final fused features are calibrated via 1 × 1 convolution and batch normalization, outputting boundary-enhanced feature maps that provide highly discriminative boundary guidance for subsequent segmentation. BCAM achieves precise capture of weak boundaries with a lightweight structure, effectively addressing issues such as edge adhesion and contour missing in liver tumor segmentation while maintaining extremely low computational overhead.

Results

Datasets and evaluation metrics

The experiments in this paper use the internationally recognized LiTS (Liver Tumor Segmentation Challenge) dataset, which contains 131 clinical abdominal contrast-enhanced CT scans. This dataset is an authoritative benchmark in the field of liver tumor segmentation, with standardized annotations and sufficient case diversity to effectively support model training, validation, and comparative evaluation43. To ensure experimental rigor and generalizability, all cases are randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio, containing 104, 13, and 14 cases, respectively. This ensures balanced distributions of tumor size, morphology, and benign/malignant ratios, avoiding overfitting due to data distribution bias. The annotation rules are as follows: liver region labeled as 1, tumor region labeled as 2, and background region labeled as 0. Representative data examples are shown in Fig. 6.

As shown in Table 1, the dataset contains a total of 58,638 slice images, including 45,186 in the training set, 8,448 in the validation set, and 5,004 in the test set. Tumor sizes range from 0.3 mm³ to 5 × 106 mm³, with a median volume of 3.6 cm³; tumor diameters range from 3 mm to 6.5 cm, with small lesions (≤ 5 mm) accounting for 17.2% and large lesions (≥ 3 cm) accounting for 28.6%. The proportions of cases with clear, blurred, and irregular tumor boundaries in the dataset are 31.7%, 44.3%, and 24.0%, respectively, exhibiting strong heterogeneity and providing a thorough test of models’ capability to segment complex lesions.

CT image HU values typically range from [− 1000, 1000], with significant differences in HU distribution across different tissues. To highlight the liver and tumor regions and suppress interference from irrelevant tissues, this paper applies HU value truncation preprocessing to CT images, as shown in Fig. 6. Through multiple comparative experiments, the interval [− 200, 250] is ultimately selected as the effective truncation range, which preserves tumor details and boundary information while removing noise interference from air, fat, gastrointestinal gas, etc., improving the contrast of target regions.

To enhance model generalization and alleviate overfitting, this paper adopts an online data augmentation strategy, including random rotation (± 15°), horizontal/vertical flipping, translation (± 10 pixels), and scaling (0.9 ~ 1.1), to increase data diversity. Meanwhile, to reduce computational cost and unify input size, all CT slices are resampled to a resolution of 224 × 224.

The main experimental configuration is an Intel (R) Core (TM) i9-13900KF @ 3.00 GHz CPU, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, and 64G of RAM. All experiments are implemented using the PyTorch 2.0 deep learning framework with Python 3.9. The network input image size is uniformly normalized to 224 × 224 × 1, and the batch size is set to 24. The Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.0005 and a weight decay coefficient of 0.001. The learning rate scheduling strategy employs Cosine Annealing decay to ensure stable convergence during training. This strategy dynamically adjusts the learning rate by simulating the physical annealing process, effectively preventing the model from getting trapped in local optima. The model is trained for 100 epochs. Gradient clipping and early stopping strategies are introduced during training to improve training stability and prevent overfitting.

Ablation study

To investigate the synergistic effects and progressive gains of each core module in M²B-Net, this paper gradually decomposes the complete network into five comparative models for ablation experiments: Base, Base + MDSLA (B + M), Base + MDSLA + MaSA (B + M+M), Base + MDSLA + MaSA + MSFRM (B + M+M + M), and M²B-Net. Here, Base represents the baseline network with only the basic encoder-decoder structure; B + M indicates the embedding of the multi-dimensional spatial location attention module on top of Base; B + M+M further introduces the Manhattan self-attention module; B + M+M + M additionally adds the multi-scale feature refinement module; and M²B-Net is the complete network integrating all innovative modules. From Table 2, it can be seen that the Base model’s segmentation performance is at a baseline level, with a DPC of only 0.68 ± 0.18, while the error metrics ASD, MSD, VOE, and RVD are all the highest, reflecting the significant limitations of the baseline network in capturing complex spatial locations of tumors, weak boundary details, and multi-scale lesion features. Compared to the Base model, Base + MDSLA effectively strengthens the spatial localization and channel discriminative capabilities of features by introducing the multi-dimensional spatial location attention module, significantly improving segmentation performance. The DPC increases to 0.69 ± 0.12, while ASD and MSD decrease to 7.91 ± 6.37 and 7.77 ± 4.10, respectively, quantitatively verifying the promoting effect of the coordinate perception mechanism for precise tumor region recognition.

After further adding the MaSA Manhattan self-attention module, the network achieves additional performance gains, with DPC increasing to 0.71 ± 0.14 and ASD decreasing to 6.86 ± 7.20. The MaSA module constructs global spatial dependencies through Manhattan distance constraints, suppressing background noise interference and enhancing the spatial continuity of target regions, significantly strengthening the network’s ability to model irregular tumor morphology. After further introducing the MSFRM multi-scale feature refinement module, the model achieves dimension unification, spatial alignment, and deep fusion of cross-scale features, further improving feature utilization and representation robustness. The DPC continues to increase to 0.75 ± 0.11, while VOE remains at a low level, fully demonstrating the key role of multi-scale information fusion in improving segmentation consistency and stability.

The complete model M²B-Net proposed in this paper integrates MDSLA, MaSA, MSFRM, and the BCAM boundary enhancement module, achieving synergistic optimization of spatial localization, global modeling, multi-scale fusion, and boundary calibration, attaining the best performance across all metrics: DPC reaches 0.77 ± 0.13, ASD significantly decreases to 2.99 ± 2.03, MSD decreases to 5.20 ± 3.07, and VOE and RVD also maintain optimal values.

Combined with the visual segmentation results in Fig. 8, the effectiveness of each module can be more intuitively verified: the Base model (Fig. 8a) exhibits obvious boundary blurring and regional omission issues in its segmentation results, with poor fitting of tumor contours. With the sequential introduction of MDSLA (Fig. 8b), MaSA (Fig. 8c), and MSFRM (Fig. 8d), the tumor segmentation contours gradually become clearer and more complete, with significantly enhanced recognition capability for small lesions and edge-adherent regions. The final M²B-Net (Fig. 8e) segmentation results highly coincide with the ground truth, not only achieving precise coverage of tumor regions but also effectively preserving the fine morphology of liver contours and tumor boundaries, fully demonstrating the dual improvement of segmentation accuracy and visual quality through the synergistic effects of the modules.

The above quantitative and visual results collectively confirm that each core module added progressively brings stable performance gains, while the complete M²B-Net fully leverages the complementary advantages of all modules, effectively addressing the core issues of multi-scale adaptation, weak boundary recognition, and insufficient robustness in liver tumor segmentation, providing reliable technical support for high-precision automatic liver tumor segmentation.

To investigate the optimal ratio between the tumor region weight w₁ and the background weight w₀ in the weighted loss function, this paper conducts ablation experiments with six gradient ratios. The quantitative results of each group on the LiTS test set are shown in Table 4. From the overall trend of the metrics, it can be seen that the weight ratio between tumor and background significantly affects segmentation performance. Moderately increasing the tumor weight guides the model to learn better features, while excessively high or low weights reduce segmentation accuracy. With an equal weight of 1.0/1.0, the model achieves moderate performance but struggles with the foreground-background imbalance in liver tumor data. As the tumor weight w₁ is appropriately increased, segmentation accuracy gradually improves, with the optimal results achieved at 2.5/0.3, where the best balance between enhancing lesion feature learning and suppressing background noise is attained. Further increasing to 3.0/0.4 causes a significant decline in metrics, indicating that excessive emphasis on tumor weight easily induces overfitting and mis-segmentation, reducing robustness. The intermediate ratio groups show relatively stable performance, further confirming that moderately increasing the tumor weight effectively alleviates class imbalance. In summary, an optimal interval exists for the weight ratio, and w₁/w₀=2.5/0.3 enables the model to achieve optimal balance in boundary accuracy, volume consistency, and global overlap, providing a robust loss function optimization strategy for high-precision liver tumor segmentation.

In terms of model efficiency summarized in Table 5, M²B-Net achieves favorable overall trade-off among parameter size, computational overhead and inference latency. The proposed network has 21.7 M parameters with 28.5 GFLOPs computational complexity. Compared with nnUNet-light and MobileUNETR, M²B-Net slightly increases parameter count and computation consumption, while its cost is substantially lower than four conventional counterparts including SegNet, TD-Net, SBC-Net and RIS-UNet. For runtime efficiency, the model costs 4.1 h for full training and only 37 s for single-case inference. Its training and inference speeds are comparable to the two lightweight baselines and overwhelmingly superior to the other four heavy-weight comparison models with larger parameters. These results demonstrate that M²B-Net moderately raises parameters and computational cost to obtain improved liver tumor segmentation accuracy without obvious degradation of inference speed. The proposed method realizes a balanced compromise among segmentation precision, parameter budget and inference speed, balancing segmentation performance and practical deployment cost, which enables its rapid deployment in clinical practice.

From the quantitative metrics, it can be seen that M²B-Net achieves the best overall performance across all evaluation metrics. The method achieve DPC of 0.77 ± 0.13, higher than SegNet, TD-Net, SBC-Net, and RIS-UNet, while also obtaining the lowest ASD and minimal MSD of 2.99 ± 2.03 mm and 5.20 ± 3.07 mm, respectively. It also performs best in terms of RVD and VOE, indicating that M²B-Net not only achieves higher segmentation accuracy and more precise tumor boundary localization, but also produces segmentation results that are more consistent with the ground truth in terms of volume.

From the Fig. 10, it can be intuitively observed that SegNet’s segmentation results exhibit obvious under-segmentation and boundary blurring, making it difficult to completely extract the tumor region. TD-Net and SBC-Net can roughly locate the tumor region but suffer from insufficient boundary smoothness, often resulting in over-segmentation or missing segments. Constrained by the inherent feature extraction limitations for lightweight design, the lightweight baselines MobileUNETR and nnUNet-light suffer from missed segmentation for tiny lesions and insufficient fitting accuracy at the boundaries of irregular tumors. RIS-UNet produces more complete segmentation contours but still deviates from the ground truth for small tumors and blurred boundaries. In contrast, M²B-Net’s segmentation results are much closer to the ground truth, with clear and smooth boundaries, achieving precise segmentation for tumors of varying sizes and irregular morphologies, with overall visual quality significantly superior to the other comparison algorithms.

Combining the quantitative and qualitative results, it can be concluded that M²B-Net, by strengthening multi-scale context fusion and boundary awareness, effectively alleviates the issues of boundary blurring, small target omission, and incomplete segmentation commonly seen in traditional networks. It outperforms existing mainstream algorithms in overall accuracy, boundary precision, and robustness, demonstrating stronger potential for clinical application.

Limitations

Our method also needs improvement. The current scope of M2B-Net liver tumor segmentation constrains its use, necessitating validation on novel datasets to broaden its applicability. In our future work, we plan to construct a dedicated clinical dataset with pathological labels, which will enable us to perform rigorous subgroup analysis for validating and optimizing our segmentation network’s performance on different tumor types (e.g., metastases vs. HCC vs. benign lesions). In addition, we have elucidated the overall beneficial effect of each module on tumor segmentation and conducted analyses across different tumor size subgroups. However, we did not perform other subgroup (such as low-contrast lesions and blurred boundaries) analyses to clarify the specific advantages of each module. In the future, we will conduct subgroup analyses using a self-constructed dataset to optimize the modules.

Conclusions

To address the challenges of difficult multi-scale lesion adaptation, insufficient weak boundary capture, lack of global context, and high model computational cost in automatic liver tumor segmentation, this paper proposes a lightweight Multi-scale Multi-attention Boundary-aware Network (M²B-Net). Built upon a U-shaped architecture, the network employs: multi-dimensional spatial location attention for precise target region localization and feature enhancement; Manhattan self-attention for efficient global dependency modeling to improve adaptability to irregular tumors; a multi-scale feature refinement module for cross-scale feature alignment and fusion to alleviate small lesion omission; and a boundary-aware convolutional attention module to enhance detail representation of blurred boundaries and improve contour integrity. Experiments on the LiTS dataset show that the proposed M²B-Net achieves a Dice coefficient of 0.77 ± 0.13, a volumetric overlap error of 0.35 ± 0.15, an average symmetric surface distance of 2.99 ± 2.03 mm, and only 21.7 M parameters, demonstrating excellent performance in both accuracy and efficiency. The method effectively addresses key challenges in liver tumor segmentation, achieving lightweight and efficient inference while maintaining high precision, with strong potential for clinical deployment.

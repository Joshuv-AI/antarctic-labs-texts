Assessment of psychometric properties of instruments for measuring health system literacy: A systematic review study protocol using COSMIN guidelines
Authors: Anh Thu Vo, Lisa Fang, Lixia Yang, Robin Urquhart, Yanqing Yi, Peizhong Peter Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionThe healthcare system is complex, involving multiple interactions among individuals, organisations and systems. Many patients, particularly those with limited health system literacy, struggle to navigate it. A reliable instrument is crucial for accurately measuring individuals’ navigational abilities. However, a systematic review of psychometric properties of existing instruments for measuring health system literacy has not been conducted. This review aims to provide a comprehensive assessment of methodological quality and measurement properties of these instruments.Methods and analysisA search was conducted in MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, APA Psyfor, SCOPUS, Web of Science and Google Scholar from the date the database was created to 14 December 2024. Eligible studies include those published in English and focusing on developing and validating an instrument for measuring the construct of health system literacy in adults aged 18 or older. Two researchers will independently review, select and extract eligible studies, while all members of the research team will be involved in assessing methodological quality and the quality of measurement properties. This systematic review will adhere to the Consensus-based standards for the selection of health measurement instruments (COSMIN) guidelines for conducting systematic reviews of psychometric properties. We will provide summary tables of study characteristics and instrument characteristics used to measure health system literacy. Measurement properties and their quality will be assessed using the COSMIN standard criteria and the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE). We will also evaluate methodological quality of measurement properties using the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist.Ethics and disseminationThis review does not collect original data, so obtaining ethical approval is not applicable. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals, which can assist researchers and providers in choosing the appropriate instrument measuring health system literacy.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251004362.Keywords: Health Literacy, Systematic Review, Psychometrics

Introduction

Many countries are encountering ‘silos syndrome’ in their healthcare system.1 2 Siloed healthcare systems are often characterised by limited continuity and coordination among healthcare providers and settings, making it difficult for healthcare providers to thoroughly understand their patients’ disease conditions.1,3 Different doctors often concentrate on one disease as medical guidelines are typically developed for individual conditions.4 Specialists also focus on their practice on specific diseases, which was reinforced by fee-for-service payments.4 As a result, treatment plans tend to prioritise symptom management rather than provide a holistic care.1 5 Patients with chronic conditions must engage with multiple healthcare providers across siloed settings and navigate the health system for their ongoing treatment.1 2 4 6

Navigating health system refers to ‘the process by which patients and/or their caregivers move into and through the multiple parts of the healthcare enterprise to gain access to and use its services in a manner that maximises the likelihood of gaining the positive health outcomes available’.7 In the context of complex and fragmented healthcare settings, the need to communicate with different healthcare providers often leaves patients feeling overwhelmed.4 Many patients fall through the cracks8 and encounter negative consequences due to the fragmentation of care, including dissatisfaction with the system,5 receiving unnecessary healthcare services5 6 and increased treatment cost burden.5 6 9 Patients and/or their caregivers need enhanced navigational health or health system literacy to successfully navigate complex healthcare systems.10 These skills can empower them to find the right provider and access appropriate care at the right time and place.10,12 Therefore, it is necessary to know an individual’s ability to understand and navigate the healthcare system.

Navigating healthcare systems is a complex process because it depends on many factors, not solely an individual’s ability and skills but also the complexity of the healthcare system and available health information.13 Health literacy is only met when these abilities and skills are matched with the complexity and demand of health information, services, tasks and systems.13 However, health services and information are not often understandable and user-friendly, which prevents their users from successfully navigating the system.13 14 Having a suitable instrument to evaluate health system literacy can provide us with a comprehensive understanding of health system-related tasks and abilities to navigate these tasks among populations.

While topics of general health literacy have been studied for decades,14,17 health system literacy has not been a focus for many researchers. Health system literacy is often measured using the (sub)scales of common and well-known health literacy instruments, including the Health Literacy Questionnaire (HLQ)15,21 and The European Health Literacy Questionnaire (HLS-EU-Q47).22 The ability to navigate health systems has also been evaluated by self-reported questionnaires, comprising Barriers to Care Questionnaire (BCQ),23 Healthcare Navigation Self-Sufficiency Scale (HN3S)24 and Navigating Ability (NAV2).25 Another new 12-item scale, namely the navigational health, literacy (NAV-HL), has been developed specifically for measuring navigational skills.26 27 However, this instrument has been used mainly in European countries.26,28 Moreover, understanding health insurance and successfully navigating a complex insurance system requires significant health insurance literacy.29 Most instruments for measuring healthcare navigation skills may inadequately assess these aspects.15,25 Instead, there are a few specific instruments for measuring health insurance literacy, including 21-Item Health Insurance Literacy Measure14 30 and other questionnaires.31 32

To date, the variety of instruments that are used to measure navigational health system has led to inconsistency in assessing navigational health system skills. Selecting a measure often relies on specific objectives,33 yet the lack of standardised tools poses challenges for researchers and healthcare professionals in identifying appropriate instruments to evaluate healthcare system navigation ability among populations. Non-standardised tools cannot provide a comprehensive understanding of these navigational skills across different levels of healthcare system because they fail to capture full domains of key concepts regarding health system navigation.33 This limitation can hinder the optimisation of resource allocations and development of interventions aimed at improving navigation processes and enhancing health system literacy. It poses difficulties in comparing these skills among different populations. Moreover, knowledge of psychometric properties of instruments is necessary because instruments with inadequate good measurement properties can yield less reliable outcome assessments.34 35 Scholtes et al. (2011) stated that a systematic review of psychometric properties using rigorous criteria is the most appropriate approach to identify the most reliable and valid instrument.36 However, a systematic evaluation of the methodological quality of studies on measurement properties of health system literacy has not been conducted.

Thus, this systematic review fills knowledge gaps through evaluating psychometric properties of instruments measuring abilities to understand and navigate healthcare systems using Consensus-based Standards for the selection of Health Measurement Instrument (COSMIN) systematic review. The study has twofold aims: first, to identify instruments that have been used to measure health system literacy among adult populations from validation studies, and second, to evaluate the methodological quality and quality of measurement properties of these instruments. From reviewing systematically psychometric properties of current instruments, this study provides evidence regarding qualities of existing instruments and their measurement properties as well as existing literature gaps in instruments wherein future studies are needed. Healthcare providers and researchers can use these findings as references when they seek appropriate measures for enhancing their understanding of how well their patients or study populations understand and navigate the healthcare system.

Methods

Study design

This protocol is reported using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocol (PRISMA-P) 201537 (see online supplemental file 1). The systematic review will adhere to the COSMIN guidelines for conducting systematic reviews of psychometric properties38 and the PRISMA guideline 2020.39 This protocol was registered on PROSPERO (https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420251004362)

Standardised criteria that are used in systematic reviews of measures’ psychometric properties are necessary for evaluating methodological quality as well as qualities of the instrument itself.36 40 These criteria may help identify measures with poor quality or highlight insufficient measurement validity, indicating the need for future research or the development of new measures.40 The COSMIN group proposed these standardised criteria and procedures for conducting related systematic reviews and provided a manual to facilitate the selection of health outcome measures in research and clinical settings.36 40 The COSMIN manuals clearly explain measurement properties and terminologies and are publicly available on the COSMIN website. This systematic review will follow the COSMIN guidance and apply standardised criteria to evaluate qualities of instruments used to measure health system literacy. Additionally, the PRISMA guideline 2020 will be used to ensure transparent and accurate reporting of the systematic review, particularly in terms of identification, selection, appraisal and synthesis.39

Search strategy

We collaborated with a professional librarian to develop search strategies for the review. We searched seven electronic databases: MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, APA Psyfor, SCOPUS, Web of Science and Google Scholar.

For Google Scholar, we searched the first 10 pages (100 records for title and abstract screening). Search terms related to key concepts are health system literacy, navigational health system literacy, instruments and psychometric properties. Briscoe et al. (2022) revealed that the majority of relevant studies are concentrated within the first 100 results, although screening a higher proportion of results increases the likelihood of identifying additional relevant studies for review.41 Similarly, Bramer et al. (2017) suggested that when the number of references retrieved from electronic databases is low, the number of results from Google Scholar may be limited to 100 results.42 In our search, the number of references from electronic databases ranged from 6 to 34, and therefore limiting screening to the first 100 results from Google Scholar can be considered appropriate. Our detailed search strategies for these databases, including key search terms, are illustrated in online supplemental file 2.

Inclusion criteria

Population

This systematic review will include all adults aged 18 or older, both patients and general public, in medical and non-medical settings. This eligibility criterion will allow us to provide a comprehensive review of instruments used to evaluate health system literacy across a wide range of populations.

Instruments

Any instrument or its subscale that measures a person’s understanding of the healthcare system and ability to navigate it will be included in the review. We will include any type of instruments such as scales, questionnaires and checklists. These could be administered through various modes, including online self-reported survey, interview or postal questionnaires.

Construct

Griese et al. (2020)27 proposed a conceptual framework for critical health literacy-NAV tasks at three levels: (A) system level – ‘get an overview of healthcare system, its structures, areas, functioning and type of healthcare services’, ‘be aware of healthcare costs and coverage’, ‘be aware of rights and responsibilities’, ‘be aware of ongoing system changes and possible consequences for healthcare’; (B) organisation level – ‘find out about quality and judge if personal preference are met’, ‘take decision concerning concrete services’, ‘be aware of formal access requirements and rules of accessing and using healthcare’, ‘find out about and use support options in order to navigate the healthcare system’, and ‘find one’s way around healthcare institution’ and (C) interaction level – ‘interact competently with health professionals and institutions in order to negotiate healthcare paths and settings’. Additionally, health system literacy or navigational health literacy is defined as ‘a process by which patients and/or their health caregivers move into and through the multiple parts of the healthcare enterprise to gain access to and use services’.7 11 Therefore, we will include all instruments or subscales that were used to measure adult individuals’ ability to achieve NAV tasks to gain access and use healthcare services, including healthcare plans.

Measurement properties

The purposes of the study are to evaluate quality of measurement properties of instruments for measuring health system literacy. Following the COSMIN guidelines for conducting systematic reviews of psychometric properties,38 this systematic review will evaluate main measure properties, including content validity, structural validity, cross-cultural validity or measurement, reliability, measurement errors, construct validity and responsiveness. Criterion validity and hypotheses testing for construct validity will be evaluated under construct validity. Table 1 summarises the definition and measure properties extracted.

Others

This systematic review will include all original studies that focus on developing and validating instruments for measuring the construct of the health system navigation. The review will include studies published in English up to 14 December 2024 (when the search process was performed). Not limiting the starting year will help us understand the evolution of these instruments. Additionally, we will exclude studies that employ the instrument of interest for validating other instruments.

Study selection

We will import all records into the Covidence (online) to facilitate the removal of duplicates and streamline the screening process. Two researchers (TV and LF) will independently perform titles and abstracts screening, followed by full-text review according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The reasons for excluding studies during the full-text screening will be recorded based on these criteria. The included articles will be uploaded in the reference manager, Zotero (desktop version for Mac), to organise and extract the data. Any discrepancies about inclusions of studies will be resolved through re-reviewing and discussion by both researchers (TV and LF). If a consensus cannot be achieved by discussion, a supervisor (PW) will review and make the final decision.

Data extraction

This systematic review will extract study characteristics and characteristics of instruments. The study characteristics will include author, country, year, study objectives, populations (target population), age (mean/SD), gender (% women) and disease characteristics (disease, disease duration/severity) (see online supplemental table S1). Online supplemental table S1 presents the characteristics of instruments, including the instrument’s name, the country in which measures were evaluated, setting, response rate, construct, measures (e.g., health insurance literacy or health system literacy), mode of administration, (sub)scales (number of items), range of scores, original language and available translation. TV will conduct data extraction, and LF will verify it.

Methodological quality assessment of studies

Methodological quality will be evaluated using standards in the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist, as cited in the COSMIN guidelines for conducting systematic reviews of psychometric properties.38 According to COSMIN guidelines, each standard will be rated using one of the five options: ‘Very good’, ‘Adequate’, ‘Doubtful’, ‘Inadequate’ and ‘Not applicable’. The ‘worst score count’ method will be used to rate standards per measurement property.38 TV and LF will independently evaluate the methodological quality of each study, and other co-authors will be invited to discuss and verify the assessment (see online supplemental table S2).

Assessment of measurement properties

We will rate the measurement properties of each instrument using the COSMIN criteria for good measurement properties.38 The rating for each result can be one of three categories: sufficient (+), insufficient (-) or indeterminate (?). After assessing each study’s results, we will summarise these study results and examine their consistency across studies. The overall rating for each measurement property of each instrument will be categorised as ‘sufficient (+)’, ‘insufficient (-)’ or ‘indeterminate (?)’ when the ratings from each study are consistent. Conversely, we will explore appropriate explanations for inconsistency if these ratings are not consistent. By adhering to the COSMIN guidelines, study results will be summarised and the overall rating for each property will be classified by subgroups if differences in study population are a likely explanation for the reason. We can only summarise and provide overall rating for study results with very good or adequate methodological quality if the study quality is a likely reason for inconsistent results. Otherwise, we will summarise all results and rate the summarised result as inconsistent (±). This step will be performed independently by two researchers (TV and LF) and verified by other co-authors (see online supplemental table S3).

Grade the quality of measurement properties

The quality of measurement properties will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE), as cited in the COSMIN guidelines for conducting systematic reviews of psychometric properties.38 The GRADE can determine the level of confidence in the overall rating of measurement properties per the instrument by considering four factors: risk of bias, unexplained inconsistency, imprecision and indirectness.38 If any factor is present, the quality of evidence will be downgraded by one or two levels per factor depending on the seriousness of the risk.38 This step will be conducted by two researchers (TV, LF), and the other co-authors will be invited to grade the quality of measurement properties (see online supplemental table S4).

Discussion

The systematic review will summarise existing instruments for measuring health system literacy and provide a comprehensive evaluation of their psychometric properties using the COSMIN guideline. Our findings will assist healthcare providers in selecting the most reliable and valid instrument for measuring their patients’ ability to navigate the healthcare system, which ultimately will support tailored interventions to reduce barriers to accessing care and to improve health outcomes. The findings will also inform policymakers who may adopt high-quality measures to evaluate and address barriers in healthcare navigation at the system level. The review will highlight the content and the constructs of current instruments used to measure health system literacy. Doing so may strengthen factors and items representing individuals’ understanding and ability to navigate the healthcare system. Additionally, an understanding of changes in measures’ constructs through cross-cultural validity can reinforce the conceptual framework of healthcare system navigation in a specific context. Additionally, a comprehensive understanding of instruments with good measurement properties is necessary for researchers and policy makers to consider and evaluate health policy initiatives to enhance health system literacy for the general population. Moreover, our findings will identify methodological gaps in the development and validation of the instruments based on the COSMIN standard criteria,38 which future studies may then address.

Despite the potentially significant contributions of this systematic review to the literature, it has some limitations. First, we will exclude articles published in languages other than English, so our findings will provide a partial understanding of quality of measurement properties of instruments published in the English language. Second, we will exclude studies conducted with participants under 18, so knowledge of health system literacy in these populations needs to be explored in future research. Moreover, it is important to note that some studies might have good measurement properties, but the information reported in the article may not be sufficient based on the COSMIN standards, which can affect their quality of evidence.

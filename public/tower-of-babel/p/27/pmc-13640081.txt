Background

Oral health is defined by WHO as freedom from oral or facial pain, oral cancer, oral infection or injury, periodontal disease and tooth loss is closely related to the general health and quality of life (QOL) of adults; as it is implicated in impaired oral function, affects social interaction and reduces self-esteem [1]. This suggests the full participation of the patient in contributing to his oral health apart from the objective methods of oral health assessment completed by dentists. The participation of patient in this task can be done with the help of certain indicators such as the oral health related quality of life (OHRQOL). This indicator helps to measure the extent to which oral health conditions impact on a patient’s behavior and social functioning [2]. Poor oral health can affect quality of life by causing pain, discomfort and disability [3]. This concept, known as oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), incorporates an individual’s perception of their oral health, as well as the psychological, functional and social implications of this health problem [4]. Measuring OHRQoL in epidemiological studies can help determine a population’s treatment needs and is an important aspect of oral health assessment [5]. Many oral diseases can be prevented. This highlights the importance of focusing on oral health behaviours and the concept of oral health literacy (OHL), defined as ‘the ability of individuals to obtain, process and understand basic health information and services necessary to make appropriate oral health decisions’ [6]. OHL has been shown to influence oral health: specifically, inadequate OHL contributes to disparities in oral health, an increased need for emergency dental care, and an increased risk of dental caries and periodontal disease. Conversely, adequate OHL contributes to improved oral health and reduced inequalities in this area [7].

For over a decade, oral health literacy (OHL) has attracted significant interest from health professionals and policymakers due to its substantial influence on oral health outcomes [4]. OHL is considered a multifaceted concept and has been the subject of regular studies in dental research [8]. In reality, many factors such as dental anxiety and OHL affect oral health. However, oral well-being has a significant impact on overall health and quality of life (QOL) by affecting oral functions and social interactions. Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQOL) serves as a measure to assess the impact of oral health on the overall well-being of individuals [9]. OHRQOL can play a vital role at the population level in a country’s development, prosperity and social progress. It is therefore imperative to identify the factors associated with it in order to design and implement the strategies necessary to improve oral health. It is indicated as a potential determinant of oral health behaviours, outcomes, and access to dental services. Research in this area shows that tooth decay can affect the OHRQOL indicator, affecting the perception of the patient towards its oral health [10].

In our context, no study has been conducted to address the effect of dental caries on the OHRQOL of adults in Ebolowa, Cameroon. Good oral health improves quality of life, necessary to improve an individual productivity in life. Therefore the aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of dental caries on OHRQOL in adults in Ebolowa, Cameroon.

Methods

Design and study population

This cross‑sectional study was conducted in people aged over 15 years living in Ebolowa, South of Cameroon. Data collection was conducted from October 2022 to May 2023. Patients and their attendees (e.g., patients’ family members and relatives) visiting the outpatient department of the dental unit were selected. Patients who were fifteen years old or more, and were residents of Ebolowa and its surroundings were included in the study. A simple random sampling technique was adopted for the current study.

Data collection

The assessment tool had three parts:

The demographic data including age and sex. This part was completed with the interview.Oral situation including the number of decayed, missed, and filled teeth were assessed using the DMFT index. Subjects may not be able to recall whether the missing teeth or portions of teeth resulted from dental decay or another reason such as a broken filling. This is called recall bias, and it can make the results unreliable. The SiC index was used to reduce this bias. The SiC index in this study, was calculated by selecting the one third of the sample with the highest caries score; then, the mean DMFT for this subgroup constituted the SiC index value. Dental caries risk was assessed using the CAMBRA caries risk assessment tool, which included eight risk and protective factors and four disease indicators. Participants were categorized as low risk (If there are no disease indicators, very few or no risk factors and the protective factors prevail, the patient is at low risk); moderate risk (carious lesion in previous 12 months, visible plaque, suboptimal fluoride, and irregular dental care); and high risk (One or more disease indicators, if there is also hyposalivation, the patient is at extreme risk. Even if there are no positive disease indicators, the patient can still be at high risk if the risk factors definitively outweigh the protective factors.) accordingly [11]. Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the Ebolowa Regional Hospital Center Ethics Committee for Research in Human Health, Cameroon. All study procedure was done in accordance with the 2013 revised Helsinki Declaration and the Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Conference on Harmonization. All participants provided a written informed consent form before enrollment into the study.

Statiscal analysis

The data were transferred to Rstudio for statistical analysis. Descriptive analysis (percentages, mean with standard deviation) was used to summarize the data. The normality of the OHIP-14 score was evaluated using Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. ANOVA, simple and multiple linear regression were used for data analysis between the dependent variable (OHIP-14 score) and independent variables (SiC index, sex, age and caries risk assessment). Multiple linear regression was used to study the influence of dental caries on oral health related quality of life adjusted to other factors. The level of significance was set at P < 0.05.

Discussion

Of the 309 subjects consulted, females constituted 52% and males 48%. The mean age of the study population was 37.12 (SD ± 14.95). This was different from the results of Bukhari [13] in 2019, who had a mean age of 31.7 (SD ± 14.3).

The severity (average OHIP-14 score) of OHRQOL in this study population (15.65) was similar to the study done by Papagiannopoulou et al. [14] in 2012. On the other hand, certain studies had a score less than the result of the present study. Bukhari [13] reported a score of 12.7, Acharya and Sangam [15] reported a score of 13.4, and Montero-Martin et al. [16] a score of 11. These disparities can be explained by the fact that assessment of quality of life, as well as OHRQOL, depends on subject’s prospects and experience, which change according to socioeconomic, social, demographic, psychosocial and cultural factors [17].

In the current study, the domains that poorly affected OHRQOL were physical pain, psychological discomfort and physical disability. This was similar to the study done in the west region of Cameroon [10]. Physical pain was equally the main item in other studies. From these findings, we can clearly consider pain to be a major public health problem. Physical pain was expressed by the presence of pain and discomfort during feeding.

Mental health according to WHO is a state of mental well-being that enables people to cop with stress of life, realize their abilities, learn well and work well, and contribute to their community [18]. It was mainly affected in our study and expressed in terms of discomfort of the teeth present (31.4%) nervousness due to the presence of teeth (28.2%). Tamanna et al. [19] equally stressed out the association between mental health and oral diseases.

Concerning caries risk assessment, 54% of the study population had a ‘high risk’ index score, which was similar to the study done by Iyer et al. [20]. This could be explained by the fact that participants were recruited in the dental service of the health facility. 17% of the subjects had a SiC index equal or above SiC score, which was similar to the study done by Bukhari et al. [13]. This is mainly due to the fact the most affected domain of OHIP-14 score was physical pain and the mean DMFT was 5. From this, we can assume that dental caries in this study was probably associated with dental pain.

Statistical analysis reported a significant influence of caries risk assessment on OHRQOL where subjects with ‘high risk’ score equally had high OHIP-14 scores. This result was similar from the study led by Iyer and Jawdekar [20]. This result suggests the “risk based” necessity of caries management, targeted at improving OHRQOL. Risk assessment can be a valuable tool in motivating subjects to take part in oral health preventive programs. In the current study, multiple linear regression analysis showed that OHIP-14 score had a positive relation with SiC index adjusted on caries risk assessment which equally had a positive relation. This was similar to other studies [21–23]. This multiple regression showed that restorative work in conjunction with chemical therapy can improve OHRQOL.

The study had some limitations, causal relationships in the association between the studied variables and OHRQOL couldn’t be determined. The expected sample wasn’t attainable.

Conclusion

Dental caries affects OHRQOL in adults. Using OHRQoL as an assessment tool, along with a dental clinical indicator, can be beneficial in planning oral health programs. This can be helpful for the development of oral health policy in our country in a public health perspective. Efforts should be oriented by dentists towards reducing dental caries and improving OHRQOL.

Endoscopic hydrodissection for vein harvesting in coronary artery bypass grafting: The minimally invasive no-touch (MINT) technique
Authors: John F Gallagher, Victor Garcia, Steven Escaravage, Pamela Gallagher, Paul J Gallagher, Danielle Diegisser, William Davalan, Walid Ben Ali, Maximilian Y Emmert, Daniel Fusco, Louis P Perrault
Published: 2025

MINT preserves endothelium and eNOS versus EVH/No-Touch: SEM and eNOS assay.

Central Message

MINT vein harvesting preserves endothelial integrity better than standard EVH, offering a safer, truly minimally invasive option for improving CABG outcomes.

Saphenous vein grafts remain widely used conduits for coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), despite a 25% incidence of vein graft failure within the first 12 to 18 months, largely attributable to injury sustained at the time of harvesting and storage.1 Endoscopic vein harvesting (EVH) reduces postoperative leg wound complications but is fraught with greater rates of vein graft disease resulting from venous trauma during endoscopic handling.2 Hence, there remains a significant need to improve EVH techniques to reduce vein damage and enhance graft quality.

Methods

We introduce a novel harvesting technique using hydrodissection to preserve graft endothelial function, the MINT (minimally invasive no-touch) technique. This work reports the first clinical experience and proof-of-concept of MINT for saphenous vein graft conduits in CABG. The primary outcome was the preservation of the structural and functional integrity of the endothelium of the greater saphenous vein (GSV) graft. Three techniques were used.

•The MINT technique: A 2-cm longitudinal incision is made above the knee, and the vein is dissected in a “no-touch” fashion. This involves the off-label use of a commercially available EVH apparatus in conjunction with a patented (patent numbers: 63/638,992, US 10,687,793 issued June 23, 2020; US 11,540,817 issued January 3, 202; 3 US Ser. From March 2023 to July 2024, a series of 24 patients with triple-vessel disease provided consent for this study and underwent CABG with cardiopulmonary bypass performed by a single surgeon (D.F.) and single physician assistant PA-C (S.E.). Seventeen patients underwent MINT harvest of the GSV combined with separate GSV samples harvested by the “no-touch” technique. Seven patients underwent GSV harvest via standard EVH combined with separate “no-touch” samples. The study was approved by the institutional review board (IRB) at St Vincent's Hospital (IRB no.: HHC-2024-0110, October 10, 2024). The data underlying this article will be shared on reasonable request to the corresponding author.

Selected samples were examined using a Zeiss Supra 40 Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM 2 KV) at magnifications of × 500, × 1000, and × 1500. Freshly harvested vein specimens were placed in DuraGraft solution (Marizyme) at room temperature and transported to the vascular biology laboratory (New York Medical College) within 2 hours of harvesting. Endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity was assessed using the NOS Activity Kit (Cayman Chemical; item no. 781001). eNOS activity/nitric oxide generation was evaluated using radiolabeled L-arginine to L-citrulline conversion measured via a scintillation counter providing raw data in counts per minute. Sample counts per minute (vascular rings) were normalized to their respective protein concentration. Each patient sample underwent a “no-touch” sampling providing an internal control; therefore, the appropriate comparison to determine percent or fold-changes within a patient and between groups are showcased as those relative to the “no-touch” procedure.

Results

There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics of patients between the standard EVH and MINT groups. GSV segments harvested using the MINT technique showed a moist, pink salmon−colored vein with preserved perivascular adipose tissue. Results of SEM examination are presented in Figure 1, B-D. Hence, the novel vein harvesting MINT technique using hydrodissection is demonstrated to be safe, feasible, and effective for harvesting GSV conduits. SEM examination of MINT segments reveals an intact endothelial cobblestone pattern, indicating excellent structural preservation. In addition, the MINT technique results in significantly superior eNOS activity compared with other harvesting methods, supporting its efficacy in maintaining endothelial function.4 CABG using venous conduits with preserved endothelial integrity and function with continued release of nitric oxide and prostacyclin should translate into superior long-term patency rates and significant clinical benefits for patients.5

Discussion

The novel MINT technique addresses the limitations and the needed improvements in EVH approaches. Standard EVH technique with CO2 insufflation has remained essentially unchanged over the last 25 years.1 The MINT combines the benefits of minimizing leg wound complications while potentially improving outcomes of patients with coronary artery disease (superior long-term patency and cardiac event-free survival) in a simple, reproducible, and easily teachable fashion because of lack of blunt manipulation and constant 360° visualization. The MINT technique minimizes blunt trauma to the GSV because of limited contact with the vein, reducing macro- and microthrombi formation caused by endothelial damage, which contributes to vein graft failure.3 Hydrodissection with localized delivery of pharmacologic concentrations of heparin eliminates the need for systemic anticoagulation, and papaverine effectively prevents GSV spasm. The fluid environment mitigates thermal injury to the main trunk of the GSV during the division of side branches with cautery that can be a nidus for intimal hyperplasia. Furthermore, the toxic, nonhumidified, acidic CO2 environment of standard EVH is replaced with a fluid environment maintained at an optimal pH of 7.4, preserving vein integrity and function.

Hydrodissection with the novel MINT technique is a truly minimally invasive, endoscopic no-touch procedure, with superior preservation of endothelial function which could optimize CABG results. These improved results should also be achieved when using the MINT procedure for radial artery harvesting and lower extremity bypass. Of note, this is a nonrandomized proof-of-concept study with a limited sample size. Additional trials and biological assessments (ie, proteomic and metabolomic) will provide further insights into the current benefits observed within this new approach.

From performance to prediction: extracting aging data from the effects of base load aging on washing machines for a machine learning model
Authors: Shaojin Ma, Xue Bai, Yan Bai, Jiajia Shao, Shuai Yuan, Junyu Gao, Jian Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The standard testing of washing machine performance faces reliability challenges, which stem from the uncontrollable degradation of base load. This study provided a quantified standard for base load age to enhance the reliability and stability of the washing machine performance testing process. First, the impact of base load aging on the cleaning performance and water extraction performance was investigated. Simultaneously, the changes in reflectance of the base load were recorded. Then, MLR and ANN models were developed using the remaining moisture content and reflectance of the base load to predict base load age. It was found that the water extraction performance is more easily affected by the aging of the base load than the cleaning performance. In addition, ANN has better performance in predicting base load age, resulting in an Rc2 of 0.978, RMSEC of 9.295 h, and Rcv2 of 0.972 h, RMSECV of 10.639 h for the front-loading washing machine, and Rc2 of 0.958, RMSEC of 12.948 h, and Rcv2 of 0.940, RMSECV of 15.572 h for the top-loading washing machine. This study establishes a theoretical foundation for optimizing base load age regulation. Further study could expand the sample size to enhance the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22047-6.

Introduction

Washing machines are common household appliances that help to save time and effort spent in washing clothes. In the European Union (EU), over 15 million washing machines were sold to domestic consumers annually, contributing to around 5% of the total domestic electricity consumption1. Different types of washing machines, such as impeller type, drum type, and mixer type, have varying washing performance2. To assist consumers in their selection and for market regulation in China, each washing machine must undergo performance testing before being released to the market. The testing methods for electric washing machines in Chinese National Standards (GB/T 4288 − 2018) are similar to the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards. According to the IEC standard (IEC 60456), the performances of the washing machine include standard cleaning performance, remaining moisture content, rinsing performance, energy consumption, water consumption, program duration, etc. The washing performance test is conducted by operating the washing machine with standard textile test materials and the detergents under certain conditions. Standard test materials include base loads and standard stain swatches, which were attached to the base loads to simulate contaminated clothing.

Base loads include cotton, synthetics/blends, and polyester according to IEC 60,456: 2024. In the latest standard released by IEC, it is specified that the weighted average age of the base load should be between 20 test runs and 60 test runs. In China’s latest national standard (GB/T 4288 − 2018), only cotton base load fabrics are used, and the effective usage time is specified to be within the range of 4 h to 180 h. One possible reason for the requirement on the base load age in washing machine performance testing standards is that the fabric degrades during the washing process, which is caused by the abrasion of wet fabric and cleaning agents3. This could lead to a reduction in the base load’s mass and results in greater fluctuations during the washing machine’s load balancing. For example, if the washing machine to be tested has a 10 kg capacity, after placing the rated quantity and type of base load (at this point, the weight of the base load is less than 10 kg), specific base loads need to be used for balancing. The number of aged base loads used for balancing should be greater than the number of brand-new base loads used for the same purpose.

Both natural (e.g., cotton and wool) and synthetic textile fibers may be released from the laundering. It was estimated that around 18.3 thousand tonnes of cotton and 12.1 thousand tonnes of synthetic fibers would be released from global washing machines in one year4. A recent study also estimated that approximately 5.2 kg of microfibers would enter the aquatic environment annyally in a medium-sized city5. The tiny size allows of microfibers them to be easily transported by wind and water, while their hydrophobic properties and surface functional groups promote the adsorption of inorganic/organic pollutants. These characteristics make microplastics ideal carriers for pathogenic microorganisms and toxic chemicals in the environment6. Researches have reported that the type of washing machine, temperature and detergent can influence the releasing of microfibers in clothes7. In addition, the textile type and textile mass per unit area have been reported to have influence on microfiber emissions from top-loading washing machines8. However, there is currently no research exploring the impact of cotton fabric aging on the washing performance.

On the other hand, in order to control the age, a system of tracking the number of test runs for each load item is required in IEC standards. Electronic systems such as radio frequency identification (RFID) technology may be used. RFID chip/s should be attached to the load in such a way that mechanical action is not influenced. However, whether the use of RFID chips will affect the washing process remains to be determined.

Post-washing evaluation of the washed base load, combined with machine learning algorithms, can help avoid potential interference during the washing process. Machine learning has been widely used in several fields9–11. For instance, in the field of material science, machine learning models such as random forest, linear regression, support vector regression, were employed to predict natural aging times of carbon fiber reinforced polymer-to-aluminum (CFRP/AL)12–14. In the field of textile, previous studies have employed classification and predictive modelling based on an imaging system to estimate the properties of fabric such as strength, elasticity and breathability15–19. Image features were extracted as input for a traditional machine learning model or resized images were directly used as input of a deep learning model. For instance, an artificial neural network (ANN) was used for the prediction of areal mass, tensile strength and elongation at break values of Polypropylene nonwoven fabrics at various mass20. In addition, a CNN model was developed for the detection of fabric defects21. A pairwise-potential activation layer was introduced to CNN, resulting in an average precision and recall over 90% and 80%, respectively. Although the above studies have made significant progress in fabric performance analysis, no research has yet been found discussing methods for predicting fabric aging during the washing process.

This study explored the impact of base load aging on the test results during washing machine performance testing and investigated the feasibility of quantitatively predicting the base load age. Specifically, the variations of cleaning performance and water extraction performance of the washing machine with increasing base load age were documented. In addition, the color changes of the base load during the aging process were analyzed. Finally, regression models were developed using the parameters of base load to predict its age.

Materials and methods

Test load and washing machines

Test loads used in the experiment consisted 2 bed sheets, 4 shirts, 28 towels, and 6 handkerchiefs, all made of 100% cotton. The warp yarn count, weft yarn count, and mass per unit area of the base load was 21 ± 2 Ne, 21 ± 2 Ne, and 173 g/m2 respectively. All load items were folded following standardized procedures specified in GB/T 4288-2018, which align with the standard published in IEC. In detail, the bed sheets were folded into thirds to form a letter “Z” by the following steps: (1) grasp the bed sheet in the center, (2) shake the bed sheet so that it hangs loosely, (3) fold it twice to a third of its total size, (4) and compress the folded bed sheet lightly before placing it into the drum. The shirts were folded in the following steps: (1) grasp the shirt in the center, and (2) shake the shirt so that it hangs loosely. The towels and handkerchiefs were folded following the procedure analogous to that of the shirts. Standard stain swatches specified in the GB/T 4288-2018 include carbon black, protein, and sebum. In this study, carbon black stain swatches were used. They were positioned at designated positions on the base load, with specific mounting methods specified in GB/T 4288-2018. It should be noted that, not all base loads were attached with stain swatches. In the loading procedure, base loads with and without soiled stain swatches were alternately stacked. Washing machines are classified into manual and automatic types, which may be either semi- or fully automatic depending on the loading conditions (top-loading or front-loading)15. In this study, both front-loading (Electrolux Wascator FOM71 CLS) and top-loading (MS100-BD896U1, Haier) washing machines were employed. Each layer of the base load configuration was manually pressed downward with gentle force following placement. Standard powder detergent was used in this experiment. The detergent was added to the washing machine for each individual test run. Following detergent addition, the front-loading washing machine was initiated in the Cotton 60 °C program, whereas the top-loading washing machine was initiated in the standardized energy efficiency testing program. Throughout the experiment, the ambient temperature and humidity were maintained at (23 ± 2) °C and (55 ± 2) %, respectively.

Performance of the washing machine

Data collection was conducted within a duration of 4 to 210 h. The first measurement was taken at 4 h, followed by the second at 10 h, with subsequent data acquisitions performed every 10 h thereafter. The reason a 10-hour interval was chosen is mainly due to the following: (a) After approximately 10 h of operation, the color difference between the new base load and the base load with 10 h of age can be visually observe, (b) The reflectance varies at different positions on the same base load. Although a multi-point averaging method was used, it was found that when the operation time is within 10 h, the inter-group error of reflectance between different base load ages is smaller than the intra-group error. A total of 22 sets of data were collected. Three types of data were collected, namely the detergency rate (for assessing the cleaning performance of washing machine), remaining moisture content (for assessing the water extraction performance of the washing machine), and the reflectance of the base load (for assessing the color of the base load). The detergency rate and remaining moisture content were measured immediately after each wash cycle, whereas the reflectance of the base load was measured after the base load has been dried.

The cleaning performance and water extraction performance of the washing machine were evaluated with increasing base load age. The cleaning performance was assessed through reflectance measurements of the stain swatches, which were taken with a whiteness meter (PN-48B, PNSHAR). The reflectance of the stain swatches was measured both before and after washing. The average value of the four readings for each soil type was used as the final reflectance. Each piece of soiling stain swatches was divided into four measurement sections along its midline and across both front and back sides. Modelling and evaluation

Multiple linear regression (MLR) and artificial neural network (ANN) were applied to develop regression models in this study. MLR is a commonly used statistical model for predicting the linear relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables22,23. In MLR, the dependent variable Y (base load age) is linearly related to multiple independent variables x1, x2, …., xn, j as characteristics of the base load (i.e., remaining moisture content and reflectance). MLR can be expressed as Eq. (3):3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y={\beta _0}+{\beta _1}{x_1}+{\beta _2}{x_2}+ \cdot \cdot \cdot +{\beta _n}{x_n}+\varepsilon$$\end{document} where β0 is the constant term, β1, β2, …, βn are the coefficient for the independent variable, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon$$\end{document} is the error term. These parameters are estimated by training the model.

ANN is a commonly used statistical model for predicting the nonlinear relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables24. ANN is inspired by the thinking mechanism of the human brain. The ANN architecture is structured with three fundamental components: (1) an input layer that receives operational parameters, (2) one or multiple nonlinear hidden layers implementing hierarchical feature abstraction through activation functions, and (3) an output layer that synthesizes these transformed representations into predicted values via linear combination. The connection weights are refined during training. The network adapts its internal parameters, capturing intricate nonlinear relationships between input and output variables, thus providing a powerful mechanism for predicting the base load age25.

In this work, remaining moisture content and reflectance were the independent features used in MLR and ANN and base load age was used as the dependent/input feature. In the independent variable dataset, the remaining moisture content and reflectance of the base loads have their own measurements. To obtain accurate prediction, normalization was performed prior to model development. Modelling and preprocessing were conducted in MATLAB R2024a (Mathworks, Inc) with the PLS_Toolbox (version 8.0.2, Eigenvector Research, Inc), which is a highly popular program widely used across various fields26–28. To verify the MLR model performance, the correlation coefficient value (R2) and root mean square error of calibration (Rc2 and RMSEC) and cross-validation (Rcv2 and RMSECV) were used in this study. For MLR and ANN modelling, the Venetian Blinds (w/4 splits and 1 samples per split.) cross-validation method was used to avoid over-fitting. The ANN model was trained using backpropagation with a fixed learning rate of 0.125. The learning iterations were 20.

Results

Washing machine performance and reflectance of base load

The variations in the cleaning performance and water extraction performance of the front-loading and top-loading washing machine with base load age are shown in Fig. 3. In Fig. 3A, significant discrepancies in detergency rate fluctuations can be observed between front-loading and top-loading washing machines. With the aging of base loads, the detergency rate of top-loading washing machines exhibits a variability range of 26% to 33%, significantly broader than the fluctuation range observed in front-loading washing machines. This indicates that base load age has a greater influence on the cleaning performance evaluation in top-loading configurations compared to front-loading washing machines. In Fig. 3B, with the aging of base loads, the remaining moisture content of the base loads in both front-loading and top-loading washing machines exhibits an initial ascending phase followed by stabilization after operational cycles. In addition, the remaining moisture content reflects the water extraction performance of the washing machine. The higher remaining moisture content of the base load, the worse the water extraction performance. Compared to the front-loading washing machine, the water extraction performance of top-loading washing machine was more vulnerable to the aging of cotton base loads. When the base load age was greater than 70 h, the impact of base load aging on water extraction performance began to decrease, which may be because the fiber release rate started to significantly decrease after around 70 h.

Regressing model based on MLR and ANN

Based on the observation in above, the base load age seems to be correlated with its remaining moisture content and reflectance. Therefore, MLR models were developed using the reflectance value and remaining moisture content of the base load under different ages. Specifically, the reflectance value obtained from the top, middle, and bottom layers, as well as the mean value of the three, were separately considered. Table 3 shows the MLR results attained from front-loading and top-loading washing machines. For the front-loading washing machine, the best MLR result was obtained when using the top layer reflectance and remaining moisture content as independent variables, with Rc2 of 0.920, RMSEC of 17.883 h, and Rcv2 of 0.915, RMSECV of 18.390 h. MAE o 16.134 h. Similar results were obtained when using mean reflectance value and bottom layer reflectance value. For the top-loading washing machine, the use of different types of reflectance data did not lead to significant variations in the model results. The MLR model obtained by the bottom layer reflectance value and remaining moisture content showed slightly better performance than the other three, resulting in an Rc2 of 0.866, RMSEC of 23.077 h, and Rcv2 of 0.818, RMSECV of 27.635 h, MAE of 17.531 h. It can also be observed that the load distribution prediction method based on MLR is more suitable for front-loading washing machines.

The scatter plot of the best MLR model for the front-loading and top-loading washing machine using the cross-validation dataset is shown in Fig. 5. Although both exhibit high R² values, the age of a subset of base load is predicted by the model to be less than zero, which contradicts the reality. In the case of the front-loading washing machine, the 4 h and 10 h base load ages were predicted as smaller than 0 h. In the case of the top-loading washing machine, the 4 h base load age was predicted as smaller than 0 h. In both scenarios, when the base load age exceeds 10 h, the predicted values of age are predominantly higher than the actual values.

Similarly, the ANN model was developed using the reflectance value and remaining moisture content of the base load under different ages. In comparison to the MLR models, ANN achieved a significant improvement in the prediction of base load ages for each case. This demonstrates that nonlinear relationships exist between reflectance, remaining moisture content, and base age, which leads to the inferior performance of the MLR model in predicting base load age.

The scatter plot of the best ANN model for the front-loading and top-loading washing machine using the cross-validation dataset is shown in Fig. 6. It can be observed that the optimal ANN model for top-loading washing machines still exhibits unrealistic negative base load age predictions in practice. In summary, the ANN-based base load age prediction model utilizing the reflectance of the bottom-layer towel base load and the remaining moisture content of all base loads demonstrated the best performance and practicality.

Discussion

The trends in detergency rate and remaining moisture content with the aging of the base load reflect the impact of base load aging on the standard testing process. On one hand, the detergency rate of the base load shows fluctuations and a slight upward trend as its lifespan increases, which can affect the cleaning performance ratio test. The cleaning performance ratio is calculated based on the ratio of cleaning efficiency between the prototype washing machine and the reference machine. If there is a significant difference in the average base load age used in the reference machine each time, it may reduce the reliability of the cleaning performance ratio value for the test machine. On the other hand, the remaining moisture content of the base load shows a significant increase as its age grows, which can impact the dehydration rate test. For the same washing machine, using base loads with significantly different average age to test the dehydration rate can result in large errors. Overall, the variation in base load age has a far greater impact on the dehydration rate test than on the cleaning performance ratio test.

Modeling the reflectance of napkins from different layers (top, middle, bottom) results in varying outcomes, whether using MLR or ANN models. This suggests that the position of the napkin in the washing machine affects its aging rate. To further explore the fundamental impact of napkin position on modeling results, it may be worth considering the mechanical actions during the washing process (e.g., movement trajectory, residence time distribution, etc.) and investigating the correlation between base load position and post-wash reflectance. However, as shown in Tables 3 and 4, the impact of positional differences on modeling results is not significant. In addition, from the scatter plot, it can be seen that the model’s prediction performance is better for the 4–50 h range compared to the range greater than 150 h. This may be because the rate of change in reflectance and remaining moisture content during the early stage of base load aging was greater than during the later stage of aging. As a result, the increase in aging time at equal intervals leads to less noticeable changes in the features.

In the best base load age prediction models for both front-loading washing machines and top-loading washing machines obtained using MLR and ANN, negative predicted values occurred. This is because the model is simply learning a mathematical mapping from input features to output targets. If the feature combinations in the test set differ from those in the training set, or if there is noise in the data, the model may extrapolate a negative value. Particularly in regions where the predicted values are close to 0, the model’s uncertainty is highest, and it is easy for the model to “cross” the boundary and produce negative values. This issue might be addressed by applying mathematical transformations to the target value y (e.g., logarithmic transformation), changing the activation function of the output layer from linear to a function that can produce positive outputs (such as the ReLU function), etc.

Regardless of the dataset used, the ANN model demonstrates better base load age prediction ability than the MLR model. This indicates that nonlinear models are better suited to predict the base load age. This may be related to the fact that the moisture content and reflectance of the base load exhibit opposing trends as the base load age increases. In future applications, if a wireless transmission system for reflectance and moisture content can be developed, along with a mobile app that integrates an ANN model to receive reflectance and moisture content values in real time, it would enable rapid assessment and effective monitoring of the base load age. This will make the base load age control in the standard testing process more strict and efficient, and provide timely warnings for base loads with non-compliant ages, avoiding the impact on washing machine performance testing caused by base loads with unsatisfactory ages. In addition, since front-loading and top-loading washing machines showed distinct behaviors, future testing protocols should include differentiated base load management strategies.

Although the ANN model has achieved relatively satisfactory results, the model has certain limitations when it comes to future deployment applications. On one hand, it is necessary to determine whether the replacement of the washing machine significantly impacts the aging rate of the base load. In addition, different types of washing machines have varying washing durations, which may also affect the deployment of the model. The shortening or extension of washing cycle durations will inevitably have different effects on the base load. For example, if the rinse and spin times in the standard energy efficiency test are 30 min each, and the quick wash has rinse and spin times of 15 min each, the model would still be applicable, as the washing process duration changes linearly. However, if the intensive program has a rinse time of 45 min and a spin time of 25 min, it is necessary to first determine which process (rinse or spin) causes more significant wear on the base load. Essentially, the relationship between variations in rinse duration and base load wear, as well as the relationship between variations in spin duration and base load wear, needs to be investigated. On the other hand, different national standards have varying requirements for the parameters of the base load, detergents, and water hardness, which could affect the model’s universality. To address these issues, transfer learning and self-updating algorithms could be developed in future research29. For example, network-based transfer learning can be employed. Specifically, the first n layers of the current ANN network can be directly used as the first n layers of the target network, with the remaining layers of the target network being randomly initialized and trained using the target dataset. The target dataset consists of the features of the new load fabric, which only needs to contain a small number of samples. However, if the new load fabric parameters differ significantly from those used in this study (e.g., synthetic or blended fabrics), the target model could be negatively impacted and rebuilding the model is recommended.

Conclusion

In this study, the impact of base load ages on the cleaning performance and water extraction performance of washing machines were identified. Base load age within a duration of 4 to 210 h were tested. The detergency rate (reflecting the cleaning performance) and remaining moisture content (reflecting the water extraction performance) of the washing machine were evaluated with increasing base load age, and the reflectance of the base load under each tested age was also recorded. Both front-loading and top-loading washing machine were employed. The detergency rate of top-loading washing machines exhibits a variability range of 26% to 33%, whereas the detergency rate of the top-loading washing machine fluctuates between 31% and 36%. The remaining moisture content of the top-loading washing machine increases from around 55% to 75%, while the moisture content of the front-loading washing machine increases from around 64% to 77%. When the base load age was greater than 70 h, the impact of base load aging on water extraction performance began to decrease. In addition, the reflectance of cotton towels exhibited a progressive decline with increasing base load ages, irrespective of its layer position within the washing machine.

Regression modes were employed to develop prediction model for base load ages using the remaining moisture content and reflectance. For the regression model developed using MLR, the best result was obtained with Rc2 of 0.920, RMSEC of 17.883 h, and Rcv2 of 0.915, RMSECV of 18.390 h for front-loading washing machine, and Rc2 of 0.866, RMSEC of 23.077 h, and Rcv2 of 0.818, RMSECV of 27.635 h for top-loading washing machine. For the regression model developed using ANN, the best result was obtained with Rc2 of 0.978, RMSEC of 9.295 h, and Rcv2 of 0.972 h, RMSECV of 10.639 h, MAE of 7.518 h for front-loading washing machine, and Rc2 of 0.958, RMSEC of 12.948 h, and Rcv2 of 0.940, RMSECV of 15.572 h, MAE of 10.350 h for the top-loading washing machine. According to the scatter plot of prediction results, the ANN-based base load age prediction model utilizing the reflectance of the bottom-layer towel base load and the remaining moisture content of all base loads demonstrated the best performance. The method proposed in this study can be used in industrial washing machine testing labs. On the other hand, controlling the base load age can prevent excessive use of the fabric, which may contribute to reducing microfiber emissions. Although venetian blinds” cross-validation method was used, independent validation better reflects the stability of the model. Therefore, future research should prioritize expanding the sample size to improve model stability. In addition, future research could consider developing a base load age prediction model based on imaging methods. This non-contact base load age management approach could be included in washing machine performance testing standards as a benchmark for base load age management.

Comparative outcomes of aflibercept biosimilars and reference aflibercept in nAMD: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Korolos Sawires, Harrish Nithianandan, Sohel Somani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the literature-pooled functional, anatomical and safety outcomes of aflibercept biosimilars compared with reference aflibercept for neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).Methods and analysisSystematic review and meta-analysis. Medline, Embase and CENTRAL were searched from inception to 2 June 2025. Phase 3 randomised controlled trials (RCTs) comparing aflibercept biosimilars with the reference product in patients with nAMD were included. Two independent reviewers conducted screening, data extraction, risk of bias (RoB 2) assessment and certainty of evidence assessment (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation), with a third reviewer resolving discrepancies. Primary outcomes included mean difference (MD) change in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and retinal thickness over time, risk ratio (RR) for proportion of participants gaining >15 Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters and RR for adverse events. Meta-regression was used to evaluate the temporal stability of continuous outcomes.ResultsSix RCTs involving 2044 participants (1026 biosimilar, 1018 reference) were included. Five aflibercept biosimilars were evaluated, representing more than 50% of the eight biosimilars currently approved worldwide. Meta-regression revealed no significant difference in MD change in BCVA over time between biosimilar and reference aflibercept (slope: 0.0321 letters/week; p=0.1013). For MD change in retinal thickness, an initial anatomical advantage for reference aflibercept at week 0 (intercept: 9.58 µm; p=0.0449) was not sustained over time (slope: –0.1685 µm/week; p=0.2303). The pooled RR for gaining >15 ETDRS letters from baseline was 1.19 (95% CI 0.98 to 1.45; p=0.079). No statistically significant differences were observed across 15 safety outcomes.ConclusionThis systematic review and meta-analysis found no statistically significant differences in functional, anatomical or safety outcomes between aflibercept biosimilars and the reference product for nAMD, based on moderate to high certainty evidence. Functional and anatomical outcomes appeared stable across multiple timepoints. Further long-term pharmacovigilance studies and real-world data beyond 56 weeks are warranted.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251048633.

Introduction

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is an acquired, chronically progressive degenerative disease of the retina and a leading cause of irreversible vision loss in developed countries.13 The prevalence of AMD is estimated at 10%–13% among individuals aged 65 and older.46 Approximately 80% of AMD cases are non-neovascular (dry AMD); however, despite being less common, neovascular AMD (nAMD) accounts for up to 90% of severe vision loss associated with the disease.2 3 The hallmark of nAMD is choroidal neovascularisation (CNV), which may lead to subretinal or sub-retinal pigment epithelium (sub-RPE) haemorrhage and subsequent fibrosis.2 3 Clinically, nAMD typically manifests as decreased visual acuity, blurred vision, metamorphopsia, micropsia and central scotomas.3

Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) levels are elevated in nAMD and are the principal driver of CNV and increased vascular permeability.2 7 As such, intravitreal anti-VEGF injections are the current standard of care for nAMD.3 Pegaptanib (Macugen, Pfizer), introduced in 2004, was the first anti-VEGF agent approved for ophthalmic use. Subsequently, bevacizumab (Avastin, Genentech, San Francisco, California, USA), ranibizumab (Lucentis, Genentech, San Francisco, California, USA) and aflibercept (Eylea, Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, Tarrytown, New York, USA) became widely used for the treatment of nAMD.8 These medications inhibit VEGF, thereby reducing angiogenesis and limiting associated leakage, fluid accumulation and fibrosis.8 More recently, faricimab (Vabysmo, Genentech, San Francisco, California, USA) and high-dose aflibercept (Eylea HD, Regeneron Pharmaceuticals) have been approved for use in nAMD. Faricimab is a bispecific monoclonal antibody that targets both VEGF-A and angiopoietin-2, while aflibercept HD delivers a higher molar dose than the original formulation, aiming to improve treatment durability.911 With the expiration of patents for originator products such as ranibizumab and aflibercept, biosimilar alternatives are now emerging.

The US Food and Drug Administration defines a biosimilar as a large, complex biological molecule produced by a living system that is highly similar to an already approved reference product, with no clinically meaningful differences in terms of safety, purity or potency.12 For biosimilar approval, regulatory agencies require demonstration of comparable efficacy and safety to the reference biological, often through phase 3 non-inferiority randomised controlled trials (RCTs).13 Several RCTs have evaluated the efficacy and safety of aflibercept biosimilars compared with reference aflibercept for the treatment of nAMD.1419 To better inform clinical decisions, a pooled quantitative synthesis of existing studies is warranted. Thus, the objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to provide a literature-pooled summary of the functional, anatomical and safety outcomes of aflibercept biosimilars relative to the reference product in patients with nAMD.

Methods

The study was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251048633) and adheres to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study selection and extraction

A librarian-assisted search strategy (online supplemental table 1) was used to search Medline, EMBASE and CENTRAL from inception to 2 June 2025. RCTs comparing the efficacy and safety of biosimilar aflibercept to the reference product in patients with nAMD were included. Specifically, we included studies reporting changes in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and retinal thickness at different timepoints from baseline, proportion of patients gaining >15 Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters from baseline and adverse events. Measures of retinal thickness reported included central subfield thickness and central retinal thickness. Only explicitly reported data were used. Bibliographies were checked to identify other potential studies. Only studies published in English were included and no date limits were applied. Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) was used to manage study references. Two reviewers (KS, HN) independently completed title and abstract screening, full-text screening, data collection, risk of bias (ROB) assessment and evaluation of the certainty of evidence. The Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomised trials (RoB-2) was used to complete the ROB assessment for included studies.20 The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework was used to evaluate the certainty of evidence. Discrepancies at any stage were resolved by a third author (SS).

The data extraction form was created using Microsoft Excel (V.16.62; Redmond, Washington, USA). The following data was collected from each included RCT: study characteristics (ie, author, year, country and study design), patient characteristics (ie, age, sex and number of treated eyes), treatment characteristics (ie, biosimilar name, dosing regimen, follow-up duration), functional outcomes (change in BCVA at different timepoints, proportion of patients gaining >15 ETDRS letters from baseline), anatomical outcomes (change in retinal thickness at different timepoints) and adverse events.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

All meta-analyses were performed in R (V.4.4.1; R Core Team, 2024) using the ‘metafor’ and ‘meta’ packages with a significance level of 0.05. A random effects model using the restricted maximum likelihood method was applied throughout to account for between study heterogeneity. Continuous variables were extracted as means±SD, while binary variables were recorded as counts from RCTs. The primary outcomes included the mean difference (MD) in change in BCVA (ETDRS letters) over time, the MD in change in retinal thickness over time, the risk ratio (RR) of adverse events and RR for proportion of participants gaining >15 ETDRS letters from baseline. Effect sizes for continuous outcomes were calculated as (biosimilar–reference). The inverse variance method was used for continuous outcomes, while the Mantel-Haenszel method was used for binary outcomes. For continuous outcomes such as the MD change in BCVA and retinal thickness, which can vary based on the time of assessment, we performed a meta-regression analysis with time as a moderator to explore temporal effects. Conversely, for binary outcomes, we performed standard pairwise meta-analyses. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the Cochran’s Q test and I² statistic, with thresholds interpreted according to the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s regression test.

Results

Search results and study characteristics

From 110 records screened, 12 studies underwent full-text review, and 6 met the inclusion criteria for this systematic review and meta-analysis (figure 1). A summary of the included study characteristics is presented in table 1. All six studies were phase 3 RCTs designed to assess non-inferiority. Collectively, these trials included 2044 participants, with 1026 in the aflibercept biosimilar groups and 1018 in the aflibercept reference groups. Five different biosimilars (P041, QL1207, SB15, SOK583A1 and SCD411) were evaluated. Two of the studies, by Woo et al and Sadda et al, reported sequential results for biosimilar SB15, covering weeks 0–32 and 32–56, respectively.18 19 Results for individual studies are presented in online supplemental table 2.

Risk of bias and grade results

Risk of bias assessment for all included studies is presented in online supplemental figure 1 using the ROBVIS tool. Three RCTs were deemed to be low risk of bias, while three were considered to have some concerns. As this study included RCTs, our GRADE assessment initially assigned a ‘high’ evidence certainty level to all outcomes. 17 of 18 outcomes were downgraded to moderate certainty due to imprecision (15/17) and publication bias (2/17). Funnel plots and Egger’s regression test for all outcomes are presented in online supplemental figure 2. One outcome remained rated as high certainty. A GRADE summary of findings is outlined in online supplemental table 3.

Meta-analysis of functional outcomes

Five studies explicitly reported the mean change in BCVA at different time points.1519 Figure 2A illustrates the meta-regression of the MD change in BCVA over time. The slope coefficient of the regression model was 0.0321 ETDRS letters per week, suggesting a trend towards greater visual acuity gains in the biosimilar group over time; however, this was not statistically significant (95% CI –0.0063 to 0.0704; p=0.1013). The intercept estimate at week 0 was –0.5479 letters (95% CI –1.5371 to 0.4413; p=0.2776), slightly favouring reference aflibercept, though also not statistically significant. There was no significant heterogeneity between studies (Q=1.3388, p=0.9695; I2=0). Inclusion of time as a moderator improved model fit, as evidenced by a lower Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) (19.40 vs 22.19) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) (18.78 vs 22.08), as well as a higher log-likelihood (–6.70 vs –9.09), compared with the model without a moderator. Overall, the meta-regression model revealed the MD change in BCVA between biosimilar and reference aflibercept remained stable over time, with no significant advantage observed for either treatment across the range of study timepoints.

(B) Meta-analysis forest plot for proportion of participants gaining >15 ETDRS letters from baseline. BCVA, best-corrected visual acuity; ETDRS, Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study; MH, Mantel-Haenszel.

Five studies explicitly reported the proportion of participants gaining >15 ETDRS letters from baseline.141618 19 Figure 2B depicts the pairwise meta-analysis of the RR for this outcome. The pooled RR using a random-effects model was 1.19 (95% CI 0.98 to 1.45, p=0.079), favouring the biosimilar group; however, this result did not reach statistical significance. There was low heterogeneity between studies (Q=5.41, p=0.2482; I2=26.0%).

Meta-analysis of anatomical outcomes

Five studies explicitly reported the mean change in retinal thickness at different timepoints.1519 Figure 3 presents the meta-regression of the MD change in retinal thickness over time. The slope coefficient of the regression model was –0.1685 µm per week (95% CI −0.4437 to 0.1068; p=0.2303), indicating a non-significant trend towards a greater reduction in retinal thickness in the biosimilar group over time. The intercept estimate at week 0 was 9.5753 µm (95% CI 0.2179 to 18.9327; p=0.0449), indicating a statistically significant initial advantage for reference aflibercept. There was no significant heterogeneity between studies (Q=9.9613, p=0.4439; I2=5.66%). Inclusion of time as a moderator improved model fit, as evidenced by a lower AIC (84.89 vs 90.71) and BIC (85.80 vs 91.51), as well as a higher log-likelihood (–39.44 vs –43.36), compared with the model without a moderator. Overall, the meta-regression model demonstrated the MD change in retinal thickness between biosimilar and reference aflibercept remained stable over time, with an early advantage for the reference product that was not sustained at later study timepoints.

Meta-analysis of safety outcomes

Figure 4 presents all pairwise meta-analyses of adverse events across studies. There was no statistically significant difference in the pooled RR between biosimilar and reference aflibercept for total adverse events (figure 4A: RR=1.01, 95% CI 0.95 to 1.08; p=0.6933; I2=0%), total ocular adverse events (figure 4B: RR=1.06, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.25; p=0.4439; I2=19.1%), treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs) related to the study drug (figure 4C: RR=0.89, 95% CI 0.44 to 1.79; p=0.7480; I2=0%), TEAE leading to discontinuation of the study drug (figure 4D: RR=0.91, 95% CI 0.50 to 1.67; p=0.7684; I2=0%), total serious adverse events (SAEs) (figure 4E: RR=1.14, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.47; p=0.3133; I2=0%), ocular SAE (figure 4F: RR=1.78, 95% CI 0.78 to 4.09; p=0.1740; I2=0%), non-ocular SAE (figure 4G: RR=1.02, 95% CI 0.78 to 1.32; p=0.8982; I2=0%), conjunctival haemorrhage (figure 4H: RR=1.34, 95% CI 0.75 to 2.37; p=0.3204; I2=3.4%), retinal haemorrhage (figure 4I: RR=1.09, 95% CI 0.27 to 4.46; p=0.9019; I2=36.4%), endophthalmitis (figure 4J: RR=1.59, 95% CI 0.20 to 12.86; p=0.6611; I2=0%), RPE tear (figure 4K: RR=1.69, 95% CI 0.42 to 6.86; p=0.4605; I2=0%), cataracts (figure 4L: RR=1.00, 95% CI 0.42 to 2.39; p=0.9961; I2=0%), intraocular pressure (IOP) related adverse events (figure 4M: RR=0.91, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.55; p=0.7246; I2=0%), eye pain (figure 4N: RR=1.14, 95% CI 0.58 to 2.24; p=0.7052; I2=7.8%) and visual impairment (figure 4O: RR=1.06, 95% CI 0.54 to 2.09; p=0.8651; I2=0%).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis of six phase 3 non-inferiority RCTs involving 2044 participants found no statistically significant differences between aflibercept biosimilars and reference aflibercept across functional, anatomical and safety outcomes for the treatment of nAMD. Specifically, there were no differences observed in the MD change in BCVA or retinal thickness over time, the RR for proportion of participants gaining >15 ETDRS letters or the RR for adverse events. These pooled results reinforce the conclusions of individual RCTs evaluating the non-inferiority of aflibercept biosimilars. As such, this meta-analysis contributes important evidence supporting the clinical equivalence of aflibercept biosimilars.

For the outcome of MD change in BCVA (ETDRS letters), the meta-regression model demonstrated no statistically significant difference between aflibercept biosimilars and the reference product across eight follow-up timepoints ranging from 8 to 56 weeks, indicating stable and comparable functional efficacy over time. Similarly, although the pooled RR for the proportion of participants gaining >15 ETDRS letters numerically favoured biosimilars (RR=1.19, 95% CI 0.98 to 1.45; p=0.079), the result did not reach statistical significance. For the outcome of MD change in retinal thickness, the meta-regression revealed a statistically significant intercept estimate favouring the reference product at week 0. However, this early difference was not sustained over time, as there was no statistically significant slope, indicating no persistent anatomical advantage of the reference product over biosimilars across 12 timepoints ranging from 4 to 56 weeks.

Across all safety outcomes analysed, there were no statistically significant differences between aflibercept biosimilars and the reference product. This included total adverse events, ocular adverse events, SAEs, TEAEs and specific ocular complications such as retinal haemorrhage, IOP-related events and endophthalmitis. The pooled RRs for all safety outcomes had CIs crossing the null value, and heterogeneity was non-significant (I²<40%), further supporting the consistency of safety findings across studies. These results are reassuring from a pharmacovigilance perspective and suggest that aflibercept biosimilars do not pose greater safety risks compared with the reference product. Nonetheless, long-term post-marketing surveillance remains essential to confirm these findings over extended follow-up periods exceeding 56 weeks.

Beyond clinical outcomes, economic implications are also critical to consider. Originator biologicals place a significant financial burden on healthcare systems due to high cost. In 2023, aflibercept was the second highest drug by total Medicare spending in the USA, with expenditures exceeding US$3.1 billion, illustrating the substantial economic impact of anti-VEGF therapy.21 The introduction of aflibercept biosimilars holds considerable potential for reducing healthcare expenditures while maintaining comparable clinical efficacy and safety. As outlined by Sharma et al, as of February 2025, eight different aflibercept biosimilars have been approved globally (Europe=6, USA=5, Korea=2, China=1, Iran=1).22 Of these, five are represented in the present systematic review and meta-analysis.

The use of biosimilars in ophthalmology is relatively nascent compared with other disciplines such as rheumatology and oncology.23 24 Consequently, clinicians may be hesitant to adopt anti-VEGF biosimilars in early practice.23 A 2023 survey by Sharma et al of 112 retina specialists in the United States and Europe found that 58.9% expressed concerns about biosimilar efficacy, 67.8% regarding efficacy for extrapolated indications and 50% for both safety and immunogenicity.25 In contrast, a 2024 survey paper by the same authors found that among 231 ophthalmologists in India, 74.5% and 73.6% considered biosimilar ranibizumab to be equivalent in efficacy and safety to the reference product, respectively. Moreover, biosimilar ranibizumab usage was 50%–100% among 51% of survey respondents, suggesting potential regional differences in the early adoption of anti-VEGF biosimilars.26 Another potential explanation for the slower uptake of anti-VEGF biosimilars is the lack of a pre-filled syringe (PFS) formulation, which has historically been approved years after the vial formulation for originator anti-VEGF medications.22 27 PFS formulations offer advantages such as greater precision in the dose and volume of medication administered, reduced injection time and a potential decreased risk of endophthalmitis compared with vial-based delivery.26 28 29

From a patient perspective, the introduction of aflibercept biosimilars may be met with both optimism and caution. While their lower cost may improve affordability and access, some patients may be hesitant to transition to a new agent given the relative novelty of biosimilars in ophthalmology. A 2023 systematic review by Wu et al explored patients’ perceptions of biosimilars across various medical disciplines and found that in 22 of the 31 included studies, over half of patients were willing to accept them. However, patients with prior positive experience with the reference drug were more likely to express reluctance towards switching. This reluctance stemmed from a perceived superiority of the reference drug, lack of pharmacovigilance data comparing a biosimilar with the reference drug, a desire to maintain treatment efficacy and to minimise potential adverse effects of switching.30 These findings are consistent with survey results presented by Tran et al at the 2024 Canadian Ophthalmological Society Annual Meeting, where most patients reported discomfort with switching to biosimilars and emphasised physician input as a key factor in treatment decisions.31 As such, clear communication about the rigorous regulatory pathways for biosimilar approval, real world safety monitoring and the comparable functional, anatomical and safety outcomes demonstrated in RCTs will be critical to building trust. Ultimately, patient acceptance will depend on reassurance from treating ophthalmologists, transparent communication and dissemination of long-term efficacy and safety data.

As aflibercept biosimilars continue to gain approval and enter clinical markets, ongoing evaluation will be critical to support their appropriate integration into practice. This includes the generation of robust real-world evidence, long-term safety monitoring and health economic analyses. Addressing clinician concerns may further support uptake. While aflibercept biosimilars represent a promising strategy to alleviate the financial burden of anti-VEGF therapy, their long-term impact on clinical outcomes, cost-effectiveness and access to care remains to be fully elucidated.

This systematic review and meta-analysis has several limitations. First, although all included studies were phase 3 RCTs, the total number of eligible trials was limited and some biosimilars were represented by only a single study, which limits our ability to conduct molecule-specific meta-analyses and draw robust conclusions about the safety and efficacy of individual biosimilars beyond their respective trials. Second, variability in adverse event reporting across trials resulted in several safety outcomes being analysable only in a subset of studies, potentially limiting our ability to comprehensively evaluate the incidence of rare but clinically important ocular complications. Third, the analysis relied on aggregate data rather than individual patient-level data, precluding adjustment for potential confounders. Finally, follow-up durations were limited to a maximum of 56 weeks; longer-term efficacy and safety comparisons between biosimilars and reference aflibercept remain to be established.

In conclusion, based on moderate to high certainty evidence, this systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates that over 50% of currently approved aflibercept biosimilars are comparable to reference aflibercept in terms of efficacy, anatomical outcomes and safety for the treatment of nAMD. Functional and anatomical outcomes remained consistent across multiple follow-up timepoints, as demonstrated by meta-regression analyses, and no significant differences were observed in the incidence of adverse events. As more biosimilars enter the global market, continued pharmacovigilance and real-world effectiveness studies will be essential to support their adoption and optimise patient care.

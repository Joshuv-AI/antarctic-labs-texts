Introduction

Primary cardiac tumours are exceedingly rare with incidence of 0.001% to 0.03% in autopsy studies.1 Among primary malignant cardiac tumours, cardiac sarcomas represent the most common subtype and are associated with an aggressive clinical course with dismal prognosis. We present an interesting case of intimal sarcoma causing functional mitral stenosis and rapid clinical deterioration at the time of diagnosis. This case highlights the diagnostic complexity and the requirement of a multi-disciplinary team in the challenging management of this rare malignancy.

Case presentation

A 64-year-old female with a medical history notable for hypertension and dyslipidemia presented to the hospital with new onset rapid atrial fibrillation, weight loss and decompensated heart failure. Her initial vitals showed a heart rate of 110 beats per minute and blood pressure of 115/56 mmHg. Physical examination revealed a volume overloaded state with elevated jugular venous pressure and fine crackles to bilateral lungs. Cardiovascular exam was unremarkable with normal first and second heart sounds with no added sounds or murmurs. Initial laboratory results revealed normal white blood cell count of 10.4 × 109/L, haemoglobin 151 g/L and thrombocytopenia 88 × 109/L. Renal function was normal with creatinine of 78 umol/L and eGFR 69 mL/min. NT-pro BNP was elevated at 4545 ng/L. A CT chest was performed for suspicion of a pulmonary embolism revealing a large filling defect in the left atrium but no pulmonary embolism. She was admitted to the cardiac ICU where she was initially treated with intravenous furosemide and digoxin for rate control. A transthoracic echocardiogram confirmed a large 46 mm by 32 mm mass attached to the left atrium protruding into the left ventricle and obstructing the mitral valve causing a significant mitral gradient of 15 mmHg at 77 bpm (see Supplementary material online, Videos S1–S4). Left atrium was moderately dilated by volume index (43 mL/m2) with mildly elevated pulmonary artery systolic pressure of 42 mmHg, in keeping with severe functional mitral stenosis. There was no metastatic disease on full-body CT scan.

The patient had recurrent episodes of flash pulmonary oedema with supine positioning requiring mechanical ventilation. Her admission was complicated by acute kidney injury with rapid creatinine increase to 221 umol/L within 48 h, presumed secondary to diuresis, as well as an embolic stroke. A multi-disciplinary discussion was held with the patient, cardiovascular surgeon, medical oncologist, and the CICU team. Given the patient’s haemodynamic instability, the plan was to undergo surgical debulking with palliative intent. The patient underwent subtotal resection (see Supplementary material online, Video S5) with pathology showing an undifferentiated pleomorphic spindle cell tumour with necrosis and MDM2 nuclear immunohistochemistry staining, in keeping with a high-grade intimal sarcoma (Figure 1A–D).

(A) Gross anatomical pathology of partially resected cardiac tumour. (B) Pleomorphic spindle cell lesion with brisk mitotic activity. (C) MDM2 nuclear staining, in keeping with a diagnosis of intimal sarcoma. (D) Negative staining for SMA.

The patient was discharged in stable condition. Compounding her clinical complexity, she and her family did have significant anxiety and distress as they navigated how to cope with such a severe illness. Due to substantial risk of recurrent cardiac events, liposomal doxorubicin was recommended to reduce risk of further cardiotoxicity. Surveillance imaging in the form of echocardiogram and CT were obtained to determine efficacy of the chemotherapy. Initial stabilization of disease was seen. However, repeat CT after 6 cycles demonstrated interval progression as well as a carotid artery thromboembolism. Her warfarin was switched to low molecular weight heparin, and she was planned for transition to second-line therapy with pazopanib and palliative radiation. In the interim, she had developed lingual sensation deficits with MRI head showing numerous enhancing lesions in the supratentorial region and cerebellum concerning for metastatic disease.

Unfortunately, the patient died 6 months after her diagnosis from a catastrophic posterior circulation and brainstem haemorrhagic stroke.

Discussion

Intimal sarcoma, also known as cardiac undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, is a highly aggressive mesenchymal neoplasm that most commonly arise in the left atrium and is characterized by MDM2 amplification.2 Clinical presentation ranges from decompensated heart failure to complete absence of symptoms. Diagnosis is often delayed, as patients typically remain asymptomatic until the disease is advanced and affects cardiac function. Consequently, prognosis is poor due to late-stage detection and extensive local invasion or distant metastases. Similar to the present case, several reports have described intimal sarcomas causing functional mitral stenosis through intra-cardiac obstruction.3–6 The mainstay of treatment is complete surgical resection, which has been shown to significantly improve survival. Median cumulative survival with surgical resection can be improved to 12 months compared with 1 month in non-resected patients.7 However, complete resection is often difficult due to aggressive extension of disease and sub-total resection is associated with high rates of recurrence. Incomplete resection may still be pursued as a palliative strategy to alleviate symptoms and allow patients time to process their diagnosis.

Systemic chemotherapy is indicated for advanced grade disease, recurrent tumours, or incompletely resected tumours. While evidence is limited to small case series and retrospective studies, intimal sarcomas have demonstrated responsiveness to anthracycline-based regimens, either as a single agent or in combination with ifosfamide.8 In patients who are non-responsive to first-line therapy or who have contraindications to anthracyclines, second-line agents such as gemcitabine or pazopanib may be considered.9,10 Adjuvant radiation therapy has proven to be beneficial in doubling median survival in patients with primary cardiac spindle cell sarcoma following surgical resection compared with surgery alone.11

Due to the rarity and clinical complexity of primary cardiac tumours, evidence-based guidelines are extremely limited, and management decisions should be guided by a continuous communication of the multi-disciplinary team. This case highlights the variable and often fulminant clinical course of cardiac intimal sarcoma. The rapid progression from diagnosis to death highlights the critical need for early recognition and coordinated care across disciplines to address not only cancer related impact but also coagulation and heart function risks. Furthermore, the psychosocial burden of this diagnosis on patients and their families can be profound. As such, integration of supportive services—including psychiatry, psychology, and social work—should be considered essential in the comprehensive management of these patients, given the potential for sudden deterioration and catastrophic outcomes despite best efforts.

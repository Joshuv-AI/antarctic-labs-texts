Exploring the advantages of mixed reality technology in Robot-assisted laparoscopic partial adrenalectomy
Authors: Xin Chang Zou, Zhe Hao Wu, Xiang Da Xu, Hai Chao Chao, Jian Biao Huang, Tao Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and purposeAs an emerging interactive display technology, mixed reality(MR) allows for the integration of multi-dimensional virtual objects with the real world, enhancing the precision of surgical operations. This technology has demonstrated significant value in renal tumor surgeries, but its application in adrenal tumor surgeries remains unclear. This study aims to investigate the clinical efficacy and potential benefits of mixed reality technology in robot-assisted laparoscopic partial adrenalectomy (RALPA) for treating adrenal tumors.Patients and methodsA total of 82 eligible patients who underwent RALPA for adrenal tumors at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University from September 2020 to October 2024 were included in this retrospective study. The patients were divided into two groups based on their preoperative diagnosis and treatment: 32 patients in the mixed reality (MR)group and 50 patients in the traditional CT imaging (control) group. All patients received bilateral adrenal gland CT scans, both plain and enhanced. The MR group utilized CT data to create a virtual model, which was then used for surgical planning and intraoperative guidance through a 3D tablet computer and Hololens glasses. Preoperative variables and perioperative indicators were collected to evaluate surgical outcomes in both groups.ResultsPostoperative statistics indicated that the operation time for the MR group was significantly shorter than that of the control group (102.00(98.00 103.00) minutes vs. 103.50(101.00 106.75) minutes, p = 0.017). The estimated intraoperative blood loss was also lower in the MR group compared to the control group (58.50(53.00 63.00) ml vs. 77.00(72.00 86.00) ml, p < 0.001). Additionally, the recovery time for gastrointestinal function in the MR group was significantly shorter than that in the control group (28.00(26.00 30.00) hours vs. 32.00(30.00 35.00) hours, p < 0.001). While there was no significant difference in the incidence of postoperative complications between the two groups, the postoperative hospitalization duration for patients in the MR group was significantly shorter (4.00(4.00 5.00) days vs. 5.00(5.00 5.00), p = 0.004). No statistically significant difference was observed in the spectrum of potential adrenal diseases between the MR and control groups (p > 0.05).ConclusionCompared to traditional CT imaging, RALPA guided by mixed reality technology may be associated with a modest reduction in surgical time, a decrease in intraoperative blood loss, and faster recovery of gastrointestinal function. However, the clinical significance of these observed differences remains limited, and the findings should be interpreted cautiously due to the retrospective study design. Prospective controlled studies are needed to confirm whether MR technology provides a true clinical advantage over conventional imaging in adrenal surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04398-0.

Introduction

Adrenal tumors originate from the adrenal glands and may be benign or malignant based on histology [1]. Although relatively rare, they are clinically significant among endocrine tumors. Adrenocortical tumors have an incidence of around four per million, while adrenomedullary tumors like pheochromocytomas are even less common [2, 3]. Clinical manifestations vary depending on functional status and hormonal activity. Cortisol or catecholamine secreting tumors can lead to systemic endocrine disturbances including hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and glucose intolerance, which complicate both diagnosis and preoperative management [4]. For patients with primary aldosteronism, total adrenalectomy remains the standard surgical approach. It is important to emphasize that partial adrenalectomy should be reserved only for selected cases where there exists a strong clinical rationale for adrenal preservation. Functional status directly influences surgical planning. Nonfunctional tumors typically allow straightforward resection, whereas hormonally active tumors require meticulous perioperative preparation and refined dissection techniques due to hypervascularity or inflammatory changes. Tumor size, location, and suspicion of malignancy also affect the choice between minimally invasive and open surgical approaches. Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of treatment. Postoperative recovery, recurrence rates, and long-term metabolic outcomes are strongly influenced by the underlying disease pathophysiology, emphasizing the need for individualized treatment strategies and multidisciplinary collaboration [5]. Laparoscopic adrenalectomy is now considered the gold standard due to its minimal invasiveness, faster recovery, and lower complication rates [6]. RALPA offers improved precision and is increasingly used for complex anatomical cases, demonstrating benefits for various tumor types and postoperative biochemical outcomes [7]. However, complex tumors—especially those deeply located or adherent to major vessels—remain challenging. In these situations, RALPA guided solely by conventional CT imaging may have limitations [8].

The application of mixed reality technology in clinical operations has garnered widespread attention in recent years. MR provides new perspectives and tools for surgical procedures by seamlessly integrating virtual information with the real environment [9, 10]. This technology can create a three-dimensional model of the patient’s organs and virtually superimpose it on the surgical area, enabling the surgeon to access critical anatomical information in real-time during surgery, thereby enhancing the accuracy and safety of the procedure [11, 12]. MR has been shown to have significant guiding value in the surgical resection of renal tumors. Additionally, holographic images based on mixed reality can effectively assist in the successful implementation of percutaneous renal puncture [13, 14]. These applications not only improve visualization during surgery but also reduce the incidence of postoperative complications, reflecting the great potential and importance of mixed reality technology in modern medicine [15].

This study is the first to construct a three-dimensional model of adrenal tumors using mixed reality and to explore the application advantages of MR in RALPA.

Patients and methods

Patients

A retrospective analysis was conducted on the relevant data of 82 suitable patients with adrenal tumors who underwent RALPA at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University from September 2020 to October 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1)Aged between 21 and 80 years; (2)Diagnosed with an adrenal tumor via enhanced CT within one month before surgery; (3)Complete clinical and imaging data available. The exclusion criteria included: (1)The presence of other tumors in the ipsilateral urinary tract; (2)Abnormal coagulation function or the use of oral anticoagulants; (3)Severe cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, or systemic diseases. The patients were divided into two groups based on preoperative diagnosis and treatment: 32 patients in the MR group and 50 patients in the traditional CT imaging(control) group.

Clinical data collection

The collected data included the patient’s age, gender, BMI, underlying conditions(hypertension and diabetes), history of previous abdominal surgeries, tumor location, tumor diameter, American Society of Anesthesiologists(ASA) grade, operation time, blood loss, postoperative hospitalization duration, and postoperative complications. The study also recorded the pathological type of the tumor and the recovery time for postoperative gastrointestinal function.

The primary endpoint of this study was to compare operative time between the MR group and the control group. Secondary endpoints included differences in mean blood loss, the time to recovery of gastrointestinal function, complication rates, and the duration of postoperative hospital stay.

CT image collection and mixed reality model construction

All patients underwent both plain and enhanced CT scans of the bilateral adrenal glands. To ensure consistency in the mixed reality reconstruction model, scans were performed using our hospital’s GE Light Speed 64- and 128-slice VCT machines, with a slice thickness of 1.25 mm. Patients were positioned supine and instructed to control their breathing as directed. First, a conventional plain scan was conducted, followed by the injection of iohexol-enhanced contrast agent through the cubital vein. Subsequently, scans were performed during the arterial phase, venous phase, and delayed phase. All plain scan and enhanced CT images were collected and stored in DICOM format. The imaging data was imported into medical image processing software (Smart Vision SDVWorks, Shenzhen Yitu Co., Ltd.), where thin-section images (1.25 mm thickness) corresponding to each scanning stage were selected. Specific modules were used to segment the adrenal glands, tumors, blood vessels, adjacent organs, and other structures. Any unrecognized regions were segmented manually. Kidneys and arteries were highlighted in red, adrenal glands in green, veins in blue, and tumors in yellow. The resulting images were then visualized using HoloLens glasses and a Nubian glasses-free 3D tablet (see Fig. 1).

Technical considerations

All patients underwent RALPA, with all procedures utilizing the retroperitoneal approach. The MR group employed a mixed reality-based 3D model, utilizing a 3D tablet computer and HoloLens glasses for preoperative planning and intraoperative guidance. In contrast, the control group relied on traditional enhanced CT images for surgical guidance. All surgeries were performed by an experienced surgical team.

Mixed reality applications and surgical methods

The mixed reality system, incorporating glass-free 3D tablets and HoloLens headsets, was used to improve patient communication and spatial understanding of adrenal anatomy through interactive 3D visualizations. During surgery, the system provided real-time holographic guidance by superimposing reconstructed models onto the surgical field, enhancing orientation and dissection precision, especially in complex cases with challenging anatomy or proximity to critical structures. The registration and overlay of 3D models were performed autonomously by the surgical team using anatomical landmarks (e.g., the renal hilum, psoas major muscle, and twelfth rib) without requiring expert assistance, with a mean registration error of 0.86 mm. The system dynamically compensated for positional differences between preoperative MR (supine) and intraoperative positioning (lateral decubitus), as well as for respiratory movement and soft tissue deformation during dissection, ensuring accurate alignment throughout the procedure. Additionally, HoloLens glasses facilitated the manipulation of visibility for various tissues and organs, allowing for adjustments in viewing angles, zooming in and out of specific areas and overall structures, modifying distances, and segmenting components. This aided in the real-time monitoring of tumors, adrenal glands, surrounding blood vessels, and adjacent organs (Fig. 2). No intraoperative failures or system interruptions were recorded.

After successful anesthesia, the patient was positioned in a 90° lateral decubitus position, with the lumbar bridge elevated, and the surgical field was disinfected using routine draping. A 3 cm incision was made with a sharp knife on the iliac crest along the mid-axillary line. The peritoneum was separated using large curved forceps, and a balloon was inserted and inflated with 1000 ml of fluid to expand the retroperitoneal space. Guided by finger placement, an assistant’s 12 mm trocar was then positioned about 8 cm below and inside the space, penetrating 8 mm into the front edge of the psoas muscle, 2 cm below the 12th rib. A da Vinci 8 mm trocar was placed in the posterior axillary line and secured with silk thread for the da Vinci’s special bipolar electrocoagulation forceps. The da Vinci laparoscope was then inserted, followed by another da Vinci 8 mm trocar in the subcostal axillary line for the da Vinci’s special monopolar electric scissors. The three robotic arms were connected to the trocars, and the robotic laparoscope, monopolar electric scissors, and bipolar electrocoagulation forceps were introduced. The lateral peritoneum was incised, the extraperitoneal fat was separated, and Gerota’s fascia was dissected. The adrenal gland was separated along the inner and upper part of the kidney’s upper pole, followed by separation from the outside and above. After removing some of the upper pole fat, the tumor was completely freed from below the adrenal gland. The adrenal tumor was subsequently fully excised, and, based on the surgeon’s expertise and the patient’s specific condition, a decision was made to preserve part of the normal adrenal tissue to ensure continued functionality. Upon examination, no active bleeding was detected, and a pararenal drainage tube was placed. The scope was withdrawn, and the cannula was removed. The specimen was extracted through the incision in the posterior axillary line. After counting the instruments and gauze, the wound was sutured in layers. All surgical procedures in both groups were performed by the same experienced surgical team, with the MR cases performed after the team had completed a standardized training protocol of five MR-assisted cases to mitigate the learning curve effect.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 27.0 was used to analyze and process the data. All measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as counts (%). Single-factor analysis was employed to compare differences in clinical factors. For measurement data, t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests were used, whereas chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests were applied for categorical variables. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patients

In this study, retroperitoneal robot-assisted laparoscopic adrenal tumor resection was successfully performed in both groups. None of the patients required conversion to open surgery. The MR group utilized mixed reality technology for preoperative evaluation and intraoperative navigation, while the control group relied on traditional contrast-enhanced CT images for surgical guidance. No significant differences were found between the two groups in terms of age (p = 0.377), gender (p = 0.297), BMI (p = 0.422), history of hypertension (p = 0.386), history of diabetes (p = 1.000), history of previous abdominal surgery (p = 0.666), tumor size (p = 0.117), tumor location (p = 0.184), and ASA grade (p = 0.909) (all p > 0.05) (Table 1).

Postoperative statistical data indicated that the MR group had a shorter operation time compared to the control group (102.00(98.00-103.00) minutes vs. 103.50(101.00-106.75) minutes, p = 0.017), suggesting that MR technology may contribute to reducing the duration of surgery. Additionally, the MR group experienced significantly less blood loss than the control group (58.50(53.00–63.00) ml vs. 77.00(72.00–86.00) ml, p < 0.001). The gastrointestinal function recovery time was also significantly shorter in the MR group than in the control group (28.00(26.00–30.00) hours vs. 32.00(30.00–35.00) hours, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the postoperative hospital stay for the MR group was significantly shorter (4.00(4.00–5.00) days vs. 5.00(5.00–5.00), p = 0.004). However, there were no significant differences in the incidence of postoperative complications between the two groups (3.12% vs. 6.00%). Complications in four patients were all classified as Grade I according to the Clavien-Dindo classification. Moreover, no statistically significant differences were found in the pathological types between the MR group and the control group (p > 0.05) (Table 2).

Discussion

In recent decades, robotic minimally invasive surgery has advanced rapidly, profoundly transforming surgical practice [16]. In laparoscopic adrenal tumor surgery, robotic systems provide exceptional precision and control through high-definition 3D visualization and multi-articulated instruments, enabling smaller incisions, reduced postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays, and faster recovery [17–18]. Enhanced instrument maneuverability minimizes damage to periadrenal tissues and decreases complication risks. Robotic assistance also improves surgical success and complete resection rates via accurate anatomical reconstruction [19, 20].

Conventional CT dependent robotic surgery, however, remains constrained by its reliance on static planar images without real-time 3D feedback, limiting comprehension of complex anatomy and adaptability during surgery [21–23]. MR technology blends virtual and physical worlds via real-time interaction and 3D visualization, allowing interaction with digital objects in real space [24–26]. With progress in computing and sensors since the 1990s, MR now serves healthcare, education, and industrial applications [27]. In medicine, MR assists in surgical planning and training by offering detailed anatomical understanding through 3D models, increasing operative accuracy [28]. Studies by Li et al. and Nithipalan et al. have validated MR utility in laparoscopic nephrectomy and prostate biopsy, showing improvements in planning, navigation, teaching, and outcomes, including reduced operating time and blood loss [29, 30]. Its use in urological training and performance has also been shown to be as effective as traditional methods while offering superior immersive experience [31, 32]. Despite these advances, MR application in adrenal tumor surgery is still nascent. Our group introduced MR in robot assisted laparoscopic adrenal resection, using HoloLens to overlay real-time 3D models of tumors and critical structures into the surgeon’s field of view. The system provides continuous spatial updates and visual warnings when approaching vital tissues, thereby increasing safety and efficiency Fig. 3. It also supports dynamic surgical navigation and preoperative simulation, which builds surgical confidence and improves actual procedural safety and efficiency [33].

Adrenal tumor pathology significantly affects outcomes. Aldosterone-producing adenomas, often smaller and less vascular, allow shorter operations, whereas cortisol-secreting adenomas frequently require longer dissection due to adhesions, increasing time and blood loss [34, 17]. MR navigation shortens operation time and reduces bleeding for both types, and improves recovery through more precise resection [35]. The MR-assisted group also showed faster gastrointestinal recovery, likely due to improved spatial perception reducing intestinal manipulation and ileus risk [36].

This study compared outcomes between two patient groups undergoing RALPA with or without MR assistance. The results demonstrated that the MR group exhibited a significantly shorter operative time (102.00(98.00-103.00) minutes vs. 103.50(101.00-106.75) minutes, p = 0.017), as well as faster recovery of gastrointestinal function (28.00(26.00–30.00) hours vs. 32.00(30.00–35.00) hours, p < 0.001), reduced blood loss (58.50(53.00–63.00) ml vs. 77.00(72.00–86.00) ml, p < 0.001), and a shorter postoperative hospital stay (4.00(4.00–5.00) days vs. 5.00(5.00–5.00), p = 0.004). The incidence of postoperative complications was lower in the MR group (1/32 vs. 3/32), although this difference did not reach statistical significance.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective nature of this study introduces the possibility of selection bias despite the implementation of strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. Second, the relatively small sample size may limit the statistical power and generalizability of the results. Third, variations in drainage tube duration among patients represent a potential confounding factor that was not adequately controlled in our analysis. Fourth, the single-center setting may restrict the applicability of our conclusions to broader clinical practice environments. Furthermore, baseline differences between groups, specifically in gender distribution and pheochromocytoma case numbers, may have influenced the observed outcomes. Both factors are known to significantly affect operative duration in retroperitoneal adrenal surgery and could account for the relatively modest difference in surgical time between groups. Importantly, the observed reduction in operative time (approximately 1.5 min) has limited clinical significance, and this modest difference may not be a major clinical advantage of MR technology. Additionally, several real-world clinical barriers to the widespread adoption of MR technology should be considered, including its cost-effectiveness, the associated learning curve for surgeons, accessibility in resource-limited hospital settings, and potential intraoperative limitations such as registration failure or interference with surgical workflow. To address these limitations, we are planning a large-scale, multi-center prospective study that will focus on a single tumor type, specifically aldosteronoma, to better isolate the effect of mixed reality technology in partial adrenalectomy while controlling for potential confounding variables.

Conclusion

Mixed reality technology in RALPA may represent a notable innovation in surgical practice. Compared to traditional CT imaging, MR assistance may be associated with improved procedural accuracy, safety, and patient outcomes. However, given the limitations of the retrospective study design, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Prospective controlled studies are warranted to further validate the potential advantages of MR technology and its prospects for integration into future surgical practice.

Tamm plasmonic-based elliptical anisotropic photonic crystals with multifunctional optical features
Authors: Shohreh Jooyandeh, Mahmood Hosseini Farzad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent years, multifunctional photonic structures have attracted increasing interest due to their ability to perform diverse optical functions within a single platform. In this work, we design and investigate a one-dimensional anisotropic photonic crystal that simultaneously exhibits high-performance optical sensing, filtering, direction-dependent reflection and absorption, as well as an electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT)-like phenomenon. The structure consists of periodic layers of SiO₂ and a layer with a variable refractive index ranging from 1.36 to 1.44, along with a thin gold film that supports the excitation of a Tamm plasmon mode (TPM). To enhance and diversify the optical functionalities, an elliptically anisotropic metamaterial layer is incorporated into the periodic structure. Numerical simulations reveal that the structure not only exhibits a sharp TPM-related resonance shift in response to refractive index variations but also acts as a perfect absorber. Furthermore, the presence of anisotropy introduces polarization and propagation-direction-dependent reflection characteristics and enables the emergence of a Tamm-EIT-like transparency window in the reflection spectrum. These findings highlight the potential of the proposed anisotropic photonic crystal as a compact multifunctional platform for advanced photonic applications.Subject terms: Optics and photonics, Physics

Similar to electronic band gaps that govern electron flow in semiconductors, photonic band gaps (PBGs) enable precise manipulation of photons for applications such as wavelength-selective filtering, waveguiding, and refractive index sensing. Propagation through the PBG spectral regions is prohibited due to the destructive interference of light1. By tailoring parameters such as lattice constant, refractive index contrast, and dimensionality, photonic crystals (PhC) can confine and guide light with high precision, forming the foundation of integrated photonic circuits. Introducing metallic layers at the PhC interface leads to the formation of Tamm plasmon modes (TPMs) which are localized surface states arising from the coupling between surface plasmons and the photonic band gap2,3. These modes exhibit sharp spectral resonances and strong electromagnetic field confinement, enabling high-sensitivity sensing, ultra-narrowband filtering, and low-threshold lasing4. However, conventional TPMs based on isotropic dielectrics5 offer limited tunability, which restricts their practical applicability. To overcome these limitations, anisotropic and metamaterial-based designs have been extensively explored. Incorporating anisotropic metamaterials provides additional control over field confinement, dispersion, and polarization6,7. For instance, Wu et al.6 demonstrated that a heterostructure composed of indium tin oxide (ITO) and a hybrid one-dimensional PhC supports angle-dependent Tamm plasmon polaritons (TPPs) exhibiting blue shift under TM polarization. Recent advances have further combined TPMs with defect cavities, bound states in the continuum (BICs), and magneto-optical effects to achieve enhanced performance8–10. Hybrid platforms employing hyperbolic metamaterials or magneto-optic dielectrics enable nonreciprocal filtering, directional routing, and enhanced transverse magneto-optical Kerr effect (TMOKE)11–13. Moreover, the integration of active materials such as graphene, liquid crystals, and phase-change materials (PCMs) enables dynamic and reversible tunability9,14,15. Graphene allows electrical modulation of resonance spectra, liquid crystals provide voltage-controlled birefringence, and PCMs enable reversible phase transitions. In addition, multi-resonant and dual-mode TPMs improve sensing performance by supporting multiple coupled plasmonic modes, leading to ultra-narrow transmission peaks and high figures of merit16,17. These systems can simultaneously detect multiple environmental parameters, thereby enhancing both accuracy and versatility. The integration of TPMs with quantum emitters and two-dimensional materials further enables enhanced control of spontaneous emission and quantum light generation18–20. In parallel, electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT), a quantum interference effect that produces sharp transmission peaks with steep dispersion has been successfully emulated in photonic and plasmonic systems through classical mode interference. Incorporating EIT-like resonances into TPM-based structures significantly enhances sensing performance by combining high spectral selectivity with strong field confinement21. Furthermore, the combination of defect layers, metamaterials, and metallic films enables the realization of tunable ultra-narrowband filters with high quality factors. Anisotropic photonic crystals, characterized by direction-dependent refractive indices, provide polarization selectivity, dispersion engineering, and strong birefringence. The incorporation of elliptically anisotropic metamaterials further enhances these properties, supporting hyperbolic dispersion, polarization-dependent TPMs, and improved TMOKE responses22–24. With the increasing demand for compact and multifunctional photonic devices, recent research has focused on integrated systems capable of performing multiple optical functions such as filtering, sensing, and polarization control within a single platform25,26. Incorporating anisotropic layers and metamaterials introduces direction-dependent permittivity tensors that enable tunable polarization behavior, nonreciprocal propagation, and EIT-like responses27,28. These hybrid and reconfigurable systems represent a significant step toward practical, integrated, and actively tunable photonic platforms for sensing, communication, and quantum technologies14. Recent studies have further highlighted the potential of hybrid photonic crystal structures for novel light–matter interactions. For example, angle-independent coupling between topological interface states and Tamm plasmon polaritons has been demonstrated in heterostructures composed of photonic crystals incorporating hyperbolic metamaterials29, enabling strong polarization-dependent absorption over a wide angular range. In another approach, graphene-assisted Tamm plasmon cavities have been employed for self-referencing refractive index sensing30, where strong mode coupling achieved through optimization of the cavity layer and tuning of the graphene chemical potential results in high sensitivity and an enhanced figure of merit.

In this study, we propose and numerically investigate the optical properties of a one-dimensional photonic crystal structure comprising alternating layers of SiO₂, a tunable dielectric medium with a refractive index ranging from n = 1.36 to n = 1.44, an elliptically anisotropic metamaterial, and a thin gold film supporting Tamm plasmon modes. This configuration enables the systematic investigation of photonic crystal effects both in the presence and absence of the anisotropic layer. Sensitivity analysis indicates that the refractive index sensitivity of the structure without the anisotropic layer is approximately 202.5 nm/RIU, whereas it increases to about 300 nm/RIU with the inclusion of the anisotropic layer. This range of indices (1.36–1.44) encompasses a wide variety of relevant materials, including biological fluids, common organic solvents, transparent polymers, and aqueous solutions, which are critical for the design and optimization of optical sensors. To further explore the structure’s performance, a 1000 nm-thick silicon defect layer was introduced in the middle of the proposed photonic crystal, resulting in the emergence of a quasi-electromagnetically induced transparency (quasi-EIT) phenomenon. Overall, the proposed design for a Tamm plasmonic-based elliptical anisotropic photonic crystal represents a multifunctional platform, simultaneously enabling refractive index sensing, spectral filtering, and functionality in photonic applications.

For the basic configuration, without the use of metamaterial layers, the TPM effect is observed, yielding a sensitivity of 202.5 nm/RIU, as shown in Fig. 6 and defined by Eq. (13). Upon incorporating metamaterial layers into the structure, the sensitivity increases markedly to 300 nm/RIU (Fig. 5), demonstrating a substantial performance enhancement enabled by the metamaterial integration. This improvement can be attributed to the enhanced separation of TPM resonance dip positions along the wavelength axis, as clearly observed in Fig. 5 compared to Fig. 6. Notably, as summarized in Table 1, most previously reported Tamm plasmon-based refractive index sensors exhibit sensitivities below 300 nm/RIU. For example, hybrid Tamm–cavity configurations typically achieve sensitivities in the range of 51–259 nm/RIU, while Tamm plasmon H₂ sensors report values around 237 nm/RIU. In contrast, the optimized structure proposed here reaches a sensitivity of 300 nm/RIU over the refractive index range of 1.36–1.44, positioning it among the highest reported values. This result highlights the effectiveness of incorporating anisotropic metamaterial layers in enhancing sensor performance beyond conventional designs. Furthermore, earlier studies on porous silicon-based TP structures combined with metal layers have demonstrated comparatively lower sensitivities within similar refractive index ranges. Tamm-EIT phenomenon

A notable feature in the reflection spectra of the proposed structure with an introduced defect layer is the emergence of a quasi-electromagnetically induced transparency (quasi-EIT) associated with the Tamm plasmon mode. This behavior is observed only within a limited range of incident angles that support the formation of the Tamm plasmon mode. As shown by the two-dimensional reflectivity map (R-map), this diagram is used to determine the angular and spectral position of the resonance dip (Fig. 7). The results indicate that the resonance wavelength shifts with the variation of the incident angle, confirming the angular dispersion of the Tamm plasmon mode. In this context, the blue regions in the R-map represent the angular and wavelength ranges where the Tamm plasmon mode can be excited. Based on the R-map analysis, an incidence angle of 65° is selected as a representative angle, since a strong, stable, and high-contrast resonance dip is observed at this condition. Furthermore, within the photonic band gap region under this angle (1000–1800 nm), a deep reflectivity minimum is observed at a wavelength of 1501.9 nm (as shown in Fig. 8(a)). This behavior indicates that the excitation of the Tamm plasmon mode occurs with maximum clarity and intensity among the investigated angles, which is in full agreement with the reflection map results. Under these condition, efficient coupling between the incident wave and the surface Tamm plasmon mode leads to the formation of a pronounced dip in the reflection spectrum.

To induce the quasi-EIT effect, a silicon (Si) defect layer with a thickness of 1000 nm is introduced near the middle of the proposed configuration, as illustrated in Fig. 9. In the absence of the defect layer, the Tamm plasmon mode appears at a wavelength of approximately 1501.9 nm, as shown in Fig. 8(a). Upon introducing the defect layer, a transparency peak corresponding to the quasi-EIT phenomenon emerges near the same wavelength as the Tamm dip, accompanied by a slight red shift toward longer wavelengths, as illustrated in Fig. 8(b).

A comparison of the spectral responses at different incident angles reveals that the most pronounced and well-defined quasi-EIT feature occurs under the selected excitation condition, whereas broader and less distinct features are observed at higher angles. Therefore, in this article, we investigate the optical properties of the proposed metamaterial-based anisotropic photonic crystal at an incident angle of 65°.

Polarization and direction dependent (reflection)

Light typically propagates symmetrically in standard optical systems, exhibiting similar reflection and transmission characteristics from either side. However, in our proposed photonic crystal Fig. 3, which combines conventional dielectric layers, anisotropic metamaterial layers, and a metal layer, the optical response is strongly dependent on both the propagation direction and the polarization of the incident light. As shown in the reflection, transmission, and absorption spectra (different parts of Fig. 11), for TM polarization, Fig. 11(a) corresponds to illumination from the left, whereas Fig. 11(b) corresponds to illumination from the right side. As shown in Fig. 11 (a), illumination from the Au layer (left side) produces a pronounced dip in reflectivity at a wavelength of about 1800 nm and nearly perfect absorption at the same wavelength. In contrast, when the direction of illumination is reversed (from the right side), the corresponding spectra do not show any absorption or transmission (see Fig. 11(b)); nearly all incident light is reflected. This asymmetric optical property for the TM mode is unique and differs from those reported for other photonic crystal structures in the literature. However, the situation is completely different for the TE mode. According to Fig. 11(c) and Fig. 11(d), for both directions of illumination, the reflectivity remains close to unity over a wide wavelength range (at least from 1600 nm to 2400 nm), while both transmission and absorption are nearly zero. Therefore, for TE polarization, we obtain a direction-independent perfect reflector in this metamaterial-based anisotropic photonic crystal. This asymmetric optical response is governed by Tamm plasmon modes at the metal–photonic crystal interface, which confine the electromagnetic field and enhance coupling between the incident wave and the surface mode. For TM polarization, the electric field component normal to the metal interface enables efficient excitation of these modes, resulting in near-perfect absorption (R ≈ 0, A ≈ 1). In contrast, TE-polarized waves cannot couple to the surface mode, and the metal layer reflects nearly all incident light (R ≈ 1, A ≈ 0). This polarization- and direction-dependent reflection originates from the structural asymmetry of the multilayer system and the anisotropic properties of the metamaterial layers38, rather than from true nonreciprocal transmission, and is fully consistent with Lorentz reciprocity39, as also reported in Ref. [36] for perfect absorption structures. Accordingly, the proposed structure does not function as an optical isolator. These results highlight the crucial role of anisotropic metamaterial properties in achieving direction and polarization dependent optical responses. The proposed configuration can be regarded as a Tamm-plasmon-assisted, polarization-selective perfect absorber, with potential applications in narrowband optical absorption and refractive index sensing.

Fabrication Techniques and Feasibility of the Proposed Device

The proposed multilayer structure can be fabricated using well-established and advanced thin-film deposition techniques. Gold layers can be deposited via thermal or electron-beam evaporation. Dielectric layers in the photonic crystal can be fabricated using sputtering or chemical vapor deposition. Air layers can be realized by incorporating sacrificial layers during fabrication, which are subsequently removed via selective etching to create nanometer-scale air gaps. This is a standard approach in multilayer photonic crystals and microcavities. The metamaterial layers in our design are ultra-thin. The fabrication of such layers requires advanced deposition techniques such as atomic layer deposition (ALD) or molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) to achieve continuous, uniform films at the nanoscale. While more challenging than conventional thin-film deposition, these methods have been successfully applied in previous studies of thin multilayer photonic structures. Layer thicknesses can be precisely controlled using in situ monitoring techniques, such as a quartz crystal microbalance or optical monitoring.

Conclusion

In this study, a multifunctional optical structure based on Tamm plasmon modes is designed and its optical properties are investigated. The anisotropic metamaterial layers in the proposed photonic crystal play a crucial role in enabling several unique properties. The strategic combination of the anisotropic metamaterial and the Tamm plasmon platform results in a compact, highly sensitive structure with multifunctional optical properties. Incorporating anisotropy not only increases the refractive index sensitivity but also enables novel light–matter interaction regimes and the emergence of a quasi-electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT)-like response associated with the Tamm mode (in the presence of a suitable defect layer). It should be noted that the EIT peak is relatively broad, and its maximum occurs at a wavelength that is red-shifted by a few nanometers with respect to the Tamm dip wavelength. On the other hand, the direction- and polarization-dependent optical response of the proposed anisotropic photonic crystal (without a defect layer) enables it to act as a unidirectional absorber or perfect reflector. This behavior arises from the combination of structural asymmetry and the anisotropic properties of the metamaterial layers, which make the coupling to Tamm plasmon polariton modes strongly dependent on both the illumination direction and polarization. As a result, for TM polarization, light is almost completely absorbed when incident from the metal side, while it is largely reflected when incident from the photonic crystal side. For TE polarization, the reflection remains nearly unity in both directions, with negligible transmission and absorption. Overall, the results underscore the capability of this anisotropic photonic crystal to serve as a compact and multifunctional solution for advanced photonic applications.

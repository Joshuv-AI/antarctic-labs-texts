Physics-constrained multimodal reinforcement learning for local UAV Navigation in complex static obstacle environments
Authors: Yawei Tian, Yu Chen, Zhongliang Deng, Heng Yang, Xinling Wen, Teng Jiang, Yapeng Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLocal navigation in complex static obstacle environments requires an unmanned aerial vehicle to reach a target while avoiding obstacles under limited local perception. U-shaped non-convex obstacles acting as local traps further increase the difficulty of detour selection and continuous-control execution. A key challenge is to jointly capture forward scene structure, surrounding geometric constraints, and execution-level control requirements within a unified decision-making pipeline. To address this challenge, we present PC-MM-SAC, a task-oriented multimodal reinforcement-learning framework with a lightweight physics-constrained action-execution layer for local navigation in complex static obstacle environments. The method learns from structured observations built from depth maps, LiDAR measurements, and task-related state variables. The layer applies bounded action mapping and yaw-rate variation limiting to improve the smoothness and continuity of continuous-control commands without introducing an additional online optimization-based controller. We further employ an event-conditioned modality value-sensitivity analysis during evaluation to characterize how the critic’s local sensitivity to different information sources varies across representative navigation phases. In AirSim experiments, PC-MM-SAC achieves a success rate of 0.88 and an episode reward of 772, attaining the highest success rate among the evaluated methods under the current task setting. Ablation and behavioral analyses suggest that multimodal observations are the primary contributor to the observed performance improvement, while the physics-constrained action-execution layer is associated with smoother trajectory execution and reduced local oscillation.Keywords: Deep reinforcement learning, Local UAV navigation, Multimodal perception, Physics-constrained action-execution layer, Policy interpretabilitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The application of drones in scenarios such as inspection, disaster response, and autonomous operations in complex environments is continuously expanding, making autonomous flight capability a core issue in related research and engineering deployment1–3. Local navigation is a key component of autonomous flight, requiring the aircraft to reach its destination while avoiding obstacles under limited perception conditions. The challenges of local navigation in complex obstacle environments arise not only from the increase in the number and spatial density of obstacles but also from the combined effects of local passability ambiguity and continuous-control constraints. Local traps represented by U-shaped non-convex obstacles further increase the difficulty of detour decision-making and trap-escape control4,5. Recent reviews of robotic positioning systems likewise highlight the importance of integrating complementary sensing and perception methods in autonomous robotic applications6.

Reinforcement learning provides a technical pathway for UAV local navigation to directly learn continuous-control strategies from perceptual inputs. Research has shown that learning-based navigation methods have gradually expanded from early vision-driven and multi-sensor fusion approaches to continuous control and autonomous decision-making in complex obstacle environments. Deep reinforcement learning has been applied to autonomous navigation and obstacle-avoidance tasks in complex environments4,5,7. Related research has further expanded to unknown environments and high-density dynamic scenarios, demonstrating strong task adaptability8,9. Studies on path tracking, speed adaptation, and autonomous flight under GPS-denied conditions further indicate the broader potential of this line of research10,11. Related AI-based control studies have explored optimized convolutional models for steering-angle prediction under varying operating conditions12. Nevertheless, local navigation in complex obstacle environments still requires further development, especially in areas such as environmental representation for local decision optimization, smooth execution of continuous actions, and behavioral analysis across different navigation stages.

To address the aforementioned issues, this paper presents PC-MM-SAC, a task-oriented multimodal reinforcement-learning framework for local UAV navigation in complex static obstacle environments. The framework uses depth maps, LiDAR measurements, and task-related state variables to construct a structured observation for SAC-based continuous control. A lightweight physics-constrained action-execution layer is introduced between policy output and environment execution. Through bounded action mapping and yaw-rate variation limiting, the layer is designed to improve the smoothness and continuity of continuous-control commands without introducing an additional online optimization-based controller. During evaluation, an event-conditioned modality value-sensitivity analysis is further employed as a post-hoc diagnostic tool to characterize how the critic’s local sensitivity to different information sources varies across representative navigation phases.

The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:

A task-oriented structured tri-modal observation design is developed for local UAV navigation in complex static obstacle environments, including non-convex local-trap scenarios. Forward-facing depth maps, LiDAR measurements, and task-related state variables are organized to provide complementary information about local scene structure, surrounding geometric constraints, and navigation-state information, respectively.

A task-oriented structured tri-modal observation design is developed for local UAV navigation in complex static obstacle environments, including non-convex local-trap scenarios. Forward-facing depth maps, LiDAR measurements, and task-related state variables are organized to provide complementary information about local scene structure, surrounding geometric constraints, and navigation-state information, respectively.

Related work

Multimodal state representation and fusion

Reinforcement learning has become an important approach to local UAV navigation and obstacle avoidance. Recent reviews show that this line of research has gradually moved from early feasibility verification in specific scenarios to continuous control and end-to-end decision-making in complex environments13. Rubí et al. apply deep reinforcement learning to quadrotor path following and reactive obstacle avoidance14, and Grando et al. further extend this line of work to mapless 3D navigation15. Studies on localized planning and navigation in unknown environments have also established a basic framework in which decision-making is driven by local perception16. Beyond standard actor–critic backbones, distributional off-policy methods such as Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) have also been developed to control value overestimation in continuous-control tasks17. From a broader perspective, visual cues and robust feature extraction have also been explored for robot guidance in visually challenging environments. For example, Ghosh et al. investigate flag-based human-supervised robot guidance using locality-preserving projections18. These works lay the foundation for perception-driven navigation. However, the construction of state representations for local navigation in complex obstacle environments remains insufficiently discussed, especially in relation to task characteristics and the organization of input information.

Camera-based perception has demonstrated practical value in UAV positioning and control. Demirhan and Premachandra develop an automated camera-based landing system, while Premachandra et al. investigate vision-based hovering control for a small aerial robot using existing floor features19,20. However, local navigation in complex obstacle environments often requires more complete local geometric information than that typically needed for task-specific positioning, hovering, or landing. Xiao et al. use visual and range information as joint inputs in multimodal reinforcement learning for navigation and report better performance than single-modality configurations21. Ma et al. further demonstrate the utility of LiDAR–camera fusion for low-altitude UAV obstacle detection22. A recent review of AI-enhanced robotic positioning also emphasizes the importance of sensor fusion in autonomous robotic applications23. These studies suggest that multimodal sensing can provide complementary decision information for navigation in complex environments.

Safe execution and physics-constrained control methods

In continuous-control tasks, a high-return policy does not necessarily produce commands that can be executed smoothly by the vehicle. Brunke et al. point out that learning-based control in real-world deployment remains constrained by physical limits, dynamic constraints, and training-safety considerations, and that control-level constraint mechanisms therefore remain important24. For UAVs, this issue is particularly relevant: excessively large velocity magnitudes, rapid yaw variations, or frequent local corrections may lead to oscillatory trajectories and discontinuous control execution. Habibi et al. incorporate obstacle-avoidance requirements into the safe control design of quadrotors25, whereas Kaymaz et al. discuss the trade-off between safety and agility in perception-driven motion planning26. Taken together, these studies show that execution constraints influence not only safety-related behavior but also trajectory continuity and control quality.

Existing studies have mainly focused on safety controllers, motion planners, and outer-loop safety mechanisms, whereas lightweight action-constraint processing at the policy-execution stage has received comparatively less attention. Recent deep-reinforcement-learning studies on path planning for real quadrotors have begun to employ instruction filters to smooth policy outputs27. Reinforcement learning has also been combined with robust control to suppress execution-stage disturbances and improve safety28. Under input delay, model uncertainty, and positioning errors, robust compensation structures and uncertainty-aware error-correction methods can further improve closed-loop reliability29–31. These studies provide useful references for execution-stage constraint design. More rigorous constrained-control approaches, including safe-reinforcement-learning methods, safety layers, barrier-function-based controllers, and MPC-based methods, address constrained navigation through explicit safety mechanisms, formally defined safety conditions, predictive models, or online optimization. The present study focuses on a complementary lightweight execution-stage mechanism that can be integrated directly into the SAC-based navigation pipeline while preserving the original learning backbone, with the aim of improving command smoothness and continuity without introducing an additional online optimization-based controller.

Interpretability analysis methods

As deep reinforcement learning is increasingly applied to navigation tasks, policy interpretability has become an important research issue. He et al. use feature attribution to generate visual and textual explanations for UAV path planning, with the aim of analyzing the basis of policy decisions32. At the methodological level, current research on interpretable reinforcement learning mainly covers input attribution, value-sensitivity analysis, policy visualization, and post-hoc explanation33. Hickling et al. systematically review interpretability methods in deep reinforcement learning together with their application scenarios34, whereas Bekkemoen provides a broader survey and taxonomy of explainable reinforcement-learning methods35. This body of work indicates that reinforcement-learning interpretability has developed a relatively clear methodological landscape and already offers useful tools for behavioral analysis in navigation tasks.

For navigation problems, however, many existing interpretability studies remain at the level of global explanation or single-step attribution. Recent reviews argue that interpretability in high-risk reinforcement-learning tasks requires finer-grained analytical approaches that are more closely linked to task semantics36,37. In robotic scenarios, layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP)-based representation analysis has been used to characterize how different observation components influence policy behavior38. Obstacle proximity, sharp turning, and goal approach correspond to different navigation phases, and the relative sensitivity to different information sources may vary across these phases. Organizing interpretability analysis around behavioral events therefore helps characterize phase-dependent modality-sensitivity patterns in a more task-relevant manner. Building on these studies, the present work employs an event-conditioned critic-gradient sensitivity analysis to examine these patterns across representative navigation phases.

Problem formulation

Task definition and environment setup

This study considers a planar local navigation task in a complex static obstacle environment. A UAV starts from an initial position, autonomously flies toward a target region within a bounded workspace, and avoids collisions with obstacles during navigation. To represent challenging local-navigation conditions, the environment contains multiple types of static obstacles, including U-shaped non-convex structures.

An episode terminates when one of the following conditions is satisfied: the UAV successfully reaches the target region, collides with an obstacle, exits the predefined workspace boundary, or reaches the maximum allowed number of steps. In the subsequent evaluation, all termination cases other than successful arrival at the target region are counted as failures. Under this setting, the task is formulated as a sequential decision-making problem with continuous states and continuous actions.

Methods

Overall framework

Figure 1 presents the overall framework of PC-MM-SAC. Based on the structured observations defined in the Problem Formulation section, the environment outputs depth images, state vectors, and LiDAR measurements at each time step. These three inputs are processed by modality-specific encoders, and the extracted features are concatenated to form a fused representation. The fused representation serves as the shared input to the actor and critic for continuous policy learning. The raw action output by the actor is then mapped by the physics-constrained action-execution layer to an executed action, which is applied in the environment. During evaluation, event segmentation is combined with modality value-sensitivity analysis to characterize phase-dependent modality-sensitivity patterns across representative navigation phases.

Results

This section presents the experimental setup and evaluation results of PC-MM-SAC, including overall performance comparison, ablation studies, controlled-perturbation analysis, trajectory-level behavioral analysis, representative failure-case examination, and event-conditioned modality value-sensitivity analysis.

Experimental setup

Environment and scenario setup

All experiments were conducted on the Microsoft AirSim simulation platform. Based on the local navigation task defined earlier, we constructed two groups of complex static-obstacle scenarios, referred to as the training environment and the validation environment. The two environments share the same obstacle categories and task setting but differ in spatial layout and goal configuration. Figure 3 shows the top and side views of the training and validation environments, including the UAV start region, the goal region, and the main obstacle structures. The observation and action spaces follow the previously defined observation and action formulations and are not repeated here.

Experimental environments for local navigation in complex static obstacle environments.

Baselines and ablation settings

To evaluate both the overall performance of the proposed method and the contribution of its individual components, we conducted two types of comparisons: navigation-method comparisons and ablation studies. In the overall comparison, two classical reactive local-navigation methods, Artificial Potential Field (APF) and Dynamic Window Approach (DWA), were included alongside four learning-based continuous-control baselines: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3), SAC, and Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC). APF generates local navigation commands from target attraction and obstacle repulsion, whereas DWA evaluates short-horizon local trajectories within a sampled velocity window. Neither APF nor DWA requires policy training or prior access to a global map. PPO, TD3, SAC, and TQC provide representative learning-based references under the unified experimental protocol. To broaden the learning-based comparison further, PC-RG-SAC is additionally included as an adaptive-fusion comparator.

The ablation study was conducted under a unified SAC backbone to isolate the contributions of multimodal observation and the physics-constrained action-execution layer. Specifically, five configurations were considered: State only, Depth + State (denoted as D+S), LiDAR + State (denoted as L+S), Depth + LiDAR + State (denoted as D+L+S), and the proposed method PC-MM-SAC. The first four configurations were used to compare the incremental contributions of different observation components, whereas PC-MM-SAC further introduced the physics-constrained action-execution layer on top of D+L+S.

Ablation analysis under different modality configurations

PC-MM-SAC exhibits a comparatively stable overall trend in the middle and later stages of training, suggesting that the lightweight action-execution layer provides an additional benefit on top of the tri-modal observation.

Robustness evaluation under controlled perturbations

To further examine evaluation-time robustness, the trained PC-MM-SAC policies were tested under five controlled perturbation conditions: sensor noise, LiDAR dropout, depth-image distortion, horizontal wind disturbance, and localization error. Each perturbation was introduced separately during evaluation, while the trained policy parameters remained unchanged. For the sensor-noise condition, Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 5% of the corresponding sensing range was added to the valid depth and LiDAR measurements. The LiDAR-dropout condition randomly removed 10% of the LiDAR points. Depth-image distortion was simulated using 5% range noise together with 10% invalid depth pixels. The wind-disturbance condition applied a horizontal wind disturbance of 0.50 m/s, whereas the localization-error condition introduced a position error of 0.30 m and a heading error within .

Table 5 summarizes the evaluation results under the controlled perturbation conditions. Compared with the unperturbed baseline, the trained policies retain a substantial level of navigation performance across the evaluated disturbances, although the magnitude of performance variation differs across perturbation types. Sensor noise and depth-image distortion are associated with the most noticeable reductions in Success Rate and Episode Reward. LiDAR dropout and localization error produce smaller changes under the current setting. Under the horizontal wind-disturbance condition, the Success Rate remains unchanged, while Episode Length and Episode Reward exhibit minor fluctuations. These results provide an initial characterization of the robustness of PC-MM-SAC under controlled simulation perturbations.

Microscopic evolution of modality value sensitivity in typical navigation events

To analyze changes in modality value sensitivity during local decision-making, Figure 10 shows the temporal evolution of modality value sensitivity for three typical events, namely Near-obstacle, Hard-turn, and Goal-approach, and Figure 11 provides representative depth-image snapshots for the Near-obstacle and Hard-turn events.

Microscopic temporal evolution of modality value sensitivity in typical navigation events.

Representative depth-image snapshots for the Near-obstacle and Hard-turn events. The first row corresponds to Near-obstacle, and the second row corresponds to Hard-turn.

In the representative Near-obstacle event, modality value sensitivity does not remain fixed. In the early stage, image value sensitivity is higher, indicating that the critic value estimate is more sensitive to forward local structure. As the UAV gets closer to the obstacle, LiDAR value sensitivity gradually increases and exceeds image value sensitivity in the later stage. Combined with the depth snapshots in Figure 11, this change corresponds to the process in which nearby obstacle boundaries become increasingly apparent and the local free space continues to shrink. This result indicates temporal heterogeneity within Near-obstacle events, characterized by a transition from higher image sensitivity to higher LiDAR sensitivity.

During the Hard-turn event, LiDAR value sensitivity remains high, while image value sensitivity remains relatively stable. The corresponding depth snapshots indicate that this phase is usually associated with rapidly approaching obstacle boundaries, abrupt changes in the passable direction, or the reopening of local space after a turn. Compared with Near-obstacle, Hard-turn is associated with relatively high sensitivity to both available turning space and the current forward structure. As a result, both the LiDAR and image modalities maintain relatively high value sensitivity during this phase.

During the Goal-approach event, state-vector value sensitivity increases noticeably, whereas LiDAR value sensitivity decreases. At this stage, the UAV is already close to the target region, local obstacle constraints are weakened, and variables such as relative distance to the goal, relative bearing, and vehicle state become more directly relevant to value evaluation. The image modality remains relatively stable during this phase, but its role is less pronounced than in the Near-obstacle and Hard-turn phases.

Overall, the representative temporal segments are consistent with the event-dependent modality-sensitivity patterns identified by the aggregated statistical analysis. These examples further illustrate local variations in critic sensitivity across navigation stages.

Conclusions

This study presented PC-MM-SAC, a task-oriented multimodal reinforcement-learning framework for local UAV navigation in complex static obstacle environments. The framework combines a structured tri-modal observation design, a lightweight physics-constrained action-execution layer, and an event-conditioned modality value-sensitivity analysis.

Under the current AirSim evaluation setting, PC-MM-SAC demonstrates superior navigation-completion performance compared with the evaluated classical reactive baselines and learning-based continuous-control baselines. The ablation results suggest that the structured multimodal observation is the primary contributor to the overall performance improvement. The combination of forward-facing depth maps, LiDAR measurements, and task-related state variables provides complementary information about local scene structure, surrounding geometric constraints, and navigation objectives.

The trajectory, state-evolution, and spatial value-distribution analyses are consistent with a positive role of the lightweight action-execution layer in improving command smoothness and continuity and reducing local oscillation. The event-conditioned analysis further provides a post-hoc diagnostic perspective on how the critic’s local sensitivity to different modalities varies across representative navigation phases. The representative failure-case analysis further indicates that, despite the overall improvement, prolonged obstacle-proximal interactions, repeated directional corrections, and persistent local oscillations can still remain challenging in a limited number of difficult cases under the current evaluation setting.

This study has several limitations. The current validation is mainly based on local navigation tasks in static obstacle simulation environments. The additional controlled-perturbation evaluation provides an initial characterization of the robustness of PC-MM-SAC under simulated disturbances. However, the current study does not yet cover dynamic obstacles, complex three-dimensional motion, or perception noise and dynamic disturbances on real platforms. Although the runtime analysis provides an initial characterization of computational latency on the desktop-based AirSim platform, embedded performance and power consumption, hardware-in-the-loop validation, and real-flight feasibility remain to be evaluated. Future work will extend the task setting to dynamic obstacles and three-dimensional environments while advancing deployment-oriented validation and introducing more fine-grained behavior-analysis tools to further investigate the internal decision patterns and modality-interaction dynamics underlying multimodal navigation policies.

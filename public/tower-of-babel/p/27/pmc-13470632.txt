Reactivation after total thyroidectomy? A thyroid eye disease case that challenges established clinical paradigms
Authors: Alejandra Niño Alarcón, Diana Cristina Henao, Leonardo Rojas, Ana María Gomez, Yeimer Ortiz Martínez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThyroid eye disease (TED) is the most common extrathyroidal manifestation of Graves’ disease, with an incidence of approximately 1.9 cases per 10,000 inhabitants per year. Its pathophysiology involves an autoimmune response mediated by TSH receptor antibodies (TRAbs), leading to inflammation, fibroblast activation, and orbital tissue remodeling. Although TED typically becomes inactive within 1–3 years, 5–10% of patients experience recurrences. Persistence or reactivation after total thyroidectomy is uncommon, and published reports remain limited. We report a 56-year-old man with Graves’ disease diagnosed in 2020 and multiple TED reactivations treated with intravenous methylprednisolone. In 2022, he was diagnosed with low-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma (T1a N0 M0) and underwent total thyroidectomy; radioiodine therapy was withheld due to orbitopathy. Despite complete surgery, he developed a new reactivation in November 2023. In March 2025, he presented with ocular pain; active TED was confirmed with a CAS of 6/7 and moderate-to-severe EUGOGO classification. Laboratory tests revealed positive TRAbs and suppressed TSH. A multidisciplinary team recommended another steroid course followed by tocilizumab. TED reactivation after thyroidectomy is rare and challenges the rationale of antigen removal. Possible mechanisms include microscopic residual thyroid tissue or persistent TRAb activity. Similar reports are limited, and this appears to be the first case described in Colombia. This case highlights the need for multidisciplinary management and the potential role of immunomodulators such as tocilizumab in refractory post-thyroidectomy TED. Background

Thyroid eye disease (TED) is the most common extrathyroidal manifestation of Graves’ disease, with an estimated incidence of 1.9 cases per 10,000 inhabitants per year (1). Among patients with autoimmune hyperthyroidism, the prevalence of TED is approximately 40% (2). Its pathophysiology involves an autoimmune response mediated by autoantibodies against the TSH receptor (TRAbs), which trigger inflammation, fibroblast infiltration, and orbital tissue remodeling (2). Although the disease typically evolves toward inactivity within 1–3 years, a subset of patients may experience recurrences (5–10%) (1). Persistence or reactivation of TED after total thyroidectomy is rare, and reports in the literature remain limited.

Case presentation

We report the case of a 56-year-old man, non-smoker, with Graves’ disease diagnosed five years earlier, who experienced multiple reactivations of TED treated with intravenous methylprednisolone pulses. At initial diagnosis, his first episode presented with a clinical activity score (CAS) – which assesses inflammatory activity in thyroid orbitopathy – of 3/7 and a moderate-to-severe European Group on Graves’ Orbitopathy (EUGOGO) status. He was treated with a total of 4.5 g of intravenous methylprednisolone over 12 weeks.

Two years later, he experienced a second episode and again received a cumulative dose of 4.5 g of methylprednisolone. Three months thereafter, he was diagnosed with papillary thyroid carcinoma, infiltrative follicular variant (T1a N0 M0 ATA – low risk, with a maximum tumor diameter of 0.8 cm), and underwent total thyroidectomy. Adjuvant radioiodine therapy was withheld due to the risk of orbitopathy exacerbation.

One year after surgery, he developed a third TED episode, with a CAS of 5/10 and moderate-to-severe EUGOGO classification. He was deemed a candidate for a regimen of 750 mg of intravenous methylprednisolone weekly for 6 weeks, followed by 500 mg weekly to complete a total 12-week course (total dose 7.5 g; cumulative dose 16.5 g).

Two years later, he again presented with ocular pain and bilateral upper eyelid edema. Active TED was documented, with a CAS of 6/7 and moderate-to-severe EUGOGO status. Physical examination revealed bilateral upper eyelid edema, spontaneous and gaze-evoked ocular pain, conjunctival hyperemia, and chemosis predominantly in the left eye, along with exophthalmos and moderate soft-tissue inflammation (Fig. 1).

Investigation

Laboratory evaluation showed positive TRAbs, inadequate TSH suppression on levothyroxine 125 μg/day, and an indeterminate biochemical response (Table 1). The patient was evaluated by a multidisciplinary orbitopathy board, which recommended a new six-week course of intravenous methylprednisolone followed by second-line immunomodulatory therapy with tocilizumab 8 mg/kg administered intravenously at weeks 0, 4, 8, and 12. The initiation of a new six-week course of intravenous methylprednisolone was primarily driven by the healthcare system-related logistical constraints that prevented timely access to second-line immunomodulatory therapy. Given evidence of ongoing active disease, deferral of treatment was considered to carry an unacceptable risk. At the time of writing of this case report, tocilizumab had not yet been administered because these access barriers persisted, necessitating continued reliance on glucocorticoids as a bridging strategy.

Treatment

Treatment was administered in accordance with the 2021 European Group on Graves’ Orbitopathy (EUGOGO) clinical practice guidelines for the medical management of active TED. During the first admission, the patient received a cumulative dose of 4.5 g of intravenous methylprednisolone over 12 weeks. In the subsequent year, a second course consisting of an additional 4.5 g of intravenous methylprednisolone was administered.

During the third admission, he was treated with intravenous methylprednisolone at a dose of 750 mg weekly for six weeks, followed by 500 mg weekly for six additional weeks, completing a 12-week regimen with a total of 7.5 g, resulting in an accumulated exposure of 16.5 g.

In the most recent admission, following evaluation by a multidisciplinary orbitopathy team, a new six-week course of intravenous methylprednisolone was initiated, followed by second-line immunomodulatory therapy with tocilizumab.

Outcome and follow-up

Unfortunately, long-term follow-up was not feasible due to administrative limitations within the national healthcare system. To date, no further TED reactivation has been documented, and IL-1-targeted therapy has not been initiated.

Discussion

Reactivation of TED after total thyroidectomy is an uncommon phenomenon that challenges the pathophysiological rationale based on the removal of the stimulating antigen. Because thyroid dysfunction and elevated TRAbs promote TED, reducing these antibodies through surgery has been proposed as potentially beneficial for patients with Graves’ disease; however, the impact of thyroidectomy on the course of TED remains controversial (3). European Group on Graves’ Orbitopathy guidance does not endorse thyroidectomy to prevent or alter the natural course of TED. Available evidence indicates that surgery to treat hyperthyroidism (including total thyroidectomy) does not significantly impact the development or progression of TED compared with other definitive treatments, and therefore, surgical choice is not motivated by orbital disease outcomes alone (1). Current treatment options include glucocorticoids, orbital radiotherapy, and monoclonal antibodies, such as tocilizumab, rituximab, and teprotumumab, for moderate-to-severe active disease.

Initially, Marcocci et al. observed that thyroidectomy did not significantly modify the clinical course of TED, a finding they attributed to the possible presence of residual thyroid tissue, which in some cases depended on the extent of resection (total vs partial) (4). In contrast, Moleti et al. reported that thyroidectomy alone was able to reduce the CAS in the short term, although it was less effective in maintaining long-term TED inactivity compared with combined thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy (5). Some authors have suggested that early surgical intervention could induce sustained remission of TED (6); however, this case demonstrates that inflammatory activity may persist or recur even in the absence of apparent thyroid tissue.

Following total thyroidectomy, a gradual decrease in TRAb levels is typically observed. Takamura et al. even reported complete elimination of these antibodies on long-term follow-up (7). Nonetheless, several mechanisms have been proposed to explain TED reactivation, including the persistence of subclinical thyroid tissue or sustained elevations in TRAb titers. In addition, some authors have suggested that persistent or rising antithyroglobulin antibodies (TgAbs) after total thyroidectomy may indicate the presence of residual thyroid tissue, including thyroglobulin-producing tumor tissue (3, 8, 9, 10, 11).

In cases of TED refractory to glucocorticoid therapy, an emerging therapeutic option is tocilizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the interleukin-6 (IL-6) receptor. This agent has demonstrated efficacy in treating active TED and refractory dysthyroid optic neuropathy (DON) and has also been associated with reductions in TRAb levels (2). Case reports and a series of nine patients aged 40–84 years with chronic moderate-to-severe active TED have described clinical improvements with subcutaneous tocilizumab after failure of previous therapies, with reductions in CAS and in TSH receptor-stimulating immunoglobulins (11, 12). However, the optimal duration of therapy with this agent remains unclear. Evidence in clinical scenarios such as the one presented continues to be limited. To our knowledge, this is the first case reported in Colombia with these clinical characteristics.

Thyroid acropachy in Graves’ disease
Authors: Talia Diker-Cohen, Keren Kaminer, Eyal Robenshtok
Published: 2025

Clinical image

A 54-year-old man presented with palpitations, emotional lability, and excessive sweating. He had been diagnosed with Graves’ disease ten years earlier but was poorly compliant with anti-thyroid therapy. On examination, he appeared anxious and restless. Bilateral exophthalmos with stare was noted, with preserved ocular motility, no lid lag, and a clinical activity score of 0 (Figure 1a). His pulse was 108 beats per minute, the thyroid was enlarged on palpation, and his hands were warm and moist and had a fine tremor. Striking digital clubbing was noted on all fingers (Figure 1b).

(a) Exophthalmos and stare consistent with thyroid ophthalmopathy. (b) Marked digital clubbing consistent with thyroid acropachy.

Thyroid tests showed suppressed thyroid-stimulating hormone (<0.01 mIU/L), elevated free thyroxine (25.75 pmol/L; reference 9.00–19.10), free triiodothyronine (17.3 pmol/L; reference 2.6–5.7), and high thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin (TSI) of >500% (reference 0–149). Due to long-term active smoking, computed tomography of the chest was performed to exclude pulmonary malignancy; findings were unremarkable.

The combination of exophthalmos and digital clubbing in the setting of active hyperthyroidism established the diagnosis of thyroid acropachy, an uncommon manifestation of autoimmune thyroid disease.1,2 Thyroid acropachy, characterized by digital clubbing, periosteal new bone formation, and soft-tissue swelling, occurs in <1% of patients with Graves’ disease and almost exclusively in association with ophthalmopathy and dermopathy.2 The pathogenesis is thought to involve stimulation of fibroblasts by thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins leading to glycosaminoglycan deposition and soft-tissue proliferation.1,3,4

The patient was counseled regarding disease complications and smoking cessation,5 restarted methimazole therapy, and demonstrated biochemical improvement with normalization of thyroid function during follow-up.

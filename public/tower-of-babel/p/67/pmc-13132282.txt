Combined open and minimally invasive strategy for thoracic ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament: a case report and technical notes
Authors: Shenghui Tang, Junhua Zuo, Hui Ren, Binwei Chen, Xiaobin Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Thoracic ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (T-OPLL) causes severe neurological deficits. Surgical management is challenging due to the thoracic spine’s anatomical complexity. This case report aims to describe a novel hybrid open-endoscopic technique for the treatment of severe thoracic OPLL, with the objective of demonstrating its feasibility and safety in a high-risk obese patient.Presentation of case:A 29-year-old male with class III obesity was hospitalized for progressive bilateral lower extremity weakness over 2 months, recently worsening with new-onset numbness. Examination revealed hypoesthesia below the costal margin, decreased lower extremity motor strength, and reduced anal tone. Integrating open and minimally invasive endoscopic techniques achieves comprehensive 360° decompression while minimizing trauma and complications. This hybrid technique is an alternative for severe thoracic spinal stenosis primarily from ventral compression.

Background

Thoracic ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (T-OPLL), characterized by pathological calcification and thickening of the posterior longitudinal ligament, represents a common etiology of thoracic spinal stenosis. Although T-OPLL is relatively rare and progresses insidiously, it can lead to severe neurological deficits, including lower extremity numbness, progressive motor weakness, gait disturbance, and bladder/bowel dysfunction, significantly impairing patients’ quality of life. Once symptomatic, the condition typically follows a progressive course with limited response to conservative management. Therefore, early surgical intervention is strongly recommended when clinical manifestations correlate with radiographic evidence of spinal cord compression[1,2].

Owing to the unique anatomical complexity of the thoracic spine, surgical management of T-OPLL presents notable clinical challenges. While various surgical approaches (including posterior laminectomy, circumferential decompression, and full endoscopic decompression) have demonstrated favorable clinical outcomes, systematic review studies have indicated they remain associated with considerable complication rates[3,4]. Consequently, the current focus of clinical research and surgical innovation in T-OPLL treatment lies in exploring techniques that maintain optimal decompression outcomes while minimizing surgical invasiveness and reducing procedure-related complications.

Minimally invasive spine surgery (MIS) has evolved significantly over the past three decades, driven by technological advances and a growing emphasis on reducing surgical morbidity. Bibliometric analyses of MIS research reveal a consistent trend toward less invasive, higher-precision techniques across surgical subspecialties[5]. Similarly, the digital transformation of surgery – including the integration of artificial intelligence, navigation, and enhanced imaging – has further refined endoscopic and computer-assisted procedures, improving safety and outcomes in complex spinal pathologies[6].

Despite these advances, severe ventral compression in T-OPLL remains a challenge due to limited working space and risk of cord injury. In this context, we present a modified hybrid technique for the treatment of thoracic OPLL that integrates open decompression with endoscopic ventral resection. The primary objective of this case report is to assess the feasibility, safety, and early clinical outcomes of this combined approach in a high-risk obese patient with severe ventral compression. To the best of our knowledge, no cases utilizing this combined surgical approach for T-OPLL have been reported. This case report has been reported in line with the SCARE checklist[7].

Case presentation

Literature search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases from inception to December 2024 using the following keywords: “thoracic OPLL,” “minimally invasive spine surgery,” “endoscopic decompression,” “hybrid technique,” and “surgical outcomes.” Included studies were limited to English-language publications describing the surgical management of T-OPLL. The final reference list was selected based on relevance and methodological quality.

General information

A 29-year-old male with class III obesity (132 kg, 176 cm, BMI >40) was hospitalized for progressive bilateral lower extremity weakness persisting over 2 months, with recent exacerbation accompanied by new-onset numbness involving the lumbar region and bilateral lower limbs during the past week. Neurological examination demonstrated hypoesthesia below the costal margin, with motor strength graded 2/5 in the right lower extremity and 3/5 in the left. Anal sphincter tone was mildly diminished, correlating with Frankel grade C impairment. Deep tendon reflexes were reduced in the right lower limb, while bilateral Babinski signs were positive. Sagittal-plane CT scan revealed beak-type ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) formation was observed at the T5/6 levels (Fig. 1A). Transverse-plane CT showed centrally located OPLL at the T5/6 level (Fig. 1B). Corresponding MRI findings demonstrated significant spinal cord compression at the T5/6 levels, showing characteristic hyperintense signal changes on T2-weighted imaging (Fig. 1C and D). These pathological changes have resulted in secondary spinal canal stenosis at the affected levels. Figure 1.Preoperative imaging data. Surgical technique

Posterior spinal decompression and pedicle screw fixation

Following successful endotracheal intubation general anesthesia, a 16 cm midline posterior incision was created over the T4–T7 region. Sequential dissection through subcutaneous tissue and fascia is performed, followed by meticulous paraspinal muscle retraction to expose the laminae and transverse processes of T4–T7. After fluoroscopic confirmation of anatomical levels, pedicle screws are implanted bilaterally under fluoroscopic guidance, with satisfactory positioning confirmed. A titanium rod was contoured and secured to the left-side screw heads. Complete T5 spinous process and laminectomy were performed using an ultrasonic bone scalpel to achieve posterior decompression of the stenotic thoracic canal.

Working port establishment

The right T6 superior articular process and adjacent ligamentum flavum were resected under direct visualization for foraminal enlargement. An 18-gauge spinal needle was percutaneously inserted about 8 cm lateral to the midline under combined fluoroscopic and direct visualization, targeting the T5/6 intervertebral space through the right neural foramen (Fig. 2A). After guidewire placement and needle withdrawal, an 8 mm skin incision was made. Sequential dilators were passed over the guidewire, followed by placement of an endoscopic working cannula (Fig. 2B). Final fluoroscopic verification confirmed optimal trajectory alignment (Fig. 2C and D). Figure 2.Intraoperative images. Endoscopic procedure

The endoscopic system was connected with continuous irrigation. Radiofrequency coagulation achieved hemostasis. The ventral ossified lesion compressing the dural sac was meticulously resected using an endoscopic diamond burr under high-magnification visualization (Fig. 2E). Loose nucleus pulposus fragments within the spinal canal and intervertebral space were removed. Complete ventral decompression was confirmed by restoration of dural pulsation and thorough inspection of the operative field (Fig. 2F). Final hemostasis was achieved before cannula removal.

Bone grafting and drain placement

The right-side titanium rod was secured, followed by cross-link fixation between the bilateral rods. After copious irrigation, the left T5/6 facet joint and transverse processes were decorticated with a high-speed burr for autologous cancellous bone grafting. A closed suction drain was placed through the right percutaneous access site. Layered wound closure was performed with interrupted absorbable sutures.

Results

The total operative time was 245 min, with estimated blood loss of 350 ml. Drain output was 180 ml over 72 h. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 7. At discharge, lower limb muscle strength had recovered to grade 3, enabling independent standing and assisted ambulation with orthotic devices. At 1-month follow-up, strength improved to grade 4; at 3 months, he could walk independently with a cane. At 6 months post-operation, the patient achieved independent ambulation without aids and complete resolution of numbness. The modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association score improved from 3 preoperatively to 6 at discharge, and further to 11 at 6 months. This indicates that the patient’s neurological function is gradually improving after the surgery. He reported high satisfaction with the surgical outcome. No complications were observed. Discussion

In this study, we successfully treated a grade III obese patient with thoracic spinal stenosis caused by OPLL in the mid-thoracic region using this innovative modified technique. The approach improved surgical efficiency, and during follow-up, the patient showed marked clinical improvement without any surgery-related complications. Compared to conventional open circumferential decompression, our hybrid approach resulted in less blood loss and shorter hospital stay[4]. While operative time was longer than standalone endoscopic procedures[8], the added open step ensured adequate working space and facilitated instrument placement in this obese patient. Furthermore, in terms of reducing surgical complications, compared to the aforementioned two surgical methods, our hybrid surgical approach demonstrates significant advantages.

Advancements in technology and surgical instruments have enabled the successful application of full endoscopic spine surgery via the transforaminal approach for ventral thoracic spinal cord compression, demonstrating favorable outcomes and minimal invasiveness[8,9]. However, reported complications persist[10,11], primarily due to the thoracic spine’s narrow canal, limited maneuver space during cannulation, and its vulnerable blood supply, collectively heightening injury risks. To address this, Ruttten et al[10] proposed in a prospective case series study that dorsal indirect decompression surgery should be performed first in severe stenosis cases. This preparatory step creates operational space and minimizes cord manipulation during subsequent ventral decompression, effectively reducing iatrogenic spinal cord injury. The patient’s class III obesity posed additional challenges, including limited intraoperative positioning, increased intra-abdominal pressure, and reduced endoscopic working space. Our hybrid approach mitigated these issues by allowing controlled open exposure followed by targeted endoscopic decompression, highlighting its suitability for complex anatomies.

Given the smaller thoracic intervertebral foramina and dorsal dural attachment of superior articular processes, drill-assisted foraminoplasty during endoscopic ventral decompression via lateral approach carries higher risks of dural/spinal cord injury, necessitating direct visual confirmation of safety. Lin et al[12] suggested in a technical note that transforaminal enlargement via the lower pedicel-ligamentum flavum tunnel approach, targeting the facet-dura interface under endoscopic guidance, may reduce risks of dural sac and thoracic cord injury. However, this technique demonstrates limited applicability, being effective only at T10–T12 levels. In this case, we performed a posterior laminectomy followed by T6 superior articular process resection using an ultrasonic bone scalpel under direct visualization, with ligamentum flavum removal to achieve adequate foraminal expansion. The working cannula was then systematically positioned under combined direct visualization and fluoroscopic guidance, effectively minimizing the risk of dural and spinal cord injury while reducing fluoroscopy use and operative time. The use of fluoroscopic guidance and endoscopic visualization in our approach shares conceptual synergy with computer-assisted technologies in arthroplasty, which bibliometric studies have shown to improve accuracy and reduce iatrogenic injury[13]. This cross-specialty validation supports the safety profile of our hybrid technique.

The impact of full endoscopic ventral decompression for thoracic spinal stenosis on spinal stability remains debated. Systematic review of research evidence indicates that resecting less than 50% of facet joints or lamina preserves stability[10,14]. Some surgeons in some case series studies have successfully decompressed by removing the inferior third of facet joints without causing segmental instability, potentially due to rib cage support and preservation of posterior ligamentous structures during endoscopic procedures[8,15]. However, concerns persist regarding stability in bilateral transforaminal approaches or thoracolumbar cases, warranting long-term follow-up and potential secondary stabilization[10,16]. The patient’s obesity likely increased mechanical stress on the construct, justifying the use of supplemental pedicle screw fixation – a decision supported by literature advocating instrumentation in cases with extensive laminectomy and comorbid obesity[14].

Limitations

First, being a single-case report with limited follow-up duration, this hybrid technique requires validation through large-scale studies with extended clinical observation. Second, while open posterior laminectomy demonstrates reduced surgical risks and tissue trauma compared to posterior circumferential decompression, it still remains an invasive procedure necessitating careful risk-benefit evaluation.

Conclusions

This study introduces a combined surgical approach for spinal canal decompression. By integrating open decompression with minimally invasive endoscopic techniques, this innovative method achieves comprehensive 360° spinal cord decompression while minimizing surgical trauma and procedure-related complications. The hybrid technique presents an alternative option for treating severe spinal stenosis primarily caused by ventral cord compression.

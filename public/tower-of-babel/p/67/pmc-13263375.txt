PPP4C-related metastasis genes serve as a prognostic signature and potential therapeutic target in renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Lili Zhang, Dengwang Chen, Mingzhen Li, Xiaoyu Li, Ying Wang, Xiaojuan Pei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Higher eukaryotes include a widely distributed serine/threonine phosphatase called PPP4C (protein phosphatase 4 catalytic subunit). Its function is to preferentially bind to regulatory subunits, forming a complex to carry out its molecular roles. Although its involvement in pancreatic, colorectal carcinoma and breast cancer has been established, the specific role of PPP4C in pan-cancer remains ambiguous. Through pan-cancer analysis, we identified PPP4C as broadly overexpressed and linked to poor outcomes. Our investigation unveiled diverse expression patterns, demonstrating notable correlations between PPP4C expression and tumor prognosis. We further explored the association between PPP4C expression and various immune-related factors, including microsatellite instability (MSI), tumor mutation burden (TMB), the immune microenvironment, immune cell infiltration, and immune checkpoint molecules. Moreover, with the use of the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database, we discovered pharmaceutical compounds that have a strong association with PPP4C. The CancerSEA database analysis revealed that PPP4C exhibited a negative correlation with metastasis in renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The prognostic prediction of RCC was effectively carried out by the risk signature of PPP4C-related metastasis genes (PrMGs), displaying AUC values of 0.728, 0.700, and 0.706 for the one, two, and three years, respectively. Furthermore, there were notable differences in the immunological state between the two risk groups. In conclusion, our research indicates that PPP4C shows promise as a prognostic biomarker, and novel gene signatures related to metastasis can be employed for prognostic prediction in ccRCC. The risk signature based on PrMGs effectively stratifies ccRCC patient prognosis and reveals distinct immune microenvironment characteristics, providing a foundation for future research into targeted strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04930-4.

Introduction

At present, cancer serves as a leading contributor to morbidity and mortality globally, representing a considerable challenge to human health [1].However, the efficacy of current treatment methods such as surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy are limited and associated with drawbacks [2]. Consequently, research efforts have increasingly focused on investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer development and treatment. Pan-cancer analysis has gained significant traction as a commonly employed methodology to explore tumor biomarkers and their associated signaling pathways, aiming to identify potential therapeutic targets [3–5].

Among the members of the PPPCs family studied in tumors, PPP4C has been identified as a key mediator in the development of breast cancer, ovarian cancer, colorectal carcinoma, lung cancer, and pancreatic cancer [6–13]. However, its role in other types of tumors remains uncertain [8, 14].

Through a detailed bioinformatic analysis, we uncovered the expression of PPP4C across diverse types of cancer, shedding light on its unfavorable prognostic implications. Subsequent analysis showcased associations between PPP4C and TMB, MSI, MMR (Mismatch Repair), immune checkpoints, and the tumor microenvironment. Additionally, a drug sensitivity analysis was performed by our team, identifying small molecule drugs that displayed significant associations with PPP4C. Next, we verified that normal lung epithelial cells expressed PPP4C at lower levels than those expressed in lung cancer cells; furthermore, we observed lower expression in normal tissue compared to lung cancer tissue. Further, we combined PPP4C-related metastasis genes (PrMGs) from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset to create a predictive signature, which we then tested in the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) cohort. Subsequently, we conducted a functional enrichment analysis to gain deeper insights into the underlying mechanisms. To conclude, our results propose that PPP4C serves as an indicative biomarker of diagnostic and prognostic importance in cancers. Furthermore, there is a difference in immunological condition between the two different risk categories, and the risk indicator of PrMGs can accurately forecast the future course of RCC. Strategies targeting metastasis could offer promising therapeutic options for RCC patients.

Methods and materials

Data source

The mRNA expression profiles and pertinent clinical data of cancer patients were obtained from the University of California Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/) [15]. During the development of the prognostic risk signature employing PrMGs, a cohort comprising 518 RCC patients from The Cancer Genome Atlas Program-kidney clear cell carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Total protein expression data for the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) [9], was conducted through the UALCAN database (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/analysis-prot.html). Immunohistochemical and immunofluorescence images depicting PPP4C expression were sourced in the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) [16].

Prognosis analysis of PPP4C

R software (Version 4.2.2, https://www.r-project.org/) was employed for the data analysis. Using the R ‘survival’ package, we analyzed and contrasted survival outcomes and durations between groups characterized by high and low PPP4C expression levels. Patients were stratified into two categories according to their expression levels: those with high PPP4C expression and those with low PPP4C expression. Patients were stratified into high and low expression groups based on the median expression value of PPP4C. We utilized R packages 'survminer' and 'survival' to produce the survival curves as part of our analysis.

Relevance to somatic mutation

Utilizing somatic mutation data sourced from the TCGA database (https://tcga.xenahubs.net), we investigated the correlation between PPP4C expression and MSI and TMB. For each sample, the MSI and TMB scores were determined using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient analysis. Additionally, the relationship between the expression levels of MMR/ICPs and PPP4C was evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Ultimately, the outcomes were showed as heat maps through the use of the R packages ‘RColorBrewer’ and ‘reshape2’.

Immune cell infiltration and immune microenvironment analysis

By employing the ESTIMATE method [17], we computed stromal and immune scores for every tumor sample and investigated how they correlate with PPP4C expression through the 'estimate' and 'limma' packages within the R software. Furthermore, we predicted the comparative scores of 26 immune cell types across 33 malignancies using the CIBERSORT macrogenetic approach [18]. We used 'ggplot2', 'ggpubr', and 'ggExtra' R packages to assess the relationship between PPP4C levels and the extent of immune cell infiltration within cancer. The statistical significance of the relationships was determined using a significance threshold of P < 0.05.

Enrichment analysis and PPI network

The STRING database was utilized to retrieve proteins that interact with PPP4C, and to build networks of protein–protein interaction (PPI) [19]. The 'clusterProfiler' R tool was employed to analyze enriched genes, with the results visualized using the 'ggplot2' R package.

GSEA analysis

We obtained the gene sets of the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) from the official Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/downloads.jsp). Functional analysis was performed using the 'limma', 'enrichplot', 'org.Hs.eg.db', and 'clusterProfiler' R-packages.

We identified the pathways exhibiting the most notable positive and negative relationships when investigating the relationship between PPP4C expression and pathways within tumors.

Cell culture

Lung cancer cell lines (H1975 and PC9) along with human lung epithelial cell line (BEAS-2B) were procured from the Chinese Academy of Sciences located in Shanghai, China. H1975 and PC9 cells were cultured using RPMI-1640 medium (Vivacell, C3010-0500, Shanghai, China) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum. Conversely, BEAS-2B cell line was grown in DMEM medium (Vivacell, C3113-0500, Shanghai, China) containing 10% fetal bovine serum. The cells were cultured at 37°C under 5% CO2 conditions.

Quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Using the FastPure Cell Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China), total RNA was isolated from cells. In accordance with the manufacturer's instructions, the First Strand cDNA Synthesis kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) was utilized for reverse transcription. Utilizing the SYBR Green qRT-PCR Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) and the 2∆Ct technique, the target gene's expression level was determined.

The primer sequences used are provided below:

PPP4C forward 5′ CACGCAGGTCTATGGCTTCTA 3′

PPP4C reverse 5′ GCTGACAGGCTGAGGTAGT 3′

β-actin forward 5′ TGGCACCCAGCACAATGAA 3′

β-actin reverse 5′ CTAAGTCATAGTCCGCCTAGAAGCA 3.

Western blotting

Before protein extraction, proteinase inhibitors were supplemented into the radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Western blot analysis was carried out using the anti-PPP4C antibody (ab195371, Abcam, UK) and anti-tubulin antibody (10068-1-AP, Proteintech) following the manufacturer's guidelines. Secondary antibodies from Thermo Fisher Scientific (31430 and 31460), including goat anti-mouse and goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP, were utilized in the research. To ensure protein loading consistency, β-tubulin was employed as a loading control. Visualization of signals was accomplished using the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (MeilunBio, MA0186-500ML, Shanghai, China).

Immunohistochemistry

The streptavidin-peroxidase (SP) method was employed for the immunohistochemistry (IHC) experiment. The sections underwent deparaffinization followed by rehydration. The slides were subjected to a 3 min pressure cooker boil in sodium citrate buffer (EDTA Antigen Retrieval Solution) for antigen retrieval. Prior to incubation with rabbit anti-PPP4C (1:300 dilution; ab195371, Abcam, UK) in blocking buffer overnight at 4 °C, endogenous peroxidase was blocked with peroxidase blockers. After rinsing with PBS three times, the sections were exposed to a secondary anti-rabbit antibody (ZSGB-BIO, PV-6000, Beijing, China) for 30 min at 37 °C. Staining of the sections was achieved using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB) and hematoxylin. Pictures were taken with a Nikon Eclipse microscope (Nikon Americas, Inc, New York, NY). 10 patients at Southern Medical University's Shenzhen Hospital who were diagnosed with lung cancer provided samples of both normal and clinical lung cancer tissues. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardian(s) prior to their participation in the study. The research procedure received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Hospital, Southern Medical University (approval No. NYSZYYEC20230048).

Drug sensitivity prediction

Using data from the GDSC database, a drug sensitivity study of PPP4C was carried out using the R packages "impute" and "limma." [20]. The correlation between PPP4C expression and the IC50 of all drugs in the GDSC/CAMP databases was calculated. The drugs presented in the figures were selected based on the strongest significant correlations (p < 0.05) observed across multiple cancer types.

Single-cell data and correlation analysis

Utilizing data from the Cancer Single-cell State Atlas (CancerSEA) database (http://biocc.hrbmu.edu.cn/CancerSEA/), we investigated how PPP4C correlates with 14 primary tumor-related cellular activities, such as EMT, metastasis, and DNA repair [21].

The database was designed to specifically cater to RNA sequencing data derived from tumor cells. Gene signatures describing fourteen functional states were selected from pertinent articles and a number of public databases, including Cyclebase, HCMDB, and StemMapper. Through Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA), functional state scores for individual cancer cells were determined based on their respective phenotypic gene sets. Subsequently, the associations between the functional states and PPP4C were investigated using Spearman's Rank Correlations (FDR < 0.05 and correlation > 0.3). The ‘ggplot2’ package was used to create visualizations showing the relationships between PPP4C and functional state scores. Additionally, we utilized EXP0064 (GSE73121) from the CancerSEA database, a dataset encompassing single-cell RNA sequencing data from cells sourced from RCC patients, to investigate the functional significance of PPP4C at the single-cell level. Genes with an absolute Pearson correlation coefficient |r|> 0.3 and a p-value < 0.05 with PPP4C expression across single RCC cells were defined as PPP4C-associated genes and are listed in Supplementary Table S1. We illustrated the association between PPP4C and the metastasis in RCC cells in a scatter plot using the ‘ggplot2’ package. Subsequently, we retrieved 166 genes associated with metastasis from the CancerSEA database and presented them in Supplementary Table S2.

We used a Venn diagram with the ‘VennDiagram’ program to determine which genes (n = 38) were shared by the two gene sets (Supplementary Table S3). These shared genes were classified as metastasis-related genes linked to PPP4C (PrMGs).

Creating a risk signature using PrMGs

Univariate Cox regression analysis identified 23 prognostic genes from 38 overlapping genes, with p-values < 0.05. In order to mitigate the potential for overfitting, Cox regression analysis was employed in conjunction with Lasso penalization to construct prognostic models [22, 23]. Shrinkage and variable selection were performed using the LASSO technique, implemented through the 'glmnet' R package.

The independent variables in the regression model were normalized expression matrices associated with prognostic genes in PrMGs, while the TCGA cohort's patient status and overall survival made up the outcome variables. Ten-fold cross-validation was utilized to determine the penalty parameter (λ) for the model, with the minimum criterion being the λ value corresponding to the smallest partial likelihood deviation. Calculation of patients' risk scores involved multiplying the normalized expression level of each gene by its corresponding regression coefficient, and then summing these products: The risk score =

IGF1R*|-0.0531180215662014|+ PTTG1*0.305248224601959 + ITGA6*|-0.152618518591753|+ SRC*0.307551586230218 + HMGA1*0.0372239499291906 + TIMP1*0.0885784208104136.

Patients were categorized into low-risk and high-risk groups based on the median risk score. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed using the 'prcomp' function from the 'stats' R package to visualize gene expression patterns associated with the signature. Furthermore, we employed t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) analysis, utilizing the ‘Rtsne’ R package, to delve deeper into the distribution of each group. The 'surv_cutpoint' function from the 'survminer' R package was employed to determine the optimal expression threshold for each gene identified in the survival analysis. To assess the predictive performance of the risk signature, a time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed using the "timeROC" R package.

Immunotherapy comparisons

We conducted an analysis to investigate the expression levels of well-established immune checkpoint genes (ICGs) within both groups, aiming to understand their role in regulating immune activation and potential implications for immune responses in the context of RCC. Furthermore, to explore potential relationships and interactions in greater depth, we analyzed the correlations among risk scores, model gene expression, and ICGs expression.

Furthermore, we utilized immunophenoscores (IPS) derived from the Cancer Immunotome Atlas (TCIA) database [24] to enhance our understanding of the immunological landscape of RCC. These scores offer valuable insights on the immune phenotype of RCC tumors and help contextualize our findings regarding immune checkpoint gene expression and its implications for RCC prognosis and treatment.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 9.5 and R software were used for data analysis in this study. For the experimental data of qRT-PCR and Western blot, One-way ANOVA (one-way analysis of variance) was used for statistical analysis. In this study, when the P-value is less than 0.05, we consider this difference to be statistically significant.

Prognostic assessments of PPP4C in pan-cancer

We sought to evaluate the correlation between PPP4C mRNA expression levels and prognosis across diverse cancer types. In order to do this, we performed survival association studies, concentrating on disease-specific survival (DSS), progression-free interval (PFI), disease-free interval (DFI), and overall survival (OS) for each kind of cancer. Our analysis revealed significant associations between PPP4C expression levels and OS in patients diagnosed with ACC, KIRC, LAML, LGG, and LIHC, based on Cox proportional hazards model analysis. The p-values for ACC, KIRC, LAML, LGG, and LIHC were 0.017, 0.002, 0.030, < 0.001, and 0.017, respectively (Fig. 4A). These results suggest that PPP4C is a high-risk gene associated with these types of cancer. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that higher PPP4C expression levels were associated with shorter survival times and decreased OS in patients with ACC, KIRC, LGG, LIHC, and LUAD. TMB, indicative of the total count of mutated genes within cancer cells, is linked to heightened neoantigen expression. It activates the patient's immune system, thereby increasing the effectiveness of immunotherapy [26]. Multiple studies have provided evidence that patients with higher TMB exhibit better responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Another crucial genetic marker in tumor therapy is MSI. MSI occurs due to faulty DNA replication and repair, resulting in the deletion or mismatch of cellular deoxyribonucleotides [27]. These genetic mismatches lead to the formation of neoantigens in cancer cells, which stimulate the immune system to expand T-cell clones and generate anti-tumor responses. Therefore, the extent of TMB and MSI in tumors is highly correlated with the efficacy of immunotherapy.

We subsequently delved deeper into the correlation between TMB, MSI, and MMR and PPP4C expression. PPP4C expression revealed a robust positive association with TMB across 17 different tumor types, as depicted in Fig. 5B. Regarding MSI, a distinct positive correlation with TMB was observed in 9 tumor types (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, PPP4C expression exhibited an inverse correlation with MMR gene expression across the majority of tumor types, particularly in BRCA, KIRC, and HNSC, as shown in Fig. 5D. These results imply a potential role for PPP4C in influencing the response to immunotherapy in these specific tumor types.

Correlation between PPP4C and the immune microenvironment alongside immune cell infiltration in pan-cancer

The presence and infiltration of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment are critical factors influencing cancer growth and prognosis. Additionally, they provide valuable insights into anticipating the reaction to tumor immunotherapy and selecting the most suitable treatment protocols [28]. To gain deeper insight into this correlation, we examined the correlation between PPP4C expression and both the immune microenvironment and immune cell infiltration.

When examine the TME scores in our study, which encompass both immune and stromal scores across 33 distinct types of neoplasms, we noted an inverse relationship between stromal scores and PPP4C expression. On the contrary, a positive correlation was noted with immune scores in the majority of neoplasms (Supplementary Fig. 3A). This led us to further investigate the correlation between immune cell infiltration and PPP4C expression. The study findings demonstrated a substantial positive relationship between PPP4C expression and the presence of CD8 + T cells, Tregs, and activated NK cells. Conversely, PPP4C expression exhibited a negative correlation with CD4 memory resting T cells infiltration (Supplementary Fig. 3B).

Enrichment analysis and PPI network in pan-cancer

To understand the potential mechanisms through which PPP4C operates, we obtained the PPI network diagram from the STRING database, illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 4A. After that, we ran enrichment analysis on the discovered genes using GO and KEGG. The examination demonstrated their participation in functions like protein phosphorylation and dephosphorylation regulation. Furthermore, we found that these genes were primarily associated with the regulation of protein phosphorylation and dephosphorylation, as well as being related to the MAPK signaling pathway, indicating that PPP4C could be integral to these cellular processes (Supplementary Fig. 4B).

PPP4C was found to be associated with metastasis in RCC (Fig. 6A). Therefore, we focused our study on RCC due to its strong association with PPP4C. In the single-cell RNA-Seq datasets of RCC, we found a robust correlation between PPP4C expression and metastasis (r = − 0.33, p < 0.01), as depicted in Fig. 6B. Consequently, we delved into the clinical implications of PPP4C in RCC by examining its regulatory role in metastasis. Variations in the immune microenvironment and immune therapy responses

The Immune Power Score (IPS) could identify individuals who may potentially benefit from immunotherapy. The hypothesis proposed that tumor samples from these individuals would exhibit a positive immune response to either PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, CTLA4 inhibitors, or potentially both (Fig. 9E-H). Patients categorized into the high-risk group demonstrated significantly higher IPS scores. This suggests a more immunologically active tumor microenvironment in this group, although the direct relationship to immunotherapy benefit requires clinical validation.

Monitoring for possible drug

To identify potential therapeutic drugs associated with PPP4C expression, we performed a Spearman correlation analysis between drug sensitivity (IC50 values) and PPP4C expression using data from the GDSC1, GDSC2, and CAMP databases. From the GDSC1 and GDSC2 datasets, we observed significant correlations between PPP4C expression and the IC50 values of several small-molecule drugs, including Bortezomib, Talazoparib, MG-132, and Topotecan. Specifically, higher PPP4C expression was associated with increased sensitivity to Talazoparib and Topotecan, while it correlated with resistance to Bortezomib and MG-132 (Supplementary Fig. 5A, B).

Additionally, analysis of the CAMP database revealed that several compounds, including STOCK1N.35874, NU.1025, MS.275, Fasudil, and Exisulind, demonstrated significant negative correlation scores across multiple cancer types. This suggests that elevated PPP4C expression may be linked to increased sensitivity to these agents in cancers such as breast (BRCA), colorectal (COAD/READ), esophageal (ESCA), and hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), among others (Supplementary Fig. 5C).

Taken together, these results indicate that PPP4C may serve as a predictive biomarker for drug sensitivity, particularly for compounds such as Talazoparib, STOCK1N.35874, and MS.275. Further functional validation through in vitro drug sensitivity assays and in vivo models is warranted to confirm these findings and explore their therapeutic potential in precision oncology.

Discussion

Protein kinases and phosphatases collaboratively maintain phosphorylation equilibrium, a balance that, when disrupted, may contribute to illnesses such as cancer [29]. In contrast to kinases, the activities, interactions, and complexes of protein phosphatases are poorly understood [30]. Protein phosphatases play a crucial role as cellular regulators in various essential processes, making them attractive therapeutic targets for a range of illnesses [31]. The serine/threonine protein phosphatase PPP4C, belonging to the PPP family, is involved in the centrosome's nucleation, growth, and maturation during cell division. [32–34]. Previous research has demonstrated that PPP4C overexpression is correlated with tumor development in multiple carcinomas, including breast cancer, lung cancer, colon cancer, and others [7, 10–12].

In this study, we initially assessed the expression of PPP4C across various cancer types and found that it was generally overexpressed compared to normal tissues. High levels of PPP4C expression were frequently linked to unfavorable prognoses. An intriguing finding was that the high-risk group, despite having poorer prognosis, exhibited higher Immunophenoscore (IPS) values. This apparent paradox aligns with observations in other cancers where tumors with active but dysfunctional immune infiltration may still have poor outcomes [35]. A high-risk, metastatic tumor may foster an "inflamed but suppressed" microenvironment rich in immune cells like Tregs (which our analysis detected) and MDSCs that create an immunosuppressive barrier, or characterized by T-cell exhaustion despite high infiltration [36]. Subsequently, we investigated the relationship between PPP4C expression and factors influencing immunotherapy. The results obtained suggest that PPP4C may influence the potential response to tumor immunotherapy. Immune checkpoints, TMB, MSI, and immune cell infiltration are crucial determinants of the response to tumor immunotherapy [37–39]. Our data thus do not claim that PPP4C increases immunotherapy sensitivity, but rather that the PPP4C-related signature identifies a subset of RCC patients with a distinct and complex immune contexture, the therapeutic implications of which must be rigorously tested clinically.

A deeper interpretation of our results must consider the unique biology of RCC. RCC is not only a highly immunogenic tumor but also characterized by profound metabolic reprogramming, notably shifts in glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and lipid metabolism [40, 41]. Interestingly, PPP4C has been implicated in regulating key metabolic sensors like AMPK and mTOR. We therefore speculate that PPP4C may influence RCC progression and metastasis not only through direct effects on cell adhesion or EMT but also by modulating the tumor’s metabolic state, which in turn can fuel aggressive behavior and shape the immune microenvironment [35, 42]. This potential intersection between PPP4C-mediated signaling, metabolism, and immune response presents a fertile ground for future mechanistic studies.

Additional tests were carried out to validate the expression pattern of PPP4C in lung cancer tissues in comparison to normal tissues, as well as in lung cancer cells in comparison to lung epithelial cells. These experiments closely align with our previous findings and contribute to enhancing the reliability and accuracy of our results. Subsequent single-cell analysis revealed that PPP4C expression correlated negatively with metastasis in RCC. Following this, a prognostic model was developed using LASSO on TCGA patient data, and the AUC curve demonstrated the model's robust predictive capability. The model was subsequently employed to categorize individuals into high and low expression categories. Our findings indicate that patients classified as high-risk in both the TCGA and ICGC cohorts exhibited markedly inferior prognoses compared to those in the low-risk category. We also investigated the connection between immunotherapy sensitivity and the risk classification of the patients. It's interesting to note that patients who were categorized as high-risk responded better to immunotherapy, which produced better clinical results.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations that must be acknowledged. First, the associations drawn between PPP4C and metastasis, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity remain inferential, derived from bioinformatic correlations. Direct causal relationships need to be established through functional experiments in RCC cell lines and animal models, including PPP4C knockdown/overexpression studies to assess impact on invasion, migration, and immune cell recruitment. Second, the prognostic model, though validated in an independent ICGC cohort, is retrospective in nature. Prospective studies in well-annotated clinical cohorts are essential to confirm its independent prognostic value and clinical applicability. Third, our immune analyses rely on computational deconvolution algorithms; validation with spatial transcriptomics or multiplex immunohistochemistry on RCC tissue would provide more definitive evidence. Finally, the therapeutic implications both for the predicted small-molecule drugs and for immunotherapy are speculative and require extensive pre-clinical and clinical validation.

Conclusions

In the present investigation, PPP4C was scrutinized using bioinformatics methods. The expression of PPP4C is upregulated in a variety of malignancies, such as lung cancer, RCC, among others. PPP4C appears to play a role in numerous tumor growth processes and is associated with the tumor immune microenvironment and immunotherapy. We utilized six PPP4C-associated metastasis genes to establish an accurate prognostic model for RCC. This model has the capability to classify patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, and the prognosis, immune status and immunotherapy effect of these two groups are significantly different. These findings suggest that the PPP4C-related signature identifies RCC patients with distinct tumor immune microenvironments. Further investigation is warranted to determine whether this immune contexture influences response to immunotherapy. Nevertheless, additional investigations are essential to delineate the impacts of PPP4C knockdown or upregulation on tumor development in vivo and in vitro, as well as to reveal its plausible mechanisms of action. These investigations are essential for a comprehensive understanding of how this gene contributes to tumor growth and influences the immune microenvironment.

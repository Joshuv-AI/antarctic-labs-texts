Activating the vagus nerve-adrenal anti-inflammatory axis to delay aging: A randomized controlled trial protocol
Authors: Chong Sun, Meihong Fu, Jiao Liu, Kebing Zheng, Shaoping Chen, Lunsha Deng, Yanping Zhuang, Kai Wang, Ruibao Ren, Chao Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAging is a primary risk factor for chronic diseases, yet safe and effective interventions remain limited. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) highlights the role of acupuncture in health preservation, with ST36 (Zusanli) being a key acupoint for longevity. Preclinical studies suggest ST36 acupuncture modulates inflammation and oxidative stress, but robust clinical evidence is lacking. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of ST36 acupuncture in delaying aging and preventing age-related diseases through biomarkers and clinical outcomes.MethodsA single-center, randomized, single-blinded, controlled trial will enroll 120 healthy participants aged 45–70 years. Participants will be allocated to manual acupuncture, sham acupuncture, or blank control groups. The intervention involves 36 sessions over 12 weeks. Primary outcomes include aging biomarkers (DNA methylation, plasma cytokines) and 3D facial/thermal imaging for biological age estimation. Secondary outcomes assess quality of life (SF-36) and sleep quality (PSQI). Adverse events will be systematically documented.DiscussionThis study will provide evidence on the anti-aging effects of ST36 acupuncture and its potential integration into preventive healthcare. Results may inform strategies to address global aging challenges and promote healthy longevity.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2500101531. Registered on 27 April 2025.

Background

Aging characterized by the progressive decline and degeneration of physiological functions over time, represents a major risk factor for the onset of pathological conditions and mortality [1]. Of the 293 commonly recognized diseases, 92 are classified as age-related, including cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, neurodegenerative disorders, and immune dysfunction [[2], [3], [4]]. The aging process imposes substantial burdens on public health, social development, and economic stability [5]. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported that, as of 2019, the global population aged 60 years and older reached 1 billion, with projections estimating an increase to 1.4 billion by 2030. This demographic shift is occurring at an unprecedented rate and is expected to accelerate in the coming decades [6]. Addressing the challenges posed by aging and population aging has become a critical global concern. Researchers are actively investigating effective strategies to delay aging, prevent age-related diseases, and promote healthy aging. Proposed interventions include lifestyle modifications [7,8].,pharmacological therapies [[9], [10], [11], [12]], and gene-based approaches [13],each aiming to restore cellular and tissue vitality, enhance health in older adults, prevent aging-related diseases, and extend lifespan. Among these, behavioral interventions are currently regarded as the most effective strategy to delay aging [14], however, adherence among older populations remains low, limiting long-term implementation. Furthermore, many anti-aging drugs and therapeutic approaches primarily focus on model organisms, lack sufficient clinical translation, and often exhibit notable side effects. Thus, identifying effective, reliable, and safe methods for delaying aging and preventing disease is essential to address the challenges posed by the accelerating aging population.Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has more than 2000 years of experience in disease prevention and aging delay [15], The theories and methods of TCM for health preservation are important measures to address global population aging, accelerate the construction of a healthy China, and comprehensively safeguard people's health [16], The State Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine has formulated the "Outline for Technological Innovation in TCM Preventive Healthcare (2013-2020)."

Acupuncture as a distinctive therapeutic approach within TCM, demonstrates significant advantages in disease prevention and delaying the aging process. Its unique mechanisms are characterized by efficacy, the absence of toxic side effects, broad applicability, ease of administration, and cost-effectiveness. Acupuncture has been widely adopted in over 180 countries and regions worldwide [17,18]. ST36 (Zusanli), recognized as one of the "four major acupuncture points," was first documented in Ling Shu·Ben Shu and is anatomically located 3 cun below the knee on the anterolateral side of the lower leg, along the line connecting Dubi (ST35) and Jiexi (ST41). The Traditional Chinese medical classic Gu Fa Xin Jie Hui Yuan Acupuncture Science suggests that “ST36 is described as treating a multitude of diseases, exhibiting the strength of Atractylodes, the warmth of Cinnamomi, the functions of Ginseng and Deer Antler, and the power of Nitrate”. Similarly, Wai Tai Mi Yao notes, “ST36 nourishes both pre- and post-natal Qi; moxibustion at ST36 can prevent the depletion of vital essence.” Historically, ST36 has been regarded as a critical acupuncture point for disease prevention and longevity [19], ST36 is one of the most frequently utilized acupuncture points in clinical practice, and its therapeutic effects have been extensively validated by modern research. These studies have demonstrated its efficacy in managing degenerative joint diseases [20], exerting anti-inflammatory effects [21], ameliorating atherosclerosis [22], alleviating pain syndromes [23], and addressing gastrointestinal dysfunctions [24]. Investigations into the therapeutic effects and mechanisms of single-point ST36 stimulation have been widely conducted using animal models [25], Recent findings [26,27] indicate that low-intensity electroacupuncture at ST36 activates sensory neurons expressing Prokr2, subsequently triggering the vagus nerve-adrenal reflex to suppress inflammatory responses. Notably, this regulatory effect exhibits specificity to particular somatic regions and acupoints. Modern scientific methodologies have elucidated the anti-inflammatory mechanisms underlying ST36 stimulation, providing a robust neuroanatomical foundation for further research. These advancements also contribute critical evidence to support the standardization and objectification of acupuncture research. Inflammation plays a central role in the aging process, as aging is often accompanied by chronic low-grade inflammation and impaired immune function, which collectively contribute to tissue degeneration [28,29], Acupuncture offers a promising non-toxic, effective, and easily administered intervention for inflammation-associated conditions, aging, and age-related diseases. However, current research on the health benefits and anti-aging mechanisms of single-point ST36 acupuncture remains limited. The specific roles and mechanisms of ST36 in disease prevention, health preservation, and anti-aging are yet to be fully defined, and robust clinical evidence to support its application is still lacking.

Building on the continuous advancements in understanding the mechanisms of acupuncture and the identification of aging biomarkers in modern medicine, this study will employ a clinical controlled trial to investigate the effects of single-point ST36 acupuncture in healthy middle-aged and elderly populations. Aging biomarkers, including DNA methylation and plasma cytokines, will be evaluated alongside advanced imaging modalities such as 3D facial imaging, infrared thermography, and artificial intelligence algorithms for biological age calculation. The study aims to assess the potential of ST36 acupuncture in mitigating inflammation, promoting health, delaying aging, and preventing age-related diseases. By exploring effective and reliable strategies for combating aging and premature aging, this research seeks to provide novel insights into anti-aging interventions. Through rigorous evidence-based evaluation, it aims to substantiate the health-preserving and anti-aging benefits of ST36 acupuncture. This investigation holds significant clinical relevance and economic value in addressing the global challenges posed by aging populations. Moreover, it contributes to the advancement of the Healthy China initiative, underscoring its importance within the broader framework of global aging trends and public health strategies.

Methods

Study design

This is a single-centre, open-label, single-blinded, randomized controlled trial will be conducted in the Haikou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Haikou, Hainan province, China, Participant recruitment will take place from June 01, 2025, and December 30, 2026. The clinical trial has been registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500101531).The trial's protocol received ethics approval from the hospital's Ethics Committee (Approval number: HKSZYYYLL-2023(KE)-10) in China.The research ethics committee will also be responsible for supervising all procedures of the study, including participant recruitment, randomization, conduction, and data storage. This trial protocol follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines for reporting [30]. Recruitment

All patients will be recruited in the outpatient clinics of the participating hospitals by local advertising, hospital website, and hospital Public Accounts. A total of 200 eligible participants will be recruited from the Haikou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Haikou, Hainan province, China.Eligible patients will be invited to take part in the study, and before enrollment, researchers will thoroughly explain the study's procedures in detail. A concise explanation of the study's objectives, procedures, treatment approaches, and associated risks will be presented to participants. Additionally, they will be explicitly informed of their right to discontinue participation in the clinical trial at any time. Participants will be enrolled following the completion of informed consent.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined based on data from our previously published pilot study [31], which was consistent with the current trial in terms of participant population and intervention (ST36 stimulation). In the pilot trial, the intervention resulted in a significant reduction in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels: from a baseline of 4.46 mmol/L (IQR: 4.32–4.71) to 3.97 mmol/L (IQR: 3.32–4.47) post-intervention, with a median reduction of 0.49 mmol/L (P = 0.004). Since direct pilot data on the primary biomarkers (DNA methylation, cytokines) were unavailable, we used the LDL-C-derived effect size for sample size estimation, given the consistency in study population and intervention.

For sample size calculation, we estimated the standard deviation (SD) from the post-intervention IQR (1.15 mmol/L) under the assumption of approximate normality using the formula SD = IQR/1.35, yielding an estimated SD of 0.85 mmol/L. We hypothesized the following effect sizes for the three groups: acupuncture group (0.49, consistent with the pilot observation), sham acupuncture group (0.15, estimated placebo effect), and blank control group (0.00, natural fluctuation). The overall mean effect was 0.213, with a between-group variance of 0.1261. The effect size f for one-way ANOVA was calculated as:、f=Σ(μi−μ)2∕kσ2=0.1261∕30.852=0.04200.7225=0.24Which represents a medium effect size per Cohen's criteria. Sample size calculation was performed using PASS 15.0 software with one-way ANOVA, α = 0.05 (two-sided), power (1−β) = 0.80, 3 groups, and effect size f = 0.24. This yielded a required sample size of 52 participants per group (total 156). To account for a 20% dropout rate, the final target sample size was adjusted to 200 participants, randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio (Manual Acupuncture Group: 67, Sham Acupuncture Groups: 67, Blank Control Group: 66).

Randomization and masking

After the baseline assessment, an independent investigator randomised eligible healthy volunteers in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive manual acupuncture, sham acupuncture, or blank control group.The outcome evaluators and statisticians were blinded to group assignments, while the intervention therapists were blinded to the outcome assessments. Statisticians generated a random number table using statistical software, which was then passed to independent third-party researchers for allocation concealment. These researchers placed the random number table into opaque, sealed envelopes, each labeled with the date and signed. The envelopes were subsequently handed over to the study coordinators. The random numbers served as participant identification codes The personal information of all the participants will always be kept confidential.The study is designed to remain blinded throughout the duration of the trial. In the event that unblinding becomes necessary, the research team will have access to the group allocation information. Any unblinding will be promptly reported in compliance with the trial protocol.

Interventions

Manual Acupuncture Group

Acupuncture was administered by licensed acupuncturists with at least 5 years of clinical experience. Acupoints were identified according to the Standard Acupuncture Nomenclature established by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2014. Bilateral ST36 acupoints on the Stomach Meridian of Foot-Yangming were selected for the acupuncture intervention. These procedures adhered strictly to the WHO Benchmarks for the Practice of Acupuncture.

Manual acupuncture intervention

Every participant assumed a supine position. After acupoint positioning, the skin at the acupoints and the acupuncturist's hands were disinfected with 75% alcohol. Disposable sterile acupuncture needles (0.25 mm × 50 mm, Huatuo, Suzhou Medical Supplies Co., Ltd., China) were used. Physicians pressed the needle holder tightly to the skin of the acupoints and then performed acupuncture. Needles were inserted perpendicularly to a depth of 20-30 mm at ST36, employing manual techniques such as lifting, thrusting, or rotating until the participant experienced the sensation of soreness, numbness, distention, or heaviness known as deqi. The needles were retained for 30 min.Finally, the needle holes were pressed with a sterilized dry cotton swab briefly after needle removal.

Moxibustion intervention

Moxa sticks (18 mm × 200 mm, Han Yi, Nanyang Hanyi Moxa Co., Ltd., China) were used for mild suspended moxibustion at bilateral ST36. The moxastick was ignited and held 2-3 cm above the acupoint, ensuring the participant felt warmth without burning. Sham Acupuncture Groups

To help maximize blinding of patients in the sham acupuncture group, non-penetrating sham acupuncture was performed at bilateral non-acupuncture points located on the anterior aspect of the leg, approximately 1–2 cm away from ST36.After sterilisation, streitberger placebo needles with blunt tip were used [33]. which can provide patient-blinding effects with a similar appearance to conventional needles. When they were fixed on the skin through plastic rings, patients felt a pricking sensation, simulating a puncture of the skin. However, instead of penetrating the skin, the needles retracted up into the shaft when they were pressed against the skin. We formulated and followed standardised step-by-step instructions and the rest of the operation is the same as that of the manual acupuncture group.Moxibustion was performed using the same type of moxa stick as used in the acupuncture group. The ignited moxa stick was placed approximately 10–15 cm above the skin surface at a non-acupuncture point located 3 cm lateral to ST36. The skin temperature was maintained at 37 °C ± 1 °C, and participants did not perceive any sensation of heat at the acupoint.

To prevent participants from observing the treatment procedure, all participants wore an eye mask during treatment. To assess the success of blinding, participants in both the acupuncture and sham acupuncture groups completed a questionnaire within 5 min after the 12-week intervention, in which they were asked to guess which type of acupuncture they had received.

Blank Control Group

Participants in the control group were instructed to maintain their usual lifestyle throughout the study period without any intervention.where participants will be asked to record their usual lifestyle each day.

Improving adherence to intervention protocols and monitoring compliance

In order to improve participant adherence and facilitate accurate monitoring, participants will receive four complimentary health assessments during the study, including evaluations of blood biomarkers, indicators of physiological aging, and overall health status through health scale assessments. All participants will be required to complete daily activity logs and fill out the Treatment Component Adherence Scale (TCAS) during the trial to ensure continued compliance with the intervention.

The study data and biological samples collection occurred at baseline, mid-intervention (4 weeks), post-intervention (12 weeks), and at a 3-month follow-up.

Baseline data, including age, sex, height, weight, BMI, medical history, and medication history, were collected from participants upon enrollment and recorded in the case report form (CRF).

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes included The Short Form 36 Health Survey(SF-36) and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) score assess participant's self-rated general health.

The SF-36 Health Survey is a widely used instrument to assess health-related quality of life, consisting of 36 items across eight domains: physical functioning, role limitations (physical and emotional), bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, and mental health. Scores are standardized on a 0 to 100 scale, with higher scores reflecting better health.

The PSQI is a widely used questionnaire to assess one's sleep quality for the past month, across 7 components: sleep duration, sleep latency, sleep efficiency, sleep disturbances, sleep medication use, daytime dysfunction, and overall sleep quality. Each component is scored from 0 to 3, yielding a total score that ranges from 0 to 21. Higher scores indicate worse sleep quality and more severe sleep disturbances.

Adverse events and management

Acupuncture-induced fainting: Immediately cease acupuncture, remove all needles, and assist the patient into a supine position with the head lowered and legs elevated. Administer warm water or sugar water orally. In severe cases, acupuncture at ST36, CV6 (Qihai), and CV4 (Guanyuan) should be performed.

Infection: Strict disinfection of both the acupuncture sites and needles is essential to prevent infection.

Subcutaneous Hematoma: After removing the needle, apply pressure on the acupuncture point to prevent hematoma formation. If a hematoma develops, apply a cold compress to stop bleeding, followed by a warm compress to promote absorption.

Burns: In the case of burns from moxibustion, remove the moxa stick and rinse the area under running water for 20-30 min. Small blisters may be left to heal naturally, while larger blisters should be treated with burn ointment to prevent infection.

Allergic Reactions: Local redness and itching are common but usually subside after a period of moxibustion. Application of an antiallergic ointment can help promote recovery.

Safety evaluation

Safety evaluations in this study will meticulously document any adverse events (AEs) related to electroacupuncture or moxibustion therapy, including but not limited to needle shock, infection, subcutaneous hematoma, burns, or allergic reactions. Documentation will include the time of occurrence, duration of symptoms, severity, management strategies, resolution time, and any clear association with the treatment. The clinician will determine whether to discontinue treatment based on the severity of the AE.

Rigorous safety monitoring is ensured through the following mechanisms: the Principal Investigator will review all AE reports within 24 h and make immediate decisions regarding participant safety; all serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported to the responsible Ethics Committee within the mandated timeframe; and a small internal data monitoring committee, comprising the PI and a senior clinician not involved in the study, will meet bi-monthly to review cumulative safety data.In the event that an AE is identified as being related to the trial protocol, the research team will bear the treatment costs and provide corresponding financial compensation for any harm caused.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis will be conducted by statisticians blinded to the randomization process, using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).Continuous variables will be presented as mean the mean with standard deviation or median and interquartile range. For tests across groups, we used analysis of variance or the Kruskal-Wallis test when relevant. Qualitative data will be described by frequency tables, percentages, or composition ratios; comparative analyses will be conducted using the chi-squared test, Fisher's exact test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, or Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test.Statistical significance is set at value of p < 0.05.

In clinical trials, addressing protocol non-adherence and missing data is crucial for ensuring the validity and reliability of study outcomes. In this study, for protocol non-adherence, Per-Protocol (PP) analysis is used for participants who strictly follow the protocol, while Intent-to-Treat (ITT) analysis includes all participants as originally randomized, regardless of their adherence. To address missing data, Multiple Imputation (MI) is employed to reduce bias by imputing plausible values for missing observations, thereby maintaining the integrity of the statistical analysis. The Bonferroni correction will be applied for primary outcomes to control the Type I error rate. Adjusted p-values will be reported where applicable.

Document and data management

Data collection will be performed by clinical researchers under the oversight of the principal investigator, who holds responsibility for the accuracy, completeness, and timeliness of all reported data. To ensure precise interpretation and traceability, all data must be clearly documented. CRFs and Informed Consent Form will be managed and securely stored by designated personnel, while clinical data will be maintained in a password-protected database with logic validation programs in place. The primary database will be backed up once per month. At the same time, the data analysis files will also be backed up. Study data and source documents will be retained for a minimum of two years, and any destruction will require prior authorization.

Discussion

Aging is a primary risk factor for the development of various diseases, including cancer and cardiovascular conditions. With the rapid progression of population aging, delaying aging and achieving healthy longevity have emerged as pivotal goals in contemporary medical research. Identifying effective strategies for disease prevention and aging intervention has become a critical priority to address the challenges posed by an aging population [37],TCM views aging as an inherent process in the life cycle of all living beings. The principal contributors to aging, as per TCM, include the depletion of kidney qi and essence, imbalances in qi, blood, yin, and yang, decline in organ function, and the loss of vitality [38], The foundational principles and clinical applications of acupuncture in aging delay are comprehensively documented in ∗Huangdi Neijing∗. To date, over 14,000 clinical trials on acupuncture have been published [39]. Acupuncture has primarily been utilized in the treatment of pain syndromes [[40], [41], [42]],degenerative osteoarthropathy [43],insomnia [44],gastrointestinal disorders [45]and cardiovascular diseases [46]. However, clinical applications have largely focused on disease onset, with limited exploration of acupuncture's potential in disease prevention and natural disease progression intervention. The timing of acupuncture intervention should be advanced to earlier stages [47]. Future efforts should emphasize promoting acupuncture's role in early disease prevention and health maintenance.ST36, a key acupuncture point for health maintenance, plays a significant role in delaying aging. Its anatomical structure and functional mechanisms have been thoroughly studied, providing a definitive biological foundation for further investigations. Although research on ST36's role in aging is limited, prior studies have indicated its potential benefits. Bai et al. [48] demonstrated that acupuncture at ST36 enhances the antioxidant capacity of aged female rats and decelerates ovarian tissue aging. Ding et al. [49] reported that ST36 acupuncture positively modulates the expression of brain-aging-related genes, potentially delaying brain aging and improving cognitive function. Wang et al. [50] observed that ST36 acupuncture in aging model mice enhances antioxidant capacity, reduces lipid peroxidation damage, and mitigates skin aging. Despite these findings, most research on ST36 has been conducted in animal models, with insufficient studies exploring its molecular mechanisms in disease prevention and aging delay. Further integration of modern medical technology with clinical research is essential to elucidate acupuncture's role in aging intervention.

This clinical trial aims to evaluate the effects of single-point ST36 acupuncture in healthy middle-aged and elderly populations. Biomarkers of aging, including aging-related secretory phenotypes, facial aging clocks, DNA methylation clocks, and other biological and physiological markers, will be assessed to determine its efficacy in delaying aging and preventing disease. By selecting ST36 as a single-point intervention, this study addresses challenges associated with the high individualization of acupuncture protocols, diverse acupoint combinations, and reproducibility issues in trial design. The findings aim to provide robust evidence-based support for the use of single-point acupuncture at ST36 in disease prevention and aging delay while laying the groundwork for further elucidating its scientific mechanisms. This trial ultimately seeks to identify innovative strategies for aging intervention and contribute to the broader integration of TCM methods into aging research.

Ethics declarations

Ethics approval and consent to participate.

The trial's protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee at the Haikou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval number: HKSZYYYLL-2023(KE)-10). Participants provided written informed consent during the screening process, parental consent will also be obtained for participants under 18, and the study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Any important protocol modifications, such as changes to eligibility criteria, outcomes, or analyses, will be communicated to all relevant parties in a timely and transparent manner. The research team will inform investigators, Ethics Committee, trial participants, trial registries, and regulators. In the case of substantial protocol amendments, updates will also be made to the trial registry and communicated to the relevant journals if applicable. Written documentation will be provided for all protocol changes, and approvals from the relevant regulatory bodies or ethics committees will be obtained prior to implementation.

Handling and Consent for Biological Samples and Data.

The study will explain to volunteers in advance how their biological samples and data will be used and tested, with informed consent being acquired. Following sample collection, the samples will be properly stored for future analysis. Any remaining samples and data will be kept with participant consent after the study concludes, potentially for use in future research.

Consent for publication

Not applicable.

Trial status

The clinical trial has been registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry The study will be conducted in the Haikou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine from June 2025 to December 2026.

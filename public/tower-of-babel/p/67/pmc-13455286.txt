Spontaneous vesicovaginal fistula in a retroviral positive multiparous woman: a case report
Authors: Adegoriola Olubisi Ojurongbe, Uche Augustine Akunaeziri, Talemoh Wycliffe Dah, Idikwu George Ogbu, Paulinus Chukwuemeka Ugwu, Usman Danladi Aduga, Chibueze Solomon Omeke, Sharifah Musa Isa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVesicovaginal fistula (VVF) is a major cause of morbidity with severe socio-economic consequences affecting the quality of life of women and their families. Prolonged obstructed labour remains the leading cause of VVF in sub-Saharan African and Southeast Asia. However, in rare cases VVF can occur spontaneously without an identifiable cause.Case presentationIn this report we describe a case of spontaneous simple VVF measuring 2 × 2 cm in the lower third of the anterior vaginal wall in a 47-year-old Nigerian woman who is HIV-positive and on Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy (HAART). She has a history of three previous uncomplicated caesarean sections. Leakage of urine was noticed 13 months prior to presentation and at that time, her viral load was high with a low CD4 count. She was evaluated and had vaginal repair of the fistula.ConclusionsThis report suggests the possibility of a potential association between chronic immunosuppression and fistula formation. Therefore, it is important for clinicians to consider the possibility of spontaneous fistula formation in HIV-positive patients, especially in the absence of more common causes.

Introduction

Vesicovaginal fistula (VVF) is a distressing clinical condition characterized by an abnormal communication between the bladder and the vagina resulting in involuntary and continuous leakage of urine [1–3]. It is a major cause of morbidity in Sub-Saharan Africa with its attendant consequences on the quality of life of women and that of their families. There are about 2 million women globally suffering from vesicovaginal fistula with Nigeria accounting for about 7.5% of the global burden [4, 5]. The commonest cause of VVF is pressure necrosis from prolonged obstructed labor in countries where women do not have timely access to quality emergency obstetric care [4]. Notably, there is still a high burden of obstetric fistulas in Sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asian countries of the world [5, 6]; whereas, in well-developed countries of the world, fistula occur primarily as a result of surgical complications [1, 6]. The other causes of VVF include iatrogenic injuries when carrying out pelvic surgeries, pelvic trauma, malignancies, radiation therapy, foreign bodies, instrumental vaginal deliveries and sexual assault [7]. Vesicovaginal fistula can be classified based on anatomical location, size, degree of scarring and ease of access for repair [2, 3, 8]. However, in rare cases VVF can occur spontaneously without an identifiable cause. In this case report we present a multiparous HIV-positive woman who developed a spontaneous simple vesicovaginal fistula in the absence of other known causes of fistula, thus suggesting a potential association between chronic immunosuppression and fistula formation.

Case presentation

A 47-year-old Nigerian woman with a history of three previous caesarean sections presented with 13 months history of involuntary leakage of urine per vaginam. Her last child birth was 3 years ago and last menstrual period was on the 18/12/2024. She was a HIV-positive woman diagnosed 21 years ago and has been on Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy (HAART) in a sero-concordant monogamous relationship. She was initially on Tenofovir, Lamivudine and Efavirenz but was not compliant with the medication due to side effects, such as nightmares, bizarre dreams, insomnia and body rash. Her viral load 10 months prior to presentation was 115,000 copies/ml and CD4 count was 215 cells/mm3. Due to poor compliance as a result of the side effects of the drug and the high viral load at the time, the drug combination was then changed to Tenofovir, Lamivudine and Dolutegravir. Her last viral load about 4 months prior to presentation was less than 20 copies/ml. Her husband was also HIV-positive and on HAART. All her children are HIV-negative.

She had delivered four times in the past. The first was a spontaneous vaginal delivery about 25 years ago, while the remaining deliveries were by caesarean sections 15, 11 and 3 years ago, respectively. The caesarean sections were uneventful. There was no history of malignancy, pelvic trauma, radiation therapy or sexual assault in her. She had cervical cancer screening (Papanicolaou smear) done twice in the past (2 years and 1 year ago) which were Negative for Intraepithelial Lesion or Malignancy (NILM).

Examination at presentation revealed a young, chronically ill-looking woman. She appeared malnourished with a body mass index of 16 kg/m2. Abdominal examination revealed a Pfannenstiel scar that healed by primary intention. There was no area of tenderness and no palpably enlarged organs. Bowel sound was present and normoactive. Pelvic examination revealed a wet perineum. There were some skin excoriations noted. Digital speculum examination showed a defect that measured 2 × 2 cm located in the lower one-third of the anterior vaginal wall (Fig. 1). No vaginal scarring was noted. She had some investigations done. Pre-operative packed cell volume was 33% and two units of blood were made available. Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C and Venereal Disease Research Laboratory test (VDRL) were all negative. Renal and Liver function tests were within normal ranges. Urinalysis was negative for protein and glucose. Urine culture yielded no bacterial growth. Chest X-ray and electrocardiography showed normal findings. Abdomino-pelvic scan done revealed a normal study. Tuberculosis Gene Xpert test was negative.

The urologists reviewed the patient and a cystoscopy revealed normal bladder mucosa with no suspicious lesions. A biopsy of the bladder mucosa revealed normal urothelium and lamina propria and tissue taken from the fistula margin was also negative for malignancy.

Another Pap smear was done which was Negative for Intraepithelial Lesion or Malignancy.

A diagnosis of a Spontaneous Vesicovaginal Fistula in a multiparous woman with background Retroviral Disease as made.

She was counseled on the findings and line of management. She was admitted for fistula repair and a written informed consent was obtained. She had a transvaginal VVF repair under spinal anesthesia. An intraoperative dye test revealed a single fistula measuring 2 × 2 cm, about 6 cm from the external urethral meatus (Fig. 2). The vaginal mucosa was dissected off the underlying bladder wall and a multilayer closure of the bladder and vaginal tissues was done using polyglactin 2/0 suture in the horizontal and vertical directions, respectively, ensuring tension-free repair (Fig. 3). A dye test using methylene blue was done to confirm the integrity of the closure and no leakage was observed (Fig. 4). Estimated blood loss was 100mls and the immediate post operation condition was satisfactory. She was placed on antibiotics, intravenous infusions and analgesics. A urethral catheter was left in situ for 14 days. Urine samples were taken on the third, seventh and fourteenth days after surgery for microscopy, culture and sensitivity which yielded no bacterial growth. She was seen at the Urogynecology clinic after discharge and she had no complaint. At the 6-week follow-up, the patient remained continent, with no recurrence of urine leakage. The importance of compliance to HAART and good nutrition was emphasized. She was discharged from the clinic.

Discussion

Vesicovaginal fistula is a major cause of distressing morbidity with a great impact on the quality of life of affected women and that of their families. Pressure necrosis from prolonged obstructed labor still remains the leading cause in Sub-Saharan Africa and Asia, due to lack of timely access to quality emergency obstetric care [5, 6]. The concept of spontaneous VVF is an exceptionally rare entity. It is the development of a vesicovaginal fistula in the absence of other known causes, such as prolonged labor, trauma or other causes and this makes it unique. A case of spontaneous VVF from genitourinary tuberculosis had been reported by some researchers in the past [9, 10]. In the case report by Shah et al. [9], bladder mucosal biopsy at cystoscopy revealed tubercular infection. In our patient, bladder mucosal biopsy revealed normal urothelium and lamina propria with no evidence of malignancy or chronic granulomatous infection.

Congenital vesicovaginal fistula without any history of trauma, labour or infection have also been reported in women found to have concomitant menstrual outflow obstruction and in women having normal menstruation [11]. In some of these cases, there are other anomalies of the urogenital system, such as abnormal ureteric insertion and distal vaginal agenesis. This is different from our case as she had no congenital structural anomalies.

Our patient in this report had three caesarean sections in our facility for various obstetric indications and they were uneventful. The last caesarean section was 3 years ago. There were no documented iatrogenic injuries and no puerperal complications. A vesicovaginal fistula can occur after a caesarean section by some mechanisms: ischemia, scar contracture, chronic inflammation, and infection. For this index case, her caesarean sections were uneventful, with no history of dense adhesions (the operation notes were reviewed) or adherence of the bladder to the lower uterine segment requiring extensive dissection which can lead to iatrogenic bladder injury or devascularization of tissue. She was not in labour prior to the surgeries as pressure necrosis can lead to the development of a vesicovaginal fistula. There was no history of puerperal sepsis, wound breakdown, or surgical site infection following any of the surgeries. Therefore, it is unlikely that this vesicovaginal fistula developed from the previous surgeries she had.

There have been reported cases of delayed presentation of fistula after caesarean section occurring after many months or years. This is more common with vesicouterine fistula and less common with vesicovaginal fistula. In some of these cases, adhesions requiring extensive dissection, difficulty dissecting the bladder off the lower uterine segment, postoperative haematoma, puerperal sepsis are possible causes of a fistula that develops long after a caesarean section [12]. In this case, all previous surgeries were uneventful with no adhesions or challenges or post operative complications from the documentation in the case file.

Prior to surgery, she had a cystoscopy with biopsy of the bladder mucosa and fistula site and a Papanicolaou smear was also done. This is because an underlying genital tract (for example, cervical cancer) or bladder malignancy may be the cause [13]. This was ruled out in this case.

The most probable risk factor was her HIV status as her viral load prior to the development of the fistula was high with a low CD4 count. Spontaneous fistula formation raises important clinical questions regarding underlying mechanisms. The possible mechanism includes chronic ischemic microvascular damage, subclinical infections (recurrent urinary tract infections), and immune dysregulation leading to weakened bladder and impaired tissue healing and ultimately causing fistula formation [14, 15]. HIV does not directly cause vesicovaginal fistula. It indirectly increases the risk in a number of ways. Increased susceptibility to infections such as urinary tract infections, pelvic infections, opportunistic infections can cause tissue necrosis which may lead to fistula formation. The immunosuppressive effect of HIV leads to impaired immunity leading to delayed wound healing. HIV also increases the risk of cervical cancer which can lead to the development of a vesicovaginal fistula. Radiotherapy for cervical cancer is also a risk factor [13]. A Papanicolaou smear and biopsy from the fistula site were performed to exclude malignancy.

Our patient appeared malnourished and this can contribute to the development of a vesicovaginal fistula in several ways. Insufficient calcium and vitamin D as the girl child grows could result in pelvic deformities or underdeveloped pelvis which predisposes women to cephalopelvic disproportion with resultant prolonged obstructed labour which will lead to pressure necrosis and fistula formation [16]. Our patient did not have prolonged obstructed labour due to cephalopelvic disproportion prior to the development of the VVF. Malnutrition, particularly deficiency in protein, vitamins A, C, and E, as well as zinc contribute to poor wound healing which will compromise the tensile strength in tissues. This can lead to the development of a fistula [17]. The patient in this case was chronically ill-looking and underweight due to her retroviral status which is a known cause of reduced immunity and delayed wound healing.

The fundamental principles of management and repair of vesicovaginal fistula was used in this index case and continence was achieved.

Conclusion

This case presents one of the rare instances of spontaneous VVF in an HIV-positive patient, who had three previous uncomplicated caesarean sections in the past. Her viral load at the time the fistula developed was high with a low CD4 count. This case raises the possibility of an association between chronic immunosuppression and fistula formation. Further studies are needed to determine if there is an established association between chronic immunosuppression from HIV infection and the development of a fistula especially in the absence of more common causes.

Risk prediction of continuous renal replacement therapy in patients with acute kidney injury after lung transplantation
Authors: Shuo Ding, Huiru Lin, Juan Chen, Shixiong Mai, Ganggui Zhu, Gaoqin Teng, Jingyu Chen, Man Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study used least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis to identify the influencing factors of continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) implementation in patients with acute kidney injury (AKI) following lung transplantation (LTx), and subsequently constructed a nomogram model for predicting CRRT risk based on these determinants. The model aims to provide decision-making support for early clinical intervention in high-risk populations.MethodsThis retrospective study collected clinical data and laboratory parameters from patients who underwent LTx at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine between June 2018 and January 2024. Postoperative AKI was diagnosed and staged according to the serum creatinine (sCr)-based criteria defined by the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes guidelines. Patients were grouped by CRRT receipt. Variables were initially screened using LASSO regression to minimize overfitting, followed by multivariate logistic regression analysis to construct a CRRT risk prediction model. The predictive efficacy and clinical practicability of the model were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis.ResultsA total of 448 LTx patients were included in this study, of whom 340 (75.9%) developed creatinine-elevated AKI. Among these AKI patients, 79 (23.2%) received CRRT. Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified the following independent risk factors for CRRT in post-LTx AKI patients: advanced age, increased intraoperative blood loss, higher intraoperative fluid input, bilateral LTx, prolonged postoperative extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support duration, delayed time to peak sCr, rapid sCr elevation rate, and greater magnitude of sCr rise. Conversely, preoperative mechanical ventilation (MV) emerged as a protective factor. Validation analyses demonstrated that the CRRT risk prediction nomogram model, incorporating nine variables (age, preoperative MV, LTx type, intraoperative blood loss, intraoperative net fluid balance, postoperative ECMO duration, time to sCr peak, sCr elevation rate, and rise magnitude), exhibited robust predictive performance and clinical applicability, significantly enhancing patient clinical benefit.ConclusionsThe risk prediction nomogram model developed in this study demonstrates high accuracy and clinical utility. This model facilitates early identification of high-risk patients by clinicians and provides critical guidance for optimizing early AKI intervention strategies and formulating personalized CRRT protocols.Keywords: Lung transplantation, Acute kidney injury, Continuous renal replacement therapy, Risk factors, Nomogram, Prediction model

Introduction

Lung transplantation (LTx) has experienced substantial growth over the past two decades, with 959 cases performed in China in 2023 alone and a cumulative total exceeding 4000 cases to date.[1] This expansion is primarily attributed to the broadening of recipient selection criteria and improved allocation/utilization efficiency of donor grafts. Advances in organ procurement, surgical techniques, and perioperative management have enhanced patient survival rates, though outcomes remain inferior to those of liver or kidney transplantation. Internationally reported survival rates post-LTx are 89.4%, 61.2%, and 33.1% at 1, 5, and 10 years, respectively, consistently lower than other solid organ transplants.[[2], [3], [4], [5], [6]] Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common postoperative complication following LTx, with its occurrence significantly impacting both short-term recovery and long-term survival. Studies have reported that approximately 33% to 69% of LTx recipients develop AKI postoperatively, and about 5% to 13% of these patients may progress to severe acute kidney failure, requiring continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT).[[7], [8], [9]] Moreover, postoperative AKI is associated with prolonged hospitalization, increased healthcare costs, and a significant increase in short-term mortality. The 30-day mortality rate for patients with AKI is approximately 20% higher compared to those without AKI.[9,10] Notably, the detrimental effects are not merely attributable to transient impairment of renal clearance function but are closely linked to the pathogenesis and progression of multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS).[11,12] This pathophysiological mechanism of multi-system interaction makes AKI play a more complex role in the overall prognosis of LTx patients.

However, specific therapeutic interventions capable of effectively reversing the pathological progression of AKI and improving clinical outcomes in critically ill patients remain lacking.[[13], [14], [15]] Current clinical management of AKI remains confined to supportive measures, such as optimizing fluid balance with diuretics, avoiding nephrotoxic agents, and providing renal replacement therapy in critical cases. The therapeutic goal focuses on halting the progression of renal impairment while promoting conditions conducive to spontaneous renal recovery. However, these strategies have limited impact on improving the clinical course or patient prognosis.[16] There remains widespread controversy regarding key clinical decisions in CRRT, particularly the optimal timing for initiation and withdrawal, as well as the optimization of anticoagulation protocols.[[17], [18], [19], [20]] As an invasive intervention, CRRT implementation also necessitates consideration of equipment material costs and specialized nursing requirements. Furthermore, potential complications during treatment include hemodynamic instability, coagulation abnormalities, catheter-related infections, technical issues (e.g., equipment malfunction and catheter dislodgement), and fluid management derangements (e.g., over-filtration or volume overload).[21] This study conducted a retrospective analysis based on clinical data from a single-center cohort of LTx recipients. The aim was to identify and validate independent risk factors for the initiation of CRRT in patients with AKI. Based on these factors, a scientific risk prediction model was developed to provide a quantitative tool for early identification of high-risk populations requiring CRRT, thereby guiding the timely implementation of individualized multimodal management protocols and intensified pharmacotherapeutic interventions.

Methods

Study design and cohort

This study is a single-center retrospective analysis focusing on patients diagnosed with AKI following LTx. We analyzed clinical data from 448 patients who underwent LTx at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine between June 2018 and January 2024. Inclusion criteria comprised patients receiving LTx (either single or bilateral LTx). Exclusion criteria were defined as meeting any of the following: (1) intraoperative or postoperative death within 7 days; (2) preoperative diagnosis of AKI or chronic kidney disease (CKD); (3) recipients of repeat LTx; and (4) non-creatinine-elevated AKI or those who did not develop AKI. All surgical procedures and perioperative management were performed in strict accordance with international guidelines by a multidisciplinary LTx team with extensive clinical experience. Clinical baseline characteristics, perioperative therapeutic data, and laboratory parameters were systematically collected (Figure 1).

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine. Ethics review approval number: (2024) No.1581. In accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki provisions for retrospective studies utilizing anonymized medical data, the requirement for informed consent was exempted.

Diagnostic criteria and indications for CRRT initiation

In this study, AKI was defined and staged strictly according to the serum creatinine (sCr)-based criteria outlined in the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines,[22] with the diagnostic time window limited to within 7 days post-LTx. AKI was diagnosed if either of the following criteria was met: (1) an absolute sCr increase ≥26.5 µmol/L within 48 h, or (2) sCr rising to ≥1.5 times the baseline value within 7 days postoperatively. Severity staging was determined as follows (using the last pretransplant sCr measurement as baseline): AKI stage 1: Absolute sCr increase ≥26.5 µmol/L or sCr 1.5 to <2.0 times the baseline value; AKI stage 2: sCr 2.0 to <3.0 times the baseline value; AKI stage 3: sCr ≥3.0 times baseline, or absolute sCr ≥353.6 µmol/L, or initiation of renal replacement therapy, or estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <35 mL/(min·1.73 m²) in patients aged <18 years. The eGFR was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) equation. Due to incomplete urine output data and heterogeneity in measurement methods in this retrospective study, urine output assessment was not included in this analysis.

The diagnosis of CKD also follows the KDIGO guidelines and requires meeting at least one of the following criteria, with symptoms persisting for ≥3 months: (1) eGFR <60 mL/(min·1.73 m²), or (2) the presence of one or more markers of kidney damage, including proteinuria (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, ACR ≥30 mg/g), persistent hematuria, abnormal urinary sediment, electrolyte and other abnormalities caused by tubular dysfunction, imaging or histological abnormalities in the kidney, or a history of kidney transplantation.[23]

CRRT initiation criteria in this study mainly complied with the KDIGO guidelines.[22] Absolute indications included: severe metabolic acidosis (blood pH <7.1), life-threatening hyperkalemia (serum potassium >6.5 mmol/L or rapid elevation with concomitant cardiac toxicity), uremia-induced severe complications (e.g., encephalopathy or pericarditis), and diuretic-resistant fluid overload (e.g., acute pulmonary edema). Additionally, relative indications encompassed: refractory fluid overload and metabolite accumulation that patients could not tolerate, such as hemodynamic instability and/or MODS, accompanied by cerebral edema, increased intracranial pressure, etc.; or severe internal environment disorders, acute liver failure, septic shock, severe tumor lysis syndrome, and heat stroke. Finally, patients were divided into the CRRT group and the non-CRRT group based on whether they were subjected to CRRT intervention.

Data collection

Data collection in this study integrated systematic literature review and clinical expertise-guided variable selection. The basic information and preoperative baseline data of patients included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), primary pulmonary disease, comorbidities, Charlson comorbidity index (CCI), smoking history, and preoperative use of mechanical ventilation (MV) or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support. Laboratory test indicators included baseline sCr, blood gas analysis, hemoglobin (Hb), white blood cell (WBC) count, absolute lymphocyte count, C-reactive protein (CRP), panel reactive antibody (PRA), including PRA-I and PRA-II, albumin, total protein, total cholesterol, and others. Surgical variables recorded were: LTx type (single/bilateral), duration of surgery, intraoperative blood loss, net fluid balance, and intraoperative ECMO utilization. Postoperative monitoring included immediate central venous pressure (CVP) and diastolic pressure, duration of postoperative ECMO support, AKI staging, and sCr levels on postoperative days 1, 3, 7, as well as peak sCr levels. From these data, we calculated the magnitude and rate of sCr elevation.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical methods were used to summarize baseline characteristics. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, and as median and interquartile range (IQR) for skewed data. Categorical variables were presented as percentages. Independent samples t-tests were used to compare continuous data between the two groups. For groups with unequal variances, Welch’s t-test was applied. In cases of non-normally distributed data, the Mann–Whitney U test was used. Additionally, categorical data were compared using the chi-squared (χ²) test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). AKI patients were randomly divided into training and validation sets in a 6:4 ratio using a randomization tool, and a random seed was set to ensure reproducibility. To identify potential predictors, a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was conducted on the training set, which effectively eliminated irrelevant variables or those exhibiting multicollinearity, thus minimizing the impact of high-dimensional data. Subsequently, the selected features from the LASSO model were incorporated into a logistic regression analysis to construct a risk prediction model for CRRT. A nomogram was developed based on the training set and validated using the validation set. The features are presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The model’s discriminatory performance was assessed using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). A C-index close to 1 indicates high predictive accuracy, while an AUC greater than 0.80 indicates good discrimination. The calibration of the nomogram was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, which assesses the consistency between predicted and observed CRRT outcomes. Finally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to determine the clinical applicability of the nomogram by calculating the net benefit at different threshold probabilities.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients

Among the 448 LTx patients included in the analysis, 340 patients (75.9%) developed creatinine elevation-type AKI within the first 7 days post-surgery. The median age of AKI patients following LTx was 58 years (IQR: 48–65), with 82.6% of them being male. The most common underlying diseases were interstitial lung disease (ILD; 47.9% [n=163]), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD; 21.2% [n=72]), and silicosis (11.8% [n=40]). Among these patients, 232 (68.2%) underwent bilateral LTx, while 108 (31.8%) received a single-lung transplant. Regarding AKI severity, 33.8% (n=115) had stage 1, 32.1% (n=109) had stage 2, and 34.1% (n=116) had stage 3. A total of 23.2% (n=79) of AKI patients required CRRT. The average time from AKI diagnosis to initiation of CRRT was 84.1 h, and the average duration of CRRT was 28.1 days. The remaining patients did not undergo CRRT. Compared to the non-CRRT group, the CRRT group had a higher risk of poor prognosis. On 28 days post-surgery, the survival rate for CRRT patients was 60.8% (n=48), while it was 97.3% (n=254) for the non-CRRT group. On 90 days post-surgery, survival rates for the two groups were 31.6% (n=25) and 89.3% (n=233), respectively. Detailed baseline characteristics are presented in Tables 1 and 2.

Laboratory blood test analysis revealed significant differences between the CRRT and non-CRRT groups in terms of absolute lymphocyte count (median[IQR]: 0.9 [0.6–1.6] × 109/L vs. 1.3 [0.9–1.9] × 109/L, P=0.003) and CRP (median[IQR]: 10.1 [4.3–43.3] vs. 7.3 [2.9–21.8] mg/L, P=0.009). Furthermore, the CRRT group had a higher proportion of patients requiring preoperative MV (29.1% vs. 13.8%, P=0.002) and preoperative ECMO support (16.5% vs. 3.1%, P <0.001). All patients were randomly assigned to either the training set (n=203) or the validation set (n=137).

Independent risk factors in the training set

This study included 32 variables derived from baseline characteristics, surgical data, and laboratory tests. To address the potential risk of overfitting in high-dimensional data and to improve model robustness, we first performed an initial feature selection using LASSO regression to identify potential predictors. During this process, models were fitted using different regularization parameter (λ) values, and a path coefficient plot was generated using the log(λ) sequence to visualize how the regression coefficients changed with λ. After determining the optimal parameter for the LASSO model, we plotted the binomial deviance curve and selected the λ value based on the one standard error (1-SE rule). Ultimately, 12 variables with non-zero coefficients were selected under this λ value (Figure 2).

Development of the predictive model

The 12 selected predictors were included in a multivariable logistic regression analysis, with results presented in Table 3. The analysis identified several independent predictors for CRRT in LTx patients with postoperative AKI, including age, preoperative MV, intraoperative blood loss, intraoperative fluid balance, type of surgery, postoperative ECMO support duration, peak sCr time, sCr peak growth rate, and sCr peak increase. The nomogram model, constructed based on the results of the multivariable logistic regression analysis, integrates key predictors of CRRT risk and quantifies the risk weight of each variable. In this model, the duration of postoperative ECMO support was identified as the strongest predictor of CRRT, with its contribution score significantly higher than that of other variables. Although the type of lung transplant (OR=9.248) showed the highest ORs, its clinical applicability is limited due to its binary nature (single/bilateral transplant). Notably, preoperative MV (β=−1.885, OR=0.152) was identified as a protective factor, with a negative association to the risk score (Figure 3).

Validation of the predictive model

In this study, the concordance index (C-index) for the training set was 0.972 (95% CI: 0.954 to 0.990), while the C-index for the validation set was 0.882 (95% CI: 0.871 to 0.894), retaining 90.5% of the predictive performance from the training set (with a performance decay rate of approximately 9.5%). According to Swets’ diagnostic efficacy classification (C-index >0.7 indicates clinical utility), the model demonstrated good discrimination in the validation cohort (Class B).

Additionally, the sensitivity and specificity of the predictive model were assessed using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. The results showed that the model achieved an AUC of 0.972 (95% CI: 0.954 to 0.990) in the training set. In the validation set, the AUC was 0.882 (95% CI: 0.814 to 0.950). The observed decrease in the AUC from the training to the validation set indicates an expected degree of performance shrinkage. Nevertheless, the model’s performance on the validation set remained strong. Furthermore, our internal cross-validation confirms that the model is robust and not substantially overfitted (Figure 4A and B).

The calibration performance of the model was assessed using calibration curves and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. The calibration curves for both the training and validation cohorts closely aligned with the ideal diagonal, indicating strong agreement between the predicted probabilities and the observed outcomes. The model demonstrated excellent calibration, with the calibration slope nearing 1. Furthermore, the Hosmer–Lemeshow test showed no significant differences (P >0.05) (Figure 4C and D).

The DCA of the nomogram demonstrated that it provides significant net benefits in predicting patients who require CRRT, further highlighting the model’s potential for application in clinical settings (Figure 4E and F).

Discussion

CRRT is a commonly used supportive treatment for renal dysfunction, effectively removing excess fluids and metabolic waste from the body, thereby improving kidney function. However, CRRT inevitably increases healthcare costs and is often associated with complications such as bleeding, thrombosis, infection, and even potential damage to other organ systems. These issues can delay kidney recovery, prolong hospitalization, and increase the risk of adverse outcomes.[24,25] In our study, patients with AKI following LTx who required CRRT had poorer prognoses, with a significantly higher risk of mortality compared to those who did not receive CRRT. Therefore, accurately assessing whether a patient requires CRRT has become a critical issue in clinical management.

Currently, no nomogram has been developed to predict the risk of CRRT in patients with AKI following LTx. Previous studies have primarily focused on the occurrence and prognosis of postoperative AKI, with limited attention given to CRRT intervention as a primary outcome. This study fills that gap by retrospectively comparing data from LTx patients with AKI who received either conservative treatment or CRRT. We have constructed and validated a predictive model for CRRT requirement, offering a new tool for making more precise decisions regarding kidney replacement therapy. This model demonstrates strong practical value and promising applicability. The study identified several independent risk factors for requiring CRRT in AKI patients after LTx, including: increasing age, higher surgical blood loss, positive fluid balance during surgery, bilateral LTx, preoperative MV, longer postoperative ECMO support, delayed peak sCr levels, faster rate of sCr increase, and greater sCr elevation. In subsequent validation analyses, the model showed excellent predictive discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. It holds significant value for patient stratification and personalized treatment, enabling early identification of AKI patients at risk for CRRT and allowing for earlier and more aggressive interventions to minimize or avoid the need for CRRT.

When analyzing clinical data, LASSO regression is an effective method for controlling model complexity through its penalty mechanism, allowing for the selection of the most predictive variables. This technique has been widely used in various clinical studies. In this research, we employed LASSO regression to perform an initial screening of clinical variables that could influence the need for CRRT, and subsequently developed a risk prediction model. Compared to traditional regression methods, LASSO regression has the advantage of retaining important variables while eliminating redundant or irrelevant ones. It effectively addresses multicollinearity issues and optimizes model performance, thus improving the accuracy and generalizability of the predictive model. Through this process, we identified a set of independent risk factors closely associated with CRRT requirement, providing valuable insights for clinical decision-making and shedding light on the potential mechanisms behind the onset and progression of AKI.

Specifically, factors such as increasing age, excessive intraoperative blood loss, bilateral LTx, preoperative MV, prolonged ECMO support, and rapid increases in sCr levels are significant risk factors for CRRT intervention in postoperative AKI patients. Several studies provide supportive evidence for these associations.[9,[26], [27], [28], [29], [30]] Bilateral LTx is a well-known high-risk factor for both postoperative AKI and the requirement for CRRT. This is likely related to factors such as the patient’s overall baseline health status, prolonged surgery duration, increased use of ECMO, higher risk of intraoperative blood loss, and elevated transfusion rates.[[31], [32], [33]] As a critical technique for managing severe illness, ECMO provides effective cardiopulmonary support and plays a crucial role in the perioperative care of end-stage lung disease patients. Its applications include bridging therapy before transplantation, intraoperative respiratory and circulatory support, and management of complications such as primary graft dysfunction (PGD) following surgery. Clinical data indicate that the incidence of AKI in ECMO-supported patients ranges from 30% to 70%,[[34], [35], [36], [37]] significantly higher than in patients with AKI caused by other factors,[38,39] with most cases occurring within the first 48 h of initiating treatment. Approximately half of these patients require concurrent CRRT,[40] with the main indications being volume overload (43%), AKI (35%), and preventive fluid management (16%).[41] The pathophysiology of ECMO-related AKI is complex and not fully understood, with potential risk factors including ischemia-reperfusion injury, release of pro-inflammatory mediators, oxidative stress, low cardiac output syndrome, fluid overload, hemolysis, bleeding, and thrombosis.[[42], [43], [44], [45], [46], [47]] Prolonged ECMO support often leads to increased exposure to these risk factors and the potential for cumulative effects.

In this study, preoperative MV was identified as a protective factor for CRRT. The underlying protective mechanism may involve the interaction and regulation of multiple organs, optimizing pathophysiological homeostasis. The key lies in improving renal perfusion, inflammation control, and overall homeostasis through the optimization of respiratory support. Early respiratory support enhances the balance of oxygen supply and demand throughout the body, reducing oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, while alleviating hypoxia-induced vasoconstriction. This, in turn, indirectly improves renal perfusion and lowers the risk of AKI. Additionally, lung-protective ventilation strategies may modulate systemic inflammation by inhibiting the inflammatory cascade in the lung-kidney interaction, especially in patients with ARDS and sepsis.[[48], [49], [50], [51], [52]] MV also plays a role in regulating metabolism and acid-base balance, correcting hypoxemia and hypercapnia-induced acidosis, and improving oxygenation and hemodynamics. This helps reduce lactate accumulation, maintain homeostasis, and ultimately decrease the need for CRRT. It is undeniable that this association could also be influenced by potential selection bias. Patients stable enough to undergo preoperative intubation and subsequent optimization might represent a different population compared to those requiring emergent ECMO or those too unstable for any bridging support. This aspect may require further validation in a larger cohort with a bigger sample size and greater diversity.

It is important to note that during the development of the predictive model, “surgical blood loss” was initially identified as an independent predictor through the preliminary selection via LASSO regression. However, in subsequent multivariate regression analysis, due to sample size limitations, data fluctuations, or other potential statistical factors, it did not reach the conventional level of statistical significance (P=0.062). Nevertheless, considering the significant clinical relevance of intraoperative blood loss in the development of AKI and its importance in perioperative management following LTx, we chose not to exclude it from the model. Several previous clinical studies also support its potential value in predicting and managing AKI after LTx.

Compared to traditional risk assessment tools, the nomogram proposed in this study integrates a comprehensive set of key clinical variables—spanning baseline, intraoperative, and early postoperative periods—to offer a more precise and personalized prognosis prediction. This multifaceted approach enables the early identification of high-risk patients in the immediate postoperative phase, facilitating timely and personalized treatment decisions. Presented in an intuitive format, the model is highly practical for rapid deployment at the bedside.

A pertinent consideration is the model’s applicability for early prediction, particularly as it incorporates sCr trajectory parameters that are linked to CRRT criteria. We emphasize that the primary goal of this study was predictive accuracy to simulate clinical reasoning, not etiological inference. The distinctive value of our model lies in its ability to synthesize these dynamic postoperative markers with baseline and intraoperative factors to generate a composite risk score before absolute CRRT thresholds are met. In our cohort, the average time interval from surgery to the CRRT outcome exceeds 84.1 h (>3.5 days), whereas our model can be applied on postoperative day 1. This provides a crucial lead time of several days, allowing it to function as a genuine early warning system.

The proposed clinical workflow is straightforward. The optimal time to apply the nomogram is at the moment of postoperative AKI diagnosis. According to the KDIGO criteria, this can occur as early as within the first 48 h post-surgery based on sCr changes, and up to the seventh postoperative day. Clinicians can input the readily available patient data into the nomogram upon AKI diagnosis. A resulting high-risk score would prompt immediate and intensified actions, such as meticulous fluid balance management, avoidance of nephrotoxins, optimization of hemodynamics and ECMO flows, and early nephrology consultation. Compared to routine clinical judgment, this approach represents a paradigm shift from reactive to proactive management. This proactive, model-guided strategy has the potential to mitigate the progression of renal injury, potentially obviating the need for CRRT, or ensuring its timely and planned initiation, thereby ultimately improving resource allocation and patient outcomes.

When contextualized within the broader field of solid organ transplantation, our study adds a crucial piece to the puzzle of postoperative AKI management. Similar predictive models have been used for AKI risk assessment following organ transplants, such as heart and liver transplants, highlighting the importance of multivariable risk evaluation in managing postoperative AKI.[[53], [54], [55]] A comparative analysis reveals that while the predictive factors for AKI may differ between transplant types, most studies emphasize the impact of intraoperative fluid management, blood loss, and the patient’s underlying health condition on both postoperative AKI and the need for CRRT. However, the unique pathophysiology of end-stage lung disease, the routine use of intraoperative and postoperative ECMO, and the specific impact of single vs. bilateral transplantation, render LTx recipients a distinct population. To the best of our knowledge, our model represents the first validated nomogram in China that specifically integrates these LTx-specific factors for predicting CRRT, thereby addressing a significant gap in the clinical management of this complex patient group.

Strengths and limitations

The clinical predictive model developed in this study has undergone thorough validation and offers the advantage of simplicity, making it suitable for broad application in clinical practice. Compared to traditional statistical modeling methods, the LASSO algorithm utilizes its shrinkage property to select variables, resulting in greater stability. Additionally, the selection of predictive factors does not rely on conventional statistical significance thresholds but instead optimizes the model’s generalizability through coefficient shrinkage. Nevertheless, our clinical predictive model is not without its limitations. First, the exclusion of patients who died within 7 days post-LTx, while intended to focus the model on patients with a completed opportunity to receive CRRT, may have introduced selection bias by excluding the highest-risk cohort. Consequently, the predictive performance of our nomogram in this specific subpopulation requires further investigation. The development and validation of the model were conducted internally, constrained by the available data. While this allowed for extensive optimization within a controlled environment, it also limits its applicability in other settings. Furthermore, as a single-center study, the model cannot fully eliminate potential selection or information biases, highlighting the need for multi-center studies for deeper exploration and validation. Additionally, the retrospective data collection did not include certain factors that may influence the need for CRRT, such as hourly urine output, net fluid balance, and diuretic usage during the perioperative period. The reliance solely on sCr criteria for AKI diagnosis might have led to an underestimation of AKI incidence, particularly for stage 1 AKI, which could have influenced the cohort composition. Future prospective studies should be conducted to further validate the observed associations, explore causal relationships, and incorporate novel biomarkers related to AKI, to better address clinical needs.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our analysis of the baseline characteristics, surgical information, and laboratory findings of AKI patients following LTx at our center identified several independent risk factors for CRRT intervention. These include older age, preoperative MV, excessive intraoperative blood loss, large fluid intake during surgery, bilateral LTx, prolonged ECMO support, delayed peak sCr levels, significant increases, and rapid escalation of sCr levels. Based on these factors, we developed a simple and reproducible clinical prediction model in the form of a nomogram. This model can assist clinicians in promptly and effectively assessing the progression of AKI in patients, enabling early identification and diagnosis of those who may require CRRT intervention. As such, the nomogram holds substantial clinical value and can play a crucial role in the management of AKI patients, guiding decisions on whether to pursue conservative treatment or initiate CRRT support.

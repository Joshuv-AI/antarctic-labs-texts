A novel method for the precise second mesiobuccal canal orifice location: A combined strategy for enhanced clinical practice
Authors: Yuhan Wang, Lingyun Li, Lu Zhang, Xiaoyan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/purposeSuccessful root canal treatment relies on thorough canal disinfection. The second mesiobuccal (MB2) canals in maxillary molars poses a significant clinical challenge due to its complex anatomy and high prevalence. The conventional perpendicular line method for MB2 canal orifice localization has notable limitations in achieving precise point identification. This study evaluates a novel combined method that integrates the perpendicular line and parallel line principles to enhance the accuracy of MB2 canal orifice localization.Materials and methodsThis study analyzed 73 CBCT scans of maxillary first molars with identifiable MB2 canals. A novel combined method integrating the perpendicular line and parallel line principles was compared with the conventional perpendicular line method for MB2 canal orifice localization. The 95 % confidence ellipse area was substantially smaller for the combined method (1.63 mm2 vs 2.39 mm2). Introduction

The success of root canal treatment fundamentally depends on the thorough cleaning, shaping, and three-dimensional obturation of the root canal system.1,2 A comprehensive understanding of root canal anatomy and its variations is therefore crucial for achieving successful outcomes. In recent decades, the anatomy of the mesiobuccal (MB) root in maxillary molars has seized significant interest among researchers and dentists.3 This attention is originated from the frequent presence of two primary canals within this root, the first MB canal and the second mesiobuccal (MB2) canal.4

Reported prevalence of the MB2 canal in maxillary molars varies widely, from 10 % to 95 %, influenced by factors such as the detection technique as well as ethnic and demographic variables including location, age, and gender.5–7 The MB2 canal in maxillary first molars is often characterized by its narrow, curved, and calcified nature, with its orifice concealed within the pulp chamber floor. This makes accurate localization challenging, leading to a high risk of missed MB2 canals and subsequent failure of endodontic treatment in maxillary molars.8,9 Studies indicated that most missed canals that require endodontic retreatment are found in maxillary first molars,10 with approximately 93 % of these located specifically within the mesiobuccal root.6,11

Currently, a commonly used and taught clinical method for locating MB2 canal orifice is based on the perpendicular line connecting the MB and palatal (P) canal orifices, which is referred to as the perpendicular line method in this study.12–15 In this method, a line is first drawn between the MB and P orifices, followed by a perpendicular line extended from the distobuccal (DB) orifice. The MB2 canal orifice is generally expected to lie near the intersection of these two lines. Despite its widespread use, this method has notable limitations. The predicted location of the MB2 canal orifice could not be confined to a single point but rather presented as an area with indistinct boundaries. On the other side, although cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) has been widely recommended and applied to detect complex anatomies such as the MB2 canal orifice,16,17 it increases radiation risks and the financial burden.18 Moreover, it remains difficult to accurately translate the MB2 location identified on CBCT images into the clinical field due to differences between radiographic visualization and actual anatomy.19,20 Therefore, improving the perpendicular line method is essential for a more precise and efficient localization of potential MB2 canal orifices and to provide better guidance for dentists during complicated endodontic treatments.

Recently, an alternative approach has been proposed that connects the DB and P canal orifices.21,22 They observed that the angle between the MB-MB2 line and the DB-P line is approximately 2.3 ± 5.7°, suggesting these lines are almost parallel. While this provides another anatomical reference, the MB2 canal orifice predicted by this approach still lies within a linear range rather than a single point. Furthermore, it offers limited guidance for determining the buccolingual position of the MB2 canal orifice, particularly its distance from the MB orifice.

Because the perpendicular line method effectively narrows the mesiodistal prediction range, while the parallel line method improves buccolingual estimation, we hypothesized that combining these two techniques could enhance localization accuracy. This study therefore aims to evaluate a new integrated clinical technique for locating MB2 canal orifice by performing detailed CBCT-based measurements on maxillary first molars. Our objective is to determine whether this combined method improves prediction precision over the perpendicular line method, with the goal of facilitating the efficiency and accuracy of clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Collection and selection of cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) scans

Patients who underwent CBCT of the maxillary molars due to a diagnostic need at the Peking University Hospital of Stomatology between January and December in 2024 were enrolled.

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board at Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology (PKUSSIIT2025040), and the patients provided informed consent before inclusion into the study. The inclusion and exclusion criteria are as follows:

Inclusion criteria

A total of 73 CBCT scans of maxillary first molars were included in this study, obtained from 43 male and 30 female patients. The CBCT scans were stratified by the patients’ age into the following groups: under 30 years (n = 19), 30–39 years (n = 26), 40–49 years (n = 8), 50–59 years (n = 9), and 60 years or older (n = 11).

Methods of predicting the second mesiobuccal (MB2) canal orifice location

The DICOM data from the CBCT scans were processed and measured using Materialise Mimics Research 21.0 (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium). After the data were imported into the software, the detailed procedures for predicting the location of the MB2 canal orifice using the perpendicular line method and the combined method were implemented as described in Fig. 1. In brief, a reference plane was selected approximately 1 mm below the pulp chamber floor (Fig. 1A). On this plane, the precise locations of MB, MB2, DB, and P canal orifices were identified and marked (Fig. 1B). A line was drawn connecting the MB and P orifices (MB-P) and another line between DB and P orifices (DB-P) (Fig. 1C).

The procedure of predicting the MB2 canal orifice location using the perpendicular line and combined methods.

MB, mesiobuccal. MB2, second mesiobuccal. DB, distobuccal. P, palatal.

For the perpendicular line method, a perpendicular line was then extended from the DB orifice to the MB-P line. The intersection point (MB2′) was selected as the predicted location of the MB2 canal orifice (Fig. 1D), and the distance between MB2’ and the actual MB2 is measured for accuracy assessment (Fig. 1E).

In the combined method, which integrates the principles of the perpendicular line method and the parallel line method, a line parallel to the DB-P line was drawn through the MB orifice. The intersection between this parallel line and the perpendicular line from the perpendicular line method defines the predicted MB2 location (MB2″) (Fig. 1F). The distance between this predicted point and the actual MB2 canal orifice was measured (Fig. 1G).

Measurement of the predicted and actual second mesiobuccal (MB2) canal orifice location

To quantify the predictions, a coordinate system was defined with the MB-MB2 line as the X-axis. The Y-axis was defined by the line perpendicular to the X-axis that passed through MB2, which was set as the origin (showed in Fig. 2). The predicted MB2 locations from both methods were expressed as coordinates for subsequent comparative measurements and statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis were performed using Python 3.7 (Python Software Foundation). The normality of data distribution for continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Based on the distribution characteristics, parametric or non-parametric tests were applied accordingly. For normally distributed data, intergroup comparisons were conducted using independent t-tests, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for multiple group comparisons; these results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. For data violating the normality assumption, the Mann–Whitney U test was utilized for intergroup comparisons, with results expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). The precision of measurements was evaluated by calculating 95 % confidence intervals (CI) for mean distances. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 for all analysis.

Results

Visualization of the predicted second mesiobuccal (MB2) canal orifice location

The predicted MB2 canal orifice locations were plotted within the established coordinate system and overlaid as a bivariate scatter plot onto a representative CBCT image (Fig. 2), allowing visual comparison of the prediction distribution against the actual pulp chamber floor anatomy.

The plot analysis revealed a distinct superiority of the combined method over the perpendicular line method in locating the MB2 canal orifice. Data points for the combined method formed a tight cluster near the origin (the actual MB2 canal orifice location), demonstrating high accuracy and precision. In contrast, predictions from the perpendicular line method exhibited a wide, scattered distribution, indicating substantial variability. This visualization confirmed that the combined method provides a more reliable and accurate prediction of the MB2 canal orifice location.

Comparison of confidence ellipses between perpendicular line method and combined method

The spatial distributions of MB2 localization determined by the perpendicular line and combined methods, represented by 95 % confidence ellipses, are illustrated in Fig. 3. The data points corresponding to each method are shown, along with their respective mean centers and confidence regions. The perpendicular line method (Fig. 3, blue ellipse) exhibited a wider dispersion, with a 95 % confidence ellipse area of 2.39 mm2, indicating greater variability in predicting the MB2 canal orifice position. In contrast, the combined method (Fig. 3, red ellipse) showed a more compact distribution, with an ellipse area of 1.63 mm2, suggesting improved spatial consistency and accuracy. The mean position of the combined method was also closer to the actual MB2 canal orifice. Therefore, integrating the perpendicular and parallel line method reduced positional deviation and enhanced the precision of MB2 canal orifice localization compared with the perpendicular line method alone.

Comparison of linear distance by age group between perpendicular line method and combined method

Across all age groups, the combined method demonstrated significantly greater accuracy than the perpendicular line method in predicting MB2 canal orifice locations (Table 1, Fig. 4A). The perpendicular line method exhibited relatively stable median distance errors, ranging from 1.087 mm to 1.207 mm across all age groups. In contrast, the combined method substantially reduced localization errors, with median values ranging from 0.443 mm to 0.767 mm. The most precise predictions were observed in the 30–39 years group, while the ≥60 years group showed slightly reduced precision. Box plot analysis consistently revealed narrower IQR and lower median values for the combined method across all age groups, with statistically significant differences between methods (P < 0.05).

Discussion

The present study proposed a localization strategy that integrated the established perpendicular line method and the parallel line method. While these anatomical principles have been used individually, their combination offered a refined geometric approach, making it more convenient and accurate to localize MB2 canal orifices of the maxillary first molars in clinical practice. Our results consistently indicated that this integrative method significantly enhanced both the accuracy and precision in locating the MB2 canal orifice compared to the perpendicular line method alone.

Based on previous research, dental anatomical variations are known to be associated with factors such as population, age, and sex.5–7 The maxillary first molar is widely regarded as one of the most challenging teeth to treat. This difficulty arises from several anatomical features, including variations in the number of roots and root canals, the direction of the canals, the complex morphology of the pulp chamber, and limited visual access to the apical region.23,24 Similarly, morphological variations and the number of root canals in maxillary first molars have also been frequently related to challenges in root canal treatment,25 indicating the need to develop effective principles to clinically locate the root canal orifices.

Although CBCT is widely used in the clinical evaluation of tooth roots and root canal anatomy owing to its high accuracy, minimal distortion, three-dimensional visualization, and noninvasiveness,26–28 many endodontists are still suffering from the difficulties in translating location-related information obtained from CBCT to the actual anatomy on the pulp chamber floor.19,20,29 Our findings provide a more reliable “navigational map” for the endodontists operating within the complex and often obscured anatomy of the pulp chamber floor.

The superior performance of the combined method can be attributed to its effective synthesis of the complementary strengths of its two constituent techniques, providing a stronger evidence for its clinical application. Geometrically, relying on a single method defined a linear distribution of potential locations, leaving the precise point along that line ambiguous. By introducing a second vector based on the parallel line method, the combined approach effectively triangulates the orifice location. The perpendicular line method effectively constrains the predicted area in the mesiodistal dimension, while the parallel line method provides a crucial reference for the buccolingual position. This intersection aligned more closely with the natural anatomy of the pulp chamber floor. By establishing the predicted location at the intersection of a line parallel to DB-P through MB and the perpendicular from DB to MB-P, the combined method overcomed the primary limitation of each individual method brought by the tendency of both methods to define a range rather than a specific single point. This synthesis was clearly reflected in the quantitative results: the scatter plot analysis (Fig. 2) demonstrated tighter clustering of data points around the actual MB2 location for the combined method; the confidence ellipse analysis (Fig. 3) revealed substantially reduced spatial dispersion (1.63 mm2 for combined method vs 2.39 mm2 for perpendicular line method); and analysis of linear distance distribution by age group (Table 1, Fig. 4) consistently showed lower median distance errors across all age groups (0.443–0.767 mm for combined method vs 1.087–1.207 mm for perpendicular line method). The statistical significance of these improvements (P < 0.05) further validates the enhanced spatial localization capability of the combined approach.

Our findings extend previous studies that separately validated the perpendicular MB-P line12–14 and parallel DBP line21,22 as anatomical guides. Rather than creating a new anatomy-based principles, this study validated that the combination of these existing principles could produce a synergistic effect, contributing to superior predictive capability compared to either method alone. This combined method provided substantial clinical implications: as the MB2 canal orifice is often obscured by dentin shelves, pulp stones, or hemorrhage, a more accurate preoperative prediction could improve clinical efficiency, reduce unnecessary dentin removal, and enhance detection success.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. This study was a CBCT-based investigation, which, while providing an anatomical model, is an in vitro simulation of the clinical conditions. The presence of bleeding, restricted visibility, and variable tactile feedback during actual clinical procedures may influence the practical applicability and accuracy of the method.

Within the limitations of this study, it could be concluded that the combined method, integrating the perpendicular line and parallel line principles, provided a significantly more accurate and precise technique for predicting the MB2 canal orifice location in maxillary first molars. This technique offers a valuable clinical strategy to enhance operative efficiency and improve the outcome of root canal treatment involving the complex and often hidden MB2 canal orifices in the maxillary first molars.

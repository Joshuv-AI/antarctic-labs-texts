Reply
Authors: Takuro Imaoka, Shingo Sakashita, Shohei Koyama, Ikuo Sekine, Kazuko Tajiri
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Li and colleagues for their thoughtful comments and address the 2 issues they raised. The accompanying editorial by Chen and Moslehi1 provides a mechanistic framework that complements both their critique and our findings.

We agree that the 8% cutoff should be regarded as exploratory and that the limited sample size does not allow precise threshold estimation. However, the prognostic signal of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression did not depend on the use of a cutoff. When analyzed as a continuous variable, each 1% increase in PD-L1+ cells was associated with a higher risk of major adverse cardiac events.2 These results suggest a graded prognostic association independent of any specific threshold. We also agree that an important next step is to determine whether PD-L1 adds incremental prognostic value beyond established markers such as active thymoma, cardiomuscular symptom, QRS voltage, left ventricular ejection fraction, and troponin levels.3 A multivariable model incorporating these variables was not feasible because of the limited sample size and event number. Larger biopsy-based cohorts are needed to address this issue.

Regarding the biological meaning of myocardial PD-L1 expression, we suggest that the 3 possibilities outlined by Li et al, including compensatory immune regulation, interferon-driven inflammation, and secondary tissue injury, are not mutually exclusive. As described in the editorial by Chen and Moslehi,1 interferon gamma released by activated T cells activates Janus kinase–signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling and induces PD-L1 expression. PD-L1 normally suppresses T-cell activation; however, under checkpoint blockade, this regulatory pathway may be insufficient to counteract excessive myocardial inflammation. In this context, our data are most consistent with PD-L1 expression representing a marker of the intensity of overwhelmed immune suppression rather than a single disease mechanism.

Our multiplex immunohistochemistry analysis evaluated PD-L1 expression separately in different cell types. PD-L1+ macrophages and cardiomyocytes were significantly more abundant in patients with major adverse cardiac events, whereas PD-L1+ endothelial cells were not. Future work should investigate macrophage heterogeneity. Ma et al4 demonstrated that interferon-γ blockade and macrophage depletion reduced cardiac Cxcl9+Cxcl10+ macrophages and prolonged survival in a murine model of immune checkpoint inhibitor myocarditis. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic approaches targeting this subset, with similar analyses for other compartments, may help clarify whether specific PD-L1–expressing populations mediate disease progression or indicate disease severity.

We regard our findings as hypothesis-generating and hope they encourage prospective studies integrating clinical, pathologic, and molecular risk stratification.

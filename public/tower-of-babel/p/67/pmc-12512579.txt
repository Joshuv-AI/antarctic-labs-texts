Correlation between nurses’ knowledge and practices in prevention of catheter-associated urinary tract infection (CAUTI) with UTI incidence in ICU
Authors: Ata Karami, Mohammad Azad Majedi, Naser Kamyari, Salam Vatandost
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe way of Foley insertion and required cares after that are key factors to prevent catheter-associated urinary tract infection. Nurses play the main role in catheter placement and care, so their knowledge and performance are important. This study aims to determine Correlation Between Nurses’ Knowledge and Practices in prevention of catheter‐associated urinary tract infection with Urinary Tract Infection Incidence in Intensive Care Unit.MethodsThis correlational study was conducted with the cross-sectional method. 105 nurses from five Intensive Care Unit wards was selected with census sampling. The data about nurses’ knowledge and practice to prevent catheter-associated urinary tract infection was collected through standard questionnaires with self-reporting. The incidence of Urinary Tract Infection in the last six months according to statistics recorded by the hospital infection control center was considered. The descriptive results of nurses’ knowledge, their practice, Urinary Tract Infection prevalence, and the correlation between these factors was examined.ResultsThe average work experience of nurses was 2.90 ± 1.57 years. Only 16.20% of nurses had taken part in workshops related to Urinary Tract Infection prevention. The average score of knowledge was 73.26, and for practice was 2.85 ± 0.37, which both were in a low level. The overall incidence of Urinary Tract Infection in the mentioned period was 2.99%. Urinary Tract Infection prevalence had an inverse and significant correlation with nurses’ knowledge and practice, 0.461 and 0.491 respectively.ConclusionNurses’ knowledge and practice to prevent catheter-associated urinary tract infection were at poor level. Therefore, considering the negative and significant correlation of these variables with incidence of Urinary Tract Infection in this study, it is required that nurses be trained about specialized trainings related to urinary catheter care.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Prevalence, Urinary tract infection, Practice, Knowledge, Nurse, Catheter-associated urinary tract infection, Intensive care unit

Background

Urinary tract infections (UTI) are one of the hospital acquired infections, which account for 40% of this group of infections [1, 2]. More than 150 million people infected with UTI every year [1]. The incidence of this infection in hospitalized patients in Intensive care unit (ICU) wards varies from 9% to 37% [3]. UTI can cause some clinical situation like prolonged hospitalization, overuse of antibiotics [4]. drug resistance, urethral stricture, pyelonephritis, cystitis and sepsis. These consequences, in turn, can increase mortality [5].

UTIs can stem from fistulas between the digestive tract and urinary tract, transmission through blood and urethra, with urinary catheters being the most significant contributing factor, increasing the risk of infection in medical centers up to 14 times [6]. Approximately 70% to 80% of all acquired UTIs are due to inappropriate catheterization and not following the catheterization-related instructions of control and prevention of diseases center. This infection is known as catheter-associate urinary tract infection (CAUTI) [7].In a meta-analysis study overall CAUTI incidence was 2.83 per 1,000 catheter-days across 623 ICUs in 37 countries, based on 1.66 million patient-days of data [8]. Data from over 860 hospitals using the Iranian Nosocomial Infection Surveillance System show a UTI rate of 3.32 per 1,000 patient-days [9]. The world health organization(WHO) see educating to health care providers as the most important factor to control and prevent infection [10] as CAUTI tend to is preventable with following appropriate instructions and measures like washing hands with proper techniques, high quality care of inserted catheter, avoiding prolonged and unnecessary catheterization, and correct removal [11].

Nurses play the main role in managing urinary catheter and preventing CAUTI because insertion and maintenance of this catheter is within the scope of their duties [12]. Recent studies emphasize the critical role of nurses in preventing catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTIs). Mohammad & Abusaad reported Applying a nursing care bundle increased nurses’ knowledge and practices, resulting in a 30% reduction in catheter-associated urinary tract infections [13]. Gupta et al. (2023) reported a 52% reduction in CAUTI rates in the cardiac ICU following a coordinated strategy centered on nursing staff empowerment [14]. Another study reported implementing a bundled care model in critical care units led to a 66% reduction in CAUTI rates, decreasing from 3.84 to 1.31 per 1,000 catheter-days [15]. Therefore, having the required knowledge and practice is necessary for nurses to prevent CAUTI and to provide a safe care [16]. It is shown that nurses have a low level of knowledge and practice in terms of preventing CAUTI. A study in Ethiopia, for instance, showed that 63% of nurses had a poor knowledge and 48% of them had a poor practice to prevent CAUTI [17]. Another study in Saudi Arabia, which was held among the nurses of intensive care units, showed that more than 60% of nurses had poor knowledge and more than 80% of them had a poor level of practice in this matter [7].

The number of Studies about the relationship between nurses’ knowledge and their practice to prevent CAUTI studies is limited, requiring more examination. Therefore, considering the high prevalence of UTI as a hospital infection, subsequent complaints following it, and nurses’ critical role in this matter, this study was conducted to determine the Correlation Between Nurses’ Knowledge and Practices in prevention of catheter-associated urinary tract infection (CAUTI) with UTI Incidence in ICU.

Methods

Study and samples

This correlational study was conducted in 2023 with a cross-sectional method in Kowsar hospital of Kurdistan University of Medical Sciences, which is the biggest hospital in Kurdistan province with 500 beds. There are five ICU wards with 50 beds in total. This hospital is a trauma and surgery center, but medical patients are also admitted there. The inclusion criteria include: nurses were inclination to participate and working in mentioned ICU wards at least for the last 6 months (December 21, 2022 to June 21, 2023). All ICU nurses who were available during data collection were included to the study with census method, and incomplete questionnaires were removed. All inpatients hospitalized during the mentioned six-month period in ICUs (December 21, 2022 to June 21, 2023) were included to the study. In the current study setting, CAUTIs were identified based on the APSCI guidelines [18], adapted to the ICU patient population. For conscious patients, clinical assessment included symptoms such as Bacteriuria, fever, dysuria, urgency, and suprapubic tenderness, followed by urine culture confirmation with a bacterial count of more than 10 5 colony-forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL) in sterile sample. However, in non-conscious or sedated ICU patients, who cannot report urinary symptoms such as frequency or dysuria, diagnosis relies on observable signs, including Bacteriuria, fever and changes in mental status or level of consciousness, along with positive urine cultures meeting the same bacterial threshold. Patients whose urine culture samples were positive at least 48 h after catheterization in the ICU wards or 48 hours’ post-removal of urinary Catheter were considered as positive cases.

Measurement

The data was collected through the standard questionnaire of nurses’ knowledge and practice in preventing CAUTI designed and psychometricised by Ivy Mong et al., with self-reporting method. The content validity index (CVI) was reported (0.83–1). Knowledge scale test-retest interclass correlation reported an interclass correlation coefficient of 0.82. Cronbach’s alpha for the practice scale was 0.83. this questionnaire, knowledge questionnaire includes 27 true-false questions which 80% or more of true answers is considered as well knowledge, and the percentage less than 80 is considered as poor knowledge. The practice questionnaire includes 23 questions in Likert scale method with four-choice answers having a range of completely agree (score 4) to completely disagree (score 1). In terms of practice, the average of overall score is reported from 1 to 4, with interpreting score 3 or more as well practice and score 3 or fewer is considered as poor practice [12]. After obtaining permission from the questionnaire original author, translation and psychometric of the Persian version was done. The validity of the study was assessed with qualitative method by the opinion of 10 nursing faculty members after forward and backward translation. The Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) value was 0.82, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant (p < 0.03), confirming the adequacy of the sample for factor analysis. Factor loadings were acceptable, with values up to 0.48. Construct validity was further supported by confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), with a χ²/df ratio of 2 and a Comparative Fit Index (CFI) of 0.95, indicating a good model fit. These findings demonstrate that the Persian version of the questionnaire has acceptable validity and reliability for use in our study. In addition, the reliability of the knowledge and practice questionnaires in this study is 0.78 and 0.82, respectively.

The prevalence of UTI in each ICU wards in the last six months (December 22, 2022 to June 21, 2023) was taken from the director of infection control according to hospital official statistics. The result of nurses’ knowledge and practice was measured separately for each ward.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The study was reviewed by the Research Ethics Committee at Kurdistan University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUK.REC.1402.071). All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations (Helsinki Declaration of Ethical Principles for Medical Research). Written informed consent was obtained from all the participants prior to enrollment in this study. In an illiterate person, verbal informed consent and a thumbprint were used.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS, version 16.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Shapiro–Wilk test indicated that the data were normally distributed. Descriptive statistics, including mean and standard deviation, were calculated. For pairwise comparisons, independent-samples t-tests were applied, while comparisons involving more than two groups were assessed using one-way ANOVA. Spearman’s correlation was used to examine relationships between variables. A significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The results of present study showed the majority of nurses have not participated in educational workshops related to CAUTI, while UTI is the most common hospital infection, and urinary catheter being the main way to get this infection. Although the most of the participants had the experience of taking part in workshops about general prevention of infection, paying attention to common and important diseases in medical centers is required, and specialized courses should be held and participated so that staff have the maximum knowledge and skill in those subjects [19]. As a result, it is needed that specialized education and relearning courses according to guidelines related to CAUTI be considered.

In the present study, the level of knowledge and practice of nurses to prevent CAUTI was poor. Review of previous studies related to this subject like Daminas et al. [20], in Spain showed a poor level of knowledge and practice to prevent CAUTI, while a review study in 2023 in Malaysia reported that nurses’ knowledge and practice to prevent CAUTI was in a good level [19]. In the previous studies factors like get training [21, 22] and more work experience [17, 23] have been considered as influential factors on the favorable nurses’ knowledge in prevention of CAUTI. On the other hand, a good level of knowledge [24], work experience [25], and higher education degree have been mentioned as impactful factors on nurses’ practice to prevent CAUTI [21, 26]. These reasons could be justifying for poor knowledge in the present study, as well, because the average work experience of the nurses was low and the majority of them had not received specialized trainings about prevention of UTI. Furthermore, poor knowledge plus the mentioned factors can justify the poor practice. Consequently, paying attention to these factors is necessary to improve nurses’ knowledge and practice to decrease the incidence of UTI.

The incidence of UTI in the present study was 2.99%, which was consistent with (0.32% to 9.1%), as a Meta-analysis in Iran reported about the overall prevalence of hospital infections in this country [27]. Another study in Iran reported the incidence of hospital infection in ICU at 9.6% [9]. However, a study in India reported that the incidence of UTI in ICU was 10.57% [28]. The incidence of UTI in the present study was lower than the mentioned studies. This difference could be caused by factors like clinical features and demographics of observed cases. Other risk factors like blood infections, gastrointestinal infections, obstructive diseases of the urinary system, using immunosuppressive drugs, diabetes [29], and background factors like higher age and female gender can increase the risk of UTI [30, 31]. Because the current study setting is a major trauma and surgical center, most of whom are young and male, this young age means that they are at lower risk of underlying diseases that predispose them to urinary tract infections, such as diabetes and urinary tract obstruction.

The present study showed Nurses’ knowledge to prevent CAUTI has a reversed and significant moderate correlation with the incidence of UTI. The results of the study of Sorbye et al. in 2016, indicated a significant impact of improving nurses’ knowledge to decrease UTI [32]. In addition, the results of the study of Mody et al. [33], Mazi et al. [34], Samra et al. [35] showed that education according to guidelines by nurses can decrease CAUTI, significantly. According to interventional studies, the use of the face-to-face method [36], Peer-to-Peer Teaching [37], education package [38] and training according to need [39] enhances nurses’ knowledge about the prevention of CAUTI. Although in the present study, the knowledge score based on the history of participation in a specific workshop on the prevention of urinary tract infection did not have a statistically significant difference, which could be affected by other demographic characteristics of nurses, it is recommended to hold this workshop due to the differences in the method of preventing this infection compared to other hospital infections and its high prevalence.

Like the knowledge variable, Nurses’ practice to prevent CAUTI has a reversed and significant correlation with the incidence of UTI at, which indicates a moderate correlation. A systematic review by Liang (2025) demonstrated that adherence to protocols by nurses was associated with a 55.9% reduction in the incidence of CAUTI [40]. Similarly, a six-year longitudinal study by Manguetti et al. showed that the implementation of a daily checklist addressing catheter indication and care by nurses significantly reduced the incidence of UTI in ICU settings [41]. Other studies have also reported a significant impact of nurses’ clinical practice on reducing the occurrence of UTI [32, 35]. Mohammad & Abusaad’s study showed that the nurses’ performance in preventing CAUTI showed a significant improvement after implementing the care bundle, particularly in adherence to hand hygiene, aseptic techniques, and appropriate catheter maintenance [13]. A Randomized control trial study by Lengetti et al. showed that master learning, in comparison with traditional education, has a better effect on nurses, practice in insertion and care of urinary catheter [42].

On the other hand, the results of the present study showed that there is a moderate correlation between knowledge and practice of CAUTI prevention. The results of several other studies also indicate a relationship between knowledge and practice of CAUTI prevention in nurses, such as in the study of Veer et al. a positive moderate correlation [43], The studies by Mohammad et al. [12], Mong et al. [12], and Tobin et al. [44] all reported a significant relationship. Considering the significant influence of nurses’ knowledge on their practice to prevent CAUTI, it is important to pay attention to improve their knowledge through specialized educations in academic and in-service education Based on standard protocols and guidelines such as CDC and WHO regarding prevention of CAUTI. Therefore, according to these results, it can be seen that improving nurses’ knowledge and practice decreases the incidence of UTI in ICU patients, considerably. This correlation indicates that to decrease the incidence of UTI as a most common hospital infection, a specific attention should be paid to knowledge and practice of nurses as the variables for decreasing CAUTI, having the potential to improve. This accomplishment can be achieved through paying attention to academic educations, workshops, in-service trainings, and assessing nurses’ clinical practice.

Limitations

This study relied on self-reported questionnaires to assess nurses’ knowledge and practice, which may introduce response bias. Additionally, the study was conducted in a single hospital, which may affect the generalizability of the findings to other settings. Also another the limitations of our study was the use of a census sampling method, which was necessitated by the small sample size available in the research setting. Finally, other potential risk factors for UTI, such as patient comorbidities and catheterization duration, were not controlled for in the analysis.

Implications

Although this study has limitations, the significant inverse correlation between nurses’ knowledge/practice and UTI incidence underscores the potential impact of improved competency on patient outcomes. Hospital administrators and policymakers should prioritize ongoing, specialized training and regular assessment of clinical practices. Ultimately, investing in nurse education may substantially reduce CAUTI rates and associated complications in intensive care settings.

Conclusion

The results indicated that nurses’ knowledge and practice to prevent CAUTI were at poor level. Therefore, considering the negative and significant correlation of these variables with incidence of UTI in this study, it is required that nurses be trained about specialized trainings related to urinary catheter care. In addition, their clinical practice in this matter should be paid more attention and more assessed. Improvement in these factors can lead to decrease of the number of patients infected to the most common hospital infection, reducing the possible negative consequences.

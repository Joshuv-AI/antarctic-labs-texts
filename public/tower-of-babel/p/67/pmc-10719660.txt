Computed tomography imaging and clinical characteristics of pulmonary ground‐glass nodules ≤2 cm with micropapillary pattern
Authors: Chen‐ran Guo, Lin Xu, Xiao Li, Yi‐lin Fu, Hui Wang, Rui Han, Geng‐sheng Li, Zhen Feng, Meng Li, Wan‐gang Ren, Zhong‐min Peng
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to investigate the imaging features, lymph node metastasis, and genetic mutations in micropapillary lung adenocarcinoma (imaging with mixed ground‐glass nodules) ≤2 cm, to provide a more precise and refined basis for the selection of lung segment resection.MethodsA retrospective analysis of 162 patients with surgically resected pathologically confirmed cancers ≤2.0 cm in diameter (50 cases of micropapillary mixed ground‐glass nodules [mGGNs], 50 cases of nonmicropapillary mGGNs, and 62 cases of micropapillary SNs [solid nodules]) was performed. mGGNs were classified into five categories according to imaging features. The distribution of these five morphologies in micropapillary with mGGN and nonmicropapillary with mGGN was analyzed. The postoperative pathology and prognosis of lymph node metastasis were also compared between micropapillary mGGNs and micropapillary with SNs. After searching the TCGA database, we demonstrated heterogeneity, high malignancy and high risk of microcapillary lung cancer cancers.ResultsDifferent pathological subtypes of mGGN differed in morphological features (p < 0.05). The rate of lymph node metastasis was significantly higher in micropapillary mGGNs than in nonmicropapillary mGGNs. In the TCGA database samples, lactate transmembrane protein activity, collagen transcription score, and fibroblast EMT score were remarkably higher in micropapillary adenocarcinoma. Other pathological subtypes had a better survival prognosis and longer disease‐free survival compared with micropapillary adenocarcinoma.ConclusionmGGNs ≤2 cm with a micropapillary pattern have a higher risk of lymph node metastasis compared with SNs, and computed tomography (CT) imaging features can assist in their diagnosis.Keywords: ground‐glass nodules, lung adenocarcinoma, lymph nodes, micropapillary pattern, morphological

INTRODUCTION

Lung cancer has been confirmed as the second most common malignancy and the most lethal malignancy. A total of 2.2 million new cases of lung cancer and 1.8 million deaths of lung cancer were reported by the end of 2021. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) has been confirmed as the most common pathological type of lung cancer. 1 , 2 The subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma comprise lepidic adenocarcinoma, acinar adenocarcinoma, papillary adenocarcinoma, micropapillary adenocarcinoma, as well as solid adenocarcinoma, as indicated by the 2015 World Health Organization classification for lung cancer. 3 Given a frequently poor prognosis of patients with micropapillary adenocarcinoma, 4 , 5 the pathological subtypes of lung cancer are significantly correlated with their prognosis. Even a micropapillary component of less than 5% can have a significant detrimental effect on the overall survival rate. 6

Surgical resection has been confirmed as the gold standard of treatment for early‐stage lung cancer, and pulmonary lobectomy has been considered the standard mode of surgery since 1960. 7 The practical indication for pulmonary sublobectomy has been extended to peripheral tumors less than 2 cm in diameter without lymph node metastasis over the past few years. 8 , 9 Thus, the selection of surgical approaches for the above‐mentioned type of lung cancer has been extensively investigated. The existing research on JCOG0802 has suggested that patients undergoing pulmonary segmentectomy have a better survival rate than those receiving pulmonary lobectomy with a tumor diameter <2 cm and a solid component ratio >0.5. 10 However, existing research has reported that micropapillary proportion ≥5% is an independent risk factor for recurrence after sub‐obar resections for LUAD ≤2 cm. 11 Accordingly, whether pulmonary segmentectomy applies to tumors that contain micropapillary adenocarcinoma with solid components >0.5 and tumor diameters <2 cm remains a subject of debate.

Thus, identifying lung cancer with micropapillary adenocarcinoma in mixed ground‐glass nodules (mGGN) becomes of critical significance with regard to the selection of surgical approaches and prediction of the prognosis. However, at this stage, imaging research on micropapillary adenocarcinoma has largely focused on solid lesions, and the correlation between pathological subtypes, imaging features and gene mutations in micropapillary adenocarcinoma of mGGN has been rarely investigated. Existing advances in next‐generation sequencing (NGS) technology have markedly contributed to the molecular basis of histological subtyping, whereas there has been scarce research on tumors with micropapillary components. In this study, RNA‐seq analysis of micropapillary adenocarcinoma and other types of LUAD was conducted to provide more insights into the high proliferation, metabolism, and aggressiveness of micropapillary adenocarcinoma from a bioinformatic perspective. The present study aimed to explore the relevant correlations and JCOG0802 findings in terms of pathology, imaging, lymph node metastases, and genetics to lay a more accurate and refined basis for the selection of lung segmental resection.

METHODS

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shandong Provincial Hospital in full compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and due to the retrospective nature of this study, the written informed consent for this retrospective study was waived.

A total of 111 patients (mixed ground‐glass nodules 50 cases and solid nodules 61 cases) were reviewed. All patients underwent radical resection of pulmonary carcinoma with pathologically confirmed adenocarcinoma with a micropapillary component from January 2018 to December 2021 in the thoracic surgery department of Shandong Provincial Hospital. The inclusion criteria of patients are elucidated as follows: (a) All patients had a chest computed tomography (CT) scan 2 weeks before surgery, and postoperative pathology reported invasive adenocarcinoma with a micropapillary component (micropapillary component ≥5%). (b) Maximal lesion diameter ≤2 cm in the CT images. (c) None of the patients had any other tumor history. All tumors were staged in accordance with the eighth edition of the TNM stage classification for lung cancer (Union for International Cancer Control/American Joint Committee on Cancer). 12 The pathological subtypes categorization conformed to the WHO Sort of Thoracic Tumors (fifth edition). 3 For the respective mGGN with a micropapillary component, one of the cases conforming to the criteria was selected as the control, such that there were a total of 50 cases. The criteria are elucidated as follows. (a) Two mutually‐referenced mGGNs had the same diameter and consolidation tumor ratio (CTR). (b) The two patients for comparison had matched age, sex, and smoking history. The patient selection flow diagram is illustrated in Figure 1. After the TCGA database (The Cancer Genome Atlas, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) was searched, a total of 196 LUAD samples with complete pathological subtypes and clinical follow‐up data were obtained, of which 26 samples had micropapillary adenocarcinoma, and 170 samples had other pathology types. The analysis was based on RNA‐seq data in TPM format.

Flow chart of patient selection.

Identification of differentially expressed genes and gene set variation analysis

Differentially expressed genes were analyzed using the Limma package, and adjusted p‐values < 0.05 and Log2 |FC| > 1 served as the thresholds to identify differentially expressed genes. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) was fulfilled using the GSVA R package (version 1.32.0) to estimate gene set enrichment scores. The enrichment scores between the two groups were determined through Wilcoxon rank‐sum test.

Pathological evaluation and defining of micropapillary pattern

All surgically resected specimens were fixed in formalin, followed by dehydration and paraffin embedding of the specimens. Paraffin sections were dewaxed and then processed by hematoxylin‐eosin (HE) staining. In accordance with the latest WHO classification, 3 a semi‐quantitative increase of 5% was adopted for all subtypes for evaluation. The respective invasive adenocarcinoma was classified into five subtypes, that is, lepidic adenocarcinoma, acinar adenocarcinoma, papillary adenocarcinoma, micropapillary adenocarcinoma, and solid adenocarcinoma. The micropapillary pattern (MPP) was defined as any subtype with at least 5% micropapillary composition.

CT examination

Patients were scanned in the fully inspiratory supine position using a Brilliance 64 (Philips Healthcare) CT scan with the following parameters: 0.625 mm slice thickness, 0.625 mm reconstruction interval, pitch 0.984, 120 kV, and 250 mA. All images were reviewed using a high‐resolution 52.8 cm, 2048 × 1560‐pixel grayscale monitor with a standard lung window (window width, 1500 HU; window level, −500 HU) and mediastinal window (window width, 350 HU; window level, 50 HU). Finally, the relevant data were statistically analyzed.

Nodule evaluation

The American Thoracic Society and the European Respiratory Society, 13 lung adenocarcinoma was classified into solid and subsolid nodules based on whether they contained ground‐glass opacity (GGO) components in accordance with the lung adenocarcinoma classification proposed by the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer. Subsequently, the diameters of the ground‐glass nodules (GGNs) and the solid component (without blood vessels) under the lung window were measured (i.e., the longest diameter and the shortest diameter under the largest section were examined, and the average was taken). 14 Consolidation tumor ratio (CTR) was derived from the ratio between the diameter of the solid component and the GGN. 15 All GGNs were assigned to three groups based on diameter (i.e., 0–1, 1–1.5, and 1.5–2.0 cm) for analysis.

The overall performance and detailed characteristics in the CT images of the collected cases were analyzed, and this study followed the classification method of existing research. 16 , 17 All mGGNs were classified into five types through CT morphological classification (Figure 2). (a) Type I (cotton ball) nodules were relatively regular round. (b) Type II (strip), the lesion appeared as an elongated strip and typically exhibited a C‐ or V‐shaped appearance. (c) Type III (bubble‐like lucencies), gas‐density vacuoles or cavities surrounded by GGO or subsolid components. (d) Type IV (lobular shape), the lesions comprised either deep or shallow lobulation, with the shallow lobulation giving lesions polygonal shapes and the deep lobulation giving lesions floral shapes. (e) Type V (solid eccentric shape), lesions exhibited the imaging morphology of mixed ground‐glass nodules (mGGNs), with their solid components exhibiting extremely appressed or leaning to one side.

After image processing in the imaging department of Shandong Provincial Hospital, imaging evaluation was performed by two radiologists with more than 5 years of radiological diagnosis experience in diagnostic imaging of chest diseases. They classified the lesions without knowing the pathological diagnoses. In cases of disagreement, consensus was reached after discussion with a third senior radiologist.

Gene testing methods

DNA was extracted from paraffin‐embedded tissue in accordance with the instructions using the Qiagen DNA FFPE kit (Qiagen). Mutations were detected in exon 18, 19, 20 and 21 of the EGFR gene EGFR mutation detection kit (SINOMD). ABI7500 (Applied Biosystems) was used to perform amplification refractory mutation system‐polymerase chain reaction (ARMS‐PCR). EGFR mutations include effective and nonsense mutations. EGFR effective mutations were defined as mutant type, and EGFR nonsense mutations (T790M) and no EGFR mutations were defined as wild‐type. Genetic test results originated from molecular diagnosis reports.

Follow‐up

Because the collected cases were all ground‐glass nodules (GGNs) with diameters <2 cm, and the postoperative period was short (1–5 years), only one of the follow‐up patients died from an accident, the survival analysis did not achieve statistical significance, such that the survival analysis was waived. This study placed a focus on the follow‐up of postoperative metastasis and postoperative genetic testing. Stage I and II patients were followed up regularly every 6 months for the first 3 years after surgery and then once per year, and the review programs primarily comprised physical examination and low‐dose chest CT. Stage III patients underwent physical examinations and CT ± enhanced chest scans every 3 to 6 months for 3 years, followed by physical examinations and CT ± enhanced chest scans every 6 months for 2 years. If required, brain MRI or PET‐CT scans were added for the above‐mentioned patients.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

All statistical analyses were conducted using software (SPSS version 26.0 for Windows; R version 4.2.0 for Windows). When over two groups of analyses were conducted, the Wilcoxon rank‐sum test was performed for nonparametric tests, whereas analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for parametric tests. Prognostic differences between two groups were analyzed using the SURVIVAL package, and the significance of prognostic differences between different sample groups was evaluated through the log‐rank test. The counting data were examined through the chi‐squared test, and the probability when the expected value in one or more cells was <5 was examined through Fisher's exact test. Quantitative data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, in between‐group contrast, two groups comparison used one‐sample t‐test, and multiple groups comparison used analysis of variance (ANOVA). p < 0.05 indicated a difference that achieved statistical significance.

RESULTS

Heterogeneity and malignancy of micropapillary lung cancer

A total of 26 LUAD patients with micropapillary‐dominated pathological types and 170 cases of other pathological types (excluding solid types) were obtained after the TCGA database was searched. Significant heterogeneity was identified between micropapillary pathological types and other pathologic types (Figure 3, Figure 3b, and Table S1). Moreover, the activity of lactate transmembrane protein was increased in micropapillary lung adenocarcinoma in the TCGA cohort (Figure 3c and Table S2).

The collagen transcription scores were significantly higher in micropapillary lung adenocarcinomas compared with other histological types, as indicated by the comparison of micropapillary pathological types with other pathological types (Figure 3d and Table S2). Moreover, the fibroblast EMT score of micropapillary lung adenocarcinoma was significantly higher than that of other pathological types (Figure 3d and Table S2). Furthermore, patients with other pathological subtypes had a better survival prognosis (Figure 3e) and longer disease‐free survival (Figure 3f) compared with patients with micropapillary lung adenocarcinoma.

Basic characteristics

A total of 50 cases (Table 1) of mGGNs with micropapillary adenocarcinoma were included (26 males and 24 females), with the age ranging from 38 to 77 years (61.42 ± 9.32). To be specific, 31 cases were aged over 60 years old. There were 18 smokers and 32 nonsmokers. There were 23 cases with normal BMI, 21 overweight cases, as well as six obesity cases; 45 cases underwent pulmonary lobectomy, and five cases received pulmonary sublobectomy (four cases had segmentectomy, and one case underwent wedge resection). Furthermore, 35, 10, and five cases were clinical stage I, II, and III among 50 cases, respectively.

Morphological classification of mGGNs with MPP and without MPP

Tables 2, 3, 4 lists the morphological classification of different groups of mGGN. This classification achieved statistical significance (p < 0.05), regardless of whether it was assigned to the 0–1.0 cm (Table 2), 1.0–1.5 cm (Table 3), or 1.5–2.0 cm (Table 4) groups. Type I was most commonly identified in the 0–1.0 cm (63.6%) and 1.0–1.5 cm (50.0%) groups of nonmicropapillary lung cancers, significantly higher than its 9.1% and 7.1% rate in the lung cancer group with MPP; the rate of 36% in the group of 1.5–2.0 cm reached over 4% in the group of micropapillary lung cancer. For types II and III, except for type III with the same proportion (18.2%) in the 0–1 cm group, the micropapillary group was slightly higher than the nonmicropapillary group in the other groups. The percentage of type IV was elevated with the diameter in the MPP group by 27.3%, 28.6%, and 40.0%, respectively, higher than 0%, 0%, and 24.0% in the nonmicropapillary group. Moreover, the proportions of type V in 0–1.0, 1.0–1.5, and 1.5–2.0 cm groups accounted for 45.5%, 42.9%, and 40.0%, higher than 0%, 14.3%, and 8.0% in the nonmicropapillary group. Micropapillary lung cancer was concentrated in types IV and V whether in the 0–1.0, 1.0–1.5, or 1.5–2.0 cm group, significantly higher than in the nonmicropapillary group.

Comparison of postoperative pathological lymph node metastases in micropapillary ground‐glass nodules, nonmicropapillary ground‐glass nodules, and micropapillary solid nodules

The rate of postoperative pathological lymph node metastasis in micropapillary GGNs reached 26%, significantly higher than 2% of nonmicropapillary GGNs (Table 6), which achieved statistical significance (p = 0.001). In the comparison of postoperative pathological lymph node metastasis in micropapillary mGGNs and micropapillary solid nodules (SNs), they were assigned to three groups (0–1, 1–1.5, and 1.5–2.0 cm), respectively (Tables 7, 8, 9). The comparison between the three groups achieved p > 0.05. Except for the lymph node metastasis rate of micropapillary mGGNs in the 1.5–2.0 cm group (44%), higher than that in the SNs group (38.7%), the lymph node metastasis rate of the SNs group in the remaining two groups exceeded that in the mGGNs group. In general, the SN lymph node metastasis rate (32.3%) did not significantly exceed that in the mGGN group (26.0%).

DISCUSSION

Lung cancer has the highest fatality rate among cancers, and its incidence has increased over the past two decades. 1 A randomized controlled trial 18 conducted by the Lung Cancer Study Group has confirmed lobectomy combined with systematic lymph node dissection as the standard procedure for stage T1N0M0 lung cancer. Moreover, there is no significant difference in survival rate between segmental and lobectomy for GGOs with a diameter <2 cm and a solid component <25%. 19 A recent study has suggested that invasive adenocarcinoma with micropapillary components is more prone to recurrence and metastasis, thus resulting in a poor prognosis. Nitadori 11 and Su et al. 21 demonstrated that limited resection of lung cancer with micropapillary component ≥5% is correlated with a greater risk of recurrence than the same lobectomy. Neither segmental resection nor wedge resection applies to this type of lung cancer. However, the results of segmentectomy for mGGNs with a diameter <2 cm are increasingly positive, 10 , 22 , 23 and there is a tendency to prefer a sublobectomy with less injury. Thus, the scope of surgical excision of GGOs and the selection criteria for lobectomy and segmentectomy are still being explored. 22 , 23 , 24 The JCOG0802 study has indicated that local recurrence occurred more frequently in the segmentectomy group, which was surprising to a certain extent. 10 The authors of this study consider that the above‐described phenomenon may result in the malignant differences of different pathological subtypes of lung cancer.

The TCGA database was searched to compare transcriptome features of micropapillary with other pathological types to verify heterogeneity of micropapillary subtypes compared with other subtypes (excluding solid subtypes). Hypoxia and acidification are vital features of the tumor microenvironment, thus markedly reducing the function of antitumor effector cells and then inducing immune tolerance. 25 GSVA was adopted to compare micropapillary with other pathological types, and the tumor cell proliferation rate was higher in the tumor microenvironment of patients with micropapillary pathologic type, 26 and hypoxia 27 and glycolysis 28 appeared to be more significant. Previous research has suggested that cancer‐associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are significantly correlated with cancer occurrence, progression, extracellular matrix remodeling (ECM), as well as drug resistance of cancer cells. 29 , 30 Moreover, ECMs remodeling by CAFs may serve as a mechanical barrier to prevent immune effector cells from migrating and infiltrating into tumor parenchyma. 31 Moreover, the micropapillary LUAD fibroblast extracellular matrix remodeling score 32 was significantly higher than that of other types, probably contributing to the immune escape of cancer cells. As a result, the distant metastasis of cancer cells and the failure of immunotherapy are promoted. Finally, the clinical and follow‐up information regarding micropapillary and other types in the TCGA cohort was acquired and analyzed, and survival analysis suggested that the other pathologic subtypes were less malignant than micropapillary LUAD.

In general, intraoperative frozen section (FS) serves as a vital means to determine surgical strategy. 33 Existing research 34 , 35 has reported that the accuracy of FS in diagnosing the degree of infiltration of lung adenocarcinoma during the operation ranges from 84.4% to 92.0%. However, intraoperative FS diagnosis of subtypes of invasive adenocarcinoma cancer (IAC) is still challenging. Yeh et al. 36 have suggested that intraoperative FS was 68% consistent with postoperative paraffin diagnosis of major pathological subtypes, and the interobserver agreement among three pathologists was 64%. The specificity of FS diagnosis of micropapillary dominant lung adenocarcinoma was up to 99%, but the sensitivity was only 21%. When only the presence or absence of micropapillary subtypes was considered, the diagnostic sensitivity of FS was still only 37%. TrejiBittar et al. 37 also found that the sensitivity of FS in diagnosing micropapillary was significantly lower than that of other pathological subtypes. Moreover, intraoperative FS often gives ambiguous conclusions (e.g., at least MIA). All of the above‐described can increase the difficulty facing the surgeon in determining the scope of surgical resection. Thus, for this type of lung cancers, more effective means should be developed to help confirm the surgical method. To be specific, thin‐slice CT imaging, a noninvasive means, takes on a great significance to the diagnosis of micropapillary small‐diameter lung cancer.

Solid lesions >2 cm in the traditional sense usually show features (e.g., burr sign, pleural drawing sign, bronchial truncation sign, and vascular cluster sign on CT), 38 , 39 whereas the above‐mentioned features have a limited application in diagnosing mGGNs. This study reported that for mGGNs ≤1 cm, type I and II have a high probability of nonmicropapillary lung cancer, which can be considered an exclusion factor for lung cancer with MPP. If other types occur, vigilance should be given, among which type III can be considered a suggestive factor for the diagnosis of micropapillary lung cancer, while type IV and V occupy a large proportion in the micropapillary group. In addition, among nodules with diameters ≤1.0 cm, types IV and V only existed in the micropapillary group, so type IV and V were considered high‐risk indicators for 0–1 cm mGGNs with MPP. In our collected cases, the solid components of many micropapillary GGNs had a great degree of eccentricity, and almost all the solid components showed a state of adherent growth. Whether this phenomenon is related to the growth infiltration mode of micropapillary lung cancer will also be one of our future research directions.

Moreover, in small size lung cancer with MPP, both SNs and mGGNs show a high rate of lymph node metastasis, which will undoubtedly have a great negative effect on the prognosis of patients. In this study, an IAC with mGGN in preoperative imaging that contains only 5% micropapillary components also shows the possibility of N2. In addition, when the observation period is not long enough, the lymph node metastasis shows a negative result. If the observation period is extended, the rate of lymph node metastasis will only be higher. MPP with a micropapillary component ≥5% was also considered an independent predictor of pathological N2. 40 In general, the lymph node metastasis rate (32.3%) of SNs was higher than that of GGNs (26.0%), but the two did not differ significantly. Accordingly, for those MPP lung cancer whereas mGGN imaging, lobectomy and systematic lymph node dissection should be preferred. This type of high‐risk lung cancer should be identified from mGGN before surgery, both for the selection of surgical methods and the evaluation of subsequent adjuvant therapy. The imaging results of the respective case should be evaluated in depth, and intraoperative lymph nodes should also be evaluated if necessary to more accurately determine the suitable population for segmentectomy. On that basis, patients can truly benefit from the above‐mentioned result of suitable population.

Activating mutations in EGFR were first reported in 2004. 41 , 42 Its biological and clinical significance has been extensively studied. 43 Kobayashi et al. 44 suggested that mGGNs without driver gene activation have been more commonly manifested as AAH and AIS. Moreover, the vast majority of LUAD with invasive behavior has appeared in the EGFR mutant population (57/68, 83.8%) in the driver gene activation population. According to the study of Zhong et al., 45 EGFR was the main gene mutation difference in GGNs at different pathological stages. Recently, studies have reported 46 that EGFR mutation is the most common genetic change in micropapillary lung cancer, and patients with EGFR mutation are more likely to develop occult lymph node metastasis. In the current guidelines, no genetic testing has been recommended for GGN, and the data collected are mainly related to the part of patients who requested genetic testing, or patients with lymph node metastasis. The major gene mutation is EGFR, consistent with existing research. 44 , 46 Although genetic testing for GGNs is not recommended in the existing guidelines, genetic testing should be strongly recommended for such GGNs with micropapillary.

There were still some shortcomings in this study. First, the sample size was small because of the small proportion of micropapillary small size lung cancer with imaging findings of GGO, and the preoperative imaging of some patients was completed outside the hospital, such that the results are not available. Second, the farthest patients were 5 years ago, lacking a long enough follow‐up time, and this study was more about discovery, aiming at the diagnosis of mGGNs with MPP, as well as its prognosis and the difference between segmental and lobotomy. We plan to carry out a prospective randomized controlled study in the future. For the prognosis and heterogeneity of micropapillary, based on the actual situation, the method of bioinformatic analysis used for verification is still indirect, which needs to be improved in the subsequent stage.

In conclusion, compared with SNs, patients with mGGNs with MPP ≤2 cm also had a high risk of lymph node metastasis and postoperative recurrent metastasis. Sublobectomy should be rigorously selected for these lesions, and CT imaging features can be conducive to diagnosis to a certain extent.

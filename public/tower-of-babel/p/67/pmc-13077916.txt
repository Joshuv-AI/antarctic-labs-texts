Immune dysregulation complicated by recurrent in-stent restenosis in patients with multivessel coronary artery disease: a case report
Authors: Jianbang Jiang, Zhiwei Huang, Chongjian Li, Hongliang Zhang, Zhenyan Zhao, Fang Yang, Yusheng Gai, Fei Li, Cancan Gong, Yongjian Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe management of complex coronary artery disease (CAD) predominantly relies on percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), yet in-stent restenosis (ISR) remains a critical long-term complication. Emerging evidence implicates immune dysregulation in ISR pathogenesis. Case presentationWe present a 74-year-old female with multivessel CAD, recurrent ISR, and comorbidities including hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Laboratory studies revealed hypogammaglobulinemia M (IgM: 0.306 g/L), an elevated CD4/CD8 ratio (3.24), and elevated hypogammaglobulinemia G3 (IgG3: 0.963 g/L). Intravascular imaging and PET/CT demonstrated neoatherosclerosis with peristrut inflammation. Despite multiple PCIs and guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), she experienced recurrent angina. Following adjunctive anti-inflammatory therapy with prednisone and immunomodulation with mycophenolate mofetil, she achieved symptomatic and angiographic stabilization.ConclusionThis case suggests a potential association between immune dysregulation and recurrent ISR. Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory therapy may represent a novel adjunctive strategy in selected refractory cases, though causality remains speculative and requires further validation.

Introduction

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is a cornerstone in the treatment of complex coronary artery disease (CAD), yet in-stent restenosis (ISR) remains a significant long-term complication [1]. While traditional risk factors such as diabetes and hyperlipidemia contribute to ISR, recent studies suggest that immune-inflammatory pathways may also play a role in its pathogenesis [2, 3]. This study reports a case of recurrent ISR; by integrating systematic immune profiling with serial multimodality imaging, we provide a novel, mechanistically grounded perspective that enhances the current understanding of ISR. Here, we report a case of recurrent ISR in a patient with multivessel CAD and laboratory evidence of immune dysregulation, who responded to adjunctive immunomodulatory therapy.

Case presentation

Patient information and timeline

A 74-year-old woman presented on February 18, 2025, with worsening chest pain over 10 days. Her cardiac history included intermittent angina since early 2024, with prior coronary angiography (CAG) (Fig. 1) showing multivessel disease. Clinical findings and diagnostic assessment

On cardiovascular examination, the heart rhythm was regular with diminished intensity of heart sounds, and a grade 3/6 holosystolic murmur was audible at the mitral area. ECG showed ST-segment depression during chest pain (Fig. 2). Laboratory results included elevated NT-proBNP 2562 pg/mL (ref: 0-250 pg/mL) and troponin I 0.031 ng/mL (ref: 0-0.02 ng/mL). LDL-C: 1.92 mmol/L. Lp(a): 246.64 mg/L. HbA1c: 6.9%. Immunological studies showed IgM 0.306 g/L (ref: 0.63–2.77), CD4/CD8 ratio 3.24 (ref: 0.71–2.78), and IgG3 0.963 g/L (ref: 0.11–0.85). Additional immune markers, including C-reactive protein (CRP), antinuclear antibodies (ANA), and cytokine profiles, were all within normal limits. Coronary CT angiography and PET/CT revealed ISR with perivascular inflammation (Fig. 3A-B).

Therapeutic intervention

Despite GDMT including dual antiplatelet therapy and high-intensity statins, the patient experienced recurrent angina. Repeat CAG (February 28, 2025) revealed the following: proximal LM: 90% stenosis (ISR) (Fig. 1E). The patient refused coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). Percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty (PTCA) was performed on the left main lesion (Fig. 1F).

After multidisciplinary discussion and informed consent, immunomodulatory therapy was initiated on March 5, 2025, with prednisone acetate 30 mg/day and mycophenolate mofetil 1000 mg/day. The regimen was chosen based on prior reports of glucocorticoid and mycophenolate use in refractory vascular inflammation, with dosing adjusted for age and renal function. A multidisciplinary team evaluated the benefit-risk profile, considering the absence of active infection, malignancy, or other contraindications. The treatment was planned for an initial duration of 6 months. The standardized follow-up strategy incorporated systematic clinical evaluation, laboratory assessments (complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, and immune marker profiling), and multimodality imaging (echocardiography, ECG, CCTA, PET/CT) plus invasive coronary angiography (CAG) at 3 and 6 months after the index procedure. Prednisone was tapered by 5 mg every two weeks, while mycophenolate mofetil was maintained (Table 2). In May 2025, OCT-guided drug-coated balloon angioplasty was performed for residual ISR.

Follow-up and outcomes

Follow-up assessment revealed a decrease in IgG3 from 0.963 g/L to 0.408 g/L (ref: 0.11–0.85). CCTA (Fig. 3C) revealed a patent stent in the proximal LM-LAD without significant stenosis at either end. PET/CT (Fig. 3D) in May 2025 showed reduced FDG uptake (SUVmax 4.6 vs. 5.9, Δ = 22.03%). Echocardiography demonstrated improved left ventricular dimensions. No further angina episodes occurred during follow-up.

Discussion

This case draws attention to a potential association between immune dysregulation and recurrent ISR. While our observations support a correlative link between immune dysregulation and recurrent ISR, it is crucial to emphasize that definitive causal relationships cannot be established from a single case study. The detected immunological abnormalities may either initiate pro-inflammatory signaling cascades in the vasculature or arise as a downstream consequence of repeated stent-induced vascular injury. The patient exhibited abnormal immune markers—low IgM, high IgG3 and an elevated CD4/CD8 ratio—along with imaging evidence of peristrut inflammation. These immunological findings are supported by existing literature linking such profiles to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. An altered CD4/CD8 ratio has been identified as a potential biomarker in coronary artery disease, reflecting T-cell subset imbalance that may contribute to vascular pathology [4]. Furthermore, a low level of natural IgM is an independent predictor for vein graft stenosis, suggesting a protective role against excessive fibroproliferative response [5, 6]. Conversely, a high IgG3 level, particularly against oxidized LDL, is considered pro-inflammatory and pro-atherogenic, potentially driving plaque progression and instability [7]. Emerging evidence suggests that immune dysregulation might be an under-recognized contributor to refractory ISR rather than an exceedingly rare occurrence, with sporadic case reports hinting at similar pathophysiological links [8].

The decision to initiate dual immunosuppression in a patient with multivessel coronary artery disease was considered high-risk and was undertaken only after multidisciplinary consensus and thorough patient counseling. The regimen was selected based on prior reports of its use in refractory vascular inflammation [7, 9, 10], though its application in ISR remains exploratory.

The observed reduction in PET/CT FDG uptake after treatment likely reflects diminished vascular inflammation, consistent with the use of this modality to monitor arterial inflammatory activity [11]. However, this clinical improvement probably resulted from a multimodal approach encompassing guideline-directed medical therapy, repeat revascularisation, and adjunctive immunomodulation, rather than from immunosuppressive therapy in isolation. Anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive therapies provide novel therapeutic strategies for patients with such immune abnormalities, although treatment optimization still requires evidence-based validation.

Limitations

Notwithstanding its clinical insights, the present case report has several key limitations that warrant consideration: a single-case design, lack of long-term safety data, and absence of serial immune phenotyping. The presence of significant comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension) likely contributed to the ISR risk, confounding the isolated role of immune dysregulation. The absence of IgM level normalization post-treatment and the lack of longitudinal follow-up data on the CD4/CD8 T-cell ratio hinder a thorough understanding of the extent and trajectory of therapeutic immune remodeling. The lack of extended follow-up data restricts conclusions about the durability of treatment responses and long-term safety profiles. Despite our use of PET/CT FDG uptake to monitor relative metabolic activity changes at the target site, this modality is technically restricted by suboptimal sensitivity and spatial resolution for the precise quantification of focal coronary inflammation; accordingly, the reliability of its quantitative readouts requires rigorous validation in well-designed prospective studies.

Future studies should systematically evaluate immune profiles in ISR patients and explore targeted immunomodulatory strategies in randomized trials. This case raises several pertinent questions for future research: Should immune phenotyping be standardized as part of the routine evaluation for recurrent ISR? Can validated immune signatures be used to personalize immunomodulatory therapy selection for ISR patients? What are the optimal safety and efficacy endpoints to guide the development of such immunomodulatory interventions? Answering these questions via prospective, multicenter studies will be instrumental in translating this preliminary observation into a clinically viable therapeutic strategy for refractory ISR.

Conclusion

Taken together, this case provides compelling preliminary evidence that immune dysregulation may be linked to refractory ISR in a distinct subgroup of patients. The synergistic integration of multimodality imaging and immune profiling enables the precise characterization of concealed inflammatory activity, which in turn guides the development of rational multimodal treatment strategies. While our work remains exploratory and limited to a single case, it underscores the critical need for large-scale, systematic immune evaluation in recurrent ISR cohorts and highlights the translational potential of future clinical trials designed to test targeted anti-inflammatory interventions in this therapeutically underserved patient population.

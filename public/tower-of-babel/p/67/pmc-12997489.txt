Genomic and phenotypic insight into Clostridioides difficile RT027 isolates from China reveals diverse virulence associated with clinical symptoms
Authors: Wenjia Wang, Shan Lin, Yun Luo, Chunhong Shao, Qikai Shi, Jing Shao, Yu Chen, Hui Hu, Shuangshuang Wan, Xiaojun Song, Dazhi Jin, Yan Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTClostridioides difficile (C. difficile) ribotype 027 (RT027) has caused severe outbreaks in North America and Europe over the past 20 years. However, RT027 infections are rare in Asia, particularly in China, with limited severe cases. To clarify its molecular and phenotypic features, we investigated 11 RT027 isolates collected from Shandong, China. Whole-genome sequencing, comparative transcriptomics, CRISPR-Cas analysis, and pan-genome profiling were combined with phenotypic assays of toxin production, sporulation, antimicrobial resistance, and motility. Evolutionary analysis demonstrated that isolates from China clearly diverged from the FQR2 lineage and were phylogenetically closer to FQR1, forming a distinct sublineage. All isolates from Shandong, China encoded a complete tcd and cdt locus and harboured rifamycin and aminoglycoside resistance genes. However, transcriptomic profiling demonstrated significantly reduced expression of binary toxin genes (cdtAB, cdtR), decreased spo0A transcription, and downregulation of flagellar pathways. Phenotypic assays confirmed impaired sporulation, motility and cytotoxicity, while TcdB production and adhesion was comparable to reference strains. CRISPR-Cas elements were conserved but showed reduced transcriptional activity, suggesting diminished host–pathogen interactions. The pan-genome revealed high genomic conservation, consistent with limited functional diversity. Together, these data indicate that RT027 isolates circulating in China possess unique evolutionary trajectories and attenuated virulence traits, helping to explain the rarity of severe RT027 infections in Asia. These findings provide important insights into the regional epidemiology of C. difficile and inform strategies for diagnosis, treatment, and prevention.

Introduction

Clostridioides difficile is a Gram-positive, anaerobic, spores-forming bacterium, which produces toxins that damage the colonic epithelium. C. difficile infection (CDI) leads to symptoms ranging from mild diarrhea to severe pseudomembranous colitis associated with antibiotic exposure [1]. C. difficile infection (CDI) poses a significant public health burden, for example, it is associated with an estimated 15,000 to 30,000 annual deaths and imposes a financial burden of billions of dollars on the healthcare system annually in the United States [2,3], especially the emergence of hypervirulent lineage (for example, ribotype 027, RT027; also called type BI or North American Pulsed-Field (NAP) type NAP1) [4].

C. difficile virulence requires the production of at least one of its major exotoxins, toxin A and/or toxin B [5]. Toxin A and toxin B are encoded by tcdA and tcdB genes located in the 19.6-kb pathogenicity locus (PaLoc), encoding glucosyltransferases that trigger enterocyte cytoskeleton alterations and lead to cell lysis and inflammation. Some C. difficile strains, including PCR ribotypes 027 and 078, produce binary toxin also known as C. difficile transferase(CDT), an actin-specific ADP ribosyltransferase expressed by the cdtAB genes which increases C. difficile virulence and disease severity [6]. The hypervirulent C. difficile RT027 strains simultaneously express all three toxins and have a deletion in the tcdC gene on PaLoc, which is thought to be a negative regulator of toxin expression, leading to increased virulence in C. difficile RT027 strains. In addition to these toxins, C. difficile pathogenicity is also associated with the formation of spores facilitating C. difficile transmission, a series of adhesins promoting intestinal colonization, and biofilms contributing to both C. difficile virulence and antimicrobial resistance [6].

The prevalence and molecular epidemiology of C. difficile exhibit marked geographic heterogeneity. The hypervirulent RT027/BI/NAP1 lineage has caused widespread outbreaks and become one of dominant strains in healthcare settings across North America and Europe since the early 2000s [7]. Nevertheless, targeted public health interventions, particularly antimicrobial stewardship programs restricting fluoroquinolone use, have been temporally associated with a subsequent reduction in the incidence and clinical impact of RT027 in these regions [8,9]. Outside North America and Europe, RT027 has been reported in several countries but often with lower prevalence and sporadic occurrence. In Asia, RT027 has been identified in Japan, South Korea, and China, though it does not dominate local CDI [10-12]. Similar patterns are observed in Australia and Latin America, whereas available data from Africa and the Middle East indicate that RT027 is rarely encountered [13-17]. The constrained spread of RT027 outside Europe and North America may be attributed to differences in antimicrobial stewardship, host genetics, healthcare infrastructure, and competing local strains. Continued genomic surveillance is essential to monitor potential shifts in its global distribution.

The epidemiological pattern of RT027 in China presents a distinct and yet unresolved scenario. Despite the global prominence of this lineage, only sporadic RT027 cases have been documented in China, and no sustained transmission or large-scale outbreaks have been reported within Asia [18]. Instead, national and regional surveillance consistently identify RT017 (ST37), RT046 (ST35), and RT012 (ST54) as the dominant circulating ribotypes [19,20]. Since the first confirmed RT027 isolate was recovered from a patient with Crohn’s disease in 2014 [21], additional genetically divergent isolates have been identified among outpatients in Beijing [22]. Subsequent investigations, including a genomic characterization of a nosocomial cluster by Jia et al., confirmed the presence and intermittent circulation of RT027 within Chinese healthcare settings [23], and more recent reports have even documented its detection in companion animals, suggesting potential cross-species dissemination [24]. A comparative genomic study by Lv et al. proposed that Chinese RT027 isolates may harbour attenuated virulence and transmissibility relative to their Western counterparts [25]. Although this hypothesis offers a plausible explanation for the limited epidemiological footprint of RT027 in China, it remains speculative due to the absence of direct functional or mechanistic validation.

To determine RT027 genome evolution and virulence diversity, we collected C. difficile RT027 isolates from CDI patients in Shandong, China during 2018 to 2020, and performed whole genome sequencing, transcriptome sequencing, and phenotypic characterization. We identified features associated with C. difficile RT027 pathogenicity including evolutionary differences, expression of Cdt pathogenicity locus and sporulation related genes. In addition, analysis of CRISPR-Cas system on C. difficile genome was used to elucidate the possible association between host and CRISPR-Cas expression regulation in C. difficile infection. We also analyzed phenotypical characteristics that are important for the biology of C. difficile including antibiotic resistance, motility, sporulation and TcdB concentration measurement. Overall, our study describes genotypic and phenotypic variety within C. difficile RT027 in China.

Methods

Study design

C. difficile RT027 isolates were collected from patients with diarrhea during 2018–2020 and reviewed with the approval of the Ethics Committee of Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University (SZRJJ: NO. 2021-078). An aliquot of the stool specimen was tested by GeneXpert C. difficile platform (Cepheid, CA, USA) according to manufacturer’s instructions to rapidly detect the presence of tcdB, the binary toxin gene (cdt) and the deletion of tcdc, and to presumptively identify RT027 [26]. Stool samples (tcdB + Δtcdc + cdt+) indicative of RT027 by the Xpert assay were isolated from stool specimens and grown on taurocholate-cefoxitin-cycloserine-fructose agar (TCCFA) and grown anaerobically at 37 °C for 48 h to obtain individual isolates. Single colonies were sub-cultured, and species identification was confirmed using capillary gel electrophoresis ribotyping to definitively confirm their classification as RT027 [27]. C. difficile RT027 isolates were subjected to genomic, transcriptomic and phenotypic analysis and C. difficile R20291 (BI/NAP1/027) (stored in our laboratory) was utilized as a reference strain in our study.

Classification of CDI severities

Based on recommendations from Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA)/Society for Healthcare Epidemiology of America (SHEA) and European Society of Clinical Microbiology and Infectious Diseases (ESCMID), CDI severities were categorized into mild-to-moderate CDI, severe CDI and fulminant CDI (life-threatening or complicated CDI) [28,29]. Briefly, mild-to-moderate CDI patients had white blood cell (WBC) count less than 15,000 cells/µL and serum creatinine (SCr) less than 1.5 times the baseline with watery diarrhea (≥ 3 loose stools in 24 h) without systemic signs of severe disease such as fever or hypotension. Severe CDI patients experienced profuse diarrhea accompanied by severe abdominal pain and systemic symptoms such as fever (> 38.5°C) with WBC count ≥ 15,000 cells/µL and SCr ≥ 1.5 times. Patients with fulminant CDI often exhibited hypotension, septic shock, or multiorgan failure with WBC count ≥ 35,000 cells/µL or leukopenia (< 4,000 cells/µL), rising serum lactate levels and radiological evidence of toxic megacolon or colonic perforation.

Case definition and clinical data categorization

CDI cases were classified based on the 2017 Infectious Diseases Society of America and Society for Healthcare Epidemiology of America (IDSA/SHEA) clinical practice guidelines [3], as follows:

Healthcare-onset CDI (HO-CDI): Defined by a positive test result for C. difficile from a stool specimen collected ≥ 48 h after admission to the healthcare facility.

Community-associated CDI (CA-CDI): Defined by a positive test result collected < 48 h after admission, provided that the patient had not been discharged from any healthcare facility in the 12 weeks preceding the symptom onset.

All cases in this study were categorized according to these criteria.

Genomic analysis

Paired-end sequencing (2 × 150 bp) of RT027 isolates were performed using Illumina HiSeq platform at Novogene. The raw sequence reads were evaluated by FastQC v0.11.8 accessible to the public via https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/. The sequence reads were trimmed by Trimmomatic v0.38 [30] to remove adapters and low-quality reads using following parameters: “LEADING:3 TRAILING:3 SLIDINGWINDOW:4:15 MINLEN:40.” The filtered reads were assembled using SPAdes v3.15.3 [31] with the “careful” option and the coverage was approximately 445× (Table S1). Average nucleotide identity (ANI) was calculated by FastANI v1.32 [32]. Antimicrobial resistance genes in the assemblies were identified by AMRFinderPlus 3.11 with database version 2023-04-17.1 [33].

Phylogenetic tree

RT027 isolates in this study and 161 RT027 isolates from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) were included for phylogenetic tree construction. The 35 public genomes from the United States (n = 7), United Kingdom (n = 6), Korea (n = 4), Australia (n = 2), Germany (n = 1), Canada (n = 1), China (Zhejiang, n = 2; Beijing, n = 13) (Table S2) [23,25,34] and RT027 isolates from this study were included in phylogenetic tree construction together with antimicrobial resistance and virulence genotype profiles. 161 RT027 isolates from NCBI and RT027 isolates in this study were employed for an unrooted phylogenetic tree to represent FQR1 and FQR2 lineage (Tables S2 and S3). Nodes labelled as FQR1 or FQR2 were defined by He and colleagues [34]. Phylogenetic relationships were inferred using kSNP3 v3.1.2 based on core SNPs [35] using C. difficile RT027 strain R20291 (NC_013316.1) as the reference genome. High-quality core SNPs were used to construct a phylogenetic tree. The parsimony tree was visualized by iTOL (http://itol.embl.de/) [36].

Variant calling

The filtered sequence reads for each isolate were independently aligned to the C. difficile RT027 reference genome R20291 (NC_013316.1) using Bowtie2 v2.3.4 [37]. The aligned sequences were converted into binary BAM files and sorted by Samtools v1.14 [38]. PCR duplicate removal and variant calling were performed using GATK v4.2.4.0 [39]. The variant table in VCF format was annotated by ANNOVAR [40].

RNA-seq analysis

Total RNA was extracted from C. difficile RT027 isolates using TRIzol® Reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and genomic DNA was removed using DNase I (Takara Bio, Kusatsu, Japan). Libraries for RNA sequencing were prepared following TruSeq RNA sample preparation Kit from Illumina (San Diego, CA, USA), using 5 μg of total RNA. After rRNA was removed by RiboZero rRNA removal kit (Epicenter, Madison, WI, USA), cDNA was synthesized and adaptors were added according to manufacturer’s instructions. RNA sequencing was performed by Illumina NovaSeq 6000 paired-end sequencing (2 × 150 bp, BIOZERON Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China).

The sequenced reads were trimmed and qualified by Trimmomatic v0.38 with parameters described before [30] and were mapped to the C. difficile R20291 genome (RefSeq Accession NC_013316.1) by STAR [41] using standard parameters. The reference RNA-Seq data of R20291 strains were downloaded from NCBI (accession number GSE234961) [42]. The expression and differential expression values were estimated by DESeq2 [43] with a significance threshold of log2-fold change cut-off of 1 and adjusted p-value of 1 × 10−5. Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using ShinyGO v0.80 [44] with the C. difficile String-DB reference.

CRISPR-Cas loci and arrays analysis and pan-genome estimation

CRISPR-Cas loci and spacers of CRISPR-Cas systems in C. difficile isolates were identified by CRISPRCasTyper v1.6.3 with “prodigal meta” parameters [45].

C. difficile RT027 genomes annotated by Prokka v1.14.6 [46] were used as input for pan-genome analysis. Pan-genome analysis was performed on Roary v3.13.0 [47] and PanGP v1.0.1 [48] with default parameters.

Antibiotic susceptibility testing

Antibiotic susceptibility testing was conducted using the agar dilution method, in accordance with the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines [49]. A total of 12 antibiotics were evaluated: clindamycin, ciprofloxacin, erythromycin, fusidic acid, gatifloxacin, levofloxacin, metronidazole, moxifloxacin, piperacillin, rifampicin, tetracycline, and vancomycin. C. difficile ATCC 700057 and Bacteroides fragilis ATCC 25285 were used as control strains. Minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were interpreted based on the CLSI M100 recommendations for clindamycin, metronidazole, moxifloxacin, piperacillin, and tetracycline; the EUCAST guidelines (www.eucast.org) for fusidic acid, rifampicin, and vancomycin; and the CLSI guidelines for moxifloxacin in anaerobes, which were applied to ciprofloxacin, gatifloxacin, and levofloxacin. The erythromycin breakpoint was set at 8 µg/mL, as previously reported [50].

Measurement of tcdB concentration, sporulation capacity, and motility in C. difficile

RT027 isolates in our study and R20291 were cultured in brain heart infusion (BHI) broth supplemented with 5 g/L yeast and 1 g/L L-cycloserine for 24 h. After centrifugation at 8,000 rpm for 5 min at 4°C, the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany). TcdB concentrations were measured by indirect ELISA. Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with 100 µL of supernatant and standard toxin (serial dilutions from 12.5 to 200 ng/mL, prepared in the laboratory) overnight at 4 °C. After blocking with 200 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 0.5% bovine serum albumin for 2 h, the plates were incubated with 100 µL of diluted E3 antibody (1:4000, prepared in the laboratory) for 1 h. Following washing three times with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 (PBST), 100 µL of diluted anti-MYC tag antibody (1:5000, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) was added and incubated for 1 h. After washing three times with PBST, 100 µL of TMB substrate (Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) was added and incubated for 15 min in the dark. The reaction was stopped by adding 100 µL of stop solution (Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), and absorbance was measured at 450 nm. TcdB concentrations were calculated from the standard curve.

Sporulation capacity was assessed as previously described [51]. Briefly, RT027 isolates and R20291 were cultured in BHI broth at 37 °C for 48 h in an anaerobic chamber. The BHI cultures were adjusted to approximately OD600 = 1, then subjected to heat treatment at 60 °C for 25 min. The cultures were then plated on BHI agar plates containing 2% agar and 0.1% taurocholate. After 48 h of anaerobic culture, colony-forming units were counted.

Motility assays were performed as previously described [52]. Briefly, RT027 isolates and R20291 were cultured in motility agar tubes containing BHI medium supplemented with 0.175% agar. These tubes were inoculated by stabbing and incubated anaerobically at 37°C for 16 h and motility was assessed by measuring the diameter of bacterial growth.

Analysis of host cell adhesion and cytotoxicity

Caco-2 cell adhesion assay. Caco-2 cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells/mL and cultured to confluence. C. difficile strains were grown to mid-log phase (OD₆₀₀ = 0.6 to 0.8) in BHIS broth, washed, and resuspended in antibiotic-free MEM medium adjusted to a McFarland standard of 1. Confluent Caco-2 monolayers were incubated with 1 mL of bacterial suspension for 1 h at 37°C under anaerobic conditions. Non-adherent bacteria were removed by washing twice with DPBS. Adherent bacteria were released by trypsinization, serially diluted, plated on BHIS agar, and enumerated after 24–48 h of anaerobic incubation. Adhesion was expressed as colony-forming units (CFU) per mL.

Cytotoxicity assay. Bacterial culture supernatants were prepared by growing C. difficile strains in BHI broth for 24 h anaerobically. Cultures were centrifuged, and the supernatant was filtered (0.22 μm) and concentrated using a 100-kDa centrifugal filter. Caco-2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 1 × 10⁴ cells per well. After 24 h, 10 μL of the concentrated supernatant was added to the cells. Following a 12-hour incubation, cell viability was determined using a CCK-8 assay according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan). The optical density at 450 nm was measured, and results were normalized to untreated control cells. Culture medium (BHI) and normal saline (NS) served as negative controls.

Results

Clinical and epidemiological observations of C. difficile RT027 infected patients

We analyzed clinical data of 11 patients infected with C. difficile RT027 at a hospital in Shandong, China, between 2018 and 2020. The patients were predominantly elderly (mean age 75.63 years), comprising six men and five women (Table 1). Among these, six cases had a documented history of antibiotic exposure, two had no history of antibiotic exposure, while three patients lacked recent antibiotic usage data. All patients exhibited serum creatinine (SCr) less than 1.5 times the baseline and none of the cases presented with ileus or septic shock at diagnosis (Table 1). Based on the IDSA/SHEA and ESCMID guidelines and patients’ underlying comorbidities, all cases were classified as mild-to-moderate CDI. Evolutionary analysis of C. difficile RT027 isolated in from China

To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying phenotypic differences, whole-genome sequencing was performed for the 11 isolates, using C. difficile R20291 as the reference genome (Table S1). To understand their evolutionary context, we incorporated additional RT027 genome data from China (Beijing, n = 13; Zhejiang, n = 2) and other countries (n = 20) into a phylogenetic analysis (Table S2). Chinese RT027 isolates clustered by geographic region and formed a distinct subclade that was most closely related to RT027 isolates from Korea (Figure 1(A)). Phylogenetic analysis using kSNP3 revealed that the Chinese and Korean subclades were separated by only five branch-defining core SNPs, suggesting a close genetic relationship between the two groups. Two distinct fluoroquinolone-resistant (FQR) lineages of RT027 arose from independent mutation events in the United States during the early 1990s and subsequently disseminated globally. However, the unrooted phylogenetic tree constructed by global RT027 isolates showed isolates in China were phylogenetically distinct from these widespread FQR lineages and formed a separate sublineage close to FQR1 (Table S3, Figure 1(B)). These results indicate that C. difficile RT027 in China exhibited a notable divergence in evolutionary trajectory compared to C. difficile RT027 in other countries. Genomic characteristics of C. difficile RT027 in Shandong, China

11 C. difficile RT027 isolates in our study exhibited high genome-wide similarity at average nucleotide identity (ANI) scores exceeding 99.9% and encode identical PaLoc sequences (Figure S2). Comparative genomic analysis revealed several antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes shared between the 11 C. difficile RT027 isolates and R20291, including genes conferring resistance to quinolones, beta-lactams, aminoglycosides, and vancomycin. However, the 11 C. difficile RT027 isolates harboured additional resistance genes, rpoB (R505 K, conferring rifamycin resistance) and aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes, which were absent in R20291 (Figure 1(A)).

A total of 57 genetic variations were identified in the Shandong isolates compared to R20291, including 43 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), five stop-gain mutations, and nine deletions (DELs) (Table S4). Noteworthy mutations included a deletion (c.396_404delCCCAACAGG) in the bclA3 gene, which encodes a spore coat protein, and a missense mutation (c.467C > T, p.Pro156Leu) in the slpA gene, which encodes the major surface-layer protein. These mutations may influence the virulence and structural properties of C. difficile RT027.

Low transcription of binary toxins and impaired initiation of sporulation could contribute to different epidemiological patterns of C. difficile RT027 in China

To gain a further understanding of the differences in gene expression between R20291 and strains isolated from China, comparative RNA-Seq analysis was conducted following culture. Principal component analysis (PCA) and correlation analysis revealed that the 11 C. difficile RT027 isolates from our study formed a distinct cluster separate from the R20291 strain (Figure S3A and S3B). Comparative transcriptomic analysis identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) across the genome (Figure S3C, Table S5). Gene set enrichment analysis highlighted significant alterations in metabolic pathways, transport systems, membrane integrity, and other essential biological processes (Figure 2(A,B)). Remarkably, downregulated genes were significantly clustered in flagellum related pathway (Figure 2(B)). These findings suggest that transcriptional differences may underlie the distinct pathogenic profiles observed in C. difficile RT027 isolates. Notably, in isolates from China, the transcription levels of tcdA and tcdB were comparable, indicating that the transcription of toxins A and B does not significantly contribute to the low virulence observed in these RT027 isolates (Figure 2(C)). However, it is remarkable that cdtAB, which encode the binary toxin, were significantly downregulated in RT027 isolates in China (Figure 2(C)). Correspondingly, cdtR, which is upstream of cdtAB and act as a gene encoding regulatory protein in CdtLoc, showed marked transcriptional repression. This suggested that reduced expression of binary toxin genes that may lead to diminished virulence and disease severity in isolates from China.

In all endospore-forming bacteria, Spo0A is an essential conserved regulator of sporulation and governed the onset of sporulation. The transcript levels for spo0A were decreased in these isolates from China (Figure 2(C)). Interestingly, despite the repression of spo0A, transcription of stage II-V sporulation genes was activated, likely as a compensatory mechanism in response to impaired sporulation initiation.

Collectively, our data link differences in disease severity to transcriptional changes in several virulence-associated traits, including binary toxin genes, sporulation regulators, and genes associated with cell structure (Figure 2(C)). These transcriptomic changes likely play a crucial role in shaping the pathogenic potential of C. difficile RT027 in different epidemiological contexts.

CRISPR system and pan-genome analysis in C. difficile RT027 isolates in China

The CRISPR-Cas system, a key adaptive immune mechanism in C. difficile, contributes to survival within the host environment. Analysis of the CRISPR-Cas system in Chinese C. difficile RT027 isolates revealed that these isolates harbour the same endogenous type I-B CRISPR-Cas system as the reference strain R20291 (Figure 3(A)). The number of CRISPR spacers was comparable among isolates from China, R20291, and CD630 (Figure 3(B)). However, transcriptomic data demonstrated reduced expression of CRISPR-associated genes and regulatory elements in Chinese isolates (Figure 3(C)). Specifically, the downregulation of cas5 and cas6, proteins essential for crRNA processing, suggests decreased functionality of the CRISPR-Cas system. This reduced expression may weaken the interaction between these strains and their host, thereby diminishing their fitness in the intestinal environment and contributing to reduced virulence and disease severity. To quantify the entire genomic repertoire of C. difficile RT027 population, we generated characterization of pan-genome and core genome in C. difficile RT027 isolates. The pan-genome was comprising 4007 genes, while the core genomes were 3559 genes (representing 88.8% of the pan-genome) (Figure 3(D)). The pan-genome showed characteristics of a high level of genome conservation which indicated high genome-wide similarity shared in C. difficile RT027 isolates in this study.

Phenotype analysis of C. difficile RT027 in China

To evaluate whether genomic differences identified among Chinese RT027 isolates translate into altered antimicrobial resistance phenotypes, antibiotic susceptibility testing was performed in 11 C. difficile RT027 isolates (Table S6). These isolates displayed high minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for rifamycin (64-128 μg/mL), whereas R20291 was susceptible (MIC ≤ 0.06 μg/mL). Antibiotic susceptibility testing was consistent with our genomic findings that Chinese RT027 isolates harboured rifamycin resistance genes, rpoB. These results indicate that C. difficile RT027 in Shandong, China, may face broader antimicrobial resistance challenges compared to reference strains.

Given that sporulation is a key determinant of environmental persistence and transmission of C. difficile, we performed a sporulation assay comparing the 11 RT027 isolates with the reference strain R20291. The 11 Chinese isolates produced fewer spores (average 1.79 × 10⁷ CFU/mL) compared to R20291 (average 4.72 × 10⁷ CFU/mL, Figure 4(A)). While spore production varied among the Chinese isolates, it consistently remained significantly lower than the reference strain, aligning with RNA-Seq findings that spo0A exhibited decreased expression in Chinese isolates. These results suggest that C. difficile RT027 isolates from China exhibit reduced virulence factors, particularly regarding sporulation capacity, compared to hypervirulent strains. Because toxin production, particularly TcdB, is central to C. difficile pathogenicity, TcdB concentrations produced by the 11 C. difficile RT027 isolates were quantified and compared with those of the reference strain R20291 (Figure 4(B)). The mean TcdB concentration among the RT027 isolates was 23.48 ng/μL, which did not differ significantly from the level produced by R20291 (35.25 ng/μL; P > 0.5). Consistent with the toxin quantification results, RNA-seq analysis demonstrated that the transcriptional levels of tcdB in these 11 RT027 isolates were comparable to those observed in R20291 (Figure 2(C)). These findings collectively indicate that reduced TcdB production is unlikely to account for the attenuated phenotype observed in the Chinese RT027 isolates.

As bacterial motility contributes to colonization efficiency and disease progression, motility of 11 RT027 isolates were measured and compared to the reference strain R20291. All isolates in China exhibited diffuse spreading growth indicative of reduced motility compared with R20291 (Figure 4(C)). This observation is consistent with the results of transcriptome enrichment analysis.

To further assess host–pathogen interactions and functional virulence at the cellular level, we assessed the ability of the 11 RT027 isolates to adhere to human intestinal epithelial cells and their cytotoxic potential in vitro. Adherence assays using Caco-2 cells revealed that all 11 clinical isolates retained robust adhesive capability. The mean adherent bacterial counts ranged from (2.14 ± 0.23) × 10⁵ to (2.73 ± 0.12) × 10⁵ CFU/mL, which were not statistically different from the epidemic reference strains R20291 [(3.01 ± 0.31) × 10⁵ CFU/mL] (Figure 4(D)). Cytotoxicity assays measuring the effect of bacterial culture supernatants on Caco-2 cell viability showed marked variation. 7 of our 11 RT027 clinical isolates (SDJ2, SDJ3, SDJ4, SDJ6, SDJ7, SDJ9, and SDJ11) exhibited significantly attenuated cytotoxicity compared to R20291 (P < 0.01), as indicated by higher cell viability rates (Figure 4(E)). This reduced cytotoxic potency correlates with the less severe clinical manifestations observed in the corresponding patients and suggests dysregulation in toxin production or activity as a key factor in the attenuated virulence of these Chinese RT027 strains.

Discussion

The epidemiological profile of C. difficile RT027 in China contrasts sharply with its historical dominance in North America and Europe. Whereas ribotypes such as RT017 and RT046 remain the major circulating strains in China [19,20], RT027 continues to be detected at low but persistent levels. As we reported [53], 15.1% (41/272) of patients with diarrhea tested positive for RT027 during our surveillance from July 2018 to May 2020, and 90.2% (37/41) of patients presented mild-to-moderate CDI. By contrast, RT027 was the most common ribotype (23.1%) among isolates submitted to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) from EIP sites between 2012 and 2017 in the US [54], though its prevalence has declined, in part due to antimicrobial stewardship [8,9]. Ongoing surveillance in our center indicates that RT027 continues to be detected, accounting for approximately 10% of toxigenic C. difficile isolates in recent years (data not shown). Collectively, these findings suggest that the Chinese RT027 lineage may possess distinct biological attributes. However, prior investigations have been largely descriptive or genomic in scope, with a critical gap existing in the integrated functional characterization of Chinese RT027 lineage. Our study addresses this gap by presenting the most comprehensive genomic, transcriptomic, and phenotypic analysis of Chinese RT027 isolates to date, providing a holistic understanding of their unique characteristics.

C. difficile RT027 emerged two distinct epidemic lineages, FQR1 and FQR2 (fluoroquinolone resistance, FQR) after mutation acquisition events [34]. Earlier genomic analysis involving two isolates suggested that Chinese strains may fall outside these lineages and potentially exhibit reduced virulence [25]. By incorporating a larger cohort and integrating publicly available genomes, our analysis robustly confirms that Chinese RT027 isolates form an evolutionarily distinct cluster, closely related to isolates from Korea (Figure 1). This unique evolutionary trajectory in East Asia suggests possible region-specific genetic drift or adaptation events that may influence virulence, transmissibility, or ecological fitness.

Genomic analysis and pan-genome analysis demonstrated high genome-wide similarity shared in C. difficile RT027 isolates in this study (Figure 2(A), Figure 3(D)). We still identified several variants harboured in genomes of C. difficile RT027 isolates in China. bclA3 gene harbours a c.396_404delCCCAACAGG mutation shared in all 11 isolates. The function of this gene has been reported as a spore surface collagen, which is essential for the spore to enter the intestinal epithelial mucosa and adhere to the intestinal mucosa [55]. In mouse models, BclA3 protein can cause the recurrence of C. difficile infection [56]. The deletion of this gene may contribute to decreasing virulence of C. difficile RT027 in China. We also identified other variants such as a c.467C > T in slpA gene, located in the SLPH domain of SlpA protein, may have an important effect on the folding and assembly process of S-layer [57]. Recent seminal work by Wang et al. (2024) has systematically delineated the multifaceted roles of SlpA in C. difficile pathogenesis [58]. This study, utilizing a complete slpA knockout mutant, revealed that SlpA is not essential but is a master regulator of cell morphology, wall integrity, toxin production, sporulation, motility, and, intriguingly, exerts a dual function by promoting host-cell adhesion while paradoxically suppressing biofilm formation. Our phenotypic characterization of the Chinese RT027 isolates provides functional insights that align with and extend these genomic observations. Crucially, our in vitro adhesion assays showed that all clinical isolates retained adhesion capabilities comparable to the hypervirulent reference strains (Figure 4(D)), despite harbouring the slpA mutation. This suggests that the specific c.467C > T variant may not severely impair SlpA's core function in mediating host-cell attachment in our isolates. More directly, cytotoxicity assays revealed that a majority (7/11) of the Chinese isolates exhibited significantly attenuated toxicity towards Caco-2 cells compared to R20291 (Figure 4(E)). This reduced cytotoxic potency provides a compelling in vitro correlate to the milder clinical disease observed in patients and points to alterations in toxin activity or regulation as a key attenuating factor. Collectively, these results indicate that the attenuated virulence of the Chinese RT027 lineage is likely not driven by defective adhesion, but may involve a combination of factors impacting dysregulation of toxin function, with the contribution of the slpA point mutation requiring further mechanistic dissection.

All our Chinese isolates harboured the rpoB R505K substitution, a mutation consistently associated with high-level rifamycin resistance [59]. While rifaximin, a non-absorbable rifamycin, is increasingly used as an adjunct or “chaser” therapy following standard vancomycin treatment to prevent recurrent CDI, pre-existing or emergent rifamycin resistance has been correlated with treatment failures and poorer outcomes in several reports [60,61]. The strikingly high prevalence of rifampicin resistance among C. difficile isolates from tuberculosis patients, as observed in Obuch-Woszczatyński et al. (2013) [62] and Kullin et al. (2018) [63], which directly linked prolonged rifampicin therapy to the selection and dominance of resistant clones, which is a cornerstone of tuberculosis treatment. In our study, the low genomic diversity among isolates suggests possible clonal transmission within the hospital associated dissemination of rifamycin-resistant clones. Collectively, these observations underscore the need for heightened clinical vigilance for severe, refractory or recurrent CDI, particularly in the context of institutional clusters. In such settings, antimicrobial susceptibility testing, including rifampicin E-test as a surrogate for rifaximin susceptibility, together with genomic surveillance, should be considered prior to the use of rifamycin-based regimens.

Genomic analysis doesn’t reveal a large number of mutations in C. difficile isolated in China, which may support C. difficile as a species with relatively low mutation rates (approximately one nucleotide per genome per year in C. difficile) [64]. However, we still observed remarkable changes in the transcriptome in C. difficile isolated from China. Notably, the binary toxin genes (cdtAB) were significantly downregulated, correlating with reduced virulence. Recent studies showed CDT is an important virulence factors in C. difficile pathogenesis via activating cytotoxic responses and increasing inflammatory response in human [6]. The observed repression of cdtAB transcription in Chinese RT027 isolates may therefore explain their association with milder disease outcomes. In addition, the sporulation regulator spo0A exhibited decreased expression in Chinese isolates, consistent with their reduced spore production. We observed that isolates in China exhibited weaker mobility compared to reference strain and transcriptome enrichment analysis showed that the down-regulated genes were enriched in flagellar related pathways, which may be responsible for the decreased motility of isolates in China. Given the essential role of spores in transmission and persistence, this finding aligns with the limited spread of RT027 in China. Interestingly, compensatory activation of downstream sporulation genes was observed, suggesting a potential regulatory feedback mechanism that warrants further investigation.

The CRISPR-Cas system plays a vital role in bacterial defense against phages and horizontal gene transfer, thereby influencing strain adaptability and virulence [65]. While Chinese RT027 isolates possess the type I-B CRISPR-Cas system [66], transcriptomic analysis revealed reduced expression of key components, including cas6 and cas5. These proteins are critical for crRNA processing [66,67], and their downregulation may attenuate CRISPR activity, potentially compromising the strain’s ability to interact with and adapt to the host microbiota. Given the high phage density in the gut microbiota, an active CRISPR-Cas system may confer a competitive advantage. The reduced CRISPR activity observed in Chinese isolates may reflect a trade-off that affects fitness and virulence. This highlights the need for further functional studies to elucidate the role of CRISPR-Cas systems in C. difficile pathogenesis.

The dominance of RT017/018 in China and other Asian countries contrasts with the limited prevalence of the globally successful RT027 lineage. Our data indicate that the sporulation capacity of our RT027 isolates (approximately 2 × 10⁷) is notably higher than that reported for RT017 isolates (approximately 1 × 10⁵) [20]. Therefore, impaired sporulation does not appear to be a factor restricting the spread of RT027 in this region. Instead, regional antibiotic selection pressures and local strain adaptation likely play decisive roles. As summarized by Tickler et al. [68], the global emergence of RT027 was closely associated with widespread fluoroquinolone use, with gyrA mutations providing a selective advantage in hospitals across North America and Europe. In Asia, however, the antibiotic ecology differs. Xu et al. demonstrated that Chinese RT017 isolates exhibit high genomic mutation rates, a multidrug-resistant profile, and have formed stable local sublineages through multiple independent introduction events [20]. Their high resistance to antibiotics commonly used in the region, such as MLSB (Macrolide-Lincosamide-Streptogramin B) and fluoroquinolones, likely grants them a competitive edge under local antimicrobial selection. Moreover, the long-term establishment of RT017 in Asia has been accompanied by accumulated virulence-associated mutations particularly in glycometabolic and amino acid biosynthetic pathways, which are associated with increased toxin production and may further enhance transmission efficiency. Thus, although RT027 possesses a strong inherent sporulation capability, its establishment in Asia may be hindered by competition from already well-adapted, resistant local lineages like RT017. In conclusion, the limited dominance of RT027 in Asia probably results not from a transmission deficiency but from the interplay of local antibiotic practices, infection control measures, and the high adaptive fitness of endemic strains. Future studies integrating regional antibiotic consumption data, competitive colonization models, and genomic surveillance could further clarify the drivers behind the geographical distribution of C. difficile lineages.

An important observation in our clinical cohort was the classification of all cases as mild-to-moderate CDI, despite four patients presenting with leukocytosis (WBC ≥15,000 cells/µL), which constitutes a laboratory criterion for severe CDI according to IDSA guidelines [3]. This classification was based on a comprehensive clinical assessment. First, these patients notably lacked other critical indicators of severe or fulminant CDI, such as significant renal impairment (serum creatinine <1.5 times baseline), ileus, hypotension, or shock. Second, the elevated WBC in these individuals was likely attributable to their significant underlying comorbidities including active pneumonia, malignancy, and Parkinson's disease, rather than to CDI itself, as their leukocyte counts remained stable before and after the onset of diarrhea. This consistent pattern of attenuated clinical severity further supports our laboratory findings of reduced virulence in these Chinese RT027 isolates, suggesting that their pathogenic potential in vivo may be lower than that of historical epidemic strains.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be noted. The generalizability of our findings may be constrained by the relatively small sample size and the single-center design of our study. Future multi-center investigations with larger cohorts are warranted to validate our observations and explore the broader epidemiological and host–pathogen interactions of RT027 in China. Additionally, functional validation of the identified genetic mutations and transcriptional changes such as high-resolution spore purification and imaging will be critical to definitively elucidate their roles in pathogenesis.

In conclusion, this study highlights the distinct genomic and transcriptomic landscape of C. difficile RT027 in China, offering novel insights into its reduced virulence and limited epidemiological impact. The reduced virulence and limited spread of RT027 in China suggest that this strain poses a lower risk to healthcare systems compared to its counterparts in North America and Europe. However, the presence of unique antimicrobial resistance genes underscores the importance of continuous monitoring to prevent the emergence of multidrug-resistant strains. Moreover, the identification of mutations in virulence-associated genes and the downregulation of key transcriptional pathways provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying regional differences in CDI severity. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of C. difficile biology and pave the way for future research into the molecular determinants of CDI. Moreover, this study provides valuable insights that could enhance the prevention and control strategies for CDI in China.

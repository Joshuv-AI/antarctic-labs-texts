Effect of Ward Noise Reduction Technology Combined with Music Therapy on Negative Emotions in Inpatients Undergoing Gastric Cancer Radiotherapy: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Fengyun Zhao, Zhen Sun, Wenbo Niu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Gastric cancer is a common malignant tumour in clinics. Noise affects the condition of patients with gastric cancer to a certain extent. This study aims to explore an effective noise control measure.Methods and materials:This study retrospectively analysed the clinical data of 108 patients with gastric cancer who received radiotherapy in the oncology department of JiaoZhou Central Hospital from March 2021 to March 2022, and excluded eight patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria. The remaining patients were divided into a control group (CG, music therapy, n = 48) and a study group (SG, ward noise reduction technology + music therapy, n = 52) in accordance with different management modes. The key causes of noise in the ward that each patient thought were collected by questionnaire, and the ward noise, psychological state, and sleep changes in the two groups were observed under different management modes.Results:The noise was mainly from patients and their family members, call bell, monitors, treatment carts, medical staff and surrounding environment. After the management, SG had lower noise decibel values in daytime and nighttime and significantly lower scores of anxiety and depression than CG (P < 0.01). The sleep quality scores of the two groups after the management were lower than those before management (P < 0.001) and the sleep quality score of SG was significantly lower than that of CG (P < 0.01).Conclusions:Ward noise reduction technology combined with music therapy is an effective method to effectively reduce the ward noise and improve the clinical condition of patients.

Key points: (1)The ward noise of inpatients with gastric cancer radiotherapy comes from patients and their family members, call bell, monitors, treatment carts, medical staff and surrounding environment.(2)The implementation of the ward noise reduction technology can reduce the noise decibel value in the patient’s ward.(3)Ward noise reduction technology combined with music therapy can effectively improve the adverse emotions and sleep quality of inpatients with gastric cancer radiotherapy.

INTRODUCTION

Noise affects the emotional state of patients. Those in general wards are often exposed to excessive noise and activities, and high levels of noise are associated with anxiety and depression in patients.[1] Therefore, hospitals need to take certain measures to improve the acoustic environment of the ward and give inpatients a good hospitalisation environment that is conducive to improving condition and accelerating rehabilitation. Gastric cancer is one of the most common cancers, accounting for a large proportion of morbidity and mortality in cancer-related diseases worldwide.[2] Radiotherapy is a treatment method for patients with advanced gastric cancer; it can reduce tumour volume, control tumour development and prolong the survival period.[3] Patients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy not only endure the pain of the disease but also bear the adverse reactions caused by radiotherapy. Negative emotions have a remarkable adverse effect on the prognosis of patients with gastric cancer, and the main risk factor for negative emotions is adverse events during treatment.[4] Therefore, rehabilitation in a quiet and comfortable ward environment is important for inpatients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy, laying the foundation for improvement of the condition.

The hospital ward is usually crowded with people so noise can be easily produced. Repeated overexposure to noise at or above 85 dBA can cause permanent hearing loss and tinnitus, and noise is associated with cardiovascular disease and depressive disorder.[5] Music therapy is an emerging interdisciplinary subject integrating music, medicine and psychology, with a positive effect on people’s physiology, psychology and emotion; it has been gradually applied in the medical field.[6] Music therapy can make inpatients in a relaxed state to relieve stress, reduce anxiety and pain and improve sleep quality, which are all beneficial to the treatment and rehabilitation of patients.[7] For inpatients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy, in addition to their internal factors, the hospitalisation environment is an important factor affecting their adverse emotions. A good and comfortable ward environment can improve the emotions of patients.

As an important place for disease treatment and patient rehabilitation, hospitals should attach great importance to the management of the acoustic environment by providing a quiet and comfortable ward environment for patients to the greatest extent. Ward noise reduction technology can find noise sources, take measures to control noises from the source, and minimise the effect of noise on the emotions of inpatients with gastric cancer. In addition, music therapy can alleviate the emotions of patients, thus improving the symptoms of anxiety and depression. A clinical meta-study pointed out that music therapy effectively improves the mental health of patients with advanced cancer in palliative treatment.[8] Another study showed that music therapy can reduce the anxiety symptoms of nursing staff.[9] However, few studies have been conducted on the effect of ward noise reduction technology combined with music therapy on the emotions of patients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy. Therefore, in the present study, inpatients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy were selected as research subjects to understand the effect of the ward environment on them, thereby providing warning for hospitals, strengthening attention to ward noise, providing targeted improvement measures, and enhancing the wellbeing of patients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This study conforming to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) has been approved by the ethical committee of JiaoZhou Central Hospital (approval No.: 20201276; approval date: 20181213). This study was a retrospective study, so patients’ informed consent was exempt.

Recruitment subjects

The research subjects were 108 patients with gastric cancer who received radiotherapy in the Oncology Department of JiaoZhou Central Hospital from March 2021 to March 2022. Eight patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded, and a total of 100 patients were finally enrolled.

Clinical measures

The control group (CG) adopted music therapy, and the specific content is as follows: before management, the nursing staff assessed the basic situation of the patients in detail, including past medical history, disease condition, family situation and educational levels, and understand the reasons of the patients’ negative emotions through conversation to provide targeted management. The nursing staff introduced the implementation process and advantages of music therapy to patients and received the recognition of patients and their families, thereby improving their compliance. Before music therapy was implemented, the nursing staff adjusted the temperature and humidity in the ward and always paid attention to cleanliness, orderliness, and fresh air to provide patients with a comfortable treatment environment. During the treatment, the nursing staff drew the curtain and advised the patients to empty their bladders and wear loose clothing as much as possible to eliminate all constraints. They also instructed the patients to lie on the bed and then carried out relaxation training, thereby enhancing sensory sensitivity and eliminating distractions. The nursing staff prepared a number of music and then played appropriate music in accordance with the patients’ emotional state and preference for music. Most of the music was soothing, soft and relaxing and the music volume was controlled below 40 decibels. The medical staff guided the patients to imagine a better future in music to establish a positive listening experience. Then, they guided the patients to imagine the beauty of nature so that they could relax and get rid of the tension caused by the disease. Each treatment time was controlled at about 1 hour, two times per day, and the management period was 4 weeks.

Ward noise reduction management was implemented in the study group (SG) on the basis of the above, and the steps are as follows: firstly, the nursing staff measured the noise decibel value in the ward and collected and analysed the noise sources. Through noise monitoring, the noise was found to be mainly coming from patients and their family members, call bell, monitors, treatment carts, medical staff and surrounding environment. The concrete implementation measures are as follows: (1) after hospitalisation, the medical staff informed patients and their families to control the volume when moving and speaking in the ward area, reduce the volume of the speech and remain quiet as much as possible to avoid disturbing other patients. The nurse on duty needed to remind the person with excessive volume during ward inspection. In addition, strengthening the management of companionship and visitation was essential. One accompanying family member was required for each patient, and the visiting time and personnel were specified. The visiting time should not exceed 30 minutes, and the visitors were instructed to pay attention to their voices and remain quiet. Meanwhile, strengthening the training of nursing workers and cleaners, stipulating the cleaning time and urging them not to make any noise were necessary. The nurses were informed to arrange work reasonably; operate intensively; avoid speaking and discussing loudly in public places, such as corridors and nurse stations; and reduce the number of nighttime ambulations and visits to the ward to avoid interfering with patients’ sleep. (2) In view of the noise generated by the ward call bell, the medical staff analysed the reason for the ringing and explored rectification measures to reduce the frequency and volume of the bell ringing so that they and the patients can accurately hear the call and voice. When the nurses needed to communicate with the patients, they should go to the ward bedside to communicate rather than using a call bell. The call bell at night should be improved, thus reducing the frequency of use, and the cautions for using the call bell at night should be told to patients. If it is not an emergency, the patients can go to the nurse station to inform them of their own needs rather than using the call bell to avoid disturbing other patients at night. (3) For the noise generated by the monitors, the medical staff set a reasonable alarm limit and reduced the alarm volume of the monitors. Moreover, the monitor display should not be placed directly facing the patients. (4) For the noise of instruments and equipment, such as treatment carts, relevant equipment personnel were required to regularly check the equipment and repair and replace any faults in a timely manner to avoid unnecessary noise. Soft rubber wheels were installed on the treatment carts to reduce the noise caused by friction with the ground, and rubber pads were installed in the ward chairs and cabinets to reduce the noise from collision. (5) For the noise produced by the surrounding environment, patients could close doors and windows and draw the curtain during rest to reduce the effect of noise from the external environment.

Statistical methods

The clinical data collected were processed uniformly using SPSS (version 26.0; IBM Company; Armonk, NY, USA). GraphPad Prism (version 7, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) and Microsoft Excel (version 2019, Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA, USA) were used to draw the data. The data collected included enumeration and measurement data. The enumeration data (gender, clinical stages, educational levels, monthly income, tumour location, residence and proportion of noise sources) were tested by χ2 test and expressed as (n [%]. The Shapiro-Wilk method was used to test the normality of the measurement data. The measurement data conforming to normal distribution (age) were expressed as (x ± s) and tested by t-test, whereas those not conforming to normal distribution (BMI, SAS scores, SDS scores and PSQI scores) were expressed as M (P25, P75) and tested by nonparametric test. P < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

Ward noise is a crucial environmental factor that causes harm to human health and affects patients’ emotions.[1314] A study pointed out that with the acceleration of urbanisation and the changes in lifestyle, noise has become a part of life.[15] Due to the long treatment cycle of gastric cancer, patients with advanced gastric cancer are greatly affected by the disease and treatment. The fear and resistance of treatment and the pain caused by physical damage make patients generate negative emotions, such as anxiety and depression, and serious psychological problems. A study showed that excessive noise in hospitals interferes with patients and medical staff and has a negative effect on patients’ sleep quality, mental health and hospitalisation time, which can all interfere with patients’ rest and sleep whilst affecting their emotions.[16] With the in-depth understanding and research of diseases, modern medicine has changed from the simple biological disease model to the ‘bio-psycho-social’ medical model. This model believes that the human body is not a simple biological process, and social environment and psychological factors play a role in the physiological or disease process. A study showed that insomnia is a common complication in patients with cancer and the insomnia rate of these patients is about three times higher than that of the normal population.[17]

A study demonstrated that the sleep-disturbing factors are noise and medical devices.[18] According to the survey, the ward noise mainly comes from patients and their family members, call bell, monitors, treatment carts, medical staff and surrounding environment. After ward noise reduction was implemented, SG had a significantly lower noise level in the daytime and nighttime than CG, indicating that the noise reduction management effectively reduces the ward noise and lays the foundation for the rehabilitation of patients. In addition, due to the unstable emotional state of patients with gastric cancer, music therapy, which is gradually applied in the medical field, was adopted in this study. Based on the theory and method of psychotherapy, music therapy uses the unique physiological and psychological effects of music; combines the knowledge of music, medicine and psychology; and adopts various music rhythms to make patients have different experiences and feelings. Music has a special ability to inspire emotions[19] and improve moods. A study pointed out that music is a potentially promising method for controlling pain for patients undergoing office hysteroscopy.[20] The results of the present study revealed that SG had lower SAS and SDS scores after the management than CG, manifesting that the combined management of noise reduction management and music therapy remarkably improves the negative emotions of the patients. At the low noise exposure with probability of 100% and at the low noise sensitivity with probability of 100%, the probability of the variables of low annoyance increased by 53% and 40%, respectively. At the low noise exposure and low noise sensitivity with probability of 100%, the low annoyance increased by 72%, indicating that annoyance is more influenced by noise exposure than sensitivity.[21] Therefore, through noise reduction management, a noise reduction program was developed to create a quiet hospital environment for patients, with a great effect on the improvement of patients’ emotions, and music therapy was conducted. The rhythm of music acting on the brain can stimulate the brain, activate brain cells, improve the excitability of nerves and promote the secretion of biochemical substances, thereby alleviating the anxiety and depressive symptoms of patients. A study showed that music therapy may become a valuable clinical means to improve cognitive ability.[22]

Tang et al.[23] revealed that the application of music therapy can relieve pain and anxiety of amongst patients with small cell lung cancer treated with platinum-based chemotherapy and improve their sleep quality. The brain centre related to stress, especially the amygdala, may be a channel for noise to trigger and cause disease changes. High noise levels can lead to the activation of the amygdala, which is a deep brain structure that plays a central role in dealing with emotions and coping with stress.[24] The results of the present study showed that SG had a significantly lower PSQI score after management than CG (P < 0.01), suggesting that noise reduction technology combined with music therapy improves the negative emotions and sleep quality of patients. The reason may be that no noisy sound is present to interfere with the patients’ rest, thus mitigating the adverse effects of the environment on them. Moreover, music therapy can regulate the sympathetic and parasympathetic nerves; relax the body and mind; and effectively eliminate the anxiety, tension and other emotions, which are all conducive to improving the sleep quality of patients. A study found that music therapy can improve behavioural disorders, anxiety and agitation in subjects diagnosed with dementia.[25]

This study has some limitations. The results have some bias due to the research time, sample size and evaluation indicators. The evaluation indicators mostly reflect the subjective feelings of patients and may lack objectivity. Carrying out multi-sample and multicentre research is essential to further confirm the mechanism of noise management and music therapy.

CONCLUSION

The results showed that ward noise reduction technology combined with music therapy is a reliable solution to improve the negative emotions and sleep quality of patients undergoing gastric cancer radiotherapy. Its application can benefit patients to a certain extent so it has a certain clinical promotion value.

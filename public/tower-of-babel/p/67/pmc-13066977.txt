Prognostic role of lymph node-related indices and development/internal validation of a novel nomogram in colorectal cancer patients with synchronous lung metastases: a study based on the SEER database
Authors: Kexing Xi, Yingyun Xi, Youbo Wu, Gengmin Zhou, Guoxin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients with colorectal cancer (CRC) and synchronous lung metastases face a poor prognosis, yet effective prognostic predictors or models for this specific population remain limited. This study aimed to assess the impact of lymph node‑related indices and to construct a novel prognostic nomogram for survival in CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases.MethodsWe retrospectively retrieved data for CRC patients diagnosed with synchronous lung metastases between January 2010 and December 2019 from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Based on predefined exclusion criteria, a subset of patients was excluded from the analysis. The optimal cut-off values for lymph node-related indices were determined using X-tile software. Survival differences between groups were assessed with the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression was employed to identify risk factors for overall survival (OS); variables that showed significance in univariate analysis were included in the subsequent multivariate model. Based on the independent prognostic factors identified in the multivariate analysis, a novel nomogram was constructed to predict patient survival. The discriminative ability and predictive accuracy of the nomogram were evaluated using calibration curves and the concordance index (C-index).ResultsA total of 652 CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases were included in this present study, comprising 328 men and 324 women. The median age of the cohort was 63 years (range, 27–84 years). Patients with total number of lymph nodes (TLN) ≥12 had significantly better OS than those with TLN <12 (3‑year OS: 47.8% vs. 26.4%; 5‑year OS: 22.7% vs. 16.2%; P<0.001). Similarly, patients with number of negative lymph nodes (NLN) ≥7 showed superior OS compared to those with NLN <7 (3‑year OS: 51.7% vs. 21.4%; 5‑year OS: 23.9% vs. 11.7%; P<0.001). Furthermore, OS was significantly poorer in patients with number of positive lymph nodes (PLN) ≥11 than in those with PLN <11 (3‑year OS: 19.3% vs. 48.0%; 5‑year OS: 4.3% vs. 23.6%; P<0.001). Multivariate Cox analysis identified TLN, NLN, and PLN as independent prognostic factors for OS [TLN: hazard ratio (HR) =0.627, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.481–0.816, P=0.001; NLN: HR =0.555, 95% CI: 0.433–0.709, P<0.001; PLN: HR =1.994, 95% CI: 1.485–2.678, P<0.001]. The novel nomogram achieved a C‑index of 0.69 (95% CI: 0.66–0.72). Calibration curves for 3‑year survival probability showed excellent agreement between predicted and observed outcomes.ConclusionsLymph node‑related indices are significant prognostic factors for CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases. A nomogram incorporating these indices may serve as a practical and effective tool for predicting outcomes in this specific patient population.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most prevalent malignancies globally (1). Approximately 30% of patients present with metastatic disease at initial diagnosis (2), with the lungs representing the second most common site of metastasis. It is estimated that lung metastases occur in 10–15% of all CRC cases (3). To date, the prognosis for CRC patients with lung metastases remains poor, with reported 5-year survival rates of below 5% in untreated patients (4).

Previous studies have identified several prognostic factors in CRC patients with lung metastases, including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) level, primary tumor location, and lymph node status, among others (5-7). While numerous prognostic factors have been identified for CRC patients with lung metastases, a reliable tool for predicting survival outcomes in this population is currently lacking. Furthermore, accumulating evidence indicates that lymph node indices—including the total number of lymph nodes (TLN), the number of positive lymph nodes (PLN), and the number of negative lymph nodes (NLN)—serve as significant determinants of survival in CRC (8-10). However, the prognostic impact of these lymph node indices in CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases remains unclear.

Given the poor prognosis associated with synchronous lung metastases in CRC patients, identifying novel prognostic factors and developing accurate predictive tools for this population are critically important. However, few studies to date have utilized lymph node-related indices to construct a prognostic model specifically for predicting survival in CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases. We begin by emphasizing the distinct clinical challenges and biological behavior of CRC with synchronous lung metastases, noting its growing incidence due to improved imaging and its unique management dilemmas (e.g., surgical resectability, systemic therapy sequencing) that make precise survival prediction critical for clinical decision-making (11). We acknowledge established models like the Clinical Risk Score (CRS) and Fong’s score, but clarify that these were primarily developed and validated for patients with liver metastases undergoing resection, and their performance in cohorts with dominant or exclusive lung metastases remains suboptimal and less well-validated (12,13). The need for metastasis site-specific prognostic tools is explained, as factors driving liver and lung metastasis may differ biologically and clinically (14). Herein, this study aimed to assess the impact of lymph node-related indices in CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases and develop a nomogram to predict the prognosis of these patients. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-aw-2169/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

Clinicopathologic information of CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases from January 2010 to December 2019 was extracted from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Patients were excluded from the study based on the following criteria: (I) with preoperative therapy; (II) with second tumor; (III) non-lung metastases or combined with extrapulmonary metastases; (IV) clinicopathologic information was incomplete; (V) primary tumor without surgical resection; (VI) not adenocarcinoma; (VII) follow-up time within one month; (VIII) patients with incomplete tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) information; (IX) <18 or ≥85 years old; (X) patients with stage N1c. Finally, 652 patients were enrolled in this present study. Overall survival (OS) is defined as the time interval from the date of diagnosis to the death. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Statistical analysis

Survival rates were calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared with the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied for both univariate and multivariate analyses. Variables that showed significant differences in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. The optimal cutoff points were determined using X-tile software (version 3.6.1, Yale University, USA). The performance of the nomogram was assessed via calibration curves and the concordance index (C-index). All P values reported in this study are two‑sided, and statistical significance was defined as P<0.05. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2; http://www.r-project.org/) and SPSS (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Characteristics of patients and disease

A total of 652 patients were included in this study based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The cohort comprised 328 men and 324 women, with a median age of 63 years (range, 27–84 years). Most patients were White. Pretreatment CEA was positive in nearly half of the patients. Colon cancer accounted for 80.4% (524/652) of cases. More than half of the tumors were moderately differentiated, and the majority presented with T3–4 stage and node-positive (N+) disease. Prognostic role of TLN

Patients were stratified into two groups according to TLN: TLN <12 and TLN ≥12 (Figure 1). Survival analysis revealed that patients with TLN ≥12 had significantly better OS than those with TLN <12 (3-year OS: 47.8% vs. 26.4%; 5-year OS: 22.7% vs. 16.2%; P<0.001) (Figure 2). Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that age, pretreatment CEA level, perineural invasion, tumor grade, T stage, N stage, and TLN were significantly associated with OS (all P<0.05) (Table 1). Furthermore, TLN remained an independent prognostic factor for OS in the multivariate analysis [hazard ratio (HR) =0.627, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.481–0.816, P=0.001] (Table 2).

Prognostic role of NLN and PLN

Patients were stratified into two groups based on NLN: NLN <7 and NLN ≥7 (Figure 1). Patients with NLN<7 had significantly worse OS compared to those with NLN≥7 (3-year OS rate: 21.4% vs. 51.7%; 5-year OS rate: 11.7% vs. 23.9%, P<0.001) (Figure 3). Similarly, patients were categorized according to PLN into two groups: PLN <11 and PLN ≥11 (Figure 1). The 3-year OS rates were 48.0% for PLN <11 and 19.3% for PLN ≥11, while the corresponding 5-year OS rates were 23.6% and 4.3%, respectively (P<0.001) (Figure 4). Univariate analysis confirmed that both NLN and PLN were significantly associated with OS (both P<0.001) (Table 1). In the multivariate analysis, NLN and PLN remained independent prognostic factors (NLN: HR =0.555, 95% CI: 0.433–0.709, P<0.001; PLN: HR =1.994, 95% CI: 1.485–2.678, P<0.001) (Table 3).

Constructing and validating a novel prognostic nomogram

Based on the independent prognostic factors identified in the multivariate analysis, a novel nomogram was developed to predict survival outcomes of CRC patients with synchronous lung metastases. The nomogram incorporates five variables: age, T stage, NLN, PLN, and postoperative chemotherapy (Figure 5). By summing the individual scores assigned to each variable, a total score can be calculated for each patient, with a higher total score indicating a poorer prognosis. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling on the same dataset of 652 patients. The nomogram achieved a C-index of 0.69 (95% CI: 0.66–0.72), indicating relatively good discriminative ability. Furthermore, the calibration curve showed close agreement between the predicted and observed survival probabilities (Figure 6).

Discussion

Lung is the second most common site of metastasis in CRC. Multiple factors are known to influence the survival of CRC patients with lung metastases, making accurate prognosis prediction challenging (15-18). Consequently, developing a reliable prognostic prediction model for this population is essential. Lymph node-related characteristics serve as key prognostic indicators. Integrating these factors into a comprehensive model is therefore crucial for effectively evaluating the prognosis of CRC patients with lung metastases.

TLN serves as both a quality indicator for surgical lymph node dissection and a significant prognostic factor in CRC (19,20). Our analysis revealed that patients with TLN <12 had significantly worse OS than those with TLN ≥12 (3-year OS: 26.4% vs. 47.8%; 5-year OS: 16.2% vs. 22.7%; P<0.001). This finding suggests that examining at least 12 lymph nodes may improve prognosis in CRC patients with lung metastases, underscoring the continued importance of adequate lymph node evaluation even in this metastatic setting.

Previous studies have established the prognostic significance of NLN in CRC. For example, Johnson et al. reported that a higher NLN was associated with reduced disease-specific mortality in patients with stage IIIB and IIIC colon cancer (9). In stage IIIB patients, the cumulative 5-year cancer mortality was 27% for those with NLN ≥13, compared with 45% for those with NLN ≤3 (P<0.001). Similarly, among stage IIIC patients, the corresponding rates were 42% vs. 65% (P<0.001) (9). Consistent with these findings, our study in CRC patients with lung metastases also demonstrated a significant survival benefit with higher NLN. Specifically, the 3-year OS rate was 51.7% in patients with NLN ≥7, markedly higher than the 21.4% observed in those with NLN <7 (P<0.001).

Lymph node status is a well-established prognostic indicator in CRC (21,22). Numerous studies have further identified PLN as a significant determinant of survival outcomes in CRC patients (10,23). Consistent with these reports, our findings in CRC patients with lung metastases also demonstrated that a higher PLN was associated with worse OS. Specifically, the 3-year OS rate was 48.0% for patients with PLN <11, compared to only 19.3% for those with PLN ≥11. This pronounced survival disadvantage in patients with elevated PLN suggests that more intensive systemic therapy may be warranted in this subgroup. Moreover, incorporating PLN into prognostic assessment could offer valuable additional information for risk stratification in CRC patients with lung metastases. In addition, we observed that the HR for the “chemotherapy” variable was notably high. This may be attributable to selection bias, whereby patients in better clinical condition were more likely to receive chemotherapy.

Based on the results of the Cox regression analysis, we constructed a novel prognostic nomogram incorporating five variables: age, T stage, NLN, PLN, and postoperative chemotherapy. The nomogram demonstrated good calibration, with predicted survival probabilities showing close agreement with actual observations. Compared to individual prognostic variables, this integrated model provides more accurate prognostic information for CRC patients with lung metastases. At the same time, we note that the model’s C-index is not particularly high. This may be due to biases inherent in using the SEER database, which lacks detailed data on chemotherapy regimens, targeted therapies, and molecular genetics-all of which are important factors influencing survival in stage IV CRC patients.

In summary, lymph node-related indices play a critical role in prognosticating CRC patients with lung metastases. These indices offer valuable information for guiding treatment planning and outcome assessment. In this study, we focused specifically on CRC patients with lung metastases—a population characterized by heterogeneous treatment approaches and markedly variable prognoses. Our findings confirm the significant prognostic contribution of lymph node-related indices in this setting, which may aid in developing individualized treatment strategies. Thus, the results of our study provide a foundation and direction for future research in this field.

Nevertheless, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, its retrospective design may introduce inherent biases. Second, the SEER database lacks detailed information on key variables such as the number and location of lung metastases, as well as specific chemotherapy regimens, potentially introducing further bias. Third, the nomogram was developed and validated internally using SEER data and currently lacks external validation. Future large‑sample, multicenter, prospective studies are warranted to confirm and refine our findings.

Conclusions

Our findings underscore the substantial prognostic value of lymph node-related parameters in CRC patients presenting with synchronous lung metastases, offering insights that may inform the development of personalized therapeutic approaches. As such, this study lays a groundwork and delineates a trajectory for subsequent investigations in this domain.

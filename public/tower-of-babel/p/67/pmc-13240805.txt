Comment on “Resident Participation in Operative Fixation of Distal Radius Fractures: A Comparison of Outcomes”
Authors: Yogesh Pandey, Neeraj Singh, Monika Srivastav
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the article “Resident participation in operative fixation of distal radius fractures: a comparison of outcomes,”1 which evaluates resident participation in open reduction internal fixation of distal radius fractures. The use of a noninferiority framework, adjustment for demographic and surgical variables, and inclusion of radiographic, radiation, and operative outcomes make this study valuable for trainee education and perioperative counseling.

The interpretation of noninferiority depends on how operative autonomy was assigned. Resident involvement was determined intraoperatively by the attending surgeon, so the exposure reflects real-time judgments about fracture complexity, resident capability, and technical difficulty. This makes the resident participation variable partly a marker of case selection rather than a measure of trainee performance.2 The higher use of nonvolar locking plate constructs and longer operative times in nonresident-dominant cases further suggests that attending-only cases may represent a different technical category.

The noninferiority margin of 15% also deserves clinical scrutiny. A uniform margin across radiation, operative time, reduction parameters, complications, and reoperations may not map onto patient-relevant thresholds.3 For example, a small difference in plate position or reoperation risk may carry a different meaning than a similar proportional difference in fluoroscopy events.

Patient-facing interpretation also requires attention to how “resident participation” is communicated. Group 1 included cases with no resident or a resident functioning as an assistant, whereas groups 2 and 3 represented progressively greater operative participation. Separating passive presence from technical autonomy may help patients better understand trainee involvement during consent discussions.4

We commend the authors for providing evidence that supervised resident participation in distal radius fracture open reduction internal fixation can be incorporated without compromising operative metrics. Future studies should prospectively define autonomy levels before incision, stratify by fracture complexity and fixation construct, distinguish assistant-level presence from operative autonomy, and include longer-term functional outcomes to better separate safe graduated autonomy from selective assignment of less complex cases.

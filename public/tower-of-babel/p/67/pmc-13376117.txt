Marangoni convection MHD flow of hybrid nanofluid in a saturated porous medium
Authors: Waqas Ahmad, Muhammad Saqib, Ilyas Khan, Tareq Alqahtani, Mohsen Bakouri, Mohammad Yar, Arshad Khan, N F M Noor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo enhance the productivity of heat exchangers, a new type of heat transfer fluid called hybrid nanofluid was characterized. An abundance of industrial and technological processes depends on heat transfer through these fluid flows, whether in laminar or turbulent conditions. The thermal resistance of the fluid based on a heat transfer system might be increased to improve a significant number of applications. Therefore, the present investigation, establishing a framework for differential equations to examine the influence of thermal radiation and heat source/sink on magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) Marangoni convection flow of hybrid nanofluid (HNF) in a Saturated Porous medium. By introducing new similarity variables, the problem of Marangoni convection is simplified to account for the effects of radiation and porous medium. This study’s originality is the combined impact of the heat source and porous material. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations for momentum and energy are transformed into ordinary differential equations by applying the required similarity adjustment. The shooting strategy is used to achieve numerical solutions for certain modeled ordinary differential equations. Excellent agreement with the shooting technique in the MATLAB (bvp4c) code is demonstrated by a comprehensive numerical comparison conducted for a variety of variables. Investigations are conducted into specific physical characteristics related to temperature and velocity profiles. For several factors, the results are tabulated and graphically analyzed. The velocity plots are increasing with the rise in the Stretching parameter, and volume quantity of copper, while for large values of marangoni parameter, porosity parameter, and Magnetic parameter the opposite trend shows. Larger volume concentrations of copper, larger values of heat source parameter, thermal radiation, and stretching parameter features all increase the temperature pattern, while opposite trends shows for porosity components, magnetic, and marangoni parameter. The graph of streamlines and isotherms are also plotted. The numerical results were compared with previous published work.Keywords: Hybrid nanofluid, Marangoni convection, Heat transfer, Porous medium, Magnetic field, Stretching sheet

Introduction

A medium is considered porous if it has pores or empty areas that allow fluids to flow through it. Porous mediums include things found in nature, such as dirt, rocks, sand, wood structures, and biological tissues. The porosity characteristics of this material are frequently used to customize it. The extensive use of porous media, particularly in the fields of applied science and engineering, has drawn the interest of scientists and researchers to conduct further studies and find solutions to the practical problems associated with this medium. Examples of these applications include electronic cooling, thermal insulation, geothermal systems, and drug delivery in biomedical applications. There have been several discussions on reviews about the porous medium by Khanafer et al. [1] and, Raj et al. [1, 2] Knowing the importance of the porous medium. Tawade et al. [3], Dharmaiah et al. [4], Rashad et al. [4], Reddy et al. [5], Asghar et al. [6] and Khalil et al. [7] have included a discussion on porous medium in their article. The porous media increases the fluid flows surface area or volume, which may affect the amount of heat transition, whether the mono or hybrid nanofluid, the heat transmission might be enhanced when it is taken into consideration.

During the previous 20 years, nanofluids have garnered significant attention from researchers due to their demonstrated effectiveness in enhancing thermal conductivity. These versatile fluids find applications across a range of fields, including nanomaterial processing, automotive cooling, heat exchangers, micro polymer films, and biomedical technologies. Nanofluids consist of nanometer-sized particles of diverse shapes and sizes suspended in conventional liquids. Metallic or nonmetallic nanoparticles, along with their oxides, ranging from 1 to 100 nm, can be blended with conventional fluids of various compositions and shapes to create these innovative nanofluids. Choi [8] first explored this phenomenon and coined the term “nanofluids” to describe such fluids. Mousavi et al. [9, 10] used modern techniques for improving passive heat transition. Du et al. Although a lot of studies have been done on nanofluids. Recently, researchers have been interested in a relatively new class of materials called hybrid nanofluids. The two main methods for creating hybrid nanofluids are either distributing nanoparticles into the base fluid or combining two or more types of nanofluids and floating them in a base fluid to form composites. These fluids density, viscosity, specific heat, thermal conductivity, and heat transport characteristics are evaluated, particularly in various heat exchanger applications. In heat exchangers, the use of hybrid nanofluids has demonstrated promise in increasing thermal conductivity and raising heat transfer efficiency. The focus of researchers on hybrid nanofluids stems from their potential to enhance heat transfer rates while simultaneously reducing production costs. This emerging class of heat transport fluids has spurred investigations into real-world challenges by numerous researchers. Additionally, hybrid nanofluids exhibit heightened thermophysical properties and heat transfer ratios, distinguishing them as subclasses of nanofluids. Composed of two distinct kinds of nanoparticles dispersed across an elementary fluid, they have better heat transfer ratios than traditional nanofluids that’s why the scientific and commercial research communities are very interested in them. When fluid flow is impacted by a magnetic field, it is referred to as the magneto-hydrodynamics (MHD) effect. This phenomenon results in the production of the resistive Lorentz force, and it is most noticeable in fluids that conduct electricity, such as plasmas, liquid metals, salt water, and electrolytes. When the MHD effect is present, fluid temperature and concentration increase while fluid movement is slowed down. Therefore, if the effect of the flow is controlled, the flows movement from a laminar to a turbulent condition may be postponed. The practical implementation of hybrid nanofluids in systems involving Marangoni convection and porous media faces several key challenges. Firstly, the stability and uniform dispersion of nanoparticles within the base fluid are difficult to maintain under real operating conditions, as agglomeration or sedimentation can significantly reduce heat transfer efficiency. Secondly, accurate control of the Marangoni effect in experimental or industrial systems is challenging due to the sensitivity of surface tension gradients to temperature and concentration variations. Thirdly, porous media introduce additional complexities, such as non-uniform permeability, pore clogging, and unpredictable flow resistance, which can alter the expected thermal and flow behavior predicted by theoretical models. Moreover, the high cost and synthesis difficulty of hybrid nanofluids, along with issues of corrosion, compatibility, and long-term reliability, further limit their large-scale or continuous applications. Hence, despite promising theoretical and numerical results, extensive experimental validation and optimization are still required before hybrid nanofluids can be reliably used in real systems involving Marangoni convection and porous structures. Numerical computations (bvp4c MATLAB)

The boundary value problem is formulated as a system of differential equations subject to specific boundary conditions. The first step in solving such a problem is to identify the differential equations that accurately describe the physical phenomena under consideration. In this work, a computational approach is employed to solve a set of differential equations (Eqs. 8 and 9) along with the corresponding boundary conditions (Eq. 10). The numerical computations are performed in MATLAB using the built-in bvp4c solver, which is specifically designed for boundary value problems (BVPs). This solver is based on the finite difference method and implements a three-stage Lobatto IIIa formula for numerical integration, providing stable and accurate results. MATLAB requires the usage of user-defined functions such as Sol = bvp4c("@OdeBVP", "@OdeBC", "@solinit", "options") for the numerical computation. The differential Eqs. 8 and 9, which define the fundamental problem, are coded in the function "@OdeBVP," whereas the BCs that the solutions must satisfy are coded in "@OdeBC." The "solinit" function is essential in this situation since it allows the numerical process to be set up by providing the initial mesh locations and an approximation of the solution at these mesh points.

It is possible to customize the solver’s behavior by configuring different integration settings using the optional "options" function. Cubic polynomials are utilized to approximate the solution inside different interval segments. The resulting approximation is then pieced collectively to generate a continuous function. Piece-wise cubic polynomials are used for collocation in this broad approach. Fourth-order accuracy is guaranteed by this procedure, suggesting a quite high degree of precision in the numerical outputs. This framework’s central idea is the "residual." This function evaluates the degree to which the numerical solution closely resembles the real solution. During the computational analysis, the residual is employed to control errors and guide the selection of grid points by [40, 41].

Initially, a predetermined level of relative tolerance error was defined and boundary layer thickness was set at a certain level in this investigation. We are employing a numerical solver program, in order to tackle a certain problem. Essential to note is that when we run the solver, we take the first guess into account. It is crucial to remember that a number of factors affect the replies calculation times and correctness, including the initial estimations, the relative tolerance error, and the general features of the problem. This implies that, depending on the situation, there may be little difference in the outcomes from work done by others for validation or from earlier studies.

Conclusion

As we already know, nanofluids are unique liquids created by combining ordinary liquids with tiny particles. Combining nanofluids may result in highly efficient heat transmission. This is because, in comparison to conventional fluids, they have substantially greater heat conductivity. To alter the characteristics of nanofluids, researchers can alter the kind and quantity of particles introduced. By comprehending the behavior of nanofluids, better cooling agents for computers and motors may be created. In this sense, hybrid nanofluids are unique mixtures of various nanoparticles and ordinary fluids. Their exceptional ability to cool things down makes them comparable to superfluid. When a combination of nanoparticles is used, their performance can surpass that of ordinary nanofluids. Hybrid nanofluids are ideal for preventing burning in electronics and equipment because of their exceptional stability and dependability over extended periods of time. In this work the effect of Marangoni convection on hybrid nanofluid flow past a stretching sheet embedded in porous medium is investigated. By using the appropriate similarity transformation, the governing nonlinear PDEs of momentum and energy are transformed into ODEs. The computational solution of these modeled ordinary differential equations are determined by applying the shooting technique. A comparative analysis using the MATLAB integrated tool bvp4c and the shooting technique for different variables has shown outstanding agreement. Different physical characteristics related to the dimensionless velocity are analyzed, and for varying numbers of the various associated variables, a visual and tabular analysis of the temperature figure is conducted.

The problem with the boundary layer related to the influence of thermal radiation and heat source/sink on MHD Marangoni convection flow of hybrid nanofluid in a saturated porous medium is successfully completed. Limitations of the problem

The current study is limited by some simple assumptions, such as steady, laminar, and two-dimensional flow. Apart from density fluctuations in the buoyancy term, the fluid characteristics are regarded as constant. Simplified Darcy relations are used to simulate the porous media, although they might not accurately represent intricate microstructures. Approximations such as the Rosseland model, which are only applicable to optically thick media, are used to address thermal radiation. Furthermore, Joule heating, induced magnetic field, and viscous dissipation effects are frequently disregarded, which might affect accuracy in high-intensity regimes.

Future work

Future research may extend the existing model to include unstable and three-dimensional flow patterns for a more realistic examination. Model accuracy can be increased by include Joule heating effects, viscosity dissipation, and changing fluid characteristics. Further research may be conducted on Varoius non-Newtonian fluids, such as Maxwell, Williamson, Casson, second-grade, third-grade, and Jeffrey fluids. To investigate the behavior of many solutions, sophisticated numerical methods and stability analysis can be used. The study may potentially be expanded to ternary or tetra nanofluids in order to increase the efficiency of heat transmission. Machine learning-based prediction models and experimental validation may be explored to enhance practical applicability.

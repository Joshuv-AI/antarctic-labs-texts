A collaborative multi-party encryption for mitigating man-in-the-middle attacks in smart grid and energy IoT systems
Authors: Mai Alfawair
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The digitalization of energy systems, particularly smart grids and energy IoT infrastructures, has amplified cybersecurity risks, with distributed devices such as smart meters, gateways, and controllers being highly susceptible to man-in-the-middle (MitM) and replay attacks. This study introduces a collaborative multi-party encryption scheme that integrates RSA- and ElGamal-inspired mechanisms into a transactional, nested key structure. Unlike conventional public–private key approaches, the scheme employs a collaboratively generated master key across multiple intermediate nodes, ensuring layered confidentiality and decentralized trust without reliance on third parties. Formal analysis under the Dolev–Yao threat model demonstrates provable resistance to MitM attacks, while comparative evaluation highlights collusion resistance and confidentiality guarantees. Experimental results show that encryption remains lightweight, with minimal computational burden on resource-constrained IoT devices, whereas decryption time scales with the number of participating nodes. Message overhead grows linearly with the number of encryption layers but remains manageable for IoT deployments. Comparative benchmarks confirm that the proposed method uniquely combines MitM resistance, collaborative trust, and IoT suitability–features not jointly achieved by existing schemes. Overall, the scheme provides a resilient and scalable security solution for anonymous, decentralized energy infrastructures, supporting the integrity and privacy of next-generation smart energy systems.

Introduction

In today’s interconnected and data-driven world, protecting sensitive information and ensuring data integrity are paramount. The rise of cloud-based services and distributed IoT infrastructures has amplified the need for robust encryption and secure transmission mechanisms. These challenges are particularly critical in IoT architectures that rely on hierarchical data aggregation and multi-hop communication, as seen in smart cities, industrial IoT, and healthcare systems. Within the energy sector, the integration of smart grids, renewable energy platforms, and distributed IoT-based monitoring underscores the need for lightweight yet resilient cryptographic solutions that safeguard data across resource-constrained devices and critical infrastructure.

To this end, multiple improvements of asymmetric encryption techniques like RSA and El Gamal offered beneficial solutions to cope with specific critical attacks1–6. However, the relentless evolution of such attack threats pushes researchers to continue enhancing the security of the transmitted digital information. The heterogeneity of the connected entities in the network poses another crucial challenge because a considerable amount of generated data has been issued from embedded devices that suffer from resource limitations7–9. These low-end devices often have limited capacity to deal with such attacks as MitM and Black Hole attacks, which pose a significant risk to data security10–12.

IoT-based infrastructures rely on seamless communication between smart meters, grid controllers, sensors, and cloud-based services to ensure reliable operation. However, these IoT devices, often constrained by limited resources, face significant security challenges13. These devices, while essential for various applications, can inadvertently become part of large-scale Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks14–16. Their vulnerabilities make them attractive targets for malicious actors seeking to exploit their weaknesses. When these low-end IoT devices interact with the Internet and other platforms, data exchange occurs. However, this data can be breached, misplaced, or stolen, leading to improper use. Ensuring secure data exchange is crucial to protecting sensitive information. Maintaining interoperability is essential for IoT systems. Balancing interoperability with an appropriate level of security is a delicate task17–19. Security policies of these low-end devices play a pivotal role in managing data transmission. These policies must consider IoT characteristics such as low power consumption, limited memory, and constrained computing and storage resources. Proposing adequate encryption techniques to safeguard low-end IoT devices requires addressing their vulnerabilities, ensuring secure data exchange, and optimizing their performance to meet IoT demands. In response to these challenges, this paper explores an innovative encryption policy that harnesses the inherent complexities of discrete logarithmic and factorization problems20. Other recent studies explored emerging cyber threats and countermeasures in the IoT and critical infrastructure domains.

The authors of21,22conducted a systematic analysis of cybersecurity threats. The studies identified malware, spyware, and man-in-the-middle attacks as the most prevalent risks and emphasized the urgent need for layered mitigation strategies. This aligns with the threat model addressed in our proposed study and supports the relevance of multi-layered security measures, certainly in IoT design. In parallel, Aljumaiah et al23. evaluated the effectiveness of the NIST cybersecurity framework in protecting critical infrastructure systems. Their findings highlighted that most threats, including human vulnerabilities and system misconfigurations, can be addressed using a structured cybersecurity approach. Second, the scheme is designed for IoT environments, particularly those involving data aggregation across a network queue of heterogeneous devices. In such scenarios, only lightweight encryption is required on low-end edge devices to preserve their limited computational resources. Related work

Cybersecurity in energy systems, particularly within IoT-enabled infrastructures, requires solutions that are both resilient to advanced attacks and feasible for resource-constrained devices. Prior research has highlighted key strategies to enhance protection against threats such as man-in-the-middle and replay attacks. Gugueoth et al26. reviewed IoT security vulnerabilities and emphasized blockchain-based methods to counter threats such as impersonation, eavesdropping, and MITM. While decentralized solutions provide stronger trust guarantees, they introduce challenges in scalability and energy efficiency–critical considerations for smart grids and energy IoT.

Recent works in energy cybersecurity emphasize this trend. Xiong et al27. proposed a hybrid quantum-resistant encryption scheme to secure communication in smart grids, demonstrating the growing need for cryptography tailored to energy infrastructures. Kosugi et al22. experimentally validated the resilience of encrypted control systems against MitM attacks, showing how encryption mechanisms directly influence operational security in energy control networks. Similarly, Basri et al28. introduced trust-based methods for detecting MitM in IoT communications, which complements encryption by providing an additional defense layer. Finally, surveys such as Singh et al19. stress the importance of lightweight asymmetric encryption for resource-constrained IoT devices, a critical factor in large-scale energy systems. The work of Badar et al29. introduced Q-BLAISE, a quantum-resilient security architecture for Smart Grid IoT that integrates post-quantum cryptography, consortium blockchain, edge computing, and an AI-driven intrusion detection system. The framework employs NIST-recommended lattice-based primitives (e.g., CRYSTALS-Kyber and CRYSTALS-Dilithium) to provide authentication and key establishment resistant to both classical and quantum adversaries, while blockchain logging ensures tamper-resistant auditing of authentication events. Performance evaluations demonstrate reduced computational, communication, and energy overhead compared to existing lightweight authentication protocols. The proposed solution relies on a multi-layered infrastructure involving blockchain and AI components, which increases system complexity and assumes the availability of trusted edge gateways and service providers.

Existing research underscores two critical requirements for energy cybersecurity: (i) scalable, lightweight cryptography suitable for IoT devices, and (ii) robust resistance to MitM attacks in decentralized, anonymous environments. Building on these insights, our proposed multi-party encryption scheme addresses these gaps by enhancing resilience while maintaining efficiency in energy system applications. Several studies proposed adequate solutions and encryption schemes adapted to the IoT context. In this section, we discuss recent work and compare this contribution with proposals in the literature. The work of30 provides an in-depth analysis of functional encryption (FE) and its applicability in IoT environments. The authors highlight the advantages of FE, including fine-grained access control, enhanced data security, and support for various IoT applications. The study’s strength lies in its comprehensive review of FE schemes and their impact on IoT security. However, a weakness is the limited discussion on the practical implementation challenges of FE in resource-constrained IoT devices. The authors in31 proposed a novel framework that significantly enhances IoT security by integrating a zero-trust architecture with blockchain technology. This approach offers a robust method to mitigate security threats prevalent in IoT environments. A key strength is its scalability and adaptability, ensuring secure interactions among a multitude of devices. The use of blockchain ensures immutability and transparency, crucial for maintaining data integrity. However, a notable weakness is the potential computational overhead introduced by blockchain operations, which may be challenging for resource-constrained IoT devices. Additionally, the framework’s reliance on blockchain could introduce latency issues, affecting real-time application performance. Despite these limitations, the proposed framework presents a promising direction for advancing IoT security.

An innovative trust-based framework proposed by Basri et al. in28 for detecting MitM attacks in IoT networks by leveraging end-to-end (E2E) packet delays as a trust metric. Their approach relies on estimating expected delays using Kalman filters and LSTM networks to identify anomalies. However, the model assumes the availability of consistent historical delay data, which may not be feasible in all dynamic IoT environments. Additionally, the framework does not account for adversaries capable of mimicking standard delay patterns, which may limit its effectiveness against certain attacks. The reliance on infrastructure for real-time delay monitoring and the absence of cryptographic guarantees further constrain its applicability in highly resource-constrained or decentralized settings. The authors in32 proposed a Software-Defined Networking (SDN)-based framework for detecting and mitigating MitM attacks targeting IoT environments. Their approach uses real-time monitoring of ARP packet rates and IP-MAC mismatches to trigger the insertion of flow rules that block traffic at the switch level. While the framework demonstrates high-speed detection with mitigation times as low as 1 ms, it is evaluated entirely within a simulated environment using Mininet, limiting its assessment under real-world deployment constraints. Additionally, the approach depends heavily on predefined ARP thresholds and does not address adversarial strategies that mimic legitimate traffic. The method’s scalability to large, heterogeneous IoT networks also remains untested.

Badar et al33. proposed an identity-based mutual authentication and key agreement protocol for smart grid environments using Physically Unclonable Functions (PUFs). Their scheme targets supply-line surveillance systems and addresses both cyber threats and physical attacks by leveraging the unclonability of hardware-based PUFs deployed at sensor nodes. The protocol relies on lightweight cryptographic primitives, making it suitable for resource-constrained devices, and provides mutual authentication, sensor anonymity, and resistance to impersonation and replay attacks. However, the work focuses primarily on authentication and session establishment, assumes a trusted control center and secure registration phase, and does not address end-to-end data confidentiality or collaborative protection during multi-hop transmission. A similar work by Zahoor et al34. proposed a lightweight, anonymous, authenticated key agreement protocol for smart power grid systems using Physically Unclonable Functions (PUFs). The protocol employs lightweight cryptographic primitives and PUF-based challenge–response pairs to achieve mutual authentication, anonymity, resistance to impersonation and replay attacks, and robustness against physical and cloning attacks. Formal security analysis under the ROR model and experimental evaluation on Arduino platforms demonstrate improved computation, communication, and energy efficiency compared to several existing authentication schemes. The work primarily focuses on authentication and session key establishment between smart meters and utility centers, and does not address end-to-end data confidentiality or collaborative protection mechanisms during multi-hop data transmission.

The study in35presents a private blockchain-based encryption framework that employs computational intelligence to enhance data security. Their approach effectively addresses the security challenges existing E-health monitoring systems face, offering promising solutions. Their provided framework exhibits robust performance, with training accuracy of 0.93 and validation accuracy of 0.91. While integrating blockchain technology and security measures presents complex challenges, this research contributes a meaningful and valuable model for secure data encryption. As datasets expand, the proposed framework demonstrates scalability, making it a compelling healthcare data protection solution. However, their proposed solution does not explore the practical implementation challenges in deploying a private blockchain-based encryption framework. Scalability is a real concern in practice35; does not address scalability issues associated with real-world untrusted healthcare data systems.

In36, the authors proposed an efficient, privacy-preserving, traceable attribute-based encryption scheme for blockchain applications that addresses critical issues in data sharing and access control. This innovative approach offers several advantages, including rapid ciphertext generation via precomputation based on public parameters. Their scheme ensures hidden policies by utilizing attribute bloom filters, effectively concealing attributes within anonymous access control structures. The scheme introduces publicly verifiable white-box traceability, allowing third parties to identify the source of private-key abuse. Embedding user signatures and the attribute authority’s main secret keys strengthens the security against key misuse. However, this scheme represents the presence of the third-party constraint, which is not always available.

In the IoT and Blockchain integration, the authors in37 addressed critical security and data privacy challenges. This work significantly enhances the security of transmitted transactions by introducing an additional layer of transaction encryption using the hybrid Elliptic Curve ElGamal (EC-ElGamal) method. Furthermore, it employs genetic algorithms to optimize the SHA-384 hash process, resulting in a remarkable 53% improvement in hash quality. The implementation of these enhancements reduces transaction processing time by 20% and block validation time by 22%, ultimately leading to a 7% reduction in storage costs. These achievements underscore the potential to improve security and performance in Lightweight Scalable Blockchain (LSB) applications in the IoT domain.

For IoT-WLAN environments, the authors in38 present an enhanced EAP-based authentication protocol that provides perfect forward secrecy, explicit key confirmation, identity protection, and efficient fast reconnection mechanisms. Their design addresses insider threats, key-compromise impersonation (KCI), replay attacks, and denial-of-service resilience while significantly reducing computational overhead compared to existing EAP-based methods. Formal analysis under the Random Oracle Model and automated verification using ProVerif further support the protocol’s security claims. The inclusion of efficient fast reconnection mechanisms is particularly relevant for large-scale heterogeneous deployments where intermittent connectivity and mobility are common. While such EAP-based frameworks focus on secure access control and session management at the network layer, our scheme operates at the transactional encryption layer to reduce dependence on the confidentiality of intermediate routing nodes. Integrating lightweight authentication protocols, such as38, with decentralized multi-party encryption may provide a comprehensive end-to-end security architecture for smart grid and energy IoT systems.

Using more than two large prime numbers in the RSA cryptosystem can enhance the algorithm’s efficiency under certain circumstances. Several recently proposed studies focus on using more than two large primes. Implementing a four-prime RSA algorithm can balance the cost of computation and improve efficiency by using the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT); large number operations are broken down into smaller, more manageable computations, significantly speeding up the process39. A four-prime RSA digital signature algorithm has been shown to improve operation efficiency and resistance to common attacks. This method combines the CRT with the Montgomery algorithm for optimized modular exponentiation40.

Das et al. in41 introduced an asymmetric-key cryptography scheme, called the ACAFP algorithm, that leverages four prime numbers to enhance RSA’s security and efficiency. Their approach, which employs the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT), significantly reduces computational overhead and increases encryption robustness against factorization attacks. Despite these improvements, the increased complexity and practical implementation challenges pose hurdles. This innovative method underscores the potential for cryptographic advancements and highlights the need for further real-world validation.

In resource-constrained healthcare systems, Wang et al42. propose a lightweight authentication and key agreement protocol tailored for IoMT environments. Their scheme relies exclusively on hash functions and XOR operations to minimize computational and communication overhead while achieving mutual authentication and establishing a session key. The protocol is formally verified under the Dolev–Yao and Canetti–Krawczyk adversary models and is shown to resist replay, impersonation, insider, and desynchronization attacks. Performance analysis demonstrates ultra-low latency suitable for life-critical medical applications. This line of research underscores the need to balance strong security guarantees with strict efficiency requirements in heterogeneous, resource-limited deployments. While42 primarily addresses authentication and session key agreement in healthcare environments, our work focuses on collaborative encryption across decentralized routing paths. Together, these approaches illustrate how authentication efficiency and trust-minimized confidentiality mechanisms can contribute to the security of large-scale critical infrastructure systems.

Recent work by Xiong et al27. introduces QRHE-IoT, a quantum-resistant hybrid encryption scheme explicitly designed for IoT-enabled smart grids. The scheme combines symmetric and asymmetric cryptographic techniques with lattice-based quantum-resistant algorithms such as LWE and Ring-LWE to provide end-to-end security. QRHE-IoT emphasizes adaptability across heterogeneous IoT devices by dynamically adjusting computational complexity based on device capabilities, thereby ensuring feasibility even for resource-constrained nodes. The authors claim that QRHE-IoT offers strong resistance to classical and quantum threats–including brute-force and man-in-the-middle attacks–and maintains scalability and efficiency in large-scale IoT networks. This contribution highlights the growing interest in hybrid and post-quantum encryption models tailored to domain-specific IoT deployments, such as smart grids. The work in43 investigates authentication vulnerabilities in Vehicular Ad-hoc Networks (VANETs), with particular focus on desynchronization and identity-linking attacks in previously proposed schemes. The authors demonstrate that improper timestamp window management can enable denial-of-service (DoS) attacks by manipulating message delays. To address these issues, they propose an enhanced elliptic-curve-based authentication protocol that employs short-lived, pseudonymous certificates and mutual authentication between vehicles and roadside units. Their design achieves resistance against replay, impersonation, desynchronization, and privacy-linking attacks while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for mobile environments.

This work highlights the critical importance of formally analyzing availability-related vulnerabilities in dynamic network environments. In contrast to authentication-layer designs such as43, which focus on identity verification and session establishment robustness, our work addresses confidentiality preservation in multi-hop decentralized routing. The two approaches are complementary: authentication mechanisms ensure entity legitimacy, whereas our transactional multi-party encryption scheme minimizes trust in intermediate routing nodes once communication is established.

Zhang and Wang44 propose a hybrid encryption model that combines an improved Blowfish symmetric cipher with Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) to address security and performance challenges in IoT environments. Their scheme optimizes the Blowfish algorithm by modifying its F-function to enable faster execution via parallel processing, while using ECC exclusively to secure the symmetric key and digital signature. This design minimizes memory usage and computational load, achieving over 50% improvement in throughput compared to RSA and other classical methods across tests with various data sizes. The model is particularly notable for separating data and key encryption processes, thereby maintaining strong security guarantees with minimal execution overhead on constrained devices. Their work reinforces the growing trend toward hybrid cryptographic frameworks tailored to balance efficiency and robustness in IoT deployments.

An asymmetric Computing Key Exchange ACKE protocol for IoT Environments is proposed in45 that presents a novel approach to key exchange in IoT environments, leveraging the Diffie–Hellman key exchange protocol and the Subset Product problem to create an asymmetric computing cryptosystem. This protocol significantly reduces the computational load on resource-constrained IoT devices by offloading more complexity to the server side, making it suitable for practical IoT applications. The experimental results on an IoT simulation platform show promising improvements in efficiency. However, the paper’s focus on theoretical and simulated environments may limit its applicability to real-world scenarios without further empirical validation. Additionally, the reliance on specific hardware configurations could pose compatibility issues across diverse IoT devices.

Michelena et al46. proposed an intelligent intrusion detection system (IDS) to detect MitM attacks in MQTT-based IoT networks using supervised machine learning models and dimensionality reduction techniques such as PCA. Their approach focused on anomaly-based detection, training classifiers such as k-NN, decision trees, and neural networks on a specially constructed MQTT dataset. While their method offers real-time MitM detection via intelligent IDS deployment, Michelena’s approach assumes post-attack anomaly detection in a brokered architecture. Furthermore, as highlighted by Michelena et al., their solution relies on supervised learning techniques and labeled datasets for evaluation, which can limit scalability and generalization to larger, real-world IoT datasets.

Contributions

The proposed energy-based transactional encryption scheme introduces a novel approach tailored to decentralized, resource-constrained environments, such as IoT networks and architectures. By rethinking traditional public-key infrastructure, the scheme prioritizes collaborative trust, computational efficiency, and end-to-end privacy. Novelty to threshold and functional encryption: Existing multi-party and threshold cryptographic schemes (e.g., threshold RSA/ElGamal and functional encryption) typically decompose a pre-existing secret (private key or decryption capability) into shares, such that any authorized subset of parties can reconstruct a signature/decryption output or evaluate a function on ciphertext. In such settings, the protected secret exists before protocol execution, and the protocol’s goal is to enable the joint use of that fixed secret under an access structure (e.g., t-of-n). The proposed layered key-building mechanism is transactional and constructive: it does not split a single long-term private key, nor does it distribute function-specific decryption keys. Instead, each forwarding entity contributes fresh randomness that is algebraically combined into a transaction-specific decryption state, which does not exist before the message is routed. Decryption is therefore tied to the ordered sequence of contributors: the ciphertext is progressively transformed along the path, and recovery requires reversing these transformations in the opposite direction. This ”path-bound” and ”order-sensitive” construction differentiates the scheme from threshold approaches (which are typically order-agnostic and subset-based) and from functional encryption (which binds decryption to an authorized function rather than to the path-dependent accumulation of transformations). As a result, the scheme is best viewed as collaborative, multi-hop encryption that binds confidentiality to route participation rather than as a variant of threshold key sharing.

This hybrid, transactional design merges the strengths of RSA’s modular arithmetic and ElGamal’s randomization, reimagining their roles in a multi-entity, anonymous context. The result is a unique cryptosystem that enhances security and performance by distributing trust and cryptographic workload across multiple nodes without the typical restrictions or computational burdens of classical asymmetric schemes.

The proposed encryption scheme differs from other asymmetric encryption schemes, such as RSA and ElGamal, by offering a hybrid approach that combines their strengths to form a transactional nested stacked cryptosystem, mitigating computational overhead and avoiding restrictions. The hybrid and transactional design draws on the modular exponentiation structure commonly associated with RSA and the randomness-based exponent-hiding characteristic of ElGamal, but reinterprets their roles in a multi-entity, anonymous setting. In particular, the security of the proposed scheme does not rely on the hardness of factoring the public modulus N, as in classical RSA. Energy-based transactional multi-party encryption scheme

The growing digitalization of energy systems, including smart grids and distributed energy IoT infrastructures, demands encryption mechanisms that ensure robust protection against evolving cyber threats. Traditional asymmetric encryption, or public-key encryption, has been the cornerstone of secure communication in open and untrusted environments, providing confidentiality, authentication, key exchange, and non-repudiation. However, classical approaches that rely on fixed key pairs often struggle in decentralized, resource-constrained energy systems, where attackers can exploit vulnerabilities, such as man-in-the-middle attacks.

In the context of energy systems, where data integrity and secure command transmission are critical for operational reliability, encryption must balance strong security with computational efficiency. Conventional cryptographic systems operate by applying well-defined mathematical transformations to plaintext to produce ciphertext, using distinct but related keys for encryption and decryption. The security of such schemes depends heavily on the cryptographic key length and computational complexity of the underlying algorithms. Yet, many energy IoT devices, such as smart meters and controllers, lack the processing power to sustain heavy cryptographic loads.

To address these limitations, we propose an energy-based transactional multi-party encryption scheme. Unlike traditional methods, this approach uses multiple intermediate entities to collaboratively construct layered encryption, eliminating reliance on conventional public-private key pairs. The scheme distributes cryptographic operations across a network of nodes, thereby reducing the burden on low-resource devices while enhancing resilience against interception and unauthorized decryption. Formal security analysis

We analyze the proposed nested multi-party encryption scheme against common cryptographic threats. The scheme relies on collaborative key construction and layered encryption, where each participating node contributes a secret component. The final encryption key is formed by sequentially composing these components, and the ciphertext is decrypted in reverse order. This structure provides layered confidentiality and decentralization of trust.

The proposed layered encryption structure ensures that an intercepted ciphertext at any point along the transmission path remains unintelligible unless all participating node secrets are known. Each intermediate node adds an encryption layer, creating a nested ciphertext that can be decrypted only sequentially if the complete set of secret keys for each layer is available. This design inherently protects against message interception and unauthorized decryption. In terms of adversarial modeling, we explicitly adopt the symbolic Dolev-Yao threat model, in which the adversary has complete control over the network, including the ability to intercept, modify, inject, or replay messages, but cannot compromise the underlying cryptographic primitives. Under this model, the scheme is provably resistant to MitM attacks because any alteration to the ciphertext or omission of any encryption layer results in decryption failure, effectively preventing adversarial manipulation. Regarding overloading, we evaluated the message overhead introduced by the layered encryption structure. The results indicate that ciphertext size increases proportionally with the number of encryption layers added by intermediate nodes, resulting in a linear growth in message size compared to traditional single-layer encryption schemes. Despite this increase, the overhead remains manageable and justified within the context of IoT and decentralized environments, where the enhanced security guarantees and resistance to man-in-the-middle attacks are critical. This trade-off between message size and security robustness is a key aspect of our scheme’s practical applicability in multi-hop, anonymous communication scenarios.

Additionally, we have added a discussion comparing our scheme with standard lightweight cryptographic protocols commonly used in resource-constrained environments.

Although a full adversarial simulation is beyond the scope of the current paper, the formal IND-CPA security model and proofs presented provide a strong theoretical foundation for security guarantees. Future work will explore detailed simulations and empirical adversarial testing to complement the formal analysis.

Scalability is one of the most important parameters. The proposed scheme is scalable, supporting an arbitrary number of intermediate nodes. However, practical deployment requires a secure and reliable mechanism for coordinating and synchronizing among participating nodes. Failure or compromise of any node in the chain could raise concerns about the system’s overall availability and fault tolerance.

Replay attacks involve retransmitting previously captured messages to disrupt or deceive system components. While the encryption scheme ensures confidentiality by using random values during encryption, it can also be used to detect replayed ciphertexts, such as by using timestamps, nonces, or session identifiers. While using fresh randomness in the encryption process ensures semantic security, randomness alone does not prevent the replay of previously observed ciphertexts. To explicitly address replay attacks, the proposed scheme incorporates a freshness parameter consisting of a nonce and a timestamp. Specifically, the source appends a nonce \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textsf{nonce}$$\end{document} (or a timestamp \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textsf{ts}$$\end{document}) to the plaintext prior to encryption and includes its hash commitment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H(m \,\Vert \, \textsf{nonce})$$\end{document} within the ciphertext. The destination verifies freshness by checking whether the received nonce has been previously processed or whether the timestamp falls within an acceptable time window. Ciphertexts containing stale or duplicate freshness values are discarded without re-execution.

On the other hand, the compositional nature of the final encryption key provides resilience against partial key exposure. Since each node’s secret contributes to the complete key, compromising a subset of node secrets does not enable an adversary to decrypt the message or derive the full key. However, a complete compromise of all node-specific secrets would break the confidentiality guarantees. In the symbolic model, the scheme provides semantic security against chosen-plaintext attacks because no decryption oracle is available to the adversary. However, to protect against chosen-ciphertext attacks in practical implementations, it is necessary to use a strategy during encryption that ensures key updating at each stage.

Deployment Considerations and Real-World Validation

While the experimental evaluation on a PC platform and a Raspberry Pi 4 demonstrates computational feasibility on resource-constrained devices, these results were obtained under controlled laboratory conditions. Real-world smart grid deployments involve heterogeneous devices, variable network latency, intermittent connectivity, and packet loss. Such factors may influence both the timing of transactional key propagation and multi-hop decryption. In practical smart grid and energy IoT environments, communication channels are subject to stochastic delays and partial route failures. Network congestion, electromagnetic interference, and gateway-level buffering can introduce additional latency beyond pure cryptographic processing time. Moreover, heterogeneous hardware profiles across substations, neighborhood gateways, and edge controllers may lead to asymmetric performance characteristics. Therefore, large-scale deployment would require integration with routing-layer resilience mechanisms, adaptive timeout policies, and dynamic path selection strategies. Such extensions would provide a more comprehensive assessment of scalability, robustness, and operational viability in realistic smart grid infrastructures.

Comparative and benefits

The proposed nested encryption scheme is well-suited for anonymous, decentralized environments, such as blockchain-based systems and distributed applications, where preserving anonymity and eliminating dependence on centralized trust authorities are critical. In contrast to traditional asymmetric encryption techniques that often depend on a third-party key distribution system, our transactional anonymous encryption model eliminates the need for such intermediaries, making it especially valuable in environments where a trusted third party is unavailable or undesirable.

To evaluate the computational complexity of the proposed scheme, we compared it against the RSA algorithm. Each data point reflects the average of 30 runs using randomized keys and plaintext messages, executed in a Python 3.x environment with arbitrary-precision libraries to simulate practical cryptographic workloads.

One of the most critical insights, especially in IoT applications, is that encryption is the primary task performed by edge devices, not decryption. As shown in Table 5, our scheme achieves efficient encryption times even with increasing key sizes, e.g., 9.47 ms for a 1024-bit key, which remain within an acceptable range for resource-constrained devices. While decryption time in the proposed scheme is higher due to the nested, first-in-last-out decryption structure and collaborative key handling, this burden is offloaded to the receiving side, which is often a more capable device, e.g., a server, gateway, or data aggregator. Therefore, this asymmetry is a practical and deliberate design choice suited to real-world deployment scenarios.

Furthermore, the increased decryption time adds computational complexity, enhancing resistance to brute-force attacks and improving overall system security, especially in anonymous environments. Multi-route construction for reliability and trustworthiness enhancement

The requirement that all intermediate nodes along a single transaction path be online and cooperative introduces an availability risk in smart grid environments, where nodes may intermittently fail due to power loss, communication disruption, or maintenance events. The proposed transactional-key encryption addresses this limitation by extending the transactional encryption framework to support multi-route operations. Instead of relying on a single route between the source and destination, the source node selects multiple candidate routes based on the required quality of service. It independently constructs a transactional encryption instance over each route. Each route involves a distinct (or partially distinct) set of intermediate nodes and generates an independent ciphertext using fresh randomness. The destination attempts decryption on each completed route and accepts the first successfully recovered plaintext. This strategy applies the quality of service concept and offers several benefits, such:Improved availability: Decryption succeeds as long as at least one route remains fully operational, transforming the reliability requirement from an all-or-nothing single chain to an any-one-of-r model.Resilience to denial-of-service: A malicious or unavailable intermediate node can disrupt only the routes it participates in, without preventing delivery over alternative paths.Containment of collusion: Even if multiple nodes collude across one route, they cannot leverage this to decrypt ciphertexts constructed on other routes, as each transactional key is independently generated.Preservation of anonymous routing principle: No route or node is required in the routing process; confidentiality remains bound to correct participation along a given transaction rather than to node identities.Cross-route integrity confirmation: When decryption succeeds on two or more independent routes, the destination can compare the recovered plaintexts (or their hash commitments) across routes. Agreement among independently constructed transactions provides an additional confirmation of correctness and integrity, while any discrepancy immediately signals tampering or abnormal behavior along one or more routes.However, multiple route selection introduces new challenges that must be considered. The primary cost of multi-route transactional encryption is increased resource consumption. Constructing r independent routes requires approximately r times the communication overhead and per-route cryptographic processing. Additional routing state may be required to identify disjoint or partially disjoint paths, which can be obtained using existing smart grid routing or redundancy mechanisms. Importantly, no additional trusted infrastructure, PKI, or third-party coordination is required. The value of r can be dynamically tuned based on network conditions, reliability requirements, and energy constraints, enabling deployments to balance availability and overhead. In practice, r can be kept small (e.g., two or three routes) to achieve substantial reliability gains without excessive overhead. The multi-route extension is therefore compatible with resource-constrained smart grid deployments and can be selectively enabled for high-priority or mission-critical messages. The results indicate that the proposed scheme incurs higher energy consumption than lightweight symmetric primitives due to modular exponentiation, but remains practical for gateway-level and intermediate smart grid nodes. Importantly, the transactional design enables energy–reliability trade-offs: the number of participating routes and intermediate nodes can be adjusted to balance confidentiality, availability, and energy expenditure depending on application requirements. When the multi-route extension is enabled, energy consumption increases approximately linearly with the number of routes. However, this overhead is compensated by improved reliability and integrity confirmation, allowing deployments to selectively apply redundancy only to high-priority or mission-critical data.

Conclusion

The proposed nested encryption scheme is designed for efficient operation in untrusted environments and shows strong potential to address the security challenges of emerging technologies, including blockchain-based systems and decentralized applications. By leveraging intermediate nodes to form a collaborative encryption pathway, the scheme introduces a novel approach to establishing secure and trustworthy communication in resource-constrained and distributed settings. Collectively, these contributions underscore a continued commitment to advancing cryptographic research while reinforcing privacy and integrity in an increasingly interconnected digital landscape.

Experimental evaluations highlight the practicality of the scheme. Results indicate that encryption remains lightweight across varying network sizes, imposing minimal computational and energy overhead on resource-constrained IoT devices. Although decryption time scales with the number of participating nodes, the approach remains acceptable for moderate-scale deployments, making it feasible for real-world energy IoT environments. Communication overhead was observed to increase linearly with the depth of nested encryption layers, but the added cost remains manageable and is offset by the enhanced security guarantees.

Security analysis under the Dolev–Yao threat model confirmed strong resilience against man-in-the-middle and replay attacks. In particular, the collaborative key generation process prevents unauthorized interception and ensures confidentiality even in anonymous environments. Comparative benchmarks further demonstrate that the scheme combines confidentiality, collusion resistance, and collaborative trust mechanisms in ways that existing encryption methods do not. We observe that the proposed approach strengthens the foundations of secure communications in decentralized infrastructures. It provides a scalable, standards-aligned security solution suitable for next-generation smart energy systems and other distributed architectures. Future work will extend the scheme by incorporating post-quantum cryptographic primitives, validating performance in large-scale heterogeneous IoT deployments, and exploring applications in privacy-preserving data analytics and secure multi-party computation.

Future work

While the proposed scheme demonstrates strong potential for securing energy IoT environments, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the decryption process scales with the number of participating nodes, which may introduce performance bottlenecks in extensive networks. The layered design also increases communication overhead, potentially affecting latency-sensitive applications, even though it remains manageable in moderate-scale deployments. In addition, collaborative key generation and distribution, although central to enhancing trust, introduces operational complexity and requires secure initialization procedures that are not yet fully addressed. The current evaluation is limited to controlled simulations, and performance, energy consumption, and fault tolerance must be validated under real-world heterogeneous IoT conditions. Moreover, the security analysis is limited to the Dolev–Yao model, leaving resilience against side-channel attacks, insider collusion, and post-quantum adversaries as open challenges. Finally, while tailored for energy IoT systems, broader applicability across domains such as healthcare, finance, and industrial IoT remains to be empirically demonstrated.

Looking ahead, several directions merit further investigation. One promising avenue is to integrate post-quantum cryptographic primitives into the nested scheme to ensure long-term resilience against quantum-enabled adversaries. Large-scale, real-world IoT deployments should also be explored to evaluate network performance, energy efficiency, and fault tolerance under dynamic conditions. Moreover, developing rigorous formal security proofs and analyzing the scheme’s resistance to sophisticated threats, such as collusion or insider attacks, would strengthen its theoretical foundations. Finally, adapting the approach to support privacy-preserving data analytics and secure multi-party computation could extend its utility to domains such as healthcare, industrial monitoring, and federated learning systems.

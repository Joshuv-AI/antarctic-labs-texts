Electric vehicle power system in intelligent manufacturing based on soft computing optimization
Authors: Shangyi Zhao, Ming Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Soft computing technology has attracted extensive attention in computer engineering and automatic control domains because it can deal with uncertainties, fuzziness, and complex practical problems. This study adopts a Genetic Algorithm (GA) in soft computing technology to realize the cooperative optimization of electric vehicle's dynamic and economic performance. The advantage of soft computing technology lies in its adaptability to uncertainty, fuzziness, and complex practical problems, making GA an effective tool for solving complex optimization problems. Firstly, the electric vehicles' power system structure and energy management strategy are investigated and analyzed. Secondly, the improved non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) is selected to optimize the parameters of electric vehicles because of its simple operation and high optimization accuracy. Thirdly, NSGA-II is used to construct the electric vehicles' power and energy configuration, with power and economic performance as the main optimization objectives. Meanwhile, a fuzzy logic controller is designed to adjust the parameters of GA online, so that the optimization process is closer to the actual operating conditions. Finally, the relevant variables are selected to achieve the optimization goal, the optimization objective function and constraint conditions are established, and the model is simulated and evaluated. The results show that the optimized electric vehicle's acceleration time is remarkably reduced, the dynamic performance is improved by more than 7 %, and the power loss is reduced by 5 %. In addition, compared with the current multi-objective optimization model, this model enables electric vehicles to travel longer distances under the same power. This study provides a new idea and method for the performance optimization of electric vehicles. Moreover, it offers a valuable reference for the innovation and development of electric vehicle technology in the intelligent manufacturing field. This study indicates that electric vehicles could be more efficient, energy-saving, and environmentally friendly to serve people's travel needs in the future.

Introduction

In recent years, because of its advantages in dealing with fuzziness, uncertainties, and complicated practical problems, soft computing technology has become a research direction in the field of computer engineering and automatic control. As an important development direction of the modern manufacturing industry, intelligent manufacturing has an urgent need for the ability to detect and respond to events in real-time. In this context, the application prospect of soft computing technology in the field of intelligent manufacturing is expected. With the increasingly prominent global environmental problems, electric vehicles have emerged as a key direction for the transformation and upgrading of the automobile industry due to their clean energy benefits. However, in the development of electric vehicles, the problem of collaborative optimization between their dynamic performance and economic performance has become increasingly prominent. Prioritizing dynamic performance often leads to a decline in economic performance, while focusing excessively on economic performance can limit dynamic enhancements. Therefore, finding a balance between these two aspects is an urgent problem that needs to be addressed.

New energy vehicles have been developed in response to the growing global concern for environmental protection, and their capabilities to reduce air pollution, alleviate oil depletion pressures, and safeguard the environment by saving energy and emitting fewer emissions. The power system of an electric vehicle is crucial to enhancing its energy efficiency as an eco-friendly transportation option [[1], [2], [3]]. However, optimizing the interaction and control strategies among components like the motor, battery pack, controller, and transmission remains a complex problem. Conventional approaches often fall short in meeting the unique requirements of electric vehicles, necessitating new design and optimization methods to improve performance and reliability. As a result, researchers in related fields have increasingly focused on the power systems of electric vehicles.

Pourmatin et al. (2023) [4] pointed out that traditional approaches to designing and developing electric vehicle power systems heavily relied on modeling, experience, and testing. While test-based methods offer high accuracy and reliability, they are often expensive and time-consuming, making it challenging to meet real-time and global power system optimization requirements. Experience-based approaches, rooted in expert knowledge and data mining, are efficient and feasible but lack systematicity and dependability [5,6]. Simulation-based approaches enable swift optimization and high-efficiency control strategy design, provided the accuracy of the simulation model and the reliability of the results are ensured. The Genetic Algorithm (GA), known for its powerful search and optimization capabilities, has seen widespread use in complex system optimization and control, yielding positive outcomes. In addition to optimizing important factors like battery capacity and motor control strategy, applying GA to the optimization of electric vehicle power systems also allows for full adaptation to the unique requirements of electric vehicles. This application notably enhances vehicle performance, cruising range, and energy efficiency [7,8]. Banani Ardecani et al. (2023) [9] highlighted that accurate estimation of travel time information was one of the fundamental tasks of urban traffic control. Meanwhile, blurring multiple sensors could produce more accurate information to measure traffic flow characteristics than using individual sensors alone.

In conclusion, under the trend of a low-carbon economy, investigating the optimization of GA for electric vehicle power systems is crucial for achieving a balanced improvement in economic and power performance. This study selects GA as a representative of soft computing technology to address the challenge of optimizing electric vehicle parameters for enhanced performance. The novel aspect of this study lies in its attempt to solve the issue of the inherent trade-off between power and economic performance in electric vehicles. The parameters of electric vehicles are optimized using the upgraded Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm-II (NSGA-II), chosen for its user-friendly interface and high optimization accuracy. By applying NSGA-II, the study aims to optimize key parameters of electric vehicles, creating a power and energy management model tailored for them. To provide experimental guidance for enhancing the power and economic performance of electric vehicles in the ensuing transportation industry, the model is lastly assessed and examined by tests.

Literature review

In the soft computing direction of intelligent manufacturing systems, recent research has concentrated on the collaborative optimization of power performance and economic performance of electric vehicles. Researchers actively explore different optimization methods and control strategies in computer engineering and automatic control to realize the optimal performance in electric vehicles. Especially, in the application of soft computing technology, GA has garnered significant attention for addressing the optimization challenges in electric vehicle power systems. Soft computing technology is considered as an effective means to solve complex optimization problems in intelligent manufacturing because of its adaptability to complex practical problems. In the optimization of electric vehicle power systems, while traditional PID control methods and machine learning technologies have been extensively studied, they fall short in balancing power and economic performance. Consequently, researchers are increasingly turning to GA to achieve this balance, leveraging its capabilities for the collaborative optimization of the dynamic and economic performance of electric vehicles.

For example, several academics are currently exploring ways to optimize the electric vehicle power system. In 2020, Tran et al. developed a hybrid electric vehicle that matched the competition's performance parameters. Simulation results demonstrated that the design model could satisfy the demands in acceleration, braking, mileage, fuel economy, and emission [10]. In their review and analysis of related studies on multi-speed discrete transmission, continuously variable transmission, and multi-motor configuration powertrain optimization for electric vehicles, Mazali et al. (2022) concluded that the main focus of future advancements in electric vehicle technology could focus on enhancing powertrain efficiency and performance [11]. Zhou et al. (2022) proposed a self-optimizing power-matching method using deep deterministic gradient strategies. Results indicated that this approach could produce longer battery life and lower energy consumption beyond expectations [12]. Mao et al. (2022) employed Modelica to model energy systems, motor systems, and mechanical components, presenting a modeling approach for the drive system of an electric tractor. The findings supported the simulation's validity and the model's accuracy, laying the groundwork for upcoming agricultural machinery research and development. Their findings validated the simulation's accuracy, laying a foundation for future research and development in agricultural machinery [13].

Of course, numerous academics optimize power systems and resources using sophisticated algorithms. Ali et al. (2021) significantly enhanced the performance of electric vehicle power systems by proposing robust linear parameter change control tuned by GA for energy consumption optimization [14]. Based on edge computing, Lv & Qiao (2020) introduced an ideal edge server resource deployment scheme. Their research highlighted that strategic placement of edge servers could achieve cooperative optimal resource allocation [15]. Eckert et al. (2022) utilized interactive adaptive weight GA to improve the powertrain and control of a series of electro-hydraulic hybrid electric vehicles. The findings suggested that the hydraulic accumulator's high power density effectively mitigated battery aging and served as a peak power buffer unit [16]. Leise et al. (2022) applied the multi-island GA to determine the transmission ratios and other critical parameters for the cooperative operation mode of a hybrid energy storage system based on the conventional energy management strategy and the auxiliary power plant based on the equivalent minimum fuel consumption strategy. The outcomes demonstrated increased fuel economy and reduced battery degradation in the optimized operation mode [17].

Through the research and analysis of the aforementioned scholars, it has been found that despite extensive research on the optimization and energy management of electric vehicle power systems, there are still unresolved issues, such as the conflict between the overall power performance and the economic performance of electric vehicles. Hence, this study introduces GA to optimize the algorithm and applies it to the electric vehicle power system's optimization. The optimization of the energy structure of electric vehicles in the later stage can greatly benefit from this in practice.

Soft computing optimization method for electric vehicle power system

Structure and energy control analysis of electric vehicle power system in intelligent manufacturing

With the continuous development of intelligent manufacturing technology, the electric vehicle is a key component of intelligent transportation. Moreover, optimizing its power system holds significant importance for realizing efficient, intelligent, and sustainable transportation. The application of soft computing technology provides a new idea and method to enhance electric vehicle power systems. The electric motor serves as the primary power source for electric vehicles, offering maximum torque output below its base speed and consistent torque output at low speeds. It has the advantage of steady power output at high speed and can be output with constant power above the base speed [18,19]. Parameters such as acceleration, climbing ability, and other operational requirements dictate the design criteria for selecting key components like the engine, drive motor, power battery, and front and rear axle retarder in the power system [20]. This approach ensures that the electric vehicle's power source aligns optimally with driving conditions, operating within high-efficiency ranges to enhance energy management techniques. As a result, it offers an optional high-efficiency range for energy management techniques, which is crucial to increase vehicle fuel efficiency and lower emissions. The power system of electric vehicles generates a lot of heat during operation. If the thermal management system is not designed properly or fails, it may cause related components to overheat, which affects the performance and life of the system, and even causes serious accidents such as fire. Thermal management systems for electric vehicles require effective heat dissipation and cooling of key components such as power batteries, motors, and controllers. In addition, it is also necessary to design an overtemperature protection mechanism to limit the output power in time and remind the driver and passenger when the temperature of the relevant parts exceeds the normal range.

Application of soft computing and genetic algorithm in power system optimization of electric vehicles

The performance optimization of electric vehicles involves many parameters and variables that are interrelated and influence each other, constituting a complex optimization problem. With its strong ability to deal with complex issues, GA can effectively deal with such problems and find satisfactory solutions. This section introduces the application of soft computing and GA in the optimization of electric vehicle power systems in detail. This section focuses on the advantages and characteristics of GA as a representative of soft computing technology in optimizing electric vehicle power systems, and its application in solving multi-objective optimization (MOO) problems. In comparison to conventional algorithms, GA has some advantages because it is an intelligent algorithm that has been created using the theory and laws of biological evolution. GA can simulate the natural selection and evolution processes and look for the best solutions to various optimization problems. GA does not directly address the spatial parameters of a problem during optimization. Instead, it employs mathematical transformations and computer simulations to encode and solve multi-objective issues [25,26]. The power system's values in Fig. 2 are first initialized, and then they are coded. The initial population is generated after determining the fitness function. Lastly, new populations are generated by genetic processes such as selection, hybridization, and variation, and the adaptation values are determined. At the same time, it is necessary to determine whether the adaptation value of the new generation group meets the requirements, if it does, the decryption process continues. If the conditions are not met, the genetic manipulation starts all over again and continues to create new species. The genetic operations in GA consist of three primary elements: selection operator, crossing operator, and mutation operator. The “selection operator" mimics biological evolution by determining whether specific individuals should be retained based on their fitness function values [27]. Generally, higher fitness increases the likelihood of selection, akin to natural selection where physical fitness influences survival and reproduction. The weight transformation process in GA faces challenges such as susceptibility to subjective biases of the optimizer, sensitivity to the shape of the Pareto optimal frontier, and high computational workload leading to inefficiencies. This study utilizes an enhanced NSGA-II to distribute multi-objective distribution in the Pareto frontier as evenly as feasible [28]. The benefits of this approach are primarily as follows. First, rather of finding Pareto solutions one at a time, the Pareto frontier can be discovered all at once. Second, it may tackle the optimization problem without fully understanding all of the optimization problem's characteristics, leveraging GA's inherent self-organizing and self-adapting capabilities.

In practical applications, GA, representing soft computing technology, plays a crucial role in optimizing electric vehicle power systems. It effectively balances the trade-offs between dynamic and economic performance within intelligent manufacturing systems, offering novel solutions to enhance electric vehicle performance in this context.

The MOO model of dynamic performance and economic performance of electric vehicles

This study primarily focuses on the overall vehicle power demand, aiming to address electric vehicles' conflicting and constraining dynamics and economic performance. An MOO model for the dynamic and economic performance of electric vehicles is developed. A unified evaluation function is established by comprehensively considering their relationship, and appropriate optimization variables and constraints can be selected. Leveraging soft computing technology, especially GA, a series of non-dominant solutions is sought to realize the collaborative optimization of electric vehicle power systems. The output power is switched via changes in vehicle speed, and three fixed powers are initially chosen as the engine's operational levels. The control strategy further optimizes three key switching thresholds for the engine: the engine's power during point switching and the speed threshold for switching. Optimal matching of the reduction ratio for front and rear axle retarders is crucial to maximize ideal power and economy within practical operational ranges, as selecting values within theoretical limits may not yield optimal results. Eventually, a power and energy management model for electric vehicles is developed based on the NSGA- II algorithm. The implementation process of NSGA-II is similar to that of GA, but two key steps of non-dominated sorting and congestion distance calculation are added. (1) Non-dominated sorting: The solutions in the population are sorted according to the non-dominated relationship. A non-dominant solution is one in which no other solution in the current population can simultaneously outperform it on all objectives. Each layer represents a Pareto Front, and the solutions of the same layer are incomparable between multiple targets. (2) Congestion distance calculation: For the solutions of the same layer, the congestion distance between them is calculated to evaluate the diversity of solutions. Solutions with larger congestion distances are more advantageous in maintaining population diversity. (3) Selection operation: In the selection operation, priority should be given to solutions with higher non-dominated sorting levels. If the levels are the same, the solution with a larger congestion distance is selected. (4) Crossover and mutation operations: The same as GA, crossover and mutation operations are performed to generate new solutions. (5) Iteration: It needs to repeat non-dominated sorting, congestion distance calculation, selection, crossover, and mutation operations until termination conditions are met.

Due to the complexity and uncertainties of electric vehicle power systems (such as battery aging, ambient temperature changes, load fluctuations, etc.), these models may not accurately reflect the dynamic characteristics of the actual system. Therefore, the introduction of a Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC) becomes an effective solution, which can deal with these uncertainties and dynamically adjust the parameters of the optimization algorithm according to real-time feedback.

The exact value of the input variable is converted to the membership of the fuzzy set. In the optimization of the electric vehicle power system, input variables may include battery power, motor temperature, current speed, accelerator pedal position, etc. These variables are quantized to a certain scope of discourse, and then converted into fuzzy sets according to the preset membership function. The rule base contains a series of fuzzy rules for decision-making. These rules are based on expert empirical knowledge or experimental data and describe the control actions that should be taken under different input conditions. For example, “If the battery is low and the motor temperature is moderate, the crossover rate is increased to speed up the search".

According to the fuzzy set of input variables and the rules in the rule base, the fuzzy set of control output is obtained by logical reasoning. The inference machine adopts Mamdani inference method.

Simulation experiment evaluation

In this study, two advanced software platforms Cruise and Isight are jointly used to comprehensively evaluate the performance and efficiency of the NSGA-II-based electric vehicle power and energy management model. This study selects a lithium iron phosphate battery, with a capacity of 101.5 kWh. The ideal set of fuel consumption numbers and parameter values is discovered after building up 50 particles and iterating 50 times. This is done using the vector operation approach to speed up the iteration. Before optimization, the total fuel consumption per 100 km was 5.6279 L; after optimization, it was 4.0236 L and fuel consumption decreased by 28.51 %. The conventional NSGA-II is utilized to optimize the threshold of the control strategy in the APU, the power of the three-point engine, and the main reduction ratio of the front and rear main reducers.

To evaluate the performance of the model constructed here, the proposed algorithm's energy consumption is compared with that of particle swarm optimization (PSO) [29], ant colony algorithm (ACA) [30], GA [31], and Leise et al. (2022).

The case focuses on an electric bus fleet in a large city, which consists of 100 electric buses of the same model, with an average daily mileage of more than 300 km. As urban environmental requirements increase and operating cost pressures increase, fleet managers are looking to optimize powertrain parameters to improve energy efficiency, reduce operating costs, and extend battery life. The algorithm proposed here is used to search and optimize the parameters of dynamic systems globally. The algorithm adjusts the parameters of motor control strategy, energy recovery efficiency, and battery management strategy through iterative calculation to find the optimal solution under the constraint conditions.

After optimization, the average power consumption of 100 km of the test vehicle is reduced by about 15 %, and the operating cost is markedly reduced. Through reasonable charging and discharging strategy and temperature management, the battery life is expected to be extended by about 20 %; While ensuring energy saving, the vehicle's dynamic performance is not affected, and the passengers have a good ride experience.

This study's successful application shows that this technology has a broad application prospect in intelligent manufacturing. With the continuous development of intelligent manufacturing technology, soft computing technology plays an increasingly important role in optimization, control, and prediction. Especially in the face of uncertainty, complexity, and large-scale data, this technology can better deal with practical problems in intelligent manufacturing systems and offer robust support for the efficient, smart, and sustainable development of intelligent manufacturing. To sum up, this study fully combines soft computing in intelligent manufacturing and realizes the collaborative optimization of power and economic performance for electric vehicles by applying GA. The analysis of optimization results and experimental evaluation suggest that soft computing technology has prominent advantages in the electric vehicles' performance optimization.

Conclusion

This study aims to integrate soft computing in intelligent manufacturing, employing GA to optimize electric vehicle power systems and achieve a collaborative balance between power and economic performance. Soft computing technology, particularly GA, offers an effective solution for optimizing electric vehicle power systems. As a global optimization technology, GA can find the balance between power and economic performance, realizing collaborative optimization. In this study, GA is introduced and improved through the NSGA-II algorithm, which is designed to optimize electric vehicle parameters. An electric vehicle power and energy management model based on NSGA-II is constructed to address the conflicting and restrictive relationship between power and economic performance. Simulation results reveal that the proposed algorithm's acceleration time is significantly shorter than that of other algorithms, its power loss is reduced by 5 %, and its vehicle dynamic performance is improved by 7 %. Meanwhile, the proposed algorithm maintains the average optimization accuracy of 89.99 % for power system parameters, providing a robust experimental foundation for enhancing electric vehicle performance in future transportation fields. It can be found that through the optimization of soft computing technology, the optimized electric vehicle power system has achieved significant improvement in power performance and economic performance. The results of shortened acceleration time, improved power performance, and reduced power consumption show that soft computing technology has important advantages in the performance optimization of electric vehicles. This study offers novel ideas and empirical support for the application of soft computing technology in intelligent manufacturing. Moreover. it is expected to provide effective solutions for a wider range of optimization problems in intelligent manufacturing systems. The application prospect of soft computing technology in intelligent manufacturing is very broad, and it can bring positive promotion to the efficient, intelligent, and sustainable development of intelligent manufacturing.

There are still some deficiencies in this study. First, due to experimental conditions and time constraints, the optimization of electric vehicle power systems is still in the simulation stage. The prototype and vehicle tests have not yet been carried out, and the performance analysis of the power system can be further carried out in the actual prototype. Second, when the constraints hold, modeling the three-point control strategy of the engine results in the operation of the engine being equal to the steady-state process, and the fuel consumption rate is calculated using linear interpolation. Therefore, in subsequent model improvements, more accurate software should be used to more precisely check the start and stop of the motor and changes in the engine load. This can improve accuracy and enhance the power performance of electric vehicles.

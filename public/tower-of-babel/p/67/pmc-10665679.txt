Preparation of transparent hard GPTMS-Al2O3 thin films on a polycarbonate substrate
Authors: Zahra Asgharian, Akbar Eshaghi, Mazaher Ramazani, Narges Kiomarsipour
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractUsing polycarbonate (PC) due to its incredible properties as a suitable replacement for inorganic glasses has been increasing. Nevertheless, the low hardness limits its applications in more industries. Hence, it requires to be coated with hard anti-scratch optical coatings to overcome these limitations. In the current work, sol-gel-prepared thin films based on (3-Glycidyloxypropyl) trimethoxysilane (GPTMS)-Al2O3 were applied on PC substrates. First, silica and alumina sols were synthesized by a sol-gel process, individually. Then, the prepared sols were mixed at different ratios and dip-coated on the plasma etched pretreatment PC substrates. Curing of the samples was done in an oven at 100 °C. The produced thin films were characterized by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), X-ray diffraction (XRD), Attenuated total reflectance -Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (ATR-FTIR), and UV/Vis spectroscopy techniques. The obtained results indicated that the PC hardness increased from 6B to H by deposition of nano-hybrid coatings. After applying a nano-hybrid coating, transmittance was increased from 88 % to 91 % and the reflectance decreased from 10 % to 5 %. Sample with a 1:4 GPTMS-Al2O3 ratio showed the best results.Keywords: Sol-gel, Scratch resistance, Polycarbonate, Optical coatings, Thin film

Introduction

Polycarbonate (PC) is one of the most versatile materials being attractive in the recent years. This material contains unique properties such as high optical transmittance, low density, high fracture toughness, low production cost and high productivity. It has become the felicitous replacement for inorganic heavy glass materials in various applications such as solar cells, display panels, contact lenses, hard disks, smart windows and in the automotive industry. However, low scratch and abrasion resistance, and low solvent stability has been limited its application in abrasive outdoor environments. For this reason, using hard coatings as a protective layer on PC is inevitable and necessary [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]].

Organic-inorganic hybrid coatings with covalent bonds between organic and inorganic parts can be produced easily by the sol-gel method. In this group of materials, there are intermolecular interactions between organic and inorganic parts and the resulting structure shows organic and inorganic properties simultaneously. These coatings have the flexibility and adhesion to the polymeric substrate from the organic part and hardness and high thermal and environmental stability from the inorganic part [1,6,7].

Coated PC with wet chemical methods, like the sol-gel process, in more cases is better than vacuum or gaseous methods. In this process, hybrid structures would be made by metal alkoxides with colloidal nanoparticles dispersed in the polymeric sol homogeneously. Sol-gel method advantages have been proved in various industries and can open new windows to new useful materials [2]. On the other hand, nano-hybrid coating adhesion on the PC substrates due to its low free surface energy is weak. For increasing PC surface energy and nano-hybrid coating adhesion, PC was plasma treated before coating deposition. Many reports have shown that introducing inorganic nanoparticles would improve mechanical and thermal properties significantly. Also, the inorganic particle size produced by the sol-gel method in the organic matrix is in the nano scale and coatings are transparent and suitable for optical applications [8]. According to previous studies, it has been found that they have used SiO2, TiO2, TEOS, GPTMS, Al2O3 and so on to deposit transparent hard anti-scratch coatings on PC substrate. Eshaghi [9] deposited hard transparent nano-composite coating based on SiO2-GPTMS/PFTS by sol-gel method to improve PC substrate properties. He added colloidal silica nanoparticles into the GPTMS sol and then applied it on the PC substrate by a dip-coating method. For enhancing coating adhesion to the substrate, PC was activated by oxygen plasma treatment. He indicated that thin nano-hybrid coating improved PC transmittance in the visible region. The increase in the transparency was related to low refractive index of nano-hybrid coating. Shahnooshi et al. [10] investigated using SiO2/SiO2–TiO2 layers to produce scratch resistance and anti-fogging coating on the PC substrate. PC substrate was etched by oxygen plasma before coating deposition. The coatings thicknesses were about 33 nm for the SiO2 layer and about 140 nm for TiO2–SiO2 coating. Tape test results showed 5B adhesion strength of the coating on the PC substate. Pencil hardness measurements showed F and 2H for uncoated and coated PC samples, respectively. Suriano [11] used nano-hybrid coating for increasing scratch resistance of PC substrate. The obtained coatings were completely homogeneous and contained Zr–O–Si bonds. They showed better pencil hardness of 4-degree improvement than pristine PC substrate (6B). So, adding ZrO2 led to coating scratch resistance improvement. Many investigations have been done on nano-hybrid coatings applied on the PC substrate but there are a few papers on atmospheric pressure plasma etching treatment effects on coating adhesion and increasing optical and mechanical properties simultaneously. A comprehensive investigation of surface treatment factors of PC and their effects on coating adhesion and optical and mechanical properties has not been reported until now. In the present work, sol-gel nano-hybrid coatings were prepared based on the cPTMS and aluminum tre-sec botuxide to improve the PC optical and mechanical properties.

Experimental

Materials

(3-Glycidyloxypropyl) trimethoxysilane (GPTMS), aluminum tri(sec-butoxide), ethyl acetoacetate, absolute ethanol, bisphenol A, and acetic acid were all purchased from Merck Company and used as received. All of the sol preparation steps were carried out at room pressure and temperature.

PC substrate pre-treatment

After cutting the PC sheets to the suitable size, they were washed with detergent and rinsed with water. Then, they were plasma treated by a discharge barrier dielectric system operated at atmospheric pressure for 30 s.

Sol preparation

Silica sol was prepared by 4 ml GPTMS mixed with 20 ml distilled water under stirring. After 20 min, 34 ml of absolute ethanol was added and stirred for another 45 min. The pH of the sol was adjusted at 2–3 by acetic acid. After adding acetic acid, the obtained sol became transparent and clear. The sol was stirred for 48 h to complete the hydrolysis and condensation processes. Then, a specific amount of the bisphenol A was added to the solution and stirred for 2 h.

Alumina sol was prepared by adding 1 ml ethyl acetoacetate and 2 ml aluminum tri(sec-butoxide) into a container and stirring for 45 min. Then, 15 ml absolute ethanol was added to the solution and after 30 min, 2 ml distilled water and 0.2 ml nitric acid were added to the solution. After agitation, two sols were mixed at different sol ratios and stirred for 1 h. In Table 1, sol ratios and samples codes are listed. The plasma treated PC substrates were dip-coated on the repared sols (at 6 mm/s upward speed). Coated samples were dried in an oven at 100 °C for 2 h.

Characterizations

A tape test according to the ASTM D3359 standard was used for adhesion strength evaluation. A pencil hardness test was used for investigating the scratch resistance of the samples and carried out according to the ASTM D3363 standard. UV/Vis spectra were taken by a UV–Vis Spectrometer PG Instrument at 400–800 nm. Coating's morphology and thickness was evaluated by a field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, Hitachi S4160, cold field emission, voltage 15 kV). Coating structures were studied by X-ray diffraction done by AW-XDM300، Step time = 1s، Step size = 0. 05 at 40 kV and 30 mA. ATR-FTIR Spectroscopy (PerkinElmer Model Specuum400) in the wavenumber range from 4000 to 500 cm−1 was used for chemical composition analysis.

Results and discussions

XRD patterns of the GPTMS coated and GPTMS-Al2O3 coated samples were shown in Fig. 1. It is clear that the GPTMS coated sample had an amorphous structure and there were no crystalline peaks. It was reported that the coatings prepared from GPTMS sol have an amorphous structure [12,13] and the broad peak revealed at about 2θ = 20.0° proved the amorphous GPTMS coating structure. Due to the small amount of addition of alumina sol, peak intensities did not change it can be inferred that the broad peak at about 2θ = 23° was characteristic of the amorphous silica. Al2O3 peaks can be identified at 2θ = 26.3°, 29.7°, 36.7°, 39.2° and 47.8 according to reference code ICDD 00–010–0173 [14]. According to Fig. 2, XRD pattern of the GPTMS-Al2O3 coating, Peaks at 2θ = 26.1°, 35.8°, 38.9°, 44.9°, 52.3°, 56.2°, 61.1°, 66.4°, 68.3°, 76.5°, 81.1°, 84.5°, 85.5°, 89.0°, 92.1° and 95.3° were assigned to (012), (104), (110), (113), (024), (116), (018), (214), (300), (101), (220), (411), (312), (012), (134) and (229) respectively. Therefore, the prepared coatings included crystal and amorphous structures derived from Al2O3 and GPTMS.

UV/Vis transmittance and reflectance spectra of the uncoated and coated PC substrates were indicated in Fig. 3, Fig. 4. The average transmittance and reflectance of the uncoated PC were 88 % and 10 % in the visible region. After applying nano-hybrid coating on the PC substrate, its transmittance increased from about 2.5 % to 91 % and GPTMS coating showed the highest transmittance. The obtained transmittance proved that the prepared coatings were transparent in the visible region. Maximum transmittance of antireflection coatings decreased with acid-catalyzed silica weight ratio. The precise transmittance amount can be measured by Fresnel equations [15]. Uncoated PC reflectance as indicated in Fig. 4 was equal to 10 % and decreased to 5 % after applying nano-hybrid coating. Z4 with silica to alumina sol ratio equal to 4:1 showed minimum reflectance. Z5 sample showed higher reflectance. Because GPTMS and alumina refractive indices were equal to 1.3 and 1.9, respectively. By adding alumina to the GPTMS sol reflectance was increased. Thus, the enhancement of optical transparency of the nanohybrid coatings can be related to low refractive index. This fact finally led to decrease the reflection of the incident light and increase the transmittance.

ATR-FTIR spectra of the uncoated PC, and coated PC with GPTMS and Z2 were indicated in Fig. 5. The emerged bands at 960 and 750 cm−1 can be attributed to the epoxy ring [16]. The epoxy band intensity was decreased after adding alumina that showed an interaction between the epoxy ring and alumina and cross-linking. The intense band at 1100 cm−1 was related to the Si–O–Si and Si–OH groups. The C–O vibrational band of the epoxy group from the GPTMS emerged at 916 cm−1. Alumina cross-linked with the silica network and some of the Si–O–Si bonds were replaced Si–O–Al bonds that resulted in improved adhesion [16]. 1639 cm−1 was attributed to adsorbed water. The band at 3142, 3657, 3468, and 354 cm−1 were related to the OH group. Al–OH bond was indicated at 1028 cm−1 and adsorbed water by the sample was presented at 3441 cm−1 [17].

It can be concluded from the FE-SEM images that the GP2, Z2, and Z4 were applied on the substrate surfaces as well as possible and there were no flaking or delamination, phase separation, and cracking and the coatings were homogenous and smooth. The Tape test and pencil hardness results were listed in Table 2. It can be found that the uncoated PC pencil hardness was equal to 6B and after applying GPTMS increased to 4B and after applying GPTMS-alumina nano-hybrid coatings increased to H. It was reported that hardness increased by polyhedral oligomeric silsesquioxane condensation. In these samples, inorganic oligomers were almost closed polyhedrons that after organic polymerization bonded covalently and became very hard [18]. Obtained results confirmed that nano-hybrid coatings had good adhesion to the substrate (5B) and there was no coating delamination or flaking. Low-pressure plasma treatment used for activating the substrates was safe for polymeric materials and led to increasing free surface energy and good adhesion and formation of covalent bonding between coating and substrate [11]. The silica coating's good adhesion may result from linear chain structures that bonded strongly to the substrate [19]. It was shown that the hardness of the hybrid coatings was increased by alumina nanoparticles [20].

Conclusion

In the current paper, nano-hybrid silica-alumina coatings were coated successfully on the PC substrate. All amorphous coatings were homogenous and smooth. The pencil hardness and adhesion strength of the coatings were equal to H and 5B, respectively. Adding alumina resulted in accelerating the opening epoxy ring process. PC transmittance increased to 91 % and reflectance decreased to 5 %. The Z4 sample (silica to alumina sol ratio equal to 4:1) was introduced as the best sample. So, it can be concluded that nano-hybrid coating based on the GPTMS and alumina can be used as a hard transparent coating for PC in solar cell, automotive and optical lens applications.

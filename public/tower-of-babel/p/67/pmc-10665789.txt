Epidemiological characteristics and therapeutic advances of EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations in non‐small cell lung cancer
Authors: Binyang Pan, Jiaqi Liang, Haochun Shi, Kungeng Rao, Weigang Guo, Cheng Zhan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe third most prevalent type of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation, EGFR exon 20 insertions (EGFRex20ins), involves 2%–12% of all cases of EGFR‐positive non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Approximately 90% of the mutations occur within the loop structure region, and the most frequently reported subtypes are A767_V769dup and S768_D770dup, which together account for almost 50% of instances. Apart from the unique subtype of A763_Y764insFQEA, NSCLCs with EGFRex20ins are resistant to approved EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and are also insensitive to chemotherapy or immunotherapy. A new modality of treatment for NSCLC patients with EGFRx20ins has been established with the approval of mobocertinib and amivantamab. There are also numerous novel targeted treatments for NSCLC with EGFRex20ins in development, which are anticipated to improve this patient population's survival even further. This review provides a reference for the clinical management of these patients by summarizing the most recent epidemiological, and clinicopathological characteristics, diagnostic techniques, and therapeutic advances of EGFRex20ins in NSCLC.Keywords: EGFR exon 20 insertions, epidemiology, non‐small lung cancer, targeted therapy

INTRODUCTION

Non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer‐related deaths worldwide. 1 While driver gene mutations, including the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), have been extensively reported in NSCLC patients, EGFR exon 20 insertions (EGFRex20ins) are the third most frequent type of EGFR mutations, following exon 21 point mutations L858R and exon 19 deletions. 2 , 3 , 4 The majority of patients with EGFRex20ins, in contrast to those with classical EGFR mutations, are highly resistant to current EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), classical chemotherapy, and immunotherapy, and their survival is quite unsatisfactory, 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 which emphasizes the urgent need for alternative treatment approaches for this subset of patients. 3 , 7 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 In this study, we review the epidemiology, clinicopathological characteristics, detection strategies, and clinical status of EGFRex20ins‐mutant NSCLC patients by aggregating published literature, conference reports, and clinical trial findings. To offer clinical practitioners and researchers fresh insights and views, we also highlight recently developed targeted therapies for EGFRex20ins mutations that have been approved or are being studied.

STRUCTURE AND FUNCTION OF EGFREX20INS

EGFR belongs to the ErbB receptor family, a subgroup of the receptor tyrosine kinase superfamily. 15 EGFR plays a crucial role in multiple cellular processes, including cell proliferation, motility, survival, and differentiation. 15 Typically, it is activated upon binding to various ligands, such as EGF, transforming growth factor α (TGFα), amphiregulin (AREG), epigen, β‐cellulin, heparin‐binding EGF (HB‐EGF), and epiregulin. Upon ligand binding, the C‐terminal tail of the receptor is phosphorylated on tyrosine residues, which enables interactions with downstream effectors such as Shc1 and Grb2, ultimately regulating cellular processes through pathways like phosphatidylinositol 3‐kinase (PI3K)/AKT and mitogen‐activated protein kinase (MAPK). 16

In the development of human cancers, the aberrant activation of EGFR plays a significant role in driving excessive cell proliferation and differentiation. 17 Several mechanisms can disrupt the tightly regulated EGFR signaling. One prominent example is seen in NSCLC with EGFR classical mutations (19 deletions and L858R), which lead to constitutive activation of EGFR independent of ligand binding, accounting for the most two prevalent mutations in NSCLC. 15 These mutations tend to alter the equilibrium within the kinase domain, favoring an active conformation, and effectively acting as a “superacceptor” in asymmetric kinase dimers. 17

In contrast, EGFRex20ins, the third most common mutation in NSCLC, is often characterized by 3–21 base pair in‐frame insertions or duplications between amino acid (AA) 761 and AA775. 18 While this mutation also occurs in the kinase domain, it presents unique challenges for targeted drug development due to the distinctive “αC‐in” constitutive conformation and the significantly expanded space in the ATP‐binding pocket, creating substantial steric hindrance. 19 Structurally, exon 20 of EGFR spans AA 762–823, encoding the tyrosine kinase domain, and contains the regulatory αC helix (AA762‐766) and adjacent loop (AA767‐774). 7 , 19 Based on the insertion site and the amino acid sequence, this mutation can be further categorized into three categories: insertions in the αC helix, insertions occurring in the C‐terminal near‐loop (767–772) of the αC helix (Ex20ins‐NL), and the insertions in the far‐loop (773–775, Ex20ins‐FL). 20 Different insertion sites have been reported to be associated with varying sensitivity to epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR‐TKIs). 19 , 21 Proportionally, the major insertion sites are reported to be at AA769, AA770, and AA773, with over 90% of insertions occurring within the loop structure, with the Ex20ins‐NL being the most frequent (approximately 70%). 4 , 16 , 22 , 23 The most prevalent subtypes of EGFRex20ins mutation are A767_V769dup, followed by S768_D770dup, which together account for nearly 50% of all EGFRex20ins mutations. These are N771_H773dup and A763_Y764insFQEA, with a frequency of 5%–10%. 6 , 7 , 24 , 25

Detection techniques of EGFRex20ins

Due to the low incidence and great heterogeneity of EGFRex20ins, reliable diagnosis of this population of patients is critical for directing treatment. Today, clinical diagnostics mainly rely on polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and next‐generation sequencing (NGS). 13 While PCR demonstrates high specificity, it is limited to detecting just known mutant subtypes. As a result, the ability of PCR to discover low‐frequency uncommon mutant variants can be significantly constrained. 7 , 24 , 26 , 27 In contrast, NGS with high sensitivity and specificity can reveal both known and unknown genetic mutations, especially for individuals carrying rare EGFRex20ins subtypes. Hence, NGS‐based assays are preferable. Studies have indicated that PCR‐based assays can only identify about 11.8%–58.9% of EGFRex20ins mutations detected by NGS, indicating that more than 40% of such patients may be missed by PCR‐based assays. 20 , 22 , 26 Given the better depth and breadth of NGS detection, it has been widely used in clinical practice in recent years, perhaps accounting for the increased detection frequency of EGFRex20ins mutations in recent years. 23

It is worth mentioning that collecting tissue samples acceptable for molecular testing is difficult in a considerable proportion of advanced NSCLC patients (approximately 30%). 25 Thus, liquid biopsy based on circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) in plasma is also of tremendous value. Yet, one of the main challenges of liquid biopsy technology for NSCLC patients is how to use high‐sensitivity techniques to avoid false‐negative hazards while limiting false‐positive outcomes. It has been shown that PCR‐based approaches such as qPCR or digital PCR have significantly lower sensitivity compared to NGS in liquid biopsy, whereas the specificity of both detection methods is around 90%. 25

Epidemiology of EGFRex20ins in NSCLC

According to the mutation data of 58 756 NSCLCs in the latest release of the Catalog of Somatic Mutations in Cancer (COSMIC) v97, 28 the incidence of EGFRex20ins was 0.46% (238/51855), 0.07% (4/6118), and 0% (0/783) in adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma, respectively. 28 It has also previously been reported in EGFR‐mutant NSCLCs that the prevalence of EGFRex20ins was 1.49% (238/15959) in adenocarcinoma, 0.97% (4/414) in squamous cell carcinoma, and 0% (0/35) in large cell carcinoma. 28 Mack et al. performed comprehensive genomic testing on plasma samples from 8388 patients with advanced NSCLCs and found that EGFRex20ins mutations accounted for 1.2% of NSCLCs and 4.5% of EGFR mutations, respectively. 29 In addition, a meta‐analysis involving 80 high‐quality studies found an incidence of EGFRex20ins in all NSCLCs of 0.7% (0.4%–1.1%), 6.1% (4.0%–9.4%) in all EGFR‐mutant NSCLCs. 30 In other large study cohorts, the prevalence of NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins among EGFR‐mutant patients ranges from 1.6% to 12%. 4 , 6 , 7 , 31 , 32 In terms of racial differences, the Asia Pacific region is considered to have the highest mutation frequency, although there is currently a lack of high‐quality meta‐analyses specific to the Asia Pacific population. Several large cohort studies have indicated that the mutation frequency of EGFRex20ins in this region is slightly higher than 2% among any NSCLCs. 6 , 12 , 33 In the European region, a meta‐analysis conducted across nine major European countries showed that EGFRex20ins mutations accounted for 0.7% of all NSCLC cases (95% CI: 0.4, 1.1), which is lower than in the Asia Pacific region. 30 As for the United States (US), the mutation frequency is currently less clear due to a lack of large‐sample research evidence. There have been reports indicating a range of 0.5% to 2.6% for EGFRexon20 mutations in the US region. 34

The mutation exhibited varying prevalence across different demographic and clinical characteristics. Notably, it was more common in women, with a frequency of 47% among EGFRex20ins cases compared to 28% in EGFR wild‐type (WT) cases (p = 0.003). 11 Never‐smokers displayed a significantly higher occurrence of the mutation, accounting for 56% of patients with EGFRex20ins compared to only 20% in EGFR WT cases (p < 0.0001). 36 Furthermore, patients harboring EGFRex20ins were significantly younger than those with classical mutations (median age 60 vs. 66 years, p = 0.017). 10 All reports consistently confirmed that adenocarcinoma is the predominant histological subtype associated with this mutation variant (from 77% to 100%). 6 , 7 , 33 Moreover, patients with EGFRex20ins generally exhibit a poorer prognosis. Multiple reports have indicated that the median OS for these patients falls within the range of 4.8–16.8 months, which is nearly half as long as the OS observed in patients with classical mutations, ranging from 17.3 to 31.6 months. 37 , 38 , 39 , 40 In terms of metastatic distribution, bone metastasis (21.6%) emerged as the most prevalent site in individuals with EGFRex20ins, followed by central nervous system (CNS) metastases (13.0%) and liver involvement (17.4%). 10 Patients with intrathoracic metastases were more frequently observed among those with EGFRex20ins (52.2%) compared to individuals with classical mutations (35.2%), although these differences did not attain statistical significance. 10

According to the reports from the majority of studies, EGFRex20ins and EGFR classical mutations are highly mutually exclusive. 6 , 7 , 12 , 33 , 41 However, a study focused on Hispanics showed that up to 36.4% of patients with EGFRex20ins also carried classical mutations. 35 It is worth noting that in this study, none of the patients were found to have tumor protein P53 (TP53) mutation, which is in contrast to several other studies where TP53 had the highest comutation frequency with EGFRex20ins, ranging from 35% to 65%. 35 Additionally, 16.6%–37.9% of patients were found to have EGFR amplification, and a significant proportion had comutations in tumor suppressor genes such as retinoblastoma protein 1 (RB1), cyclin‐dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (CDKN2A/B), as shown in Table 1. 6 , 7 , 12 , 33 , 35 Comprehensive genomic analysis has shown that TP53 mutation frequently co‐occurs with H773dup and A767_V769dup, while RB1 mutation commonly co‐occurs with H773_V774insAH, suggesting that different subtypes of EGFRex20ins mutation may require distinct genomic backgrounds to drive tumorigenesis and maintenance. 6 , 7 In patients with NSCLC harboring EGFRex20ins, approximately 75% exhibit negative expression of programmed death ligand 1 (PD‐L1) and a lower average tumor mutational burden (TMB) of approximately four mutations per megabase. While there have been reports suggesting that TP53 mutations may be related to TKI resistance, the unique genomic profiling of EGFRex20ins and its correlation with treatment efficacy and prognosis and the underlying mechanisms may require further exploration. 42 , 43 , 44

CLINICAL EFFICACY OF CONVENTIONAL EGFR‐TKI, CHEMOTHERAPY, AND IMMUNOTHERAPY IN EGFREX20INS POSITIVE NSCLC PATIENTS

Classical EGFR‐TKI

EGFRex20ins mutations produce changes in the drug‐binding pocket, resulting in steric hindrance and the inability of most EGFR‐TKIs to bind effectively. 45 As indicated in Table 2, successive generations of EGFR‐TKIs, including erlotinib, afatinib, gefitinib, and osimertinib, display exceedingly poor effectiveness in NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins mutations. According to a meta‐analysis enrolled 8 studies and 206 pooled patients, first‐line treatment with EGFR‐TKIs resulted in a pooled objective response rate (ORR) of just 6.8% and a pooled progression‐free survival (PFS) of 3 months. 14 Relative to classical EGFR mutations, patients with advanced‐stage NSCLC harboring EGFRex20ins mutations have considerably shorter PFS (2.9 vs. 10.5 months, p < 0.001) and worse 5‐year survival rates (8% vs. 19%) after treatment with first‐generation TKIs. 53 While third‐generation TKI osimertinib has shown promising efficacy for EGFRex20ins mutations in a small Chinese cohort study (n = 53), another study enrolled 62 Chinese NSCLC patients with different subtypes of EGFRex20ins mutations reported an ORR of only 6.5% and a PFS as short as 2.3 months. 46 , 54

Nonetheless, EGFRex20ins mutations demonstrate considerable variation in drug sensitivity due to the multiplicity of insertion sites. Despite that the sensitivity of loop insertions to EGFR‐TKIs is reduced due to the increased steric hindrance of the drug‐binding pocket, ex20ins‐NL were observed to exhibit higher sensitivity to second‐generation and ex20ins‐active TKIs as compared to ex20ins‐FL in preclinical studies, consistent with relevant clinical studies. 21 , 46 , 55 , 56 , 57 In addition, multiple investigations have demonstrated that a particular subtype, A763_Y764insFQEA, is extremely responsive to various conventional EGFR‐TKIs including osimertinib. 6 , 31 , 46 At a structural and enzyme kinetic level, A763_Y764insFQEA is an insertion into αC helix which may more closely resemble the L858R and exon 19 deletion mutants than other EGFRex20ins mutations. 19 , 21 Consequently, it is categorized as a classical‐like mutation located distantly from the ATP‐binding pocket. 21 In summary, the majority of NSCLC cases with EGFRex20ins mutations exhibit extremely low sensitivity to classical TKIs, with an ORR of approximately 10% and a PFS of no more than 3 months. The outcomes of EGFRex20ins tumors vary based on the specific insertion site and content, highlighting the importance of personalized treatment strategies for these patients.

Chemotherapy

The present study demonstrates that platinum‐based chemotherapy provides higher efficacy than conventional TKIs in both first‐ and second‐line treatment for patients with EGFRex20ins‐positive NSCLC. 12 , 17 , 31 , 48 , 50 Hence, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines prescribe platinum‐based doublet chemotherapy as the standard first‐line treatment for advanced NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins. 12 , 13 According to the Flatiron Health electronic health record database as of February 2020, chemotherapy and chemotherapy‐containing regimens are the most often utilized first‐line treatments for EGFR exon 20 insertion‐positive NSCLC, accounting for 56.6% of all treatment options. 51

The efficacy of chemotherapy can be found in Table 2. Yet, a systematic literature review based on real‐world evidence has indicated that individuals with NSCLC harboring EGFRex20ins only modestly benefit from current standard‐of‐care therapies. In the first‐line setting, treatment with platinum doublet chemotherapy leads to a pooled PFS of 5.6 months and a pooled ORR of 24.6%. 14 In a cohort of 57 patients, the ORR was close to 20%, with a PFS of 4.5–5.7 months. 51 Furthermore, a Chinese retrospective analysis indicated that among 104 NSCLC patients who received first‐line platinum‐based chemotherapy (84.6% received pemetrexed/platinum‐based regimens), the ORR was 19.2% and the PFS was 6.4 months. 12

Of note, there are many chemotherapy regimens currently available combined with platinum, principally including pemetrexed, paclitaxel, docetaxel, albumin‐bound paclitaxel, and gemcitabine, but some studies have suggested that platinum/pemetrexed‐based regimens may lead to improved outcomes. 48 As established by Wu and colleagues, pemetrexed‐containing therapies were associated with superior PFS (6.2 vs. 2.7 months; p < 0.001) and overall survival (OS) (28.0 vs. 15.4 months; p = 0.009) compared to regimens lacking pemetrexed. 31 , 48

In conclusion, the use of chemotherapy yields an ORR of around 20% and a PFS of approximately 6 months. This represents a significant advantage compared to classical TKIs, with an almost two‐fold increase in efficacy.

Immunotherapy

The efficacy of immunotherapy in this subset of patients is generally unsatisfactory, and EGFRex20ins‐positive individuals are found to be less sensitive to immunotherapy compared to NSCLC patients lacking driver oncogenic mutations. 5 , 58 Data from the Flatiron Health electronic health database showed that as first‐line therapy, the ORR of immunotherapy monotherapy was 9.1%, PFS was 3.1 months, and OS was 11.0 months, while the ORR of immunotherapy combined with platinum‐based chemotherapy was 18.8%, PFS was 4.5 months, and OS was 11.3 months. 51

Owing to inadequate efficacy as a first‐line treatment, immunotherapy is widely utilized as a second‐line treatment for EGFRex20ins‐positive patients, and it has been reported that roughly 30% of patients receive immunotherapy as the most commonly used medication in the second‐line therapy. 5 However, as second‐line or later therapy, the ORR of immunotherapy was only 3.1%, the PFS was 2.3 months, and the OS was 8.1 months. 51 A retrospective real‐world study in China evaluated the feasibility of combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy; however, the findings showed that combination therapy did not significantly enhance PFS compared to chemotherapy alone, regardless of PD‐L1 expression and TMB. 52 However, there was also a retrospective report suggesting that NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins may benefit more from immunotherapy compared to those with EGFR‐sensitizing mutations. 59 Since NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins are generally excluded from clinical trials exploring immunotherapy, further convincing evidence is needed addressing the efficacy of immunotherapy in this patient population.

In summary, monotherapy with immunotherapy demonstrates efficacy comparable to classical KIs. When combined with chemotherapy, it may even show improved efficacy. However, whether this combination therapy outperforms chemotherapy alone requires further validation through clinical trials and research.

Emerging therapeutic strategies for NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins

As previously discussed, EGFRex20ins‐positive NSCLC patients have considerable obstacles in treatment, with minimal benefits from EGFR‐TKIs, chemotherapy, and immunotherapies, leading to poor survival results. There is an urgent need for innovative targeted therapy for these patients. Emerging drugs, such as mobocertinib and amivantamab, have recently been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to treat advanced‐stage NSCLC with EGFRex20ins, with numerous more new drugs currently in various phases of research. NOVEL EGFR‐TKIS

Mobocertinib

Mobocertinib is an orally administered, pan‐EGFR mutation selective, and irreversible EGFR‐TKI. 67 The mechanism of action of mobocertinib is similar to that of second and third‐generation EGFR‐TKIs, which exert their effects by irreversibly binding to the cysteine residue at position 797 in EGFR. 67 To improve the selectivity of mobocertinib to enhance its antitumor effectiveness and reduce dose‐dependent drug toxicity, researchers have inserted isopropyl groups into the pyrimidine ring to strengthen its affinity for EGFRex20ins. 67 In addition to targeting EGFRex20ins, mobocertinib has also shown activity against common EGFR mutations, including L858R and exon 19 deletions, in preclinical studies. 67

FDA approval for mobocertinib was based on an open‐label, phase 1/2, nonrandomized clinical trial in platinum‐pretreated patients with EGFRex20ins‐positive metastatic NSCLC (NCT02716116). 61 , 68 The trial enrolled 114 platinum‐pretreated patients with EGFRex20ins‐positive metastatic NSCLC (PPP cohort), including a dose escalation cohort (n = 6), a dose expansion cohort (n = 22), and a single‐arm expansion cohort (EXCLAIM, n = 86, with 10 patients excluded from the PPP cohort due to no prior platinum‐containing chemotherapy). 61 Patients received daily oral mobocertinib at 160 mg. At the data cutoff, in the PPP cohort, the independent reading committee (IRC) assessed ORR was 28% (95% CI: 20%–37%), and the investigator‐assessed ORR was 35% (95% CI: 26%–45%). The IRC‐assessed PFS was 7.3 months (95% CI: 5.5–9.2), and the OS was 24.0 months (95% CI: 14.6–28.8). 49 In the EXCLAIM cohort, the IRC‐assessed ORR was 25% (95% CI: 17%–35%), while the investigator‐assessed ORR was 32% (95% CI: 23%–43%). 61 Patients who had not previously received immunotherapy or EGFR‐TKI treatment and did not have baseline brain metastases often had higher response rates. 61 Based on these results, mobocertinib was approved by the FDA in September 2021 for the treatment of platinum‐pretreated patients with EGFRex20ins‐positive locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC. 68

In terms of safety, mobocertinib has a high rate of severe adverse events (AEs) (46%), which include diarrhea, dyspnea, vomiting, fever, acute kidney injury, nausea, pleural effusion, and heart failure. In the PPP cohort, the discovery that AEs leading to dose reduction were linked with poorer ORR and shorter PFS underlines the need for early detection and management of drug‐related toxicities when utilizing mobocertinib. 69 , 70

Currently, various clinical trials on mobocertinib are being widely undertaken abroad. One randomized, phase 3, multicenter, open‐label research (NCT04129502) intends to examine the efficacy of mobocertinib as a first‐line therapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC patients with EGFRexon20ins and compare it to the current standard therapy of platinum‐based chemotherapy. The trial is intended to enroll 352 patients, and the primary endpoint is the IRC‐confirmed PFS. The subsequent results have the potential to change the standard of care for EGFRex20ins‐positive NSCLC patients. 71

Poziotinib

Poziotinib, a novel second‐generation EGFR‐TKI, shows similarities with the second‐generation EGFR‐TKI afatinib structurally, comprising a tiny and flexible quinazoline core. 45 However, poziotinib stands apart from afatinib due to its larger freedom of rotation around the amine and ether groups, as well as an enhanced halogenation of the terminal benzene ring. 45 Because of its reduced size and flexibility, poziotinib might more efficiently bind EGFRex20ins. 45 In vitro, investigations have shown the efficacy of poziotinib against the frequent EGFR and HER2 exon 20 insertion mutations in Ba/F3 models. The average IC50 values of poziotinib were reported to be roughly 100 times greater than those of osimertinib and 40 times stronger than that of afatinib in EGFRex20ins expressing cell lines. 45

After promising preclinical findings, phase 2 research (NCT03066206) was initiated to examine the effectiveness and safety of poziotinib in patients with metastatic NSCLC harboring EGFRex20ins. 62 The trial involved 50 heavily pretreated patients, of whom 94% (n = 47) had received at least one prior systemic therapy, and 68% (n = 34) had received two or more prior lines of therapy. 62 Patients received 16 mg of poziotinib orally daily. Among the intention‐to‐treat (ITT) group (n = 50), the confirmed ORR was 32.0%, with a PFS of 5.5 months. The most prevalent treatment‐related AEs were diarrhea, skin rash, oral mucositis, paronychia, and dry skin, most of which were grade 1 or 2. AEs leading to dose reduction occurred in 72% (n = 36) of patients. 62

The ZENITH20‐1 study evaluated the efficacy and safety of poziotinib in patients with NSCLC harboring EGFR exon 20 insertions who had received at least one prior line of treatment. Although not meeting the primary endpoint in the as‐treated population with an ORR of 14.8%, the evaluable population (n = 88) demonstrated an ORR of 19.3%. 72 In the follow‐up ZENITH20‐3 trial (n = 79), the ITT analysis showed an ORR of 27.8% and a PFS of 7.2 months. The AE profile was similar to that of second‐generation EGFR‐TKIs, with ≥ grade 3 rash seen in 33% of patients and diarrhea in 23%. 73

To examine the influence of lower dosages and novel dosing regimens on tolerance and safety, ZENITH20‐5 included 40 participants. Preliminary results showed that twice daily dosing resulted in a decreased anticipated AE rate (31% vs. 21% and 27% vs. 16%, respectively) in the first treatment cycle. Twice daily dosing schedules also resulted in a relative reduction in dose interruptions by 38% and 52%, respectively, hence eliminating interventions to complete the medication cycle due to tolerance concerns. 73

Sunvozertinib

Sunvozertinib is a highly selective, irreversible oral EGFR‐TKI with significant efficacy against a variety of EGFR and HER2 mutations, including EGFRexon20ins, EGFR sensitizing mutations, T790M, and EGFR rare mutations, as verified by preclinical investigations. 74 Two ongoing phase 1/2 studies (NCT03974022 and CTR20192097) have demonstrated the promising antitumor efficacy of sunvozertinib in locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC patients with EGFRexon20ins, with an ORR of 48.4% (15/31) at the recommended phase 2 dose of 300 mg once daily, even in patients with prior amivantamab or anti‐PD(L)1 treatment or baseline brain metastasis. The most prevalent AEs during therapy were diarrhea (grade 3, 5.2%) and skin rash (grade 3, 1%), which occurred in 53.9% and 40.2% of patients, respectively. 74 , 75 , 76 Based on these studies, sunvozertinib has been granted the Breakthrough Therapy Designation by both the National Food and Drug Administration (NMPA) in December 2020 and the US FDA in January 2022 for the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with EGFRex20ins who have progressed after prior systemic chemotherapy.

Preliminary results of another ongoing phase 2, single‐arm, multicenter study (CTR20211009) presented at the 2022 European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) meeting showed a confirmed ORR of 59.8% (58/97) and 48.5% (16/33) among a total of 97 EGFRex20ins positive patients and patients with baseline brain metastasis, respectively. 63 Several phase 2/3 studies of sunvozertinib are currently underway, which may establish it as a new standard of care for NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins (Table 4).

Zipalertinib

Zipalertinib (formerly known as CLN‐081, TAS6417) is a novel EGFR‐TKI that targets the ATP‐binding site in the hinge region of EGFR and specifically inhibits EGFRex20ins. This is owing to its unusual structure, including a peculiar core structure of 6‐methyl‐8,9‐dihydropyrimido (5,4‐b) indolizine. 77 In NIH/3 T3 and Ba/F3 cell line models carrying distinct EGFRex20ins subtypes, zipalertinib displayed a wild‐type to mutant IC50 ratio of 134‐fold and reduced the proliferation of human EGFRex20ins cells and promoted tumor regression in xenograft models. 77

In a phase 1/2a dose escalation experiment utilizing an accelerated titration and rolling six design, zipalertinib has exhibited promising results (NCT04036682). 78 As of May 9, 2022, 73 patients with EGFRex20ins NSCLC who had received at least one line of platinum‐containing systemic chemotherapy in the past were enrolled, with 66% (48/73) having received two or more lines of prior therapy. In the subgroup of 39 patients receiving 100 mg twice a day, the ORR was 41% (16/39). Across all dose levels, the PFS was 10 months (95% CI: 6–12). For patients receiving 100 mg twice a day, the PFS extended to 12 months (95% CI: 5–NR). It is worth noting that among the 29 patients who had previously received EGFR‐TKIs (exon‐20 and non‐exon‐20), 34% achieved a PR. This suggests that zipalertinib may offer new hope for patients who have developed secondary resistance after targeted therapy. 78 The most prevalent treatment‐related adverse events were rash (80%), paronychia (32%), and diarrhea (30%). Grade 3 or higher treatment‐related adverse events included anemia (10%) and liver dysfunction (4%). 78 In summary, zipalertinib demonstrated favorable efficacy and safety in the clinical trial, particularly in terms of its long‐term efficacy. 79

Furmonertinib

Furmonertinib, a highly selective and irreversible third‐generation EGFR‐TKI, has shown significant success in treating NSCLC patients with EGFR sensitizing or T790M mutations, as well as those with central nervous system involvements. 80 , 81 , 82 Preclinical studies have indicated that furmonertinib is efficient in suppressing EGFRex20ins‐carrying Ba/F3 cells in vitro, with an average IC50 of 11‐20 nM.

To investigate the efficacy and safety of furmonertinib in NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins, a phase Ib multicenter trial (NCT04858958) was done. In cohort 1, 10 treatment‐naive advanced NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins were treated with furmonertinib 240 mg QD. Preliminary data reported at the 2021 ESMO conference showed that among these patients, seven achieved partial response (PR), three had stable disease (SD), and all patients displayed tumor shrinkage in the target area (−43.0%, 95% CI: −72.3%, −3.0%). Common AEs included diarrhea, paronychia, skin cracking, anorexia, and upper respiratory tract infection. No grade 3 or higher AEs were observed, and no dose reduction or discontinuation due to AEs was reported.

BISPECIFIC ANTIBODY

Amivantamab

Amivantamab (JNJ‐61186372) is a completely human bispecific antibody targeting EGFR and mesenchymal‐epithelial transition factor (MET), with shown antitumor effects in preclinical investigations. 83 , 84 It exerts its effect through several mechanisms, including suppression of downstream signaling cascades, as well as activating NK cells and macrophages through the Fc domain to stimulate antibody‐dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and trogocytosis. 69 , 70

In a phase 1, open‐label study (CHRYSALIS; NCT02609776), 81 patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC who had received a median of two prior therapies were treated with amivantamab. The results showed an ORR of 40%, with a PFS of 8.3 months and an OS of 22.8 months. 60 AEs were associated with the inhibition of EGFR and MET pathways, including rash, paronychia, stomatitis, hypoalbuminemia, and peripheral edema. Five patients had interstitial lung disease, and severe AEs occurred in 30% of patients. 60 Based on the outcomes of the CHRYSALIS trial, amivantamab was approved by the US FDA in May 2021 for the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with EGFRexon20ins that progressed after prior platinum‐based chemotherapy. 84

Amivantamab is now being explored as a first‐line treatment for EGFRexon20ins NSCLC patients in ongoing clinical trials. The effectiveness of amivantamab in combination with lazertinib (a third EGFR‐TKI) is being studied in the CHRYSALIS‐2 trial (NCT04077463), which involves patients who have already received standard treatment, including osimertinib. 85 Moreover, the safety and efficacy of amivantamab plus carboplatin/pemetrexed versus carboplatin/pemetrexed alone is being examined in the phase 3 study called PAPILLON (NCT04538664). This trial may help evaluate if amivantamab can substitute platinum‐containing chemotherapy as a first‐line treatment for EGFRexon20ins NSCLC. 86 Another phase 3 clinical trial, MARIPOSA (NCT04487080), is exploring the efficacy of amivantamab in combination with lapatinib versus osimertinib in advanced and metastatic NSCLC patients with EGFR exon 19 and 21 mutations. The outcomes of this trial may broaden the use of amivantamab as part of combination therapy for more prevalent EGFR mutation subtypes. 87

HSP‐90 INHIBITOR

Luminespib

Luminespib is a heat shock protein 90 (HSP90) inhibitor that reduces downstream signaling cascades and induces apoptosis by inhibiting the HSP90 chaperone machinery exclusively linked with EGFRex20ins, hence limiting ubiquitin‐mediated protein degradation. 88 , 89

A phase 2 study of luminespib in NSCLC patients with EGFRex20ins demonstrated an ORR of 17.2% (5/29) in 29 stage IV NSCLC patients enrolled, a PFS of 2.8 months, a OS of 9.9 months, and a severe drug‐related adverse event rate of 20.7% (6/29). 90 Another open‐label, randomized phase 2 study assessed the efficacy of luminespib with pemetrexed or docetaxel in NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations, although none of the endpoints were superior to chemotherapy. 91

Other agents

Additionally, a multitude of medicines are underway in phase I clinical trials to investigate their safety and efficacy against EGFRex20ins mutation, exhibiting strong affinity and robust antitumor activity (Table 4). 92 , 93 , 94 , 95 , 96 , 97 For instance, BAY‐2927088 is a reversible small molecule inhibitor that specifically targets EGFRex20ins mutation with 40‐fold higher selectivity for EGFRex20ins mutation over wild‐type EGFR, and is expected to decrease the toxicity associated with wild‐type EGFR inhibition. 95 Another potent covalent inhibitor, BLU‐451, has demonstrated favorable permeability in the central nervous system in preclinical experiments, making it a promising candidate for NSCLC patients with brain metastases compared to other drugs targeting EGFRexon20ins that are currently in development. 96 Additionally, there are various prospective medicines targeting EGFRex20ins mutations in preclinical studies that display exceptional selectivity and robust antitumor effectiveness in vivo and in vitro among multiple experimental models. 98 , 99 , 100 , 101 However, the clinical benefit of these medications still needs to be verified in large‐scale research.

CONCLUSIONS

The poor prognosis and substantial heterogeneity of EGFRex20ins‐positive NSCLC patients provide a huge clinical challenge, underlining the urgent need for better therapy alternatives. In recent years, landmark progress has been made in targeted therapy for this patient population, with the FDA‐approved and NCCN‐recommended drugs mobocertinib and amivantamab leading the way. The two drugs have distinct mechanisms of action and routes of administration, presenting patients with additional treatment options. Moreover, some extremely promising targeted treatments are now in development, with the potential to considerably boost clinical outcomes for this subset of patients. In general, there is still significant potential for improvement in the current clinical practice or research setting for EGFRex20ins‐positive NSCLC.

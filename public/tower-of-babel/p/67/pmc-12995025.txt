Goat milk-derived bioactive peptides as a health promoter: an insight based on recent in silico studies
Authors: Syeda Aamna Hassan, Waqas Asghar, Saba Yousaf, Hafiz Ubaid ur Rahman, Komal Javed, Nauman Khalid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Goat milk is traditionally known for its nutritional value, greater digestibility, and cultural significance. More recently, goat milk has been explored as a promising natural source of novel bioactive peptides (BAPs) with significant biological activity. BAPs derived from goat milk have emerged as high potential candidates for their therapeutic and health-promoting attributes owing to their diverse functionality as natural antioxidants, antihypertensives, antimicrobials, antidiabetics, anti-hypercholesterolemic, anticancer, as well as immunomodulatory therapeutic aids. The integration of powerful computational biology approaches, such as machine learning and artificial intelligence, with the research in the domains of bioinformatics and food sciences has greatly accelerated and improved the discovery pipelines for milk protein-derived BAPs. This, in turn, has enabled rapid and cost-effective in silico identification, high-throughput screening, and functional prediction of these BAPs prior to being subjected to experimental validation and testing through conventional in vitro/in vivo methods. The utilization of various in silico techniques, such as bioactivity relationship modeling tools, molecular docking, and design of experiment (DoE) approaches for optimizing production, identification, and performance in biological systems, as well as future potential are investigated in the current review.

Introduction

Milk has been a source of nourishment for humans for centuries, being a part of their diet in its natural form, as well as various products derived from it. Widely regarded as one of the nature’s ‘most complete’ and wholesome foods, milk and dairy products are nutrient-dense and excellent sources of energy, protein, and a wide variety of micronutrients, such as minerals (sodium, magnesium, calcium, zinc, phosphorus, and potassium) in a readily absorbable bioform, and vitamins (vitamin C and some B complex vitamins) (Górska-Warsewicz et al., 2019). This comprehensive nutrient matrix is vital to human development and health throughout life. In addition, milk proteins and their fractions are viable sources of bioactive peptides (BAPs) with significant therapeutic potential and health benefits for humans, such as antioxidant activity, antimicrobial activity, and immunomodulation (Zaky et al., 2022).

Traditionally, the focus has been on the milk from what are regarded as major dairy species, i.e., cattle, contributing to around 85% of the world’s total milk production (Faccia et al., 2020). However, more recently, the focus has shifted to what are regarded as minor dairy species, such as camel, sheep, goat, and buffalo (Arain et al., 2022; Guha et al., 2021), with the current review focusing, in particular on goat milk. Goat milk, conventionally known for its unique nutritional profile, digestibility, and cultural significance (ALKaisy et al., 2023), is gaining increasing recognition for potential functional and health attributes, owing to the presence of bioactive constituents, in particular, BAPs derived from its protein content, short- and medium-chain fatty acids, oligosaccharides, and phospholipids (dos Santos et al., 2023). These components have been shown to potentially prevent various chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), and maintain gastrointestinal (GI) health (ALKaisy et al., 2023). For instance, in India, goat milk has been used for centuries in formulating Ayurvedic medications for various ailments and allergies, as well as nutraceuticals (Verma et al., 2020).

The shift from major dairy species to minor dairy species can be attributed to the fact that the primary sequences of milk proteins from different dairy species are characteristically distinct, thereby also influencing the physicochemical, biological, and techno-functional attributes of the BAPs released from them (FitzGerald et al., 2020). Goat milk proteins have a unique amino acid profile, superior digestibility, and low allergenicity (Li et al., 2022), owing to lower α-casein (highly allergenic) and higher β-casein (A2 type) levels compared to bovine milk (Muñoz-Salinas et al., 2022). The BAP profile in goat milk, therefore, differs significantly from that of bovine milk, both in terms of diversity and potency. Given that goat milk is rich in β-casein, κ-casein, and α-lactalbumin, the hydrolyzed components from these two proteins have the potential to yield more potent BAPs with a broad range of bioactive potential (Zhang et al., 2025). On the contrary, bovine milk’s higher A1 β-casein yields fewer, and comparatively, less potent BAP variants (such as the problematic beta-casomorphin 7, BCM-7) (Muñoz-Salinas et al., 2022). In essence, these compositional shifts guide the overall BAP diversity and potency in goat milk (Table 1).

Antimicrobial

BAPs are protein fractions that are either naturally present in milk or embedded in parent protein structures and are activated after the cleavage of the proteins by the action of the endogenous and exogenous enzymes, as well as microbial fermentation (Akbarian et al., 2022). These highly bioactive molecules are made up of characteristically short peptide chains with 2–20 amino acids (primarily arginine, proline, and lysine, and hydrophobic residues), and a molar mass of < 6000 Da (Koirala et al., 2023), and are gaining increasing research focus owing to their purported nutritional and health benefits. BAPs offer many therapeutic benefits compared to conventional medicines and protein-based biopharmaceuticals (Samtiya et al., 2022). For instance, BAPs have a high target tissue specificity, efficacy, and potency (even at low concentrations represented by EC50 values within the nanomolar range, or lower), as well as negligible toxicity, and fewer side effects and immunogenicity (Purohit et al., 2024), a factor, crucial to the treatment of chronic disease conditions (Akbarian et al., 2022). Conversely, synthetic chemical compounds commonly employed as drugs have been shown to exert a cumulative effect on the body (Riaz et al., 2023). Moreover, the inherent properties and multifaceted physiological attributes of the BAPs make them excellent candidates for the development of therapeutic compounds, particularly in the context of the modern drug development pipelines (Purohit et al., 2024). Furthermore, the high specificity of BAPs can be attributed to their potential as therapeutics that are tolerable, safe, and highly effective (Rossino et al., 2023).

Conventional procedures for BAP discovery involve protein extraction, substrate, and enzyme selection, in vitro bioactivity screening, proteolysis (facilitated by various proteases under different conditions), repetition of the refining process, purification, characterization, and subsequent identification of the peptides, and ultimately, activity assessment, and validation through in vivo trials (Sansi et al., 2022). The time-intensive and non-targeted nature of the process, however, is a significant impediment to its success and efficiency. The in silico analysis, employing bioinformatics and various computational resources for the management, curation, and interpretation of information, has, therefore, emerged as a targeted and powerful tool for BAP discovery owing to more rapid results acquisition and recovery rates, reduced reagent consumption, and resultant cost-effectiveness (Aguiar et al., 2024; Nongonierma & FitzGerald, 2017), as described in Fig. 1.

Recently, Utilization of computational tools that leverage bioinformatics and machine learning (ML) techniques for predicting the bioactive properties of goat milk BAPs is also gaining traction (Du et al., 2024), with particular focus on analysis of peptide sequences, assessment of potential health benefits, and driving experimental validation (Coscueta et al., 2022). Existing research has mostly explored bioactivities attributed to goat milk BAPs on an individual basis, with some studies utilizing the in silico aspect for prediction, while the others exploring in vitro or in vivo models (animal). A critical research gap exists, whereby, a unified review, exclusively focused on synthesizing the diverse bioactivities associated with goat milk BAPs, and the prediction of their bioactive potential through in silico tools such as BIOPEP-UWM, PeptideRanker etc., is lacking. This review aims to address the gap for aiding in advancement of precision nutrition applications, particularly in the context of therapeutic peptide design (tailored to unique goat milk BAP sequences), and the wider domain of nutrigenomics. Methodology

A comprehensive literature search was conducted using keyword combinations such as, ‘goat milk bioactive peptides’, ‘goat milk BAPs’, ‘in silico tools goat milk BAPs’, ‘in silico approaches goat milk BAPs’, ‘computational tools goat milk BAPs’. The databases searched included PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, and Google Scholar databases from January 2015 to December 2025 for identification of studies, with recency of research as the prime focus for the inclusion of the majority of the studies, along with including the studies only associated with goat milk proteins, and in silico approaches. Close to 100 journal articles satisfied the criteria, and after review, 90 were shortlisted for inclusion in this review. The shortlisted studies were then categorized into ‘main narrative (bioactivity-focused)’, and ‘support narrative (focused on building the narrative around the main narrative studies)’ for distribution among the co-authors.

Antioxidant properties

Oxidative stress can be regarded as the primary driver and major contributor to the onset and progression of various health disorders. Originating from the excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), reactive nitrogen species (RNS), and other free radicals, oxidative stress has been linked to the causation of several chronic diseases, such as diabetes, diabetes-associated nephropathy and neuropathy, obesity, CVDs, cancer, as well as neurological disorders, including Parkinson’s disease (PD), amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) (Reddy, 2023).

Oxidative stress, if exacerbated, can lead to the accelerated formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs), which, through their interactions with receptors for advanced glycation end products (RAGEs), instigate a myriad of signaling cascades resulting in the formation of inflammatory cytokines and further exacerbating oxidative stress outcomes (Khalid et al., 2022). Oxidative stress can also induce the generation of a highly complex mixture of lipid peroxidation products (LPPs), such as 4-hydroxy-trans-2-nonenal (HNE) and malondialdehyde (MDA), that have been implicated in undesirable DNA modifications and, consequently, carcinogenesis (Li et al., 2022).

Milk-derived BAPs are highly capable free radical scavengers, lipid peroxidation inhibitors, chelators of transition metal ions, and enhancers of endogenous antioxidant enzyme activities, thereby playing a vital role in reducing oxidative stress, slowing down aging, and preventing various chronic diseases, such as diabetes, CVDs, and certain cancers (Zhang et al. 2024a, b). The probable mechanism underpinning this phenomenon is the intracellular conversion of cysteine into glutathione, a potent antioxidant variously indicated to have a protective role against oxidative damage (Kashung & Karuthapandian, 2025). BAPs comprising amino acids such as histidine, cysteine (SH group), and tryptophan induce antioxidant activity by way of single electron transfer, while those containing tyrosine achieve it through the transfer of hydrogen atoms (Esfandi et al., 2019).

By leveraging computational tools for the production, analysis, and optimization of peptide sequences for their functional properties, several in silico studies have demonstrated the significant antioxidant potential of goat milk protein-derived BAPs. For instance, Coscueta et al. (2022) used bioinformatics tools BIOPEP and PeptideRanker for screening and prediction (through analysis of sequence features, physicochemical properties, and known activity levels from databases) of BAPs from goat milk casein hydrolysates. The study identified BAPs such as YPYQGPIVL and QSLVYPFTGPIPNSL that demonstrated mild antioxidant activity pre-digestion, with a marked increase in activity observed post the simulated GI digestion, indicating the release of peptide-encoded antioxidant sequences. Similarly, Shu et al. (2018) indicated that goat milk fermented with Lactobacillus casei L61 showed increased production of BAPs with antioxidant potential. The team used response surface methodology (RSM) for the optimization of fermentation conditions, which led to significant enhancement of both hydroxyl and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical-scavenging activities, as well as increased stability under simulated GI conditions. Another study by Zhang et al. (2025), similarly, involved the identification of BAPs produced as a result of enzymatic hydrolysis of goat milk proteins, and subsequent annotation of their functional properties. Approaches such as molecular docking and peptidomics were utilized for the investigation of the antioxidant potential of the identified BAPs (LSMTDTR, QEALELIR, NIPVGILR, and QAQNVQHY), and the findings from in vitro experiments demonstrated the IC50 values for DPPH radical scavenging assay (and ACE inhibition assay using purified angiotensin-converting enzyme, ACE) at 4.466, 4.496, 4.626, and 3.875 mg/mL for the four BAPs, respectively. The findings indicated the strong antioxidant potential of these BAPs for the mitigation of oxidative stress. Incorporation of antioxidant BAPs from goat milk into food formulations has the potential to scavenge free radicals, reduce lipid oxidation, improve product stability, and contribute to extending shelf life of the food formulation naturally without the need of synthetic food additives (chemical antioxidants such as butylated hydroxytoluene, BHT). In turn, such food formulations, upon consumption, can also deliver health benefits, for instance, reduction of oxidative stress and inflammation, thereby aiding in chronic disease prevention in the long-term (Zhu et al., 2022). Anticancer attributes

Cancer is a complex, large, and heterogeneous group of diseases characterized by the development of abnormal cells, their uncontrolled proliferation, and the metastasis of these abnormal cells in the human body, with the potential to affect any other tissue (Schwartz, 2024). A major cause of mortality worldwide, cancer affects both affluent and developing countries (Boire et al., 2024). Traditional procedures such as surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, molecular targeted therapy, and antibody-drug conjugates have been associated with deleterious side effects, low specificity, and issues of multidrug resistance stemming from the use of conventional anticancer drugs (Qu et al., 2024). This has necessitated the research and development of novel therapeutic modalities, and BAPs from various sources, both animal and plant, have emerged as a viable solution for augmenting conventional cancer treatment therapies.

Recently, some milk and dairy products have been noted for the presence of nutrient proteins that release BAPs (following enzyme-mediated digestive degradation) with antiproliferative potential against cancer cell growth in humans (Zhang et al., 2023). Similarly, goat milk BAPs derived from casein and whey fractions (through enzymatic hydrolysis involving pepsin and trypsin) have demonstrated promising anticancer potential (Cakir & Tunali-Akbay, 2021). The mechanisms of action include triggering of programmed cell death through activation of caspases, and disruption of mitochondrial membranes (Ghadiri et al., 2024), inhibition of cyclin-dependent kinases, thereby inducing stoppage of cancer cell proliferation at specific checkpoints (Wang et al., 2017), reducing cancer cell adhesion and migration through interference with matrix metalloproteinase activity, and integrin signaling (Zhang et al. 2024a, b), enhancing cytotoxicity of natural killer cells against tumor cells (Wang et al., 2017), and inhibiting signaling of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) through peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPAR-γ or PPARG), resulting in reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines responsible for promoting tumor growth (Hamdi et al., 2025).

Research studies involving the use of in silico tools for anticancer screening are limited, and still a nascent domain that can be explored further. Sahna et al. (2022), for instance, investigated the effects of goat milk casein and whey protein-derived BAPs on MCF-7 breast cancer cells through in vitro assays. The study involved the measurement of cell death (via MTT assay and flow cytometry), identification of the peptides from the hydrolyzed goat milk protein fractions (through Liquid Chromatography-Quadrupole Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry LC-Q-TOF/MS proteomic analysis of the treated cells) and evaluation of cell death mechanisms by comparing the data obtained from biochemical and cell culture analyses with in silico analyses (using PeptideRanker and BIOPEP-UWM). Goat milk was subjected to hydrolysis with the aid of pepsin or trypsin, and the obtained BAPs were evaluated using the PeptideRanker tool (BAPs with scores of more than 0.5 were designated as bioactive). The biological activities of the BAPs were determined through the BIOPEP tool. The obtained BAPs were then docked with various target enzymes by using the HPEPDOCK molecular docking server. Docking analyses showed that BAPs obtained from pepsin-treated casein fractions (starting with the highest bioactivity score, DYRWIAL> QIMSSPWGEMYNIF> CDELGIMIWQDF> QYPYQGPIVL> SSGLGNVPRPYQ) and trypsin-treated whey fractions (starting with the highest bioactivity score, QYKIPDWFLNR> ALPMHIR> LVMFQRR) may impact the survival rates of breast cancer cells, exerting their activity through various mechanistic pathways, and that these BAPs have the potential to be utilized as anti-tumor agents in breast cancer therapies.

Pratik et al. (2025) in a recent study used various in silico tools for investigating the anticancer potential of BAPs derived from six species (cow, water buffalo, yak, goat, sheep, and camel) of the order Artiodactyla. The team collected amino acid sequences of major milk proteins (from UniProt database) and subjected them to simulated in silico digestion using PeptideCutter. This was followed by screening of obtained BAPs (~ 1268) using ML-based prediction model, AntiCP 2.0, and prioritizing them as per their anticancer scores. The 23 candidate BAPs, including five originating from goat milk proteins (P7-P11), were 3D structure-modeled for evaluating stability and structural validity using AlphaFold2. Molecular docking simulations focused on key breast cancer targets, myeloid cell leukemia-1 (MCL-1) and estrogen-related receptor alpha (ERα), indicated positive affinity between specific BAPs and these targets. In particular, BAP P5 (from buffalo milk) and P18 (from sheep milk) demonstrated potent binding affinities (-7.4 kcal/mol, receptor ERα and − 8.1 kcal/mol, receptor MCL-1 respectively) towards the targets and were comparable to those exhibited by reference inhibitors (-8.0 kcal/mol, receptor ERα, and − 9.9 kcal/mol, receptor MCL-1). Goat milk BAP P11 exhibited the third best affinity (-7.7 kcal/mol) against MCL-1 and indicates that it could be a potential target for future studies.

In their study, Sansi et al. (2022) used BIOPEP-UWM to simulate enzymatic digestion of various goat milk proteins (α-s1-, α-s2-, β-, and κ-caseins, α-lactoglobulin, and β-lactalbumin) and identified 83 potential BAPs with antimicrobial activity. The methodology from the study can be replicated for the discovery of BAPs with anticancer action. Similarly, during the same study, a selected number of BAPs were investigated for their interactions with targets such as bacterial enzyme, dihydropteroate synthase (DHPS), with the results singling out three BAPs with high target binding affinity. This approach, likewise, can be replicated for cancer-associated receptors. Moreover, BAPs with antioxidant potential and sourced from pepsin-treated goat casein/whey hydrolysates, such as β-caseins, and β -lactoglobulin have demonstrated broad-spectrum cytoprotective effects, which, in turn can have potential for use in combating cancer (Ahmed et al., 2015).

These studies highlight the potential of in silico prediction workflows for identifying promising anticancer candidate BAPs from goat milk (and other species). However, this potential is marred by several limitations that hamper the translation of in silico models to in vivo efficacy. For instance, tumor heterogeneity (presence of non-genetic, metabolically quiescent persister cells) significantly impacts the performance of in silico tools like BIOPEP, that predicts anticancer potential through identification of bioactive motifs associated with anticancer activity (e.g., cationic short amino acid sequences of arginine or lysine that selectively bind with the anionic components of cancer cell membranes, thereby disrupting and destabilizing them). This is the primary reason why many anticancer BAPs have high predicted activities but low bioavailability (Abbas et al., 2023). Another factor in this context is the validation discrepancy, stemming from the fact that many studies involving BAPs exhibit false positives of around 30–60%, whereby the lactic acid bacteria (LAB)-derived goat milk BAPs predicted to possess anticancer potential owing to motif density, may lose potency when exposed to gastric conditions, or hypoxic tumor microenvironment. This calls for broader multi-omic validation, as in silico models lack synergies or resistances characteristically expressed by in vivo models (Coelho et al., 2024).

Antidiabetic and antihypercholesterolemic role

Diabetes remains a global public health concern, with a dramatic rise in both its prevalence and impact over the last few decades. Affecting a diverse range of demographics, diabetes is a significant contributor to global morbidity and mortality levels, through both its direct involvement, as well as being a precursor to other chronic illnesses, such as ischemic heart disease (IHD), and strokes, the two leading causes of death (CODs) worldwide (Hossain et al., 2024). Conventional treatment therapies for diabetes include lifestyle modification, gene therapy, stem cell therapy, medical nutrition therapy (MNT), and medications such as metformin, sulfonylureas, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors, as well as insulin therapy (Aloke et al., 2022). Also in recent years, in silico studies involving goat milk proteins have shown promise as a source of BAPs with antidiabetic potential, induced, in particular, by their inhibition of the enzyme, dipeptidyl peptidase-IV (DPP-IV), a key target in the management of type 2 diabetes (T2DM) (Wu et al., 2024).

In the study by Sansi et al. (2022), the BIOPEP-UWM database analyses of the goat milk proteins indicated the presence of fragments with a high frequency of DPP-IV inhibitory motifs, suggesting potential antidiabetic activity. The findings were validated by Wu et al. (2024), where initial screening using the PeptideRanker tool and subsequent peptidomic analysis (iDPPIV-SCM) and molecular docking simulations (LibDock) identified two novel BAPs (LLLP and YPF) that exhibited strong DPP-IV inhibition, with binding energies of -7.5 kcal/mol and − 7.2 kcal/mol, respectively. The two BAPs, therefore, can be utilized as functional food ingredients for formulating antidiabetic dietary solutions (Fig. 2).

Hypercholesterolemia, a form of hyperlipidemia and dyslipidemia, is a well-established and robust risk factor associated with the pathogenesis and development of various cardiovascular disorders, such as IHD, atherosclerosis, and coronary heart disease (CHD) (Abera et al., 2024). Conventional treatment of hypercholesterolemia involves lifestyle modifications and pharmacologic interventions with statins, as well as other lipid-lowering therapies (LLTs) (Abbasi et al., 2024). By targeting key lipid-metabolizing enzymes, goat milk-derived BAPs have emerged as a novel approach to managing hypercholesterolemia (Singh & Gaur, 2024). The in silico studies, coupled with in vitro experimentation, have revealed strain- and substrate-specific mechanistic pathways for inhibition of enzymes. For instance, in a recent study by Mudgil et al. (2024), goat milk fermented with Pediococcus pentosaceus MF000957 yielded BAPs that exhibited strong pancreatic lipase (PL) inhibitory activity. In particular, the molecular docking tool HPEPDOCK 2.0 predicted two BAPs, IHPFAQAQS and DMESTEVFTKK, with docking scores of − 209.141 and − 190.132, respectively, as active PL inhibitors, owing to their high binding affinity for active sites of PL, as well as binding energy, and the number of binding sites on the PL (five).

Additionally, these BAPs were significant for their low binding energies ( ≤ − 7.5 kcal/mol), non-toxicity, non-carcinogenicity, and optimal drug-likeness attributes, for instance, high GI absorption. Another study investigated the potential of BAPs derived from probiotic-fermented goat milk in the context of cholesteryl esterase (CE) inhibition. The findings of the study indicated that goat milk fermented with probiotic strains exhibited superior CE inhibition activity. The in silico analysis using the BIOPEP-UWM and PeptideRanker tools, as well as a web server-based program PepSite2, and High Ambiguity Driven protein-protein DOCKing (HADDOCK) server, suggested that goat milk proteins presented as a more suitable substrate for the proteolytic enzymes produced by various probiotic strains used for fermentation, thereby facilitating the formation of shorter BAPs with more potent bioactivity. The analysis also indicated that goat milk BAPs inhibited CE through indirect mechanisms, such as BAP-enzyme interactions away from the active site. Moreover, tools such as SwissADME and BIOPEP-UWM have also been employed for ADME prediction of goat milk BAPs and validated the stability, solubility, and oral bioavailability of these BAPs (Sansi et al., 2022). These findings are key to the potential use of goat milk BAPs for formulating novel dairy products with hypolipidemic activities.

Antihypertensive properties

Hypertension, also known as high blood pressure (140/90 mmHg or higher), is a serious health problem that can increase the risk of brain, kidney, heart, and other diseases, as well as a major cause of premature deaths globally (WHO 2024). An essential and single modifiable risk factor for reducing mortality and morbidity around the world, hypertension impacts approximately 33% of the global population (Tomaszewski & Itoh, 2023; Zhou et al., 2021). The general strategies for regulating hypertension include lifestyle interventions such as dietary modification and improved physical activity levels (Kreutz et al., 2024), and pharmacological therapies, such as blood pressure-lowering classes of drugs, e.g., angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), beta-blockers (BBs), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers (CCBs), and thiazide diuretics (TLs) (Mancia et al., 2023). The primary target of ACEIs is the enzyme, Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE; Peptidyl-dipeptide hydrolase, EC 3.4.15.1).

ACE is one of the most significant enzymes that play a major role in the renin-angiotensin system (RAS). The enzyme is involved in the conversion of decapeptide angiotensin-I (Ang I) to octapeptide angiotensin-II (Ang II), a potent vasoconstrictor that causes arterial constriction, leading to elevated blood pressure (Sompinit et al., 2020). Additionally, ACE also contributes to the degradation of bradykinin, a vasodilator, into inactive peptides (via the cleavage of C-terminal dipeptides), which consequently diminishes endothelial-dependent vasodilation and induces a marked increase in vascular resistance resulting in blood pressure elevation (Ahmad et al., 2023). Consequently, inhibition of the enzyme ACE is regarded as the major target for mitigating symptoms of hypertension and lowering blood pressure (Chen et al., 2021). Conventionally, this inhibition has been achieved through the previously mentioned class of drugs, ACEIs.

ACEIs, such as enalapril and captopril, are the most commonly prescribed class of medications for the treatment of CVDs and renal disorders, such as hypertension, acute coronary syndrome, heart failure, diabetes, and nephrotic syndrome (Ahmad et al., 2023). However, their use has been linked to various adverse side effects, ranging from mild to severe, such as nonproductive cough (dry cough), dizziness or lightheadedness, fatigue, skin rashes, angioedema, taste disturbances, renal dysfunction, and hypotension (Sompinit et al., 2020). BAPs with antihypertensive activity, therefore, are a natural and healthier alternative to synthetic ACEIs as a non-pharmacologic therapy for the treatment of hypertension (Shi et al., 2017). In the context of dairy products, goat milk caseins and whey proteins are a rich source of ACE inhibitory BAPs, often released via fermentation by lactic acid bacteria (LAB), and enzyme-aided hydrolysis (Du & Jia, 2023).

Zhang et al. (2025) studied goat milk proteins (fresh and defatted goat milk) for the generation of BAPs through enzymatic hydrolysis. Utilizing peptidomics and molecular docking tools, the study investigated the antihypertensive attributes of the identified BAPs by elucidating the underpinning molecular mechanisms that impart bioactivity. Researchers used BIOPEP-UWM tool to simulate the enzyme digestion properties attributed to the proteases, as well as the prediction of the functional activities of the desired BAPs. Subsequent in vitro analysis showed that four BAPs (LSMTDTR, NIPVGILR, QAQNVQHY, and QEALELIR) identified through in silico approaches had IC50 values (using ACE inhibition assay using purified angiotensin-converting enzyme, ACE) of 2.087, 2.845, 4.884, and 7.584 mg/mL, indicating strong ACE-inhibiting potential. In another study, Xing et al. (2024) studied two BAPs (SLPQ and PYVRYL) with ACE-inhibitory potential by investigating their endothelial effects on the EA.hy926 cell line. PEPFOLD V3.5 was used for the prediction of the 3D structural model of the two BAPs, whereby the tool performs 3D modeling based on the amino acid sequences available in the BAP structure and selects the lowest-energy folding mode. This was followed by a molecular docking analysis using the CDOCKER program, and the results were evaluated based on three parameters, i.e., interaction site, type of interaction, and the -CDOCKER interaction energy (-CIE) score. The in vitro experimentation results of the study identified SLPQ with a superior potential as an ACEi BAP (IC50 values of 0.48 mM in vitro using human endothelial cell culture, EA.hy926 cells), owing to significantly enhanced production of nitric oxide (NO), and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) expression (p < 0.05), as well as the potential to partially mitigate endothelial cell dysfunction through activation of various signaling pathways and interactions. AutoDock tools were used to assess BAP binding with ACE active sites in a study by Qiao et al. (2022). The study focus was the separation and identification of an ACE-inhibitory BAP from goat milk casein (β-casein) hydrolysates, and the evaluation of its potential for mitigating Ang II-mediated detrimental effects on vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). The isolated BAP, FPQYLQYPY, exhibited strong non-competitive inhibitory activity through interactions with ACE’s C-domains and the potential to prevent excessive proliferation and migration of the VSMCs, as well as the regulation of vascular remodeling, and therefore, vascular dysfunction. Antimicrobial and Immunomodulatory properties

Despite a marked epidemiological shift towards the increasing global health burden of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), communicable (infectious) diseases continue to be one of the major causes of morbidity, mortality, and disability worldwide, particularly in middle- and low-income countries (Li et al., 2024). Conventional therapies to address this challenge have included antibiotics, antiviral drugs, antifungals, and antiparasitics (antiprotozoal and antihelminthic agents). However, this approach is facing two formidable challenges. Firstly, there is the increasing incidence of antibiotic resistance (AMR) owing to the misuse and overuse of antibiotics, consequent increased tolerance to antibacterial agents, compromised efficacy (therapeutic effect) of the antibiotics, and the emergence of multidrug-resistant bacterial strains (Muteeb et al., 2023). Secondly, there are the challenges associated with the development of vaccines, such as an incomplete understanding of how immunity develops, host-pathogen genetic variability, high developmental costs, extended development times, and the growing societal context of vaccine hesitancy (Kennedy et al., 2020), and the ‘anti-vax’ movement (Gallegos et al., 2023). Antimicrobial BAPs, sourced from sources such as goat milk, and with significantly demonstrated broad-spectrum efficacy, have emerged as promising candidates to circumvent such impediments and combat infectious diseases (Saubenova et al., 2025).

Sansi et al. (2022) predicted and analyzed the antimicrobial BAPs from goat milk proteins using a wide variety of in silico tools such as BIOPEP-UWM, PeptideRanker, Clustal Omega, ToxinPred, CAMPR3, AllergenFP, ProtParam, and PepCalp. The in silico gastrointestinal enzymatic hydrolysis of goat milk proteins by trypsin, pepsin, and chymotrypsin was carried out using the BIOPEB-UWM (enzyme action) database. 83 potential antimicrobial BAPs were yielded, with most of them released from α-s1-casein, α-s2-casein, β-casein, κ-casein, β-lactoglobulin, and α-lactalbumin. The antimicrobial activity prediction of the BAPs was performed on the CAMPR3 online server by using four in silico tools: Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), and Discriminant Analysis. Based on this predictive modeling, 17 BAPs were analyzed further by AutoDock tools based on ligand binding energy, out of which three BAPs, AVPQR, PVPQK, and PYRX, were identified as potential antimicrobials, owing to demonstrating the highest binding affinity, as well as stable interactions with the binding pocket residues of the DHPS enzyme. Additionally, all identified antimicrobial BAPs were deemed to be non-toxic, while 54 out of the 83 were confirmed as non-allergenic.

Iram et al. (2022) generated 569 peptide sequences from goat milk fermented with Lactobacillus rhamnosus C25. The protein fractions generated were then subjected to ultrafiltration membrane-aided separation with molecular weight (MW) cut-offs at 3, 4, and 10 kDa, followed by the investigation of their antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. 36 sequences were predicted as antimicrobial BAPs, with subsequent in silico analysis (using the CAMPR3, BIOPEP, SwePep, PeptideDB, and EROP-Moscow databases) indicating that the majority of these (21) were cationic, an important consideration in the context of peptide interactions with the negatively charged bacterial membranes. The < 5 kDa BAPs exhibited the highest antimicrobial activity, in particular, against Salmonella typhi, Bacillus cereus, Listeria monocytogenes, Escherichia coli, and E. faecalis. Further analysis involving molecular docking tools such as PyRx (AutoDock Vina) identified two BAPs, KSFCPAPVAPPPPT and IGHFKLIFSLLRV, as high-potent antimicrobials that exhibited high binding affinities (-7.6 and − 7.5 kcal/mol, respectively) for the MurD ligase enzyme, a key factor in the bacterial cell wall synthesis. Moreover, none of the antimicrobial BAPs were deemed toxic.

A previous study (Tomazou et al., 2019) predicted (using the tool AMPA antimicrobial peptide predictor), screened (through the CellPPD predictor tool), and analyzed BAPs from goat milk whey and feta cheese proteomes for resistance against proteolytic degradation in the GI tract. The analysis indicated that the proteomes from goat milk feta cheese yielded the highest number of antimicrobial BAPs, the highest antimicrobial effect, and the highest resistance to proteolysis. Moreover, the computational models (using databases such as dbAMP, APD, DBAASP, CAMP, and LAMP) ranked BAPs such as VLNENLLR and IKHQGLPQE based on antimicrobial potency as well as GI stability, outlining their potential as oral delivery solutions for targeting drug-resistant bacteria and use in the formulation of various food products with extended shelf life (Sansi et al., 2022). The human immune system plays a significant role in protecting against various pathogenic infections and maintaining homeostasis (Nakajima et al., 2021). In this regard, the substances that can modify the immune system to fight against diseases are termed immunomodulators (Mathipa-Mdakane & Thantsha, 2022). Multiple sites of the immune system can be modulated by different immunosuppressants, which are used in the form of variously classified drugs, such as histamine agents, corticosteroids, cellular signaling inhibitors, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for the treatment of autoimmune diseases (Strzelec et al., 2023). BAPs with immunomodulatory activity, derived from different protein sources, have been suggested as a potential natural alternative for the regulation of the immune system to fight against infections and autoimmune conditions (Cai et al., 2020). The in silico approaches aid in prediction of immunomodulatory potential of these BAPs through analysis of sequence motifs, simulations involving epitope binding, and ML models entrained on known cytokine and receptor interactions (Mattei et al., 2022). However, research studies involving in silico approaches for predicting the immunomodulatory role of goat milk BAPs remain fragmented.

BAPs derived from goat milk proteins, such as caseins and lactoferrins, have been shown to exhibit immunomodulatory, as well as antimicrobial activities (ALKaisy et al., 2023). These BAPs modulate the immune system through mechanisms such as cytokine regulation (reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines e.g., TNF-α and IL-8 in immune cells, and regulation of production of cytokines e.g., interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) and interleukin-10 (IL-10), factors critical to balancing of immune responses) (Liang et al., 2022; ALKaisy et al., 2023), immune cell activation (enhancing phagocytic activity in macrophages and promotion of lymphocyte proliferation, and stimulation of antibody synthesis) (Vargas-Bello-Pérez et al., 2019; Mohanty et al., 2016), oxidative stress reduction and inhibition of inflammatory pathways, such as NF-κB signaling (Samtiya et al., 2022), induction of immune tolerance (through suppression of IgE-mediated allergic reactions) (ALKaisy et al., 2023), and improvement of gut microbiome interactions, thereby enhancing mucosal immunity and reduction of systemic inflammation (Samtiya et al., 2022).

Bhavaniramya et al. (2022), in a receptor interaction modeling study, explored the use of goat milk BAPs as a potential treatment regimen for COVID-19 patients. The research team observed high expression levels of the cytokine interleukin-6 (IL-6) in these patients, as was the case in other disease conditions. IL-6 binds with the receptor IL-6Rα (two types: membrane bound, mIL-6R, and soluble, sIL-6R) for its activation and is directly implicated in various immune-associated mechanisms such as hematopoiesis, acute inflammatory responses, and immune control. Inhibition of these binding interactions, therefore, could reduce the over-expression of IL-6 and modulate SARS-CoV-2-induced symptoms. Goat milk BAPs were investigated as IL-6 targets through the use of molecular docking mechanisms (HPEPDOCK for blind peptide docking, PatchDock and FireDock for geometry-based docking and refinement, and Glide SP Docking for final scoring). Three BAPs (VLVLDTDYK, YLGYLEQLLR, and AMKPWIQPK), with strong binding conformations, emerged as promising IL-6 inhibitors and targets for future applications.

Iram et al. (2022) and Sansi et al. (2022) employed various in silico tools such as CAMPR3, BIOPEP-UWM, SwePep, PeptideDB, PeptideRanker, Clustal Omega, SwissADME, and EROP-Moscow, as well as AutoDock Vina, for the identification and prediction of BAPs from goat milk. The BAPs exhibited antimicrobial, non-allergenic, and non-toxic attributes, and potent inhibition of various bacterial enzymes, in particular, DHPS and MurD ligase. These enzymes are critical for the regulation of essential metabolic pathways in bacteria. BAPs, with their inhibitory potential against these enzymes, therefore, can be useful tools for combating various infections. Given that immune cell activation in response to infections, in differing scenarios triggers the production of macrophages, lymphocytes, and antibodies, the BAPs with antimicrobial action may also have immunomodulatory characteristics. In addition, goat milk-derived BAPs have exhibited anti-inflammatory effects (Shu et al., 2018; Coscueta et al., 2022; ALKaisy et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025), and they may also contribute to immune system modulation (Fig. 3).

Potential for clinical translation: limitations and challenges

BAPs, regarded as ‘new generation biological activity regulators’, represent an emerging area of research in functional foods, and nutraceuticals, and demonstrate an increasing potential for targeted therapies in nutrigenomics (e.g., modulation of PI3K/AKT pathways in the context of metabolic health), as well as pharmaceutical applications geared towards the management of diseases and chronic conditions such as T2DM (through inhibition of key enzymes, improvement of insulin sensitivity, and restoration of β-cell function), inflammation, and hypertension (Fernando & Wu, 2025). The clinical translation of these functional ingredients is increasingly being driven by ML and AI-based prediction approaches, as well as in silico tools (some of which are discussed in this review). However, this translation from lab-scale findings to real-world context is not without various challenges, some of which are outlined below (Gao et al., 2025):

Some constraints associated with limitations of in silico tools also present as a significant hurdle:

Lack of real-world physiological context: As mentioned earlier, in silico predictions often overlook some key physiological factors that are highly dynamic in nature, such as, blood flow, pH gradients and transit time in GI tract, enzymatic interactions, microbiome influences, as well as individual genomic variables (e.g., those underpinning metabolism). This manifests in overoptimistic estimates regarding ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) dynamics and necessitates both in vitro and in vivo validation for establishing realistic BAP activity levels. The absence or lack thereof of this validation can result in significant number of false positives (up to 60%) for bioavailability forecasts (Coelho et al., 2024; Cacace et al., 2021). The need for cross-validation models is also evidenced by poor predictivity scores (Q2 < 0.5) commonly associated with in silico BAP prediction models (when used as standalones), resulting in unreliable patterns for real-world translation and application (Choi et al., 2017).Bias in training data: The in silico tools like BIOPEP and PeptideRanker are, in most part, reliant on databases with a limited number of known BAPs (often skewed towards bovine sources). This gives rise to gross underrepresentation of BAPs from non-bovine species, and rare sequences (e.g., goat milk BAPs). Moreover, as in silico models are trained on already established databases containing previously identified BAPs (representing common motifs), these models tend to overpredict the bioactivity levels (Coscueta et al., 2022).Lack of systemic mechanism: Current in silico tools are effective in predicting and optimizing isolated molecular mechanisms/interactions, rather than able to address these mechanisms in the more complex and dynamic systemic settings characteristic of a whole organism. This gives rise to gross underrepresentation of BAPs from non-bovine species, and rare sequences (e.g., goat milk BAPs). Conclusion and future research priorities

The application of in silico approaches for investigations into the goat milk-derived BAP discovery has significantly aided in gauging their potential health benefits. Various computational tools, such as ML algorithms (e.g., SVM, RF, ANN), AI-driven predictive analytics, and molecular docking (AutoDock Vina, PyRx, Chimera) have contributed to revolutionizing the identification, characterization, and optimization processes related to BAPs, thereby accelerating the screening and enhancing the precision for prediction of BAP attributes, such as stability, bioactivity, and bioavailability. However, the validation of the in silico-predicted BAPs remains a significant challenge, necessitating the need for integrative computational-experimental workflows and systems-level validation frameworks for ensuring translational success. In this regard, integrating AI-driven bioinformatic tools with multi-omics approaches (encompassing peptidomics and quantitative proteomics) offers excellent opportunities for refining the BAP discovery pipelines. Moreover, the accuracy of these BAP predictions can be significantly enhanced through further incorporation of high-throughput screening, deep learning models (DeepACP, iAMP-2 L, AIPpred, AMPfun), and molecular dynamics (GROMACS, AMBER, Desmon) solutions.

Future research should involve an emphasis on the development of more robust in silico workflows, bridging the gap between computational prediction and real-world clinical applicability (through assessment of changes in protein expression, gene regulation, and metabolic profiles). Furthermore, some of the key research priorities in the context of goat milk-specific BAP data include enhancement of predictive models through creation of new and expansion of existing databases, investigation into casein alleles in terms of how they affect the release of BAPs, development of standardized in vitro/in vivo assays, screen for as yet unexplored bioactivities, such as anticancer and immunomodulation, and improvement of physiochemical modeling. This would ensure that the promising BAP candidates of today are the viable nutraceuticals of tomorrow, with a much more defined role in disease prevention and health promotion through the development of goat milk BAP-based functional foods and precision nutrition solutions.

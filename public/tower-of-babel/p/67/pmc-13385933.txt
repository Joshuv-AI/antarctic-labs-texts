Energy-efficient information processing and eligibility-trace plasticity in the Drosophila optic lobe connectome
Authors: Nalin Dhiman, Siddharth Panwar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sensory circuits operate under finite metabolic budgets, so their information processing should be evaluated together with energetic cost. Using structural graph models, connectome-constrained dynamics, and an explicit energy proxy, we quantify energy-information trade-offs in the Drosophila optic lobe connectome. Cell-type symmetries compress connectome description length by 9–16% relative to independent-edge baselines, showing that a substantial fraction of the wiring diagram is statistically regular rather than idiosyncratic. In a canonical four-way motion-decoding regime, the real connectome supports above-chance decoding and a positive decoder-based mutual-information lower bound (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}=0.408\pm 0.050$$\end{document} bits across seeds), whereas label shuffling and connectivity shuffling collapse the bound to zero. In a separate energy-efficiency regime with topology- and weight-matched nulls, absolute decoder bounds depend on the operating point and null family, so we focus on efficiency within that regime rather than on raw information differences across regimes. Under this comparison, the real network achieves lower total energy than the connectivity null (Real/Null_Conn \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 0.80$$\end{document}) and higher bits-per-energy across the tested cost-weight sweep. Across retinotopic patches and compute-matched budgets, eligibility-trace plasticity (E-prop) aligns learned wiring geometry to the connectome more efficiently than REINFORCE-style policy gradients. Additional trajectory and motif analyses show that this advantage is visible over training, while motif similarity and geometry alignment capture partly distinct aspects of biological realism. Taken together, these results support a conservative interpretation: the optic lobe connectome contains compressible structure, that structure supports decodable signal, the real graph is more energy-efficient than matched nulls within the Part D regime, and eligibility-trace learning provides a plausible route toward that regime.

Introduction

Sensory systems operate under finite metabolic budgets, and neural signaling is one of the dominant costs those budgets must support1–3. The core tension is simple to state and hard to satisfy: sensory systems must transmit and transform signals reliably, yet every spike and synaptic event consumes energy1,2,4. Energy budgets are not small corrections; they can shape coding strategies, circuit architectures, and even evolutionary trajectories3,5. A growing body of theory argues that efficient sensory systems should maximize task-relevant information per unit cost, rather than maximizing information alone6–8. The open challenge is measurement: in real biological circuits, where should we draw the boundary between “structure”, “function”, and “cost” and how can we quantify their trade-offs without drifting into narrative hand-waving?

Connectomics offers a new experimental axis for this problem. Dense reconstructions now map synapses at scale, providing concrete constraints on who can influence whom and at what wiring distance9–11. The optic lobe is an especially attractive testbed: it is large, layered, retinotopically organized, and exhibits algorithmically interpretable computations such as local motion detection. However, wiring diagrams alone do not answer whether a circuit is efficient: we need explicit models that (i) quantify compressible regularities in the graph, (ii) expose what information is supported by connectome-constrained dynamics, and (iii) account for energetic and wiring costs.

We therefore frame the optic lobe connectome as an energy-constrained communication system. Our analysis is organized into five parts that move from “what is there” to “what it does” to “how it could be learned”:Part A (Data integrity) defines canonical neuron subsets and audits cell-type and retinotopy metadata, emphasizing that missingness is structured rather than random.Part B (Structural information) fits explicit probabilistic graph models and computes minimum description length (MDL) in bits, interpreting compression as a measure of circuit regularity12.Part C (Functional information) stimulates a connectome-constrained dynamical model and estimates decoder-based mutual-information lower bounds, with strict null controls13,14.Part D (Energy efficiency) augments the functional analysis with an energetic proxy motivated by empirical signaling costs1,2, comparing real wiring to matched nulls.Part E (Plasticity) benchmarks candidate learning rules for aligning wiring geometry under cost penalties, highlighting compute-matched comparisons between eligibility-trace learning and policy-gradient baselines15–17.Our goal is not to claim an ultimate “optimality” of the connectome, which would be an extraordinary and likely false statement. Instead, we test a narrower, falsifiable hypothesis: structured optic lobe wiring supports decodable visual information at lower energetic cost than matched randomizations, and local eligibility-trace plasticity provides a plausible mechanism for approaching this efficient regime. The Part B asks whether the connectome is statistically compressible. Part C asks whether that structured wiring supports decodable signal. Part D asks whether comparable information can be carried more efficiently by the real graph than by matched nulls. Part E then asks whether a biologically plausible local learning rule can move a circuit toward that same regime. Framing the study this way helps separate what is measured directly from what is inferred mechanistically.

Results

Part A Data integrity and retinotopic coverage set the constraints for inference

Any efficiency claim is only as good as the metadata that supports it. We first audit the canonical dataset used in downstream analyses: cell-type labels, retinotopy coordinates, and synapse-weight distributions. A majority of neurons in the analyzed subset have type annotations, while retinotopy mapping is substantially less complete (Fig. 1). Two implications follow immediately. First, geometry-dependent models must explicitly restrict to mapped neurons or adopt principled missing-data treatments; otherwise spatial effects can be confounded by selection. Second, missingness can bias network statistics: the mapped subset differs in degree/connectivity patterns from the full typed population (Fig. 1), meaning that “mapped-core” results are not automatically representative of “all typed” neurons.

To make these constraints explicit, we carry two aligned datasets through the pipeline: a typed population (maximizing inclusion for type-dependent analyses) and a mapped core (restricting to neurons with reliable retinotopy for spatial analyses). We treat differences between these subsets as informative (selection effects) rather than as mere noise. In particular, whenever a spatial model appears to change conclusions, we ask whether the change persists when restricted to a comparable subset. This point is easy to overlook but important for the rest of the study. Parts B and C ask somewhat different questions and do not use identical subsets; if we ignored that fact, we could mistake a sampling effect for a biological effect. Part B Structural information: type symmetries compress the connectome; spatial extensions require conservative treatment

We quantify structural regularity via minimum description length (MDL), interpreted as the number of bits required to describe the connectome after exploiting a model’s symmetries12. For a graph G and fitted parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{\theta }$$\end{document}, the description length is1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} L(G) = -\log _2 p(G \mid \hat{\theta }) + \textrm{penalty}(\hat{\theta }), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where the penalty discourages over-parameterization (BIC). Lower is better.

We compare three nested models. M0 is an independent-edge baseline with a single connection probability (Erdős-Rényi-like). M2 is a type-dependent stochastic block model (SBM), where connection probabilities depend on pre/post cell types18,19. M3 extends M2 by allowing edge probabilities to vary with retinotopic offset (a spatial/graphon-like term), intended to capture convolutional structure implied by retinotopy.

Type structure yields a robust MDL improvement. Relative to M0, the type SBM compresses the connectome by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim$$\end{document}9% in the typed dataset and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim$$\end{document}16% in the mapped core (Fig. 2). This makes a qualitative observation quantitative: much of the wiring diagram is explained by cell-type identity, consistent with repeated motifs and stereotyped connectivity rules in the optic lobe.

Spatial extensions require more care. In our most conservative MDL setting (strict BIC), a flexible distance-binned spatial model can be penalized heavily, such that total L(G) increases even if the likelihood improves (Supplementary Fig. S2). We do not interpret this as “no geometry”; rather, it indicates that naive spatial parameterizations can overfit unless regularized or pooled. Accordingly, we treat M3 as a diagnostic tool: it suggests residual geometry in the likelihood term, but our main structural claim is anchored on the robust type-level compression. We now report held-out Poisson NLL diagnostics on the raw mapped-core graph as well. Under an exact dyad split, M2 generalizes substantially better than M0 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.0742\pm 0.0003$$\end{document} versus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.1318\pm 0.0002$$\end{document} bits per held-out dyad). For M3, an additional large-sample dyad evaluation shows that real coordinates outperform a coordinate-shuffled spatial null (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.416\pm 0.041$$\end{document} versus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.527\pm 0.037$$\end{document} bits per held-out dyad), but the unregularized distance-binned model still underperforms M2 on held-out dyads. We therefore interpret residual geometry as present but not yet parameterized parsimoniously enough to displace M2 as the selected structural model. In simple terms, Part B now supports two separate statements. First, cell-type organization is a robust and compressible regularity of the optic lobe graph. Second, spatial structure is real, but our current M3 implementation is better viewed as evidence that geometry matters than as the final model one should select. Part C Functional information: decoder-based bounds show decodable signal that vanishes under null controls

Structural regularity is not the same as computation. We therefore ask whether connectome-constrained dynamics carry decodable information about a stimulus variable. We stimulate a dynamical model with motion labels S (four-way; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H(S)=2$$\end{document} bits when balanced) and compute population responses R. To quantify information without full density estimation, we use a decoder-based variational bound. For a decoder \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q_\phi (s\mid r)$$\end{document} with cross-entropy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{CE}$$\end{document} in bits, a standard variational argument yields2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} I(S;R) \ge I_{\textrm{lb}}(S;R) = \max \left( 0, H(S) - \textrm{CE}_{\textrm{bits}}\right) , \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where we clip at zero because mutual information is non-negative by definition13,14. This estimate is intentionally conservative: it can increase with better decoders, richer readouts, or improved dynamics models.

A global linear readout from the real connectome achieves above-chance accuracy and a positive information bound (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}=0.408\pm 0.050$$\end{document} bits across five seeds; Fig. 3a). Two null controls collapse this bound to zero. Label shuffling (destroying stimulus-response coupling) yields chance accuracy and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}=0$$\end{document}, as required. Connectivity shuffling (destroying structured motifs while preserving coarse statistics) also yields chance accuracy and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}=0$$\end{document}, indicating that decodable signal depends on structured wiring rather than on trivial decoder biases. To make the decoder dependence explicit rather than merely asserted, we added a matched-split sensitivity analysis with two alternative low-capacity decoders applied to the archived raw Part C activity tensors (Supplementary Fig. S11). In that sensitivity analysis, LabelShuffle still collapses to zero, but ConnShuffle no longer does. This is precisely why we keep the Part C claim narrow: the canonical result is tied to the linear readout and simulation regime used in the main Part C analysis, not to a decoder-invariant estimate of the exact information carried by the circuit.

Because optic lobe computations are retinotopically organized, we also perform spatially restricted decoding in retinotopic tiles. Local readouts yield smaller information bounds on average (Fig. 3b), consistent with early-stage information being distributed across the visual field rather than pooled globally. However, this local comparison is statistically underpowered in the present release: across the five paired seeds the real-minus-label-shuffle difference is positive but the paired permutation test gives \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p=0.063$$\end{document}. We therefore treat the local effect as trend-level rather than as standalone confirmatory evidence and retain it mainly as a spatial diagnostic. Tile sampling density is heterogeneous (Supplementary Fig. S3), motivating minimum-count thresholds and explicit uncertainty reporting. Part D Energetic accounting reveals higher bits-per-energy in the real connectome than in matched null networks

Information transmission is only meaningful if we also account for its energetic price. We define an energetic proxy combining synaptic signaling costs and wiring-distance costs, motivated by empirical constraints on cortical energy budgets1,2. Because exact coefficients are uncertain, we emphasize (i) comparisons to matched nulls and (ii) robustness across plausible weightings (Supplementary Fig. S5). In compact form, we write3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} E_{\textrm{tot}} = \alpha E_{\textrm{met}} + \beta E_{\textrm{syn}} + \gamma E_{\textrm{wire}}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{met}}$$\end{document} summarizes total activity over the trial, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{syn}}$$\end{document} weights that activity by outgoing synaptic strength, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{wire}}$$\end{document} adds an activity-weighted distance penalty to active long-range connections. These are reported in arbitrary proxy units rather than physical Joules, with a small fixed baseline term held outside the sweep (Supplementary Methods). The coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma )$$\end{document} therefore do not claim to be exact ATP conversion factors. They define a family of plausible cost weightings, which is why the study focuses on robustness across the sweep rather than on one absolute number.

Part D uses a distinct operating regime from Part C. The decoder, response aggregation, and null suite are chosen to test energy efficiency within a matched family of topology and weight controls, rather than to reproduce the strict negative controls of the canonical Part C benchmark. We therefore compare Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. S6 only within this regime, not directly against the absolute \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}$$\end{document} values from Part C.

We compare the real connectome to two matched controls: Null_Conn, a coarse connectivity-target shuffle that disrupts source-target assignments, and Null_Strength, a fixed-topology weight-permutation control. These are intentionally modest nulls rather than exact degree- or reciprocity-preserving ensembles, so our claim is correspondingly modest as well. Across weightings, the real network exhibits a favorable information-energy trade-off. Accordingly, the central Part D conclusion is about efficiency relative to these matched controls, not about a large raw-information gap.

Part E Eligibility-trace learning robustly aligns wiring geometry under cost constraints and outperforms REINFORCE under compute-matched budgets

Structural and energetic signatures raise a mechanistic question: what kind of synaptic plasticity could plausibly construct or refine a circuit toward an efficient wiring regime? We evaluate candidate learning rules on a connectome-alignment objective that rewards reproducing structured connectivity while penalizing wiring/energy costs. Candidate rules include eligibility-trace learning (E-prop), which uses local synaptic traces combined with global modulatory/error signals15,16, and a classic policy-gradient baseline (REINFORCE)17. All rules are compared against the same high-level objective,4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} J = I_{\textrm{lb}} - \lambda E_{\textrm{tot}}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}so the comparison is not between different goals, but between different optimization strategies for the same goal. To make that comparison mechanistically explicit, note that a recurrent weight \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{ij}$$\end{document} affects J only through the postsynaptic state trajectory it helps generate. Writing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_i(t)$$\end{document} for the state of neuron i, the chain rule gives\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{\partial J}{\partial w_{ij}} \approx \sum _t \underbrace{\frac{\partial J}{\partial x_i(t)}}_{L_i(t)\ \mathrm {(learning signal)}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial x_i(t)}{\partial w_{ij}}}_{e_{ij}(t)\ \mathrm {(eligibility trace)}},$$\end{document}so the update naturally factorizes into a synapse-local term \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e_{ij}(t)$$\end{document} and a neuron-level learning signal \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_i(t)$$\end{document}. In the rate-model implementation, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e_{ij}(t)$$\end{document} can be updated online from presynaptic activity and the local slope of the postsynaptic nonlinearity, without storing a full history of the network.

The energy term enters this same learning signal rather than being attached as a post hoc regularizer after learning. Because \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J=I_{\textrm{lb}}-\lambda E_{\textrm{tot}}$$\end{document}, we can write\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_i(t)= \frac{\partial I_{\textrm{lb}}}{\partial x_i(t)} -\lambda \frac{\partial E_{\textrm{tot}}}{\partial x_i(t)}.$$\end{document}The first term reinforces activity patterns that improve decoder performance, whereas the second suppresses activity patterns that increase metabolic, synaptic, or wiring-related cost without sufficient informational gain. E-prop therefore approximates the gradient of the same information-energy objective using local traces and a low-dimensional broadcast signal. REINFORCE, by contrast, estimates improvement by perturbing weights and comparing noisy outcomes. This difference matters because the perturbation-based strategy generally requires many more forward passes to obtain a usable learning signal.

Viewed this way, the components of the study are not independent modules. Structural information enters through the fixed connectome mask and geometry, which determine which synapses exist and over what distances they act. Functional dynamics enter through the recurrent responses that drive the decoder and determine \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}$$\end{document}. Energetics enter through \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{met}}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{syn}}$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{wire}}$$\end{document}. A learning rule satisfies all three criteria only if it can use local synaptic traces to increase decoder-based information on that anatomical scaffold while simultaneously pushing the solution away from globally expensive activity patterns and long weighted edges. Supplementary Note S3 provides the full derivation, but the main point is simple: in our framework, structure, dynamics, and energy are coupled at every update step rather than compared only after the fact.

Across retinotopic patches, eligibility-trace learning achieves substantially better alignment to the connectome (lower distance-to-connectome) and wins the majority of patches (Fig. 5a). This advantage persists across patch sizes (Fig. 5b), indicating that the effect is not a narrow artifact of one scale. Crucially, we also benchmark compute fairness: matching methods by the number of forward passes reveals that eligibility-trace learning reaches strong alignment with far fewer passes than REINFORCE (Fig. 5c). Archived training logs from the representative v6.3 patch confirm that this endpoint advantage is visible throughout training rather than only at convergence (Supplementary Fig. S8): E-prop reaches its best objective at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 186$$\end{document} forward passes while driving the normalized energy proxy to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 0.40$$\end{document} of its initial value, whereas REINFORCE requires \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 509$$\end{document} forward passes and remains near its initial energy level even when its decoder information eventually increases. This matters biologically: a plausible learning rule should be not only local, but also sample-efficient under noisy feedback.

We interpret these results conservatively. They do not establish that any single learning rule is uniquely realized in vivo. Rather, they support a broader conclusion: learning mechanisms based on local eligibility traces provide a robust and computationally efficient route to achieving wiring objectives that are consistent with observed connectome structure under energetic constraints. To connect these optimization results back to circuit organization, we now analyze motifs at two levels. First, a representative-patch rerun with saved final weights (Supplementary Fig. S9) shows that learned graphs do not become motif-random: they retain structured recurrent and reciprocal organization, but with rule-specific signatures. Second, a new multi-patch motif generalization analysis (Supplementary Fig. S10) shows that geometry alignment and motif similarity are related but not identical targets. In that broader rerun, E-prop remains the best rule in connectome-alignment distance across all tested patches, whereas REINFORCE is often closest to the biological patch in the combined motif-fingerprint space. Interpretation and limitations

Three themes emerge. First, the connectome is structurally redundant in a measurable way: type symmetries yield significant compression under MDL. Second, structured wiring supports decodable motion information in a connectome-constrained dynamics model, but all mutual-information values reported here are conservative lower bounds and depend on the decoder, operating point, and null family. Third, energetic efficiency is visible relative to matched nulls: our claim is not global optimality, but a consistent reduction in energetic cost for comparable information transfer in the Part D regime.

Several limitations deserve explicit emphasis. The local decoding result remains underpowered with five paired seeds, so we now describe it as trend-level rather than definitive. The Part C and Part D information analyses use different readout/null configurations; we therefore avoid direct cross-panel comparison of their raw \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I_{\textrm{lb}}$$\end{document} values and instead focus on the within-regime claims each analysis can support. A new decoder-family sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Fig. S11) also shows that some Part C condition rankings are not invariant to preprocessing and decoder choice, reinforcing the need to interpret all reported values as lower bounds within a fixed decoder family rather than as absolute information measurements. Our energy model is a proxy, not a direct metabolic measurement; we therefore emphasize robustness across weightings rather than absolute ATP units. Our dynamical model is rate-based for tractability; extending to spiking models and validating against physiology would strengthen biological interpretation. Finally, our learning-rule comparison records forward-pass and runtime costs as explicit compute proxies, but it does not directly measure the physical energy consumed by optimization hardware.

Discussion

Energy-efficient information processing is often invoked as a guiding principle for sensory systems, but it is less often quantified in a way that cleanly links structure, function, energetics, and learning. In this study, instead of asking whether the optic lobe is globally optimal, we ask whether the available connectome supports a consistent picture across several measurable levels. Our results support four linked conclusions.

Symmetry as compression and wiring economy The MDL compression achieved by type-dependent models quantifies the extent to which optic lobe wiring contains repeated motifs and regularities. Such regularities reduce “description length” not only in a statistical sense, but plausibly also in developmental and genetic specification terms12,5. From a physical perspective, repeated local motifs are consistent with wiring economy constraints that favor short-range, repeated connectivity over bespoke long-range edges7,20.

Information is distributed, and the reported values are regime-specific lower bounds Decoder-based bounds indicate that the real connectome supports decodable motion information, while this signal vanishes under the strict Part C negative controls. The study now makes equally explicit that Part D answers a different question with a different operating regime. That clarification is important because it prevents the reader from comparing unlike with unlike: Part C establishes non-zero decodable signal under canonical label/connectivity controls, whereas Part D tests whether the real graph is more efficient than matched topology- and weight-allocation nulls. In both cases, the reported values are lower bounds rather than exact mutual-information estimates. That is a conservative choice in a high-dimensional biological system, where exact information estimation is generally unstable and strongly estimator-dependent13,21,14.

Structure, dynamics, and energetics meet in a single objective The most compact mechanistic summary of the paper is the objective \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J = I_{\textrm{lb}} - \lambda E_{\textrm{tot}}$$\end{document}. Structural regularities constrain which interactions are available, functional dynamics determine which of those interactions yield decodable motion signals, and the energetic term penalizes activity and long wiring. The key outcome here is that this bridge is now stated at the level of the update rule rather than only at the level of the final score. In the learning analysis, each synapse carries a local eligibility trace that records its recent causal contribution, while a neuron-level learning signal contains both an information-seeking component and an energy-penalizing component. The anatomical scaffold therefore enters through the masked connectome and distances, the functional term enters through the decoder-based lower bound, and the energetic term enters through the explicit gradients of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{met}}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{syn}}$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{\textrm{wire}}$$\end{document}. The rule does not optimize these criteria in three separate stages. It combines them at each update, favoring changes that improve decoder information on the observed scaffold while disfavoring changes that achieve that gain only through higher overall activity or longer effective wiring. That is the sense in which structural information, functional dynamics, and energetics are coupled rather than being separate post hoc analyses.

Eligibility traces remain favorable under matched compute proxies Eligibility-trace plasticity outperforms REINFORCE under compute-matched budgets, aligning with theoretical expectations about variance reduction and credit assignment in recurrent systems15,17. At 200 forward passes, E-prop reaches \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D_{3z}=1.27$$\end{document}, compared with 1.36 for RewardHebb and 3.36 for REINFORCE; even when REINFORCE is granted 10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document} more forward passes, its median distance remains worse (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D_{3z}=2.28$$\end{document}). The new trajectory analysis in Supplementary Fig. S8 strengthens this comparison by showing the full optimization paths: E-prop improves the combined objective while reducing the normalized energy proxy to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 0.40$$\end{document} of its initial value, RewardHebb reaches an even lower-energy regime but with weaker information gain, REINFORCE stays much higher-energy for most of training, and Oja shows little improvement in the combined objective. The archived size-1000 benchmark also records practical runtime: matched-budget E-prop and RewardHebb both run in about 4 seconds, whereas the 2000-forward REINFORCE reference takes about 9 seconds. Put differently, E-prop is not simply “better at the end”; it is more efficient in the way it gets there. We interpret forward passes and runtime as practical compute proxies rather than literal metabolic measurements of learning, but the ranking is still informative: a local eligibility-based rule achieves better alignment without requiring larger optimization budgets.

Motif organization provides a biological interpretation layer The motif analysis is now both broader and more careful. A representative-patch rerun (Supplementary Fig. S9) saves the learned final weights for each rule, aggregates them to type-level graphs, and compares focal triads against degree-preserving rewired nulls. A further multi-patch rerun (Supplementary Fig. S10) shows that these motif signatures are not confined to one example patch. The biological initialization combines high reciprocity with enriched recurrent motifs relative to those nulls. Learned rules preserve that non-random structure, but not in the same way. E-prop and RewardHebb tend to maintain stronger recurrent-triad enrichment while also achieving the best geometry-alignment distances; REINFORCE is often closer to the biological patch in the combined motif-fingerprint distance despite being worse in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D_{3z}$$\end{document}. This partial dissociation is useful rather than problematic: motif similarity is not just a disguised version of the geometry score, but an additional descriptor of what each learning rule preserves. The analysis is still type-level rather than cell-level, so it should be read as a structured interpretation layer rather than a full motif census.

What the study does and does not claim Taken together, Parts B-E support a coherent but deliberately modest picture. The optic lobe connectome is statistically regular, those regularities support decodable motion-related signals, and the real graph carries comparable information more efficiently than matched nulls in the Part D regime. A local eligibility-based learning rule can move learned graphs toward the geometric and energetic profile of the connectome more efficiently than perturbation-based baselines. What we do not claim is that the optic lobe is globally optimal, that our energy proxy is a direct metabolic assay, that the reported information values are exact, or that one learning rule has been uniquely identified as the rule used in vivo.

More broadly, energy-efficient computation can be viewed as a thermodynamic problem: information processing cannot be dissociated from physical cost, and constraints like Landauer’s principle provide a fundamental lower bound on energy dissipation per erased bit22. Biological circuits operate far above that limit, but the direction of pressure is the same: reduce unnecessary activity, reduce wiring length, and concentrate energetic investment where it yields information gains. Our analyses offer a quantitative path toward that viewpoint in connectomics.

Future work can sharpen the picture by (i) increasing the number of seeds for local decoding and rerunning the full dyad-split held-out NLL evaluation for spatial models; (ii) incorporating richer cell-intrinsic nonlinearities and synaptic dynamics; (iii) connecting energetic proxies to measured ATP/oxygen consumption or calcium imaging; and (iv) extending motif analyses from type-level summaries to cell-level subcircuits and identified motion pathways.

Methods

Connectome data and canonical subsets

We use the optic lobe connectome and associated metadata provided via neuPrint/FlyEM resources9,10. Neurons are assigned cell types where available. Retinotopy coordinates define “mapped” neurons and retinotopic tiles. We analyze (i) a typed dataset maximizing inclusion for type-dependent analyses and (ii) a mapped-core subset requiring retinotopy for spatial analyses. Quality control includes synaptic-weight distribution checks and audits of missingness biases (Fig. 1, Supplementary Fig. S1).

Structural models and MDL

We fit probabilistic generative models for directed edges. The independent-edge baseline (M0) assumes a single connection probability. The type SBM (M2) assigns probabilities by pre/post type. The spatial extension (M3) modulates probabilities by retinotopic offset. Parameters are fit by maximum likelihood. Description length is computed as negative log-likelihood plus a complexity penalty (BIC-style) in bits (Eq. 1), enabling model comparison18,19,12. Supplementary Fig. S2 decomposes likelihood gains and penalty terms for spatial extensions.

Dynamical model and information bounds

We simulate connectome-constrained dynamics in a recurrent rate model with inputs corresponding to motion labels and outputs corresponding to population activity. We compute decoder-based mutual-information lower bounds using Eq. 2, with cross-entropy expressed in base-2 units. Global decoding uses a linear classifier on all neurons; local decoding restricts to neurons within retinotopic tiles. Null controls include label shuffling and connectivity shuffling to dissociate decoder artifacts from structured-wiring effects (Fig. 3).

Energetic cost model and efficiency

We define an energetic proxy combining synaptic transmission cost and wiring-distance cost. Costs are aggregated over activity and synaptic weights, with coefficients swept over a grid to assess sensitivity (Supplementary Fig. S5). Bits-per-energy efficiency is computed as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta = I_{\textrm{lb}} / E$$\end{document}. Matched null networks preserve weight/degree statistics to isolate the contribution of structured connectivity and geometry (Fig. 4).

Learning rules and benchmarking

We optimize the energy-constrained objective in Eq. 4 over spatial patches using multiple learning rules. The main-text mechanistic form is\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta w_{ij} \propto \sum _t L_i(t)\,e_{ij}(t),$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e_{ij}(t)$$\end{document} is a local eligibility trace and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_i(t)=\partial I_{\textrm{lb}}/\partial x_i(t)-\lambda \,\partial E_{\textrm{tot}}/\partial x_i(t)$$\end{document} is the neuron-level learning signal. In other words, the energy term is introduced directly into the same update that carries task information: it is not a separate after-the-fact penalty. Eligibility-trace learning (E-prop) uses this factorization to provide an online approximation to recurrent credit assignment15,16. REINFORCE provides a stochastic perturbation-based policy-gradient baseline17. RewardHebb and Oja serve as additional local baselines with simpler update structure.

This organization is what allows one learning rule to satisfy the study’s three main criteria at once. The connectome mask and distances define the structural scaffold on which updates are allowed; the recurrent dynamics generate the activity trajectories that determine the decoder lower bound; and the energy components bias learning away from globally expensive solutions. We evaluate the resulting rules by (i) patch-wise generalization (distance-to-connectome), (ii) robustness across patch sizes, and (iii) compute fairness by matching forward-pass budgets (Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. S7). Additional archived-log trajectory diagnostics, representative-patch motif fingerprints, and multi-patch motif generalization are reported in Supplementary Figs. S8-S10.

Research and Evaluation of a Nanometer Plugging Agent for Shale Gas Horizontal Wells
Authors: Yuexin Tian, Xiangjun Liu, Yintao Liu, Haifeng Dong, Guodong Zhang, Biao Su, Xiaoli Yang, Jinjun Huang, Lixi Liang, Jian Xiong, Xiao Zhuo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDue to the low permeability of shales, drilling fluid filtrate is very likely to intrude into the formation along the nanomicron pore joints of shale, leading to microfracture expansion and causing a well wall destabilization phenomenon. Based on the characteristics of the formation shale, a new nano plugging agent, styrene (St)/2-acrylamido-2-methylpropanesulfonic acid (AMPS)/ethyl acrylate (EA), was synthesized by emulsion polymerization using St, EA, and AMPS as reaction monomers. The analysis results using infrared spectroscopy and transmission electron microscopy showed that the product met the expected design. The particle size of the nano plugging agent St/AMPS/EA was mainly concentrated in the range of 50–130 nm. Thermogravimetric analysis revealed that the initial thermal decomposition temperature of St/AMPS/EA is 363.8 °C, indicating strong thermal stability. At a 3% concentration, the permeability of the mud cake decreased by 87.35%, and the maximum differential stress of the shale increased from 141.7 to 174.4 MPa. Stability analysis showed that the plugging agent maintained good plugging performance under various pH values and salinity conditions with fluid loss consistently around 3.2 mm. This nano plugging agent significantly improves wellbore stability, making it suitable for drilling in the Longmaxi Formation shale with developed microfractures, and provides valuable insights for similar formations.

Introduction

At present, the horizontal well drilling technology is predominantly utilized in the Longmaxi Formation shale. To maintain the stability of the wellbore during drilling, most wells use long horizontal sections with oil-based drilling fluids.1−5 However, these fluids present challenges including high costs, considerable environmental pollution, and issues with safe waste disposal, occasionally leading to destabilization of the wellbore.6 In light of environmental concerns, there is a shift toward water-based drilling fluids as a more environmentally friendly alternative to oil-based ones.7 Additionally, the usual pressure difference between the drilling fluid column and formation pressure can cause drilling fluids to penetrate the shale’s nanopore joints. This penetration leads to the hydration and expansion of clay minerals within the shale, leading to wellbore instability, manifesting as shale spalling and blockage.8 Addressing the blocking of these nanopores to prevent liquid intrusion is crucial for ensuring long-term well-wall stability.9 Conventional sealants, including nanosilica, nanoiron trioxide, bitumen, and ultrafine calcium carbonate, are ineffective at forming seals in the shale’s nanopore joints. The particles fail to enter these joints, accumulating externally and offering no long-term stabilization.

Advancements in nanotechnology have significantly influenced drilling fluid formulations, as evidenced by a series of developments from 2005 to 2016. In 2005, Xiang and Clapper10 developed MAX-SHIELD, a nanosized plugging polymer with deformability, primarily composed of hydroxybutadiene emulsion, with a main particle size below 0.2 μm as determined by laser measurement. Del et al.,11 in 2007, developed a nanoparaffin emulsion with a 30–80 nm particle size using a homogenizing method, exhibiting excellent stability and heat resistance. By 2011, Spisak12 showed that nanoparticles (NPs) could effectively seal Atoka Shale pore throats and fractures, enhancing the efficiency of foam-drilling fluids. In 2014, Wang et al.13 synthesized a polymeric NP (PNP), a spherical nanomaterial with a core–shell structure, measuring 375 nm in size, which significantly improved the various properties of the base slurry without affecting drilling fluid rheology. In the same year, El Sherbeny et al.14 synthesized a new polymeric NP, optimized for blocking effects in both water-based and oil-based drilling fluids. Xie,15 in 2015, synthesized water-based nanoblockers using piperazine, diethylenetriamine, and divinyl sulfone, with the particle size predominantly under 100 nm and excellent thermal stability. By 2016, Zhao et al.16 developed a nanoemulsified paraffin (PF-EPF) with exceptional low-temperature resistance, significantly reducing active pharmaceuticalingredient (API) filtration loss in water-based drilling fluid. Concurrently, Wang et al.17 synthesized polymeric NP SD-seal with a median size of 20 nm, capable of effectively reducing the permeability of the Longmaxi Formation shale by more than 90%. These developments illustrate the progressive evolution of nanobased sealants in enhancing drilling fluid performance and wellbore stability.

This study specifically designed and developed the styrene (St)/2-acrylamido-2-methylpropanesulfonic acid (AMPS)/ethyl acrylate (EA) nano blocking agent to meet the specific needs of the Longmaxi Formation shale in Sichuan, optimizing its application effectiveness in this formation. St/AMPS/EA was synthesized through a soap-free emulsion polymerization using St, EA, and AMPS. The comprehensive characterization of St/AMPS/EA's structure was conducted using infrared spectroscopy, particle size analysis, transmission electron microscopy, and atomic force microscopy. Its blocking efficacy was rigorously evaluated through shale microfracture simulation experiments and tensile strength tests on the shale samples. The results demonstrated that St/AMPS/EA exhibits exceptional blocking capabilities with notable hydrophobicity and stability, making it particularly suitable for drilling operations in the microfracture-prone Longmaxi Formation shale. This research not only provides a practical solution for microfracture plugging in the Longmaxi Formation but also serves as a reference for addressing similar challenges in other formations.

Experimental Section

Materials

St (analytical grade), EA (analytical grade), AMPS (analytical grade), and potassium persulfate (analytical grade) were used.

Nano WSD

Nano WSD is composed mainly of nanosilica (SiO2), polyethylene glycol (PEG), propylene glycol (PG), and deionized water. Nanosilica is the primary component, accounting for 50% of the total weight. The nanosilica particles have a size range of 20–30 nm, offering a high specific surface area and good dispersibility, which helps fill microfractures and nanopores. Polyethylene glycol, serving as a dispersant, constitutes 30% of the total weight and effectively disperses the nanosilica particles to prevent aggregation. Propylene glycol, accounting for 10% of the total weight, is used to adjust the solution’s viscosity, making it easier to inject. Deionized water makes up the remaining 10% of the solvent.

Nano NR-1

NR-1 primarily consists of nano alumina (Al2O3), poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA), ethylene glycol (EG), and deionized water. Nano alumina is the main component, making up 60% of the total weight. The nano alumina particles are sized between 10 and 20 nm, providing excellent heat resistance and abrasion resistance, which enhances the blocking effect. PVA, used as a film-forming agent, constitutes 20% of the total weight and forms a robust film on the fracture surfaces, improving blocking strength. EG, comprising 10% of the total weight, adjusts the solution’s viscosity for better flowability. Deionized water, the remaining 10%, serves as the solvent.

Synthesis of the Nano St/AMPS/EA Plugging Agent

100 g of deionized water was added to a four-necked flask with an electric stirring paddle and a condenser tube, and 18.2 g of St, 2.1 g of EA, and 10.8 g of AMPS were added sequentially under stirring conditions; they were stirred for 30 min at room temperature at 2000 r/min, pre-emulsified for 30 min at room temperature, and oxygen was discharged by passing nitrogen. Subsequently, the temperature was increased to 30 °C while the speed was reduced to 1500 r/min, and it was stirred for 30 min; subsequently, 1 g of initiator potassium persulfate was added dropwise under stirring conditions, and the reaction was slowly increased to 80 °C for 3 h. Subsequently, the reaction was held for 1 h, cooled to room temperature, and filtered through a 100-mesh filter tank to obtain a viscous white liquid nano blocking material solution. Stability Analysis

The stability of the blocking agent is a prerequisite for its blocking effect, which is tested by a TLAB dispersion stability analyzer (model MA2000). The light emitted from the NIR light source is directed to the sample, and part of the light is transmitted through the sample and received by the transmittance detector, while the other part is reflected by the reflectance detector. The two detectors convert the received light into data information expressed as a percentage. The optical detector head is scanned every 40 μm along the sample from bottom to top to collect the transmitted and reflected light, and the scanning curve depicts the relationship between the transmitted and reflected light with the height of the sample at different moments. Results and Discussion

Shale Microstructure Analysis

Nitrogen Adsorption Analysis

The specific surface area is an important parameter to measure the adsorption characteristics of shale. A large specific surface area often increases shale hydration, which has a great impact on rock mechanical parameters.18 The larger the specific surface area, the greater the hydration and swelling capacity, and the greater the impact on the stability of the shale borehole wall, the smaller the specific surface area, the weaker the hydration and swelling capacity, and the less the impact on the stability of the borehole.19

In this study, the N2–BET method was used to measure the specific surface area, that is, the amount of nitrogen adsorbed by the shale samples at different pressures at a liquid nitrogen temperature of −196 °C. The specific surface area of shale was calculated according to the multilayer adsorption theory proposed by Brunauer, Emmett, and Teller.20 The experiments were analyzed and tested by a NOVA2000 specific surface area analyzer from Conta, USA. The results of the specific surface area tests are shown in Figure 2a,b.

Specific surface area and high-pressure piezometric pore size distribution curves of Longmaxi Formation shale: (a) specific surface area test of the Longmaxi Formation outcrop shale, (b) specific pore volume test of the Longmaxi Formation outcrop shale, (c) pore size distribution of the shale rock samples from the Longmaxi Formation, and (d) pore size distribution of the shale outcrop rock samples.

High-Pressure Mercury Pressing Analysis

The pore size distribution measured by high-pressure mercury piezometry can reflect the development of microfractures and pore spaces and their connectivity. The pore size distribution characteristics of rock samples and outcrop rock samples of the Longmaxi Formation shale formation were tested with a high-pressure mercury piezometer, and the test results are shown in Figure 2c,d.

As can be seen from Figure 2a,b, the specific surface area distribution of the tested Longmaxi Formation outcrop shale ranges from 0.476 to 8.610 m2/g, and the specific pore volume distribution ranges from 0.0014 to 0.0221 mL/g, indicating that the majority of pores in this shale matrix are nanopores with relatively weak hydration and swelling capacity. As shown in Figure 2c,d, the Longmaxi Formation shale exhibits a broad pore size distribution, ranging from a few nanometers all the way to tens of micrometers, and the mesoporous (2–50 nm) pores of the shale matrix are developed, with peaks at 3–10 nm, indicating the presence of micro- and nanoscale fractures in the Longmaxi Formation shale. As the organic carbon content in shale increases, the specific surface area and specific pore volume of shale increase, the microporous structure becomes more pronounced, the nonhomogeneous pore structure becomes more evident, and the shale adsorption capacity increases, and it is difficult to detach the adsorbed gas, so it is difficult for the liquid phase to enter the mesopores. For the pore structure and microfractures of the shale with a pore size larger than 50 nm, the liquid phase, under the effect of capillary self-absorption and pressure transfer, can have mass transfer and energy transfer with the adsorbed gas in the shale pores, which reduces the strength of shale. The pore characteristics of the Longmaxi Formation shale are the basis for selecting the size of the blocking agent. Only by blocking these microscale and nanoscale fractures and pores, where mass and energy transfer can occur, can the liquid-phase intrusion and pressure transfer be effectively stopped.

Microfracture Conditions in the Longmaxi Formation Shale

Relevant research literature shows21,22 that the Longmaxi Formation shale is highly compacted and tightly structured, with microfracture development; the openness of its microfractures is around 5 μm and the length is around 30 μm; the pores formed by the sedimentation of the formation are evident, and intergranular micropores exist in the rock samples; the diameter of the pores is about 10 μm; and the existence of these micropores and microfractures is a good storage space for shale gas and its extraction seepage. The existence of these micropores and microfractures is a good channel for shale gas storage space and its extraction, but it also becomes a channel for drilling fluid filtrate intrusion and hydrostatic pressure transfer, which can easily induce well-wall instability in shale formations.23

From the perspective of rock mechanics, the presence of microfractures can compromise the rock’s integrity and diminish its mechanical properties while providing a channel for drilling fluid filtrate and pressure transfer during the drilling process. Under the joint action of a positive drilling pressure difference, chemical potential difference, and capillary force, the drilling fluid filtrate intrudes into the formation along the fractures or microfractures, which may induce hydraulic splitting and intensify the rock fragmentation of the formation at the well wall on the one hand and increase the chance and degree of interaction between the drilling fluid and the clay minerals and organic matter in the formation on the other, which reduces the formation strength and intensifies well wall destabilization.24

Thermogravimetric Analysis

In this section, thermal weight loss curves (TGs) and microquotient weight loss curves (DTGs) were measured using a thermogravimetric analyzer (model STA449F3) for evaluating the thermal stability of the blocking agent St/AMPS/EA, and the results are shown in Figure 6.

Thermogravimetric analysis of blocking agent St/AMPS/EA.

As can be seen from Figure 6, with the increase in temperature, the St/AMPS/EA sample weight initially remained basically unchanged; when the temperature increased to 339.50 °C, the sample mass began to decrease and the TG curve changed, consistent with the microquotient DTG curve, then the starting weight loss temperature T of the sample became 363.80 °C; as the temperature continued to increase to 434.67 °C, the DTG curve appeared to peak. This indicated that the temperature at this time was the maximum weight loss rate temperature Tf; when the temperature increased to 467.43 °C, the TG curve dropped to the lowest value and was stable, and the DTG curve value was 0, indicating that this temperature was the termination temperature of the thermal weight loss of the sample. From the beginning to the end of the thermal weight loss, the weight loss rate of the sample was 94.46%, and most of the constituents of the blocking agent St/AMPS/EA were cleaved and disappeared after the high temperature. This indicated that the blocking agent developed in this paper had strong high temperature-stability properties.

Stability Analysis

Emulsion stability is a prerequisite for its blocking effect, and its temperature resistance and storage stability are investigated by indoor experiments.

The liquid to be tested was aged by rolling in a roller oven at 120 °C for 16 h and then cooled to room temperature. The stability of the freshly prepared emulsion and the emulsion after hot rolling was tested using an NIR dispersion stability analyzer, and the test results are shown in Figure 8.

NIR scan of the blocker St/AMPS/EA.

As shown in Figure 8, there was minimal change in the NIR scanning curves of the emulsions before and after hot rolling. In the transmission (projection area), 11 curves overlapped, forming a straight line. In the back scattering (reflection area), 11 curves essentially overlapped, forming a straight line: the reflection and projection curves of the emulsions of the same height overlapped at different moments; the transmittance and reflectance of different heights were basically the same, indicating that the emulsions were stable and homogeneous and had good temperature resistance.

To investigate the applicability of the blocking agent St/AMPS/EA in different geological environments, we prepared a 3% concentration solution of St/AMPS/EA nano blocking agent, diluted with deionized water. The experiment mainly observed the blocking performance of St/AMPS/EA nano blocking agent under different pH and salinity conditions and was divided into two parts. The experimental procedure under pH conditions was as follows: the stock solution was divided into three portions for pH adjustment. (1) Hydrochloric acid (HCl) was used to adjust the pH of the first solution to acidic conditions (pH 4). (2) A phosphate buffer was used to adjust the pH of the second solution to neutral conditions (pH 7). (3) Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) was used to adjust the pH of the third solution to alkaline conditions (pH 10). It was ensured that the pH was accurately measured and maintained throughout the experimental process. The experimental procedure under different salinity conditions was as follows: saline solutions with different salinity levels (1, 3, 5, 7, and 9% NaCl by weight) were prepared by using deionized water. The saline solutions were mixed with the St/AMPS/EA stock solution to achieve the desired salinity levels while maintaining a 3% concentration of blocking agent.

Subsequently, the filtration loss of St/AMPS/EA was measured in each pH-adjusted and saline solution, and the experimental results are shown in Figure 9.

Filtration performance of the nano blocking agent St/AMPS/EA under different environmental conditions ((a) pH; (b) salinity).

As shown in Figure 9, the nano blocking agent St/AMPS/EA demonstrates stable performance under various environmental conditions. With changes in pH and salinity in the environment, its filtration loss does not exhibit significant fluctuations, consistently remaining around 3.2 mL. In comparison, the filtration loss of water under different pH conditions is approximately 10.4 mL, and under different NaCl concentrations, it is around 11.0 mL. This result indicates that St/AMPS/EA can maintain stable blocking effects across a wide range of pH values and salinity levels, exhibiting excellent environmental adaptability. Additionally, the pH resistance of St/AMPS/EA is noteworthy. In acidic, neutral, and alkaline environments, St/AMPS/EA maintains its structural integrity and blocking efficacy, which can be attributed to its unique chemical composition and polymer structure. Specifically, the monomer AMPS provides good acid and alkali resistance, enabling St/AMPS/EA to adapt to varying pH environments and in further enhancing its application potential in oilfield development.

Mechanistic Analysis

It can be found from the core microfracture simulation blocking experiments and shale tensile strength experiments that the blocking slurry containing St/AMPS/EA can significantly improve the anticollapse blocking ability of the rock samples. The schematic diagram of the plugging agent blocking action is shown in Figure 10.

Diagram of the St/AMPS/EA blocking agent's blocking action.

The blocking agent contains NPs with an average diameter of 100 nm along with larger and smaller particles. This size distribution allows the particles to progressively fill fractures of various sizes, enhancing the overall blocking performance. The rigidity provided by the benzene ring in the St/AMPS/EA structure contributes to the resistance to penetration, ensuring the stability and effectiveness of the blocking process. The blocking agent’s NPs possess high surface area and surface activity, enabling them to interact with pore walls through physical and chemical adsorption, thereby forming a stable blocking layer. In the process of rock formation, most of the rock matrix surface has been hydroxylated, which can form hydrogen bonds with the plugging agent particles. The interaction between plugging agent particles and the interaction between the plugging agent and the shale produces a binding force, which not only restricts the movement of the plugging agent particles to form a dense plugging section but also strengthens the cementing force by bonding the rock particles together near the well wall, thereby playing a role in stabilizing the wellbore.

In summary, compared to existing plugging technologies, our developed nano plugging agent St/AMPS/EA exhibits several unique advantages. First, St/AMPS/EA demonstrates superior blocking performance with a significant reduction in permeability, which is crucial for effective wellbore stability. Second, the thermal stability of St/AMPS/EA is remarkable, allowing it to maintain its blocking properties under high-temperature conditions commonly encountered in deep-well operations. Lastly, St/AMPS/EA is environmentally friendly, as it is based on water-soluble polymers, reducing the environmental impact associated with traditional oil-based plugging agents. These unique attributes underscore the significance of St/AMPS/EA in advancing blocking technology for the oil and gas industry.

In addition, the nanocontainer emulsion is stable and homogeneous and has excellent temperature and storage stability.

4.Mechanism of plugging action: The plugging agent particles are dispersed in the plugging slurry in nanosize by self-emulsification, and after entering the formation with the filtrate, they accumulate in the microfracture to form a hydrophobic plugging section with certain pressure-bearing capacity; the interaction between the plugging agent and the shale generates binding force, which binds the rock particles near the well wall to enhance its cementing force, thus playing the role of retarding water and pressure transfer to stabilize the well wall.

OT17 a novel microsatellite stable colorectal cancer cell line and organoid model for investigating BRAF V600E mutant tumorigenesis and targeted therapeutics
Authors: Chen Zheng, Xuan Tang, Shuang Wang, Yuxuan Xu, Keyang Jia, Ganglong Gao, Guiying Wei, Gengming Niu, Yiwen Wu, Xiaozhe Qian, Ying Zhu, Dongxi Xiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Human cancer cell lines serve as essential in vitro models for investigating tumor biology, carcinogenesis, molecular genetics, metastasis, and tumor evolution. In this study, we establish and characterize the OT17 cell line, derived from a moderately to poorly differentiated colorectal adenocarcinoma surgical specimen. Genetic analysis of OT17 revealed key mutations, including BRAF V600E, APC, and ERBB2, along with a deletion in TP53. Immunohistochemical profiling confirmed the expression of MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2, indicating a microsatellite-stable (MSS) phenotype consistent with the primary tumor. The OT17 cell line demonstrated robust tumorigenicity in vivo, achieving a 100 % success rate in forming subcutaneous tumors in NOD-scid Il2rg−/−(NSG)(NSG) mice. Additionally, a corresponding 3D organoid model (CO17) was established, which retained genetic concordance with OT17, further validating its relevance for preclinical applications. Together, the OT17 cell line and CO17 organoid system represent robust models for advancing colorectal cancer research, with potential applications in therapeutic screening, immunotherapy development, and MSS tumor biology studies.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common malignancy worldwide, both in incidence and in cancer-related mortality rates [1,2]. Although advancements in early detection and screening have improved patient outcomes, approximately 20 % of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, and even early-stage CRC often progresses to metastatic disease [1,3]. Resistance to conventional chemotherapeutic agents and the limited efficacy of available targeted therapies highlight the urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches to improve treatment efficacy [4,5].

The marked intratumoral genetic heterogeneity observed in CRC significantly influences responses to immunotherapy [6,7]. A subset of CRC cases demonstrates high microsatellite instability (MSI), which is associated with greater responsiveness to immunotherapy due to enhanced immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment [8,9]. In contrast, microsatellite-stable (MSS) CRC, which frequently arises from defects in mismatch repair genes such as MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2, exhibits lower sensitivity to immunotherapy [[10], [11], [12]]. Understanding the mechanisms underlying immune microenvironment (IME) remodeling in MSS-CRC is therefore essential to advancing therapeutic strategies and overcoming resistance.

Cell lines serve as valuable models for elucidating disease pathophysiology, uncovering underlying molecular mechanisms, and facilitating preclinical drug screening, thereby guiding clinical decision-making [[13], [14], [15]]. In this study, we report the establishment of OT17, a colorectal adenocarcinoma cell line derived from a 37-year-old male patient. The OT17 cell line was characterized through a range of assays, including proliferation, short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, karyotyping, in vivo tumorigenicity assessment in NSG mice, and histopathological analysis. Whole-exome sequencing (WES) of OT17 revealed both known and novel mutations, positioning this cell line as a valuable model for investigating molecular pathways in CRC and assessing potential therapeutic agents.

Results

Morphological and growth characteristics of the OT17 cell line

Primary tumor tissues were surgically obtained, minced, and digested with collagenase IV at 37 °C for 30 min. Following digestion, the solution was neutralized, filtered to remove undigested fragments, and centrifuged. The cell suspension was treated for erythrocyte lysis and subsequently cultured in T25 flasks with 1640 medium. After three weeks, cell clones began forming, and by three months, a stable cell line was established. The OT17 cell line has now been passed over 30 generations. Microscopically, OT17 cells displayed epithelial morphology with island-like distribution and a doubling time of approximately 44 h (Fig. 1a–f). Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining revealed a high nucleocytoplasmic ratio and morphological consistency with the primary tumor (Fig. 1g and h). Additionally, a patient-derived organoid model, CO17, was developed from the same tissue. Cells were embedded in Matrigel and cultured in 48-well plates, forming organoids within 14 days and achieving stable propagation by 7 days. Short tandem repeat (STR) and karyotype analysis

STR profiling verified the novelty of OT17 by confirming no match with any cell lines in the ATCC, DSMZ, and ExPASy databases (Fig. 2a). STR results also showed the absence of mycoplasma or bacterial contamination. Karyotype analysis of the 13th-generation OT17 cells revealed complex chromosomal abnormalities, with 86 % of cells exhibiting a near-tetraploid karyotype and 14 % showing near-pentaploid configurations. Tumorigenicity in NSG mice

The tumorigenic potential of OT17 was evaluated by subcutaneous injection into NSG mice. By day 5 post-injection, all mice developed palpable tumors, achieving a 100 % tumorigenicity rate. Tumor growth was rapid, reaching an average volume of 1300 mm3 by day 18, indicating the high malignancy and aggressive growth characteristics of OT17 (Fig. 2c–e).

To examine phenotypic and histological features, OT17 cells were cultured in Matrigel (OT17-3D), alongside CO17 organoids and mouse xenograft tumors. Histopathological analysis confirmed consistency across these models. Immunohistochemistry revealed a microsatellite-stable (MSS) phenotype with CK7(−), CK20(+), MLH1(+), MSH2(+), MSH6(+), and PMS2(+) expression. Whole exome sequencing (WES)

WES of OT17 cells and CO17 organoids revealed high genetic concordance. Both models shared key mutations, including TP53, APC, and EGFR deletions, and the oncogenic BRAF V600E mutation, a hallmark of poor prognosis in CRC. Circos plots highlighted the genetic consistency between the cell line and organoid, underscoring their utility for studying tumorigenesis, drug resistance, and metastasis in BRAF-mutant CRC. These models hold significant potential for precision and personalized therapeutic development (Fig. 3c and d).

Drug sensitivity of BRAF V600E-Mutant CRC organoids

The BRAF V600E mutation, known for its association with poor outcomes in CRC, drives constitutive MAPK pathway activation [16]. While standard chemotherapy regimens, such as FOLFOX (oxaliplatin, leucovorin, and fluorouracil) and FOLFIRI (irinotecan, leucovorin, and fluorouracil), are foundational treatments, their efficacy as monotherapies in BRAF V600E mutant CRC is limited, leading to frequent inclusion in combination regimens to enhance therapeutic outcomes. To explore therapeutic vulnerabilities, we assessed the drug sensitivity of the CO17 organoid (harboring the BRAF V600E mutation) and two wild-type (WT) organoids, CO12 and CO118 (Fig. 4a–c). CO17 demonstrated heightened sensitivity to the BRAF inhibitor dabrafenib, with further potentiation when combined with the MEK inhibitor trametinib. This combination therapy significantly suppressed tumor growth compared to either drug alone (Fig. 4d–f). In clinical practice, patients with BRAF-mutant CRC are commonly treated with a combination of BRAF inhibitors and EGFR inhibitors. To assess whether the CO17 organoid model recapitulates the clinical drug response, we co-administered 140 μg/mL cetuximab at each gradient concentration of dabrafenib, in order to evaluate whether EGFR inhibition could enhance the antitumor efficacy of dabrafenib.

The IC50 curve analysis showed that (Fig. 4g), at high concentrations of dabrafenib (200 μM and 67 μM), the addition of cetuximab markedly enhanced the cytotoxic effect on organoids, resulting in significantly lower survival rates compared with dabrafenib monotherapy (6.4 % vs. 8.7 %; 34.18 % vs. 49.03 %). However, at lower concentrations of dabrafenib, the combined treatment did not demonstrate a significant difference compared with dabrafenib alone. Moreover, cetuximab monotherapy had almost no inhibitory effect on organoid growth, which is consistent with clinical observations that patients with BRAF-mutant CRC rarely benefit from EGFR blockade alone.

Taken together, these findings demonstrate that the CO17 organoid model faithfully mirrors the clinical drug response of BRAF-mutant CRC. Importantly, combined treatment with a BRAF inhibitor and an EGFR inhibitor achieved a more pronounced antitumor effect than either agent alone.

Comparison with existing BRAF-mutant CRC cell lines

To compare the characteristics and differences between OT17 and existing BRAF-mutant CRC lines, we evaluated the in vitro growth and in vivo tumorigenicity of the representative BRAF-mutant cell line HT29. The results showed that HT29 proliferated much faster than OT17 in vitro (Fig. 5a); however, its tumor formation in vivo was slower compared with OT17(Fig. 5b and c). This finding suggests that OT17 possesses certain advantages in establishing in vivo colorectal tumor models. Discussion

CRC accounts for approximately 10 % of all global cancer cases and deaths, ranking among the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with an estimated 900,000 deaths annually [1]. Approximately 20 % of CRC cases are diagnosed at a metastatic stage, and many patients with initially localized disease eventually progress to metastatic disease, which is associated with a five-year survival rate of less than 20 % [1,2,10]. Despite advances in chemotherapy and targeted therapies, survival outcomes for metastatic CRC remain suboptimal [8,11]. Preclinical models, including tumor cell lines, patient-derived xenografts (PDX), and organoids, are pivotal for elucidating CRC biology and advancing therapeutic development [[17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]]. Widely used CRC cell lines, such as HCT116 and SW480, have facilitated significant progress in CRC research due to their ease of culture and stable propagation [[23], [24], [25], [26]]. However, these models represent only a subset of CRC subtypes, underscoring the critical need for additional models that reflect the genetic diversity of CRC.

In this study, we report the successful establishment of the OT17 cell line and its corresponding 3D organoid model, CO17, derived from a 37-year-old male patient with moderately to poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma of the sigmoid colon. Genomic characterization revealed critical mutations, including BRAF V600E and ERBB2, alongside deletions in TP53. These alterations align with those observed in aggressive CRC subtypes, highlighting OT17 as a representative model for studying high-risk CRC [[27], [28], [29]]. Both OT17 and CO17 retained the microsatellite-stable (MSS) phenotype of the primary tumor, a characteristic associated with low immunogenicity and poor immune cell infiltration. This feature positions OT17 and CO17 as valuable models for exploring immune evasion mechanisms in “cold” tumors and for testing personalized therapeutic strategies to enhance immunotherapy's efficacy in MSS CRC [[27], [28], [29]].

Chromosomal analysis revealed significant instability in OT17, characterized by a near-tetraploid karyotype with frequent amplifications, deletions, and rearrangements. These aberrations contribute to oncogene activation and tumor suppressor inactivation, hallmarks of CRC tumorigenesis [[30], [31], [32], [33]]. STR profiling confirmed OT17 as a unique cell line, distinct from existing CRC lines cataloged in major repositories, establishing its novelty and relevance. Moreover, OT17 demonstrated robust tumorigenicity in NSG mice, with a 100 % tumor formation rate, reflecting its aggressive phenotype and chromosomal instability.

The OT17 cell line was established from a patient with stage IV microsatellite-stable (MSS) CRC harboring a BRAF V600E mutation. In combination with its isogenic three-dimensional organoid counterpart (CO17) and the corresponding CDX model, OT17 constitutes an integrated tissue–PDO–cell–in vivo resource. This platform overcomes a major limitation of existing BRAF V600E models, which are predominantly microsatellite instable (MSI-H) or carry extensive genomic rearrangements and generally lack matched PDOs. Compared with classical CRC models such as HT-29 and COLO205, OT17 exhibits rapid and highly penetrant tumor formation (100 % engraftment) in NSG mice following subcutaneous implantation and is capable of stable growth in vitro without exogenous growth factor supplementation. Taken together, OT17 represents a robust and reproducible research platform that uniquely combines an MSS genetic background, a complete cell–PDO–CDX continuum, excellent operability in vitro and in vivo, and fully traceable genomic and pharmacological datasets. This makes OT17 one of the most clinically relevant preclinical models for heterogeneous MSS/BRAF V600E CRC, providing a valuable foundation for studies on precision therapeutics and resistance mechanisms.

In summary, the OT17 cell line, enriched with distinct genetic alterations in BRAF V600E, ERBB2, and APC, significantly expands the repertoire of CRC models. This novel MSS CRC model provides a powerful tool for studying tumor biology, immune resistance, and drug response in CRC. Its genetic and phenotypic concordance with the original tumor underscores its utility in preclinical studies, particularly for understanding mechanisms of immune exclusion and resistance in MSS CRC. Ultimately, OT17 holds great potential to facilitate the development of more effective, targeted therapies for CRC, particularly in addressing the unmet clinical needs of MSS subtypes.

Materials and methods

Specimen collection

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Ren Ji Hospital, School of Medicine, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, with the reference number RA-2022-039. Additionally, informed consent was obtained from the patients or their families. In this paper, a clinical sample was taken from a 37-year-old Chinese male patient diagnosed with sigmoid colon cancer and who did not receive any preoperative radiotherapy or chemotherapy. Histopathology of the tissue sample showed that the tumor was ulcerated, solid, 7∗5 cm in size, invaded the plasma membrane, and showed extensive intraparenchymal emboli with peripheral lymph node metastasis. Histopathologic classification was moderate to low differentiated adenocarcinoma.

Cultivation of OT17 cell line

Tissue samples were washed three times with PBS containing 2 % penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco,15070063) for 5 min each time. Next, the tissues were trimmed and obvious necrotic and fatty tissues were cut off. Remaining tissue samples were minced with scissors and added to a digestion solution containing 1 mg/mL collagenase 4 (sigma, C5138-5G), hyaluronidase (Solarbio, H8030), and allosteric nuclease (yeasen, 20156ES60). Digestion was carried out at 37 °C with gentle shaking at 60–80 rpm for approximately 30 min. Hyaluronidase was included to reduce matrix viscosity, thereby enhancing collagenase penetration and accelerating tissue dissociation. This also minimized the need for prolonged mechanical shearing and improved cell viability. The allosteric nuclease was added to degrade free nucleic acids, which otherwise increase solution viscosity and promote cell clumping. Digestion was terminated by adding more than 3 times the volume of PBS containing 1 % FBS and 2 % P/S (Gibco, A5669701; 15070063), then the cell suspension was centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min after filtration using a 70um filter. After the cells precipitated lysed erythrocytes (TBD, NH4CL2009), they were centrifuged again at 1500 rpm for 5 min and the supernatant was removed. The cell sediment was resuspended using 1640 complete medium (1640 + 10 %FBS+1 %P/S) and incubated flat in T25 bottles (Corning, 430639). When the primary cells were attached to the wall and grew to a certain density, they were passaged. By differential adhesion, after passaging and allowing the cells to settle for approximately 20 min, the supernatant was carefully removed and transferred into a new culture flask. Repeat the treatment several times to remove the interference of fibroblasts and purify the tumor cell line. After three passages, fibroblasts could be eliminated [34]. Other normal cells gradually died after 3–5 passages. The remaining cells were subjected to limiting dilution, and wells containing only a single cell in a 96-well plate were selected for expansion to establish monoclonal cell lines. After several treatments, OT17 has been able to pass on stably for about two months and has now passed on for more than 30 generations. After STR identification, OT17 is a new human CRC cell line and is free from any bacterial and mycoplasma contamination.

Cell proliferation

Cells were digested and plated in four six-well plates at a density of 1∗10^5 per well. Three parallel wells were digested daily for cell counting for a total of eight days and growth curves were plotted. The daily steps were as follows: discard the supernatant of cell line medium of 6-well plate, add 1 ml PBS to wash once, discard PBS. 500ul trypsin was added to each well and placed in 37° incubator for digestion. After the cells showed a single cell state under the microscope, 2 ml of FBS-containing medium was added for neutralization and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 3 min. Discard the supernatant, add 1 ml of medium to resuspend the cells, take 10ul of cell suspension and mix it with 10ul AO/PI (Count Star, RE010213) cell counting reagent and put it into countstar counter for counting.

STR identification

DNA was extracted with a genome extraction kit (Axygen, AP-96-BL-GDNA-4), amplified using a 21- STR amplification protocol, and tested for STR loci and the sex gene Amelogenin on an ABI Model 3730XL Genetic Analyzer. The STR typing data of the samples were compared with the reference data in the database, and the cell line identification comparison revealed that the data of at least 13 STR loci had a match of less than 80 %, and therefore OT17 was considered to be a new cell line.

Karyotype analysis

When the cells grew to logarithmic phase, the cells were treated with 0.2ug/ml colchicine (Macklin, C804812) and continued to be placed in culture at 37° for 2–4 h. At the end of the incubation, the cells were digested and centrifuged to remove the supernatant. Hypotonic potassium chloride (Macklin, P816354) was added to the cell precipitate and placed at 37° for 15 min. Cells were fixed using a methanol glacial acetic acid mixture (ratio 3:1) (Macklin, M813895; A801295) to ensure that the cells were individually dispersed. The cell suspension was aspirated and dropped onto pre-cooled slides, dried at room temperature, observed under a microscope and photographed.

Tumorigenicity

This study was approved by the Laboratory Animal Welfare Ethics Committee of Ren Ji Hospital, School of Medicine, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, with the reference number RJ-2024-087A. Six-week-old female NSG mice (severe combined immunodeficiency) (model organisms, China) were inoculated axillaryly with 3∗106 of the OT17 cell line in a volume of 150 μl. These animals were housed in a laminar-flow environment under aseptic conditions with unrestricted access to water and food. Tumor volumes were recorded twice a week. When the tumor volume reached 1500 mm3, the mice were executed for HE and pathological examination.

Immunohistochemistry

Paraffin-embedded tissues were sliced and placed in a drying oven to bake the sections for 1–2 h. Paraffin sections were dewaxed and hydrated by xylene and gradient alcohol, and antigen repair was performed using the EDTA (MXB, SP KIT-A3) method. After cooling to room temperature, endogenous peroxidase (MXB, SP KIT-A3) was blocked. Closure was performed using goat serum (MXB, SP KIT-A3) and primary antibody (MXB) was added dropwise and incubated overnight for 4 days. The next day, the primary antibody was washed, the HRP-labeled secondary antibody (MXB, KIT-0038) was incubated at room temperature, and after washing, DAB (MXB, KIT-5230) color development was performed. After hematoxylin (BASO, BA4021) staining of nuclei, the sections were dehydrated and sealed. Sections were scanned by a fully automated digital scanner (UNIC, PRECIEC500). Primary antibody stock numbers: CDX2 (RMA-0631); CK7 (Kit-0021); CK20 (Kit-0025); CEA (Kit-0008); PMS2 (RMA-0775); MLH1 (MAB-0789); MSH2 (MAB-0836); MSH6 (MAB-0831).

WES

Genomic DNA was extracted using a DNA extraction kit (QIAGEN,13323).The DNA was randomly interrupted into 150-250bp fragments using a sonicator and ligated to a junction. PCR amplification was performed using universal primers complementary to the junction sequence to form a sequencing library. The library was hybridized using Agilent Sureselect probe in hybridization buffer, and the hybridized target fragments were captured and isolated and purified using magnetic beads, and PCR amplified again to obtain the sequencing library. After passing the library inspection, PE150 sequencing was performed on the library using the NGS platform.

Drug sensitivity test

Organoids were digested into single cells during passage and cell counts were performed. Each well of the 96-well plate contains 3000 cells of organoids, using a 2 μL matrigel system for plating. After waiting for 3–4 days, when the organoids reach approximately 50 μm in size, add the drugs Dabrafenib (MCE, HY-14660) and Trametinib (MCE, HY-10999). The maximum concentration is 200 μM, and a gradient dilution method will be used for successive dilutions. After four days of drug treatment, discard the supernatant of each well, add 100ul CTG detection reagent (EZB, EZB-CV1), blow and mix well, and then place in a 37° incubator for 30 min of incubation. At the end of incubation, chemiluminescence detection was performed and data were analyzed. Drug sensitivity was evaluated by half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50): Cell viability was measured using the CellTiter-Glo assay. All luminescence readings were background-subtracted and normalized to the percentage of control viability. Dose–response data [log10(dose) vs. response] were fitted with a four-parameter logistic regression model (Bottom fixed at 0, Top fixed at 100, HillSlope variable) in GraphPad Prism 10, which automatically generated IC50 values with 95 % confidence intervals.

Statistical analyses

Student's t-test or ANOVA test was utilized to compare the significance of the difference between groups by Graphpad Prism software version 9.

National Crisis-Induced Psychological Stress and Increased Herpes Zoster Incidence: A 10-Year Retrospective Study
Authors: Michael Cleiman, Ayal Hassidim, Rony Shreberk-Hassidim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPsychological stress is known to affect dermatological conditions, but its impact during national crises is not well established.ObjectivesTo assess the association between crisis-induced stress and the incidence of infectious and inflammatory skin diseases, focusing on herpes zoster.MethodsA retrospective cross-sectional study was performed using emergency room records from Hadassah Medical Center, Israel. Skin-related visits were compared between October and December 2023 (national crisis), and the same months in 2014–2022: between the COVID-19 lockdowns (March–May 2020, December 2020–February 2021) and pre-pandemic years (2014–2022). Logistic regression models, adjusted for age and sex, evaluated disease incidence.ResultsAmong 1,644 patients, infectious skin diseases increased significantly during the October–December 2023 crisis (odds ratio (OR)=2.106; P<0.001), mainly due to herpes zoster (OR=2.616; P=0.009). No significant change was observed for inflammatory skin diseases. During COVID-19 lockdowns, no significant difference was found, likely reflecting reduced healthcare utilization.ConclusionsNational crisis-related stress was associated with higher incidence of infectious skin diseases, particularly herpes zoster, but not with inflammatory conditions. These findings support targeted interventions, including vaccination and stress management, during crises.Keywords: Psychological stress, Herpes zoster, Infectious skin diseases, National crisis, Inflammatory skin diseases

Introduction

The intricate relationship between mental health and dermatological conditions has gained increasing attention in recent years as researchers explore the nuanced ways in which psychological stress can manifest on the skin [1–3]. Many patients report a temporal association between stressful events and the exacerbation of their dermatological condition. As a result, dermatologists have an increased awareness of the detrimental impact of psychosocial stress on dermatologic health.

The cutaneous stress response 4 involves multiple central and peripheral pathways. Central mechanisms include the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the locus ceruleus-norepinephrine (LC-NE) sympathetic adrenomedullary system, while peripheral pathways involve the intracutaneous HPA axis and the release of mediators from peripheral sensory and autonomic nerves. These interconnected pathways influence immune function, skin barrier integrity, wound healing, and infection susceptibility [1,4]. As a result, psychological stress can contribute to the manifestation of infectious skin diseases such as herpes zoster and of inflammatory skin conditions, including psoriasis and atopic dermatitis [5–7].

Inflammatory and infectious skin diseases impose a significant health burden across diverse populations. Inflammatory conditions encompass a broad spectrum, including psoriasis vulgaris, atopic and contact dermatitis, and papulosquamous disorders like lichen planus. These diseases often stem from immune dysregulation or environmental triggers, with growing evidence linking mental stress to exacerbations of psoriasis and atopic dermatitis [8]. Conversely, infectious skin diseases result from bacterial (e.g., impetigo, cellulitis), viral (e.g., warts, molluscum contagiosum), or fungal (e.g., tinea pedis) pathogens. The manifestation and severity of both inflammatory and infectious skin diseases are influenced by genetics, immune status, and environmental exposures, highlighting the complexity of dermatological health [9].

The annual emergency room (ER) visits in Israel are estimated at 3 million, with approximately 10,000 cases attributed to noninfectious skin diseases and 20,000 to skin-related symptoms [10]. Hadassah Medical Center, which includes several facilities, serves as a tertiary care hospital for a catchment area of one million inhabitants in Jerusalem and its vicinity. These hospitals handle over 150,000 annual visits, with a subset related to skin conditions, including psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, and herpes zoster [10].

In 2023, Israel experienced a national crisis that profoundly impacted the population’s health, mental well-being, and financial stability [11,12]. Such crises can increase vulnerability to health concerns, potentially leading to a higher incidence or severity of stress-related skin diseases.

Objectives

This study aimed to: i) examine the incidence of stress-related dermatological conditions during the first three months following the onset of the national crisis (October–December 2023); ii) compare ER referral rates in this period with the corresponding months from 2014–2022, including the COVID-19 lockdowns, which were also characterized by heightened psychological distress [5,6]; iii) identify trends in dermatological health during distinct periods of societal stress to provide insights into the broader impact of mental stress on skin diseases and inform clinical and public health strategies.

Methods

This study conducted a comparative analysis of referrals to the ER dermatological services at Hadassah Medical Center (including Ein Kerem and Mount Scopus hospitals) between 07 October and 31 December 2023 and the corresponding periods in 2014–2022. The objective was to examine the correlation between psychological stress following a national crisis and the incidence of skin diseases.

Study Design

This cross-sectional study included individuals who were referred to the Emergency Medicine Center at Hadassah Medical Center for skin-related symptoms (e.g., rash, itching) and underwent evaluation or consultation by a dermatologist at the Emergency Medicine Center.

The study covered patients referred between 07 October and 31 December in 2014–2023. Patients who did not receive a dermatological examination or consultation by a qualified dermatologist were excluded.

A total of 1,644 patients were included in the study: 137 patients were referred to the ER between 07 October and 31 December 2023, and 1,507 patients referred to the ER during the same period in 2014–2022.

The dermatological examination involved a systematic assessment of the entire skin, using good lighting and, when appropriate, magnification. The examination consisted of general observation, inspection of the entire skin surface, and palpation to assess lesion characteristics. Special techniques, such as dermoscopy, Wood’s lamp examination, or laboratory tests (e.g., swabs, skin scrapings, or biopsies), were used to aid diagnosis when indicated. The findings were documented using precise dermatological terminology and were classified according to ICD-9 criteria.

Data Collection Tool

Patient data were obtained from the electronic medical records system at Hadassah Medical Center, which contains detailed patient information. The collected data included sociodemographic characteristics, skin disease and diagnoses (classified under ICD-9), details of referrals to the emergency room, medical and pharmaceutical history, and background information on psychiatric diagnoses.

Skin diseases were categorized into eight dependent variables based on ICD-9 classifications (detailed in Table S1): inflammatory skin diseases, with subcategorizations for psoriasis, eczematous diseases, and other non-papulosquamous/non-bullous inflammatory conditions, or infectious skin diseases, with subcategorizations for non-bullous infectious skin diseases, bullous infectious skin diseases excluding herpes zoster, and herpes zoster.

The explanatory variable “Time Period” was defined as a dichotomous variable: “Yes” for October–December 2023 and “No” for all other periods, based on the patient’s visit to the ER.

The sociodemographic parameters were recategorized as follows: i) residential district: south, north, center, or Jerusalem district of Israel; ii) birthplace: “Born in Israel” or “Not Born in Israel”; iii) underlying mental health status: Yes/No, based on documented psychiatric conditions in the patient’s medical history.

Additionally, vital signs and laboratory results were obtained from the patient’s initial ER evaluation.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data, with categorical variables presented as frequencies (%) and continuous variables as means with standard deviation (SD) for each study period. To assess associations between the dependent variables (inflammatory skin diseases, infectious skin diseases, and herpes zoster) and independent variables, including sociodemographic factors and medical history, bivariate analyses were performed. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test, while Fisher’s exact test was applied for non-parametric categorical variables. For normally distributed continuous variables, a t-test was used. No independent variable met the threshold for confounding (P≤0.1) in the tested associations.

In total, three separate logistic regression analyses were performed to examine the association between the different time periods and each dependent variable while controlling for age and sex as global confounders. A p-value of ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS v25 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethics

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee for Scientific Research on Human Subjects at the Hadassah Medical Center (approval #0715-23-HMO).

A total of 1,644 patients were included in the study, with 8.3% (N=137) presenting to the ER between 07 October and 31 December 2023, and 91.7% (N=1,507) presenting during the same period from 2014 to 2022.

For inflammatory skin diseases, the time period variable (October–December 2023 vs. 2014–2022) was not significantly associated with disease incidence (OR=0.874; P=0.682; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.459–1.665). Similarly, none of the individual inflammatory skin disease subcategories (psoriasis, eczematous diseases, other non-bullous/non-papulosquamous conditions) showed a statistically significant association with the time period variable.

In contrast, for infectious skin diseases, the time period was significantly associated with disease incidence (OR=2.106; P<0.001; 95% CI: 1.389–3.194). However, when analyzing specific subcategories, only herpes zoster (HZ) showed a statistically significant increase in incidence during the crisis period (OR=2.616; P=0.009; 95% CI: 1.277–5.358). The other infectious skin disease subcategories (non-bullous infectious skin diseases and bullous infectious skin diseases excluding HZ) did not show a significant association with the time period variable.

Discussion

The aim of this study was to examine the association between mental stress and the incidence of skin diseases, specifically inflammatory and infectious skin diseases, during the period of national crisis (October–December 2023 compared to the same period in 2014–2022). Results revealed a significant association between the time period and both herpes zoster and overall infectious skin diseases, with a higher incidence observed in October–December 2023 compared to the 10 previous years (2014–2022).

For infectious skin diseases, the analysis showed a significant association with the crisis time period, with the odds of developing these conditions more than doubling in 2023 (OR=2.106; P<0.001; 95% CI: 1.389–3.194). Similarly, the incidence rate of herpes zoster was significantly associated with the crisis period (OR=2.616; P= 0.009; 95% CI 1.277–5.358), suggesting that the increase in cases may be linked to this particular time frame.

In contrast, no significant association was found between the crisis time period and inflammatory skin diseases, despite the broader increase in infectious skin diseases.

The observed rise in herpes zoster and other infectious skin diseases in late 2023 may be attributed to several factors. A plausible explanation is the impact of heightened stress during the national crisis in Israel (October–December 2023). This crisis likely led to widespread psychological distress, social isolation, and disruptions in healthcare access, all of which could have negatively affected individuals’ mental health and overall physical well-being.

Stress is a well-documented trigger for varicella-zoster reactivation, which causes herpes zoster, and may explain the significant increase in herpes zoster cases observed at the end of 2023 [14]. Similarly, other infectious skin diseases could have been influenced by changes in lifestyle, hygiene practices, or healthcare-seeking behavior during the crisis period.

Interestingly, while a strong association was found between the crisis time period and infectious skin diseases, no similar trend was observed for inflammatory skin diseases. This discrepancy suggests that the pathophysiology of inflammatory skin diseases such as psoriasis or eczema may be less affected by acute stressors or short-term healthcare disruptions compared to infectious diseases [15–17]. Instead, inflammatory skin diseases may be driven by factors such as genetic predisposition, long-term environmental influences, or chronic lifestyle factors, which are less likely to fluctuate significantly over a short time frame [8].

Our findings align with previous studies that have reported increased infectious disease incidence during periods of heightened stress or public health emergencies [18,19]. For example, psychological stress has been shown to trigger viral reactivation and increase the frequency of infections such as herpes zoster [14,20]. Furthermore, environmental stressors and shifts in health behaviors during both the COVID-19 pandemic and the 2023 national crisis may have contributed to the observed rise in infectious skin diseases [21–23].

As shown in Figure 1, our observed increase in herpes zoster incidence during the 2023 crisis period stands in stark contrast to both our own historical data (2016–2022) and long-term international trends. Large-scale population-based studies, such as by Kawai et al. [24], have demonstrated a consistent and significant temporal increase in HZ incidence over the past six decades, with rates rising more than fourfold in some regions and a sustained annual increase of approximately 2.5%. While this gradual upward trajectory aligns with demographic shifts like aging populations, the 47% year-over-year surge we observed in 2023 (Z-score=4.7) far exceeds these expected trends. This anomaly strongly suggests that acute stressors rather than continued demographic changes drove the crisis-period spike, highlighting the unique immunological impact of the 2023 national crisis.

The lack of a significant association between COVID lockdowns and skin disease incidence contrasts with our findings during the 2023 crisis period. One possible explanation is the reduced utilization of healthcare services during lockdowns, with fewer patients seeking emergency room care [25]. This decline in ER visits may have led to underreporting or decreased diagnosis rates for skin conditions, potentially masking any true associations. Additionally, while psychological stress levels were elevated during COVID-19 lockdowns, the nature of stressors and behavioral changes (e.g., social distancing, hygiene practices, limited exposure to infectious agents) may have played a role in mitigating the impact on skin disease incidence compared to the 2023 national crisis.

The findings of this study underscore the importance of ongoing surveillance and targeted interventions for infectious skin diseases, particularly during periods of heightened stress such as national crises or public health emergencies. Healthcare providers should remain vigilant about the increased risk of herpes zoster and other infectious skin conditions during such times and consider preventative strategies, including vaccination and stress management interventions, to reduce the burden of these conditions.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions about causality. The observed association between time period and skin disease incidence does not imply a direct cause-and-effect relationship. Additionally, potential confounders such as vaccination status, individual stress levels, and underlying comorbidities were not accounted for, which could have influenced the results. In practice, vaccination coverage was unlikely to account for the observed changes. The live-attenuated zoster vaccine (Zostavax®, Merck & Co., Inc., USA) has been available in Israel for over a decade; however, as it was never included in the National Health Insurance, its population-level uptake remains limited. The recombinant vaccine (Shingrix®, GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals, Belgium) was added to the National Insurance only in July 2023 [26]. Hence, both baseline and crisis-period populations had low vaccine penetration, reinforcing the likelihood that acute psychological stress rather than changes in immunization coverage underlie the HZ surge observed.

Another important consideration is that the observed increase in HZ ER visits partly reflects a shift in the location of care, with patients seeking hospital ER care due to reduced accessibility or capacity in community clinics during the 2023 national crisis. However, the highly selective increase observed only in infectious diseases, driven by HZ, and the absence of a similar increase in inflammatory conditions suggest a genuine increase in disease incidence due to stress-mediated immunosuppression rather than to a generalized shift in healthcare utilization. Furthermore, the lack of a significant association during the COVID-19 lockdowns, which also featured restricted community care, supports this interpretation.

Notably, no significant association was found between the crisis time period and inflammatory skin diseases, despite the broader increase in infectious skin diseases. The discrepancy suggests that the pathophysiology of inflammatory skin diseases such as psoriasis or eczema may be less affected by acute stressors or short-term healthcare disruptions compared to infectious diseases. Alternatively, the lack of a significant association might be influenced by a lag time between the acute stressor and the subsequent inflammatory flare, meaning that an increase in these conditions may manifest only after the three-month observation period. Instead, inflammatory skin diseases may be driven by factors such as genetic predisposition, long-term environmental influences, or chronic lifestyle factors, which are less likely to fluctuate significantly over a short time frame.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates a significant increase in herpes zoster and infectious skin diseases during the first months of the 2023 national crisis in Israel, whereas no corresponding increase was observed for inflammatory skin diseases. These findings suggest that acute societal stress may differentially affect dermatological conditions, with infectious diseases appearing more sensitive to short-term psychological stress. Increased clinical awareness and continued surveillance of stress-related infectious skin diseases may help improve patient care during future national crises and other large-scale stressful events.

FAK inhibition following acute olfactory epithelial inflammation promotes neurogenesis and functional recovery through stem cell CNTF
Authors: Derek Cox, Brian Wang, Jaeden Pyburn, Morning Dove Rose, Diego J Rodriguez-Gil, Theo Hagg, Cuihong Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe sense of smell is maintained by regenerating olfactory sensory neurons (OSNs) from basal stem cells in the olfactory epithelium (OE). Acute inflammation destroys OSNs, causing hyposmia and anosmia, but activates basal cells. Manipulation of signaling pathways to promote basal cell proliferation and neuroregeneration would reveal novel therapeutic targets for smell deficits. We found that ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF) from horizontal basal cells (HBCs, quiescent stem cells) promotes neuroregeneration and functional recovery following methimazole-induced acute injury. Moreover, inhibition of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) upregulates CNTF in naïve OE. Here, we show that the small molecule FAK inhibitor increased CNTF expression in cultured primary HBCs isolated from methimazole-treated mice. Although methimazole-induced CNTF did not seem to be through FAK signaling, inducible cre-lox knockout of FAK in HBCs in mice further increased CNTF expression, as well as Mash1, a marker for globose basal cells (GBCs, neuronal progenitors), and GBC proliferation. Moreover, intranasal aspiration, but not systemic treatment, of a water-soluble pharmacological FAK inhibitor (FAK14) 3 days following methimazole, dose-dependently increased CNTF and Mash1 expression, and GBC proliferation. Intranasal FAK14 also enhanced methimazole-induced regeneration of new OSNs in CNTF+/+, but not in CNTF−/−, mice, demonstrating that FAK14 boosts neuroregeneration through additional CNTF following acute inflammation. Finally, intranasal FAK14 instillation following methimazole improved the functional recovery of smell. This study identifies the therapeutic potential of intranasal application of FAK inhibitors to enhance olfactory neuroregeneration and function following injury.

To maintain the sense of smell, nerve cells in the nose are constantly replaced by local stem cells through proliferation of progenitor cells. Acute inflammation destroys nerve cells, causing a loss of smell. We previously demonstrated in mice that ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF) produced by stem cells is required for progenitor proliferation. We now show that inhibition of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) following acute inflammation injury boosts progenitor proliferation by increasing CNTF. Notably, FAK inhibition also enhances nerve cell regeneration and smell recovery. FAK inhibitor drugs applied intranasally might be a therapeutic strategy to restore smell after injury or aging.

Introduction

The sense of smell is maintained by continuously generating new olfactory sensory neurons (OSNs) from basal stem cells in the olfactory epithelium (OE) throughout the lifespan.1,2 Basal stem cells, horizontal basal cells (HBCs), are quiescent and directly attached to the basement membrane, while neuronal progenitor cells, globose basal cells (GBCs), reside above HBCs and are responsible for producing new OSNs through proliferation and differentiation under normal conditions.3,4 Both HBCs and GBCs are located in the basal layer of the OE. Sustentacular cells cover the apical layer of the OE. HBC dormancy is maintained via Notch1-mediated expression of p635,6 and elimination of Notch1 ligand Jagged1 by destroying sustentacular cells activates HBCs.6 Loss of sustentacular cells subsequently induces OSN apoptosis and loss,7 and the OE is reconstituted in approximately 2-3 weeks by HBCs-derived GBCs, as well as GBC-mediated OSN regeneration.4,8,9 OSNs are located in the middle layer of OE with their dendritic tips directly exposed to the external environment. Inspired toxins and chemicals,7,10 inflammation, such as allergies and sinus infections,11–14 viral infection of the nose,15 including COVID-19 infection of sustentacular cells,16–18 and aging19,20 destroy OSNs, leading to hyposmia or anosmia. Although OE injury upregulates neuroregeneration to restore olfactory deficits,7,10,21 smell function often does not recover in a substantial number of patients, including COVID-19 patients,16–18 and unfortunately no treatment is available. Studying the mechanisms underpinning damage-induced OE neuroregeneration may provide novel therapeutic targets for these olfactory deficits.

Methimazole has been widely used in rodents as an OE injury model22 because it induces acute inflammation in the OE, damaging OSNs and causing loss of the sense of smell.8,23–25 The loss of OSNs is considered secondary to the death of sustentacular cells.8 After methimazole, HBCs are activated at 1 day by downregulation of p63,9 and HBC proliferation transiently increases at 2-3 days via TNF activation of NF-kB signaling, returning to a steady control level at 5-8 days. NPY- and FGF2-mediated activation of p44/42 ERK signaling promotes GBC proliferation.26,27 This signaling pathway might contribute to injury-induced OSN regeneration since ATP is released upon OE injury28 and ATP induces NPY and FGF2 expression via activation of purinergic receptors.27,29 Our previous study demonstrates that ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF) is expressed in HBCs, whereas the CNTF-specific CNTFRα receptor is expressed by GBCs.30 Further, CNTF is upregulated and required for GBC proliferation, OSN regeneration, and recovery of smell function following methimazole.31 These data indicate that CNTF-CNTFRα signaling between HBCs and GBCs mediates methimazole-induced OE neuroregeneration. Here, we investigated whether manipulating the intracellular focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling pathway to upregulate CNTF would accelerate or enhance repair following methimazole.

FAK is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase and regulates gene expression and cell shape, adhesion, and migration.32 In the brain, FAK suppresses CNTF expression.33 Inhibition of FAK upregulates CNTF expression and promotes neurogenesis in the adult mouse brain.33,34 Our recently published study shows that FAK inhibition also increases CNTF expression in adult mouse OE.30 Importantly, inhibition of FAK promotes normal ongoing GBC proliferation and neuroregeneration in mouse OE through CNTF.30 We now investigated whether FAK inhibition would promote neuroregeneration after methimazole-induced acute inflammation.

Materials and methods

Mice

A total of 323 mice were used, including C57BL/6, CNTF, CK5cre, FAKfl/fl, and Mash1cre-Tdt. Adult C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks old, JAX 000664) were purchased from Jackson Laboratory. Our CNTF mice have been backcrossed to C57BL/6J nine times (99.8% C57BL/6J)30,35 and heterozygotes were bred to yield CNTF+/+ and CNTF−/− littermates. CK5Cre (B6N.129S6(Cg)-Krttm.11(cre/ERT2)Blh/J, JAX 029155) and FAKfl/fl (B6.129X1-Ptk2tm1Lfr/Mmucd) breeders were purchased from Jackson Laboratory and the MMRRC, respectively. CK5cre mice were bred with FAKfl/fl mice to generate hemizygous mice that were further backcrossed with FAKfl/fl mice to produce CK5cre-FAKfl/fl and FAKfl/fl control littermates. To induce FAK gene excision in CK5cre-FAKfl/fl mice, we injected a single dose of tamoxifen (i.p., 250 mg/kg,6 T5648, Sigma), and mice were used for experiments 3 days later. Tamoxifen-injected FAKfl/fl mice were used as controls. Floxed Tdtomato (Tdt, B6.Cg-Gt(ROSA)26Sortm6(CAG-tdTomato)Hze/J, JAX 007914) and Mash1Cre (Ascl1tm.11(Cre/ERT2)Jejo/J, JAX 012882) breeders were purchased from Jackson Laboratory and bred to obtain double heterozygotes (Mash1cre-Tdt). To induce Tdt expression in Mash1+ GBCs, mice received a single dose of 4-OH-tamoxifen (i.p., 40 mg/kg). All mice were used in the experiments at 8-12 weeks of age, and no sex differences were found (data not shown). Mouse breeding, maintenance, and experiments were approved by the University Committee on Animal Care at East Tennessee State University and complied with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Primary HBC cultures

Adult primary HBCs were cultured as we did previously.31 Briefly, the OE was dissected from 8-10 week old male C57BL/6 mice at 3 days after methimazole (i.p., 75 mg/kg, #46429. Sigma-Aldrich) and pooled (5 mice/group). The OE tissue was dissociated with Collagenase Type I and DNase I at 37 °C. Cells were cultured in fresh growth media containing NeuroCult medium with 10% Proliferation Supplement, 20 ng/mL EGF and 10 ng/mL bFGF for 4 days (Media were changed every 2 days). Then, HBCs were incubated with vehicle (0.05% DMSO) or the FAK inhibitor PF573228 (10 µM, #3239, Tocris) for 4 h. RT-qPCR was used to analyze mRNA in cell lysates. CNTF protein in the conditioned media was measured by ELISA (ERCNTF, Thermofisher). Some of the HBCs were immunostained with rabbit anti-CK5 antibody (1:500, ab53121, RRID: AB_869889), and nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst. The positive staining was validated by the absence of staining when the primary antibody was omitted.

Methimazole, BrdU, and FAK inhibitor treatments

C57BL/6 mice received saline or methimazole followed by OE tissue collection at 3 or 5 days later. Some mice had three daily intranasal aspirations of 50 µL saline or FAK inhibitor (PF573228, 100 µM, or FAK14, 10-1000 µM, #3414, Tocris) or systemic FAK inhibitor treatment (i.p., PF573228, 5 mg/kg, FAK14, 10 mg/kg) at 3-5 days after methimazole. The OE tissue was then collected 4 h after the last intranasal or systemic treatment. To measure basal cell proliferation or OSN regeneration, bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU, i.p., 50 mg/kg, #B5002, Sigma-Aldrich) was injected 4 h after the last intranasal or systemic treatment, and the OE tissue was collected 2 h or 3 weeks after BrdU injection.30,31,36 For CK5cre-FAKfl/fl, FAKfl/fl, and Mash1cre-Tdt mice, the OE tissue was collected in naïve mice or 5 days after methimazole with or without BrdU injection (i.p., 50 mg/kg) 2 h before tissue collection.

Tissue collection and histological analyses

Mice were anesthetized with Avertin (i.p., 400 mg/kg, 2,2,2-tribromoethanol in 20 mL of 2% 2-methyl-2-butanol in saline, T48402, Millipore Sigma) followed by perfusion with 4% paraformaldehyde. Frozen coronal OE sections (20 µm) were stained with rat anti-BrdU (1:500, Ab6362, RRID: AB_305426) and/or rabbit anti-CK5 (1:500), goat anti-olfactory marker protein (OMP, 1:1000, #544-10001, RRID: AB_664696), mouse anti-Mash1 (1:50, #556604, RRID: AB_396479), chicken anti-GFP (1:500, Ab13970, RRID: AB_300798) antibodies. Immunoreactivity was detected using 488- or Cy3-conjugated immunoglobulins (1:200, Jackson ImmunoResearch) on a Leica TCS SP8 confocal laser scanning microscope. Nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst. BrdU+ nuclei in the ectoturbinate 2 and endoturbinate II of the OE were quantified, as described previously.30,36 Briefly, BrdU+ nuclei in the basal or middle layer of the OE, including the ectoturbinate 2 and endoturbinate II, in three sections per mouse were counted by a researcher blinded to genotypes and treatments and then normalized to the linear length of the ectoturbinate 2 and endoturbinate II (Figure S1). Representative images were taken from the ectoturbinate 2, delineated by a dashed line rectangle.

Protein and mRNA measurements

Mice were anesthetized with Avertin and briefly perfused with cold 0.1M PBS. The OE tissue was then collected, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until processed. RT-qPCR and western blot measured mRNA and proteins, as we described previously.30,31 The following primers from Applied Biosystems were used in RT-qPCR: GAPDH (4352932E), CNTF (mM00446373_m1), Ki67 (mM01278608_m1), PCNA (Mm22448100_g1), TNF (Mm00443258_m1), IL-6 (Mm00446190_m1), LIF (Mm00434762_g1), Mash1 (Mm03058063_m1), and IL-1β (Mm00434228_m1). Data were analyzed using 2−ΔΔCt method. Antibodies used in the western blot included mouse anti-CNTF (1:500, MAB338, RRID: AB_2083064), rabbit anti-phospho-FAK-Tyr397 (1:500, #3283, RRID: AB_2173659), rabbit anti-FAK (1:1000, #3285, RRID: AB_2269034), and rabbit anti-β actin (1:2000, #4970, RRID: AB_2223172).

Olfactory function tests

As we previously did,20,30 buried food and olfactory habituation/dishabituation tests were used to measure smell function deficits and recovery following methimazole. C57BL/6 mice received saline or methimazole, followed by intranasal aspiration of saline or FAK14 at 3-5 days. The first and second trials of the buried food test were performed at 1 and 3 days after the last intranasal aspiration. The habituation/dishabituation test was at 4 days after the last intranasal aspiration. These data were labeled as “1 day.” The other set of mice underwent the same set of behavioral tests at 3 weeks after the last intranasal aspiration, and data were labeled as “3 weeks.”

Buried food test

Two 5-min trials were performed, one in which a piece of sugary cereal was buried beneath the bedding to measure the ability to detect novel odorants, and the other with food placed on the surface of the bedding to assess locomotor ability. Mice were fasted for 16-18 h before each trial, and latencies to find cereal were measured. If mice could not find the buried cereal within the 5-min test, food was manually presented to the mice, and only mice that ate the food were used for data analysis.

Olfactory habituation/dishabituation test

Mice were exposed to two different odorants, (+)-limonene and (−)-limonene (100 mM in sunflower seed oil, 62118 and 62128, SigmaAldrich), over the course of three 2-minute trials for each odorant with a 30-second break between trials. Habituation was measured through exposure to the same odorant, whereas dishabituation (odorant discrimination) was assessed between the last (+)-limonene and the first (−)-limonene exposure. The investigation time when the mouse oriented its nose to the cotton tip covered with one of the odorants within a 3 cm radius was measured using recorded videos by two technicians blinded to treatments and genotypes, and the average investigation times are presented. Mice were acclimated to the test cage for 30 min followed by exposure to three 2-minute trials of water to reduce novelty-induced exploratory activity during the subsequent trials with (+)-limonene and (−)-limonene.20,37

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed by 2-tailed t-tests when two groups were presented. 1 or 2-way ANOVA followed by Tukey post hoc tests was used with more than two groups. Pearson Correlation analyzed linear correlation among cytokines. For behavioral data, 1-way ANOVA or 2-way repeated measures (RM) ANOVA followed by Newman-Keuls post hoc tests was used. Significance was set at P < .05 (GraphPad Prism 6.0). Data are presented as mean ± SEM.

Results

FAK inhibition further increases CNTF in HBCs isolated from methimazole-treated mice

Our previous study demonstrates that HBCs isolated from methimazole-treated mice have more CNTF mRNA and release twice as much CNTF protein as those from saline-treated control mice.31 Now we investigated whether FAK inhibition following methimazole further increases these levels. We isolated HBCs from methimazole-treated mice and incubated them with vehicle or the FAK inhibitor PF573228. Cell colonies were composed of 91 ± 5% CK5+ HBCs (Figure 1A). Compared to vehicle, incubation of HBCs with PF573228 increased CNTF mRNA by 50% and released 15% more CNTF protein into the culture media (Figure 1B). CNTF protein was not detected in cell lysates, suggesting that CNTF is released immediately once produced and mainly serves as a paracrine signaling molecule. PF573228-mediated inhibition of FAK was confirmed by reduction of pFAK levels in cell lysates (Figure 1C). PF573228 also increased the related gp130 cytokine LIF but did not significantly affect IL-6 (Figure 1D). Furthermore, PF573228 reduced HBC proliferation measured by proliferation markers, Ki67 and PCNA (Figure 1E), suggesting that FAK inhibition-induced CNTF is not attributed to higher numbers of HBCs. Together, these data indicate that FAK inhibition following methimazole increases CNTF expression and release. Thus, FAK inhibition-induced CNTF might serve as paracrine signaling for GBCs that express CNTFRα in the mouse OE.30

FAK knockout in HBCs upregulates CNTF and promotes GBC proliferation after methimazole

To test the role of FAK in HBCs in vivo, we selectively deleted the FAK gene in HBCs in CK5cre-FAKfl/fl mice with FAKfl/fl mice serving as controls. Our previous study already validated specific cre recombinase in CK5+ HBCs using CK5cre-Tdt mice.31 CK5cre-FAKfl/fl mice had 30% and 15% higher levels of CNTF and Mash1, a GBC marker, compared to control FAKfl/fl littemates, but comparable levels of LIF and IL-6 mRNA (Figure 2A). Knockout of FAK in HBCs doubled CNTF protein levels (Figure 2C). Methimazole-induced injury upregulates CNTF and Mash1 expression in the OE of adult mice.31 CNTF and Mash1 were higher in CK5cre-FAKfl/fl than FAKfl/fl mice after methimazole treatment (Figure 2B and C). LIF and IL-6 levels were comparable in methimazole-treated CK5cre-FAKfl/fl and FAKfl/fl mice (Figure 2B). These data indicate a FAK-mediated regulation of HBC CNTF under normal conditions and following methimazole, and inhibition of HBC FAK promotes GBC proliferation, possibly through CNTF. Knockout of FAK in HBCs did not affect methimazole-induced inflammation measured by pro-inflammatory TNF (Figure 2B), consistent with the fact that HBCs do not contribute to methimazole-induced inflammation.31 To confirm the methimazole effect on CNTF in CK5cre-FAKfl/fl and FAKfl/fl mice, we treated CK5Cre-FAKfl/fl and FAKfl/fl mice with saline or methimazole and measured CNTF mRNA levels in the OE 5 days later. As shown in Figure 2D, in saline-treated groups, CK5Cre-FAKfl/fl mice had 30% more CNTF than FAKfl/fl mice, consistent with data in Figure 2A. Methimazole upregulated CNTF in both types of mice, but methimazole induced more CNTF in CK5Cre-FAKfl/fl than FAKfl/fl mice, suggesting that FAK inhibition enhances methimazole-induced HBC CNTF.

Following methimazole, HBCs proliferate at 2-3 days,8,9 followed by GBC proliferation at 3-5 days. In the OE, HBCs do not express CNTF receptors,31 and CNTF has no effect on HBC proliferation.38 To test whether FAK inhibition-induced HBC CNTF affected GBC proliferation, we measured BrdU-labeled proliferating GBCs at 5 days post-methimazole. BrdU was injected into FAKfl/fl and CK5cre-FAKfl/fl mice at 5 days post-saline or methimazole, and we quantified BrdU+ nuclei in the OE 2 h later. In saline-treated mice, BrdU+ nuclei (green) resided above the basement membrane and beneath the middle layer of OSNs stained with OMP (red), and were barely located in HBCs stained with CK5+ (red, Figure 2E). Only 1.0%-1.2% of BrdU+ nuclei were detected in CK5+ HBCs (Figure 2F), indicating that knockout of FAK in HBCs does not alter HBC proliferation under normal conditions. The lack of OMP+ OSNs in methimazole-treated mice confirmed methimazole-induced depletion of OSNs (Figure 2G). Quantification shows that methimazole increased BrdU+ nuclei in both genotypes but induced 37% more BrdU+ nuclei in CK5cre-FAKfl/fl than FAKfl/fl mice (Figure 2H). BrdU+ nuclei were colocalized with Mash1+ nuclei in GBCs (Figure 2I and J) but barely localized in CK5+ HBCs (Figure 2K), confirming GBC proliferation at 5 days post-methimazole. Collectively, these data demonstrate that knockout of FAK in HBCs enhances methimazole-induced GBC proliferation.

Intranasal aspiration of a water-soluble FAK inhibitor enhances methimazole-induced CNTF and Mash1

Methimazole-induced injury upregulates CNTF expression in the OE at 3 and 5 days.31 To test whether this is through FAK signaling, we measured pFAK levels using western blot (Figure 3A). Methimazole increased CNTF protein at both 3 and 5 days (Figure 3B) but did not alter pFAK levels (Figure 3C), indicating that FAK signaling is not involved. FAK knockout in HBCs enhanced methimazole-induced CNTF expression and GBC proliferation (Figure 2), suggesting that FAK inhibition following methimazole might boost these responses. Therefore, we daily treated mice with vehicle (0.5% DMSO) or PF573228 via intranasal aspiration at 3-5 days post-methimazole. OE tissue was collected 4 h after treatment. Surprisingly, intranasal DMSO vehicle treatment blocked the expected increases in CNTF and Mash1, and intranasal PF573228 did not cause a greater decrease compared to vehicle (Figure S2A and B). DMSO has been shown to restore contact inhibition-induced growth arrest and prevent high-density-dependent apoptosis in vitro,39 suggesting that DMSO may activate downstream signaling of cell-extracellular matrix and cell–cell interactions, including FAK. Thus, the blockade of methimazole-induced CNTF and no effect of PF573228 on CNTF might be due to FAK activation by the DMSO vehicle. DMSO is known to have anti-inflammatory activity.40–43 Indeed, DMSO vehicle-treated methimazole mice had much reduced levels of TNF, IL-6, IL-1β, and LIF compared to saline-treated methimazole mice (first two columns, Figure S2C-F vs Figure 4C-F). Following methimazole, CNTF expression was positively correlated with IL-6 and showed no linear relationships with TNF, IL-1β, and LIF (Figure S3), suggesting that methimazole-induced IL-6 stimulates CNTF.

Instead of the DMSO-soluble inhibitor, we selected a water-soluble FAK inhibitor, FAK14, and administered it intranasally and daily at concentrations ranging from 10 to 1000 µM to C57BL/6 mice from 3 to 5 days post-methimazole. Tissues were collected 4 h after the last treatment. Methimazole induced 2.3-fold of CNTF (Figure 4A) and 5.7-fold of Mash1 mRNA (Figure 4B), and FAK14 dose-dependently enhanced them with significance at the 100-1000 µM doses. In mice with intranasal aspiration of saline, methimazole increased TNF (Figure 4C) and IL-6 (Figure 4D) mRNA levels by 8.1 and 6.4-fold, respectively, consistent with our previous study in methimazole mice without intranasal aspiration treatment,31 and demonstrates that intranasal saline did not affect methimazole-induced acute inflammation. Methimazole also increased IL-1β 3.8-fold (Figure 4E) and LIF 2.9-fold (Figure 4F). FAK14 at 10-100 µM did not affect methimazole-induced TNF (Figure 4C) and IL-1β (Figure 4E). However, it increased these cytokines when the concentration exceeded 300 µM, which matches the additional increases in CNTF and Mash1 at these high concentrations. This again suggests that these pro-inflammatory cytokines might stimulate CNTF and Mash1 following methimazole. FAK14 decreased IL-6 (Figure 4D) and did not alter LIF (Figure 4F) at all doses tested. For the subsequent experiments, we chose 100 µM FAK14, which further upregulates methimazole-induced CNTF and Mash1 without altering TNF and IL-1β.

To determine whether intranasal aspiration has better outcomes than systemic treatment, we compared the effect of three daily intranasal vs systemic FAK14 treatments at 3-5 days post-methimazole by intranasal aspiration of 100 µM FAK14 vs i.p. injection of 3 mg/kg FAK14, the dose that successfully inhibits FAK activation and upregulates CNTF in brain tissue.44 Consistent with results in Figure 4, intranasal FAK14 aspiration enhanced methimazole-induced CNTF (Figure 5A and B) and Mash1 (Figure 5C), had no effects on TNF (Figure 5D), IL-1β (Figure 5E), and LIF (Figure 5F), as well as reduced IL-6 (Figure 5G). Intranasal FAK14-mediated inhibition of FAK signaling was validated by a 60% reduction of pFAK levels in the OE (Figure 5H). Systemic FAK14 treatment did not affect CNTF and Mash1 (Figure 5A-C), consistent with not affecting pFAK levels in the OE (Figure 5H). Collectively, these data indicate that intranasal FAK14 treatment following methimazole boosts CNTF and Mash1 expression, whereas systemic treatment has no effect.

Intranasal FAK14 enhances methimazole-induced GBC proliferation and neuroregeneration via CNTF

We treated C57BL/6 mice with saline or methimazole, followed by either intranasal aspiration or intraperitoneal injection of FAK14, daily from 3 to 5 days. BrdU was given 2 h before tissue collection. Compared to saline, methimazole increased BrdU+ nuclei by 2.7-fold (Figure 6A and B). Intranasal FAK14 aspiration further increased the number of BrdU+ nuclei 2-fold, whereas systemic treatment had no effect (Figure 6A and B). To further test the effect of intranasal FAK14 on Mash1+ GBC proliferation, we labeled Mash1+ GBCs with Tdt at 3 days post-methimazole by injecting 4-OH-tamoxifen into Mash1cre-Tdt mice, and then gave 3 days of intranasal aspiration of saline or FAK14. Compared to saline, FAK14 increased Tdt+ cells by 57% (Figure 6C and D), and Tdt+ cells resided above the CK5+ HBCs. Collectively, these data demonstrate that intranasal FAK14 treatment following methimazole promotes GBC proliferation, especially Mash1+ GBCs. We next defined the role of intranasal FAK14 in OSN regeneration using a BrdU pulse-chase method.30,31,36 To also determine the mediating role of CNTF, BrdU was injected into CNTF+/+ and CNTF−/− mice at 5 days post-methimazole. BrdU+ nuclei in the middle layer of the OE were counted 3 weeks later, when BrdU-incorporated GBCs differentiate into OSNs and migrate into the middle layer, as shown in Figure 6E, where BrdU+ nuclei were found in OMP+ OSNs. Compared to saline, FAK14 increased BrdU+ nuclei in CNTF+/+ mice but had no effect in CNTF−/− mice (Figure 6F), demonstrating that intranasal FAK14 treatment following methimazole promotes OSN regeneration through CNTF.

Intranasal FAK14 aspiration following methimazole promotes recovery of olfactory function

To test whether FAK14-induced neuroregeneration improves olfactory functional recovery, we treated C57BL/6 mice with saline or methimazole followed by daily intranasal aspiration of saline or FAK14 at 3-5 days. The mice then underwent buried food and olfactory habituation/dishabituation tests at 1-4 days (labeled as 1 day) or 3 weeks after the last intranasal aspiration to measure olfactory function deficits and recovery.31 In the buried food test, we measured the latency to find buried or presented food in fasted mice. Mice treated with methimazole were unable to find buried food during the 5-minute testing period at 1-4 days after the last intranasal saline aspiration (Figure 7A, blue), confirming methimazole-induced deficits in smell function due to depletion of OSNs in the OE. Control mice with intranasal saline aspiration (open circles) found buried food with an average latency of 31 seconds, which is consistent with our previous studies in naïve mice,30,31 confirming that intranasal aspiration of saline does not interfere with odorant detection. Half of the injured mice treated with saline mice displayed incomplete recovery at 3 weeks (orange) compared to 1-4 days (blue). In contrast, the mice with intranasal FAK14 aspiration (red) resumed their latencies to control levels. The effects of methimazole and FAK14 on latencies were not attributed to locomotor function since all mice had similar latencies (average less than 11 seconds for all groups) when tested with a cereal presented on the surface of the bedding (Figure 7B). In the habituation/dishabituation test, the times when mice investigated (+)-limonene and (−)-limonene, two isomer of a natural aromatic compound with orange and piney scents, respectively, were recorded. The (+) limonene was presented three times, followed by three times of (−)-limonene. Generally, mice spend less time on a previously investigated odorant (habituation) and more time investigating a novel odorant (dishabituation or odorant discrimination). Uninjured control mice exhibited habituation to both isomers, that is, trial 1 vs 3 and trial 4 vs 6, and dishabituation from trial 3 (+)-limonene to trial 4 (−)-limonene (Figure 7C, dashed black line). At 1-4 days after methimazole, habituation and dishabituation were absent (blue line), again indicating smell function was impaired. At 3 weeks after methimazole, mice could not discriminate (−)-limonene from (+)-limonene (orange line), whereas mice treated with FAK14 resumed habituation to both isomers and dishabituation (red line), that is, returning to control levels. The investigation time for the first trials (both isomers) was also reduced by methimazole and returned to control levels at 3 weeks with intranasal FAK14 aspiration (Figure 7D). Collectively, both behavioral tests reveal that intranasal FAK14 treatment following methimazole improves recovery of smell function, possibly due to its effect on OSN regeneration in the OE.

Discussion

This study shows that intranasal application of a water-soluble FAK inhibitor following acute inflammation in the adult mouse OE promotes GBC proliferation and OSN regeneration by upregulating CNTF expression in HBCs. This intranasal treatment also accelerates the recovery of smell function. Importantly, the same compound delivered systemically has no effect, suggesting that the intranasal regimen for the FAK inhibitor has more advantages to target the olfactory system than systemic administration.

CNTF, IL-6, and LIF are members of a gp130 family of cytokines, In the brain, CNTF is only expressed by astrocytes.45 CNTF is stimulated by IL-6 via activation of JAK-STAT3705 signaling and suppressed by FAK-activated JNK-STAT3727 pathway.33,34 Selective knockout of FAK in astrocytes or pharmacological inhibition with FAK inhibitors upregulates brain CNTF.33,34 In the OE, HBCs express CNTF,30 and methimazole promotes CNTF expression in vitro and in vivo.31 Here, inhibition of FAK induced CNTF mRNA expression and the release of CNTF protein from primary HBCs isolated from methimazole-treated mice, suggesting that FAK inhibition following methimazole induces additional CNTF.

The signaling underlying the methimazole-induced CNTF in HBCs is unknown. Our data indicate that methimazole does not alter FAK signaling, suggesting involvement of another pathway. In astrocytes, IL-6 stimulates CNTF expression by binding to the IL-6Rα/gp130 receptor complex and activating JAK-STAT3705 signaling.33 This is likely to have occurred in the methimazole-induced CNTF, since methimazole upregulates IL-6.31 Indeed, in methimazole-treated mice, CNTF expression was positively correlated with IL-6 (Figure S3A).

FAK suppresses CNTF expression in astrocytes via JNK-activated STAT3727 signaling in vitro and in vivo.33,34 Selective knockout of FAK in astrocytes, FAK inhibitor or JNK inhibitor upregulates CNTF in the brain.33,34 Our data show that selective knockout of FAK in HBCs resulted in more CNTF in the OE of methimazole-treated CK5cre-FAKfl/fl mice than FAKfl/fl littermates, suggesting that inhibition of FAK following methimazole-induced acute inflammation upregulates CNTF in HBCs. Indeed, intranasal aspiration of FAK14 further boosted CNTF induction in methimazole-treated mice. Future studies will investigate whether FAK inhibition-induced CNTF in HBCs is through the JNK-STAT3727 pathway. We have not observed the FAK14 effect on inflammation in methimazole-treated mice until higher concentrations than 300 µM were used.

CNTF, LIF, and IL-6 cooperate to regulate neural stem cell proliferation, differentiation, and self-renewal. In the subventricular zone, CNTF promotes the proliferation of intermediate neuronal progenitor cells that actively divide to produce new neurons.46 LIF promotes stem cell self-renewal at the expense of neuroblast formation,47 while IL-6 maintains stemness and inhibits neuronal differentiation.48,49 Selective knockout of FAK in HBCs increased expression of GBC marker, Mash1, and enhanced methimazole-induced GBC proliferation measured by BrdU incorporation assay. Intranasal FAK14 aspiration following methimazole also increased Mash1, supporting the hypothesis that CNTF acts on GBCs to promote their proliferation,31 as it does in the brain.46 Indeed, Mash1 expression was positively correlated to CNTF in intranasal FAK14-treated mice (Figure S4). FAK14 did not affect LIF but reduced methimazole-induced IL-6 in the OE. Systemic FAK14 did not alter FAK signaling in the OE, suggesting that FAK14-mediated reductions of IL-6 are possibly not through FAK signaling. In the brain, IL-6 counteracts CNTF effects on promoting neuronal progenitor cell proliferation.46 Thus, reductions of IL-6 in the OE might facilitate the effect of FAK14 on promoting GBC proliferation.

Methimazole is known to induce an acute inflammatory response and deplete OSNs in the OE.4,8,23,24,50 These were confirmed in this study by increased TNF, IL-1β and IL-6 and absence of OMP+ OSNs in the OE at 5 days. After methimazole, HBCs are activated at 1 day and proliferate at 2-3 days via TNF activation of NF-KB signaling,8,9 while GBCs that express CNTFRα proliferate at 3-5 days through CNTF signaling.31 CNTF does not affect HBC proliferation in vitro.38 Thus, increasing CNTF at 3-5 days post-methimazole by intranasal aspiration of FAK14 likely promotes the proliferation of neighboring GBCs via a paracrine role. Indeed, intranasal FAK14 treatment at 3-5 days enhanced methimazole-induced numbers of BrdU-labeled basal cells and Tdt-labeled Mash1+ GBCs, suggesting that FAK14 promotes GBC proliferation, possibly through increased CNTF. Systemic FAK14 treatment had no effect, possibly due to the lack of effect on CNTF. In the subventricular zone, CNTF promotes C-cell proliferation via FGF2 signaling.46 In the OE, FGF2 and its mitogenic effect on GBCs were reported as one of the downstream signals upon purinergic receptor activation.29 It remains to be determined whether CNTF acts through the same pathway in GBCs following methimazole.

After methimazole-induced injury, most of the OSNs are regenerated by 3 weeks through GBC proliferation and differentiation,4,25,51,52 although a small number of new OSNs are generated at 5 days.8 GBCs actively divide to produce new neurons to replace dying OSNs under normal conditions53–55 or reconstitute the OE following injury.4,25,51,52 Sox2 and Wnt signaling have been reported to regulate these functions.56,57 We previously found that deletion of CNTF reduces methimazole-induced OSN regeneration at 3 weeks,31 supporting that CNTF-mediated increase of GBC proliferation leads to more OSNs. CNTF is unlikely to directly promote neuronal lineage differentiation/maturation since CNTF returns to control levels at 3 weeks post-methimazole when OSNs differentiate and maturate.31 To test whether FAK14-induced GBC proliferation results in more new OSNs in the methimazole model, we quantified nuclei retaining BrdU in the middle OSN layer of the OE at 3 weeks post-methimazole as an index of new regenerated OSNs. FAK14 aspiration at 3-5 days enhanced BrdU+ OSNs in methimazole-treated CNTF+/+ but not CNTF−/− mice, indicating that intranasal FAK14 treatment following methimazole boosts OSN regeneration through CNTF, most likely CNTF-promoted GBC proliferation. The lack of FAK14 effect in CNTF−/− mice is not due to less methimazole-induced OE injury since CNTF+/+ and CNTF−/− mice exhibit similar levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and OE injury pathology at 5 days following methimazole.31

Methimazole depletes OSNs, which leads to losing the ability to discriminate between two odors at 2-3 weeks, but resumes the ability after 6 weeks of recovery.31,58 Our data show that mice could not smell (+)-limonene and (−)-limonene as novel odorants, as well as discriminate (+)-limonene vs (−)-limonene at 6-9 days post-methimazole, that is, 1-4 days after the last saline aspiration. The ability recovered partially at 3 weeks since the mice with intranasal aspiration of saline displayed habituation to (+)-limonene. However, mice with intranasal aspiration of FAK14 resumed habituation to both isomers and their ability to discriminate between (+)-limonene and (−)-limonene, suggesting a full recovery of the olfactory function. In mice treated with intranasal saline, none were able to find the buried food within 5 min of the testing period at 6 days post-methimazole, suggesting impaired olfactory function. At 3 weeks, some of them could find the buried food with longer latencies. Mice treated with intranasal FAK14 exhibited comparable latencies as control mice that did not receive methimazole. This is compatible with the olfactory habituation/dishabituation data, again suggesting that olfactory function is totally recovered. Collectively, both behavioral tests indicate that intranasal FAK14 treatment at 3-5 days following methimazole accelerates smell function recovery, possibly due to the FAK14 effect on OSN regeneration. Our previous study demonstrates that the olfactory function is completely recovered 6 weeks after methimazole,31 consistent with the fact that it takes 6 weeks for newly generated OSNs to extend their axons to the olfactory bulb and integrate into olfactory bulb circuits following methimazole.58 Olfactory ensheathing cells, located in the OE lamina propria and olfactory bulb, facilitate axon extension.59,60 Olfactory ensheathing cells also express CNTF.30 CNTF promotes axon growth in the optic nerve61,62 and spinal cord63 following injury. It remains to be determined whether CNTF also promotes axon outgrowth and/or functional synapse formation in the olfactory bulb. We could not exclude the possibility that olfactory ensheathing cells might also contribute to methimazole- and/or FAK14-induced CNTF in the OE in vivo.30,64,65 Future studies should investigate whether intranasal aspiration of FAK14 induces CNTF expression in olfactory ensheathing cells and whether CNTF affects axon growth following methimazole.

Conclusions

This study provides evidence that intranasal aspiration of a water-soluble FAK inhibitor following methimazole-induced OE injury upregulates CNTF in HBCs, which acts as a paracrine ­signaling to promote GBC proliferation, OSN regeneration, and smell function recovery. These results advance our knowledge of the olfactory stem cell niche that maintains olfactory neuroregeneration and function. They also point to opportunities to ­investigate FAK inhibitors as an intranasal therapeutic regimen to restore smell function following viral infections, chemical injury, and aging.

Impact of early initiation of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 inhibitors in patients with acute coronary syndrome: A systematic review meta-analysis
Authors: Lakshmi Nagendra, Kunal Mahajan, Gunjan Gupta, Deep Dutta
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveScant data is available on the efficacy and safety of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type-9 inhibitors (PCSK9i) for early and rapid reduction of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) within 4–8 weeks of an acute event in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS). We undertook this meta-analysis to address this knowledge-gap.MethodsElectronic databases were searched for RCTs involving patients with ACS receiving PCSK9i in intervention arm, and placebo/active comparator in control arm. Primary outcome was to evaluate changes in 1-month LDL-C post ACS. Secondary outcomes were to evaluate alterations in other lipid parameters and adverse events.ResultsFrom initially screened 194 articles, data from 3 studies was analyzed. After 4-weeks therapy, patients receiving PCSK9i had lower LDL-C [MD -0.95 mmol/L (95%CI:-1.51 to −0.40); P = 0.0007; I2 = 96%, total cholesterol (TC) [MD-1.05 mmol/L (95%CI:-1.83 to −0.27); P = 0.009; I2 = 94%] and triglycerides (TG) [MD-0.27 mmol/L (95%CI:-0.44 to −0.10); P = 0.002; I2 = 0%] compared to controls. After 4–8 weeks therapy, patients receiving PCSK9i has lower apolipoprotein B [MD-27.74% (95%CI:-42.59 to −12.89); P = 0.0003; I2 = 89%] as compared to controls. High density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) [MD 0.05 mmol/L (95%CI:-0.00–0.11); P = 0.05; I2 = 0%], lipoprotein(a) [MD-20.63 mmol/L (95%CI:-41.86– 0.59); P = 0.06; I2 = 54%] and apolipoprotein A1 [MD 0.02 g/L (95%CI:-0.02–0.07); P = 0.32; I2 = 0%] were comparable between groups. Hospital readmission for ACS was significantly lower in group receiving PCSK9i compared to controls [OR0.25 (95%CI:0.07–0.85); P = 0.03; I2 = 0%]. Occurrence of cardiac death [OR3.75 (95%CI:0.41–34.22); P = 0.24; I2 = 0%], serious adverse events [OR0.71 (95% CI:0.13–3.83); P = 0.69; I2 = 70%] and total adverse events [OR1.01 (95%CI: 0.19–5.30); P = 0.99; I2 = 92%] was comparable between groups.ConclusionPCSK9i are highly effective in early reduction of LDL-C along with reduction of early hospital readmissions post-ACS.Keywords: PCSK9 antibodies, PCSK9 inhibitors, meta-Analysis, Acute coronary syndrome, Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol

Introduction

Patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) are at a maximal risk of recurrent events in the initial few weeks after the initial/index event.1 Rapid reduction of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) has been demonstrated to reduce cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in these patients, and prevent further progression of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). The rapid reduction in LDL-C levels has a major role in prevention of recurrence of events.2 Hence all major global guidelines recommend in hospital initiation of high dose statins to prevent these early recurrent events.3 However, studies have consistently shown that most patients fail to achieve the therapeutic LDL-C targets early in the course of treatment initiation (2–4 weeks), due to various reasons like delayed onset of action of statins, high dose statin intolerance and treatment non-compliance.4 Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type-9 inhibitors (PCSK9i) are antibodies which have been demonstrated to have a very potent LDL-C lowering effect in different studies across the globe.5 It has been hypothesised that addition of PCSK9i to high dose statins in the very high-risk, acute (within days) phase of ACS should bring down LDL-C levels more rapidly and effectively, and hence should have a role in preventing future events in the initial few weeks after the index event. Several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have been published evaluating the role of PCSK9i in ACS.6, 7, 8, 9

Scant data is available on the efficacy and safety of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type-9 inhibitors (PCSK9i) for early and rapid reduction of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) within 4–8 weeks of an acute event in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Hence, we did this meta-analysis to evaluate the quantum of LDL-C reduction and impact on other clinical and biochemical parameters in the initial few weeks after index ACS event.

Methods

Methodology

The meta-analysis was done using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines having PROSPERO registration number of CRD42023421035.10 All randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published till March 2023 were considered for this meta-analysis. Using PICOS criteria, RCTs having patients with an acute event of ACS who received PCSK9i within 48 h of ACS event along with high dose statin in the intervention arm, with control arm receiving placebo or any other approved medication for hypercholesterolemia along with high dose statin were included in this meta-analysis. RCTs were included which had defined ACS in their study as unstable angina, non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction and ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction. Patients with other forms of ASCVD were excluded. Patients with advanced kidney disease, liver disease, prior history of statin intolerance were excluded. The primary outcome was to evaluate the changes in LDL-C at 4 weeks post event. The secondary outcomes of this study were to evaluate the alterations in other lipid parameters, recurrence or readmission for any cardiovascular event, adverse reactions, cardiac death at 4 weeks and further longer follow-up. Analysis of the outcomes was done based on whether the control group received an active comparator (any other lipid lowering medication) – labelled here as the active control group (ACG) or a placebo – labelled as passive control Group (PCG).

We systematically searched Embase database, Web of science, Cochrane library, Medline (PubMed), clinicaltrials.gov, CNKI database, ctri. nic.in, and Google scholar as either key-words or MESH terms using a Boolean search strategy (PCSK9 inhibitors [MeSH]) OR (PCSK9 antibodies) OR (alirocumab) OR (evolocumab) AND (acute coronary syndrome {All Fields]) OR (ACS) OR (myocardial infarction [All Fields]).

Data extraction with regards to all the primary and secondary outcomes stated above was carried out independently by two authors. Multiple publications from the same group on the same cohort of patients were pooled together and considered as a single study for our meta-analysis. Details have been elaborated in previous meta-analysis published by our group.10 The risk of bias assessment was done by 3 authors using the risk of bias assessment tool in Review Manager (Revman) Version 5.4 software. The different types of bias looked for have been elaborated in previous metanalyses by our group.10,11

The international system of units (SI units) was used for all the analysis done. Continuous variable outcomes were presented as mean differences (MD). For dichotomous variables, outcomes were expressed as risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) and as hazard ratios (HR) for adverse events. RevMan 5.4 was used for doing all the statistical analysis and generation of Forest plots in this meta-analysis. Random effect model for analysis expressed as 95% confidence intervals (95%CI). The forest plot generated for all the different outcomes was used to assess the heterogeneity. We specifically used Chi2 test on N-1 degrees of freedom, with an alpha of 0.05 used for statistical significance and with the I2 test.12,13 The details of heterogeneity analysis have been elaborated elsewhere.10,11

Grading of results is important as it helps us to understand the quality of the results generated in a meta-analysis. After all any meta-analysis can be as good as the quality of RCTs used in the analysis. The grading/certainty of the evidence of some of the major secondary outcomes and primary outcome in this meta-analysis was done using Grades of Recommendation, Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach.14 The details have been elaborated elsewhere.10,11 Publication bias was assessed by plotting the Funnel Plot.14,15 The details of how the Funnel plots were plotted have been elaborated elsewhere.10,11 Funnel plots drawn have been elaborated in Supplementary Fig. 1. Table-2 was generated using the GRADE software (https://gdt.gradepro.org/app/) which highlights the grading of key outcomes. Since ethical approval was already taken for the all the RCTs which were included in this meta-analysis and no new patients were evaluated here, there was no need for separate ethical approval for this meta-analysis.

Results

A total of 194 articles were found after the initial search (Fig. 1). Forty-two duplicate articles were removed. One hundred and fifty-two articles were screened by their titles, abstracts, followed by full-texts, and the search was then reduced down to 19 studies which were evaluated in detail for inclusion in this meta-analysis (Fig. 1). Four RCTs in people with T2DM which fulfilled most of the criteria were considered for analysis in this meta-analysis.6, 7, 8, 9 However, the study by Raber et al was excluded as it evaluated lipid parameters at 1 year of follow up and not in the initial 2–4 weeks of PCSK9i initiation in ACS.9 Hence data from only 3 RCTs (Koskinas et al, Leucker et al and Hao et al) were analysed in this meta-analysis.6, 7, 8 The details and characteristics of the patients in the 3 RCTs analysed in this meta-analysis have been elaborated in Table 1.

Effect of PCSK9i on primary outcomes

LDL-C

Data from 3 studies involving 500 people with ACS was analysed to find out the impact of early treatment with PCSK9i on LDL-C after 4 weeks of treatment. As compared to controls, patients receiving PCSK9i had significantly lower LDL-C at 4 weeks [MD -0.95 mmol/L (95% CI: −1.51 to −0.40); P = 0.0007; I2 = 96% (high heterogeneity (HH)); very low certainty of evidence (VLCE); Fig. 3a].

Discussion

The positive impact of PCSK9i therapy on long term cardiovascular outcomes is well established.16,17 There is accumulating evidence for early LDL lowering and improved cardiovascular outcomes with the use of PCSK9i in the early phase of an ACS event. A recently published systematic review highlighted a significant decrease in LDL-C from initiation of evolocumab therapy to 8 weeks following ACS compared to placebo.18 Our meta-analysis highlights the impressive additional LDL-C and total cholesterol lowering when PCSK9i is added to high dose statin within 48 h of an ACS event, even after 4 weeks of therapy. An additional −28.16% and −19.83% lowering was seen in LDL-C and total cholesterol respectively when PCSK9 inhibitors were added over high dose statins. Interestingly, an additional −11.04% lowering in serum triglycerides was noted when PCSK9 inhibitors were added to high dose statins. The current paradigm for lipid management favours a stepwise approach in patients with ACS and calls for early high-intensity statin initiation, followed by the addition of ezetimibe, and finally, consideration of PCSK9i treatment if LDL-C levels continue to be elevated.19,20 However, with such a strategy, PCSK9i therapy would be considered for ACS patients with elevated LDL-C levels only a few months after their index event. However, the risk of subsequent ischaemic events is highest in the immediate aftermath of an ACS. The risk of experiencing a subsequent ischaemic event is almost 6-fold higher immediately following discharge for the index ACS, as compared with the period ≥1 year after hospital discharge. This significantly high risk just after ACS hospitalisation highlights the value of risk reduction methods when used during or right after ACS hospitalisation.21

Traditionally, the thrombotic and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) pathophysiological pathways have been identified as the leading mechanisms involved in ACS recurrence.22 The early clinical benefit observed in the acute phase after ACS has been hypothesised to be a result of additional beneficial biologic effects of statins on endothelial function, coagulation and inflammation; however, that benefit is thought to be mediated, at least in part, by the decline in LDL-C levels.23 Recognizing the importance of early reduction, guidelines recommend a LDL-C goal of <55 mg/dL along with a reduction in LDL-C levels by 50%.24

Though, statins form the cornerstones of LDL lowering after an ACS event, majority of patients do not achieve LDL targets and there is still a high proportion of patients who experience recurrent ACS. High dose atorvastatin reduced the occurrence of death from any Cause or a major Cardiovascular Event by 17% at 4 weeks in the PROVE IT trial.23 An important observation noted in this meta-analysis was a nearly 75% reduction in hospital re-admission for a recurrent cardiovascular event when PCSK9i was added over high dose statin at the time of index ACS event.

In addition to LDL-C lowering, PCSK9i can act in pleotropic ways that can contribute to the improved cardiovascular outcomes. Plaque regression is another metric which leads to favourable cardiovascular outcomes following ACS. In the GLAGOV (Global Assessment of Plaque Regression with a PCSK9 Antibody as Measured by Intravascular ultrasonography) trial, 968 patients with coronary artery disease were randomised to either evolocumab or a placebo and underwent repeated measurements of the volume of coronary atheroma using intravascular ultrasonography. In the evolocumab group, the LDL-C level was lower (36.6 vs. 93.0 mg/dL), and the atheroma volume was significantly decreased (−0.95%) compared to the placebo group.25 Increased lipoprotein (a) is known to contribute to residual cardiovascular risk in people with ACS.26 At present, we do not have good reliable pharmacologic agents to reduce lipoprotein (a) and lipoprotein apheresis is the only option for very high Lp(a) with progressive cardiovascular disease despite optimal management of risk factors.27 In this regard, a healthy additional −29.26% reduction in lipoprotein (a) was noted with PCSK9 inhibitors when added to high dose statins in patients with ACS.

Addition of PCSK9 inhibitor to high dose statin was well tolerated without any increase in total or severe adverse events. A limitation of this meta-analysis is that only evolocumab was the PCSK9i evaluated in all the 3 RCTs in this meta-analysis. Hence whether these results can be replicated with other PCSK9i remains an area of research. Also, all the outcomes of PCSK9i in this meta-analysis has been obtained compared to placebo. Ezetemibe and bempedoic acid are other potent LDL-C and total cholesterol lowering agents. Hence studies are warranted comparing PCSK9i to ezetimibe and bempedoic acid, when added over background high dose statins in patients with an acute ACS event. Advantage of PCSK9i in ACS is the need for 1–2 injections only at the time of ACS, ensuring good patient compliance.

To conclude, it may be said that addition of PCSK9i to high dose statin at the time of ACS event is associated with a significantly better reduction of LDL-C, total cholesterol and lipoprotein (a) at 1–2 months follow up, which translates into a significantly lower hospital re-admission for a recurrent event post an index event of ACS.

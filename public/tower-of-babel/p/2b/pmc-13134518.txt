Circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1 Induces Mitophagy‐Mediated Ferroptosis via HMOX1 mRNA Stabilization in Pancreatic Cancer
Authors: Qun Zhao, Xihao Yao, Jingjing Lu, Qiyang Chen, Zhiwei Wang, Xiaohong Li, Yuhua Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPancreatic cancer (PC) represents a highly aggressive malignancy characterized by a 5 year survival rate of less than 12%. Recent investigations suggest that mitophagy may constitute a potential therapeutic target for PC. This study aims to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying circ‐0030167's regulation of PC, extending our prior investigations. mRNA sequencing analysis demonstrated significant enrichment of mitophagy‐related signaling pathways in PC cells overexpressing circ‐0030167. Integrated analysis utilizing RNA‐binding protein (RBP) databases identified IGF2BP1 as a binding partner, a finding corroborated by RNA pull‐down assays, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiments, and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) validation. Cell‐derived xenograft (CDX) assays confirmed that circ‐0030167 enhances IGF2BP1 protein stability. Subsequent bioinformatic analysis combined with mRNA‐seq data revealed HMOX1 as a downstream target gene within the circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1‐mediated mitophagy pathway. Functional assays measuring ferroptosis‐related parameters—cell viability, reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, malondialdehyde (MDA) content, and Fe2+ concentration—established that the circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1 axis modulates mitophagy‐mediated ferroptosis through HMOX1. Furthermore, in vivo animal studies demonstrated that circ‐0030167 overexpression markedly suppresses PC tumor growth. In conclusion, our findings identify the circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1/HMOX1 axis as a critical regulatory factor targeting mitochondria‐mediated ferroptosis, thereby presenting a novel therapeutic target for PC combination therapies.Keywords: Circ‐0030167, ferroptosis, HMOX1, IGF2BP1, mitophagy

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is an exceptionally lethal malignant neoplasm, characterized by a dismal 5 year survival rate of merely 12% [1]. While surgical intervention continues to be the cornerstone of treatment for patients diagnosed with PC, the innovation of chemotherapy and immunotherapy constitutes the core for improving the prognosis of patients. The main mechanism underlying chemotherapy, immunity, and targeted therapy in PC lies in the induction of programmed cell death within cancer cells [2]. Mitophagy and ferroptosis are new forms of programmed cell death, providing innovative targets and pathways for therapeutic interventions in PC [3].

Mitophagy represents a specialized form of autophagy that enhances cellular metabolism by sequestering damaged mitochondria [4, 5]. Mitophagy exhibits multifaceted roles in PC; prior research has demonstrated that STOML2 modulates mitophagy via the PARL/PINK1 signaling axis, consequently attenuating chemoresistance [6]. Furthermore, recent investigations demonstrate that mitophagy modulates cellular demise mechanisms [7]. MTE upregulates HMOX‐1, inducing cellular oxidative stress and causing mitochondrial dysfunction; this consequently activates mitophagy, which accelerates ferroptosis in osteosarcoma cells [8].

Ferroptosis is a newly discovered form of cell death resulting from the accumulation of lipid peroxides mediated by divalent iron ions [9, 10]. Research has indicated that targeting ferroptosis in PC cells has emerged as a new focus in the fields of chemotherapy and immunotherapy [11, 12]. Nevertheless, researchers have gradually discovered that the simple targeted induction of ferroptosis in cancer cells has a two‐sided effect on the treatment of PC [13], while eliminating cancer cells, drugs also pose a risk of damaging the local tumor immune microenvironment [14]. Hence, the search for more stable and targeted forms of ferroptosis is the key to overcoming these limitations.

As a recently identified class of non‐coding RNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs) are defined by their covalently closed, single‐stranded circular configuration [15, 16]. This distinctive topological configuration confers resistance to exonuclease‐mediated degradation, thereby facilitating their stable expression and functional activity across diverse cellular environments [17]. CircRNAs function as key regulators in the malignant progression of PC, critically modulating fundamental oncogenic processes including cellular proliferation, invasion, migration, and the maintenance of stemness properties [18, 19]. CircEPS15 enhances PINK1‐PRKN‐dependent mitophagy, facilitating damaged mitochondria elimination and mitochondrial homeostasis maintenance [20]. Circ‐0030167 was first identified via genome‐wide Salzman circRNA sequencing, formed by reverse splicing of exon 3 in TPT1 pre‐mRNA. Our investigations revealed its overexpression in exosomes from bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, modulating PC malignant progression [21]. Yet, whether circ‐0030167 regulates the malignant progression of PC through mitophagy and ferroptosis is unknown.

In this study, we found that circ‐0030167 plays a critical regulatory role in mitophagy and ferroptosis in PC. This circular RNA enhances the stability of IGFBP1 protein through direct molecular interaction. This interaction promotes IGF2BP1 binding to HMOX1 mRNA, thereby stabilizing HMOX1 mRNA through m6A‐dependent mechanisms. These findings establish novel theoretical foundations and identify molecular targets for PC diagnosis and treatment.

Methods and Materials

Cell Culture and Transfection

The human pancreatic cancer cell lines PANC‐1 and MIA PaCa‐2, along with the normal pancreatic ductal epithelial cells HPDE6‐C7, were sourced from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Shanghai. PANC‐1 and HPDE6‐C7 cells were cultured in high‐glucose DMEM (Gibco, USA) containing 10% FBS (Gibco) and 1% penicillin (Invitrogen, USA). MIA‐PaCa‐2 cells were grown in high‐glucose DMEM (Gibco) containing 10% FBS (Gibco), 5% HS (Gibco), and 1% sodium pyruvate and streptomycin (Invitrogen). All cells were grown in a 5% CO2, 37°C humidified incubator.

OE‐circ‐0030167, si‐IGF2BP1, pcDNA‐IGF2BP1, and si‐HMOX1 were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China) (Table 1) and transfected into PANC‐1 and MIA‐PaCa‐2 cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA), following the manufacturer's instructions. Two days post‐transfection, the cells were harvested for assessment of interference efficiency or utilized in subsequent experiments.

Primers, probe, siRNAs, and OE sequences.

CCK‐8 Assay

Cellular proliferation was evaluated using a CCK‐8 and Colony Formation assay. Prior to plating in 96‐well plates at a density of 1000 cells per well, cells were transfected with either OE‐circ‐0030167 or control constructs. At designated time points (24, 48, 72, 96, or 120 h), 10 μL of CCK‐8 solution (Dojindo, Japan) was added to each well, followed by an additional incubation period of 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was subsequently measured using a microplate reader (Molecular Devices, USA).

Analysis of Public Datasets

The analysis of the TCGA dataset was conducted using both TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) and GEPIA (http://gepia.cancer‐pku.cn/). The downstream target proteins of circ‐0030167 were predicted through a combined analysis utilizing RBPmap (https://rbpmap.technion.ac.il/) and RNA inter (http://www.rna‐society.org/rnainter/).

Quantitative Real‐Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT‐qPCR)

Trizol (Invitrogen) was utilized for RNA extraction from cells in accordance with the provided instructions. A High Capacity cDNA Synthesis kit (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA) was used to synthesize DNA. All qPCR reactions were performed with a SYBR Premix Ex Taq kit (Vazyme Biotech Co. Ltd) and a real‐time quantitative PCR instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., UK). To measure RNA expression, Circ‐0030167, IGF2BP1, HMOX1, GPX4, and LC3 primers were used (RiboBio, China) (Table 1). GAPDH rRNA served as controls for mRNA and circRNA expression, respectively.

ROS Analyses

Cells were treated to plating in 96‐well plates (2 × 10 [3]/ml), and 200 μL of the ROS detection kit (DOJINDO, Japan) was added to the cells. The cells were then incubated at 37°C for 20 min and washed three times with serum‐free cell culture medium. Finally, the optical density (OD) was measured by the microplate reader at 525 nm (Flouroscan FL, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The ROS content was quantified through spectrophotometric analysis.

Fe2+ Analyses

Cells were treated to plating in 96‐well plates (2 × 103/mL), and 1 μmol/L of the Fe2+ detection kit (DOJINDO, Japan) was added to the cells. The cells were then incubated at 37°C for 30 min. The optical density (OD) at 580 nm was measured by the microplate reader (Flouroscan FL, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The Fe2+ content was calculated by the spectrophotometric analysis.

MDA Analyses

The cell lysate was centrifuged at 12,000 g and 4°C for 10 min to obtain the supernatant (100 μL). Then, 200 μL of the MDA detection working solution was added, mixed thoroughly, and heated at 100°C for 15 min. The mixture was cooled to room temperature (RT) and centrifuged at 1000 g and RT for 10 min to obtain the supernatant (200 μL). The optical density (OD) at 532 nm was measured by the microplate reader (Flouroscan FL, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The MDA content was calculated by the standard curve method.

Pull‐Down Assay

The RNA‐pulldown assay kit (Genecreate, Wuhan, China) was utilized to detect the associated RBP proteins of hsa_circ_0030167. Biotin‐labeled probes targeting the binding sites of hsa_circ_0030167 were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) (Table 1). The probes were incubated with cell lysates to form RNA‐protein complexes. Subsequently, these complexes were isolated using streptavidin‐conjugated magnetic beads. After separation via 10% polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE), the associated RBPs of hsa_circ_0030167 were analyzed through Western blotting.

RIP Assay

The RIP assay was conducted using the RIP detection kit (Genecreate, Wuhan, China). Cell lysates were incubated with magnetic beads and either anti‐IGF2BP1 (Abcam, UK) or anti‐IgG (antibody clone, China). The enriched RNA was analyzed using real‐time quantitative PCR.

MeRIP

The experiment utilized the MeRIP m6A transcriptome analysis kit (BersinBio, Guangzhou, China), strictly adhering to the manufacturer's protocol. Initially, total RNA was fragmented. Subsequently, the fragmented RNA was incubated with anti‐m6A antibody‐conjugated magnetic beads at 4°C for 2 h. Following extensive washing, the enriched m6A‐modified RNA was eluted and purified using the RNeasy Mini KitmirVana PARIS RNA and Native Protein Purification Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The purified RNA was then subjected to real‐time quantitative PCR analysis.

Transmission Electron Microscopy

Cancer cells were fixed with 3% glutaraldehyde and 2% paraformaldehyde. Samples were post‐fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide and stained with 1% uranyl acetate. Specimens were dehydrated in ethanol, then infiltrated and embedded. Ultra‐thin sections were cut using a Leica Ultracut microtome. Staining was done with uranyl acetate and lead citrate in a Leica EM staining machine. Finally, samples were examined under a JEM 1010 transmission electron microscope at 80 kV.

RNA Degradation Assay

In the cellular culture system, actinomycin D (ACTD) was administered at a concentration of 1 μg/mL to assess RNA stability. RNA specimens were harvested at 0, 4, 8, and 12 h post‐treatment for subsequent RT‐qPCR analysis. Transcript levels were normalized against circ‐0030167 or HMOX1 mRNA expression at the 0 h timepoint, which served as internal reference controls.

Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization

RNA FISH was performed with HANBIO reagents (Shanghai, China). PC cells were seeded on slides in a 24‐well plate. After fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilization with 0.3% Triton X‐100, nonspecific binding was blocked by incubation at 37°C for 30 min. Hybridization with circ‐0030167 probe conjugated to SA‐Cy3 was conducted overnight at 37°C to visualize RNA signals. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and images were acquired using a confocal microscope. Probe sequences are in Table 1.

Confocal Microscopy

Complete medium and polybrene were utilized to prepare a working solution with a concentration of 2 μg/mL. PC cells were extracted and infected with mRFP‐GFP‐LC3 lentivirus by employing the 1/2 small‐volume infection method. The cells were replaced with fresh complete medium 24 h after infection. MitoBright LT Deep Red probe and DAPI staining were added to the cell culture medium and observed through confocal microscopy (LSM 980, Carl Zeiss AG, Germany). The Pearson coefficient was utilized to quantify the fluorescence co‐localization.

Western‐Blot

Proteins from PC tissue and cells were extracted with RIPA buffer and quantified using BCA kitBeyotime Biotechnology. Samples were separated on SDS‐PAGE gels and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at RT and then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies (1:1000). After incubation, membranes were treated with HRP‐labeled secondary antibody (1:5000) for 2 h at RT. Proteins were visualized by chemiluminescence and normalized to β‐actin using Image‐Pro Plus. Antibodies for HMOX1, IGF2B1, LC3, GPX4, and β‐actin were from Abcam. Secondary antibodies (HRP Goat Anti‐Rabbit and Anti‐Mouse IgG) were from Cell Signaling Technology.

Immunoblot Analysis

Ki67/IGF2BP1/HMOX1/GPX4/LC3 protein levels were assessed by IHC staining. Paraffin‐embedded tumor tissue sections (4 μm) were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibody (ABclonal, China), stained for 30 min at room temperature with HRP‐conjugated secondary antibody (ABclonal, China), visualized with DAB (ABclonal, China), and imaged using a Leica DMi8 microscope.

Xenograft Model Assays

Female BALB/c nude mice (6 weeks old) were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center of Nantong University and housed under specific pathogen‐free conditions at the animal care facility of Nantong University. The mice underwent subcutaneous implantation in the left axilla with 100 μL of MIA‐PaCa‐2 cells (1 × 106 cells) following transfection with OE‐circ‐0030167. After a period of 24 days, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with 3% sodium pentobarbital (30 mg/kg) and euthanized via cervical dislocation.

Statistical Analysis

GraphPad Prism 10.0 was used for all statistical analyses. Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Student's t‐test compared differences between two groups, while one‐way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test was used for multiple groups. p < 0.05 indicated significant differences.

Results

Circ‐0030167 Is Significantly Downregulated in PC Tissues and Regulates Cellular Mitophagy

Circ_0030167 is derived from back‐splicing of exon 3 at the TPT1 gene locus, yielding a 191‐nucleotide sequence (Figure 1A). Circ‐0030167 expression was significantly downregulated in PC tissue compared to normal pancreatic tissue. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed a marked reduction in PC tissues relative to adjacent non‐cancerous tissues (Figure 1B,C). Moreover, elevated circ‐0030167 expression was associated with significantly improved patient survival prognosis (Figure 1D). To further elucidate the role of circ‐0030167 in PC cells, mRNA microarray analysis was conducted on cells both prior to and following transfection with OE‐circ‐0030167. mRNA‐seq analysis demonstrated that circ‐0030167 overexpression induced global transcriptional alterations (Figure 1E,F). Subsequent to circ‐0030167 overexpression, significant perturbations in mitophagy occurred within PC cells (Figure 1G,H). These findings indicate that OE‐circ‐0030167 might regulate PC cells through mitophagy.

OE‐Circ‐0030167 Increased the Level of Mitophagy in PC Cells

To validate OE‐circ‐0030167‐enhanced mitophagy in PC cells, we analyzed LC3 expression via Western blot [4]. Results showed elevated LC3B in the OE‐circ‐0030167 group, indicating enhanced mitophagy (Figure 2A). TEM analysis revealed mitochondrial structural damage and autophagosomal engulfment in circ‐0030167‐overexpressing PC cells versus controls. (Figure 2B). Confocal microscopy confirmed that circ‐0030167 overexpression promoted mitolysosome formation, evidenced by MitoTracker and LysoTracker co‐localization (Figure 2C). The administration of mdivi‐1, a recognized mitophagy inhibitor [22], led to a downregulation of LC3B‐II expression in PC cells overexpressing circ‐0030167 (Figure 2D), and reduced the quantity of mitochondria‐associated autophagosomes as observed by transmission electron microscopy and confocal microscopy (Figure 2E,F). These data demonstrated that OE‐circ‐0030167 augmented mitophagy levels in PC cells.

Circ‐0030167 Promotes Mitophagy in PC Cells by Stabilizing IGF2BP1 Protein

RBP is the primary pathway for functional modification of circRNA‐binding proteins, modulating pancreatic cancer progression [23, 24]. To elucidate circ‐0030167's role in PC cells, we used a Venn diagram to analyze RBP map and RNA interactome database, identifying IGF2BP1 as a candidate (Figure 3A). Furthermore, we employed RBPsuite to validate that IGF2BP1 may serve as a potential interacting protein of circ‐0030137 (Figure 3B). The RNA pull‐down results demonstrated that IGF2BP1 showed significant enrichment among proteins associated with circ‐0030167(Figure 3C). In addition, RIP detection confirmed the interaction between IGF2BP1 and circ‐0030167(Figure 3D). FISH and immunofluorescence showed colocalization of circ‐0030167 and IGF2BP1 in PC cells, suggesting direct interaction (Figure 3E). To clarify this, we assessed OE‐circ‐0030167's effects on IGF2BP1 mRNA and protein levels. RT‐qPCR showed no significant change in IGF2BP1 mRNA with circ‐0030167 overexpression (Figure 3F). Conversely, it increased IGF2BP1 protein abundance (Western blot, Figure 3G). Moreover, it prolonged IGF2BP1 half‐life (Figure 3H). These suggest circ‐0030167 enhances IGF2BP1 protein stability.

Circ‐0030167 Enhances HMOX‐1 mRNA Stability by IGF2BP1

We elucidated how circ‐0030167 modulates mitophagy in PC via IGF2BP1 by identifying HMOX1 as a key regulator through analysis of mitophagy upregulation, mRNA profiles, and MitoCarta3.0 (Figure 5A). PINK1 is a core mitophagy regulator. TCGA analysis revealed a positive correlation between HMOX1 expression and PC, supporting HMOX1's association with mitophagy (Figure 5B). RT‐qPCR and Western blot showed circ‐0030167 overexpression increased HMOX1 mRNA and protein levels (Figure 5C,D). Correlation analysis showed a positive association between IGF2BP1 and HMOX1 expression, indicating regulatory interplay (Figure 5E). RIP assays confirmed direct HMOX1 mRNA‐IGF2BP1 interaction (Figure 5F). IGF2BP1, an m6A reader, stabilizes target mRNA. SRAMP predicted m6A sites on HMOX1 mRNA (Figure 5G). RT‐qPCR and MERIP demonstrated HMOX1 mRNA enrichment by m6A antibodies, suggesting IGF2BP1 binds and stabilizes it via m6A recognition (Figure 5H). Overexpression studies examined circ‐0030167‐IGF2BP1 interaction effects on HMOX1 expression. ACTD‐based stability analyses showed circ‐0030167 overexpression extended HMOX1 mRNA half‐life (Figure 5I).

To address the core question of m6A‐dependency for IGF2BP1 function, we have treated cells with a selective m6A methyltransferase inhibitor (STM2457) to globally reduce m6A methylation (Figure S1A), which significantly reduced IGF2BP1 binding to HMOX1 mRNA in RIP‐qPCR assays (Figure S1B). Abrogated the stabilizing effect of IGF2BP1 on HMOX1 mRNA was measured by actinomycin D chase assays (Figure S1C,D). RIP validated enhanced IGF2BP1 binding to HMOX1 mRNA with circ‐0030167 overexpression (Figure 5J). Combined circ‐0030167 overexpression and si‐IGF2BP1 altered HMOX1 expression, indicating enhanced binding affinity (Figure 5K–M). Thus, circ‐0030167 enhances HMOX1 mRNA stability m6A‐dependently via IGF2BP1.

Circ‐0030167 Facilitates Mitophagy‐Mediated Ferroptosis of PC via HMOX1

We investigated if circ‐0030167 regulates mitophagy in PC via HMOX1 by knocking down HMOX1 (Figure 6A,B). HMOX1 depletion abolished circ‐0030167's mitophagy‐promoting effects in PC cells (Figure 6C–F). HMOX1 is a core regulator of mitophagy and ferroptosis. Volcano plot analysis identified HMOX1 as a key ferroptosis regulator in PC (Figure 7A), correlating positively with ROS accumulation (Figure 7B). GSEA linked circ‐0030167 to ferroptosis function (Figure 7C), and HMOX1 expression correlated with ferroptosis‐inducing molecules NCOA4, FTH1, ACSL4, and TFRC in PC tissues (Figure 7D–G). Furthermore, GPX4 overexpression can significantly rescue the observed reduction in cell viability, accumulation of Fe2+, ROS, and MDA induced by HMOX1 (Figure S2). Fer‐1 rescued circ‐0030167 overexpression‐induced viability inhibition (Figure 7H). To test circ‐0030167 modulation of ferroptosis via mitophagy, we overexpressed it and treated cells with Mdivi‐1. Circ‐0030167 increased oxidative stress (MDA, ROS), enhanced iron release, and downregulated GPX4 (Figure 7I–M). Mdivi‐1 reversed these effects, restoring GPX4, viability, and reducing oxidative stress and iron release, indicating circ‐0030167 promotes ferroptosis through mitophagy in PC cells.

We collected PC tissues and quantified circ‐0030167, mitophagy markers (PINK1), and ferroptosis markers (HMOX1, GPX4). Statistical analyses showed circ‐0030167 positively correlated with PINK1 and HMOX1, and negatively correlated with GPX4 (Figure S3). This evidence strengthens clinical relevance and supports the functional link between circ‐0030167 downregulation and mitophagy/ferroptosis activation in pancreatic cancer.

Our study demonstrates that circ‐0030167 augments mitophagy‐associated ferroptosis in PC cells through its interaction with IGF2BP1, thereby enhancing the stability of HMOX1 mRNA. (Figure 10). These findings suggest that circ‐0030167 induces mitophagy‐mediated ferroptosis in vivo.

Discussion

Although early diagnostic methodologies are critically important in PC, existing preventive and therapeutic interventions remain markedly inadequate [25]. Consequently, the identification of effective biomarkers constitutes a pivotal strategy for formulating novel therapeutic strategies against PC [26, 27]. Recent investigations demonstrate that circular RNAs (circRNAs) exert critical regulatory functions in the pathogenesis and progression of PC, suggesting that targeting circRNAs represents a promising frontier for oncological therapeutics [15, 28, 29].

Previously, we have identified circ0030167 as a suppressor of stemness and malignant progression in PC cells [21]. Our investigation demonstrated that circ‐0030167 exhibits significantly reduced expression in PC tissues, with its expression levels demonstrating a significant positive correlation with patient survival. Furthermore, functional clustering analysis of mRNA‐seq data derived from circ‐0030167‐overexpressing PC cells established that this circRNA primarily regulates PC cells through mitophagy mechanisms (Figure 1). Through validation employing autophagy‐related protein LC3 expression levels, transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and confocal microscopy, we observed a significant elevation in mitophagy within PC cells (Figure 2). In vivo experiments utilizing circ‐0030167 overexpression confirmed that circ‐0030167 suppresses subcutaneous tumor growth in PC (Figure 9). Research elucidating the regulatory functions of circRNAs on mitophagy is well‐documented: for instance, m5C‐modified circREB1 induces mitophagy to promote malignancy progression in lung cancer [30], while circERCC2 enhances mitophagy via the miR‐182‐5p/SIRT1 axis, accelerating intervertebral disc degeneration [31].

CircRNAs can interact with one or more proteins, functioning as protein baits, scaffolds, or recruiters [32, 33]. Subsequently, circRNAs orchestrate cellular functions through specific protein interactions [34]. For instance, exosomal circ_0003057 enhances osteogenic/odontogenic differentiation of human dental pulp stem cells (hDPSCs) by interacting with EIF4A3 and upregulating ANKH expression [35]. Furthermore, circ0006646 impedes the interaction between nucleolin (NCL) and the E3 ubiquitin ligase tripartite motif‐containing 21 (TRIM21), thus attenuating ubiquitin‐mediated degradation of NCL [36, 37, 38]. Here, through RBP‐related database retrieval, bioinformatics analysis, pull‐down, RIP, and FISH functional experiments, we demonstrated that IGF2BP1 serves as the primary protein binding target of circ‐0030167. Furthermore, circ‐0030167 enhances IGF2BP1 protein stability via this binding interaction (Figure 3). Overexpression of IGF2BP1 was shown to significantly enhance mitophagy in PC cells, while si‐IGF2BP1 partially reversed the enhanced mitophagic activity induced by circ‐0030167 overexpression (Figure 4). Mechanistically, these findings suggest that circ‐0030167 may activate mitophagy through its interaction with IGF2BP1.

Bioinformatic integration of mRNA sequencing data demonstrated that heme oxygenase‐1 (HMOX1), a member of the heme oxygenase family, represents a potential downstream target mediating circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1‐regulated mitophagy. HMOX1 serves as the rate‐limiting enzyme in heme catabolism. It primarily modulates cellular function through the regulation of oxidative stress and inflammatory responses, which consequently participates in the pathogenesis of numerous diseases [39, 40]. Genetic inhibition of HOMX‐1 overexpression in HT22 cells mitigated both mitochondrial damage and hypertrophy induced by PM2.5 exposure [41]. Subsequent functional assays demonstrated that HMOX1 ablation entirely abolished the mediatory function of circRNA‐0030167 in promoting mitophagy within PC cells (Figure 6).

Following circ‐0030167 expression, RT‐qPCR and Western blotting showed HMOX1 upregulation at transcriptional and posttranscriptional levels. IGF2BP1, a major RNA‐binding protein localized to the membrane and cytoplasm, regulates RNA m6A modifications or downstream signaling by binding RBP structures [42, 43]. As an m6A recognition protein, it controls mRNA fate and enhances target mRNA stability via post‐transcriptional regulation [44, 45]. We propose circ‐0030167 binds and stabilizes HMOX1 mRNA through IGF2BP1's m6A site. RIP and MeRIP assays validated IGF2BP1 interaction with m6A‐modified HMOX1 mRNA. Our findings demonstrate circ‐0030167 increases HMOX1 stability via IGF2BP1, promoting m6A‐dependent mitophagy in PC cells (Figure 5).

Dysregulated HMOX1 expression has been documented across diverse solid tumors and exhibits an intricate linkage with malignant phenotypes, including ferroptosis [46, 47]. As a hemoglobin‐degrading enzyme, HMOX1 plays a critical role in regulating ferroptosis through the mitophagy process [8]. Genetic inhibition of HOMX‐1 overexpression in HT22 cells mitigated both mitochondrial damage and hypertrophy induced by PM2.5 exposure [41]. Mitophagy ameliorates cisplatin‐induced ferroptosis in renal tubular cells via the ROS/HOMX‐1/GPX4 signaling axis [48]. We demonstrated that overexpression of circ‐0030167 induced ferroptosis in PC cells, which was rescued by the mitophagy inhibitor Mdivi‐1 (Figure 7). Subsequently, functional validation experiments conducted across circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1/HMOX1 axis‐defined groups confirmed that HMOX1, transcriptionally regulated by the circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1 axis, promotes mitophagy‐mediated ferroptosis PC cells (Figure 8). Therefore, circ‐0030167/IGF2BP1 coordinates mitophagy and ferroptosis functions in PC cells through targeting HMOX1, and this coordination augments antitumor efficacy.

In conclusion, as an RBP binding target of circ‐0030167, IGF2BP1 regulates the stability of HMOX1 mRNA. Elucidating a novel mechanism in which circ‐0030167 targets IGF2BP1 to stabilize HMOX1 mRNA and induces mitophagy‐mediated ferroptosis in PC cells provides a promising therapeutic avenue (Figure 10).

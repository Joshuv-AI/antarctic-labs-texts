Micro-computed and cone beam-computed tomographies for identification of Canals’ morphological patterns of mandibular canines in Egyptian and Saudi Subpopulations: A comparative study
Authors: Manal Mohamed Abdelbaki, Mey A. Al-Habib, Mona Abdulaziz Alsulaiman, Ahmed Abdel Rahman Hashem
Published: 2024

Introduction

Morphological studies of the root canal system are fundamental to successful endodontic treatment. Understanding root canal system morphology, along with its variations and deviations from the norm, is mandatory (Altunsoy et al. 2014). This knowledge directly impacts the success of endodontic treatment by helping to prevent complications related to undetected root canals (Mahmoud, 2021).

A multitude of methods have been proposed and applied to depict normal canal system anatomy and many possible aberrations (Cantator et al. 2006), (Wolf et al. 2021). Vertucci’s classification is widely used to describe canal systems for educational and treatment purposes (Vertucci J. 1984), (Vertucci 2005). However, Ahmed and his co-workers (Ahmed and Dummer, 2018) (Ahmed et al., 2018) presented a new classification method that helped in a more accurate and detailed portrait, including the whole canal system and its ramifications in a simplified comprehensible manner.

Recently, cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) has allowed for more definite and confirmed three-dimensional (3D) morphological and clinical studies (Naseri et al. 2019), (Mustasfa et al. 2024) and retrospective in vivo studies (Alshayban et al. 2022), (Hashem, 2023), (Al-Dahman , et al. 2019), (Mirza et al 2022) always with racial referral. However, when Micro Computed Tomography(Micro-CT) was used in similar ex vivo studies from the same populations, different incidences and anatomical details were more clearly defined and confirmed (Mustasfa et al. 2024) (Zhang et al. 2023), (Bai et al. 2023). These innovations and enhanced precision led Ahmed et al. to present a complementary and explanatory issue that applies to studies conducted using the two new imaging systems (Ahmed, 2018). Nonetheless, their recent report depended principally on Micro-CT pictures as the gold standard benchmark for clear presentation of ex vivo morphological examples (Ahmed et al. 2021). It was then applied to CBCT images, both clinical or ex vivo, to simulate clinical scenarios.

Racial and/or population backgrounds behind many morphological tooth and canal anatomical patterns have been lately highlighted (Wolf et al. 2021), (Mustafa et al. 2024). Current related surveys showed many similar studies that were conducted with miscellaneous methods, mostly using a non-standardized tactic and on a single race (Tang et al. 2023), (Eghbal et al. 2020), (Al-Dahman et al. 2019), (Mirza et al 2022). Nevertheless, very few studies have focused on more than one subpopulation (Saad and Al-Yahya 2003), (Wolf et al. 2021). Recently, the globalization of this important issue was recognized through a new worldwide cross-sectional multicenter study on morphological variations of mandibular canines (Martins, et al. 2024). They reported differences in the incidence of canal configuration between African and Middle Eastern populations. However, the entire study was based on root canal configurations, as shown through a large field-of-view CBCT.

The purpose of this joint investigation was to portray and compare the morphological patterns of mandibular canine root canals and their incidence in Egyptian and Saudi subpopulations using Micro-CT and CBCT.

The null hypothesis was that there is no difference in the morphological patterns of mandibular canine teeth among Egyptian and Saudi subpopulations using either Micro-CT or CBCT.

Materials and Methods

Teeth grouping

Teeth collected from the Egyptian subpopulation were nominated as group EG, while teeth collected from the Saudi subpopulation were nominated as group SA.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using EPI INFO version 7.2.5.0. When applying a 99 % confidence interval with a 5 % margin of error and finite population correction, the process is as follows:

For CBCT examination, according to the results of previous studies (Bai, Binging et al, 2023) in which the prevalence of type I (single canal to the apex) in CBCT was 94 %, the minimum accepted sample size was 102 (+2) teeth per group.

For the Micro-CT examination, according to the results of the previous study (Tang, Ying. et al, 2023), type II (2–1) prevalence was 0.9 %. The minimum acceptable sample size was 24(+2) teeth per group.

Teeth selection and preparation

Teeth were collected from a pool of disinfected extracted teeth. The collected teeth met the following pre-specified criteria: single-rooted, no fractures in crowns or roots, no cracks, no external root resorption, and a fully formed apex. Radiography revealed no previous RCT, internal resorption, or calcification. The teeth were cleaned to remove calculus, washed, and disinfected by submerging in 0.5 percent NaOCl for 30 min before pouching and autoclaving.

Morphometric measurements

Were performed using Vernier calipers (Ingco HVC01150, Vernier Caliper 150 mm)(Chen. Min et al, 2022). Tooth length (L) was measured from the highest point on the mesial coronal slope to the root apex. Mesio-distal width (MD) was measured as the largest width at the cement-dentinal junction. Labio-lingual dimension (LL) was measured as the largest labio-lingual width at the cement-dentinal junction.

CBCT examination

Acquisition of CBCT images, three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction, and canal anatomy depiction

Images of the two groups were developed separately using i-CAT imaging system (Imaging Sciences International, Tokyo, Japan). The i-CAT has an amorphous silicon flat piece. Teeth samples were arranged in horseshoe-like wax rims, ready for imaging. In this study, the radiographic arena was 4 cm in height and16 cm in diameter. The scan time was 26.9 s. The working strictures were 120 kVp and 5 mA, with a slice thickness of 0.125 mm. One cycle of the 360° scan collected all forecast data for the reconstruction of tooth samples from groups EG and SA. Images were acquired and preserved separately on a computer for inspection of different plans.

CBCT images processing

The area of interest was the total tooth area from the coronal tip to the summit of the root apex of each tooth in each group. The tooth samples from the EG and SA groups were oriented with their long axes parallel to the sagittal and coronal reference lines, and perpendicular to the axial reference lines. Different morphological patterns were observed and recorded.

Micro-CT examination

Selected teeth from each study group were subjected to Micro-CT. Image acquisition, three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction, and canal(s) anatomy portrayal were detailed.

The volume of interest was selected as the total tooth length (Moore, Fitz-Walter, and Parashos 2009). For a detailed sagittal examination, the teeth were sliced starting from the cemento-enamel junction up to the root end in eight consecutive slices.

Software NRecon version 1.6.4.8 SkyScan 2011(Belgium), was used for raw data reconstruction to a bitam format using the equation: Result File Type = BMP, Pixel Size (µm) = 13.72966.

3D models of the examined canines were recorded, and external and internal tooth anatomies were obtained by volume rendering.

Specimens’ pictures examination

CBCT teeth specimens were examined separately by two evaluators, twice for 10 days between the two evaluations. The anatomical root canal types and aberrations were captured, calculated, and classified twice using Vertucci’s (Vertucci 2005) and Ahmed’s (Ahmed 2021) classifications. Accordingly sampled teeth were sent for Micro-CT examination (Pires et al 2024). Coronal plans were used to examine the root canal cross sections at eight levels spaced 3 mm apart at the first three levels from the canal orifice and 2 mm apart at the five preceding levels to the apical root end. The physiological and accessory foramina were also ascertained and described.

Comparison between CBCT and Micro-CT images was based on the ability to reveal and depict detailed micro-anatomy precisely on same specimen.

Statistical analysis

Data for morphometric measurements were tabulated, statistically analyzed, and compared. The mean and standard deviation values of L, MD, and LL were calculated for each group. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk tests were used to explore the data for normality. As it showed a parametric distribution, an independent sample test was selected for the comparison of non-related samples between more than two groups. The significance level was set at P≤0.05. Statistical analyses were doneby means of IBM® SPSS® Statistics Version 20 for Windows.

Results of CBCT and Micro-CT data are presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between groups were performed usingchi-square and Fisher’s exact tests.

Results

Kappa test results

Inter-examiner agreement was determined using the Kappa test as follows: 0.976 and 0.972 for canal type according to the Vertucci classification in the EG and SA group, respectively. It was 0.955 for canal type according to Ahmed’s classification. In contrast, 0.967 and 0.955 were recorded for the presence of apical ramifications in the EG and SA groups, respectively, and 0.88 for the presence of accessory foramina in the SA group. These values indicate good-to-very good agreement for all measurements.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated and compared the root canal morphology of canines from Egyptian and Saudi subpopulations. These two countries were selected for the following reasons. First, they represent two different ethnic groups: Africans and Asians. Second, dental specialists from the two countries shared dental treatments with both citizens. To our knowledge, no similar study has been mutually conducted before.

Significant differences were detected between the two studied groups for most of studied parameters. Accordingly, the tested hypothesis was rejected.

In the present study, morphometric tooth measurement showed a statistically significant longer tooth length and wider labio-lingual width in EG canine teeth than in SA teeth. The average tooth length for EG was 26.10 mm, which is within the highest internationally recorded length of 26.5 mms (Aminsobhani, et al. 2013). Saad and Al-Yahia found differences in tooth width and location at the Cemento-Dentinal Junction(CDJ) between Egyptian and Saudi populations (Saad and Al-Yahya 2003).

In our investigation, type I single-canalled mandibular canines were found in 94.23 % of the EG group and 92.3 % of the SA group. Nevertheless, in a global CBCT article in a worldwide review of the configuration of mandibular canines (Martins et al, 2024), the authors reported on a similar percentage of single canalled canines in Egyptians (94.0 %). On the other hand, in the same study, the percentage frequency in the Saudi subpopulation was reported to be 94.0 %. This figure was found to vary greatly in the literature concerning the Saudi population, where it was reported to be 91.8 % (Asiri, et al. 2022), 95.4 % (Al-Dahman et al. 2019), 97.48 % (Mirza et al 2022), and 98.3 % (Alshayban et al, 2022). This may be due to the broadness of Saudi Arabia.

A statistically significant higher incidence of type III (1TN1-2-1) canal configuration was found in 7.7 % and 5.8 % of mandibular canines in the Egyptian and Saudi subpopulations, respectively This difference may be related to the different populations. Therefore, it should be noted that a warning is mandatory during the preparation of such canals in a single-rooted anterior tooth.

Knowing and anticipating the detailed anatomical individuality and uniqueness of root and canal system variations is mandatory for both conventional nonsurgical and surgical treatments among populations. Micro-CT (in vitro) and CBCT (clinically) serve two interrelated tasks, with no replacement of the former by the latter. In the present study, CBCT was primarily used to examine whole-tooth samples from both subpopulations. Fifty-two teeth with filtered results were subjected to Micro-CT for further examination. The purpose of this investigation was to depict the differences in sensitivity for disclosing the anatomical details of aberrant canals, in line with a previous recent study (Wolf et al. 2021). (Pires, et al. 2024). Nevertheless, the detailed anatomical features observed using Micro-CT in the present study should be highlighted. These details could not be picked up and detected by CBCT due to the difference in voxel size (Pires et al, 2024). They mentioned that, accessory canals are not usually observed in CBCT studies ‘’except if large ‘’. Points of strength:

Recently, global studies that highlight the ethnicity of root canal system configurations are worldwide study of multicenter, cross-sectional type that offer many clinical, educational, and forensic benefits. This study is pioneering in that it presents a type of standardized collaborative joint research that uses the same study design, sample size, and viewing and interpreting methodologies as controllable variables.

Limitations:

First: the sample teeth did not consider the demographic factors for each of the studied populations. Second, the sex factor was not included. Accordingly, the results cannot be generalized and further ex vivo and in vivo studies on a larger sample size is deemed necessary.

Conclusions

Differences in incidence of root canal configurations of mandibular canines were depicted in Egyptian and Saudi subpopulations. The mandibular canines in the Egyptian group were longer and wider in labio-lingual width. The patterns 1TN1-1-1and 1TN1-2-1were the most common with different percentages in both studied groups, whereas 1TN1-1-2 was found solely in Saudis and detected only with Micro-CT. These morphological patterns should be considered clinically.

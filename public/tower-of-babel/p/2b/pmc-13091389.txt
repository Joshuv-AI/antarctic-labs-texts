The synergistic effects of soy protein isolate and curdlan on salt-reduced hairtail sausages: Investigation into saltiness perception and quality characteristics
Authors: Siqi Yang, Qianqian Zhang, Tong Zou, Baodong Zheng, Yi Zhang, Hongliang Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study explored the effects of the synergistic action of soy protein isolate (SPI) with curdlan (Cur) or gellan gum (GL) on the quality and flavor of salt-reduced hairtail sausage. Compared with the directly reduced-salt sausage (RS), the texture properties, rheological properties, and water retention capacity of RS-SPI-Cur or RS-SPI-GL were significantly improved due to the formation of a stable three-dimensional network structure. This network microstructure caused the uneven distribution of sodium ions, thereby enhancing saltiness perception. Sensory evaluation and e-tongue analysis confirmed that SPI and polysaccharides were beneficial for the release of sodium ions and enhanced saltiness perception. Chemical interaction and FT-IR analysis indicated that the observed improvements were driven by enhanced hydrogen bonding and protein cross-linking and promoted the shift of α-helix and random coil to β-sheet. These findings provided critical insights into the potential of SPI and polysaccharides as functional ingredients for developing high-quality, reduced-salt surimi products.

Introduction

Dietary factors pose a greater global burden in terms of mortality and disability-adjusted life years than any other risk, even smoking. While salt, sugar, and fat have dominated public health discussions, high sodium consumption is the leading dietary risk. It contributes to more than 2% of annual deaths and impacts cardiovascular health in a vast population (Yang, Zheng, Huang, Zhang, & Zeng, 2024). Given these statistics; reducing dietary sodium has become an imperative; yet achieving this goal remains a central challenge for product development. Salt not only promotes the flavor and maintains food characteristics; but also has preservative properties that extend the shelf life (Gao et al., 2024). For human health, moderate salt intake is necessary because sodium is an important electrolyte for maintaining nerve conduction, muscle function and acid-base balance in the body. Therefore, the primary challenge in reducing dietary salt is maintaining food characteristics and consumer acceptance.

Taste perception is a complex, multisensory process that arises from interactions at three key levels: physicochemical characteristics of matrix, oral processing dynamics, and neural signaling in the gustatory system (Hu et al., 2024). Food salt reduction measures that have been reported include the use of sodium substitutes; modification of the physical form of salt; novel processing techniques and modification of the food matrix structure. When food is consumed; salt ions are released into the mouth and the ions are transported by diffusion toward the taste buds; where they activate taste receptors to perceive the saltiness (Yang et al., 2024). Generally; potassium; magnesium and calcium are used as substitutes for sodium ions; glutamate; phosphate and lactate are replaced chloride ions; while salts with larger anions don't provide much of salty flavor (Busch, Yong, & Goh, 2013). These sodium substitutes are usually only used in small quantities; excessive substitution can cause bitter and metallic flavors; as well as negatively affect the texture; moisture; and other qualities of food (Chen, Liu, Xiang, Sun, & Tomasevic, 2023; Gao et al., 2024). Altering the physical form of salt has the effect of increasing saltiness by reducing the size or changing the shape of the salt particles. However, these salt particles are expensive and are more suitable for spreading onto the surface of solids (e.g. crisps) to enhance the perception of saltiness (Rios-Mera, Selani, Patinho, Saldaña, & Contreras-Castillo, 2021). For surimi products; saltiness perception is strongly influenced by textural characteristics and the extent of juice release during mastication. Novel processing techniques; including DC magnetic fields; pulsed electric fields etc. can improve saltiness by altering the microstructure and textural properties; but are not conducive to industrial application. Part of the gel-type colloidal molecules can be used as texture modifiers to regulate the textural properties and microstructure of food matrices; accelerating the release of salts from the matrices and thus improving the saltiness perception (Liu; McClements; Jin; & Chen). The gelatinous colloid possesses an inherent three-dimensional network structure; enhancing gel strength in surimi products and mitigating quality deterioration associated with salt reduction. It was shown that NaCl concentrations exceeding 0.3 M can promote the effective dissolution of MP and contribute to the establishment of a dense gel-based network structure; thus enhancing the quality performance of meat products in terms of emulsification; water retention and gelling ability. Carbohydrates and proteins are common biopolymers capable of forming hydrogels; combining two or more of these components can yield composite gel systems with superior physicochemical properties compared to single polymer gels (Zhou, Wang, Gao, Yan, & Wu, 2024). Soybean protein isolate is a non-meat protein widely used in meat products. It has good emulsification and gelation properties that improve the water holding capacity and texture quality of meat products (Li, Kang, Sukmanov, & Ma, 2021). Zhang et al. (2023) used SPI as a gel enhancer for reduced salt surimi and found that the gel network formed by SPI and myofibrillar proteins facilitated water retention in educed salt surimi. Curdlan is a bacterial extracellular polysaccharide produced by alkaline faecal bacteria or Agrobacterium (Liu et al., 2023). Curdlan can form two types of gels under different heating conditions: reversible gel and irreversible gel. Thermally reversible gels were formed after heating at 55 °C–60 °C then cooling it; whereas thermally irreversible gels were formed after heating at above 80 °C and cooling. Zhang et al. (2023) found that curdlan significantly improved the gel strength and microstructure of low-salt Nemipterus virgatus surimi. Gellan gum is an anionic extracellular bacterial polysaccharide with a brittle structure; which is conducive to flavor substance release (Morris, Nishinari, & Rinaudo, 2012). However, few studies have been conducted to improve saltiness of reduced salt surimi products by adding both proteins and polysaccharides.

In this study, we added both soybean protein isolate (SPI) and curdlan (Cur) or SPI and gellan gum (GL) to salt-reduced sausages, and evaluated the influences of SPI and polysaccharides on saltiness perception of salt-reduced hairtail sausages through sensory analyses, e-tongue, e-nose, and sodium ion release in the mouth. Microscopically, water migration and distribution, sodium ion distribution and intermolecular interaction forces were determined to elucidate the mechanisms promoting sodium ion release, aiming to provide a scientific basis for reducing salt content in surimi gelatinous foods.

Materials and methods

Reagents and materials

SPI was from Linyi Shandong biological Co., LTD (Shandong, China). Gellan gum was from Xinjiang Fufeng Biotechnology Co., LTD (Xinjiang, China). Curdlan was obtained from Hyos Biotechnology Co., LTD (Shandong, China). Commercial frozen hairtail surimi was from Haixin Foods Co., Ltd. (Fujian, China). BCA protein quantitation kit was purchased from Solarboi Technology Co., LTD (Beijing, China). NaCl and urea were purchased from Xilong Chemical Co., Ltd. (Guangdong, China). β-mercaptoethanol was from Maclin Technology Co., LTD (Shanghai, China).

Preparation of hairtail sausage

According to the formulations of (Man, Sun, Lin, Ren, & Li, 2024; Wang, Feng, & Xia, 2021) With a few minor adjustments. Commercial frozen hairtail surimi was at 4 °C for 4 h. First, salt and ice water were mixed into surimi and homogenized using a meat mincer for 1 min at a speed of 1500 r/min. The mixture was then held for 2 min to facilitate the dissolution of myofibrillar proteins before the addition of pork fat and ice water. These ingredients were subsequently chopped under the same conditions (1500 r/min for 1 min). Subsequently, potato starch, white granulated sugar, monosodium glutamate, phosphate, white pepper powder and ice water were added, and the mixture was homogenized for 3 min under the same conditions. 10% SPI and 1.5% polysaccharides of experimental group were also added during this process. The addition amounts of protein and polysaccharide were obtained from previous experiments and the specific data are shown in Fig. S1. The ice water was added several times. The surimi was in the automatic machine (Yong kang technology Co., LTD, Zhe jiang, China) to form 15 cm sausages each. The sausages were heated in a water bath at 90 °C for 30 min (Zhang, Pan, et al., 2023), then stored at 4 °C to obtain hairtail sausages. The formulations of all experimental groups are shown in Supplement Table S1. The high-salt group was used as the control group with 3 g salt /100 g hairtail surimi, the reduced-salt group with 2.25 g salt /100 g hairtail surimi. The preparation of sausage samples with different formulations was uniformly carried out according to the aforementioned process.

Determination of texture profile analysis (TPA)

The parameters of texture analyzer (TA-XT. plus, Stable Micro Systems Ltd., UK) were referring to Zhang et al. (2023). The test speed was set to 60 mm/min, and the samples were compressed to 50% deformation. Hardness, springiness, cohesiveness, and chewiness were recorded.

Determination of rheology characteristic

The dynamic rheological properties were conducted using HR 20 rheometer (Waters Instruments Co., LTD, USA) referring to (Li et al., 2022). The viscosity curves of sausages were determined at shear rates of 0.1–100 s−1 at a constant temperature of 25 °C. The storage modulus (G') and loss modulus (G") of sausages were conducted at fixed strain of 1% and frequency of 0.1 to 100 rad/s.

Observation of microstructure

The microstructures of gel samples were visualized by following the procedure of Wang et al. (2021) using a scanning electron microscope (Ai Lang Instrument Co., LTD, Shanghai, China). Microstructure of all samples was observed at an accelerating voltage of 20 kV. The images were captured at the magnification of 200 × .

Observation of distribution of sodium elements

A scanning electron microscope (Ai Lang Instrument Co., LTD, Shanghai, China) was used to identify the observation area and magnification, and then an energy spectrometer (Oxford Instrument Technology LTD, Shanghai, China) was operated to observe the sodium elemental distributions at an accelerating voltage of 20 kV (Filgueras, Peyrin, Vénien, Hénot, & Astruc, 2016).

Low-field nucleic magnetic resonance (LF-NMR) analysis

10 g sausage was loaded into the NMR tube to enable signal detection. Instrument (MesoMR23-060 V-1, Shanghai Niumag Corporation, Shanghai, China) parameters refer to Zhang et al. (2023). Pulse parameters typically recorded were as follows: sampling frequency and main frequency were 21 MHz, 90° pulse width was 14.20 us, 180° pulse width was 28.40 us, peak offset was 0.01 s, echo time was 0.2 s, and number of echoes was 10,000. The measured T2 of transverse relaxation time was obtained by software inversion. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of sausage by SE sequence determination. The sausage was cut into cylinders (diameter 2.5 cm, height 2.0 cm), placed into an MRI tube, and the hydrogen proton density map was obtained using color processing image software (Zhang et al., 2023).

Determination of water holding capacity (WHC)

WHC of sausages was following Zhang et al.(2023). m1 sausage were put into the centrifuge tube containing filter paper, then centrifuged 20 min at 4000 r/min by a L4-5K low-speed centrifuge (Kecheng Instrument Equipment Co., LTD, Hunan, China). Record the weight after centrifugation as m2. WHC was calculated as follows:WHC%=m2m1×100%

Determination of cooking loss

Take 2-cm-long cylindrical sausage portions, whose initial weight is m₁. These samples were sealed in a plastic bag, sealed, steamed in a 90 °C water bath for 20 min, transferred to 4 °C and stand for 24 h. Subsequently, the surface moisture was blotted with filter paper, and the samples were re-weighed to determine m₂ (Kang, Chen, & Ma, 2016). The calculation formula of cooking loss is as follows:cooking loss%=m1−m2m1×100%

Sensory evaluation analysis

A questionnaire was designed to investigate the interests of the sensory evaluation volunteers, as well as to select those who did not dislike fish products, had no related allergies, and had not taken any drugs that affect sensory functions (Wang et al., 2023). Subsequently; 20 sensory evaluators were carefully selected through basic taste recognition (threshold test) and basic taste ranking test. In the basic taste ranking test; volunteers were required to accurately identify and rank the concentration gradients of the salt solution after tasting. All sausages were randomly numbered after being cut to a uniform shape and size. Volunteers were randomly assigned samples. As shown in Table S2; color; smell; flavor; saltiness and texture of hairtail sausages were evaluated with reference to the 100-point scale of (Wu et al., 2024). The sensory assessment was repeated three times per person. The sensory evaluation in this study does not require ethical permission. All participants signed a form of agreement (Fig. S2) confirming their consent to participate in the sensory evaluation test and protecting their rights and privacy.

Determination of temporal dominance of sensations (TDS)

Sensory temporal dominance was measured applying SensoMaker software. The reviewer put the sausage in the mouth, clicked start and began chewing, based on the most important sensory descriptors at the current moment during chewing, including sweet, sour, bitter, salty and fresh. A new descriptor was clicked when the dominant flavor changed. Chewing of unsalted soda crackers and rinsing with water to remove residual flavor to avoid interference between different samples (Pineau et al., 2009).

Determination of electronic tongue

The determination of the electronic tongue (SA402B, INSENT, Fukuoka, Japan) was slightly modified according to the study of Wu et al. (2024). 10 g hairtail sausage were homogenized with 50 mL of distilled water and crushed to extract the flavor substance. The mixture was centrifuged at 4000 r/min for 5 min to obtain the supernatant for electronic tongue determination. The electronic tongue equipped with sourness, sweet, bitterness, saltiness, umami, richness, astringency probes.

Determination of electronic nose

The experiment of electronic nose (Pen3, AIRSENSE Analytics, Schwerin, Germany) was referred to You et al. (2024). 5 g sausage was taken in a 50 mL headspace vial and left for 30 min to release volatile compounds. The pre-injection equilibration time was set to 120 s and the volume of headspace air was 2400 μL. To prevent cross-contamination, clean air was introduced into the sensor chamber for 120 s after each measurement.

Measurement of sodium ion release during oral processing

The dynamic release of sodium during oral mastication is referenced by Wang et al. (2023) with slight modification. 3 assessors were selected from the above sensory evaluators. The hairtail sausages with the same shape and weight were chewed for 5 s, 10 s, 15 s, and 20 s. The chewing frequency was controlled by a metronome (72 times/min). After chewing, the food scraps were spat out and centrifuged (9000 r/min, 15 min) at 4 °C to collect supernatant. The content of sodium in supernatant was measured by elemental analyzer.

Chemical interaction forces analysis

Five mixed solutions including S1 (0.05 M NaCl), S2 (0.6 M NaCl), S3 (0.6 M NaCl, 1.5 M urea), S4 (0.6 M NaCl, 8 M urea), and S5 (0.6 M NaCl, 8 M Urea, 0.5 M β-ME) were prepared, and then 2 g minced hairtail sausage were mixed with 10 mL above solution respectively and placed at 4 °C for 1 h. Then centrifuged at 8000 r/min for 10 min and determined the protein content in the supernatant by BCA protein quantitation kit. Finally, the ionic bonding content was expressed as the difference in protein content dissolved in solution between S1 and S2; hydrogen bonding content was expressed as the difference in protein content dissolved in solution between S3 and S2: the hydrophobic interaction content was expressed as the difference in protein content dissolved in solution between S4 and S3; the disulphide bonding content was expressed as the difference in protein content dissolved in solution between S5 and S4; and the results were reported in terms of the amount of solubilized protein per millilitre of solution (mg /mL).

Fourier infrared spectrometer (FT-IR) analysis

Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (IS10, Nicolet, Madison, WI, USA) was employed to obtain the spectra different treatments of hairtail sausages to analyze the changes in force. Measurements were conducted over a wavenumber range of 4000 to 500 cm−1 at a resolution of 4 cm−1 (Wang et al., 2021).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted in triplicate, and the results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). ANOVA was conducted using SPSS 20.0 software (SPSS Inc., USA), with post-hoc comparisons carried out via Duncan's multiple range test. Differences among sample groups were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results and discussion

Texture characterization

As shown in Fig. 1a-d, in the hairtail sausage system, directly reducing salt addition by 25% significantly impaired textural properties (p < 0.05) compared to HS, as evidenced by decreases in key parameters such as hardness, cohesiveness, springiness, and chewiness. This is because of the multifunctional roles of NaCl during the meat protein gelation process. Not only does it promote the solubilization of myofibrillar proteins (particularly myosin and actin) and the exposure of hydrophobic groups, but it also modulates intermolecular forces between proteins via electrostatic shielding. The reduction of salt decreases ionic strength, which in turn reduces protein extractability and gel-forming capacity, ultimately compromising the textural integrity of the final product (Yang et al., 2026). Notably; when SPI was incorporated into RS system; the textural properties of the sausages were significantly improved (p < 0.05). As a functional plant-based protein; SPI exhibits excellent gelling capacity. SPI aggregates can rigidly fill the gel matrix by electrostatic screening with NaCl and form a double net structure with myofibrillar fibrillar proteins; leading to modified textural property of surimi gels (Zhang, Pan, et al., 2023). When SPI was combined with functional polysaccharides (Cur or GL); the textural properties of reduced-salt sausages are further enhanced. This synergistic effect is particularly evident in RS-SPI-Cur; which demonstrates superior textural performance; with hardness of 39.69 ± 2.18 N; cohesiveness of 0.602 ± 0.006; springiness of 8.38 ± 0.18 mm; chewiness of 61.43 ± 2.95 mj. This could be attributed to the swelling of the gel during the heating process and the gradual unfolding of hydrogen bonds between its single-helix molecular structure; which led to the enhancement of the interaction between the protein and water molecules; thus increasing the hardness and elasticity of the surimi gel. Compared to the fish ball system supplemented with protein as reported by Yao, Lin, Zhang, Zheng, and Zeng (2025), the simultaneous incorporation of both protein and polysaccharides in this study achieved a significant enhancement in textural properties, confirming the critical role of polysaccharides in compensating for the limitations of single-protein gels.Fig. 1Effect of different treatments on (a) hardness, (b) cohesiveness, (c) springiness, (d)chewiness, (e) storage modulus and (f) loss modulus of hairtail sausage. Analyzing texture changes from the perspective of the correlation between senses and physical properties is of great significance for product evaluation (Wang et al., 2024). Hardness represents the force required to compress food between the teeth or between the top and the tongue (Yuan, Xu, Cui, & Wang, 2019). A higher hardness value indicates a firmer bite and greater structural integrity of the product. Springiness indicates the ability of the gel to be compressed and then spring back (Tao et al., 2022). High springiness value means the product can rapidly recover its shape after deformation; contributing to a desirable Bouncy or resilient mouthfeel; which is a key characteristic of high-quality surimi products. Cohesiveness; the degree of compression between the teeth before the food matrix breaks; is related to the internal strength and structural integrity of the gel. Chewiness is the energy required to burst the gel into a state ready for swallowing (Yuan et al., 2019). It is the core parameter for evaluating the chewability and the pleasure of eating meat products.

Analysis of rheological characterization

The rheological characterization are indicators for evaluation of gel viscoelasticity, which indirectly reflect structural characteristics of surimi and the interactions between its components (Zhao, Zou, Sun, & Yu, 2020). The rheological characterization of hairtail sausage and salt-reduced hairtail sausage frequency scans are shown in Fig. 1e-f. Within frequency range of 0.1–100 rad/s; both G' and G" increased progressively with rising frequency. Notably; G' remained consistently higher than G" across the entire range; indicating dominant elastic behavior and characteristic solid-like gel properties (Man et al., 2024). G' and G" of RS-SPI; RS-SPI-Cur and RS-SPI-GL were higher than those of HS and RS; probably because during the heating process; SPI may cause solubilization and denaturation; physically filling surimi gel network; or generating covalent binding with myofibrillar proteins; thereby enhancing the strength of the fishmeal gel (Zhang, Guo, & Shi, 2023). The enhanced rheological of the reduced-salt sausages with polysaccharides and SPI was significantly higher than that of the SPI-only group in gels partly attributed to additional covalent and non-covalent interactions between polysaccharides and proteins. It was found that the improved rheological of hydrophilic polysaccharides was more effective (Pereira et al., 2021). In addition; curdlan possessed a unique irreversible thermogel property; which played a crucial role in constructing high strength gel networks. This thermally irreversible characteristic ensured that the gel structure maintains extremely high stability after undergoing heating and cooling cycles; effectively resisting structural collapse. The synergistic gelation between polysaccharides and proteins arises from enhanced molecular interactions and structural reorganization; including head denaturation; tail cross-linking; or end-to-end protein association; all of which contribute to improved viscoelasticity in surimi gel formation (Liu, McClements, Jin, & Chen, 2024).

Analysis of microstructure

The strength of surimi gel saltiness perception is closely related to the amount of sodium ions released. Sodium ions are released continuously during chewing, but most of the sodium ions are ingested unperceived. As shown in Fig. 2a, RS exhibited continuous dense structure, while the incorporation of SPI and polysaccharides facilitated the development of an interconnected network within the surimi gel. The formation of three-dimensional network structure may be contributed by the disulphide bonds formed between proteins and polysaccharides through interactions (Gao et al., 2022). The development of a three-dimensional network enables efficient release and sustained delivery of flavor compounds (Liu et al., 2024). Compared to RS-SPI-GL; RS-SPI-Cur developed continuous and homogeneous network structure; which is consistent with studies such as Zhou et al. (2024) in which the addition of moderate amounts of curdlan contributed to the formation of a denser and more homogeneous network structure.Fig. 2(a)Microstructure of different treatments (200× magnification) and (b) distribution of sodium elements of hairtail sausage. Distribution of sodium element

The different forms of sodium chloride presence in food have an impact on saltiness perception, and optimizing the spatial distribution of sodium chloride within the food matrix represents a promising strategy for salt reduction, enhancing its dissolution and release during oral processing (Sun et al., 2021). From Fig. 2b; it can be seen that sodium ions show relatively uniform distribution in HS and RS; while they exhibit inhomogeneous distribution in RS-SPI; RS-SPI-Cur and RS-SPI-GL systems. Protein and polysaccharide molecules formed a three-dimensional network structure by cross-linking with myofibrillar proteins. Sodium ions can be encapsulated in the pores of the gel network. As the gel formed and stabilized; the sodium ions were fixed at specific positions; thereby achieving the capture and retention of sodium ions (Yang et al., 2024). Contrast effects in the mouth enhance the overall response from taste receptors in the high NaCl concentration layer compared to stimuli elicited by uniform distribution. When salt is uniform distribution in food; high-salt regions rupture or dissolve during chewing; rapidly releasing a large amount of sodium ions within a short timeframe. These ions quickly interact with salt taste receptors on the tongue's taste buds; generating intense taste signals. This pulsatile pattern of sodium release activates neural responses in the gustatory system more effectively than the slow; steady release observed in uniformly salted foods. When evaluating saltiness intensity; the human brain tends to rely more on the peak concentration of the taste stimulus rather than the overall average concentration (Vinitha, Sethupathy, Moses, & Anandharamakrishnan, 2022).

Water distribution

LF-NMR and MRI can provide insight into water distribution, migration and binding capacity of hairtail sausages. As shown in the Fig. 3a, the addition of SPI and polysaccharides increased the amount of bound water in the sausage compared to RS, resulting from the formation of hydrogen bonds between SPI and polysaccharides and water molecules. The increase in bound water facilitates the binding of sodium ions to enhance saltiness perception and contributes to the formation of an organized gel network to enhance gel storage stability (Liu et al., 2025). In hairtail sausage; immobilized water was the predominant state of water; and the T22 peak of salt-reduced fish guts treated with addition of proteins and polysaccharides was shifted toward lower relaxation times; suggesting that immobilized water mobility in the samples was relatively low (Wu et al., 2024). In addition the peak T22 of these surimi sausage was shortened; indicating reduced free water mobility and the conversion of free water into bound water (Teng et al., 2024). The migration of water from a free state to a bound state not only optimized the gel microstructure but also influenced saltiness perception by altering the mass transfer kinetics of sodium ions. The shortening of the T22 relaxation time indicated that water molecules were tightly constrained within the gel network, effectively inhibiting the loss of juice during the initial stages of chewing. The signal intensity of MRI was positively correlated with water content, and red color indicated high proton density. As can be seen from Fig. 3b, RS yellow region changed to red with the addition of SPI and polysaccharide, where the addition of both SPI and curdlan had the strongest signal intensity for the water content of salt-reduced hairtail sausages, indicating that curdlan had the best effect on enhancing the plasmonic density and effectively decreasing the water loss. This study was consistent with Zhou et al. (2024) that cross-linking of hydrocolloid molecular chains locks a large amount of water into the gel network. Since a significant portion of sodium resides in the aqueous phase of the food matrix; its release from the food bolus is highly dependent on water content. Higher moisture levels promote sodium availability during mastication; thereby enhancing saltiness perception. In this context; incorporating proteins and polysaccharides can serve as an effective strategy to modulate sodium release and reduce overall salt content (Wang et al., 2023).

Analysis of water holding capacity

WHC reflects the water trapping capacity of the network structure of surimi gel, which is a particularly important index to evaluate the quality of surimi. The higher WHC value, the more water trapped in the gel (Wu et al., 2024). Fig. 3c showed WHC of normal and reduced salt treated hairtail sausages. Compared with HS; direct reduction of salt content resulted in a significant decrease (p < 0.05) in WHC of the sausages; probably because direct reduction of salt content reduced the amount of myofibrillar fibrillar proteins in the minced surimi; and the gel structure of surimi was loosened; which was not conducive to retain water (Zhang, Pan, et al., 2023). At the salt content of 2.25%; WHC of the gel was significantly increased after SPI was added (p < 0.05); which was consistent with Zhang et al. (2023); SPI had the ability to improve WHC of the gel. On the basis of SPI; the addition of curdlan significantly improved the water holding capacity; reaching 98.13% ± 0.61%. Polysaccharides with hydrophilic groups were introduced into the system to fill the pore structure inside the gel matrix; thus enhancing the binding with water molecules (Liu et al., 2024). It is worth noting that the water-holding capacity achieved by the “protein-polysaccharide” complex is significantly superior to 75.44% of the low-salt fish ball system reported by Li et al. (2022) which only added amino acids. This finding substantiates the superiority of the formulation in enhancing the water retention properties of low-salt meat products.

Analysis of cooking loss

Cooking loss is the loss of water in the gel during temperature-controlled heating. RS cooking loss rate was 3.70% ± 0.37% (Fig. 3d), compared to RS, RS-SPI, RS-SPI-Cur and RS-SPI-GL cooking loss rates were reduced by 1.62%, 2.33%, and 1.86%, respectively, indicating that SPI and polysaccharides have a good ability to reduce the cooking loss. This phenomenon was explained as proteins and polysaccharides inhibited myofibrillar protein aggregation, formed of water-locked gel structure, and improved the binding capacity of water molecules (Liu et al., 2024). Because of its high water-absorption capacity; Curdlan can adsorb up to 100 times the amount of water and bind the free water tightly; it has shown the best results in compensating for the loss of quality in salt-reduced surimi sausages. Previous studies have also shown that the addition of Curdlan improves the water retention capacity of low-salt pork sausages and low-salt chicken mince (Lee & Chin, 2019; Zhao, Zhou, Xu, Zeng, & Xing, 2022). In addition, the addition of curdlan does not affect the denaturation of myofibrillar proteins during the heating process, but affects the interactions between the myofibrillar proteins, increasing their thermal transition temperatures and decreasing the water loss (Wei et al., 2018).

Sensory evaluation

Sensory evaluation including color, odor, flavor, salty and texture was shown in Fig. 4a. In terms of salty sensory evaluation, HS had the lowest salty score of 17.14 ± 1.53 and was considered slightly salty but acceptable. After salt reduction, the scores were all improved, with RS and RS-SPI showing less salty but acceptable, but with the addition of both SPI and polysaccharides, the salt-reduced sausages were considered to be suitably salty, with RS-SPI-Cur having the highest salty score of 21.43 ± 1.43. The enhancement mechanism of salty flavor is mainly due to the uneven distribution of sodium in the food matrix by the addition of SPI and polysaccharides, which provide discontinuous stimulation of the neural taste receptors, thus reducing their adaptability to salty stimuli (Vinitha et al., 2022). Compared to HS; the direct reduction of 25% salt content in hairtail sausage resulted in lower flavor and texture scores and no change in odor; while the addition of protein and polysaccharide increased all the three scores; smelled and tasted umami; and after chewing; it was found that the structure of surimi sausage was dense; with uniformly small pore spaces and elasticity. This indicated that SPI and polysaccharides could compensate for the flavor and texture deficiencies caused by the reducing salt content of surimi gels. Protein and polysaccharides can significantly enhanced gel and sensory properties of surimi; and polysaccharides can reduce the release of fishy components through adsorption; so that the salt reduced sausage show umami by smelling or tasting. At 0.6 s of chewing the sausage, umami and sweet were perceived at the same time, saltiness was perceived at 1.6 s, and the intensity of saltiness perception was gradually strengthened during the chewing process, and bitterness appeared in the later stage of chewing at 8.6 s, which may be due to the bitter flavor appearing from too much saltiness. Electronic tongue analysis

In order to investigate the role of proteins and polysaccharides in enhancing saltiness perception, this study used electronic tongue to analyze taste eigenvalues of HS and salt-reduced hairtail sausages. In Fig. 4h, the electronic tongue analysis showed that sweet, umami and saltiness had higher eigenvalues among the seven tastes detected in the sausages and were considered to be the dominant tastes, while sourness, bitterness and astringency had negative values indicating that it was not detected. Direct reduction of salt content resulted in lower salty, umami, sweet and richness of hairtail sausage, but the addition of SPI and polysaccharides resulted in higher sweet, salty and umami eigenvalues. This is probably owing to the cross-linking of proteins and polysaccharides to generate a network structure in the surimi gel, which facilitates the release of tastes molecules (Liu et al., 2024). The result is in agreement with the sensory evaluation.

Electronic nose analysis

The electronic nose is a common tool for analyzing odorous substances, where slight changes in the volatile compound composition can be captured by the sensor. Its array of sensors can specifically respond to different categories of volatile organic compounds, thereby capturing subtle changes in the composition of flavors. As shown in Fig. 4i, the radar diagram of HS and salt-reduced hairtail sausages showed that the response values of W1C, W5S, W5C, W1S, W1W, W2S and W2W sensors increased with the addition of SPI and polysaccharide. The results showed that the flavor components of low salt surimi, such as aromatic components, nitrogen oxides, alkanes, methyl groups, inorganic sulfides, alcohols, aldehydes and organic sulfides were improved to compensate for the loss of flavor caused by direct salt reduction. It indicated that the overall abundance and diversity of flavor volatile components have been enhanced under low-salt conditions.

The underlying mechanism may involve SPI physically adsorbing or chemically binding flavor molecules through its amino acid side chains, amino termini, and hydrophobic microdomains, thereby modulating the release kinetics of flavor compounds. Simultaneously, polysaccharides can adsorb volatile odorants via hydrogen bonding or van der Waals forces, delaying their volatilization and enhancing the matrix's capacity to retain flavor (Xue et al., 2021). This synergistic interaction contributes to the overall improvement of flavor profile and sensory balance in surimi-based products.

Chemical interaction

The chemical interaction forces in the sausage gel include ionic bonding, hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bonding. As can be seen in Fig. 6a and Fig. 6b, disulfide bond was the main component in the chemical force, and the content of RS-SPI, RS-SPI-Cur and RS-SPI-GL disulfide bonds was significantly higher than that of HS and RS (p < 0.05). This was due to the fact that myofibrillar proteins were predominantly present in the sausages, and sulfhydryl groups of the proteins were converted to disulfide bonds in the heated tissues. The incorporation of SPI into surimi led to a rise in disulfide bond content, strengthening the gel's chemical cross-linking. This improvement supported intermolecular interactions, including SPI self-association and between SPI and myofibrillar protein binding, thereby reinforcing the gel matrix. Specifically, the thermal denaturation of SPI exposed buried free sulfhydryl groups, which subsequently underwent thiol-disulfide exchange reactions with the myofibrillar protein network; this bridging effectively integrates the plant protein into the gel matrix, thereby enhancing the gel properties. Zhang, Pan, et al. (2023) demonstrated that SPI as a gel enhancer for salt-reduced surimi also maintains the gel structure through disulfide bonding. Correlation analysis of saltiness perception

Fig. 7a showed the correlation analysis of salty taste perception with textural properties, flavor characteristics and chemical interaction forces of hairtail sausage. Saltiness exhibited a significant positive correlation (p < 0.05) with hardness and chewiness; this co-variation suggests that the incorporation of SPI and polysaccharides simultaneously enhanced both textural properties and sodium ion release kinetics. Furthermore, sensory evaluation revealed that saltiness was positively correlated with odor intensity and showed a strong positive association with overall flavor (p < 0.05). This indicates that SPI and cur, by improving the texture, create a synergistic effect between the release of volatile substances and taste perception. In terms of chemical interaction, hydrogen and disulphide bonds of the sausages contributed significantly to the perception of salty flavor. Therefore, we have inferred that the mechanism by which proteins and polysaccharides contribute to the perception of saltiness in hairtail sausages is as follows: First, the incorporation of proteins and polysaccharides constructs a more rigid gel network characterized by increased brittleness, mediated by elevated levels of disulfide bonds and hydrogen bonds. Upon mastication, this high-hardness gel structure undergoes brittle fracture rather than plastic deformation under occlusal forces, causing the gel to instantaneously disintegrate into micro-fragments. This drastic structural disruption significantly increases the specific surface area, thereby accelerating the dynamic release of sodium ions from the matrix and facilitating their rapid diffusion to the taste receptors on the surface of the tongue. Secondly, concomitant with gel fragmentation, entrapped taste-active compounds (such as sweet and umami amino acids) and volatile flavor molecules are synchronously and rapidly released. Mechanism of SPI and polysaccharides to improve quality

SPI and polysaccharides are instrumental in converting the initially loose and inhomogeneous matrix of reduced-salt hairtail sausages into a uniform, interconnected network, thereby enhancing gel strength and water retention. Drawing on relevant studies and the present findings, a mechanistic model is proposed to illustrate how the incorporation of SPI and polysaccharides enhances the texture of reduced-salt surimi gels (Fig. 7b). When the NaCl concentration in the hairtail surimi gel system was reduced, myofibrillar proteins failed to fully unfold and extend, remaining in a compact, folded conformation with their reactive groups buried and inaccessible for interaction (Zhang, Pan, et al., 2023). During thermal processing; the reduced unfolding of proteins caused by low ionic strength limited molecular interactions and impaired the formation of a continuous three-dimensional network structure. This “bad” gel structure was improved with the addition of SPI. Firstly; this was because SPI possesses favorable functional properties; including high water absorption; pronounced swelling capacity; and the ability to undergo thermal denaturation and form a stable gel network; so after SPI was added to the gel matrix; SPI existed in the gel network in a physically filled manner to improve gel properties. The 11S globulin in soybean isolate protein can combine with myofibrillar protein to form a stable gel network structure; which binds more free water and improves product quality (Ge et al., 2023). When polysaccharides were added; the polysaccharides formed filamentous connections in the gel network and filled the interstices of the surimi gel increasing the water-holding properties of the gel; which consequently increased the self-diffusion rate of sodium (Teng et al., 2024). Therefore, the simultaneous addition of protein and polysaccharides could effectively compensate for the quality loss of hairtair sausages caused by salt reduction.

Conclusions

This study investigated the synergistic effects of SPI and polysaccharides (curdlan, gellan gum) on saltiness perception in salt-reduced hairtail sausages. The SPI-curdlan composite yielded superior textural properties and moisture retention compared to normal-salt, direct-reduction, and SPI-only controls. Integrated sensory evaluation, electronic tongue analysis, and sodium release kinetics confirmed that this co-formulation significantly enhanced saltiness perception. Mechanistically, SPI and curdlan synergy facilitated a non-uniform salt distribution within the gel matrix, enabling a 25% sodium reduction without compromising sensory quality or product integrity. Therefore, this study develops a salt-reduced surimi product with non-uniform salt distribution to provide new ideas for reducing salt content in surimi foods. Future research should further elucidate the microscopic migration mechanism of sodium ions within the “non-uniform salt distribution” of sausages during dynamic chewing, as well as its kinetic interactions with saliva, to construct a more precise structure activity relationship model. Concurrently, it is essential to evaluate the impact of this strategy on the physicochemical stability and microbial safety of products during long term storage, and to explore its applicability in other meat products or plant-based protein gels, thereby promoting the industrial application of low-salt healthy meat products.

Automated early ovarian cancer detection system based on bioinformatics
Authors: Li Xiao, Hui Li, Yanyang Jin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOvarian cancer is a common gynecological tumor, with a high mortality rate and difficult clinical treatment. Early detection of ovarian cancer has significant diagnostic value. In response to the problem of poor diagnostic performance of traditional early diagnosis methods, this article designed an automated early ovarian cancer detection system to improve the detection of early ovarian cancer. The conventional early diagnosis methods include serum CA125 (carbohydrate antigen 125) detection and positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) imaging. This article combined serum CA125 detection and PET/CT imaging to detect the CA125 level and maximum standardized uptake value (SUV) in patient’s serum. When the CA125 level exceeded 35U/ml and the maximum SUV value exceeded 2.5, the test was considered positive. This article selected 200 patients from Jingzhou Hospital for the experiment and compared the three detection methods. The average specificity of single serum CA125 detection, single PET/CT imaging, and automated detection in patients under 50 were 61.24%, 79.57%, and 97.79%, respectively. The automated early ovarian cancer detection system designed in this article can significantly improve the specificity of early ovarian cancer detection and has excellent application value for early ovarian cancer detection.Keywords: Early ovarian cancer, Automatic detection, Bioimaging informatics, Serum CA125Subject terms: Biological techniques, Biotechnology, Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

The treatment effect of ovarian cancer in clinical practice is poor, mainly because patients are often diagnosed with ovarian cancer in the middle and late stages when the cancer cells in the patient’s body have already spread widely. To improve the clinical treatment effect of ovarian cancer, it is necessary to detect ovarian cancer as soon as possible and receive timely treatment. However, early ovarian cancer has no obvious symptoms and there is no effective detection method for early ovarian cancer. According to statistics, the case fatality rate of female patients with ovarian cancer in the United States in 2016 was as high as 63.9%. Many patients died because of the low recognition rate of early ovarian cancer. At present, the main diagnostic methods for ovarian cancer are serum testing and B-ultrasound examination. B-ultrasound, or ultrasound examination, is a medical imaging technique that uses high-frequency sound waves to reflect through human tissues. B-ultrasound can detect and evaluate the condition of internal organs, soft tissues, blood vessels, and fetuses, and has the advantages of non-invasive, real-time, and dynamic observation. Although positive patients with ovarian cancer can be detected to a certain extent, the sensitivity and specificity of early diagnosis are not high. Serum CA125 detection is the main early detection method, which achieves early ovarian cancer detection by measuring CA125 levels. With the development of bioinformatics technology, analyzing medical images enables the diagnosis of various diseases. PET/CT integrates functional and anatomical images. This article integrates PET/CT with serum CA125 detection to achieve automated early ovarian cancer detection and improve the early diagnostic efficiency of ovarian cancer patients.

Ovarian cancer poses great harm to women, and once cancer cells metastasize, it can seriously affect the patient. Early detection of ovarian cancer has important clinical value. Elias’s (2018) study achieved a two-stage strategy of triggering transvaginal ultrasound to detect malignant pelvic masses by increasing serum CA125 levels1. Through serum testing, early ovarian cancer detection can be achieved, reducing the mortality rate of ovarian cancer patients. Bast et al. (2020) pointed out that early detection of ovarian cancer remains an important unmet medical need, and effective screening can reduce mortality by 10–30%2. The detection of serum CA125 or transvaginal ultrasound is not very good. Simmons et al. used probe design principles and blinded 75 women with invasive epithelial ovarian cancer and 547 corresponding healthy individuals from the ovarian cancer screening trial for control3. He used multiple bead-based immunoassays to measure the concentration of multiple markers in ultra-low serum volume, which has high measurement accuracy. Skubitz et al. pointed out that the ovarian cancer biomarker CA125 is not sensitive or specific enough for screening of the general population4. Simultaneous measurement of 92 serum protein biomarker can be used to develop a multi-protein classifier for ovarian cancer detection. Early detection of ovarian cancer can reduce patient mortality, but a single serum CA125 test is ineffective and lacks the use of medical images to assist in early ovarian cancer detection.

Early ovarian cancer has no obvious physical manifestations, and traditional pathological, serum, and other treatment methods are difficult to accurately detect. Bioimaging can be used to observe the metabolic changes of tumors in the human body through images. Many people use medical imaging to detect early ovarian cancer. To provide patients with the most favorable treatment, healthcare professionals must have a basic understanding of the signs and symptoms of ovarian cancer, as well as the available imaging tools and treatment plans, such as the use of CT images for early ovarian cancer detection5. Sun et al. used PET/CT imaging to detect serum tumor molecular markers, which was the first application of cytoreductive surgery and chemotherapy in 69 patients with ovarian cancer6. PET/CT detects and locates tumor tissue by injecting radiolabeled glucose, which is taken up by metabolically active cancer cells in the body, and then performing a full-body scan to generate accurate three-dimensional images. Combined with serum tumor marker testing, it can provide a more comprehensive disease assessment, help doctors develop more accurate treatment plans, and improve treatment outcomes. Rubello et al. used PET/CT to monitor the chemotherapy process of ovarian cancer and improve the initial diagnosis of ovarian cancer by analyzing medical imaging7. Wong and Stephen combined liquid biopsy and PET/CT to detect early cancer8. Early detection is a long-term goal in cancer diagnosis, and positive judgments can be accurately made through biological imaging. PET/CT, as a new imaging detection method, can observe the metabolism of patients with early ovarian tumors, but there is a lack of Conjoint analysis of PET/CT detection and serum CA125 detection. Accurate early detection of ovarian cancer is crucial. Methods

Ovarian malignant tumor is a common disease of female genitals, with a high incidence rate and mortality. The pathogenic factors of ovarian cancer are still unclear and early patients lack effective diagnostic methods, which leads to the diagnosed ovarian cancer patients often in the late stage, with great difficulty in treatment9,10.

Ovarian cancer can cause symptoms such as abdominal pain, menstrual disorders, vaginal bleeding, and frequent urination in patients, seriously affecting their health. The organizational structure of the ovary is very complex, and there is a possibility of benign and malignant tumor lesions, which makes the ovary the main organ for women to develop tumors. The position of the ovaries in the human body is unique, located deep in the pelvic cavity and obstructed by various subcutaneous fat and internal organs. Moreover, early ovarian cancer has no obvious symptoms, which makes detection of early ovarian cancer extremely difficult.

Ovarian cancer is a malignant tumor that occurs in the ovarian region, divided into three types: primary, secondary, and metastatic11,12. Primary ovarian cancer can be divided into adenocarcinoma, papillary carcinoma, adenoid carcinoma, etc. Secondary ovarian cancer is mostly caused by the malignant transformation of ovarian cysts or other ovarian tumors, while metastatic ovarian cancer is caused by the metastasis of other tissues and organs to the ovary. Most patients with ovarian cancer are asymptomatic in the early stage, but the cancer cells grow rapidly. In the short term, abdominal distension, abdominal mass and ascites may appear, and in the late stage, compression symptoms and abdominal pain may appear. Pathological changes in ovarian tissue are difficult to detect, and even if symptoms exist in the early stages, these early symptoms are not specific, making screening for early ovarian cancer difficult. When female patients experience symptoms such as bloating and ascites, it indicates that they have entered the advanced stage of ovarian cancer.

Ovarian cancer is very harmful. With the increase in ovarian cancer, patients’ pelvic veins would be squeezed, causing serious edema of the lower limbs. Cancerous cells can damage normal ovarian tissue, causing reduced menstruation and severe abdominal pain in patients. The enlargement of ovarian cancer can also cause symptoms of ascites, leading to gastrointestinal complications in patients13,14. Ovarian cancer can severely damage normal tissue cells in the human body in its advanced stage, and the 5-year survival rate of advanced patients is very low, about 30%.

The treatment of advanced ovarian cancer is difficult and the case fatality rate is high, so it is very important to detect early ovarian cancer. There are many methods for detecting early ovarian cancer, such as a b-ultrasound examination, ultrasound examination, and serological examination. However, traditional methods for detecting early ovarian cancer have obvious shortcomings, making it difficult to accurately identify and intervene. Early diagnosis of B-ultrasound involves collecting images of the patient’s ovaries using sound waves, and screening is performed by comparing the size and morphological position of ovarian tissue.

With the development of medical technology, tumor markers are gradually being applied to cancer diagnosis, and CA125 is a common early ovarian cancer detection indicator15,16. CA125 is a carbohydrate antigen that should not exceed 35U/ml in normal human serum. However, when patients develop ovarian cancer, the level of CA125 in their serum increases. CA125 is a tumor marker commonly used in the detection of ovarian cancer in women, which can effectively monitor the diagnosis and prognosis of ovarian cancer17. Ovarian cancer can be divided into four stages based on the degree of cancer cell metastasis, and the survival rate of patients in different stages varies significantly. As a populous country, China has a high incidence of ovarian cancer every year. According to statistics, the survival rates of ovarian cancer patients at different stages in China in 2020 are shown in Table 1.

The specific CA125 determination method used in this study is the chemiluminescent immunoassay. This method detects the concentration of CA125 in serum by generating a chemiluminescent signal through the binding of labeled antibodies to the CA125 antigen. The specific detection system used is Roche’s Elecsys CA125 II, which has high sensitivity and specificity and can accurately distinguish ovarian cancer patients from healthy individuals. The determination process includes sample preparation, antibody reaction and determination of luminescent signals, and is operated by automated equipment to ensure accurate and consistent results.

With the development of computer technology, it is possible to combine computer technology with the medical industry, and achieve medical diagnosis by processing and analyzing medical images. Bioinformatics integrates multiple disciplines such as biology and computer science, utilizing intelligent medical devices to collect medical image data and analyze medical images. Bioinformatics is widely applied in medical diagnosis, mainly through the analysis of imaging data for tumor diagnosis, treatment, and predictive evaluation.

Bioinformatics can be well applied to early ovarian cancer detection by collecting ovarian lesion image information from patients through medical equipment. By analyzing the lesion morphology of ovarian images and using PET/CT imaging for early ovarian cancer detection, the main focus is on analyzing the maximum SUV value. The maximum SUV value is an indicator used to distinguish between malignant tumors and benign lesions.

With the development of medical imaging technology, various medical images can provide characteristic information of diseases, enabling effective diagnosis and targeted treatment. Common medical images include PET images, CT images, etc. PET imaging is an imaging device that reflects pathological molecules and genes. With the development of medical imaging technology, PET imaging can be combined with CT imaging, which can reflect both the anatomical morphology of the patient’s lesion and the molecular characteristics of the disease.

PET/CT imaging can be well applied to the detection of early ovarian cancer, accurately observing the metabolism of ovarian cancer cells, as well as observing the morphology and size of ovarian cancer tissue. PET/CT imaging combines the advantages of both PET and CT imaging methods to accurately analyze the status of lesions. PET/CT imaging can provide early diagnosis for diseases without clinical features or morphological changes. The PET/CT imaging of early ovarian cancer is shown in Fig. 1.

When using PET/CT imaging for early ovarian cancer detection, the main focus is on analyzing the maximum SUV value, which is an indicator used to distinguish malignant tumors from benign lesions. The higher the SUV index, the higher the degree of disease, and the criteria for judging malignant tumors are:

When the maximum value of an SUV exceeds 2.5, it is judged as a malignant tumor, and the 2.5 value is the boundary between malignant tumors and benign lesions.

PET/CT imaging can detect early ovarian cancer lesions by observing the maximum SUV value, and PET/CT imaging can accurately reflect the metabolic status of ovarian cancer cells. The disadvantage is that the PET/CT detection method is easily influenced by objective factors, such as the patient’s age, physical movement, and the dentures worn by the patient, which can have a certain impact on the detection results. The PET/CT detection method is prone to false positives.

In order to effectively eliminate the drawbacks of PET/CT detection, this paper conducts a Conjoint analysis of serum CA125 detection and PET/CT detection, and carries out accurate detection of early ovarian cancer by analyzing the level of CA125 in serum and PET/CT image analysis. The combination of serum CA125 detection and PET/CT detection can effectively combine the advantages of both detection methods to achieve timely and early detection of ovarian cancer. Only when the serum CA125 test is positive and PET/CT test is also positive, can the test result be determined as early ovarian cancer, and the rest of the test results are negative. That is to say, serum CA125 test is positive while PET/CT test is negative; serum CA125 test was negative and PET/CT test was positive; when the serum CA125 test and PET/CT test are both negative, the test result is negative.

Serum CA125 test is positive: If only serum CA125 test result is positive and PET/CT test result is negative, it cannot be determined as early ovarian cancer.

Only PET/CT test is positive: If only PET/CT test result is positive and serum CA125 test result is negative, it cannot be determined as early ovarian cancer.

Both are negative: If both serum CA125 and PET/CT test results are negative, the possibility of early ovarian cancer is ruled out.

The combination of serum CA125 detection and PET/CT detection achieves automated early ovarian cancer detection through the analysis of detection indicators. The interface of the automated early ovarian cancer detection system is shown in Fig. 2.

Early ovarian cancer detection experiment

The pathogenesis of ovarian cancer is not clear, but its occurrence may be related to factors such as genetics and living environment. Ovarian cancer is a high incidence disease in women, which has a significant impact on both the physical and psychological well-being of patients.

The treatment of ovarian cancer generally involves maximizing the removal of cancerous tissue through surgery to control the progression of the tumor. However, in actual clinical diagnosis, the majority of diagnosed ovarian cancer patients are in the middle to late stage, which is very unfavorable for cancer treatment. The key to the treatment of ovarian cancer lies in early detection, but the characterization of early ovarian cancer is not clear, making it difficult to accurately detect early ovarian cancer.

Serum CA125 is a glycoprotein that can be used for ovarian cancer tumor detection. However, when a patient develops ovarian cancer, the serum CA125 level in the body increases. It can be determined whether they have ovarian cancer by detecting the serum CA125 level in the human body. With the development of bioinformatics, the application of medical images is becoming increasingly widespread. PET/CT images can clearly observe the characteristic information of ovarian cancer lesions.

This article designs an automated early ovarian cancer detection system that combines serum CA125 detection with PET/CT imaging detection and achieves early ovarian cancer detection by automatically judging the detected data. In order to effectively analyze the performance of the early ovarian cancer detection system designed in this article, this article selected suspected ovarian cancer patients who underwent examination at a hospital in Jingzhou, China in 2023 for experimental analysis. Among these 120 ovarian cancer patients, the disease stages were subdivided as follows: stage I (limited to the ovaries): 35 cases; stage II (invasion of the pelvis): 28 cases; stage III (invasion of the peritoneum and/or lymph node metastasis): 42 cases; stage IV (distant metastasis): 15 cases. The pathological and clinical characteristics of each stage patient were recorded in detail for subsequent analysis and treatment effect evaluation. Among the 200 participants, 80 were under 50 years old and 120 were over or equal to 50 years old.

In order to effectively demonstrate the advantages of automated early ovarian cancer detection systems, this article compares a single serum CA125 detection method, a single PET/CT imaging detection method, and a combination of serum CA125 and PET/CT imaging detection. In this paper, the venous blood volume of patients was 5 ml, and the serum was separated after centrifugation. The serum CA125 level of patients was detected by chemiluminescence Immunoassay. In general, the normal range of CA125 is generally less than or equal to 35U/ml. Before PET/CT imaging testing, patients need to maintain a 6-h fast and receive fluorodeoxyglucose injections with the assistance of professional medical staff. PET/CT imaging is performed after resting for one hour. This article collects the maximum SUV value in PET/CT imaging, and when the maximum SUV value is greater than 2.5, it is detected as positive.

This article divides patients into age groups under 50 years old and over 50 years old and then the serum CA125 level is measured. Age is one of the important risk factors for ovarian cancer, and 50 years old is often regarded as a critical point because women usually experience menopause around the age of 50, and changes in hormone levels may affect the occurrence and development of cancer. The age limit may affect survival results because elderly patients usually have a poorer prognosis. In addition, age may affect the results of PET/CT and CA125 determinations because metabolic and physiological changes with age may affect the sensitivity and specificity of these tests. Therefore, it is explicitly stipulated to divide patients aged 50 into groups greater than or equal to 50 years old for more consistent and scientific analysis. Conclusions

Ovarian cancer is a common malignant tumor in gynecology, which poses great harm to patients. Ovarian cancer does not have typical characteristics in the early stages of onset, which leads to ovarian cancer often occurring in the late stage when diagnosed. The accelerated pace of people’s lives and the deterioration of their living environment are all triggers for the occurrence of ovarian cancer, and accurate detection of early ovarian cancer has broad clinical value. This article designed an automated early ovarian cancer detection system that combined serum CA125 detection with PET/CT imaging to comprehensively analyze the patient’s serum CA125 level and SUV maximum value for early ovarian cancer detection. This article compared three early ovarian cancer detection methods and collected 200 patients for analysis. The results indicate that the combined detection method can improve detection accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. The automated early ovarian cancer detection system designed in this article can provide effective data for clinical doctors to conduct early ovarian cancer detection. However, this article only analyzed 200 patients for early ovarian cancer detection, and the selected sample data is not enough. Therefore, selecting more patients for early ovarian cancer testing would be the direction of future research.

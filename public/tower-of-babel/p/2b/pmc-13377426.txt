Real-world treatment satisfaction and experience with once-weekly tirzepatide: Insights from prescribing physicians and people with obesity or overweight
Authors: Theresa Hunter Gibble, Andrea Leith, Lewis Harrison, Claire Gerber, Sonya Kokil Raikar, Era Seth, Esther Artime
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTirzepatide, a once-weekly glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, has demonstrated its efficacy in clinical trials; however, real-world evidence on treatment satisfaction and experiences among people with obesity or overweight (PwO) and their prescribing physicians remains limited. This study aimed to assess the real-world clinical characteristics, treatment patterns, and satisfaction with tirzepatide among PwO without type 2 diabetes (T2D) and their prescribing physicians in the United States.MethodsThis secondary analysis utilized data from a cross-sectional survey of physicians and PwO from October 2023 to April 2024, and from October 2024 to January 2025. Physicians provided data for PwO meeting selection criteria, at data capture/previous body mass index (BMI) ≥30 or ≥27 and < 30 kg/m2 with at least one obesity-related complication (ORC), without T2D, enrolled in a weight management program, and were receiving tirzepatide at the time of data collection. Sociodemographic and clinical characteristics, treatment history, and satisfaction measures were analyzed.ResultsData from 151 physicians and 199 PwO without T2D using tirzepatide were included. Most PwO (84.4%) had ≥1 ORC, with hypertension and dyslipidemia being the most prevalent. Over 90% (n = 45/48) of PwO reported likelihood to recommend tirzepatide reflecting high satisfaction and willingness to recommend tirzepatide to others. Physicians reported long-term safety, ease of dosing, and reduced appetite as key reasons for prescribing tirzepatide. Physicians reported that tirzepatide treatment was successful in the majority of people (78.5%) being treated for obesity.ConclusionsThe data in these surveys reflected that PwO without T2D prescribed tirzepatide had high motivation, adherence, and satisfaction. These findings provide real-world insights into treatment and satisfaction with tirzepatide.

Introduction

Globally, obesity or overweight is projected to affect nearly 3 billion adults, around 50% of the world's population, by 2030 [1]. Obesity, a chronic metabolic disease, is associated with an increased risk of ≥200 obesity-related complications (ORCs), including type 2 diabetes (T2D), cardiovascular diseases, obstructive sleep apnea, osteoarthritis, and chronic kidney disease, impairing overall health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [2].

Clinical guidelines recommend the use of obesity management medications (OMMs) as an adjunct to lifestyle interventions and behavioral therapy for the management of weight, ORCs, and HRQoL improvement [3]. Tirzepatide is a once-weekly glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) approved in the United States (US) for the treatment of obesity in November 2023 [4]. Tirzepatide has demonstrated safety and efficacy in multiple phase 3 clinical trials among adults living with obesity or overweight, to support weight reduction and maintenance [[5], [6], [7]].

While findings from clinical trials offer valuable insights on treatment efficacy and safety, there is a paucity of recent real-world evidence on the treatment experiences of people living with obesity or overweight (PwO) and how clinical outcomes are perceived by them and their physicians. This study aimed to better understand the disease characteristics and real-world experiences of people with obesity without T2D who are using tirzepatide, including their obesity management journey.

Methods

Study design and data source

This secondary analysis used data from the Adelphi Real World (ARW) Obesity III Disease Specific Programme (DSP)™, a US cross-sectional survey linking physician-reported and participant-reported data. The DSP methodology has been published and validated previously [[8], [9], [10], [11]]. Deidentified and pseudo-anonymized data were collected cross-sectionally at a single point in time and retrospectively for historical information via three questionnaires:

PwO provided their informed consent prior to participation The DSP was conducted in accordance with the European Pharmaceutical Market Research Association Code of Conduct and ARW standard operating procedures and submitted to the Pearl Institutional Review Board in the US (IRB number: 22-ADRW-136).

Study population

Data were collected in the US from October 2023 to April 2024, and from October 2024 to January 2025, when an additional group of PwO receiving tirzepatide was included. Physicians were eligible for inclusion if they were personally responsible for management and treatment decisions for ≥10 PwO in a typical month and fulfilled all survey requirements.

PwO were eligible if they were ≥18 years old, had a physician-confirmed diagnosis of obesity or overweight (defined at time of data collection/previous BMI ≥30 or ≥27 and < 30 kg/m2 with ≥1 ORC), without T2D, on a weight management program, and not part of a clinical trial for obesity. This analysis included PwO receiving tirzepatide at the time of data collection. At the time of data collection, PwO may be at different time points of their tirzepatide treatment journeys.

Outcomes

Physician-completed questionnaires captured PwO sociodemographic and clinical characteristics at data collection. Details on physician reported disease management i.e., prior/current obesity treatments and treatment goals aside from weight reduction, prior and current OMM use, and details on tirzepatide dose at data collection were also reported. The questionnaires also extracted information on the characteristics and beliefs of prescribing physicians about their PwO who were prescribed tirzepatide: prescribing reasons, physician-perceived level of treatment response, PwO adherence and compliance to treatment, reasons for discontinuation. Adherence was defined as taking medication exactly as agreed with their physician, for example, taking the prescribed amount at the time of day recommended and compliance was defined as the extent to which the patient's behavior in taking their medication matches the prescriber's recommendations. Physicians were asked to rank statements regarding the likelihood of PwO in achieving goals from one to five (1 [extremely unlikely] to 5 [extremely likely]), and motivation of PwO to lose weight (low to very high). Physicians' concern that PwO will not achieve their goal weight and physician-perceived level of concern PwO has about their weight loss and maintenance (1 [not at all concerned] to 5 [extremely concerned]) were also reported.

Voluntarily PwO-completed questionnaires captured treatment experience, satisfaction with tirzepatide, weight loss goals and adherence using the 11-item ADelphi Adherence Questionnaire© (ADAQ).[[12], [13], [14]].

Statistical analysis

Analysis was conducted using Stata 18 software, version 18.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX) and/or IBM® SPSS® Data Collection Survey Reporter v7.5. Descriptive statistics presented categorical variables as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as means and standard deviations (SDs).

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 151 physicians provided data for 199 PwO on tirzepatide without T2D. The mean (SD) age of PwO receiving tirzepatide treatment (N = 199) was 49.4 (14.2) years; the majority were female (67.3%) (Table S1). Mean time since treatment initiation was 6.3 (4.1) months (median [IQR]: 7 [2.8 to 9.9] months). Mean (SD) BMI and weight at the start of treatment were 36.4 (6.0) kg/m2 and 231.0 (44.4) lbs, respectively. Additionally, 84.4% had ≥1 ORC; hypertension was the most prevalent ORC (42.7%), followed by dyslipidemia (32.2%).

Disease management, reasons for prescribing tirzepatide, and physician's beliefs

Prior prescribed OMM use was reported for 13.6% (n = 27) of participants; 39.7% (n = 79) were receiving tirzepatide 2.5 mg or 5 mg (Table S2). Before tirzepatide initiation, diet, exercise, and pharmacologic approaches were commonly used for weight management; diet and exercise also remained common concomitant strategies during treatment (Table 1).

Physicians most often reported prior unsuccessful attempts with diet/exercise, the presence of ≥1 ORC, and desire for rapid weight loss as reasons for starting a weight reduction program. Common treatment goals beyond weight reduction included improving quality of life, mobility, mental health, and cardiometabolic parameters. Treatment response: physician perception of prescribing tirzepatide

Physician experience: According to the physicians, participants were fairly-to-fully compliant with their tirzepatide (n = 171, 85.9%) and were mostly-to-completely adherent (n = 170, 85.4%; Table 2). Of the 11 participants with reduced compliance or adherence, key reasons included stigma associated with taking obesity medications (n = 5, 45.5%), change in routine (n = 4, 36.4%), and challenges with taking medication while working or traveling (n = 4, 36.4%). The mean duration of the current tirzepatide treatment was 6.3 months, and the mean (SD) body weight change noted was −19.6 (22.4) pounds corresponding to and percent change of −8.4 (9.4) %. Physicians perceived the current tirzepatide treatment to be successful for 78.5% of the participants and that it was ‘too early to tell’ for 18.8% of the participants (Fig. 1B). The key reasons that physicians chose to prescribe tirzepatide were its safe and tolerable long-term use (n = 15/24, 62.5%), easy dosage regimen (n = 14, 58.3%), reduced food cravings/appetite (n = 14, 58.3%), few associated side effects (n = 11, 45.8%), compliance (n = 11, 45.8%), highest % weight loss achieved compared to other OMMs (n = 10, 41.7%), and effective in achieving desired weight in ≤6 months (n = 10, 41.7%) and in ≤12 months (n = 8, 33.3%) (Table 2).

Treatment response: PwO perception and experience of using tirzepatide

PwO reported experience: These results are based on responses from PwO who voluntarily completed the self-reported questionnaire, had matching electronic records, and had a stated answer to the specific question. Of the N = 199 PwO for whom physicians provided data, a total of 54 (27.1%) PwO themselves responded to at least one survey question, but response rates for each question vary.

More than three-quarters of the PwO on tirzepatide reported that they were yet to achieve their goal weight (n = 41, 75.9%). Among participants who provided a stated response to ‘If you have successfully lost weight, which of the following changes have you experienced?’ (n = 43), many reported feeling better about themselves (n = 27, 62.8%), being able to fit into smaller sized clothing (n = 20, 46.5%), feeling less out of breath (n = 18, 41.9%), and feeling improvement in their physical appearance (n = 18, 41.9%; Table 3).

The majority of the participants (n = 45/48, 93.8%) reported that they would recommend their tirzepatide to others for the treatment of obesity (Fig. 2A). More than half of the PwO preferred face-to-face physician consultations (n = 29/53, 54.7%) rather than telemedicine (n = 5, 9.4%), while others had no preference on how they visit their HCPs (n = 19, 35.8%). PwO (n = 50) reported that key reasons to initiate tirzepatide were the worry about the effect of weight on health (n = 31, 62%), and because they were advised by their doctors (n = 24, 48%; Table 3). The mean (SD) ADAQ score for PwO on tirzepatide was 0.3 (0.4), suggesting that these people were adherent to their treatment regimen (Fig. 2B).

Discussion

This study provides insights into the real-world experiences of physicians and PwO without T2D who were prescribed tirzepatide.

The majority of PwO had ≥1 ORC at data collection, with hypertension and dyslipidemia being the most prevalent ORCs. These results are consistent with previous studies reporting multiple ORCs and impaired quality of life in PwO [15,16]. Furthermore, questionnaires completed by physicians reflected that the treatment of obesity and addressing ORCs were the key reasons for first prescriber consultation. Studies have shown that even modest weight reduction can lead to meaningful improvements in HRQoL [17]. In contrast to earlier research showing lower motivation among PwO who did not view obesity as an important disease (odds ratio [95% CI]: 0.49 [0.41–0.58]) [18]. Physicians in the current study reported high PwO motivation and concern about weight reduction and maintenance. This suggests a growing recognition of obesity as a serious health concern and may reflect a positive shift in attitudes of PwO towards weight management. Furthermore, prescribing physicians reported that most of the participants were compliant and adherent to the prescribed tirzepatide treatment. Overall, these observations suggest high adherence and positive treatment experiences among PwO without T2D receiving tirzepatide.

Under-prescribing and low utilization of OMMs in eligible US populations have been reported previously [19]. Similarly, in the current study, 13.6% of participants had prior use of prescribed OMMs, as reported by their physicians. At the time of this study, tirzepatide was approved in the US for obesity treatment and at data collection, most of the individuals were receiving 2.5–5 mg of tirzepatide doses indicating that many participants may have recently started taking tirzepatide or had not yet escalated their dose. Even though PwO were on lower doses, over an average of 6 months on treatment, they lost 19.6 pounds of their body weight.

Physician-reported reasons for prescribing tirzepatide were consistent with recent literature, including safety, reduced appetite and cravings and minimal side effects [[20], [21], [22]]. This further supports the satisfaction results where ≥90% of the PwO (n = 45/48) receiving tirzepatide reported that they would recommend tirzepatide to others. Furthermore, PwO who were successful in losing weight expressed benefits beyond weight reduction, such as feeling better about themselves, improved health, fitness, and mood, and developing new habits. These findings are consistent with the qualitative results reported by Ingels et al., highlighting the perceived gains in self-confidence, health, fitness, mood, and the formation of new health habits among PwO while being enrolled in weight-management programs [23].

Limitations: The Adelphi Obesity DSP survey was distributed via physicians, so it did not include PwO who had not recently consulted a physician, which may impact the generalizability of the sample. The results should be interpreted with caution, considering the lower proportion of PwO who responded to the survey questions. Furthermore, the study did not comprehensively assess insurance coverage or other access-related barriers, which may influence access to treatment, treatment uptake, and willingness to initiate therapy.

Conclusion

These real-world findings offer insights into the real-world experiences of people living with obesity and their prescribing physicians. These findings support the effectiveness and positive patient experience with tirzepatide and suggest opportunities for healthcare providers to enhance obesity care.

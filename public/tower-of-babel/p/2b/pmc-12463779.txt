Identification of immunosuppressive CD4+ T-cell subtype and construction of prognostic model for hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Chuxuan Wang, Lianchi Li, Yingrui Lou, Lan Zhang, Feifei Tian, Taoyou Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCD4+ T cells, as critical mediators of immune regulation, have not been fully elucidated in their role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) immune evasion.MethodsWe analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from 16 HCC patients (GSE149614). Cell types were annotated based on cluster-specific differentially expressed genes (DEGs), utilizing the SingleR and PISCES for automated cell type assignment, with reference to established markers from previous studies. Metabolic reprogramming, transcriptional networks, and intercellular communication were assessed via GSVA, SCENIC, and CellChat. A prognostic model integrating TCGA-LIHC and validated in GSE109211 was developed using LASSO Cox regression. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations evaluated the therapeutic potential of prioritized targets.ResultsThis study identifies SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells as key drivers of HCC immune evasion. This subtype was enriched in tumor tissues, exhibited elevated immune checkpoint gene expression scores, and demonstrated metabolic reprogramming favoring tumor growth via glycolysis and reactive oxygen species (ROS) pathways. CellChat analysis revealed that SPP1-mediated interactions construct a multi-layered immunosuppressive network involving macrophages and CD8+ T cells. A prognostic model based on nine genes (LIG1, NOP56, MIS18A, SMC4, CDCA8, DTYMK, ASF1B, RAD51, LGALS3) was developed using TCGA-LIHC data and validated in an independent cohort (GSE109211), achieving robust predictive performance (AUC > 0.7). Molecular docking and dynamics simulations confirmed the drug ability of these targets, which means these genes may serve as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.ConclusionsOur findings establish SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells as central regulators of HCC immune suppression, offering novel strategies to enhance HCC immunotherapy.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03556-2.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the most prevalent malignancy, represents a significant global health challenge due to its high incidence and poor prognosis [1]. Despite progress in early detection and surgical techniques, the majority of patients receive a late-stage diagnosis, limiting the effectiveness of conventional therapies [2]. The tumor microenvironment (TME), a complex ecosystem consisting of immune cells, stromal elements, and malignant hepatocytes, is crucial in promoting HCC progression and resistance to treatment [3]. Recent evidence underscores the role of immunosuppressive components within the TME in facilitating immune evasion, although the underlying mechanisms require further elucidation [4, 5].

While research has extensively profiled certain immune cell subsets in the TME, such as CD8+ T cells and natural killer (NK) cells [6, 7], CD4+ T cells, which are central to immune regulation, have been relatively neglected. Although regulatory T cells (Tregs) expressing CTLA-4 and PD-1 have been linked to HCC immunosuppression [8, 9], the broader heterogeneity of CD4+ T cell subsets and their metabolic adaptations remain understudied [10]. The development of single-cell sequencing technologies in recent years has provided new tools to uncover the heterogeneity of immune cells within the TME [11, 12]. Preliminary studies indicate that CD4+ T cells, central orchestrators of adaptive immunity, exhibit functional plasticity in the TME [13, 14].

Current limitations in HCC immunotherapy stem from an incomplete understanding of CD4+ T cell subpopulation dynamics [15, 16]. Addressing these gaps requires integrating multi-omics approaches to delineate how specific CD4+ T cell subsets remodel the TME. In this study, we employ a combination of single-cell RNA sequencing, transcription factor analysis, metabolic pathway analysis, and cell-cell communication analysis to systematically investigate the heterogeneity of CD4+ T-cell subsets in HCC tumor tissues, focusing on the transcriptional characteristics, metabolic states, and cell-cell communication properties of SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells, to reveal the unique roles and mechanisms of this subtype in the HCC immunosuppressive microenvironment at the molecular, cellular, and signaling network levels. Furthermore, we develop a prognostic risk model and explore therapeutic potential through molecular docking and dynamics simulations.

This study not only addresses a significant gap in the field but also provides a foundation for developing immunotherapeutic strategies targeting SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells. Through this study, we hope to provide new insights and strategies for the precise development of HCC immunotherapy.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data (GSE149614) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, comprising 16 samples (8 tumor and 8 adjacent non-tumor tissues) from 10 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients [17]. Bulk RNA-seq data for 371 HCC tumors and 50 normal liver tissues were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-LIHC) using the TCGAbiolinks R package. Clinical and survival data were retrieved from UCSC Xena. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 (v1.42.1) [18] with thresholds of |log2FC| >1.5 and p < 0.05. The GSE109211 dataset served as an independent validation cohort [19].

scRNA-seq data processing

Raw scRNA-seq data were processed using Seurat (v5.0.3) [20]. Low-quality cells were filtered based on mitochondrial gene content (> 5%) and gene counts (< 200 or > 2,500). Data normalization, scaling, and identifying highly variable genes (HVGs; top 2,000) were performed before principal component analysis (PCA). Harmony was applied to correct batch effects across samples [21].

Dimensionality reduction and cell annotation

Dimensionality reduction employed t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) using 17 principal components at clustering resolution 0.5. Cluster marker genes were identified with |log₂FC| ≥ 0.25 and adjusted p < 0.05. Cell annotation utilized SingleR (v2.4.1) [22] complemented by canonical marker curation.

For CD4⁺ T cell subtyping, the PISCES pipeline [23], executed ARACNe-VIPER [24], analysis. Parameters included Mutual Information (MI) significance at p = 1 × 10⁻⁸ and Poisson-tested edge significance at p < 0.05 during 100 bootstrap iterations, followed by protein activity-based core analysis.

Malignant cell identification

Copy number variation (CNV) analysis was performed using InferCNV (v1.14.2) [25] to distinguish malignant hepatocytes from regular counterparts.

Functional scoring

Cellular states (naïve T cells, cytotoxicity, regulatory T cells, cytokines, immune checkpoints) were quantified using AUCell (v1.24.0) [26] with predefined gene sets [27, 28].

Pathway enrichment analysis

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA v4.3.3) and Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA v1.50.5) [29] were applied to hallmark gene sets. KEGG and GO analyses were conducted using clusterProfiler (v4.10.1) [30] with adjusted p < 0.05.

Transcriptional network inference

SCENIC (v1.3.1) [31] with GENIE3 (v1.24.0) and RcisTarget (v1.19.2) was used to construct transcription factor (TF)-target networks. TF motifs were analyzed using hg19 reference.

Metabolic profiling

Metabolic pathway activity in CD4+ T cell subsets was quantified via metabolism (v0.2.1) [32] using KEGG pathways and AUCell scoring.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis

CD4+ T cell differentiation trajectories were inferred using Monocle (v2.30.1) [33] with Seurat cluster labels as input.

Cell–cell communication

CellChat (v1.6.1) [34] analyzed ligand-receptor interactions between CD4+ T cell subsets and other cell types.

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network

STRING [35] (interaction score > 0.7) and Cytoscape [36] identified the top 80 hub genes from PPI networks.

Prognostic model construction

A LASSO Cox model (glmnet v4.1.8) [37] selected nine prognostic genes from 80 candidates. Risk scores were calculated using gene expression and regression coefficients. Model performance was evaluated via ROC analysis.

Bayesian deconvolution

BayesPrism (v2.2.2) [38] estimated cell-type proportions in bulk RNA-seq data using scRNA-seq as prior, processing raw count matrices with parameters outlier.cut = 0.01 and outlier.fraction = 0.1.

Molecular docking

Protein structures (PDB) with resolution < 2.5 Å were preprocessed using AutoDock Tools. Ligands were optimized with Open Babel. AutoDock Vina [39, 40] predicted binding modes (box size: 25 Å³). The top 10 poses were analyzed in PyMOL.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations

AMBER14SB [41, 42] and GAFF2 [43] force fields were used for proteins and ligands, respectively. Systems were solvated in TIP4P water, neutralized with ions, and minimized. NVT/NPT equilibration preceded 10-ns production runs (GROMACS 2020.6) [44]. Binding free energy was calculated via MM/PB(GB)SA (gmx_MMPBSA) [45, 46].

Results

Single-cell landscape of HCC ecosystems

Analysis of 16 HCC tissues revealed six major cell types via t-SNE (Fig. 1a, b). Immune subtypes included T cells (CD3D/G), B cells (MS4A1, CD79A), and macrophages (CD68, CD163), with substantial interpatient heterogeneity (Fig. 1c, d). Non-immune cells comprised endothelial cells (PECAM1), fibroblasts (ACTA2, PDGFRB), and hepatocytes (MDK/GPC3). InferCNV confirmed that malignant hepatocytes exhibited significant CNVs (Fig. 1e) and retained hepatocyte markers (ALB, TTR, APOA2, APOA1, AMBP, APOH) (Fig. 1f).

GSEA revealed concurrent dysregulation of oncogenic and immunomodulatory pathways [47, 48]in malignant hepatocytes (Fig. 1g). MYC signaling was significantly activated, while immune-related pathways including IL-2/STAT5 signaling and IFN-γ response were downregulated. In addition, elevated CD47 and FGL1 expression further suggested immune checkpoint-driven evasion (Fig. 1h).

Single-cell genomic profiling of T cells in HCC

Unsupervised clustering of T cells identified three significant subtypes (Fig. 2a), including NK cells (KLRF1/KLRD1), CD4+ T cells (CCR7/IL2/FOXP3), and CD8+ T cells (CD8A/B).

NK cells were enriched in adjacent non-tumor tissues showed elevated CD69 and NR4A1 expression characteristic of tissue-resident memory subtypes, along with cytotoxic (GNLY, GZMB, PRF1) and inhibitory (HAVCR2) signatures (Fig. 2b) [49].CD8⁺ T cells exhibited cytotoxic markers (GZMK, GZMA, NKG7) [50–52], with RUNX3 expression indicating hepatic residency and elevated LAG3 and PDCD1 reflecting exhaustion (Fig. 2b) [53, 54] and predominantly localized to NORMAL samples (Fig. 2c).

CD4+ T cells exhibited regulatory (FOXP3, CTLA4, IL2RA) and activated (TNFRSF9, ICOS) profiles [55]. Co-expression of exhaustion markers (CTLA-4, TIGIT, PDCD1) and proliferative capacity suggested an immunosuppressive cycling subtype comprising Tregs and CD8+ T cells [56, 57] (Fig. 2b). Notably, CD4+ T cells were proportionally enriched in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non-tumor counterparts (Fig. 2c). Consequently, this study delved into a detailed CD4+ T cell subtype analysis.

Further clustering of CD4⁺ T cells identified four distinct subtypes: SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells, MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells, AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells, and AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells (Fig. 3a). These subtypes were consistently identified across all patients and tissue compartments (Fig. 3b).

DEGs analysis revealed elevated expression of immunosuppression-associated SPP1 [58–60], oxidative stress (NQO1), metabolic reprogramming (CKB) and inflammatory (MMP12) [61–63]genes in SPP1⁺TNFRSF18⁺ CD4⁺ T cells (Fig. 3c). Furthermore, SPP1⁺TNFRSF18⁺ CD4⁺ T cells characteristically upregulated immunomodulatory SPP1 [64]and T cell-regulatory TNFRSF18 in CD4⁺ T cell subtypes [65] (Fig. 3d).

Quantitative analysis of subtype distribution revealed significant shifts between tumor and non-tumor tissues. The proportions of SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells and AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells were markedly increased in tumor tissues, whereas MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells and AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells were reduced (Fig. 3e). Spearman correlation analysis delineated inter-subtype relationships that SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells and AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells exhibited minimal correlations with other subsets. In contrast, MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells and AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells showed strong positive correlations, suggesting cooperative interactions within the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 3f).

Functional scoring of CD4+ T cell subtypes assessed naïve, cytotoxic, regulatory (Treg), cytokine, and immune checkpoint gene (ICG) expression (Fig. 3g). SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells exhibited immune checkpoint genes (ICGs) engagement. In contrast, MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells demonstrated enhanced cytotoxic potential and cytokine-related gene signatures, whereas AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells predominantly displayed naïve T cell characteristics. AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells showed reduced Treg and ICG expression compared to the SPP1+ subtype, highlighting functional divergence within the tumor microenvironment.

Functional analysis of SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells in the HCC immune ecosystem

We performed Monocle-based state trajectory inference to delineate the dynamic immune states and transitional patterns of CD4+ T cells (Fig. 4a). Pseudotime analysis of CD4+ T cells in the tumor (TUMOR) and adjacent non-tumor (NORMAL) tissues revealed distinct distribution patterns. TUMOR-derived CD4+ T cells predominantly occupied pseudotime states 1 and 4, whereas NORMAL-derived cells localized to states 2 and 3, indicating shared transition trajectories but divergent cellular states between tissue compartments (Fig. 4b). Subtype mapping across pseudotime stages demonstrated stage-specific dominance. SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells clustered in state 1, MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells in state 2, AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells in state 3, and AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells in state 4 (Fig. 4c, d).

Immune checkpoint analysis during state transitions revealed heterogeneous expression patterns. Immune checkpoint genes, BTLA, CTLA4, LAG3, and TIGIT, were upregulated in tumor-derived CD4+ T cells at state one compared to non-tumor counterparts. At the same time, PDCD1 exhibited an opposing trend (Fig. 4e). This spatial heterogeneity underscores the potential for targeted immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) strategies in HCC.

To further characterize subtype-specific immunometabolic status, we conducted Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA), transcription factor (TF) profiling, and metabolic pathway analysis. SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells exhibited enrichment in glycolysis (HIF-1 signaling) and reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolic pathways (Fig. 4f), supporting their role in fueling tumor proliferation and genomic instability via ROS-mediated DNA damage [66, 67]. GO/KEGG analyses linked this subtype to chemotaxis, B cell activation, and oxidative stress (Fig. 4i). TF profiling identified ETV4, MYBL2, ETV5, FOXA1, and HNF4A as key regulators (Fig. 4g), with ETV4 and ETV5 driving tumor growth and Th17 differentiation [68, 69], MYBL2 correlating with poor prognosis [70], FOXA1 and HNF4A modulating metabolic reprogramming [71–73]. Metabolic flux analysis confirmed reliance on glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and fatty acid degradation (Fig. 4h), highlighting their dual role in self-maintenance and TME modulation.

MMP12+JUNB+ CD4+ T cells displayed pro-inflammatory features, marked by elevated NFKB1 activity and TNF-α/NF-κB pathway activation. AKR1B10+CCR7+ CD4+ T cells exhibited KLF3, RARG, and HOXA10 dominance, with metabolic activity centered on nitrogen and propionate metabolism. AKR1B10+TIGIT+ CD4+ T cells showed enrichment in G2/M checkpoint and E2F target pathways, coupled with high expression of proliferation-associated TFs (STAT2, NFIC, NFYB, ZFP91) [74–77], confirming their actively cycling state (Fig. 4f-h).

Interaction analysis of SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells

CellChat analysis revealed distinct cell-cell communication patterns between SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells and other cell types (Fig. 5a, b). Thirty-five conserved pathways, including CXCL, MIF, and TRAIL, were active across TUMOR and NORMAL tissues. In comparison, 27 pathways, including LT and IL10, were uniquely enriched in tumors (Fig. 5c). LTβR signaling promoted inflammation-driven carcinogenesis via NF-κB-mediated chemokine secretion [78], and IL-10 upregulated PD-L1 to suppress CD8+ T cell activity [79].

TUMOR-specific interactions included enhanced CD86 signaling between SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells and macrophages (Fig. 5e) and reduced CXCL12-CXCR4 communication with B cells (Fig. 5g). Upregulated SPP1-integrin interactions, such as SPP1-ITGA5/ITGB1, in TUMOR samples (Fig. 5f) suggested macrophage-mediated immunosuppression [58]. In contrast, diminished CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling correlated with impaired B cell infiltration [80] and altered macrophage polarization [81].

In addition, two other critical intercellular signaling axes were identified as central regulators of immunosuppressive microenvironments in tumor tissues. These axes are centered on bidirectional crosstalk between malignant cells and SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells and downstream suppression of CD8+ T cell functionality (Fig. 6).

In HCC, tumor cells exhibited tumor-specific upregulation of CD99-CD99 and MDK-NCL signaling axes with SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells (Fig. 6a). CD99, a transmembrane glycoprotein, facilitated direct contact between malignant cells and CD4+ T cells via homotypic interactions, enabling inhibitory signaling (e.g. PD-L1/PD-1 axis). Aberrant CD99 expression altered CD4+ T cell infiltration patterns and activated MAPK/NF-κB pathways, indirectly promoting immune evasion [82]. Midkine (MDK), a pro-tumorigenic factor overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), binds nucleolin (NCL) on CD4+ T cells. Upregulated MDK-NCL signaling in tumor tissues exhibited dual roles in promoting tumor survival through PI3K/AKT/ERK pathway activation and suppressing antitumor immunity via regulatory T cell (Treg) differentiation or direct inhibition of effector T cell function [83]. These findings highlight MDK-NCL as a critical axis linking tumor cell proliferation to immune evasion in HCC.

In TUMOR samples, SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells communicated with CD8+ T cells primarily via the SPP1-CD44 axis (Fig. 6b). This interaction induced CD8+ T cell exhaustion and synergized with TGF-β signaling to reinforce immune tolerance, attenuating anti-tumor responses [84]. Notably, macrophages amplified this network by transmitting upregulated SPP1-associated signals to SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells, propagating inhibitory cues to CD8+ T cells. This hierarchical SPP1-centric crosstalk established a multi-layered immunosuppressive framework within the HCC microenvironment.

Prognostic model construction and validation

Bayesian deconvolution of TCGA-LIHC data linked SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell infiltration to poor overall survival (Fig. 7a). The intersection of bulk (3,855 DEGs) and single-cell data identified 160 immune-related genes (Fig. 7b, c). PPI network analysis prioritized 80 hub genes, refined to 9 prognostic markers (LIG1, NOP56, MIS18A, SMC4, CDCA8, DTYMK, ASF1B, RAD51, LGALS3) via LASSO Cox regression (λ = 0.019) (Fig. 7d).

High-risk patients (stratified by median risk score) exhibited elevated SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell infiltration (p < 0.001) and upregulated prognostic gene expression (Fig. 7e, f). The model demonstrated strong predictive power in TCGA (1-/3-/5-year AUC: 0.786/0.725/0.809) (Fig. 7g) and GSE109211 (AUC > 0.8) (Fig. 7h).

Molecular docking and dynamics simulations

RAD51, LGALS3, and DTYMK were prioritized for molecular docking from nine prognostic genes. Semi-flexible docking predicted binding pockets, with grid boxes centered on active sites. The top three poses per target were selected based on conformation scores (Table 1). Molecular dynamics simulations (10 ns) revealed stable ligand-target complexes (Fig. 8). Root mean square deviation (RMSD) analysis indicated equilibration within 10 ns. MM/PB(GB)SA calculations identified Luxeptinib (RAD51), Deleobuvir sodium (LGALS3), and AMG-900 (DTYMK) as optimal binders with the lowest binding free energies (Fig. 9).

Discussion

This study comprehensively characterizes the role of CD4+ T cells in shaping the immunosuppressive microenvironment of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), with a novel focus on the SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell subtype. We systematically delineated its pathway enrichment, transcriptional profiles, and metabolic reprogramming, analyzed its dynamic states during HCC progression, mapped its intercellular communication networks, and evaluated its clinical prognostic significance. Furthermore, molecular docking and dynamics simulations explored the therapeutic potential of prognosis-associated genes, offering new insights for precision immunotherapies targeting tumor microenvironments.

Compared to existing literature, this work advances understanding in three key areas. First, while prior studies emphasized immune checkpoint molecules (e.g., CTLA-4, PD-1) in CD4+ T cell-mediated effector T cell suppression [85], our integrative analysis of metabolic traits, transcription factor networks and cellular crosstalk provides a multidimensional view of SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell functionality. Second, we linked their activity to immune cell regulation beyond the established roles of transcription factors in tumor proliferation and metastasis (80), revealing new tumor-immune interaction paradigms. Third, our findings support the cancer immunoediting hypothesis [86], demonstrating that HCC progression relies on enhanced immunosuppressive cell functions, which is evidenced by upregulated SPP1, immune checkpoint genes, and SPP1-CD44-mediated interactions in this subtype [87].

Nevertheless, this study has certain limitations that need to be acknowledged. First, the restricted cohort size for single-cell RNA sequencing may not fully capture the heterogeneity of hepatocellular carcinoma, necessitating larger validation cohorts. Second, the bioinformatics-derived inferences, particularly the immunosuppressive role of SPP1⁺TNFRSF18⁺ CD4⁺ T cells, lack experimental confirmation through in vitro or in vivo functional studies. Third, the absence of incomplete clinical treatment data precludes assessment of the prognostic model’s clinical utility in treatment stratification. Fourth, molecular docking analyses remain preliminary without structural validation. Given these constraints, all conclusions regarding CD4⁺ T cell functions should be interpreted as correlative rather than causal.

Conclusions

Our study systematically characterizes the immunosuppressive role of the SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell subpopulation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) through integrative single-cell and bulk transcriptomic analyses. By profiling 16 HCC tumor and adjacent non-tumor tissues, we identified six major cell populations, with a focus on CD4+ T cell heterogeneity. We delineated four CD4+ T cell subsets, among which the SPP1+TNFRSF18+ subpopulation was significantly enriched in tumors.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed that SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cells predominantly reside in the early transitional phase, characterized by high expression of immune checkpoint genes. Functional profiling demonstrated their metabolic reprogramming toward glycolysis and reactive oxygen species (ROS) pathways, supported by upregulated transcription factors linked to proliferation and metabolic adaptation. Cell communication analysis further uncovered their central role in orchestrating immunosuppression: macrophage-derived SPP1-integrin signals (e.g., SPP1-ITGA5/ITGB1) enhanced inhibitory crosstalk, while malignant cells engaged via CD99-CD99 and MDK-NCL axes. Concurrently, SPP1-CD44 interactions induced CD8+ T cell exhaustion, collectively establishing a multi-layered immunosuppressive network.

Leveraging TCGA-LIHC and independent validation (GSE109211), we developed a nine-gene prognostic model (LIG1, NOP56, MIS18A, SMC4, CDCA8, DTYMK, ASF1B, RAD51, LGALS3) that robustly predicted patient survival (AUC > 0.7). High-risk patients exhibited increased SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell infiltration, corroborating their clinical relevance. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations prioritized three druggable targets, RAD51, LGALS3, and DTYMK. These targets respectively demonstrated stable binding to clinical-stage inhibitors, Luxeptinib, Deleobuvir sodium, and AMG-900, highlighting their potential as therapeutic candidates.

These findings identify SPP1 + TNFRSF18 + CD4 + T cells as associated with HCC immunosuppression through computational analysis of single-cell and bulk transcriptomic data. The observed associations between SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell infiltration, immunosuppressive signaling networks, and poor clinical outcomes provide valuable insights for understanding HCC tumor microenvironment heterogeneity.The nine-gene prognostic model and identified druggable targets represent promising candidates that warrant further experimental investigation to confirm their functional roles and therapeutic potential.

Limitations

Our study was based on transcriptome sequencing data from publicly accessible repositories. Due to limited funding, the risk prognosis model established for the SPP1+TNFRSF18+ CD4+ T cell subtype can only be verified by molecular docking and dynamics simulations.

Auditory neuromodulation-based prescription digital therapeutic for insomnia disorder: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial (SERENE)

Introduction

Background and rationale {9a}

Insomnia is a prevalent sleep disorder characterized by difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep, or early morning awakening, associated with significant daytime consequences. The disorder affects 10–15% of adults worldwide, with chronic insomnia representing a substantial public health burden [1]. In the Republic of Korea, approximately 800,000 individuals received treatment for insomnia in 2022, representing an annual increase of 4.7%, with healthcare costs reaching 94.4 billion KRW [2]. Insomnia significantly impacts quality of life and is associated with numerous adverse outcomes including fatigue, impaired concentration, mood disturbances, depression, and reduced work productivity [1].

Current treatment approaches for insomnia present a fundamental trade-off. CBT-I, recommended as first-line treatment by clinical practice guidelines, demonstrates sustained long-term efficacy but faces significant implementation barriers including limited therapist availability, high cost, and substantial time commitment [3, 4]. Pharmacotherapy provides rapid symptom relief but carries concerns of dependence, tolerance, and adverse cognitive effects that limit long-term use [5, 6]. These limitations have created a treatment gap, with most patients unable to access interventions offering both rapid efficacy and favorable safety profiles.

Prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs) have emerged as scalable alternatives to address these accessibility barriers, with CBT-I-based platforms such as Somryst receiving regulatory approval [7, 8]. However, these existing PDTs typically inherit the fundamental limitation of behavioral interventions: therapeutic benefits typically require 6 to 8 weeks of treatment to manifest, yet treatment adherence to digital CBT-I remains suboptimal, with approximately half of prescribed therapeutic exercises left uncompleted [9–11]. Thus, there remains an unmet need for digital therapeutics that can combine the earlier onset of therapeutic effect comparable to pharmacotherapy with the favorable safety profile of non-pharmacological interventions.

BELL-001 represents a PDT that targets physiological mechanisms underlying sleep onset. Developed by BELL Therapeutics Inc. (Seoul, Republic of Korea) in compliance with ISO 13485 standards for medical device development, the intervention is designed to induce autonomic downregulation through real-time biosignal-responsive acoustic neuromodulation, targeting hyperarousal as a core pathophysiological feature of insomnia. This approach may enable rapid onset of therapeutic effects without the multi-week behavioral modification required by CBT-I-based interventions.

The therapeutic mechanism of BELL-001 is grounded in the neurophysiology of sleep–wake regulation. Hyperarousal, characterized by elevated sympathetic nervous system activity and heightened cortical activation, is increasingly recognized as a core pathophysiological feature of insomnia [1, 12]. BELL-001 addresses this through auditory neuromodulation that promotes parasympathetic dominance and reduces cortical hyperactivation [13, 14]. Given that the intervention directly targets autonomic dysregulation, rapid onset of therapeutic effects may be anticipated. Preliminary proof-of-concept studies have provided exploratory evidence supporting this potential. Preliminary proof-of-concept studies have demonstrated the device’s potential efficacy. In a randomized crossover trial using polysomnography (n = 10), a single-session intervention significantly reduced sleep onset latency by 11.2 min (28.8%, p = 0.004), improved sleep efficiency by 8.5% (p = 0.009), decreased wake after sleep onset by 28.1 min (p = 0.018), and increased deep sleep (N3) proportion by 9.2% (p = 0.017). Furthermore, a 4-week usability evaluation (n = 34, per-protocol analysis) demonstrated a mean reduction of 7.29 points (SD 5.40) in Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) scores, representing a large effect size (unpublished data, Bell Therapeutics Inc., 2025). However, these findings were derived from open-label studies with small sample sizes, necessitating confirmation through a rigorous randomized controlled trial. Therefore, this pivotal clinical trial, named SERENE: Sleep Efficacy Randomized Evaluation of a Novel digital therapeutic for insomnia, was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of BELL-001 for adult insomnia treatment.

Methods

Trial design {10, 12, 19, 20, 21a, 21b, 22, 23, 24a, 24b, 24c}

This trial is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled, superiority trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of BELL-001 for improving insomnia symptoms in adults diagnosed with insomnia according to ICD-10 criteria (nonorganic insomnia disorder, F51.0, or disorders of initiating and maintaining sleep, G47.0). The total study duration is approximately 5 weeks, comprising a 1-week screening period and a 4-week treatment period.

The sample size calculation is based on the primary endpoint of ISI change from baseline to week 4. Assuming a mean difference of 4.17 points between groups, a standard deviation of 5.40 points [15], a two-sided significance level of 0.05, and 95% power, 45 participants per group are required. Accounting for an anticipated dropout rate of 20%, a minimum of 114 participants (57 per group) will be enrolled and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the treatment group or the control group. Participants will be recruited through outpatient clinics at participating sites, online and offline advertisements, and referrals from healthcare providers. Recruitment progress will be monitored centrally, and site-specific recruitment strategies will be adjusted as needed.

Participants in the treatment group will use BELL-001, while those in the control group will use a sham device identical in appearance and user interface. Randomization will be performed centrally via an Interactive Web-based Response System (IWRS), stratified by baseline ISI severity (moderate: 15–21 vs. severe: 22–28) using permuted blocks of varying sizes (2, 4, 6, and 8) generated by an independent statistician with a prespecified seed value. The randomization sequence will be concealed within the IWRS, and study personnel will not have access to the allocation sequence until the baseline visit (visit 2). This is a double-blind trial; participants, investigators, and outcome assessors will be blinded to treatment allocation. Emergency unblinding will be available through the IWRS when essential for participant safety. Figure 1 provides an overview of the study design.

Study design and participant flow of the SERENE trial. The study comprises a 1-week screening period followed by a 4-week treatment period. After eligibility assessment at visit 1, participants complete sleep diaries for 7 days during the screening period. Eligible participants are then randomized in a 1:1 ratio at visit 2 (baseline) to receive either BELL-001 or sham device for 4 weeks. The study consists of six visits: two in-person visits (visit 1 for screening and visit 2 for baseline/randomization), three telephone visits (visits 3–5 for weekly follow-up), and one final in-person visit (visit 6 at week 4). Both participants and investigators remain blinded throughout the treatment period

Study setting {13, 14b}

This trial will be conducted at six clinical sites in the Republic of Korea: Seoul National University Hospital, Kyung Hee University Hospital at Gangdong, Kangbuk Samsung Hospital, Kyung Hee University Medical Center, CHA Bundang Medical Center, and International St. Mary’s Hospital. All participating sites are staffed by investigators specializing in sleep medicine with experience in conducting clinical trials.

Patient and public involvement {11}

There was no patient or public involvement in the design, conduct, or reporting plans of this trial.

Intervention description {15a, 9b, 15c}

BELL-001 (version 2.0) is an auditory neuromodulation-based PDT developed by BELL Therapeutics Inc. (Seoul, Republic of Korea). The application is delivered via smartphone without requiring additional hardware. The intervention provides real-time personalized auditory stimulation using relaxation-inducing sounds designed based on neurophysiological principles, tailored to the user’s respiratory patterns to promote autonomic downregulation. The application offers two modes of operation: in Manual mode, users input their breathing patterns, which generates real-time acoustic feedback; in Automatic mode, the application provides auditory feedback to guide breathing without requiring manual input (Fig. 2).

Overview of BELL-001 intervention and user interface. A Schematic overview of the BELL-001 auditory neuromodulation intervention. The application captures the user’s respiratory patterns and delivers real-time personalized acoustic stimulation to promote autonomic downregulation. B User interface of the BELL-001 mobile application. The application offers two modes of operation: Manual mode (left), where users input their breathing patterns by touching the screen during inhalation, and Automatic mode (right), where the application provides auditory feedback to guide breathing without requiring manual input

Participants are instructed to use the device nightly at bedtime for up to 60 min throughout the 4-week treatment period; participants may fall asleep while using the device. The intervention is fully automated and self-administered; no human involvement is required beyond the initial prescription by a physician. BELL-001 was developed using user-centered design principles (e.g., an intuitive interface, brief daily sessions) to minimize user burden and promote sustained engagement. Upon first use, participants complete an in-app tutorial that guides them through the device operation. A user manual is also available for download within the application at any time.

Device usage is automatically logged by the application. The application generates automated reminders if weekly usage falls below the recommended threshold of three times per week. Study personnel review adherence data during weekly telephone visits and provide encouragement or troubleshooting as needed.

The sham device is identical in appearance and user interface to BELL-001 but delivers non-therapeutic ambient sounds without the active auditory neuromodulation algorithm. A sham device was selected as the comparator to maintain double-blind conditions and to isolate the specific therapeutic effect of BELL-001’s auditory neuromodulation from placebo effects associated with device use. This design allows for rigorous assessment of whether the observed effects are attributable to the active intervention rather than participant expectations or general relaxation from device engagement.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {15d}

Participants taking hypnotics (excluding as-needed hypnotics), antidepressants, anxiolytics, anticonvulsants, or antipsychotics prior to enrollment are permitted to continue these medications provided the type, dosage, and regimen remain unchanged throughout the study period. Initiation of new medications or dose modifications of existing medications that may affect efficacy evaluation is prohibited. PRN hypnotics are not permitted during the trial. Non-pharmacological insomnia treatments, including CBT-I, light therapy, and traditional Korean medicine therapies for sleep, are also prohibited.

Outcomes {16}

The primary efficacy outcome is the change in Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) total score from baseline to week 4, compared between treatment groups. The ISI is a validated 7-item patient-reported outcome (PRO) instrument that assesses the nature, severity, and impact of insomnia over the past 2 weeks [15]. Each item is rated on a 0–4 scale, yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 28, with higher scores indicating greater insomnia severity. The ISI will be administered electronically at screening (visit 1), baseline (visit 2), week 2 (visit 4), and week 4 (visit 6). The primary efficacy endpoint is the change in ISI total score from baseline to week 4. A between-group difference in the change at week 4 from baseline of at least 3 points on the ISI is considered the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) [16].

Secondary efficacy outcomes include: (1) change in ISI total score from baseline to week 2; (2) responder rate at week 4, defined as the proportion of participants achieving a reduction of ≥ 6 points in ISI total score from baseline [17]. Additional secondary endpoints include changes in daily sleep diary parameters [18], namely subjective sleep onset latency (sSL), subjective sleep quality (sSQ), subjective wake after sleep onset (sWASO), subjective sleep efficiency (sSE), and subjective total sleep time (sTST). Further secondary endpoints comprise changes from baseline to weeks 2 and 4 in the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) [19], Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS) [20], Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) [21], Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) [22], EuroQol-5 Dimensions-5 Levels (EQ-5D-5L) [23], and the Work Productivity and Activity Impairment Questionnaire (WPAI) [24].

Safety outcomes include the incidence, severity, and relationship to the investigational device of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), adverse device effects (ADEs), and serious adverse events, which will be collected throughout the study period.

Participant timeline {18}

Each participant will be involved in the study for approximately 5 weeks, comprising a 1-week screening period followed by a 4-week treatment period. At the screening visit (visit 1), written informed consent will be obtained, eligibility will be assessed, baseline demographic and clinical information will be collected, and the ISI will be administered. Participants will also be instructed to complete a daily electronic sleep diary for 7 days.

At the baseline visit (visit 2, week 0), eligibility will be reconfirmed and participants will be randomized via an interactive web response system to the treatment or control group. Training on the use of the investigational device will be provided, and participants will initiate the assigned intervention.

Weekly telephone contacts will be conducted at weeks 1, 2, and 3 (visits 3, 4, and 5) to monitor adverse events, review device usage, and record concomitant medications. At the week 2 contact (visit 4), the ISI and secondary efficacy questionnaires will also be administered electronically.

The end-of-study visit (visit 6, week 4) will be conducted in person and will include final efficacy assessments (ISI and all secondary outcome measures), review of sleep diary data and device adherence, and collection of safety information. Data collection and management {25a, 25b, 26, 33}

Data will be collected using electronic case report forms (eCRFs) and the study device application. The primary outcome (ISI) and daily sleep diary variables will be administered as electronic patient-reported outcomes (ePROs) and transmitted directly to the central database. Secondary questionnaires (ESS, FSS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, EQ-5D-5L, WPAI) will be completed on paper and entered into the eCRF by trained site personnel. Device usage data will be automatically captured and time-stamped by the application. To minimize data entry errors, site staff will be trained in standardized procedures, and the eCRF system will include range and consistency checks. Participants will receive weekly telephone contacts to encourage retention and monitor adverse events; if a participant discontinues, site staff will attempt to collect key outcome data to minimize missing data. The database will be locked after all data have been entered, verified, and queries resolved. All participant data will be handled in accordance with the Personal Information Protection Act of Korea, with participants identified by a unique study code and personal identifiers stored separately in a secure location.

Statistical methods {27a, 27b, 27c, 27 d}

The primary analysis will be conducted on the full analysis set (FAS), defined as all randomized participants who use the study device at least once and have at least one post-baseline efficacy assessment. The primary endpoint (change in ISI total score from baseline to week 4) will be analyzed using ANCOVA with treatment group as a fixed effect and baseline ISI score as a covariate. Secondary continuous endpoints will also be analyzed using ANCOVA with respective baseline values as covariates. The responder rate will be analyzed using logistic regression adjusted for baseline ISI score. Safety analyses will be performed on the safety set; adverse events will be summarized by treatment group and compared using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. A pre-specified subgroup analysis will explore treatment effects by baseline insomnia severity (moderate vs. severe). For the primary endpoint, missing data will be handled using the last observation carried forward (LOCF) method. A sensitivity analysis using the per-protocol set will assess robustness of the primary findings. The PPS includes FAS participants who used the device at least three times per week on average, completed the study as planned, and had no major protocol deviations.

Oversight and monitoring {28a, 28b, 17, 29}

A formal data monitoring committee was not established for this low-risk trial with short treatment duration and no planned interim analyses. No interim analyses or stopping guidelines are planned.

Trial monitoring will be conducted by a contract research organization delegated by the sponsor, with regular site visits to verify protocol adherence and data accuracy. All adverse events, adverse device effects, serious adverse events, and serious adverse device effects will be collected throughout the study and followed until resolution. Serious adverse events will be reported to the sponsor within 24 h and to the IRB as required. Serious and unexpected adverse device effects will be reported to the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety within 7 days if life-threatening or fatal, and within 15 days for other serious events.

Ethics and consent {30, 31, 32a, 32b, 15b, 34}

This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, ISO 14155:2020, and Korean Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The protocol has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kyung Hee University Hospital at Gangdong (KHNMC IRB 2025-11-028), The protocol is under review at Kangbuk Samsung Hospital (2025-12-006), Kyung Hee University Medical Center (2025-12-035), CHA Bundang Medical Center (2025-12-042), Seoul National University Hospital, and International St. Mary’s Hospital. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to any study-related procedures. No biological specimens will be collected, and no ancillary studies are planned; therefore, additional consent provisions are not applicable. Participants may discontinue at any time by withdrawing consent; if safety assessments change the risk–benefit profile, the intervention may be discontinued by the sponsor, investigator, IRB, or regulatory authority. Major protocol amendments require IRB and MFDS approval before implementation; participants will be re-consented as appropriate. Participants who experience adverse events related to the device will receive appropriate medical care, with compensation provided through clinical trial liability insurance obtained by the sponsor.

Discussion

The SERENE trial represents a phase 3 evaluation of an auditory neuromodulation-based prescription digital therapeutic (PDT) that targets physiological mechanisms underlying sleep onset. Unlike existing digital therapeutics based on cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), BELL-001 is designed to induce autonomic downregulation through real-time biosignal-responsive acoustic neuromodulation, targeting hyperarousal as a core pathophysiological feature of insomnia. This study addresses a critical unmet need by evaluating whether this approach can provide earlier onset of therapeutic effects while maintaining the favorable safety profile of non-pharmacological interventions, as measured by the Insomnia Severity Index [15] and comprehensive secondary outcomes.

Several design features of this trial merit discussion. First, the choice of a sham-controlled design reflects regulatory expectations for establishing the efficacy of digital therapeutics. The identical appearance and user experience of the active and sham applications are intended to optimize blinding integrity, while controlling for non-specific effects such as attention, expectancy, and the relaxation that may be induced by ambient sound exposure alone. This rigorous design increases the likelihood that any observed treatment effects can be attributed specifically to the auditory neuromodulation mechanism rather than to placebo response.

Second, the 4-week intervention duration represents a deliberate departure from conventional digital CBT-I trials, which typically require 6–9 weeks to demonstrate efficacy [8]. This shorter timeframe is aligned with the pharmacomimetic mechanism of BELL-001, which is designed to induce acute rather than gradual therapeutic effects. Proof-of-concept data demonstrating significant improvements in objective sleep parameters after a single session support this accelerated timeline [unpublished data, Bell Therapeutics Inc., 2025]. If the SERENE trial confirms a rapid onset of action, this would represent an advancement over existing digital therapeutics and could help address a key limitation of CBT-I–based approaches, namely the relatively slow development of clinical benefit.

Third, this trial employs a comprehensive set of patient-reported outcomes to capture the multidimensional impact of insomnia and its treatment. Beyond the primary endpoint of ISI change, secondary endpoints encompass sleep diary parameters, daytime sleepiness, fatigue, mood, quality of life, and work productivity. This approach enables evaluation of clinically meaningful associations between sleep improvement and broader functional outcomes, providing a more complete picture of the therapeutic value of BELL-001.

This trial also has several potential limitations. The study population consists of relatively homogeneous Korean adults, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic or cultural groups. In addition, the exclusion of participants with major psychiatric comorbidities and concurrent use of psychotropic medications means that the trial may not fully reflect real-world clinical practice, where such comorbidities and polypharmacy are common. Furthermore, the intervention and follow-up period are limited to 4 weeks; therefore, the long-term durability of treatment effects, the possibility of rebound after discontinuation, and the need for maintenance or booster use cannot be evaluated within this study. Finally, all efficacy outcomes are based on self-reported measures, which may be susceptible to subjective bias despite the double-blind design. Future research should assess BELL-001 in more diverse populations, under naturalistic conditions, and with longer follow-up to clarify long-term outcomes.

In conclusion, the SERENE trial will provide pivotal evidence on the efficacy and safety of BELL-001 as a novel pharmacomimetic PDT for insomnia. If efficacy is confirmed, this study may provide empirical support for the development of mechanism-informed digital therapeutics that target insomnia through non-pharmacological neuromodulation strategies.

Clinical efficacy analysis of secukinumab combined with calcipotriol cream in the treatment of moderate to severe psoriasis in elderly patients
Authors: Ling Lin, Wei Li, Qian Li, Xinjing Gao, Jingxin Zeng, Xin Zhou, Weiyu Liu, Xuelian Zhong, Yunqing Yang, Xibao Zhang, Quan Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPsoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory skin disorder, with plaque psoriasis being the most common clinical subtype, especially in elderly patients. Conventional therapies often provide suboptimal outcomes, necessitating the exploration of novel treatment approaches. This study evaluates the clinical efficacy of combining Secukinumab with Calcipotriol Cream in elderly patients with moderate to severe psoriasis.MethodsA total of 160 elderly patients (≥ 65 years) with plaque psoriasis were retrospectively analyzed between January 2020 and December 2024. All patients received baseline therapy with oral acitretin and topical clobetasol. The observation group (n = 80) additionally received secukinumab and calcipotriol cream. The control group (n = 80) received baseline therapy only. The primary endpoint was overall clinical efficacy based on Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) improvement. Secondary endpoints included changes in PASI scores, Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI), skin barrier parameters (stratum corneum hydration and sebum content), and incidence of adverse events over 12 weeks.ResultsThe total efficacy rate was significantly higher in the observation group than in the control group (90.0% vs. 72.5%, χ² = 8.306, P = 0.040). PASI scores were significantly reduced in both groups, with a greater reduction observed in the observation group (16.12 ± 3.13 vs. 18.98 ± 3.92; P < 0.001). Improvements in stratum corneum hydration (54.08 ± 6.05 vs. 50.20 ± 5.74; P < 0.001) and sebum content (138.20 ± 12.39 vs. 129.72 ± 10.68; P < 0.001) were also greater in the observation group. DLQI scores improved more significantly in the observation group (7.82 ± 1.05 vs. 9.52 ± 1.38; P < 0.001). Adverse events were infrequent and comparable between groups (P = 0.168). Post hoc power analysis confirmed sufficient statistical power (> 80%) across all primary endpoints.ConclusionsThis retrospective study suggests that secukinumab combined with calcipotriol cream improves clinical outcomes and quality of life with good tolerability in elderly patients with moderate to severe psoriasis, supporting its potential therapeutic utility.Clinical trialNot applicable.Keywords: Psoriasis, Secukinumab, Calcipotriol cream, Elderly patients, Clinical efficacy

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disorder characterized by erythematous plaques covered with silvery scales. It affects approximately 2–3% of the global population, with a prevalence that increases with age. Among the various subtypes, plaque psoriasis is the most common form, representing about 80–90% of cases [1–3]. Elderly patients, defined as individuals aged 65 years and above, often present with moderate to severe manifestations of the disease, leading to significant impairment in quality of life and increased risk of comorbidities. Traditional treatment modalities for psoriasis in elderly patients have included topical agents, phototherapy, systemic therapies, and biologic agents. However, these approaches are often associated with limitations such as variable efficacy, adverse effects, and reduced tolerability in the elderly population due to age-related physiological changes and comorbidities [4–6]. Hence, there is a growing interest in exploring novel therapeutic strategies that can offer improved outcomes with an acceptable safety profile in this vulnerable patient group.

Secukinumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting interleukin-17 A (IL-17 A), has emerged as an effective therapeutic option for moderate to severe psoriasis across different age groups. Its ability to induce rapid and sustained skin clearance, together with a favorable safety and tolerability profile, has been demonstrated in multiple clinical studies [7, 8]. Calcipotriol, a synthetic vitamin D analog, is widely used as an adjunctive therapy in psoriasis management and has been shown to enhance therapeutic efficacy when combined with other treatment modalities. The rationale for combining secukinumab with calcipotriol is based on their complementary mechanisms of action targeting distinct pathogenic pathways in psoriasis. Specifically, secukinumab suppresses IL-17 A mediated inflammatory signaling, whereas calcipotriol exerts anti-proliferative and immunomodulatory effects on keratinocytes, potentially improving treatment response and reducing the risk of disease recurrence [9, 10].

Despite the established efficacy of biologic monotherapy in psoriasis, data on structured multimodal treatment strategies specifically designed for elderly patients remain limited [11, 12]. This population frequently presents with impaired skin barrier function, multiple comorbidities, and relative contraindications to long-term systemic immunosuppression. Furthermore, although the anti-inflammatory effects of secukinumab are well documented, few studies have systematically evaluated its combined use with acitretin, topical corticosteroids, and vitamin D3 analogs in a comprehensive regimen aimed at achieving both clinical remission and skin barrier restoration [13, 14]. To address this gap, this study aimed to assess the incremental efficacy and safety of adding secukinumab and calcipotriol cream to conventional therapy with acitretin and topical corticosteroids in elderly patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.

Methods

Study design

An exhaustive retrospective evaluation was conducted at our hospital from January 2020 to December 2024 to assess the clinical efficacy of combining secukinumab with calcipotriol cream in treating moderate to severe psoriasis in elderly patients. The study included two groups. All enrolled patients received standard baseline therapy consisting of oral acitretin capsules (20 mg once daily) and topical clobetasol propionate cream (0.05%), applied once daily at bedtime to affected areas after cleansing. The control group was treated with this conventional regimen alone for 12 weeks. The observation group received the same baseline therapy plus additional treatment with secukinumab (300 mg administered subcutaneously at Weeks 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, and 12) [15] and calcipotriol cream, applied once daily 30 min after clobetasol. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects. This retrospective study was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of our hospital, and conducted in accordance with the relevant guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. All methods followed ethical principles for medical research involving human subjects. Data confidentiality was maintained, with personal identifiers removed prior to analysis to ensure participant privacy.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: Elderly patients aged 65 years and above with a diagnosis of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis according to established clinical criteria, who are willing and able to provide informed consent for participation in the study and can comply with the study protocol and follow-up visits. Exclusion Criteria: Patients with a history of hypersensitivity or allergic reactions to secukinumab, calcipotriol, or related compounds, or those with uncontrolled or severe systemic diseases such as uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, severe cardiovascular diseases, or active malignancies, are excluded from the study. Additionally, individuals who have received systemic immunosuppressive therapies such as corticosteroids, methotrexate, or cyclosporine within the last 6 months, or those with active infections including hepatitis B or C, tuberculosis, or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), are also ineligible for participation.

The primary efficacy endpoints were assessed based on the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score. Clinical outcomes were categorized as follows:

Significant Improvement: PASI score reduction of 60% to 89%.

Effective: PASI score reduction of 30% to 59%.

The combined efficacy rate, comprising the sum of effective, significantly improved, and clinically remitted cases, was calculated to evaluate overall treatment efficacy. All primary efficacy endpoints were evaluated at baseline and at Week 12 following completion of the treatment protocol.

Secondary efficacy parameters

Assessment of Clinical Parameters: PASI scoring comprised four components: upper limbs, head, lower limbs, and trunk, with scoring based on the severity of lesions (scaling, erythema, and induration) and the percentage of affected body surface area. The weighted sum of individual scores from each body region yielded the total PASI score, ranging from 0 to 72, indicative of disease severity.

All secondary efficacy parameters were measured at two time points: prior to treatment initiation and at the end of the 12-week treatment period.

Follow-up duration

As this was a retrospective study, follow-up data were extracted from the institutional electronic medical records system. All included patients completed a 12-week treatment period and were followed up accordingly. Clinical assessments including PASI scores, DLQI, stratum corneum hydration, sebum content, and adverse event monitoring, were documented at baseline and at week 12, coinciding with the final dose of secukinumab for the observation group (administered at weeks 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, and 12). The calcipotriol cream in the observation group was applied 30 min after topical clobetasol propionate, in line with standard clinical practice. Follow-up evaluations were performed by dermatologists and recorded in the patient records as part of routine care. Only patients with complete and time-aligned follow-up documentation were included in the final analysis. No patients were lost to follow-up during the 12-week treatment window.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0. For categorical data, percentages were used for description, and the chi-square test was employed. For continuous variables, Bartlett’s test for homogeneity of variances and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for normality were conducted to ensure data met the assumptions of equal variances and approximate normal distribution, respectively. Descriptive statistics were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). Independent sample t-test was utilized for between-group comparisons, while paired sample t-test was employed for within-group comparisons. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics between observation and control groups

The baseline characteristics of the observation group (n = 80) and the control group (n = 80) are presented in Table 1. Gender distribution was comparable, with 62.5% males in the observation group and 70% males in the control group (P = 0.403). The mean age was similar between groups (70.92 ± 6.21 vs. 71.19 ± 6.01 years, P = 0.780). The mean body mass index (BMI) was 24.32 ± 1.24 kg/m² in the observation group and 23.71 ± 1.58 kg/m² in the control group, with a statistically significant difference (P = 0.007). The PASI scores did not differ significantly between groups (33.15 ± 5.77 vs. 33.80 ± 5.99, P = 0.486). The DLQI score was 17.89 ± 2.86 in the observation group and 18.95 ± 3.28 in the control group, with a statistically significant difference (P = 0.031). The mean disease duration was 17.10 ± 3.30 months in the observation group and 17.85 ± 3.55 months in the control group (P = 0.168). Regarding lesion characteristics, 35% of patients in the observation group and 40% in the control group had red lesions, while 65% and 60% had pink lesions, respectively (P = 0.624). The mean lesion area was 26.39 ± 5.00% in the observation group and 25.80 ± 3.85% in the control group (P = 0.404). Itch severity distribution was as follows: mild itching was reported in 30% of the observation group and 20% of the control group, moderate itching in 45% and 55%, and severe itching in 25% and 25%, respectively (P = 0.301).

Comparison of therapeutic outcomes between observation and control groups

Clinical remission was achieved in 28 patients (35%) in the observation group and 20 patients (25%) in the control group. Comparison of PASI scores following treatment between observation and control groups

PASI scores before and after treatment are presented in Table 3. In the observation group, the mean PASI score decreased from 33.15 ± 5.77 at baseline to 16.12 ± 3.13 after treatment, with a statistically significant reduction (t = 23.205, P < 0.01). In the control group, the mean PASI score decreased from 33.80 ± 5.99 to 18.98 ± 3.92, also showing a statistically significant reduction (t = 18.517, P < 0.01). Between-group comparison showed no significant difference in baseline PASI scores (t = 0.699, P = 0.486). However, post-treatment scores were significantly lower in the observation group compared with the control group (t = 5.099, P < 0.001).

Comparison of adverse reactions between observation and control groups

As shown in Table 7, the overall incidence of adverse reactions was lower in the observation group (10.0%) compared to the control group (17.5%), although this difference did not reach statistical significance (χ² = 1.897, P = 0.168). Gastrointestinal reactions were the most frequently reported adverse events in both groups, occurring in 7.5% of patients in the observation group and 10.0% in the control group. Cases of blood lipid abnormalities were recorded in 2.5% of the observation group and 5.0% of the control group. Skin itching was reported only in the control group (2.5%), while no instances of elevated transaminase levels were observed in either group. Although numerical differences were noted across several categories, none were statistically significant. These findings suggest that both treatment regimens were generally well tolerated, and the addition of secukinumab and calcipotriol did not lead to an increased incidence of adverse events.

Post Hoc power analysis

A post hoc power analysis was performed based on four key outcome measures: PASI scores (post-treatment), DLQI scores (post-treatment), sebum content (post-treatment), and overall therapeutic efficacy rate. Using effect size estimates (Cohen’s d or h) and corresponding group means or proportions, the calculated statistical powers were approximately 99.9%, 100%, 99.6%, and 83.0%, respectively. These results confirm that the study was sufficiently powered (≥ 80%) to detect meaningful differences across all key clinical outcomes.

Discussion

Although secukinumab has demonstrated high PASI 90/100 response rates as monotherapy in randomized controlled trials, clinical practice in patients with moderate to severe psoriasis, particularly in elderly individuals, often involves combination therapeutic strategies to optimize early disease control, enhance skin barrier recovery, and reduce the risk of disease instability [17–20]. Acitretin has been reported to exert synergistic effects when combined with biologic therapies, especially in patients with extensive hyperkeratotic lesions, palmoplantar involvement, or metabolic comorbidities, and may contribute to improved long-term disease maintenance [21–25]. In addition, topical corticosteroids and vitamin D3 analogs (e.g., calcipotriol) are frequently used as adjunctive therapies during the early phase of biologic treatment. Their concomitant use has been supported by multiple clinical studies and expert consensus reports, particularly for patients with residual or refractory localized plaques, and may facilitate faster clinical response and improved skin comfort [26–29]. In this context, the present study provides novel evidence by systematically evaluating a comprehensive combination regimen in an elderly population, integrating clinical efficacy, skin barrier function, and quality-of-life outcomes. Our findings demonstrate that the full combination regimen was associated with greater improvements in disease severity, skin barrier parameters, and quality of life compared with conventional treatment alone, without an increased incidence of adverse events, supporting the potential clinical value of combination therapeutic strategies in moderate to severe psoriasis. From a clinical perspective, these findings have important implications for the management of moderate to severe psoriasis in elderly patients. This population often presents with long-standing disease, impaired skin barrier function, and multiple comorbidities, which may limit the effectiveness of monotherapy and complicate treatment selection. The demonstrated benefits of the combination regimen suggest that a step-up, multimodal therapeutic approach may offer more comprehensive disease control by simultaneously addressing systemic inflammation, epidermal dysregulation, and patient-reported quality-of-life impairment. Moreover, the absence of an increased incidence of adverse events in the combination group supports the feasibility and tolerability of this strategy in routine clinical settings. By improving both objective disease severity and subjective patient outcomes without compromising safety, this combination approach may help clinicians achieve more stable disease control, enhance treatment satisfaction, and potentially improve adherence in elderly patients with moderate to severe psoriasis.

Baseline characteristics were largely comparable between the observation and control groups, with no significant differences in age, sex distribution, disease duration, baseline PASI scores, lesion characteristics, or itch severity, supporting the overall comparability of the two cohorts. Although statistically significant differences were observed in BMI and baseline DLQI scores, these differences were modest in magnitude and unlikely to fully account for the pronounced post-treatment differences observed across multiple outcome domains. Nevertheless, these imbalances should be considered when interpreting the results. With respect to overall therapeutic response, the observation group achieved a significantly higher total efficacy rate than the control group (90.0% vs. 72.5%), driven primarily by a lower proportion of ineffective responses. This finding suggests that the addition of secukinumab and calcipotriol to conventional therapy may enhance the likelihood of achieving clinically meaningful improvement in elderly patients with moderate to severe psoriasis [30–32]. Consistent with this observation, PASI scores declined significantly in both groups following treatment, reflecting the effectiveness of systemic and topical therapy in reducing disease activity [15, 33]. Importantly, the magnitude of PASI reduction was significantly greater in the observation group, indicating superior control of cutaneous inflammation and lesion severity with the combination regimen. This between-group difference in post-treatment PASI scores supports the added benefit of biologic escalation beyond conventional therapy alone.

Beyond disease severity, improvements in skin barrier–related parameters further distinguished the two treatment strategies. Both stratum corneum hydration and sebum content increased significantly after treatment in both groups; however, post-treatment values were consistently higher in the observation group. These findings suggest that the combination regimen may confer additional benefits in restoring epidermal barrier function, which is a clinically relevant but often underappreciated dimension of psoriasis management, particularly in elderly patients with xerosis and barrier impairment. Quality-of-life outcomes mirrored the objective clinical improvements [34, 35]. DLQI scores decreased significantly in both groups, indicating meaningful symptomatic and functional improvement. Despite a slightly higher baseline DLQI score in the control group, post-treatment DLQI values were substantially lower in the observation group, suggesting that the greater improvements in disease severity and skin barrier function translated into superior patient-reported outcomes. From a safety perspective, both treatment regimens were generally well tolerated over the 12-week period. Although the overall incidence of adverse events was numerically lower in the observation group, no statistically significant differences were observed between groups. The absence of increased adverse reactions with the addition of secukinumab and calcipotriol supports the short-term safety and feasibility of this combination approach in an elderly population [36, 37].

The enhanced efficacy observed with the combination of secukinumab, acitretin, topical corticosteroids, and calcipotriol may be attributed to their complementary mechanisms of action targeting multiple pathogenic pathways of psoriasis [14, 38, 39]. Secukinumab provides potent systemic anti-inflammatory effects through selective inhibition of IL-17 A, resulting in rapid suppression of immune-mediated inflammation [38]. However, epidermal hyperproliferation, abnormal differentiation, and skin barrier dysfunction may persist despite effective cytokine blockade, particularly in patients with long-standing or extensive disease [14, 39]. Acitretin plays a key role in normalizing keratinocyte proliferation and differentiation, thereby addressing epidermal abnormalities that may not be fully controlled by immunomodulation alone [40]. Concurrently, topical clobetasol propionate provides rapid local anti-inflammatory effects, while calcipotriol promotes keratinocyte differentiation and barrier restoration [41]. Together, these complementary actions support a more comprehensive therapeutic approach integrating systemic immune control with local epidermal repair.

Building upon these foundational studies, our research advances the understanding of combination therapy in psoriasis by not only confirming the efficacy of secukinumab with acitretin but also providing novel evidence on its application in elderly patients. Ekinci et al. [13]reported a small case series demonstrating high efficacy and favorable safety of secukinumab combined with low-dose acitretin in three multi-drug-refractory psoriasis patients with severe disease phenotypes. Their findings support the feasibility of biologic–retinoid combination therapy in difficult-to-treat cases. Consistent with this observation, our study confirms the benefit of secukinumab-based combination therapy; however, it extends these findings by providing controlled, statistically robust evidence in a larger elderly cohort, incorporating standardized efficacy endpoints, quality-of-life assessment, and objective skin barrier measurements. Xu et al. [42] summarized existing evidence and expert recommendations for palmoplantar pustulosis, highlighting the frequent use of acitretin and topical corticosteroids and the potential role of biologics, including secukinumab, particularly in refractory cases. However, their conclusions were limited by heterogeneous outcomes and a lack of high-quality clinical data. Our findings are concordant with their conceptual support for combination strategies, while advancing the field by providing original clinical data demonstrating that a structured biologic-based combination regimen yields measurable improvements in disease severity, skin barrier function, and patient-reported outcomes. Bonomo et al. [43] analyzed baseline characteristics of patients receiving combination systemic therapy within a large psoriasis registry, focusing on demographic and clinical factors associated with treatment selection rather than therapeutic outcomes. While their study contextualized real-world use of combination regimens, it did not evaluate efficacy, quality of life, or skin barrier recovery. In contrast, our study directly addresses this unmet need by systematically assessing clinical effectiveness, tolerability, and patient-centered outcomes of a biologic-based combination regimen in an elderly population.

Limitations and future directions

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. As a retrospective study, it is inherently subject to selection bias and residual confounding, which cannot be fully eliminated despite strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. Treatment allocation was not randomized but based on shared decision-making between physicians and patients, potentially affecting group comparability. In addition, reliance on routinely collected clinical records may introduce variability in data completeness and accuracy. The relatively short follow-up period precluded assessment of long-term efficacy and durability of treatment response. Importantly, the absence of biochemical and immunological marker data represents an inherent limitation, as mechanistic interpretations were based on established biological pathways and prior evidence rather than direct immune or inflammatory measurements from the study cohort. Moreover, only a single combination regimen was evaluated, and alternative therapeutic strategies were not explored. To address these limitations, future research should employ prospective, multicenter randomized controlled designs with longer follow-up, incorporate objective immunological and inflammatory biomarkers, and compare different combination strategies to provide more robust mechanistic insight and enhance the generalizability of the findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, secukinumab combined with calcipotriol cream was associated with superior clinical outcomes, including greater PASI reduction, improved skin barrier function, and enhanced quality of life, compared with conventional therapy alone in elderly patients with moderate to severe psoriasis. Despite modest baseline differences in BMI and DLQI, the overall therapeutic response consistently favored the combination regimen. Adverse events were infrequent and comparable between groups, indicating good tolerability and short-term safety. These findings support the clinical utility of this combination approach, although prospective multicenter studies with longer follow-up and biomarker-based mechanistic evaluation are needed to further validate efficacy and safety.

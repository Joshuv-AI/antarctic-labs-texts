Regret Bound for Multi-Armed Bandits with Discounting: A Robust Stochastic Control Approach
Authors: Zhou Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe investigate the exact regret bound for stochastic multi-armed bandit problems with discounting, by formulating the regret bound as the value of a robust stochastic control problem. We first show that the regret bound coincides with the value associated with the Bayesian optimization problem under the worst prior. However, a solution to this Bayesian optimization may not be an optimal strategy for the robust control problem. To resolve this dilemma, we introduce an entropy regularization term into the cost function. In this way, we prove the existence of a saddle point for the entropy-regularized robust control. Moreover, the unique optimal strategy for the Bayesian optimization under the worst prior is also an (almost) optimal strategy for the original robust stochastic control problem.Keywords: Multi-armed bandits with discounting, Regret bound, Robust stochastic control, Bayesian optimization, Entropy regularization

Introduction

In a basic multi-armed bandit (MAB) problem, an agent selects an arm among K arms (i.e., make a decision from K options) at each time. The agent receives the reward of the selected arm, but cannot observe the outcome of other arms. The distribution of each arm is unknown to the agent, and the agent could learn the distribution by observing the outcomes of selected arms. The agent aims to find a best strategy to maximize the cumulative reward or minimize the regret, and thus face the tradeoff between exploration and exploitation. MAB problems have been investigated extensively in many variations in the literature, and have a lot of applications in different fields. We refer to the book [1] and the references therein.

A popular research direction for MAB is to design certain algorithms and analyse their regret bound asymptotically in terms of the number of rounds T and the number of arms K. The regret bound (e.g., O(KTln(T))) typically is only in the asymptotic sense, and it is not clear what would be the exact bound (e.g., not clear what is the constant term in O(KTln(T), and it could be very large). The same applies to the lower bound in the worst case scenario (e.g., O(KT)) for the regret. In addition, such analysis for the regret bound cannot be readily applied to the case when the MAB problem admits discounting.

Our result also provides a mathematical justification, from a robust control point of view, for the involvement of entropy regularization in reinforcement learning and stochastic control (see e.g., [2], [3] and the references therein).

We apply the minimax theorem to show (1) and Kakutani-Fan’s fixed-point theorem to prove the existence of a saddle point. A technical difficulty for the proof of (1) lies in the nonconvexity of the map π↦V(q,π) (see Remark 3.1 and Example 3.1). We work on the law of (At)t conditioned on the path of (Xt)t, instead of π, in order to recover the convexity (linearity). Another challenge, specifically for the proof of Theorem 2.2, is the noncompactness of the relaxed control set. To overcome this difficulty, we establish the uniform continuity of optimal relaxed control strategies under certain assumptions for models θ∈Θ. This enables us to apply the Arzela-Ascoli theorem and focus on a compact subset of the control set. A key step in the proof of our results is to show the continuity of the map (q,π)↦Vη,λ(q,π), which is not straightforward.

Our results contribute to the literature of reinforcement learning including the analysis of regret bound for MAB problems. In most cases regret bounds obtained in the literature are in the asymptotic sense, and cannot deal with the situation when discounting is presented. Here we consider the exact bound under the case with discounting using a stochastic control approach. We provide a method to compute the exact bound and obtain an (almost) optimal strategy, by converting the robust control problem to a Bayesian optimization that can be solved using the dynamic programming principle. Admittedly, when the set of models Θ is infinite, the dynamic programming equation for V˜(q) (and V˜η,λ(q)), q∈P(Θ), would be infinite dimensional, and how to numerically solve such equation remains to be investigated. We leave it for future research.

Our paper also contributes to the research of stochastic control. There have been a lot of works on stochastic control under model uncertainty, see [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], to name a few. In these papers, an underlying model can be time-varying and the set of models is closed under pasting. Consequently, (up to some technicalities) the dynamic programming principle can be directly applied. In our case, since each model θ is static and thus the set of models over multiple time periods is not closed under pasting. As a result, dynamic programming cannot be directly applied. Such difficulty is overcome with the equivalence between the robust control and Bayesian optimization, as is shown in the paper. Our results also indicate that involving an entropy term is helpful in finding an optimal control strategy. This provides a justification, from a robust control point of view, for the entropy regularization in many stochastic control and optimal stopping problems considered these days (see [2], [3], [9], [10], [11], among others). Let us also mention the works [12], [13], [14] where a different framework, called adaptive robust control, is used to deal with model uncertainty.

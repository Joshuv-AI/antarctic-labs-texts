lnfraspinatus-teres minor interfascial block combined with periarticular infiltration versus periarticular infiltration analgesia alone for postoperative analgesia in patients undergoing arthroscopic shoulder surgery: protocol for a randomized controlled clinical trial
Authors: Xueyang Li, Xiao Huang, Danxu Ma, Lina Yang, Dandan Lin, Yuan Sun, Anshi Wu, Huilong Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough interscalene nerve block is considered as the standard analgesic method for arthroscopic shoulder surgery, it is not indicated for patients with respiratory comorbidity. Periarticular infiltration is usually used for postoperative analgesia following orthopedic surgery, with uncertain analgesic effects. Infraspinatus-teres minor interfascial (ITM) block is an innovative method used in arthroscopic shoulder surgery. We supposed that ITM block combined with periarticular infiltration could provide adequate analgesia for patients undergoing arthroscopic shoulder surgery.MethodsThis is a randomized, parallel-controlled, prospective, single-center study. Fifty-six patients scheduled for arthroscopic shoulder surgery will be randomized into two groups: ITM block combined with periarticular infiltration group (ITM group) and periarticular infiltration group (PAI group). Periarticular infiltration will be administered to all patients following surgery. The primary outcome is the time from the end of surgery to the first press of the patient-controlled analgesia pump (PCA) in the two groups. Secondary outcomes are the following: intraoperative remifentanil and propofol consumption, hemodynamic changes, VAS scores, frequency of PCA compression, remedy analgesic consumption, length of hospital stay, patients satisfaction score, and adverse reactions.DiscussionThis study will demonstrate the analgesic efficacy of ITM block combined with periarticular infiltration in arthroscopic shoulder surgery.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/) ChiCTR2400088821. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Pain after arthroscopic shoulder surgery remains to be a challenge [1, 2]. The interscalene nerve block (ISB) is a standardized and reliable method of analgesia in arthroscopy shoulder surgery [3, 4]. However, ipsilateral phrenic nerve block following ISB is not a good choice for patients with poor respiratory function due to its negative effects, and the ISB also has other side effects such as nerve damage and rebound pain [5–8]. As a legitimate substitute for ISB, combined suprascapular and axillary nerve block can lower the prevalence of diaphragmatic dysfunction [9, 10]. This technique does not accommodate catheterization with continuous obstruction, as it must be performed in two positions. Periarticular infiltration (PAI) is a popular technique in multimodal analgesia regimens for arthroscopic shoulder surgery [11]. Transarticular periarticular infiltration analgesia is safe but has limited analgesic effect and a short duration of action. In 2024, Kim et al. introduced a unique method combining suprascapular and axillary nerve blocks called infraspinatus-teres minor interfascial (ITM) block. This method showed that injection in the contact plane of the fascial interface between the IS and the Tm leads to drug diffusion into the axillary and suprascapular nerves. In both cadavers and case reports, Kim et al. demonstrated the role of ITM block for patients with acute postoperative pain and persistent shoulder discomfort [12].

To our best knowledge, no studies have examined the analgesic efficacy of ITM block combined with PAI in arthroscopic shoulder surgery. Interest in identifying alternative modalities of analgesia for patients who are not candidates for ISB analgesia in shoulder surgery is increasing. We hypothesize that ITM block combined with PAI would provide better analgesia compared with PAI alone in arthroscopic shoulder surgery.

Objectives {7}

The ITM block permits diffusion of injections to the suprascapular and axillary nerves; therefore, we hypothesized that the ITM block combined with PAI may be an effective tool to reduce pain intensity in patients undergoing arthroscopy shoulder surgery.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Patients only in the ITM group will receive 25 ml 0.4% ropivacaine as ITM block. The risks of ropivacaine infusion and systemic toxicity are communicated to patients during the informed consent interview.

Intervention description {11a}

The ITM block will be administered according to the methods described by Kim et al. with patients in lateral positions before induction of anesthesia. The skin is prepared using an antiseptic solution, and the transducer will be wrapped in a sterile cover. We will tilt the 6–13-MHz high-frequency linear probe at an angle so that it is perpendicular to the IS and Tm directions to obtain a transverse scan of the ITM block muscle. The IS and Tm tendon unit can be recognized under the corner after palpation of the posterior external corner of the acromion. By moving the probe mediocaudally, the interfacial structure of the IS and Tm can be traced along the glenohumeral joint, and scapular neck area to the mid-lateral border of the scapular region. The interfascial plane between IS and Tm on the posterior surface area of the neck of the scapula is located between the spinoglenoid notch and quadrilateral space and is the optimal target site for ITM block. The puncture target is located approximately 3 cm medial to the posterior lateral angle of the acromion of the scapula. The probe will be inserted laterally between the IS and TM using an in-plane approach technique, and 25 ml of solution will be injected after a bloodless retraction (Fig. 2).

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

An independent investigator performs the pre-surgical assessment. The investigator will strictly control the exclusion and inclusion criteria. In addition, randomization of this trial will be performed by another investigator to ensure impartiality and minimize the potential for bias.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Patients will receive a standard monitoring system after arriving in the operating room, including blood pressure (BP), electrocardiogram (ECG), heart rate (HR), pulse oximetry saturation (SpO2), and bispectral index (BIS). Radial artery puncture will be performed under local anesthesia to monitor invasive arterial blood pressure. Ultrasound-guided ITM block will be performed in a lateral position before general anesthesia induction. General anesthesia will be induced with sufentanil 0.5 ug/kg, propofol 2.5 mg/kg, and rocuronium 0.8 mg/kg. After induction, the trachea will be intubated and mechanical ventilation is initiated in a volume-controlled mode with a tidal volume set at 6–8 ml/kg, and the respiratory rate will be set at 12–16 breaths per minute. Proper anesthesia depth will be maintained by micropump infusion of propofol (4–8 mg kg−1 h−1) and remifentanil (0.1–0.3 μg kg−1 min−1) keeping BIS values between 40 and 60. The anesthetic dose will be adjusted according to the intraoperative monitoring and the duration of the surgery, maintaining hemodynamic parameters within 20% of baseline values. At the end of the surgical procedure, all the patients will be injected with 20 ml of 0.5% ropivacaine into the periarticular area by the surgeon. Intravenous patient-controlled analgesia pump (PCA) will be performed in both groups immediately after patients wake at the end of surgery until 48 h. PCA, sufentanil 2 ug/kg + 0.9% saline to 100 ml, with a lockout interval of 15 min, a background infusion of 1 ml/h, followed by the self-administered additional dose of 2 ml. If the VAS score is higher than 4, 50 mg of flurbiprofen will be injected intravenously as a rescue analgesic.

Participant timeline {13}

For included participants, enrolment will be performed 1 day before surgery. Then, random allocation will be performed by a specific statistician before intervention. Patients will be included from September 1, 2024, to March 31, 2025.

Sample size {14}

The minimal sample size was obtained based on a preliminary study with 10 patients, which indicated that the time from the end of surgery to the first PCA pressed in group ITM and group PAI was 5 and 3.5 h respectively, with a standard deviation of 1.8. Considering the power of 0.8, alpha of 0.05, and 10% loss in follow-up, we planned to recruit at least 28 patients per group.

Recruitment {15}

Our hospital has extensive clinical experience in arthroscopic shoulder surgery. A sufficient source of patients is available to ensure the completion of the recruitment.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

This is a randomized, controlled trial. The random allocation sequence will be conducted using a professional online tool for randomized clinical trials called “Randomizer” (https://www.randomizer.at/, Randomizer Version 2.1.0, Institute for Medical Informatics, Statistics and Documentation, Medical University of Graz).

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomized information will be concealed in a sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelope in the custody of a secretary who will be no longer involved in the trial. Randomization will take place after the participants’ final confirmation of enrolment.

Implementation {16c}

An independent investigator without any other role in this study will perform the randomization.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Followers and data collators will be blinded to the experimental groupings.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

To ensure prompt access to participants’ treatment information in the event of an emergency, a 24-h unblinding phone number will be provided. The principal investigator will assess the need for open blindness. If needed, they can quickly perform emergency unblinding through the unblinding phone number. Once unblinding occurs, the research team leader and ethics committee must be notified as soon as possible to ensure that all relevant researchers understand what happened and can take appropriate action accordingly. The time, cause, and result of unblinding must be documented in the source document to ensure the integrity and traceability of the data for use in subsequent analysis, audits, or reviews.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

The data will be collected in the electronic medical record system or case report forms (CRFs) to ensure data consistency and completeness. A detailed operations manual or training guide will be developed for each investigator to ensure accuracy and consistency in data collection. Database permissions are strictly set to ensure that only authorized personnel can access and use the data for research purposes only. Appropriate statistical methods will be selected for analysis and predetermined in the study design. Validation and review of preliminary results will be conducted to ensure reliability and accuracy.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Researchers should provide clear and accurate information so that participants can fully understand the purpose, process, possible risks, and benefits of the trial. Follow-up will take place in a relatively quiet environment, which will help participants relax and better communicate with the researcher. Participants will be informed of possible adverse reactions. Participants have the right to withdraw from the study at any time for any reason. The reason for withdrawal will be recorded in the original document and the Ethics Committee will be notified.

Data management {19}

Preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative follow-up data is gathered and stored properly as study material. All collected data will be enrolled in a CRF, designed for each included patient. All data will be input into the electronic case report file and any traces of entries, modifications, deletions, etc., will be kept in a log showing who made the changes and when. Paper-based data will be entered into the electronic case report file by two research assistants using duplicate entries. Electronic data will be copied. The researcher double-checks the data against the CRF to confirm the quality of the data. A particular biostatistician finishes the screening and randomization of the study data.

Confidentiality {27}

Each participant’s data will be assigned a unique identification number and coded for processing. These data will be securely stored in cabinets with physical safeguards to prevent unauthorized access. We will be able to strictly protect participant privacy and data confidentiality throughout the trial.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

No biological samples will be collected in this trial.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Statistical analysis will be conducted using SPSS V.25 statistical software (IBM, Armonk, NY). Data distribution will be assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed continuous variables will be shown as mean (SD), and those without normally distributed data will be performed as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Categorical variables will be shown as numbers (percentages). Between-group differences will be assessed by performing the independent Student’s t-test for continuous normal variables, while non-parametric data will be compared by the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. A chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test will be conducted for categorical variables. Repeated-measures analysis of variance will be used within groups for data with normal distribution. Any changes to the statistical analysis program will be noted in the next publications. P < 0.05 will be regarded as significant.

Interim analyses {21b}

No interim analyses are planned, as this is a low-risk intervention trial. Accordingly, no formal stopping rules have been specified. The trial will only be stopped early in case of unexpected safety concerns, upon the recommendation of the principal investigator and the ethics committee.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Additional analyses are not planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

This is an intention-to-treat study. To ensure that more participants adhere to the protocol, we will explain the intervention in detail and emphasize matters of collaboration with the participant during the pre-anesthetic evaluation and obtaining informed consent. For missing data in intention-to-treat analyses, established guidelines or protocols need to be followed to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The following describes the composition and roles of the coordinating center, trial steering committee, and other personnel responsible for day-to-day trial management. The trial’s daily operations are managed by a dedicated coordinating centre, which is responsible for overseeing data management, implementing randomization, ensuring quality control, and monitoring adherence to the study protocol across all participating sites. A trial steering committee (TSC), composed of the principal investigator and two co-investigators, provides overall scientific and strategic oversight, monitors trial progress, reviews safety and ethical considerations, and reports any serious incidents to the Ethics Committee. The TSC meets monthly to ensure the trial is conducted according to protocol. Research nurses are responsible for participant recruitment, obtaining informed consent, supporting intervention delivery, monitoring participant safety, and collecting study data. Trial coordinators facilitate communication between the coordinating center, research nurses, and clinical sites, organize meetings, track study milestones, and address operational issues to ensure smooth trial execution. All personnel involved in trial management operate independently of the study’s outcome assessment to preserve trial integrity. There is no stakeholder or private sector involvement in trial management.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

We will not set up the data monitoring committee. The principal investigator is responsible for the project implementation, and management, and for overseeing the data collection.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Safety monitoring is always followed throughout the study. All adverse events associated with the study interventions will be documented in the study database and reported to the Adverse Event Registry System of Beijing Chao-Yang Hospital, Capital Medical University, as required within a specified time frame based on severity. The Clinical Trial Oversight Committee of Beijing Chao-Yang Hospital, Capital Medical University, is responsible for deciding whether to stop this study on a case-by-case basis.

Clinically relevant adverse events associated with treatment will be taken into account: infection, pneumothorax, and local anesthetic toxicity over the trial period. If an adverse event occurs, the researcher should explain it to the participant and require the participant to truthfully report any changes in his/her condition after treatment. For adverse events occurring during the study, the symptoms, extent, duration, treatment, and experience should be recorded on the CRF. If a serious adverse event occurs in the study, the project leader must adopt immediate actions to ensure the safety of the subjects. The investigator should also inform the Ethics Committee, the trial steering committee, and the relevant regulatory bodies of the expectedness, severity, gravity, and causality, as necessary. When a severe adverse incident happens in a clinical study that warrants urgent unblinding, it should be unblinded by the project leader, the investigator, and the clinical supervisor.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

An independent staff member who is not involved in the study served as an independent reviewer for the duration of the trial. This staff member will have expertise in data auditing and will conduct an independent review of the data every 2 weeks, including a review of CRF content, missing data, duplicate data, incorrect data, and informed consent documents. All errors will be documented and reported to the principal investigator. Monthly meeting will be convened by the project management team to review trial conduct, recruitment status, and data quality issues. The TSC and Ethics Committee meet to review conduct throughout the trial period. Since the intervention in this study is considered low risk, a data monitoring committee was not established.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Any proposed changes to the protocol will first be reviewed and approved by the principal investigator, and then submitted to the Ethics Committee for approval. Following approval, the sponsor and funder will be notified. The principal investigator will subsequently inform all participating centers, and a copy of the revised protocol will be provided to each site for inclusion in the Investigator Site File. Any deviations from the approved protocol will be fully documented using a breach report form. Discussion

Finding potential alternative techniques that provide effective pain relief with minimal side effects in arthroscopic shoulder surgery is needed. Local anesthetic injected into the joint space and surrounding structures in PAI analgesia has been shown to reduce shoulder pain and opioid consumption [13, 14]. The mechanism of PAI analgesia in orthopedic joint surgery is thought to be related to the direct blockade of the abundant free nerve endings and mechanoreceptors on the surface of the glenohumeral joint capsule [15]. However, it is less effective compared with other regional anesthetic techniques.

ITM block provides anesthesia for pain in the shoulder region. In ITM block, local anesthetic is injected into the interfascial plane between IS and Tm. The main mechanism of action of the ITM block was demonstrated to be the blockade of both the suprascapular nerves on the IS fossa and axillary nerves at the quadrilateral space [12].

In our center, the ultrasound-guided technique for ITM block is usually carried out by experienced anesthesiologists to reduce the underlying risks as much as possible. In addition, the PAI is carried out by the same experienced surgeon to minimize confounding factors. Therefore, we aim to examine whether ITM block could be an adequate choice in arthroscopic shoulder surgery. To our knowledge, this is the first randomized controlled trial to confirm whether ITM block combined with PAI provides sufficient and effective effects in arthroscopic shoulder surgery. The expected finding will provide clinical evidence to affirm the effect of ITM block and promote its application in arthroscopic shoulder surgery.

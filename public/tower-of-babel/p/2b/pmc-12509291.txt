Exploring a potential novel marker for short-term mortality in decompensated cirrhosis: insights from a multicentre study
Authors: Jin Cheng, Honglei Ju, Guixiang Wang, Chiyi He, Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe derived monocyte to lymphocyte ratio (dMLR) serves as an indicator of the overall systemic inflammatory and immune status. This study seeks to investigate the correlation between dMLR and 90-day mortality in patients diagnosed with decompensated liver cirrhosis (DLC).MethodsA total of 1,342 eligible patients from three medical centres were included in this study. Multivariable Firth-corrected logistic regression analysis was conducted to explore the independent association between dMLR and 90-day mortality. Additionally, restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was employed to examine the relationship between dMLR and mortality risk. These findings were subsequently validated in two independent cohorts. In the total cohort, the prognostic accuracy of dMLR for predicting outcomes in DLC patients was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ResultsIn the initial cohort, the observed 90-day mortality rate was 12.5%. ROC analysis showed that dMLR had comparable predictive ability to the Model for End-Stage Liver Disease. The multivariable Firth-corrected logistic regression analysis revealed that dMLR is an independent predictor of 90-day mortality. Furthermore, RCS analysis demonstrated that an increase in dMLR values corresponded with an elevated risk of mortality. These findings were corroborated in two validation cohorts.ConclusionsThe dMLR is independently associated with 90-day mortality in patients with DLC, with an increasing risk of death corresponding to higher dMLR values. dMLR may function as a simple, cost-effective, and reliable biomarker, assisting clinicians in identifying high-risk patients and may guide risk stratification and management.Keywords: Decompensated liver cirrhosis, derived monocyte to lymphocyte ratio, inflammation, immunity, mortality

Introduction

Patients diagnosed with decompensated liver cirrhosis (DLC) experience a considerable disease burden, which encompasses frequent hospital admissions, a significant deterioration in quality of life, and elevated mortality rates [1,2]. Previous studies have indicated that the short-term mortality rate associated with DLC varies between 7% and 20% [3–5]. The advancement of liver cirrhosis (LC) is further complicated by the concurrent presence and interconnected damage caused by systemic inflammation and immune deficiency, both of which are believed to be closely linked to the prognosis of DLC [6]. As the disease progresses to the decompensated stage, excessive systemic compensatory anti-inflammatory responses, coupled with the depletion and dysregulation of immune effector cells, contribute to multi-organ failure, ultimately leading to patient mortality [7].

The derived monocyte to lymphocyte ratio (dMLR) serves as an indicator of the systemic inflammatory and immune status of patients. This ratio was initially introduced by Tong et al. as a serum marker for predicting lymph node metastasis in patients with gastric signet ring cell carcinoma [8]. Given that DLC is characterized by systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation, the relationship between dMLR and patient prognosis remains unclear. Therefore, this study aims to explore the correlation between dMLR and short-term mortality in patients with DLC.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective multicentre study encompassed patients from three hospitals in China. The initial cohort comprised patients diagnosed with DLC who were admitted to Maanshan People’s Hospital between March 2019 and June 2024. Patients were excluded if they met any of the following conditions: (1) previous hospitalization due to DLC, (2) history of malignancy, (3) autoimmune disorders, (4) primary renal disease, (5) severe cardiac or cerebrovascular comorbidities, (6) acute infectious diseases, (7) missing essential clinical information, or (8) missing essential clinical information. Following these exclusion criteria, additional DLC patients were selected from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Wannan Medical College (March 2016 to June 2024) and Yijishan Hospital of Wannan Medical College (January 2018 to June 2024), designated as Validation Cohort 1 and Validation Cohort 2, respectively. Baseline demographic and clinical information were collected from electronic medical records, including aetiology of cirrhosis, age, sex, diabetes mellitus (no/yes), hypertension (no/yes), and the presence or absence of complications (variceal bleeding, hepatorenal syndrome, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis). Laboratory parameters included complete blood count, liver function, renal function, and coagulation function. The outcome was 90-day all-cause mortality, ascertained through a review of medical records or direct communication with patients or their relatives.

Definitions

DLC refers to a stage of liver cirrhosis characterized by the development of complications such as ascites, oesophageal variceal bleeding, and hepatic encephalopathy [1,9]. The dMLR is calculated based on previous study [8], using the following formula: monocyte count (×10^9/L)/(white blood cell count (×10^9/L) - neutrophil count (×10^9/L))*100. Additionally, the Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) score was calculated as previously described [10].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables exhibiting a normal distribution are reported as means ± standard deviation, with differences between two groups analysed using independent sample t-tests. For continuous variables that follow a non-normal distribution, data are reported as medians (interquartile range), and group differences are assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies (percentages), with differences between groups are evaluated using the chi-square test. Univariable Firth-corrected logistic regression analysis was used to assess the association between each variable and the outcome, and variables with a p-value less than 0.05 were included in the multivariable analysis. Subsequently, multivariable Firth-corrected logistic regression analysis was performed to determine the independent association between dMLR and outcome. Firth correction is a penalized likelihood method that improves the estimation of logistic regression models by reducing bias, particularly when dealing with small event counts [11,12]. The Brier score is used to determine the level of agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. Additionally, restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was utilized to elucidate the relationship between dMLR (continuous variable) and the risk of outcome based on the multivariate analysis. The findings were further validated in two independent validation cohorts. In the total cohort, the predictive accuracy of dMLR for outcome was examined through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and Youden’s index was used to determine the optimal cut-off value of the dMLR; furthermore, a nomogram was developed by incorporating dMLR and MELD score. Delong test was used to compare the AUC values. All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.2) and MedCalc (version 15.2). All statistical tests were two-tailed, with a p-value of less than 0.05 deemed statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

This study encompassed a total of 1,342 eligible patients diagnosed with DLC. The initial cohort comprised 271 patients, while two independent validation cohorts included 218 and 853 patients, respectively. Within the initial cohort, the mean age of patients was 67 years, with a predominance of male (57.6%). The leading cause of DLC was identified as chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection (63.5%). Notable complications observed included ascites (55.4%), esophagogastric variceal bleeding (50.6%), hepatic encephalopathy (10.7%), hepatorenal syndrome (6.3%), and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (5.5%). The 90-day mortality rate was recorded at 12.5%. A comparative analysis revealed that the mortality group exhibited significantly elevated levels of neutrophils (Neut), monocytes (Mono), total bilirubin (Tbil), creatinine (Cr), prothrombin time (PT), dMLR, and MELD score, while demonstrating lower levels of lymphocyte count (LYM), haemoglobin (HGB) and albumin (Alb) in contrast to the survival group. The dMLR was found to be independently associated with short-term mortality

Univariate analysis identified several variables that were significantly correlated with the outcome, including HGB, Alb, Tbil, Cr, PT, and dMLR (p < 0.05). Subsequent multivariate analysis confirmed that dMLR was independently associated with 90-day mortality (Model’s Brier score = 0.073) (Table 2). The RCS analysis indicated a positive correlation between dMLR values and mortality risk, demonstrating that the risk of mortality escalated with increasing dMLR values (Figure 1a).

Validation cohorts

In the validation cohorts, the observed 90-day mortality rates were 9.2% for validation cohort 1 and 9.8% for validation cohort 2. Multivariate analysis demonstrated that the dMLR was independently associated with 90-day mortality in both validation cohorts (Validation cohort 1 Model’s Brier score = 0.063, Validation cohort 2 Model’s Brier score = 0.070) (Table 2). Additionally, the RCS analysis suggested that the mortality risk escalated with increasing dMLR values, corroborating the results obtained from the initial cohort (Figure 1b and 1c).

ROC analysis and nomogram development

In the total cohort, the area under the curve (AUC) for dMLR in predicting outcome was determined to be 0.745, and dMLR demonstrated comparable predictive ability to the MELD score (0.785, p > 0.05) (Figure 2). The cut-off value for dMLR was 25.77. Patients with a dMLR value greater than 25.77 were in the high-risk group for mortality. Considering practical application, we developed a nomogram integrating the dMLR and MELD score (supplementary Figure 1). Furthermore, the AUC of the nomgram was significantly higher than that of the dMLR (0.825 vs. 0.745, p < 0.05) or MELD score (0.825 vs. 0.785, p < 0.05).

Discussion

This research, conducted through a multicentre retrospective cohort analysis, identified that the dMLR is independently associated with 90-day short-term mortality in patients with DLC. Meanwhile, the risk of mortality escalates with increasing dMLR values. These findings indicate that dMLR has the potential to serve as a simple and effective serum marker for assessing short-term mortality in this patient population, thereby offering a novel instrument for clinical risk stratification.

DLC represents the terminal phase of chronic liver disease, characterized by a complex interplay between inflammatory activation and immune suppression [13,14]. Monocytes are integral to the inflammatory response, with their elevated levels typically linked to the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin-1 and tumour necrosis factor-alpha [15]. Previous studies have demonstrated that monocyte dysfunction is a significant manifestation of immune dysregulation in cirrhosis, closely associated with an increased mortality risk among affected individuals [16,17]. Monocytes initiate inflammatory pathways and produce pro-inflammatory cytokines by recognizing damage-associated molecular patterns and pathogen-associated molecular patterns; alterations in their functionality are significantly correlated with adverse outcomes in LC [18,19]. A study by Cardoso et al. established a strong association between an increase in classical monocytes and the severity of LC as well as 90-day mortality [19]. Furthermore, recent findings by Wu et al. indicated that the upregulation of the triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells-1 on monocytes in patients with HBV-related cirrhosis exacerbates liver inflammation and fibrosis progression by facilitating the release of inflammatory cytokines and the activation of inflammatory cells [20]. Additionally, research by Brenig et al. revealed that an increase in AXL-expressing monocytes serve as a marker of immune dysfunction in LC, with higher counts correlating with greater disease severity, complications, and increased mortality [21].

In the decompensated phase of cirrhosis, lymphocyte dysfunction is closely linked to immune suppression, significantly heightening mortality risk [7,22]. A study by Boni indicated that lymphocyte dysfunction contributes to persistent HBV infection, thereby elevating the risk of developing and progressing LC [23]. Research by Weiss et al. further demonstrated that lymphocyte dysfunction is intricately associated with the immunosuppressive state in DLC patients, potentially serving as a critical factor in the elevated in-hospital mortality rate s observed in this cohort [24].

The dMLR serves as a reflection of the overall inflammatory and immune status in patients with cirrhosis. An increase in dMLR may signify a worsening immune imbalance, suggesting that the intensification of the inflammatory state and immune suppression is closely linked to the progression and prognosis of cirrhosis. The current study’s findings, which establish a significant association between elevated dMLR and 90-day mortality, further validate its utility as an indicator of both inflammation and immune dysregulation. Consequently, the findings of this study may offer a simple and reliable tool for predicting short-term mortality in DLC patients, aiding in the identification of high-risk individuals and guide clinical decision-making.

The denominator of the dMLR calculation formula includes monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils in addition to the components found in the MLR. Monocytes, as core components of innate immunity, not only participate in the defense of the first line of defense but also serve as a bridge between innate and adaptive immunity through differentiation and antigen presentation [25]. A study by Cardoso et al. confirmed that classical monocytes are independently and positively associated with the severity of liver cirrhosis and 90-day mortality [19]. Eosinophils also play a significant role in liver fibrosis and immune inflammation, and their abnormal levels are closely related to the risk of infection and prognosis in cirrhotic patients [26]. Zeng et al. found that in cirrhotic patients with esophagogastric variceal bleeding, reduced peripheral blood eosinophils were significantly associated with an increased risk of 90-day post-discharge mortality, and their decreased levels can reflect compromised immune function and inflammatory imbalance [27]. The role of basophils in cirrhosis is less studied. Basophils can regulate immune responses by releasing cytokines [28], and they may influence the liver’s immune microenvironment through their secreted cytokines, thereby affecting the prognosis of cirrhotic patients. Furthermore, dMLR may offer more advantages compared to MLR because it includes other granulocytes beyond neutrophils. Immature or poorly differentiated neutrophils can be released in a proinflammatory environment, which rapidly increases neutrophil generation [29]. The MELD score is a well-established and widely used metric. The variables required for the calculation of the dMLR (monocytes, white blood cells, neutrophils) are entirely different from those used for the MELD score. Therefore, dMLR may have good complementarity with the MELD score.

Despite the substantial sample size and multi-center validation of this study, several limitations persist. Firstly, as a retrospective study, the data were derived from pre-existing medical records, which may be subject to issues such as incomplete data and selection bias. Consequently, further prospective studies are warranted to validate our findings. Secondly, the specific biological mechanisms through which the dMLR impacts prognosis in patients with DLC require additional investigation to clarify its role within the intricate pathophysiological context.

Conclusions

Taken together, the dMLR is independently associated with 90-day mortality in patients with DLC, indicating an increased risk of mortality associated with higher dMLR values. This metric may function as a simple, cost-effective, and reliable marker, enabling clinicians to identify high-risk patients at an early stage and to guide personalized treatment strategies.

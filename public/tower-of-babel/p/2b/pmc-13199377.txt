Hybrid feature selection for IoMT based intrusion detection system for integrating mutual information filtering with deep learning based accelerated metaheuristic optimization
Authors: Saranya Palaniappan, Sudhakar Sengan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid development of the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) has significantly expanded the cybersecurity attack perimeter of healthcare systems (HCS). Security measures for mitigating intrusions in HCS have direct patient safety benefits beyond the operational impacts evident in conventional information and communication technology (ICT) contexts. Feature selection (FS) is one of the primary and most important preliminary processing phases for accurate intrusion detection in the IoMT. This phase reduces the high-dimensional traffic feature space to a minimal filtering subset, which is beneficial for deployments with limited resources. Current methods have important limitations: while filter methods (FM) are fast, they don’t account for complex inter-feature dependencies; and wrapper methods (WM) account for these inter-feature dependencies, but the outcomes have a high computational cost, with standard processing times ranging from 1800 to 5300 s due to the requirement to retrain the machine learning (ML) model at each search iteration. To address this trade-off, the present research study provides a method that combines mutual information and deep learning binary dragonfly algorithm (MI + DL-BDA). The proposed model employs a BDA wrapper search guided by ProxyNet and MI filtering to achieve significantly lower dimensionality. A low-cost MLP (Multilevel Processing) proxy with efficient channel attention (ECA), ProxyNet implements frozen-weight inference at 10–50 ms per measurement to replace periodic classifier retraining. To account for the asymmetric risk profile in HCS, where missed detections are more significant than false alarms, the fitness function was developed with stringent safety limits, prioritizing recall at FPR ≤ 1%. Evaluated on CICIoMT2024 (43 features, 18 attack types, 40 medical devices), the proposed MI + DL-BDA achieves 65.1% dimensionality reduction while preserving 98.6–99.4% of full-feature detection performance, with Recall @ FPR ≤ 1% = 0.938 and PR-AUC = 0.956 in 502.6 s, representing a 9.2–10.6× speedup over conventional WM. Cross-dataset evaluation on IoMT-TrafficData (IP flow, 27 features, 8 attack types) independently confirms these findings, with a 55.6% reduction and 99.0–99.7% performance retention in 234.5 s across a distinct device ecosystem and attack classification. Subset stability is correctly measured as the average pairwise Jaccard similarity across 5 autonomous runs, and the statistical significance of performance advantages over 9 baseline methods is confirmed via Wilcoxon signed-rank testing. Ablation analysis quantifies the individual contributions of each network component, with BDA optimization and ECA attention ranking as the most consequential, at 2.24 pp and 0.79 pp, respectively.

Introduction

The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) has primarily redesigned clinical attention delivery by enabling continuous patient monitoring, remote diagnostics, and real-time Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) across interconnected medical devices. With the global IoMT device population projected to exceed 50 billion by 2030, hospital networks now integrate heterogeneous endpoints—implantable sensors, infusion pumps, patient monitors, and wearable devices—generating continuous physiological data streams across wireless and hospital Information Technology (IT) set-up1. Such communication presents significant cybersecurity risks. Security breaches in the IoMT have an immediate impact on patient safety, including fraudulent use of clinical monitoring data and loss of drug delivery systems. They are caused by ransomware failures, unlike conventional information technology systems, where malicious attacks result in functional or financial losses. Cybersecurity breaches can have a severe, possibly life-threatening impact, as the WannaCry ransomware attack on the UK National Health Service proved. As a result, healthcare systems (HCS) require robust, domain-specific Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs)2.

By deploying IoMT, conventional IDS faces greater challenges. Signature-based IDSs fail to detect zero-day attacks due to the multiple traffic flows in multi-device clinical networks; however, they generate a high number of false positives (FPs)3. Apart from contributing to detection quality, IoMT datasets, such as CICIoMT2024, have 40–100 features distributed across tens of millions of flow records, imposing redundancy on healthcare industry edge devices with limited resources due to the computational and memory requirements of statistical inference4. Therefore, to implement accurate IoMT intrusion detection, Feature Selection (FS) is important. This method minimizes the amount of information while still providing the filtering data needed for accurate early detection of attacks.

In the context of the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT), existing feature selection (FS) methods require significant improvements. Effectively applying Filter Methods (FM), including Mutual Information (MI) and χ² testing, overlooks communication impacts between correlated traffic features, as evident in multi-stage IoMT attacks, because these methods test features individually5,6. Due to the need to perform analytical Machine Learning (ML) tests at each search iteration, Wrapper Methods (WM), including Genetic Algorithm (GA), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), incur high Computational Cost (CC). But they also identify feature relationships using iterative classifier training. Typical wrapper processing times on IoMT-scale datasets range from 1800 to 5300 s7,8. The CC is minimized by hybrid methods that integrate a filter before performing wrapper search. However, those methods continue to rely on conventional accuracy metrics and shallow fitness proxies that do not account for HCS’s asymmetric risk profile. In HCS, missed attack detections are more significant than FP9,10. Critically, few existing methods explicitly formulate the optimisation objective under False Positive Rate (FPR) constraints, despite FPR control being a primary functional requirement for IoMT-IDS deployment11.

This research recommends a hybrid FS that integrates MI filtering with the Deep Learning-enhanced Binary Dragonfly Algorithm (DL-BDA), an IDS for the IoMT, to address those drawbacks. To significantly reduce fitness evaluation time from 30 to 180 s per iteration to 10–50 ms, the trained model includes ProxyNet, a lightweight MLP network with Efficient Channel Attention (ECA), as a trainable fitness proxy. The reason for this is to eliminate multiple full-classifier reinforcement learning (RL) runs with frozen weights during wrapper search. To consider its clinical application objective of avoiding missed detections in life-threatening monitoring contexts, the fitness function is defined under specific safety constraints, giving preference to recall at FPR ≤ 1%.

Related works

The rapid growth of IoMT has raised serious cybersecurity challenges to healthcare networks. Deb et al.12 divided real IoMT attacks into three main types: firmware tampering, unauthorised access to private data, and DoS attacks. They also noted that standard IT security measures do not work well due to the specialized protocols and operational constraints in medical devices. Doménech et al.13 noted that using IoMT-specific datasets is vital for accurate IDS. They found that when models trained on common IoT data are used in an HCS, FNR increases by 12–18%. Dzamesi and Elsayed14 also reported that even small-scale DDoS and malware attacks can disrupt clinical tests without being detected by traditional signature-based IDSs.

Rule-based IDS has limited use in dynamic IoMT environments due to poor adaptability and frequent FP, as reported by Elrawy et al.15. These deficiencies have driven a shift toward Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL)-based adaptive IDS. A Host-Based Intrusion Detection System (HIIDS) was provided by Saif et al.16. It integrates a Random Forest (RF) classification algorithm with a GA-driven FS; the resulting model achieves 94.2% accuracy but takes over 3 h to optimize.

In the related healthcare monitoring context, Aditya et al.17 proposed LRRN-DLNN, integrating Lasso Regression-based FS with metaheuristic optimization for disease classification and malicious activity prediction, achieving 98.9% accuracy. However, the work targets general clinical monitoring rather than network-level intrusion scenarios specific to IoMT environments.

On the CICIoMT2024 dataset, an RL implemented on a Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) for 8–12 h by Shaikh et al.18 attained 96.3% recall at a 1% False Positive Rate (FPR). A hybrid BiLSTM-DNN, termed HBiLD-IDS, was developed by Benahmed et al.19. Its inference time was less than 15 ms due to normalisation and filtering, but it was more vulnerable to new attacks because it relied primarily on static features.

Extending adaptive detection to Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), Sisodiya et al.20 developed a hybrid AI-powered cyber range network that combines Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) trained on large-scale security event logs for attack detection in CPS. Their findings confirm that even well-optimized DL detection pipelines incur significant CC when deployed across heterogeneous network conditions. This limitation supports the requirement for surrogate-accelerated test methods in resource-constrained IoMT settings.

The FS remains a critical step in managing the high dimensionality of IoMT network traffic. Zouhri et al.21 evaluated multiple statistical FM and found that MI-based ranking performs best at capturing non-linear feature dependencies. However, these filters miss meaningful cross-feature relationships that could help identify multi-stage attacks. Wrapper-based metaheuristics like Differential Evolution and PSO, examined by Al-Yaseen et al.22 and Hassan et al.23, directly measure classifier performance and achieve strong accuracy, but they require 2–5 h of FS due to repeated model retraining.

HM usually aims to combine the fast processing of FM with the precise evaluation of WM. Logeswari et al.24 combined correlation-based filtering with GA optimization, thereby reducing the running time by nearly 64% without apparent loss in accuracy. Umer et al.25 adopted the FS, which first used multi-head attention and then a wrapper refinement stage; the model achieved 97.2% accuracy with 58% fewer features, but the method still relied on GPU hardware, which limits simple deployment.

Kurdi et al.26 achieved almost 4.2× faster evaluation with their proposed model using Information Gain filtering with an RF classifier, though retraining the ensemble still considered significant computation time. Pourardebil Khah et al.27 combined RF importance values with Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) in their work; this model achieved 61% feature reduction and 96.7% accuracy. At the same time, Huang et al.28 integrated ReliefF, MI, and a stacking ensemble; accuracy improved by about 2.4–3.8 points, but the overall method still took over 5 h to run on a medium dataset, which is still quite heavy for real-time use.

Beyond CC, the invention of detection objectives itself remains an underexplored dimension in existing hybrid IDS literature. Most models optimize for overall accuracy or F1-score without explicitly constraining FPR to clinically safe thresholds.

Aditya et al.11 addressed this in the enterprise cybersecurity context with OD-DRUNN-XAI, which integrates Deep Recurrent Networks (DRN) with explainability modules and achieves an FPR of 6.24%—representing nearly a 60% improvement over conventional DRNN baselines on enterprise network data. Although evaluated outside the IoMT domain, this work establishes that safety-oriented FPR control is measurable and achievable within DL-based detection pipelines, and motivates the explicit FPR-constrained recall metric adopted in the fitness formulation of the present study.

Although there has been plenty of research in the IDS field, real-time IDS in IoMT networks still faces many complex challenges. Most hybrid models are found to still rely on computationally intensive wrapper measurements. Many of them also use shallow classifiers that cannot properly learn temporal changes in network behavior. Another problem is that they primarily focus on overall accuracy rather than safety-related measures, such as recall when the FPR is limited. The present study seeks to address these gaps by proposing a hybrid model based on MI and DL-BDA.

Proposed methodology

Figure 1 presents the overall architecture of the proposed hybrid FS method. The first stage employs MI filtering, which evaluates the statistical dependency between each feature and its corresponding class label, and eliminates features with low discriminative weight. The resulting reduced subset is then passed to the next stage.

The second stage uses a metaheuristic-based wrapper optimization with DL-BDA, which refines the MI-filtered feature space using a population search inspired by dragonfly group foraging behavior. In this phase, each dragonfly is treated as a binary vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m$$\end{document}, representing one candidate FS configuration.

ProxyNet, a lightweight DL proxy, is used to compute the classification performance \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J\left(m\right)$$\end{document}defined in Eq. (6). This replaces repeated full classifier retraining during search, substantially reducing fitness evaluation cost. After the algorithm reaches convergence, the DL-BDA returns the optimal binary mask \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${m}^{*}$$\end{document}that satisfies the optimization target in Eq. Principle of dragonfly algorithm

The dragonfly Algorithm is a bio-inspired metaheuristic that mimics the static and dynamic swarm behaviour of dragonflies during hunting and migration. The algorithm’s ability to balance exploration and exploitation is governed by five main behavioural factors: separation, alignment, cohesion, attraction to food, and avoidance of attacks. These behaviours collectively guide the swarm toward better solutions while maintaining population diversity and preventing premature convergence to a local optimum (Fig. 2).

Each dragonfly maintains an appropriate distance from nearby neighbours through separation (Fig. 2a).

Hyperparameters and implementation

Table 1 provides the hyperparameter configuration required to train the ML. Once MI filtering had eliminated redundant features from the search space, a psychological weight (f = 1.0 for food attraction) should be used to prioritize use over exploration. In the initial iterations, the rate of weight loss (0.9→0.4) supports complete exploration, but in subsequent phases it gradually shifts toward more targeted exploitation. The fitness weights address the imbalanced risk profile of IoMT environments, where missed detections are more significant in clinical terms than CC, by allocating 80% to detection performance (α + β = 0.8) and 20% to CC (γ + δ = 0.2). Experimental setup

Dataset description

The MI + DL-BDA introduced is tested on two benchmark datasets that include multiple device systems, protocol stacks, and traffic features. The data is used to assess its adaptability across heterogeneous IoMT environments.

CICIoMT2024

The Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (University of New Brunswick) released the CICIoMT202429 benchmark for IDS research in Medical IoT networks, which serves as the primary testing dataset. This collection includes 40 medical devices, including diabetes medicines and infusion pumps for treatment, electrocardiography (ECG) units, pulse oximeters, respiratory monitors, and wearable sensors that communicate via Wi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy, and MQTT (Message Queue Telemetry Transport). A total of 25 of these tools are actual HCS, and 15 are simulated. A total of 18 attack types are divided into 5 groups: benign traffic, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks (4 types), reconnaissance attacks (4 types), attacks targeting MQTT (5 types), and spoofing. Enabling state-aware anomaly detection based on behavioral deviations from predictable functional behaviors, a key structural feature is lifecycle-based profiling across 4 functional states: power-on, idle, active, and interactive. Nearly 8.8 million bidirectional network flow records, each described by 43 features, emerged from traffic records extracted using CICFlowMeter.

The set of features includes flow metrics such as time interval, packet count, and length; rate-based IDS; packet size distributions; inter-arrival times; TCP flag frequencies; header- and window-related attributes; and directional behavioral signifiers that detect asymmetry between forward and backward traffic. The data shows a significant disparity between the two classes in the dataset; just 2.6% of the records indicate normal traffic, while 97.4% indicate attack traffic. By implementing a class-weighted binary cross-entropy loss with weights proportional to class frequencies, this study can address this imbalance during ProxyNet training while preserving the initially generated data distribution (i.e., without synthetic oversampling) and ensuring a realistic class prior relevant to deployment conditions. To avoid data loss, the dataset is split into three parts: 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. This partitioning is done logically by timestamp. IoMT-TrafficData (IP-flow configuration)

Areia et al. [2024] designed the second dataset, IoMT-TrafficData, which is available in the IEEE journal Access. It simulates a sports HCS where patients track health metrics using interconnected wearables and networked medical devices. The test environment consisted of a general Internet of Things (IoT) setup using MQTT and the Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP), an attacker’s sector for malicious deployment, and a wireless body area network (WBAN) with wearable devices communicating via Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5.0/BLE 4.0. The device context and traffic profile change dramatically from CICIoMT2024 in this network, which is modelled after HCS. In HCS, old and new sensor technology, as well as patient-worn devices, coexist across multiple protocols.

Over an average of 31.7 h of network activity, the dataset features 8 attack types. These include 4 types of HTTP-DoS (Denial-of-Service) (Apache Killer, R-U-Dead-Yet, Slow Read, and Slowloris), ARP spoofing, CAM table overflow, MQTT flooding, and network scanning. All of these attacks were executed using standard penetration testing tools. The average data can be selected from three different sub-dataset configurations: BLE (27 features), IP-packet (22 features), and IP-flow (23 features). For semantically consistent cross-dataset comparison, the IP-flow configuration is chosen for cross-dataset evaluation because it provides bidirectional flow-level feature representation that is most aligned with the CICIoMT2024 feature space. The 3,243,185 bidirectional session records were generated by aggregating Zeek Flowmeter traffic flows. The identical chronological partitioning of 70/15/15 is used. A. Analysis of FM

A. Analysis of FM

Analysis of FM

FM achieves fast selection (12–42 s) across both datasets, but detection accuracy is generally low. To demonstrate that MI ranking without wrapper correction is not able to identify the compact selective subset required under safety-critical FPR constraints, MI-Only achieves Recall = 0.893 and PR-AUC = 0.923 on CICIoMT2024 while maintaining only 26 features. The result is 4.5 points lower than MI + DL-BDA, further signifying that MI alone does not meet the requirements. Since unsupervised linear transformations discard class-discriminative structure, principal component analysis provides the worst results (recall = 0.882, PR-AUC = 0.917). Although Chi-Square improves marginally (Recall = 0.887, PR-AUC = 0.920), the methodology remains constrained by a single-factor evaluation that does not account for feature dependence. The same ordering is reproduced in IoMT-TrafficData, where FM achieves Recall = 0.916–0.923, versus 0.989 for MI + DL-BDA—a gap of 6.6–7.3 pp reflecting the more separable traffic structure of this dataset, which increases the performance cost of suboptimal feature selection.

B. Analysis of WM

B. Analysis of WM

Analysis of WM

WM achieves detection performance nearest to MI + DL-BDA but at a prohibitive CC. On CICIoMT2024, GA-Wrapper attains the highest wrapper Recall (0.936) and PR-AUC (0.955) but requires 5341 s—a 10.6× runtime overhead relative to MI + DL-BDA. The proposed method exceeds all 3 wrapper baselines in Recall (0.938 vs. 0.931–0.936) and dimensionality reduction (65.1% vs. 55.8–60.5%), while delivering 9.2–10.6× speedup through ProxyNet surrogate fitness evaluation. On IoMT-TrafficData, MI + DL-BDA achieves Recall = 0.989 in 234.5 s vs. GA-Wrapper’s Recall = 0.987 in 2156 s, signifying that the performance and efficiency advantages are consistent across datasets.

C. Analysis of HM

C. Analysis of HM

Analysis of HM

Hybrid MI + metaheuristic baselines share the MI prefiltering stage with the proposed method and therefore represent the most directly comparable configurations. With a Pr-AUC of 0.956 and a recall of 0.937, MI + GA generates the most accurate hybrid baseline result on CICIoMT2024 in 2756 s. MI + DL-BDA improves recall by 0.1% and achieves 5.5 times faster execution, demonstrating that a design contribution that truly matters is the substitution of ProxyNet surrogate inference for RF-based fitness evaluation. With an approximate improved recall of 0.989 vs. 0.988, MI + DL-BDA performs better than MI + GA on IoMT-TrafficData, which results in a 4.8× speedup (234.5 vs. 1124 s). When comparing the two datasets, the overall metric performance advantage over HM is minimal and reliable, but the CC advantage is significant and relevant for deployment in IoMT environments with limited resources.

Conclusion and future work

This study presented a hybrid FS for IoMT intrusion detection that addresses the computational bottleneck of wrapper-based optimisation by leveraging DL-accelerated fitness evaluation. The proposed MI + DL-BDA combines mutual information filtering with Binary Dragonfly Algorithm search, augmented by ProxyNet—a lightweight MLP-ECA surrogate that replaces repeated full classifier retraining with frozen-weight inference during metaheuristic search. The two-stage architecture separates the complementary roles of statistical relevance filtering and combinatorial wrapper optimisation, achieving dimensionality reduction and detection performance that neither stage can achieve on its own.

Evaluation on CICIoMT2024 demonstrated 65.1% dimensionality reduction (43→15 features) while preserving 98.6–99.4% of full-feature detection performance across RF, DNN, and LR classifiers, with Recall @ FPR ≤ 1% = 0.938 and PR-AUC = 0.956 achieved in 502.6 s—a 9.2–10.6× speedup relative to conventional WM.

IoMT-TrafficData (IP-flow) cross-dataset evaluation confirmed those findings on its own dataset, with reduced dimensionality of 55.6% (27 features to 12) and performance retention of 99.0–99.7% in 234.5 s. This result demonstrates that the technique is adaptable across numerous device ecosystems, protocol stacks, and attack classification systems. Flow Duration, Flow Bytes/s, and PSH Flag Count are high-importance features that converge across both datasets. These results indicate that the system can identify distinct IoMT traffic features, rather than objects specific to individual datasets. The performance-efficiency balance is influenced by 5 components: MI filtering, ProxyNet surrogate, ECA attention, BDA wrapper optimisation, and elite preservation. The two most significant components, according to ablation analysis, are BDA optimization and ECA attention.

Assuming that the recommended model’s CC accounts for 87% of total latency in ProxyNet training and only 7.3% in BDA search, this shows that the availability of a one-time training phase, rather than per-inference overhead, is the most significant factor in deployment adaptability. Application deployment on computing devices with limited CC has been enabled by minimising memory and inference requirements relative to the full 43-feature space, using the selected 15-feature subset. The design is important for conducting direct validation on medical edge devices with limited resources before deployment, as the current study was tested on server-grade computer hardware.

In the future, research will focus on developing federated FS for distributed IoMT environments. The result will allow collaborative model training without requiring the sharing of raw patient data, which is a demand for centralized data aggregation in environments where privacy regulations can be a limitation. When it comes to safety-critical CDSS, where detection decisions need to be auditable, the use of accessible mechanisms, such as attention-based FS and SHAP-based importance analysis, would enhance interpretability and support regulatory compliance. The temporal non-stationarity of IoMT traffic would be addressed by a variant of the data stream FS that handles concept drift. This feature was important because device populations are constantly evolving, and new attack variants are emerging30,31.

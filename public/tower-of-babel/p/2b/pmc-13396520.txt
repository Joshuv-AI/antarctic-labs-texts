IALA-BPNN: superior house price prediction through multi-strategy optimized Artificial Lemming Algorithm and BP neural network
Authors: Qifeng Guo, Shengpeng Li, Tianqi Xia, Daren Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAgainst this backdrop of a global economic downturn, the real estate market exhibits characteristics of both a consumer good and an investment asset, with housing prices displaying fluctuating nonlinear trends. Predicting these trends holds significant importance for governments, developers, and individuals. Addressing the vulnerability of BP neural networks (BPNN) to initial weights and biases in housing price forecasting, this study enhances the Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA) by incorporating pinhole imaging learning, evolutionary mutation perturbation, and golden sine development strategies. This improved ALA is applied to update the BPNN network structure, establishing the novel IALA-BPNN housing price prediction model. Experiments using four public datasets compare IALA-BPNN against eight comparable hybrid models, validating the model’s success and the effectiveness of the three improvement strategies. Further comparisons with four machine learning models (RF, SVM, LSTM, CNN) demonstrate the model’s strong competitiveness in housing price forecasting. Finally, a Shanghai housing price dataset is constructed. Comparing IALA-BPNN with the top-performing models from the previous experiments reveals that IALA-BPNN achieves significant improvements across evaluation metrics: MAE (13.63%), MAPE (14.18%), RMSE (14.99%), R2 (9.38%), SMAPE (19.15%), SD (8.46%), and Time (34.30%), demonstrating its practical application potential.Keywords: House price prediction, Artificial Lemming Algorithm, BP neural network, Shanghai housing prices, Optimize algorithmSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Housing prices serve as a core indicator in the real estate market, closely tied to economic conditions and public welfare. They have both consumption and investment attributes in the modern economy and hold significant importance. In the post-pandemic era, with the global economy tightening, uncertainties in the real estate market have increased1. Under these circumstances, accurately forecasting housing price trends and investigating their patterns and drivers has become imperative. Current housing price prediction research primarily falls into two categories: econometric models and neural networks2.

Scholars have explored the feasibility of housing price prediction models from an economic perspective. Zhang, Zhishuo3 designs an objective housing price prediction scheme based on decision trees, achieving optimal results when compared with advanced machine learning models. Zhang, Qingqi4 utilizes Spearman’s correlation coefficient to analyze key factors influencing housing prices. Subsequently, a multiple linear regression model is constructed for predicting housing prices, utilizing an integrated analysis approach. Song, Yi et al.5 constructs a housing price prediction model based on this hedonic model, incorporating this anchoring effect into structural and location features to screen for impacts on accessibility indices. Despite significant achievements in econometric research, the emergence of computer technology and algorithmic optimization has opened new avenues for machine learning approaches in housing price forecasting6.

To capture information-rich predictive variables and enhance forecasting accuracy, Shao, Jin et al.7 proposes a multi-feature selection multiscale analysis method demonstrating superior predictive performance. Zhan, Choujun et al.8 introduces a housing price prediction framework showcasing the innovation of combining a hybrid Bayesian optimization model with bagging, stacking and transformer techniques, achieving RMSE reductions of 25.56% and 5.11% respectively in experiments. Wang, Pengkun et al.9 proposes the ensemble model JGC-MMN, which leverages a modified dense-connection convolutional network to learn features and spatio-temporal correlations by integrating current components with future predictions, thereby improving housing price forecasting. The aforementioned studies demonstrate machine learning achievements in housing price forecasting. However, complex neural networks inevitably entail higher computational resource consumption and substantial data requirements10–12. Therefore, the BP neural network with a simple structure and small data requirements has gradually become a key technology in related prediction fields13.

Zhou, Zhipeng14 constructs a grey neural network hybrid model that integrates BP neural networks to forecast actual trends in commercial aviation near-miss incidents. Sun, Ziyi15 and his research group develop a housing price forecasting model utilizing a hybrid genetic particle swarm-optimized BP neural network. This model is applied to predict the average prices of residential commercial properties in Chongqing for 2021. Meanwhile, Kumar Navin16 introduces an innovative telematics framework specifically designed for electric vehicles. This framework leverages a BP neural network to estimate battery state-of-charge, aiming to boost the efficiency of widespread charging infrastructure. Additionally, Udaybhanu, Gadi17 employs a feedforward artificial neural network (FFANN) with backpropagation (BP) to forecast the laminar combustion velocity of an iso-octane/air mixture. These above studies show that BP neural network has achieved extensive research results in related fields, but the research applying it to housing price prediction is relatively rare. Despite numerous advantages, BP neural networks exhibit performance susceptibility to network weights and biases. Consequently, necessary optimization measures are required to enhance their predictive capabilities18.

After the optimization technology emerges, it is widely applied in various fields by scholars19–21. Many scholars replace the gradient descent method with optimization algorithms to update the weights and biases of BP neural network, hoping to obtain better network performance. Wang, Yuqin et al.22 designs an adaptive genetic backpropagation neural network algorithm, combining the global search advantage of genetic algorithms with the local optimization characteristics of BP neural networks, thereby improving optimization efficiency and accuracy. Jiang, Dong et al.23 proposes a multi-objective optimization program for dual-active-bridge converters based on a non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm, embedding a BP neural network to predict vibrations in power electronic transformers. Fan, Ding24 employes a genetic algorithm-backpropagation (GA-BP) network for evaluation to enhance the service quality of urban water bodies and green spaces, promoting the development of healthy urban environments. Pitchandi, Perumal25 proposes a Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCSO)-based anomaly detection model for power line inspections using a backpropagation neural algorithm. These studies demonstrate the effectiveness and success of optimization techniques in enhancing BP neural networks. Therefore, this paper will update the weights and biases of BP neural network using optimization technology and apply this hybrid model to the prediction of nonlinear and multi-factor fluctuating housing prices, which is of certain feasibility.

The Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA) is a novel optimization algorithm proposed by Yaning Xiao et al.26 in 2025. With its unique optimization mechanism, this algorithm has been widely applied in various research fields. In the study of an optimization model for photovoltaic intelligent selection of energy sharing regions and flexible load allocation, Yu, Jie et al.27 employs an enhanced artificial lemming algorithm to solve the problem. Wang, Huan28 designs a temperature compensation algorithm for pressure scanners, integrating the advantages of the Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model in ensemble learning with the artificial lemming algorithm. To develop regression-based machine learning models for predicting the hydrogen storage capacity of metal-organic frameworks, Zhang, Jiamin et al.18 uses an improved artificial lemming algorithm to select the hyperparameters of these machine learning models. Given the superiority of the ALA algorithm, it is chosen as the basic optimization technique method. The gradient descent method of the BP neural network is replaced with the random optimization approach of the ALA algorithm to update the weights and biases of the hidden layer, addressing the housing price prediction problem. However, preliminary experiments reveals that the ALA algorithm suffers from reduced population diversity during optimization, struggles to escape local optima in the middle of iterations, and fails to maintain good convergence quality while ensuring rapid convergence.

Therefore, to address the above issues, this paper makes necessary improvements to the ALA algorithm. The contributions of this paper are as follows:

In view of the problems that the existing optimization algorithm-BPNN hybrid model encounters in housing price prediction, such as being prone to getting stuck in local optima, imbalance between global search and local exploitation, and insufficient stability, this paper proposes an improved IALA-BPNN model. This model introduces three strategies, pinhole imaging learning, evolutionary mutation perturbation, and golden sine exploitation, into the ALA, respectively used to enhance population diversity, improve the ability to escape local optima, and strengthen the ability for fine search in the later stage, thereby constructing a collaborative optimization mechanism more suitable for predicting complex housing price data.

Relevant work

BP neural network (BPNN)

It is acknowledged that advanced models such as XGBoost, LightGBM, and various deep learning architectures have achieved remarkable success in housing price prediction tasks. However, the selection of BPNN in this study is motivated by three specific considerations. First, BPNN possesses a relatively simple structure and requires fewer training samples compared to deep learning models40, which typically demand large-scale datasets to avoid overfitting. This characteristic makes BPNN particularly suitable for scenarios where data acquisition is costly or sample sizes are limited, such as the Shanghai-specific housing dataset constructed in this study. Second, unlike ensemble tree-based models that often behave as black boxes with limited interpretability of internal parameter dynamics41, BPNN allows for a clearer demonstration of how optimization algorithms directly influence weight and bias updates. This transparency facilitates a more rigorous evaluation of the proposed IALA algorithm’s effectiveness. Third, focusing on BPNN provides a controlled baseline for assessing the net impact of the proposed optimization strategies without confounding effects introduced by more complex model architectures. Therefore, while BPNN may not represent the most advanced architecture in the current literature, it serves as an appropriate and competitive testbed for validating the proposed optimization methodology, with the understanding that the IALA algorithm can be generalized to other neural network architectures in future work.

Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA)

The Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA) is a novel optimization algorithm proposed by Yaning Xiao et al.26 in 2025. The ALA algorithm draws inspiration from lemmings. These lemmings exhibit extremely high reproductive capacity and consume large amounts of food to replenish energy. In spring, they migrate to highlands for breeding, returning to the tundra in autumn. Lemmings possess keen senses of smell and hearing, aiding them in foraging and evading predators. When danger arises, they signal warnings and retreat into underground burrows, capable of sprinting swiftly to escape hazardous areas. Additionally, lemmings excel at constructing intricate burrows that provide shelter from predators and extreme weather, while also serving as storage for food and breeding grounds. The ALA algorithm is explicitly designed based on these four core lemming behaviors: migration, burrowing, foraging, and predator avoidance. Proposal of the IALA-BP model

Pinhole imaging learning strategy

The ALA algorithm simulates the “long-distance migration-burrowing” mechanism of lemming populations to achieve global search, demonstrating high efficiency in complex nonlinear optimization problems. However, when confronted with high-dimensional, multi-modal, and time-varying problems, traditional population migration mechanisms may fail to rapidly adjust search directions, hindering comprehensive exploration of the solution space. Therefore, optimizing the search mechanism of the ALA algorithm is necessary.

The Pinhole Imaging Learning (PIL) strategy is an optimization approach based on physical optics principles. Its core concept originates from the inverted mapping characteristic of “pinhole imaging,” where an external scene is inverted and projected onto an imaging plane through a small aperture. This “inverse mapping” mechanism is abstracted as a global search guidance strategy within optimization algorithms. Within the optimization algorithm, PIL achieves a key improvement by constructing a “virtual pinhole” to perform a nonlinear mapping of population positions, as expressed in Eq. 16. Here, lower and upper represent the lower and upper bounds of the optimization variable, respectively; δ is a proportional factor closely related to the iteration count.

Figure 3 illustrates the mathematical model of the improved learning strategy based on the optical pinhole imaging principle. In the figure, light rays emitted from the left light source pass through the aperture in the diaphragm, forming an inverted real image on the right imaging plane. This physical process is abstracted into the individual update mechanism within the optimization algorithm: the initial solution (e.g., point A) generates a mirrored solution in its corresponding symmetric region (e.g., point A’) by simulating the geometric relationship of pinhole imaging. This expands the search range and enhances population diversity. The strategy introduces a reverse learning concept, utilizing the symmetric positions of the current solution to construct potentially high-quality solutions.

Evolutionary mutation perturbation strategy

The ALA algorithm possesses a “population migration” characteristic that enables rapid convergence. However, excessively fast convergence may cause the algorithm to miss the global optimum. During the escape phase of the ALA algorithm, the singular nature of the spiral factor E assessment may render the “foraging” mechanism ineffective for individual escape. The Evolutionary Mutation Perturbation Strategy simulates the continuous genetic crossover mechanism in biological evolution. It introduces Gaussian perturbations and dynamic parameter adjustments during optimization to generate candidate solutions, dynamically replacing the original position based on fitness evaluation. This strategy actively guides the population out of local optima traps, forming a closed-loop of “exploration-perturbation-re-exploration.” Its mathematical expressions are shown in Eqs. 17 and 18:

The Evolutionary Mutation Perturbation strategy breaks convergence inertia through reverse perturbation. Combined with the ALA algorithm, it achieves a dynamic equilibrium of “global exploration-local exploitation,” preventing the population from becoming trapped in local optima due to the “convergence effect.”

Golden sine development strategy

Although the ALA algorithm may converge rapidly in specific scenarios (such as early iteration stages), fast convergence does not necessarily equate to high-quality convergence. In complex multi-modal optimization problems (e.g., housing price prediction, high-dimensional nonlinear systems), the original algorithm is prone to getting stuck in local optima, leading to fluctuations in solution quality. ALA relies on a single development strategy, potentially exhibiting “premature convergence” in later iterations and failing to conduct global searches for optimal solutions. The Golden Sine strategy mathematically integrates the golden ratio with the sine function, providing an enhanced driving force for the ALA algorithm. The golden ratio (gold ≈ 1.618) is a classical optimization tool. Its self-similar division characteristic guides the sine function to more efficiently approach the target solution within the solution space. Experimental parameter settings

This study comprises five experimental sections with different validation strategies tailored to each experimental purpose. For the comparative experiments in Sects. 5.1, 5.2, and 5.4 (comparisons with eight BPNN-related hybrid models, two gradient-based baselines, and five machine learning models), k-fold cross-validation (k = 10) is adopted to ensure robustness and generalizability. A fixed random seed (rng = 42) is used to guarantee reproducibility across all models.

For the ablation study in Sect. 5.3, which aims to evaluate the individual contributions of the three enhancement strategies rather than to generalize across different data partitions, a single train-test split with a 7:3 ratio is employed. This simplified validation is sufficient for ablation analysis because the relative performance differences among strategy variants are consistent and the computational cost of cross-validation would be unnecessary for this comparative purpose.

For the self-built Shanghai dataset experiment in Sect. 5.5, a single 7:3 train-test split is also adopted to maintain consistency with the original experimental design. To further validate the statistical significance of the performance differences, Bootstrap resampling is conducted. Specifically, 1,000 bootstrap samples are drawn with replacement from the test set prediction errors, and the 95% confidence intervals and p-values for the MAE differences between IALA-BPNN and each baseline model are calculated.

For all metaheuristic algorithms, the maximum number of iterations (max_iter) is set to 100, and the population size (Nall) is 30. For each experimental trial with cross-validation, the results are averaged over all folds; for single-split experiments, 30 independent runs are conducted and the mean values of all evaluation metrics are reported, with optimal values highlighted in bold. The objective is to validate whether the gradient-free IALA-BPNN can achieve competitive or superior prediction performance compared to conventional gradient-based training methods in the context of housing price forecasting.

Standard BPNN: This model adopts the classic backpropagation algorithm with the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimizer, which is the default training function in MATLAB’s newff implementation. The hidden layer uses the tansig activation function, while the output layer employs purelin. The maximum number of training epochs is set to 200, the learning rate to 0.01, and the target error to 1 × 10− 6.

Figure 13 presents the dynamic changes in exploration and exploitation ratios of the IALA algorithm across the four housing price datasets (Boston, Ca-Bay, Ca-Land, and Taiwan). At the initial stage (iteration 1–5), the exploration ratio dominates at approximately 95%, while the exploitation ratio is only 5%. This deliberate bias toward exploration enables the algorithm to broadly scan the search space, maintain population diversity, and avoid premature convergence to suboptimal regions. During the 5–20 iterations, the exploration proportion continues to decrease while the exploitation proportion keeps increasing. As the optimization progresses to iteration 20–50, the exploration ratio decreases to 16% and exploitation increases to 84%. This gradual transition reflects an adaptive mechanism that systematically shifts focus from global exploration to local exploitation. From iteration 50 through the final iteration 100, the exploitation ratio stabilizes at above 90%, indicating that the algorithm has successfully converged to an optimal or near-optimal solution and is performing only fine-tuning operations. This dynamic pattern directly validates the complementary roles of the three enhancement strategies. Specifically, the pinhole imaging learning strategy enhances early-stage exploration by expanding the search range of the population, while the evolutionary mutation perturbation strategy further maintains diversity through stochastic perturbations, collectively preventing the algorithm from being trapped in local optima. In contrast, the golden sine development strategy drives the exploitation phase in later iterations, guiding the search toward promising regions with a golden-section-based mechanism that ensures precise convergence. Notably, this exploration-exploitation pattern is nearly identical across all four datasets, despite their varying feature dimensions (Boston with 13 features, Ca-Bay with 8 features, Ca-Land with 8 features, and Taiwan with 6 features) and different housing market characteristics, demonstrating that the adaptive balance mechanism is robust and generalizable. In summary, the quantitative analysis presented in Fig. 13 demonstrates that the IALA algorithm effectively balances exploration and exploitation throughout the optimization process: early-stage exploration dominance prevents premature convergence, mid-stage balanced transition enables systematic search refinement, and late-stage exploitation dominance ensures accurate final convergence.

Comparison of self-built datasets

Through experiments in Sects. 5.1 and 5.2, the IALA-BPNN model is evaluated against comparable models and machine learning models in housing price prediction across four public datasets. The experiments validate the model’s success in housing price forecasting and demonstrate the performance enhancements achieved by the three improvement strategies. As shown in Sect. 5.1, BKA-BPNN ranks second only to IALA-BPNN in evaluation metrics and convergence curves, establishing itself as the second-best housing price model. Similarly, Sect. 5.2 results confirm CNN maintains its position as the top performer. In summary, experiments on the self-built dataset in this subsection compare the original BPNN, BKA-BPNN, and CNN with the IALA-BPNN model. The parameter settings of the comparison model remain as shown in Sect. 4.3.

As shown in Table 17 from the self-built dataset experiments, the IALA-BPNN model outperforms the comparison models in most key evaluation metrics, providing additional validation of its effectiveness and superiority in housing price prediction. IALA-BPNN achieves the lowest MAE (4.5717) and SMAPE (5.0934), demonstrating minimal prediction error and the best symmetric mean absolute percentage error. Additionally, its R2 value (0.8478) is the highest among all models, indicating the strongest explanatory power for the target variable and optimal goodness-of-fit. Regarding the SD metric for measuring prediction stability, IALA-BPNN (0.3904) also significantly outperforms other models, demonstrating more stable output performance. Although the CNN model holds a slight advantage in MAPE (0.5221) and runtime (25.7433s), the IALA-BPNN outperforms both BPNN and BKA-BPNN in MAPE (0.5562) while achieving a significantly lower runtime (33.6243s). This demonstrates a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency. Compared to the unoptimized BPNN, IALA-BPNN demonstrates significant improvements across all metrics. Against the second-best BKA-BPNN, IALA-BPNN leads comprehensively in MAE, R², SMAPE, and SD, highlighting the combined performance gains from its novel pinhole imaging learning, evolutionary mutation perturbation, and golden sine development strategies. In summary, IALA-BPNN demonstrates comprehensive advantages of high accuracy, stability, and practicality on the self-built dataset, confirming its potential for real-world applications.

To assess whether the performance differences between IALA-BPNN and the baseline models are statistically significant, a Bootstrap resampling procedure with 1,000 iterations was conducted on the test set predictions. The specific results are shown in the Fig. 20. The MAE of each model on the original test set is first computed. IALA-BPNN achieves the lowest MAE (1.8,637), followed by BKA-BPNN (4.0144), BPNN (4.1647), and CNN (4.8808). The Bootstrap analysis yields the following results. For the comparison between IALA-BPNN and BPNN, the mean MAE difference is 2.2941 in favor of IALA-BPNN, with a 95% confidence interval of [1.8211, 2.7934] and a p-value less than 0.001. For IALA-BPNN versus BKA-BPNN, the mean difference is 2.1433, with a 95% confidence interval of [1.7074, 2.6651] and a p-value below 0.001. For IALA-BPNN versus CNN, the mean difference is 3.0133, with a 95% confidence interval of [2.4695, 3.6203] and a p-value also below 0.001. In all three comparisons, the 95% confidence intervals of the MAE differences do not contain zero, and the p-values are all less than 0.001. These results provide strong statistical evidence that IALA-BPNN significantly outperforms BPNN, BKA-BPNN, and CNN on the self-built Shanghai housing price dataset. The findings confirm that the observed performance advantages of the proposed model are unlikely to arise from random sampling variability, thereby supporting the robustness and practical effectiveness of IALA-BPNN in real-world housing price prediction scenarios.

Conclusion

The housing price prediction is influenced by numerous factors, presenting a complex characteristic of non-linearity and multiple trends. This study aims to address the issues of insufficient population diversity, easy falling into local optima, and poor convergence quality in the ALA algorithm optimization of the BPNN. With adopting the pinhole imaging learning strategy, evolutionary mutation perturbation strategy, and golden sine development strategy for improvement, a new IALA algorithm is formed to solve the problem of housing price prediction. Subsequently, housing price prediction experiments are conducted across four public datasets: first comparing the IALA-BPNN model against the original BPNN and eight other similar optimization models (PSO, SaDE, WO, BKA, SBOA, WSO, EHO and ALA), followed by validation against four classical machine learning models (RF, SVM, LSTM, CNN). Experimental results demonstrate the IALA-BPNN model’s superior performance in housing price forecasting, validating the effectiveness of the three improvement strategies. Furthermore, the model exhibits strong competitiveness compared to machine learning models. Finally, to validate practical applicability, a Shanghai housing price dataset is constructed. By comparing the optimized models, the IALA-BPNN model’s comprehensive advantages in high accuracy, stability, and practicality are highlighted, demonstrating its real-world potential.

However, this study has certain limitations. (1) The current model does not systematically analyze the differences in the impact of various features on house prices. Low-correlation features may interfere with the model’s performance. Future research can introduce feature selection methods to optimize the feature space, enhance the model’s robustness by quantifying feature importance, and especially for prediction tasks in high-dimensional complex datasets. (2) The existing studies assume that the data is complete and free of noise. However, actual housing data often have issues such as missing values, outliers, or irregular collection intervals. This study did not verify the performance of the model in scenarios with incomplete data, which may limit its application in practice; future work should develop data cleaning methods or robust training strategies to enhance the model’s adaptability to noisy data. (3) In this study, the IALA random optimization method is adopted to replace the gradient descent method for updating the weights and biases of the BP neural network. The rationality and effectiveness of this approach are verified. However, a systematic comparison with mainstream gradient descent optimizers such as Adam, RMSProp, and SGD variants is not conducted. Due to limitations in computing resources and experimental time, this comparative experiment has not been carried out yet. Future work will include supplementary comparative experiments to more comprehensively evaluate the practical relevance and applicable scenarios of the proposed method. (4) The current study focuses on housing price prediction for a single region (Shanghai). Future research could expand to cross-regional transfer learning strategies, combining federated learning techniques to achieve collaborative optimization for multi-city housing price prediction. Additionally, exploring dynamic modeling of external factors such as climate and policy impacts is warranted.

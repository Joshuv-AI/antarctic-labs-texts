Programmable broadband ultrathin micropolarizer based on photoalignment technology
Authors: Ziling Huang, Yan Wu, Site Luo, Huihui Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A method for creating broadband ultrathin azo dye micropolarizers is introduced, utilizing photoalignment technology and liquid crystal polymers to organize azo dyes into a long-range ordered structure. Achievements include a 3.63 μm thick polarizer with an extinction ratio over 140 and polarization efficiency over 99.3% across 400–650 nm, 5 μm × 5 μm pixel pitch flexible ultrathin checkerboard arrays and 6 μm pixel pitch flexible ultrathin one-dimensional grating with a 0.45 duty ratio through mask exposure technology. After a substrate-transfer technique, the bending cycle test demonstrates the mechanical stability and durability of the broadband ultrathin flexible polarizers, highlighting their potential for widespread use in integrated flexible optoelectronic systems.

Introduction

Polarization serves as a key attribute in optical analysis, uncovering hidden aspects from surface textures to biomaterial properties and is used in biological imaging1,2, remote sensing3 and industrial monitoring4,5. Embedding microscale patterned polarizers onto CMOS sensors has been a breakthrough, creating polarization-sensitive imaging systems vital for both exploratory and applied research6–9. Achieving high performance in these systems requires polarizers with suitable absorption spectrum, high extinction ratios and elements with high-resolution, precisely aligned with the sensor’s pixels. Moreover, incorporating flexible, ultrathin micropolarizers not only streamlines system integration but also endows the system with the resilience to bending stresses, enhancing its practical robustness.

Various architectures have been explored in the domain of patterned polarizers, ranging from AlGaInP multiple quantum wire heterostructure10 to metallic wire-grid structures11–13 patterned aluminum-coated birefringent YVO4 crystals14 and liquid crystal-based micropolarizer15,16. Notably, wire-grid micropolarizer, as exemplified by the implementation of Sony’s IMX250MZR camera, have achieved commercial success. These polarizers consist of pixelated wire grid polarizer arrays aligned at orthogonal polarization states (0°, 45°, 90°, and 135°), facilitating the computation of Stokes parameters (S0, S1, S2) at the pixel level through intensity detection. The manufacturing process of these wire-grid polarizers typically involves sub-micron patterning through electron beam lithography (EBL) followed by inductively coupled plasma reactive ion etching (ICP-RIE), demanding high precision and substantial investment in capital equipment. Despite their precision, these metallic grating structures present significant fragility, posing challenges in packaging and device integration. Conversely, azo dye micropolarizers have become a focal point in current research due to the advantages conferred by photoalignment fabrication techniques. These methods enable cost-effective and large-area production.

According to the guest-host system and photoalignment fabrication technique, the azo dye is used as a guest material and the liquid crystal precursor is used as the host material. The azo dye will coordinate and arrange in the direction of the liquid crystal precursor17. A disadvantage of traditional guest-host system polarizers is the miscibility of the dichroic dye and liquid crystal precursor, which results low extinction ratio18,19. The guest-host systems polarizers achieve high-precision patterning by using a wire grid mask for UV-polarized light exposure. The process of using a wire grid mask in the visible range involves complex selective lithography processes. Jia et al. proposed 2 × N micropolarizer arrays for reconstruction accuracy with a degree of linear polarization (DoLP)20. It is difficult to process complex multi-angle linear polarizers with high resolution. Our previous work proposed a fabrication technique of flexible ultrathin high-resolution patterned polarizers by photoalignment technique and laser direct writing technique to achieve an 8 μm precision micropolarizer array. However, The AD1 dichroic polarizer only works at 450 nm because of the single azo dye21.

To solve these problems, we investigated a technique that utilizes the alignment of azo dye molecules on liquid crystal precursor (LCP) combined with contact mask exposure technology for the fabrication of programmable broadband ultrathin polarizers. LCP serves as both an alignment layer for azo dyes and a flexible substrate for micropolarization. The dichroic polarizers achieved an extinction ratio over 140 from 400 nm to 650 nm. The contact mask exposure technique enables the creation of various complex microstructures in a simple, fast, cost-effective, and efficient manner. Our fabrication process successfully fabricated 5 μm size flexible ultrathin checkerboard polarizers and 6 μm size flexible ultrathin one-dimensional (1D) gratings. The ultrathin thickness and enhanced bending toughness render them highly potential for integration into polarized imaging systems.

Experiment and method

This study presents the fabrication of a programmable broadband ultrathin polarizer based on photoalignment technology, demonstrating an extinction ratio exceeding 140 within the range of 400–650 nm. The fabrication process of the programmable broadband ultrathin polarizers can be summarized as follows. First, a clean quartz substrate was prepared (Fig. 1a). Next, a 0.5% mass fraction solution of SD1 in dimethylformamide (DMF) was spin-coated onto the quartz at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by a heating process at 100 °C for 3 min (Fig. 1b). The sample then received photoalignment using 405 nm linearly polarized light for 4 min (Fig. 1c). In the subsequent step, the sample, contacted with a mask tightly, was exposed to the same polarized light for 10 min to transfer the designed profiles (Fig. 1d). A liquid crystal precursor solution, comprising 15% RM257 and 0.75% photo-initiator 184 in toluene, was then spin-coated onto the photoalignment layer at 4000 r/min for 30 s (Fig. 1e), followed by UV light curing for 3 min. This spin-coating and curing process was repeated for creating multilayer structures. Subsequently, the dye solution was spin-coated onto the LCP layer at 4000 r/min for 30 s (Fig. 1f). Poly-dimethylsiloxane (PDMS), selected for its excellent light transmittance, mechanical flexibility, and stability, was then spin-coated onto the dye layer at 1000 r/min for 30 s (Fig. 1g) and was cured at 120 ℃ for an hour, enhancing interfacial adhesion and enabling the separation of the dye/LCP layer from the glass substrate. Finally, the sample was immersed in deionized water for 3 min to yield a programmable broadband ultrathin polarizer (Fig. 1h).

Results and discussion

To achieve a broadband ultrathin polarizer, we first investigated three dyes with different absorption wavelength, 4,4′-([1,1′-biphenyl]-4,4′-diylbis(diazene-2,1-diyl))bis(N, N-di-butylani-line) (AD1), Sudan III, and Sudan Black B, whose chemical structures are presented in Fig. 1i and the normalized absorption spectra were analyzed using a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV3600i Plus) (Fig. 1j). The absorption peaks for AD1, Sudan III, and Sudan Black B are observed at 452 nm, 518 nm, and 596 nm respectively. The number of LCP layers of the test samples is three. The AD1 flexible patterned polarizer, when analyzed at polarization angles of 0° and 90°, exhibits distinctive characteristics as shown in Fig. 2a and b. At 0°, the direction of AD1 molecules is parallel to the analyzer axis and obtained a bright state with a CIE1931 chromaticity coordinates (0.33, 0.34) (Fig. 2a), while at 90°, the contrast is reversed and obtained the minimum brightness state (Fig. 2b). The cross-sectional structure of AD1 polarizer, is smooth with a thickness of 3.81 μm, where the thickness of its LCP layers and AD1 is 3.7 μm and 0.11 μm, examined through scanning electron microscopy (SEM) in Fig. 2c. The polarized property of the manufactured dichroic linear polarizer is characterized by measuring the transmittance (T//, T⊥) using the UV-vis spectrophotometer and calculating ER and PE by Eqs. (1) and (2). The polarizer shows an extinction ratio peak of 189 at 436 nm, as shown in Fig. 2d and a polarization efficiency peck of 99.47%.

The extinction ratio remains over 140 from 400 nm to 650 nm, basically consistent in the visible spectrum. the polarization efficiency remains over 99.3% from 400 nm to 650 nm affirming the film’s suitability as a broadband linear polarizer.

The accuracy of the pattern exerts a significant impact on the application of the patterned linear polarizers. In this letter, we utilize mask-based exposure for the fabrication of patterned polarizing devices by contacting sample with mask tightly and exposing them to the 405 nm linearly polarized light together to transfer the designed pattern on the mask, as shown in Fig. 3a. The checkerboard pattern with accuracy from 5 μm to 10 μm exhibits a distinct contrast between its bright and dark states, using polarizing microscope with a 50x objective, as shown in Fig. 3b–g. It shows that an array of various dye molecules can be arranged within a 5 μm range. The micropolarizer achieves a resolution of 5 μm, comparable to the accuracy of commercially available CMOS with the pixel of 10–50 μm15. For the 5 –10 μm checkerboard micropolarizer, the average defective pixel density of checkerboard micropolarizer with the pixel from the 5 μm to 10 μm is 60, 20, 10, 8, 6, and 5 defective pixels per 5,000 pixels, respectively. The defective pixels are predominantly concentrated along the edges of the polarizer. Moreover, there is an exponential increase in the number of defective pixels as the pixel size decreases.

The one-dimensional (1D) grating is an efficient way to distribute optical signal into an array of receivers22 and a valued candidate for the metasurfaces and photonic crystals23. Utilizing mask exposure, a broadband 1D grating with an accuracy of 6 μm and a duty ratio of 0.45 exhibits spectral diffraction effects. The 1D grating is tested with a three-color laser, a quarter-wave plate to control the incident polarization state (Fig. 4a). A power meter (Thorlabs PM100D) was used to measure the intensity of each order. The optical performance post-cyclic deformation is a critical determinant of the mechanical durability of flexible polarizers. The extinction ratio at 650 nm for the flexible polarizer was tested following 1000 continuous bending cycles with a bending radius of 1 mm under conditions of 20 °C and 40% humidity. As the number of folds increased, a slight decrease in the extinction ratio was observed. However, the ratio remained above 85% within 1000 folds, as depicted in Fig. 5. This stable extinction ratio yields a pronounced contrast in light intensity, minimally impacting practical applications. And we conducted folding tests on micropolarizer with a pixel of 5 μm to test the microstructure stability and the result showed its structural stability in folding test. Flexible polarizers, characterized by their chemical and mechanical stability, offer a versatile approach to capturing light polarization and integrating into advanced, highly integrated flexible optoelectronic devices.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we investigated flexible, ultrathin micro-structured polarizers using an azo dye independent layering technique with LCP and contacting mask exposure technology. Studies on various azo dyes demonstrate that the effectiveness and universality of this technique across different azo dye organizations. The application of contact mask exposure technology enables the creation of a chessboard micropolarizer array with a precision of 5 μm and a 1D grating with a precision of 6 μm and a 0.45 duty ratio. Ultimately, the fabrication of flexible, ultrathin patterned polarizers holds polarization effects within the visible light range of 400–650 nm. Future work will involve fabricating full Stokes micropolarizers using the multiple exposure technique. This method can be applied to produce various high-precision azo dye with optical properties, thus eliminating the barriers to manufacturing high performance flexible polarizers. It opens extensive applications for flexible polarizers in integrated flexible optoelectronic systems.

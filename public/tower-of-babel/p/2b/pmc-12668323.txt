Association between family factors of school-aged children with specific learning disabilities and academic performance in Northeast Thailand: A matched case-control study
Authors: Uraiwan Tiengsomboon, Varisara Luvira
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Family plays a crucial role in the development of children with specific learning disabilities. This study aimed to examine the association between family factors in children with specific learning disabilities and their academic performance.MATERIAL AND METHOD:The research was a matched case-control study with 138 individuals in each group: primary caregivers of children with specific learning disabilities who had good academic performance and primary caregivers of children with specific learning disabilities who had low to fair academic performance. Data were collected through a questionnaire that was sent by post. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and inferential statistics, specifically through conditional logistic regression analysis. A multivariable analysis method was employed to account for confounding variables and presented as an adjusted odd ratio (ORadj).RESULTS:The study found statistically significant factors that influence the good academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities (P value < 0.05), which include extended family (ORadj = 5.52, 95% CI 2.58–11.78), good family relationships (ORadj = 2.92, 95% CI 1.26–6.75), and the primary caregiver who does not feel burdened by responsibility for the child (ORadj = 13.45, 95% CI 4.78–37.86).CONCLUSION:The academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities can be influenced by various factors related to their families and caregivers. Individuals involved in the care of children with specific learning disabilities should be attentive and knowledgeable about these factors to encourage the learning potential of these children.

Introduction

Specific learning disorder is a biologically based neurodevelopmental condition that causes learning problems. Learning difficulties may present themselves in fundamental skills, including reading, written expression, or mathematical calculations. Learning difficulties should result in significant impacts on academic or occupational performance as well as on activities of daily living.[1] Students with specific learning disabilities have a higher likelihood of grade repetition or academic failure compared to their peers without such disabilities.[2] Specific learning disability therapy relies on remedial intervention, which uses behavioral and medicinal methods to enhance functioning. A multidisciplinary team must work together to manage a child with learning disabilities.[3] Educators with expertise in supporting children with special needs are typically tasked with implementing remedial interventions.[1]

Children diagnosed with specific learning disabilities are a distinct group of individuals who experience difficulties with their learning process. However, with appropriate support and intervention, these children can achieve their learning potential and ultimately thrive in society. Academic performance is a crucial indicator for assessing the efficacy of learning and development. Limited research has been conducted on the factors associated with academic performance in children with specific learning disabilities. However, existing studies have identified subject-specific academic emotions as a significant factor in academic performance.[4] Additionally, it is interesting that studies have found that personal, familial, and school-related variables have a substantial influence on the academic performance of students with disabilities.[5] Thai children with learning disabilities obtain inclusive education. Its academic structure includes an Individualized Education Program (IEP). IEP is a crucial tool in the education system to develop, guide, and implement transition plans. These plans are designed to support students in achieving successful outcomes in postsecondary settings.[6] For skill development, children with specific learning disabilities frequently need customized education. This approach requires family participation.

Children with specific learning disabilities may behave like their typically developing peers, yet they face learning disabilities. Consequently, this misunderstanding by parents can result in negative attitudes and reactions toward their children with specific learning disabilities. These reactions may manifest as rejection, denial, overprotection, and feelings of hopelessness.[7] Insufficient knowledge, challenges in adaptation, and parental burden arise from this issue[7], leading to adverse impacts on the child. A nurturing and supportive home environment is crucial for positively influencing the learning behavior of a child with a specific learning disability.[78] Family involvement in treating specific learning disabilities is essential for supporting skill development in children with these challenges, providing an assessment of the child’s strengths and areas for improvement. Regarding the areas of improvement, it is essential to devise strategies for further development. The method of development can be incorporated based on the guidance of specialist educators. Children with specific learning disabilities must engage in extensive repetition and exercise to learn a variety of skills, continually choosing a variety of activities that are appropriate for promoting the development of children’s potential.[9]

Prior research has indicated that within families in which children have specific learning disabilities, certain parenting practices should be promoted or discouraged to enhance parent–child relationships. Effective parenting practices include involved and positive parenting.[10] A positive parent–child relationship benefits children by improving school performance, reducing psychological distress, and enhancing long-term academic and social adjustment.[11] However, a study of families with children with specific learning disabilities found that caregiving responsibilities imposed physical, personal, social, financial, and emotional burdens on most parents.[7] These burdens can result in strained relationships between the parents and the child.[12] Improving the level of social support within the family is one strategy that can effectively alleviate these challenges, thereby promoting the overall wellbeing and growth of children with learning disabilities.[13]

Based on the previous data, families are essential in nurturing the potential of children with specific learning disabilities. However, parents often face a significant burden that can interfere with their children’s learning development. Therefore, the researcher is interested in investigating the association between family factors in children with specific learning disabilities and academic performance. The family factors in this study include family characteristics (family structure, family relationships, family functioning) and primary caregivers (caregiver’s attitude toward children, caregiver burden, parenting practices). Prior studies in the field of family research have mainly focused on investigating family roles, perceptions, and burdens by families who have children with specific learning disabilities; however, there is a lack of comprehensive data regarding family factors that are associated with the academic performance of children with specific learning disabilities. The researcher gathered data in Sisaket Province, a province located in the northeastern region of Thailand. This province features a rural community and a diverse population with various ethnic groups and religious beliefs. This study investigates the associations between family factors of school-aged children with specific learning disabilities and academic performance. The family factors include family structure, family characteristics, and caregivers. These findings can subsequently be utilized to offer guidance and support to families of children with specific learning disabilities within the community.

Materials and Method

Study design and setting

The study design employed is a matched case-control study with a 1:1 ratio, where schools, grade levels, and gender were matched. This study was conducted in inclusive schools where children with specific learning disabilities (SLDs) were integrated with typically developing children. The schools included in the study were located within the primary education area of Sisaket Province. The data were collected between January and June of 2023.

Study sample and sampling

The target population is the primary caregivers who are responsible for school-aged children with specific learning disabilities in the areas of reading, writing, or numeracy, aged 6–12. These children have been diagnosed by a professional, and their information has been registered in the school’s profile for at least 6 months. The children study in an inclusive school under the Sisaket Primary Educational Service Area. This study was divided into two groups: one consisting of children who demonstrated good academic performance and another consisting of children who exhibited inadequate academic performance.

The caregivers of children in both groups entering the study had to meet inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria are being the child’s primary caregiver for at least 6 months without receiving any form of compensation, the primary caregiver being 20 years of age or older, able to communicate, understand, and respond to questions. The exclusion criteria were primary caregivers who could not participate in the interview due to illnesses or other conditions and had passed the preliminary test.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined using a formula for matched case-control studies.[13] A preliminary study was conducted on a comparable population of 30 individuals. The study’s findings were used to determine the sample size for this research with a case-control ratio of 1:1. Each group had a calculated sample size of 114 individuals. The study’s sample size was modified to consider a possible 20% participant loss. The total sample size per group was 138 individuals.

Data collection tools and technique

The data collected from the caregivers were obtained through a self-administered questionnaire. The questionnaire used in this research was developed by the researcher based on academic literature and related studies. Additionally, experts in the field of child development and child psychiatry were also consulted during the development process. The questionnaire comprised comprehensive items regarding demographic data of primary caregivers and children, family characteristics (family structure, family relationships, family functioning), and primary caregivers (caregiver’s attitude toward children, caregiver burden, parenting practices). The questionnaire in this research had a content validity value of 0.90 and a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.93.

The researcher composed a formal letter to reach out to the Office of Educational Administration and school directors in Sisaket Province Primary Educational Service Area 1-4. The purpose of the letter was to seek permission to collect research data and access student information from files. The researcher aimed to select suitable volunteers who meet the criteria to participate in the research study and subsequently randomize them for inclusion in the study. This study collected data from January 17, 2023 to May 31, 2023.

The Sisaket Provincial Primary Educational Service Area 1-4 oversees inclusive education in 785 schools, serving 13,704 students with specific learning difficulties. There are 10,087 males and 3617 females. The researcher selected the study sample by matching criteria for the exposed group (case) and randomly choosing participants from the unexposed group (control) based on criteria that included schools, gender, and grade level. This method ensured an unbiased and reliable sample selection for the study. The case group includes primary caregivers of school-age children with specific learning disabilities and good academic performance. The control group includes primary caregivers of school-age children with specific learning disabilities and low to fair academic performance.

After obtaining the target population, the researcher sent a document explaining and requesting written consent to participate in the research (consent form) to the volunteers. This document is attached to the research questionnaire envelope, which is sent by post. This allows the volunteers to independently read and comprehend the information. If individuals are interested in participating in the research, they can indicate their acceptance by signing the research consent form and completing the research questionnaire. The questionnaire can be completed once and typically takes around 20–30 minutes. Participants are under no obligation to answer the questionnaire, and their decision to do so will not affect the research participants in any way. The research questionnaire will be returned approximately 1 week after it is received. With a stamp that the researcher affixed to the envelope, it was returned to the researcher at the address specified on the envelope, without any indication of the identity of the research participants.

Ethical considerations

This study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of the Office of Human Research Ethics at Khon Kaen University (HE 651519). The study was conducted following the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. The participants were provided with a comprehensive overview of the study, including its purpose, methodology, and expected outcomes. Participants were asked to provide their informed consent before taking part as participation was voluntary. Participants had the freedom to withdraw from the study at any point and for any reason, with no impact on the treatment of the sample.

Data analysis

Analyzing the demographic data of the sample group involved utilizing descriptive analysis such as frequency, percent, mean, standard deviation, median, and interquartile range. Using inferential analysis for the relationship between various variables is analyzed in accordance with the research objectives. The STATA Version 15.1 program was used to conduct a conditional logistic regression analysis to compare the differences between case groups and control groups. The multivariable analysis method was employed to control for confounding variables.

Results

1. Demographic data of primary caregiver.

The majority of the primary caregivers of children with specific learning disabilities, those with good academic performance, and those with low to fair academic performance were female (63.1% and 74.7%, respectively). The primary caregivers of children with specific learning disabilities, who achieved good academic results, were predominantly in the age range of 25–39 years, accounting for 61.6%. The majority had a marital status, (living together) 76.1%, 51.5% had achieved a bachelor’s degree or higher, 66.7% were the mother of a child, 93.5% had an occupation, and 58% had a sufficient income. The primary caregivers of children with specific learning disabilities, who achieved low to fair academic results, were predominantly in the age range of 40–58 years, accounting for 55.1%. The majority had a marital status (living together), 61.6%, 48.5% had achieved a primary education, 51.5% were the mother of a child, 83.3% had an occupation, and 65.9% had an insufficient income [Table 1].

3. Factors affecting good academic performance of school-aged children with specific learning disabilities.

The association between family characteristics (family structure, family relationships, family functioning), primary caregivers (caregiver’s attitude toward children, caregiver burden, parenting practices), and good learning performance of school-aged children with learning disabilities was analyzed. Multivariate conditional logistic analysis, while controlling for the impact of other variables, revealed the following factors that influence the good academic performance of school-aged children with specific learning disabilities. Significant statistical factors (P value < 0.05) were extended family structure (ORadj = 5.52, 95% CI 2.58–11.78), good family relationships (ORadj = 2.92 95%, CI 1.26–6.75), and the primary caregiver feeling burdened by responsibility for the child (ORadj = 13.45 95% CI 4.78–37.86) [Table 3].

Discussion

Family structures, such as extended family, good family relationships, and caregivers not feeling burdened, were identified as factors that influence the good academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities in the study.

Research has shown that school-age children with specific learning disabilities who come from families with an extended family structure experience a significantly greater impact on their academic performance compared to children from nonextended families. This is similar to previous studies of children, which found that extended family has a positive effect on development and educational success.[1516] Due to the extended structure of a family, there are numerous family members. It is a characteristic of a family that exhibits a supportive dynamic, contributing to the wellbeing of one another through various means such as child-rearing, household chores, and financial support. This collective effort enhances the overall quality of parenting within the household.[17] Children with specific learning disabilities require various support to improve their learning abilities. Family plays a crucial role in enhancing learning.[7]

The academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities is positively influenced by the presence of good family relationships, as compared to school-age children with learning disabilities who reside in families with poor family relationships.

These findings correspond with research on children, indicating that positive familial relationships, establishing a good home environment, significantly influence children’s academic performance.[18] Loving and supportive family members enhance each other’s mental wellbeing. A strong focus on mental wellbeing is essential for fostering a positive learning environment and nurturing healthy family relationships. When children feel secure and supported at home, it greatly enhances their ability to learn.[19] The family environment was found to have a significant impact on the learning behavior of children with specific learning disabilities.[78]

The academic performance of the school is significantly influenced by the care of a primary caregiver who does not feel burdened by the care of children with specific learning disabilities. This is in accordance with the information that revealed a significant impact of caregiver burden on quality of life and psychological distress of the caregiver.[2021] If the caregiver burden is significant, it could potentially impact the quality or efficiency of caring for children with specific learning disabilities. Consequently, it has an impact on the development of children’s learning abilities.

Limitations and recommendations

This study collected data from inclusive schools in Sisaket Province. The research design is a case control study, ensuring reliable data on factors from this study. The study offers insights applicable to the care and guidance of families with children facing specific learning disabilities, focusing on family and caregiver factors that affect academic performance. This study took place in Thailand, a country with limited resources, especially regarding instructors for children with specific learning disabilities who may lack direct expertise in special education. Applications of the data from this study should take into consideration the potentially varying context.

This study revealed that the academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities is influenced by family structure, including extended family, good family relationships, and caregivers not feeling burdened. These findings can offer recommendations to families with school-age children with specific learning disabilities. For instance, the presence of several adults in the household contributes to the care of children, thereby alleviating the burden. In situations where the number of adult family members is fixed, it may be beneficial to seek assistance in caring for the children periodically, thereby alleviating the responsibilities of family members in child-rearing. The opportunity for family members to recognize the advantages of building healthy family relationships should be incorporated, which contributes positively to the academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities.

Conclusions

Extended family structure, good relationships within the family, and primary caregivers not feeling burdened by childcare are all factors that contribute to the good academic performance of school-age children with specific learning disabilities. Therefore, it is important for families, schools, organizations, and individuals responsible for the care of children with specific learning disabilities to prioritize and be cognizant of these aspects to facilitate the learning development of school-aged children with specific learning disabilities more suitably.

Organic cultivation on the Yungui plateau improves green tea flavor while reducing heavy metal accumulation
Authors: Ning Yuan, Bin Cui, Jinliang Shao, Fanqiao Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrganic (ORG) and conventional (CON) teas are produced under distinct cultivation systems that may influence tea quality and safety. However, comparative studies focusing on plateau regions remain limited. In this study, tea samples from the same cultivar cultivated under ORG and CON systems in the Yungui Plateau were comparatively analyzed in terms of physicochemical properties, heavy metal contents, and volatile profiles. Compared with CON green tea, the contents of tea polyphenols, catechin, and quercetin in ORG green tea increased by 6.95%, 75.9%, and 11.9%, respectively, whereas the contents of free amino acids and theanine decreased. The concentrations of cadmium, lead, and chromium were 52.9%–96.8% lower in ORG tea. Compared with CON tea, ORG green tea exhibited 71.9% and 171% higher fruity and woody aroma intensities, respectively, whereas the intensity of the grassy note decreased by 93.8%. Overall, organic cultivation significantly improved the flavor quality and safety of plateau green tea.Keywords: Green tea, Organic agriculture, Secondary metabolites, Heavy metal, Volatile organic compounds

Introduction

Green tea has a long history in China, which is one of the world's major green tea-producing countries and the largest producer of organic green tea (Zhen et al., 2023). China's annual organic green tea production is estimated at 220,000–250,000 tons, accounting for approximately 7.40% of the country's total tea output (Li et al., 2024). Owing to its favorable agroecological conditions and supportive government policies, Yunnan Province has become the largest organic green tea-producing region in China (Wang, Yue, et al., 2024). However, comparative information on the quality and flavor characteristics of organic and conventional teas from high-altitude regions remains limited.

Most studies indicate that organic green tea contains higher levels of tea polyphenols and lower levels of free amino acids (Kazimierczak et al., 2015; Piyasena & Hettiarachchi, 2023). These differences are particularly evident in the composition of key catechins and phenolic compounds. Specifically, the contents of epigallocatechin (EGC), epicatechin (EC), epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), flavonols and tannins are significantly higher in organic green tea than in conventionally grown green tea (Bagchi et al., 2015; Kim et al., 2015). Organic cultivation is characterized by the restricted use of synthetic pesticides and fertilisers, which may expose plants to greater biotic pressure and nutrient limitation. Previous studies have shown that such stresses can activate plant defense metabolism and stimulate the biosynthesis of phenolic compounds, including catechins and flavonoids (Kazimierczak et al., 2015; Nadeem et al., 2025). Furthermore, organic fertilisers can upregulate key genes such as DFR2, ANS3 and ANR2 in the phenylpropanoid/flavonoid pathway, thereby directing increased carbon flux towards catechin synthesis (Raza et al., 2024). Organic management often leads to nitrogen (N) deficiency, reducing the synthesis of free amino acids while promoting the accumulation of polyphenols and cellulose (Han et al., 2018). From a nutritional perspective, plants rich in catechins and flavonoids may provide health benefits owing to their potential bioactive properties (Sajid et al., 2025; Su et al., 2023).

Mineral elements and heavy metals are crucial components of green tea quality; however, no consensus has yet been reached regarding the differences in their content between organic and conventional green teas. According to studies by Cheng et al., 2020 and Zhou, Deng, et al., 2025, the content of sodium (Na) and copper (Cu) in organic green tea is generally higher than that in conventional green tea. However, their findings regarding harmful elements are inconsistent. Cheng et al., 2020 reported higher levels of nickel, arsenic (As), cadmium (Cd), and lead (Pb) in organic green tea, while Zhou, Hu, et al., 2025 found lower amounts of Pb and Cd, with only slightly higher chromium (Cr) content than that of conventional green tea. According to an analysis of 16 elements in green tea by Xu et al., 2019, only selenium, Na and zinc (Zn) were present at higher levels in organic green tea, while the remaining 13 elements—including mineral elements such as iron (Fe), magnesium (Mg) and manganese (Mn), as well as harmful elements such as Cr, Pb and Cd—were more abundant in conventional green tea. A recent study reported that organic tea contains lower levels of heavy metals and higher levels of essential minerals than conventional tea (Zhou, Deng, et al., 2025). The type of organic fertiliser used is also a significant factor: fertiliser based on pig manure tends to increase Cu and Zn levels in green tea leaves, whereas fertilisers those derived from green tea waste elevate calcium (Ca), Mg, Fe and Mn content (Zhou et al., 2023). Additionally, in conventional green tea plantations, the spraying of pesticides containing metal ions can lead to substantial accumulation of metallic elements (Hu et al., 2023). However, most existing studies were conducted in plain or hilly tea regions, and no consistent conclusion has been drawn for plateau-grown green tea, especially under the unique agro-ecological conditions of the Yungui Plateau.

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) constitute an essential aromatic property of green tea, contributing 25%–35% to its sensory profile (Li et al., 2017). To date, direct comparative studies on the flavor profiles of green teas produced under organic and conventional farming systems remain limited. Fertiliser regime has been identified as an important factor influencing tea aroma characteristics (Yang et al., 2025). Transcriptomic and metabolomic investigations have demonstrated that organic fertiliser activates phenylalanine - and fatty acid-derived metabolic pathways, elevating levels of floral and fruity volatiles—including indole, decanal, linalool, geraniol and cis-jasmone—which contributes to a more pronounced orchid-like aroma and improved sensory quality in green tea (Yang et al., 2025). Organic manure significantly increased the concentrations of key aroma compounds, including d-limonene, cis-jasmone, nonanal, linalool, cis-3-hexenyl hexanoate and cis-3-hexyl benzoate (Huang et al., 2022). Huang et al., 2025 reported that ORG fertiliser increased the concentrations of 1-hexanol, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, (E,E)-2,4-heptadienal, E-nerolidol and 3-octen-2-one in Wuyi rock tea by 56.55%, 104.44%, 64.39%, 64.10% and 48.10%, respectively, thereby significantly enhancing its aroma quality.

Besides fertiliser regime, pesticide use represents another critical management practice distinguishing organic and conventional systems, and it may also strongly influence tea aroma formation. In contrast to the extensive application of pesticides in conventional tea plantations, pesticide use is strictly prohibited in organic tea production. The application of cypermethrin resulted in a 30.7% decrease in the aroma intensity of cinnamon tea and a 52.9% reduction in the total concentration of aroma compounds in Huangyan tea (Chen et al., 2013). The concentrations of characteristic aromatic components, namely α-farnesene and nerolidol, were reduced by 24.6% and 26.8%, respectively (Chen et al., 2013). Imidacloprid application can suppress the expression of the CsHPL gene and decrease the levels of (Z)-3-hexenal, hexenal, (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol, and (Z)-3-hexenyl acetate in tea plants (Zhou, Cheng, et al., 2019). These findings underscore the significant influence of management practices on green tea flavor.

This study aims to systematically compare the physicochemical quality attributes, heavy metal accumulation, and volatile flavor components of organic and conventional green teas produced in the Yungui Plateau region. We hypothesized that (i) the greater biotic and nutrient-related stresses associated with organic cultivation may enhance the accumulation of phenolic compounds in tea leaves, and (ii) differences in cultivation practices between organic and conventional systems may lead to distinct flavor characteristics through changes in both non-volatile taste compounds and aroma-active volatiles. To test these hypotheses, green tea samples from organic and conventional tea gardens on the Yungui Plateau were collected and analyzed for secondary metabolites, minerals, heavy metals, and VOCs using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry/optical emission spectrometry (ICP-MS/OES), and headspace solid-phase microextraction gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME–GC–MS).

Materials and methods

Study area and experimental design

This study employed a field comparative trial to systematically evaluate the effects of organic (ORG) and conventional (CON) cultivation systems on tea quality under practical production conditions. Two adjacent long-term tea plantations with consistent site environmental conditions were selected as experimental plots. The two fields differed mainly in fertilization regimes and agrochemical application strategies. Samples were collected from Simao District, Puer City, Yunnan Province, China (22°45′4′′N, 100°52′43′′E), a typical region of high-altitude Puer green tea cultivation (Fig. 1). The region experiences a subtropical monsoon climate, with an annual temperature range of 15 °C–23 °C, annual precipitation ranging from 1100 to 2780 mm and a frost-free period lasting ∼315 days over the past three decades (He et al., 2024). The soil is classified as Rhodic Kandiudults according to the US Soil Taxonomy system (Giovagnotti & Giovagnotti & Giovagnotti, 1999).

Experimental site.

ORG and CON green tea gardens were first established in 1998, and the age of the tea trees is 20–25 years. The row spacing of green tea trees is 1.5 m, and the plant spacing is 0.3 m. From 2010 to 2023, the ORG green tea garden received annual fertilization rates of 58.8 kg N, 74.2 kg P2O5 and 31.7 kg K2O per hm2. In comparison, the CON green tea garden received 389 kg N, 41.0 kg P2O5 and 47.5 kg K2O per hm2 annually. Details of fertilization and farming practices for the ORG and CON green tea gardens are provided in Table S1. Other agricultural operations, such as pruning, tillage and harvesting, were similar between the two systems.

Sampling

For the ORG and CON green tea gardens, the cultivar Yun Kang 10 (Camellia sinensis var. assamica) was chosen for the study, and the tea was harvested on 13–14 July 2023. To avoid potential edge effects, 50 sampling points were systematically established in the central regions of the ORG and CON tea plantations. At each sampling point, samples were collected from three adjacent green tea trees. The picking standard for green tea plants is one bud with two leaves or one with one leaf. During sampling, tea leaves of uniform maturity and free of visible pest and disease symptoms were selected. The freshly harvested green tea underwent initial processing steps—including withering, rolling and sun drying—before being ground for experimental analysis.

Determination of major chemical constituents in green tea

Water extracts, tea polyphenols, caffeine, total free amino acids and theanine were quantified according to national standards GB/T 8305–2013, GB/T 8313–2018, GB/T 8312–2013, GB/T 8314–2013 and GB/T 23193–2017, respectively. Briefly, water extracts was determined gravimetrically after hot-water extraction, tea polyphenols were measured via the Folin-Ciocalteu colorimetric method, total free amino acids were determined by the ninhydrin colorimetric method, and caffeine and theanine were quantified by HPLC.

Determination of catechin monomers, gallic acid and flavonol content in green tea

Green tea catechin monomers, gallic acid and flavonols were detected using HPLC (Waters Alliance e2695–2998) with slight modifications (Sukhonthara & Thipnate, 2025; Wang et al., 2019): 1.00 g of ground green tea was weighed into a 50-mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 10 mL of 80% methanol and sonication for 30 min. The mixture was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 5 min, and the supernatant was transferred to a 25-mL volumetric flask. The residue was re-extracted with another 10 mL of 80% methanol, centrifuged again and the supernatants were combined. The combined extract was diluted to 25 mL with 80% methanol, and 1.0 mL of the final solution was transferred to an HPLC vial and filtered through a 0.22-μm ORG-phase membrane prior to analysis.

Chromatographic conditions: The chromatographic column used is a Waters C18 column (4.6 mm × 250 mm; 5.0 μm), with a column temperature of 30 °C and a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min. The injection volume was 10.0 μL. Mobile phase A comprised acetonitrile, and mobile phase B comprised a 0.5% aqueous acetic acid solution. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–20 min, 2%–15% A; 20–35 min, 15%–20% A; 35–45 min, 20%–30% A; 45–50 min, 30%–45% A; 50–60 min, 45% A; 60–61 min, 45%–95% A; 61–67 min, 95% A; 67–68 min, 95%–2% A; and 68–75 min, 2% A. Detection wavelengths were set at 270 and 360 nm, respectively.

Determination of mineral and hazardous element contents in green tea

Mineral and hazardous element contents were determined according to the national standard methods GB 5009.268–2025 and GB/T 30376–2013. The analytical approach was consistent with procedures commonly used for food contaminant determination and safety assessment (Hussain, 2025). Approximately 0.250 g of green tea sample was weighed into a glass digestion tube, followed by the addition of 8 mL of nitric acid for overnight predigestion at room temperature. The following day, the tubes were placed in a graphite block digester and heated sequentially—first at 120 °C for 30 min, then at 140 °C for 3 h. The temperature was ultimately raised to 170 °C and maintained until the volume had been reduced to 1–2 mL. After cooling, the digest was made up to 50 mL with high-purity water and then passed through medium-speed quantitative filter paper into a 15-mL centrifuge tube. National first-grade certified reference material GBW10049 (scallion, GSB-27) was included as a quality control material throughout the analytical procedure. The recoveries of the target elements ranged from 94.2% to 103%, indicating satisfactory analytical accuracy.

The concentrations of potassium (K), Ca, Mg and Mn in the digests were determined using ICP-OES, while Cr, As, Cd, Pb, Cu, Fe and Zn were quantified using ICP-MS.

Determination of VOCs in green tea

The VOCs in green tea were determined using GC–MS with a 7890B gas chromatograph in series with a 5977B mass spectrometer (Agilent, Palo Alto, CA, USA) equipped with an HP-Innowax capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm; 0.25 μm). After grinding the green tea, 5 g of the ground material was weighed into a headspace vial, followed by the addition of 20 μL of 14 ppm 3-nonanone (CAS# 925–78-0). The GC–MS conditions were as follows: the column box temperature was set at 40 °C for 6 min, then, increased at 4 °C/min to 200 °C and held for 5 min and ultimately increased at 15 °C/min to 250 °C and held for a further 5 min. The injection port temperature was set at 250 °C, with 99.99% pure helium as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. The transmission line temperature was 250 °C, the ion source temperature was 230 °C and the quadrupole temperature was 150 °C. The electron ionisation energy was 70 eV, and the scan mode was m/z 45–450. The injection was operated in splitless mode.

MassHunter Agilent Analysis (Qualitative Analysis 10.0, Agilent) software was used to process the original data, and the compound retention time and peak area were calculated (Song et al., 2023). The VOCs were identified through a comparison of their experimental mass spectra with entries in the National Institute of Standards and Technology MS library database (Giannetti et al., 2019). Reagents

Standard substances—including EGCG, EGC, EC, epicatechin gallate (ECG), catechin (C), gallic catechins (GC), gallic acid, kaempferol, quercetin, kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, hyperoside, rutinose and theanine (all ≥98% purity)—as well as methanol, acetonitrile, acetic acid and 3-nonanone, were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai, China).

Statistical analysis

SPSS (version 23.0, International Business Machines Corporation, USA) was used to perform independent sample t-tests and linear discriminant analysis, and data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. SIMCA (version 14.1, Umetrics, Sweden) was used for orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). The explanatory power of the model should be >0.5, and its predictive ability should be close to 1 (Worley & Powers, 2016). Origin (version 2024b, Origin Lab, USA) was used for data visualisation.

Results

Physicochemical properties of green tea

Tea polyphenol content was significantly higher in ORG green tea than in CON green tea (p < 0.001), by 6.95% (Fig. 2a). However, ORG significantly reduced the contents of total free amino acids, theanine and water extract by 14.3%, 16.2% and 2.91%, respectively, compared to CON (p < 0.01, Figs. 2b-d). The phenol-to-amino acid ratio of ORG green tea was significantly higher than that of CON green tea (p < 0.001, Fig. 2e), indicating that ORG green tea has a stronger bitter and astringent taste and lower freshness. There was no significant difference in caffeine content between the two farming modes (Fig. 2f).

Catechin monomers

Six catechin monomers in ORG and CON green teas were analyzed in the current study. The C content of ORG green teas was significantly higher than that of CON green tea by 75.9% (p < 0.001, Fig. 3a). Compared with CON green tea, ORG green tea exhibited significant reductions in EC, GC, and EGC contents (p < 0.001) and EGCG content (p < 0.05), by 22.9%, 31.7%, 29.5% and 7.93%, respectively (Figs. 3b-e). No significant difference was observed in ECG levels between ORG and CON green tea (Fig. 3f). The ester-type and non-ester catechins in ORG green tea were significantly lower than those in CON green tea, indicating that ORG green tea has lower antioxidant potential and less layered tea infusion taste compared with CON green tea (Figs. 3g-h). The catechin quality index reflects green tea tenderness. ORG green tea had a 24.2% higher catechin quality index than CON green tea, demonstrating that ORG green tea possessed a superior tenderness characteristic compared with CON green tea (Fig. 3i).

Gallic acid and flavonols

Gallic acid and flavonols are the primary components responsible for the liquor colour of green tea and play a significant role in determining its quality. In the present study, ORG green tea exhibited a markedly higher gallic acid content—72.7% greater than that of CON green tea (p < 0.001, Fig. 4a). Among the flavonols, ORG green tea exhibited a significantly higher quercetin content—11.9% greater than that of CON green tea (p < 0.001, Fig. 4b). By contrast, the contents of kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, hyperoside and rutin in CON green tea were 1.64, 0.354 and 1.71 mg/g, respectively, representing increases of 14.3%, 30.5% and 21.3% compared to ORG green tea (p < 0.001, Figs. 4c–e). No significant difference was observed in kaempferol content between ORG and CON treatments (Fig. 4f).

Mineral and heavy metal

This study investigated the contents of eight mineral elements (K, Ca, Fe, Cu, Mg, Zn, P and Mn) and four heavy metals (Cd, Pb, Cr and As) in ORG and CON green teas. The contents of K, Ca, Fe and Cu in CON green tea were 20,739, 5236, 49.1 and 8.38 mg/kg, respectively—significantly higher than those in ORG green tea by 16.0%, 23.2%, 80.2% and 8.64%, respectively (p < 0.01, Figs. 5a–d). The concentrations of Cd, Pb, and Cr in ORG green tea were 11.1, 8.62, and 47.9 μg/kg, respectively, representing reductions of 52.9%, 96.8%, and 80.8% compared with CON green tea (p < 0.001, Table 1). However, the heavy metal content of each treatment was far below the national standard limits. No significant differences in Mg, Zn, P, Mn, and As were observed between treatments (Fig. 5e–h; Table 1).

Correlation analysis

Pearson correlation analysis of 28 quality indicators of green tea revealed that catechin monomers were strongly associated with quality parameters. For instance, GC, EGC, and EC showed strong positive correlations with rutin, hyperoside, and kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, while GC and EGC were strongly positively correlations with Ca, Fe, Cd, Pb, and Cr. In contrast, tea polyphenols exhibited strong negative correlations with rutin, hyperoside, EC, and Ca. The phenol-ammonia ratio was significantly negatively correlated with GC, EGC, Fe, Cd, Pb, and Cr, while C was negatively correlated with Ca, Mn, and Cd. Additionally, Ca displayed negative correlations with quercetin and gallic acid (Fig. 6a).

Linear discriminant analysis

Based on a comparison of physicochemical differences between ORG and CON green tea leaves, a linear discriminant analysis (LDA) model was established to identify key indicators and construct a classification function. To reduce model redundancy and multicollinearity, only variables with a significance level of p < 0.05 and a variance inflation factor less than 10 were retained. Ultimately, 14 indicators were selected for establishing the LDA model. The final discriminant function obtained: F (X) = 30.185 + 0.297 X tea polyphenols − 0.860 X theanine − 0.598 X water extract − 3.148 X kaempferol-3-O-ratinoside + 1298.895 X quercetin + 7.494 X gallic acid + 0.496 X C − 0.136 X EGCG − 0.002 X Ca − 0.039 X Fe − 0.387 X Cu − 0.048 X Cd − 0.010 X Pb − 0.014 X Cr. The discriminant model demonstrates excellent classification performance. The overall accuracy rate of the original classification reached 97.8%, with ORG green tea achieving 100% accuracy and CON green tea at 95.9%. Leave-one-out cross-validation yielded an accuracy of 96.7%—only 1.1 percentage points below the original figure, confirming the model's robustness. The model demonstrated 100% sensitivity and 93.9% specificity for organic tea (Table 2). The discriminant model, which incorporates 14 indicators including tea polyphenols, shows high accuracy in classification and robust generalization, effectively distinguishing ORG from CON grown green tea.

Qualitative and quantitative analyses of VOCs

A total of 92 VOCs were identified in ORG and CON green teas, of which 62 were shared between the two. A total of 15 unique VOCs were found in each type (Fig. 7a). The compounds unique to ORG green tea were primarily alcohols, ketones and aromatic hydrocarbons, whereas those unique to CON green tea were primarily alcohols, olefins and esters. The primary categories of VOCs included alcohols (18 in ORG vs. 17 in CON), aromatic hydrocarbons (11 in ORG vs. 13 in CON), ketones (10 in ORG vs. 12 in CON), aldehydes (10 in ORG vs. 10 in CON), esters (7 in ORG vs. 7 in CON), alkanes (6 in ORG vs. 7 in CON), olefins (9 in ORG vs. 6 in CON), acids (3 in ORG vs. 3 in CON), phenols (2 in ORG vs. 1 in CON) and amides (1 in ORG vs. 1 in CON) (Table S2).

In ORG green tea, alcohols, olefins, phenols, acids, esters, and amides constituted 20.3%, 6.35%, 0.460%, 10.1%, 5.90%, and 0.780% of the total VOCs, respectively—significantly higher than the corresponding 12.8%, 3.43%, 0.220%, 8.19%, 4.79%, and 0.540% in CON green tea, with increases of 60.0%, 83.0%, 107%, 21.0%, 22.0%, and 43.0%, respectively (p < 0.05). Conversely, in CON green tea, aromatic hydrocarbons, aldehydes and alkanes accounted for 34.7%, 13.8%, and 10.5% of the total VOCs, respectively—significantly higher than the corresponding 25.8%, 10.8%, and 8.99% in ORG green tea, with increases of 36.0%, 30.0% and 18.0%, respectively (p < 0.01). Ketone compounds showed no significant difference between ORG and CON green teas (Fig. 6b; Table S2).

A total of 15 compounds were significantly enriched in ORG and CON green teas, respectively. The enriched compounds in ORG green tea were primarily alcohols and aromatic hydrocarbons, whereas those in CON green tea included aldehydes, aromatic hydrocarbons and ketones. Among the 15 VOCs enriched in ORG green tea, the relative increases compared to CON green tea ranged from 17.9% to 439% (Fig. 7c). Conversely, the 15 VOCs enriched in CON green tea showed relative increases ranging from 17.9% to 203% compared to ORG green tea (Fig. 7d).

Chemometric analysis of green tea VOCs

To enable a more precise comparison of the differences between green teas produced under ORG and CON farming systems, an OPLS-DA model was constructed, yielding the following parameters: R2X = 0.739, R2Y = 0.993 and Q2 = 0.988. During cross-validation, the R2 and Q2 values at x = 1 consistently exceeded those of the scatter points to the left, indicating no overfitting in the model (Fig. 8a). The model effectively separates ORG green teas to the left end of the y-axis and CON green teas to the right. This clear categorisation underscores significant VOC differences between ORG and CON green teas. All samples fell within the 95% confidence interval, with no significant outliers observed (Fig. 8b).

Using the variable importance in projection (VIP) scores from the OPLS-DA model, we identified 17 compounds with VIP > 1 and p < 0.05 (Fig. 8c). These 17 VOC compounds were crucial for distinguishing between ORG and CON green teas. These compounds serve as aroma markers to distinguish ORG green teas and CON green teas (Table 3).

Flavor characteristics

Through OAV analysis of identified VOCs, we found that ORG- and CON-grown green teas contain 35 compounds with OAV > 1. The synergistic interactions among these 35 VOCs constitute the aroma framework of green tea. Following OAV analysis of key VOCs, we identified 3,5-octadien-2-one, irisone, jasmone, 1-octen-3-ol, linalool, β-cyclocitral, phenylethylene and 1-methylnaphthalene as compounds with OAV > 100. These VOCs play crucial roles in shaping the flavor profile of green tea and are regarded as primary flavor compounds (Table S3).

Based on the odor descriptions of VOCs outlined on the referenced website (https://www.chemicalbook.com/), green tea can be classified into six primary aroma types: fruity, floral, raw green, earthy, fatty and woody. The cumulative aroma OAVs for ORG green tea were 220 (fruity), 18057 (floral), 338 (green), 730 (earthy), 3317 (fatty) and 22.1 (woody), respectively, while those for CON green tea were 128, 21092, 5431, 825, 2879 and 8.14, respectively. ORG green tea outperformed CON green tea in fruity, woody and fatty aromas, with OAV increases of 71.9%, 171% and 15.2%, respectively. However, the floral, raw green, and earthy aromas of ORG green tea decreased by 14.4%, 93.8%, and 11.5%, respectively (Table S4, Fig. 7b). Notably, the enhanced fruity and woody attributes in ORG green tea were mainly attributed to the presence of unique odor-active compounds, including 3,7-dimethylocta-1,5,7-trien-3-ol, δ-dodecalactone, and α-farnesene, which were detected exclusively in ORG samples. In contrast, the lower level of green/grassy notes in ORG green tea was mainly due to the detection of 2,6-nonadienal ((2E,6Z)-), a compound characterized by a strong green odor, exclusively in CON green tea. Correlation analysis revealed that tea polyphenols and free amino acids were positively associated with fruity and woody aroma notes (Fig. S1), suggesting that although higher polyphenol content in ORG green tea may increase perceived bitterness, the enhanced aroma components could partially compensate for this effect.

Discussion

Influence of farming methods on green tea quality

Quantitative analysis of free amino acids in ORG and CON green tea gardens in Jinhua, Yiwu and Zhangzhou, China, revealed that the content of total free amino acids in samples from the CON green tea garden was generally high (Han et al., 2018). Our results corroborate this view: the total free amino acid content in CON green tea was significantly higher than that in ORG green tea, and the same pattern was observed for theanine, the most abundant free amino acid in green tea (Lin et al., 2023). In our study, N application in the CON green tea garden averaged 389 kg/hm2, considerably exceeding the 58.8 kg/hm2 applied in the ORG green tea garden (Table S1). Chemical N fertilisers rapidly increase soil N levels, allowing green tea trees to quickly absorb large amounts of N and synthesise amino acids. The N in ORG fertilisers must first undergo mineralisation before it can be taken up by the green tea plant, which inherently constrains N utilisation efficiency (Wang et al., 2023). Antagonism between P and N suppresses N uptake in green tea plants, while elevated soil pH further limits the efficiency of N acquisition (Jia et al., 2024; Peng et al., 2022). The soil available P and pH in ORG green tea gardens were significantly higher than those in CON green tea gardens (Table S5), which may impair N absorption efficiency of green tea trees, resulting in a low N absorption efficiency compared to CON green tea gardens. Beyond amino acid biosynthesis, reduced N availability in ORG systems restricts the biosynthesis of acyl donors such as p-coumaroyl-CoA, consequently downregulating the activities of ester-type catechin-specific enzymes UGT84A22 and SCPL-AT, ultimately diminishing the accumulation of EGCG, ECG and GC (Wang et al., 2025). This observation aligns with the present data (Fig. 3).

Environmental factors associated with cultivation can significantly influence flavonoid biosynthesis and catechin accumulation. For instance Che et al. 2014 showed that catechin content and galloylation vary with altitude-related gradients, and Liu et al., 2018 demonstrated that temperature fluctuations and solar radiation modulate genes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis. The observed increase in C, concomitant with decreases in other monomers, likely reflects the combined effects of organic cultivation and environmental modulation of flavonoid metabolism. According to the C/N balance theory, a negative correlation exists between free amino acids and green tea polyphenols (Han et al., 2018). The present data confirm this inverse relationship. When N is abundant, green tea plants preferentially synthesise N-rich compounds (amino acids, caffeine and proteins). By contrast, N limitation shifts metabolism towards C-based secondary metabolites such as green tea polyphenols and cellulose (Brandt & Mølgaard, 2001). Polyphenol accumulation is further enhanced by environmental stress, and higher polyphenol concentrations are generally interpreted as a signature of greater plant stress (Bagchi et al., 2020; Han et al., 2018). Rutin, hyperoside, kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, quercetin, kaempferol, and gallic acid are representative green tea polyphenols that contribute significantly to antioxidant capacity (Wang et al., 2016).

Beyond their contribution to tea quality and antioxidant capacity, tea polyphenols have attracted increasing attention due to their diverse biological activities and potential functional applications (Hassan et al., 2023). Previous studies have demonstrated that tea polyphenols can serve as functional components in biomedical and nanomaterial systems owing to their redox properties and biocompatibility (Abdolahad et al., 2013). Therefore, the elevated polyphenol content observed in ORG green tea may indicate additional potential functional benefits beyond traditional quality attributes.

Consistent with previous reports that organically grown green tea is enriched in quercetin (Kazimierczak et al., 2015), our study found that ORG green tea exhibited higher concentrations of quercetin and gallic acid, accompanied by lower levels of flavonoid glycosides such as kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, rutin, and hyperoside (Fig. 4). This redistribution can be explained by a stress-mediated shift in phenylpropanoid flux: under biotic or abiotic pressures (e.g., pest attack or limited nutrient supply), plants prioritize the allocation of limited resources to ‘core’ defense/antioxidant molecules like quercetin and gallic acid, while the synthesis of their downstream glycosides is relatively down-regulated (Agati et al., 2011; Zhang et al., 2022). Consequently, quercetin and gallic acid accumulate, whereas the synthesis of their downstream glycosides (rutin, hyperoside and kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside) is relatively down-regulated.

Effects of ORG fertiliser on mineral and heavy metals levels in green tea

Environmental conditions and elemental availability influence the uptake of minerals and heavy metals by tea plants. Previous studies have shown that ORG fertilisers can reduce the availability of heavy metal ions in soil by improving the soil environment, increasing soil pH and ORG matter content and promoting the formation of carbonate or hydroxy complexes of heavy metal ions (Colombo et al., 2014). ORG matter (e.g., humic and fulvic acids) released during the decomposition of ORG fertilisers can form sparingly soluble complexes with Cu2+, thus reducing its bioavailability (Silva et al., 2016). The application of ORG fertilisers elevates soil pH, thereby inducing the precipitation of ions as ferric hydroxide and consequently diminishing Fe availability (Thomas et al., 2019).

In the present study, the ORG green tea received a cumulative 77.3 t/hm2 of ORG fertiliser between 2010 and 2023—32-fold the 2.4 t/hm2 applied to the CON (Table S1). Correspondingly, the ORG soil exhibited significantly higher pH (5.52 vs. 4.85), alkaline hydrolyzed N, available phosphorus, and available potassium than the CON soil (Table S5). These results indicate that long-term organic management substantially altered soil physicochemical properties. The higher soil pH together with ORG matter-derived ligands may reduce heavy metal bioavailability through precipitation and complexation processes, thereby limiting metal uptake by green tea.

In addition, long-term field experiments indicate that although the total concentrations of most heavy metals in soil increase with rising ORG-fertiliser inputs, their available fractions remain unchanged (Wiszniewska et al., 2016). Therefore, the lower concentrations of Cd, Pb, and Cr observed in ORG green tea are more likely attributable to reduced metal bioavailability associated with long-term improvements in soil properties, rather than solely differences in metal inputs from fertilisers.

Unlike Cd, Pb, and Cr, As concentrations did not differ significantly between ORG and CON green tea. As accumulation in plants is often influenced more strongly by geological background than by agricultural management practices (Punshon et al., 2017). Therefore, the absence of a significant treatment effect observed in this study may indicate that As accumulation was more strongly controlled by site-specific environmental conditions than by production mode. Because soil heavy metal concentrations and environmental contamination levels were not measured in the present study, the relative importance of soil background conditions and management practices remains unclear and warrants further investigation.

In addition to soil-mediated mechanisms, the correlation analysis suggests that interactions between catechins and metal elements may also contribute to the observed elemental accumulation patterns. GC and EGC, with multiple hydroxyl groups capable of binding metal ions, may influence metal reactivity and polyphenol stability. Previous studies have demonstrated that tea polyphenols can interact with iron ions and modify their redox behaviour (Akhavan et al., 2012). In this study, GC and EGC were positively correlated with Fe, Cd, Pb, and Cr (Fig. 6a) and were lower in ORG green tea than in CON green tea (Fig. 3), suggesting a potential linkage between catechin accumulation and metal content through metal–polyphenol interactions. Moreover, the substantially lower concentrations of Cd, Pb, and Cr observed in ORG green tea are of particular relevance to food safety and consumer health, as reduced heavy metal accumulation may decrease long-term dietary exposure to potentially toxic elements and thereby enhance consumer safety (Akhavan et al., 2016; Saleem et al., 2025). This preventive effect at the production stage complements ongoing technological efforts aimed at the detection and control of hazardous metal ions in biological systems (Rabiee et al., 2022). Taken together, these findings suggest that the lower heavy metal concentrations in ORG green tea are primarily associated with reduced metal bioavailability under long-term organic management, whereas the observed catechin–metal correlations indicate a potential role of metal–polyphenol interactions in shaping elemental accumulation patterns.

Effects of fertiliser application and field management on green tea flavor

Green tea is a type of unfermented tea, and its main classes of VOCs include aromatic hydrocarbons (25.8% in ORG vs. 34.7% in CON), alcohols (20.3% in ORG vs. 12.8% in CON), aldehydes (10.8% in ORG vs. 13.8% in CON) and ketones (9.68% in ORG vs. 11.1% in CON) (Table. S2). Most previous studies support these results, using HS-SPME coupled with GC–MS researchers have identified aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, and esters as the dominant VOCs in green tea (Xie et al., 2025). Even with different extraction methods, consistent results can be obtained. For example, Wang et al., 2020 used stir bar sorptive extraction combined with GC–MS and reported that alcohols, aldehydes, aromatic hydrocarbons, and esters were the primary VOCs, representing approximately 54% of the total. In ORG green tea Li et al., 2024 used simultaneous distillation–extraction combined with GC–MS and identified ketones, alcohols, aromatic hydrocarbons, and aldehydes as the main VOC components.

N plays a crucial role in regulating green tea flavor (Guo et al., 2026). However, long-term excessive N fertilization has reduced the content of benzyl alcohol and phenylethyl alcohol in green tea, thereby impairing the formation of floral and fruity aromas while enhancing green flavor notes, ultimately leading to a decline in overall aroma quality (Huang et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2022). High N increased the levels of monogalactosyl diacylglycerol (36:6) and digalactosyl diacylglycerol (36:6) in new shoots during lipid metabolism, further raising the content of hexenals and hexenols, and resulting in inferior green tea flavor (Liu et al., 2017). The annual N application rate in CON green tea gardens in this study was ∼398 kg/hm2, considerably higher than the 58.8 kg/hm2 applied in ORG green tea gardens (Table S1). The content of benzyl alcohol and phenylethyl alcohol in CON green tea with a high N application rate was lower than in ORG green tea (Table S2), and the green-note OAV of CON green tea was >16 times that of ORG green tea (Fig. 7b, Table S4). ORG fertiliser significantly enhances the floral and fruity aroma of green tea by upregulating genes related to phenylalanine and fatty acid metabolism (Yang et al., 2025). The prominent floral notes in CON green tea were mainly attributed to its higher ionone content. As ionone has an extremely low odor threshold (0.000081 mg/kg), even small changes in its concentration can strongly enhance perceived floral aroma.

The synergistic effects of N and K affects the flavor quality of green tea. The highest flavor index was obtained when the N:K ratio is 1:0.83, which increases the contents of alcohol compounds such as linalool oxides, geraniol, and benzyl alcohol. The N:K ratio in ORG green tea gardens was 1:0.54 (N 58.8 kg/hm2; K 31.7 kg/hm2), representing a significant advantage compared to the N:K ratio of 1:0.12 (N 389 kg/hm2; K 47.5 kg/hm2) observed in CON green tea gardens. ORG green tea exhibited a 283% higher content of linalool oxide than that in CON green tea (Fig. 7c), and the benzyl alcohol content showed a trend that ORG green tea was higher than CON green tea, and geraniol was detected only in ORG green tea (Table S2). These results underline the importance of potassium application in promoting the accumulation of alcohol compounds in green tea.

There was a significant positive correlation between P content and the concentrations of geraniol and linalool. Elevated P levels were associated with stronger floral aromas in green tea, whereas low P conditions favored the formation of fruity notes (Zhou, Hu, et al., 2025). High P application (112 kg P2O5 hm2) significantly decreased the abundance of several metabolites in green tea leaves, including 3-ethyl-4-methylpyrrole-2,5-dione and 3-octen-2-ol (Zhou, Liu, et al., 2019). In the present study, ORG green tea gardens received an average P application rate of 74.2 kg/hm2, significantly exceeding the 41.0 kg/hm2 applied in CON gardens (Table S1). Correspondingly, soil available P content was markedly higher (Table S5). Under high-P conditions, 3-ethyl-4-methylpyrrole-2,5-dione was below the detection limit in the ORG green tea, which corroborating the observation by Zhou, Cheng, et al. (2019) that high P suppresses the accumulation of this metabolite. Concomitantly, higher levels of geraniol and linalool oxide were observed in ORG tea, supporting the conclusion of Zhou, Hu, et al. (2025) that P supply promotes the biosynthesis of linalool and geraniol.

In addition to fertilisers, the widespread use of pesticides can disrupt the metabolism of tea plants and further affect the flavor quality (Yu et al., 2024). Glyphosate inhibits glutamine synthetase activity, induces N imbalance, promotes the synthesis of protein amino acids, suppresses the formation of non-protein amino acids, and thus reduces the freshness and taste quality of green tea (Yu & Yang, 2020). Moreover, glyphosate activates the phenylpropanoid pathway in green tea plants, redirecting the biosynthesis of catechins and certain flavonoids towards phenolic acids and lignin, which leads to a dull and unbalanced flavor profile of green tea (Yu et al., 2024). In CON green tea gardens, intensive application of pesticides including imidacloprid and glyphosate was identified as a key factor responsible for the inferior flavor of green tea. In contrast, ORG green tea gardens support higher biodiversity (Hazarika et al., 2009). The tea green leafhopper is the major pest in tea plantations. Interestingly, recent studies have demonstrated that the tea green leafhopper can act as a crucial promoter of flavor formation in green tea. Its feeding behaviour triggers defensive biochemical responses in tea plants, enhances the accumulation of secondary metabolites such as terpenes and flavonoids, and contributes to the formation of rich floral and fruity aromas in green tea (Kanu et al., 2025). Sensory evaluation was not performed in this study; therefore, the aroma differences identified by OAV analysis require further validation by trained sensory panels.

Practical applications, limitations, and future directions

The high classification accuracy of the LDA model (97.8%) highlights its potential for green tea authentication and quality-control applications. The discriminant indicators identified in this study, including tea polyphenols, catechin-related compounds, and heavy metals (Cd, Pb, and Cr), may serve as practical biomarkers for distinguishing organically and conventionally produced green tea. In addition, the enhanced phenolic and catechin profiles observed in ORG green tea suggest potential opportunities for the development of functional food and nutraceutical products (Pertiwi et al., 2025).

Despite the promising performance of the model, it was validated using leave-one-out cross-validation rather than an independent external dataset, and all samples originated from a single tea-producing region. Therefore, further studies involving more diverse geographical regions and cultivation conditions are needed to verify its robustness and transferability. Additional studies are also warranted to further validate and extend the present findings. Moreover, given that reduced concentrations of Cd, Pb, and Cr were important discriminative characteristics of ORG green tea, future research should investigate whether lower heavy metal accumulation translates into measurable long-term health benefits and reduced dietary exposure risks for consumers (Rueangsri et al., 2024).

Conclusions

This study systematically compared ORG and CON green teas from the Yungui Plateau in terms of quality attributes, aroma characteristics, safety, and authentication potential. ORG green tea contained significantly higher levels of tea polyphenols (+6.95%) but lower levels of free amino acids (−14.3%) and theanine (−16.2%), indicating a potential trade-off between bitterness/astringency and freshness. In addition, the concentrations of Cd, Pb, and Cr in ORG green tea were reduced by 52.9%–96.8% compared with CON green tea, highlighting its potential safety advantage. ORG green tea also exhibited enhanced fruity and woody aroma characteristics, which were associated with unique odor-active compounds. Furthermore, the LDA model achieved a classification accuracy of 97.8%, demonstrating strong potential for the authentication of organically produced green tea. Overall, ORG cultivation altered multiple aspects of green tea quality, resulting in both advantages and limitations, while providing distinctive compositional features that can be used for reliable product discrimination.

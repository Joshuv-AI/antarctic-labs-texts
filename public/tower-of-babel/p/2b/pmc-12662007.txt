Air quality monitoring at Universidad Iberoamericana, Mexico City: Ozone and meteorological data during the rainy and ozone seasons (Mar–Nov 2024)
Authors: Ana Laura Jiménez Chávez, D.A. Perez-DeLaMora, Milagros Santos-Moreno, Ana Vaqueiro Parra, Javier Rincón Gallardo Pose, Jorge Ángel González-Ordiano
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This dataset contains hourly-averaged measurements of ozone and meteorological variables collected between March 1 and November 30, 2024, at Universidad Iberoamericana, Mexico City (latitude: 19.372292, longitude:99.263679). The data capture conditions across both the ozone season (March–June) and the rainy season (May–October) in a high-altitude urban environment. Variables include ozone concentration (ppb), temperature ( °C), relative humidity (%), wind direction (°), wind speed (m/s), atmospheric pressure (hPa), accumulated precipitation (mm), and solar radiation (W/m²). The dataset was obtained using a calibrated low-cost sensor system, which transmitted data to the cloud for subsequent download in CSV format. This open-access dataset is intended to support research on the interactions between meteorological conditions and air quality. It can be reused in studies focused on air quality modelling, atmospheric dynamics, environmental monitoring, or urban climate in megacities with similar characteristics.

Background

Air pollution remains a critical issue in Mexico City, driven by high emissions, challenging topography, and limited atmospheric ventilation. Ozone often exceeds air quality standards during the dry season due to intense solar radiation and photochemical reactions. Although this dataset covers only one western Mexico City site, it captures these patterns during the 2024 ozone and rainy seasons, contributing valuable insight into local-scale atmospheric variability.

Building on this context, this data article presents a raw, high-resolution dataset of hourly ozone and meteorological variables collected at Universidad Iberoamericana, Mexico City, from March to November 2024. It covers both the ozone season (March–June) and the rainy season (May–October), capturing atmospheric dynamics in a high-altitude urban environment. Variables include ozone concentration (ppb), temperature ( °C), relative humidity ( %), wind direction (°), wind speed (m/s), atmospheric pressure (hPa), accumulated precipitation (mm), and solar radiation (W/m²). Data were obtained from calibrated low-cost sensors and downloaded in CSV format without post-processing.

To further understand the significance of this dataset, it is important to note that surface ozone forms through photochemical reactions involving NOx and VOCs, intensified by solar radiation and heat. The U.S. EPA considers 8-hour ozone averages above 70 ppb potentially harmful to health [[1], [2]]. In Mexico City, the ozone season officially runs from February to June, when such conditions prevail [3].

Taken together, these data offer a foundation for research on air quality, urban meteorology, and environmental health. To make the most of this resource, researchers and policymakers are encouraged to explore the dataset for analyses, model calibration, or policy assessments. The article accompanies the dataset described in Pérez et al. [4], providing context and guidance for effective reuse.

The dataset comprises hourly averaged measurements collected from March 1 to November 30, 2024, at a single monitoring site. The site is located at Universidad Iberoamericana in the Santa Fe district of western Mexico City (Fig.1b). A mix of corporate office buildings, residential towers, and major road infrastructure characterizes this area. To the west, the site is bounded by the Mexico-Toluca highway, a high-traffic corridor. To the east lie commercial developments and the Samara Shops complex. Alameda Poniente, a green urban park, is situated to the northeast. The built environment and traffic patterns in Santa Fe may influence localized air quality and meteorological conditions captured in the dataset. Santa Fe shows a mixed-use urban zone with dense development and limited natural ventilation compared to the city basin’s eastern sectors. The combination of elevated traffic intensity and topography can contribute to pollutant accumulation under stagnant meteorological conditions. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

•Location: Universidad Iberoamericana, Mexico City (19.372292, −99.263679), at an altitude of 2400 m The air quality and weather stations were installed on the rooftop of a university building, about 15 m above ground level. The site has unobstructed exposure to ambient air. The instruments were positioned away from direct emission sources. This ensures that measurements represent the general atmospheric conditions of the area rather than localized pollution.•Sensors: Measurements were obtained using two instruments manufactured by Smability S.A. de C.V. The file format is CSV (comma separated). The data structure consists of rows representing hourly measurements, each with a timestamp formatted as DD-MM-YY HH:MM (e.g., 01–03–24 14:00), using a 24-hour time. This structure is helpful for building heat maps or graphics.

Ozone–precipitation trends

The dataset includes hourly values for ozone concentration and accumulated precipitation from March to November 2024. These variables are suitable for exploring potential temporal correlations. Data users can isolate days with measurable precipitation and compare ozone levels in subsequent hours.

Seasonality of ozone formation

The dataset spans the seasonal transition from March to June 2024 and includes hourly observations of ozone concentrations along with solar radiation, temperature, wind speed, and other meteorological variables. All data are provided as hourly averages and may be used to examine seasonal variation in ozone-related parameters.

Diurnal and monthly visualization

The dataset structure allows for generation of hourly and monthly plots of ozone and meteorological parameters. Timestamped entries facilitate the creation of heatmaps or time-resolved graphs for visualizing daily and seasonal patterns.

Limitations

•While the dataset provides valuable hourly resolution and covers critical seasonal periods in Mexico City, several limitations should be noted:•The dataset is based on a single monitoring location, which may limit spatial generalization across other areas of the city.•Data were collected using calibrated low-cost sensors. Although verified through validation procedures, precision may differ from certified regulatory stations.•The sensor platform does not include measurements of NOx or VOCs, which are important ozone precursors and could enhance the analysis of ozone formation pathways.•Precipitation measurements rely on accumulation detection and may not capture brief or light rain events with high temporal precision.•The dataset spans March to November 2024, covering the ozone and rainy seasons in Mexico City. While this range includes key periods of atmospheric variability, it does not represent a complete annual cycle. The selected dates reflect both the scientific focus on these seasons and operational constraints related to sensor deployment and academic planning.

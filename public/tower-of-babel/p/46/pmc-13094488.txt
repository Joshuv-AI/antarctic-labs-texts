Transcriptome profile of Min pig muscle response to cold stress
Authors: Wentao Wang, Liang Wang, Shuo Yang, Di Liu, Dongjie Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Cold stress significantly alters gene transcription in pig muscle tissue.•Cold stress changes some biological pathways of pig muscle.•Pig muscle resist cold stress by altering vesicle transport.Keywords: Min pig, Muscle, Cold stress, Transcriptome analysis, ceRNA

Introduction

Cold stress is common in organisms living at high altitudes and in cold regions. The stability of internal environment of animals would be changed after being subjected to cold stress, and severe cases of cold stress can lead to biological damage (Cao et al., 2022). The body appeared symptoms of hypothermia, cognitive impairment, liver injury, and cardiovascular disease, leading to increased mortality (Kong et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022; Yadav et al., 2024). Thus, cold stress poses a significant threat to animal husbandry at high altitudes and in cold regions. Pigs, an important part of the livestock industry, are more susceptible to environmental temperatures than other livestock and poultry because newborn piglets lack sufficient fat reserves to provide insulation. In addition, the temperature regulation centers are not fully developed in piglets; thus, piglets do not adapt well to changes in external temperature (Zhang et al., 2022). Cold stress increases the incidence of diseases and mortality and decreases meat quality in pigs (Lesiow & Xiong, 2024).

In China, there are some indigenous pig breeds, such as the Min pig (Zhang et al., 2022a), Mashen pig (Li et al., 2023), and Tibetan (Yang et al., 2023) pig breeds, have developed cold resistance due to long-term living at high altitudes or in cold regions. Min pigs live mainly in Heilongjiang Province of northeastern China, where the average annual temperature ranges from −5 °C to 5 °C. Extremely cold weather of −20 °C to −30 °C occurs in January and December. Min pigs are raised in outdoor semi-open sheds, where they freely feed and grow normally without suffering from frostbite. According to Ai et al., the cold-resistant characteristics of the Min pig may have come from an extinct genus of pigs, and the inter-genus hybridization event occurred hundreds of thousands of years ago (Ai et al., 2015). Lin et al. demonstrated that, in Min pig and Tibetan pig, UCP3 can partially replace the function of UCP1 to induce white fat browning. Thereby increasing the energy metabolism of adipose tissue. According to the gene sequence of UCP3, pigs can be divided into cold-resistant and non-cold-resistant varieties (Lin et al., 2017). In addition, the β3-adrenergic receptor (ADRB3) induces white fat browning in Min pigs when it was suffered cold stimulation (Zhu et al., 2024). Guo et al. conducted a multi-omics analysis of epigenetic responses to cold stimulation in two different tissues of Min and Enshi black pigs. The H3K27ac signal in the diencephalon of Min pigs changed dramatically, while in the adipose tissue changed slightly; the opposite results were observed in Enshi black pigs (Guo et al., 2024). Chronic cold stress induces intestinal mucosal injury in Large White pigs, and supplementing with an appropriate amount of glucose can alleviate this injury. However, intestinal mucosal injury does not occur under the same experimental conditions in Min pigs (Sun et al., 2022). Due to these complex effects, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying cold stress in Min pigs are unclear.

Brown adipose tissue (BAT), cutaneous blood flow, and skeletal muscle are important thermoeffectors (Mota & Madden, 2024). BAT does not exist in birds and pigs. Thus, for these two animals, the non-shivering thermogenesis (NST) of muscles is more important than that of adipose. Muscles as thermogenic organs appeared in vertebrates much earlier than BAT appeared in eukaryotic mammals (Bal & Periasamy, 2020). It is widely believed that the uncoupling of sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase (SERCA) involving sarcolipin (SLN) is the basis for NST in skeletal muscles (Rowland et al., 2015). Bardova et al. suggested that the NST of muscels is likely due to the body’s need to adapt to external environmental pressures (Bardova et al., 2024). Adrenergic NST is insufficient in BAT; thus, NST in skeletal muscle may be adaptively augmented (Janovska et al., 2023). Thus, we hypothesize that pig skeletal muscle plays an important role in coping with environmental stress caused by cold stress. In our study, one group of Min piglets experienced 3days and 4 °C of low temperature cold stress, another group acted as control group with 25 °C normal temperature. Based on the transcriptome sequencing results of the longest dorsal muscle in the experimental and control groups, the differentially expressed (DE) lncRNA, circRNA, and miRNA and the enriched pathways and interaction relationships were analyzed to determine the transcriptional response of pig muscle to cold stress. This result reveals the muscle transcriptome changes of a cold resistant pig breed under cold stress, providing theoretical support for the future cultivation of cold resistant pig breeds.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and preparation

Six female Min piglets with similar weight (5.12 ± 0.46 kg) at 30 days were randomly divided into two groups, one group is control group (25 °C, n = 3) and another group is cold stress (4 °C, n = 3) group. Sufficient water and feed were provided. After housing in respiratory metabolism cages for 3 days at 25 °C or 4 °C (Table S1), the pigs were slaughtered. The longest dorsal muscle was collected and rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen (−196 °C). The total RNA of each sample was extracted using a TRIzol® Reagent Kit (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). A Qubit RNA Assay Kit (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used quantify the total RNA, and the rRNA was removed using a Ribo-Zero™ Magnetic Kit (Epicentre, Madison, WI, USA). Whole transcriptome sequencing was performed by Novogene Technology Co., Ltd.

Analysis of mRNA and lncRNA in Min pig muscle after cold stress

The raw reads is confirmed as a clean reads after screening according to the following criteria: sequences containing adapter reads; more unknown nucleotides exceeding 10%; reads containing bases with Qphred score ≤ 5 and accounting for >50% of total bases. The clean reads were then matched with the Sus scrofa (v11.1) genome sequence using HISAT2 (v2.2.1). StringTie (v1.3.3) was used to assemble the aligned reads and reconstruct the transcriptome. The assembled transcriptome was compared with the known annotation file of the reference genome, and known mRNAs and lncRNAs were filtered out, leaving unknown transcriptomes. Multiple coding potential prediction software (CPC2 (v3.2.0), CNCI, CPAT, and PFAM (v1.3)) were used for joint analysis. Only transcriptomes that are jointly identified as "non-coding" by all software were retained as novel mRNA and lncRNAs. Software DESeq2 (R-3.1.2) was uesed to screened the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and lncRNAs between RT and CS groups according to the criteria of |log2FC| > 1 and P < 0.05. The functions of DE lncRNAs were predicted by their target genes. Genes located in the upstream or downstream 100 kb interval of lncRNA are defined as cis-regulated targent genes. The Spearman correlation coefficient between lncRNAs and genes expression levels greater than 0.9 were defined as transregulated target genes.

Analysis of miRNA in Min pig muscle after cold stress

After removing the 3′ adapter and garbage sequence from the raw data, clean reads were obtained with a base length of 18–25 nt. Bowtie (v2.0.6) was used to align the clean read to the reference genome of pigs (v11.1). DESeq2 (v R-3.1.2) was used for standardization and differentiation analysis to identify miRNAs with significant changes in expression between RT and CS groups. The threshold for significant DE miRNAs was set as |log2FC| > 1 and P < 0.05.

Analysis of circRNA in Min pig muscle after cold stress

Perform quality control on the offline sequencing data, remove adapters and various low-quality sequences. Hisat2 (v2.0.5) was used to align clean reads with the reference genome of pigs. Find_circ and CIRI2 (v2.0.5) were used to identify circRNAs and construct circRNA reference sequences. When using find_circ for circRNA identification, first, 20 nt anchor sequences were extracted from both ends of reads that had not been aligned to the reference sequence. Each pair of anchor sequences was then aligned to the reference sequence again. If the 5′ end of the anchor sequence aligned to the reference sequence, the 3′ end of the anchor sequence aligned upstream of this site, and there was a splicing site (GT-AG) between the two sequences, this read was considered as a candidate circRNA. Finally, candidate circRNAs with a read count of ≥2 were identified as circRNAs. Using the CIRI2 script, input the SAM file generated after sequence alignment, the reference genome FASTA file, and an optional gene annotation GTF file to predict circRNAs. The constructed circRNA sequence was used as a reference genome and Hisat2 was used to align again to screen for high confidence reads from circRNA. Count the expression of circRNA and annotate the circRNA information. DESeq2 (v R-3.1.2) was used to performe DE circRNAs between RT and GC groups. The threshold for significant DE circRNAs was set as |log2FC| > 1 and P < 0.05.

GO terms and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of mRNA and ncRNA

Perform GO and KEGG enrichment analysis on the DEGs, target genes of lncRNA and miRNA, and source genes of circRNA. ClusterProfiler (v R-3.2.4) was used to GO analysis and Kobas (v 2.0) was used to KEGG analysis. The significance criteria were all P < 0.05.

CeRNA network construction of lncRNA/miRNA–mRNA

In order to construct an lncRNA/miRNA-mRNA regulatory network, a joint analysis was conducted on DE lncRNA, miRNA, and mRNA. MiRcode database was used to predict miRNAs that interact with DElncRNAs. MiRWalk, Target scan, and miRDB databases were used to predict the target genes of miRNA (binding probability: 0.95; binding site position: 3 '- UTR). Cytoscape (v 3.8) was used to construct the lncRNA/miRNA-mRNA regulatory network and the results were graphically displayed.

RT-qPCR validation and statistical analysis

To verify the sequencing results, random mRNA, lncRNA, miRNAs, and circRNA in the datasets were validated using qRT-PCR with U6 as the housekeeping gene. The selected up/down-regulated ncRNAs and mRNAs were verified using specific primers and conditions (Table 1). 2–∆∆CT method was used to quantify the genes expression level. Student’s t-tests were conducted with GraphPad Prism (v 8.0) software to compare the differences between RT group and CS group (P < 0.05).

Results

Identification of DE mRNAs between the CS and RT groups

To assess mRNA expression levels in the CS and RT groups, six mRNA libraries were constructed. Compared with the RT group, there were 711 DEGs in the CS group (P < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1), including 346 up-regulated genes and 365 down-regulated genes, (Fig. 1A and Table S2). Hierarchical cluster analysis indicated that some DEGs regulate the same pathway or possess comparable functions (Fig. 1B). The GO analysis of DEGs showed that forty-six GO terms with P values < 0.05 were identified and existed three ontology of GO (Fig. 1C and Table S3). The enriched biological processes included proteolysis, oxidation–reduction, cell adhesion, and biological adhesion. The enriched molecular functions included metallopeptidase activity, endopeptidase activity, peptidase activity, and cofactor binding. The enriched cellular components included the extracellular region, proteasome complex, and endopeptidase complex. The DEGs were aligned within the KEGG pathway database. The proteasome, drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, protein digestion and absorption, and MAPK signaling pathways were enriched (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1D and Table S4).

Functional analysis of DE lncRNAs between the CS and RT groups

A lot of novel lncRNAs were found and they were classified into four types (Fig. 2A). To ensure that the novel lncRNAs met the general characteristics, the exon numbers, transcript lengths, and ORF lengths were compared between the novel lncRNAs and the annotated mRNA. The exon numbers of the novel lncRNAs were similar to the exon numbers of the annotated lncRNAs but were significantly lower than the exon numbers of the mRNAs. The transcript lengths were predominantly 1000 bp, and the ORFs were mainly 200–300 bp, which are shorter than the ORFs of the mRNAs (Fig. 2B). The DE lncRNAs between CS and RT groups included 95 up-regulated and 84 down-regulated lncRNAs (Fig. 2C and Table S5). Target genes were predicted for DE lncRNAs, and the functional enrichment analysis of DE lncRNAs was predicted using these genes. For the cis-target genes, 22 GO terms were identified (Fig. 2D and Table S6). The enriched BPs included the defense response, immune response, immune system process, and protein phosphorylation. The enriched CCs included the extracellular region, cell periphery, and supramolecular complex. The enriched MFs included cytokine receptor binding, signaling receptor binding, and nucleotidyltransferase activity. Sixteen KEGG pathways were enriched by the DE lncRNAs (P < 0.05), including the Thyroid hormone signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, and Cell cycle and Apoptosis (Fig. 2E and Table S7). Thirty GOs were extracted for the trans-target genes (Fig. 2F and Table S8). The enriched BPs included the cellular catabolic process, catabolic process, and cellular protein catabolic process. The enriched CCs included proteasome core complex, proteasome complex, and endopeptidase complex. The enriched MFs included cation channel activity, oxidoreductase activity, and ATPase activity. Fifteen KEGG pathways (P < 0.05) were enriched by the DE lncRNAs, including Proteasome, Protein digestion and absorption, DNA replication, and Nucleotide excision repair (Fig. 2 G and Table S9).

Overview of the DE circRNA between the CS and RT groups

CircRNA was a special type of ncRNA with a closed circular sturcture. The profile of circRNAs in the rRNA-depleted muscle tissue obtained from the CS and RT groups. The sequencing results revealed 13,279 circRNAs, which were widely distributed on nineteen pairs of chromosomes in pigs. If classification based on their sources, the circRNAs from exonic regions occupied the highest proportion (92.85%); then the circRNAs from intronic and intergenic regions (4.34% and ∼2.81%, respectively). To identify the DE circRNAs involved in cold stress, the expression patterns in the CS and RT groups were compared. The 127 DE circRNAs included 60 up-regulated and 67 down-regulated genes (Fig. 3A, B, and Table S10). The GO analysis identified 30 BPs, including the regulation of RNA biosynthetic process, biosynthetic process, and macromolecule biosynthetic process. The dominant CC was the nucleus, and the dominant MFs were cation binding, metal ion binding, and zinc ion binding (Fig. 3C and Table S11). Pathway analysis based on DE circRNAs revealed significant changes in Osteoclast differentiation and B cell receptor signaling pathways (Fig. 3D and Table S12).

Overview of the DE miRNA between the CS and RT groups

RNA sequencing was performed to elucidate the roles of DE miRNAs in the CS group. The sequenced small RNAs included 55.1% known miRNAs and 0.28% novel miRNAs (Fig. 4A). To identify DE miRNAs involved in cold stress, the expression patterns between the CS and RT groups were compared. The 32 DE miRNAs included 9 up-regulated and 23 down-regulated genes (Fig. 4B and Table S13). DE miRNAs targeted 2571 genes. GO analysis revealed 62 BPs, including phosphorylation and protein phosphorylation. The dominant CC was the nucleus, and the dominant MFs included the nucleus, membrane-enclosed lumen, kinase activity, and phosphotransferase activity (Fig. 4C and Table S14). The cell adhesion molecules, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, and Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy pathways were the dominant functions of the DE miRNAs (Fig. 4D and Table S15).

CeRNA network construction

The ceRNA regulatory mechanism achieves precise regulation of gene expression by competitively binding to miRNA. It reveals another complex pattern of gene expression regulation beyond the miRNA regulatory network. In this experiment, lncRNA/miRNA–mRNA network was developed. Eleven lncRNA/miRNA–mRNA relationship pairs were identified, involving nine mRNAs (Fig. 5). Ssc-miR-370, ssc-miR-133a-3p, and ssc-miR-532–3p may play important roles in regulating responses to cold stress, especially ssc-miR-370, which also participates in the regulation of RFX4, TTC9B, and HSPB1 gene expressions.

Verification of complete transcriptome sequencing results

To validate the sequencing results, the qRT-PCR was conducted using the same samples for sequencing. Two genes and four ncRNAs were selected randomly, including OTOR, CST6, lncRNA: ENSSSCG00040062468, lncRNA: ENSSSCG00040011712, miR-196a, and miR-192 verification. The results showed that the experimental results were basically consistent with the sequencing results (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Due to limited sample availability, this study only set up three biological replicates. By strictly controlling experimental conditions and utilizing a high-sensitivity detection platform, it minimized technical variability as much as possible. However, the results still have certain limitations. In subsequent studies, it will consider appropriately increasing the sample size to compensate for this limitation. Omics is a systems biology method involving the comprehensive study of specific molecular populations within an organism. This research approach avoids the limitations of traditional single-molecule studies and provides a new perspective on life sciences. MiRNA, lncRNA, and circRNA are three common non-coding RNAs that compete or collaborate to regulate mRNA transcription and expression, playing important roles in regulating body temperature (Ingelsosn-Filpula et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021). For example, miR-669a-5p regulats the differentiation of mice adipocytes under cold stress and plays a role in fat browing (Tan et al., 2022). LncRNA AK029592 can sponge miR-199a-5p in adipocytes to stimulate thermogenic gene expression (Hong et al., 2024). Although the ncRNA expression profile in low-temperature environments has been studied, none of the studies involved large livestock, such as pigs. So, the expression profile of ncRNA in muscle from Min pigs subjected to cold stress was analyzed.

Pigs cannot form brown fat in the traditional sense due to a mutation in the UCP1 gene. Nevertheless, UCP3 may compensate for the lack of UCP1, allowing pigs to form beige fat to resist cold (Guo et al., 2024). In addition to adipose tissue, muscle tissue is an important organ for regulating heat production in mammals. Muscle tissue can regulate body heat production via shivering thermogenesis and non-shivering thermogenesis (Pani et al., 2023). Pigs have a high muscle content, and muscle may be a more important thermogenic organ than adipose tissue. However, according to current research reports, the mechanism of heat production in pig muscle tissue under cold conditions is somewhat controversial. The widely accepted SERCA regulation mode has not been validated in pig muscle tissue (Bal et al., 2018), and heat production in cold-resistant and non-cold-resistant pig breeds may be significantly different (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023). Therefore, temperature regulation in pig muscle tissue exposed to cold environments requires more investigation.

Many factors influence the selection of cold treatment time, such as experimental objectives, pig weight, and age. Therefore, researchers choose different treatment time, such as 48 h (Yang et al., 2021), 4 days (Li et al., 2023), 21d (Sun et al., 2022) and 30 d (Huang et al., 2025). In this study, since the experimental material was young piglets, a 3-day cold treatment time was selected. After subjecting piglets to 3 days of cold stimulation, significant changes in mRNAs and ncRNAs were observed in the longest dorsal muscle. The genes of down-regulated corresponding to mRNAs, circRNAs, and miRNAs, but not lncRNAs, was higher than the genes of up-regulated, indicating that cold stress tended to inhibit gene expression. This finding is inconsistent with our previous findings that more genes are activated under acute cold stress and chronic low-temperature acclimation (Zhang et al., 2022). However, low-temperature (35 °C) induction in cultured myoblasts resulted in more down-regulated genes compared with the standard culture temperature (37 °C) (Sarais et al., 2023).

Gene changes after cold stimulation are complex and influenced by various factors, including breed, age, duration and intensity of cold stimulation, and gender (Coughlin et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2025). Breed is an important factor affecting the cold resistance of pigs. For instance, pig breeds living in cold regions generally exhibit more cold-resistant characteristics compared to those living in tropical regions. Indigenous pig breeds were generally more adaptable to cold environments than commercial pig breeds such as Yorkshine and Landrace. Currently, the mechanisms by which pigs resist cold environments were not fully elucidated, but it was generally believed that there were differences between cold-resistant and sensitive pig breeds. For example, under the same cold stimulus, cold-resistant pig breeds adapt to cold stress through thermogenesis, energy metabolism, mitochondrial activity, and immune response, while Yorkshine primarily respond to cold stress through thermogenesis and glycolipid energy metabolism (Li et al., 2023).

According to the pathway analysis of the DEGs, the proteasome pathway of skeletal muscle was significantly activated in response to cold stress. PSMC3, PSME4, PSMC4, PSMD3, PSMA7, PSMA1, PSMD2, PSMD11, PSMB3, PSMB4, and PSMC2 of the proteasome pathway were all significantly up-regulated. Thus, the degradation of proteins is enhanced in response to cold stress. Proteasomes are critical for maintaining proteostasis, influencing stress resilience, lifespan, and thermal adaptability. Cold stress may up-regulate the expression of proteasome pathway genes in various organisms, from Caenorhabditis elegans to Gracilariopsis lemaneiformis to model mice (Dubey et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2023). In addition to the proteasome pathway, cellular metabolism, proliferation, differentiation, and tissue repair pathways were altered in response to cold stress. Furthermore, the AMPK and PPAR metabolic pathways, MAPK and PI3K Akt proliferation-related pathways, and TGF-β and renin-angiotensin system inflammatory and fibrosis-related pathways were regulated (Table S3).

Non-coding RNA plays an important role in regulating mRNA transcription. Thus, analyzing the target genes of ncRNA elucidates the affected biological functions. Based on their target genes, cold stress-induced pathways related to the nervous system (Parkinson’s disease and neurodegeneration), immune system (IL-17 signaling pathway), energy balance and lipid metabolism (Fat digestion and absorption), cell cycle (Necroptosis), DNA stability (DNA replication, nucleotide excision repair), and the endocrine system (thyroid hormone signaling pathway) are involved in the response to stress. This is consistent with the symptoms of cognitive decline, motor dysfunction, memory loss, and muscle atrophy that occur in response to cold stimulation (Akan et al., 2023). Programmed necrosis and changes in DNA stability can accelerate cellular aging or apoptosis. At the same time, energy metabolism is significantly changed; consumption is increased because the body needs to consume more energy substances to maintain a constant body temperature (Feyera et al., 2023).

The construction of the ceRNA network enables us to gain a more intuitive understanding of the regulatory relationships among lncRNA, miRNA and mRNA under cold stress. A regulatory pathway related to vesicle transport was discovered. A key gene in this pathway is YKT6. Ssc-miR-6782–3p inhibits YKT6 expression, and lncRNA ENSSSCT00040014532 competes with ssc-miR-6782–3p for binding. YKT6 is highly conserved in eukaryotes, primarily involved in vesicle transport, cell membrane fusion, and the formation and release of exosomes (Zhang et al., 2024). Our previous research involving whole genome sequence analysis of the Min pig demonstrated that the vesicular transport pathway of Min pigs is subject to natural selection (Zhang et al., 2022b). Vesicles, as crucial carriers of intercellular communication, were selectively loaded with specific bioactive molecules (such as miRNA and proteins) under cold stress. These vesicles can be released from activated tissues (such as brown adipose tissue) and reach distant organs (such as liver) through body fluid circulation, enabling cross-tissue information transmission and coordinating systemic metabolic (Xu et al., 2023). Ssc-miR-6782–3p plays an important regulatory role in intestinal stress caused by piglet weaning (Jang & Lee, 2021). The changes in intracellular vesicle transport in response to cold stress may be induced by increased protein degradation and alterations in lipid metabolism (Shimoyama et al., 2020; Tomanek, 2014).

A more complex ceRNA regulatory network involves five genes, including RFX4, TTC9B, and HSPB1; three miRNAs, including ssc-miR-370 and ssc-miR-133a-3p; and four lncRNAs, including ENSSSCT00040061639. RFX4 belongs to the regulatory factor X family, the main function of this family member is to participate in the regulation of the body’s adaptability to the environment (Zhao et al., 2024). HSPB1 belongs to the HSP20 family. It can respond differently to different types of cellular stress. For example, it acts as an antioxidant during oxidative stress, but can also act as an anti apoptotic agent during chemical stress (Liang et al., 2023; Sluzala et al., 2025). Ssc-miR-133a-3p belongs to the MyomiRsis and highly expressed in pig muscle (Daza et al., 2017; Zuo et al., 2015). Ssc-miR-133a-3p is involved in the regulation of subcutaneous fat development in pigs via the PPAR signaling pathway (Zhang et al., 2022c). The construction of the ceRNA network is based on the results of bioinformatics analysis and exhibits obvious predictive power. Subsequent experiments will focus on the ENSSSCT00040061639-ssc-miR-370/ssc-miR-133a-3p-YKT6 regulatory axis to verify whether they conforms to the “ceRNA competitive adsorption” mechanism.

Conclusion

The expression profiles of complete transcriptome in Min pig skeletal muscle after cold stress were described in this study. Under a 3-day cold stress, more mRNA and ncRNA expression were inhibited in the muscle of Min pig. Combined with previous studies, we screened three lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA networks. Cold stress changed the proteasome, nervous system, cell cycle, and DNA stability pathways. These findings will provide theoretical support for further research on the adaptation mechanism of Min pig muscle tissue under cold stress.

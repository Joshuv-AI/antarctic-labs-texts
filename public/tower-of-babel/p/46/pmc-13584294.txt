Comparative efficacy of postoperative adjuvant chemoradiotherapy versus no chemoradiotherapy in the treatment of pancreatic adenocarcinoma
Authors: Bixin Ren, Qi Guo, Jiayan Ma, Wenlang Zhu, Yong Wang, Zhengyang Feng, Yuntian Shen, Yaqun Zhu, Liwei Xie, Qiliang Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe benefit of adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (CRT) for pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) is debated. This study aimed to elucidate the impact of adjuvant CRT on survival outcomes.MethodsWe searched major medical databases for clinical and retrospective studies comparing postoperative CRT vs. no CRT in PAAD. Data were synthesized using RevMan 5.4 and Stata 17.0 software. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for overall survival (OS), local recurrence, and distant metastasis.ResultsOur analysis included 19 studies involving 4,581 patients. Adjuvant CRT was associated with a significant improvement in 2-year OS compared to no CRT (OR = 0.63, 95% CI: 0.53–0.76, P < 0.001). This benefit was consistent in subgroup analyses compared to chemotherapy alone (OR = 0.54, P = 0.0003) and the observation group (OR = 0.61, P < 0.001). The 5-year OS analysis showed a non-significant trend favoring CRT (OR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.43–1.02, P = 0.06). In subgroup analyses, CRT was associated with a significant survival benefit compared with observation alone (OR = 0.67, 95% CI: 0.47–0.97, P = 0.03), but not compared with chemotherapy alone (OR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.06–5.46, P = 0.62). Notably, patients with high-risk features, including lymph node-positive (2-year OS: OR = 0.47, P < 0.001; 5-year OS: OR = 0.53, P < 0.001) and margin-positive disease (2-year OS: OR = 0.45, P < 0.001), derived substantial survival benefits from CRT. Furthermore, CRT was significantly associated with reduced local recurrence rates (OR = 0.27, P = 0.008) but did not affect the incidence of distant metastasis (OR = 1.11, P = 0.72).ConclusionsThis study shows that adjuvant CRT significantly improves 2-year OS and local tumor control in resected PAAD, especially for patients with lymph node metastasis. However, its effect on long-term (5-year) survival is limited, and it was not associated with distant metastasis. These findings support the selective use of adjuvant CRT for local control in high-risk patient subgroups, such as those with positive lymph nodes or positive surgical margins. Further research is needed to prospectively validate these findings and identify patients most likely to benefit.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16408-1.

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer is a malignant tumor originating from the pancreatic epithelium and endocrine cells, with diverse pathological types. Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) is the most common type, accounting for 80–90% of cases, while other types are relatively rare. PAAD is projected to account for approximately 67,530 new cases in the United States in 2026 [1]. Upon diagnosis, the clinical presentation of PAAD varies among patients: 20% are found with resectable tumors, 30% are diagnosed with locally advanced tumors that are inoperable due to arterial involvement, and a significant 50% present with metastatic disease [2]. Despite the curative potential of surgery, PAAD has a poor prognosis. Its mortality rate is projected to rise, moving from the third to the second highest among all cancers in the next two decades [3].

The latest NCCN guidelines identify chemotherapy as the cornerstone of adjuvant therapy for pancreatic cancer. Adjuvant radiotherapy is not mandatory, the guidelines recommend individualized assessment to determine if consolidative chemoradiotherapy (CRT) is appropriate. Research indicates that a portion of PAAD patients experience only local recurrence without evidence of systemic metastases at post-mortem examination. This suggests that there may be a subset of patients who could potentially benefit from enhanced survival through improved local tumor control via radiotherapy; however, evidence to substantiate this hypothesis is currently insufficient [4]. Numerous studies have indicated the advantages of adjuvant radiation, yet none have specifically investigated its efficacy in the context of margin-positive/negative and/or node-positive/negative subsets of pancreatic cancer patients, a focus that is critical to contemporary clinical practice [5–8].

Hence, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to examine the application of CRT in patients with PAAD and to elucidate its impact on overall survival (OS) and the risk of relapse, encompassing both local recurrence and distant metastasis rates. To our knowledge, no recent meta-analysis has assessed the influence of postoperative adjuvant CRT on these critical endpoints within this patient cohort. Our investigation highlights the significant impact of CRT on survival and its role in reducing the incidence of both local recurrence and distant metastasis. Identifying which patients benefit from CRT and defining the specific subgroups that respond to it are crucial for designing a prospective, randomized trial for this aggressive malignancy. Our findings seek to provide meaningful evidence to inform the role of adjuvant radiation therapy in PAAD management.

Materials and methods

Literature search strategy

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [9]. The PRISMA_2020_checklist was presented in Table S1. A comprehensive literature search was performed in PubMed, EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library from their inception until October 2025, restricting the scope to human studies. We conducted a systematic literature search using Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms, including “Pancreatic Neoplasms”, “Adenocarcinoma, Pancreatic”, and “Carcinoma, Pancreatic Ductal” in conjunction with “CRT, Adjuvant” and “Radiotherapy, Adjuvant.” These were supplemented with corresponding free-text keywords to ensure a comprehensive search. To reduce the risk of missing relevant studies, we manually screened the reference lists of all included studies and relevant review articles.

Selection criteria

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) All included studies enrolled patients with pathologically confirmed pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Given that pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) accounts for > 90% of pancreatic adenocarcinoma cases, the study populations were considered representative of PDAC. For studies that included both pancreatic and periampullary cancers, only data from the pancreatic cancer subgroups were extracted; (2) studies comparing postoperative adjuvant CRT (experimental arm: surgical resection followed by CRT) with no CRT (control arm: surgical resection followed by chemotherapy alone or observation only); (3) studies reporting at least one outcome of interest (2- or 5-year OS, local recurrence, or distant metastasis); (4) studies published in peer-reviewed journals in English.

Studies were excluded if they met the following criteria: (1) non-primary research (e.g., reviews, meta-analyses, editorials, conference abstracts) or preclinical studies (cell line or animal model-based); (2) publications with overlapping patient cohorts or duplicate datasets; (3) insufficient methodological details regarding sample processing and analysis procedures; (4) inadequate data to support meaningful quantitative synthesis; (5) non-English language publications.

Data source and collection process

To minimize subjective influences, two independent authors (BR and QG) extracted the data. In the event of discrepancies, the literature was re-examined, and any conflicts were resolved through collaborative discussion among the team members. The following information was systematically collected: (1) Patient demographics and clinical characteristics at initial inclusion, including gender, age, tumor size or T-stage, surgical margin status, lymph node metastasis, vascular invasion, pathological grade, and postoperative complications; (2) Study-related parameters, such as study period, study type, tumor stage, final number of patients included in the analysis, positive margin rate, lymph node positivity rate, radiation dose, key chemotherapeutic agents, and follow-up duration. The endpoints of interest included 2 and 5-year OS, local recurrence rate, and distant metastasis rate. Distant metastasis was defined as the presence of metastases in organs such as the liver, peritoneum, or lungs, as confirmed by imaging or pathology in the included studies. In instances where 2 and 5-year survival data were not explicitly provided, it was extrapolated from survival curves. In instances where multiple studies emanated from the same institution, the study with the greater patient population was preferentially selected. In cases where the patient numbers were comparable, the most recently published study was chosen. Studies utilizing database sources were excluded to avoid data overlap.

Quality assessment

Given that the majority of studies incorporated into this meta-analysis were non-randomized, the quality of each study was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale [10]. Studies were classified as high-quality if they scored 8–9, moderate-quality if they scored 6–7, and low-quality if they scored 5 or below. Following the Cochrane Handbook recommendations, the authors reached consensus to exclude low-quality studies from the aggregate analyses.

Data synthesis and statistical analyses

The comparison of 2- and 5-year OS rates between the CRT and no CRT groups was quantified using odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Given that the control arm included both patients receiving chemotherapy alone and those under observation, further subgroup analyses were conducted to compare CRT against chemotherapy alone and against observation. Additionally, a subgroup analysis was performed based on the period before and after 2010, due to advancements in radiotherapy techniques around that time. Data from the original publications were inputted into the meta-analysis using RevMan 5.4 and Stata 17.0 software packages. For all aggregated analyses, heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochrane’s Q statistic and the I2 statistic [11]. The levels of heterogeneity were classified as follows: low (I2 < 25%), moderate (I2 = 25–50%), and high (I2 > 50%). The random-effects model was employed when the I2 statistic exceeded 50% (indicating high heterogeneity); otherwise, the fixed-effects model was utilized. Publication bias was assessed utilizing both visual funnel plot analysis and Egger’s test. The presence of visual asymmetry in the funnel plot, coupled with a two-tailed P-value from Egger’s test below 0.1, was considered indicative of significant bias [12]. All outcomes were deemed statistically significant if they yielded a two-sided P-value of 0.05 or lower.

Results

Search results and characteristics of studies

A rigorous screening process was conducted adhering to the inclusion criteria and thoroughly examined the full texts of potentially suitable studies. As a result, 18 articles containing 19 studies were selected for inclusion [13–30]. Figure 1 illustrates the flowchart detailing the literature search and study selection methodology.

The cohorts consisted predominantly of males (53.6% across studies), with median ages typically ranging from the late 50s to early 80s. Of the patients, 74.4% presented with tumors larger than 3 cm or classified as T3 stage. 2 and 5-year OS with postoperative adjuvant CRT

The analysis revealed a notable improvement in 2-year OS rates for patients receiving postoperative adjuvant CRT compared to those receiving chemotherapy alone or under observation (Fig. 2). Adjuvant CRT was associated with a significant improvement in OS compared to no CRT (OR = 0.63, 95% CI: 0.53–0.76, P < 0.001), with low to moderate heterogeneity observed (I2 = 40%, P = 0.04). This benefit remained consistent in subgroup analyses, showing superiority over chemotherapy alone (OR = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.39–0.75, P = 0.0003) and observation (OR = 0.61, 95% CI: 0.53–0.71, P < 0.001).

Literature publication bias evaluation

Publication bias was assessed using both visual funnel plot inspection and Egger’s regression test, with a two-tailed P-value below 0.1 from Egger’s test considered indicative of significant publication bias. As shown in Table 4, Egger’s test for the overall 2-year OS analysis yielded a P-value of 0.20, which was above the predefined threshold of 0.1, suggesting no statistically significant publication bias for this outcome. Similarly, no significant publication bias was detected for the 5-year OS overall analysis (P = 0.57), for the 2-year OS after 2010 subgroup analysis (P = 0.31), or for the analyses limited to studies with a sample size larger than 100 (2-year OS: P = 0.94; 5-year OS: P = 0.71).

Discussion

This meta-analysis included 19 studies that examined the effectiveness of CRT for treating PAAD. Based on the results of this meta-analysis, adjuvant CRT significantly improved 2-year OS compared to no radiotherapy. Furthermore, this benefit was consistently observed across subgroup analyses, including comparisons against chemotherapy alone and observation. While the overall 5-year survival analysis showed a non-significant trend favoring CRT, a significant survival benefit with CRT was observed specifically in the subgroup compared with observation alone (OR = 0.67, P = 0.03). Notably, patients with high-risk features, such as lymph node-positive or margin-positive disease, derived substantial survival benefits from CRT. Additionally, CRT was associated with a significant reduction in local recurrence rates, though it did not impact the incidence of distant metastasis.

Our findings partially diverge from a previous meta-analysis conducted by Pasqualetti et al., who evaluated the impact of postoperative radiotherapy through a meta-analysis of six prospective randomized trials. They concluded that adjuvant radiotherapy conferred no universal benefit to all patients with pancreatic cancer, but was potentially advantageous for a subset of patients with residual local disease [31]. The studies included in their analysis span from 1985 to 2012, which does not reflect the most current research. Over time, radiotherapy techniques have significantly advanced, with modern modalities such as intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) or image-guided radiotherapy (IGRT) offering improved benefits to patients by mitigating limitations associated with treating areas that include large portions of the gastrointestinal tract. The absence of these modern techniques in the included studies may have influenced the conclusions drawn. Consequently, incorporating more recent research could lead to different outcomes. Furthermore, the above-mentioned study did not elaborate on the effects of postoperative adjuvant CRT on lymph node-positive patients or local recurrence rates. In contrast, our study included both retrospective studies and several recent randomized controlled trials, focusing on the long-term survival benefits of adjuvant CRT, particularly the impact on local recurrence and distant metastasis. Subgroup analysis of studies from before and after 2010 yielded a 2-year, but not 5-year, OS benefit with CRT, with an emerging trend toward benefit in the post-2010 cohort. One explanation is that patients with less aggressive tumors already have favorable long-term outcomes with surgery and chemotherapy alone, leaving little room for CRT to further improve 5-year survival. Conversely, patients with high-risk features benefit from CRT through better local control in the short term, but their long-term survival is still limited by distant metastases. In other words, CRT appears to confer greater benefit to patients with more severe disease.

PAAD is characterized by a high recurrence rate post-resection, which underpins the biological rationale for adjuvant therapy. To date, no consensus has been reached on a standard adjuvant treatment protocol for PAAD patients, and the debate remains open as to whether the incorporation of adjuvant radiotherapy can enhance OS. Numerous studies have validated the efficacy of adjuvant chemotherapy in PAAD [32–34]. Over the past decade, the FOLFIRINOX regimen (comprising fluorouracil, leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin) has emerged as a new standard of care for fit patients undergoing resection for resectable pancreatic tumors [35]. Kamarajah et al. conducted a systematic review and network meta-analysis of phase III randomized controlled trials, which indicated that chemotherapy with S1 or mFFX is a potent adjuvant treatment for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, enhancing survival post-surgery and warranting consideration as a standard adjuvant therapy option [36]. These findings suggest that PAAD patients can derive significant benefits from adjuvant chemotherapy. Consistent with these findings, our study demonstrated chemotherapy’s efficacy by comparing patients who received CRT with those who received chemotherapy alone or were under observation.

Whether radiotherapy provides additional benefit to adjuvant chemotherapy after pancreatic cancer surgery remains controversial. Ma et al. analyzed data from the National Cancer Database on resected PAAD patients with stage I–II, cT1–3, N0–1, and M0 disease, and concluded that adjuvant chemotherapy followed by concurrent chemoradiation was associated with improved survival [37]. Kamarajah et al. further demonstrated that, following neoadjuvant chemotherapy and surgical resection for pancreatic cancer, the addition of adjuvant CRT, as opposed to chemotherapy alone, was linked to higher survival rates in patients with LVI-positive or grade III malignancies [4]. However, some studies have shown that adjuvant CRT may not confer benefits to PAAD patients. Parikh et al. found that adjuvant CRT may reduce local recurrence but did not impact distant recurrence, thus having a limited effect on OS [38]. Among the studies included in our meta-analysis, Merchant et al. concluded that CRT did not improve patient outcomes; however, it is noteworthy that the radiotherapy group had a 33% margin-positive rate and a 65% node-positive rate [18]. Consequently, the inability of radiotherapy to improve 5-year OS may be attributed to the higher prevalence of margin-positive and node-positive patients within the radiotherapy cohort.

Regarding the toxicity profile of adjuvant therapy, Venkatesulu and colleagues conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to examine the impact of radiation-induced lymphopenia on PAAD outcomes. Their findings indicated that radiation-related lymphopenia is linked to an elevated risk of mortality and a diminished median OS [39]. In the studies included in our analysis, radiotherapy-related toxicities were documented in only three studies [13, 21, 27]. Neoptolemos et al. reported that grade 3 or 4 adverse events occurred in 29 patients: hematologic events in 7 (2 receiving chemotherapy alone and 5 on combination therapy), stomatitis in 9 (4 on chemotherapy alone and 5 on combination therapy), diarrhea in 6 (2 on chemotherapy alone and 4 on combination therapy), and other adverse events in 7 (2 on CRT alone, 3 on chemotherapy alone, and 2 on combination therapy). Here, combination therapy refers to chemotherapy combined with CRT [13]. Laethem et al. demonstrated that adjuvant CRT for pancreatic cancer was generally well-tolerated, with no fatalities attributed to toxicity [21]. Schmidt et al. reported that grade 3 or 4 toxicities associated with PAAD were observed in 85% of patients receiving adjuvant CRT following chemotherapy (primarily neutropenia and dehydration) and in 16% of patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy alone (mainly diarrhea) [27]. Although postoperative CRT may increase the risk of adverse effects, it rarely results in patient death.

This study is not without its limitations. Primarily, the included studies comprised a mix of retrospective analyses, which are susceptible to selection bias and variations in treatment protocols over the course of the study periods. Nonetheless, all studies compared the use of CRT against no CRT and evaluated identical endpoints, leading us to believe that a meta-analysis is justified to substantiate the impact of CRT on patients with PAAD. Secondly, there is a potential for publication bias; our evaluation of the literature revealed the presence of publication bias in the 2-year OS) data. However, the findings from the 5-year OS analysis align with those of the 2-year OS, suggesting that publication bias may not significantly influence the ultimate outcomes. Lastly, the patient population in some of the studies was relatively small, which could affect the generalizability of the results.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this meta-analysis demonstrates that postoperative adjuvant CRT significantly improves survival outcomes in PAAD, particularly for high-risk patients with lymph node-positive disease. Adjuvant CRT provides a substantial survival advantage at 2 years and effectively reduces local recurrence rates. However, its benefit on long-term survival remains limited, and it shows no significant impact on distant metastasis control. These findings support the selective use of adjuvant CRT for local tumor control in specific patient subgroups.

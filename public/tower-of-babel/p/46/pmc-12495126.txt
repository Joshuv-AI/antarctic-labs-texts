Low‐Grade Fetal Adenocarcinoma of the Lung on 18F‐FDG PET/CT
Authors: Honghong Liu, Hongwei Yang, Ruimin Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTFetal adenocarcinoma of the lung (FLAC) is extremely rare without specific common symptoms and is difficult to distinguish from other lung cancers just by clinical symptoms and traditional imaging. We reported a case of FLAC that underwent 18F‐FDG PET‐CT examination and summarized the findings of it.

A 36‐year‐old female patient was hospitalized owing to an occupying lesion discovered incidentally in the left upper lobe, with inconspicuous clinical symptoms and normal blood routine examination results and tumor markers. Hence, 18F‐fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography/Computed Tomography (18F‐FDG PET/CT) scanning was completed for further diagnosis. Informed consent was signed before the examination. There was significantly enhanced FDG uptake into the left lung, as evidenced by the maximal intensity projection (MIP) image (Figure 1a, arrow). A 3.8 cm × 3.0 cm × 2.8 cm mass was found in the left upper lobe (Figure 1b–d, arrows) on CT, with a SUVmax of 8.6 on PET and fused PET/CT images (Figure 1e–j, arrows). As the 18F‐FDG PET/CT imaging exhibited hypermetabolism of glucose within the tumor, a video‐assisted thoracoscopic lobectomy was performed on the patient with suspected lung cancer. Photomicrographs of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining depicted a tumor morphology whereby glandular structures were surrounded by glycogen‐rich columnar cells and morule formation (Figure 2a, ×40). Besides, immunohistochemical examination showed positive staining for β‐catenin (Figure 2b, ×40). As revealed by postoperative histopathology, the mass was low‐grade fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung (L‐FLAC).

FLAC is an uncommon form of rare lung adenocarcinoma subtype accounting for 0.1%–0.5% of all pulmonary neoplasms, the first case of which was reported in 1982 by Kradin [1]. No specific common symptoms are developed in FLAC patients, making it difficult to distinguish FLAC from other respiratory diseases just by clinical symptoms. FLAC is typically classified as low‐ (L‐FLAC) and high‐level (H‐FLAC) [2]. L‐FLAC generally takes place in young adult women showing a good prognosis with a 5‐year survival rate greater than 80% [3]. The expression of β‐Catenin in the nucleus and cytoplasm of FLAC, however not in common adenocarcinoma, enables the differential diagnosis of the two types of tumors [3]. Only a few articles have reported the appearance of FLAC on 18F‐FDG PET/CT till now, and an enhanced FDG accumulation on PET was reported [2]. 18F‐FDG PET/CT imaging has a possible role and usefulness for staging and restaging for FLAC. Surgical resection was the main treatment for FLAC. The final diagnosis should be based on histopathologic and immunohistochemical examination results.

Futile Radiotherapy for Patients With Cancer Near the End of Life
Authors: Alok Deshane, Morgan Freret, Victoria S. Brennan, Puneeth Iyengar, Daniel R. Gomez, Divya Yerramilli, Amy J. Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cohort study assesses the association between use of palliative radiotherapy and inpatient radiation oncology consultation among patients with limited life expectancy.

Introduction

Palliative radiotherapy (RT) can effectively alleviate symptoms1,2 but is often inappropriate for patients at the end of life (EOL). In a phase 3 randomized clinical trial of patients with brain metastases near EOL, whole-brain radiation added to best supportive care did not improve quality of life or survival.3 Although palliative RT may benefit patients in their last few months of life, failure to weigh benefits against harms, including acute toxic effects, treatment time, and resource utilization, may lead to inappropriate treatment.4,5,6,7 Because tumor regression typically occurs over weeks rather than days, radiobiologic benefit is unlikely in patients with extremely limited life expectancy. Previous studies have reported an association between a dedicated inpatient radiation oncology consult (IROC) service and shorter hospital stays8 and goal-appropriate care.9 To understand its impact, we examined whether IROC implementation was associated with reduced futile RT at EOL.

Methods

We retrospectively reviewed clinical records from IROC service at a tertiary care cancer hospital from January 2019 through December 2023 to identify patients who died within 14 days of consultation; last data abstraction occurred in March 2026. The Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center Institutional Review Board approved the study and waived informed consent because of minimal risk. We followed the STROBE reporting guideline.

Patients were grouped into before (consultation before January 2020, when IROC service began) or after (consultation from January 2021 through December 2023) IROC implementation; 2020 was excluded to reduce confounding from COVID-19–related practice changes (eFigure in Supplement 1). Demographic characteristics included age, race and ethnicity (patient-reported), and sex. Clinical characteristics included Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS), disease histologic type, and systemic therapy receipt. KPS was obtained from RT notes at consultation or, if unavailable, the most recent prior value. RT-specific variables included utilization and completion rates, fractionation, technique, and indication.

We used χ2 and Mann-Whitney tests to compare categorical and continuous variables, respectively. Two-sided P < .05 indicated statistical significance. Data analysis was performed with IBM SPSS v29.0.1.0 (IBM).

Results

We identified 450 patients (median [range] age, 63 [12-94] years; 231 females [51.3%]) who died within 14 days of consultation (Table). Overall, 101 patients (22.4%) received RT and 349 (77.6%) had RT deferred. Systemic therapy was provided to 111 patients (24.7%) and did not differ by RT receipt (30 of 101 [29.7%] vs 81 of 349 [23.2%]). RT receipt did not differ significantly by race and ethnicity, but a higher proportion of Black patients had RT deferred than received RT (47 of 349 [13.5%] vs 6 of 101 [5.9%]).

Among patients who received RT, 13 (12.9%) were administered 8 Gy in 1 fraction and 11 (10.9%) had stereotactic body RT or radiosurgery. Fifty-four patients (53.5%) had RT courses aborted before completion. KPS values were available for 94.0% (328) of patients with deferred RT and 100% of patients who received RT. Median KPS was higher for those receiving RT, although the difference was not statistically significant. Patients most commonly received RT for cord compression (25 [24.7%]) and central nervous system disease (23 [22.7%]) (Figure, A). Forty-nine patients (10.9%) were discharged to hospice, 67 (14.9%) were discharged from the hospital, and 334 (74.3%) died while hospitalized. Before vs after IROC implementation, disposition did not significantly differ between patients who received RT and those with deferred RT.

Discussion

Among patients who died within 14 days of consultation, most died while hospitalized and most RT courses were discontinued, suggesting limited opportunity for clinical benefit. IROC service implementation was associated with a significant reduction in RT delivery. After IROC implementation, fewer patients died in the hospital after receiving inpatient RT and fewer RT courses were discontinued.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design precludes causal associations, and comparisons between the before and after periods are subject to unmeasured confounders, including temporal changes in practice patterns, patient selection, and COVID-19–era practice shifts. Although outcome is difficult to determine precisely, 14-day mortality was selected because RT benefit is unlikely to be realized within that interval. In our cancer center, patients may pursue more aggressive care than in community settings.

Despite these limitations, our results suggest that a dedicated IROC service could improve treatment selection near EOL. Although not statistically significant, there were numerical differences in RT receipt by race and ethnicity, consistent with previous patterns10 and warranting further study of opportunities for equitable care. Reduced RT delivery, hospital deaths after RT receipt, and discontinued courses suggest a decrease in nonbeneficial treatment in patients with limited life expectancy. Additionally, specialized palliative consultation models may reduce futile interventions near EOL.

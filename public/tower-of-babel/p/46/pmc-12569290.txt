Unveiling the role of protein palmitoylation in gastric cancer diagnosis via machine learning
Authors: Zhong hua Shen, Jingyu Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractGastric cancer (GC) is a highly morbid and mortal gastrointestinal malignancy, urgently requiring sensitive and specific biomarkers for detection. Protein palmitoylation, a reversible lipid modification process, has been connected to tumor formation, yet its function in gastric cancer (GC) is still insufficiently explored. This research creatively combined palmitoylation-associated characteristics with machine learning methods, utilizing the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework to boost the interpretability of the model. Gene expression profiling datasets from public repositories were collected, with batch effects corrected. Genes with differential expression (DEGs) were pinpointed, and an analysis of functional enrichment was carried out. Through intersection analysis of DEGs and a palmitoylation gene set, and integration of LASSO regression, SVM-RFE, and random forest algorithms, four core genes (ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1, and MAL) were selected. Ten machine learning models were built, among which the Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model achieved the optimal performance (AUC = 0.963). SHAP analysis uncovered the notable contributions of the four core genes to model classification. The study also explored gene expression characteristics, immune cell correlations, and spatial heterogeneity. However, it has limitations such as lack of in-vivo animal model validation, unclear core gene–immune cell interaction mechanisms, and insufficient sample diversity. Overall, this research provides new insights into GC pathogenesis and directions for future studies on diagnosis and treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03804-5.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Protein palmitoylation, Machine learning, SHAP algorithm, Biomarker, ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1, MAL

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC), a gastrointestinal malignant tumor with high incidence and fatality rates [1–4], highlights an urgent requirement for identifying sensitive and specific biomarkers for its diagnosis.

One of the most common types of protein lipid modification in eukaryotic cells is S-palmitoylation, which involves the covalent linkage of palmitic acid (a 16-carbon saturated fatty acid) to the side chain of specific cysteine residues in proteins [5]. The main source of cellular palmitate is fatty acid conversion, which is catalyzed by fatty acid synthase (FASN) [6].

Recent studies have established a strong association between aberrant protein palmitoylation and tumorigenesis and progression [7–13]. Nonetheless, research addressing dysregulated palmitoylation in gastric cancer remains limited.

Although many excellent algorithms have been proposed for the study of important molecules [14]. The opaque decision-making processes of machine learning algorithms hinder clinical interpretability, particularly in identifying specific clinical variables that influence individual patient outcomes. This explainability deficit currently impedes the adoption of advanced ML techniques in clinical decision support systems [15] and represents a fundamental challenge for healthcare AI implementation [16]. To address this critical limitation, our study innovatively integrates state-of-the-art ML algorithms with a framework based on SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) [17]. This method allows investigators to pinpoint key factors affecting palmitoylation and other molecular markers, thus greatly boosting the interpretability of the model and speeding up the application of research results in clinical settings.

Building upon this foundation, our research aims to: Integrate palmitoylation-related features with machine learning algorithms to construct a novel diagnostic model for gastric cancer, and utilize the SHAP algorithm to perform an in-depth analysis of the critical factors influencing the diagnostic model. Our ultimate objective is to develop a diagnostic tool characterized by high interpretability and clinical utility. This research seeks to provide innovative avenues and direction for early-stage GC screening and personalized patient management strategies.

Materials and methods

Data collection

We acquired gene expression profiling datasets from two public data repositories. The initial source is the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), from which we fetched multiple datasets, specifically GSE118916, GSE29998, GSE54129, GSE79973, and GSE84437. Besides, we obtained related data from the UCSC XENA database (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). Batch effects across datasets were corrected using the ComBat algorithm (implemented in the R package "sva") to ensure data homogeneity and eliminate technical variability [18]. The gene set related to palmitoylation was obtained from GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/).

Differential expressed genes (DEGs) analysis

The limma package [19] pinpointed genes with differential expression (DEGs) between disease and control groups according to the thresholds of |log₂FC|> 0.585 and adjusted p-value < 0.05 [20]. DEGs related to gastric cancer are shown in a heatmap. A volcano plot illustrates the overall distribution of DEGs, with red used for upregulated genes and blue for downregulated ones; both plots apply the stated significance standard (adjusted p-value < 0.05).

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional annotation of Gene Ontology (GO)—encompassing biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components—along with enrichment analyses of Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways were conducted via the clusterProfiler package [21]. Bar charts visualize results meeting the significance threshold (P < 0.05), highlighting key enriched biological processes and signaling pathways associated with the DEGs.

Feature gene selection and model construction

To precisely identify palmitoylation-associated core genes, an intersection analysis was conducted between differentially expressed genes and a palmitoylation gene set. Three machine learning approaches were applied: LASSO regression (via ‘glmnet [22]’, optimized by tenfold cross-validation to minimize MSE for feature selection), SVM-RFE (via ‘e1071’ [23], iteratively pruning non-informative features), and random forest (via ‘randomForest [24]’, ranking genes by variable importance). Results from these methods were integrated using a Venn diagram to identify consensus core Hub genes, ensuring robust, bias-minimized selection of key genetic regulators in the palmitoylation network.

Model evaluation and validation

A comprehensive panel of ten machine learning algorithms, including Partial Least Squares (PLS), Random Forest (RF), Decision Tree Classifier (DTS), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), XGBoost, Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Neural Network (NeuralNet), and Generalized Linear Model with Boosting (glmBoost), were systematically employed for predictive model development. Hyperparameter optimization was performed using the `caret` package, implementing a nested fivefold cross-validation strategy repeated five times to ensure robustness and generalizability. Model effectiveness was thoroughly assessed using the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC), which was calculated from an independent test dataset. Comparative analysis of multi-model ROC curves was conducted to identify the optimal algorithmic approach, which was then selected for downstream validation and interpretation. This structured methodology ensures unbiased algorithmic comparison and facilitates the selection of the most discriminative model for the given biological context.

SHAP interpretability analysis

To break down the role of single genes in model predictions, we utilized SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis through the kernelshap package with the optimized model. Feature importance was visualized through four complementary methods: Bar Charts: Ordering genes based on mean absolute SHAP values to give priority to predictors. Swarm Diagrams: Showing the spread of SHAP values across separate samples. Dependence Charts: Investigating monotonic connections between gene expression levels and their SHAP values. Waterfall/Single-Sample Force Plots: Decomposing prediction trajectories for specific samples. This multimodal approach enabled comprehensive assessment of both global and sample-specific gene effects on model outcomes.

HUB gene differential expression analysis

Variations in HUB gene expression between groups were assessed using non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, executed through the stat_compare_means function in the ggpubr package. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Genomic annotation

A circular genomic graphic was created with the circlize package. The hg38 reference genome was loaded with genomicIdeogram, and gene annotations were displayed in an "inside" orientation using genomicLabels, enabling intuitive chromosomal localization of features.

Spatial transcriptomic analysis

Raw spatial transcriptomic data (GSE290924) were preprocessed using Seurat, including normalization and background correction. Clustering methods and dedicated R packages were utilized for dimensionality reduction, cell type labeling, and the visual display of gene expression patterns.

Cell culture

GES-1, MKN, and HGC cell lines were acquired from the Shanghai Institute of Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. Cells were thawed in a 37 °C water bath, resuspended in complete medium (10% FBS), centrifuged, and cultured at 37 °C/5% CO2. At 80–90% confluence, cells were passaged using trypsin, neutralized, and subcultured at a 1:3 ratio. Medium was refreshed every 2–3 days to maintain optimal growth conditions.

RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted with Trizol reagent (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Kit (TaKaRa). Quantitative PCR was carried out with SYBR Green on a LightCycler 480 system. Data were evaluated using GraphPad Prism, employing Student’s t-test or ANOVA (P < 0.05). Outcomes are shown as "Mean ± Standard Deviation (SD)" and visualized through bar/line graphs. Primer sequences are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

All statistical evaluations were carried out with R version 4.4.3 and GraphPad Prism version 9.0. For pair-wise group comparisons, Student’s t-test was adopted. When dealing with comparisons of three or more groups, nonparametric one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was implemented. Two-way ANOVA was used to examine proliferation data and tumor growth curves, with proper post-hoc tests employed to handle multiple comparisons. The threshold for statistical significance was established at P < 0.05 across all tests.

Data preprocessing and differential expression analysis

Before batch correction (Fig. 1A), samples from diverse datasets (e.g., GSE118916, TCGA) were sparsely distributed in principal component analysis (PCA). Post-correction (Fig. 1B), the samples clustered tightly, demonstrating effective elimination of batch effects. To display the differential analysis outcomes of gastric cancer data, a heatmap was plotted (Fig. 1D). This analytical process resulted in the detection of 1011 genes with differential expression (DEGs) in total. In particular, 513 genes showed upregulation and 498 genes displayed downregulation (Fig. 1C). These findings formed the basis for subsequent analyses.​

Functional enrichment results

GO analysis revealed significant enrichment of DEGs in extracellular matrix (ECM) organization (P < 0.001) and related processes within extracellular structures (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1E). The results of KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant enrichment in the extracellular structure organization (P < 0.01) and the Cell cycle (P < 0.05), as depicted in Fig. 1F. These findings strongly imply that the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) play pivotal roles in regulating cell proliferation and facilitating signal transduction processes.

Feature gene selection and model performance

To further screen the core genes associated with palmitoylation, this study employed three algorithms: LASSO regression, SVM-RFE, and random forest. LASSO regression analysis effectively pinpointed 75 core genes associated with palmitoylation, as illustrated in Fig. 2A, B. The SVM-RFE algorithm further optimized the feature selection and narrowed down the list to 30 core genes (Fig. 2C, D). In addition, the random forest algorithm was employed to screen out 30 genes, and the detailed results are presented in Fig. 2E, F. A Venn diagram was used to illustrate the intersections among the results of LASSO regression, SVM-RFE, and random forest (Fig. 3A), revealing four common genes: ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1 and MAL.

Contribution analysis of key genes

SHAP analysis identified significant contributions of four core genes to classification (Fig. 3B, C). COL4A1 had the highest mean absolute SHAP value (0.0814), indicating the greatest impact of its expression changes on predictions, followed by RBM20 (0.0669), MAL (0.0582), and ASPA (0.0458). The prediction value can be increased by COL4A1, but ASPA, COL4A1 and MAL reduce the model's prediction value (Fig. 3D).

Model performance comparison

The AUC values for the ten constructed models spanned from 0.835 to 0.963, as depicted in Fig. 3E. Among these models, the GBM demonstrated the most superior performance, achieving an AUC of 0.963 on the test set. It was closely succeeded by Neural Network (NeuralNet), which obtained an AUC value of 0.957, and the support vector machine (SVM), with an AUC of 0.929. In contrast, DTS demonstrated relatively inferior efficacy, with area under the curve values falling under 0.90.

Single-sample prediction interpretation

A waterfall plot revealed that positive contributions from COL4A1 and MAL primarily drove the prediction results (Fig. 3F). A single-sample force plot visually illustrated the cumulative impact of each gene on the prediction probability (Fig. 3G). Interactions among ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1 and MAL were predicted using the GeneMANIA(https://genemania.org/) database.

Genome distribution analysis revealed differential chromosomal localization of target genes: ASPA (Chr17), RBM20 (Chr10), COL4A1 (Chr13), and MAL (Chr2) (Fig. 4B). The four core genes were displayed on a volcano plot (Fig. 4C). Notably, all four genes showed significantly elevated expression in the experimental cohort compared to controls (Fig. 4D).

Immune cell heterogeneity

We used CIBERSORT to calculate the content of immune cells. Bar chart analysis showed distinct immune cell profiles between groups. Significant intercorrelations were observed among most immune cell populations (P < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Box-and-whisker plots demonstrated that the content of most immune cells was increased in the tumor group (P < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Heatmaps showed that ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1 and MAL were correlated with most immune cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4C).

Discussion

Gastric adenocarcinoma (STAD) has the highest incidence and mortality rates among digestive system cancers in East Asia 21. The lack of reliable diagnostic options, trustworthy biomarkers, and specific symptoms in the early stage often leads many patients to miss the opportunity for surgical treatment. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a reliable diagnostic marker for the early detection and diagnosis of gastric cancer. In this study, a palmitoylation-related gene diagnostic model was established in a gastric cancer cohort, and for the first time, the SHAP algorithm was used to evaluate the gastric cancer palmitoylation-related gene diagnostic model and screen out the molecules with the greatest contribution. In previous gastric cancer studies, research on the mechanism of palmitoylation has been limited to basic medical research. We are the first to establish a palmitoylation-related gene diagnostic model and, for the first time, use the SHAP algorithm to screen molecules for studying potential markers of gastric cancer, and verify the spatial heterogeneity of related molecules in spatial transcriptomics.

Firstly, we used three machine learning algorithms, namely LASSO, SVM, and random forest, to screen out 4 molecules. Meanwhile, the SHAP algorithm was used to rank these 4 molecules in the diagnostic model, and COL4A1 was found to contribute the most to the model. To explore the possible mechanisms, we also analyzed the correlation between the 4 molecules and the gastric cancer immune microenvironment, and found that the relationship between the 4 molecules and the immune microenvironment was statistically significant. This means that in our next research project, we can study the potential regulatory mechanisms between the immune microenvironment and these 4 molecules. Finally, we downloaded gastric cancer spatial transcriptomics from the GEO database for analysis and found that the 4 molecules have strong spatial heterogeneity in their spatial distribution.

Usually, in previous diagnostic models, people do not know the contribution degree of the genes composing the model. For example, our SHAP algorithm solves this problem and concludes that COL4A1 contributes the most to the model.

Literature evidence shows that Sun et al. [25] reported reduced ASPA expression in breast and prostate cancer samples, Long et al. [26] found lower ASPA expression in gliomas compared to normal tissues, and Tsen et al. [27] demonstrated that effective targeting of ASPA may reduce glioma progression, all consistent with our finding of decreased ASPA expression in GC. RBM20, crucial for RNA spliceosome formation and alternative splicing [28], is associated with poor prognosis in liver cancer [29] and shows lower levels in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) [30]. The MAPK/ELK1 signaling pathway affects pre-mRNA splicing by regulating RBM20 expression [31], potentially influencing the function of heart disease-related genes. This study systematically investigated the expression pattern of COL4A1 in gastric cancer tissues and its distribution in spatial transcriptomics. The results showed that COL4A1 is significantly highly expressed in gastric cancer tissues, suggesting that COL4A1 may act as an oncogenic factor involved in the progression of gastric cancer. As a key subunit of type IV collagen, COL4A1 is a major structural component of the basement membrane [32]. Under normal physiological conditions, COL4A1 participates in cell adhesion, signal transduction, and maintenance of tissue homeostasis through interactions with molecules such as integrins and laminins [33].

Currently, research on the relationship between COL4A1 and gastric cancer is relatively limited, but existing studies have shown that COL4A1 plays a pro-cancer role in various cancers. For example, in studies on oral squamous cell carcinoma, COL4A1 promotes tumor proliferation and migration [34]; in urothelial carcinoma, COL4A1 promotes tumor proliferation and migration [35]; in liver cancer, the upregulation of COL4A1 promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of HCC cells through FAK-Src signal transduction [36]. The results of this study are consistent with these research trends, further supporting the potential role of COL4A1 as an oncogene.

This study has several limitations: First, although core gene expression and functional relevance were validated via bioinformatics and cell experiments, in vivo animal model validation is lacking, requiring further investigation of their real biological functions and regulatory mechanisms in living organisms. Second, the specific molecular mechanisms of core gene-immune cell interactions remain unclear, necessitating in-depth exploration using techniques like co-immunoprecipitation and single-cell sequencing. Finally, the study relies on public datasets and partial cell lines, with insufficient sample diversity; future research should include more clinical samples to enhance the universality and clinical application value of the results.

Based on these findings and limitations, future research can proceed in three directions: constructing GC animal models to investigate the in vivo functions of ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1, and MAL and their potential as therapeutic targets; deciphering the molecular mechanisms of core gene-immune cell interactions to provide a theoretical basis for immune-modulated GC therapy; and expanding sample size to analyze the relationship between gene expression and clinical characteristics, offering more accurate biomarkers for GC precision diagnosis and personalized treatment.

In conclusion, this study reveals the critical roles and underlying mechanisms of palmitoylation-related core genes in GC through multi-omics integration and experimental validation, providing novel insights and theoretical foundations for understanding GC pathogenesis and developing innovative therapeutic strategies.

Conclusion

This study, through multi-omics integration and experimental validation, has revealed the critical roles and underlying mechanisms of palmitoylation-related core genes in gastric cancer. By integrating palmitoylation-related features with machine learning algorithms and using the SHAP framework, a novel diagnostic model with high interpretability was explored. The identified core genes, ASPA, RBM20, COL4A1, and MAL, showed significant differential expression, specific chromosomal localization, and correlations with immune cells, providing valuable clues for understanding GC pathogenesis. Although the study has certain limitations, it offers innovative directions for future research, including constructing GC animal models, deciphering core gene–immune cell interaction mechanisms, and expanding sample size. These efforts are expected to contribute to the development of more accurate diagnostic biomarkers and personalized treatment strategies for gastric cancer.

Extremal topological indices of some nanostructures
Authors: Shivani Rai, Biswajit Deb, Zahid Raza, Sourav Mondal
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this work, general formulas of degree-based and neighborhood degree sum-based topological indices for a 2D lattice of H-Naphtalenic nanotubes and pent-heptagonal nanosheets are determined. As an example, six neighborhood degree sum-based topological indices are computed using the obtained formula. Furthermore, the extremal cases of these nanostructures with a fixed number of blocks are characterized for a given degree-based or neighborhood degree sum-based topological index. Also a visual comparison of these indices is shown. Additionally, it is demonstrated how beneficial the indices are for representing structure-property relationships.Keywords: Topological index, Neighborhood degree sum, H-Naphtalenic nanotubes, Pent-heptagonal nanosheets

Introduction

A graph Γ consists of a non-empty set V(Γ) whose elements are called vertices and a set E(Γ) of unordered pairs of distinct vertices of Γ. The elements of E(Γ) are called the edges of Γ. The degree of a vertex v is defined as the count of vertices connected to it, and the total sum of degrees of these connected vertices is referred to as the neighborhood degree sum (NDS) of vertex v. For other basic terminologies and concepts, [7] can be referred to. In the context of chemical graph theory, some numerical attributes commonly called as topological indices defined using the branching pattern of molecules serve as a useful tool to predict their properties and activities. Harold Weiner, a chemist, developed the first index in 1947 to forecast the boiling point of alkanes [23]. Numerous indices fall broadly into three categories namely distance-based, degree-based and spectral-based indices [1], [4], [5], [6], [11], [12], [15], [18], [19], [20], [22], [23]. Henceforth, degree-based and neighborhood degree-sum based topological indices shall be referred to by DBTI and NDSBTI, respectively. Throughout this article, we will denote any arbitrary DBTI and NDSBTI by Φ and Z, respectively. An interesting field of research is the study of these indices to predict the characteristics of chemical compounds and nanostructures. Recently, various metal-organic frameworks' reduced reverse D-based indices and their closed NDS versions were calculated [24]. Additionally, several NDSBTI for zinc-based metal-organic frameworks were computed in 2023 along similar lines [26]. Moreover, several topological indices based on open as well as closed neighborhood degree sums were found for the metal-insulator transition super lattice network [25]. Application potential and mathematical properties of the Sanskruti index are recently investigated by Mondal and Das [14]. A substantial amount of study has been done in determining the DBTI of nanostructures like the carbon nanocones and nanotori [10], NDSBTI of carbon nanotubes [9], boron triangular nanotubes [2], graphene [13], etc. Some other nanomaterials include the H-Naphtalenic nanotubes (HN[k,l]) and pent-heptagonal nanosheets (PHV[k,l] and PHH[k,l]). The structure of HN[k,l] consists of a combination of C6, C4 and C8 rings as shown in Fig. 1. The number of pairs of hexagons in the first row is denoted by k and the number of alternate hexagons in a column by l. The structures of PHV[k,l] and PHH[k,l], consist of a concatenation of C5 and C7 rings as shown in Fig. 2 and Fig. 3. In PHV[k,l] (PHH[k,l]) the number of pentagons (heptagons) in the top row is denoted by l and the number of repetitions of the first four rows of the vertices and edges that occur alternatively is denoted by k. In this article, Γ[k,l] is the system of H-Naphtalenic nanotubes HN[k,l] (or pent-heptagonal nanosheets PHV[k,l] or PHH[k,l]) where the total number of blocks in the system is b=kl.

In [8], various DBTI and NDSBTI like GA5, ABC and their neighborhood versions etc., are computed for HN[k,l], PHV[k,l] and PHH[k,l]. In 2020, a general formula was deduced for the computation of DBTI of graphene systems [21]. Furthermore, for a given DBTI the extremal graphene with fixed number of hexagons was characterized. Motivated by these observations, general formulas for the computation of DBTI and NDSBTI topological index for HN[k,l], PHV[k,l], and PHH[k,l] are determined. Also, given any DBTI or NDSBTI, the extremal cases of these nanostructures with a fixed b are characterized. Moreover, comparison of these indices is demonstrated graphically and the significance of these indices in structure-property modelling is studied.

Comparative study of the topological indices

A comparative analysis of the six NDSBTI namely M2nm, FN⁎, ND3, ND5, NH, and NI is presented here for the three systems of nanostructures undertaken in this study. In each of these systems under consideration, all these indices respond differently. The 3D plots of these indices for all the three system of nanostructures of HN[k,l], PHV[k,l] and PHH[k,l] for k and l values both ranging from 1 to 100, are given in Figs. 4 (i-iii), 5 (i-iii), 7(i-iii), 8(i-iii), 10 (i-iii), 11 (i-iii). For all these three structures it can be concluded from the vertical axis of the figures that the NDSBTI can be expressed in the given order: M2nm≤NH≤ND5≤NI≤FN⁎≤ND3. It can be concluded that the behavior of ND3 is the most dominating whereas M2nm is the least dominating than the rest of the indices. From [15], [16], [17] it can be inferred that these indices possess remarkable discrimination potential and can model the various physicochemical properties of a certain class of isomers of the benchmark data sets very well. Hence, the computation of these indices is useful for the study of these three nanostructures.

Conclusion

In this study general formulas for DBTI and NDSBTI of a 2D lattice of H-Naphtalenic nanotubes and two types of pent-heptagonal nanosheets are presented. As an example, six NDSBTI for HN[k,l], PHV[k,l], and PHH[k,l] are computed using the formula. Moreover, the extremal nanostructures with a fixed number of blocks b=kl with respect to any DBTI and NDSBTI are characterized. Furthermore, a comparative analysis of these indices is presented graphically. It is noticed that the increase in M2nm was the most gradual and the increase in ND3 was the most rapid compared to the other indices taken into account. Also, the applicability of NM1, NM2, GA5, M2nm, FN⁎, ND3, ND5, and NH in structure-property modelling is explored taking into consideration the benchmark dataset of the benzenoid hydrocarbons. In the future, the general expression of these indices for other nanostructures as well as extremal structures with respect to these indices can be explored.

Iliac artery involvement determines optimal therapy in acute uncomplicated type B aortic dissection
Authors: Lauren V. Huckaby, Hai Dong, Minliang Liu, Maria Aslam, Hannah L. Cebull, Rudolph L. Gleason, John N. Oshinski, John A. Elefteriades, Bradley G. Leshnower
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe optimal treatment for acute uncomplicated type B aortic dissection (AUTBAD) remains unknown. The aim of this study was to characterize the efficacy of optimal medical therapy (OMT) in the treatment of AUTBAD and identify anatomic risk factors for OMT failure.MethodsA retrospective review of an aortic database from 2 aortic centers identified 293 patients with AUTBAD treated initially with OMT from 1995 to 2025. Inclusion criteria consisted of patients with initial imaging and death status available for analysis. An aortic event was defined as the need for an aortic intervention or an aortic-related death.ResultsIn total, 153 (52.2%) patients required an aortic intervention or experienced an aortic-related death during the study period. Aortic event-free survival at 1, 5, and 10 years was 62.9%, 43.8%, and 37.6%. For the 140 (47.8%) patients successfully maintained on OMT alone, aortic-related survival was 89.4%, 82.2%, and 76.9% at 1, 5, and 10 years. Patients with type B aortic dissection extending into the iliac arteries had a superior aortic event-free survival at 1 (iliac 70.8% vs no iliac 55.5%), 5 (iliac 49.5% vs no iliac 37.2%), and 10 years (iliac 44.6% vs no iliac 28.8%, P = .008) compared with patients without iliac involvement. Iliac involvement was independently associated with freedom from aortic intervention or aortic-related death (hazard ratio, 0.69, P = .049). Aortic intervention resulted in improved survival (P < .001) compared with OMT in patients without iliac involvement but had no impact in patients with iliac involvement.ConclusionsPatients with AUTBAD and dissections ending proximal to the aortoiliac bifurcation benefit from TEVAR compared with OMT. Conversely, patients with dissections extending into the iliac arteries should be treated with OMT alone.

Central Message

In medically managed acute uncomplicated type B aortic dissection, endovascular repair improves long-term survival only in those patients without iliac artery involvement.

Perspective

Historically, patients with acute uncomplicated type B aortic dissection were managed with optimal medical therapy; however, favorable outcomes with endovascular intervention have challenged this paradigm. This work demonstrates a novel radiographic feature to predict survival benefit of endovascular repair.

The optimal therapy for patients with acute uncomplicated type B aortic dissection (AUTBAD) remains unknown. Historically, optimal medical therapy (OMT) has been the primary treatment, with surgical intervention reserved for the development of late complications, consisting primarily of aneurysmal degeneration and/or false lumen rupture.1,2 This strategy has produced excellent short-term survival, but poor long-term outcomes, with 5-year intervention-free survival rates reported at ≤50%.3,4 These unsatisfactory outcomes with OMT combined with the outstanding results of thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for patients with acute TBAD complicated by malperfusion and rupture has prompted many aortic specialists to perform TEVAR as the primary therapy for patients with acute uncomplicated TBAD.5, 6, 7, 8 However, TEVAR carries the risk of stroke, paraplegia, retrograde type A aortic dissection, and may induce aortic stiffening, which has been shown to be an independent predictor of stroke, heart failure, and cardiovascular mortality.9, 10, 11, 12 In the cohort of patients with TBAD that maintain stable aortic diameters with OMT, TEVAR represents an unnecessary intervention that carries unnecessary risks. Data from the ongoing IMPROVE-AD trial, a prospective randomized controlled trial of TEVAR versus OMT for acute uncomplicated TBAD, may provide answers regarding the optimal therapy for this lethal aortic syndrome.13

Ideally, a risk-stratification model based on clinical and imaging-based parameters at the time of diagnosis could be developed that would allow early identification of patients with AUTBAD who will ultimately develop false lumen aneurysms. These patients could undergo TEVAR in the acute or subacute phase of TBAD, when the mechanical properties of the dissection flap are conducive for positive aortic remodeling. Unfortunately, previous investigations to define anatomic predictors of OMT failure have been largely unsuccessful. Maximum aortic diameter remains as the sole predictor of OMT failure that has been consistently identified and validated across multiple studies.14 However, the majority of these studies have been limited by small sample sizes, a lack of baseline imaging data, and minimal long-term follow-up.

Despite an increased accumulation of data and improved outcomes, questions persist regarding the optimal treatment of patients with AUTBAD. The aims of the current study are (1) to accurately characterize the role of OMT versus surgical intervention in AUTBAD and (2) to identify anatomic predictors of OMT failure on the basis of a comprehensive analysis of the initial imaging studies used to make the diagnosis of TBAD. Achievement of these goals would provide further data for the development of a risk-stratification tool that could identify patients who would benefit from early TEVAR and those that will be appropriately treated with OMT alone.

Methods

Patient Cohort

This study was conducted with the approval of the institutional review boards at Emory University and Yale University in compliance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act regulations and the Declaration of Helsinki (STUDY00002737, approved July 23, 2021). The institutional review boards waived the need for patient consent. The Emory and Yale Aortic Databases were each queried from 1995 to 2025 and 293 patients with AUTBAD who were initially treated with OMT and had their initial diagnostic imaging study available for analysis were identified. AUTBAD was defined as an aortic dissection originating at the origin of or distal to the left subclavian artery without malperfusion or rupture that did not involve the ascending aorta and was diagnosed within 14 days of symptom onset.15 Patients with acute type B intramural hematoma, acute TBAD complicated by malperfusion or rupture, or patients with uncomplicated TBAD without available contrast-enhanced cross-sectional imaging for analysis were excluded from the study.

Clinical and Imaging Data

Both the Emory and Yale Aortic Databases contained patient demographics and comorbidities that were extracted from the electronic medical record. Anatomic data regarding the location of the primary tear, extent of dissection, aortic diameters, aortic false lumen status, and true lumen/false lumen origin of the aortic branches were collected from the initial diagnostic contrast-enhanced computed tomography scan performed at the index hospitalization by each of the site principal investigators (J.A.E., B.G.L.). Data regarding open or endovascular interventions were obtained by review of operative notes from the electronic medical record. Criteria for aortic intervention at both centers were (1) aortic size ≥5.5 cm or ≥5 cm in patients with connective tissue disorder; (2) rapid growth defined as 5 mm/12 months after the first year; or (3) symptomatic aneurysms. These criteria did not change throughout the duration of the study, despite the introduction of endovascular therapy. Survival status was assessed on the basis of the multimodal Yale Aortic Institute Methodology for retrospective studies.16 Deaths were categorized as either nonaortic or aortic related on the basis of long-term follow-up data after chart review and telephone calls performed by a team of clinical research nurses and adjudicated by the site principal investigators (J.A.E., B.G.L.). Patients with unknown cause of death were excluded from the study. An aortic event was defined by the following criteria: (1) the occurrence of open or endovascular aortic surgical intervention or (2) aortic-related death.

Statistical Analysis

The Student t test and one-way analysis of variance testing were used for comparison of parametric data and Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis testing were used for nonparametric data. The χ2 or Fisher exact test was used to compare categorical data. Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to model survival after diagnosis of type B aortic dissection with log-rank testing to compare survival among subpopulations. Competing risks regression, with non-aortic death as the competing risk, was used to identify independent radiologic predictors of aortic intervention or aortic-related death. In multivariable regressions that accounted for patient demographic and preoperative characteristics, we included only covariates with a variance inflation factor <5 to reduce potential multicollinearity that could affect coefficient magnitude and statistical significance in the Fine-Gray model.17,18 The final model included demographic factors (age, sex, and race), clinical comorbidities (connective tissue disease, history of congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, or cerebrovascular accident), anatomic characteristics (iliac artery involvement, location of primary intimal tear, renal artery involvement, and presence of any vessels arising from the false lumen), and treatment era. In all analyses, 2-sided P-values < .05 were considered statistically significant. Subanalysis of the longitudinal risk of aortic events was performed and stratified by the type of intervention (ie, open surgical vs endovascular) and the extent of dissection.

The location of the primary intimal tear was in Ishimaru19 zone 3 in 88.5% of patients, followed by zone 4 at 4.5% and zone 5 at 4.6%. The dissection flap extended distal to the celiac artery in 91.4% of patients, and involved the iliac arteries (zones 10, 11) in 54.3% of patients. A schematic demonstrating the relative proportion of visceral vessel perfusion from the false lumen, true lumen, or both lumens is shown in Figure E1. The maximum diameters of the descending thoracic and abdominal aorta at the time of TBAD diagnosis were 4.0 cm [interquartile range {IQR}, 3.5, 4.5] and 3.3 cm [IQR, 2.9, 3.8]. The false lumen was patent, partially thrombosed, and completely thrombosed in 47.7%, 39.7%, and 12.6% of patients. Over time, the proportion of endovascular interventions relative to open interventions increased (Table E1).

Efficacy of OMT in Uncomplicated TBAD

During the duration of the study, 153 (52.2%) patients required an aortic intervention or experienced an aortic-related death. Aortic events occurred at a median of 107 [IQR, 18, 909] days from diagnosis, and the median follow-up of patients remaining on OMT was 1826 [IQR, 494, 3156] days. There were no significant differences in age, preoperative comorbidities, or dissection-related anatomic features between patients who were maintained on OMT during the study compared with those who experienced an aortic event (Table 1). Freedom from aortic intervention or aortic-related death at 1, 5, and 10 years was 62.9%, 43.8%, and 37.6% (Figure 1). In contrast, freedom from aortic intervention or all-cause death at 1, 5, and 10 years was 58.9%, 37.2%, and 26.4% (P = .079). In the cohort of 140 patients who remained on OMT and did not require aortic intervention, freedom from aortic-related death at 1, 5, and 10 years was 89.4%, 82.2%, and 76.9%. Freedom from all-cause death at 1, 5, and 10 years was 80.5%, 65.0%, and 48.0% (P = .011, Figure E2). Outcomes of Aortic Interventions in Uncomplicated TBAD

Of the 131 patients who underwent aortic intervention during the study period, 60 (45.8%) underwent TEVAR and 71 (54.2%) had open descending or thoracoabdominal aortic replacement. Overall survival at 1, 5, and 10 years with TEVAR was 90.3%, 87.4%, and 66.6%, compared with 87.8%, 72.9%, and 49.2% with open repair. Overall survival with OMT at 1, 5, and 10 years was 80.5%, 65.0%, and 48.0%. By pairwise comparison, only the survival difference between OMT and TEVAR was significant (Figure E3, P = .009).

Analysis of Patients With TBAD With and Without Iliac Artery Involvement

An analysis was performed on the basis of the distal extent of the dissection. Patients with TBAD ending before the aortoiliac bifurcation (n = 131, zones 4-9) were compared with patients with TBAD extending into the iliac arteries (n = 153, zones 10-11). On Kaplan-Meier analysis, aortic event-free survival was significantly improved at 1 (iliac 70.8% vs no iliac 55.5%), 5 (iliac 49.5% vs no iliac 37.2%), and 10 years (iliac 44.6% vs no iliac 28.8%) in patients with iliac artery involvement of their TBAD (P = .008, Figure 2). An analysis of the outcomes of zone 10-11 patients demonstrated (1) 29 (17.9%) patients died of an aortic-related death; (2) 65 (40.1%) patients required an aortic intervention, and (3) 68 (42.0%) patients were successfully treated with OMT alone. Discussion

In the spectrum of patients presenting with acute aortic dissection, patients with uncomplicated TBAD remain a paradox and may pose the greatest challenge for long-term survival. In the acute phase, patients with complicated TBAD have a significantly greater chance of experiencing a lethal aortic event than patients with uncomplicated TBAD. Open or endovascular aortic surgery remains the primary therapy and has improved survival. In contrast, the optimal treatment for patients with AUTBAD remains a conundrum. Historically, AUTBAD has been considered a “medical” disease treated with antihypertensive therapy and surveillance imaging (OMT). In select patients, OMT remains an effective long-term strategy, and aortic diameters in the dissected thoracoabdominal aorta exhibit minimal growth with low rates of rupture. However, OMT is ineffective in a substantial number of patients with uncomplicated TBAD, with ≤50% 5-year intervention-free survival rates reported in multiple studies.3,4 Ideally, patients with uncomplicated TBAD at high risk for late complications (ie, false lumen aneurysm formation and/or rupture) could be identified early and undergo TEVAR, whereas low-risk patients could remain on OMT.

It is in this context that we undertook a rigorous investigation of patients with uncomplicated TBAD with the goals of clearly defining the efficacy of OMT and attempting to identify anatomic predictors of OMT failure. To accomplish these objectives, we analyzed a database from 2 high-volume aortic centers with granular data regarding survival and the cause of death for nonsurvivors treated with OMT alone. This allowed the determination of aortic-related mortality for the patients treated with OMT alone in the current study. The importance of aortic-related mortality is that it allows for a better assessment of the efficacy of OMT in the treatment of AUTBAD.

For the entire cohort in the current study, the results of patients treated with OMT were sobering, with intervention-free aortic-related survival of 62.9%, 43.8%, and 37.6% at 1, 5, and 10 years (Figure 1). In this analysis, there was no statistical difference between aortic-related and all-cause mortality. However, the indication for aortic intervention, particularly in the endovascular era, can be highly subjective and vary significantly between surgeons. For this reason, we compared all-cause and aortic-related mortality in patients treated with OMT alone (n = 140) who did not receive aortic intervention during the study period (Figure E2). In this analysis, aortic-related survival at 1, 5, and 10 years was 89.4%, 82.2%, and 76.9%, and significantly improved compared with 80.5%, 65.0%, and 48.0% when using all-cause mortality data (P = .011). These data demonstrate that excessive mortality can be inappropriately attributed to OMT when using overall survival data and highlights the importance of aortic-related mortality in evaluating the true efficacy of OMT. Indeed, OMT may be more effective than the poor outcomes reported from recent “natural history” studies of patients with uncomplicated TBAD.3,4,20,21

Our next analysis evaluated the impact of aortic surgery on survival in patients with AUTBAD. During the study, 131 patients (44.7%) patients required either open or endovascular aortic surgery on the distal aorta. For the entire cohort there was a nonsignificant survival advantage for aortic intervention (open or endovascular) over OMT at 1, 5, and 10 years (Figure 3, A). When we analyzed survival with respect to the type of surgery (open vs endovascular) compared with OMT, there was a 5-year survival benefit for TEVAR compared with OMT (TEVAR 87.4% vs OMT 60.4%, P = .009). There were no statistical survival differences between TEVAR and open surgery, and open surgery and OMT (Figure E3). The survival advantage associated with endovascular therapy is likely related to the aortic remodeling observed after TEVAR. These data support the case for TEVAR to replace OMT as a first-line therapy for acute uncomplicated TBAD.22

The novel findings of this study pertain to the analysis of outcomes based on extension of the dissection flap distal to the aorto-iliac bifurcation. Surprisingly, patients with more extensive dissections involving the iliac arteries had superior freedom from intervention or aortic related death at 1, 5, and 10 years (P = .008, Figure 2). When we examined the impact of aortic intervention on the basis of iliac artery involvement, there was a clear difference in the 2 groups. In patients with dissections ending proximal to the aorto-iliac bifurcation (zones 4-9), there was a significant survival benefit with aortic intervention compared with OMT at 1, 5, and 10 years (P < .001, Figure 3, B). However, in patients with dissections extending into the iliac arteries (zones 10-11), there was no difference in survival between patients who underwent aortic intervention compared with those treated with OMT (P = .201, Figure 3, C). When we compared outcomes of patients treated with OMT with and without iliac involvement, there was a significant survival advantage for patients with dissections extending into the iliac arteries (P < .001, Figure 4). We hypothesize that these outcomes are related to increased false lumen outflow resulting in a reduction in diastolic false lumen pressure due to the large diameter of the iliac artery relative to the smaller diameter abdominal aortic branches. The significance of these findings are that we have identified for the first time, distinct subgroups of patients with uncomplicated TBAD based on anatomy that may benefit from early intervention (zones 4-9), and others that should be treated with OMT alone (zones 10-11).

Limitations of the current study include the lack of information regarding cause of death in the aortic intervention group and lack of knowledge regarding patient compliance with optimal medical therapy. Although we were able to obtain granular data regarding cause of death in the OMT group, the aortic intervention group was more challenging. We were able to comprehensively assess mortality status of the patients, but not the cause of death. As with all studies analyzing the efficacy of medical therapy in the treatment of a disease, outcomes are directly dependent upon patient compliance with their medical regimen. Outcomes from OMT in the current study are directly related to patients being able to afford and take their blood pressure medicine. Unfortunately, we do not have detailed blood pressure data to evaluate patient compliance, and therefore, uncontrolled hypertension related to noncompliance could have negatively impacted outcomes with OMT. Our study spans three decades of treatment and thus may be influenced by trends in diagnosis and treatment. We have addressed this by adjusting for treatment era in our multivariable analyses, but we cannot rule out the possibility that other unmeasured factors may have influenced care over the time period.

Conclusions

An analysis of clinical and radiologic features from a database of patients presenting with acute uncomplicated TBAD has yielded several important results. Intervention-free, aortic-related survival at 5 years was 43.8% in patients with AUTBAD initially treated with OMT. However, OMT provides excellent outcomes in patients not requiring open or endovascular aortic repair with an 82% aortic-related 5-year survival. Novel data has been presented that identifies 2 anatomically distinct cohorts of patients with AUTBAD who have significantly different outcomes when treated with OMT. Patients with AUTBAD with dissections extending into the iliac arteries have a superior adjusted aortic event-free survival compared with patients without iliac artery involvement. Those patients without iliac artery involvement have significantly improved survival when undergoing aortic interventions as opposed to being treated with OMT alone. Conversely, patients with AUTBAD with dissections extending to the iliac arteries do not appear to benefit from aortic intervention and treatment with OMT alone should be considered. These data provide novel insight into the optimal treatment of AUTBAD. On the basis of this anatomically driven analysis, both OMT and TEVAR play significant roles in the management of this potentially lethal disease.

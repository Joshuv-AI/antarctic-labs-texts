Gel-based Ocular Phantom Models in Glaucoma Diagnostics: A Bibliometric and Narrative Review
Authors: Neha Mohanty, Nikhil Shrivastav, Renu Thakur
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGel-based ocular phantom models provide a controlled platform to test the biomechanical factors and validate emerging diagnostic technologies. Despite the growing application in ophthalmic research, no comprehensive bibliometric analysis has been conducted to map their evolution and translational relevance in glaucoma diagnostics.PurposeThe study aimed to bridge this gap by identifying global research trends and highlighting the emerging role of gel-based ocular phantoms in intraocular pressure (IOP) assessment and glaucoma-related diagnostic applications.Materials and methodsA bibliometric analysis of open-access literature (2020–2025) was conducted using the Scopus database. Keyword co-occurrence and temporal overlay analysis were performed using VOSviewer to assess research trends, geographical contributions, and thematic evolution from general tissue-mimicking models to glaucoma-specific applications.ResultsA total of 1,116 publications related to gel or tissue-mimicking phantoms were identified. The results showed a progressive narrowing of research focus from generic tissue-mimicking phantoms toward ocular biomechanical models for IOP assessment, with 62 publications specifically related to glaucoma, IOP, biomechanical modeling, sensor validation, or fluid dynamics. The United States and China emerged as leading contributors. The overlay visualization demonstrated a recent shift toward ocular biomechanics in glaucoma diagnostic simulation, reflecting growing clinical translation.ConclusionOcular gel phantoms are emerging as an integral experimental platform for addressing the biomechanical variability and enhancing safer preclinical validation of glaucoma diagnostic instruments.Clinical significanceThe findings of this study highlight the importance of ocular biomechanics in glaucoma diagnostics and suggest the importance of gel phantom integration for preclinical validation of ocular biomechanics-related instruments to enhance accuracy.

Introduction

Phantoms that resemble tissues, mostly those made of gels or soft biomaterials, have become invaluable in the field of biomedical research and clinical simulation. In the past, these phantoms were created to test and check imaging systems [ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and photoacoustic] in controlled and repeatable conditions.1,2 These phantom gels allow optical, acoustic, mechanical, or dielectric behavior to be tuned that resembles the human tissues, allowing scientists to simulate real-world conditions without the ethical or logistical issues that come with in vivo or ex vivo testing.2–4 The use of gel and soft-tissue phantoms in healthcare has, over time, been extended beyond imaging to include clinical simulation, surgical training, and biomechanical tests as well as medical device validation. But limited phantom research has specifically focused on ophthalmology, taking the eye as an anatomic/biomechanical system. Over the past years, various phantoms have been discussed in the literature; gel phantoms have been customized to investigate thermal therapy models and simulate tissue reaction to heat, and to test wearable devices by using tissues that resemble human bodies.4,5 Phantoms that replicate tissues of the breast,6,7 dry phantoms used for testing structure-activity relationship (SAR),4,8 and tissues that mimic the optical properties of skin have also been developed.4,9 This change is also an indication of an increase in a translational impulse: researchers do not just desire anatomical or optical equivalents but also material and mechanical equivalents, which will act like living tissues in clinical situations. Researchers have recently started developing ocular-specific phantoms (eye phantoms) to replicate the key aspects of the eyeball, such as corneal curvature, intraocular volume and elasticity, and simulate the physiological response of intraocular pressure (IOP) changes and structural deformation.10,18 Ocular phantom-based studies have demonstrated their utility in validating glaucoma-related diagnostic devices under controlled pressure conditions.11,19–21 This provides a safer, replicable alternative to human or animal eyes that are useful for testing devices and surgical training. The growing integration of minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS) and pressure-sensing technologies highlights the importance of clinically realistic ocular phantom models in the preclinical validation and surgical simulation.22,23 The clinical implication is more evident when the study is focused on glaucoma and IOP. The increase in IOP is one of the major risk factors of glaucoma, which is a leading cause of irreversible blindness in the world.24,25 The conventional methods of tonometry and IOP measurements are affected by biomechanical characteristics of the cornea, including thickness, stiffness, and viscoelasticity, which may influence measurement accuracy and interpretation of results.15,26 The recent literature further emphasizes the growing importance of corneal biomechanical assessment in glaucoma diagnosis and disease monitoring.26–29 Thus, eye-phantom models with consideration of these biomechanical aspects are necessary to enhance the accuracy of measurements and validate new diagnostic tools.20,21 Clinical studies have demonstrated that changes in corneal biomechanical properties are associated with visual field progression and glaucoma, underscoring the need for controlled experimental simulation platforms.26,30,31 Despite the progress in the field, to our best knowledge, no such bibliometric study maps the evolution of gel phantoms from global healthcare tissue models to glaucoma- or IOP-based applications. Understanding this transition is important to identify emerging trends, research gaps, and their translational potential to improve glaucoma diagnostics. The study aims to map the global research landscape of gel-based phantoms with a focus on ocular biomechanics and glaucoma-related applications (Fig. 1).

Materials and Methods

A comprehensive search was conducted using the Scopus database on December 5, 2025, at 02:52 pm, using Boolean operators combined keywords: (phantom or gel phantom or hydrogel phantom or tissue-mimicking phantom or ocular phantom) and (eye or IOP or glaucoma). The selection as the primary database was based on its broad coverage of peer-reviewed literature across biomedical and interdisciplinary domains, which is essential for capturing research on tissue-mimicking phantoms. The time frame (2020–2025) was selected to capture the emerging trends and recent developments, particularly related to glaucoma diagnostics and biomechanical modeling approaches in ocular phantom research. Only open-access articles were included in the bibliometric analysis to ensure reproducibility, transparency, and accessibility of data extraction for independent verification of bibliometric findings. This approach represents a trade-off between reproducibility and completeness, as relevant subscription-based studies may have been excluded. The search process adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines, as represented in Figure 2. The final dataset was exported in comma-separated values (CSV) format and analyzed using VOSviewer version 1.6.20 for keyword co-occurrence, thematic clusters, and bibliometric visualizations.

Results

The final dataset comprised 1,116 publications related to gel or tissue-mimicking phantoms. Among them, 149 studies focused on the field of healthcare-related applications, including clinical simulation, imaging validation, and surgical or robotic training. This was narrowed down to 106 publications that contained information on ocular or eye-based phantom models, out of which only 62 articles were focused on glaucoma, IOP, biomechanical modeling, sensor validation, or fluid dynamics. The datasets were analyzed for global publication trends, keyword networks, and overlay visualization of gel phantom research application (average year of publication) in the global landscape, healthcare, ocular phantom applications, and glaucoma/IOP-specific phantoms.

The co-occurrence network on the global scale, as represented in Figure 5, shows that gel phantom research is structured around some large thematic clusters. The highest cluster is associated with medical imaging modalities, specifically computed tomography (CT), MRI, and optical coherence tomography, suggesting that imaging performance measurement is still at the center of phantom development. A second large cluster related to machine learning and deep learning indicates the increased attention to the application of phantom-generated data. The new associations with augmented reality and virtual reality indicate the widening of the application in surgical planning and simulation. The overlay map shows that more recent focus has been made on 3D printing, machine learning, deep learning, augmented reality, and virtual reality, which has shown a shift in computational imaging, personalized fabrication, and simulation-based applications. Applications in healthcare are particularly evident in image-guided surgery and simulation as dominant applications of gel phantoms in the field of healthcare. Strong links to minimally invasive procedures and radiation indicate their important role in training, safety evaluation, and accuracy of procedures. The overall network demonstrates that the gel phantoms are increasingly being utilized as a bridge between physical simulation and digitally assisted interventions in healthcare.

Discussion

The bibliometric study demonstrates a translational progression of gel-based phantom studies, evolving from generic tissue-mimicking phantoms toward clinically important applications in ocular biomechanics and glaucoma diagnostics. The general shift reflects a growing awareness of the limitations of the traditional IOP measurement method and the need for controlled experimental platforms to address these challenges. Although IOP remains the most significant modifiable risk factor in glaucoma, evidence suggests that measurement of IOP alone does not fully capture the complexity of ocular biomechanics.24,32,33 Hydrogels, polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) cryogel, and other tissue-like materials still form the backbone of phantom fabrication due to their ability to mimic the structural, acoustic, and mechanical behavior of human soft tissue, facilitating multimodal imaging and simulation.34–37 The growth of phantom-based research into simulation, material modeling, and surgical and device testing aligns with literature that describes phantoms as repeatable and ethically desirable research tools used in translational healthcare.18,38–41 In eye care, our results are indicative of a growing interest in corneal biomechanics and deformation behavior, which has been demonstrated to affect the accuracy of IOP measurements owing to the presence of corneal thickness, viscoelasticity, and rigidity variation, which significantly influence the accuracy of applanation-based IOP measurements.42,43 Recent clinical studies further support the association between the corneal biomechanical parameters and the diagnosis of glaucoma.27,28,30,31 Ocular models of phantoms that mimic changes in pressure have shown controlled corneal deformation and have been shown to be useful in testing the tonometry and next-generation IOP devices without the risk of human or animal exposure.20,21,44–47 With the widespread adoption of MIGS, clinically realistic eye phantoms can improve training of procedures and enable device testing before patient use, which will increase safety and workflow optimization.22,23

Clinical Translation in Glaucoma Practice

The clinical relevance of gel-based ocular phantoms in glaucoma practice, despite being widely explored for biomechanical simulation, is based on their ability to help address diagnostic-related uncertainties that are hard to isolate in vivo. In routine clinical practice, measurements of IOP are affected by corneal thickness, stiffness, and viscoelasticity, which often contribute to variability among different tonometry techniques.15,26,42,43 Phantom ocular models with tunable biomechanical behavior are a controlled system in which these variables can be systematically varied, and the resultant changes in IOP measurements can be maximally exploited to improve calibration and interpretation of IOP measurements.20,21,44–47 There is growing evidence on the role of corneal biomechanical parameters, especially corneal hysteresis, as an independent predictor of glaucoma progression.27–29,31,33 However, it is difficult to isolate their respective contributions in clinical studies. Phantom models enable the simulation of biomechanical variability regardless of IOP, thus supporting the development and validation of risk stratification methods. These models are important in the validation of emerging diagnostic technologies such as optical coherence elastography and biomechanics-based tonometry.20,21,48–50 Ocular phantoms also offer a safe and reproducible platform for surgical simulation, such as MIGS, device testing, and training, thus improving safety and maximizing clinical outcomes.22,23 Together, these applications make gel-based ocular phantoms translational applications that bridge experimental research and clinical care of glaucoma.

The temporal overlay visualization in the present study highlights a recent shift toward integration of ocular biomechanics into glaucoma phantom research. This suggests a broader transition from a pressure-driven diagnostic approach toward combined biomechanical and imaging-based strategies of assessment. The new interest in glaucoma-oriented phantom models implies potential to improve the reliability of diagnostics, aiding the calibration of devices, and also allowing biomechanical testing, which under in vivo conditions would be impractical.48,49 The bibliometric visualizations illustrate the thematic clustering and relative concentration of research activity across the evolving domain applications.

The limitations of this study include the limitation of bibliometric analysis to the Scopus database, which may not be representative of all the relevant studies indexed in other databases. The inclusion of only open-access publications may exclude high-impact subscription-based studies and limit the completeness of the dataset. The chosen timeframe (2020–2025), though helpful to detect the recent trends, might under-represent the earlier foundational developments in the research of ocular phantoms. These factors should be taken into consideration when interpreting the generalization of the findings.

The findings emphasize the evolving role of gel-based ocular phantom models as a translational bridge between laboratory experiments and a patient-centered glaucoma diagnostic approach. These models contribute toward improving the safety and reliability of glaucoma diagnostic and surgical technologies by supporting device validation through a reproducible simulation platform.

Conclusion

The advancement of gel phantom-related research toward glaucoma-focused applications highlights the increasing usefulness of ocular phantoms that replicate the biomechanical accuracy of the human eye. This underscores their integral role in providing an experimental simulation platform, enhancing the validity of IOP measurement devices, and enabling safer validation of new diagnostic and surgical technologies.

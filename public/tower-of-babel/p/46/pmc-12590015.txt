Health and health related outcomes of Chinese adult surgical patients managed by a nurse-led discharge service: A meta-analysis
Authors: Mei Lin, Shi Lan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients’ discharge from the hospital is a major factor for the continuous normal functioning of the inpatient health care service. A nurse-led discharge service for surgical patients in China refers to the fact where, with the advice of the treating doctor, a senior trained nurse takes the main responsibility to prepare and manage a post-operative patient’s discharge from the hospital, rather than solely depending on the corresponding physician to handle the discharge process. However, conclusions from individual studies are often controversial. Meta-analyses provide more precise estimates of the effect size compared to that of any single study that could have been achieved, reducing the uncertainty of results thereby generating a more efficient overall estimate of the effect providing a single, synthesized conclusion with high level evidence that could be used to develop clinical practice guidelines and inform decision-making in various fields. Therefore, because of the several positive aspects of meta-analyses compared to a single individual study, we aimed to systematically show the health and health related outcomes of Chinese adult surgical patients managed by a nurse-led discharge service through a meta-analysis.MethodsMEDical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System Online, Web of Science, Excerpta Medica dataBASE, the Cochrane library, Google scholar and http://www.Clinicaltrials.gov were the search databases. In this meta-analysis, the statistical work was carried out by the RevMan software version 5.4. For dichotomous data, risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were used to represent the results following the meta-analysis. For continuous data, mean and standard deviation were the inputs, and weighted mean differences (WMD) with 95% CI were used to represent the results following the meta-analysis.ResultsA total number of 1059 participants were included in this meta-analysis whereby 504 patients were assigned to the nurse-led discharge group whereas 555 patients were assigned to the control group. Most of the studies were randomized trials and participants were enrolled from year 2001 to 2024. All the participants were of Chinese origin and were from mainland China, Hong Kong or Taiwan. Results of this meta-analysis showed that this nurse-led discharge service in Chinese adult surgical inpatients was associated with a significantly lower risk of re-admission with RR: 0.46, 95% CI: 0.30 – 0.70; P = 0.0003. However, the risk of mortality was not significantly different (RR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.31 – 1.44; P = 0.31. Moreover, the mean length of hospital stay with WMD: [-0.57; 95% CI: (-2.09 – 0.96); P = 0.47] and quality of life with WMD [5.48, 95% CI: (-1.92 – 12.88); P = 0.15] were similarly manifested.ConclusionsBased on the results of this meta-analysis, a nurse-led discharge service in Chinese adult surgical inpatients is effective in terms of reducing the risk of re-admission to the hospital. However, no significant difference has been observed in terms of improving mortality risk, the length of hospital stay and the quality of life. Even though our meta-analysis could clarify inconsistent finding from individual studies, providing a single, synthesized conclusion, with higher level evidence, newer larger studies would be required to confirm whether a nurse-led discharge service would really be effective in China due to limited data.

Introduction

Patients’ discharge from the hospital is a major factor for the continuous normal functioning of the inpatient health care service (Gonçalves-Bradley et al., 2022). Hospital discharge planning is a major approach in the health care system (Cantrell et al., 2024). However, delayed hospital discharge, which occurs when a patient has been medically discharged from the hospital or clinic, but remains in the medical institution for other non-medical reasons, without having access to any treatment, is still a major concern and this remains an international problem which could be addressed by the implementation of multidisciplinary teams (Cadel et al., 2021). Several reasons have been noted for delayed hospital discharge. It has been observed that even though patients have been discharged from the hospital, they still remain in the hospital due to non-payment of bills, or waiting for the completion of paper works by the concerned medical officers or waiting for the availability of an ambulance to transport them back (Luis et al., 2023). This waiting period poses a problem when new patients require admission and are waiting for a vacant bed (Rojas-García et al., 2018). Furthermore, this delayed discharge can sometimes be a problem when unwanted hospital-related events or complications occur due to this additional hospital stay period (Rosman et al., 2015).

For surgical inpatients, delayed hospital discharge is also of concern. Sometimes surgeons are busy in operation theatres and could not attend patients in post-operative wards. Consulting surgeons prior to discharge in stable post-operative patients might today lead to a delay in hospital discharge (Cadel et al., 2021). Today, senior nurses are permitted to discharge patients following surgical interventions in some countries (Thoma and Waite, 2018). Nurses nowadays have an important role in improving patient care and facilitating hospital discharge (Akiboye et al., 2021). However, the efficacy of a nurse-led discharge service still requires further exploration. In addition, several nurse interventions have been observed in the United States of America and the United Kingdom (Maier, 2019). Today, this practice is gaining traction in China.

According to the latest demographic data, China has entered an aging society (Office for National Statistics, 2022) (Office for National Statistics 2022). Therefore, middle-aged and elderly people are the main targeted groups for continuing care services (defined as the fact that treatment and care a patient received should be transferred likewise without interruption as they transferred from hospitals or other healthcare facilities to their homes or communities) in China. China attaches great importance to the development of continuity of care and has implemented policies such as Outline of the Healthy China 2030 Plan and the Opinions of the State Council on Accelerating the Development of the Elderly Service Industry, which have contributed to the development of continuity of care modes such as hospital discharge planning, referral services, long-term care, home care, chronic disease case management, follow-up consultation and outreach service (Chen et al., 2017). Also, China has been giving increasing support to the development of continuity of care with the issuance of policies such as the National Nursing Career Development Plan, the Guidelines on promoting the reform and development of nursing services and so on implying the need to expand the coverage of nursing services as well as the promotion of the continuity of care so that patients can get access to continuous health care services (National Health and Family Planning Commission 2018).

Therefore, nurse-led discharge services were promoted. A nurse-led discharge service for surgical patients in China refers to the fact where, with the advice of the treating doctor, a senior trained nurse takes the main responsibility to prepare and manage a post-operative patient’s discharge from the hospital, including assessing readiness, providing relevant instructions, and coordinating post-discharge care, rather than solely depending on the corresponding physician to handle the discharge process. Studies have shown that early discharge from the hospital by the nurses and later being followed up by the surgeons resulted in better patients’ satisfaction (Everall et al., 2019). In addition, the study by Wong et al. showed that nurse-led discharge was almost similar to the usual care discharge in terms of decreasing hospital re-admissions (Wong et al., 2021).

Few studies were published on surgical patients managed by a nurse-led discharge service. Several of the individual studies were conducted from China. Meta-analyses provide more precise estimates of the effect size compared to that of any single study that could have been achieved, reducing the uncertainty of results. In addition, by pooling data from several studies through meta-analyses, the effective sample size could increase, improving the ability to detect a treatment effect. Moreover, a meta-analysis could assess the consistency of results across studies thereby generating a more efficient overall estimate of the effect. Also, meta-analyses could clarify inconsistent finding from individual studies, providing a single, synthesized conclusion on whether an intervention is effective therefore, providing high level evidence that could be used to develop clinical practice guidelines and inform decision-making in various fields. Therefore, because of the several positive aspects of meta-analyses compared to a single individual study, we aimed to systematically show the health and health related outcomes of Chinese adult surgical patients managed by a nurse-led discharge service through a meta-analysis.

Methods

Search databases and searched strategies

Relevant studies showing a nurse-led discharge service following surgery were searched through electronic databases from January 2025 to February 2025. MEDical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System Online (MEDLINE), Web of Science, Excerpta Medica dataBASE (EMBASE), the Cochrane library, Google scholar and http://www.Clinicaltrials.gov were the search databases. In addition, reference lists of suitable publications were also searched for relevant studies.

During the search process, the following searched terms were used to look for relevant studies from the searched databases: ‘Nurse-led discharge and surgical patients’; ‘Nurse-led discharge post-operative’; ‘Nurse-led discharge service and surgical patients’; ‘Post-surgical patients discharge and nurse’.

Criteria for inclusion and exclusion

Studies were selected based on the criteria of inclusion and exclusion. The criteria for inclusion were: (a) studies that compared the outcomes observed with a nurse-led discharge service versus a control group; (b) studies that were based on post-operative patients or surgical inpatients; (c) studies that were randomized trials or observational studies; (d) studies that were based on patients of Chinese origin.

The criteria for exclusion were: (a) studies that were meta-analyses, narrative or systematic reviews; (b) studies that were case studies or editorials; (c) studies that were not based on surgical in-patients; (d) studies that did not include participants of Chinese origin; (e) repeated studies.

The endpoints which were selected to be assessed in this meta-analysis were the outcomes that were reported in two or more original studies for comparison. The endpoints which were assessed in this meta-analysis included: (a) the risk of re-admission or re-hospitalization after recently being discharged from the hospital; (b) the risk of mortality; (c) the length of hospital stay defined as the number of days that the patients were admitted for treatment; (d) the quality of life which was defined as the health status, comfort and stable life following discharge from the hospital.

These endpoints were classified in subgroups and then analysis was carried out. Similar endpoints from different studies were classified as one particular subgroup to be analyzed.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Data were independently extracted by the authors of this meta-analysis. First of all the publication year, the authors’ names, the time period of participants’ enrollment, the total number of participants assigned to the nurse-led discharge group and the control group respectively, the type of study, the number of events associated with each outcome for example, if mortality was considered an outcome, the number of deaths which occurred were noted, the mean and standard deviation, the baseline characteristics, the endpoints, the follow-up time period were carefully extracted. If any author disagreed upon inclusion or exclusion of certain data, the problem was brought forward to a discussion panel with all the authors as well as the corresponding author, and a final decision was reached after carefully discussing the matter.

Assessment of the risk of bias is a major component of systematic reviews. Introduction of bias maybe due to confounding factors, incorrect criteria during the selection of participants to be included in the study, departures from the intended exposure, small errors during measurement of outcomes, lack of outcome data and selective reporting. Therefore, for the maintenance of transparency in the systematic review findings, the methodological quality of the randomized trial was assessed with reference to the criteria set by the Cochrane Collaboration (Higgins et al., 2011) whereas the quality assessment of the observational studies was carried out by the Newcastle Ottawa Scale tool (Stang, 2010).

The recommendations suggested by the Cochrane collaboration included: a random sequence generation (selection bias); an allocation concealment (selection bias); the blinding of participants and personnel (performance bias); the blinding of outcome assessment (detection bias); an incomplete outcome data (attrition bias); selective reporting (reporting bias); and other bias.

For the assessment of non-randomized trials by the Newcastle Ottawa scale, a 'star system' was developed in which a study is judged on three broad perspectives: the selection of the study groups; the comparability of the groups; and the ascertainment of either the exposure or outcome of interest for case-control or cohort studies respectively:

The selection category comprised of representative of the exposed cohort; selection of the external control; ascertainment of exposure; and outcome of interest not present at the start of the study. The comparability category comprised of the main factor and additional factor based on comparability of cohorts whereas the outcome category comprised of the assessment of the outcomes; the sufficient follow-up time period; and adequacy of follow-up.

Statistical analysis

In this meta-analysis, the statistical work was carried out by the RevMan software version 5.4. Heterogeneity was assessed by two methods: the Q statistical test whereby a subgroup analysis assessing a specific outcome with a P value less or equal to 0.05 was considered as statistically significant, and the I2 statistic test whereby heterogeneity increases with an increased I2 value. If the I2 value is greater than 50 %, a random effects statistical model was used during analysis, whereas when the I2 value was less than 50 %, a fixed effects statistical model was used.

For dichotomous data, risk ratios (RR) with 95 % confidence intervals (CI) were used to represent the results following data analysis. For continuous data, mean and standard deviation were the inputs, and weighted mean differences (WMD) with 95 % CI were used to represent the results following data analysis.

Sensitivity analysis was also carried out. Each of the study which was included in this meta-analysis was excluded one at a time and a new analysis was carried out each time and the result obtained was compared with the main result of this analysis to verify for any significant difference.

Publication bias was also assessed using funnel plots. Funnel plots were generated from the RevMan software. Publication bias was visually assessed based on the symmetry of the funnel plots.

Compliance with ethical guidelines

This paper does not include experiment carried out on animals or humans by any of the authors, therefore, an ethical or board review approval was not required for this study.

Results

Search outcomes

This is a meta-analysis and therefore the Preferred Reporting Items in Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) guideline (Page et al., 2021) was followed. After a thorough search from the previously mentioned electronic databases, a total number of 376 publications were obtained. Following a careful assessment of the titles and abstracts, an initial round of elimination was carried out whereby irrelevant studies were directly eliminated. This involved the elimination of 345 publications because they were not based on a nurse-led discharge service programs. Few were based on nurses’ implication in surgery but were not based on any nurse-led discharge service. Finally, 31 full texts articles were assessed for eligibility.

Another round of elimination was carried out based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria and hence, studies were eliminated due to the following reasons:(a)They were meta-analyses and systematic reviews (n = 2);(b)They did not include participants of Chinese origin (n = 6);(c)They did not include surgical inpatients (n = 3);(d)Case studies (n = 2);(e)Repeated studies (n = 12).

Main results of this analysis

In this analysis, the endpoints which were reported from different studies were classified in specific subgroups and they were then compared.

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

Sensitivity analysis was carried out and consistent results were observed throughout.

When analyzing the risk of re-admission, when study Hu2020 was excluded, and a new analysis was carried out, the result was RR: 0.50, 95 % CI: 0.31 – 0.81; P = 0.005, which was not significantly different from the result of the main analysis. For the same endpoint, when the study Huang2005 was excluded and a new analysis was carried out, still the risk of re-admission was not significantly different (RR: 0.50, 95 % CI: 0.31 – 0.79; P = 0.003) from the main result of this analysis. When study Lin2024 was excluded, the new analysis assessing risk of re-admission was still in favor of the main result of this analysis with RR: 0.51, 95 % CI: 0.33 – 0.79; P = 0.003). Similarly, when studies Shyu2005, Shyu2020 and Yang2025 were excluded each at a time, the results which were obtained were still similar to the main result of this analysis showing consistent results throughout.

Even when assessing the risk of mortality, when study Shyu2005 was excluded and a new analysis was carried out, the result was similar with RR: 0.71, 95 % CI: 0.32 – 1.57; P = 0.40). When study Shyu2020 was excluded and a new analysis assessing the risk of mortality was carried out, still, the result was similar to the main result of this analysis with RR: 0.34, 95 % CI: 0.01 – 8.16; P = 0.50.

Sensitivity analysis was also carried out for the subgroup assessing the length of hospital stay and the quality of life. When study Huang2005 was excluded and a new analysis was carried out, the length of hospital stay was not significantly different from the main result with WMD: 0.25, 95 % CI: −0.65 – 1.16; P = 0.59. Similarly, when study Shyu2005 was excluded and a new analysis was conducted to assess the length of hospital stay, the result which was generated was similar with the main result of this analysis with WMD: −0.73, 95 % CI: −2.97 – 1.51; P = 0.52.

Discussion

In this meta-analysis, we systematically demonstrated that a nurse-led discharge service was associated with a significantly lower risk of re-admission to the hospital among Chinese patients. However, there was no significant difference in terms of risk of mortality, length of hospital stay and quality of life.

To demonstrate this significantly lower risk of re-admission, 504 participants from all the 6 studies were included in the nurse-led discharge group versus 555 participants which were involved in the control group. A fixed effect statistical model was used since a lower heterogeneity (I2 = 42 %) was seen during subgroup analysis. A statistically significant result in favor of this nurse led discharge service was obtained (P = 0.0003). However, when mortality risk was analyzed, only 3 studies were involved with a total number of 211 participants in the experimental group and 214 participants in the control group were included and the final result was not significant statistically for risk of mortality (P = 0.31).

When we analyzed the length of hospital stay using continuous data (mean and standard deviation), 3 studies were included with a total number of 236 participants in the nurse-led discharge group versus 288 participants in the control group. Due to the presence of a higher heterogeneity (I2 = 76 %) during the analysis of the length of hospital stay, a random effect statistical model was used and an insignificant result was obtained (P = 0.47). Similarly, when the quality of life was analyzed, 3 studies were involved with a total number of 209 participants in the nurse-led discharge service versus 207 participants in the control group.

Similarly, another meta-analysis was published based on the general population (including participants from United States, Canada and so on) including Chinese and non-Chinese patients (Mao et al., 2022). The analysis showed a nurse-led discharge service to be associated with significantly lower re-admission rate, emergency visit and quality of life. However, in our current study which included only patients of Chinese origin, quality of life was not significantly different.

Our current meta-analysis was based on Chinese surgical inpatients. However, another meta-analysis based on the effectiveness of a nurse-led early discharge planning program for hospital in-patients with chronic disease or rehabilitation need which included 3438 general participants around the globe showed that compared to standard care, this nurse-led discharge program was apparently associated with several positive aspects of care including lower risk of re-admission, mortality and improvement in the quality of life of the patients (Zhu et al., 2015).

In a randomized controlled trial based on patient, carer and health service outcomes of nurse-led early discharge service after surgery for breast cancer whereby 108 participants underwent mastectomy, the authors showed that a nurse-led early discharge had no adverse impact on the quality of life or the satisfaction of patients and this practice was safely implemented (Wells et al., 2004). The authors showed that their study provided evidence of the benefits of an early discharge service. The mean hospital stay was 3.96 days for those patients in the nurse-led early discharge group versus 6.22 days in the control group. However, the patients preferred to stay in the hospital until their wounds were completely recovered. Participants from both groups were very satisfied. Those who were discharged early by the nurse-led team stated that they got the opportunity to be home with their families whereas those patients who were in the control group praised the support they received by the other patients when they were admitted in ward. 71 % of the participants in the nurse-led discharge group stated that they would opt for this nurse-led discharge service in the future.

A recent study which compared a nurse-led discharge service versus surgeon-led discharge for patients who were candidates for laparoscopic surgery also showed the former discharge service not to be associated with a higher risk of re-admission (Yang et al., 2025). In fact, re-admission was statistically the same for those who received discharge through a nurse-led service or who were discharged by the surgeon. The authors therefore stated that a nurse-led discharge service was as effective as discharge done by the treating surgeon.

A nurse could be understood to try his best to help and care for the patients in order to alleviate their pain and suffering and improving their quality of life (Edberg et al., 2008). Hence, a nurse-led discharge program could result in the completely fit patients being discharged from the hospital, and patients are well advised about further management, medication use, wound dressing and rehabilitations at home. Also, in a Chinese retrospective cohort study including 706 participants discharged with T tube post-operatively (Wang et al., 2023), patients who were assigned to a nurse-led transitional care program were more satisfied and were had a better quality of life post-operatively showing that a nurse-led program could be more beneficial. Furthermore, in a single-center, propensity score matched control before-after study, whereby 2851 Chinese patients were admitted for abdominal surgery, the authors demonstrated that a nurse-led pain relief model could significantly enhance the quality and intensity of abdominal pain among those post-operative patients, indicating that such a nursing intervention could be effective for providing a better pain control management among similar patients (Fang et al., 2021). Further studies should explore this aspect of nurse-led discharge program.

Limitations

This study also has limitations. First of all, due to the inclusion of only a restricted number of participants, this might affect the results of this meta-analysis. In addition, difference in outcome measurement could impact the effect sizes and this could be another limitation of this meta-analysis. Moreover, another limitation might be the fact that surgical procedures were not similar in all the studies. This could have affected outcomes such as the length of hospital stay and other clinical outcomes. Furthermore, a higher heterogeneity was observed during assessment of length of hospital stay and quality of life. Furthermore, the duration of follow up time period was neglected in this meta-analysis. In addition, co-morbid conditions such as patients with diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, hypertension and other past medical history which could affect the length of hospital stay were not considered in this meta-analysis and were not taken into consideration since those co-morbidities were not reported in the original studies. At last, even if meta-analyses often provide more precise estimates of the effect size compared to that of any single study that could have been achieved, reducing the uncertainty of results thereby generating a more efficient overall estimate of the effect providing a single, synthesized conclusion with high level evidence that could be used to develop clinical practice guidelines and inform decision-making, the sample size in this analysis was still limited to provide robust results. At last, even though our analysis showed substantial heterogeneity for the key outcomes, except for the use of an I2 statistical test and the random effects statistical model, we could not conduct further appropriate analyses including pre-specified subgroup analyses to investigate the sources of heterogeneity. This could also be a limitation in this analysis.

Conclusions

Based on the results of this meta-analysis, a nurse-led discharge service in Chinese adult surgical inpatients is effective in terms of reducing the risk of re-admission to the hospital. However, no significant difference has been observed in terms of improving mortality risk, the length of hospital stay and the quality of life. Even though our meta-analysis could clarify inconsistent finding from individual studies, providing a single, synthesized conclusion, with higher level evidence, newer larger studies would be required to confirm whether a nurse-led discharge service would really be effective in China due to limited data.

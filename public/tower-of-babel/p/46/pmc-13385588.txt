NSP-AE: Neuro-symbolic process autoencoder for time anomaly detection
Authors: Na Fang, Ke Lu, Xianwen Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTemporal anomaly detection is a critical task in business process management, aiming to identify process instances whose temporal behavior deviates from expected specifications. Unlike activity-sequence compliance checking, temporal anomaly detection focuses on temporal attributes such as waiting times and event intervals during actual execution. Some instances may exhibit abnormal temporal behavior even when their control flow remains compliant, making such deviations difficult to uncover from control-flow information alone. Existing methods typically model activity transition relationships or treat temporal information merely as auxiliary neural-network features, while few methods jointly incorporate process structural constraints and temporal deviation signals into anomaly scoring. This paper proposes NSP-AE, a neural-symbolic process autoencoder for temporal anomaly detection. First, each event trace is represented using activity, resource, and local delta-time information. Second, a Petri net is discovered from the training log using Inductive Miner, and prefix replay is used to derive the set of activities reachable at the current process state. Third, this reachable activity set is transformed into a dynamic compliance mask and embedded into the decoder to constrain the activity prediction space. Finally, NSP-AE performs trace-level anomaly scoring by combining activity prediction deviation, compliance-constraint signals, and log-space time-interval deviation. Controlled timestamp perturbation experiments on four public event logs show that NSP-AE outperforms comparison baselines on most logs, with statistically significant advantages on multiple logs.Keywords: process mining, neuro-symbolic fusion, anomaly detection, temporal anomalies, compliance masking

1 Introduction

Business process anomaly detection is a critical task in business process management, aimed at identifying process instances that deviate from expected operational patterns in event logs. In scenarios such as enterprise information systems, healthcare processes, financial approvals, and IT service management, event logs record the activities, resources, timestamps, and related attributes of process instances during execution. Conducting anomaly detection on these logs helps administrators identify potential risks before processes cause serious consequences and take appropriate intervention measures accordingly.

Existing research on process anomaly detection primarily focuses on control flow deviations, such as activity skipping, insertion, substitution, or sequence errors. For such issues, existing methods typically determine whether a process instance deviates from common execution paths by analyzing activity transition probabilities, sequence likelihood, sequence prediction, or reconstruction errors. 1

However, compliance with activity sequences does not necessarily imply normality in process execution time. In real-world business scenarios, some risks do not manifest as obvious step errors but are hidden within the temporal dimension. For example, in an emergency sepsis treatment process, the steps from triage and registration to receiving critical treatment may fully comply with protocols; however, abnormally prolonged waiting times between critical events can still lead to severe consequences. Similarly, a credit approval process may include all necessary approval activities yet deviate significantly from expectations in terms of waiting times or event intervals. Such anomalies present a “compliant appearance” in terms of control flow but exhibit abnormalities in temporal attributes; therefore, methods relying solely on activity sequences or control flow information struggle to fully uncover these issues.

Although existing methods have incorporated temporal information, their application still falls short of the task addressed in this paper. Scoring methods based on sequence statistics or activity relationships primarily characterize discrete event patterns and lack direct modeling of continuous time intervals; in scenarios where the activity sequence remains unchanged and only timestamps are perturbed, such scores typically fail to provide effective discrimination. Neural sequence models can jointly encode timestamps or time intervals with attributes such as activities and resources, 2 but when training and scoring are primarily driven by activity prediction or sequence reconstruction, time deviation signals may be weakened.3,4 Furthermore, Petri nets and consistency checks provide explicit structural semantics, but they are often used as compliance analysis tools and rarely directly contribute to candidate activity constraints and anomaly scoring in neural models.

A key challenge in temporal anomaly detection lies in how to model and score temporal interval deviations while ensuring reachability within the control flow structure. Therefore, the model must not only identify whether a trace conforms to common activity sequences but also determine, within structurally compliant candidate paths, whether its temporal behavior deviates from normal patterns. To this end, this paper proposes NSP-AE, a neural-symbolic process autoencoder for temporal anomaly detection. Unlike methods that treat time merely as an auxiliary input feature, NSP-AE extracts Petri nets from training logs and transforms the set of reachable activities at the current process state into a dynamic compliance mask, which is embedded into the decoder to constrain the activity prediction space. Under this structural constraint, the model further utilizes local time interval representations and log-space time interval deviation signals to score traces that exhibit control-flow consistency but abnormal temporal behavior.

The core idea of this paper is not to replace existing components such as LSTMs, autoencoders, or Petri nets individually, but rather, inspired by the concept of neural-symbolic learning, 5 to integrate neural prediction, signal reconstruction, process structural constraints, and temporal deviation scoring into a single detection framework. 2 Related work

2.1 Business process anomaly detection

Business process anomaly detection aims to identify process executions that deviate from expected behavior. A closely related line of research is conformance checking, which quantifies discrepancies between observed traces and a reference process model using measures such as fitness and appropriateness. Rozinat quantifies discrepancies between the model structure and execution logs of a business process by calculating their fitness and appropriateness. 6 Subsequent research further refined the consistency evaluation by incorporating dimensions such as Precision 7 ; the Alignment method formalizes the problem of optimal alignment between traces and models, thereby enabling the localization of specific insertion, skipping, or replacement operations. 8 While such methods possess clear process semantics, they incur high computational overhead in large-scale or online monitoring scenarios.

To reduce modeling and computational costs, statistical anomaly detection methods attempt to construct anomaly scores directly from logs. Ko and Comuzzi introduced statistical leverage into business process event log anomaly detection to identify anomalous traces containing sequence patterns such as activity substitutions, skips, and rework. 9 The likelihood graph proposed by Böhmer et al. incorporates perspectives such as activities, resources, and time into the anomaly scoring of process execution events, enabling the characterization of multi-attribute event relationships to some extent. 10 Such methods can efficiently detect statistical deviations in discrete event relationships, but their scoring typically relies on activity or attribute relationships in historical logs, and their ability to directly characterize continuous time intervals and long-tail time distributions remains limited.

In recent years, deep learning techniques have been widely applied to process anomaly detection tasks. BINet, 11 proposed by Nolle et al., and autoencoder methods 12 can learn representations from time series and detect anomalies through multi-task prediction or reconstruction error; graph autoencoders further attempt to enhance anomaly detection capabilities by leveraging the structure of activity relationships. 13

Deep anomaly detection is no longer limited to simple reconstruction error but is gradually emphasizing issues such as temporal memory, structured relationships, adaptation to distribution shifts, and health state representation. Gao et al. pointed out that standard autoencoders may overgeneralize and reconstruct anomalous samples too well, thereby reducing the reconstruction difference between anomalous and normal samples. 14 Xie et al. proposed HASGDetector, which partitions long-term, large-scale host behavior into subgraphs and captures context and temporal dependencies through large-scale attribute heterogeneous graphs to reduce computational overhead and the risk of false positives and false negatives on large-scale security data. 15 Zhang et al. focused on distribution shifts in fault diagnosis caused by unseen operating conditions. They used a modified 1-D stable diffusion to generate unseen operating condition samples and trained the model using adversarial examples generated by the fast gradient sign method, thereby enhancing generalization to unknown operating conditions. 16 Han et al., focusing on unsupervised anomaly detection, proposed the ISLAD model, which combines scale distribution learning with compact representation learning to enhance robustness against subtle anomalies and training data contamination. 17 These studies demonstrate that robustly representing samples under complex conditions is crucial for anomaly detection. Based on this, this paper incorporates process structural constraints and local temporal deviation signals into a unified scoring framework.

2.2 Time anomaly detection and time-aware process monitoring

Time is a critical feature in business process management. Research on remaining time prediction, process performance analysis, and deadline risk assessment all rely on the modeling of temporal behavior. Rogge-Solti et al. utilized non-Markovian stochastic Petri nets to analyze long-span distributed time, conducting remaining duration predictions for processes involving complex patterns such as concurrency, cycles, and choices, and assessing the risk of deadline violations. 18 Tax et al. introduced LSTM models to establish a predictive framework for various forecasting tasks, enabling effective prediction of the next activity, timestamp, and remaining time; models based on the GRU architecture and attention mechanisms further improved prediction accuracy. 2 Ni et al. introduced multiscale time, integrating information at different granularities—such as event attributes, subsequences, and complete instances—and proposed a hierarchical Transformer-based prediction method. 19 Related reviews also indicate that deep learning has become a key technical approach for process duration prediction and predictive monitoring.20,21

Neural network models have advantages in analyzing event context. ProcessTransformer 22 introduced the Transformer into predictive business process monitoring, using self-attention mechanisms to model long-range dependencies; Rama-Maneiro 23 et al. pointed out that relying solely on sequence models may overlook structural information such as loops, concurrency, and branching in processes, thus requiring consideration of the integration between event logs and process structure. However, time prediction is not equivalent to time anomaly detection. Prediction tasks typically optimize mean error, with the goal of fitting common behavior; temporal anomaly detection, on the other hand, requires the model to be sensitive to abnormal deviations in wait times, event intervals, or execution speeds. A model may achieve low error in remaining time prediction but still fail to reliably distinguish between normal fluctuations and abnormal delays.

The temporal anomaly detection task addressed in this paper further employs a controlled setting: the activity sequences in the test set remain unchanged, and anomaly labels are derived from perturbations in the temporal dimension. This setup intentionally excludes differences in control flow, allowing the experiments to more directly test whether the model truly utilizes local temporal interval information. Accordingly, the experimental results are not interpreted as a comprehensive evaluation of naturally labeled anomalies, but rather as a measure of detection capability under controlled temporal anomalies.

2.3 Neuro-symbolic learning and process-structural constraints

Neuro-symbolic learning aims to combine the representation learning capabilities of neural networks with the structured reasoning capabilities of symbolic systems. 5 Garcez and Lamb 24 view neuro-symbolic AI as an important direction for the development of artificial intelligence; Hitzler and Sarker 25 summarized various methods for incorporating logical rules, knowledge graphs, or symbolic constraints into neural models. The common underlying idea is that when explicit structural knowledge exists in a task, one should not rely solely on neural networks to implicitly learn this knowledge from data; rather, it should be incorporated into the model in the form of constraints, structures, or reasoning modules.

In the field of process mining, Petri nets are a classic formal tool for describing process states, activity enablement relationships, and control flow semantics. 26 As one of the most widely used algorithms today, Inductive Miner can mine Petri net structures with well-formalized characteristics from event logs, providing relatively accurate structural prior knowledge. 27 Subsequent research on process discovery has further addressed issues such as uncertain event data, automatic process discovery evaluation, and the selection of data-driven predictive monitoring methods28–30; recent work has also evaluated generative capabilities in process mining tasks from the perspective of large language models. 31

Unlike the aforementioned work, this paper focuses on the use of structural constraints in temporal anomaly detection. NSP-AE transforms the set of reachable activities in the current process state into a dynamic compliance mask, which is then fed into a neural decoder and a trace scoring mechanism. Given that process discovery models may be incomplete or noisy, this paper avoids interpreting process structural constraints as absolutely complete rules during mask generation; instead, it treats them as constraints on the candidate activity space and as signals for compliance scoring.

Definition 2 (Petri Net) 26 : A Petri net system is defined as a quintuple PN=(P,T,F,m0,mf) , where P is a finite set of places representing process states; T is a finite set of transitions corresponding to process activities; F⊆(P×T)∪(T×P) is a flow relation defining the connections between states and transitions; m0 and mf are the initial and final markings, respectively.

Let l:T→A be the mapping of transitions to activity labels; then the set of enabled activities at the current marking can be expressed as E(m)={l(t)∣t∈T,and t is enabled under m} .

Raw timestamps, with their absolute starting-point differences and monotonically cumulative nature, are not suitable for directly representing local temporal patterns. The model in this paper focuses on the local time intervals between adjacent events, rather than the cumulative time from the start of the trace to the current position. Therefore, timestamps are first converted into cumulative relative time relative to the trace’s starting point, and Δτi inputs are obtained through adjacent differencing.

Definition 5 (Controlled Time Anomaly Detection Task): Given a training log Ltrain and a test trace set Ltest , the objective is to learn a scoring function S(σ) such that traces with perturbed time intervals receive higher anomaly scores. In the experiments of this paper, the activity sequences of the test traces remain unchanged, and anomalies are generated by shortening, lengthening, or combining perturbations to the time intervals between adjacent events. Therefore, the results of this paper support an assessment of the model’s ability to detect controlled-time anomalies and should not be interpreted as a direct validation of the natural anomaly labels in the raw logs.

The core challenge of temporal anomaly detection lies in its compliance camouflage characteristic: anomalous traces may appear entirely consistent with normal processes at the activity sequence level but deviate from historical patterns in the continuous time dimension. If a model focuses solely on activity transitions or sequence reconstruction, it may fail to identify such temporal risks hidden within compliant paths.

4.1 Event representation

Feature engineering is crucial in the detection of temporal anomalies in processes. Raw timestamps in event logs are not suitable for use as direct input features, as they are monotonically increasing and have irregular time spans. Consequently, the start times of different traces may differ by a few seconds or even several years, resulting in an extremely wide numerical range for the features. Furthermore, timestamps obscure the local flow patterns between adjacent events, making it difficult to intuitively discern the speed and duration of activities. In light of this, this paper adopts time intervals as the core representation to accurately capture relative temporal dynamics.

Recent component analysis shows that replacing cumulative time with delta-time yields clear mean AUC gains on Sepsis and BPI2012_O, but results in a slight decrease on Helpdesk. Therefore, this paper interprets it as a time representation that is consistent with the task setting and effective in most test logs, rather than a universally optimal choice across all datasets.

4.2 Neural encoder and decoder

NSP-AE adopts an encoder-decoder architecture. The encoder takes a sequence of event representations as input and outputs hidden states at each position. The officially optimized configuration uses a unidirectional LSTM encoder, ensuring that the representation at position i depends only on the current and historical events, thereby aligning with the runtime prefix monitoring scenario.

The decoder also uses a unidirectional LSTM and outputs multiple prediction heads at each position. The activity prediction head outputs unmasked activity logits; the numerical head outputs numerical feature predictions; and the time head outputs positive time interval predictions via Softplus. Activity targets use shifted prediction, meaning that the activity prediction target at position i is ai+1 , with no subsequent activity target at the final position. For the time component, during training and evaluation, the model’s predicted time intervals are compared with the observed delta-time. Therefore, NSP-AE does not simply reconstruct the entire input trace, but rather simultaneously learns activity prediction, auxiliary numerical feature estimation, and local time interval estimation within the same encoder-decoder framework. In the subsequent evaluation, this paper constructs trace-level anomaly scores by utilizing neural activity prediction deviation, Petri net compliance constraints, auxiliary numerical deviation, and log-space time interval deviation, respectively.

4.3 Dynamic compliance masks for Petri Nets

The Petri net mask is the symbolic constraint module of NSP-AE. This paper first uses Inductive Miner to discover a Petri net (PN) from the training log Ltrain . Inductive Miner targets structured process models and is commonly used to obtain replayable process models suitable for consistency analysis. After obtaining the PN, NSP-AE performs token replay on the trace prefix to estimate the marked state at the current position in the process execution.

This process suppresses the probability of structurally unreachable activities to approximately 0, thereby defining the activity prediction space within structurally reachable paths. In other words, Mi[a]=0 indicates that activity a is executable in the current process state, while Mi[a]=−C indicates that activity a is structurally unreachable. The mask transforms the symbolic process knowledge in the Petri net into numerical constraints that the neural decoder can directly utilize.

For example, in a loan approval process, if the prefix is “Application, Review,” the process specification allows “Approval” or “Rejection” as successor activities. If the set of activities is ordered as “Application, Review, Approval, Rejection,” and the neural network’s raw logits are zi=[1.2,0.8,2.1,1.9] , then the mask can be written as Mi=[−C,−C,0,0] . After applying softmax(zi+Mi) , the probabilities for “Application” and “Review” are suppressed to near zero, and the probability mass is distributed solely between “Approval” and “Rejection.” Without this mask, the model would need to rely entirely on the data to learn that “Application” and “Review” should not occur again after this prefix; the dynamic compliance mask, however, explicitly injects this control-flow rule into the prediction space as a structural constraint.

5 Experiments

This section evaluates the detection capabilities of NSP-AE under a controlled temporal anomaly protocol, focusing on whether the model can distinguish normal traces from anomalous ones by utilizing local temporal interval deviation signals under conditions where the control flow is fixed but the temporal dimension is perturbed. The experiments address the following three research questions:

(Detection Performance): Under the controlled-time anomaly protocol, does NSP-AE achieve a higher AUC-ROC compared to the statistical baseline, traditional neural autoencoders, BINet, and Transformer baselines?

(Statistical Reliability): Are the differences between NSP-AE and each baseline statistically significant across matched random seed?

(Component Contributions): Do the Δτi input, time loss weights, Petri net mask, and unidirectional encoder contribute to the system’s expected functionality?

Statistics for the experimental datasets.

The dataset Sepsis is sourced from the sepsis case management system of an emergency department at a hospital in the Netherlands and records the complete clinical care process from patient admission to discharge. This dataset is characterized by long event chains (averaging 14.5 events) and a wide variety of activity types (16 in total), covering various clinical activities such as registration, triage, laboratory testing, medication administration, and nursing care. The time intervals range from minutes to several days, making it suitable for evaluating a model’s ability to capture complex temporal patterns. The dataset BPI2012_O originates from the loan application approval process at a Dutch financial institution and is one of the most commonly used benchmark datasets in the field of business process mining. This paper utilizes a subset of activities prefixed with “O” (the Offer phase), which focuses on operations during the application submission stage; these traces are relatively short but event-dense. The dataset BPI2013 records the event management process at Volvo IT; with an average trace length of only 4.5 events, it falls under the short-trace scenario. The dataset Helpdesk comes from the ticket processing system of an IT help desk; it is of moderate size and features a clear phased structure.

Each dataset was split into a training set and a test set in an 80%/20% ratio. The formal experiments did not use a validation set or early stopping. Anomalies were injected only into the test set; the training set was not subjected to manual time perturbations and was used as-is based on the original logs. We set up controlled time anomalies: first, 30% of the test traces were randomly selected as anomaly candidates; for each candidate trace, an adjacent event interval was randomly selected; then randomly apply perturbations in the form of too fast, too slow, or a combination of both. A too-fast perturbations shorten the original interval to 1% to 10% of its original value; a too-slow perturbations expand the original interval to 20 to 100 times its original value; the combination mode includes extremely fast interval perturbations. After perturbation, the timestamps of subsequent events are shifted to preserve the temporal order of events. The sequence of activity labels remains unchanged throughout the process.

The purpose of this setup is to isolate the temporal dimension, preventing control-flow information from directly explaining anomalous labels. Therefore, the experimental results primarily address whether the model can distinguish between normal and anomalous traces based on temporal interval deviations while the activity sequence remains unchanged.

5.3 Parameter settings and statistical tests

The experiments used 10 random seeds, with the seeds for the training/testing split, anomaly injection, and model training kept consistent. All neural models used the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 32, and a maximum sequence length of 50. The hidden dimension for the main experiments of LSTM-AE, BINet, Transformer, and NSP-AE was set to 64; while the hidden dimension for DAE was 128. The baseline models—LSTM-AE, DAE, BINet, Transformer, and NSP-AE—were trained for 30 epochs; the optimized NSP-AE, ablation variants, and sensitivity analysis models were trained for 50 epochs. Petri net discovery was performed using Inductive Miner.

The primary evaluation metric is AUC-ROC. AUC-ROC is independent of a fixed threshold and is suitable for anomaly detection tasks with an imbalanced normal-to-anomaly ratio. For comparisons between NSP-AE and the various baselines, this paper performs two-sided paired t-tests under the same random seed and applies Holm’s correction for multiple comparisons within each dataset. This paper reports means, sample standard deviations, and corrected significance conclusions.

Looking at the overall results, NSP-AE achieved the highest average AUC on all four datasets. This advantage was most pronounced on BPI2012_O, where NSP-AE achieved an AUC of 0.792, while the mean AUCs of the comparison methods ranged from 0.4889 to 0.5351. The results for BPI2013 were relatively limited. NSP-AE’s average AUC on this dataset was 0.519, which was still higher than that of the comparison methods, but the mean values for all methods were close to 0.5. This result may be related to the limited available prefix context under short-trace conditions, the log-time distribution characteristics, and perturbation separability. Therefore, BPI2013 is better suited as a case where method performance is constrained rather than as evidence of a consistent improvement.

The performance of different types of baselines also reflects the characteristics of this task. Likelihood-based methods, which rely primarily on activity transition probabilities, achieved AUCs close to random levels across all four datasets, consistent with the protocol where activity sequences remain unchanged during the testing phase. LSTM-AE, DAE, BINet, and Transformer introduce neural sequence modeling capabilities, but their mean AUCs remain lower than that of NSP-AE overall. These results indicate that, in controlled temporal anomaly detection, relying solely on activity sequence modeling or treating time as a regular input is insufficient to consistently achieve high discriminative power; incorporating both local time interval deviations and process structure constraints into the scoring process is the key distinction of NSP-AE relative to these comparison methods.

Overall, RQ1 is supported at the level of mean AUC-ROC: under the unified controlled temporal anomaly protocol, NSP-AE achieved the highest mean AUC on all four logs and showed clearer discriminative advantages on Sepsis, BPI2012_O, and Helpdesk. Because the AUC values of all methods on BPI2013 were close to 0.5, this log is not interpreted as evidence of stable performance improvement; instead, it is reported as a limiting case under short-trace conditions. 5.5 Statistical tests

To further compare the relative performance of the various methods across different logs and random seeds, this paper conducted a Friedman-Nemenyi analysis using 40 dataset-seed combinations as blocks, with the six methods as treatments. The results in Figure 3 show that the Friedman test statistic is 56.157, with a p-value of 7.5430×10−11 and a Nemenyi critical difference of 1.1921, indicating that there are significant differences in the overall rankings across the different methods. The average rank of NSP-AE was 1.600, outperforming LSTM-AE (3.225), DAE (3.900), Likelihood (4.025), Transformer (4.050), and BINet (4.200). Pairwise tests across datasets show that NSP-AE achieved Holm-corrected statistical significance relative to all baselines on Sepsis, BPI2012_O, and Helpdesk; on BPI2013, NSP-AE had the highest average AUC, but the corrected differences relative to the baselines were not statistically significant. It should be noted that the above groups are not independent datasets but are derived from combinations of datasets and random seeds. Therefore, this paper treats this analysis as supplementary ranking results across datasets and random seed conditions, rather than as primary evidence to replace per-dataset statistical tests.

Friedman-Nemenyi critical-difference.

Therefore, the answer to RQ2 is dataset-dependent: on Sepsis, BPI2012_O, and Helpdesk, the advantages of NSP-AE over the baselines remained statistically significant after Holm correction; on BPI2013, although NSP-AE achieved the highest mean AUC, the statistical tests did not support significant differences. Accordingly, this paper limits the interpretation of statistical reliability to the three logs where significance was observed and reports BPI2013 as a non-significant case.

5.6 Component analysis

To address RQ3, this paper further analyzes the functional roles of the main components. The component analysis employs a stepwise configuration approach to examine the effects of unidirectional encoders, local time interval inputs, time loss weights, and Petri net masks on the AUC. This analysis is intended to characterize the actual roles of each component under the current controlled-time anomaly detection protocol, rather than assuming that each component yields a monotonically increasing gain across all datasets.

Figure 4 shows the AUC-ROC visualization results for stepwise configuration changes; the error bars represent the standard deviation of samples across 10 random seeds. This experiment was run on the Sepsis, BPI2012_O, and Helpdesk datasets; BPI2013 was not included in the component analysis.

Stepwise AUC-ROC changes across component configurations.

As shown in Figure 4, the delta-time input is the component producing the largest positive mean AUC change on the Sepsis and BPI2012_O datasets. Compared to the unidirectional cumulative time configuration, adding delta-time increased the AUC by 0.0483 on Sepsis and by 0.1031 on BPI2012_O. However, the average effect of delta-time on Helpdesk was -0.0079, indicating that this feature design does not yield improvements across all logs. This result is consistent with the task rationale of this paper: delta-time more directly characterizes the intervals between adjacent events, but its effectiveness is influenced by trace length, activity structure, and temporal distribution.

The effect of the time loss weight λtime=2.0 is also dataset-dependent. In the stepwise configuration, increasing λtime from 0.5 to 2.0 resulted in a decrease in the average AUC of 0.0221 and 0.0049 for Sepsis and BPI2012_O, respectively, while the AUC for Helpdesk increased by 0.0058. This indicates that a higher time loss weight does not necessarily lead to a higher AUC; in the main configuration of this paper, λtime=2.0 is used as a fixed experimental setting.

Petri net masking resulted in slight positive changes in AUC across all three component analysis datasets: +0.0031 for Sepsis, BPI2012_O at +0.0003, and Helpdesk at +0.0014. These results suggest that the direct gain in AUC from masking is limited, and its primary effect is observed in prediction space constraints and compliance scores. 6 Conclusions

This paper proposes NSP-AE, a neural-symbolic process autoencoder for detecting temporal anomalies in business processes. Designed to address scenarios where activity sequences are compliant but temporal behavior is anomalous, this method integrates Petri net structural constraints, neural prediction and reconstruction signals, and log-space time interval deviations into a single trace-level anomaly scoring framework. Compared to methods that model only activity transitions or treat time merely as an auxiliary input feature, NSP-AE explicitly utilizes local delta-time and incorporates time interval deviations as the primary signal in the scoring, while introducing process structural constraints through dynamic compliance masks.

Controlled temporal anomaly experiments based on four public event logs show that NSP-AE achieves higher AUCs than the Likelihood, LSTM-AE, DAE, BINet, and Transformer baselines on the Sepsis, BPI2012_O, and Helpdesk datasets, reaching statistical significance in Holm-corrected paired tests. On BPI2013, NSP-AE’s average AUC was higher than that of the baselines, but the difference was not statistically significant after correction; therefore, this dataset is reported as a limiting case. Component analysis indicates that the effects of delta-time input, time loss weights, and Petri net masking are dataset-dependent; the unidirectional encoder primarily serves as a structural design consistent with online prefix scoring rather than a performance component solely aimed at maximizing offline AUC.

This study still has limitations. First, the experimental anomaly labels were generated by controlled temporal perturbations rather than naturally annotated anomalies in the raw logs; therefore, the results primarily support conclusions under the controlled temporal anomaly protocol. Second, the AUC gain from the Petri net mask in the current component analysis is small; its value lies more in constraining the candidate activity space and explaining compliance scores. Third, the effectiveness of delta-time and time weighting is dataset-dependent; it remains challenging to learn stable temporal patterns from short-trace logs. Future work will consider the evaluation of naturally annotated temporal anomalies, the extension of symbolic constraints from resource and organizational perspectives, and uncertainty-aware scoring under conditions of noisy process discovery.

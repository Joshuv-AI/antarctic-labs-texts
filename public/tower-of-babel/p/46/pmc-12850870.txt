Assessment of the learning curve for peroral endoscopic myotomy with fundoplication for a single operator
Authors: Michael Bejjani, Farimah Fayyaz, Divya Rayapati, Michel Almardini, Romina Roshanshad, Manol Jovani, Mouen A Khashab
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsPeroral endoscopic myotomy with fundoplication (POEM-F) is a novel procedure that provides a preventative interventional option to reduce rates of post-POEM gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) via anterior myotomy followed by a partial anterior wrap. Defining the learning curve (LC) is necessary to create adequate subspecialty training programs and quality assurance.MethodsThis study is a retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained data set of patients who underwent POEM-F at a single tertiary referral center. The primary outcome was the LC for POEM-F, defined by the number of cases needed to achieve proficiency and mastery, assessed using cumulative sum analysis. Sequential time-block analysis also was performed.ResultsThirty-one consecutive patients underwent POEM-F. The same endoscopist performed all procedures using the same technique. Technical success was achieved in 96.8% and clinical success (Eckardt score <3) in 100% of patients. The mean (standard deviation) total procedural and fundoplication alone times were 93.8 (19.6) minutes and 55.5 (18.2) minutes, respectively. Over a median follow-up of 14 weeks, the mean Eckardt score decreased from 7.3 to 0.3. At follow-up, Los Angeles reflux esophagitis of grades B, C, and D was observed in 2 patients (10.5%), 1 (5.3%), and none, respectively. There was only 1 postprocedural adverse event, graded as mild and successfully treated conservatively. Evaluation of the cumulative sum curve using the fundoplication time revealed that 17 cases were needed to achieve proficiency and 19 cases to achieve mastery. These results were confirmed with sequential time-block analysis.ConclusionsWe report data on the LC for POEM-F. Approximately 17 procedures may be the threshold to achieve proficiency, and approximately 19 cases to master the technique, although larger studies with more endoscopists, both experts and novices, are required.

Introduction

Achalasia historically has been managed surgically with Heller myotomy with partial or total fundoplication.1 Peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM) was introduced in 2009 and since then, it has been considered as a primary treatment option for achalasia and is endorsed by multiple societal guidelines.2, 3, 4, 5 Compared with Heller myotomy, treatment of achalasia with POEM often results in greater rates of post-POEM gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), noted as high as 58%.6,7 This side effect also is potentially associated with an increased risk of subsequent development of Barrett’s esophagus and neoplasia.8 The more novel peroral endoscopic myotomy with fundoplication (POEM-F) provides a preventative interventional option to reduce rates of post-POEM GERD via anterior myotomy followed by a partial anterior wrap.9 This procedure was introduced as an alternative to the laparoscopic Heller-Dor (partial) fundoplication procedure by Inoue et al.9 After completion of the anterior myotomy, transmural dissection into the peritoneum then allows for a loop ligating device to create a wrap between the gastric fundus and myotomy. Previous studies conducted in Japan and India, as well as the first cohort in the United States, have shown the procedure's technical success, safety, and impressive short-term outcomes.1

Although the standard POEM procedure has become widely adopted with well-characterized learning curves (LCs), POEM-F introduces additional technical complexity because of the incorporation of an endoscopic fundoplication step, including the need to maintain peritoneal insufflation and monitor the intra-abdominal pressure, achieve stable entry of devices such as the endoloop into the tunnel, and operate them effectively in a confined space. These steps demand advanced spatial orientation and endoscopic control beyond that which is required for standard POEM. The potential of this novel yet challenging procedure to reduce adverse events (AEs) in the treatment of achalasia, such as post-POEM GERD, highlights the need for further investigation into endoscopist training requirements. Given the technical and operative difficulty of the POEM-F procedure, the educational training requirements and the number of cases necessary to achieve proficiency and mastery remain to be studied. Therefore, the aim of this study was to characterize the LC for POEM-F by determining the number of cases required to achieve procedural proficiency and mastery using cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis of fundoplication time.

Methods

The present study is a retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained data set of patients with confirmed diagnosis of achalasia or esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction by manometry evaluation who underwent POEM-F between March 2022 and September 2023 at a tertiary referral center by a single operator who completed more than 1500 cases of POEM (M.K.). However, the operator had no previous experience with endoscopic fundoplication at the time of initiating POEM-F.

The demographic data, procedural characteristics, technical success (completion of POEM and fundoplication), clinical success (postprocedural Eckardt Score [ES] of <3 or a 1-point decrease in ES in cases with a baseline of <3), and AEs (graded according to the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy lexicon)10 were recorded. Total procedural time included the time from the scope-in to the final scope-out of the procedure and the fundoplication time included the time from attempting peritoneal entry to the final scope-out of the procedure.

POEM-F procedure technique

The POEM-F procedure (Video 1, available online at www.igiejournal.org) consists of 2 major steps: POEM and fundoplication. A detailed technique of POEM-F has been described previously.1 All procedures are performed in the endoscopy unit with patients in the supine position and under general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation. An experienced endoscopist (M.K.) performed the procedures with the assistance of an advanced endoscopy fellow.

A standard anterior POEM was performed using a high-definition gastroscope with a transparent distal attachment. To reduce the risk of mucosal shearing, a longer mucosal incision than standard was made, followed by submucosal tunneling extending 3 cm into the stomach and myotomy starting 3 cm distal to the mucosal incision and extending 3 cm into gastric cardia.

The fundoplication part consists of 3 steps: entry into the peritoneal cavity, anchoring the loop ligating device (PolyLoop; Olympus America), and closure of the loop ligating device. Entry into the peritoneal cavity involved careful dissection of the serosa overlying the gastric myotomy and perigastric fat using a combination of coagulation grasper (Coagrasper; Olympus America, Center Valley, Pa, USA) and an insulated-tip knife (ITknife2; Olympus America) with forced coagulation electrocautery (Erbe, Marietta, Ga, USA). Intervening blood vessels were coagulated using the soft coagulation setting, effect 4.5 (Erbe). Upon entering the peritoneal cavity, a Veress needle (GeniCon, Winter Park, Fla, USA) was placed for inducing a controlled capnoperitoneum.

An ultraslim 5.4-mm gastroscope (GIF-XP190N; Olympus America) was advanced by the fellow alongside into the stomach and retroflexed to view the fundus. The fundus was grasped and retracted into the tunnel to simulate the fundoplication wrap, which was monitored by the ultraslim gastroscope. When an adequate wrap was observed, the corresponding spot on the fundic serosa was marked using cautery. A through-the-scope clip (TTSC; Micro-Tech Endoscopy, Ann Arbor, Mich, USA) was used to grasp the tip of the 3-cm loop ligating device. The standard gastroscope along with the loop ligating device was advanced through the submucosal tunnel to enter the peritoneal cavity. The loop ligating device was fixed to the distal anchoring site, with 4 TTSCs. The proximal end of the loop ligating device was then affixed to the distal extent of the myotomy with 4 TTSCs, completing the anchoring process.

Finally, the loop ligating device was gradually tightened, partially rotating and pulling the distal anchoring site (gastric fundus serosa typically the anterior wall) toward the proximal anchoring site (located at the distal end of the myotomy), narrowing the gastroesophageal junction hiatus. After complete loop closure, the device tails were cut, and standard mucosal closure was performed.

Additional steps of the procedure included distensibility measurements using the Endoflip 300 impedance planimetry system (Medtronic, Minneapolis, Minn, USA) before POEM, after myotomy completion, and postfundoplication but before mucosal closure.

CUSUM curve analysis

The endpoint of this study was to determine the LC for POEM-F by assessing the number of cases needed to achieve proficiency and mastery. To achieve this, CUSUM curve analysis was used to assess both proficiency and mastery in terms of fundoplication time. The overall mean fundoplication time was used as the target value. Mastery was defined as the number of procedures at which the average time of procedures was consistently similar to, or lower than, the target value, as observed with a plateau or descent in the CUSUM graph. Fundoplication mean time comparison of sequential blocks of patients was also used to assess the LC. Given the relatively small number of patients in our study, the cohort was divided into 2 sequential blocks: cases 1 to 16 and 17 to 30. This cutoff was selected on the basis of the observed inflection point in fundoplication time reduction.

Statistical analysis

The findings were presented either as mean (standard deviation) or as median with interquartile range for continuous variables, and as percentages for categorical variables. Continuous variables were compared using the 2-sample t test or Mann-Whitney U test, whereas categorical variables were assessed using χ2 or Fisher exact tests. Linear regression was used for comparing fundoplication time in sequential time-block analysis. A 2-sided P value of less than .05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 31 patients underwent the POEM-F procedure at 1 tertiary referral center during the study period between March 2022 and September 2023. Among these patients, 6 were previously included in the publication by Shrigiriwar et al,1 in which the authors primarily evaluated the technical feasibility, safety, and outcomes of POEM-F. In contrast, our study focused on assessing the LC of the POEM-F procedure. The procedure was done with the same technique by the same operator. With regard to the baseline characteristics, 38.7% were female, and the mean age was 48.0 years. POEM-F was mostly performed for type II achalasia (28, 90.3%). No patients in the cohort had a sigmoid-shaped esophagus. Further baseline characteristics of these patients are summarized in Table 1.

Procedural and clinical outcomes

Technical success was achieved in 30 patients (96.8%). The 1 patient with technical failure had severely tortuous and dilated esophageal lumen resulting in poor maneuverability and mucosal shearing with the anterior approach to submucosal tunneling. Consequently, the decision was made to abort POEM-F and convert to posterior esophageal POEM. In all successful cases, a complete wrap was confirmed endoscopically from the gastric side at the end of the procedure. Clinical success (ES <3) was achieved in all patients (100%) with technically successful POEM-F. The mean procedural time was 93.8 (19.6) minutes. Over a median follow-up of 14 weeks, the mean ES decreased from 7.3 to 0.3. At follow-up, Los Angeles reflux esophagitis of grades B, C, and D was observed in 2 patients (10.5%), 1 patient (5.3%), and none, respectively. There were no intraprocedural AEs and only 1 postprocedural AE, which was graded as mild and successfully treated conservatively. Further procedural and clinical outcomes are detailed in Table 2.

LC analysis

The mean procedural time was 93.8 (19.6) minutes and the mean fundoplication time was 55.5 (18.2) minutes (Table 2). Evaluation of the CUSUM curve revealed that 17 cases were needed to achieve proficiency and 19 cases were needed to achieve mastery of the technique (Fig. 1). Starting at 17 cases, we observed an inflection of the CUSUM curve, which means that from this point forward, fundoplication time began to shorten compared with the target value, indicating that the operator had acquired proficiency. Starting at 19 cases, we observed a consistent descent of the graph, which means that fundoplication time was consistently similar to or lower than the target value, denoting that the operator had acquired mastery of the technique.

Cumulative sum (CUSUM) graph for learning curve of peroral endoscopic myotomy with fundoplication.

These results were confirmed by comparing the sequential blocks of the fundoplication times. The mean fundoplication time for patients 17 to 30 was significantly lower when compared with the first 16 patients: −8.2 minutes (95% confidence interval, −16.2 to −0.3 minutes; P =.04).

Discussion

In this single-center, single-operator study, we explored the LC for POEM-F. Using different metrics to assess the LC, we were able to determine the breakpoints for achieving proficiency and mastery for POEM-F. We found that 17 cases were needed to achieve proficiency and 19 cases were needed to achieve mastery of the fundoplication procedure. These results were then confirmed by comparing sequential time blocks. Given that AEs and technical success rates remained stable throughout the series, the observed reduction in fundoplication time likely reflects improved technical efficiency rather than an improvement in patient outcomes. Although procedure time is a practical surrogate for operator efficiency, it does not necessarily correlate with clinical benefit, and our results should be interpreted with this limitation in mind. Future studies should investigate whether improved procedural efficiency leads to better patient clinical outcomes.

POEM-F is a complex, combined procedure of 2 individually established procedures known as POEM and fundoplication. These 2 techniques have emerged as innovative treatments for achalasia and refractory GERD, respectively. Due to the high incidence rate of post-POEM GERD (39%-58%),6,7,11 POEM-F recently was introduced to prevent this issue from occurring. Recent studies, including the first U.S. cohort of POEM-F involving 6 patients, have suggested the feasibility and safety of the procedure with excellent short-term outcomes.1 Our study is conducted by a single operator with previous extensive experience in POEMs and natural orifice translumenal endoscopic surgery (NOTES) at a tertiary referral center. This scenario represents the optimal environment for investigating the number of procedures necessary to attain expertise and mastery in this technique. Enhancing our comprehension of the minimal number of procedures needed to achieve proficiency and mastery in POEM-F will aid in designing training initiatives and postfellowship pathways for the future.

Although the technique and procedural details of POEM-F differ from those of individual POEM, it is important to mention that the single operator of this LC has extensive experience in these respective procedures. In fact, the LC published for the POEM procedure by the same operator showed that approximately 13 cases were needed to reach a learning plateau.12

A retrospective study by Bapaye et al13 evaluating the first 25 patients undergoing POEM-F at their center showed a significant improvement in procedural efficiency. The moving average curve of total procedure time decreased from 88 minutes for the first 5 cases to 51.2 minutes for subsequent cases (P < .05). The difference in the LC between our study and Bapaye et al13 could be attributed to differences in analysis methodology, patient selection, procedural complexity, and institutional factors. The study by Bapaye et al13 evaluated procedural trends in POEM-F using a moving average analysis of total procedure time across 23 cases. Although their findings suggested a reduction in time after the initial 5 procedures, the analysis was descriptive and did not include statistical methods to objectively define learning milestones. In contrast, our study focused specifically on fundoplication time and applied CUSUM analysis to objectively assess the number of cases required to achieve proficiency and mastery. These methodological differences may explain the difference in learning thresholds identified between the 2 studies.13

This study focused on evaluating the LC of POEM-F; therefore, comprehensive reflux follow-up was not uniformly performed. Not all patients underwent systematic postprocedural assessment of GERD, such as pH monitoring, symptom scoring, or follow-up endoscopy, limiting our ability to assess the effectiveness of POEM-F in reflux prevention. Of the 19 patients who underwent follow-up endoscopy at 3 to 6 months postprocedure in our study, 15.8% were found to have grade B, C, or D esophagitis. This rate is lower than the 19.4% reported after standard POEM in a large meta-analysis.11 Similarly, a recent meta-analysis on POEM-F reported a pooled rate of Los Angeles grade B, C, or D esophagitis of 14.4% (95% confidence interval, 6.1-33.9).14 These findings suggest that POEM-F may offer some protection against post-POEM GERD; however, further long-term comprehensive evaluation is needed to confirm its effectiveness in reflux prevention.

Although POEM-F demonstrated technical feasibility and consistent wrap formation at 3- to 6-month follow-up, the relationship between procedural experience and long-term reflux control remains unclear. Sustained reflux prevention may rely not only on experience but also on refinements in technique and instrumentation. The use of dedicated tools, such as durable through-the-scope suturing systems, may improve long-term outcomes. Further studies incorporating structured reflux evaluation and long-term follow-up are needed to better understand the clinical impact of the POEM-F LC.

When one compares the LC of POEM-F with that of Heller myotomy with fundoplication, which has been known as the historical treatment for achalasia, evidence from a large single-center study involving 463 cases demonstrated that proficiency in laparoscopic Heller myotomy with fundoplication typically is achieved after approximately 16 cases.15 However, it is important to note that POEM-F involves a NOTES-based approach, which differs significantly from laparoscopic surgery in terms of access, visualization, and instrumentation. Therefore, the LC of POEM-F may not directly mirror that of laparoscopic fundoplication.

It is essential to acknowledge the limitations of this study. As mentioned previously, this LC was determined on the basis of a single operator with extensive experience in other advanced procedures such as POEM and NOTES, so the results may not be generalizable. There will certainly be variability in the LCs and number of procedures needed to attain proficiency and mastery among different operators. This variability is mainly due to differences in experience levels, techniques, and available resources. Thus, our analysis may underestimate the number of cases needed for operators with no previous experience in POEM or NOTES. In addition, the absence of a standardized definition for assessing the LC poses challenges in comparing results across different studies. Incorporation of AEs, technical difficulty, and wrap integrity into the CUSUM analysis was limited by the small number of events and incomplete follow-up data, which precluded reliable trend assessment for these outcomes. Future studies could incorporate these additional factors. The relatively short median follow-up of 14 weeks in our study prevents a comprehensive assessment of long-term wrap integrity and procedural success. Future studies with extended follow-up will be needed to evaluate its durability over time.

In conclusion, our study provides valuable insights into the LC associated with the POEM-F procedure, demonstrating an improvement in fundoplication time with increasing experience. However, the implications of this increased procedural efficiency on long-term clinical outcomes remain uncertain, and further studies are warranted to determine whether it translates into durable therapeutic benefit.

Ethical approval and informed consent

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and conducted in accordance with institutional ethical standards and the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent for study participation was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. All patients had provided informed consent for the procedures performed, including discussion of risks and benefits, and for the use of their images and recordings for research purposes.

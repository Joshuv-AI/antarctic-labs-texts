Modification of the existing maximum residue level for mepiquat chloride in honey
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant INNVIGO Sp. z o. o. submitted a request to the competent national authority in the Netherlands to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) in honey for the variant (mepiquat chloride) of the active substance mepiquat. The data submitted in support of the request was found to be sufficient to derive MRL proposal for honey. Adequate analytical method based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) is available for enforcement of the residues of mepiquat (expressed as mepiquat chloride) in honey at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of residues from honey resulting from the use of mepiquat chloride according to the reported agricultural practice is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.Keywords: consumer risk assessment, honey, mepiquat chloride, MRL, pesticide

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, INNVIGO Sp. z o. o. submitted an application to the competent national authority in the Netherlands (evaluating Member State, EMS) to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) in honey for the variant (mepiquat chloride) of the active substance mepiquat.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 30 August 2023. The appointed EMS the Netherlands assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 22 March 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 10 May 2024 to 31 May 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 26 July 2024. To accommodate the potential transfer of residues in honey from the existing use of mepiquat chloride, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRL in honey from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.05 to 1.5 mg/kg.

On 29 August 2024 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA identified data gaps and points which needed further clarification and requested the EMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 5 June, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

Given the latest EU pesticide peer review for renewal of approval has been endorsed during the course of the present assessment, the latest conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 have been considered for the present assessment, as well as the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application. Based on these outcomes, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of mepiquat chloride following foliar treatment was investigated in crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops, cereals and pulses/oilseeds. Mepiquat chloride was found to be the major residue.

Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of mepiquat chloride (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that the active substance is stable.

In rotational crops, similar pathway as in primary crop was observed since the major residue identified was the parent compound.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in plant metabolism studies and hydrolysis studies, a general residue definition for both monitoring and risk assessment for all plant products was proposed as sum of mepiquat and its salts, expressed as mepiquat chloride. These residue definitions are applicable to primary crops, rotational crops and processed products. Since mepiquat chloride metabolism in primary and rotational crops as well as the possible degradation in processed products have been sufficiently addressed, EFSA concludes that these residue definitions are applicable also to honey.

Studies investigating the magnitude of residues in honey were submitted in the current application and were sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 1.5 mg/kg for honey.

Sufficiently validated analytical method based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) is available to quantify residues in honey according to the enforcement residue definition. The method enables quantification of residues at or above 0.01 mg/kg in honey (LOQ).

The toxicological profile of mepiquat chloride was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.065 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.3 mg/kg bw. It is noted that this new ADI is lower than the former value of 0.2 mg/kg bw per day.

The consumer risk assessment was conducted with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). The short‐term (acute) exposure assessment did not exceed the ARfD for honey. For the calculation of the chronic exposure, EFSA updated the most recent consumer exposure calculation conducted in a previous MRL assessment with the median residue value (STMR) derived for honey from the submitted tunnel residue trials; some Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) newly implemented into the EU MRL Regulation were also considered in the assessment. The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for 30% of the ADI (NL toddler). The contribution of residues expected in honey to the overall long‐term exposure accounted for 0.08% of the ADI (DE child). No long‐term consumer intake concerns were identified for any of the European diets incorporated in EFSA PRIMo.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of mepiquat chloride resulting from the existing uses and from residues in honey (resulting from the use of mepiquat chloride on wheat with potential residue transfer from non‐target plants) will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the health‐based guidance values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

EFSA proposes to amend the existing MRL as reported in the summary table below.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) in honey for the variant (mepiquat chloride) of the active substance mepiquat. The present MRL application is related to the existing use on wheat that may lead to residues of mepiquat chloride in honey due to carry‐over from melliferous non‐target plants attractive to bees that may be present in the field during the treatment. The detailed description of the EU authorised use of mepiquat chloride on wheat, which is the basis for the present MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

Mepiquat chloride is the ISO common name for 1,1‐dimethylpiperidinium chloride (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Since the final decision on the renewal of approval under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 became available during the course of the present assessment, EFSA decided to consider the outcome of the most recent peer review process. Although, from a formal perspective, the assessment should have been conducted in accordance with the data requirements of Regulation (EC) No 544/2011 applicable at the time of dossier submission, and thus based on the first peer review, this approach was adopted to avoid issuing an outdated conclusion. This is further supported by the fact that the assessment for honey is required under Regulation (EC) No 283/2013 and consideration of the new endpoints did not significantly affect the already submitted data package in support of the risk assessment.

Mepiquat chloride was evaluated in the framework Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 1 with Finland designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative uses as a foliar treatment on cereals/grass crops. The renewal assessment report (RAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (2024c). The approval of mepiquat chloride was renewed 2 on 1 March 2025.

The EU MRLs for mepiquat chloride are established in Annexes II and IIIA of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 3 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been conducted (EFSA, 2015) and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued several reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for mepiquat chloride (EFSA, 2016, 2018b, 2018c, 2018d, 2019b, 2019c, 2024a). The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations. 4 Certain Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) have been taken over in the EU MRL legislation. 5

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 6 the applicant INNVIGO Sp. z o. o. submitted on 30 August 2023 an application to the competent national authority in the Netherlands, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

The appointed EMS the Netherlands assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 22 March 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 10 May 2024 to 31 May 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 26 July 2024. To accommodate for the intended use of mepiquat chloride, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRL in honey from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.05 to 1.5 mg/kg.

On 29 August 2024 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA identified data gaps and points which needed further clarification and requested the EMS to address them. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 5 June 2026, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the EMS (The Netherlands, 2024), the RAR and its revision (Finland, 2018, 2024) prepared under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009, the Commission review report on mepiquat chloride (European Commission, 2024), the conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance mepiquat chloride (EFSA, 2024c), as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on mepiquat chloride (EFSA, 2015, 2016, 2018b, 2018c, 2018d, 2019c, 2024a), including the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation No 396/2005.

Given the latest EU pesticide peer review for renewal of approval has been endorsed and since the present assessment addresses residues in honey, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 283/2013 7 and the corresponding guidance documents were applied (European Commission, 2010, 2018, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c; OECD, 2007a, 2007b, 2007c, 2007d, 2007e, 2009, 2014, 2016, 2021). The assessment is conducted in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 8

An updated list of end points, including the studies assessed by EFSA in the framework of this MRL application and the end points of relevant studies assessed previously, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the EMS (Netherlands) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) are made publicly available as background documents to this reasoned opinion. 9

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of mepiquat chloride in primary crops belonging to the group of fruit crops (grapes), cereals/grass (wheat, barley) and pulses/oilseeds (cotton, oilseed rape) following foliar application has been evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2024c).

In the crops tested, mepiquat chloride was found to be the predominant component (71% to 99%) of the total radioactive residues (TRR).

Nature of residues in rotational crops

According to the soil degradation studies evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review, the DT90 value of mepiquat chloride is 118 days. No other soil relevant metabolites of mepiquat chloride were identified. Study investigating the nature of residues in rotational crops was evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2024c).

From the rotational crop metabolism study using wheat, radish and lettuce planted in soil treated with mepiquat chloride, the accumulation of radioactivity in the plants indicated uptake of residues from the soil to wheat and radishes while in lettuce (where total residue was < 0.01–0.011 mg/kg) the uptake was low. The only compound identified was mepiquat chloride at levels below 0.01 mg/kg (except in wheat chaff, at 120 plant‐back interval (PBI): 0.011 mg/kg). The remaining extractable radioactivity was sufficiently characterised. Overall, a similar metabolic pattern as in primary crops was observed in rotational crops.

Based on the rotational crop metabolism study, mepiquat chloride residues are not expected to exceed 0.01 mg/kg in rotational crops at any PBI (EFSA, 2024c).

For the current assessment on honey, no further information is required.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

Study investigating the effect of processing on the nature of mepiquat chloride was evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2024c).

The study showed that mepiquat chloride is hydrolytically stable under the standard processing conditions representative of pasteurisation, baking/brewing/boiling and sterilisation.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

Not relevant for the present MRL application on honey (see Section 3).

Storage stability of residues in plants

Not relevant for the present MRL application on honey (see Section 3).

Proposed residue definitions

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in plant metabolism studies and the results of hydrolysis study, the following residue definition for enforcement and risk assessment for all plant commodities following foliar application have been proposed in the framework of the EU peer review (EFSA, 2024c):

Sum of mepiquat and its salts, expressed as mepiquat chloride.

Sum of mepiquat and its salts, expressed as mepiquat chloride.

The same residue definitions are applicable to rotational crops and processed products.

The residue definition for enforcement of mepiquat in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical to the above‐mentioned residue definition.

RESIDUES IN HONEY

Nature of residues in honey

Honey is produced by bees from sugary secretions of plants (floral nectar mainly) through regurgitation, enzymatic conversion and water evaporation and followed by storage in the beehives for a certain time period.

In the absence of specific metabolism studies with honeybees, studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops and rotational crops and studies investigating the degradation during pasteurisation should be considered to determine the nature of residues in honey (European Commission, 2018). It is likely that the nature of residues in pollen and nectar collected from primary and rotational crops, as well as in honey (resulting from the residues in floral nectar), is the same as in primary and rotational crops. The studies indicated that mepiquat chloride is the predominant residue in plants. In the available hydrolysis study, mepiquat chloride remained stable under pasteurisation conditions that are relevant for nature of residues in honey.

Considering that sufficient data investigating the metabolic profile of mepiquat chloride in primary and rotational crops and the degradation of the active substance under standard hydrolysis conditions are available, no further information is required for the current application according to the pertinent guidelines.

Analytical methods for enforcement in honey

In the framework of the present assessment, the applicant submitted a new method for enforcement of mepiquat chloride residues in honey (The Netherlands, 2024).

The method, based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) after extraction with methanol and acid/water is sufficiently validated and allows the determination of mepiquat chloride in honey at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. Confirmation was performed by use of a second mass transition, and a valid independent laboratory validation (ILV) has been provided.

Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for enforcement of residues in honey is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023c).

Storage stability of residues in honey

Data on the storage stability of mepiquat chloride residues in honey has not been provided. This data is not required since the analysis of the samples from provided tunnel residue trials was conducted within 30 days (OECD, 2007d).

Proposed residue definitions

In the absence of specific metabolism studies on honey, the studies investigating the nature of residues in primary and rotational crops and studies investigating the degradation of the active substance during pasteurisation are considered to derive the residue definitions for honey; the same residue definitions as mentioned for plant commodities are therefore proposed.

Magnitude of residues in honey

Studies investigating the magnitude of residues in honey were submitted in the present application.

The EU authorised GAP selected by the applicant for this assessment is on wheat and consists of a single foliar application at a maximum nominal application rate of 390 g of active substance per hectare (g a.s./ha), applied up to BBCH 49. The applicant considered this GAP relevant for the assessment of mepiquat chloride residues in honey due to the presence of melliferous non‐target plants in field at the treatment of the target crop.

By means of a clarification request, EFSA initially questioned the selection of the GAP triggering the MRL request in honey, noting that this GAP is on a non‐melliferous crop. However, as per SANTE/11956/2016, residues occurrence in honey due to non‐target plants is a relevant scenario which could trigger an MRL request (European Commission, 2018). Therefore, EFSA proceeded with the present application on wheat.

It is noted that some uses on melliferous crops were reported by the EMS (The Netherlands, 2024), however none of them appears to be clearly supported by the tunnel residue trials; these GAPs were not further considered in this opinion.

In support of the proposed GAP in wheat, four independent (tunnel) residue trials were conducted in 2022 in Germany (two trials), France and Spain using oilseed rape as a surrogate melliferous crop. Mepiquat chloride was applied once by foliar spray at a nominal rate of 390 g a.s./ha at BBCH 63–64, which corresponds to the flowering period of oilseed rape. This application timing (during flowering) is acceptable as it aims to address flowering of melliferous non‐target plants that grow in the same field as wheat (target plant). The treatment regime in the provided trials was therefore adequate to address residues in honey that occur due to the proposed GAP on wheat.

Honey samples were collected when water content was < 20%, at BBCH 65–69. Samples were stored for less than 30 days before analysis. Residues of mepiquat chloride, determined according to the risk assessment and monitoring residue definition ranged from 0.01 to 0.57 mg/kg. One trial showed markedly lower residues (0.01 mg/kg) than the other three (≥ 0.4 mg/kg). This difference was considered to reflect natural variability in environmental conditions, as no adverse effects on bee health or specific events that could explain the lower residues were observed during the experiment.

No residues above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg were determined in control specimens.

According to the assessment of the EMS, the analytical method used is sufficiently validated for the quantification of residues of mepiquat chloride and is fit for purpose. Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for data generation in honey samples is not available and is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023c).

The available tunnel residue trials are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 1.5 mg/kg for honey.

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 1.5 mg/kg as well as risk assessment values for honey (see Appendix B.3.2). This MRL was requested based on a GAP on wheat, taking into account potential residue transfer from non‐target plants.

In Section 4, EFSA assessed whether residues in honey resulting from the authorised use on wheat are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

It should be noted that currently, MRLs set for honey are not applicable to other apicultural products according to Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/62. 10

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018a, 2019a). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) for mepiquat chloride used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI and ARfD values) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2024).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology and focused on honey, which is the only commodity assessed in this application. The calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived for honey from tunnel residue trials assessed in this application. The complete list of input values can be found in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for the commodity (honey) assessed in this application.

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the previous MRL application a comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment was conducted, taking into account the existing uses at EU level (EFSA, 2024a). EFSA updated the calculation with the relevant supervised trial median residue (STMR) value derived for honey from the tunnel residue trials submitted in support of this MRL application. In addition, some CXLs implemented into the EU MRL Regulation were considered in the assessment (FAO and WHO, 2024). It is also noted that the ADI derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review of 2024 is lower than the former value used in previous MRL assessments; ADI is now 0.065 mg/kg bw per day instead of 0.2 mg/kg bw per day. The input values used in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.1.

It is noted that the risk assessment residue definition for animal commodities has been slightly changed during the latest peer review process where conjugates of the already included metabolite 4‐hydroxy mepiquat have been also included (EFSA, 2024c). The tentative conversion factors (CFs) derived during the peer review for liver (1.74) and milk (2.58) were considered in the present risk assessment.

The estimated long‐term dietary intake was 30% of the ADI (NL toddler). The contribution of residues expected in honey (the only commodity assessed in the present application) to the overall long‐term exposure is very low and is reported in more detail in Appendix B.4.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of mepiquat chloride resulting from the existing uses and from residues in honey (resulting from the use of mepiquat chloride on wheat with potential residue transfer from non‐target plants) is unlikely to present a risk to consumers' health.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal for honey. This MRL was requested based on a GAP on wheat, taking into account potential residue transfer from non‐target plants.

EFSA concluded that the residues in honey resulting from the use of mepiquat chloride assessed in the present opinion (use on wheat with potential transfer from non‐target plants) will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the health‐based guidance values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

The MRL recommendations are summarised in Appendix B.5.

The predictive value of neutrophil to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio in early pregnancy for gestational diabetes based on nomogram
Authors: Yuehan Ma, Jianbing Sun, Ning Yuan, Sixu Xin, Dan Zhao, Xiaomei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly prediction of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is particularly important. The role of neutrophil to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) ratio (NHR) in GDM is unclear. This study aims to explore the predictive value of early pregnancy NHR in GDM. A case-control study was conducted involving 1421 pregnant women, randomly divided into a training dataset and a validation set. Routine clinical and laboratory information were collected. Logistic regression was performed and nomogram was established to determine the diagnostic efficacy of NHR for GDM. We evaluated nomogram through receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Compared with non-GDM, early pregnancy NHR was significantly elevated in GDM. Logistic regression indicated that NHR was an independent risk factor for GDM. A nomogram model was constructed using NHR, age, body mass index, glycated hemoglobin, and fasting blood glucose as factors to predict GDM in early pregnancy. The calibration curve and DCA demonstrated strong predictive ability. The DCA result indicated that the model has clinical application value. In GDM subjects, NHR levels significantly increase in early pregnancy and are closely related to the occurrence of GDM. NHR has good predictive diagnostic performance.Keywords: Gestational diabetes mellitus, Neutrophil/high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio, InflammationSubject terms: Endocrine system and metabolic diseases, Predictive markers

Introduction

Gestational diabetes (GDM) is defined as hyperglycemia first diagnosed or occurring during pregnancy. With the improvement of people’s living standards and the increase of maternal age, the morbidity of GDM has increased year by year1–3. GDM is closely associated with an increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preterm birth, macrosomia, and placental abruption and so on4. However, early prediction of GDM may help mitigate the short-term and long-term complications associated with GDM, and even prevent GDM occurring. Therefore, early prediction of GDM is crucial.

According to reports, chronic inflammation is associated with insulin resistance and obesity in patients with GDM, and serum inflammatory factors can be used to determine its early onset5–7. A cohort study suggests that inflammatory factors may be biomarkers of GDM, which are unrelated to body mass index (BMI), and other features such as immune endocrine interactions may play central roles in the development of GDM5. Recently, it has been reported that the ratio of neutrophils to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) (NHR), as a new inflammatory marker, plays an important role in predicting many diseases, such as non-alcoholic fatty liver (NAFLD), atherosclerotic heart disease, depression, etc8–13. However, there is little or no research on the predictive value of NHR on GDM currently.

Our research aims to comparing the changes of NHR levels in early pregnancy between GDM and non-GMD populations, and evaluating the predictive value of NHR for GDM in early pregnancy. Finally, we establish a nomogram model, by which the risk of GDM occurrence in first trimester can be predicted.

Results

Comparison of clinical characteristics of subjects in early pregnancy

In the comparison of general information between the two groups of participants, the GDM group showed significant increases in age, systolic blood pressure (SBP), and BMI compared to the non-GDM group (p < 0 05). However, there was no statistically significant difference in diastolic blood pressure (DBP) between the two groups (p > 0 05). In the comparison of laboratory indicators, the white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil (NE), platelet (PLT), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting blood glucose (FPG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in the GDM group were significantly higher than those in the non-GDM group (p < 0.05). However, compared with the non-GDM group, the HDL-C level in the GDM group was lower (p < 0. 05). However, there was no statistically significant difference in lymphocyte (LYM), monocyte (MO) and hemoglobin (HGB) levels between the two groups (p > 0 05). (Table 1)

Building a nomogram model and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves analysis for predicting GDM

In the training set, a nomogram model was constructed using NHR, age, BMI, HbA1c and FPG as factors to predict the risk of GDM in early pregnancy (Fig. 1A). Area under the curve (AUC) aimed to evaluate the discriminability of the nomogram model, with a training set AUC of 0.76 and a 95% CI of 0.72–0.80 (p < 0.05, Fig. 1B). The internal validation results displayed that the AUC of the validation set is 0.72, and the CI of 95% is 0.66–0.78 (p < 0.05, Fig. 1B).

Calibration curve and DCA analysis of nomogram model

The DCA results revealed that the model has a high clinical net benefit, as shown in Fig. 1E,F.

Discussion

GDM is the most common metabolic disorder during pregnancy. As of now, 17.5 -19.2% of pregnant women in China have been diagnosed with GDM4. GDM can increase adverse reactions in pregnant women and offspring in both the short and long term. For pregnant women, the occurrence of GDM can lead to acute complications such as cesarean section, hypertension, preeclampsia, and polyhydramnios14. In addition, the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease of mothers will increase significantly in the future15,16. In offspring, GDM can lead to short-term complications such as macrosomia, hypoglycemia, preterm birth, fetal growth restriction, birth injury and respiratory distress, and long-term complications such as obesity and diabetes2,16. Researchers have found that multiple factors, such as age, gestational weeks, race, obesity, lifestyle, environment, and metabolism17,18, may increase the risk of GDM development. Due to the complex etiology, numerous short-term and long-term complications, and far-reaching impact of GDM, there is an increasing demand for early prediction and intervention of GDM. Owing to the oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) conducted during mid pregnancy, pregnant women may miss early warning signals of abnormal blood sugar levels. This leads to pregnant women missing the critical period for preventing GDM. Therefore, there is an urgent need to study biomarkers and predictive models for predicting GDM in early pregnancy.

Compared to non-pregnant states, pregnancy is characterized by changes in the inflammatory spectrum, and an imbalance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory factors may lead to various complications during pregnancy19. At present, insulin resistance caused by chronic inflammation is a research hotspot in the pathogenesis of GDM20,21. Recent studies have demonstrated that inflammation exacerbated by dysbiosis of the gut microbiota may lead to the occurrence of GDM, and short chain fatty acids (SCFAs) produced by the gut microbiota can serve as early biomarkers of GDM5. In addition, studies have found that elevated levels of the systemic immune inflammation index (SII) and systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI) during pregnancy are associated with an increased risk of GDM22. Therefore, chronic inflammation is an important potential influencing factor for the occurrence and development of GDM. The discovery of early inflammatory markers will help predict and intervene early in GDM, thereby effectively preventing its occurrence.

Neutrophils are not only the first defense cells in acute inflammation, but also play important roles in chronic inflammation. The mechanisms by which neutrophils participate in chronic inflammation are mainly to promote macrophage recruitment, intensify interactions with antigen-presenting cells, and trigger inflammatory responses23,24. Consistently, our research also verified that the level of neutrophils in GDM patients during early pregnancy was higher than that in non-GDM participants. Multiple studies have manifested that blood lipids are closely related to inflammation. HDL-C, as a typical lipid related biomarker, can clear excess cholesterol from peripheral tissues. Meanwhile, it can also inhibit the expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules, preventing monocytes from accumulating towards the arterial wall25,26. In addition, HDL-C plays an important role in the activation, migration, and diffusion of neutrophils27. HDL-C plays an important protective role in inflammation by above mechanisms. The ratio of neutrophils to HDL-C, NHR, is a recently discovered novel inflammatory marker. The role of NHR has been studied in various diseases currently. Kou et al. analyze over 400 patients who underwent coronary angiography and report that NHR is an independent risk factor for predicting coronary artery disease10. Research has proved that NHR may be a widely used biomarker for predicting coronary artery disease and assessing the degree of coronary artery stenosis10. Jia et al. exhibit that NHR levels are significantly elevated in the NAFLD group compared to the non-NAFLD group. NHR is an independent risk factor for NAFLD in healthy individuals9. In addition, there is a report indicates that NHR may be a potential biomarker for the risk of depression, which is of great significance for early detection and personalized treatment of depression8. However, most studies about NHR focus on NAFLD and atherosclerotic heart disease. There is little or no relevant research on the relationship between early pregnancy NHR and GDM currently.

In our article, the levels of early pregnancy NHR were compared for the first time between GDM and non-GDM groups. We discovered that the GDM group had significantly higher NHR levels compared to the non-GDM group. This indicated that GDM patients may have already exhibit mild inflammation in early pregnancy. Correlation analyses displayed that NHR was significantly positively correlated with age and BMI levels, indicating that the older the pregnant woman and the higher BMI, the stronger the inflammatory response in the body may be. This is also consistent with previous studies which proves that an increase in BMI or obesity can trigger inflammation28. In addition, logistic regression analyses indicated that NHR in early pregnancy was an independent risk factor for GDM. The higher the NHR in early pregnancy, the higher the risk of developing GDM. Finally, our study constructed a nomogram prediction model using NHR in early pregnancy and factors of major concern in clinical practice, including age, BMI, HbA1c, and FPG. Combining with internal validation results, the model demonstrated good predictive ability.

In summary, the NHR levels changes of GDM patients in early pregnancy were reported for the first time in our experiment. Not only that, this article employed the nomogram model to evaluate the predictive value of NHR in early pregnancy for GDM. NHR in early pregnancy is expected to serve as a biomarker for GDM, providing strong evidence for early diagnosis of GDM.

Materials and methods

Participants: This is a case-control study. A total of 1502 pregnant women were recruited from Peking University International Hospital between September 2016 and August 2019. Inclusion criteria: (1) Pregnant women age ≥ 18 years old; (2) Diagnosed gestational age < 12 weeks; (3) Subjects regularly performed obstetric examinations and delivered at our hospital. Exclusion criteria: (1) Pre-pregnancy diabetes; (2) Pregnant women with inflammation related diseases, such as respiratory or urinary tract infections; (3) Subjects with liver, kidney, pancreatic diseases, hematological disorders, autoimmune diseases, and tumors. 1421 participants with complete data were included in this study. 292 pregnant women with GDM were included in the GDM group, and 1129 pregnant women were included in non-GDM group. All participants were randomly assigned to either the training set (994 participants [70%]) or the validation set (427 participants [30%]) in a 7:3 ratio, using random numbers generated by computer software. The study has been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of our hospital and all pregnant women have given informed consent.

Methods: Subjects’ general information were collected, including age, parity, gestational weeks, and medical history. The height, weight, and blood pressure of the subjects were measured on an empty stomach in the morning, and we calculated their BMI. BMI was calculated using the following formula: weight (kg)/height2 (m2). All pregnant women were fasted for 8–12 h at 8 weeks of pregnancy, and elbow vein blood was drawn on an empty stomach the next morning to WBC, NE, LYM, MO, HGB, PLT, FPG, HbA1c, TC, TG, LDL-C and HDL-C. HbA1c was detected using a G8 automatic HbA1c analyzer with high-performance liquid chromatography. XN2000 fully automatic blood cell analyzer and supporting reagents for detecting whole blood cell count AU5821 fully automatic biochemical analyzer and Beijing Lidman glucose assay kit, along with accompanying calibration standards, were used to detect FPG. The above tests were performed in the laboratory of the Department of Laboratory Medicine of Peking University International Hospital, which has been accredited by the China National Accreditation Committee.

Definition: According to the guidelines of the International Association of diabetes and Pregnancy Research Groups29, GDM is diagnosed as a participant who takes 75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks of pregnancy to measure FPG, blood glucose 1 h after taking sugar (1hPG), and blood glucose 2 h after taking sugar (2hPG). According to the OGTT diagnostic criteria, the upper limit of blood glucose values at fasting and 1 and 2 h after taking sugar is 5.1, 10.0, and 8.5 mmol/L respectively. Any point where the blood glucose value reaches or exceeds the above criteria is diagnosed as GDM. These diagnostic criteria are recommended by the American Diabetes Association (ADA)30 and the Chinese Diabetes Association31. Pre-pregnancy diabetes is defined as type 1 diabetes mellitus, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or a special type of diabetes diagnosed before pregnancy. NHR is defined as neutrophils (×109/L)/HDL-C (mmol/L).

Data statistics: The obtained data was statistically analyzed using SPSS 25.0 software. Continuous variables that follow a normal distribution were described by mean ± standard deviation. Independent sample t-tests were used for inter group comparisons, while the median (interquartile range) of non-normal distribution data was represented. Mann Whitney U test was used for inter group comparisons. Pearson was applied to analyze correlations. We operated univariate logistic regression and multivariate logistic regression to analyze the risk factors of GDM. The establishment and internal validation of column charts were carried out using R version 4.4.0 (2024-04-24), along with Zstats 1.0 (www.zstats.net). 1421 pregnant women were randomly divided into a training group of 994 participants and a validation group of 427 participants. The calibration curve was performed to evaluate the accuracy of the prediction model (Hosmer Lemeshow test was used to evaluate the goodness of fit). The ROC curve was used to evaluate the discriminative ability of column charts based on the area under the ROC curve (AUC). Decision curve analysis was used to determine the clinical utility of column charts based on net returns at different threshold probabilities in the GDM queue32. All critical values were determined by the total risk score in the training queue. P < 0.05 indicated significant differences.

Serum SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 Levels in Children with Immunoglobulin A Vasculitis: An Exploratory Study
Authors: Gulbahar Kurt Bayir, Ferhat Demir, Suleyman Caner Karahan, Mukaddes Kalyoncu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSCUBE1 and SCUBE2 are vascular-associated proteins involved in endothelial processes. Although these molecules have been investigated in several vascular and inflammatory disorders, their potential involvement in pediatric immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV) has not yet been elucidated. IgAV is the most common systemic vasculitis of childhood, characterized by leukocytoclastic vasculitis and immune complex deposition, yet its biomarker profile remains incompletely understood. We explored the association of SCUBE proteins with inflammatory markers in pediatric IgAV.MethodsTwenty-six children diagnosed with IgAV according to the Ankara 2008 criteria were prospectively included. Serum SCUBE1, SCUBE2, interleukin (IL)-1, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) levels were measured during both the active and recovery phases of the disease. Findings were compared with age- and sex-matched healthy controls using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) methods.ResultsIL-1 and IL-6 levels were significantly elevated in patients during the active phase compared with healthy controls (p = 0.006 and p = 0.01, respectively), supporting their role in acute inflammation. SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 levels did not significantly differ between active and recovery phases. However, SCUBE2 levels were significantly lower in the recovery phase than in controls (p = 0.008), suggesting potential downregulation following acute inflammation.ConclusionsOur findings indicate that SCUBE2 may be more closely related to late-stage vascular stabilization or tissue repair mechanisms rather than acute inflammatory activity in IgAV. This preliminary finding suggests that SCUBE2 may be associated with post-inflammatory processes; however, further studies are required to clarify its clinical relevance.

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV) is the most common childhood vasculitis, yet reliable endothelial-related biomarkers reflecting inflammatory activity and disease course remain limited.SCUBE proteins are endothelial-associated molecules with potential relevance to vascular biology, but have not been well characterized in pediatric IgAV.This study aimed to explore whether serum SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 levels are associated with disease activity and inflammatory cytokines in children with IgAV. What was learned from the study? Interleukin-1 (IL-1) and Interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels were significantly higher during the active phase of IgAV compared with healthy controls, and IL-1 was also elevated in the active phase compared to the recovery phase, supporting a role for IL-1-mediated inflammation in IgAV.SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 levels did not differ between active and recovery phases or according to clinical severity, suggesting that these proteins are not major markers of acute, non-thrombotic inflammation in pediatric IgAV.Lower SCUBE2 levels observed during the recovery phase compared with healthy controls represent a novel but exploratory finding, highlighting the need for longitudinal studies to clarify its potential relevance in post-inflammatory or non-acute vascular processes.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV) is a systemic vessel vasculitis characterized by purpuric rash (Fig. 1) in the absence of thrombocytopenia, and other symptoms may include arthritis, nephritis, and gastrointestinal (GI) signs. The disease primarily affects children aged 2–15 years. Its annual incidence is approximately 13.5 cases per 100,000, and it is twice as common in boys as in girls. The exact causes and development of self-limiting IgAV are not fully understood [1–3]. Numerous studies have indicated that proinflammatory cytokines and IgA are involved in the pathogenesis of IgAV. Cytokines are known to induce inflammation by promoting the release of chemokines from endothelial cells, which increases the production of cell adhesion molecules and facilitates the migration and adhesion of inflammatory cells [3–5]. Purevdorj et al. [6] found that the serum levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and C-reactive protein (CRP) in patients with IgAV were significantly higher than in healthy volunteers. Furthermore, a study by Besbas et al. [7] involving 20 children with IgAV found that tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) levels were significantly higher during the acute phase of the disease than in the remission period. Research has shown that cytokines can affect vascular endothelial cells, resulting in vascular injury by increasing the expression of adhesion molecules. Proinflammatory cytokines, such as TNFα, IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ), and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), are released by endothelial cells and significantly contribute to the inflammatory response associated with the development of IgAV [7, 8]. Increased levels of TNFα have been demonstrated in both the plasma and urine of individuals with IgA nephritis [9, 10].Fig. 1Skin involvement in immunoglobulin A vasculitis: a purpuric rash on the feet, b purpuric rash on the heel, and c bullous rash on the feet and legs

The epidermal growth factor (EGF) superfamily includes cytokine-like mediators, extracellular matrix proteins, and a group of growth factors. A new gene related to EGF was isolated in developing mice [5]. Grimmond et al. isolated the first gene in a new family of mammalian genes that encoded an EGF-related protein containing a CUB (C1s-like) domain [11]. In human endothelial cells, a full-length cDNA was identified that encoded one CUB domain at the carboxyl terminus and contained ten EGF-like domains. This protein was called SCUBE1, which stands for signal peptide-CUB-EGF-like domain containing protein 1. The associated gene has been called SCUBE1. A second closely related gene, SCUBE2, has also been identified [11–13].

Initially, both genes were thought to be involved in embryonic development. However, their gene products were also found in human vascular endothelial cells, platelets, and atherosclerotic vascular lesions [5]. In endothelial cells, the expression of SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 is downregulated after TNFα and IL-1β treatment in vitro, as well as following injection of lipopolysaccharide, a stimulant of general inflammation in vivo. The SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 genes respond to inflammatory stimuli in vivo, indicating a potential role for the SCUBE gene family in the inflammatory response. Although the precise functions are currently unknown, it is thought that they may have potential roles in development, as was initially thought, but also inflammation and thrombosis [13]. Recent studies have shown that the SCUBE1 protein is abundantly expressed in human platelets. This protein is confirmed to be present on the cell surface of activated platelets and is also stored within the platelet α-granules. After platelet stimulation, SCUBE1 translocates to the platelet surface and is released into plasma [14, 15].

Dai et al. [16] showed that plasma SCUBE1 concentrations were elevated in patients with acute coronary syndrome and acute ischemic stroke. Orem et al. [17] reported that in patients with Behçet’s disease, SCUBE1 levels were elevated and may be linked to thrombotic activation, endothelial damage, and inflammation, all of which are involved in the pathogenesis of Behçet’s disease. In addition, these authors suggested that serum SCUBE1 levels were superior to CRP and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) as a biomarker for disease activity. Thus, there is ample evidence that SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 play roles in various pathophysiological processes, including those involving vascular endothelial cells, inflammation, cancer metastasis, and vascular diseases [16–18].

Another marker studied in IgA vasculitis is pentraxin-3 (PTX-3), a long pentraxin produced locally by endothelial cells, macrophages, and various inflammatory cells in response to IL-1 and TNFα, and proposed as a sensitive indicator of endothelial activation and small-vessel inflammation [19]. Prospective studies have demonstrated that elevated serum PTX-3 levels at disease onset are associated with increased severity of subsequent renal involvement and nephritis in children with IgA vasculitis [20]. Accumulating evidence further highlights the pivotal role of endothelial activation and small-vessel injury in the pathogenesis of pediatric IgA vasculitis and its renal complications. While inflammatory biomarkers such as PTX-3 primarily reflect acute inflammatory activation, they provide limited information regarding endothelial repair and vascular remodeling. In contrast, SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 are endothelial- and platelet-derived molecules involved in angiogenesis, vascular homeostasis, and post-inflammatory endothelial responses. Therefore, evaluating SCUBE proteins may offer a complementary perspective on vascular processes in IgA vasculitis beyond conventional inflammatory markers. Considering the reported roles of SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 in vascular biology, as well as in endothelial injury and repair processes, we hypothesized that SCUBE family proteins may be involved in the inflammatory and post-inflammatory processes of IgAV. We further postulated that the expression of proinflammatory cytokines, acting in various combinations, might contribute to the development and progression of IgAV, rather than serving as direct therapeutic targets at present. To the best of our knowledge at the time of this study, no prior research has examined the association between SCUBE proteins and IgAV in pediatric populations. Therefore, the present study aimed to explore serum SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 levels in children with IgAV during the active and recovery phases of the disease and to examine their possible relationship with selected proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-1, IL-6, and TNFα, in a predominantly mild IgAV cohort.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

Patients who visited the outpatient clinic of the Division of Pediatric Rheumatology, Department of Pediatrics, Faculty of Medicine, at Karadeniz Technical University and were diagnosed with IgAV according to the Ankara 2008 diagnostic criteria [21], between January 2016 and May 2017, were included in the study. Patients who had used steroids or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) within the last 3 months were excluded from the study.

After approval was received from the ethics committee, blood samples were collected from patients initially in the active phase of the disease and subsequently during the recovery phase, as well as from an age- and gender-matched healthy control group attending the Healthy Child Polyclinic. Blood samples were collected from patients with IgAV during an acute phase exacerbation and then approximately 3 months (recovery phase) after the acute phase ended. These patients had not used NSAIDs or steroids prior to their visit. Patients were divided into two groups: group 1 included patients with involvement limited to the skin and joints; group 2 comprised patients with skin involvement, those with other system involvement, or patients with skin and joint involvement accompanied by issues in other systems. During the follow-up period, none of the patients received systemic corticosteroids or NSAIDs; only supportive care was provided. Blood was collected from healthy children as a control group. Controls were matched for age and gender and had no history of rheumatological diseases in their immediate family. At presentation, decimal age, gender, clinical findings, and laboratory results, including ESR, CRP, and white blood cell and platelet counts, were documented. Levels of IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 were measured by commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) methods.

Serum SCUBE1 levels were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China; Cat. No. E-EL-H5405). The assay measurement range was 0.625–40 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 0.375 ng/mL, according to the manufacturer’s specifications. The intraassay coefficient of variation was 6.4%. The coefficient of determination (R2) of the standard curve was 0.9986. Results are expressed in nanograms per milliliter.

Serum SCUBE2 levels were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (YLbiont, Shanghai, China; Cat. No. YLA3733HU). The assay measurement range was 0.16–10 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 0.04 ng/mL, according to the manufacturer’s specifications. The intraassay and interassay coefficients of variation were < 8% and < 10%, respectively. R2 of the standard curve was 0.9974. Results are expressed in picograms per milliliter.

Serum IL-1 levels were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China; Cat. No. E-EL-H0088). According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the assay measurement range was 1.6–100 pg/mL, with a sensitivity of 1.1 pg/mL. The intraassay coefficient of variation was 6.0%. R2 of the standard curve was 0.9983. Results are expressed in picograms per milliliter.

Serum IL-6 levels were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China; Cat. No. E-EL-H0102). According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the assay measurement range was 1.56–100 pg/mL, with a sensitivity of 0.92 pg/mL. The intraassay coefficient of variation was 5.5%. R2 of the standard curve was 0.999. Results are expressed in picograms per milliliter.

Serum TNFα levels were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China; Cat. No. E-EL-H0109). According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the assay measurement range was 7.8–500 pg/mL, with a sensitivity of 2.3 pg/mL. R2 of the standard curve was 0.9975. Results are expressed in picograms per milliliter.

Specimen Collection and Measurements

The levels of IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 were measured in serum obtained from 5–6-mL blood samples collected into gel-activated vacutainers and kept at room temperature for at least 20 min to allow clotting. Then the samples were centrifuged at 1800×g for 10 min and the serum was separated and stored at − 80 °C prior to analysis. Serum levels of SCUBE1, SCUBE2, IL-1, IL-6, and TNFα were measured using commercial ELISA kits (Elabscience, Cat No. E-EL-H0109, Wuhan, China). The results are presented as nanograms per millliter for SCUBE-1, IL-1, IL-6, and TNFα, and as picograms per milliliter for SCUBE-2. Clinical scoring for children with IgAV was based on both clinical and laboratory findings and used de Mattia et al.’s system, with scores ranging from 0 to 3, as detailed in Table 1 [21]. The evaluation considered symptoms in the joints, GI tract, and kidneys, using a four-point scale: 0 = no symptoms, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, and 3 = severe. During the enrollment period, leukocytosis was identified in patients when their white blood cell count exceeded the normal range for their age [22]. Values exceeding 30 mm/h for ESR and 0.5 mg/dL for CRP levels were considered high. A platelet count above 400,000/μL was classified as thrombocytosis [23].

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. As most continuous variables did not follow a normal distribution, data are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Comparisons between the active and recovery periods in the same patients (paired measurements) were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Comparisons between patients and healthy controls (independent measurements) were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test. For variables showing normal distribution, paired-samples t tests were used for within-patient comparisons, and independent-samples t tests were applied for comparisons between patient and control groups. The relationships between continuous variables were examined using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Given the multiple comparisons performed, the results should be interpreted with caution and are considered exploratory. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics Approval

The study protocol adhered to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine at Karadeniz Technical University (Approval number 2015/191). This research was also supported by our university’s Scientific Research Projects fund (BAP Number TTU2016-5643). Informed consent was obtained from all participants or their parents in both patient and control groups prior to their enrollment.

Results

A total of 26 patients, comprising 13 boys (50%) and 13 girls (50%), diagnosed with IgAV between ages 3 and 17 years, were included in the study. The mean age of the patients was 7.20 ± 3.33 years. The mean age of the healthy control group (n = 26), matched for age and gender, was 7.15 ± 3.34 years.

In the laboratory findings, microscopic hematuria was present in five (19.2%) patients with renal involvement, and proteinuria was noted in three (11.5%). Notably, none of the patients in the study presented with severe GI involvement, macroscopic hematuria, nephrotic syndrome, or nephritic syndrome. All patients had normal renal function tests. One patient (3.8%) exhibited leukocytosis, while four patients (15.3%) showed thrombocytosis. Among patients, 15 (57.6%) had elevated CRP levels, and 3 (11.5%) had elevated ESR. The severity of IgAV disease was assessed in all patients. According to the de Mattia clinical severity score, 12 patients (46.2%) had a score of 1, while 14 patients (53.8%) had a score of 2 or higher, indicating mild to moderate disease severity in the majority of the cohort. In the subgroup of patients with a de Mattia severity score ≥ 2 (n = 14), no significant differences were observed in SCUBE1, SCUBE2, IL-1, IL-6, or TNFα levels between the active and recovery phases (p = 0.39, p = 0.47, p = 0.36, p = 0.63, and p = 0.24, respectively).

Group 1 (14 patients with only skin and joint involvement) was compared with group 2 (12 patients with skin involvement and other systemic complications, or skin and joint involvement accompanied by other systemic issues). The comparison evaluated CRP levels, ESR, and white blood cell and platelet counts. No significant differences were found between the two groups for any of the measurements (p = 0.50 for CRP, p = 0.69 for ESR, p = 0.41 for white blood cell count, and p = 0.83 for platelet count).

Comparison of active-phase and recovery-phase levels of IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 in patients with group 1 IgAV showed no differences (p = 0.19, p = 0.43, p = 0.68, p = 0.36, and p = 0.97, respectively). Similarly, in group 2, no differences were observed between the active and recovery periods (p = 0.09, p = 0.2, p = 0.13, p = 0.06, and p = 0.11, for the same sequence of parameters, respectively). A comparison of IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 levels during the active period between group 1 and group 2 of patients with IgAV again showed no differences (p = 0.13, p = 0.32, p = 0.58, p = 0.57, and p = 0.57, respectively).

The severity of IgAV disease was assessed in all patients. Among the patients assessed, 14 (53.8%) had scores above 2, while only 5 (19.2%) scored 4 or higher. Furthermore, in patients with a severity score of 2 or higher, there was no significant difference in levels of IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 between the active and recovery periods of the disease (p = 0.36, p = 0.63, p = 0.24, p = 0.39, and p = 0.47, respectively). Of note, no significant correlation was found between severity score and age (r = − 0.07, p = 0.70).

The level of IL-1 was significantly higher during the active phase of the disease compared to the recovery phase (p = 0.03, as shown in Table 3). However, no differences were observed in the levels of IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 between the active and recovery periods of the disease (Table 3). Discussion

In this prospective study, we evaluated serum levels of SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 in children with IgA vasculitis during the active and recovery phases and examined their relationship with selected proinflammatory cytokines. SCUBE1 levels showed a non-significant trend toward higher values during recovery, whereas SCUBE2 levels were significantly lower during recovery than in healthy controls. Taken together, these findings suggest that cytokine activity and SCUBE protein expression may reflect different aspects of endothelial involvement across disease phases in pediatric IgA vasculitis.

In the present study, the average age of the patients was 7.2 years, which is in keeping with studies reported from our region and other existing literature [24] . Previous studies have shown an increase in white blood cell count, platelet count, ESR, and CRP levels, which suggests an acute inflammatory process in patients with IgAV [25–28].

In a study by Trapani et al. [29], an increased ESR was detected in 57% of patients with IgAV, while leukocytosis was present in only 21% of patients. Candemir et al. [30] reported that leukocytosis was present in 13.3%, thrombocytosis in 35.5%, the ESR was elevated in 64%, and CRP tested positive in 97% of patients with IgAV. In the present study, the proportion of patients with elevated CRP was lower at 57.6%, while those with elevation of ESR constituted less than 10% of the patient cohort. However, we defined high ESR values as those exceeding 30 mm/h, which may have contributed to the low rate of patients with elevated ESR. Leukocytosis was observed in only 3.8%, which is much less prevalent than in earlier studies. We assessed leukocytosis using age-specific normal values for white blood cell count. In addition, thrombocytosis was present in only 15.3% of the patients in our study. We found that platelet counts, white blood cell counts, ESR, and CRP levels were not associated with IgAV systemic involvement, nor were they specific laboratory indicators of inflammation in patients with IgAV. Furthermore, platelet and white blood cell counts, as well as ESR and CRP levels, did not correlate with the clinical scores indicating disease severity. This lack of association may be related to the predominantly mild clinical presentation of the patients included in the study. Importantly, patients with more severe disease who required steroid or NSAID treatment were excluded, which may represent a selection-related limitation and restrict the generalizability of these findings.

The IL-1, IL-6, TNFα, SCUBE1, and SCUBE2 levels of patients with a severity score of 2 or above were compared between the active and recovery periods, but no difference was found between the periods. The study cohort mainly consisted of patients with mild IgAV, as individuals who had received prior steroid or NSAID therapy were intentionally excluded. In our cohort of patients with IgAV, IL-1 levels measured during the active phase of the disease were significantly higher than during the recovery phase. Although a weak positive correlation was observed between IL-1 levels and CRP and ESR, these associations did not reach statistical significance. These findings suggest that IL-1 may be involved in inflammatory activation during the acute phase of IgAV; however, its role should be interpreted cautiously, given the exploratory nature of the analysis and the mild disease profile of the cohort.

Lin et al. [31] demonstrated that serum levels of IL-6 were significantly elevated in patients with IgAV during the acute phase of the disease. These authors suggested that IL-6 may play a role in the pathogenesis of IgAV and that thrombocytosis observed in patients with IgAV is a form of inflammatory reactive thrombocytosis. In the present study, we found no significant difference in IL-6 levels between the active and recovery periods of the disease. This discrepancy may be attributable to the limited sample size and the exclusion of patients with more severe disease manifestations [31].

Ha [32] found that serum TNFα levels increased during the acute phase and may serve as markers for monitoring disease activity in patients with IgAV and severe renal involvement. In the present study, TNFα was examined in patients with more clinically severe disease (severity score > 2) during the active and recovery periods, and no significant difference was found. TNFα levels were compared between the active period of the disease and the healthy control group, and again, no difference was observed. These findings may reflect the absence of severe renal involvement in our cohort.

PTX-3 is a multifunctional mediator of innate immunity that regulates complement activation through interactions with components of the classical, lectin, and alternative pathways. PTX-3 expression has been demonstrated in mesangial and endothelial cells in IgA nephropathy, supporting its role as a biomarker of acute inflammatory endothelial activation and complement-mediated glomerular injury [20].

In contrast, SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 are endothelial- and platelet-derived proteins primarily implicated in angiogenesis, vascular homeostasis, and endothelial stabilization. Rather than reflecting acute complement-driven inflammation, SCUBE proteins may be more closely related to post-inflammatory vascular remodeling and repair processes. Accordingly, evaluating SCUBE proteins alongside inflammatory endothelial biomarkers such as PTX-3 may help distinguish between biomarkers reflecting active inflammatory endothelial injury and those associated with subsequent vascular recovery or remodeling in IgA vasculitis.

Ulusoy et al. [33] were the first to report that SCUBE1 levels, which have been shown to increase in acute ischemic events, were also high in pre- and post-hemodialysis patients without the occurrence of ischemic events. The reason for the high SCUBE1 levels in this patient group may be because of platelet activation, potentially through blood passage through the pump rollers or the presence of microbubbles in the blood as it passes through the dialysis equipment. Hemodialysis patients are also known to exhibit chronic inflammation, which may have caused an increase in SCUBE1 levels.

Considering only the active period, no significant difference was found between group 1 and group 2 for any of the biomarkers. IgAV is characterized by inflammation in the vascular wall, but thrombotic complications do not play a role in the pathogenesis [34]. The absence of differences between groups may reflect the overall mild disease severity of the cohort.

Orem et al. [17] reported that SCUBE1 may be a biomarker for Behçet’s disease, but it was not superior to CRP and ESR. In the present study, no significant relationship was found between patients with high CRP or ESR and SCUBE1 and SCUBE2. No significant difference was found between patients with thrombocytosis and those with SCUBE1 or SCUBE2. These negative findings may be attributable to the small sample size and the exclusion of patients with more pronounced inflammatory activity. Therefore, the present results should be considered exploratory rather than definitive.

Previous studies have reported the presence of SCUBE2 in diffuse intimal thickening and advanced atherosclerotic plaques in human coronary arteries. Immunohistochemistry analysis of serial sections from human coronary arteries has demonstrated a significant co-localization of SCUBE2 with macrophages in plaque lesions [35]. In the present study, when we compared patients in the recovery phase with controls, SCUBE2 serum levels were significantly lower in the recovery group. This observation suggests that SCUBE2 may be involved in late-phase processes such as vascular repair rather than acute inflammation; however, this hypothesis remains speculative and requires further investigation. Several alternative explanations should be considered, including interassay variability, sampling timing, and the selection of a predominantly mild patient cohort. In addition, the hypothesis that SCUBE2 may be sequestered at sites of tissue repair cannot be confirmed by the present data.

Limitations

There are several limitations to this study that should be acknowledged. First, cytokine and SCUBE protein levels were assessed using ELISA-based assays, which can be affected by pre-analytical and interassay variability. ELISA quantifies circulating protein concentrations but does not reflect functional activity or dynamic cytokine interactions, and interassay variability may affect absolute values.

Furthermore, patients with more severe IgA vasculitis (IgAV) who required steroid or NSAID treatment were excluded from the study. This exclusion resulted in a relatively small sample size and a cohort predominantly characterized by mild disease. As a result, this selection may limit the generalizability of the findings and could partly explain the absence of stronger associations. A formal power calculation was not performed due to the lack of prior pediatric IgAV studies providing reliable effect-size estimates, which is an additional limitation.

Consequently, the results should be interpreted as exploratory. Future studies with larger sample sizes and longitudinal designs that include patients with a broader spectrum of disease severity are needed to clarify the potential role of SCUBE proteins in IgAV.

Conclusion

This study explores the relationship between SCUBE1, SCUBE2, and IgA vasculitis and contributes preliminary data to a relatively underexplored area. In IgAV, the pathogenesis of which has not been fully elucidated, no significant relationship was found between the newly identified SCUBE1 and SCUBE2 proteins, which are thought to play a role in inflammation, and the active phase and recovery periods of patients with IgAV. This may be because IgAV is a non-thrombotic disease associated with inflammation in the vascular wall. However, serum SCUBE2 was significantly lower in patients with IgAV in the recovery period compared to healthy peers. We suggest that SCUBE2 may be associated with late-period pathological events other than inflammation or repair mechanisms in patients with IgAV. A further possibility is that SCUBE2 may have been removed from the circulation to perform some unidentified biological role or to suppress its inflammatory effects during the recovery phase. Therefore, the observed reduction in SCUBE2 levels during the recovery phase should be interpreted cautiously. While this finding may suggest a potential role of SCUBE2 in late-phase processes, such as vascular remodeling or repair, further studies with larger and more diverse patient populations are required to clarify its biological relevance. Furthermore, larger studies of IgAV, including patients with the most severe clinical findings, may reveal significant associations that were not present in our smaller, selected, mild disease severity cohort.

A lighter hybrid feature fusion framework for polyp segmentation
Authors: He Xue, Luo Yonggang, Liu Min, Li Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractColonoscopy is widely recognized as the most effective method for the detection of colon polyps, which is crucial for early screening of colorectal cancer. Polyp identification and segmentation in colonoscopy images require specialized medical knowledge and are often labor-intensive and expensive. Deep learning provides an intelligent and efficient approach for polyp segmentation. However, the variability in polyp size and the heterogeneity of polyp boundaries and interiors pose challenges for accurate segmentation. Currently, Transformer-based methods have become a mainstream trend for polyp segmentation. However, these methods tend to overlook local details due to the inherent characteristics of Transformer, leading to inferior results. Moreover, the computational burden brought by self-attention mechanisms hinders the practical application of these models. To address these issues, we propose a novel CNN-Transformer hybrid model for polyp segmentation (CTHP). CTHP combines the strengths of CNN, which excels at modeling local information, and Transformer, which excels at modeling global semantics, to enhance segmentation accuracy. We transform the self-attention computation over the entire feature map into the width and height directions, significantly improving computational efficiency. Additionally, we design a new information propagation module and introduce additional positional bias coefficients during the attention computation process, which reduces the dispersal of information introduced by deep and mixed feature fusion in the Transformer. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that our proposed model achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmark datasets for polyp segmentation. Furthermore, cross-domain generalization experiments show that our model exhibits excellent generalization performance.Keywords: Polyp segmentation, Deep learning, Transformer, GeneralizationSubject terms: Biological techniques, Biotechnology, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Medical research, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

As of 2023, globally, the number of deaths from colorectal cancer (CRC) still accounts for the majority of cancer-related deaths1. CRC is a common form of cancer, and medical researchers widely believe that the majority of CRCs develop from the progressive deterioration of colon polyps2. Researchers believe that timely detection and removal of these adenomatous polyps through early screening can significantly reduce the risk of developing cancer or greatly improve the survival rate of CRC. Colon screening is typically performed by obtaining images of the colon’s interior through colonoscopy, and further assessment is made by specialized physicians to determine the presence of polyps and the need for removal. However, currently, colonoscopy screenings conducted by expert practitioners have two major flaws. The first and most critical flaw is the negligence in polyp detection. According to literature statistics, 17%-28% of colon polyps are missed during the assessment process in registered colonoscopy screenings3–5. The second flaw lies in the expertise required for the medical image interpretation of colonoscopy, making it extremely labor-intensive and costly6. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a segmentation method that is efficient, highly accurate, and accessible with low technical requirements. In recent years, with the flourishing development of computer vision, deep learning based accurate segmentation algorithms have offered a solution to these problems in the form of polyp segmentation tasks7,8. Considering the practical application value of such algorithms, they have gained widespread attention in both industry and academia.

Currently, there are two main challenges in polyp segmentation, as shown in Fig. 1. The first challenge is the variability in polyp size. According to statistics from all available datasets, the polyp size in the training set images can range from 0.4% to 81.6% of the whole image, while in the test set images, this range is from 0.1% to 63.2%9. The second challenge lies in the blurred edges and internal inhomogeneity of polyps. Polyps themselves are similar to surrounding tissues, and due to their protruding shape, they are susceptible to lighting conditions. Under illumination, polyps often exhibit unclear edges and significant internal variations. These difficulties pose a severe challenge to accurately segmenting the polyp regions.

Illustration of the challenges in polyp segmentation. Polyps tend to vary widely in size, and polyps are similar to surrounding tissues. In addition, the internal changes of polyps are also large. All of these make it difficult to accurately segment the polyp margin.

In the early stages of the rise of polyp segmentation, due to the powerful image processing capabilities of convolutional neural network (CNN)10 at that time, most polyp segmentation algorithms were based on various CNN-based models6,11–15. These methods, leveraging the inherent properties of convolutional operations, employed a top-down approach to model the images. They were able to adequately exploit local relationships and local information, achieving satisfactory performance at the time. In recent years, the emergence of Transformers16 in natural language processing and image processing has brought new design insights and strategies to medical image processing tasks. Several Transformer-based methods have emerged, once again surpassing the state-of-the-art (SOTA) segmentation accuracy17–21. Unlike CNN, which relies on kernel-based computations, Transformer models the relationships between all image patches, adaptively blending and extracting the most informative features. Consequently, Transformer excels in modeling long-range dependencies. However, these Transformer-based methods inherently have some limitations. Firstly, the Transformer’s operation mode, based on a relatively global receptive field, makes it challenging to capture task-specific key local information. As the model deepens, global features are continuously blended until convergence, leading to inevitable attention dispersion. This makes it difficult for Transformers to accurately acquire local details in dense prediction tasks such as polyp segmentation. Secondly, Transformer is based on a large number of stacked multi-head self-attention modules, which introduce a considerable computational burden in terms of parameter calculations. This situation is even more pronounced when dealing with dense prediction tasks.

A compromised solution lies in the hybrid CNN-Transformer models. The motivation behind this strategy is to leverage the complementary strengths of CNN and Transformers to achieve superior segmentation performance. Although these methods have recently achieved SOTA performance in polyp segmentation tasks, they still face inherent challenges. Firstly, as mentioned earlier, there is the issue of attention dispersion still in the hybrid models. It has been pointed out that due to the presence of the CNN branch, attention dispersion in the hybrid model may occur even earlier18. This is essentially a result of the Transformer’s lack of local modeling capabilities, leading to overall overfitting to the CNN. Secondly, there is a concern regarding computational burden consistently. While the hybrid strategy improves segmentation accuracy, it also introduces higher model parameters and computational complexity. This inevitably becomes an obstacle for practical applications of the hybrid models.

To achieve more efficient and accurate polyp detection and segmentation, addressing the aforementioned issues, we propose a novel CNN-Transformer Hybrid model for Polyp segmentation (CTHP). CTHP combines the strengths of CNN, which excels in modeling local information, and Transformer, which excels in modeling global semantics, thereby enhancing segmentation accuracy. Specifically, CTHP consists of two segmentation paradigms: convolutional paradigm (CP) and Transformer paradigm (TP). These two paradigms operate in parallel to process input images. For TP, we aim to reduce the parameter burden and improve computational efficiency of the self-attention modules. To achieve this, we transform the self-attention computation across the entire feature map into computations along the width and height directions, greatly enhancing computational efficiency. Additionally, to combat attention dispersion, we design an information propagation module (IPM) and introduce additional position bias term during the self-attention calculation process. IPM reduces information diffusion caused by deep blending in the Transformer, while the new position bias term enhances the spatial modeling capability of the Transformer for local regions. For CP, we design a fully convolutional network with IPM to improve the segmentation accuracy of fine-grained local areas and establish more refined dependencies. The predictions from both paradigms are finally fused through a prediction head to generate the final prediction results. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CTHP, achieves SOTA performance on multiple polyp segmentation benchmarks. Moreover, cross-domain generalization experiments show that CTHP exhibits robust generalization ability beyond the training set. In summary, our contributions are as follows:

Related work

CNN-based segmentation methods

At the beginning of the rise of deep learning based polyp segmentation task, CNN dominated the field with its excellent performance and maintained its dominant position for several years. Brandao et al.11 first proposed using fully convolutional network (FCN) for polyp segmentation in images. To achieve dense predictions at high resolution, Ronneberger et al.6 introduced the U-Net model, which adopts an encoder-decoder U-shaped architecture. Building upon this, models such as ResUNet12, UNet++13, and ResUNet++14 further improved segmentation performance. Additionally, some methods have employed dual decoder structures to capture and fuse more information. PsiNet22 designed two additional decoder branches to learn contour information and distance maps estimation. DDANet23 introduced an attention map decoder to assist in enhancing the feature representation capability of the original decoder. ACSNet24 included a global-local adaptive selection module to achieve comprehensive inference for polyp regions of different sizes. UACANet25 utilized a bottom-up propagation mechanism to compute foreground and background regions. MSRF-Net26 introduced dense fusion modules to enable interaction across multiple receptive fields. TGANet27 employed an auxiliary classification task to learn polyps of varying sizes. Sultan et al.28 proposed LG-CNN, which simultaneously captures local fine-grained details and global context. Although the design goals of Sultan et al. are similar to those of the proposed CTHP, the LG-CNN-based model focuses on regional modeling within a pure CNN framework, rather than being a hybrid model like CTHP.

Despite the significant progress made by these CNN-based studies, their modeling capability for long-range dependencies remains weak due to the inherent local receptive field limitation of convolutional operations, which hinders their further development.

Transformer-based segmentation methods

In recent years, Transformer has not only rapidly developed in the field of natural language processing but have also shown increasing dominance in computer vision. As a result, numerous polyp segmentation methods based on Transformer have emerged. Wang et al.17 proposed SSFormer, which uses pyramid Transformer to improve model generalization performance. Valanarasu et al.18 introduced MedT, which combines global and local features captured by Transformer to enhance segmentation performance. DuAT29 introduced a global-to-local spatial aggregation module to locate entities of various sizes. PVT-CASCADE30 proposed a hierarchical visual converter to integrate feature maps at different scales.

With the powerful capabilities of Transformer, although these methods have achieved satisfactory performance, the enormous computational burden brought by the self-attention computation hinders their scalability in practical scenarios. Similarly, models that combine CNN and Transformer to obtain better segmentation performance also face the same issue. For example, TransUnet19 employed CNN as the model backbone to capture local features and utilized Transformer modules to simultaneously obtain global features. TransFuse20 directly used CNN and Transformer as parallel branches. FCBFormer31 leveraged a Transformer to extract edge information, while a parallel FCN is employed to further enhance local features. MGCBFormer32 utilized a multiscale grid-prior and nested channel attention block to achieve optimal feature representation.

Although these methods to some extent address the local receptive field problem of CNN, the use of Transformer significantly increases computational complexity. This research landscape inspires us to explore lighter architectures while improving performance.

Proposed method

Overview

The overview of the proposed CTHP is illustrated in Fig. 2. CTHP consists of a convolutional paradigm (CP) and a Transformer paradigm (TP). Given an input image, it is concurrently fed into the two paradigms for polyp region prediction. In TP, the input image is processed into overlapping patches using overlapping patch embedding, and subsequently mapped into a high-dimensional space to obtain multi-scale feature maps through linear embedding. During this process, the original self-attention operation is replaced with a lighter axial attention operation, and additional position bias terms are introduced to enhance spatial information of the image. The encoded multi-scale feature maps are then processed through channel concatenation and upsampling operations to obtain the TP prediction map. In CP, the input image is processed into multi-scale feature maps through multiple downsampling convolution operations, and similarly processed through channel concatenation and upsampling operations to obtain the CP prediction map. In both paradigms, multiple information propagation modules (IPM) are employed to maintain the coherence of feature information and reduce the impact of information diffusion and attention dispersion. The TP prediction map and the CP prediction map are fused through a prediction head for channel fusion, resulting in the final prediction result.

Due to the presence of self-attention mechanism, the feature maps outputted by PVT capture non-local features, thereby establishing better long-range dependencies. However, it is evident that the computation of the affinity matrix in self-attention is computationally expensive. Although PVT reduces the size of the feature maps for khw and vhw to alleviate the computational burden, it is still suboptimal. Additionally, compared to convolutional layers, self-attention layers lack the corresponding positional information, which is not conducive for the model to perceive the overall structure of polyp regions. Inspired by the designs of PVTv235 and literature36, we propose solutions to address these two drawbacks. To address the lack of positional information guidance in the self-attention layers, we make two improvements. Firstly, we modify the uniform patch embedding of the input image to overlapping patch embedding, which helps to capture boundary information and ensure local continuity. Secondly, we introduce additional learnable position bias terms in the self-attention operation, which evolve during the training process. The inclusion of position biases endows the self-attention with the ability to encode spatial information. For the issue of computational complexity, we introduce the axial attention mechanism that transforms the self-attention computation over the entire feature map into computations along the height and width directions. Information propagation module

Inspired by the designs in CNN to address the issues of gradient vanishing and information dissipation, we propose a novel information propagation module (IPM) to tackle the attention disappearance problem in TP, as shown in Fig. 4. Specifically, IPM consists of layer normalization37, SiLU activation function38, 3×3 convolutional layer, and residual connections. In TP, IPM is interleaved in the encoding and decoding processes. Experimental results demonstrate that IPM effectively enhances prediction stability.

Illustration of proposed Information Propagation Module.

Decoding

The multi-scale feature maps outputted from the encoder are concatenated with adjacent feature maps through IPM and upsampling operations, resulting in the prediction map MTP∈RH4×W4×64. To maintain the consistency in size between the prediction map and the input image, we further perform upsampling operations on MTP, yielding MTP∈RH×W×64.

Convolutional paradigm

Inspired by literature6,11,31, the convolutional paradigm (CP) of CTHP is a U-shaped network structure composed of down-sampling convolutional layers, up-sampling layers, and IPM, as shown in Fig. 5. This design enables the extraction of fine-grained features at multiple scales to complement the sparsity of segmentation information in TP. In the encoding phase, the input image goes through multiple consecutive down-sampling convolutional layers to obtain multi-scale feature maps Fi∈RH2i×W2i×Ci, i∈1,2,3,4,5. In the decoding phase, Fi are up-sampled and concatenated to obtain output prediction maps MCP∈RH×W×32.

Experiments

Dataset details

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed CTHP in polyp segmentation, we conducted experiments and performance assessments on two popular publicly available benchmarks, Kvasir-SEG12 and CVC-ClinicDB39.

Kvasir-SEG

Kvasir-SEG consists of 1000 polyp images, each accompanied by a corresponding ground truth mask. The dataset encompasses various polyp morphologies, ensuring diversity in the data. The image resolutions range from 332x487 to 1920x1072 pixels. All ground truth masks were annotated and verified by medical experts.

CVC-ClinicDB

CVC-ClinicDB comprises 612 polyp images along with their corresponding ground truth masks. These images were captured from 31 different video colonoscopy sequences and have a resolution of 384x288 pixels. Similarly, the ground truth masks were annotated and verified by medical experts.

Following the settings in literature9,12,17,39, we divide these two datasets into training (80%), validation (10%), and testing sets (10%).

Implementation details

We implemented the proposed CTHP using PyTorch. Consistent with the settings in literature17,31, all polyp images were resized to a resolution of 352x352 pixels. Various image augmentation techniques were employed, including Gaussian blur, colour jitter, horizontal and vertical flips, and affine transforms. Using a batch size of 16, we trained the model for 200 epochs. The Adam40 optimizer was utilized with an initial learning rate of 1e-4. All experiments were conducted on a single Nvidia RTX3090 GPU.

Failure case discussions

Although the proposed CTHP model generally achieves high accuracy in locating and segmenting most polyp samples, it can still encounter errors or omissions in edge segmentation in some particularly challenging scenarios. Some failure cases are illustrated in Fig. 7. As observed, when shadows or reflections cause blurring of the polyp edges (e.g., the second example in the first row and the first example in the second row), CTHP may make incorrect edge predictions or even miss polyp tissues, especially when there are multiple polyps with significant size variations in the image.

Illustration of some failure cases output by CTHP.

To address these shortcomings, we believe that incorporating an image denoising module could enhance the model’s performance. By reducing strong reflections or increasing contrast, the edges of polyp tissues could be made clearer, thereby improving the model’s robustness in these challenging scenarios. This is an area we plan to refine in our future work.

Conclusion

In order to achieve more efficient and accurate polyp detection and segmentation, we propose a novel hybrid model called the CNN-Transformer hybrid model for polyp segmentation (CTHP). The CTHP model combines the strengths of the CNN, which excels in modeling local information, and the Transformer, which excels in modeling global semantics. This combination enhances the accuracy of polyp segmentation. The CTHP model consists of two parallel segmentation paradigms: the convolutional paradigm (CP) and the Transformer paradigm (TP). In the TP, we focus on reducing the parameter burden and improving the computational efficiency of the self-attention modules. To achieve this, we transform the self-attention computation across the entire feature map into computations along the width and height directions, significantly enhancing computational efficiency. Furthermore, in order to combat attention dispersion, we introduce an information propagation module (IPM) and an additional position bias term during the self-attention calculation process. The IPM reduces information diffusion caused by deep blending in the Transformer, while the new position bias term enhances the spatial modeling capability of the Transformer for local regions. In the CP, we design a fully convolutional network with the IPM to improve the segmentation accuracy of fine-grained local areas and establish more refined dependencies. The predictions from both paradigms are fused through a prediction head to generate final prediction results. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CTHP model achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on multiple polyp segmentation benchmarks. Additionally, cross-domain generalization experiments show that CTHP exhibits robust generalization ability beyond the training set.

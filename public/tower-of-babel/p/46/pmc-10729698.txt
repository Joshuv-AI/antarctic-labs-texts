To compare the efficacy of intrathecal nalbuphine versus fentanyl as adjuvant to 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine in parturients undergoing elective lower segment caesarean section

Thejasvi B R, Dayananda V P

BMCRI, Bengaluru.

Background and Aims: Neuraxial opioids are widely used as spinal adjuvants with local anaesthetics. This study aimed to compare the effects of adding nalbuphine or fentanyl as an adjuvant to 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine on the characteristics of spinal blockade and post-operative analgesia.

Methods: 100 parturients from the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) I/II scheduled for elective caesarean section were randomly allocated to Group A and received 10mg of 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine(2ml), and 10 µg of intrathecal fentanyl (0.2 ml); Group B received the same dose of hyperbaric bupivacaine [2ml) and 1mg of nalbuphine (0.1ml)and (0.1ml Normal Saline (NS)] and Group C received the same hyperbaric bupivacaine (2ml) and 0.2ml of NS. The subarachnoid block characteristics, intraoperative haemodynamic status, duration of postoperative analgesia and amount of postoperative analgesic requirements were compared.

Results: Patients who received intrathecal nalbuphine had a significantly faster onset of sensory and motor blockade than fentanyl. The duration of motor blockade was longer with nalbuphine than with fentanyl [Table 1]. The requirement of postoperative rescue analgesic in 24 hours was lesser with nalbuphine(P<0.001) as compared to fentanyl(P<0.014).

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0112

Effectiveness of preoperative melatonin on postoperative pain and perioperative anxiety and its comparison with oral pregabalin in parturients undergoing caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia- a randomised study.

Shubha Hegde, Madhu Arun, Preeti K

Bangalore Medical College and Research Institute.

Background and Aims: Perioperative anxiety and postoperative pain are the most important factors affecting recovery after caesarean section due to the additional challenge of impaired maternal ability in nursing the newborn. Melatonin and pregabalin have multiple actions and are associated with haemodynamic stability.[1,2]

Methods: 64 parturients with American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) II status, with gestational age more than 34weeks and no intake of narcotics 24 hours before surgery, were randomly assigned to two groups (P and M) where group P received pregabalin 75mg, and group M received melatonin 5 mg, 1 hour before caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia (interspace, needle, drug dose, position were fixed). Our primary objective was to evaluate the efficacy of pre-emptive oral melatonin and pregabalin on perioperative anxiety and postoperative pain (using HAM-A, VAS, RAS), and secondary objectives were to assess their effects on spinal anaesthesia, haemodynamic response and neonatal outcome.

Results: The total duration of analgesia was significantly longer in group P(320±37.325 min, P<0.001). In group P, the onset of sensory blockade (1.44±0.504 min, P=0.045) was faster, time to rescue analgesia (320.63±37.325 min, P<0.001) and two segment regression time (65.94±6.278min, P<0.001) were prolonged. Heart rates were comparable except at 6 min (92.84±6.859, P<0.05) and 10 min (90.75±7.582, P<0.05) intraoperatively, which was higher in group P. The two groups ‘ mean arterial pressures were comparable intraoperatively, except they were lower in the M group at 10,15,20,25,30 and 40 min(P<0.05). Out of 64 patients, 4 had nausea, and 3 had headache. Three patients in the P group and 4 in the M group had side effects. APGAR score was unaffected in both groups.

Conclusion: Preemptive pregabalin 75mg provided a longer duration of analgesia, faster onset of sensory block, longer duration to rescue analgesia and prolonged two-segment regression time. Melatonin 5mg caused significant hypotension intraoperatively.

Keywords: Caesarean section, melatonin, pregabalin, post-operative pain, spinal anaesthesia.

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0170

A Double-Blinded Prospective Randomised Control Study of Pre-Operative Administration of Intravenous Mephentermine vs Intramuscular Mephentermine in preventing Post Spinal Hypotension in Caesarean Section

Sagnik Datta, Suman Chattopadhyay, Arun Kumar Mandi

Midnapore Medical College & Hospital.

Background and Aims: Post-spinal hypotension is common in caesarean section. In this study, we investigated the effect of pre-operative mephentermine by either intramuscular or intravenous route in lower segment caesarean section (LSCS) under spinal anaesthesia for the reduction in incidence of hypotension, rescue vasopressor administration and adverse effects.

Methods: This prospective, randomised, double-blinded, placebo-controlled study was planned on 120 healthy consenting term parturients designed for low-risk LSCS divided into 3 equal groups. Group M received 15mg intramuscular mephentermine bolus 10 minutes before spinal anaesthesia. Group I and Group C received 10mg intravenous mephentermine and normal saline bolus just before spinal anaesthesia, respectively. Proper masking was performed with all patients receiving similar IV (drug) and IM (saline) injections or vice versa as per group. Haemodynamic parameters, hypotension events, requirement of rescue vasopressor, adverse reaction and neonatal outcomes were recorded till 2 hours post-operatively.

Results: Mid-term analysis revealed that Group I had significant adverse effects like headaches. So, the study was curtailed after taking 25, 22 and 20 patients in Group M, Group I and Group C, respectively. Analysis revealed higher mean arterial pressure in Group I at 5,10,15 minutes. Group C had a higher incidence of hypotension, nausea and vomiting. Other adverse effects were higher in Group I. Apgar score was better in Group M. Other study parameters were comparable in all groups.

Conclusion: Prophylactic intramuscular mephentermine is better than intravenous mephentermine in the prevention of spinal hypotension in lower segment caesarean section and is associated with fewer side effects to both mother and baby

Keywords: Hypotension, mephentermine, spinal,

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0189

Ultrasound Guided Transversus Abdominis Plane Block With Ropivacaine or Ropivacaine With Dexamethasone For Postoperative Analgesia In Patients Undergoing Lower Segment Caesarean Section

Padial RS, Reshma B M, Harsoor SS, Sahana

Dr B R Ambedkar Medical College and Hospital, Bengaluru.

Background and Aims: Postoperative pain following LSCS can disturb postpartum mothers. USG-guided TAP block has enabled us to provide excellent analgesia. Thus, we studied the efficacy of 0.2% ropivacaine with or without dexamethasone in USG-guided TAP block in providing postoperative analgesia following LSCS.

Methods: Informed consent was taken after IEC clearance (No: EC144) and CTRI registration (CTRI/2021/03/032153). The study was conducted on 60 patients posted for LSCS under spinal anaesthesia. The study population in Group A received 0.2% ropivacaine 20 ml on either side, and Group B received 0.2% ropivacaine 20 ml with dexamethasone 4mg on either side. Post-operative analgesia was assessed every 2 hours until 24 hours, both at rest and on movement, starting from the TAP block. The time to first postoperative analgesia, as evidenced by NRS≥3, was noted, and several rescue analgesia doses and Patient satisfaction scores were recorded—complications, if any, were reported and treated.

Results: Time to first rescue analgesia in group A and group B were 15.21±5.26 h (95% CI =13.34-17.70) 22.8 ±4.7 h (95% CI =22.67-29.76) (P <0.001) respectively. The total doses of injection tramadol required in the postoperative period were 1.07±0.52 in group A and 0.4 ±0.49 in group B. (P<0.001.) Patient satisfaction score was 100% in group B, compared to 50% in group A(P<0.001). None of the patients in either group had any episodes of vomiting and block-related complications in the postoperative period.

Conclusion: We conclude that adding dexamethasone to ropivacaine in the TAP block after LSCS significantly prolongs the duration of postoperative analgesia and reduces the number of analgesic doses in the postoperative period.

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0359

Comparison of median and paramedian approach effectiveness for spinal anaesthesia for caesarean delivery: An observational study.

Bhagirath Hadiyal, Hemangini M Patel

Government Medical College, Surat.

Background and Aims: Median and paramedian approaches for spinal anaesthesia are associated with different advantages and limitations and are related to technical variations. [1,2] Comparison of the effectiveness of the median and paramedian approach to evaluate success rate concerning the number of attempts and several passes and incidence of post-dural puncture headache (PDPH) and low back pain.

Methods: 200 pregnant patients were enrolled. Patients given spinal anaesthesia by median approach or paramedian approach were taken as group M or PM, respectively. Spinal anaesthesia was attempted with Quinke's spinal needle by either method, and Inj. bupivacaine heavy (0.5%) 2ml was injected intrathecally to achieve a T6 level of spinal anaesthesia. Time to identify landmarks, administer spinal anaesthesia and incidence of complications were noted. Patients were asked for PDPH or low back pain at 24h, 48h and 1 week after spinal anaesthesia with the help of a Numeric rating scale (0-10).

Result: The mean of several attempts was 1.24±0.49 in group M and 1.14±0.40 in group PM (p>0.05). The mean number of passes was 1.78±0.79 in group M and 1.74±0.81 in group PM (p>0.05). No patient developed PDPH. In group M, 23% and in group PM, 15% patients develop low backache.

Conclusion: The paramedian approach was equally effective as a median approach.

Keywords: Median, Paramedian, Spinal Anaesthesia.

ABSTRACT NO.: ASB0497

Comparison of the effect of hyperbaric ropivacaine with or without fentanyl in parturients undergoing elective caesarean section under subarachnoid block

Veswudu Swuro, Neelam Meena, Geeta Singariya, U. D Sharma

S. N Medical College, Jodhpur, Rajasthan.

Background and Aims: Ropivacaine can be used for spinal anaesthesia for lower segment caesarean section (LSCS). [1,2] To compare the clinical efficacy and safety of intrathecal fentanyl (10mcg) added to 0.75% hyperbaric ropivacaine in parturients undergoing LSCS under spinal anaesthesia.

Methods: The study was conducted in a tertiary health care institute after seeking clearance from an institutional ethical committee and obtaining written and informed consent from the patients. The sample size included 25 patients in each group. All data was analysed using the SPSS 26 version. A P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Inclusion criteria included pregnant females aged between 20 – 35 years with >37 weeks of singleton pregnancy of ASA physical status of grade – II only. The patients were allocated to either of the two groups viz Group RS: Ropivacaine heavy (0.75%) 2 ml + 0.2ml normal saline and Group RF: Ropivacaine heavy (0.75%) 2ml + 10µg fentanyl.

Results: The time to first request analgesics was longer in Group RF(199.4±20.37) when compared with Group RS(155±14.36). It was statistically significant (P=0.001). The time of onset of sensory block was slower in group RF (2.68 ± 0.4) when compared with group RS (2.8 ± 0.52) (P=0.36).

The mean time to achieve the highest sensory level was longer in group RS (4.52 ± 0.52) when compared with group RF (4.3 ± 0.46). It was statistically not significant (P=0.11). The average time taken after spinal puncture to delivery was longer in group RF (9.2 ± 0.82) when compared with group RS (8.9 ± 1). The average time to achieve modified Bromage score 3 was 4.3 minutes in group RS and 4.25 minutes in group RF. It was statistically not significant (P= 0.75). Total duration of surgery was longer in group RS (48.8 ± 8.05) compared to group RF (45.36 ± 8.29). It was statistically not significant (P=0.14). The mean umbilical vein pO2 was comparable in Group RF (28.15±3.97) and Group RS (26.8±04.28). It was statistically not significant (P=0.05). In group RS, 4 % of patients experienced bradycardia, 32% experienced hypotension, 4 % experienced nausea and 8 % experienced shivering. In group RF, 20% of patients experienced hypotension, 4% experienced nausea and 4% experienced pruritus.

Conclusion: Adding fentanyl 10µg to hyperbaric ropivacaine 15mg for spinal anaesthesia increases the duration of analgesia in the early postoperative period in patients undergoing LSCS without increasing maternal or neonatal side effects.

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0535

A randomised controlled trial comparing the efficacy of intravenous oxytocin and intravenous carbetocin for haemodynamic stability in patients undergoing caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia.

Sabari CGS, Kedareshwara. K S, Vinayaka Jannu, Priyanka Gadvi

Jawaharlal Nehru Medical College, Belagavi, Karnataka.

Background and Aims: Oxytocin is the preferred uterotonic for managing PPH with significant cardiovascular effects. Carbetocin, a synthetic oxytocin analogue, combines the safety and efficacy profile of oxytocin.[1,2] We compared the haemodynamic responses following intravenous oxytocin and carbetocin in patients undergoing LSCS under SAB.

Methods: 150 pregnant women undergoing elective caesarean section under SAB were enrolled for the study. Patients were randomly assigned to two groups; Group-1 patients received 100µg of intravenous carbetocin administered over 1 minute, and Group-2 patients received 3U of intravenous oxytocin administered over 1 minute after delivery of anterior shoulder followed by an infusion of 10U per hour for 4 hours. Heart rate and mean arterial pressures were recorded every 15 seconds for 5 minutes following drug administration. Data were analysed using Student's unpaired t-test and Student's paired t-test wherever appropriate. A p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results: Oxytocin produced clinically significant tachycardia (≥20%) and hypotension (>30%) after 30 seconds following administration, with peak changes over the 3rd minute of infusion. However, these effects were sustained throughout the study period. Carbetocin demonstrated stable haemodynamic effects compared to oxytocin (p <0.0001). The requirements of additional uterotonics and total intraoperative blood loss were comparable between both groups[Figures].

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0577

Comparison of two doses Of Norepinephrine (6µg versus eight µg) for treatment of Spinal Anaesthesia Induced Hypotension in caesarean section under Spinal Anaesthesia

Sourojita Neogy

Calcutta National Medical College and Hospital.

Background and Aims: Several measures, including vasopressors, have been tried to correct hypotension following spinal anaesthesia in Caesarean section. Researchers have found that norepinephrine is less likely to induce bradycardia and decrease cardiac output. This study aimed to compare two doses of norepinephrine (6 and 8 micrograms) for treating hypotension.

Methods: A prospective observational study was conducted on 80 non-labouring women at term 18-40 years, height 140-180 cm, with singleton pregnancy without comorbidities. All patients were co-loaded with 10-15 ml/kg Ringers Lactate, and multiparameter monitors were attached. Baseline MAP, HR, SPO2 and ECG were recorded. SA was administered, hypotension was considered when the MAP value was less than 20% from baseline, and the first bolus was given according to the allocated group. The number of rescue bolus doses, total dose of norepinephrine, episodes of hypotension, bradycardia, PONV, shivering, hypertension, tachycardia, APGAR score at 5th minute and umbilical cord blood pH were recorded. SPSS software version 22 was used for statistical analysis. For categorical variables, percentages were calculated, and the Chi-Square test / Fisher's Exact test was applied.

Results: There was less incidence of hypotension(3.28±1.36 vs 2.03+-1.02 P<0.001), less frequent requirement of dose(2.33±1.42 vs 1.03+-1.12 P <0.001) and less total rescue dose(13.95±8.53 vs 7.93+-8.97 P <0.001) in the 8 µg group compared to the 6 µg group.

Conclusion: 8 micrograms norepinephrine is superior to 6 micrograms norepinephrine for treating spinal anaesthesia-induced hypotension in a caesarean section.

Keywords: Caesarean section, Hypotension, Norepinephrine, Spinal Anaesthesia

ABSTRACT NO.: ABS0666

Comparison of norepinephrine and phenylephrine boluses for the treatment of hypotension during spinal anaesthesia for elective caesarean section- a prospective interventional study

Sanath Kumar Jeganathan, Manjula BM, Hemalatha S, Gurudatt CL

JSS Medical College and Hospital, Mysuru.

Background and Aims: Spinal anaesthesia-induced hypotension is a common complication because of sympathetic blockade up to the T4 level. Phenylephrine has a rapid onset, shorter duration of action, and maintains good foetal acid-base status. Norepinephrine has adverse chronotropic action due to weak beta and potent alpha effects.[1,2]

Methods: A total of 60 parturients were included in the study and were randomly divided into two groups using the Sheffield shield opaque envelope technique. They were divided into Group N(30), which received 4mcg of norepinephrine, and Group P(30), which received 50mcg of phenylepinephrine as IV boluses for hypotension following subarachnoid block. The primary objective was to compare the number of bolus doses of either norepinephrine or phenylephrine. The duration of sustainability of blood pressure with each blouse was noted. Incidence of bradycardia, hypotension, and the total doses of vasopressor given intraoperatively were stressed. Episodes of nausea, vomiting and dizziness were recorded.

Results: The number of boluses of vasopressors required to treat hypotension was significantly lower in group N (2 ± 1.14 vs. 2.3 ± 1.11, P = 0.001). In Group N, 100% had 0 dose of phenylephrine. In Group P, 0.00% had 0 doses of phenylephrine, 23.33% had 1 dose, 43.33% had 2, 20% had 3, 6.67% had 4 and 6.67% had 5. There was a significant difference in the number of doses of phenylephrine distribution between the two groups. The episodes of hypotension and the number of bolus doses of norepinephrine were recorded and analysed [Figures 1, 2]. The heart rate was lower in group P, but this difference was not statistically significant. Maternal complications such as nausea, vomiting, and shivering were comparable between the groups. The foetal parameters were also similar between the two groups.

Conclusion: Intermittent norepinephrine boluses are effective in managing spinal-induced hypotension during caesarean section. The neonatal outcomes were similar in both groups. Norepinephrine boluses can be considered as an alternative to phenylephrine boluses.

A comparative study of intrathecal administration of 0.5% bupivacaine (12 mg) and 0.5% bupivacaine (8 mg) with fentanyl 25ug for ephedrine requirements during caesarean section

Umesh G, Leena H Parate

M S Ramaiah Medical College and Hospital, Bengaluru.

Background and Aims: Opioids act synergistically with local anaesthetics to increase sensory block without increasing sympathetic block.[1,2] Our study aimed to investigate the efficacy of intrathecal fentanyl as an adjuvant to low-dose bupivacaine versus normal-dose bupivacaine on the vasopressor requirement in caesarean section.

Methods: One hundred and ten women scheduled for caesarean delivery were randomly divided into two groups. Group B patients received 12mg of 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine, and Group B+F patients received 8 mg of 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine plus 25 µg fentanyl. A decrease in systolic blood pressure <95 mmHg or >25% from baseline was considered hypotension and was treated with ephedrine 6 mg plus a 100 mL fluid bolus intravenously. Maternal haemodynamics, sensory and motor block, duration of analgesia, occurrence of maternal side effects and Apgar score of the newborn were compared between the groups.

Results: Group B+F had a mean ephedrine requirement of 9.71 mg, and Group B had a mean ephedrine requirement of 28.25 mg. A statistically significant difference was found between the study and control groups for the highest level of sensory block (p-value -0.01). There was a statistically significant difference between the groups regarding the duration of postoperative analgesia (p-value < 0.001).

Conclusion: Bupivacaine 8mg and fentanyl 25 µg combination for spinal anaesthesia in caesarean section provides less hypotension and lesser vasopressor requirements and prolongs the duration of analgesia during caesarean section compared with bupivacaine 12mg alone.

Background and Aims: Spinal anaesthesia is the most widely used regional anaesthesia procedure. Postdural puncture backache is a complication of spinal anaesthesia. We aimed to compare the incidence of post-dural puncture backache between median and paramedian spinal anaesthesia techniques.

Methods: 120 patients of ASA grade 1 and 2, age group 19-45 years, were taken up for the study. Patients were randomly divided into 60 patients in each group: Median and Paramedian. Exclusion criteria included patients with back deformity, a history of back pain, and contraindications for spinal anaesthesia. Patients with more than two attempts and failed SA were excluded.

Results: Overall, low back pain incidence was higher in the median group than in the paramedian. The incidence at 24 hours was not statistically significant. Conclusion: The overall incidence of back pain between the two techniques was higher in the median group than in the paramedian group. At 24 hours, there was no statistically significant difference. At 7 days and one month, there was a statistically significant difference between the two techniques. The incidence of low back pain was more with the median than with the paramedian technique.

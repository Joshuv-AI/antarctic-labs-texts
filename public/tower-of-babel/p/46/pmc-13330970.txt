ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPsoriasis is a chronic inflammatory disease induced by genetic, immune and environmental interactions. The central mechanism is the aberrant activation of the Th17/IL-23 pathway, which leads to uncontrolled proliferation of keratinocytes and systemic inflammation. Psoriasis affects about 1–3% of the global population, with 70–90% of patients suffering from moderate to severe itching of the skin, 50% of patients suffering from thickening or loss of nails, and even 30% of patients suffering from a combination of arthropathy, and 59.1% of patients suffering from a psychological burden due to social discrimination, which greatly reduces the quality of life. Moreover, there has been no bibliometric analysis of studies on smart diagnosis and treatment of psoriasis, and it is hoped that this study will contribute to the development of smart diagnosis and treatment.ObjectiveBy integrating cutting-edge research trends into intelligent diagnostic and therapeutic systems, advancing precision diagnosis and personalized treatment for psoriasis.MethodsRetrieve literature related to intelligent diagnosis and treatment of psoriasis from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), The search period spans from January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2025. VOSviewer 1.6.20 and CiteSpace 6.4.1 software were employed to analyze co-cited literature, keywords, research trends, and international collaboration patterns.DiscussionWith the help of intelligent image recognition, multimodal data fusion to carry out intelligent diagnosis and treatment, to promote the development of precision medicine, reduce medical costs, and achieve the purpose of improving long-term management, the technology can be further expanded to atopic dermatitis, eczema, etc, to further improve the quality of life of patients, and is expected to become a new model of dermatology diagnosis and treatment.Keywords: psoriasis, intelligent diagnosis and treatment, literature visualisation, CiteSpace, VOSviewer

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated skin disorder classified as a common chronic inflammatory skin disease. Its prevalence varies significantly across different regions worldwide, with rates as high as 11.4%.1 Typically characterised by erythematous, thickened skin covered with silvery-white scales.2 Skin symptoms usually appear as red patches covered with silvery-white scales, with thin films and punctate haemorrhages visible when the scales are scraped off, usually on the elbows, knees, scalp and lumbosacral region;3 Psoriasis is usually triggered when T-lymphocytes are overactive and release immune abnormalities such as IL-17 and TNF-a;4 Skin cells grow faster in psoriasis patients, normal skin renewal takes about 28 days, while psoriasis patients’ skin renewal takes only 3–4 days.5 About 1/3 of patients have a family history of psoriasis, and a specific gene, HLA-C, is known to be a key gene in the causation of psoriasis;6 In addition to this, psoriasis can usually be caused by infections, stress, skin trauma, smoking, obesity, and the use of certain antihypertensive medications.7–9 By knowing these psoriasis symptoms, people can diagnose psoriasis by means of medical experience and visual observation. However, clinical symptoms such as erythema and psoriasis co-exist in other skin diseases, so there are limitations in early diagnosis.10 Along with the development of artificial intelligence (AI), intelligent diagnostics are beginning to be gradually applied to diagnose psoriasis, for example, computer vision (CV) and image recognition, multimodal data fusion, natural language processing (NLP).11 Intelligent diagnosis of psoriasis has evolved from single image recognition to a closed-loop system with multimodal fusion, subject to clinical validation and ethical compliance. In the future, AI will largely assist dermatologists in diagnosis, but the final diagnosis still needs to be combined with physician experience and pathological gold standard. In this study, we systematically sort out the current research status, knowledge structure and evolutionary path in the field of AI-assisted treatment and diagnosis of psoriasis through bibliometric methods, so as to provide a reference for future research.

The integration of intelligent diagnosis and intelligent treatment for psoriasis can help improve the accuracy of treatment. Intelligent diagnosis can realize early typing through image recognition or biomarker analysis, while intelligent treatment can optimize the treatment drugs and phototherapy programs based on the diagnosis results, and then dynamically adjust the strategy. The integration of smart diagnosis and smart treatment for psoriasis can also help improve clinical efficiency. Specialists may have limitations in the process of traditional diagnosis, and the use of AI combined with remote diagnosis and treatment can greatly improve the efficiency of psoriasis treatment; while bibliometric studies on smart diagnosis and treatment for psoriasis can reveal the migration of research hotspots and further track research hotspots, and also help to identify emerging cross-cutting areas, and help to identify and fill research gaps. It can also help to identify emerging cross-cutting areas, help to identify and fill the research gaps, and promote the cooperation among academic fields to overcome the imbalance of research organizations, so as to help mankind to face and overcome psoriasis together for the benefit of the health of the whole mankind.

Materials and Methods

Data Sources and Filtering

In order to explore the research literature related to smart diagnosis and smart treatment of psoriasis during the last 20 years, a search was conducted on 2 February 2026 based on Web of Science core databases, which were searched from 1 January 2005 to 31 December 2025, The advanced search formula for literature related to the intelligent diagnosis of psoriasis is:((TS=(psoriasis)) AND TS=(artificial diagnoses OR machine diagnosis OR digital diagnose OR intelligent diagnose OR AI diagnose OR smart diagnose OR autonomous diagnose OR algorithmic diagnose)) NOT ALL=(treatment OR therapy), the advanced search formula for literature related to smart treatment of psoriasis is:((TS=(psoriasis)) AND TS=(AI Therapy OR smart healthcare OR intelligent medical treatment OR digital therapeutics OR digital therapy OR hybrid therapy models OR digital therapeutics OR intelligent treatment OR technical treat)) NOT ALL=(diagnose OR diagnosis). During the retrieval process, both the broad term “AI” and the narrow terms “digital diagnose” and “digital therapy” were included to balance search coverage and accuracy. Additionally, keyword combinations were further optimized; for example, the inclusion of “autonomous diagnose” enhanced the comprehensiveness of the search results. Furthermore, the search query strictly distinguished between diagnostic-related literature and therapeutic-related literature to avoid content overlap.

The data was downloaded in “Full Record and Cited References” format and exported as a “plain text file” using the “Export Record to File” option. These files were then imported into VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.4.1), Pajek (1.0.0.0) and Microsoft Excel for analysis. Statistical Methods

The data covers the period from January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2025. Using Excel 2016, we analyzed search results from Web of Science during this timeframe and compiled statistics for countries with ≥6 publications. VOSviewer software was employed to visualize co-citation networks, keyword networks, and country co-occurrence networks. Pajek software was then used to fine-tune the networks generated by VOSviewer. In the psoriasis intelligent diagnosis research visualized with VOSviewer, the quantitative thresholds for all research projects were: co-citation frequency ≥3, keyword occurrence frequency ≥2, and all countries with collaborative relationships. For psoriasis treatment research visualized with VOSviewer, the thresholds for inclusion were: co-cited documents ≥2 times, keywords appearing ≥2 times, and all countries with collaborative relationships. When performing emergent analysis of keywords with CiteSpace, the γ value was selected as the screening criterion with a threshold of γ=0.3, while all other parameters remained at default settings.

Cited Literature Analysis

Co-citation analysis based on VOSviewer (Figure 2b). A total of 3910 nodes were co-analysed in relation to intelligent psoriasis diagnosis. Setting the co-citation threshold to 3 yielded 154 co-cited publications. Among these, the paper Deep Neural Network-Based Dermatologist-Level Skin Cancer Classification by Andre Esteva et al (2017) received the highest citation count (16 citations). This study demonstrates that large-scale convolutional neural network models can achieve skin cancer diagnosis levels comparable to dermatologists, exhibiting significant application potential in the field of mobile healthcare.12

Analysis of Research Hotspots

Using VOSviewer software, 541 keywords were aggregated. Setting the co-occurrence threshold to 2 yielded 119 keywords meeting the criteria. Based on these 119 keywords, a keyword network diagram and keyword density map for intelligent psoriasis diagnosis were constructed. Research hotspots in the field of intelligent psoriasis diagnosis include: classification studies of psoriasis, machine learning applications for psoriasis, diagnostic imaging for psoriasis, and epidemiological analysis of psoriasis.13–16 “psoriasis”, “artificial intelligence”, “diagnosis”, “classification”, and “deep learning” are the core keywords of this study, closely interlinked with other terms. These keywords are grouped into five clusters: the green cluster concerns differential diagnosis of psoriasis based on image and computer processing; the blue cluster represents the pathological mechanisms of psoriasis; the red cluster involves AI applications for disease management and risk assessment; the pink cluster denotes the use of AI for dermatological diagnosis (Figure 2c). Regarding the intelligent diagnosis density map for psoriasis, the density fill colour surrounding keywords such as “psoriasis”“artificial intelligence”“diagnosis”“deep thinking” and “classification” approaches yellow, indicating that AI-based psoriasis subtyping and classification represent prominent research topics within this field. The density fill colour surrounding keywords such as “management”“inflammation” “burden”“risk” and “prediction” leans towards blue, indicating that clinical management and diagnostic risk prediction in intelligent psoriasis diagnosis retain potential for further exploration and are anticipated to become future research focal points (Figure 2d).

Analysis of Country Cooperation

Analyzing international collaboration in the field of intelligent psoriasis treatment using VOSviewer software (Figure 2e). This study covers 40 countries. Among them, 32 countries have established international collaborative relationships. Within these 32 countries, the top four nations in terms of published papers are China, the USA, India, and England. This indicates that these three countries hold a leading position in the field of intelligent psoriasis diagnosis and maintain relatively close cooperative ties with other nations. However, direct collaboration between China and the USA remains limited. Therefore, it is anticipated that China, the USA, India, and England should strengthen their leadership role in guiding other nations toward breakthroughs in intelligent psoriasis diagnosis. Concurrently, efforts should focus on enhancing cooperation between China and the USA in this research domain.

Cited Literature Analysis

Using VOSviewer to analyze co-citation analysis (Figure 3b). In the co-cited references on intelligent treatment for psoriasis, a total of 4101 nodes were analyzed. Setting the co-citation threshold to 2 yielded 203 co-cited publications. Among these, the highest citation counts were recorded for the 2020 review “Pathophysiology, Clinical Presentation, and Treatment of Psoriasis: A Review” by April W. Armstrong et al,22 the 2021 paper “Psoriasis” by Christopher E M. Griffiths et al,23 and the 1978 study “Severe psoriasis--oral therapy with a new retinoid” by T Fredriksson et al,24 each cited 8 times.

Research and treatment of psoriasis have evolved from drug discovery and mechanism elucidation to optimized management. The pioneering 1978 study by Fredriksson et al first demonstrated the efficacy of oral retinoic acid derivatives for severe psoriasis and established the PASI efficacy assessment system still in use today. A 2021 review by Griffiths et al clarified the central role of the IL-17/IL-23 immune axis, driving a therapeutic revolution in targeted biologics. Building upon this foundation, Armstrong et al’s 2020 commentary further proposed strategies for stratified treatment and comprehensive management of comorbidities, while outlining the application prospects of AI in achieving personalized precision therapy. Collectively, these three works exemplify the systematic developmental trajectory of this field—from effective treatment and targeted intervention to intelligent management.

Keyword density maps and a keyword network diagram for intelligent psoriasis treatment were constructed based on these 109 keywords. Keywords can be broadly categorized into four clusters: The blue cluster represents the fundamental pathological mechanisms of psoriasis; The red cluster signifies intelligent treatment approaches and population-specific research for psoriasis; The green cluster denotes research into therapeutic drug formulation development; The yellow cluster represents studies on drug delivery technologies (Figure 3c). Nanocarrier-mediated targeted delivery of methotrexate and AI-enabled development of clinical tools for psoriasis typing as research hotspots in the field of smart psoriasis treatment.25–27

In the psoriasis smart treatment density map, the deeper yellow shading surrounding keywords such as “psoriasis” “treatment” “formulation” “drug delivery” and “biotherapy” indicates that R&D for psoriasis biotherapies based on formulations and drug delivery systems is a recent research hotspot.28 However, keywords like “artificial intelligence” and “digital health” exhibit a gradient from yellow to blue, suggesting that AI-driven research in intelligent psoriasis treatment has yet to reach its peak and is poised for rapid growth in the coming years (Figure 3d).

Discussion

The development of intelligent diagnosis and treatment for psoriasis has progressed through three distinct phases. Intelligent diagnosis is increasingly evolving toward multimodal data fusion technologies, while intelligent treatment is advancing toward AI-driven personalized therapy (Table 3).

Research Status of Psoriasis Intelligent Diagnosis and Treatment Across Different Time Periods

The core of intelligent psoriasis diagnosis and treatment lies in integrating intelligent diagnosis and intelligent therapy to form a closed-loop system. First, integrate multimodal diagnostic data such as images and genomes to obtain diagnostic results; Then, using artificial intelligence to classify psoriasis, in order to provide personalized treatment plans; Subsequently, leveraging AI-optimized therapeutic interventions, personalized treatment is achieved through optimized drug delivery or adjusted phototherapy parameters; Following treatment completion, AI compares and evaluates the patient’s condition before and after treatment, The AI continuously monitors treatment outcomes, adjusting the treatment plan immediately if any deterioration in the patient’s condition occurs. By leveraging AI, this approach creates a closed-loop system integrating intelligent diagnosis and treatment, significantly enhancing the efficiency of intelligent psoriasis diagnosis and therapy.38

Currently, the primary collaborative relationships in psoriasis smart diagnosis and treatment research exist among the USA, China, and India, with these three countries contributing the highest number of publications. Efforts should be made to gradually enhance the leading role of China, the USA, and India in driving countries with moderate publication volumes, such as England and Italy, thereby strengthening the influence of these moderate-volume countries on those with lower publication output.

Regarding research gaps, intelligent diagnosis of psoriasis still lacks model validation and standardized imaging protocols,39 Data models lack dark-skinned populations such as Africans and Latinos, most studies stop at diagnostic severity scores not predicted by biologic response, and samples of rare subtypes of psoriasis such as pustular and erythrodermic are scarce; Existing data on smart therapy for psoriasis are static images at a single time point, lacking longitudinal tracking of relapses or remissions, and further follow-up is still lacking,40 Existing smart treatment accuracy rates are derived from retrospective data and lack validation in randomised controlled trials.

Over the past two decades, the field of intelligent psoriasis diagnosis and treatment has evolved from single-image recognition to multimodal fusion, with deep learning models progressively achieving high-accuracy classification. Treatment research is shifting from nanodelivery systems toward AI-driven dynamic protocol optimization. AI-driven psoriasis diagnosis and treatment demonstrate significant efficacy,41,42 gradually advancing innovations in personalized interventions.

Conclusion

In summary, this bibliometric analysis systematically reviews the knowledge structure and evolutionary trajectory of AI-assisted psoriasis diagnosis and treatment over the past two decades. The field has evolved from single-image recognition to multimodal data fusion, and from traditional drug delivery models to AI-driven optimisation of personalised treatment. Despite significant progress, challenges remain, including insufficient representation of population diversity and the need for prospective clinical trials. Future research should focus on strengthening international collaboration and addressing these research gaps in order to fully realise the potential of intelligent systems in enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of psoriasis diagnosis and treatment.

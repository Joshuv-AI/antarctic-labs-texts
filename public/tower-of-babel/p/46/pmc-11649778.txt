A New Species of Heligmonellidae (Nematoda) in Euryoryzomys russatus (Rodentia: Sigmodontinae) in the Atlantic Forest of Northeast Brazil
Authors: Natália Alves Costa, Rosana Gentile, Martin R Alvarez, Arnaldo Maldonado Junior
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe genus Stilestrongylus (Freitas, Lent, and Almeida, 1937) is defined by having 24 or more subequal cuticular ridges, an asymmetrical caudal bursa, and a hypertrophied genital cone. It comprises 25 recognized species, mainly parasitizing cricetids, and shows significant diversification linked to the evolution of its hosts. Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. is a newly described nematode species identified in the small intestine of the rodent Euryoryzomys russatus (Cricetidae, Sigmodontinae) from the Atlantic Forest of Northeast Brazil. This study aims to characterize the new species and its prevalence in host populations.MethodsA total of 43 Euryoryzomys russatus individuals were collected and analyzed for parasitic infections. Morphological features of Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. were examined, focusing on the number of ridges, synlophe characteristics, caudal bursa type, lobe hypertrophy, and the spicule length to body length ratio.ResultsThe new species exhibited a prevalence of 21% among the sampled hosts. Distinctive morphological features were observed, including:the synlophe structure exhibited specific ridge patterns that differentiate it from other species within the genus; the caudal bursa was identified as a type 2-2-1; hypertrophy of the right lobe was observed, indicating notable morphological adaptation; rays 4 and 5 were found to be equivalent in size and diverged at the distal end; the spicule length to body length ratio (SpL/BL) was measured at 30–36%, representing the largest ratio recorded for the genus. In comparison, other Stilestrongylus species displayed the following ratios: S. rolandoi (21–33%), S. lanfrediae (25–29%), S. kaaguyporai (20–24%), S. inexpectatus (20–23%), and S. stilesi (26–28%).ConclusionsThis report presents Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. as a new species of nematode, contributing to the diversity of the genus Stilestrongylus. The findings underscore the importance of studying host-parasite interactions within the Atlantic Forest ecosystem and provide a basis for future ecological and parasitological research.Keywords: Trichostrongyloidea, Stilestrongylus, Parasite, Rodent, Scanning electron microscopy

Introduction

The Helligmonellidae family (Skrajabin and Schikhobalova, 1952) comprises nematodes belonging to five subfamilies: Tricholinstowiinae, Heligmonellinae, Pudicinae, Brevistriatinae and Nippostrongylinae. Among these, the taxonomic characters for the genera of the subfamily Nippostrongylinae are based on the number of cuticular ridges of the synlophe, the degree of inclination in relation to the body axis and the presence or absence of a gradient in the size and orientation of the cuticular ridges in relation to the sagittal axis. This subfamily occurs in rodents worldwide, particularly in cricetids in the Americas [1]. In Brazil, the genera Hassalstrongylus, Stilestrongylus, Guerrerostrongylus, Trichofreitasia, Heligmostrongylus, and Nippostrongylus have been recorded [2, 3]. Particularly, the genus Stilestrongylus Freitas, Lent and Almeida, 1937 presents the following main taxonomic characteristics: 24 or more subequal cuticular ridges on the synlophe in the middle of the body, a markedly asymmetrical caudal bursa and a hypertrophied genital cone [4].

The genus Stilestrongylus includes 25 recognized species, of which 23 are parasites of cricetid rodents, one of murines, and one of echimids [5–8]. This genus is widely distributed throughout the Neotropical region, showing great diversification that follows the radiation of its sigmodontine hosts [9, 10].

The rodent Euryoryzomys russatus (Wagner, 1848) (syn. Oryzomys russatus) (Rodentia, Sigmodontinae), also known as the russet rice-rat, has terrestrial habits, feeds on seeds, fruits, and insects, and occurs in eastern Brazil, northeastern Argentina and eastern Paraguay [11, 12]. This rodent is a forest specialist associated with forested coastal plains and evergreen and semi-deciduous forests [13]. This species avoids open vegetation types such as Cerrado and Chaco, preferring primary forests [14]. In Brazil, it occurs in the Atlantic Forest from Bahia to the state of Rio Grande do Sul, including eastern Minas Gerais [15]. In this study, a new species of Stilestrongylus was described parasitizing E. russatus in areas of the agroforestry system at the Pratigi Environmental Protection Area, municipality of Igrapiúna, state of Bahia, northeast Brazil.

Materials and Methods

The study was carried out in the Pratigi Environmental Protection Area (13° 51’ S; 39° 16’ W) in the municipality of Igrapiúna, southern Bahia, during a comprehensive study of mammal diversity and parasites in the Atlantic Forest. The rodents were captured in areas of agroforestry systems (rubber, cocoa, clove, and peach palm production) and in dense ombrophilous forests [16] in Fazendas Reunidas Vale do Juliana. Captures were conducted in two sampling periods during ten consecutive nights each, in August 2014 and March 2015. Animals were captured with Sherman® (7.62 cm x 9.53 cm x 30.48 cm), Tomahwak® (40.64 cm x 12.70 cm x 12.70 cm) and pitfall traps. Live traps were placed on the ground along six transects of 15 trapping points. Pitfall traps (60-liter buckets) were installed along four transects. The spacing between transects was 500 m and between traps 20 m. The bait used was a mixture of peanut butter, sardines in soybean oil, banana, cornmeal, and oat flakes.

The rodents were identified based on their external morphology and cranial morphometry. The animals were submitted to taxidermy and deposited in the “Alexandre Rodrigues Ferreira” Mammal Collection at the State University of Santa Cruz (CMARF-UESC). The animals were captured under the authorization of the Chico Mendes Institute for Conservation and Biodiversity (ICMBio; license number 17131-4) of the Brazilian government and the Ethics Committee on the Use of Animals (CEUA) of the Oswaldo Cruz Foundation (license number LW-39/14). All procedures involving animal handling and biological sampling were carried out using biosafety techniques and equipment [17].

We examined the stomach, intestine, liver, lungs, abdominal and thoracic cavities of the hosts for helminths presence. We collected the nematodes from the small intestine, washed them briefly in 0.9% NaCl physiological solution, and fixed them in 70% ethanol. We examined fifteen male and 10 female nematodes for their morphological characteristics.

To observe these characteristics and describe the internal structures, we cleared the helminths in 0.5% lactophenol or 50% glycerol/alcohol and mounted them between glass slides and coverslips. Drawings were made using a Nikon Eclipse E200MVR light microscope (Nikon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). We observed and measured the structures under a compound microscope (Zeiss Standard 20) from digital images captured by TCapture Imaging Application software Version 5.1.1.0 (N). Measurements were presented in micrometers (except when indicated) for holotype or allotype specimens, the means and standard deviation are followed by the range enclosed in parentheses. The synlophe was studied in five males and five females, and the total number of dorsal and ventral ridges was counted according to Durette-Desset and Digiani [18]. The terminology used in relation to the caudal bursa was followed by Durette-Desset et al. [19]. Holotype, allotype, and paratype specimens were deposited in the Helminthological Collection of the Oswaldo Cruz Institute (CHIOC) of the Oswaldo Cruz Foundation in the state of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

For scanning electron microscopy (SEM), four specimens (two males and two females) were analyzed according to the modified protocol of Souza et al. [20]. Subsequently, the samples were critical-point dried using CO2, mounted on metal “stubs” and coated with gold (20 nm). The specimens were examined using a JEOL JSM-6390 LV microscope (JEOL, Tokyo, Japan) at the Rudolf Barth Electron Microscopy Platform Oswaldo Cruz Institute, Fiocruz PDTIS/FIOCRUZ.

Description

General: small coiled nematodes curved dorsoventrally. Excretory pore located at the posterior end of the oesophagus (Fig. 1A). Cephalic vesicle present. Males and females with a cuticle with longitudinal ridges arising posterior to the cephalic vesicle, ending immediately anterior to the caudal bursa in males, before the anus in females. Synlophe with 23 ridges (11/12) in males and 23 (10/13) in females, at the level of the intestinal oesophageal junction (Fig. 1B, E); 23 ridges (11/12) in males and 23 (11/12) in females in the middle of the body (Fig. 1C, F); 22 (12/10) in males anterior to the caudal bursa and 22 (10/12) in females, anterior to the vulva (Fig. 1D, G). In the middle of the body, ridges slightly unequal in size, with smaller ridges on the right ventral part (Fig. 1C, F). Double axis of ridge orientation; right axis inclined at approximately 62° and left axis at 75° in relation to the sagittal axis in males. In females, the right axis is inclined at approximately 65° and the left axis at 42° in relation to the sagittal axis. In females, the left dorsal ridges are different, with a larger size at the level of the esophageal-intestinal junction. In males, the ridges are equal in size in the region anterior to the caudal bursa, and most of the ridges are parallel to the surface of the body.

Male (range and average) based on 11 specimens: Length 3.00–3.26 (3.15 ± 0,09) mm, 103–130 μm (117 ± 6,58) width at mid-body. Cephalic vesicle 56–70 (59 ± 3,84) long and 26–28 (27 ± 0,85) wide. Excretory pore and nerve ring located at 246–394 (310 ± 46,67) (n = 9) and 103–147 (128 ± 16,42) (n = 8), respectively, from the anterior end. Oesophagus 388 − 267 (324 ± 32,94) long (Fig. 1A). Asymmetrical caudal bursa with the right lobe more developed than the left lobe (CB DS RL+) (Fig. 1H), both type 2-2-1. Ray 2 in the right lobe shorter than ray 2 in the left lobe. Ray 3 longer than ray 2, curved ventrally, not reaching the margin of the pouch. Ray 4 robust with the same size as ray 5, bifurcating distally, ray 4 curved anteriorly, and ray 5 slightly curved posteriorly. Rays 4 and 5 follow the same trunk bifurcated at the V-shaped end. Ray 6 small, appearing at the same level as the bifurcation of ray 3. Rays 8 appeared asymmetrically on the dorsal trunk, and the left ray was more robust than the right ray. Dorsal ray divided in the distal third into two branches, each divided at the end into two subequal branches, rays 9 (external) slightly longer than rays 10 (internal) (Figs. 1H and 2B). Spicules alate, equal in length, 950–1171 (1041 ± 65,02), spicule length in relation to body length (SpL/BL) 33% (30–36%) (Fig. 2A). Gubernaculum21–27 (24 ± 1,71) long and 18–23 (20 ± 1,56) wide. Genital cone 49–56 (54 ± 2,16) long and 37–42 (39 ± 1,83) wide (Fig. 1H).

Female: (range and mean) based on 10 specimens: Length 2.78–3.69 (3.11 ± 0,25) mm, 80–130 (103 ± 19,27) µm wide in the central region of the body (Fig. 3A). Cephalic vesicle present, 43–65 (52 ± 6,10) long, 20–27 (24 ± 1,92) wide (Fig. 3B). Distance from the anterior end to the nerve ring and excretory pore: 115–127 (121 ± 6,00) (n = 2) and 313–350 (329 ± 13,03) (n = 6), respectively. Deridio not visible. Esophagus 305–386 (336 ± 27,83) long. Uterus monodelphic, posterior end strongly invaginated, with cuticular ridges reaching the distal end. Vulva located 134–195 (154 ± 20,44) (n = 9) from the caudal end, vagina vera 7–12 (9 ± 1,59) long, vestibule 40–80 (59 ± 14,35) long (n = 6), sphincter 24–49 (33 ± 8,51) long and 40 − 20 (29 ± 6,72) wide (n = 6) and infundibulum 56–96 (72 ± 16,44) (n = 7) long, uterus 910–1100 (963 ± 79,73) long (n = 4), number of eggs 8–12 (n = 4). Discussion

Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n.sp. is characterized by presenting a markedly asymmetrical caudal bursa with a 2-2-1 type pattern, hypertrophied genital cone, rays 8 with asymmetrical dorsal insertions and asymmetrical dorsal rays. Among the 25 species belonging to the genus Stilestrongylus, four share a ratio of spicule length to body length larger than 25%. Those species are Stilestrongylus rolandoi Boullosa, Simões, Andrade-Silva Gentile and Maldonado, 2018 (SpL/BL 21–33%) in Euryoryzomys russatus from Santa Catarina, Brazil; S. lanfrediae (SpL/BL 25–29%) Souza, Digiani, Simões, Luque, Rodrigues- Silva and Maldonado Jr., 2009 in Oligoryzomys nigripes Olfers, 1818 in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil; S. stilesi Freitas, Lent and Almeida, 1937 (SpL/BL 26–28%) in Holochilus balnearum from Argentina; and S. moreli Diaw, 1976 (SpL/BL 33–40%) in Phyllotis boliviensis (syn. Auliscomys boliviensis) from Bolivia [7, 21–23].

Digiani and Durette-Desset [6] noted that the differentiation of Hassalstrongylus and Stilestrongylus has become somewhat problematic due to the description of several species in which synlophes overlap between species of both genera. Stilestrongylus includes species with more than 24 subequal cuticular ridges on the mid-body synlophe, a markedly asymmetrical caudal bursa, and a hypertrophied genital cone. In contrast, Hassalstrongylus has been defined as including species with 19 to 24 cuticular ridges, unequal in size, an asymmetrical or subsymmetrical caudal bursa, and a nonhypertrophied genital cone [24]. A differentiation based solely on the number of ridges in the synlophe may be inadequate to distinguish these genera. Stilestrongylus eta and S. aculeata have fewer than 24 ridges on the mid-body. Stilestrongylus valdivianus, described as parasitizing Phyllotis micropus in Chile, has 24 ridges on the male’s synlophe and 23 on the female’s [25]. Simões et al. [21] proposed that characteristics for the genus Stilestrongylus should include species with more than 17 cuticular ridges in the mid-body instead of 24, as proposed by Durette-Desset [24].

The species in the present study has 23 ridges on the synlophe (11/12) in males and 23 (11/12) in females in the mid-body; therefore, it was not possible to differentiate the genus based on this taxonomic character. For the genus assignment, the characters from the bursa were used. The specimens presented a well-developed genital cone and asymmetry of the caudal bursa that were similar to those found in the genus Stilestrongylus. The asymmetry of the caudal bursa in these specimens is determined not only by a different development of the lateral lobes but also by the asymmetry of the branches of the dorsal rays and the difference in size, shape and insertion of the rays 8.

Stilestrongylus eta (Travassos, 1937) and S. dessetae Yoyotte Vado, 1972 are easily differentiated from these specimens because they have subsymmetric bursas. In S. peromysci, the bursa has not been fully open, and only the left lobe shows the type 2–3 pattern. Stilestrongylus rolandoi presents a type 1–4 caudal bursa pattern, and the left ray 2 is markedly smaller than the ray 2 of the right lobe, in contrast to our specimens, in which it presents the type 2-2-1 pattern of the bursa [26].

Currently, eight species of the Heligmonelidae family have been described as parasitizing E. russatus. These species are H. luquei Costa, Maldonado, Bóia, Lucio and Simões, 2014, Stilestrongylus rolandoi , Stilestrongylus kaaguyporai Panisse and Digiani, 2018, S. moreli, S. lanfrediae, Guerrerostrongylus ulysi Digiani, Notarnicola and Navone, 2012, G. gomesae Simões, Santos and Maldonado Júnior, 2012 and G. zeta (Travassos, 1937) [2, 27–32].

The new species presents caudal bursa pattern of type 2-2-1 and hypertrophy of the right lobe; rays 4 and 5 are of equivalent length and divergent at the end and have long spicules. Although S. lanfrediae presents caudal bursa pattern of type 2-2-1, the new species differs in that the left lobe is more developed than the right lobe. Stilestrongylus stilesi differs from Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. by having rays 2 and 3 diverging at the base of its length. S. moreli has rays 2 and 3 in a V formation but differs from our specimens by having right rays 2 and 3 straighter than Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. The new species differs from S. aculeata by presenting the insertion of ray 6 at the base. Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. has long spicules (SpL/BL 30–36%), surpassing those of S. rolandoi (SpL/BL 21–33%). Thus, the presence of specific characters in the caudal bursa allows us to consider the specimens found in E. russatus as a new species.

The subfamily Nippostrongylinae Durette-Desset, 1971 covers 30 genera of nematodes that parasitize rodents and have a wide geographic distribution [33, 34]. In the Neotropical region, these nematodes predominantly infect rodents of the sigmodontinae subfamily [35]. These parasites have direct life cycles, in which the first three larval stages develop in the environment, and the third stage is responsible for infecting the definitive host [3, 19]. Thus, the presence and persistence of these parasites in a given area depend both on the presence of suitable definitive hosts and on the existence of favorable environmental conditions, such as temperature, humidity, type of soil and vegetation, which enable the development of free-living stages.

Regarding the ecology of this helminth, a study of the community ecology of helminths from sigmodontine hosts was carried out in the type locality of this new species of Stilestrongylus [36]. Eight species of sigmodontine hosts were found that harbored 16 helminth species. As reported above, S. magnumspiculum n. sp. occurred in E. russatus and H. seuanezi. E. russatus had three species of helminths in this study, Syphacia sp.1, Raillietina sp., in addition to the present species. H. seuanezi had four species, Syphacia sp.2, Hassalstrongylus lauroi, Raillietina sp., in addition to the present species. Stilestrongylus magnumspiculum n. sp. was a dominant species of the helminth component community of E. russatus, however, co-dominant in H. seuanezi, being less abundant and prevalent in the latter. Seven helminth species have been recorded parasitizing E. russatus and five in H. seuanezi in Brazil [36].

Despite the existing studies on parasite communities in small rodents of the Atlantic Forest, new species are constantly being discovered, emphasizing the necessity to continue conducting this type of research.

Introduction

Frailty is a complex, multidimensional biopsychosocial syndrome with increased susceptibility of the organism and decreased resistance to stress, which is caused by a decline in the physiological reserves of the older adults.1 With the rapid growth of the global ageing population, the number of individuals affected by frailty continues to rise.2 Frailty has emerged as one of the most serious global public health challenges in the 21st century.3 In older adults, frailty significantly increases the risk of death.4 It is also linked to a higher risk of adverse health outcomes, including mobility limitations, falls, hospitalisations, cognitive decline, loneliness, depression and poor quality of life.5

Pre-frailty is the transitional state between robust health and frailty.6 The prevalence of pre-frailty, assessed using the Multidimensional Prognostic Index (MPI), is 36.4% overall, with 39.3% observed in hospital settings and 20% in nursing homes.7 Frailty is dynamic and exists on a continuum from healthy to frail. An individual’s level of frailty can increase or decrease over time.811

Identifying the risk of progression from pre-frailty to frailty supports secondary prevention. Timely communication of the frailty risk to pre-frail individuals helps raise awareness and encourages preventive action.12 However, the reversible target of pre-frailty is often overlooked.13

In recent years, a number of longitudinal studies have been published examining the risk factors for progression of pre-frailty into frailty over time. Previous findings suggest that predictors such as loneliness,14 chewing difficulties,15 poor nutrition16 and abdominal obesity17 may increase this risk. However, the existing literature remains fragmented. Study populations differ, frailty definitions vary and the measurement of predictors is often inconsistent. To our knowledge, no systematic review has comprehensively synthesised these predictors. This represents a critical gap in the evidence.

A systematic review would provide a robust evidence base, facilitating more precise targeting of interventions and optimising resource allocation in healthcare. By systematically analysing existing data, researchers can identify modifiable risk factors and support early detection of high-risk individuals. It may also inform personalised care plans and guide timely interventions in clinical and community settings. This supports timely and targeted interventions such as nutritional support, exercise programmes and medication review to prevent or delay the onset of frailty. Synthesising the available evidence will also help optimise healthcare resource allocation.

Therefore, this systematic review will synthesise existing evidence on predictors of progression from pre-frailty to frailty in older adults, with the aim of informing early identification and prevention strategies.

Methods and analysis

Objective

This systematic review and meta-analysis will aim to identify predictors associated with the progression from a pre-frailty to frailty in older adults. This review question is structured using the PEO (Population–Exposure–Outcome) framework.18

Study design

This protocol was reported in accordance with the PRISMA-P guideline (Shamseer et al., 2015). The final systematic review will be reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.19 The PRISMA-P checklist is provided in online supplemental material 1. This study is scheduled to begin on 1 December 2024, with an anticipated end date of 31 August 2025.

Search strategy

We will conduct a comprehensive literature search in the following databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, PsycINFO, CNKI, Wanfang Database, the China Science and Technology Journal Database, and the China Biomedical Literature Database from inception to the latest search date. Key terms such as “risk factors,” “pre-frailty” and “frailty” will be used in combination with Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords. The search strategy will be adapted for each database. The detailed search strategy in PubMed is provided in online supplemental material 2. We will also manually screen the reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews to identify additional eligible articles.

Study selection

Endnote 21 software will be used to manage all retrieved references. Duplicates will be removed before screening. Two reviewers (FYZ and LL) will independently screen the titles, abstracts and keywords of all studies to identify potentially eligible articles. Full texts of the remaining studies will then be reviewed in detail and reasons for study exclusion will be recorded. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third author (LPZ). The study selection process will be presented using a PRISMA flow diagram.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies will be included if they: (1) use a longitudinal cohort design; (2) involve older adults aged ≥60 years who are classified as pre-frail at baseline using a validated frailty assessment tool; (3) report frailty progression as an outcome and (4) assess at least one exposure or predictor associated with an increased risk of frailty.

Studies will be excluded if they: (1) do not report or allow calculation of effect estimates such as relative risks (RRs), ORs, HRs and 95% CIs; (2) provide insufficient methodological detail or incomplete sample characteristics; (3) fail to provide additional information after reasonable attempts to contact the authors for clarification or missing data.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Data extraction will be performed by two independent reviewers using a standardised form. Extracted data will include: first author, year of publication, country, study design, duration of follow-up, frailty assessment tool, sample size, outcome events, participant characteristics, exposures and measurement tools for predictors. The methodological quality of included studies will be assessed using the Newcastle Ottawa Scale (NOS).20 In addition, the overall certainty of the evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.21 Both assessments will be conducted independently by two reviewers. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. Studies will not be excluded based on risk of bias alone. Quality ratings will be used to inform the interpretation of results and guide sensitivity analyses where applicable.

Meta-analysis

Statistical analysis will be performed using R studio. A meta-analysis will be conducted to examine the predictors of frailty progression by pooling adjusted effect estimates. The heterogeneity of studies will be assessed using the I2 statistic.22 Moderate or high heterogeneity will be considered present when I2>50% or 75%, respectively, with a significance level of p<0.05. A random-effects model will be applied when substantial heterogeneity is present.23 If considerable heterogeneity is detected, subgroup analyses will be conducted based on the type of frailty assessment instrument used. Meta-regression will also be performed to explore whether differences in frailty measurement tools explain differences in effect estimates.24 Sensitivity analyses will be conducted using leave-one-out methods to evaluate the robustness of the findings.25 Publication bias will be assessed using the Egger’s test.26

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the general public will not be engaged in the process of designing, conducting, reporting or disseminating the findings of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

This study does not involve the collection of primary or individual-level data and therefore does not require ethical approval. All data included in this systematic review will be obtained from publicly available sources such as peer-reviewed journal articles and scientific databases. Literature search results and references will be managed using EndNote for citation organisation citations and duplicates will be removed. Extracted data will be recorded in Microsoft Excel using a standardised data extraction form. All screening decisions and extraction logs will be documented and securely stored to ensure transparency, traceability and reproducibility. The results of this systematic review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed academic journal and presentation at relevant scientific conferences.

Life skills that promote school health in adolescents: a concept analysis
Authors: Daniela Gutiérrez Torres, Angélica María Ospina Romero, Alejandra Fuentes-Ramírez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLife skills are essential for school health in adolescents; however, their conceptual understanding remains limited, so this concept will be explored.ObjectiveTo analyse the concept of "life skills that promote school health in adolescents" in the literature.MethodConcept analysis followed the eight steps described by Walker and Avant. In the evidence synthesis, an integrative review was conducted, following the PRISMA 2020 methodological guidelines. The literature review was performed on the Web of Science platform, and in the PubMed, Scopus, and ScienceDirect databases, for articles published between 2020 and 2024, written in Spanish or English, and including qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods studies, or systematic reviews.ResultsEighteen studies were included, from which four key attributes of life skills were identified: decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem solving. These skills contribute to reducing risky behaviours, improving resilience, and strengthening overall well-being.ConclusionsDespite the limited evidence linking life skills that promote adolescent school health these can be defined as a set of essential psychosocial competencies that enable adolescents to effectively cope with health-related challenges in school and social environments. The theoretical framework for understanding life skills provided in this study can be used to develop specific educational programs and interventions for adolescents.Keywords: Concept analysis, Nursing, Life skills, School health promotion, Adolescents

Introduction

The World Health Organization (WHO) defines life skills as"the aptitudes necessary for appropriate and positive behaviour that enable us to effectively face the demands and challenges of daily life" [1]. These skills are essential for personal development and fostering resilience, responsible decision-making, and conflict resolution in different social contexts [2]. In the school environment, the teaching of life skills takes on critical importance, given that adolescents face multiple challenges that can impact their physical, emotional, and social well-being [3].

School health promotion, understood as the process that empowers communities to improve their health by strengthening their personal and collective capacities [3], has proven to be an effective strategy for fostering healthy lifestyles. Using this approach, students develop skills that contribute to disease prevention and the development of sustainable habits to achieve an optimal quality of life [2].

However, the school environment is not only a space for academic learning; it is also a key setting for intervening in factors that determine health, especially during adolescence, a stage characterized by significant biological, psychological, and social changes [4]. In this context, life skills emerge as an effective means to strengthen young people's ability to cope with risky situations, manage emotions, and adopt healthy behaviours that transcend the school stage [1].

School nursing plays a fundamental role in this process. As agents of change, nursing professionals contribute to the promotion of these skills through specific interventions that include educational programs, prevention activities, and psychosocial support. These actions not only promote physical activity, healthy eating, and injury prevention but also enhance competencies related to self-care, problem solving, and informed decision-making [4].

Despite its relevance, the concept of"life skills that promote school health in adolescents"has not been fully defined or analysed in the literature. This lack of conceptual clarity can limit the effective implementation of strategies and programs aimed at promoting them. Although numerous studies address life skills in general, few focus on their impact within the school context or their relationship with adolescent health [2].

Given that adolescence is a defining stage for the development of habits and behaviours that persist into adulthood, it is essential to understand the scope and core attributes of this concept. Thus, the objective of this study is to analyse the concept of"life skills that promote school health in adolescents"using the conceptual analysis method proposed by Walker and Avant.

Method

This concept analysis was conducted using Walker and Avant's systematic method, an approach grounded in linguistic philosophy that contributes to the philosophical understanding of concepts [5]. This method, which is widely recognized within the positivist paradigm, is based on the premise that concepts are stable entities that can be decomposed, analysed, and extracted from their application contexts [6]. It is also very useful for clarifying complex concepts and establishing operational definitions that can be applied in research and clinical practice.

The method proposed by Walker and Avant offers a systematic and replicable approach to concept analysis, making it particularly suitable for clarifying complex constructs such as"life skills."This methodology was selected for its ability to stabilize and operationalize abstract psychosocial concepts, facilitating their application in nursing and healthcare contexts. Despite certain limitations, such as a lower sensitivity to context, its analytical rigor aligns with the study's objective of establishing a clear and transferable definition. Compared to other approaches, such as evolutionary analysis or critical analysis, Walker and Avant's model allows for a more precise delineation of the concept, which is essential in the initial stages of theoretical development [7].

This systematic approach allows for organizing and synthesizing information obtained from diverse sources to conceptualize"life skills that promote school health in adolescents,"which lacks a unified definition in the literature. The application of the steps is directly reflected in the results. The literature review was conducted following the guidelines established by the PRISMA 2020 declaration to ensure transparency and methodological quality at each stage of the process. An inductive approach was prioritized throughout the analysis to avoid relying on preconceived categories and thus ensure that the findings emerged directly from the literature reviews. The procedure made it possible to establish the essential components of the concept and explore its internal dynamics and applicability in various school contexts. This contributed to the development of a clear conceptual framework to guide health promotion interventions and strengthen school nursing practices.

Selection criteria

The selection criteria included documents addressing the terms"life skills,""school health promotion,""adolescent health,"or"school nursing", written in Spanish or English, and qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-methods studies or systematic reviews published in scientific journals on health, education, or nursing between 2020 and 2024.

Documents published in non-peer reviewed journals, editorials, letters to the editor, opinion pieces, and articles that did not consider the concept of life skills in the context of school health or adolescence were excluded.

Data sources

In the evidence synthesis, an integrative review was conducted, following the PRISMA 2020 methodological guidelines. A search was performed in the PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect databases and on the Web of Science platform, for articles published between 2020 and 2024 (Fig. 1), using the essential terms appearing in the title, abstract, and list of keywords. The search terms used in PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science are as follows:"life skills"AND"school"AND"health"AND"adolescents"AND"nursing."

Flowchart of the concept analysis study selection process

Articles from qualitative or quantitative studies published in Spanish or English that focused on the concept of"life skills that promote school health in adolescents"were included. Three researchers independently reviewed the titles and abstracts to determine if they met the inclusion and exclusion criteria for retrieval and analysis. Following this initial step, the researchers rescreened the included articles; they confirmed they met the criteria, identified different uses of the concept, and systematically recorded characteristics of the concept that appeared repeatedly. In cases where disagreements arose between the reviewers, they were discussed until a consensus was reached. When agreement was not possible, a third author acted as arbiter to make the final decision. Definitions and examples were used in the systematic recording to explain a set of antecedents, attributes, and consequences that were associated with the concept.

Initially, 552 records were identified in the PubMed, Scopus, Science Direct, and Web of Science databases. Subsequently, 43 duplicate records were eliminated, resulting in a total of 509 studies for the screening phase. During the selection phase, 149 studies were excluded after the titles and abstracts were reviewed. During the eligibility assessment, 341 studies were excluded because they did not meet the inclusion criteria, and 1 study was discarded because of a language barrier. Ultimately, 18 studies met the established criteria, providing an empirical basis for developing a conceptual analysis of life skills and school health promotion in adolescents. In the 18 articles included, 16 are empirical studies, one is theoretical-empirical, and one corresponds to an academic column [8]. Among the empirical ones, the following designs were identified: six cross-sectional studies [9–14], three qualitative [15–17], two quasi-experimental [18, 19], two randomized controlled trials [20, 21], one feasibility study [22], one psychometric validation [23] and one factorial test protocol [24]. The theoretical-empirical article corresponds to a systematic review with meta-analysis [25].

Definition of the concept

Dictionary definitions of the concept

Life skills are defined as a set of essential competencies for decision-making, problem solving, and coping with everyday challenges. According to the WHO, life skills enable individuals to effectively confront problems and maintain optimal well-being [1]. However, this is a general definition, making it difficult to directly apply in the context of life skills education for adolescents.

Definitions of the concept reported in the literature

In their systematic review, Kirchhoff and Keller [26] specified that life skills are a set of competencies that must be developed specifically and appropriately for the age of students. These competencies enable adolescents to cope with everyday situations and make healthy decisions that promote their overall well-being. Furthermore, the study highlights that the effectiveness of educational programs is related to their ability to promote a proactive attitude towards problems and challenges. The main professionals involved in these programs are specialized school health educators who adapt the content according to the students'ages.

In their analysis of resilience and self-efficacy, Sagone et al. [27] observed that life skills are fundamental to the emotional and psychological development of adolescents. They highlighted the importance of these skills in developing resilience and self-efficacy in young people, enhancing their ability to make responsible decisions and cope with complex situations. In this study, educational psychologists are essential in evaluating the impact of these skills on adolescents'psychological well-being.

In Fallah et al. [21], life skills are understood as fundamental tools for preventing risky behaviours in adolescents, such as substance use or unprotected sex. Public health researchers play a key role in evaluating how these programs influence adolescents'overall health and in modifying risky behaviours.

Subconcepts

Subconcepts related to life skills include decision-making, stress management, problem solving, and effective communication. Research by Kirchhoff and Keller [26] highlighted the relationship between these subcomponents and the development of positive and healthy attitudes in students. Furthermore, the study established that life skills are linked to adolescents'ability to adapt to different contexts and challenges. Psychologists and educators play a fundamental role in the implementation of these skills. Similarly, in the study by Fallah et al. [21], the subcomponents studied included decision-making, stress management, and problem solving as fundamental life skills, which helped significantly reduce the tendency towards risky behaviours among participating adolescents. Relationship between basic human values and decision-making styles in adolescents

The Flinders Adolescent Decision-Making Questionnaire (FADMQ) by Mann was used to assess decision-making styles in adolescents, differentiating between adaptive and maladaptive styles. The Schwartz Values Scale (SVS) measures adolescents'personal values and is organized into different categories. Young people with an adaptive decision-making style prioritize values such as self-direction, stimulation, achievement, and power, whereas those with a maladaptive style tend to move away from self-direction and exhibit a more conservative orientation [28].

Development and validation of an intertemporal decision-making ability scale for early adolescents

The Intertemporal Decision-Making Capacity Scale was used to assess how adolescents weigh costs and benefits over time. It comprises three dimensions: cognitive control, reward valuation, and foresight. The developed scale is a valid and reliable tool for measuring intertemporal decision-making capacity in adolescents. This ability is related to self-control and the capacity to resist temptation [29].

Evaluation of the life skills of students during adolescence: scale development and analysis

The Life Skills Scale (LSS) was used to measure the general skills of adolescents in 10 areas: critical thinking, creative thinking, decision-making and problem solving, stress and emotion management, social responsibility, teamwork, self-esteem, self-awareness, empathy and interpersonal relationships, and communication skills. The LSS was validated as a reliable scale to evaluate life skills in adolescents. Students scored higher in empathy, self-awareness and self-esteem, whereas the lowest scores were received for teamwork, stress management and critical thinking. A significant relationship was found between certain skills and variables such as gender, academic level and performance in science [30].

Planning and management of academic time for university students

The planning subscale of the adapted Metacognitive Skills Inventory (MAI) was used to assess how students plan and organize their academic time. No significant differences were found in time planning by variables such as gender or work schedule. However, there were differences in the strategies used by high- and low-achieving students, especially in goal setting and the use of support tools [31].

Adolescence stress scale: development and standardization

The Adolescent Stress Scale was used to measure stress in adolescents in 10 dimensions, such as performance stress, interpersonal conflicts, punishment, insecurity, and unhealthy environments. These dimensions include stress induced by major loss, stress induced by imposition or conflict, phobic stress, stress induced by interpersonal conflict, stress induced by punishment, stress induced by illness or injury, performance stress, stress induced by imposition, stress induced by insecurity, and stress induced by unhealthy environments. This scale is useful for identifying and measuring sources of stress in adolescents in school and clinical settings. The stress factors were found to be interrelated and can be assessed with good reliability indices [32].

Resilience and regulation of emotions in adolescents: serial mediation analysis through self-esteem and perceived social support

A questionnaire was used to measure resilience, self-esteem, perceived social support, and emotional regulation. Resilience has a positive impact on emotional regulation, and this relationship is mediated by self-esteem and perceived social support. These findings suggest that strengthening self-esteem and social support can improve emotional regulation in adolescents [33].

Design and validation of a psychological well-being scale for adolescents (BIPSI)

The BIPSI was used to assess well-being in six dimensions: self-determination, positive self-concept, personal relationships, personal growth, self-regulation and control, and autonomy. The BIPSI is a valid and reliable scale for assessing psychological well-being in adolescents. It was found to be useful in educational and clinical settings for measuring young people's emotional and social development [34].

Adolescent problem-solving skills and their relationship to parenting styles

The Revised Social Problem-Solving Inventory was used to assess the adolescents'ability to solve problems in different contexts, and the Parenting Styles Scale was used to measures the type of parenting. There is a positive correlation between parenting styles and adolescents'problem-solving skills. Parenting styles play an important role in the formation of these skills, suggesting that parental training could be an effective intervention [35].

Design and validation of the means of resolving interpersonal conflicts (MERCI) scale for adolescents

The MERCI Scale was used to assess the tactics adolescents use to resolve conflicts. The MERCI scale is a valid instrument for measuring how adolescents handle interpersonal conflicts. Its use is effective in mediation and conflict resolution strategies in educational and social settings [36].

Definition of the concept

According to this analysis,"life skills that promote school health in adolescents"are a set of essential psychosocial competencies that enable adolescents to effectively face health-related challenges in school and social environments. These skills include decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem solving, facilitating the development of adaptive behaviours and the mitigation of health risks. The development and strengthening of these skills are determined by macro- and microlevel factors and by adolescents’ socioeconomic context, educational policies, access to resources, and prior experiences.

From a conceptual perspective, these skills have three fundamental dimensions:

Similarly, integrating health education processes with a preventative approach strengthens self-care, promotes safe school environments, and reduces risky behaviours. Joint work between the health and education sectors enables the design of effective interventions that impact student quality of life and promote more equitable and inclusive learning conditions, as evidenced by Rodríguez Melo et al. Discussion

The concept of"life skills that promote school health in adolescents” has been defined as a set of essential psychosocial competencies that enable adolescents to effectively face health-related challenges in school and social environments. Adolescence is a defining stage in human development. It is characterized by physical, emotional, and social changes that require strengthening psychosocial competencies to successfully face everyday challenges [9]. In this sense, life skills are fundamental tools that promote well-being, mental health, and social adaptation in adolescents. Their development in school settings fosters comprehensive education and contributes to preventing risky behaviours, promoting resilience, and constructing protective environments [22]. Supported by the literature, a discussion of the four attributes—decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem solving—follows, highlighting their relevance in developing healthy and autonomous adolescents.

The first attribute is decision-making, which has emerged as an essential skill that enables adolescents to evaluate options and choose the best alternative in different contexts, fostering their autonomy and responsibility. This finding is consistent with that reported by Blankenstein et al. [38], who highlighted that independent decision-making is an increasing challenge for adolescents, which is partially attributed to normal maturational changes in brain function. Similarly, Scholz et al. [39] reported that adolescents make more impulsive and less rational decisions than adults do because of differences in brain maturation, especially in areas related to impulse control and risk assessment. On the other hand, Van-Duijvenvoorde & Crone [40] conducted a longitudinal study that revealed that improved decision-making skills during adolescence are associated with a decrease in risky behaviours related to substance use and unprotected sexual conduct.

The second attribute is effective communication, which is fundamental to adolescent development because it allows adolescents to understand others and express their ideas and emotions clearly, facilitating healthy social interactions and strong interpersonal relationships. This result is consistent with the findings of Piccerillo and Digennaro [41], who reported that adolescents with effective communication skills have greater self-esteem and healthier interpersonal relationships. Similarly, Jiménez et al. [42] reported that teaching communication skills in school programs improved empathy and conflict resolution among students, promoting a more positive school environment.

Stress management, the third attribute, enables adolescents to manage pressure and emotions in difficult situations through strategies such as meditation, time management, and resilience. This finding is in line with that of Razza et al. [43], who found that regular mindfulness practice by adolescents significantly reduces stress and anxiety levels. Similarly, Smith et al. [44] reported that school programs that teach stress management techniques, such as deep breathing and cognitive restructuring, are effective in improving student resilience.

Problem solving, the fourth attribute, is the ability to analyse a situation, identify challenges, and find effective solutions, enabling adolescents to constructively address conflicts. This finding is consistent with that of Mondal & Mallick [45], who reported that adolescents with problem-solving skills have a greater ability to cope with stressful situations and make informed decisions. Saiz [46] highlighted that effective problem solving is associated with a lower incidence of aggressive behaviour and better social adaptation among adolescents.

Although this study is based on a solid theoretical foundation, significant gaps remain in the empirical literature. There is little evidence on the effectiveness of school programs that develop skills such as decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem-solving. This limitation highlights the need for studies that evaluate the implementation and outcomes of these competencies in real-life school settings.

The findings allow for proposals for the design of school programs. For example, it is suggested that these skills be integrated into curricular activities using active methodologies. However, it would be important to explore this in future research and in specific sociocultural contexts.

Implications for nursing practice, teachers and school health professionals

Nursing professionals who read and apply the findings of this article, especially those working in schools, are provided a solid conceptual foundation on life skills as a key strategy for promoting adolescent health. Understanding the essential attributes of these skills, such as decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem solving, enables nursing professionals and school staff to identify needs in the student population and design more relevant and effective educational interventions. This analysis provides theoretical and practical tools for structuring health education programs focused on risk prevention and strengthening adolescents'psychosocial development. Furthermore, the knowledge acquired facilitates interdisciplinary collaboration with teachers and counsellors from different disciplines, fostering a comprehensive approach to youth well-being. From a health care and leadership perspective, this article enables nursing professionals to act as agents of change within the school environment, promoting safe, empathetic, and resilient spaces. Furthermore, incorporating life skills approaches into school health policies and institutional student wellness programs strengthens the role of evidence-based decision-making in schools.

In conclusion, these results have clear practical implications that can be applied by teachers and school health professionals to strengthen students'social-emotional development.

Limitations

These limitations are related, first, to the variability of the socioeconomic and cultural contexts of educational systems. This is because the literature review conducted to deepen the philosophical understanding and conceptual clarity of life skills that promote school health in adolescents did not reveal this result. Additionally, public policies may require further analysis based on the influences of the concept. Second, this article addressed only research studies in Spanish and English and there is limited evidence linking life skills with school health promotion. Third, future research is recommended to empirically validate the attributes of the concept and its relationships with school health.

Conclusion

This article represents a useful tool for health and education professionals, offering them a clear and well-founded understanding of the concept of"life skills that promote school health in adolescents."Its usefulness lies in providing a theoretical framework that guides the planning of effective interventions focused on strengthening psychosocial competencies during adolescence. It also provides evidence-based support for the role of life skills in promoting well-being, mitigating risk, and building protective school environments.

The analysis of the concept allowed the identification of essential attributes such as decision-making, effective communication, stress management, and problem solving. These skills contribute to reducing risky behaviours, strengthening resilience, and promoting the overall well-being of adolescents in the school environment. Using the Walker and Avant method, a conceptual foundation was consolidated to guide both the research and the design of educational programs and school health interventions. Promoting these skills is essential for providing young people with tools that enable them to face life challenges in an informed, autonomous, and healthy manner.

Life skills that promote adolescent school health are defined as a set of essential psychosocial competencies that enable adolescents to effectively address health-related challenges in school and social environments. Future research can delve deeper into the social context and develop longitudinal and comparative studies that relate life skills with educational and health indicators.

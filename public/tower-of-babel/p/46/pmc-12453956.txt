Frequency of Antihypertensive Drug Classes and Single‐Pill Combinations in Obese Patients: An 8‐Year Retrospective Study
Authors: Beata Moczulska, Karolina Osowiecka, Leszek Gromadziński, Marta Majewska
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObesity is a major contributor to the development and progression of hypertension, and its coexistence significantly increases cardiovascular risk. Although numerous guidelines exist for the management of arterial hypertension, none are dedicated specifically to obese patients, despite their increasing prevalence. Since 2018, both European and Polish guidelines have recommended the use of single‐pill combinations (SPCs) at every stage of treatment. This retrospective study aimed to evaluate antihypertensive treatment patterns over the past eight years among obese patients. This analysis was restricted to obese patients with hypertension to explore prescribing patterns in a subgroup with unique pathophysiological features and high cardiovascular risk, for which specific therapeutic recommendations remain limited. The study cohort was limited to obese patients hospitalized for preoperative assessment prior to planned bariatric surgery. We analyzed medical records of 233 obese hypertensive patients divided into two cohorts: those diagnosed before 2020 and those diagnosed in 2020 or later. We observed a significant increase in the use of three or more antihypertensive drugs and a rise in the prescription of beta‐blockers and angiotensin receptor blockers after 2020. Despite these changes, the use of SPCs remained low: two‐drug SPCs were used in 35.6% of patients, and three‐drug SPCs in only 11.2%, with no significant increase in their use over time. Additionally, SGLT2 inhibitors were introduced into therapy after 2020. Our findings highlight the discrepancy between clinical guidelines and real‐world prescribing habits. Improved adherence to treatment recommendations may enhance therapeutic outcomes and medication adherence in this high‐risk group.

Introduction

Hypertension and obesity are among the most significant contributors to global cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. In 2021, more than 2 billion individuals worldwide were classified as overweight or obese [1]. Nearly 1.3 billion people suffer from hypertension globally, yet fewer than 50% are properly diagnosed and treated [2]. A positive association between increased body weight and the development of arterial hypertension has been well established in numerous epidemiological studies [3, 4, 5].

It is now well recognized that obesity not only elevates the risk of developing hypertension but also affects the pharmacological response to antihypertensive medications. However, there are currently no specific guidelines for the treatment of hypertension in obese patients; the same antihypertensive drug classes are used as in the general population. The management of this patient group remains challenging and typically requires multidrug regimens.

Since 2018, clinical guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC), the European Society of Hypertension (ESH), and the Polish Society of Hypertension (PSH) have recommended the use of fixed‐dose combination therapy at all stages of antihypertensive treatment [6, 7]. These recommendations were reaffirmed in the 2023 (ESH) and 2024 (PSH) updates. According to current guidelines, initial combination treatment with single‐pill combinations (SPCs) is recommended from the outset in most patients, whenever possible. Exceptions include patients in poor general condition or with frailty syndrome, in whom therapy should be tailored individually [8, 9].

This study aimed to review antihypertensive treatment patterns in obese patients with arterial hypertension over the past 8 years and to assess the extent to which current therapeutic practices align with contemporary clinical guidelines. Given the growing burden of obesity and its specific pathophysiological impact on hypertension management, we focused exclusively on this high‐risk group.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

This retrospective observational study included 233 obese patients (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m2) with previously diagnosed arterial hypertension. All patients were hospitalized in the Department of Cardiology and Internal Medicine, University Clinical Hospital in Olsztyn, between 2017 and 2025 for a comprehensive preoperative internal medicine evaluation prior to planned bariatric surgery. Eligibility criteria included pharmacologically treated hypertension. Arterial hypertension was confirmed based on a documented diagnosis in hospital records and a history of at least three months of continuous pharmacological treatment. The study cohort was inherently limited to individuals with obesity, all of whom met the diagnostic criteria for arterial hypertension. Patients with recent infections, fever, active malignancy, liver disease, lung disease, or heart failure were excluded from the analysis. Each participant underwent a detailed medical interview, blood pressure measurement, and laboratory testing, with a focus on current antihypertensive therapy.

Study Design

Patients were divided into two subgroups according to the year of hospitalization. Group 1: patients hospitalized before 2020, and Group 2: patients hospitalized in 2020 or later. The frequency of use of various antihypertensive drug classes was analyzed in both groups. The following therapeutic classes were assessed: β‐blockers (Bbs); Angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs); Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs); Calcium channel blockers (CCBs); Diuretics: thiazide and thiazide‐like diuretics (TDs), loop diuretics (LDs); Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs); Other antihypertensive agents, including centrally acting agents and α‐blockers (ABs). Additionally, the use of single‐pill combinations (SPCs) was recorded and compared between the two groups. Antihypertensive treatment was confirmed based on the patient's medical history and current prescriptions.

Body Mass Index (BMI)

BMI was calculated using the Quetelet index formula: weight (kg) divided by height squared (m2). Obesity was defined according to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification [10].

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data, including median values, interquartile ranges (IQRs), and percentages. The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to assess the normality of distribution for continuous variables. Differences in age and body mass index (BMI) between the two groups were evaluated using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi‐square (χ 2) test. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using Statistica software, version 13 (StatSoft, Poland; https://www.statsoft.pl).

Results

The study included 233 obese patients (BMI ≥30 kg/m2) with arterial hypertension. The patients were aged between 20 and 78 years, with a median age of 52 years. The study population consisted of 130 men (55.8%) and 103 women (44.2%). One hundred sixteen patients were hospitalized before 2020 (Group 1), and 117 were hospitalized in 2020 or later (Group 2).

Women accounted for 47.4% of Group 1 and 41.0% of Group 2. The proportion of smokers significantly decreased from 34.5% in Group 1 to 22.2% in Group 2 (p = 0.038). Patients in Group 2 were significantly older than those in Group 1 (p < 0.001; Table 1). Median systolic blood pressure (SBP) at admission was 144 mmHg (IQR 130–160) in the total cohort, with no statistically significant difference between patients admitted before 2020 and those admitted in or after 2020 (146 mmHg vs. 143 mmHg, p = 0.297). Median diastolic blood pressure (DBP) was 90 mmHg (IQR 80–99) in both groups, with no significant difference over time (p = 0.690).

The most commonly used antihypertensive drug class in the entire study population was β‐blockers (Bbs), prescribed in 55.8% of patients. Their use significantly increased from 46.6% before 2020 to 65.0% after 2020 (p = 0.005). Angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs) were used in 50.6% of patients overall, with a non‐significant decrease in use after 2020 (p = 0.265). In contrast, the use of angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) increased from 23.3% to 35.0% (p = 0.050), and the use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) rose from 10.3% to 21.4% (p = 0.021). Additionally, α‐blockers (ABs) were more frequently prescribed before 2020 (28.4%) compared to after (17.1%) (p = 0.039). No significant changes were observed in the use of calcium channel blockers (CCBs), thiazide diuretics (TDs), or loop diuretics (LDs) between the two periods (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, we presented an eight‐year overview of antihypertensive treatment in obese patients, starting from 2017, the year preceding the publication of the 2018 ESC/ESH guidelines recommending fixed‐dose combination therapy. We aimed to assess how treatment strategies evolved in the subsequent years. Notably, there are currently no specific clinical guidelines for the management of hypertension in obese patients.

In Poland, between 2019 and 2021, nearly 11 million people were affected by hypertension, yet only 23%–33% achieved adequate blood pressure control [9]. According to the ESC cardiovascular disease statistics, the prevalence of hypertension in Poland remains among the highest in Europe [11]. Importantly, the study period also encompassed the COVID‐19 pandemic, beginning in 2020, which has been shown to negatively impact hypertension control [12, 13].

Despite the introduction of clear recommendations, our findings indicate that the use of fixed‐dose dual or triple single‐pill combinations (SPCs) did not significantly increase after 2018. Although the majority of patients in both time periods were treated with at least three antihypertensive agents, the overall uptake of SPCs remained suboptimal. Surprisingly, β‐blockers (Bbs) were the most frequently prescribed drug class before 2020, despite being excluded from first‐line treatment in the 2018 ESC/ESH guidelines unless specific cardiovascular indications were present. Bbs use further increased after 2020. This trend is consistent with the findings of Dobrowolski and Prejbisz, who evaluated antihypertensive treatment patterns in the Polish population between 2019 and 2021 [14]. Among American patients, Bbs were the second most commonly used drug class in newly treated hypertensive individuals, although their use declined between 2013 and 2021 [15]. However, in other countries such as Australia, Japan, China, and Saudi Arabia, Bbs are prescribed less frequently, with usage rates ranging from 10% to 26% [16, 17, 18, 19].

Monotherapy was used in only a small percentage of patients across the entire study period (ranging from 17% to 10%). In contrast, Dobrowolski et al. reported that the rate of monotherapy in Poland in 2019 was as high as 41% [14]. Similarly, the NHANES study conducted in the United States between 2013 and 2016 showed monotherapy rates around 40% [20]. In our study, among those receiving monotherapy, ACEIs were most commonly used before 2020 (50%), followed by CCBs. After 2020, the use of ARBs significantly increased and became one of the dominant monotherapies alongside Bbs.

Overall, 46.8% of our population used either dual or triple SPCs, although triple SPCs were used in only 11% of patients. While the frequency of triple SPCs increased slightly over time, it remains unsatisfactory. Polish data from 2021 show that 30% of patients on antihypertensive therapy were using dual SPCs, whereas only 3% were using triple SPCs. Among patients taking three or more drugs, 20% used triple SPCs [9].

The START study demonstrated that SPC therapy is superior to multiple‐pill regimens, resulting in reduced all‐cause mortality and fewer cardiovascular events [21]. Furthermore, a meta‐analysis of multiple studies has shown that patients treated with SPCs exhibit better adherence and achieve lower blood pressure compared to those receiving the same treatment as separate pills [22, 23].

The observed increase in the use of β‐blockers (Bbs) after 2020 is noteworthy, especially considering that earlier ESC/ESH guidelines [6] recommended limiting BBs use to patients with specific cardiovascular comorbidities. However, the 2023 ESH update [8] reintroduced Bbs as a first‐line option, which may partially explain this trend. Similarly, the increased use of ARBs and MRAs may reflect growing awareness of their beneficial effects in patients with obesity, metabolic syndrome, and cardiorenal complications. Of particular note is the emergence of SGLT2 inhibitors (flozins) in our population after 2020. Prior to that, none of the patients had been prescribed flozins, while in the period after 2020, 18.8% received them (p < 0.001). The marked increase in the use of SGLT2 inhibitors after 2020 is consistent with their growing recognition as cardio‐ and nephroprotective agents. Although these medications are not yet formally approved for hypertension treatment, their use is justified in patients with coexisting heart failure, diabetes mellitus, or chronic kidney disease. This pattern of use aligns with the expanded indications introduced in the 2021 and 2022 ADA/EASD and KDIGO guidelines [24, 25], as well as the 2023 ESH guidelines that acknowledge their value in complex hypertensive patients with comorbid conditions [8].

The observed increase in beta‐blocker use after 2020 in our obese hypertensive cohort, despite their less favorable metabolic profile, may be explained by several factors. First, the 2023 ESH guidelines reinstated beta‐blockers among first‐line treatment options for hypertension. Second, obese patients often present with cardiovascular comorbidities—such as ischemic heart disease, tachyarrhythmias, and obstructive sleep apnea—where beta‐blockers are either indicated or advantageous. Third, the pathophysiology of obesity‐related hypertension is complex and includes increased sympathetic nervous system activation, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system overactivity, elevated hemodynamic load, and electrolyte disturbances, all of which may favor beta‐blocker therapy. Furthermore, recent evidence suggests that concerns over their metabolic impact may be overstated. Comprehensive reviews [24, 26] highlight that beta‐blockers are as effective as other drug classes in lowering BP, have robust evidence for cardiovascular risk reduction, and are safe in conditions once considered relative contraindications, thereby supporting their continued and possibly expanded role in specific patient subgroups.

Our results align in part with the findings of Cataldi et al. [25], who, in a cohort of obese hypertensive patients from Southern Italy, reported a highly heterogeneous pharmacological approach with no evidence of a preferred first‐line strategy, and frequent prescription of β‐blockers and diuretics despite potential adverse metabolic effects. Similar to our data, their analysis showed that drug choice was not influenced by obesity severity, visceral fat accumulation, or the presence of metabolic syndrome. Both studies suggest that, in real‐world practice, therapeutic decisions in obesity‐related hypertension may not fully reflect available pathophysiological insights or concerns raised in guidelines about the metabolic safety of certain agents.

The significant increase in comorbidities such as coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and hyperuricemia observed in patients treated after 2020 likely reflects both the aging of the hypertensive population and the impact of the COVID‐19 pandemic on overall cardiometabolic health [27]. Reduced physical activity, delayed medical consultations, and changes in lifestyle during lockdowns may have contributed to the deterioration of cardiovascular and metabolic profiles in this group. This is in line with observations from the ESH ABPM COVID‐19 study [28] showing a significant decline in BP control, and a 2024 cohort from Iran reporting post‐COVID increases of ∼10 mmHg systolic and ∼6 mmHg diastolic (p < 0.001).

Interestingly, despite guideline emphasis on the early use of calcium channel blockers (CCBs) and thiazide diuretics (TDs), their prescribing rates remained relatively stable over time. The decline in α‐blocker (AB) use after 2020 may be due to their limited role in hypertension management outside of specific indications such as prostatic hyperplasia.

These trends suggest that while certain prescribing practices have evolved in line with updated recommendations, others remain relatively unchanged‐highlighting the ongoing gap between evidence‐based guidelines and real‐world clinical behavior.

Conclusions

Despite the availability of clear and consistent European and national guidelines recommending the use of single‐pill combinations in the treatment of arterial hypertension, real‐world clinical practice often fails to align with these recommendations. A substantial proportion of obese hypertensive patients continue to receive multiple separate antihypertensive agents rather than fixed‐dose combinations, even when receiving three or more drugs. This prescribing pattern increases the pill burden and may negatively affect patient adherence and long‐term blood pressure control. Our findings emphasize the need for continued educational efforts targeting physicians, as well as systemic strategies to promote broader implementation of SPCs—especially in high‐risk groups such as obese patients. Increasing the use of evidence‐based, guideline‐concordant therapies may improve treatment efficacy, enhance patient compliance, and ultimately reduce cardiovascular risk in this vulnerable population.

Limitations

This study was limited to patients with obesity, which may affect the generalizability of the findings to the broader hypertensive population. Given the lack of specific antihypertensive treatment guidelines for obese individuals, this subgroup can be considered representative of a significant and clinically relevant portion of real‐world hypertensive patients. However, the relatively small sample size may not fully reflect the diversity of hypertensive obese patients in the general Polish population. The study cohort was limited to obese patients hospitalized for preoperative assessment prior to planned bariatric surgery, which precluded direct comparison with non‐obese hypertensive individuals. Future studies involving a more diverse population could help confirm whether the observed treatment patterns extend beyond the obese population.

Efficient image steganography method using contourlet transform and geometric-based pixel encryption for enhanced security
Authors: Rajesh Kumar, Sunita Singhal, Vijay Kumar Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent years, the transmission of multimedia content has become an essential aspect of information security. Information security can be divided into two essential components: content hiding and cryptography. For secure communication, both content hiding and cryptography are essential. In the proposed research, the combination of steganography with image pixel value encryption techniques has been demonstrated to enhance security. Before the embedding operation, the secret images are encrypted to improve the confidentiality of the hidden image. Encryption operation is performed using the Geometric Mean that includes “Arithmetic Progression (AP), Harmonic Progression (HP) and Geometric Progression (GP)” applied to “Secret Image”. Embedding is then carried out using the Contourlet Transform (CT) to ensure better visual quality of the stego image. The presented approach utilises a Laplacian Pyramidal Directional Filter Bank (LPDFB). Laplacian pyramids capture contours from the image and craft a linear structure using a Directional Filter Bank (DFB). Experimental results confirm that the proposed double-layer encryption-based steganography technique functions as a robust image embedding scheme, providing high imperceptibility while preserving the integrity of the secret image under various attacks, similar to a robust watermarking approach. Several types of security outbreaks, including noise tampering, compression-based tampering, rotational alterations, and cropping-based alterations, were performed on stego images and recovered extracted secret images. The average experimental results of the proposed technique achieved 45.5 dB (decibels) Peak to Signal Noise Ratio (PSNR), 0.9837 Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), 0.0020 Bit Error Rate (BER) and 0.9951 Normalized Correlation Coefficient (NCC), which is better than existing methods. The generated high-quality stego image is secured against various steganalysis tampered conditions.Keywords: Contourlet Transform, Geometric Mean, Directional Filter Bank, Multiscale Decomposition, Cyber bulliesSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Network communication is widely used nowadays due to its low cost and high availability. However, data tampering and modification remain major concerns in the era of information security. Digital Steganography is a better option to provide security. It means concealing information behind any cover media1. The cover media can be any image, text, audio, or any video files. Out of the four above stated, images are widely used for secret and secure communication2.

Using image steganographic techniques, the objectives of protecting images in network communication can be achieved successfully. A Secret image can be hidden behind a cover image. Embedding the secret image behind the cover image, called “Stego image.” Steganographic methods can generally be categorised into spatial and transform domains. The embedding procedure is used to hide secret information3. Spatial domain performs alteration in pixel values of the cover image, whereas the transform domain method performs embedding using the coefficient values of the cover image4 similar to “Discrete Fourier Domain (DFT), Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT), Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), and Contourlet Transform”5–7,9.

Some of the steganographic methods achieve excellent imperceptibility and payload capacity for the stego image. While other methods are secure against various steganalysis attacks. Although there is a trade-off between Imperceptibility and Security. The main aspects of stenography are maintaining a balance between robustness and good imperceptibility for the secure transmission of the image8.

In the current research, efficient image steganographic methods using two-way security have been proposed. It performs an encryption operation on pixels to generate an encrypted image. Furthermore, to ensure good imperceptibility, Embedding using Contourlet Transform (CT) has been performed for a better stego image9. The proposed approach is robust against various security-tampering conditions while maintaining high imperceptibility, and since the extracted secret image remains recognisable even after multiple attacks, the method exhibits the characteristics of a robust scheme, aligning more closely with robust watermarking than with fragile steganography.

Performance evaluations and robustness of various stego images have been analysed here with some standard image datasets (USC SIPI). In Sect. 3, the obtained results of image steganography have been compared with existing methods. The following is a summary of this research main contributions:

Preliminary fundamentals

In this section, some mathematical concepts and frameworks have been explored, which are utilized while designing the proposed dual-layer image steganographic system. Prior to explaining the specifics of the proposed method, a fundamental overview of the encryption operation and decomposition of the contourlet transform is given.

Contourlet transform

The Contourlet Transform (CT) is a technique for representing two-dimensional images, allowing decomposition in multiple directions and at various scales. So the contourlet transform has multiscale and multi-directional properties43,44. Decomposition structure and directional filtering process of the CT are illustrated in Fig. 1. This transform is an extended version of the wavelet transform. In the wavelet transform, Image is decomposed into 4 sub-bands: LL, HH, LH and HL orientations. Whereas CT is capable of decomposing in multiple sub-bands due to its multi-directional nature, Contourlet transforms Laplacian pyramid with Directional Filter Bank (LPDFB).

The diagram of the Contourlet Transform46.

In the contourlet transformation, multiscale decomposition is first performed using the Laplacian pyramid, resulting in a series of bandpass images. A Directional Filter Bank (DFB) is used to capture high-frequency regions for the input image45. The DFB has a good performance in image reconstruction. Decomposition is performed for each scale with respect to the two-channel Quincunx Filter Bank for each node of the given structure. Reasons for the selection of the contourlet transform are due to its multiresolution, localization, multi-directionality, and anisotropy features can be used47,48.

Generation of encrypted image using Geometric mean

This Algorithm accepts a secret image as its input. Image encryption operation for the input image using the Geometric mean (AP, GP, and HP) has been performed first. In this operation, three lists have been created, considered as sub-matrices. It means that the image can be decomposed into three sub-metrics.

The Input denoted as INPUT_S is further divided into three lists (L1, L2 and L3). After generating the list using the GENERATE_LIST functions, Arithmetic progressions are used to modify pixel values, such as a linear gradient. Geometric and harmonic progression is used to decay in brightness or intensity of colours. HP will perform the reciprocals of pixel values via inversely modifying the pixel values.

Motivation for Dividing the Image into Three Sub-Matrices (L1, L2 and L3).

Separating the pixel values of the image into L1, L2, and L3 provides two primary advantages.

Enhanced Security via Statistical Confusion

Applying three different nonlinear mappings, Geometric Progression (GP), Arithmetic Progression (AP), and Harmonic Progression (HP) to dividing the pixels into three groups changes the image’s histogram and breaks the natural relationships between nearby pixels. These heterogeneous patterns prevent predictability and increase resistance to statistical attacks, steganalysis, and intensity-based cryptanalysis.

Improved Encryption Stability and Reconstruction Accuracy

Each mapping function is chosen to match the numerical behaviour of its region:

L1 uses GP, which safely compresses mid-level intensities.

L2 uses AP, which shifts high intensities into a separate list for preventing overflow.

L3 (0–32) uses HP, which stabilizes reciprocal operations for small values.

Thus, list generation follows predefined mathematical rules rather than a randomization process. Therefore, the pixel partitioning into three deterministic sub-lists is an intentional design choice that improves cryptographic diffusion and encryption performance of the method.

Conclusion

In this research, a novel approach is introduced for pixel value image encryption utilizing Geometric Mean (GM) in combination with the Contourlet Transform to perform the embedding of the encrypted image. Contourlet Transform has multiscale and multi-directional properties. Thus, embedding of secret bits is carried out by high-frequency coefficients to enhance the structural similarity index of Stego Image and Cover Image. The key contributions of the proposed method are as follows: By using the Contourlet Transform, high-frequency coefficients are effectively selected, allowing the generated stego images to closely resemble the original cover images. As a result, both images appear nearly identical. Moreover, the method exhibits robustness similar to robust watermarking system, enabling the extraction of secret images under several steganalysis attacks, including noise, compression, rotation, and cropping. Overall, the combined use of encryption and steganography enhances security and provides better protection than existing approaches. Evaluated metrics such as PSNR, SSIM and BER confirm that the proposed method achieves better results for stego images compared to related existing algorithms. However, the method was tested only on grayscale 512 × 512 BMP images, and its applicability to colour or high-resolution images remains unexplored. Additionally, at the receiver side, if decryption is performed before the extraction of the encrypted image, it may lead to data loss or errors during the extraction process. These limitations indicate opportunities for future enhancement. The visual quality of stego images may be further improved by optimizing embedding strategies, while incorporating advanced encryption techniques can strengthen overall security. Given its efficiency and robustness, the proposed algorithm further exhibits significant promise for deployment in real-time covert communication scenarios.

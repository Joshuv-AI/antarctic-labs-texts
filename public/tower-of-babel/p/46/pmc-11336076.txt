Utilizing radiomics for differential diagnosis of inverted papilloma and chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps based on unenhanced CT scans
Authors: Shaojuan He, Yuxuan Zhao, Lei Shi, Xiaorong Yang, Xuehai Wang, Yang Luo, Mingming Wang, Xianxing Zhang, Xuezhong Li, Dexin Yu, Xin Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate whether radiomics models based on unenhanced paranasal sinuses CT images could be a useful tool for differentiating inverted papilloma (IP) from chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps (CRSwNP). This retrospective study recruited 240 patients with CRSwNP and 106 patients with IP from three centers. 253 patients from Qilu Hospital were randomly divided into the training set (n = 151) and the internal validation set (n = 102) with a ratio of 6:4. 93 patients from the other two centers were used as the external validation set. The patients with the unilateral disease (n = 115) from Qilu Hospital were selected to further develop a subgroup analysis. Lesion segmentation was manually delineated in CT images. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator algorithm was performed for feature reduction and selection. Decision tree, support vector machine, random forest, and adaptive boosting regressor were employed to establish the differential diagnosis models. 43 radiomic features were selected for modeling. Among the models, RF achieved the best results, with an AUC of 0.998, 0.943, and 0.934 in the training set, the internal validation set, and the external validation set, respectively. In the subgroup analysis, RF achieved an AUC of 0.999 in the training set and 0.963 in the internal validation set. The proposed radiomics models offered a non-invasion and accurate differential approach between IP and CRSwNP and has some significance in guiding clinicians determining the best treatment plans, as well as predicting the prognosis.Keywords: Inverted papilloma, Chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps, Differential diagnosis, RadiomicsSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Oncology

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is a chronic inflammatory condition of the nose and paranasal sinuses with a prevalence of 5–12% of the general population and is usually accompanied by nasal polyps1,2. The common treatment choice for CRS with polyps (CRSwNP) is continued appropriate medical therapy combined with endoscopic sinus surgery if pharmacotherapy fails3. When it comes to the differential diagnosis of CRSwNP, the most frequently encountered disorder is inverted papilloma (IP)4, which is a benign tumor of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses characterized by a locally aggressive growth pattern, the propensity to recur, and the potential for malignant transformation5. Moreover, the rate of malignant transformation is as high as 6–15%6,7. Compared with CRSwNP, the treatment of IP requires extensive removal of mucosa and drilling of the underlying bone8. Evidently, the treatment strategies as well as the prognosis of these two complaints are significantly different, therefore, if the two diseases can be precisely differentiated preoperatively so that precise treatment can be carried out, the prognosis of the patients will be effectively improved.

Nasal endoscopic examination or surgical excision for pathologic diagnosis is the most accurate method of identifying these two diseases, while both of them are invasive tests. Sometimes preoperative biopsy may be taken to exclude tumors. However, because of the surrounding inflammation, swelling mucosa or the forceps that change the natural structure for biopsy, the procedure can be unprecise4. The false-negative rate for biopsies of nasal polyps is statistically as high as 17%4,9. The pre-operation computed tomography (CT) scans of the paranasal sinuses are non-invasive and usually required, but these two diseases share many overlapping imaging features on conventional CT images, and it is often difficult to differentiate them based on imaging features alone by physicians10. Moreover, human visual assessment of lesions is subjective and variable depending on the examiners’ experience11.

Radiomics, a newly emerging research approach, uses an automated high-throughput extraction of large amounts of quantitative features including size, shape, and complex patterns from non-invasive medical imaging at the macroscopic scale such as CT, positron emission tomography (PET), and magnetic resonance (MR) to establish a diagnostic, prognostic, or predictive model12–14. To date, radiomics has obtained notable success in various fields, especially in cancer15,16. Zhao et al. used the radiomics technology preoperatively differentiated pulmonary cryptococcosis and lung adenocarcinoma based on the solitary pulmonary solid nodule with the AUC of 0.91 and 0.89 in the training and testing cohorts17. The radiomics analysis was also applied to predicting the efficacy of anti-HER2 therapy for patients with liver metastases from breast cancer18. Based on the radiomics, a novel multiparametric MRI radiomic nomogram for preoperative evaluation of early recurrence in resectable pancreatic cancer was developed19. However, to the best of our knowledge, no study focuses on non-invasively distinguishing CRSwNP and IP based on unenhanced CT images using radiomics. Therefore, the aim of this study was to explore the feasibility of CT-based radiomics analysis for differentiating CRSwNP and IP, which will provide a new fast, non-invasive, and accurate method for distinguishing these two diseases.

Results

Characteristics of the Patients

Overall, 443 patients were recruited in this study. We excluded 91 patients without CT data, and 6 patients lacking complete clinical data. Further details can be found in Fig. 1. Finally, 253 patients formed the training set and internal validation set, including 177 patients with CRSwNP (mean [SD] age, 43.49 [14.73] years; 55 females [31.07%]) and 76 patients with IP (mean [SD] age, 54.07 [11.94] years; 22 females [28.95%]). 93 patients were used as the external independent validation set, including 63 patients with CRSwNP (mean [SD] age, 50.44 [10.95] years; 17 females [26.98%]) and 30 patients with IP (mean [SD] age, 53.33 [11.45] years; 12 females [40.00%]). Radiomic features extraction and selection

In the present study, 1409 radiomic features were extracted from preoperative paranasal sinuses CT images from the training set. The intra-observer reliability analysis identified radiomics features with ICCs of > 0.75. The LASSO algorithm (Fig. 2A,B) was used to assess the correlations between each pair of radiomic features and reduce the dimensionality of high-dimensional features, and the optimal discriminative features were screened. Finally, the final optimal discriminative feature set was comprised of 9 first-order statistical features, 11 Gy-level run-length matrix (GLRLM), 20 grey-level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, 1 neighborhood gray-tone difference matrix feature, and 2 shape features. Based on these radiomic features and their regression coefficients, the Rad-score formula was constructed as follows: Rad-score = feature * coefficient. Subgroup analysis

For the wide applicability of the differential models, we developed the models regardless of whether the diseases were unilateral or bilateral. However, IP is typically unilateral while most of CRSwNP is bilateral, which may magnify the performance of the algorithm to some extent. To further confirm the performance of our differential approach, a subgroup analysis was conducted by selecting the patients with unilateral lesions. Compared with the results in Table 2, the four classifiers in the subgroup analysis achieved more excellent results (Table 3 and Fig. 4). Among these models, RF achieved an AUC of 0.999 (95% CI 0.997–1) in the training set and 0.963 (95% CI 0.914–1) in the internal validation set. Discussion

This study developed and validated radiomics models that preoperatively differentiated inverted papilloma from chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps based on unenhanced CT images. It has been shown that RF classifier exhibited higher AUC and accuracy than other classifiers in the validation sets.

Recently, Girdler et al. developed a convolution neural network (CNN) model based on nasal endoscopic images to classify IP, CRS, and normal findings which achieved an overall accuracy of 0.74211, but the method was invasive. Woodruff et al. demonstrated that the CT findings of IP were variable and non-specific, when it related to the appearance of benign bony changes, the possibility of potential IP would be considered20. However, not all cases with IP have bony architectural changes. In clinical practice, although contrast-enhanced CT is also helpful in distinguishing these two diseases, there are some risks induced by contrast agents such as non-specific blood pool distribution, potential renal toxicity, and adverse events21, what’s more, using contrast agents may induce increased radiation22. According to MRI findings, IP demonstrated a slightly hyperintense heterogeneous appearance on T1-weighted imaging and intermediate signal intensity on T2-weighted imaging23, while not all cases underwent MRI and the expense was higher than that of CT. Hence, a non-invasive, more convenient, and economical technique to distinguish these two diseases is needed.

Our study is the first attempt to differentiate between CRSwNP and IP using radiomics based on unenhanced paranasal sinuses CT images which achieved promising differential performance. Compared with sinonasal endoscopy, contrast-enhanced CT, and MRI, an unenhanced paranasal sinuses CT scan is convenient and facilitative, furthermore, the cost and risk are lower. Therefore, our proposed radiomics models based on unenhanced paranasal sinuses CT images will be a useful tool for differentiating IP from CRSwNP and can be widely applied in clinical tasks.

In this multicenter study, four commonly used classifier models (DT, SVM, RF, AdaBoost) were applied to evaluate the performance for discrimination. Impressively, almost all classifiers demonstrated outstanding performance in distinguishing IP from CRSwNP with satisfactory accuracy, among which RF obtained the best results with an AUC of 0.998 (95% CI 0.996–1) in the training set, 0.943 (95% CI 0.903–0.984) in the internal validation set, and 0.934 (95% CI 0.879–0.990) in the external validation set which may be the most optimum model for clinical use. The outstanding performance of RF has also been reported in various other diseases. For example, diagnosis of malaria24, prediction of sepsis after liver transplantation25, and classification of neuroimaging26. Among these diseases, the RF model exhibits optimal performance. As an ensemble machine learning model, RF improves its classification by aggregating multiple (usually hundreds) decision trees using bagging methods. It has the advantages of fast training speed with large samples, small model variance, and strong generalization ability27. Specifically, due to the bagged nature of RF’s stronger resistance to noisy samples and observations, it is more stable than other models in distinguishing IP from CRSwNP.

Furthermore, the promising distinguished ability between IP and CRSwNP of our processed radiomics models was not only shown in the training set and internal validation set, but also in the external validation set which reflected the good generalization of this study. However, since IP is mainly unilateral while CRSwNP is mostly bilateral, the promising performance of our radiomics models had the potential of being exaggerated, as we developed the models regardless the lesions were unilateral or bilateral. Importantly, to confirm the accurate differential ability of our proposed differential approach, we further selected the unilateral lesions of both CRSwNP and IP for a subgroup analysis, which also achieved outstanding performance. Additionally, as a comparison, a senior expert's differential diagnosis results were added with an accuracy of 86.167% by using the data set 1. Consequently, our proposed approach can be generally used as a differential tool for distinguishing IP from CRSwNP based on unenhanced CT images no matter whether the lesion is unilateral or bilateral, which can effectively save time and effort in clinical tasks. Moreover, it can also be used as an aid to expert diagnosis.

In total, 43 features from the CT images were extracted, consisting of 9 first-order statistical features, 11 GLRLM features, 20 GLSZM features, 1 neighborhood gray-tone difference matrix feature, and 2 shape features. These findings accentuated the significance of comprehensive features of the lesion region and references to the microscopic and macroscopic features of the lesion. Additionally, the bulk of these features cannot be observed and quantified by human naked eye, which emphasizes the privilege of using automated methodology and extraction of high-throughput radiomics features to aid radiologists and clinicians in disease diagnosis and clinical decision making. However, rather than creating “a black box”28, it is significantly needed for future practice to focus on “explainable AI”29 to explain the implication of radiomics features and the biological behavior behind them17.

Presently, texture analysis has been widely applied in the diagnosis and prediction of various diseases, as it can reflect tissue characteristics, among which higher short run low gray level emphasis (SRLGLE) has attracted widespread attention30,31. SRLGLE is a feature used to indicate the distribution of long runs and the joint distribution of short runs and low gray-level values32. Our study found GLRLM-SRLGLE was the best-performing radiomics feature for differentiating IP from CRSwNP, which indicated the difference in histological characteristics of lesions in IP and CRSwNP. Additionally, our subgroup analysis demonstrated that the best feature for the differential diagnosis of IP and CRSwNP was the GLSZM-gray-level non-uniformity (GLNU), which was a measure of the similarity of gray-level values throughout the image33.

We attempt to elucidate the pathological characteristics reflected by these two radiomic features. CRSwNP is comprised of highly polypoid mucosa with edema34. The epithelium consists of pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium, induced by chronic external stimuli. Beneath the epithelium, there is edematous loose connective tissue infiltrated by inflammatory cells, with eosinophilic infiltration being a distinct histological feature of type 2 endotype. IP demonstrates typical histopathological features of multilayered epithelium invagination into the stroma35. Squamous epithelium is common, but there may also be transitional or columnar epithelium, or a combination of these cell types. The basement membrane is usually intact, and the stroma is richly vascularized with lymphocyte and plasma cell infiltration. Overall, CRSwNP exhibit more severe tissue edema with abundant inflammatory cells infiltration and a coarser texture, whereas IP have richer stromal vascularization with inverted squamous epithelium and a finer tissue texture. This is reflected in the radiomic features, where CRSwNP have lower GLRLM-SRLGLE values (Supplementary Table 3). Song et al. demonstrated that SRLGLE is an independent predictor for identifying lymph node infiltration status in colorectal cancer, with rougher lymph node texture and a significantly decreased SRLGLE value in N1-2 patients32. In contrast, the cellular arrangement and types differ, GLSZM-GLNU likely reflects the degree of inflammation and edema in the tissues. Our subgroup analysis revealed higher GLSZM-GLNU values in highly edematous and inflamed CRSwNP (Supplementary Table 4). This is consistent with the findings of Baessler et al. who observed higher GLNU values in acute heart failure myocarditis compared to chronic heart failure myocarditis, indicating a greater degree of myocardial edema and inflammatory response36. Parvaze et al. also found higher GLNU values in high-grade gliomas compared to brain metastases, reflecting a more severe degree of brain edema37. Collectively, SRLGLE and GLNU were identified as the strongest differential factors in our analyses respectively, the use of these imaging biomarkers could help clinicians make accurate disease diagnoses.

However, larger amounts of data and multicenter tests are needed for further demonstrating and optimizing our differential model before implementing it in clinical practice to establish an AI differential diagnosis platform, which will fast and accurately distinguish these two diseases when clinicians or patients import digital CT into this platform.

Despite promising differential models, our study still has several limitations. First, the paranasal sinuses CT images were from a variety of different CT scanners, although the images normalizing before extracting the features was done, our study still has the potential for bias in the analysis. Next, all ROIs were manually segmented in each slice of the sequences of CT, which was time-consuming. We have proposed a method of automatic sinuses segmentation incorporating handcrafted segmentation in another study, which may be applied to the differentiation of IP from CRSwNP and decrease the workload of sinuses segmentation in the future38. Finally, more data from more centers should be collected to further confirm the accuracy of our proposed method and to establish an AI differential diagnosis platform.

In conclusion, this multicenter study showed the outstanding differential ability of CT-based radiomics in distinguishing inverted papilloma from chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps, which could help clinicians make accurate, non-invasive, and economical disease diagnoses and promote the development of precision therapy.

Methods

According to the Declaration of Helsinki principles, this retrospective study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (reference number: KYLL-202206-006), Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University (reference number: SWYX: NO. 2022-413), and Weihai Municipal Hospital (reference number: 2022037). All experiments were performed according to relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Patients

Eligible patients were composed of data set 1 (for model training, tuning, and internal validation) from Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (center A) and data set 2 (for independent external validation) from Shandong Provincial Hospital (center B) and Weihai Municipal Hospital (center C). We retrospectively reviewed records and paranasal sinuses unenhanced CT images of patients with CRSwNP or IP who underwent surgical excision and were confirmed by pathologic diagnosis at three centers between January 2015 and June 2022. The development of the differential models was completed in accordance with published TRIPOD reporting guidelines39.

A total of 177 patients with CRSwNP and 76 patients with IP were composed of data set 1. Data set 2 included 63 patients with CRSwNP and 30 patients with IP. Patient exclusion criteria were: (a) patients who did not receive an unenhanced CT scan of the paranasal sinuses within a week before surgery or had insufficient CT image quality, (b) patients with fungal sinusitis, cystic fibrosis, immunodeficiency diseases, or other tumors, (c) patients lacking complete clinical data (Fig. 1).

The following demographic and clinical information were recorded: gender, age, smoking, drinking, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus. The pre-operation unenhanced CT images of the paranasal sinuses were used to extract the radiomic features. The workflow of the study is illustrated in Fig. 5.

The flowchart of this study.

CT image acquisition

For center A, all preoperative CT scans of the paranasal sinuses were performed by using the following scanners: a 64- or 256- detector row scanner CT machine (Discovery CT750 HD, GE System; Brilliance iCT, Philips System) or a dual-source spiral CT scanner (SOMATOM Force, Siemens Medical System). The following specific scanning parameters were adopted: tube voltage, 80–140 kVp; tube current, automatic tube current modulation (maximum, 450 mAs); pitch factor, 1.0; slice thickness, 1 mm; and slice interval, 1 mm. For center B, the CT scan was using a dual-source spiral CT scanner (SOMATOM Force, Siemens Medical System), a 64- or 256-detector row CT scanner (Revolution Frontier, GE System; Brilliance, Philips System or Revolution CT, GE System). The scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage 120 kV, variable tube current (160–600 mA) depending on the size of the patient, collimation 128 × 0.625 mm, rotation time 0.5 s, and layer thickness 1 mm. For center C, all patients received preoperative CT scans with multidetector row CT scanners (Somatom Force CT, Siemens, Germany). The scanning parameters were the same as center B. Standardization of CT scanning range of all centers is from the top of the frontal sinuses to the hard palate and from the tip of the nose to the region posterior to the clivus.

Considering the possible differences in images from different CT scanners, we preprocessed the images before analysis, using a linear interpolation algorithm in 3D-Slicer software (version 4.11.2; https://www.slicer.org) to resample all images to a common voxel spacing of 1 × 1 × 1 mm3 to minimize bias and achieve predictive consistency, achieving normalization of CT scans.

ROI segmentation

Original Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) images were imported to the 3D-Slicer software (version 4.11.2; https://www.slicer.org) for the region of interest (ROI) segmentation in each CT slicer by two radiologists with 5-year experience, who were blinded to the pathologic diagnosis result40. If the difference between the two radiologists was ≥ 5%, the ROI was reviewed by an expert radiologist with more than 30 years of experience to reach a consensus. ROIs were manually contoured along the boundaries of the lesions layer by layer and the air and bone regions were avoided as much as possible during delineation. Besides, given the lesions of the nose and paranasal sinuses could induce mucosal edema and turbinate hypertrophy, edematous mucosa and turbinate were also segmented. Examples of patients and segmented ROIs are shown in Fig. 6. Intra- and inter-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) were used to evaluate the intra- and inter-observer reproducibility of radiomics feature extraction. First, Dr. A and Dr. B randomly analyzed the images of 20 patients to evaluate the inter-class reproducibility. Two weeks later, Dr. A repeated the same procedure. An ICC greater than 0.75 indicates good agreement of the feature extraction.

Construction of the radiomics signature

Radiomic features were extracted based on the ROI from each CT sequence using the Python software package Pyradiomics 2.2.3 (https://www.python.org/) compliant with International Biomarker Standardization Initiative (IBSI). The feature set included first-order statistical features, size and shape matrix features, textural matrices, and higher order statistics features. These features are related to lesion size, shape, texture, or heterogeneity and are considered indicators of lesion behavior or treatment response41,42. In some studies, they can reflect disease progression43 and therefore have certain clinical relevance and biological rationality.

To eliminate the differences in the value scales of the radiomics features, each feature for a specific patient was subtracted by the mean value and divided by the respective standard deviation values (Z-score normalization, transforming pixel values to have a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1, was used to standardize the data)44. Intra- and inter-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) were used to evaluate the intra- and inter-observer reproducibility of radiomics feature extraction. An ICC value higher than 0.75 was considered stable and selected for model construction45. By displaying reproducible features under different observers, imaging devices, or acquisition protocols, the reliability and universality of our research results have been enhanced. Regardless of whether the diseases were unilateral or bilateral, all the patients of data set 1 were randomly divided into two groups comprising the training set (n = 151) and internal validation set (n = 102), with a proportion of 6:4. Subsequently, given the dimensionality of the feature set and the redundancy among features, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method was used to select the optimal radiomic features from the training set. This approach is well-suited for handling high-dimensional data46, ensuring that the selected features offer distinct and complementary information. After the most useful differential features were selected from the training set, radiomics score (Rad-score) was calculated by using a formula based on the radiomics features.

Model construction and performance evaluation index

To establish the differential diagnosis models, decision tree (DT), support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), and adaptive boosting regressor (AdaBoost) classifiers were used. To estimate the generalization performance of the radiomic models, the models were validated using the independent external validation set of 93 patients from data set 2. The diagnostic performance of the radiomics models for differentiating IP from CRSwNP was evaluated based on the area under the receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set. Additionally, four indicators were also applied to evaluate the performance of the models, including positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), F1-score, and accuracy.

Additional analysis

A subgroup analysis was performed to further verify the differential performance of our proposed approach by selecting the unilateral lesions of both CRSwNP and IP. Due to the limited sample size of data set 2, the analysis was performed only using data set 1.

Statistical analysis

The continuous variables and categorical variables between IP and CRSwNP were analyzed with Mann–Whitney U test and chi-square test using SPSS (version 26.0, IBM), respectively. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. In addition, the diagnostic models were established and evaluated using R software (version 4.2.0; http://www.R-project.org). The radiomic features were automatically extracted from PyRadiomics and implemented in Python (version 3.7.1; https://www.python.org/) for each sinus. The radiomics features selection method of LASSO was performed using the “glmnet” package47. The ROC curves plotting and AUC calculation were performed using the “pROC” package. Different statistical measures were used to estimate the performances of the classifiers: true positive (TP) is the number of true predicted positive state samples; true negative (TN) is the number of true predicted negative state samples; false positive (FP) is the number of false predicted positive state samples; and false negative (FN) is the number of false predicted negative state samples. The performances of all classifiers were compared using the accuracy, sensitivity (SE), specificity (SP), positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV) and the F1-score measures as follows: accuracy = (TP + TN)/N, whereas N signified the total number of recordings, sensitivity = TP/(TP + FN), specificity = TN/(TN + FP), PPV = TP/(TP + FP), NPV = TN/(TN + FN), F1-Score = 2*TP/(2*TP + FP + FN). All the related code is available at https://github.com/Yuxuan-Zhao/CRSwNP-vs-IP.git.

Analysis of construction impact and safety evaluation of metro shield tunnel under-crossing existing bridge pile foundation
Authors: Jianhang Xu, Xianmeng Zhang, Shangyue Lin, Dan Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the continuous development of urban underground space development, shield tunnel engineering inevitably intersects with existing traffic trunk lines, municipal pipe networks and buildings in the construction process, and often faces many difficulties, such as sensitive response of surrounding environment to construction disturbance, serious consequences of risk accidents and so on. Based on practical engineering, this paper conducts the following research: (1) Numerical simulation analysis of subway shield tunnel construction under existing bridge pile foundations is carried out to preview various mechanical responses during the construction process; (2) A scientific safety evaluation system and classification standard for the construction of subway shield tunnel under the existing bridge pile foundation are constructed. At the same time, the combination evaluation method of AHP-Entropy weight method and TOPSIS method based on game theory is used to evaluate it, and it is concluded that the risk level of the construction scene is grade III; (3) Numerical simulation is used to verify the scientificity and effectiveness of four safety measures: pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations, pile foundation active underpinning, compensation for strength of pile foundation, and replacement of steel segments. Combined with practical engineering analysis, it is concluded that the optimal safety measure for this construction scenario is the replacement of steel segments.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of economic development and the ongoing urbanization process, there has been a significant migration of population toward large and medium-sized cities. Consequently, existing urban transportation systems have found it increasingly difficult to accommodate the continuous expansion of urban areas. To alleviate traffic congestion and address the challenges posed by growing urban populations, the construction of subway systems has emerged as a critical solution. Over the past decade, the development of major cities in China has accelerated significantly. The number of subway stations in urban centers has increased, and underground space construction has progressively evolved toward networked layouts, larger spaces, and higher quality standards, imposing more stringent requirements on design concepts, structural forms, and construction technologies. Furthermore, the development of urban underground infrastructure often intersects with transportation networks, municipal utilities, and buildings, facing challenges such as shallow burial, sensitive environments, and high risks1–8.

In the field of shield tunnel underpass construction, both domestically and internationally, researchers commonly employ field measurement analysis or numerical simulation methods to investigate and validate the deformation behavior of existing structures. Peng et al.9 conducted a study on the deformation characteristics of masonry structures during shield tunnel side-passing construction by analyzing on-site monitoring data from a specific project. Yu Chen et al.10 utilized numerical simulations to examine surface settlement patterns, magnitude, and extent, as well as excess pore water pressure induced by double-circle shield tunneling in a free-field environment. He Meide et al.11 developed a three-dimensional numerical model based on a subway shield tunnel project in Beijing, simulating the scenario of a tunnel passing adjacent to an existing building; this was complemented by field monitoring data to analyze the building’s deformation response. Zhilu Yang12, Wei Gang13, Hu Qiubin14, Yang Huisi15, and Hu Jiangfeng16 integrated numerical modeling with field measurements to assess the deformation of pre-existing tunnels due to adjacent shield construction, thereby deriving the associated deformation mechanisms. Wang Lixin et al.17based on a metro project in Xi’an, used numerical simulation methods to analyze the shield tunnel under the existing high-speed railway embankment using the forepoling pipe method, and obtained the displacement variation patterns of the high-speed railway embankment and roadbed. Jinkui Li et al.7 investigated the displacement and stress variations in an existing high-speed railway bridge during different phases of the Zhengzhou Metro Line 5 underpass project using numerical simulation, identifying the structural deformation pattern under such complex conditions. Qing Zhang et al.18, based on a large-diameter shield tunneling project for a railway in Chengdu, established a three-dimensional finite element model incorporating pile-soil-tunnel interaction effects and predicted the displacement trend of existing skid-mounted equipment. Lei Zhu and Chengcheng Liang19 constructed a numerical model to simulate the differential settlement of pile foundations beneath elevated bridges throughout the entire shield tunneling process and verified the bridge’s load-bearing capacity under various stages. A substantial body of prior research has employed numerical simulation techniques to study these phenomena. However, in constructing finite element models, it is common practice to homogenize geotechnical parameters and apply idealized assumptions regarding construction sequences to ensure computational convergence and result interpretability. In reality, however, geological strata often exhibit inherent heterogeneity, including interlayers and lens-shaped formations, while construction processes inevitably involve dynamic deviations. These real-world complexities may lead to discrepancies between simulated predictions and actual structural responses. Consequently, relying solely on numerical simulation results is insufficient to capture the full range of engineering variability. A comprehensive risk assessment requires the integration of theoretical analysis, field data, and a multi-index safety evaluation framework to systematically evaluate and manage construction risks.

In terms of risk assessment, Hongyu Chen et al.20,21 proposed a risk assessment method based on a paired-coupling Bayesian network model. The feasibility and effectiveness of the model were verified through case studies, and key risk factors significantly influencing deformation and settlement risks were identified. Hongyu Chen et al.22 developed a TCM-BN risk assessment method for shield tunnels crossing under existing tunnels, achieving comprehensive evaluation of dynamic risks in undercrossing tunnel construction through forward reasoning, sensitivity analysis, intensity analysis, and reverse diagnosis of events. Chenghua Shi et al.23 established a probabilistic safety evaluation framework based on statistical data of geological drilling and shield tunnel parameters in practical projects, combining the theory of unconditional random fields, collaborative Kriging theory, and random function methods. Wang Hongde and Guan Zhaohe24 constructed a risk evaluation structure model for shield tunneling under existing railways based on SPA (Set Pair Analysis) according to the characteristics of shield undercrossing construction, settlement causes, and relevant construction parameters. The SPA-RS five-element connection number model was adopted to realize risk assessment for shield tunneling under railways, determine risk levels, and verify with cases. Lu Xinyue et al.25 proposed a dynamic risk assessment method for metro tunnel construction based on dynamic Bayesian networks and fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method, which was verified for rationality through practical projects. Ying Guozhu et al.26,27 took practical projects as examples, established risk evaluation systems by analyzing and identifying hazard sources in the projects, and conducted safety evaluations. Chen Renpeng et al.28proposed a risk assessment method that integrates the fuzzy analytic hierarchy process with an interval number-based improved TOPSIS approach, and validated its effectiveness by comparing the results with actual field conditions. Through numerical simulation and comprehensive risk assessment, coupled with the implementation of targeted safety measures tailored to specific engineering contexts, the likelihood of accidents can be significantly reduced, thereby minimizing potential casualties. Currently, widely adopted safety mitigation strategies that effectively control settlement in existing bridge pile foundations include pile foundation replacement, structural reinforcement, and pre-grouting consolidation techniques29–35.

Although existing research has achieved many results in the field of analysis and risk management for shield tunneling under existing structures, there are still some shortcomings. On the one hand, the research perspective is relatively isolated. Most existing studies conduct numerical simulation, risk assessment, and safety measure analysis independently, lacking a systematic connection of “construction simulation—risk identification—measure verification”, and thus cannot provide full-process technical support for engineering. On the other hand, the construction of the risk evaluation system is highly subjective. Most studies rely on expert experience to determine evaluation indicators without systematically extracting them from literature data, which may lead to omissions or deviations in indicator selection; the calculation of weights mostly uses a single method, making it difficult to balance subjective experience and objective data. This paper proposes a full-process framework of “construction simulation—risk assessment—measure verification”, uses text mining to extract evaluation indicators, and presents a risk assessment method for shield tunneling under existing bridge pile foundations that combines the TOPSIS method with the improved AHP-entropy weight method based on game theory, which makes up for the limitations of previous studies and can provide a reference for similar engineering cases.

Project overview and construction simulation

Project overview

The tunnel section between Ludancun Station and Renminnan Station of Metro Line 9 is mainly located in Luohu District, with high-rise buildings on both sides and numerous control points. After exiting Ludancun Station, the line first under-passes Buji River, then turns southeast, goes through 2 customs dormitory buildings and 2 Chuanbu Road residential buildings, proceeds east along Chunfeng Road, and reaches Renminnan Station after undercrossing the Guangzhou-Shenzhen Railway. The Ludancun-Renminnan section features bustling traffic and dense buildings along the route. The original geomorphology of the project is a valley alluvial plain, with flat terrain. According to the on-site geological survey report, the project involves nine layers of rock and soil, and the buried depth of the groundwater table is 1.70–5.70 m. This tunnel is an urban metro shield tunnel, and the interval tunnel is constructed by the earth pressure balance shield method. The shield tunnel adopts a single-layer prefabricated lining. The connection mode between segments and between lining rings is a commonly used flexible connection. It is designed that the segments and rings are connected by mature curved bolts. The lining ring adopts the form of a universal ring segment lining ring with a wedge amount of 38 mm. The inner diameter of the segment is 5.40 m, the thickness is 0.30 m, the outer diameter is 6.00 m, the width is 1.5 m, and the number of blocks is 6. C50 concrete segments with a waterproof grade of P12 are used, crossing two layers of rock and soil bodies, namely the breccia strongly weathered zone and the moderately weathered metamorphic sandstone zone. The pile foundation of Chunfeng Road Viaduct has a buried depth of 24.55 m. The main beam concrete is C50 concrete, the pile foundation is C30 concrete, the cap is C40 concrete. Although the groundwater is corrosive to the concrete, the bridge pile foundation can still maintain normal use. The construction site of the new tunnel project mainly involves two types of rock and soil layers: the strongly weathered breccia in the corner of the fracture and the moderately weathered metamorphic sandstone. The strongly weathered breccia has loose particles and weak cementation, which is prone to plastic deformation during construction. The support system works in a coordinated manner through “synchronous grouting flexible restraint + segment rigid bearing”. The grouting slurry fills the gap behind the shield at a pressure of 0.3–0.5 MPa to counteract the initial deformation, while the segments bear the lateral pressure of 0.189 MPa from the stratum to prevent collapse. The moderately weathered metamorphic sandstone has high strength and good integrity, mainly undergoing elastic deformation. The rock and support exhibit “cooperative bearing”, with the segments directly bearing the elastic resistance of the rock stratum, and the grouting layer focusing on sealing and water isolation to reduce the impact of water-rock erosion on the stability of the support.

Construction simulation

The MIDAS GTX NX finite element software was employed to perform a finite element simulation of the newly constructed urban shield tunnel. The model extends 90 m along the tunnel axis (i.e., the width of 60 ring segments), which serves as the longitudinal excavation length (in the Z-direction). Drawing on the research experience of previous scholars, during the excavation of shield tunnels, the rock and soil mass within a range of 3–5 times the tunnel diameter, as well as existing buildings, can be affected. Given that the diameter of this new tunnel is 6 m, during the modeling process, the model framework is extended by 15 m on both sides to define the transverse width of the model (in the X-direction), and a vertical depth of 48 m below the ground surface is adopted as the vertical depth of the model (in the Y-direction). The tunnel and the existing bridge’s pile foundations and caps are meshed in units of 0.8 m, while the other parts are meshed in units of 2 m. The center plane of the existing bridge pile foundation is aligned with the central axis of the model, situated at a depth of 45 m. The burial depth of the pile foundation is 24.55 m. The pile cap has dimensions of 10 m in length, 2.5 m in width, and 1.5 m in height, with its upper surface positioned 0.34 m below the ground surface.

In the load analysis process, considering the most unfavorable situation, that is, without taking into account the diffusion effect of the soil, the load of the superstructure is evenly transferred to the cap through two symmetrically arranged pile foundations. The maximum bearing capacity of the pile foundations is adopted to simulate the superstructure and its load36.Based on the relevant specifications and the collected drawings of actual engineering projects, the designed bearing capacity of each pile at this site is N = 9232 kN. The tunnel in this area is predominantly located within the strongly weathered zone of fault breccia and moderately weathered metamorphic sandstone. The surrounding rock of the tunnel is classified as Grade IV. Taking into account a one-level reduction, according to the specifications, the height of the collapse arch at this location is h = 7.92 m. After deducting the pile–soil frictional force within the scope of the collapse arch, the bearing capacity of the pile foundation is 3007 kN. Thus, the force exerted by the pile foundation on the tunnel is F = 9232–3007 = 6225 kN. This value is utilized to simulate the friction pile. According to Equation (1), this force is converted into an equivalent load, and the load value is 498 kPa37,38. Normal constraints are imposed around the model, and a fixed constraint is set on the lower surface to restrict displacements. The load of 498 kPa is uniformly distributed above the pile cap. The top surface of the model remains unconstrained. After the installation of the tunnel lining segments, the normal constraints of the segments are activated.

Model validation

Before conducting the analysis of the numerical simulation results, it is necessary to first verify the validity of the finite element model to ensure that it can accurately reflect the various mechanical response characteristics of the pile cap during the construction process. For this purpose, the monitoring data from two actual monitoring points on the left and right sides of the center position above the existing foundation slab were selected for comparison and analysis. The monitoring frequency of these two monitoring points was once a day. In the actual project, the shield machine’s excavation speed was 6 m/day, and it took 15 days to complete the excavation of a 90 m tunnel. In the numerical simulation, this process was set with 34 construction procedures, lasting 17 days, mainly due to the certain lag of segment installation compared to shield excavation. However, in the actual construction, even in the last three segment installation stages, the shield machine continued to excavate, exerting a certain influence on the existing bridge pile foundation. Therefore, to ensure the rationality and comparability of the comparison, only the simulation results of the first 15 days were compared with the on-site monitoring data, as shown in Fig. 3. The results show that the variation trends of the simulation values and the monitoring values are highly consistent, and the determination coefficients are all close to 1, indicating that the established finite element model can well reproduce the various mechanical response characteristics of the pile cap during the construction process and can provide reliable data support for subsequent analysis.

Analysis of vertical settlement displacement of existing bridge pile foundation

To accurately serve practical engineering and timely capture the vertical settlement displacement of the deck of the existing viaduct caused by shield tunnel construction, so as to prevent accidents such as bridge side turnover and collapse, combined with the engineering reality that “the vertical settlement displacement at the bottom of the existing bridge pile foundation is greater than that at the top”, the vertical settlement displacement at the bottom of the existing bridge pile foundation is selected as the research object.

From the variation trend of the vertical settlement displacement at the bottom of the existing bridge pile foundation (Fig. 4), the vertical settlement displacement of the left existing bridge pile foundation remains basically unchanged before the shield tunnel excavation reaches 21 m (Process 7). Starting from 42 m (Process 14), the displacement changes significantly with a gradually increasing rate. When the excavation reaches 48 m (Process 16, where the shield tunnel passes directly below the existing bridge pile foundation), the change rate peaks. Thereafter, the change rate gradually decreases, and the displacement reaches a maximum of 9.38 mm when the excavation reaches 78 m (Process 26). Subsequently, the vertical settlement displacement at the bottom of the pile foundation tends to stabilize. Comply with the provisions in the “Technical Code for Structural Safety Protection of Urban Rail Transit” (CJJ/T 202-2013), specifically, “the allowable vertical settlement of existing bridge pile foundations 10 mm” and “ the settlement rate 2 mm/day”.

Stress analysis of existing bridge pile foundations

To clarify whether the existing viaduct can be used normally after the shield tunnel construction is completed, a study on the influence of shield construction on the stress of the existing viaduct was carried out. Through the structural inspection of the existing viaduct, it is determined whether the shield tunnel construction will cause damage to the structure of the existing bridge foundation, so as to determine whether the existing viaduct can operate normally.

In terms of stratum permeability, strongly weathered angular rock has a relatively high permeability coefficient, and seepage channels are prone to form around the pile foundation, thereby increasing the risk of degradation of the side friction resistance of the pile. In contrast, moderately weathered sandstone has significantly lower permeability and a strong barrier effect on groundwater flow, with a more obvious phenomenon of concentrated water pressure around the pile. Therefore, it is necessary to reasonably control the grouting pressure to avoid overloading of the support structure due to excessive horizontal hydraulic thrust. In terms of stratum mechanical parameters, the cohesion and internal friction angle and other key mechanical indicators of strongly weathered angular rock are relatively low, resulting in weak bond strength at the pile-soil interface and making the stress redistribution and stratum displacement caused by tunnel excavation more likely to induce additional settlement of the pile foundation. In contrast, moderately weathered sandstone has higher mechanical strength and integrity, and the bearing performance of the pile foundation’s rock-embedded section is stable, but stress concentration is prone to occur at its root, and the stress evolution characteristics of this area need to be closely monitored.

Figure 5 shows the stress nephograms of the existing bridge pile foundations before and after shield tunnel excavation. It can be seen from the nephograms that shield construction has no significant impact on the stress of the existing bridge pile foundations, but the effective stress around the tunnel increases significantly after construction. The top of the existing bridge pile foundations is subject to effective tensile stress in the X direction, and only effective compressive stress in the Z and Y directions. The distribution of effective compressive stress in the X direction is similar to that in the Z direction. The maximum effective compressive stress in the X direction is 0.189 MPa, occurring near the upper side of the bottom plate of the right existing bridge pile foundation, while the maximum effective tensile stress is 0.087 MPa, located at the top plate of the left existing bridge pile foundation. The maximum effective compressive stress in the Z direction is 0.202 MPa, and that in the Y direction reaches 0.440 MPa. It meets the requirements of the “Code for Design of Concrete Structures” (GB50010–2010), which stipulates that the design value of the axial compressive strength of the C30 pile foundation is 14.3 MPa and the design value of the axial tensile strength is 1.43 MPa, so the existing viaduct can still be used normally after the construction of the new tunnel is completed.

Strain analysis of existing bridge pile foundations

To accurately support practical engineering construction decisions, deeply analyze the evolution law of structural strain during construction, and effectively prevent and control risks such as structural deformation, cracking, and even instability caused by abnormal strain. Considering the complex influence of construction on structural mechanical response and the engineering reality that field measurement is difficult to cover the whole process, based on the above analysis, we select the strains of 2 nodes with obvious changes at the top and bottom of the existing bridge pile foundations as the research objects to carry out numerical simulation analysis of construction-induced strain.

After sorting and analyzing the strain data of the selected nodes, Fig. 6 is obtained. From the variation law presented in this figure, the evolution trends of surrounding rock strains at the two nodes are highly consistent.

Construction-induced strain curves of surrounding rock nodes.

After the start of shield construction, the surrounding rock strain in the top area of the existing bridge pile foundation entered a stage of continuous growth. With the continuous development of excavation operations, the surrounding rock strain in this part is affected by construction disturbances, and the value continues to rise. When the shield excavates the rock and soil mass to a depth of 81m (Process 17), the surrounding rock strain at the top of the existing bridge pile foundation reaches the maximum value. Thereafter, with the continuation of the construction process, the strain value tends to be stable and no obvious fluctuations occur.

However, the generation of surrounding rock strain at the bottom of the existing bridge pile foundation shows obvious stage characteristics: measurable strain in the bottom surrounding rock only occurs after the shield completes the crossing of the existing bridge pile foundation. After strain occurs, the strain value continues to increase due to continuous construction disturbances, reaching a peak at Process 18, then showing a slow decline before stabilizing. Based on previous research on the mechanical properties of surrounding rock, plastic failure is triggered only when the surrounding rock strain reaches 2.0%. Obviously, the surrounding rock strain induced by the current shield construction is far below this critical value, confirming that the shield construction process will not cause damage to the existing bridge pile foundation.

Construction safety evaluation

Through the above numerical simulation, it has been theoretically verified that indicators such as pile foundation settlement and structural strain are within the design allowable range, preliminarily indicating that the construction under the simulated working conditions is safe and feasible. However, it should be noted that this numerical simulation is carried out based on an idealized model and homogenized geological parameters. In actual engineering, the discreteness of stratum distribution and the dynamic deviation of construction technology may lead to differences between the mechanical response and the simulation results. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce a multi-index evaluation system through systematic safety evaluation, comprehensively determine the construction safety status, extend the simulation conclusions to real engineering scenarios, and make up for the limitations of numerical simulation in “complex working condition adaptability”. Evaluation index system and grading criteria

Through web crawler technology, systematic retrieval was conducted on the CNKI database for literature published from 2013 to 2025 using keywords such as “shield tunnel passing under existing bridges”, “shield tunnel passing under existing buildings”, and “factors influencing shield tunnel construction”. A total of 826 relevant documents were obtained. After screening (retaining 412 core journal papers and dissertations) and duplicate removal (excluding 68 duplicate documents), 344 documents were finally included in the LDA topic analysis. Subsequently, word segmentation processing, stop word filtering based on the Harbin Institute of Technology stop word list, word frequency statistics (retaining words with a frequency of 10), determination of the optimal number of topics (through perplexity curve analysis, the optimal number of topics for the LDA model was determined to be 3), and extraction of topic words and semantic mapping were carried out in sequence to complete the LDA analysis. Ultimately, a safety evaluation system for the construction of metro shield tunnels passing under existing bridge piles was established, with primary indicators including existing bridge conditions, geological conditions, and new tunnel engineering conditions, and secondary indicators including existing bridge displacement, bridge span, and 15 other factors, as shown in Fig. 8.

Safety evaluation system.

By referring to relevant literatures, standards and specifications, and combining the opinions of experts, the risk levels of metro tunnel construction safety are divided into four grades: Grade I (low-risk: regular monitoring is required), Grade II (medium-risk: special prevention and control measures need to be formulated), Grade III (relatively high-risk: immediate rectification and key monitoring are required), and Grade IV (high-risk: operation must be stopped for immediate rectification). Risk evaluation score calculation using TOPSIS method

In recent years, many scholars have turned their attention to fusing the importance degrees of multiple indicators of nodes, which can more comprehensively and reasonably evaluate nodes. Among numerous evaluation methods, the TOPSIS method is a comprehensive analysis approach suitable for multi-indicator scenarios, applicable to multi-objective decision-making analysis, and it aligns with the research content of this paper. The application approach of the TOPSIS method is as follows: The 15 core evaluation indicators developed are utilized as the evaluation attributes in TOPSIS analysis. The scoring results provided by five experts for these 15 indicators are regarded as the decision-making scenarios of TOPSIS. By determining the combined weights of each indicator, carrying out data standardization, constructing a weighted standardized matrix, calculating the positive and negative ideal solutions, and the relative closeness degree, the construction risk level is finally obtained quantitatively.

Verification of safety protection measures

Due to the small distance between the existing bridge pile foundation and the tunnel arch top and the large load, in order to ensure the normal operation of the Chunfeng Road Viaduct, based on geological exploration, engineering experience, numerical simulation and safety evaluation results, it is proposed to reinforce the bridge pile foundation by means of pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations, pile foundation active underpinning, compensation for strength of pile foundation, or set steel segments of the same size as the concrete segments in the adjacent area of the viaduct pile foundation. To select the most scientifically sound, economical, and effective protection scheme, finite element numerical simulation software will be used to conduct three-dimensional simulation analyses on selected measures, verifying their feasibility and effectiveness.

The specific plan for pile foundation active underpinning involves constructing a new bridge pile with a diameter of 1.5 m and a depth of 20 m on each side of the existing bridge pile foundation, 4 m and 6 m away from the edge respectively. A bearing platform with dimensions of 5.5 m 2.5 m 1.8 m is set up, and the load transfer is achieved through a temporary support system to complete the replacement construction of the bridge structure. The pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations plan involves implementing sleeve valve pipe pre-grouting at 13.05 m away from the edge on both sides of the bottom of the existing pile foundation, forming a 0.75 m wide and 10 m long strip-shaped concrete reinforcement body, which is effectively connected to the bridge pile foundation to enhance the overall stability and reduce the disturbance caused by shield tunneling. The compensation for strength of pile foundation plan involves constructing reinforcement piles with a diameter of 1.5 m and a depth of 20 m on both sides of the existing bridge pile foundation, 4 m and 6 m away from the edge respectively. A new bearing platform with dimensions of 21 m 2.5 m 1.5 m is built to connect them with the original pile foundation, thereby enhancing the overall bearing capacity of the pile foundation. The replace steel segments plan involves replacing the original concrete segments with steel segments of the same specification in the tunnel underpass section, i.e., from 42 to 48 m in the model, to improve the structural stiffness and anti-deformation capacity. The above figure demonstrates that the four safety protection measures can effectively mitigate the vertical settlement displacement of the existing bridge pile foundations following shield tunneling. In terms of performance, the measures are ranked in descending order of effectiveness as follows: replace steel segments, pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations, compensation for strength of pile foundation, and pile foundation active underpinning. Among them, although the steel segment replacement scheme has high construction costs, replacing the concrete segments with steel segments only in the adjacent area of the bridge pile foundations can significantly reduce the vertical settlement displacement of the existing bridge pile foundations while reducing costs, and the construction is relatively convenient, making it the optimal scheme. The pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations scheme can reduce settlement, but due to the high construction technology and requirements and the difficulty in ensuring the reinforcement effect, it is necessary to carry out technical disclosure in combination with the actual situation.

The compensation for strength of pile foundation scheme has a similar effect to the pile foundation active underpinning scheme, and only needs to connect the existing pile caps with fewer disadvantages, making it the sub-optimal scheme. The pile foundation active underpinning scheme has disadvantages such as great influence on the existing pile foundations, narrow construction space, long construction period, high management difficulty, high engineering investment, and limited effect of temporary supports on continuous beam bridges. When using it, it is necessary to balance the construction period budget and identify hazard sources. In summary, considering the comprehensive simulation analysis and factors such as construction period and budget, replacing steel segments in the adjacent area of the bridge pile foundations is the optimal scheme, followed by pre-grouting reinforcement of bridge pile foundations. In addition, the scheme should be flexibly adjusted according to the actual situation when applied.

Discussions

The subway shield tunnel section crossing the viaduct pile foundation is in a geologically complex area, and its close proximity to existing viaduct pile foundations increases the risk of construction-induced disturbances. This interaction between new tunneling and existing structures makes safety control critical, as excessive deformation could threaten both construction and viaduct stability. Three-dimensional numerical simulation is essential here: it quantifies hard-to-observe mechanical responses (e.g., pile settlement, surrounding rock strain) during construction, avoiding over-reliance on empirical judgment. Simulation results showed shield tunneling induces measurable viaduct pile settlement due to soil stress redistribution, while surrounding rock strain fluctuates initially but stabilizes as a new stress equilibrium forms.

To quantify risk, we used the AHP-entropy weight TOPSIS method (integrating subjective and objective data), which yielded a Level III risk result—indicating a need for systematic mitigation. We validated four measures: pre-grouting reinforcement, steel segment replacement, active pile underpinning, and pile foundation strengthening. All reduced deformation to acceptable levels, but comparative analysis identified steel segment replacement as preferred, as its adjustable rigidity balanced deformation control, efficiency, and cost.

This study provides actionable guidance for similar urban tunneling projects: the Level III risk classification clarifies the necessity of proactive measures, and the validation of steel segment replacement offers a technically feasible solution. Limitations should be noted, however: the AHP-entropy weight TOPSIS method assumes fixed indicator weights, potentially overlooking dynamic on-site risk changes; simulations simplified soil heterogeneity; and steel segment performance requires long-term post-construction verification. Future work could integrate real-time monitoring data to calibrate models, enhancing assessment accuracy.

Conclusions

This research focuses on the engineering challenge of a metro shield tunnel passing under the pile foundations of an existing bridge. Through a systematic study involving “numerical simulation-risk assessment-protective verification”, it systematically reveals the mechanical response mechanism and risk evolution law of the pile foundations during the construction process, forming results that are both theoretically valuable and practically applicable in engineering. The specific conclusions are as follows:

For the complex scenario of close proximity between the tunnel and the pile foundations, a three-dimensional finite element model for the construction of a metro shield tunnel passing under the pile foundations of an existing bridge was established. The results show that the maximum vertical settlement of the pile foundation is 9.38 mm, the maximum compressive stress is 0.440 MPa, and the maximum tensile stress is 0.087 MPa, all of which meet the design requirements of the existing bridge’s pile foundations. This model can directly provide reference for the mechanical response prediction before the construction of similar projects.

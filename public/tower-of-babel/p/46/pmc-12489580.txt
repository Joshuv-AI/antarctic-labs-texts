Advancing risk prediction in intracranial aneurysm clipping
Authors: Seongheon Lee
Published: 2025

Endovascular coiling has become increasingly common in the management of unruptured intracranial aneurysms, as it is generally associated with lower short-term morbidity and mortality rates compared with clipping [1,2]. However, microsurgical clipping remains essential, particularly in cases with unfavorable morphology, challenging locations, or uncertain long-term durability [3,4]. Given that clipping carries a higher early postoperative risk, accurate risk assessment is crucial.

In the past decades, risk assessment tools have been proposed to support perioperative decision-making in aneurysm clipping surgery [5,6]. While useful, these models rely primarily on static preoperative variables, such as patient demographics or aneurysm size and location. In addition, many were originally developed over a decade ago, and therefore do not fully reflect current advances in anesthesia, surgical techniques, and perioperative monitoring. Therefore, their clinical applicability in contemporary practice is limited.

In this issue of the Korean Journal of Anesthesiology, Kim et al. [7] present a multicenter study addressing these limitations. By analyzing nearly 3000 patients and validating the model on separate cohorts, they enhanced generalizability and reduced the risk of overfitting. A notable strength of this study is the inclusion of critical intraoperative factors such as operative time and blood loss, which reflects the clinical reality that perioperative risk is not fixed before the incision, but rather evolves dynamically throughout the surgery. Recent studies on perioperative stroke prediction and coronary surgery have shown that incorporating intraoperative variables can significantly enhance model accuracy [8,9]. In this context, the study by Kim et al. [7] broadens risk prediction beyond static, preoperative-only assessments and toward a more dynamic perioperative model. This allows for the likelihood of complications to be estimated and provides information that can guide intraoperative anesthetic management and shape postoperative care strategies, such as implementing support for tighter intraoperative hemodynamic control, earlier neurological evaluation, or closer monitoring in an intensive care unit for patients identified as high-risk.

However, as the model incorporates intraoperative factors, the final risk estimate cannot be calculated before surgery begins. Consequently, its value in preoperative decision-making is limited. Nevertheless, its evidence-based framework for preoperative counseling is an important advantage. Clinicians can use it to explain to patients and families the impact that intraoperative events such as prolonged operative time or significant bleeding can have on outcomes, enhancing transparency and promoting realistic understanding of potential risks.

In summary, prediction of neurological complications remains a critical aspect of aneurysm clipping surgery. In their multicenter study, Kim et al. [7] provide a more dynamic and clinically relevant risk assessment tool by integrating intraoperative variables. Although the predictive performance is modest and no model can replace clinical judgment, this study represents a meaningful step toward evidence-based perioperative care.

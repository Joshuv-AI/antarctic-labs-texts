A nurse-led digital nudging strategy to enhance home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise adherence among patients with chronic heart failure: development of a complex intervention informed by the Medical Research Council framework
Authors: Zhen Yang, Jiguang Zong, Yao Li, Xiujie Jiang, Aiping Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHome-based cardiac rehabilitation offers a cost-effective alternative model. However, long-term exercise adherence among patients remains suboptimal due to insufficient motivation. Self-Determination Theory and Nudge Theory provide the theoretical foundation for understanding behavioral drivers and designing low-burden intervention strategies. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a nurse-led, theory-driven, digitally platform-delivered nudge intervention program to improve long-term rehabilitation behavior in patients with chronic heart failure.MethodsA multi-stage mixed-methods design was employed. In Stage I, based on prior evidence, a questionnaire survey was used to explore patient preferences regarding nudge strategies and delivery media; findings were finalized through expert panel discussion. In Stage II, an expert participatory theory co-design process was adopted. Experts from diverse professional fields were invited to develop the programme theory and elucidate hypothesized mechanisms. Expert response rate, authority coefficient, and Kendall concordance coefficient were used to systematically assess expert engagement quality, professional authority, and consensus level on the theoretical framework. In Stage III, the Nominal Group Technique was utilized to draft the digital nudge strategies. Subsequently, stakeholder meetings were conducted to refine and finalize the digital nudge strategy package.ResultsIn Stage I, 486 patients with chronic heart failure participated in the survey. Patients most preferred the nudge strategies of pre-commitment (34.6%), alerts and reminders (21.8%), and social norms (18.1%), with the preferred delivery medium being an interactive online website (46.5%). In Stage II, the programme theory confirmed that pre-commitment, alerts/reminders, and social norms could activate core psychological needs (competence, autonomy, relatedness) respectively, thereby promoting sustained exercise behavior through motivational internalization. Kendall concordance coefficient for content importance and theoretical alignment were 0.765 and 0.868, respectively (p < 0.05). In Stage III, the finalized digital nudge strategies, delivered via the interactive online system, included signing and publicly displaying commitment letters, contextualized alert notifications, and relatable peer modeling cases. This nurse-led digital nudge program is theoretically capable of improving exercise adherence in home-based cardiac rehabilitation for patients with chronic heart failure.ConclusionThis study successfully developed a nurse-led digital nudge intervention. By utilizing pre-commitment, dynamic reminders, and social norms strategies to target the fulfillment of patients’ psychological needs, it provides a theory-driven, cost-effective, and patient-acceptable novel pathway to enhance adherence in home-based rehabilitation for patients with chronic heart failure. Subsequent research is needed to validate its long-term effectiveness and clinical translation value.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-026-05098-6.

Introduction

In recent years, advances in prevention and treatment technologies have led to modest improvements in the clinical prognosis of chronic heart failure. Nevertheless, chronic heart failure remains one of the leading causes of mortality in cardiovascular medicine [1]. Given its chronic trajectory and poor prognosis, chronic heart failure severely compromises patients’ quality of life while imposing substantial burdens on families and healthcare systems [2]. There is an urgent need to develop targeted interventions that mitigate cardiovascular risk factors, improve clinical outcomes, and enhance the affordability of chronic heart failure management services.

Cardiac rehabilitation has been established as an effective intervention that significantly enhances cardiopulmonary function in cardiovascular patients, with profound implications for clinical prognosis and quality of life [3, 4]. As a comprehensive approach involving multidisciplinary collaboration and patient engagement, cardiac rehabilitation aims to help patients achieve optimal physical, psychological, and social functioning for active and healthy living [5]. Current clinical guidelines universally recommend exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation as an effective and safe intervention to improve cardiovascular outcomes [6, 7]. Empirical evidence confirms that exercise-centered cardiac rehabilitation significantly enhances ventricular remodeling and diastolic function in patients with chronic heart failure, thereby improving both clinical outcomes and quality of life [8–10].

However, the chronic nature of cardiovascular diseases presents challenges for long-term center-based cardiac rehabilitation programs. Environmental barriers such as inclement weather and transportation difficulties substantially increase patients’ time commitment and impose significant economic burdens on families and society [11, 12]. Home-based cardiac rehabilitation emerges as a viable alternative [13], demonstrating equivalent benefits to center-based programs in improving cardiopulmonary function, clinical outcomes, and cardiovascular risk reduction. By eliminating clinic visits, home-based cardiac rehabilitation significantly reduces patients’ financial burdens and time constraints [14].

The efficacy of home-based cardiac rehabilitation largely depends on sustained exercise adherence among patients with chronic heart failure. Despite robust evidence demonstrating improved cardiac function, enhanced quality of life, and reduced hospital readmission rates, long-term adherence to home-based cardiac rehabilitation remains suboptimal [15]. Studies indicate that fewer than half of patients with chronic heart failure continue exercise-based home-based cardiac rehabilitation after completing center-based programs [16]. This attrition stems primarily from the disease’s progressive nature and patients’ limited exercise tolerance, leading to diminished motivation and persistence during early rehabilitation phases—often resulting in reduced exercise frequency or complete discontinuation [17].

The complexity of underlying behavioral mechanisms contributes to poor adherence. Current understanding of intrinsic behavioral drivers and their pathways remains limited, constraining the effectiveness of existing interventions. Behavioral regulation models posit that self-efficacy and motivation jointly determine short-term exercise adherence, with motivation serving as a critical predictor of long-term maintenance [18, 19]. Previous research has further established motivation as a key mediator between psychological needs and exercise adherence [20]. These findings highlight the necessity of incorporating motivational enhancement into intervention strategies to ensure sustainable rehabilitation.

Self-Determination Theory provides a comprehensive framework for understanding human motivation and personality development [21]. This theory elucidates the motivational mechanisms underlying individual behavior, emphasizing the importance of intrinsic motivation and autonomy for psychological well-being and behavioral outcomes [21]. Central to Self-Determination Theory is the proposition that behaviors are driven by distinct motivational types, with the degree of autonomy directly influencing behavioral consistency and satisfaction [21]. The theory focuses particularly on fostering autonomy and fulfilling basic psychological needs to cultivate intrinsic motivation, thereby promoting the initiation and maintenance of health behaviors.

Complementarily, Nudge Theory addresses how emotional states, cognitive biases, and social norms often lead individuals to make suboptimal decisions [22]. Through carefully designed, low-intensity interventions (nudges), individuals can be guided toward beneficial choices without restricting freedom of selection [23]. This approach modifies decision-making environments (through contextual cues or situational stimuli) to influence cognitive processes and behavioral motivation, facilitating conscious or unconscious adoption of health-promoting behaviors [23]. Digital nudge strategies operationalize this theory through information technology platforms to deliver health behavior interventions.

Currently, digital nudges have been successfully applied across multiple chronic disease domains—including medication adherence, physical activity, weight management, and dietary behaviors—demonstrating both improved health outcomes and favorable cost-effectiveness [24–27]. Consequently, digital nudging offers a promising approach to addressing adherence limitations in home-based cardiac rehabilitation for patients with chronic heart failure. As part of a larger research program, this study integrates Self-Determination Theory and Nudge Theory to develop nurse-led, digital nudge strategies aimed at enhancing exercise adherence in home-based cardiac rehabilitation for patients with chronic heart failure.

Methods

Study design

A multi-stage mixed-methods design was employed, following the Medical Research Council framework for developing complex interventions [28]. The rationale for this methodological choice stems from the inherent complexity of digital behavioral interventions. Developing an effective strategy requires synthesizing quantitative empirical data (patient preferences), qualitative theoretical constructs (psychological mechanisms), and practical clinical consensus (feasibility). A single methodological approach would be insufficient to capture these diverse dimensions. Therefore, a mixed-methods design was selected to ensure the intervention was deeply rooted in both robust theory and real-world applicability.

The development was an iterative and cumulative process consisting of three interconnected phases (Fig. 1). Phase I (Exploration) identified patient preferences and evidence-based nudge components, which provided the raw material for Phase II (Theory). In Phase II, these components were integrated into a program theory to explain the psychological mechanisms of action. Finally, Phase III (Construction) translated this theoretical blueprint into a concrete, nurse-led digital strategy through expert consensus and stakeholder refinement. This sequential approach ensures that the final intervention is grounded in evidence, guided by theory, and tailored to clinical reality.

Phase I: Exploration of research evidence

A prior scoping review was conducted to explore nudge strategies aimed at promoting home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise among patients with heart disease [29]. A total of 11 nudge strategies aimed at improving physical activity during cardiac rehabilitation in patients with heart diseases have been explored, including goal setting, information feedback, alerts and reminders, verbal reinforcement, social norms, loss aversion, pre-commitment, default options, environmental cues, peer effects, and information disclosure. 16 included studies utilized digital nudge mediums to deliver these strategies, primarily through smart health apps, interactive websites, emails, and text messages.

Within this phase, a structured questionnaire survey developed by our team (Appendix 1) and group discussion were conducted to identify preferred nudge strategies and mediums. A structured questionnaire survey was chosen to efficiently quantify patient preferences across a large and geographically dispersed sample of patients with chronic heart failure, providing robust descriptive evidence of preferred nudge strategies and delivery media. The subsequent group discussion with the research team allowed the integration of these quantitative findings with pragmatic considerations, including patient safety, implementation costs, and the availability of an existing digital platform, an approach consistent with the exploratory phase of the MRC framework for complex interventions. Patients with chronic heart failure completed the structured questionnaire; the findings informed the final selection of nudge strategies and mediums. Inclusion criteria were: diagnosis of patients with chronic heart failure; age ≥ 18 years; currently undergoing home-based cardiac rehabilitation; provision of informed consent to participate voluntarily. Exclusion criteria were: communication barriers; and presence of severe comorbid organic diseases. Participants were asked to select the one nudge strategy and one nudge medium they deemed most suitable. During the group discussion, building upon the questionnaire results and considering patient safety, implementation costs, and existing platforms, the research team collectively finalized the nudge strategies and medium. The team consisted of two nursing professors and three doctoral students, all of whom would be involved in the subsequent intervention delivery.

Phase II: Determination of program theory

Previous research developed and validated a model of the driving mechanisms for home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise adherence in patients with chronic heart failure, grounded in Self-Determination Theory [20]. The satisfaction of psychological needs related to exercise of patients can activate their exercise regulation motivation and triggers exercise adherence behavior [20]. As exercise regulation motivation becomes increasingly internalized, patients can establish clear goals and maintain and adjust their home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise consistently over the long term [20]. Consequently, addressing patients’ exercise-related psychological needs early is crucial to foster the internalization of exercise regulation motivation and, subsequently, improve exercise adherence behavior.

Nudge Theory refers to predictably influencing patient decision-making by altering the choice architecture, without forbidding any options or significantly changing economic incentives [22, 23]. It posits that modifying the decision-making environment (contextual cues or situational stimuli) can influence individual cognitive processes and behavioral motivation, promoting conscious or unconscious correct behavioral decisions, thereby increasing the likelihood of initiating and sustaining healthy behaviors. Existing research evidence demonstrates the significant effectiveness of nudge strategies in promoting home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise among cardiac patients [30–33]. These strategies are diverse and cost-effective, encompassing approaches like pre-commitment, alerts and reminders, and social norms.

In this phase, an expert-participatory theory collaborative design was adopted. This expert-participatory collaborative design, which followed the principles of a modified Delphi process, was purposefully selected to systematically synthesize specialized, multi-disciplinary knowledge and to reach a structured consensus on the programme theory, thereby ensuring both theoretical coherence and clinical relevance before moving to intervention construction [34]. Guided by principles of representativeness and expertise, 12 experts in behavioral health economics, health psychology, and cardiac rehabilitation were identified through experiential screening to participate in a collaborative design meeting. Selection criteria included: associate professor rank or higher; master’s degree or higher; and ≥ 10 years of experience in the relevant field. Behavioral health economics experts were responsible for structuring the core concepts of nudge theory and integrating/matching theoretical elements. Health psychology experts assessed the coupling and compatibility between theoretical elements and elucidated the psychosocial behavioral mechanisms of the program theory. Cardiac rehabilitation experts evaluated the applicability and feasibility of the underlying psychological strategies for patients with chronic heart failure. Ultimately, the program theory was developed and its hypothesized mechanisms were defined.

The enthusiasm of experts is measured by the response rate of invited experts. The authority coefficient is the average of the degree of familiarity with the field and the criterion of index judgment. The degree of consistency of expert opinions is determined by the Kendall concordance coefficient, which reflects the expert judgment coefficient and familiarity coefficient. The weights of the judgment coefficient and the familiarity coefficient are shown in Appendix 2. All thresholds were set at 0.70, and the significance level of the Kendall test was P < 0.05 [34, 35].

Phase III: Construction of the nudge strategy

Developing the digital nudge strategy

The Nominal Group Technique was adopted to develop the digital nudge strategy. Nominal Group Technique is a structured group decision-making method requiring members to independently generate solutions, followed by a controlled discussion phase ensuring all individual opinions are fully expressed, culminating in a consolidated solution [36]. This method balances individual creativity with group wisdom by structuring interaction in stages, making it suitable for decision-making scenarios requiring the integration of diverse perspectives.

A multidisciplinary research team was formed to ensure the scientific rigor and comprehensiveness of the nudge strategy development. Behavioral health economics experts and cardiac rehabilitation nursing specialists were responsible for designing the behavioral nudge components and related evaluation tools. Representatives of patients with chronic heart failure provided feedback on the challenges and difficulties of home-based cardiac rehabilitation management and participated in user testing and experience optimization of the draft strategy. A leadership group (responsible for coordination and decision-making) and a technical group (responsible for implementation) were established. Regular meetings and a dynamic feedback mechanism ensured research progress.

The Nominal Group Technique process comprised four stages [36]: (1) Silent Idea Generation: Members worked independently, writing down ideas and suggestions for constructing nudge strategies based on their professional knowledge, experience, and understanding of home-based cardiac rehabilitation management. (2) Idea Presentation: After the silent period, members presented their ideas one by one to the group. Discussion was strictly prohibited until all ideas were fully presented and recorded to maintain objectivity and comprehensiveness. (3) Group Discussion: The group engaged in an in-depth discussion of each idea. Members elaborated on their suggestions, explaining the underlying rationale, advantages, and potential issues. (4) Idea Ranking and Decision: Members independently ranked the ideas. Considering scientific merit, innovativeness, and alignment with practical realities, the nudge strategies were progressively refined and finalized.

Refining the digital nudge strategy

Stakeholder meetings were held to explore perspectives on the draft digital nudge strategy for home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise. Stakeholder engagement meetings were conducted to ground the theoretically-driven strategy in real-world clinical workflows and patient lived experiences. Incorporating end-user and implementer feedback is a core element of the MRC framework, enhancing the intervention’s acceptability, feasibility, and contextual appropriateness prior to formal piloting [37]. Experts from relevant fields and eligible patients were selected to discuss the strategy, aiming for further optimization.

Following principles of representativeness, authority, and expertise, the stakeholder participants included: two clinical cardiovascular physicians, two clinical cardiovascular nurses, two strategy developers/implementers from the research team, and two patients with chronic heart failure. Expert selection criteria were: associate professor rank or higher; master’s degree or higher; ≥10 years of relevant work experience. Patient selection criteria matched the inclusion/exclusion criteria from Phase I.

The theme of the stakeholder meeting was determined based on the purpose of the study. Theme 1: Is the theoretical framework of the digital nudge strategy sound? Theme 2: Is the digital nudge strategy scientifically sound and applicable? Theme 3: How adherent would patients be to this digital nudge strategy?

Prior to the meeting, participants were confirmed, and relevant information/documents were sent via email or WeChat. During the meeting, the facilitator introduced attendees, objectives, and procedures. The strategy developers then presented the digital nudge strategy in detail, inviting feedback and suggestions from experts and patients. Post-meeting, the research team transcribed the audio recordings, compiled meeting materials, conducted multiple rounds of group discussions, and finalized the revised digital nudge strategy.

Feedback from experts and patients gathered during the stakeholder meetings was systematically integrated to optimize the strategy. Following the development of the revised strategy, a second stakeholder meeting was convened to further enhance its scientific rigor and precision. The goal was to finalize an economical, convenient, patient-centered, nurse-led digital nudge strategy for home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise in patients with chronic heart failure, leveraging a digital health management platform.

Ethical considerations

This study strictly adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Review Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University (Approval No. 2024.36) prior to commencement. Researchers informed all participants about the study’s purpose, significance, and voluntary nature. Written informed consent was obtained from every participant before their formal involvement. Participant privacy and personal information were rigorously protected throughout the research. All questionnaires and audio recordings were used solely for this study and were not disclosed or used for other purposes without participant consent. In reporting the results, all participant information was anonymized to ensure full protection of privacy.

Questionnaire results revealed patients’ three most preferred nudge strategies were pre-commitment (34.6%), alerts and reminders (21.8%), and social norms (18.1%). Phase II: Determination of program theory

This phase employed an expert participatory collaborative design to formalize the program theory. As shown in Fig. 2, the program theory is determined based on the driving mechanism model and existing research evidence, combined with self-determination theory and nudge theory. Based on program theory, its mechanism assumptions are as follows: Economical and convenient nudge strategies, such as pre-commitment, alertation and reminder, and social norms, can to a certain extent respectively meet the exercise psychological needs (competence, autonomy, relatedness) of patients with chronic heart failure, continuously internalize their exercise regulation motivations, and make them feel that the motivation for self-determination will shift from the outside to the inside. This further leads to and long-term regular exercise adherence behavior.

12 invited experts participated via electronic mail correspondence, yielding 100% response rate. As presented in Table 2, the expert authority coefficient reached 0.813. Using 5-point Likert scales, experts evaluated the three-element framework’s content importance and theoretical coherence. Kendall concordance coefficient were 0.754 for content importance and 0.718 for theoretical alignment, both statistically significant at probability less than 0.05.

Phase III: Construction of nudge strategy

Developing the digital nudge strategy

The nominal group technique was implemented with four behavioral health economics specialists, four cardiac rehabilitation nursing experts, four health psychology researchers, and four chronic heart failure patient representatives. Ultimately, the initial draft of the digital nudge strategy was developed (Appendix 3).

Participants. Eligible patients met these criteria: confirmed chronic heart failure diagnosis; age eighteen years or older; currently undergoing home-based rehabilitation; provision of informed consent. Exclusion criteria included communication impairments, severe comorbidities, and absence of smartphone or messaging application access.

Intervention Setting and Duration. The behavioral intervention was delivered online through an established digital health management platform. Participants received intervention content via the WeChat application. The implementation period for the digital nudge strategy spanned three months. Outcome measurements for both primary and secondary endpoints were conducted at four time points: immediately following intervention completion, at one month post-intervention, at three months post-intervention, and at six months post-intervention. Research team members implemented all intervention components throughout the study duration.

Intervention Content. Building upon the research evidence and program theory, the initial draft of the digital digital nudge strategy was developed to enhance exercise adherence among chronic heart failure patients during home-based cardiac rehabilitation. The intervention components primarily encompassed commitment letter signing delivered in textual format, brief verbal alerts presented through audio recordings, exemplary case studies distributed via video materials, and health education sessions employing illustrated formats.

Refining the digital nudge strategy

Stakeholder consultation meetings generated substantive recommendations. Experts affirmed strong theoretical integration but advised revising public display protocols for commitment letters, adjusting health education frequency, diversifying behavioral approaches, and optimizing multimedia compatibility. Patients expressed strong acceptance, suggesting integration of illustrated content with audio narration, and enhancing real-life contextualization of multimedia materials. Discussion

Significance and innovativeness of the digital nudge strategy

This study developed a nurse-led digital nudge strategy to enhance exercise adherence in chronic heart failure patients during home-based cardiac rehabilitation. The approach innovatively integrates cutting-edge behavioral science principles into chronic disease management by applying nudge theory, while leveraging nurses’ pivotal role in long-term patient care. First, it deeply synthesizes nudge concepts with self-determination theory, employing carefully designed digital interventions—such as commitment mechanisms, social norm prompts, and decision simplification—to subtly guide patients toward initiating and sustaining exercise routines [38]. These components simultaneously address fundamental psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, thereby fostering intrinsic motivation [39]. This theoretical integration addresses a common gap identified in recent digital health literature, where interventions lacking a robust psychological framework frequently suffer from short-lived user engagement. Second, it establishes a nurse-led implementation model that combines nursing expertise in patient education, support, follow-up, and personalized guidance with the efficient outreach and automated management capabilities of digital technology [40]. While recent studies often highlight the utility of fully automated standalone health applications, emerging evidence suggests that integrating professional human oversight, such as our nurse-led approach, is crucial for mitigating digital attrition and enhancing long-term adherence in chronic disease populations. This synergy provides patients with continuous, accessible, and professional remote support. Finally, the strategy capitalizes on the WeChat platform’s extensive adoption and user-friendly interface as a delivery vehicle, enabling low-cost, high-efficiency, and scalable integration of nudge techniques into patients’ daily lives [41]. By converging behavioral science, nursing practice, and digital technology, this comprehensive intervention presents a promising new paradigm for addressing the persistent challenge of low rehabilitation adherence among home-based chronic heart failure patients. It holds significant potential to improve clinical outcomes, enhance quality of life, and alleviate healthcare system burdens.

Scientific rigor of the digital nudge strategy

The digital nudge strategy for home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise in chronic heart failure patients demonstrates methodologically robust foundations aligned with the Medical Research Council framework for complex interventions [28]. Integrating evidence from the driving mechanism model and scoping review [20, 29], the strategy synthesizes nudge principles with self-determination theory to establish a coherent program theory framework. This integration generates explicit causal hypotheses regarding intervention effectiveness. Self-determination theory posits that fulfilling basic psychological needs is essential for initiating and sustaining health behaviors [21]. Within this strategy, digitally delivered nudges target chronic heart failure patients’ fundamental psychological needs, thereby activating and progressively internalizing intrinsic exercise motivation to enhance rehabilitation adherence. Furthermore, nudge theory modifies choice architecture to steer patients toward healthier decisions without restricting options or altering economic incentives [22, 23]. The strategy incorporates multiple empirically validated nudges within a digital health platform, leveraging technology to amplify intervention efficacy and patient engagement through accessible delivery.

Stakeholder consultations further ensured clinical and practical scientific validity [37]. Cardiovascular clinicians, nurses, intervention developers, implementers, and chronic heart failure patients participated in structured meetings addressing three themes: theoretical coherence, scientific applicability, and patient adherence potential. Experts confirmed the strategy’s scientifically rigorous theoretical framework effectively activates exercise motivation and sustains adherence behaviors, while proposing refinements: optimizing public commitment displays, adjusting health education frequency, and diversifying nudge approaches. Patients recommended supplementing educational illustrations with audio narration and enhancing real-life contextualization of multimedia content. These iterative refinements through stakeholder engagement solidified both theoretical robustness and real-world operational feasibility, ensuring the strategy balances scientific rigor with practical applicability [42].

Practical applicability of the digital nudge strategy

The real-world success of digital nudge strategies hinges not only on scientific design but also on seamless implementation and acceptance by patients and healthcare providers. Leveraging a digital health management platform significantly enhances operational feasibility and scalability [43]. The intervention is delivered via the WeChat application-a widely adopted communication platform with user-friendly interfaces suitable for diverse age groups [44]. This accessibility enables patients to effortlessly receive intervention content while reducing administrative burdens for medical staff. Flexible delivery mechanisms further ensure nudges reach patients at optimal frequencies and formats, aligning with daily routines to reinforce health behaviors sustainably.

Patient acceptance and engagement constitute critical indicators of practical utility. During stakeholder consultations, participants expressed strong endorsement of the strategy, noting its ease of use and relevance to their needs. Research indicates that higher acceptance of digital content, particularly when combining multiple educational elements, improves comprehension and retention of health information [45]. Contextualized exemplary videos and life-oriented verbal alerts enhanced personal relevance, fostering deeper engagement and strengthening long-term motivation for home-based rehabilitation [46]. These high acceptance and engagement levels substantiate the strategy’s practical effectiveness.

Despite these advantages, implementation challenges exist, including limited familiarity with WeChat among some elderly patients. Proactive mitigation strategies address these barriers: structured digital literacy tutorials enhance technological proficiency, while end-to-end encryption protocols safeguard data privacy. Furthermore, the strategy’s inherent flexibility allows personalized adaptations to individual needs, effectively accommodating real-world complexities. These measures collectively ensure the digital nudge strategy maintains scientific rigor while demonstrating robust practical applicability in clinical and community settings.

While the mixed-methods approach provided a comprehensive foundation for intervention development, several inherent limitations must be acknowledged. First, Phase I relied on self-reported questionnaire data to determine patient preferences, which is susceptible to response and recall biases. Second, while the Nominal Group Technique and stakeholder meetings in Phases II and III utilized structured protocols, these qualitative consensus methods may still be subject to selection bias based on the specific composition of the expert and patient panels. Furthermore, the patient sample was drawn from a specific geographic region, which may limit the generalizability of the chosen nudge preferences to broader populations. Finally, as a developmental and formative study guided by the early phases of the MRC framework, this research establishes the theoretical and practical viability of the digital nudge strategy but does not evaluate its clinical efficacy. Future randomized controlled trials are essential to rigorously test the intervention’s actual impact on long-term exercise adherence and cardiovascular outcomes.

Conclusion

Guided by the Medical Research Council framework for developing and evaluating complex interventions, this study established a digital nudge strategy for home-based cardiac rehabilitation exercise among chronic heart failure patients, leveraging a digital information management platform. The strategy integrates evidence from the driving mechanism model and scoping review findings to deliver a scientifically grounded intervention. By utilizing an accessible digital health management system, the approach enhances operational feasibility and patient engagement, while established instructional resources and adaptable implementation protocols ensure seamless real-world execution. This methodology effectively bridges theoretical rigor with practical application in chronic disease management.

Characterization and effect of Naga Bhasma on hypoglycemic, antihyperglycemic, and antidiabetic activities in Charles Foster diabetic rats
Authors: Pravin Tate, B.J. Patgiri, P.K. Prajapati, B. Ravishankar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDiabetes, typically characterized by hyperglycaemia, is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality. Over 800 million adults were estimated to have diabetes in 2022. Ayurveda texts emphasized on the antidiabetic potential of Naga Bhasma (NB) (a lead based formulation). Naga Bhasma is used in many classical antidiabetic formulations such as Vasantkusumakara Rasa.ObjectivesPresent study was planned to evaluate hypoglycemic, antihyperglycemic, and antidiabetic activities of Naga Bhasma in euglycemic, sucrose loaded hyperglycemic and streptozotocin induced diabetic Charles Foster albino rats.Material and methodsNB was prepared following classical texts of Ayurveda. Charles Foster albino rats were used for antidiabetic study. The study was divided into: hypoglycaemic, antihyperglycemic, and antidiabetic parts. Hypoglycaemic study was aimed to assess glucose lowering activity in normal rats. Antihyperglycemic study was carried out to assess the effect of test drug on blood sugar elevation observed after administration of sucrose (40 g/kg orally). For antidiabetic study, streptozotocin was injected intraperitoneally (40 mg/kg) to induce diabetes. Vehicles were administered on 3rd day of streptozotocin injection, after confirmation of diabetes. Blood sugar was checked before and on 5th day after vehicle administration. The data was analysed by unpaired ‘t’ test.ResultsHypoglycaemic study did not show any significant effect on blood glucose in NB group. In Antihyperglycemic study, NB administered group showed only statistically non-significant increase in blood sugar level at 4th and 8th hour indicating presence of anti-hyperglycemic activity. In antidiabetic study, in control group, a statistically significant (48.90 %) elevation of blood sugar level was observed while in NB administered group highly significant (69.58 %) decrease was observed in comparison to initial values.ConclusionThe study shows that NB has moderate anti-hyperglycaemic and good anti-diabetic activity; and it does not lower the blood glucose below the normal levels.

Introduction

Diabetes, typically characterized by hyperglycaemia, is a chronic state of insulin resistance and/or insulin deficiency. Over 800 million adults were estimated to have diabetes in 2022 [1]. Type II diabetes mellitus (Type II DM) accounts for the vast majority (over 90 %) of diabetes worldwide. India and China possess nearly half of the global diabetic population and nearly one-half of the people with type II DM in the population may be undiagnosed. Delay in diagnosis, failure to treat or delay in the treatment raise the incidence of complications such as neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, heart disease, stroke etc. The causes of type II DM are not completely understood. Obesity and sedentary lifestyle, increased age, family history, ethnicity are more commonly associated with diabetes [2]. Diabetes manifests in a range of phenotypes and characterizing these phenotypes is important for ‘precision medicine’ approach. Indian people are younger age at diagnosis, have greater tendency towards β-cell failure and leaner body habitus, as compared with people of European descent and have also revealed unique responses to certain glucose-lowering medications. Examining these factors might help in developing precision medicine for diabetes [3]. India is experiencing rapid demographic and epidemiological transitions with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) causing significant disability, morbidity and mortality both in urban and rural populations and across all socioeconomic strata. Diabetes is one of the four major NCDs that contributed to nearly 58 % of the premature mortality in the age group 30–69 years [4].

Ayurveda literature has described pathophysiology and various types of Prameha (diabetes) in detail, along with its symptoms, complications and management [5]. Naga Bhasma (a lead based formulation) (NB) is a traditional medicine indicated for the treatment of a wide range of diseases. Many of the Ayurvedic antidiabetic formulations contain Naga Bhasma (e.g. Vasantkusumakara Rasa).

According to modern science, lead is a metallic irritant poison [6]. The heavy metal causes its toxicity by altering protein structure and through molecular mimicking of divalent metals like calcium. Lead toxicity occurs in two forms viz. acute lead toxicity and chronic lead toxicity (plumbism). Symptoms may vary according to concentration of lead in blood (blood lead levels) [7]. Although modern science depicts the toxic effects of lead consumption, properly prepared and cautiously used NB does not produce deleterious effects [8,9]. Green formulation is a term used for developing products and/or processes which minimize or eliminate use of hazardous substances, reduce waste, and prefer natural or renewable resources. It is especially used in pharmaceutical preparations. Nanoparticles prepared by using green formulation technique are widely used in treatment of cancer, and for enhanced drug delivery. It has reduced environmental impact due to minimum use of resources, increased safety due to reduced dose of chemicals, increased sustainability due to use of renewable resources and sustainable practices. Green formulations are prepared by using metallic or mineral origin substances along with herbal ingredients employing green chemistry principles like catalysis and atom economy. In these preparations, plant extracts are used as reducing agents. All the Ayurvedic preparations containing drugs of metallic and/or mineral origin (Bhasmas/incineration products) are processed with herbal extracts; but the key difference in between Bhasmas and green formulations is the process of incineration in case of Bhasmas (heating up to 7500 C). Although it is believed that organic part during Bhasma preparation gets burnt completely; some researches have found carbonaceous part in the final product [10,11]. Therefore herbs used in the preparation of Bhasmas may hay impact on their potency.

Ayurveda texts emphasized on the antidiabetic action of NB. Therefore, present study was carried out to evaluate efficacy of NB for hypoglycaemic activity (glucose lowering action below the normal blood glucose level) in euglycemic rats, antihyperglycemic activity in sucrose loaded Wistar strain albino rats; and antidiabetic activity in streptozotocin induced diabetes confirmed rats.

Material and methods

Drug (NB)

NB was prepared by the scholar himself, following Shodhana (general and specific purification), Pishti-Nirmana (amalgam preparation), and Bhasmikarana (incineration) process. The process of incineration was carried out with the help of purified mercury, purified sulphur and Nimbu Swarasa (juice of Citrus medica var acida of Watt). One part of purified lead was added to purified mercury, which was one forth in amount to lead, followed by trituration with one part of purified sulphur and Nimbu Swarasa. Pellets were formed and after drying, subjected to incineration. Total 30 incineration cycles were applied to form NB. A detailed characterization along with acute and chronic toxicity study of NB was also carried out [8,9].

Drug analysis

A detailed characterization including particle size analysis, X-ray diffraction (XRD), AES–ICP (Atomic emission spectrophotometry with inductively coupled plasma) analysis, and Namburi Phased Spot test (NPS) was carried out for NB [9]. For analysis, NB was coded as NM-1(30).

Particle size analysis

The sample was analysed in Sympatec HELOS (H1004) instrument, by taking water as dispersion medium and measured at 0.5/4.5–875 μm range.

X-ray diffraction

X-ray diffraction pattern was obtained from ICSD using-POWD-12++, (1997), Hill, R.J., Acta Crystallogar, using wavelength K-Alpha1; and matched against the standard reference. X-ray diffraction is determined by Braggs law (nλ = 2dsinθ). This law relates wavelength (λ) to diffraction angle (θ) and atomic spacing of the lattice within a crystalline sample (d-spacing). By using a monochromatic X-ray, the d-spacing can be determined for a specific angle. Rotating either the sample or the detector through a full range of angles creates a full profile of the material to be measured. The angle of incidence and angle of reflection are same, therefore after diffraction, X-ray beam travels at an angle of 2θ from the incident beam. A continuous plot of 2θ versus intensity is recorded over the angle range (1–100°).

Atomic emission Spectroscopy with inductively coupled plasma (AES-ICP)

Preparation of test solution: 0.250 g sample of NB was taken and 5 ml HNO3 and 5 ml HCl were added in a closed vessel device using temperature control microwave heating at 2000 C for 30 min. After cooling, it was filtered and washed by deionized water and made 100 ml solution. The test was carried out in Optima 3300 RL model of Inductive Coupled Plasma Spectrometer. The instrument was calibrated by using reference standard of 100 ppm. Reference standard identification: C.P.A.Ltd. Bulgeria.

Namburi Phased Spot test

0.5 g of Naga Bhasma was put in 1 ml of 5 N HNO3 and kept for 72 h while shaking it in between. 2 drops of this solution was put on potassium iodide impregnated paper and was observed for 72 h. The study was divided into three phases as following:

Phase I: This phase extends from application of spot to 5 min (0–5 min).

Phase II: This phase extends from 5 min of spot application to 20 min (5–20 min).

Phase III: This phase extends from 20 min of spot application to few hours. In this study, the final point was set to the time from which no colour change was observed. The time was 72 h of spot application. (20 min–72 h).

Dose determination

NB was the test drug, while for control group, distilled water was used. The dose for experimental study was calculated with the help of body surface area ratio by referring to the table of Paget and Barnes [12]. According to Rasatarangini, a classical text of Ayurveda, the dose of NB is 30–125 mg [13]. The dose in rats was calculated as follows:Rat dose = Human adult dose × body surface area ratio convertible factor= 120 mg × 0.018 (for 200 g body weight of rat)= 2.16 mg/ 200 g body weight of rat, which was approximated to 2.5 mg/ 200 g body weight of rat.= 12.5 mg/ kg body weight

The dose was fixed to 12.5 mg/kg body weight (Therapeutic Equivalent Dose-TED).

Route of drug administration

The drug was administered orally, in the form of a suspension. To prepare a suspension, NB was taken in a porcelain Kharal and 5 drops of 5 % gum acacia solution was added to it. The mixture was triturated and distilled water was added to it till the required amount of drug contained in 1 mL.

Hypoglycaemic and antihyperglycemic study

Hypoglycaemic study was aimed to look for glucose lowering activity in normal (euglycemic) rats. Blood glucose level was observed before and 1 h after drug administration. Antihyperglycemic study was carried out to see the effect of test drug on blood sugar elevation observed after administration of sucrose (40 g/kg orally). For antihyperglycemic study, blood glucose level was observed before and 2, 4, and 8 h after the drug administration.

Antidiabetic study

For antidiabetic study, streptozotocin was injected intraperitoneally at a dose of 40 mg/kg (immediately after preparation with citrate buffer) to induce diabetes. Vehicles (placebo for control group and NB for test drug group) were administered on 3rd day of streptozotocin injection, after confirmation of diabetes. Blood glucose was checked before and on 5 days after vehicle administration.

Estimation of blood glucose

The blood collected from individual rats through supraorbital puncture was fed in to the auto-anlyzer, which was pre-programmed to carry out the glucose estimations.

Serum glucose (GOD – POD method end point)

Methodology: As per Trinder's method.

Principle: Trinder's method is used to determine glucose or glucose oxidase using colorimetry. It is a reaction between hydrogen peroxide, phenol and aminoantipyrine to form a quinone (quinoneimine), catalyzed by the presence of a peroxidase (such as horseradish peroxidase). The hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is itself produced by an initial reaction where the glucose is oxidised in the presence of the glucose oxidase catalyst into H2O2 and gluconic acid. The intensity of the pink colour formed is proportional to glucose concentration. It can be measured photometrically between 490 and 540 nm.

Reagent composition (when reconstituted as directed)

Reagent 1: glucose reagent

Active ingredients are as follows: Glucose oxidase (Aspergillus) (20,000 IU/L), Peroxidase (horseradish) (3250 IU/L), 4-aminoantipyrine (0.52 mmol/L), 4-hydroxybenzoic Acid (10 mmol/L), Phosphate buffer (110 mmol/L).

Reagent 2

Glucose standard with concentration of 100 mg/dl (5.55 mmol/L).

Reagent reconstitution

The vial as allowed to attain room temperature. The contents of each vial were dissolved using glucose diluent. Volume was made to 500 ml and transferred into a clean and dry amber coloured bottle.

Statistical analysis

All the values were expressed as mean ± SEM (Standard Error of Mean). The data was analysed by unpaired ‘t’ test. A level of P < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant and the value of P < 0.01 or P < 0.001 is considered statistically highly significant. Level of significance was noted and interpreted accordingly.

Discussion

Detailed analysis of NB showed that its average volumetric diameter was 43.4 μm. Particle size may vary depending upon the preparation method and/or number of incineration cycles. Agglomeration of particles may also increase the particle size.

XRD analysis showed that the Naga Bhasma was in the form of lead sulphide (PbS). The ICP-AES analysis showed the percentage of lead as 21.112 %. As the ICPAES analysis detects elements in crystalline form, the percentage of lead in NB might have dropped denoting its mixed nature (crystalline as well as amorphous).

The test drug, NB, was found not to affect the blood glucose level in normal rats to a significant extent (5.95 % statistically non-significant increase in trial group and 11.07 % statistically non-significant decrease in control group) suggesting its lack of hypoglycaemic effect in normal euglycemic rats. The test drug also produced anti-hyperglycaemic effect against sucrose loading induced hyperglycaemia (24.65 % statistically significant increase after 2 h, and statistically non-significant increase of 8.36 %, and 9.88 % in blood glucose levels after 4 and 8 h respectively in trial group versus 24.37 %, 20.70 % and 20.50 % statistically significant increase in blood glucose levels after 2, 4, and 8 h respectively in control group). This may be due to increased insulin secretion or decrease in insulin-resistance which facilitates the cellular entry of glucose.

NB showed statistically highly significant decrease in blood glucose level in streptozotocin induced diabetes confirmed rats (statistically significant 69.58 % decrease in trial group versus statistically significant 48.90 % increase in control group). Streptozotocin is a toxic glucose analogue that preferentially accumulate in pancreatic beta cells via the GLUT2 glucose transporter. Following its uptake into the beta cells, streptozotocin is split into its glucose and methylnitrosourea moiety. Methylnitrosourea has alkylating properties, and modifies biological macromolecules, fragments DNA and destroys the beta cells, causing a state of insulin-dependent diabetes. This also inhibits glucose-induced insulin secretion [14]. As streptozotocin destroys beta cells in pancreas through its DNA alkylating action, inducing diabetes, NB might have helped in reversing the beta cell damage through its rejuvenative action, which showed remarkable glucose lowering effect in streptozotocin induced diabetic rats. This needs to be further confirmed. However, reduction in blood glucose in streptozotocin induced diabetic rats clearly shows that NB is a good antidiabetic agent. Taviad K et al. also found similar effects in a double-blind randomized controlled clinical trial [15].

Although various raw metals and minerals used in Ayurveda may have toxicity potential, they are advised to be used after various transformation procedures viz. Shodhana (purification), Marana (incineration), etc. Ayurvedic literature advices various herbs for the process of purification and incineration. A single metal or mineral typically has more than one method of incineration, using at least one herbal medium. The reasons of using various herbs for the incineration might be as follows.1.Herbs may enhance the potency of the mineral through synergism.2.Herbs may enhance target specific activity of the mineral to be incinerated.3.Availability of the herbs. In the ancient times, specific herbs might not be available to all the physicians at different geographic locations, who used to prepare medicines for their patients. Therefore, local herbs might have been used for the incineration process, specific to that geographic locations.4.Nano-materials of metals/minerals are formed during the incineration process. Herbs used during the incineration process get converted into various carbonaceous nano-materials. These form complexes with metallic/mineral nanoparticles in the Bhasma through physisorption and chemisorption. This conjugation may be responsible for the therapeutic activity of the Incinerated forms of metals and minerals (Bhasmas). Different herbs produce different compounds and complexes, resulting in altered therapeutic activity and potency of the same metal/mineral [11].

Bhasmas contain various organic materials and trace elements along with the main compound of that metal being incinerated. Presence of extraneous elements in Bhasma is a result of herbal drugs used in the preparation. The main compound/inorganic content is likely chelated with the organic ligands derived from herbal medium used in the preparation of Bhasmas [16]. Various research works have been carried out which show that metallic/mineral nanoparticles synthesized using herbs exert beneficial therapeutic effects. Copper nanoparticles coated with ocimum bassilicum showed beneficial effects in colon carcinoma. It decreased cell viability and the expression of BCL2 gene (anti-apoptotic) and increased the expression of BAX gene (pro-apoptotic) [27]. Copper nanoparticles prepared by green fabrication using Acroptilon repens extract showed enhanced anti-lung cancer activity in lung adenocarcinoma cell line (PC14) by regulating PC14 cell apoptosis and proliferation through PI3K-Akt-mTOR signalling pathway regulation [28]. Ziziphora clinopodioides green‐synthesized silver nanoparticles decreased fasting blood glucose levels and showed moderate hepatoprotective activity in streptozotocin induced diabetes and hepatotoxicity in Wistar diabetic male rats [29]. Green synthesized iron nanoparticles using Mentha spicata extract showed promising results on LS174t colon cancer cells and the effects were slightly better than Mentha spicata and Fe nanoparticles alone, separately [30]. Iron nanoparticles (FeNPs) containing Glycyrrhiza glabra L leaf produced better cytotoxicity in CCRF-CEM cell line than FeCl3 and Glycyrrhiza glabra alone [31]. Green-synthesized iron nanoparticles prepared using Alhagi maurorum leaf aqueous extract showed antioxidant and anti-human lung cancer activity in HLC-1 and PC-14 lung cancer cell lines [32]. Copper nanoparticles synthesized using Thymus fedtschenkoi leaves extract showed significant growth-inhibiting effect on the lung cancer cell lines [33]. Copper nanoparticles containing Allium eriophyllum Boiss leaf aqueous extract was found to possess higher antifungal, antibacterial and cutaneous wound healing properties than CuSO4 and Allium eriophyllum alone, separately [34]. Cu immobilized on chitosan-modified iron oxide magnetic nanoparticles [Fe3O4/CS/Cu(II) nanocomposite] were observed to possess antioxidant and anti-lung cancer activities [35]. Copper nanoparticles green-synthesized by Camellia sinensis leaf aqueous extract had better antioxidant and anti-human ovarian cancer properties; especially in SW-626 cell lines, when compared to CuCl2⋅2H2O and Camellia sinensis leaf aqueous extract [36]. Silver nanoparticles synthesized through the mediation of methanolic extract of Heracleum persicum seeds was found effective in human breast adenocarcinoma cell lines [37]. Gold nanoparticles immobilized pectin modified magnetic nanoparticles (Fe3O4/Pectin/Au) showed significant anticancer activity in adenocarcinoma cell lines [38]. Bi-metallic nanocomposite of nickel and zinc synthesized using an aqueous extract of Citrus sinensis in presence of chitosan (Ni/Zn@orange/chitosan) was observed to possess anti-breast cancer activity [39]. All these studies indicate that herbs increase the effects and target specific activity of nanomaterials.

NB was prepared using juice of Citrus medica fruits. The fruits are used for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiviral, and antihyperglycemic properties. They contain flavonoids (apigenin, hesperetin, hesperidin, naringin, naringenin, rutin, quercetin, and diosmin), coumarins (citropten, scoparone, and bergapten), terpenes (limonene, γ-terpinene, limonin, and nomilin), and phenolic acids (p-coumaric acid, trans-ferulic acid, and chlorogenic acid) along with carbohydrates, minerals, amino acids, and vitamins. Antidiabetic, anticancer, and neuroprotective activities of Citrus fruits are being vigorously tested in recent time [40,41]. Mahmoud AM et al. found that hesperidin and naringin, flavonoids from the citrus fruits, when used in high fat-fed/streptozotocin-induced diabetic rats, significantly reduced the levels of glucose and glycosylated haemoglobin while serum insulin level and hepatic and muscle glycogen were increased. Activity of enzyme hexokinase was significantly increased and activities of glucose-6-phosphatase, glycogen phosphorylase, and fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase were significantly decreased; overall results showing that both the compounds have potent antihyperglycemic activity [42,43].

The antidiabetic potential of citrus fruits may have further potentiated the antidiabetic and antihyperglycemic effect of NB through synergism. However, further research is necessary on this regard; especially because NB is prepared through ‘incineration’ process.

Limitations of the study

Results obtained in animal experimentation might not replicate in humans; therefore, more clinical trials on large number of subjects are necessary to confirm the same.

Conclusion

The study shows that NB has moderate anti-hyperglycaemic and good anti-diabetic activity; and it does not lower the blood glucose below the normal levels of blood glucose.

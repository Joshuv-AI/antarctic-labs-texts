Protocol for isothermal crystallization of photovoltaic perovskite films based on volatile solvents
Authors: Bowen Jin, Yidong Ming, Jinguo Cao, Lizhi Ren, Hong Liu, Kai Wang, Congcong Wu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe efficient functioning and stability of a perovskite photoactive layer are paramount to the performance of solar cell devices. Here, we present a protocol for the synthesis of a high-performance exemplified methylammonium lead iodide (CH3NH3PbI3 or MAPbI3) perovskite photoactive layer. We describe steps for preparing the requisite ratios of the precursor powders, synthesizing MAPbI3 single crystals, and selecting a suitable preparation technique. We then detail a flexible doping strategy for the perovskite photoactive layer.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Wang and Wu (2020, 2022, 2023).1,2,3Subject areas: Energy, Chemistry, Material Sciences

Before you begin

The protocol below describes the specific steps for the preparation of high-quality MAPbI3 perovskite films with ultrahigh (110) preferred orientation based on volatile solvents. This protocol is also applicable to the preparation of perovskite photoactive layers of other compositions (e.g., MAPbIxBr3-x).

Materials and equipment

Methylamine solution (33 wt% in absolute ethanol) is stored in a refrigerator at 2°C–8°C. DMF, DMSO, ACN, GBL, MAI, and PbI2 are placed in an N2-filled glovebox (O2 < 0.1 ppm; H2O < 0.1 ppm).

CRITICAL: Acetone and diethyl ether are flammable chemicals. One should wear a face mask, anticorrosive gloves, and experimental uniforms and operate in the fume cupboard for safety.

Alternatives: The general chemicals (e.g., acetone, isopropanol, and ethanol, etc.) used in this protocol can be replaced by other sources. The instruments applied in this protocol can be replaced by those which own similar functions.

Expected outcomes

Without complex post-treatment processes

Nucleation crystallization techniques, typically employed with traditional precursor-based perovskite films, necessitate intricate post-treatment procedures to swiftly remove high boiling point solvents, thereby attaining supersaturation of the damp film. However, these post-treatment processes usually demand meticulous control of operational parameters, which can be challenging. Contrastingly, employing highly volatile solvent-based perovskite films can facilitate the swift fabrication of superior quality films at room temperature, completely obviating the need for post-treatment. This distinct advantage of perovskite solution preparation opens up fresh avenues for large-scale production of perovskite solar cells, which could significantly expedite the development and deployment of this promising technology.

Ultrahigh (110) preferred orientation

The fabricated perovskite film exhibits an ultrahigh (110) preferred orientation. The perovskite film synthesized by the liquefied perovskite showed preferred orientation due to the unique crystallization features whereby the perovskite crystals are formed by stacking the perovskite sheet intermediate rather than the conventional nucleation process. XRD analysis was performed to illustrate the orientation of the conventional precursor-based perovskite film and volatile solution-based perovskite film, as shown in Figure 6A. The diffraction intensity of the perovskite layer synthesized by volatile solution was approximately four times higher than that of conventional precursor-based perovskite film, which is due to the peculiar crystallization characteristics whereby the perovskite crystals are formed by stacking the perovskite sheet intermediate instead of the conventional nucleation process. Figures 6B and 6C present the cross-sectional SEM of the conventional precursor-based perovskite layer and the volatile solution-based perovskite layer, respectively. The conventional precursor-based perovskite layer exhibits randomly stacked perovskite crystals, while the volatile solution-based perovskite layer displays a more ordered structure with columnar crystals vertical to the substrate, which is consistent with the analysis of XRD results, demonstrating the ultrahigh (110) preferred orientation of the perovskite films prepared by the volatile solvent.

Core-shell structure

The flexible doping strategy for the perovskite photoactive layer presents a unique advantage. With the introduction of additives, the perovskite crystals can form a core-shell structure, significantly enhancing the layer’s stability. Moreover, distinct types of additives perform unique roles in the nucleation and growth of the perovskite crystals. For instance, ethyl cellulose (EC) serves as a 'fiber-bridging' component, interlinking the neighboring perovskite halide octahedra. This interaction effectively increases the Young’s modulus and hardness of the perovskite films, thereby providing robust resistance to crack propagation, as depicted schematically in Figure 7A. Simultaneously, silk fibroin (SF) can act as a molecular template to induce the orientational growth of the perovskite crystals. This can lead to a more controlled and desirable crystalline structure in the perovskite layer, as illustrated in Figure 7B. Therefore, this strategic utilization of additives offers a viable path towards enhancing the stability and performance of perovskite solar cells.

Preparation of large-scale perovskite thin films

In contrast to traditional precursor solvents like DMF, DMSO, ACN possesses a higher vapor pressure and supersaturation. This trait makes it compatible with the perovskite solution, enabling the wet film to dry in ambient air. During the preparation process, the rapid evaporation of the solvent induces perovskite sheet intermediates to swiftly stack, thereby forming a compact and uniform perovskite film. As this ACN-based perovskite solution eliminates the need for any post-treatment, the perovskite layer can be synthesized in a reproducible and feasible manner using a straightforward one-step deposition. This simplicity and efficiency render the process highly suitable for industrial manufacturing. For instance, it can be applied to techniques such as blade and slot-die coating, as illustrated in Figure 7C. The inherent advantages of this approach herald substantial potential for scaling and commercial application of perovskite-based technologies.

Limitations

This protocol should be applied carefully as there are limitations. This protocol is also applicable to other methylammonium-based perovskite compositions (e.g., MAPb(I1-xClx)3 (Wang et al., 2020)),3 but not to formamidine-based perovskite compositions.10

Development of an interpretable machine learning model for predicting intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Rui Liu, Yu Zhang, Shengjin Han, Zhengwu Zhou, Jianzhong Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo develop a machine learning model for predicting intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia and interpret the model using SHapley additive exPlanations (SHAP) to assist clinicians in early identification of intestinal necrosis and formulating more appropriate treatment strategies.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed 324 older patients with incarcerated hernia who were admitted to the Department of Emergency Surgery and Department of Hernia and Abdominal Wall Surgery at the Affiliated Lu’an Hospital of Anhui Medical University between March 2015 and February 2024. Clinical data were collected, and patients were divided into training and testing sets in a 7:3 ratio using stratified random sampling. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator was applied for feature selection, and nine machine learning models were constructed. The optimal model was further analyzed using SHAP for interpretability.ResultsAmong the 324 patients, 81 developed intestinal necrosis, while 243 did not. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression identified nine key features. Nine machine learning algorithms were evaluated: (a) K-nearest neighbors; (b) support vector machine; (c) extreme gradient boosting; (d) approximate nearest neighbor; (e) decision tree; (f) light gradient boosting machine; (g) random forest; (h) extremely randomized trees; and (i) gradient boosting machine. The extremely randomized trees model demonstrated the best performance on the test set, with a sensitivity of 0.755, specificity of 0.932, F1-score of 0.714, accuracy of 0.796, and receiver operating characteristic area under the curve of 0.785. SHAP analysis revealed that the top nine contributing variables were procalcitonin, absolute neutrophil count, D-dimer, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, hernia disease time, duration of incarceration, femoral hernia incarceration, lymphocyte count, and C-reactive protein.ConclusionThis study developed an efficient and interpretable extremely randomized trees model for predicting intestinal necrosis risk in older incarcerated hernia patients, which can aid clinicians in identifying high-risk patients and optimizing clinical decision-making.Keywords: Older adults, incarcerated hernia, intestinal necrosis, machine learning, SHapley additive exPlanations

Introduction

Incarcerated hernia refers to a pathological condition where tissues or organs within the abdominal cavity protrude through a defect or weak point in the abdominal wall and become trapped, unable to be reduced back into the abdominal cavity. If not promptly addressed, it can lead to severe complications such as intestinal necrosis. Statistical data indicate that approximately 15%–21% of cases progress to intestinal necrosis, necessitating bowel resection. The incidence may be higher in older patients due to their reduced sensitivity to pain. 1 In current clinical practice, emergency management of incarcerated hernia involves manual reduction followed by elective surgery in the absence of obvious intestinal strangulation or necrosis. However, if intestinal necrosis is suspected, emergency surgery is required, with an average mortality rate as high as 4%.2,3 Preoperative assessment of intestinal viability is crucial for making treatment decisions. Therefore, developing a predictive model to determine the presence of intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia holds clinical significance.

With the application of big data and artificial intelligence technologies in the medical field, machine learning (ML) has demonstrated excellent performance in predicting clinical outcomes. It can analyze vast amounts of patient data and identify early disease characteristics, thereby enabling early diagnosis. 4 This, in turn, assists clinicians in making more informed treatment decisions and improving patient prognosis. However, ML models often exhibit a “black box” nature, meaning that the internal mechanisms of the model and the basis for its predictions are difficult to interpret. This is unacceptable in the medical field as healthcare professionals need to understand how the model makes predictions and recommendations based on patient characteristics and which features significantly influence the prediction outcomes.

SHapley additive exPlanations (SHAP) values assign a contribution score to each feature, indicating the impact of that feature on the prediction outcome. It provides an explanation for each individual prediction sample, detailing the magnitude and direction of each feature's contribution to the prediction result for that specific sample. This enables physicians to better understand the decision-making process of the model and helps clarify the influence of each input feature on the prediction outcome. 5

Based on this, in the present study, we constructed a ML model to predict the presence or absence of intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia using blood inflammatory markers and relevant clinical characteristic parameters at admission. Additionally, SHAP is employed to perform interpretability analysis on the model. This approach assists clinicians in early determination of intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia, thereby enabling the formulation of more appropriate treatment decisions and improving patient prognosis.

Materials and methods

Data source

This retrospective cohort study included 324 patients with incarcerated hernia who were admitted to the Department of Emergency Surgery and the Hernia & Abdominal Wall Surgery Unit at Lu’an Hospital Affiliated to Anhui Medical University between March 2015 and February 2024. There were 60 cases of femoral hernia and 264 cases of inguinal hernia; the age of the patients ranged from 60 to 83 years.

The following inclusion criteria were applied: (a) patients with incarcerated hernia requiring emergency surgery; (c) presence of hernia content in the intestinal tissue; (3) patients aged ≥60 years; and (4) relatively complete clinical and laboratory medical records. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) studies that only accepted patients who chose to go undergo manual reduction or opted for scheduled surgery; (b) hernia contents composed of non-intestinal tissues such as the omentum; (c) contraindications to combined surgery; and (d) colorectal tumor or Crohn's disease. Ethical approval for this study (KY-2025-065-01) was granted by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Lu’an Hospital Affiliated to Anhui Medical University.

Diagnosis criteria for intestinal necrosis.6,7 The intestines are purple-black or greenish-blue in color, with no luster or elasticity, there is no vascular pulsation in the mesentery, no peristalsis in the intestines, and covering the intestines with warm moist gauze for a period of time or injecting procaine does not improve the condition of the intestines. Postoperative pathology suggests intestinal necrosis.

Methods

Data collection and missing data handling

Data were collected for the following parameters: (a) demographics, including sex (male/female) and age; (b) medical history of including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), hypertension, diabetes, heart disease (HD), history of abdominal surgery (HAS), incarcerated hernia history (IHI), and renal failure); (3) clinical presentation of femoral hernia incarceration (FHI), indirect inguinal hernia (IH), intestinal obstruction (IO), duration of incarceration (<24/≥24 h), and hernia disease time (HDT); (d) medication use, including history of anticoagulant use (HAC); and (e) laboratory measures, including C-reactive protein (CRP), D-dimer, procalcitonin (PCT), white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), red cell distribution width (RDW), lymphocyte count (LYC), body mass index (BMI), red blood cell count (RBC), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cr), triglycerides (TGs), hemoglobin (Hb), albumin (ALB), and absolute neutrophil count (NEUT).

Missing data handling

Missing data (all variables had missing rates <10%) were addressed using multiple imputation. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1975, as revised in 2024. All patient details have been de-identified in this study to protect participant confidentiality. This study was reported in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines. 8

Model construction and evaluation

The patients were divided into training and test sets in a 7:3 ratio using stratified random sampling. The importance of all variables was ranked using the light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM) algorithm. Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) was used to select the features of the variables. Nine of the 33 features were selected for ML model construction, respectively: PCT, NEUT, D-dimer, NLR, HDT, duration of incarceration, FHI, LYC, and CRP. Predictive models were subsequently developed using nine ML models, including K-nearest neighbor (KNN), support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), approximate nearest neighbor (ANN), decision tree (DT), LightGBM, random forest (RF), extremely randomized trees (ET), gradient boosting machine (GBM).

ML explainable tool

The prediction model was interpreted using SHAP, a comprehensive method for accurately calculating the contribution to and impact of each feature on the final predictions. 9 The SHAP values indicate the extent to which each predictor positively or negatively influences the target variable. Additionally, a specific set of SHAP values can be used to interpret each observation in the dataset.10,11

Statistical analyses

Normally distributed continuous data were expressed as mean ± SD ( x¯ ± SD), and intergroup comparisons were performed using independent samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed continuous data were presented as median (interquartile range) values, (M(P25, P75)), and the Mann–Whitney U test was used for intergroup comparisons. Categorical data were described as frequencies (percentages), and the chi-square test was performed for comparisons. The model was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, confusion matrix, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and F1-score. SHAP plots, generated using Python, were employed for interpretability analysis of the ML model. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Explanation of the ET model using the SHAP method

As illustrated in the SHAP summary plots (Figure 6(a) and (b)), the contribution of each feature to the model was assessed by the mean SHAP value and ranked in descending order. Additionally, SHAP dependence plots were used to elucidate how individual features influenced the output of the predictive model. Figure 7 displays the relationship between the actual values of these nine features and their corresponding SHAP values, where a SHAP value >0 indicates a positive class prediction by the model (i.e. a higher risk of intestinal necrosis). Elevated CRP, and D-dimer, and PCT levels and high NEUT and NLR were associated with an increased risk of intestinal necrosis, whereas higher LYC and HDT were correlated with a reduced risk. Furthermore, a duration of incarceration >24 h and the presence of FHI were associated with a markedly elevated risk of intestinal necrosis.

Discussion

The objective of this study was to construct a ML model for predicting the presence or absence of intestinal necrosis in older incarcerated hernia patients using ML techniques and to perform interpretability analysis of the model using the SHAP method. We collected clinical data from 323 previously treated older incarcerated hernia patients. Through data processing and feature selection, nine ML models were developed. A comprehensive evaluation of various metrics revealed that the ET model demonstrated high predictive value in both training and test datasets, with high sensitivity, specificity, and AUC values. This indicates that the ET model exhibits strong discriminative and calibration capabilities in predicting intestinal necrosis in older incarcerated hernia patients. Therefore, we selected the ET model as the optimal model and performed interpretability analysis using SHAP values. SHAP values can quantify the contribution of each feature to the model's predictions while revealing the nonlinear relationships between features and the predicted outcomes.12,13

Our study identified PCT level, NEUT, D-dimer level, NLR, HDT, duration of incarceration, FHI, LYC, and CRP level as critical factors influencing intestinal necrosis in older incarcerated hernia patients. Elevated PCT, D-dimer, and CRP levels; high NEUT; and increased NLR were associated with an increased risk of intestinal necrosis, whereas higher LYC and HDT exhibited a negative correlation with necrosis risk. Moreover, when the symptom duration of incarceration was >24 h and FHI was present, the risk of intestinal necrosis rose significantly. These findings not only enhance the transparency and credibility of ML models in medical applications but also deepen clinicians’ understanding of the pathogenesis of intestinal necrosis in older incarcerated hernia patients.14–16 This evidence-based insight supports the development of more rational and effective treatment strategies.17,18

Overall, this study makes the following contributions. First, we developed an efficient and interpretable ML model to predict the risk of intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia, which can assist clinicians in early identification of older incarcerated hernia patients with intestinal necrosis, enabling timely surgical intervention and improving patient prognosis.19,20 The established ET model demonstrates high accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, making it a viable auxiliary diagnostic tool in clinical practice. It helps medical staff promptly detect and treat older incarcerated hernia patients with intestinal necrosis, thereby improving outcomes and reducing mortality.21,22 Second, this study reveals key clinical features associated with intestinal necrosis in older incarcerated hernia patients and their correlations, contributing to a deeper understanding of disease progression. These findings support the development of more rational treatment strategies.23,24

This study utilized SHAP analysis to reveal that PCT, D-dimer, and CRP levels; NEUT; NLR; HDT; duration of incarceration; FHI; and LYC are significant predictors of intestinal necrosis in older patients with incarcerated hernia. Higher levels of D-dimer, PCT, and CRP; increased NEUT; and elevated NLR were associated with an increased risk of intestinal necrosis, whereas higher LYC and HDT correlated with a reduced risk. Additionally, when the duration of incarceration was >24 h and FHI was present, the risk of intestinal necrosis significantly increased. These findings provide new insights and directions for further exploration of the mechanisms underlying intestinal necrosis. Based on these findings, more rational and effective treatment strategies can be developed for older incarcerated hernia patients with different risks of intestinal necrosis.25,26 For high-risk patients, emergency surgery should be performed, with necrotic bowel resection during the operation and avoidance of mesh placement. For low-risk patients, manual reduction can be followed with further observation and elective tension-free hernia repair to improve clinical symptoms.27–29 Furthermore, this study explored a novel approach using SHAP to enhance the interpretability of ML models. This method improves the transparency and trustworthiness of ML applications in the medical field, providing clinicians with more intuitive and reliable decision support. By applying SHAP to a ML model predicting intestinal necrosis risk in older incarcerated hernia patients, this study offers an effective solution to the black box problem commonly encountered in medical artificial intelligence (AI) research. The SHAP method not only quantifies the contribution of each feature to the outcome but also illustrates the relationship between individual features and the predicted result. 30 This enables clinicians to understand how the model generates specific predictions based on patient characteristics and identifies which features significantly influence the outcome. 31 Consequently, this approach enhances clinicians’ trust and acceptance of ML models while delivering more transparent and actionable decision-making support. 32

This study has certain limitations. First, the data were derived from a single-center retrospective observational study without an external validation cohort, which may have introduced selection bias, thereby limiting the generalizability and representativeness of the findings. Second, the dataset primarily included clinical variables and laboratory parameters, potentially omitting critical diagnostic indicators for intestinal necrosis, such as ultrasound and computed tomography data. This may have affected the comprehensiveness and depth of the results.

Conclusions

In summary, the ET model developed in this study demonstrates high sensitivity and specificity. The SHAP algorithm enables both global and individual-level interpretability of the model predictions. In clinical practice, this approach may serve as a valuable decision-support tool to assist physicians in prompt identification of older incarcerated hernia patients with concurrent intestinal necrosis, facilitating appropriate treatment decisions and ultimately improving patient outcomes.

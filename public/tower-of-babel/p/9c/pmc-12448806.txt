CircPRKD3-loaded exosomes concomitantly elicit tumor growth inhibition and glioblastoma microenvironment remodeling via inhibiting STAT3 signaling
Authors: Xiaoming Zhang, Mengyuan Jiang, Wanxiang Niu, Ben Xu, Ce Zhang, Minglong Yang, Shanshan Hu, Chaoshi Niu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlioblastoma stem cells and their exosomes (exos) are involved in shaping the immune microenvironment, which is important for tumor invasion and recurrence. However, studies involving GSC-derived exosomal circular RNAs (GDE-circRNAs) in regulating tumor microenvironment (TME) remain unknown. Here, we comprehensively evaluated the significance of a novel immune-related GDE-circRNA in the glioma microenvironment.MethodsGDE-circPRKD3 was screened out through high-throughput sequencing and verified by RT-PCR, sanger sequencing, and RNase R assays. A series of in vitro and in vivo experiments were performed to investigate the function of GDE-circPRKD3. RNA-seq, RNA immunoprecipitation, multicolor flow cytometry, and western blotting were used to explore the regulation of GDE-circPRKD3 on STAT3 signaling-mediated TME remodeling.ResultsWe have characterized a circRNA PRKD3 in GSC exosomes, and lower circPRKD3 expression predicts a worse prognosis for glioblastoma patients. Overexpression of GDE-circPRKD3 significantly impairs the biological competence of glioma and prolongs the survival of xenograft mice. GDE-circPRKD3 binds to HNRNPC in an m6A-dependent manner, accelerates mRNA decay of IL6ST, and inhibits downstream target STAT3. Notably, GDE-circPRKD3 promotes CXCL10 secretion by reprogramming tumor-associated macrophages, which in turn recruits CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes against GBM. Moreover, brain-targeted lipid nanoparticle delivery of circPRKD3 combined with immune checkpoint blockade therapy achieves significant combinatorial benefits.ConclusionsThis study provides a novel mechanism by which GDE-circPRKD3 relies on STAT3 signaling to remodel immunosuppressive TME and offers a potential RNA immunotherapy strategy for GBM treatment.Keywords: circPRKD3, exosome, glioma stem cell, STAT3, tumor microenvironment

Importance of the Study

Glioblastoma stem cells (GSCs) are critical for glioblastoma (GBM) progression. Deciphering the molecular basis of the crosstalk between GSC-exo and the immunosuppressive microenvironment could help to provide new options for the treatment of GBM. Here, we report a novel GDE-circRNA PRKD3, revealing its importance in the process of GSC regulation of the TME. GDE-circPRKD3 specifically binds to HNRNPC and disrupts its binding to IL6ST, which in turn despairs tumor growth and stemness maintenance due to STAT3 signaling. More importantly, GDE-circPRKD3 also effectively modifies tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to enhance the infiltration and killing of CD8+ T cells, thus striking the tumor again. Therefore, effective delivery of circPRKD3 using LNP and combined with ICB therapy may be a rational therapeutic strategy in GBM.

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common primary malignant intracranial neoplasm, patients are highly susceptible to recurrence, which may be due to tumor heterogeneity and the immune microenvironment.1,2 Highly heterogeneous malignant glioma cells interact with immune cells to form a tumor microenvironment (TME) characterized by a complex cellular network.3 Multiple cell types are present in the GBM microenvironment, including glioma stem cells (GSCs), non-stem tumor cells (NSTCs), tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), and other immune cells.4 Among them, GSCs are a special population and possess multidirectional differentiation potential, inducing tumor initiation and evolution.5 Thus, an in-depth exploration of the regulatory relationship between GSCs and tumor microenvironment holds the potential to mitigate GBM progression.

Exosomes (Exos) are small extracellular vesicles widely distributed in various body fluids, including peripheral blood.6 Recent studies indicate that exosomes transport tumor-associated molecules, including proteins and non-coding RNAs, influencing TME and intercellular communication between GSCs and tumor cells.7 Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are covalently closed-loop RNA molecules and primarily formed by back-splicing.8 Accumulating studies demonstrate that circRNAs can be carried by exosomes and transported to neighboring or distant cells. Tumor-derived Exo-circRNAs have potential as biomarkers for cancer detection.9 Notably, clarifying the function and potential mechanism of Exo-circRNA in TME may provide new directions for GBM treatment.

TAMs, as the most prevalent immune cells in the immunosuppressive microenvironment of gliomas, are indispensable for influencing tumor progression. It has been found to secrete immunomodulatory factors impacting T-cell activation, intratumor infiltration, and ultimately inducing tumor resistance to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) and immune escape.10 Currently, crosstalk between GSCs and TAMs has been demonstrated. However, the effect of GSC-derived Exo circRNA (GDE-circRNA) on TAMs in the GBM microenvironment remains unexplored.

In this study, we identified a downregulated circular RNA PRKD3 in GSC exosomes compared to normal exosomes (derived from human astrocytes and neural progenitor cell lines). Overexpression of GDE-circPRKD3 significantly affected GBM progression. We mechanistically revealed that GDE-circPRKD3 bound to heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein C (HNRNPC) in an m6A-dependent manner, affected mRNA decay of interleukin-6 signal transducer (IL6ST), and inhibited STAT3 pathway activation in GBM. Importantly, overexpression of GDE-circPRKD3 also enhanced CD8 T-cell infiltration and their effector molecules expression in the TME, controlled by C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 10 (CXCL10) secretion mediated by reprogramming of monocyte-derived macrophages (MDMs). Delivery of brain-targeted lipid nanoparticles (LNP) carrying in vitro circularized circPRKD3 combined with anti-PD1 ICB treatment produced a powerful synergistic effect, prolonging the overall-survival of xenograft mice to the greatest extent. In summary, our study highlights the clinical translation prospect of circPRKD3 in antitumor immunotherapy.

Materials and Methods

Human Glioma Specimens and Normal Brain Tissues

All pathologically identified glioma samples, normal brain tissues, and paired peripheral blood used in this study were obtained from patients in the Department of Neurosurgery at The First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China, with written informed consent. This study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of USTC and conducted in accordance with all relevant ethical regulations (approval No. 2019-N (H)-064). The basic characteristics of participants are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Plasmid Constructs, Lentivirus Production, and Stable Cell Lines Construction

See the Supplementary Materials and Methods for details. All targeted sequences were listed in Supplementary Table 2. Conservative of circPRKD3 were shown in Supplementary Table 3.

Animal Experiments

Four to six weeks female BALB/c nude mice and female C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd (China). For the statistic survival experiment, we kept the mice in each group under continuous observation, and recorded the time of the appearance of severe neurological symptoms (eg, seizures and inability to eat) in each mouse separately, which was taken as the endpoint of the study and was counted. For the rest of the animal experiments (eg, flow cytometry assay, immunohistochemistry, etc.), all mice were euthanized, and conducted subsequent experiments when the first mouse exhibited neurological symptoms that significantly affected their quality of life. All animal experimental protocols were approved by the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) Animal Research Ethics Committee, and all animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the USTC Guidelines for the Use of Laboratory Animals. Detailed experimental steps were described in Supplementary Materials and Methods.

RT-qPCR Analysis

Real-time PCR analyses were performed with 2 × Q3 SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Tolobio, 22204) on a LightCycler 96 instrument (Roche); GAPDH was used as an internal control. In RT-qPCR experiments involving exosomal circRNA, β-Actin was used as an internal control. Relative circRNA and mRNA expression levels were analyzed using the 2-ΔΔCt method. All primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech; detailed information is shown in Supplementary Table 2.

In Vitro Synthesis of Circular RNA

CircRNAs were synthesized by in vitro transcription using the T7 High Yield RNA Transcription kit (Novoprotein, E131-01A). Detailed experimental steps were described in Supplementary Materials and Methods.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical tests were conducted using GraphPad Prism (Version 8.0) software or SPSS (version 23.0). Data are presented as the mean ± s.d. The unpaired two-sided Student’s t-test or paired Student’s t-test or ANOVA tests were used for parametric data. Overall survival curves were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method and log-rank tests. The correlations were calculated by Pearson correlation analysis. A threshold of P < .05 was considered statistically significant for all tests.

Results

CircPRKD3 is Selectively Expressed in GSC-Derived Exosomes and is a Promising Biomarker

To investigate the role of glioma stem cells (GSCs) in regulating the tumor microenvironment, we successfully obtained a series of primary GSCs and NSTCs from freshly resected human GBM specimens. We characterized these GSCs and glioma stem-like cells derived from the U87 cell line by flow cytometry (Figure 1A). Considering that exosomes transport diverse biological components and are pivotal for intercellular communication,6 we investigated whether the GSCs regulating TME were dependent on exosomes. We successfully isolated and characterized exosomes from the supernatant of GSCs and normal cells (Supplementary Figure S1A-C). Besides, we also confirmed that GSC-exos affected the progression of glioma cells and macrophage polarization (Supplementary Figure S1D-I).

Although several studies have highlighted the importance of Exo-circRNAs in tumor progression,11 few studies have yet revealed the role of GDE-circRNAs in the GBM microenvironment. Hence, we determined to elucidate pivotal differentially expressed circRNAs (DE_circRNAs) capable of influencing TME by RNA sequencing analyses of GSC-exos. Circos plots globally and genomic-widely revealed 6096 circRNAs detected in GSC-exos and controls (Supplementary Figure S2A). A total of 617 nominally significant DE_circRNAs (115 upregulated and 502 downregulated) were found in GSC-exos (Figure 1B). Therefore, we performed RT-qPCR analysis on the top 10 downregulated circRNAs and found that circPRKD3 (hsa_circ_0000992) exhibited the lowest expression in GSC-exos (Supplementary Figure S2B).

CircPRKD3 (943nt) was formed from exon 2 of the PRKD3 gene (Figure 1C, upper) and identified by Sanger sequencing (Figure 1C, lower) and RNase R digestion (Figure 1D). Notably, circPRKD3 expression was decreased in GSCs-Exo (Figure 1E), as well as in glioma cell lines and a panel of GSCs (Supplementary Figure S2C and D). The level of circPRKD3 diminished with increasing grade of glioma tissues (Figure 1F). Besides, circPRKD3 was confirmed as a cytoplasm-localized circRNA (Figure 1G and Supplementary Figure S2E) and possessed a longer half-life (Supplementary Figure S2F). Higher expression of circPRKD3 was found to be correlated with better prognosis by analyzing the follow-up data of GBM patients (Figure 1H). The results of immunofluorescence (IF) staining of glioma tissues further suggested that circPRKD3 expression was positively correlated with pro-inflammatory macrophage infiltration within the TME (Figure 1I).

Next, we explored whether circPRKD3 could be delivered to plasma via exosomes and serve as a diagnostic biomarker for GBM patients. Firstly, we observed that circPRKD3 level was quite stable in exosomes at room temperature (Supplementary Figure S2G). CircPRKD3 expression was significantly downregulated in plasma exosomes from glioma patients compared to controls (Figure 1J). Moreover, ROC curves revealed that exosomal circPRKD3 could be used to distinguish glioma from healthy (AUC = 0.9286, 95% CI = 0.8892–0.9680, P value < .0001; Supplementary Figure S2H), and help determine the grade of malignancy of glioma (AUC = 0.8977, 95% CI = 0.8303–0.9650, P value < .0001) (Supplementary Figure S2I). In summary, we considered that circPRKD3 might be a promising diagnostic biomarker.

GDE-circPRKD3 Impedes Tumorigenicity of GBM

To investigate the function of GDE-circPRKD3, we successfully stably overexpressed and knockdown circPRKD3 in GSCs, which resulted in corresponding expression change of circPRKD3 in GSC-derived exosomes (Figure 2A, Supplementary Figure S2J and K). After treating glioma cells with GSC-derived exosomes overexpressed circPRKD3 (GDE-OE-circPRKD3)/GSC-derived exosomes knockdown circPRKD3 (GDE-KD-circPRKD3), we observed an obvious elevation/downregulation of circPRKD3 (Figure 2B and Supplementary Figure S2L) instead of its parent gene PRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S2M-Q). Meanwhile, silencing of PRKD3 was unable to affect the expression of circPRKD3 in glioma cells (Supplementary Figure S2R-S). Functionally, the proliferation (Figure 2C and D) and invasion (Figure 2E) capacity of glioma cells were remarkably reduced by GDE-OE-circPRKD3 instead of silencing PRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S2T-V). In contrast, incubating glioma cells with GDE-KD-circPRKD3 enhanced the above phenotypes of GBM compared to controls (Supplementary Figure S3A-B). Subsequently, in vivo experiments were performed to further elucidate the effects of GDE-circPRKD3 on GBM progression. Injection of GDE-OE-circPRKD3 (Figure 2F) inhibited the growth of intracranial tumor xenografts (Figure 2G and H) and prolonged the overall survival of mice (Figure 2I). Besides, the results of HE staining showed that GDE-OE-circPRKD3 impaired neoplastic progression (Figure 2J). Given the critical role of the STAT3 pathway in glioma pathogenesis,12 we used immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining to detect the expression of protein phosphorylated STAT3 (p-STAT3) and found that was markedly decreased in GDE-OE-circPRKD3 group (Figure 2K). We further observed that circPRKD3 and p-STAT3 expression were negatively correlated in multiple GBM tissues (Supplementary Figure S3C-E).

Although we found that GSCs influence tumor development by secreting the Exo-circPRKD3, the effect of circPRKD3 itself on GSCs remains unknown. Overexpression of circPRKD3 reduced the levels of stemness markers (Supplementary Figure S3F-H). In vitro limiting dilution showed that elevating circPRKD3 expression potently impaired GSC self-renewal (Supplementary Figure S3I). In vivo, enhanced circPRKD3 expression significantly inhibited the growth of glioma stem cells, prolonged the overall survival of mice, and reduced the p-STAT3 protein level (Supplementary Figure S3J-M). Taken together, GDE-circPRKD3 was critical for GBM tumorigenesis.

GDE-circPRKD3 Inhibits Glioma Progression Through the IL6ST/STAT3 Signaling Axis

To identify key target genes regulated by GDE-circPRKD3 in GBM, we co-cultured GDE-OE-circPRKD3, GDE-KD-circPRKD3 with glioma cells respectively, and performed RNA-sequencing analyses (RNA-seq). A total of three mutual differentially expressed genes were found in glioma cells (Figure 3A), and IL6ST showed the most obvious changes (Figure 3B, Supplementary Figure S4A and B). IL6ST (alias: gp130) which was an important significant signaling receptor on cell membranes and closely associated with the JAK/STAT3 pathway and the progression of various tumors.13,14 The GEPIA database suggested that IL6ST was upregulated in GBM, consistent with our validated findings (Supplementary Figure S4C and D), and higher IL6ST expression predicted poor prognosis of GBM patients (Supplementary Figure S4E). In addition, we observed that circPRKD3 was negatively correlated with IL6ST expression in GBM (Supplementary Figure S4F). IL6ST has been shown to affect the JAK/STAT3 pathway to regulate tumor development.15 The results of immunoblotting showed that the IL6ST/JAK/STAT3 axis was significantly inhibited/activated in glioma cells after treating with GDE-OE-circPRKD3 or GDE-KD-circPRKD3, respectively (Supplementary Figure S4G). Considering that IL6ST protein can be transported by loading onto the membrane surface of exosomes,16 we examined and found that the IL6ST protein levels of GDE-OE-circPRKD3 and GDE-KD-circPRKD3 were not remarkedly different compared to control exosomes (Supplementary Figure S4H). Thus, we determined that the changes in IL6ST protein induced by GDE-OE-circPRKD3 treatment were due to circPRKD3 regulation instead of vesicular IL6ST. Furthermore, we evaluated the effect of IL6ST on glioma and found that direct silencing/overexpression of IL6ST resulted in no significant changes in tumor cell growth. However, by impairing or enhancing IL6ST expression in glioma cells incubated with GSC-exos, the biological capacity of gliomas was significantly diminished or enhanced (Supplementary Figure S4I-P). We suspected this might be related to IL-6 carried in GSC-exos.17 Several studies have shown that IL6ST-mediated activation of the STAT3 pathway is critically important for GSC maintenance.18,19 Therefore, we evaluated the effect of circPRKD3 on IL6ST in GSCs. Silencing/overexpression of circPRKD3 significantly upregulated/reduced IL6ST expression in GSCs (Supplementary Figure S5A-C). Subsequently, we confirmed that circPRKD3 affected the BMX/STAT3 axis through IL6ST (Supplementary Figure S5D). Disruption/overexpression of circPRKD3 promoted/attenuated IL6ST protein and successfully enhanced/inhibited the activation of p-BMX and p-STAT3 proteins in GSCs caused by IL6 stimulation, respectively (Supplementary Figure S5E). These data suggested that circPRKD3 affected GSC maintenance by modulating the IL6ST-mediated STAT3 signaling axis.

Next, we investigated the relationship between circPRKD3 and IL6ST. Co-culturing glioma cells with GDE-OE-circPRKD3 or GDE-KD-circPRKD3 significantly attenuated/enhanced the expression of IL6ST and downstream pathway proteins (Figure 3C and D, Supplementary Figure S6A-D). In vitro rescue experiments also showed that GDE-OE-circPRKD3 effectively inhibited the accelerated tumor progression due to overexpression of IL6ST, while GDE-KD-circPRKD3 produced the opposite effect (Supplementary Figure S6E-J). Thus, we confirmed that GSCs regulate IL6ST expression in tumor cells via exosomal circPRKD3. Collectively, we validated the critical role of the circPRKD3/IL6ST/STAT3 axis in GSC stemness maintenance and GSC regulating on glioma cells.

GDE-circPRKD3/HNRNPC Jointly to Regulate IL6ST mRNA Stability and Despair GBM Progression

At present, circRNAs regulate tumor progression mainly through miRNA sponging, translating into peptides, and interacting with RNA binding protein.20 We first performed Ago2 RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays and found that circPRKD3 did not function as miRNA sponge (Supplementary Figure S7A). Based on ORF finder and CircPrimer 2.0,21 we observed that circPRKD3 might encoded a small peptide (Supplementary Figure S7B), but failure to identify by mass spectrometry.

Next, we designed specific probes antisense to the junction sites of circPRKD3 and successfully captured circPRKD3 in glioma cells (Supplementary Figure S7C). Silver staining with mass spectrometry were performed to find the differential proteins interacting with circPRKD3 (Figure 3E). Gene ontology analysis showed that differential RBPs were mainly associated with biological processes such as mRNA processing (Supplementary Figure S7D). Subsequently, we selected candidate RBPs (HNRNPC, HNRNPA2B1, AUF1, HNRNPAB, ELAVL1, TREX1) for RIP experiments, and found HNRNPC with the highest probability (Figure 3F). Co-culturing GDE-OE-circPRKD3 with glioma cells also remarkedly increased the binding of HNRNPC to circPRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S7E). We then explored which domain of HNRNPC was involved in the interaction with circPRKD3. We constructed truncated mutants of HNRNPC based on the RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (Supplementary Figure S7F) and observed that mutant HNRNPC-D2 was responsible for binding to circPRKD3 by RIP assays (Supplementary Figure S7G and H). Subsequently, we predicted the specific binding fragments of HNRNPC to circPRKD3 via catRAPID database22 and constructed truncated mutants targeting the two most probable fragments (Figure 3G). The mutant lacking 176-232aa significantly disrupted binding to circPRKD3 (Figure 3H and Supplementary Figure S7I).

Recently studies have shown that HNRNPC also functions as an m6A reader.23 Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP) revealed that circPRKD3 was significantly enriched by anti-m6A antibodies in glioma cells and GSC (Supplementary Figure S7J). We further determined that m6A demethylase ALKBH5 mainly regulates the expression and m6A modification of circPRKD3 in gliomas (Supplementary Figure S7K-P). Based on SRAMP,24 CircPrimer2.0, and RMBase v2.0 databases,25 we identified 10 potential m6A modification sites for circPRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S8A). Subsequently, we performed point mutations (from A to C) on five consecutive high-confidence m6A sites (Supplementary Figure S8B and C), and observed that these sites were extremely important for m6A modification of circPRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S8D). Among these m6A sites, m6A-Mut1 (sites-310) plays an important role in the regulation of circPRKD3 by ALKBH5 (Supplementary Figure S8E-F). In addition, the results of the RIP assays showed that mutating these m6A sites significantly reduced the binding potency of circPRKD3 to HNRNPC (Supplementary Figure S8G). To explore whether m6A modification is crucial for the biological function of circPRKD3, we successfully constructed an m6A-mut-circPRKD3 overexpressing stable cell line in GSC (Supplementary Figure S8H). After treating glioma cells with GSC-derived exosomes overexpressed m6A-mut-circPRKD3, we observed that mutation in these m6A sites of circPRKD3 severely inhibited the effects of GDE-circPRKD3 on IL6ST/STAT3 axis and the malignant capacities of GBM cells (Supplementary Figure S8I-L). However, we also noted that circPRKD3 and HNRNPC had no significant effect on their mutual expression in GBM (Supplementary Figure S9A-H). Taken together, GDE-circPRKD3 bound to HNRNPC in an m6A-dependent manner and collectively influenced GBM progression.

Next, we explored whether the circPRKD3/HNRNPC complex could jointly affect IL6ST expression. We assessed that HNRNPC was positively correlated with IL6ST expression in GBM via TCGA database (Supplementary Figure S10A). IL6ST was found to bind to HNRNPC, predominantly to the RRM domain (Supplementary Figure S10B). Further experiments validated that HNRNPC lacking of 26-77aa fragment, was unable to bind to IL6ST (Supplementary Figure S10C and D). Subsequently, we observed that overexpression/downregulation of HNRNPC increased IL6ST expression in GSC-exos-treated glioma cells (Supplementary Figure S10E and F). It has been reported that cytoplasmic HNRNPC regulates the expression of target genes by affecting their stability.26 Using Starbase and RBPmap,27 we noted that HNRNPC had potential binding sites in the 3’UTR of IL6ST (Supplementary Figure S10G). After treating with actinomycin D, we further verified the effects of HNRNPC and GDE-OE-circPRKD3 on the mRNA stability of IL6ST (Figure 3I and J). Next, we also explored the effect of circPRKD3 on HNRNPC binding to IL6ST. We found that the binding efficiency of HNRNPC to IL6ST was significantly attenuated after treating with GDE-OE-circPRKD3 (Figure 3K). However, mutation of the m6A site of circPRKD3 instead enhanced the binding of HNRNPC to IL6ST (Figure 3L). Therefore, we believed that GDE-circPRKD3 could bind HNRNPC competitively with IL6ST and promote mRNA decay of IL6ST.

To declare the regulation of IL6ST by circPRKD3 binding to HNRNPC, we performed rescue experiments and found that overexpression/downregulation of HNRNPC restored the impression on GDE-OE-circPRKD3/GDE-KD-circPRKD3 to IL6ST and related proteins, whereas the reversionary effect was significantly attenuated in HNRNPC mutants lacking the 26-77aa fragment (Supplementary Figure S11A-D). In addition, we observed that increasing HNRNPC expression effectively ameliorated the inhibition of tumor cell proliferation and invasion by GDE-OE-circPRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S11E and F), whereas HNRNPC silencing produced the opposite effect (Supplementary Figure S11G and H). Overall, we found that GDE-circPRKD3 could bind HNRNPC in an m6A-dependent manner to promote IL6ST mRNA decay and inhibit glioma progression.

GDE-circPRKD3 Enhances CD8 T-Cell Infiltration Into GBM

Previous studies have shown that the stemness of GSCs was closely associated with immunosuppressive features of TME.28 Considering that overexpression of circPRKD3 disrupted GSC self-renewal (Supplementary Figure S3), we further explored the effect of GDE-circPRKD3 on the GBM immune microenvironment. Given the high conservation of circPRKD3 between humans and mice (Supplementary Figure S12A), we stably overexpressed circPrkd3 in mouse glioma cell line GL261 and murine GSC (mGSC; Supplementary Figure S12B and C) and found exosomal circPrkd3 expression was corresponding elevated (Supplementary Figure S12D). Subsequently, we evaluated the effect of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 in an immunocompetent mouse model of GBM and observed that mice received several intra-orbital injections of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 had delayed tumor progression and improved survival benefit (Figure 4A-C). Next, we isolated brain tumors to characterize the GBM immune microenvironment. Flow cytometry showed that CD8 T and NK cells were significantly increased in mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 tumors, whereas no obvious changes in cell subsets such as CD4 T cells, macrophages, and microglia (Figure 4D and E). Immunofluorescence assays further confirmed that the distribution of CD8 T cells and NK cells within the tumor was increased by mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Figure 4F, Supplementary Figure S12E and F). Moreover, we characterized the CD8 T cells populating GBM tumors and observed a remarked increase in Granzyme B (GzB+), as well as in TNFα+ and interferon-gamma (IFNγ+) effector cells (Figure 4G, Supplementary Figure S12G and H). Interestingly, we also found similarity effects when directly elevating circPrkd3 expression in GL261 (Supplementary Figure S12I-O).

To assess the functional contribution of CD8 T cells to the therapeutic efficacy of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3, we depleted CD8 T cells in GBM tumors by αCD8 antibody (Supplementary Figure S13A). We observed that the depletion of CD8 T cells led to a remarkedly abrogation of the therapeutic benefit of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Supplementary Figure S13B-D). To further investigate the association between circPRKD3 and CD8 T cells, we analyzed the distribution of CD8 T cells in human GBM tissues through immunofluorescence staining and found that circPRKD3 expression positively correlated with CD8 T-cell infiltration within the TME (Supplementary Figure S13E). In summary, these results suggested that GDE-circPRKD3 was immunostimulatory and CD8 T cells were attributed to the antitumor immune responses in GBM.

GDE-circPRKD3 Promotes T-cell Chemokine CXCL10 Expression

To clarify the mechanism of GDE-circPRKD3 triggers antitumor immunity, we detected the expression of cytokines and chemokines, which were entrée of the tumor immune microenvironment. Multifactor analysis of the serum obtained from mice treated with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 showed that TNFα, IFNγ and the chemoattractant of CD8 T cells-CXCL10 were most obviously upregulated (Supplementary Figure S14A). The results of Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) further confirmed that secretion of CXCL10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ were elevated by mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Figure 4H, Supplementary Figure S14B and C). Consistently, direct overexpression of circPrkd3 also stimulated the expression of the above secretions (Supplementary Figure S14D). Subsequently, we confirmed that CXCL10 expression was enhanced by mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 in GBM specimens (Supplementary Figure S14E). To figure out the origin of the enhanced CXCL10 expression in TME, we performed immunofluorescence assays and found that CXCL10 was mainly expressed by TAMs (Figure 4I, Supplementary Figure S14F). Therefore, we isolated recruited MDMs or resident microglia from GBM by performing fluorescence-activated cell sorting and assessed the expression of CXCL10. We found that CXCL10 was significantly upregulated in CD49d+ MDMs but not in CD49d- microglia by treating with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Figure 4J). Then, we examined the effects of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 on regulating CXCL10 in M2-polarized bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDM) and noted that the expression of CXCL10 was increased in BMDMs and its cell supernatants (Supplementary Figure S14G and H), while overexpression of circPrkd3 led to a similar result (Supplementary Figure S14I). Additional experiments further validated that mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 predominately regulated the expression of CXCL10 in macrophages, but not microglia (Supplementary Figure S14J and K).

Importantly, we utilized Timer2.0 database29 to analyze the effect of CXCL10 in TME and revealed that CXCL10 expression was closely associated with infiltration of CD8+ T cells and M1 macrophages in GBM (Supplementary Figure S14L). After performing in vitro transwell assays, we observed that depletion of CXCL10 by αCXCL10 significantly disrupted the migration of CD8 T cells attracted by M2-BMDMs treated with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Supplementary Figure S14M). Moreover, we found that AMG487, which was a selective C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 3 (CXCR3) antagonist, could obviously attenuate the migration capacity of CD8 T cells in mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 treated M2-BMDMs group (Supplementary Figure S14N). To further investigate the role of CXCL10 in mGDE-OE-circPrkd3-induced antitumor effects, we included αCXCR3 blocking antibody into therapeutic regimens (Supplementary Figure S14O). Notably, in vivo blockade of CXCR3 substantially impaired the efficacy of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Figure 4K), and diminished the abundance of tumor-infiltrating CD8 T cells (Figure 4L, Supplementary Figure S14P and Q). However, the blockade of CXCR3 had limited impressions on the capacity of remaining T cells to produce cytotoxic cytokines (Figure 4L, Supplementary Figure S14R). These results suggested that mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 might directly promote T-cell activation, but not just affect T-cell recruitment via CXCL10. Mechanistically, we found that GDE-circPRKD3 was unable to regulate the expression of CXCL10 by affecting its mRNA stability (Supplementary Figure S14S-T). Previous studies have shown that STAT1 could function as a key transcription factor and directly promote CXCL10 expression.30 Therefore, we explored the direct regulatory relationship between GDE-circPRKD3 and STAT1, and found that GDE-circPRKD3 could promote the mRNA expression of STAT1 in macrophages (Supplementary Figure S14U-V). In conclusion, the increased CD8 T cell infiltration in TME induced by GDE-circPRKD3 was attributed to the enhanced expression of CXCL10 regulated by STAT1 in macrophage.

GDE-circPRKD3 Reprograms Tumor-Associated Macrophages

We have confirmed that T cell chemo-attractant CXCL10 was mainly secreted by TAMs, but whether TAMs were essential to the antitumor effect induced by GDE-circPRKD3 remained known. PLX3397, a colony-stimulating factor-1 receptor (CSF1R) inhibitor that could deplete macrophages,31 was taken to evaluate the function of TAMs (Figure 5A). Depletion of macrophages effectively attenuated the suppressive effect of mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 on GBM (Figure 5A and B) and decreased the abundance of CD8 T cells (Figure 5C, Supplementary Figure S15A), IFNγ+ CD8+ T cells (Supplementary Figure S15B), Granzyme B+ CD8+ T cells (Supplementary Figure S15C) and TNFα+ CD8+ T cells (Supplementary Figure S15D). These results suggested that the enhancing immune response of GDE-circPRKD3 was macrophage-dependent.

Considering that macrophage migrated into TME was a critical event in tumor progression.32 We explored and validated mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 produced a very limited impression on macrophage migration and apoptosis (Supplementary Figure S16A-C), which was consistent with our previous flow cytometry in Figure 4F. Hence, we further inquired whether macrophage polarization was altered by overexpression of GDE-circPRKD3. Data of flowcytometry demonstrated that CD86 + macrophages were upregulated by injecting with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 (Figure 5D). Treatment of gliomas with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 upregulated an M1-like program in MDMs isolated from tumors, while downregulated the M2-like program (Supplementary Figure S16D). To functionally assess the reprogramming of MDMs away from an immunosuppressive phenotype, we performed in vitro assays in which co-culturing tumoral MDMs with CSFE-labeled splenic activated CD8 T cells to analyze the proliferation of T cells. MDMs obtained from mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 treated tumors substantially ameliorated the inhibitory effect of immunosuppressive phenotype MDMs on T cell proliferation (Figure 5E).

To ascertain whether GDE-circPRKD3 could directly induce macrophage polarization, we utilized cytokines to differentiate BMDM ex vivo into M2-like macrophages and treatment with mGDE-OE-circPrkd3. The expression of M1-like signature genes was upregulated (Figure 5F-H) and M2-like genes were decreased (Supplementary Figure S16E and F). The effect of GDE-circPRKD3 on promoting polarization was also repeated in THP-1-induced macrophages (Supplementary Figure S16G and H). However, GDE-circPRKD3 failed to regulate the polarization of microglia (Supplementary Figure S16I-L). Moreover, in vitro assays further validated that mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 restored the inhibitory of M2-like BMDM on T cell proliferation (Figure 5I). To further investigate the reprogramming ability of GDE-circPRKD3 on macrophages, we performed a co-culture assay of BMDM with mouse glioma cells. Tumor cells induced the co-cultured BMDM into an M2-like phenotype, which was already reported,33 and the M2-like signature genes were diminished commitment with upregulated M1-like program upon mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 treatment (Figure 5J and K). In addition, we also verified that direct overexpression of circPRKD3 could promote macrophage polarization (Supplementary Figure S16M-P). Overall, these results demonstrated that GDE-circPRKD3 was able to reprogram tumor-associated macrophages.

Several studies have shown that macrophage polarization is closely related to IFN-γ/STAT1, NF-κB, and STAT3 signaling pathways.34–36 Thus, we examined the expression of key proteins in the above-mentioned pathways in mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 or circPrkd3 overexpressed treated M2-like BMDMs, and found that phosphorylation of P65 and STAT1 were significantly increased by mGDE-OE-circPrkd3 or overexpressed circPrkd3. Moreover, the phosphorylated STAT3 expression was significantly reduced (Figure 5L, Supplementary Figure S16Q). Besides, we also observed similar results in co-cultured assays (Figure 5L). To explore whether GDE-circPRKD3 modulates macrophage polarization relies on the IL6ST/STAT3 axis, we treated M2-macrophage with GDE-circPRKD3/mGDE-OE-circPrkd3. The results showed that overexpression of circPRKD3 remarkedly decreased the mRNA and protein levels of IL6ST, and affected the activation of phosphorylated STAT3 (Supplementary Figure S16R-S). After treating with actinomycin D, we observed the effects of GDE-OE-circPRKD3 and HNRNPC on the mRNA stability of IL6ST (Supplementary Figure S16T-W). In summary, we validated that GDE-circPRKD3 could reprogram TAMs away from M2-like immunosuppressive subtypes and promote antitumor immunity.

Targeted Delivery of LNP-circPRKD3 Combined With ICB Therapy Activates Antitumor Immunity

Based on the effective antitumor capability of GDE-circPRKD3, we selected appropriately engineered lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) for the delivery of circPRKD3 to enhance clinical application potential. LNP modified with angiopep-2,37 a brain-targeting peptide capable of assisting in the crossing of the blood-brain barrier, was applied for packaging synthesized circPRKD3 (Supplementary Figure S17A). Given that circRNA generated using T4 RNA ligase 1 was less immunogenic,38 we utilized this method to in vitro synthesize circPRKD3 and successfully confirmed its production (Supplementary Figure S17B). Cryo-electron microscopy (Figure 6A) and DLS (Supplementary Figure S17C) showed the particle distribution, zeta potential, and degree of homogeneity (PDI) of LNP, respectively, which indicated that the antibody modification had minimal effects on LNPs (Supplementary Figure S17C). After co-incubating LNPs with tumor cells, we observed that LNPs were effectively uptake and led to an increased expression of circPRKD3 (Figure 6B, Supplementary Figure S17D and E).

It is well known that early incorporation of immune checkpoint inhibitors, represented by PD-1/PD-L1, led to additional benefits of tumor therapy and has become the focus of clinical studies.39 We, therefore, evaluated the survival impact of LNP-circPRKD3 combined with early immune checkpoint blockade therapy in a GBM mouse model (Figure 6C). We found that treatment with LNP-circPRKD3 alone significantly improved overall survival in mice, while the most pronounced benefit was obtained in mice treated in combination with anti-PD-1 (Figure 6D-F). Notably, anti-PD-1 therapy alone had limited inhibition of tumor progression, which is also consistent with the results suggested by a previous study.40 Next, after injecting Cy5-Ang2-LNP-circPRKD3 in mice for 24 hours, we observed that it was mainly localized in brain tumors and liver, and resided inside the tumors based on the fluorescence results (Figure 6G, Supplementary Figure S17F). We considered that this might be related to the enhanced permeation and retention effect of LNPs.41 To assess the influence of LNP-circPRKD3 on tumor immune microenvironment, flow cytometry showed that LNP-circPRKD3 alone or in combination with anti-PD-1 significantly increased the proportions of CD8 T cells, NK cells, CD86+ macrophages, and decreased the levels of M2-phenotype macrophages (Figure 6H and I, Supplementary Figure S17G). Immunofluorescence showed similar results in the distribution of CD8 T cells (Figure 6J and K), accompanied by an increased ability of CD8 T cells to produce cytotoxic cytokines (Supplementary Figure S17H and I). Moreover, LNP-circPRKD3 alone or in combination with anti-PD-1 also significantly promoted the secretion of inflammatory chemokines (Figure 6L). The biosafety of LNP-circPRKD3 was further confirmed by the results of HE staining of various organs in mice (Supplementary Figure S17J). In addition, we also validated the efficacy of LNP-circPRKD3 using a patient-derived tumor xenograft model (PDX_2303) (Supplementary Figure S17K). As shown by survival analysis and HE staining, multiple nasal injections of LNP-circPRKD3 exerted antitumor effects significantly in the GBM PDX model (Supplementary Figure S17L and M). Together, LNP-circPRKD3 reduced tumor burden and enhanced the response to anti-PD-1 therapy.

Discussion

In summary, our study identifies exosomal circPRKD3 as a crucial regulator mediating the influence of GSCs on the tumor microenvironment. GDE-circPRKD3 promotes CXCL10 secretion by reprogramming TAMs, which in turn recruit CD8 cytotoxic lymphocytes (CTL) against GBM. We elucidate the molecular mechanism by which Exo-circPRKD3 modulates the GBM progression via the HNRNPC/IL6ST/STAT3 axis and propose a strategy for targeted delivery of circPRKD3 using lipid nanoparticles, in combination with anti-PD-1 therapy (Graphic Abstract).

GBM is a poorly immunogenic tumor for which limited progress has been shown in clinical immunotherapy so far. Thus, new strategies are urgently needed to remodel the immunosuppressive microenvironment of GBM. Building on this perspective, we found that GDE-circPRKD3 effectively drove the remodeling of immunosuppressive TME depending on the reprogramming of TAMs. It was reported that blocking CSF1R altered macrophage polarization and despaired glioma progression without affecting total TAM density in GBMs.42,43 Results of clinical trials indicated that PLX3397 alone was ineffective in GBM,44 which were also consistent with our experimental results. However, multiple oral PLX3397 significantly impaired the therapeutic efficacy induced by GDE-OE-circPRKD3. Thus, we concluded that reprogramming of TAM underlay GDE-circPRKD3-mediated anti-cancer immunity. Next, we further confirmed that GDE-circPRKD3 reprogramed immunosuppressive MDMs but not microglia in TAMs. Recently studies indicated that MDMs outnumbered microglia in late tumor stages and suppressed T cell activity.45 MDMs and microglia originated from different progenitor cells and exhibited high heterogeneity.46 These findings provided a basis for our analysis of the selective polarization of MDMs by GDE-circPRKD3. Nevertheless, we also recognized the limitations of the M1/M2 dichotomy used to categorize MDMs.

Next, we observed that in MDMs, the expression program of the pro-inflammatory chemokine CXCL10 was activated, thus enhancing CD8 T cell infiltration in GBM. This was confirmed by depletion of CXCL10 or inhibition of the CXCR3. CXCR3 has many other ligands (e.g. CXCL11). The expression of CXCL11 in the serum of GDE-circPRKD3-treated mice was upregulated but to a lesser extent than CXCL10, as shown in Supplementary Figure S14A. Therefore, we focused our research on CXCL10. Notably, GDE-OE-circPRKD3 also effectively elevated the expression levels of TNFα, IFNγ, and other inflammatory factors in the peripheral blood of mice. These inflammatory factors are closely associated with the M1 polarization of macrophages.30,47,48 In fact, we also found that GDE-circPRKD3 directly regulated the level of STAT1 mRNA in GBM, contributing to the increased secretion of CXCL10 in TME. Overall, our study suggests that GDE-circPRKD3 mediates the secretion of CXCL10 by reprogramming TAMs, which in turn affects intratumor T-cell recruitment. This finding enriches the understanding of the regulatory mechanisms between GSCs and the tumor microenvironment.

Exosomes, as an important mediator of intercellular communication, have been implicated in constructing the glioma microenvironment.49 Increasing evidence suggests that Exo-circRNAs affect the tumor microenvironment.9 In our research, we further confirmed that beyond the intrinsic effects of circPRKD3 on the stemness characteristics of GSCs, GDE-circPRKD3 significantly attenuated the growth of GBM. At the same time, we are also aware that other cells, such as NSTCs or immune cells, might also release exosomal circPRKD3, which requires further investigation in our subsequent studies. Mechanistically, we validated that GDE-circPRKD3 bound to HNRNPC in an m6A-dependent manner. HNRNPC reportedly acted as a translational controller of a specific mRNA regulon, modulating bound-mRNAs to undergo 3’ untranslated region lengthening and causing translational repression.50 HNRNPC was also reported to function as a m6A regulator by competitively bound to IGFL2-AS1 and promoted sunitinib resistance in renal cell carcinoma.51 Here, our data demonstrated that the circPRKD3/HNRNPC complex co-regulated the mRNA stability of the target gene gp130, subsequently disrupting GSC maintenance and tumor cell survival by affecting the STAT3 pathway.

IL6ST (gp130), a common signaling receptor on cell membranes, was closely associated with the progression of various tumors.11,12 It has been reported that gp130 binds to the IL-6 receptor (IL6R), Leukemia inhibitory factor receptor, or oncostatin M receptor (OSMR), thereby activating the downstream JAK/STAT3 pathway and affecting tumor progression.12 Besides, IL6ST exerted an important role in various tumor TMEs. It was reported that starvation or rapamycin induced a decreased expression of CD8, and CD8Low T cells showed enhanced ST2L and IL6ST expression in cancer.52 Breast cancer-derived exosomes transferred gp130 to induce STAT3 signaling in BMDMs and promote tumor progression.16 At present, a small-molecule gp130 inhibitor SC144 has been applied in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, breast cancer and colon carcinoma, and exerted potent antitumor efficacy with minimal toxicity.53,54 However, the potential of SC144 in glioma treatment requires further validation. Furthermore, our data suggested that GDE-circPRKD3 effectively inhibited the activation of the STAT3 pathway in macrophages. Inhibition of the STAT3 pathway has also been reported to contribute to the induction of M1 polarization in TAMs.55 Overall, the IL6ST/STAT3 axis contributed to GDE-circPRKD3-mediated antitumor immunity and laid the foundation for our subsequent studies. Given that STAT3 was a transcriptional factor widely involved in multiple activities in normal cells, direct targeting of STAT3 might lead to collateral damage.56 Therefore, we proposed that targeted delivery against circPRKD3 could be a safer and more effective therapeutic strategy.

However, the in vitro application of exosomes still faces many difficulties due to poor homogeneity, complex content composition, and unknown long-term effects on the body and safety. Therefore, we used lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), which are currently the mainstream drug delivery vehicle for vaccine preparation. The advantages of LNPs include a well-defined composition, high in vitro yield, and short in vivo half-life in organisms.57 In this study, we employed brain-targeted LNP to deliver circPRKD3 into GBM and observed promising but limited therapeutic effects. It is well known that a major mechanism underlying tumor immune evasion involves the engagement of the immune checkpoint pathways.58 To enhance the therapeutic efficacy of LNP-circPRKD3, we incorporated a PD1-blocking antibody into the treatment regimen. Despite the significant survival improvement achieved, the ultimate inability to eliminate the tumor also reminds us to further optimize our future studies, such as improving the in vitro synthesis efficiency of circPRKD3, utilizing multiple antibody modifications of LNP to enhance tumor targeting, and combining with TMZ or other targeted drug therapy. This approach has significant potential translational value and holds promise as a novel therapeutic avenue for glioma patients.

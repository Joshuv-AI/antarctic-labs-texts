Trends and challenges on inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wound from 2014 to 2023: A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Weijing Fan, Huimin Lu, Hongshuo Shi, Weian Yuan, Guobin Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe disturbance of the inflammatory microenvironment is a frequent pathological trait of diabetic wounds, contributing to the emergence of numerous chronic illnesses. This is crucial in both the development and recovery of wounds caused by diabetes. This study aims to perform a bibliometric analysis of research on the inflammatory microenvironment within the domain of diabetic wounds (DW) over the past 10 years. The objective is to map out the current global research landscape, pinpoint the most significant areas of study and offer guidance for future research avenues. Our research involved querying the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database for all pertinent studies on the inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wounds (DW). We utilized bibliometric tools such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer and R (version 4.3.1) to identify and highlight the most impactful studies in the field. The study encompassed a review of 1454 articles published from 2014 to 2023, highlighting China and the United States as pivotal nations in the research of the inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wounds (DW). Within this sphere, the University of Michigan and Harvard University in the United States, along with Shanghai Jiaotong University in China, emerged as the most prolific institutions. WANG Y from China was identified as the most productive author, while KUNKEL SL from the United States received the most citations. The research primarily focuses on topics such as wound healing, repair processes, angiogenesis, oxidative stress and macrophage activity. Additionally, “macrophage” and “delivery” were pinpointed as the leading subjects with promising research potential in this area. Research on the inflammatory microenvironment of diabetic wounds is rapidly advancing through active international collaboration. The study of new mechanisms related to the inflammatory microenvironment and the development of novel materials for repair based on this microenvironment represent emerging fields of future research, particularly in terms of translational applications. This may offer guidance and novel perspectives for further research in the area of the diabetic wound inflammatory microenvironment.Keywords: bibliometric analysis, diabetic wound, immunity, inflammatory microenvironment, visualization

INTRODUCTION

Diabetic wounds represent a significant and severe complication of diabetes, characterized by rapid progression, prolonged illness duration, high rates of disability and increased mortality. 1 In the United States alone, there are nearly 40 million individuals living with diabetes, and the prevalence of diabetic wounds is rising at an annual rate of 5%. 2 These wounds stem from localized nerve damage and peripheral vascular lesions, leading to infections, ulcers and ultimately, deep tissue necrosis of the foot, significantly impacting patient quality of life. Diabetic foot ulcers are a major cause of hospitalization and reduced life quality in these patients, with data indicating that the incidence of amputation due to diabetic foot ulcers stands at 19.03%, representing 27.3% of all amputations. 3 , 4

Inflammation plays a critical role in the body's innate immune response, aiding in the repair and restoration of damaged tissues through the activation of immune and non‐immune cells against pathogens. 5 , 6 The inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wounds encompasses various causal relationships and drivers that influence the onset, progression and healing of these wound conditions. A balanced inflammatory microenvironment facilitates the repair of chronic wounds, but disturbances in this process can lead to an excessive inflammatory response, damaging the immune system, causing pathological changes, severe tissue damage and impairing wound healing. 7 , 8 , 9 Therefore, understanding the characteristics of the inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wounds is crucial for controlling inflammation and devising strategies to accelerate wound healing. 10 , 11 , 12

Bibliometric analysis, a method for systematically and characteristically examining literature, has been extensively employed for qualitative and quantitative analysis of scientific publications. 13 , 14 This approach is now widely used in various medical fields, including gynaecology and complementary and alternative medicine, to quickly grasp research trends and hotspots and to evaluate the distribution of authors, countries/regions and journals within a research area. 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 This facilitates the identification of future research directions and developments.

In this study, a scientific knowledge map of the inflammatory microenvironment in diabetic wounds (DW) was created using tools such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer and R (version 4.3.1), aiming to summarize the research status and future development trend of inflammatory microenvironment in the field of DW, explore the hotspots and evolution trends in this domain.

METHODS

Publication sources and search methods

For this research, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database was chosen as the primary source for publications. This database is highly regarded within the academic community for its broad coverage of internationally acclaimed scientific journals known for their significant impact and exceptional quality. It offers a detailed and standardized collection of data, ideal for conducting bibliometric analyses. 19 Recognizing the dynamic and frequently updated nature of the database, a specific timeframe from January 1, 2014, to August 1, 2023, was selected for conducting the literature search. This approach was aimed at reducing potential biases that could arise from the database's rapid changes. The methodology for searching the database is outlined in Table 1. The inclusion criteria for publications were limited to articles and reviews, with an additional restriction that all documents must be in English.

Publication screening and access

Two researchers, identified as SHS and HML, independently conducted the screening process to determine the eligibility of publications for inclusion in this study. The selected literature were exported in two distinct formats: complete records and cited references. These documents were saved as plain text files, conveniently labelled “download_txt”. The content of these files encompasses a range of essential information, including the title of the publication, abstract, details of the authors, their affiliations, keywords, the publication date and the cited references. This comprehensive data extraction process is designed to facilitate a thorough analysis of the collected literature.

Data analysis

All relevant documents obtained from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database were processed using bibliometrix (based on R version 4.3.1), VOSviewer and CiteSpace for visualization and analysis. To assess research performance, two key metrics are primarily used: the number of publications (Np), which measures productivity, and the number of citations (Nc), which indicates the impact of the research.

The bibliometrix tool in R is utilized for conducting bibliometric analyses on leading research countries, institutions and journals. 20 The H‐index is a crucial metric used to evaluate the academic contributions of researchers and predict their future scientific achievements. 21 Additionally, the g‐index, an extension of the h‐index, offers a more nuanced measure of a researcher's impact and scholarly accomplishments. 19 , 22

VOSviewer, developed by the Centre for Science and Technology Studies at Leiden University, Netherlands, is a network analysis software specifically designed for scientometric research. It supports visual analysis and generates maps based on network data, illustrating connections between items via co‐citation links, co‐occurrence, citations and bibliographic coupling. VOSviewer is capable of producing three types of visual maps: network, overlay and density, facilitating an intuitive understanding of research connections and trends. 23 , 24

CiteSpace, a citation visualization and analysis software, was developed by Professor Chaomei Chen at Drexel University using Java. This tool combines scientometrics, data visualization and data mining to effectively present the structure, trends and dissemination of scientific knowledge. 25 CiteSpace excels in highlighting the key nodes and linkages within scientific research, offering insights into the evolution of fields and identifying emerging trends and patterns. 26

DISCUSSION

General information

This study presents the first bibliometric analysis identifying the key relationships and trends over time in inflammatory microenvironment research related to diabetic foot ulcers.

Compared with the traditional review, the research method of bibliometric analysis uses scientific statistical methods to quantitatively analyse the research hotspots, trends and other information, and the results are more comprehensive and objective.

By analysing data from public databases, we pinpointed hotspots and developmental trends in this area, highlighting the growing interest and potential in researching the inflammatory microenvironment's role in diabetic wound healing.

Intellectual base

Diabetic foot ulcers impact roughly 18.6 million individuals globally and 1.6 million in the United States annually, leading to decreased physical function, lower quality of life and higher healthcare costs. 31 Without treatment, these ulcers may escalate to infections, gangrene and even limb loss. About 50% of these ulcers get infected, necessitating hospitalization for up to 20% of cases, and 15%–20% of severe infections result in lower‐extremity amputations. 32 The condition is associated with a 5‐year mortality rate of 30%, increasing to over 70% following above‐foot amputations. 33 , 34 The direct annual cost of managing diabetic foot ulcers in the United States is estimated between $9 billion and $13 billion. 35

After long‐term knowledge accumulation from 2014 to 2021, the research on the inflammatory microenvironment related to diabetic wound showed a growth trend of blowout in 2022 and 2023. This indicates that research related to the inflammatory microenvironment is expected to be the direction of future chronic wound research and provide enlightening insights into the overall situation in the field of diabetic wounds.

Since 2014, the United States has been the leading country in the study, and the study size has been relatively stable. That changed in 2019 and changed completely in 2021, with China gradually becoming the country with the most published research papers and the gap with other countries widening. This phenomenon is related to the large number of diabetic wound patients in China. On the other hand, it also reflects the growing number and deepening depth of diabetic wound research in China. In this process, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Harvard University, University of Michigan and Egyptian Knowledge Bank played a key role as the main research institutions.

Interestingly, although the number of papers published by China is the first in the world in the past 5 years, the number of corresponding authors in the United States is still the first in the world. In addition, the number of international cooperative studies in the papers published by the United States is large, and the cited times and influence of the studies published in the United States are generally higher, which indicates that in the research field of diabetic wound‐related inflammatory microenvironment at present, the number of international cooperative studies in the United States is also higher. The United States is still dominant, and Chinese research needs to take on more dominant work in the future if it is to improve further.

Wound Repair and Regeneration is the journal with the highest number of publications in this field. Additionally, a significant volume of research papers has been found in materials‐related journals such as ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces and Acta Biomaterialia. This indicates that research on the inflammatory microenvironment of diabetic wounds has begun to adopt a multidisciplinary collaboration model, gradually extending from mechanistic studies to clinical material science. The overlap of journal themes represents the distribution of topics across academic journals, with three citation pathways. This means that current research on macrophages related to diabetic wounds is focused not only on basic research but also on translational medical studies.

Given the severity and poor prognosis of Diabetic Foot Ulcers (DFUs), the exploration of DFU nanomedicines has been ongoing. However, due to considerations of biosafety, especially the long‐term impacts of nanomedicine in vivo, the efficient targeting of nanomedicine to specific sites is still not fully understood. Thus, the development of nanomaterials based on the inflammatory microenvironment is still far from clinical application, with much work to be done. 36

Citation analysis reveals that the article by Paulina Krzyszczyk, published in 2018 in Frontiers in Physiology, holds the highest citation frequency to date. This article provides a comprehensive review of the physiological and pathological roles of macrophages in the inflammatory microenvironment of diabetic wounds. 28 Additionally, another study by Anna E. Boniakowski, published in 2017 in The Journal of Immunology, with a citation count of 58, reviews the phenotypic changes of macrophages in the chronic wound inflammatory microenvironment. This paper also discusses the challenges and controversies in the field. 29

The hotspots and frontiers

Based on the co‐occurrence analysis of keywords, it is possible to discern the distribution and evolution of research hotspots within a specific domain. This analysis highlights the clustering of keywords around pivotal themes in the field, with the most common keywords including “repair,” “angiogenesis,” “inflammation,” “macrophage” and “delivery”. The process of wound healing necessitates the proper orchestration of intricate biological and molecular mechanisms, including cellular functionality and proliferation. Nonetheless, the posttraumatic inflammatory response observed in wounds of diabetic individuals, which is attributed to the presence of advanced glycation end products, exhibits a protracted and persistent nature. A distinctive feature of non‐healing diabetic wounds is the compromised immune tissue homeostasis, denoted by the chronic and sustained activation of posttraumatic inflammation. 37

Through the analysis of research hotspots in different periods of time, we found that macrophages, nanoparticles and drug delivery have become an important hot topic in the study of diabetic wound inflammatory microenvironment. There is a solid experimental basis for the role of macrophages in the inflammatory microenvironment, but how to promote it to clinical practice is still a great challenge. The exploration of nanomaterials is a very good idea. Many studies have confirmed that nanomaterials in topical drug delivery systems offer enhanced permeation through the skin, enabling more efficient and targeted delivery of therapeutic agents directly to the site of action. Their small size allows them to penetrate skin barriers more effectively than larger particles, improving drug solubility and stability. This results in increased efficacy, reduced dosage requirements and minimized systemic side effects. 38 Additionally, nanomaterials can be engineered for controlled release, providing sustained drug delivery over extended periods and improving patient compliance. Angiogenesis and oxidative stress are also key areas of research interest, highlighting from an alternative perspective the complexity and multifactorial involvement in the inflammatory microenvironment of diabetic wounds. It underscores the necessity to explore the roles and degrees of participation of biological functions beyond those of macrophages.

By using bibliometric analysis, this research methodically delineates the existing scholarly discourse on Inflammatory microenvironment involvement in DW instances, concurrently pinpointing primary areas of interest and emerging trends within this field. Nonetheless, the study is subject to certain constraints. Primarily, its literature review is restricted to the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database, potentially overlooking relevant studies. This limitation stems from the current capabilities of bibliometric analysis software, which are inadequate for integrating and analysing literature across multiple databases. Moreover, the analysis was conducted using a limited set of tools, namely, VOSviewer, CiteSpace and the R bibliometrics package, which might not comprehensively represent the data. For subsequent research endeavours, there is an intention to incorporate advanced analytical tools, such as artificial neural networks, to enhance data analysis and interpretation.

CONCLUSION

Bibliometric analysis showed that the research of inflammatory microenvironment in DW has made rapid progress and has broad development prospects. The United States and China are the scientific centres for inflammatory microenvironment research in DW. In this area, the University of Michigan in the United States and Shanghai Jiao Tong University in China were the most productive. DAVIS FM from the United States is the most published and cited author. The focus is on macrophages, wound healing and angiogenesis. In addition, “macrophages” is a sports topic with potential research prospects. These analysis results will help researchers to understand the current research status and will become the focus and direction of future research on diabetic wounds. Future studies will also focus on the clinical translation of novel materials based on the repair of inflammatory microenvironment.

Study on the Microflow Field Output Performance: Effects of Rib/Channel Dimensions and Sensitivity to Operating Conditions
Authors: Shengnan Xu, Yirui Lu, Daijun Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRib/channel dimensions significantly affect mass transfer, product water discharge, and electron transport. Aiming for high-power density stacks, bipolar plate thickness has been continually reduced, and microflow fields with the width and height smaller than 1 mm currently lack thorough experimental study. In this article, three stainless-steel plates were designed and fabricated, featuring rib/channel widths of 0.2:0.8 mm (SS-0.2/0.8), 0.3:0.7 mm (SS-0.3/0.7), and 0.5:0.5 mm (SS-0.5/0.5). SS-0.5/0.5 gained a 59.05% peak power density enhancement over a graphite plate G1.0/1.0 with rib/channel widths of 1.0:1.0 mm. Furthermore, sensitivities of microflow fields to cathode inlet humidity (RHc), anode/cathode inlet pressures (Pa/Pc), and cell operating temperature (Tcell) were investigated. The optimal performance was observed at a low RHc. When RHc increased to 60%, SS-0.5/0.5 was more likely to accumulate water under the rib when operating at a high current density, rapidly increasing the mass transport resistance. Microflow fields had a negligible sensitivity to Pa/Pc since decrease in channel width below 1 mm is sufficient for the favorable mass transfer. Increasing Tcell showed a dominant impact on decreasing concentration loss via facilitating diffusion of reactant gases through the electrode surfaces and alleviating the liquid water accumulation due to an increase in the saturation vapor pressure of water.

Introduction

As direct energy conversion devices, proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs) transform hydrogen and oxygen into electricity via electrochemical reactions, with advantages such as high efficiency, zero emissions, quiet operation, and rapid response time.1−3 The bipolar plate (BPP), one of the key components of PEMFC, plays an important role in the mass, volume, and cost of fuel cell stacks.4,5 In the PEMFC structure, BPP is typically referred to as the “skeleton”, serving to provide rigid support for the stack and is responsible for current collection. Furthermore, it is designed with flow fields, to ensure the sufficient supply of reaction gases and the quick evacuation of product water, which are crucial for the optical mass transport and water management.6

Research studies have been focused on developing novel structure designs of flow fields to enhance the output performance. Based on the traditional serpentine flow field, Zhao et al.7 proposed a kind of compensating flow field to enhance distribution uniformity, water management, and thermal management. Blockages are typically introduced to increase the local reactant concentration in flow channels and to enhance forced convection effects toward the ribs.8−11 Shen et al.8 proposed the synergy angle and effective mass-transfer coefficient to evaluate the effectiveness of mass transfer, and they discovered that the higher density of blockages addition in flow channels, especially for the cathode side, led to the wider range of operating current density. However, Zhang et al.9 proposed that the excessive degree of water blockage in channels hindered the mass transfer and even damaged the membrane electrode assembly (MEA). Cai et al.10 divided the cross section shape of the blockage into the suppression part and the flow-guide part and discovered that the combination of the two parts was more beneficial to enhance the mass transfer than the only one part. Jiang et al.11 designed and compared the five blockage geometry and found that the elliptical blockage exhibited the highest improvement in the electrochemical conversion efficiency. In addition, the biomimetic flow fields such as river flow field,12 leaf-like flow field,13 flow field based on superior mesenteric artery,14 and lung-inspired flow field15 were developed to address the limitations of the conventional flow field and achieve higher output performance.

It should be noted that the innovative structures are mostly in the conceptual stage, which are complicated for manufacturing, and the main investigation is concentrated on numerical simulations. The commercialized flow fields currently tend to be designed to mature and relatively simple structures with the aim of decreasing the manufacturing cost and difficulty when amplifying the active area to improve the stack power density, and apparently the most common structures were parallel or serpentine flow fields.16,17 Therefore, based on parallel or serpentine flow fields, it can be inferred that designs of channel/rib dimensions are particularly crucial factors to be taken into consideration, which are closely relative to water–gas transport capacity18 and balance between species diffusion and electron transport.19 Muthukumar et al.20 developed the three-dimensional models of a PEMFC with various landing to channel width dimensions, and they found that 0.5 × 0.5 mm flow channel produced a larger power density than all of the other flow channels including 1 × 1, 1.5 × 1.5, and 2 × 2 mm, mainly due to higher oxygen concentration. Ozdemir and Taymaz21 investigated the effect of the channel width-to-rib width (Cw/Rw) ratio based on the single serpentine flow field design with Cw/Rw of 1:1, 1:0.5, and 0.5:1 mm, and they discovered that the highest power density was achieved at a Cw/Rw ratio of 2 due to the better water management. Ahmadi and Rostami22 investigated the effect of increasing the number of gas channels in constant MEA width, and they found that the larger channel numbers improved the species transfer toward GDL, consequently enhancing the output performance. Zhou et al.23 combined the down-the-channel performance model and genetic algorithm to investigate the effects of the rib to channel width ratio and the sum of rib and channel width, and they found that the larger RCWRs were optimal for the smaller SRCWs due to the decreasing concentration polarization for the smaller channel width and the lower ohmic losses for the higher RCWRs. Hamrang et al.24 developed a parallel-serpentine flow field with channel/rib dimension of 2:1.37 mm and discovered that increasing the ratio of channel height to channel width and ratio of rib width to channel width both contributed to the lower performance for traditional parallel serpentine flow fields. Because the larger channel height lengthened the distance of reactant gas to reach the catalyst, the larger rib width caused liquid water accumulation in GDL and CL above the rib.

Apart from optimizing flow field structures, another principle of flow field designs is to find optimal operating conditions to maximize the output power and durability.25 Critical variables include the relative humidity and pressure of the inlet gas and operating temperature. Reasonable RH of inlet gas provide sufficient water for membrane to increase proton conductivity while excessive humidity would cause water condensation in porous media that hinder reactant gas diffusion.26,27 Improving pressure of inlet gas is beneficial to increase the gas partial pressure that reduced activation overpotential28 and accelerate the gas diffusion rate to enable timely replenishment of the gases consumed at the membrane electrode.29 Ko et al.30 employed the X-ray visualization technique to analyze the effect of inlet gas pressure on water distribution, and they found that improving cathode pressure helped increase water saturation in membrane when water was deficient, thus enhancing the performance. Randrianarizafy et al.31 performed the design optimization of rib/channel patterns using a 2D cell model, and they found that the optimal channel was mainly dependent on the stoichiometry ratio of oxygen rather than relative humidity or pressure. Operating temperature is closely related to water management32 and efficiency of electrochemical reactions.33 The sensitivities of microflow fields to various operating conditions need to be investigated to reveal the optical optimal operating conditions.

Most of research studies have been relied on the computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model20−22,24 due to its advantages of cost savings and flexibility. It was noted that CFD models were commonly simplified based on several assumptions, and a large number of parameters were needed to be determined or estimated through experimental data. High-precision CFD simulations considering multiphysics, multiphase, and multiscale transport lead to a significantly increased consumption of computing resources, especially for the smaller rib/channel structure due to the required finer mesh resolution. There still remains a lack of experimental study that considers microflow field with channel due to the changes of water–gas transport behavior in the channel with the width and height smaller than 1 mm.34 Driven by the demand for high-power density stacks, BPP thickness has been continually reduced, and flow field designs have evolved toward increased refinement. Therefore, it is of significance to explore the effects of various dimension designs on microflow fields performance and identify the optimal operating conditions. This paper comprehensively investigates effects of rib/channel dimensions and sensitivity to operating conditions for microflow fields with the width and height smaller than 1 mm. The output performances of the three cathode microflow fields with various rib/channel dimensions (SS-0.2/0.8, SS-0.3/0.7, SS-0.5/0.5) were compared and analyzed by polarization curves, and their sensitivities to operating conditions including RH, inlet pressure, and operating temperature were explored. Meanwhile, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed to figure out the ohmic, charge-transfer, and mass-transfer resistances within PEMFCs. The optimal dimension design and operating condition for microflow channel were revealed and concluded. This paper was conducive to providing a useful reference for the development of flow field designs that enhance the power density of PEMFCs.

Test Methods

Polarization Curve

The output performance of the PEMFC single cell was measured by the polarization curves. The tests were conducted under the operational conditions, as detailed in Table 2. Throughout each set of experiments, the anode/cathode gas stoichiometric ratio was consistently maintained at 1.7:3, and the inlet gas temperature was kept consistent with the cell operating temperature. EIS Test

The in situ resistances of single cells were measured using the EIS. The amplitude of the alternating current (AC) signal was set to 5% of the direct current value, and the AC disturbance frequency range was from 100 mHz to 1000 Hz, enabling comprehensive characterization of the impedance behavior across a broad spectrum of frequencies relevant to PEMFC operation.

The resistances of the single cells were fitted to an equivalent circuit model using EIS data, as illustrated in Figure 4.35 The Rohm, Rct, and Rmt were represented to the ohmic resistance, charge-transfer resistance, and mass transport resistance, respectively. The constant phase elements represent the double-layer capacitance associated with the charge transfer and mass transport processes. Under low current densities, Rohm and Rct are significant in voltage loss and thus dominate the equivalent circuit model. Under higher current densities, water accumulation due to electrochemical reactions within the PEMFC becomes more prevalent, leading to flooding issues. Therefore, Rmt cannot be ignored.

Results and Analysis

Effect of Rib/Channel Dimension

As illustrated in Figure 5a,b, the overall performance of the microflow fields was significantly improved compared to that of the conventional-sized channel G-1.0/1.0. The peak power density of G-1.0/1.0 reached 695.8 mA·cm–2, whereas SS-0.5/0.5 yielded a maximum output power of 1106.7 mA·cm–2, marking a substantial enhancement of 59.05% compared to G-1.0/1.0. It was clearly demonstrated the superiority of the microflow field design proposed in this paper. The SS-0.5/0.5 exhibited superior performance over SS-0.3/0.7 and SS-0.2/0.8 in both the ohmic polarization region and the concentration polarization region. The peak power density of SS-0.5/0.5 was increased by 16.01% and 26.05%, compared to those of SS-0.3/0.7 and SS-0.8, respectively. Combined with impedance analysis at 1600 mA·cm–2 that was shown in Figure 5c,d, the key mechanisms contributing to this performance enhancement are revealed: (1) widening the rib width led to an increase in the contact area between ribs and the MEA, therefore reducing ohmic losses. (2) Narrowing the channel width decreased the cross-sectional area of the flow channels, which contributed to an increase in the turbulence intensity of airflow within the cathode flow field, accelerating the diffusion of reactant gas to the electrode surface. The SS-0.5/0.5 performed particularly well under lower humidity conditions, exhibiting a reduced demand for humidification, which was good news for the fuel cell system to remove the humidifier in the future.

(a) Polarization curves; (b) peak power density; (c) EIS test result @1600 mA·cm–2; and (d) resistance analysis of various microflow fields @1600 mA·cm–2.

It is worth noting that the effect of rib/channel dimension on the output performance was highly dependent on the specific flow field structure and operating conditions, which may yield varying findings. Yan et al.36 discovered that larger channel/rib ratio brought the better performance for the parallel channels than that for the interdigitated channels because the interdigitated channels with the forced convection were less dependent on the larger contact surface between the reactants and the diffusion layer. Shimpalee and Van Zee37 developed the parallel serpentine models with different widths of rib/channel, and they found that narrower channel with a wider rib leads to better cell performance, which was contrary to previous findings of the parallel channels.36 Based on a single straight channel CFD model for high-temperature PEMFC, Xia et al.38 found that the moderate rib/channel width of 1:1 mm achieved the maximum output power density than further decreasing or increasing the rib/channel dimension.

Sensitivity to Relative Humidity

As shown in Figure 6a, for the single cell assembled with SS-0.2/0.8, it achieved optimal performance at RHc = 40%, exhibiting the highest peak power density (898.2 mW/cm2 at 1800 mA/cm2). However, compared to under RHc values of 0% and 20%, the single cell was more prone to concentration polarization when operating at high current densities under RHc values of 40%. As the RHc increased from 20%, the current density corresponding to the concentration polarization region gradually shifted to lower current densities. When RHc increased to 60%, the output power density reached its peak at a current density of 1400 mA/cm2, after which the voltage started to decline rapidly.

Polarization curves of (a) SS-0.2/0.8; (b) SS-0.3/0.7; (c) SS-0.5/0.5 under different RHc; and (d) sensitivity to RHc.

As shown in Figure 6b, for the single cell assembled with SS-0.3/0.7, a significant enhancement in the output performance was observed when the RHc increased from 0% to 20%. The output performance curves for the cell operating at RHc = 20% showed similarity to operating at RHc = 40% but the concentration polarization loss slightly increased at RHc = 40%. Upon increasing RHc to 60%, the output power reached its peak of 799.4 mW/cm2 at a current density of 1400 mA/cm2, after which the voltage rapidly decreased, leading to a conspicuous drop in power density. By analyzing the power density curve, SS-0.3/0.7 achieved its best performance at RHc = 20% (954 mW/cm2 at 2000 mA/cm2), offering a wider range of high power densities corresponding to larger current densities.

As depicted in Figure 6c, for the single cell assembled with SS-0.5/0.5, it also attained its peak power density (1106.7 mW/cm2 at 2100 mA/cm2) at RHc = 20%. When RHc was raised from 0% to 20%, there was a marked improvement in both the electrochemical polarization region and the ohmic polarization region but a slight increase in concentration polarization losses at high current densities. As RHc increased to 40%, the current density corresponding to the concentration polarization region decreased. At RHc = 60%, voltage declined rapidly when the current density reached 1600 mA/cm2, which indicated that flooding might occur. It was illustrated that moderately enhancing the RHc was beneficial not only for maintaining an adequate water content in the PEM, which increased proton conductivity and reduced ohmic losses, but also increased the number of triple-phase reaction sites, thereby augmenting the overall effectiveness of the triple-phase reaction zone and facilitating electrochemical reactions. However, it is worth noting that a substantial amount of water could be produced at high current densities, and an increase in cathode humidity led to flooding. Moreover, a relative humidity of 100% at the anode hindered the back diffusion of water from the cathode, exacerbating the cathode flooding and consequently severely undermining the output performance. This highlighted the delicate balance required to manage humidity levels to optimize the output performance.

Figure 6d displays the strong sensitivity of microflow fields with varying dimensions to RHc. It can be revealed that SS-0.5/0.5 has less demand on increasing RHc than SS-0.2/0.8 and SS-0.3/0.7. As shown in Figure 7, EIS testing results at 500 and 1400 mA/cm2 were analyzed. It was observed that as the RHc increased, the left intersection of the EIS arc with the real axis consistently moved forward. At a current density of 500 mA/cm2, for all the microflow fields, it showed a reduction in the EIS arc when RHc was raised from 0% to 20%, followed by a slight decrease as RHc was further increased. At a current density of 1400 mA/cm2, an initial reduction in the EIS arc was observed when RHc increased from 0% to 20%, but a significant enlargement of the EIS arc was observed upon raising the RHc to 60%, which suggested that the mass-transfer impedance was assumed as a dominant factor influencing the cell performance.

Equivalent circuit fitting was performed on the Nyquist plot to further quantitatively analyze the EIS results. As illustrated in Figures 8 and 9, Rohm was decreased with the increase of current density. Meanwhile, the increase in current density led to a reduction in Rct since there was less water production from electrochemical reactions at lower current densities around 500 A/m2, especially when there was no humidification, which exacerbated the drying of the PEM and CL. The lack of water in the polymer electrolyte increased the Rct, making it more difficult for the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) to proceed. At higher current densities around 1400 A/m2, the increased water production from the reaction enhanced the hydration level of the polymer electrolyte, thus increasing the number of active sites at the triple phase boundary and promoting the ORR. As shown in Figure 9, both Rct and Rmt of SS-0.5/0.5 remained relatively lower values compared to that of SS-0.3/0.7 and SS-0.2/0.8 within the RHc range of 0% to 40%. However, when RHc was increased to 60%, both Rct and Rmt of SS-0.5/0.5 were larger than those of SS-0.3/0.7 and SS-0.2/0.8. Liquid water in the GDL produced by water vapor condensation trend to transport by capillary forces,40 and then break through randomly, which was strongly influenced by rib structure.41 It can be revealed that when the RHc exceeded the optical values, the larger rib width made it harder for the produced water accumulated under the rib to exhaust to the channel, although the smaller channel width accelerated the gas velocity. Bagherighajari et al.42 found the similar results that the higher values of rib/channel width ratio resulted in lower cell performance due to liquid water accumulation in the rib regions. Additionally, Xu et al.43 developed a water transport model and discovered that smaller rib/channel ratio promoted water drainage. The paths of gas transport under the rib were gradually blocked, leading to a reduction in the effective porosity for gas transport within the GDL and exacerbated concentration polarization, thereby increasing the Rmt. Simultaneously, the pores within the CL were occupied by liquid water, and the surface area of the liquid film continuously increased until it completely covered the surface of the Pt catalyst particles, resulting in a rapid increase in Rct.

Sensitivity to Operating Temperature

PEMFCs were typically operated within a temperature range of 65–85 °C; therefore, the cell operating temperature (Tcell) was incrementally increased by intervals of 10 from 50 up to 80 °C to explore the sensitivity of microflow fields to Tcell in the section.

As illustrated in Figure 11a, for the single cell assembled with SS-0.2/0.8, when Tcell increased from 50 to 80 °C, the peak power density was improved by 26.24%, with the corresponding current density increasing from 1300 to 2000 mA/cm2. The power density curve initially exhibited a decreasing trend before subsequently increasing. At 70 °C, the performance in the low-to-medium current density region was superior to that at 80 °C, but a notable reduction in concentration polarization was observed. As shown in Figure 11b,c, the influences of Tcell on the single cell assembled with SS-0.3/0.7 were similar to those of SS-0.5/0.5. When Tcell increased from 50 to 80 °C, both SS-0.3/0.7 and SS-0.5/0.5 experienced considerable improvements in their peak power outputs, registering enhancements of 43.33% and 32.13%, respectively. This enhancement coincided with a notable rise in the corresponding current densities, advancing from 1300 to 2000 mA/cm2 for SS-0.3/0.7 and from 1500 to 2200 mA/cm2 for SS-0.5/0.5. The increase in temperature had a significant impact on reducing concentration polarization rather than improving the performance in the low current density region. Figure 11d shows the strong sensitivity of all microflow channels to Tcell.

It has been elucidated that under relatively low operating temperatures, where effective water management was paramount due to the propensity for liquid water formation that could impede gas transport, narrower channels accelerated the gas velocity, preventing the accumulation of liquid water and maintaining paths for gas transport, consequently mitigating concentration losses. Moreover, at low operating temperatures, where gases have higher viscosities, narrower channels were more conducive to turbulent flow. Turbulence enhanced gas mixing, promoting faster delivery of gas molecules to the electrode surfaces and reducing concentration losses.

Conclusions

In this paper, effects of rib/channel width on the output performance of microflow channels and sensitivity to varying operation conditions were comprehensively investigated, and the main conclusions are summarized as follows:

(1)Widening the rib width led to an increase in the contact area between ribs and the MEA and therefore reducing ohmic losses, while narrowing the channel width increased the air velocity within the field and thereby enhancing mass transfer. The SS-0.5/0.5 design offered a good trade-off balance between ohmic losses and mass transfer rates, which could be leveraged in the development of fuel cell systems.

It suggested that microflow fields help reduce dependence on air humidification at high current densities, potentially simplifying the humidification system in fuel cell stacks. However, ensuring robust water management across a wide range of operating conditions still remains a challenge to be paid attention.

Reply
Authors: Susan M. Goodman, Michael Parides, Melanie Smith
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We appreciate Deb and Sharma's interest in our article describing the relationship of synovial fluid cell counts and synovial lymphocytic inflammation in the tissue in patients with rheumatoid arthritis. 1 We are responding to several questions they have raised.

First, Deb and Sharma note that the accuracy of our diagnostic threshold of ≥1,400 cells/μL to differentiate between high and low inflammatory synovial tissue may be overestimated, as our model was derived and tested in the same cohort. We agree and plan to validate our findings using a larger external data set. Notably, we estimated the area under the curve (AUC) using a “leave one out” cross‐validation approach to simulate an out‐of‐sample validation. Although we did not report this result in the article, that procedure yielded an AUC of 0.89, only slightly less than the reported value of 0.91. Nonetheless, given our relatively small sample size, we agree generalizability of our results remains a question; one that we will address in our continuing work.

Deb and Sharma also raise the question of overfitting of our multivariable logistic regression analysis. Although works by Riley et al 2 and van Smeden et al 3 raise concerns about the stability of coefficient estimates and potential inflation of estimated odds ratios, the ability to fit stable models from small samples is well documented (see, for example, the study by Vittinghoff and McCulloch 4 ). We did not experience any issues related to convergence in fitting our models, and there is little empirical basis to believe that the parameter estimates of model variables, or their SEs, are inflated due to the concerns raised.

We agree with the assertion by Deb and Sharma that the synovial fluid cell count reflects the biologic processes within the synovial tissue. Our study establishes this relationship, which we hope to validate in a larger cohort, as the issue of generalizability is of concern in a relatively small cohort. However, our aim was to establish this relationship between synovial fluid and synovial tissue with the goal of validating synovial fluid analysis as a surrogate for synovial biopsy.

Regarding Deb and Sharma's final point, we aimed to establish a sound and statistically valid cut point so that clinicians can assess the presence or absence of joint inflammation as reflected in more readily accessible synovial fluid. This has clinical salience, as patients with refractory rheumatoid arthritis may have either high or low inflammatory synovial tissue, and there is great clinical need to be able to differentiate between these states to choose the appropriate therapy.

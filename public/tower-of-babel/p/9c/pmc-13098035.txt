Finger Acrometastasis as the Initial Presentation of Advanced Lung Adenocarcinoma
Authors: May Lynn Chan, Mariya Mahmood, Himanshu Singh, Sugeesha Wickramasinghe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAcrometastasis is a rare presentation of advanced cancer with a poor prognosis. The most common primary cause of acrometastasis is lung cancer, followed by renal, breast and colon cancers. Here we describe an elderly woman with primary adenocarcinoma of the lung who presented with finger metastasis.Keywords: acrometastasis, finger, lung cancer

A 72‐year‐old non‐smoker presented following a fall and head injury preceded by a several‐week history of pain and swelling in her right index finger, headache and dizziness. She denied chest symptoms, weight loss, loss of appetite and trauma to her finger. Examination revealed a tender, swollen right index finger. Hand X‐ray showed lytic changes and destruction of the proximal phalynx of the right index finger, and CT head showed left temporal and right parietal bone metastases (Figure 1). Subsequent staging CT scan revealed a spiculated left upper lobe lesion, right lung nodules, intrathoracic lymphadenopathy and a left adrenal nodule (Figure 2). Following a non‐diagnostic endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS), a CT‐guided biopsy of the left lung lesion confirmed lung adenocarcinoma. Lung cancer staging was confirmed as T1cN3M1c. The patient underwent palliative radiotherapy to her left skull metastasis. She was offered palliative amputation of her right index finger for pain control. She was reviewed by the palliative care team and received hospice care before she unfortunately passed away.

(A) Photograph of the patient's right hand on admission showing right index finger swelling and erythema. (B) X‐ray image of the right index finger showing lytic lesion in the proximal phalanx. (C) CT image of the left temporal bone lytic lesion.

(A) CT chest showing left upper lobe nodule (green arrow). (B) Contralateral ground glass nodule (blue arrow). (C) Left adrenal deposit (red arrow).

Acrometastasis due to primary lung cancer is an extremely rare phenomenon accounting only for 0.1% of bony metastases. It is important to keep a high index of suspicion of acrometastases to achieve an early diagnosis despite having a poor prognosis in similar cases.

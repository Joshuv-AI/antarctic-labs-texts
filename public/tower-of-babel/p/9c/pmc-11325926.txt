A commentary on ‘Metabolic tumor burden as a prognostic indicator after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in pancreatic cancer’
Authors: Changyuan Wang, Yi Li, Bole Tian
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Pancreatic cancer is a highly malignant digestive system tumor originating from the pancreatic duct epithelium. According to the global cancer statistics in 2020, pancreatic cancer ranks seventh in the world in terms of cancer-related death causes, and the 5-year survival period of patients is less than 10%1,2. The use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy has increased over the last decade, especially in patients with borderline and locally advanced disease and, less so, for patients with resectable disease. This approach has been encouraged as it ensures that all patients receive chemotherapy, has the potential to downstage the disease, is shown to increase R0 resection rates, treats micrometastases, and will select out patients who have bad biology and will ultimately have early recurrence3.

However, evaluating treatment response objectively has not yet been established. Lee et al.4 performed a retrospective study to evaluate the treatment response after neoadjuvant chemotherapy using 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography-computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) in conjunction with carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) levels. The results of the study demonstrated that elevated metabolic tumor volume is an influential prognostic factor for worse survival, regardless of CA19-9 levels after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. In addition, patients with both elevated metabolic tumor volume and CA19-9 levels face a high risk of recurrence even after curative resection, warranting follow-up or continuous neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which could be considerable treatment.

18F-FDG PET/CT is a well-established molecular imaging technology that enables non-invasive quantification of tumor biological characteristics. It is now routinely used for diagnosis, staging, outcome prediction, and response evaluation for cancer patients. Several studies have reported that the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), a semiquantitative index that reflects tumor metabolic activity, is a significant prognostic imaging marker for solid cancers, but it cannot represent uptake for the entire tumor mass5. More recently, there has been a growing recognition of the metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), the volumetric PET parameters that have been explored as measures of metabolic tumor burden, as promising quantitative PET indices.

The current study had some limitations. First, the study design was retrospective, the sample size was small, and absence of an independent set of patients to perform verification. Furthermore, the heterogeneous treatment protocols may have a confounding effect on prognostication. Further prospective trials with larger homogeneous patient cohorts are warranted to validate the results. Second, the accuracy of the metabolic tumor burden may be affected by the false-negative and false-positive results of 18F-FDG PET. We suggest that authors try to reduce such deviation by reading 18F-FDG PET together with diagnostic CT. At last, ~22% of African Americans and 10% of Caucasians lack the Lewis or secretory gene, resulting in non-elevated CA19-9 levels pre-neoadjuvant and post-neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which may affect the results. Prospective trials from different countries should be required to confirm the conclusion.

Association of COVID-19 public health measures with cardiac non-invasive testing utilisation in Ontario, Canada: a population-based, retrospective time series analysis
Authors: Ethan Lin, Bernard Mendis, Tomi Odugbemi, Atul Sivaswamy, Jiming Fang, Idan Roifman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe COVID-19 pandemic disrupted healthcare delivery worldwide, beginning in early 2020. While cardiac non-invasive testing (NIT) plays a critical role in the evaluation and management of cardiovascular disease, the population-level association of pandemic-related public health measures on cardiac diagnostics remains unclear.ObjectiveTo compare the rates and patterns of cardiac NIT before and after the implementation of the first and subsequent provincial COVID-19 lockdown measures.Design and settingA population-based and registry-based retrospective time series analysis conducted in Ontario, Canada, a single-payer healthcare system.ParticipantsAll individuals receiving transthoracic echocardiography, stress echocardiography, nuclear imaging, cardiac CT or cardiac MRI between 17 March 2018 and 16 March 2022.Outcome measuresStandardised weekly testing rates compared across pre-pandemic and pandemic intervals using ARIMA (autoregressive integrative moving average) modelling.ResultsAmong 5 269 897 cardiac tests performed, the cumulative rates decreased from 228.8 (95% CI 228.5 to 229.0) tests per 100 000 person-weeks in the pre-pandemic period to 204.3 (95% CI 204.0 to 204.5) tests per 100 000 person-weeks in the pandemic period (p<0.0001). The first lockdown led to the most significant decrease in cumulative testing rate (105.9 per 100 000 person-weeks, 95% CI 105.4 to 106.4, p<0.0001), with an incomplete recovery over time that varied based partially on NIT modality. Outpatient testing decreased more compared with inpatient testing. The rates of transthoracic echocardiography and nuclear imaging decreased, whereas cardiac MRI remained stable and cardiac CT increased.ConclusionsDuring the COVID-19 pandemic in Ontario, cumulative cardiac NIT rates declined significantly and did not fully recover by early 2022. The magnitude and patterns of these declines raise concerns about delayed cardiovascular diagnoses while also signalling a possible shift in diagnostic strategies. Efforts should prioritise preserving access to essential diagnostic testing during future health crises.

Background

Early during the COVID-19 pandemic, increased signals for cardiovascular morbidity and mortality were observed. Cardiovascular disease was independently associated with worsened outcomes in seminal case series from China and Italy.1 2 Meanwhile, Marijon et al3 observed an almost 50% increase in out-of-hospital cardiac arrests in Paris, France during the pandemic, whereas Banerjee et al4 estimated excess deaths from cardiovascular causes of between 49 932 and 99 865 in England and Wales. More recently, long-term observational studies have also identified long-term adverse cardiovascular effects of SARS-CoV-2 infection compared with other respiratory viruses.57 While this increase in cardiovascular morbidity was well reported, it is unclear what proportion of excess events were related to SARS-CoV-2 infection itself or the indirect effects of public health measures leading to delayed presentations and altered healthcare practices, such as delays in diagnostic testing and subsequent management.8

Disruptions in the lives of patients globally and the delivery of healthcare were significant indirect effects of the pandemic, related to staffing issues, public health recommendations, infection control and prevention guidelines and altered healthcare-seeking behaviours. Cardiac catheterisation rates for acute coronary syndrome and ST-segment myocardial infarction across the USA, Italy and Spain decreased by 30–40% during the early COVID-19 pandemic.911 In England, a 40% reduction in emergency department presentations for myocardial infarction was observed, with a similar reduction in acute decompensated heart failure presentations in Toronto, Canada.12 13 In the USA, overall primary care visits decreased by 21%, and new medication prescriptions decreased by 26%.14 With mounting evidence regarding the utility of cardiac non-invasive testing (NIT) in reducing mortality and the importance of early diagnosis in the implementation of evidence-based management, it is possible that any changes in cardiac testing during the pandemic may have contributed to increased cardiac morbidity and reversal of observed declines in cardiovascular event rates over the past several decades.1518 There is a lack of population-level data regarding the association of successive public health lockdowns and cardiac NIT rates, and the extent to which these rates have recovered since.

The purpose of the present study was to compare the rates of cardiac NIT before and after the implementation of the first and subsequent provincial lockdown measures. Further, we aimed to assess whether NIT rates recovered following the lifting of restrictions. To our knowledge, a population-based evaluation of the association of the COVID-19 pandemic on cardiovascular NIT utilisation has not been conducted.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective time series study evaluated all cardiac NITs including transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), stress echocardiography, nuclear stress imaging, cardiac MRI and cardiac CT in Ontario, Canada, between 17 March 2018 and 16 March 2022. These dates were chosen such that the pre-pandemic and post-pandemic periods were similar in duration, allowing for more accurate comparisons of testing rates. With a population of over 14 million residents, Ontario is the most populous province in Canada, and the majority of residents are covered under the province’s single-payer system, Ontario Health Insurance Plan (OHIP). OHIP covers medically necessary services, including emergency visits, hospitalisations, physician visits, medical tests and laboratory tests. This makes the province an ideal setting for studying population-level trends in healthcare utilisation using publicly available information.

The index date was defined as the date the test was received during the study period. Only one test per individual per day was counted, although multiple tests could be performed on the same individual throughout the study period. Number of tests as the unit of measurement was selected to better account for trends in testing throughout the entire study period. In cases where multiple tests were conducted on the same day, one test was selected using the following hierarchy: stress echocardiography, nuclear stress imaging, cardiac MRI, cardiac CT and TTE. This was done to ensure that only one test per individual per day was counted when multiple tests were performed on the same day. This approach is consistent with prior administrative database studies evaluating cardiac diagnostic testing patterns.17 19 Individuals were excluded if they had invalid records (ie, invalid identification number, invalid birth date or death date or invalid gender), were aged <20 years old or >105 years old, were non-Ontario residents at the time of the test or were ineligible for OHIP 1 year prior to their index date such that baseline variables could not be established. Data sources

Data on the cardiac tests of interest were obtained from physician claims data in the OHIP database. The Registered Person Database, which contains demographic information for all Ontario residents covered under the provincial health insurance plan, was used to obtain age, sex and postal codes. Postal code information and population estimates were additionally obtained from the Postal Code Conversion file and CENSUS datasets. Medical history, baseline clinical characteristics and frailty data were obtained from the OHIP database, the Canadian Institute of Health Information Discharge Abstract Database (CIHI DAD) and the National Ambulatory Care Reporting System (NACRS) using validated algorithms where available. These datasets were linked using unique encoded identifiers and analysed at ICES, an independent, non-profit research institute whose legal status under Ontario’s health information privacy law allows it to collect and analyse healthcare and demographic data without consent for health system evaluation and improvement. Secure storage and access to these data are governed by internal policies and procedures approved by the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario.

Study variables

Exposure

The main exposure was the interval during which NIT was performed during the study period. These intervals were categorised as pre-pandemic and pandemic periods. The pre-pandemic period was used as a baseline for the rates of NIT in Ontario. Both periods lasted for approximately 2 years.

Pandemic period intervals were defined based on the issuance of states of emergency by the Government of Ontario, who also provided a uniform framework for the severity of lockdowns to be implemented across the province. Due to regional variabilities in the institution of lockdown periods across the province, the Canadian Institute for Health Information COVID-19 Intervention Timeline was used as reference.20 Intervals were defined as follows:

The first day of the first state of emergency corresponded to the mid-point of the study period, and the pandemic period was defined as between 17 March 2020 and 16 March 2022. A secondary exposure was the location of the test, obtained from OHIP physician claims records and categorised as inpatient or outpatient tests.

Outcomes

The outcome of this study was the age-standardised and sex-standardised rates of cardiac NIT in the pandemic versus pre-pandemic period per 100 000 person-weeks both cumulatively and per testing modality. NIT utilisation outcomes included counts of echocardiography, stress echocardiography, cardiac MRI, cardiac CT and nuclear imaging. Definitions used to ascertain the receipt of each NIT have been previously validated and published to show a high degree of accuracy.1719 2123

Baseline characteristics

Baseline socio-demographic characteristics including age, sex, rurality and income quintiles for individuals undergoing cardiac NIT were determined. Rurality was determined using postal code information and the Statistics Canada indicator of rural and small-town status based on community size. Rural communities were defined as having a community size less than 10 000 while urban communities were defined as having a community size of 10 000 residents or more.24 Income quintile, a measure of relative household income adjusted for household size and cost of living, was used as a proxy for socioeconomic status. It was calculated within each Census Metropolitan Area or Census Agglomeration and divided into quintiles, with quintile 1 representing the lowest income and quintile 5 representing the highest income.

Clinical information regarding any history of hypertension, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), congestive heart failure (CHF), cardiovascular disease, coronary revascularisation procedures (percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)) during an individual’s lifetime was determined using validated algorithms.2529 The Charlson Comorbidity Index, a measure of comorbidity in the 5 years prior to index date, was also determined and categorised as 0, 1, ≥2 or no hospitalisations.30 Finally, frailty was assessed using a validated algorithm derived from hospitalisation data within 2 years of the index date.31 Frailty scores were then categorised as low risk (<5), intermediate risk (5–15) and high risk (>15).

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics on the index date, including socio-demographic and clinical profiles, were summarised for all individuals receiving NIT during both the pre-pandemic and pandemic intervals. Categorical variables were presented using counts and proportions, while continuous variables were described using means and medians. Standardised differences were used to compare differences between periods, with values greater than 0.1 being considered significant.32 Crude counts, proportions, and age-standardised and sex-standardised rates per 100 000 person-weeks with 95% CIs were calculated for overall tests and specific cardiac NITs during the pre-pandemic and pandemic period using the yearly census-based Ontario population interpolated without consideration of death or migration as a standard population. To evaluate the variations in cardiac testing over successive lockdown periods, age-standardised and sex-standardised rates per 100 000 person-weeks with 95% CIs were also calculated for each of the defined intervals. Counts, proportions and standardised rates were calculated separately for overall tests and specific modalities, further stratified by inpatient and outpatient tests. Pre-pandemic and post-pandemic periods included approximately the same number of tests.

A time-series analysis of the cardiac NIT rates during the pre-pandemic and subsequent lockdown periods was performed using an autoregressive integrative moving average (ARIMA) model after accounting for seasonality, background trends and autocorrelation. As such, these time-series analyses are ecological in nature, and do not incorporate patient level characteristics. The ARIMA models incorporated both non-seasonal and seasonal components. First-order differencing was applied to both the non-seasonal and annual seasonal trends, and each component included a moving average term without an autoregressive term. Stationarity was assessed using the autocorrelation function (ACF) plot. In the main model for the overall cohort, the rapid decay of the ACF indicated adequate stationarity. Residual autocorrelation was evaluated using the residual ACF plot and was considered negligible, as spikes beyond the first and second lags remained within the 95% confidence bounds. To graphically represent the trends following successive lockdown periods, locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) was applied.33 34

A sensitivity analysis was conducted excluding those patients with multiple tests performed in a single day. All statistical analyses were run on SAS Enterprise Guide running SAS V.9.4 (Cary, North Carolina, USA).

Results: study characteristics

Table 1 summarises the baseline characteristics of cardiac NITs performed during the pre-pandemic and pandemic period. The overall study consisted of 5 269 897 tests, corresponding to a mean patient age of 63.7 years (SD 15.7). 2 510 479 (47.6%) tests were performed in females. 2 720 917 tests were performed during the pre-pandemic period, whereas 2 548 980 tests were performed during the pandemic period. Across all tests, approximately 63% were performed in individuals with a history of hypertension, 31% with a history of diabetes and 28% with established cardiovascular disease. 16% of tests were performed in individuals with a history of CHF, 12% with a history of PCI and 7% with a history of CABG. There were no significant differences in the rates of cardiac risk factors or established cardiovascular disease across the tests performed during the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods (standardised difference <0.10). Approximately 3% of patients received two tests on the same day (n=156 743 tests), including 105 385 with TTE and stress echocardiogram and 51 358 patients with TTE and nuclear imaging on the same day.

Table 3 summarises the corresponding standardised rates of NIT per 100 000 person weeks. In the time series analysis, the first lockdown period was associated with a statistically significant reduction in cardiac testing after accounting for background trend, seasonality and autocorrelation. There was a decrease in testing rates from 228.8/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 228.5 to 229.0) to 105.9/100 000 (95% CI 105.4 to 106.4) person-weeks during the first lockdown period (p<0.0001). ARIMA modelling found that the second and third lockdown periods were not associated with statistically significant reductions in the cumulative rates of testing compared with pre-pandemic levels (p=0.06644 and p=0.2184, respectively). Although there was a trend towards recovery to pre-pandemic rates following the lifting of restrictions, cumulative cardiac NIT rates did not fully return to pre-pandemic levels at the conclusion of our study period (see figure 2). In January 2022, despite the highest number of COVID-19-related intensive care unit and hospital admissions in Ontario,35 cumulative rates of NIT did not decrease and were higher than the preceding state of emergency (222.1/100 person-weeks (95% CI 221.6 to 222.5) vs 219.5/100 person-weeks (95% CI 218.6 to 220.4)). Overall, the cumulative rate of cardiac NIT decreased significantly from 228.8/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 228.5 to 229.0) in the pre-pandemic period to 204.3/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 204.0 to 204.5) in the pandemic period (p<0.0001).

In a sensitivity analysis excluding those who had more than one test in a single day, there was a decrease in testing rates from 226.1/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 226.1 to 226.7) to 104.7 (95% CI 104.2 to 105.2) person-weeks during the first lockdown period (p<0.0001). Overall, the cumulative rate of cardiac NITs decreased significantly from 226.1/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 226.1 to 226.7) in the pre-pandemic period to 200.8/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 200.3 to 201.2) in the pandemic period (p<0.0001).

At the level of individual NITs, cumulative rates of TTE (158.7, 95% CI 158.4 to 158.9 vs 143.5, 95% CI 143.3 to 143.7, p<0.0001), nuclear imaging (45.4, 95% CI 45.3 to 45.6 vs 37.9, 95% CI 37.8 to 38.0, p<0.0001) and stress echocardiography (20.5, 95% CI 20.5 to 20.6 vs 18.6, 95% CI 18.5 to 18.7, p<0.0001) decreased significantly during the pandemic compared with pre-pandemic periods, whereas cumulative rates of cardiac MRI (1.4, 95% CI 1.4 to 1.5 vs 1.4, 95% CI 1.4 to 1.5, p=0.9449) remained similar, and cumulative rates of cardiac CT (2.7, 95% CI 2.6 to 2.7 vs 2.8, 95% CI 2.7 to 2.8, p<0.0001) increased significantly (table 2). Among modalities with decreased usage during the pandemic period, rates of nuclear imaging and TTE did not return to pre-pandemic levels at the end of the study period. Specifically, the rate of nuclear imaging tests was 39.9/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 39.7 to 40.1) at the end of the study period compared with 45.4/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 45.3 to 45.6) prior to the pandemic. The rate of TTE was 156.1/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 155.8 to 156.5) at the end of the study period compared with 158.7/100 000 person-weeks (95% CI 158.4 to 158.9) prior to the pandemic. In comparison, rates of stress echocardiography (20.6, 95% CI 20.5 to 20.6 vs 21.3, 95% CI 21.2 to 21.5), cardiac MRI (1.4, 95% CI 1.4 to 1.5 vs 1.6, 95% CI 1.5 to 1.6) and cardiac CT (2.7, 95% CI 2.7 to 2.7 vs 3.1, 95% CI 3.1 to 3.2) rebounded beyond pre-pandemic levels during the final period of reopening (table 4).

Discussion

In this population-based retrospective time series study examining rates of cardiac testing in Ontario, Canada during the COVID-19 pandemic among approximately 14 million people, there were significant changes in rates and patterns in cardiac NIT between the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods. This decline was pronounced in the outpatient setting after implementation of the first lockdown measures, with a trend towards partial recovery following successive lockdown periods. These results were robust to a sensitivity analysis excluding tests where more than one test was performed in a single day. Baseline trends were comparable to known patterns of NIT usage.21 To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first population-based study in Canada to examine the relationship of cardiac NIT rates and public health measures (including impact and recovery) during the COVID-19 pandemic.

In the largest study examining the association of COVID-19 on NIT usage, Hirshfield et al36 observed marked reductions in cardiovascular testing in the early stages of the pandemic using survey data gathered from 909 institutions in 108 countries. In the USA, there were significant differences in NIT rates by region, with greater decreases in procedures requiring exercise-stress compared with pharmacologic-stress. Cumulative rates of TTE between March 2019 and April 2020 decreased by 80%, stress echocardiography by 81%, cardiac MRI by 72% and all NIT by 68%. Factors found to be associated with greater reduction in NIT included staff redeployment, outpatient testing, urban centres and COVID-19 prevalence. On a global scale during the same period, TTE decreased by 59%, CT coronary angiography decreased by 54%, CT coronary calcium decreased by 71%, cardiac MRI decreased by 64% and stress echocardiography by 83%. Lower gross domestic product was found to be significantly associated with a greater reduction in NIT, whereas non-teaching facilities, smaller hospital size and lack of access to personal protective equipment trended towards similar reductions.37 Follow-up studies demonstrated variable degrees of recovery globally in cardiac diagnostic testing towards pre-pandemic levels as well as modest increases in certain advanced imaging modalities such as cardiac CT, cardiac MRI and positron emission tomography.

In Canada, Mackinnon et al38 examined the healthcare resource utilisation trends and cardiovascular outcomes using CIHI DAD and NACRS over successive 3-month periods between March 2017 (ie, baseline) and March 2021 (ie, the end of the second state of emergency). Overall, they observed an 83% decrease in diagnostic testing between 2019/2020 and period 1 of the COVID-19 pandemic (15 March 2020 to 14 June 2020). The largest decrease was observed for stress testing, with smaller decreases in echocardiography. There were trends towards recovery in subsequent periods, however exact rates of imaging were not reported and were likely underestimated due to the specific administrative databases employed in the study. The present study builds on this existing body of knowledge by examining rates of NIT usage over a longer period relative to public health measures and incorporating physician claims codes.

Overall, marked changes in modality utilisation throughout the pandemic (ie, increasing use of cardiac CT, similar use of cardiac MRI and decreasing use of stress echocardiography and echocardiography) were observed and consistent with previous investigations.3639 One potential explanation for these shifts may be related to the reluctance to perform exercise stress testing and other potentially aerosol-generating procedures, prompting a preference for more advanced modalities over traditional forms of stress. The impact of triaging protocols/practices for filtering out lower priority echocardiograms and other tests cannot be ruled out.40 41 Notably, whereas other modalities trended towards recovery throughout successive lockdown periods, nuclear imaging did not recover to the same extent. One explanation may be related to the higher resource requirement for performing nuclear imaging, where the unique challenges of radiopharmaceuticals procurement, storage and administration may have been impacted disproportionately by the pandemic.42 43 Collectively, these changes may have accelerated a trend towards increasing use of cardiac CT instead of nuclear imaging, a trend that has been supported by recent European and American guidelines.44 45 The finding that the final period of lifted restrictions was associated with higher rates of cardiac CT and MRI compared with pre-pandemic levels suggests the possibility of lasting changes in diagnostic practices that warrant future study, especially with the publication of recent guidelines on the management of chronic coronary disease.

The marked reductions in rates of cardiac NIT further demonstrate the vulnerability of current cardiovascular diagnostic testing strategies to public health policies. It is not possible due to the nature of this study to determine the degree to which these decreases were related to reduced demand of testing. Possibly confounding this issue is the known association between COVID-19 and short- and long-term adverse cardiovascular outcomes, patient avoidance of healthcare contact in response to stay-at-home orders, delayed seeking of care especially in urban centres due to fear of contracting the infection and increased healthcare disparities.36 39 46 Despite an overall promising trend towards recovery in NIT rates and the second/third lockdown periods not being associated with significant reductions in NIT rates based on ARIMA modelling, the probability of future global pandemics highlights the importance of ongoing emergency-preparedness strategies to maintain diagnostic testing during times of emergency. These measures include improved patient screening, triaging priority of studies, access to personal protective equipment, adherence to universal precautions, physical distancing, cleaning of diagnostic equipment and air exchange via air filtration units after aerosol-generating procedures.47 Although further study is needed to elucidate the degree to which the worsened cardiovascular outcomes during the early pandemic may have been attributed to the reductions in diagnostic testing observed in the present study, it is possible that delays in diagnostic testing may have had an impact on potentially preventable cardiovascular events, as reported by others.37 48 49

Limitations

Limitations of this study should be noted. First, the 2021 American Heart Association Chest Pain Guidelines and ISCHEMIA trial were published during the early pandemic period and therefore may have influenced testing patterns subsequently.50 51 Second, while a time series analysis was employed to account for several factors including seasonality, potential confounders such as changing patient demographics and risk factor profiles may be present at the individual patient level. Third, due to the observational nature of these data, they along with all LOESS plots cannot be considered causal but rather are associative in nature. Finally, although Ontario’s single-payer healthcare system enables comprehensive, population-level assessment of healthcare utilisation, the generalisability of these findings to other jurisdictions should be interpreted with caution. Healthcare systems differ substantially with respect to financing models, access to care and resource allocation, all of which may influence patterns of diagnostic testing. In addition, there was considerable global heterogeneity in pandemic-related factors, including infection rates among healthcare workers, healthcare system capacity to accommodate surges, prioritisation of inpatient versus outpatient care and the timing, intensity and enforcement of public health measures. These system-level and policy differences likely contributed to variability in the degree of disruption in diagnostic testing observed across regions.5254 Further study is warranted to better understand how these jurisdiction-specific factors modify the impact of public health measures on cardiovascular diagnostic care.36

Conclusion

In the present study, the association of the COVID-19 pandemic on cardiovascular NIT rates in Ontario, Canada was evaluated at a population level among approximately 14 million people. Although the first lockdown period was associated with a dramatic reduction in testing, subsequent lockdown periods did not demonstrate similar reductions. While individual NIT testing patterns varied, overall testing rates trended towards recovery but did not fully recover to baseline levels by the end of the study period. These results reinforce the importance of having emergency-preparedness strategies in place to provide important diagnostic testing during public health crises in the future. They may also signal a shift in diagnostic testing patterns in a post-pandemic world.

The technical utilization solutions for curtailment during China’s rapid expansion of wind and PV power
Authors: Libo Zhang, Huan Zhang, Haobo Ren, Pengcheng Tang, Qunwei Wang, Dequn Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryWith the continued rapid growth of installed capacity for wind and PV power in China, the challenge of wind and PV power curtailment has become increasingly pressing. This study quantifies the spatiotemporal distributions and evolving trends of wind and PV curtailment for 31 provinces in China. The ridge regression-based model is developed to forecast provincial consumption capacities for 2030 and 2035. Using technology readiness level, levelized cost of utilization, and life cycle carbon emission, four power utilization technologies are evaluated. Building on this assessment, and subject to engineering, economic, and policy constraints, the mixed-integer programming model is formulated to minimize total utilization cost. This model identifies the optimal combination of the five utilization pathways for provinces with high projected curtailment in 2030 and 2035. The findings of this paper can provide decision-making references for selecting technical solutions and formulating policies to promote the utilization of wind and PV power.Keywords: wind power, photovoltaic power, power curtailment, power consumption projection, utilization technologies assessment

Introduction

As the world’s largest renewable energy market, China has consistently ranked first globally in installed wind and PV power capacity for consecutive years.1 The accelerated development of PV and wind power industries plays a pivotal role in driving national energy transformation, ensuring energy security, and advancing the realization of carbon neutrality goals. Among these, integrated models such as agrivoltaics and livestock photovoltaic systems have generated substantial socio-economic benefits, notably contributing to poverty reduction through rural job creation and driving infrastructure improvements in underdeveloped and remote regions.2,3 In 2024, the installed capacity of wind and PV power in China reached 521 GW and 886 GW, respectively, accounting for 42% of the total power capacity. Correspondingly, annual power generation from wind and PV resources reached 991.6 billion kWh and 834.1 billion kWh, respectively, accounting for 19.4% of the total power generation.4 There is a significant disparity between the installed capacity and power generation of wind and PV power. This is due to the inherent intermittency and volatility of wind and solar power, resulting in a mismatch between supply and demand.5 Then, over 80% of China’s high-quality wind and solar resources are concentrated in the less economically developed “Three North” regions (Northwest, Northeast and North China), while the major electricity consumption centers are located in the economically advanced southeastern coastal areas6 (the description of wind and solar energy resources in China is provided in S1). This geographical mismatch between renewable resources and energy demand has led to persistent curtailment of wind and PV power. In some areas of northern and western China, the annual curtailment rate of wind and PV power can be as high as 30%.7

Power curtailment not only leads to substantial economic losses but also reduces investment returns, hindering the sustainable development of the renewable energy industry. As a consequence, the poverty reduction, environmental, and energy security benefits of the energy transition are placed at risk.8,9 Given the continuous rapid growth of PV and wind power installed capacity, the following critical issues require in-depth research: (1) Given varying provincial resource endowments, what is the actual extent of power curtailment across different provinces of China? Existing studies have primarily used published provincial data on curtailment to analyze curtailment trends across China’s provinces. However, these data only account for curtailment resulting from system-related issues. That is, they reflect power that was generated but not connected to the grid. They do not include curtailment caused by power plants not operating normally.10,11 In fact, wind and PV power generation may not be able to operate at maximum capacity for various reasons, and the resulting curtailed generation should also be included in the curtailment rate; however, current statistics do not take this into account.12 For instance, during 2023–2024, widespread restrictions on PV power generation were imposed across many localities in Henan province. The longest restriction period lasted from 9:00 to 16:00, effectively curtailing generation during nearly the entire window of peak solar irradiance.13 However, the ingenerated electricity resulting from these scheduled outages is excluded from the published curtailment statistic. (2) Given current utilization levels and policy contexts, what future trends in curtailed wind and PV power can we anticipate as installed capacity continues to expand? During the 15th and 16th Five-Year Plan periods, the installed capacity of wind and PV power in China is projected to maintain rapid growth. Installed wind and PV power capacity is expected to exceed 2500 GW by 2030,14 reaching 3600 GW by 2035.15 This massive expansion of installed capacity will place significant strain on the grid’s integration capability. Consequently, a comprehensive assessment is required to project the curtailment levels of wind and PV power for 2030 and 2035. (3) As a critical flexible resource for balancing, energy storage can significantly improve renewable energy utilization in wind and PV power systems and effectively mitigate wind and PV power curtailment.16 As a key power transmission corridor, ultra-high voltage transmission (UHV) can serve as a virtual energy storage resource under dynamic control strategies, enabling the redistribution of system power across both temporal and spatial dimensions.17 When studying the optimal deployment of different energy storage technologies for wind and PV power utilization, representative technologies are typically selected to characterize the distinct characteristics of each storage category.18 Given the increasingly severe challenges of power curtailment, further research is needed into the technical maturity of representative utilization technologies. These technologies include UHV, lithium-ion battery storage (LIB), hydrogen energy storage (H2ES), and pumped storage hydropower (PSH). What will be the levelized cost of these technologies in 2030 and 2035? Furthermore, how will their life cycle carbon emissions differ? (4) Under engineering, economic, and policy constraints, and accounting for investment, operational, and carbon emission costs, how should the utilization solutions be optimally combined, while also accounting for local consumption by data centers, to maximize the utilization of wind and PV power while minimizing system costs?

Current research on wind and PV power curtailment can be broadly divided into two categories: analysis of curtailment phenomena and their causes, and modeling and optimization of curtailment mitigation technologies. Table 1 summarizes the existing relevant literatures.

•Analysis of power curtailment and its causes. Accurately characterizing the current state and underlying causes of power curtailment is a prerequisite for developing effective mitigation strategies. Existing research has addressed this issue across multiple spatial scales and from diverse methodological perspectives. National-level study: Chen et al.19 employed a logarithmic mean Divisia index (LMDI) decomposition framework to analyze the factors mitigating wind and PV power curtailment at both the national and regional levels in China. Luo et al.20 proposed a systematic solution to wind power curtailment in China by integrating the country’s actual conditions with the experience of developed countries, addressing the problem from the perspectives of planning, policy, and technology. Shu et al.22 systematically elucidated the root causes of wind and PV power curtailment and proposed measures for addressing the challenges of renewable energy utilization, based on an analysis of the current state of renewable energy integration and utilization in China. Du et al.23 analyzed the key factors affecting renewable energy consumption in China and proposed corresponding solutions, taking the current status of grid integration as their point of departure. Novan and Wang30 used historical hourly generation and curtailment data from the California electricity market to estimate the marginal curtailment rates for wind and PV power, finding that only 91% of the output supplied by new solar capacity contributes to increasing the state’s renewable supply, the remaining 9% being lost in the form of increased curtailment. Provincial-level studies: Pei et al.21 systematically analyzed the spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of wind power curtailment in China using available curtailment data, identifying the “Three North” regions as the primary areas affected by curtailment; however, this study did not address the new challenges arising from the recent surge in installed capacity. Tang et al.31 attributed PV power curtailment to dual factors operating on both the generation and grid sides, and analyzed large-scale curtailment events in Gansu and Xinjiang. Zhang et al.32 investigated the patterns and influencing factors of renewable energy curtailment in Qinghai and proposed strategies for large-scale renewable energy utilization. International comparative perspective: Yasuda et al.33 proposed the C-E (curtailment-energy ratio) plot method, which provides a framework for objectively quantifying and comparing curtailment phenomena across different countries. However, their analysis is primarily grounded in the electricity market environments of Europe and the United States, and the applicability of this method to China’s unique hybrid model, combining planned dispatch with market mechanisms, requires further validation. Jia and Qi34 conducted a comparative analysis of renewable energy utilization in California and Shandong; however, this comparison remained qualitative and lacked a quantitative assessment.

•Research on curtailment mitigation technologies. In response to the growing challenge of renewable energy curtailment, a growing body of research has been conducted to evaluate and analyze the effectiveness of various technical solutions. The role of energy storage in mitigating curtailment: Travaglini et al.25 compared the effectiveness of new and second-life battery storage solutions in mitigating offshore wind power curtailment. Zhang et al.26 investigated strategies for reducing curtailment through the coordinated deployment of compressed air energy storage and wind power. Wang et al.27 systematically analyzed the opportunities and challenges of the “electricity-hydrogen-electricity” pathway for renewable energy integration in China, highlighting its distinct advantages in large-scale, long-duration energy storage. Troncoso and Newborough35 examined the integration of electrolyzer facilities with wind farms to produce green hydrogen using otherwise-curtained electricity. Jiang et al.36 analyzed the mechanisms driving solar curtailment and reviewed the status of solar-to-hydrogen conversion and hydrogen storage technologies from three perspectives: hydrogen production, storage, and utilization, identifying the full utilization of this technology as a strategic pathway for China. Saadatmandi et al.37 investigated smart electric vehicle charging as a means of reducing photovoltaic energy curtailment. Alkhalidi et al.38 evaluated several energy storage systems for improving the utilization of curtailed energy, thereby reducing energy costs and carbon emissions. Li et al.39 used Lanzhou as a case study to produce hydrogen via water electrolysis powered by curtailed electricity; the feasibility of using methanol as a liquid energy carrier was also explored, given the relatively high cost and safety risks associated with hydrogen storage and transportation. Research on grid infrastructure and UHV for mitigating curtailment: Niet et al.28 demonstrated that incorporating curtailment costs into infrastructure investment assessments significantly increases the system value of energy storage and dispatchable loads, underscoring the importance of internalizing curtailment costs in investment decisions. Liu et al.29 developed a risk-oriented transmission expansion planning model based on super-quantile theory to quantify the role of grid reinforcement in reducing wind power curtailment. Wu et al.17 investigated the equivalent energy storage effects achievable by ultra-high-voltage direct-current transmission under demand response dispatch strategies, revealing the flexibility value of the transmission network in enabling spatiotemporal power transfer.

Summary of relevant research literatures

Most existing studies analyze the curtailment of wind and PV power in China based on macro-level national data. However, given that China has established differentiated curtailment rate assessment indicators for each province, local governments and power generation companies may proactively adjust their operational strategies in response to performance evaluation pressures. For instance, by intentionally limiting or suspending operations at wind and PV power plants to reduce the volume of curtailed power reported in published statistics.40 This “strategic curtailment” resulting from such artificial adjustments to generation schedules is not effectively captured by the published curtailment statistics framework.12,41 In light of the significant differences among provinces in terms of resource endowments, grid structures, and policy implementation capabilities, there is an urgent need for more targeted regional studies to reveal the actual curtailment levels in each province and the associated risk of underestimation. Second, while current research largely focuses on the characteristics of curtailed power during historical periods,19,20,21,22,23,31 systematic forecasting and assessment of future curtailment trends remain relatively underdeveloped. During the 15th Five-Year Plan period, the installed capacity for wind and PV power in China is projected to grow at an annual rate of approximately 300 GW, reaching a total of about 2500 GW by 203014 and further increasing to approximately 3600 GW by 2035.15 This rapid expansion will substantially intensify the pressure to integrate wind and PV power, potentially triggering large-scale curtailment and thereby posing an obstacle to achieving carbon neutrality goals.42 Consequently, systematically forecasting and evaluating future curtailment trends is of considerable practical significance. Furthermore, current research tends to focus on the role of individual technologies, such as ultra-high-voltage transmission or specific energy storage solutions, in mitigating curtailment.25,26,27,28,29,35,36,37,38,39 In practice, however, the effective integration of wind and PV power often relies on the coordinated operation of multiple technologies, including UHV transmission, LIB storage, and hydrogen storage systems. To support this, it is critical to quantitatively assess the performance of these technologies under combined engineering, economic, and policy constraints and to optimize their deployment specifically for regions facing high curtailment risks. Based on the above analysis, this paper systematically evaluates the curtailment trends of wind and PV power in China’s 31 provincial-level regions from 2018 to 2024 and forecasts curtailment levels for each province in 2030 and 2035. Concurrently, a quantitative assessment and comparative analysis of the technical performance, economic costs, and life cycle carbon emissions of various power utilization technologies is conducted. Based on these findings, a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model is developed with the objective of minimizing the costs associated with integrating wind and PV power. Under engineering, economic, and policy constraints, this model identifies the optimal combination of utilization technologies, thereby providing systematic decision support for the efficient utilization of renewable energy in China’s future energy system. The research framework of this paper is shown in Figure 1. And the main contributions of this paper are as follows.

•The systematic assessment framework was established, encompassing four sequential stages: quantification of current curtailment levels, projection of future curtailment trends, evaluation of utilization technologies, and optimization of utilization solutions deployment. First, by comparing theoretical utilization hours with actual utilization hours, this study quantitatively estimates the wind and PV power curtailment across China’s 31 provinces from 2018 to 2024. The findings revealed that existing reported data may underestimate the actual scale of curtailment, thereby providing policymakers with a more accurate assessment of the current state of wind and PV power curtailment. Furthermore, this paper developed a provincial-level ridge regression model for predicting wind and PV power consumption capacity. Integrated with wind and PV generation projections for 2030 and 2035, derived from high-resolution MERRA-2 meteorological data, the model was used to project future curtailment levels across provinces under both policy and low-carbon scenarios. This approach ensures that the findings possess strong policy foresight and decision-making value.

Results

The status of wind and PV power curtailment in China

Current status of wind and PV power curtailment

Figure 2 presents the calculated curtailment rates for PV and wind power in China from 2018 to 2024. Geographically, the curtailment rates for both wind and PV power in the “Three North” regions and Xizang exceed those in other provinces. PV power curtailment rates are particularly high in the Northwest and Xizang. From 2018 to 2024, the average PV power curtailment rates in Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, Xinjiang, and Xizang were 3.84%, 5.83%, 8.79%, 4.13%, 6.03%, and 23.91%, respectively. Wind power curtailment is relatively severe in the Northwest, Northeast, and North China regions, with average rates of 6.31%, 3.52%, and 5.09%, respectively, over the same period. Temporally, although the overall curtailment rates declined slightly in 2023 and 2024, the curtailed power still increased substantially compared to the 2018–2022 period. Notably, in 2024, the national curtailed wind power increased by approximately 16 TW h relative to 2018, while that of PV power rose by approximately 26.88 TW h.

Since 2018, the average curtailment rate for wind and PV power in China has shown an overall downward trend, a development often interpreted as an indication that the curtailment problem has been largely resolved. However, the curtailment rate is defined as the ratio of curtailed power generation to total wind and PV power generation. In recent years, the installed capacity of wind and PV power has expanded significantly, and power generation has increased markedly. However, the grid’s transmission capacity and peak-shaving capabilities have not kept pace, resulting in power generation growth outpacing the growth in grid integration capacity, and new power generation being unable to be utilized promptly.44 Specifically, in 2018, China’s installed wind and PV power capacities were 184.27 GW and 174.33 GW, respectively. In 2024, these figures reached 520.68 GW and 886.66 GW, representing increases of 182.56% and 410.97% compared to 2018. Given this context, even if the curtailment rate remains stable or declines slightly, the absolute curtailed power is highly probable to increase significantly. In fact, in 2024, the curtailed wind power across China amounted to approximately 43.71 TW h, while curtailed PV power totaled approximately 33.01 TW h. This scale of resource waste leads to economic losses: Based on an electricity price of 0.3 CNY/kWh, the annual loss exceeds 23 billion CNY and may also result in environmental damage.45

The significant increase in curtailed wind and PV power stems from multiple factors, the primary one being the power system’s insufficient peak-shaving capacity. This is largely because the generation profiles of solar and wind power are fundamentally misaligned with demand: PV generation peaks around noon, well before the typical evening and nighttime peak in electricity demand, while wind generation often peaks at night, coinciding with periods of low demand. Conventional thermal power sources have limited peak-shaving capacity and slow ramping rates, making them difficult to respond rapidly to the abrupt fluctuations characteristic of wind and PV power output. When wind and PV generation surges, the grid lacks sufficient flexible regulation resources to utilize the excess power and must resort to curtailment to maintain system balance.13,46 While this strategy mitigates grid operational pressure to some extent, it directly results in the waste of renewable energy, thereby undermining the environmental benefits of wind and PV power generation.

Furthermore, as shown in Figure 3, the curtailment of wind and PV power in China exhibits a highly concentrated and persistent spatial pattern. The core challenge lies in the spatial mismatch between renewable energy resources and electricity demand. China’s wind and solar resources are predominantly concentrated in the western and northern regions, whereas the “Three North” regions are characterized by relatively low economic output and limited industrialization, with local electricity demand far insufficient to utilize the vast amounts of wind and PV power that could be developed there. In contrast, the economically developed eastern regions account for over 50% of the nation’s GDP,47 yet possess relatively scarce high-quality wind and solar resources compared to the “Three North” regions. Moreover, high land-use intensity in these areas limits the available space for constructing large-scale wind and PV power bases. This spatial disparity means that clean electricity from the resource-abundant western regions must be transmitted over long distances to reach the eastern demand centers. However, existing transmission infrastructure lacks sufficient capacity. The challenges hindering grid development include not only massive investment requirements and lengthy construction periods but also persistent difficulties in cross-regional coordination. Consequently, not all electricity can be delivered in a timely manner, resulting in the curtailment of wind and PV power.48 Additionally, systemic factors are a major cause of the current severity of power curtailment. China’s power-balancing structure has long been organized on a provincial basis. Against the backdrop of an economic slowdown, most provinces face an electricity surplus, and provincial governments are often reluctant to purchase power from other provinces, even when the cost is lower, in order to protect the interests of local power generators. This has rendered cross-provincial power trading even more challenging.49,50

Existing research on curtailed wind and PV power typically relies on curtailment rates published in reports. However, the reported curtailment rates for PV and wind power measure the amount of curtailed electricity after generation but before grid connection. Because the government has imposed curtailment rate targets on each province, local governments may require wind and PV power generators to reduce their utilization hours in order to lower the reported curtailment rate. Consequently, this portion of lost power is excluded from reported curtailment statistics.12 This paper compares the amount of curtailed power derived from the reported curtailment rate with that calculated using the difference between theoretical and actual utilization hours (Figure 4). The results show that, for both wind and PV power, the curtailed power estimated from utilization hour data is higher than that reported using the published curtailment rate. This indicates that, according to the calculation method employed in this study, the currently published figures for wind and PV curtailment, particularly PV curtailment, may be somewhat underestimated. For instance, the PV installed capacity in Qinghai was 25.61 GW in 2023 and 36.63 GW in 2024. Some PV power plants were required to suspend power generation for 3–4 h during peak generation periods (midday) in 2023.52 In Henan province, which has a high installed capacity of distributed PV, an increasing number of PV power plants have been required to suspend generation for 3–6 h during midday since early 2024.13 However, these large-scale forced generation suspensions are excluded from reported figures. Consequently, reported power curtailment may underestimate the actual levels.53

Project of wind and PV power curtailment in 2030 and 2035

Figure 5 shows the wind and PV power utilization capacities of 31 provinces for 2030 and 2035. These capacities were predicted by the ridge regression model, and the regression coefficients derived from the model are presented in Tables S2 and S3. The forecasts for installed capacity in this paper are divided into two scenarios: policy scenario and low-carbon scenario. It is worth noting that Inner Mongolia and Xinjiang, representing North China and the western regions respectively, possess extremely abundant wind and solar resources and offer inherent advantages for large-scale renewable energy development. However, these regions generally face the practical constraint of relatively limited local electricity demand, resulting in a structural mismatch between generation capacity and local consumption. Against this background, cross-regional power transmission and large-scale energy storage will become key technological pathways to addressing this discrepancy.

Based on the above analysis, driven by supportive policies, technological advancements, and improvements in market mechanisms, wind and PV power generation capacity in most provinces is expected to increase significantly by 2030 and 2035. However, the comprehensive assessment of the relationship between potential generation capacity and actual power consumption indicates that, despite the rapid growth of renewable energy, curtailment will persist. The fundamental reason is that the expansion of installed capacity often outpaces improvements in the grid’s ability to integrate such power. It is worth noting that in some provinces, the high concentration of wind and solar resources coexists with relatively limited local electricity demand, creating a spatial mismatch between resource distribution and load centers that further exacerbates the imbalance between supply and demand. Assessment and optimization of wind and PV power utilization solutions

Comparative analysis of wind and PV power utilization technologies

Based on existing research into the causes of curtailed wind and PV power, this paper assessed feasible technical solutions to mitigate this issue. The comparative analysis of UHV transmission, hydrogen storage (alkaline and PEM electrolyzers), LIB storage, and pumped-storage hydroelectricity is conducted across three dimensions: technical performance, economic performance, and carbon emissions.

Technical assessment of different technologies

Based on the technology readiness level (TRL) model,63 this paper assessed the maturity of four wind and PV power utilization technologies from the conceptual stage to commercial application. Based on the nine-level TRL definitions in Table S6 and comprehensive analysis of existing literature, the results of the TRL assessments for UHV transmission, hydrogen storage, pumped-storage hydroelectricity, and LIB storage are as follows:

UHV transmission serves as a strategic pathway for China to enable large-scale, long-distance power delivery, optimize the allocation of national energy resources, and advance the clean and low-carbon transition of the energy sector. It plays a critical role in supporting the new national energy supply and consumption system. Currently, these projects have entered a phase of large-scale, high-quality development.64 Figure 7 compares UHV transmission technologies in terms of technical maturity and assesses their future development directions based on trends in relevant Chinese standards. As shown in Figure 7, the development of UHV technology in China has gone through several landmark stages. Since the beginning of the 21st century, driven by massive market demand in China, the technology has fully matured and gained widespread application. Currently, the technology has fully reached and continues to maintain the TRL 9 level. The development of UHV power grids in China has now entered its third phase of rapid expansion. During the 14th Five-Year Plan period, plans call for the construction of 24 AC lines and 14 DC lines, with an additional five lines expected to come online in 2025.68 Based on the current status of UHV grid construction and operation in China, the relevant technologies have been widely deployed in large-scale projects and validated through actual operation, demonstrating high reliability and maturity.69,70

Technology development level of UHV transmission

Although ultra-high-voltage transmission technology was initiated relatively late in China, it has developed rapidly.

To date, its safety, economic viability, and environmental sustainability have been comprehensively validated.65,66,67

Hydrogen has a high energy density, allowing substantial energy storage in compact equipment, which makes it well-suited for long-duration and large-scale storage applications.71 H2ES systems utilize surplus wind and PV power to produce hydrogen via water electrolysis. The hydrogen is stored during periods of electricity surplus and released during shortages, thereby enabling energy regulation and balancing supply and demand. China has launched multiple H2ES demonstration projects.72 In particular, under the “electricity-hydrogen-electricity” model, the production of green hydrogen supports the clean utilization of renewable energy. Figure 8 compares H2ES technologies in terms of technological maturity. In terms of technological pathways, AEL and PEM electrolysis have become mainstream, accounting for 52% and 37% of the market, respectively. The alkaline electrolysis technology has reached TRL 8, having demonstrated reliability and cost-effectiveness in MW-scale projects, while the PEM technology is transitioning from TRL 7 to TRL 8. Overall, green hydrogen production technologies are at a critical turning point, moving from TRL 7 to 8 toward full commercialization (TRL 9). The drivers of development have shifted from single-technology breakthroughs to a multi-driver model encompassing climate policy, declining renewable energy costs, and technological iteration.76,77 Currently, H2ES lacks sufficient economic competitiveness: The cost of green hydrogen remains higher than that of gray and blue hydrogen, and there is an urgent need to optimize full-cycle efficiency and reduce the levelized cost of energy. From the technical perspective, key materials for PEM electrolyzers, such as catalysts and proton exchange membranes, remain partially dependent on imports, and from the infrastructure perspective, breakthroughs are urgently needed in large-scale, low-cost storage and transportation technologies, particularly for liquid hydrogen and solid-state hydrogen. Furthermore, the hydrogen pipeline network remains underdeveloped and cannot support large-scale transmission and distribution.78 Notably, in regions rich in wind and solar resources such as Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, and Qinghai, numerous integrated “wind/PV-hydrogen storage” mega-base projects have entered the planning and construction phases. These projects are in the final stage of commercial validation through large-scale and long-duration operation.

Technology development level of hydrogen energy storage

This paper focuses on the development of hydrogen energy storage technologies in China, particularly alkaline water electrolysis and PEM water electrolysis.73,74,75

As shown in Figure 9, the development of pumped storage technology can be traced back to 1870, and from the 1990s to the early 21st century, the technology matured, reaching TRL 8–9. Since the early 21st century, with the rapid growth of renewable energy, pumped storage has leveraged its flexible regulation capabilities to become a crucial means of ensuring the stable grid integration of variable renewable sources such as wind and PV power.79 The development of pumped-storage technology in China began relatively late, with research and development starting in the late 1960s. After nearly three decades of progress, a number of pumped-storage power plants were constructed in North, East, and South China between the 1990s and the early 2000s.85 Currently, pumped storage has become the most mature, economical, and reliable large-scale regulation method within renewable power systems, with sustained and proven performance in real commercial operating environments. It is projected that about 8 GW of new pumped storage capacity will be added in 2025, bringing the total capacity to approximately 66 GW.86,87 Based on its mature commercial application and wide-scale deployment, pumped storage technology has reached TRL 9.

Technology development level of pumped-storage hydroelectricity

This study primarily analyzes the development of pumped-storage technology in China,79,80,81,82,83 and as of the end of 2024, it accounted for 42.4% of the cumulative installed capacity in the country’s electricity storage market.84

As shown in Figure 10, battery energy storage technology originated with the invention of primitive electrochemical batteries in 1800. In 1991, China began introducing and initially applying LIB storage technology. Over the following three decades, driven by policies such as mandatory storage allocation, the newly added new energy storage capacity of China reached 42.37 GW in 2024, with LIB storage accounting for a substantial 89%.90 Currently, this technology has achieved large-scale commercial application worldwide, covering grid frequency regulation, renewable energy integration, and commercial and industrial energy storage, with its technical reliability fully validated. In China, multiple hundred-megawatt-scale projects, such as Huadian Delingha and Da’an wind-PV hydrogen production and storage, entered commercial operation in 2024. In both the electric vehicle and power storage sectors, lithium-ion energy storage has entered a phase of mass production and widespread market deployment. Overall, LIB energy storage technology has now reached TRL 9, demonstrating full-scale commercial viability.

Technology development level of LIB energy storage

This study primarily analyzes the development of lithium-ion energy storage technology in China.83,88,89 To date, the technology has reached the industrialization stage, and as of the end of 2024, lithium-ion energy storage accounted for 97.1% of the cumulative installed capacity in China’s new energy storage market.84

Analysis of the TRL for various power utilization technologies indicates that UHV transmission, PSH, and LIB storage have all reached TRL 9. Despite their high level of maturity, these technologies still face a series of practical application challenges. The construction cycle for UHV power grids is typically lengthy, generally requiring about 2 years. The development of pumped storage power stations is constrained by topographical and geological conditions. LIB energy storage still requires improvement in terms of cost, safety and recycling systems: First, its economic viability is sensitive to fluctuations in lithium resource prices, with material costs 30%–40% higher than those of sodium-ion batteries; then, safety must comply with the mandatory “no fire, no explosion” requirement stipulated in the 2025 revised national standard; next, recycling systems also remain underdeveloped, the current industry standards are primarily focused on manufacturing and performance testing, lacking systematic regulations for end-of-life battery recycling and supply chain coordination. H2ES is currently in the overall planning stage. Although AEL hydrogen production technology has reached industrial maturity, its application as an energy storage solution still faces challenges related to variability adaptation, system efficiency, and economic viability. PEM electrolyzer technology, while at a lower maturity level than AEL, offers clear advantages in variability adaptation, energy efficiency, and rapid response. However, high costs and limited lifespan continue to constrain its large-scale deployment.

Economic assessment of different technologies

Low-cost wind and PV power utilization technologies constitute key supports for achieving high-penetration renewable energy systems under the goal of carbon neutrality. Centered on the levelized cost of power consumption and employing the learning curve model, this paper quantitatively examines the cost evolution of four types of power utilization technologies and compares their projected performances for the years 2025, 2030, and 2035.

Referring to relevant data from UHV demonstration projects in China, this study analyzes the variation in the annual cost of unit capacity for UHVAC and UHVDC transmission systems with respect to transmission distance. Without considering the outage loss of the transmission system, the results are presented in Figure 11A. When the levelized cost of wind and PV power is taken into account, as the transmission distance increases, the annual cost gap between UHVDC and UHVAC transmission systems gradually narrows. When the transmission distance exceeds approximately 975 km, UHVDC transmission becomes more cost-effective than AC transmission. This finding aligns with the conclusion reported in ref.65, which states that “the economic transmission distance for UHVDC systems exceeds 1000 km” The underlying reason is that, within the total investment, the proportion of line construction costs in AC systems is relatively high, with the unit length cost being about 1.5 times that of DC transmission lines. In contrast, DC systems require substantially higher investment in converter stations, with the cost of a ±800 kV converter station being approximately 4.77 billion CNY compared with about 1.42 billion CNY for an AC substation, while DC transmission offers a higher rated transmission capacity. Consequently, as transmission distance continues to increase, the economic advantages of DC transmission become more pronounced. Moreover, the reduction in annual cost of UHV transmission in 2030 and 2035 relative to 2025 is comparatively modest, owing to the fact that current UHV technologies have already achieved a high level of reliability and maturity, leaving limited scope for further technological improvements to significantly enhance overall economic efficiency.

The trends of the LCOS for PSH and LIB energy storage with varying annual utilization hours are shown in Figures 11B and 11C. Utilization hours have a significant impact on the LCOS. Without considering charging costs, when annual utilization hours exceed 2000 h, the LCOS of PSH can drop below 0.37 CNY/kWh. This makes it the lowest-cost energy storage technology at present. By 2030 and 2035, the cost is projected to decline further to 0.36 CNY/kWh and 0.35 CNY/kWh, respectively. Similar to UHV transmission, PSH has a low learning rate.91 As a result, the downward cost trend is limited, and there is even a slight risk of increase. In contrast, the current LCOS of LIB energy storage is relatively high. Figure 11C shows that, without charging costs, it ranges between 0.65 and 1.02 CNY/kWh. Its minimum value is still much higher than that of pumped storage. Therefore, its current economic viability is low. However, LIB technology has a high learning rate, varying from 12% to 30%.92 This offers significant potential for cost reduction. By 2035, the LCOS is expected to fall below 0.20 CNY/kWh. At that point, lithium-ion batteries may become the lowest-cost energy storage technology.

The changing trend of the LCOS for H2ES is shown in Figure 11D. The current LCOS ranges from 1.35 to 1.87 CNY/kWh. This is the highest among all energy storage technologies. The cost of hydrogen production using PEM electrolysis is higher than that using alkaline electrolysis. Electricity costs account for 70%–80% of the total cost in water electrolysis. As a result, the price of electricity has a significant impact on the cost of hydrogen production. With declining LCOE of wind and PV power and reductions in electrolyzer costs, the LCOS of H2ES is expected to decrease. By 2030, the range may fall to 1.17–1.44 CNY/kWh. By 2035, it could be reduced further to 0.84–1.11 CNY/kWh.

In terms of cost comparison, H2ES will remain more expensive than PSH and LIB energy storage until 2035. Its short-term economic viability is not prominent. However, in the context of the energy transition, H2ES faces a critical doubt. Its overall efficiency is only 30%–40%, which is about half that of lithium batteries. The reason it should still develop lies in its unique advantages. Compared with other energy storage technologies, H2ES offers irreplaceable benefits in energy scale, storage duration, and spatial coverage. It is widely regarded as a promising technology for large-scale and long-cycle energy storage. Estimates indicate that when the energy storage duration exceeds 6.5 h, the LCOS of H2ES can be lower than that of LIB energy storage.93 When the duration reaches 1000 h, this cost advantage increases significantly. From a system coordination perspective, H2ES is the only carrier capable of achieving multi-energy complementarity across electricity-heat-hydrogen-fuels. With the current pace of technological improvement, the cost of H2ES is expected to fall by more than 40% by 2035. Improvements in both efficiency and cost are enhancing its technical and economic viability. Under the carbon neutrality goal, H2ES is not only an energy storage technology but also a crucial link connecting the power, industrial, and transportation sectors. The global hydrogen industry currently operates in a positive cycle of policy support, scale expansion, cost reduction, and technological improvement. In the long term, the economic and strategic value of H2ES will continue to grow. In future applications, it may still offer competitive advantages in scenarios such as long-duration energy storage and cross-seasonal peak shaving.

Carbon emission analysis of different technologies

The intensive construction of projects such as UHV transmission and PSH will require large amounts of resources and energy. It will also result in relatively high carbon emissions. Therefore, it is necessary to examine the carbon emission characteristics generated during the life cycle of different utilization technologies in China. A systematic comparison of the carbon emissions for 1 kWh consumed by each measure is also needed. Using the LCA method, this study quantitatively analyzes the carbon emissions of different technical paths over their life cycle.

Based on the carbon emission inventory data of the ±800 kV UHVDC transmission project in China, this study calculates the life cycle carbon emissions of UHVDC projects. The results show that the life cycle carbon emission intensity of the ±800 kV UHVDC transmission project is 5.49 gCO2/kWh. The composition of carbon emissions shows significant variation. The carbon emissions and their proportions for each component are presented in Figure 12A. This variation is mainly caused by differences in engineering material demands at various construction stages. Transmission lines produce the highest proportion of emissions because they require large amounts of high-carbon materials such as steel, aluminum alloy, and concrete. In contrast, the converter station manufacturing process is complex, but the amount of material needed per unit length is relatively small. The grounding electrode circuit has the lowest carbon emissions due to its simple structure and low material use. The carbon emissions from the typical construction scheme of substations in UHVAC projects are similar to those from the converter stations at the sending end of UHVDC projects.94,95 Based on this finding, this study uses the carbon emissions of the converter station at the sending end of a UHVDC project as the reference value. This value is applied to estimate the carbon emission level during the construction stage of a UHVAC project. The results show that the life cycle carbon emission intensity of the UHVAC project is 8.405 g CO2/kWh. This value is slightly higher than that of the UHVDC project.

In H2ES systems, most carbon emissions come from the electrolysis of water to produce hydrogen. The storage of hydrogen and the subsequent power generation processes produce almost no direct carbon emissions. This study calculates the carbon emission intensity of hydrogen production through AEL and PEM electrolysis. When PV power is used, the carbon emission intensities are 0.191 kg CO2/kWh for AEL and 0.195 kg CO2/kWh for PEM. When wind power is used, the values drop to 0.085 kg CO2/kWh and 0.087 kg CO2/kWh, respectively. If the electricity supply comes mainly from coal-fired power grids, as in the case of Inner Mongolia, the carbon emission intensities rise sharply. Under these conditions, AEL reaches 2.591 kg CO2/kWh, and PEM reaches 2.665 kg CO2/kWh. These values are about 30 times higher than those for hydrogen production driven by PV/wind energy. This comparison highlights the decisive role of clean electricity in reducing carbon emissions from H2ES systems. Using wind and PV power for water electrolysis enables efficient consumption of renewable energy. It also reduces the carbon emissions from hydrogen production to nearly zero. This provides an important technical path for cross-seasonal, long-cycle energy storage and for deep decarbonization in the industrial sector. In the future, improvements in the energy efficiency of electrolyzers and reductions in the cost of renewable electricity will further enhance the carbon reduction potential of H2ES. It will play a greater role in supporting the construction of a clean and low-carbon energy system.

The life cycle carbon emission assessment of a PSH power station in Inner Mongolia shows that the carbon emission intensity during the construction stage is 6.18 g CO2/kWh. This value is close to the 4.88 g CO2/kWh in ref.96 Carbon emissions in this stage mainly come from the use of building materials such as steel, electricity, and concrete. During the operation stage, the carbon emission intensity is 1.93 kg CO2/kWh. This is mainly due to the large amount of grid electricity consumed in the pumping process, which generates significant emissions under the current coal-dominated energy structure. If clean energy is used for power supply in the operation stage, the carbon emission intensity can be greatly reduced. When PV power is the main source, the value drops to 0.358 kg CO2/kWh. When wind power is used, it falls further to 0.320 kg CO2/kWh. These results highlight the decisive influence of the energy structure on the environmental benefits of pumped storage projects. With the continued increase in the proportion of renewable energy capacity in China, pumped storage will not only balance the power grid and support wind and PV power consumption but also demonstrate greater potential for carbon reduction.

The total life cycle carbon emissions of LIB when consuming 1 kW h of grid electricity are 2.56 kg CO2. In the manufacturing stage, the carbon emission intensity reaches 1.10 kg CO2/kWh. This value is significantly higher than that of the other three energy storage technologies. The high emissions are due to the large amount of chemical materials and electricity required in the battery manufacturing process. During the operation stage, the carbon emission intensity is 1.46 kg CO2/kWh. This is slightly higher than in the manufacturing stage and is primarily influenced by the power supply structure of the grid. If clean energy is used for the power supply, the system’s life cycle carbon emissions can be greatly reduced. When PV power is used, the value drops to 1.31 kg CO2/kWh. When wind power is used, it falls further to 1.28 kg CO2/kWh. These results indicate that storing and consuming wind and PV power via lithium-ion batteries can improve the utilization of renewable energy and reduce life cycle carbon emissions. With the continued increase in the share of renewable energy in total power generation, the life cycle carbon emission intensity of LIB energy storage could be reduced by nearly 50%, while enabling flexible regulation of the power grid.

The UHV transmission remains the dominant technological pathway for the large-scale utilization of wind and PV power in the “Three North” regions in both 2030 and 2035 across the representative electricity utilization solutions. At both time points, UHV transmission accounts for over 57% of the total in all scenarios, representing not only the highest share among all technical options but also bearing the largest absolute volume in renewable energy utilization. This underscores its irreplaceable role in bridging the geographical distance between resource-rich areas and load centers to achieve the large-scale, cross-regional optimization of clean power allocation. However, aside from UHV transmission, the Three-North regions exhibit significant regional divergence in auxiliary utilization methods, such as energy storage technologies and on-site consumption by data centers.

Specifically, the North China region has deployed a relatively balanced mix of diversified utilization solutions, including PSH, LIB, H2ES, and direct wind and PV power consumption by data centers. In contrast, the Northeast region relies more heavily on a combination of PSH and H2ES. In this context, hydrogen energy, owing to its unique advantages in medium-to-long-term and seasonal storage, has become a key component in mitigating the output fluctuations of wind and PV power and addressing inter-temporal regulation deficiencies. In the Northwest region, the proportion of hydrogen storage is significantly higher than in other regions. This is closely associated with the region’s abundant wind and solar resources, the substantial potential for large-scale electrolysis-based hydrogen production, and the gradual expansion of hydrogen application scenarios. In this region, the primary pathway for hydrogen utilization involves converting surplus wind and PV power into hydrogen via electrolysis, followed by storage, transportation, and either final conversion or direct use, thereby enabling the transfer of renewable energy value across time and space. Overall, these regional differences reflect not only the objective variations in resource endowment, load characteristics, and infrastructure conditions across the Three-North regions but also indicate that future strategies for integrating renewable energy must be tailored to local conditions. This entails coordinating the relationship between the ultra-high-voltage backbone grid and complementary technologies such as diversified energy storage and on-site consumption.

As shown in the schematic diagram of China’s UHV backbone grid for 2025,97 electricity transmitted via UHV lines from the “Three North” regions follows the overall pattern of “west-to-east power transmission” and “north-to-south power supply”, flowing primarily to major load centers in the eastern, central, and southern regions. These data indicate that UHV transmission serves not only as a key channel for the large-scale consumption of wind and PV power across provinces and regions but also plays an irreplaceable foundational role in mitigating long-distance transmission losses and grid bottlenecks, thereby supporting the efficient nationwide allocation of renewable energy. From an economic perspective, although UHV transmission entails significant economic burdens in terms of initial investment and system operation, its comprehensive benefits-including facilitating the spatial alignment of resources and loads, reducing overall curtailment rates, and enhancing the utilization of clean energy-render it a practical and necessary pathway for achieving the cross-regional, optimized allocation of high proportions of renewable energy in China, both currently and in the foreseeable future.

Due to the inherent volatility, intermittency, and uncertainty of wind and PV power generation, a significant temporal mismatch exists between their output curves and electricity demand. This renders the rational deployment of energy storage systems a key technical approach for alleviating supply-demand imbalances and enhancing both the grid integration capacity of renewable energy and system operational flexibility. Figure 14 presents the analytical results for the optimized integration of wind and PV power using three representative technological pathways: PSH, LIB, and H2ES. The results indicate that by 2030 and 2035, the five provinces of Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, Ningxia, Qinghai, and Gansu are projected to achieve a higher proportion of wind and PV power utilization through hydrogen storage, with their absolute utilization volumes substantially exceeding those of other provinces in the “Three North” regions. Specifically, in 2030, the combined wind and PV power utilized by these five provinces through pumped-storage, LIB storage, and hydrogen storage is projected to be 72.41 TW h for Inner Mongolia, 33.01 TW h for Xinjiang, 28.85 TW h for Qinghai, and 21.87 TW h for Ningxia. By 2035, these figures are projected to rise to 107.58 TW h, 58.99 TW h, 46.51 TW h, and 43.87 TW h, respectively. Notably, compared with pumped-storage and LIB storage, hydrogen storage, despite the drawback of relatively low round-trip conversion efficiency, possesses the unique advantage of enabling large-scale, long-duration, and even seasonal energy storage. This characteristic makes it particularly suitable for regions with abundant wind and solar resources but limited local consumption capacity. Hydrogen storage can smooth fluctuations in wind and PV power output on a daily scale and offers the potential for spatiotemporal energy transfer across weeks, months, and seasons, thereby significantly reducing curtailment rates and enhancing overall system resilience. Meanwhile, pumped-storage and LIB storage primarily serve auxiliary functions such as short-term peak shaving and frequency regulation, forming a complementary technological portfolio with hydrogen storage. Overall, these findings indicate that in the “Three North” regions, areas characterized by abundant resources but relatively weak local demand, long-duration energy storage technologies, represented by hydrogen energy, will play an increasingly critical role in future power systems with high proportions of renewable energy.

In conclusion, by 2030 and 2035, UHV transmission, characterized by an extremely low power transmission cost, is expected to remain the primary pathway for utilizing wind and PV power in the Three-North regions. However, compared with LIB and H2ES, UHV transmission typically requires a longer construction period, offers less operational flexibility and has a weaker capacity to respond to fluctuations in power system demand. The construction period of a single UHV line usually lasts for two years. The main advantage of PSH lies in its capacity to store large amounts of electricity over extended periods during times of high wind and PV power generation, thereby helping to balance fluctuations in supply and demand. However, its development is strictly constrained by geographical conditions, and it may cause potential impacts on the local ecological environment. Therefore, although pumped storage serves as an indispensable stabilizer for the power system, it is not a universal solution for renewable energy consumption. LIB energy storage offers rapid charging and discharging capabilities and is well-suited for short-term peak shaving. However, the limited availability of lithium resources is considered a significant constraint on the large-scale deployment of LIB technology.98 H2ES offers advantages such as long storage duration and low carbon emissions, making it particularly suitable for cross-seasonal regulation of large-scale renewable energy. However, at present, high costs associated with electrolyzers and the relatively high LCOE of wind and PV power mean that H2ES is not yet economically viable. In the future, as the LCOE for wind and PV power continues to decline, together with cost reductions driven by technological advances and large-scale deployment of electrolyzers, the economic competitiveness of H2ES is expected to improve significantly. The abundant wind and PV resources in the Three-North regions create favorable conditions for large-scale green hydrogen production. An important prospect for hydrogen energy development is the establishment of a reverse push mechanism, particularly in the Northwest region. In this context, the local consumption of wind power and PV power faces certain challenges. Hydrogen production can serve as a means of storage and transportation, with eventual integration into the terminal energy system. In the upstream segment, the reverse push mechanism can help lower the production cost of hydrogen, while in the downstream segment, it can facilitate the expansion of application scenarios.99 Therefore, from a long-term perspective, the share of H2ES in the utilized wind and PV power generation of the Three-North regions is expected to increase steadily. It will serve as an important supplement to UHV transmission and contribute to building a more flexible, low-carbon, and sustainable power system.

Discussion

In this paper, the spatiotemporal distributions and evolving trends of wind and PV power curtailment for 31 provinces in China from 2018 to 2024 are studied, and then the curtailment levels in each province for 2030 and 2035 are projected. Next, the technical performance, economic costs, and life cycle carbon emissions of various power utilization technologies are quantitatively evaluated and compared. Finally, the MILP model is developed to minimize the consumption cost of wind and PV power, from which the optimal portfolio of utilization technologies is determined under engineering, economic, and policy constraints. The main conclusions of this study are as follows.

•Power curtailment rates for both PV and wind power are higher in the “Three North” regions and Xizang than in other provinces. The curtailment of solar power is particularly severe in the Northwest and Xizang, while wind power curtailment is more severe in the Northwest, Northeast, and North China. Temporally, the overall curtailment rate rose slightly in 2023 and 2024, while the absolute amount of wasted electricity increased markedly compared with the 2018–2022 period. In 2024, curtailed wind power exceeded the 2018 level by approximately 16.01 TW h, and curtailed PV power was about 26.883 TW h higher. Furthermore, this paper compares the amount of curtailed power derived from the published curtailment rate with that calculated using the difference between theoretical and actual utilization hours. The comparison results show that the reported curtailed power, particularly for PV, may underestimate the actual curtailment.

Limitations of the study

This study systematically investigates the trends, projections, and solutions of wind and PV power curtailment for 31 provinces in China, and the limitations of this paper mainly include the following.

•The comprehensive assessment framework developed in this study covers three dimensions: technological maturity, levelized utilization cost, and carbon emissions. To further strengthen the systematic nature of the assessment, a multi-criteria decision analysis method could be introduced to expand the criteria to broader dimensions, such as water footprint, mineral criticality, and social acceptability, thereby providing a more holistic reflection of the overall benefits of each technology.

The comprehensive assessment framework developed in this study covers three dimensions: technological maturity, levelized utilization cost, and carbon emissions. To further strengthen the systematic nature of the assessment, a multi-criteria decision analysis method could be introduced to expand the criteria to broader dimensions, such as water footprint, mineral criticality, and social acceptability, thereby providing a more holistic reflection of the overall benefits of each technology.

•This study’s assessment of solutions for utilizing wind and PV power is primarily conducted from a technical perspective. However, the effectiveness of these measures also depends heavily on institutional arrangements, including policy incentives, market pricing mechanisms, and the removal of cross-provincial transaction barriers. These non-technical factors were not incorporated into the quantitative analysis, which may limit the practical applicability of the findings. Future research could aim to develop an integrated assessment and optimization model that encompasses technical, economic, and policy dimensions.

Land use data were derived from the 2023 remote sensing monitoring results for China’s 31 provincial-level administrative regions, including municipalities directly under the central government, provided by the Resource and Environmental Science Data Center at the Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences.106 This dataset features a spatial resolution of 1 km × 1 km geographic grids and encompasses six primary land use categories, namely arable land, forest land, grassland, water bodies, urban and rural areas, industrial and mining areas, residential land and unutilized land, as well as 25 secondary categories, as presented in Table S9. Land use types are determined by a combination of natural surface conditions and human development activities, and their suitability for onshore wind or PV power development varies accordingly. The classification of relevant land use types and their corresponding suitability for onshore wind or PV power development were based on the criteria specified. ArcGIS software was employed to classify the 25 land use types across China’s 31 provinces and to identify land categories suitable for wind and PV power installations. Even after suitable land types for wind and PV power generation have been identified, the actual deployment potential remains constrained by a variety of complex factors, including natural and geographical conditions. Specifically, the siting of wind turbines must avoid regions with insufficient wind resources, densely populated urban areas or major transportation routes, mountainous terrain characterized by steep slopes that entail high construction costs and elevated geological hazards, grasslands with intensive livestock activity that may impede equipment maintenance or compromise ecological stability. Similarly, the deployment of PV panels is subject to spatial constraints and cannot be undertaken in areas exhibiting low total solar radiation, limited sunshine duration, unfavorable orientation, severe shading or inadequate geological stability. Based on the aforementioned conditions, and with due consideration of slope gradient, slope aspect, ecological conservation zones, topography and the intensity of wind and solar energy resources, with selected data sourced from OpenStreetMap,107 areas suitable for wind and PV power installation were ultimately delineated. Subsequently, the 2022 installed capacity of wind and PV power was distributed across the suitable installation areas within each province to derive preliminary power density values. By integrating the power density per unit land area with the average provincial growth rates of wind and PV installed capacity observed from 2022 to 2024, the latter was adopted as the growth rate for power density. On this basis, national and provincial policy development targets were further incorporated to estimate the wind and PV power installed capacity for each province in 2030 and 2035 under both the policy scenario and the low-carbon scenario.54,55,58,61,108 Provincial wind and PV power generation for 2030 and 2035 is simulated using hourly wind speed and solar irradiance data from NASA’s MERRA-2 dataset,109 which has a spatial resolution of 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude in China. For each province, hourly wind generation is estimated with a wind speed-power generation model (Figure S16). As MERRA-2 wind speed data are measured at 50 m, this study assumes turbine hub heights of 100 m in 2030 and 150 m in 2035, and extrapolates wind speeds to these heights using the power-law formula.110

Technology readiness level model

Stanley R. Sadin from NASA first proposed the TRL assessment framework in 1974. It is a widely adopted method for quantitatively evaluating the progress of major scientific, technological and engineering projects. The framework divides the maturity process of a technology from basic principles to successful application into four stages, comprising nine levels113,114: stage 1: Principle and technical concept validation (TRL1-TRL3); stage 2: Technology development and demonstration (TRL4-TRL6); stage 3: Product development and validation (TRL7-TRL8); stage 4: Product deployment and operational validation (TRL9). The TRL system consists of nine levels, with detailed definitions provided in Table S6. The technological analysis of four utilization solutions is shown in Methods S2.

Life cycle assessment method

The life cycle assessment (LCA) method115 is used to quantify the environmental impacts of product-related emissions over the entire life cycle. A product’s life cycle encompasses substantial resource consumption and pollutant emissions during its production, both of which contribute to its overall environmental footprint.116 In this paper, the LCA method is applied to measure the carbon emissions of four power utilization technologies. Based on the LCA framework, the life cycle carbon emissions of UHV construction, green hydrogen production (including AEL and PEM electrolyzers), LIB energy storage and PSH were quantitatively evaluated. The functional unit for the life cycle carbon emissions assessment is defined as 1 kW h of power utilized. In this paper, the openLCA software is used to evaluate the life cycle carbon emissions of each hydrogen production pathway, and IPCC 2021 is selected as the life cycle assessment method. The IPCC 2021 method is based on the global warming potential (GWP) values from the Sixth Assessment Report released by the IPCC in 2021. This method is used to assess the greenhouse gas emissions impact of products or systems. Given that the operation of the software depends on detailed data inputs, the completeness of the database directly affects the accuracy of the calculation results. To ensure the scientific robustness and reliability of the assessment, this study uses the Ecoinvent database to perform a quantitative analysis of the life cycle carbon emissions of utilization technologies.

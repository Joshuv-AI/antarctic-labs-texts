Association between treatment-emergent cytopenias and clinical responses to imetelstat in lower-risk myelodysplastic syndromes
Authors: Amer M Zeidan, Valeria Santini, María Díez-Campelo, Michael R Savona, Mikkael A Sekeres, Yazan F Madanat, Pierre Fenaux, Azra Raza, Moshe Mittelman, Sylvain Thépot, Rena Buckstein, Ulrich Germing, David Valcárcel, Anna Jonášová, Sheetal Shah, Qi Xia, Libo Sun, Shyamala Navada, Tymara Berry, Uwe Platzbecker, Rami S Komrokji
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Myelodysplastic syndromes/neoplasms (MDS) are myeloid malignancies with ineffective hematopoiesis, resulting in variable cytopenias and increased risk of progression to acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [1, 2]. Imetelstat, a first-in-class competitive telomerase inhibitor, is an approved therapy in the US and Europe for adults with lower-risk MDS (LR-MDS) with red blood cell (RBC) transfusion-dependent (TD) anemia who are relapsed or refractory to, or ineligible for, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) [3, 4]. In the Phase III IMerge trial (NCT02598661), a significantly higher proportion of patients on imetelstat achieved RBC transfusion independence (TI) than placebo [5]. The most common grade 3/4 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were neutropenia (68%) and thrombocytopenia (62%), mostly occurring within the first 3 cycles of imetelstat [5]. These events were generally transient and associated with similarly low rates of grade 3/4 clinical consequences (bleeding, infection, and febrile neutropenia) as placebo [5]. As neutropenia and thrombocytopenia occurring early with lenalidomide treatment have been correlated with clinical response in patients with del(5q) LR-MDS, suggesting an on-target, disease-modifying effect on the del(5q) clone [6], we conducted a post hoc analysis to evaluate the association between early treatment-emergent cytopenias with imetelstat and clinical responses.

Patients from IMerge Phase II [7], Phase III [5], and QTc substudy [8] who received 7.1 mg/kg imetelstat active dose (7.5 mg/kg imetelstat sodium equivalent) were included (N = 226). Patients were adults with RBC-TD LR-MDS, relapsed/refractory to, or ineligible for ESAs. Phase II and QTc substudy allowed prior MDS therapies beyond ESAs. Patients on placebo in the QTc substudy could cross over to imetelstat after 2 cycles at the investigator’s discretion.

Early cytopenias, defined by maximum percentage reduction from baseline in platelets and neutrophils within the first 2 imetelstat cycles, were explored both continuously and as dichotomized variables (≥50% maximum reduction for platelets and ≥75% maximum reduction for neutrophils), in univariate analyses. We focused on cytopenias during the first 2 treatment cycles based on the median time to achievement of 8-week RBC-TI with imetelstat (9.3 weeks) in the pooled IMerge population and the time to onset of cytopenias [5, 9]. Dichotomized thresholds were based on studies with lenalidomide [6]. Associations with clinical responses were assessed, including hemoglobin (Hb) rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks and RBC-TI (yes/no) using logistic regression and Fisher’s exact test, and Hb (g/dL) rise using linear regression and t-test for group differences. Unadjusted nominal P values were reported. Multivariate analyses were performed via stepwise linear regression (for maximum Hb [g/dL] rise) or logistic regression (for Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks/RBC-TI) with dichotomized percent maximum reduction in platelet/neutrophil counts in the presence of other potential baseline prognostic factors. Maximum Hb (g/dL) rise was defined as the difference between maximum Hb value by local laboratory assessments outside 14 days post-RBC transfusion (considered to be influenced by transfusion) and pretreatment Hb (average of all Hb values in the 8 weeks before and inclusive of the first dose date, excluding values within 14 days post-RBC transfusion for all treated patients). Variables remained in the model if P = 0.05. P values and odds ratios (OR) or coefficient estimates (CE) for the variables selected for the predictive model are provided, with a 2-sided P value of 0.05 considered significant.

Data cutoff dates were October 13, 2023 (Phase II/III) and October 13, 2024 (QTc substudy). At baseline, patients with LR-MDS with RBC-TD anemia had median Hb of 7.8 g/dL and neutrophil and platelet counts of 2.6 × 109/L and 236 × 109/L, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). Median imetelstat treatment duration was 34 weeks (0.1–260). Incidence of grade 3/4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia at any time with imetelstat was 69 and 61%, respectively, both occurring early, with median time to onset of 4.1 and 4.9 weeks. Grade 3/4 events were transient (median duration of 2.0 weeks for neutropenia and 1.3 weeks for thrombocytopenia), with 83 and 86% of events, respectively, resolving to grade ≤2 in <4 weeks. Incidence of severe clinical consequences of cytopenias was low. Grade ≥3 infection events ≤7 days of grade 3/4 neutropenia occurred in 4% of patients, grade ≥3 bleeding events ≤7 days of grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia occurred in <1%, and grade 3/4 febrile neutropenia occurred in 3%. In total, 42 (19%) patients received platelet transfusions (median transfusions per patient: 2) and 84 (37%) patients received myeloid growth factors (median doses per patient: 6).

In univariate analyses, maximum percentage reductions from baseline in platelets and neutrophils within the first 2 imetelstat cycles were each significantly associated with maximum Hb (g/dL) rise as a continuous variable (P = 0.009 and P = 0.012, respectively; Fig. 1A, C). When dichotomized, maximum Hb (g/dL) rise was significantly associated with ≥50% maximum platelet reduction and ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction (CE [95% confidence interval (CI)], 0.90 [0.11–1.68]; P = 0.025, and 0.94 [0.30–1.59]; P = 0.004, respectively; Supplementary Table 2A). Specifically, patients with ≥50% maximum platelet reduction had a significantly greater mean maximum Hb rise from pretreatment (2.07 g/dL [n = 169] vs. 1.17 g/dL [n = 39]; P = 0.003; Fig. 1B). The likelihood of achieving Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks was significantly associated with ≥50% maximum platelet reduction (OR [95% CI], 3.85 [1.44–10.28]; P = 0.007; Supplementary Table 2A), with rates of Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks of 34.2 versus 11.9% (P = 0.005; Supplementary Fig. 1). Similarly, patients with ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction had a significantly greater mean maximum Hb rise from pretreatment (2.53 g/dL [n = 70] vs. 1.58 g/dL [n = 138]; P = 0.011; Fig. 1D). No significant association between Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks and ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction was observed (P = 0.282; Supplementary Table 2A); however, a greater proportion of patients with ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction experienced Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks (34.6 vs. 27.7%; P = 0.290; Supplementary Fig. 1).

Regarding RBC-TI responses, patients treated with imetelstat who experienced a ≥ 50% maximum platelet reduction had a numerically greater ≥8-week RBC-TI rate (42.4 vs. 26.2%; P = 0.056; Fig. 2A), and a significantly greater ≥24-week RBC-TI rate (32.6 vs. 7.1%; P < 0.001; Fig. 2B). Patients with ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction had comparable ≥8-week (41.0 vs. 38.5%; P = 0.775) and ≥24-week RBC-TI rates (30.8 vs. 26.4%; P = 0.533) versus patients with <75% maximum neutrophil reduction. Similar outcomes were observed when explored for patients with maximum platelet reduction ≥50% or maximum neutrophil reduction ≥75%, with a significantly greater mean maximum Hb rise from pretreatment (2.07 g/dL [n = 170] vs. 1.13 g/dL [n = 38]; P = 0.002; Supplementary Fig. 2), greater rate of Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks (34.1 vs. 12.2%; P = 0.005; Supplementary Fig. 1), numerically greater ≥8-week RBC-TI rate (42.2 vs. 26.8%; P = 0.079; Fig. 2A), and significantly greater ≥24-week RBC-TI rate (32.4 vs. 7.3%; P < 0.001; Fig. 2B) versus patients with maximum platelet reduction <50% and maximum neutrophil reduction <75%.

In multivariate analyses, maximum Hb (g/dL) rise was significantly associated with ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction (CE [95% CI], 0.943 [0.32–1.57]; P = 0.003; Supplementary Table 2B), and the likelihood of achieving Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks was significantly associated with ≥50% maximum platelet reduction (OR [95% CI], 4.16 [1.54–11.23]; P = 0.005) after adjusting for prognostic factors. The likelihood of achieving ≥8-week and ≥24-week RBC-TI remained significantly associated with maximum Hb rise (g/dL) from pretreatment (OR [95% CI], 1.21 [1.14–1.27]; P < 0.001, and 3.87 [2.66–5.64]; P < 0.001, respectively; Supplementary Table 2B). Lastly, when maximum Hb change from pretreatment was removed as a variable from the model, the likelihood of achieving ≥8-week (OR [95% CI], 2.43 [1.11–5.31]; P = 0.026) and ≥24-week RBC-TI (OR [95% CI], 7.16 [2.10–24.37]; P = 0.002) were also significantly associated with ≥50% maximum platelet reduction.

In summary, maximum percent reduction in platelets or neutrophils, ≥75% neutrophil reduction, or ≥50% platelet reduction within the first 2 imetelstat cycles were significantly associated with greater Hb rise in univariate analyses. In multivariate analyses, ≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction was significantly associated with greater Hb rise, whereas ≥50% maximum platelet reduction was significantly associated with achieving Hb rise ≥1.5 g/dL lasting ≥8 weeks. The apparent exclusion of one or the other factor (≥75% maximum neutrophil reduction, or ≥50% maximum platelet reduction) in these 2 models is likely due to the strong correlation between maximum neutrophil and maximum platelet percentage reductions (Supplementary Fig. 3). The greater Hb rise from pretreatment emerged as a main driver for achieving ≥8-week and ≥24-week RBC-TI in the multivariate analysis. Furthermore, in IMerge Phase III, the only study with evaluable baseline and postbaseline mutation data, maximum SF3B1 variant allele frequency reduction suggestive of potential modification of the underlying disease with imetelstat was significantly correlated with maximum percentage reduction in platelets from baseline within the first 2 imetelstat cycles (P = 0.011; Supplementary Fig. 4).

Our results are reminiscent of those reported in patients with del(5q) LR-MDS treated with lenalidomide, showing that the development of early treatment-related cytopenias was associated with RBC-TI, further supporting an on-target, disease-modifying effect on the malignant clone [6, 10].

Collectively, our data suggest that treatment-emergent cytopenias with imetelstat are common, especially early in treatment, and generally transient and reversible, with low rates of severe clinical consequences, but may be predictive of a better therapeutic response. Further research is needed to confirm this on-target effect of imetelstat resulting from its activity on clonal progenitor cells and subsequent recovery of blood cell production.

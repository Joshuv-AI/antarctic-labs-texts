Magnetic resonance imaging features of RB1-deleted adipocytic neoplasms in a survivor of bilateral retinoblastoma
Authors: Jemma Ford, Derek Roebuck, Adrian Charles, Daniel Wong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor and dysplastic lipoma are closely related fatty masses associated with RB1 abnormalities. While it is not yet widely agreed that they are separate entities, the distinct morphological features of dysplastic lipomata and divergent anatomic distribution suggest that this is probable. RB1 abnormalities are present in spindle cell/pleomorphic lipoma, pleomorphic liposarcoma, and pleomorphic liposarcoma with or without myxoid component. Survivors of hereditary retinoblastoma, caused by germline mutations in the RB1 gene, have a substantially increased risk of developing benign and malignant lipomatous tumors including atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor and dysplastic lipoma. This report describes the MRI findings of several such lesions in a survivor of bilateral retinoblastoma including features such as enhancement that might otherwise be concerning for higher-grade malignancy.Keywords: Adipocytic neoplasms, Bilateral retinoblastoma, Lipoma, Lipomatous tumour, Magnetic resonance imaging, Pediatric, RB1-deleted

Introduction

Survivors of hereditary retinoblastoma have an increased risk of subsequent soft-tissue sarcomas and benign tumors linked to the RB1 mutation [1–4]. The World Health Organization classification of lipomatous tumors has evolved thanks to the understanding of their cytogenetics, molecular genetics, and immunohistochemistry [5].

Atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor (atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor) is a newly-recognized entity in the 5th edition of the WHO Classification of Soft Tissue and Bone Tumours previously known as spindle cell liposarcoma and was considered a variant of well-differentiated liposarcoma [5, 6]. Characterized by an indolent clinical course, it usually arises in the limbs, the girdles, and rarely in the retroperitoneum of middle-aged patients. Atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumors lack MDM2 or CDK4 gene amplification and de-differentiation does not occur, distinguishing them from atypical lipomatous tumor/well-differentiated liposarcomas [5]. Recurrence is limited to lesions with incomplete excision [5–7].

Dysplastic lipoma is a benign neoplasm most commonly in the lower neck and upper back region [1–3]. Initially described as “anisometric cell lipoma” due to the notable variation in fat cell size, it is not yet included in the WHO classification [1, 3]. While RB1 loss occurs in both dysplastic lipoma and atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor, the distinct morphological features and anatomic distribution of dysplastic lipomata suggest that the former may be a separate entity, although others have argued that it is a subset of atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor [1, 3, 8].

RB1 loss can also be seen in spindle cell/pleomorphic lipoma, pleomorphic liposarcoma, and pleomorphic (myxoid) liposarcoma. It is not known whether atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor and dysplastic lipoma can be distinguished from one another or reliably from any other RB1-deleted lipomatous neoplasms including those of a higher grade using MRI.

Case report

A 14-year-old girl presented with posterior neck lumps, one of which was painful.

The patient had a known germline RB1 mutation following the diagnosis of bilateral retinoblastoma, for which she underwent enucleation and adjuvant chemotherapy at the age of 2 years.

Serial ultrasound demonstrated a few hyperechoic solid lesions without demonstrable internal vascularity or deep extension that were enlarging over a 6-month period.

MRI demonstrated several lesions, more numerous than observed on ultrasound smaller than 30 mm in the subcutaneous and deep fat of the posterior neck and posterosuperior thorax. Most of these demonstrated homogenous fatty signal that was isointense to the adjacent normal subcutaneous fat with a thin hypointense rim and minimal to no central enhancement (Fig. 1). One lesion demonstrated different signal characteristics; it was mildly hypointense to adjacent fat with near complete fat suppression and showed conspicuous peripheral and central nodular enhancement (Fig. 2).

The patient underwent excision of the lesions. Macroscopically, these showed a homogenous pale yellow fatty appearance, without significant fibrosis. Histologically, they displayed typical features of dysplastic lipomata, given the sheets of predominantly mature adipocytes with significant variability in size and shape, associated with frequent single cell fat necrosis, rimmed by histiocytes (Fig. 3). Occasional spindled stromal cells showed mildly enlarged nuclei with hyperchromasia, degenerate chromatin, and intranuclear Lochkern-type inclusions. Immunohistochemistry exhibited patchy staining for CD34 with diffuse loss of RB1 expression.

Histologically the lesions displayed typical features of dysplastic lipomata. a Hematoxylin and eosin at low magnification (×100) showing lobules of fat with intervening loose fibrous bands containing few randomly-placed spindle cells. b Higher magnification (×400) showing single cell fat necrosis with rimming by foamy histiocytes and atypical spindle cells with mildly enlarged and hyperchromatic nuclei. c Immunohistochemical stain for RB1 showing loss of normal expression in nuclei of adipocytes and atypical spindle cells. Retained staining in histiocytes serves as a positive internal control

Two years later, more lesions developed in the same region. These were resected and showed similar histological features.

Discussion

Children with hereditary retinoblastoma carry a germline mutation in the RB1 tumor suppressor gene, predisposing them to a substantially increased risk of developing benign and malignant soft tissue tumors. RB1-deleted lipomatous neoplasms encompass a spectrum of lesions, ranging from spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomas to atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor, dysplastic lipoma, and pleomorphic liposarcoma as well as pleomorphic (myxoid) liposarcoma [5].

Atypical spindle cell/pleomorphic lipomatous tumor and dysplastic lipoma are closely related but distinct atypical lipomatous neoplasms which lack the MDM2 or CDK4 gene amplification that characterizes atypical lipomatous tumor/well-differentiated liposarcoma and dedifferentiated liposarcoma, entities generally requiring more aggressive management [1, 3, 5, 6]. In this case, MRI was performed following ultrasound as requested by the surgeon for planning prior to lesion excision. The spectrum of magnetic resonance appearances of these RB1-related fatty masses is not yet widely documented.

This case report underscores the importance of interpreting radiologic findings in conjunction with the patient’s specific genetic predisposition and clinical history. Although contrast enhancement in a soft tissue mass can raise concern for malignancy, its interpretation requires careful consideration, particularly in the context of a patient with a known germline RB1 mutation. Accurate diagnosis is paramount to avoid both overtreatment of low-grade lesions and undertreatment of true sarcomas.

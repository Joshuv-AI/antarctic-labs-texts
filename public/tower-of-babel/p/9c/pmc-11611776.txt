CX3CR1 + CD8 + T cells: Key players in antitumor immunity
Authors: Jiajin Ma, Yue Wu, Shaoxian Wu, Zhang Fang, Lujun Chen, Jingting Jiang, Xiao Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCX3CR1 functions as the specific receptor for the chemokine CX3CL1, demonstrating expression across a broad spectrum of immune cells. This underscores its pivotal role in communication and response mechanisms within the immune system. Upon engagement with CX3CL1, CX3CR1 initiates a cascade of downstream signaling pathways that regulate various biological functions. In the context of tumor progression, the intricate and inhibitory nature of the tumor microenvironment presents a significant challenge to current clinical treatment techniques. This review aims to comprehensively explore the tumor‐destructive potential shown by CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. Simultaneously, it investigates the promising prospects of utilizing CX3CR1 in future tumor immunotherapies.

INTRODUCTION

Chemokines, a class of small regulatory proteins, share specific molecular properties, collectively forming the chemokine protein family. They are categorized into four main groups based on the position and number of cysteine residues within their peptide structure, XCL, CCL, CXCL, and CX3CL, corresponding to their respective receptors, XCR, CCR, CXCR, and CX3CR. Among these classifications, CX3CL1, also known as fractalkine (FKN), and its receptor CX3CR1 stand out as the sole constituents of the CX3C subclass, representing a unique pair within this distinctive chemokine subfamily. Expression of CX3CR1 is observed across a diverse range of immune cellular entities, including T cells, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, dendritic cells (DCs), tumor‐associated macrophages (TAMs), and myeloid‐derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 This review delves into the biological functions of CX3CR1 on CD8+ T cells, with a specific emphasis on its crucial significance within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Furthermore, it elucidates the potential of CX3CR1 as a biomarker and examines the promising avenues it presents for augmenting tumor immunotherapy strategies.

STRUCTURE AND FUNCTIONS OF CHEMOKINE RECEPTOR CX3CR1

CX3CR1 functions as a receptor intricately associated with heterotrimeric G proteins, placing it within the G protein‐coupled receptor (GPCR) family. Comprising seven transmembrane regions, CX3CR1 consists of 355 amino acids in its structure. Structurally, it includes an external N‐terminal domain and sequential α‐helix structures ranging from TM1 to TM7, along with intracellular and extracellular loops, culminating in an internal C‐terminal segment. The intracellular loops and the C‐terminus collectively form the docking site for the α component of the G protein, while the external loop serves as the attachment region for the ligand CX3CL1. 5 , 6 The genetic locus of CX3CR1 resides on chromosome 3 at position p22.2, comprising a structure consisting of four exons flanked by three introns. Its expression is regulated by a trio of promoters strategically located in the upstream region. 7

Numerous factors exert influence over the expression of CX3CR1. For instance, studies have shown that transforming growth factor‐β (TGF‐β) can positively regulate CX3CR1 expression in rat microglia. 8 Additionally, various research investigations have revealed that environments characterized by low oxygen levels can induce the activation of CX3CR1 in cell lines of prostate cancer. 9 The regulation of CX3CL1 expression is modulated by a plethora of cytokines, including interferon‐gamma (IFN‐γ), tumor necrosis factor α (TNF‐α), interleukin‐1β (IL‐1β), and TGF‐β. These factors possess the capability to independently or synergistically modulate the expression of both CX3CL1 and its receptors. 10 , 11

Research has elucidated that the interaction between CX3CR1 and CX3CL1 elicits a conformational shift, facilitating the conversion of GDP, bound to the Gα subunit, into GTP. Subsequently, the Gα‐GTP complex and Gβγ heterodimer dissociate from the receptor. Such CX3CR1 activation initiates a cascade of downstream signaling mechanisms, including the notable mobilization of Ca2+ 12 , 13 and activation of the ERK and PI3K/Akt/PKB pathways, as evidenced in previous studies. 14 , 15 , 16 Furthermore, it initiates the Src/FAK pathway alongside the JAK2/STAT3 pathway. 17 , 18 , 19 Activation of the PI3K/Akt/PKB pathway further activates nuclear factor‐κB, thereby enhancing the cell's resistance to apoptosis and promoting the upregulation of CX3CL1 expression through an autocrine mechanism. 20 , 21 The regulation of the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) process critically depends on the JAK2/STAT3 signaling axis. Additionally, activation of the ERK/MAPK pathway significantly increases hypoxia‐inducible factor‐1α protein levels, consequently augmenting the process of angiogenesis 22 (Figure 1).

This activation sets CX3CR1 on a pathway triggering various downstream signaling cascades, including Ca2+ mobilization and activation of the ERK/MAPK and PI3K/Akt/PKB pathways, as well as the Src/FAK pathway and the JAK2/STAT3 pathway, which influences cell proliferation, migration, metastasis, apoptosis resistance, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), angiogenesis, and actin polymerization. CHARACTERISTICS OF CX3CR1 + CD8 + T CELLS

By genetically modifying T cells to overexpress CX3CR1 and introducing them into mice, the researchers have observed an enhanced ability of these modified cells to localize to colorectal tumor sites. This increased homing capacity is attributed to the heightened expression of CX3CL1 within the tumors. Subsequent experiments have indicated that mice bearing NCI‐H630 tumors, who receive adoptively transferred CX3CR1+ T cells, show significantly enhanced lymphocyte migration to the tumor sites. This intervention results in notably stronger tumor suppression effects. 23

Liu et al. undertook a meticulous analysis of 176,447 cells sourced from tumors and blood samples of 10 patients afflicted with nasopharyngeal cancer by harnessing the complementary capabilities of single‐cell transcriptomics and T‐cell receptor sequencing technologies. Their scrutiny of cell differentiation trajectories provides further insights, revealing that CD8+ T cells showing functional exhaustion within tumors likely originate from the CX3CR1+ subset circulating in the peripheral blood. Significantly, the study underscores the unparalleled cytotoxic properties shown by CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. Examination of differential gene expression in these cells illuminates their pivotal role in pathways associated with leukocyte migration across endothelial barriers and general leukocyte movement. 24

Using CX3CR1 and CD62L as discriminatory markers, the researchers categorized memory CD8+ T cells in humans and mice into four distinct subsets. They pioneer the identification of the fundamental genetic and protein features underlying the cytotoxic functions of memory CD8+ T cells. Grounded in the essential characteristics of these genes, they identify a subset of cytotoxic CX3CR1+CD62LhiCD8+ T cells that are primarily localized in the subcapsular sinus region of lymph nodes and play a crucial role in the rapid response of the immune system. 25

Recent research has classified CD8+ T cells into three distinct subpopulations based on varying levels of CX3CR1 expression: CX3CR1−, CX3CR1int, and CX3CR1hi. However, they posit that although CX3CR1−CD8+ T cells are vital for antitumor responses, their functionality is suppressed within the TME. The study reveals that selectively targeting and eliminating CX3CR1hiCD8+ T cells using diphtheria toxin does not adversely affect the efficacy of adoptive cell therapy in combating tumors. Furthermore, it was observed that CX3CR1− T cell populations show superior outcomes when combined with programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD‐L1) blockade therapy. 26

However, subsequent research utilizing single‐cell sequencing analysis illustrates that the increase in CX3CR1 expression does not correspond with an elevation in cytokine production along the differentiation gradient, as observed with granzyme expression. Conversely, the secretion of IFN‐γ and TNF initially increases with heightened levels of CX3CR1, peaking at an intermediate level of CX3CR1 expression, before declining as CX3CR1 expression continues to rise. Concurrently, an increase in CX3CR1 expression on CD8+ T cells correlates with a decrease in the production of IL‐2, suggesting that while the most highly differentiated CD8+ T cells show robust cytolytic functions (as evidenced by granzyme A, granzyme B, and perforin), their ability to secrete effector cytokines is diminished. 27

Furthermore, research has emphasized that the CX3CR1hi subpopulation is highly differentiated, characterized by the lack of expression of CD27 and CD127, yet showing high levels of expression for the killer cell lectin‐like receptor G1 (KLRG1). In melanomas, one study has found a negative correlation between the expression of co‐inhibitory receptors on tumor‐infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and the level of T cell differentiation determined by CX3CR1. 26 Simultaneously, there is a notable reduction in the expression of the co‐inhibitory receptors PD‐1, LAG‐3, and TIGIT on CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. These cells show a significant decrease in their ability to produce cytokines, such as IL‐2, IFN‐γ, and TNF‐α, when compared to the CX3CR1− subset. 26 , 28 , 29

CX3CR1 + CD8 + T CELLS AND TME

The TME plays a pivotal role in tumor development, progression, metastasis, and responses to therapy. It comprises a diverse array of cellular components and secreted factors. The recruitment of immune cells to tumors is often orchestrated by the release of lytic signaling molecules, such as chemokines or cytokines, by stromal cells 30 (Figure 2).

The synergistic interaction between CX3CL1 and interleukin‐15 (IL‐15) within the vascular endothelium cultivates the accumulation of activated, memory CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. Tumor cells exploit the upregulated expression of CX3CL1 to entice CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells into the TME. Through IL‐21 signaling, CD4+ T cells contribute to the generation of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. Enhancing CX3CL1 expression in dendritic cells (DCs) significantly enhances the trafficking capabilities of both CD8+ and CD4+ T cells to the tumor site, thereby facilitating antitumor responses. Impact of cancer cells on CX3CR1 + CD8 + T cells

Tumor entities have the capacity to attract CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells into the TME by upregulating the expression of CX3CL1. Studies have reported that CX3CL1 is overexpressed in tumor tissues of various types of cancer, including ovarian, gastric, pancreatic, and lung cancer, which can facilitate the recruitment of multiple types of immune cells expressing CX3CR1, including CD8+ T cells. 21 , 31 , 32 , 33 However, this interaction can lead to alterations in both their phenotypic characteristics and functional capacities. A study led by Conroy et al. has unveiled a significant presence of CX3CL1 in the omentum of individuals diagnosed with esophagogastric adenocarcinoma, which substantially recruit CX3CR1‐expressed T cells from the bloodstream of these patients. Further investigations reveal that FKN can induce the endocytosis of CX3CR1 in CD8+ T cells. Additionally, it has been observed that the circulating CX3CR1− subset show higher levels of L‐selectin compared to CX3CR1intCD8+ T cells. 34 This observation aligns with previous studies highlighting the crucial role of FKN in promoting the transition of CD8+ T cells from a CX3CR1int to a CX3CR1− phenotype, accompanied by alterations in their migratory capabilities. 28

Effect of CD4 + T cells on CX3CR1 + CD8 + T cells in TME

CD4+ T cells play a pivotal role in the activation of highly efficient CTLs, not only enhancing the expression of cytotoxic effector molecules but also ensuring the sustained efficacy of CTL functions throughout their lifespan. Research undertaken by Tomasz Ahrends and colleagues has shed light on the significant impact of CD4+ T cell assistance on the expression level of the CX3CR1 protein in CD8+ T cells, surpassing the expression observed in CD8+ T cells operating independently. Furthermore, they have investigated the migratory responses of both assisted and independent CD8+ T cells toward CX3CL1 in vitro, revealing that approximately 50% of assisted CD8+ T cells migrate toward CX3CL1. However, this migratory efficiency drastically decreases to approximately 5% following the inhibition of CX3CR1 using the small molecule inhibitor 18a. Further experimentation in mice indicates that CD4+ T cells enhance the extravasation potential of CTLs by upregulating the expression of CXCR4 and CX3CR1. 35 Subsequent research has emphasized the critical role of IL‐21 signaling in CD8+ T cells in maximizing the therapeutic benefits of adoptive cell transfer with IL‐21+CD4+ T cells. Effectively controlling the progression of chronic viral infections and tumor growth hinges significantly on the interplay between CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, facilitated primarily through IL‐21 signaling. This mechanism fosters the formation of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells, amplifying immune response and bolstering the body's capacity to combat such persistent threats. 36 Taken together, these observations underscore the crucial function of CD4+ T cells in impeding tumor progression by enhancing CX3CR1 expression and augmenting the ability of CTLs to infiltrate tumor sites.

Regulation of DCs on the antitumor effect of CX3CR1 + CD8 + T cells

Dendritic cells, serving as crucial antigen‐presenting cells in the body, possess essential functions required for T cell activation and immune regulation. 37 , 38 To effectively execute their antigen‐presenting functions, mature DCs can guide T cells to migrate to inflammatory or tumor sites by expressing CX3CL1. 39 , 40 Intratumoral injection of DCs overexpressing CX3CL1 can increase the infiltration of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells into the tumor, promoting the induction of tumor‐specific CTLs, thereby slowing tumor growth and improving overall survival in mice. 41 Another distinct investigation has discovered that DCs cocultured with 3LL lung cancer cells, engineered with the FKN gene, show an enhanced ability to stimulate T cell proliferation. This approach also induces phenotypic maturation of DCs and increased secretion of IL‐12. The heightened levels of IL‐12 further potentiate the proliferation of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells and reinforce their capacity to combat tumors. 42 In groundbreaking research spearheaded by Takayoshi Yamauchi, investigations into cutting‐edge cancer vaccines unveiled a remarkable finding. They discovered that a potent vaccine formulation, integrating novel antigens alongside Toll‐like receptor 3 (TLR3) and CD40 agonists, profoundly enhances the activation of crucial signaling pathways, namely CD40 and CD80/86, mediated by conventional type 1 DCs. This heightened activation is closely linked with a notable surge in both the production and prevalence of tumor‐specific CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells. 29

Chemotactic attraction of CX3CR1 + CD8 + T cells by vascular endothelial cells

While conclusive evidence delineating the precise impact of vascular endothelial cells on CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells within the TME remains scarce, scholars such as Soumya Panigrahi have delved into the function of CD8+ T cells in atherosclerosis subsequent to HIV infection, elucidating pivotal interaction mechanisms. Their finding suggests that IL‐15 possesses the capacity to elevate CX3CR1 expression levels on CD8+ T cell surfaces and amplify TNF secretion. Moreover, exposure to IL‐15 in vitro elicits enhanced CX3CL1‐dependent chemotaxis of CD8+ T cells toward endothelial cells. Simultaneously, the research reveals that the phenotype of CD8+ T cells in human atherosclerotic lesions mirrors in vivo exposure to IL‐15 and CX3CL1, culminating in an activated resident state. This revelation holds paramount significance in advancing our understanding of the mechanisms by which activated CD8+ T cells contribute to atherosclerosis through distinct signaling pathways. 43

APPLICATION VALUE

Research has reported a remarkable elevation in CX3CR1 expression on peripheral blood CD8+ T cells in renal cancer patients undergoing combined treatment with bevacizumab and atezolizumab. The heightened expression correlates directly with the increased infiltration of these cells into tumor tissues, coinciding with a rise in lymphocyte count. 44 Similarly, in the context of immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy targeting non‐small‐cell lung cancer (NSCLC), there is a notable upward trend in the population of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells within the peripheral blood. Crucially, during the initial phase of treatment, an elevation in the prevalence of this particular subset has been correlated with enhanced response rates and improved survival outcomes in patients with lung cancer. 45 Further investigations have elucidated that CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells show resilience against the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy agents. The resistance mechanism is facilitated by the action of the drug efflux protein ABCB1. Remarkably, this trait boosts the effectiveness of treatment protocols that combine chemotherapy with immunotherapy, such as the synergistic use of paclitaxel and carboplatin alongside PD‐1 inhibitors in patients with metastatic melanoma. 46 These discoveries underscore the promising prospect of leveraging the distinctive characteristics of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells to elevate response toward multifaceted treatment strategies in multiple tumors.

The latest research in the domain of cancer vaccines delves into the efficacy of an innovative antigen/TLR3/CD40 agonist vaccine in augmenting CX3CR1 expression within preclinical models. Based on their observations, the researchers propose that, in contrast to terminally differentiated effector CD8+ T cells, less differentiated T cells harbor a more potent in vivo antitumor capability. Consequently, the expansion of the CX3CR1+ subset may merely signify the presence of an efficacious CX3CR1− subcluster post unidirectional differentiation in vivo. 29 These findings emphasize the potential of CX3CR1 as a valuable periphery predictive biomarker in vaccine therapy.

As our understanding deepens regarding the complexities of the TME, our insights have propelled significant advancements in immunotherapy approaches, notably the revolutionary chimeric antigen receptor T cell (CAR‐T) therapy. However, its application to solid tumors encounters substantial challenges, particularly the limited capacity of CAR‐T cells to effectively infiltrate these tumor types. 48 Notably, the development of drug resistance in CAR‐T cells due to the inhibitory TME has emerged as a key hurdle to overcome. Efficient delivery of cytotoxic cells into tumors represents a bottleneck in addressing this challenge. 49 Trinh et al. have undertaken a study focusing on the relative presence of immune cells in normal, adjacent, and tumor tissues in primary specimens of NSCLC while also examining the expression of pivotal receptors on these cells. Their finding suggests that augmenting the expressions of either CX3CR1 or CX3CL1 can facilitate the re‐engagement of TILs within the TEM. In pursuit of practical applications, they devised an NKG2D CAR‐T cell design that also incorporates the expression of CX3CR1. Remarkably, this dual‐expression CAR‐T construct significantly augments the recruitment of cytotoxic lymphocytes to the tumor site, fortifying their efficacy in targeting and eliminating cancer cells. 47

DISCUSSION

This article provides a comprehensive review of recent research advancements in the field of tumor immunology, focusing particularly on a unique subset of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells and their distinct advantages. These cells show immense potential in tumor immunotherapy, especially within the realm of cellular immunotherapy, due to their ability to migrate toward tumors, exert cytotoxic effects, selectively recognize tumor cells, and show minimal expression of co‐inhibitory molecules. 26 , 28

Previous studies have indicated that CX3CR1‐deficient mice or those treated with CX3CR1 antagonists show reduced antitumor capabilities. 46 , 50 However, Yamauchi et al. have found that depletion of the CX3CR1+ subpopulation does not impair the antitumor efficacy of adoptively transferred Pmel‐1 Cd2‐cre/Cx3cr1 +/DTR CD8+ T cells. In their subsequent research, they have shown that less differentiated CX3CR1−CD8+ T cell subsets play a pivotal role in effectively suppressing the tumor. 26 Further studies on an AdpgkMut/TLR3/CD40 agonist vaccine against established MC38 tumors have determined that CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells are not essential for antitumor effects. 29 The reasons for these disparities are not explicitly documented, but they may relate to the following factors.

(1) CX3CR1 is not exclusively expressed on CD8+ T cells; it is also present on other immune cell types like NK cells, DCs, TAMs, and MDSCs. For instance, during the process of skin carcinogenesis in mice, CX3CR1+ TAMs show an M2‐like phenotype, expressing Wnt3a and angiogenic factors, including vascular endothelial growth factor and MMP9. Compared to WT mice, CX3CR1‐deficient mice show fewer skin tumors and a reduced incidence of the disease. 3 Additionally, other studies have found that the p16Ink4 and p21Cip1/Waf1 genes, which encode cyclin‐dependent kinase (CDK) inhibitors, are highly expressed in monocytic MDSCs (M‐MDSCs) and upregulate CX3CR1 expression by preventing CDK‐mediated phosphorylation and deactivation of SMAD3. Targeted depletion of these genes reduces CX3CR1 expression, thus diminishing M‐MDSC infiltration in CX3CL1+ TME and suppressing tumor progression in mice. 4 It is well known that tumor development is dominated by CD8+ T cells in the early stages, whereas suppressive immune cells become more prominent in the later stages. Therefore, it is crucial to explore the dynamic role of CX3CR1 expression throughout tumor development. Targeted deletion or antagonism of CX3CR1 may overlook the effects on these other cell populations.

(2) The difference could also be attributable to the low secretion of CX3CL1 by certain tumor cell lines, such as B16 melanoma, MC38 colorectal cancer, and CT26. 51 , 52 , 53 In these tumors, the function of CX3CR1 as a chemokine receptor might not substantially impact the effectiveness against tumors.

(3) The reduced expression of CXCR3 and CD62L on the surface of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells, fundamental transport molecules for T cells crossing high endothelial venules in lymphatic organs and tumor microvessels, makes it challenging for the CX3CR1+ subset to reach tumor sites. 28 , 54 , 55 , 56 Instead, the CX3CR1− subset mediates antitumor effects by migrating into the TME. Recent studies indicate that CX3CR1−CD8+ T cells can migrate directly to the TME and differentiate unidirectionally into CX3CR1+CD8+ T cell subsets within tumors, exerting antitumor effects. 57 While the antitumor capacity of CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells as effector phenotypes is undeniable in vitro, in vivo experiments in tumor‐bearing mice suggest that unleashing the effective tumor‐killing function of the CX3CR1+ subsets might not be as straightforward as expected. Resolving these disparities could pave the way for a promising strategy where CX3CR1+CD8+ T cells, expanded in vitro, are adoptively transferred into the body, allowing them to reach the tumor tissue locally and realize their full antitumor potential.

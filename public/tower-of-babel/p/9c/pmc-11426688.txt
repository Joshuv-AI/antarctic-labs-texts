Role of Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) as a Prognostic Marker in Patients with Acute Kidney Injury
Authors: Gurvinder Singh, Satpal Aloona, Maneesh Bains, Rajneesh Bains
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study aimed to investigate the prognostic significance of mean platelet volume (MPV) in 50 AKI patients. Blood samples were collected according to KDIGO guidelines, and complete blood counts, including MPV, were analyzed. Significant differences in MPV were observed among patients who recovered with or without dialysis and those who expired. A statistically significant difference was present in between the mean platelet count of patients recovered without dialysis, recovered with dialysis, and the expired patients. The Area Under Curve (AUC) for MPV scores was as high as 0.842. Indicating that in up to 84% of the pairs (death-survival) the models correctly estimated that the probability of survival was higher than that of death. MPV had an optimum cutoff point of score value <9.60 with a sensitivity of 80% and specificity of 91.11%. These findings suggested that MPV could serve as a cost-effective and superior tool to creatinine in early AKI detection.

INTRODUCTION

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is characterized by a sudden decline in kidney function, involving both structural damage and loss of function.[1] While various biomarkers have been proposed for AKI diagnosis, it remains unclear whether a single or multiple biomarker approach is optimal. Consensus definitions for AKI aim to standardize its quantitative assessment.[23]

In addition to conventional inflammation indicators like CRP, fibrinogen, and interleukins, MPV has gained attention for its association with CKD prognosis.[4] MPV, an automated measure of platelet size, reflects platelet function and activation. Elevated MPV, indicating larger and more reactive platelets, may signify increased vascular mortality risk.[5]

Despite its potential, few studies have explored MPV’s role as a prognostic factor in AKI mortality. Therefore, this study aims to investigate MPV’s utility as a prognostic marker in AKI patients. Given its simplicity and affordability, MPV evaluation holds promise for enhancing AKI prognosis assessment.

Sampling

Random.

Sample size

A total of 50 patients admitted with AKI diagnosed as per KDIGO guidelines were included in the study.

Method of collection of data

Venous blood samples were collected in tubes containing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and processed within 1 hour to minimize variations due to sample aging. Samples were maintained at room temperature.

A complete blood count was performed for all patients using a commercially available analyzer. The hemoglobin level, white blood cell count (WBC), platelet count, and MPV values were recorded for each patient.

MPV was determined within 30 min of sample collection using an automated analyzer. The reference range for the MPV was between 7.0 and 11 fl.

There was male predominance consistent with the study done by Li J et al.[6] who investigated whether the combination of Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE II) score and MPV could have a better performance in predicting prognosis of patients with AKI who received continuous renal replacement therapy. They also reported male predominance in their study with 65.9% males.

The mean age of our patients was 54.98 years, lower than the 61.4 years reported by Li JH et al.,[7] highlighting the susceptibility of older individuals to AKI due to the structural and functional deterioration of the kidneys associated with the aging process, a decreased renal reserve, the presence of comorbidities, and the reduced ability to recover.[8] In the present study, a statistically significant difference was present in between the mean age of patients recovered without dialysis, recovered with dialysis and the expired patients [Table 4].

Most of the patients in the present study have serum creatinine ≤1.5 (60%) followed by 1.6-2.3% (30%) and >2.3 (10%). In the present study mean serum creatinine is 1.77 mg/dL [Table 4]. A statistically significant difference was present in between the mean serum creatinine of patients recovered without dialysis, those recovered with dialysis, and the expired patients [Table 4]. Interestingly, changes in MPV preceded creatinine elevation, suggesting MPV’s potential as an early AKI indicator. MPV evaluation could be more cost-effective and superior to creatinine in timely AKI detection, pending further investigation with larger sample sizes.[9]

The mean platelet count was lower in expired patients compared to those who recovered, consistent with previous findings. We found a statistically significant difference in mean platelet count among recovery groups. Similarly, MPV was higher in expired patients, with a significant difference noted among recovery groups. The AUC for MPV scores was 0.842, indicating a high predictive value for survival [Table 1]. Li J et al.[6] identified an optimal MPV cutoff of <9.60 with 80% sensitivity and 91.11% specificity for predicting mortality.

In a study done by Han JS et al.,[10] MPV >10.2 fL was associated with 28-day all-cause mortality in AKI patients undergoing continuous renal replacement therapy.

Yousefichaijan P et al.[11] in their study stated that the sensitivity and specificity of MPV for the diagnosis of loss and failure were higher than those of the other inflammation markers. MPV <8.2 fL was significantly associated with poor prognosis in renal functions. Abnormally low MPV values correlate primarily with thrombocytopenia when it is due to impaired production as seen in aplastic anemia.

However, conflicting results were reported in previous studies, possibly due to sample sizes (<400) and single-center designs. Beyazit Y et al.[12] found lower MPV correlated with the severity of inflammation in acute pancreatitis patients, while Safak S et al.[13] observed a negative correlation between MPV and various inflammatory disorders.

CONCLUSION

Routine MPV assessment informs on inflammatory conditions’ prognosis. Labs must standardize procedures. Establishing MPV cutoffs could gauge inflammation severity, disease presence, and treatment response.

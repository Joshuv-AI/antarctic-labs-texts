Prognostic value of miR-21 in colorectal cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: updated systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jing Huang, Qiang He, Jufang Wang, Chunbao Zhai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to conduct a systematic review of the prognostic value of miR-21 expression levels in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC), pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC).Methods and ResultsWe searched the PubMed, Embase, and CENTRAL databases for relevant studies published up to August 25, 2023. Sixteen studies, totaling 3096 patients, were included. High miR-21 expression was associated with higher hazard ratio (HR) values and decreased overall survival (OS) in CRC and PDAC, but this effect was not statistically significant in ESCC. The pooled HRs for CRC, PDAC, and ESCC were 1.54 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.14-2.07; P = 0.004; I2 = 71%), 2.11 (95% CI: 1.81-2.46; P < 0.001; I2 = 0%), and 1.79 (95% CI: 0.82-3.92; P = 0.15; I2 = 75%), respectively. Patients with high miR-21 expression had higher HR values and decreased disease-free survival (DFS) compared to those with low miR-21 expression in all three cancer types. The pooled HRs for CRC, PDAC, and ESCC were 1.88 (95% CI: 1.36-2.60; P < 0.001; I2 = 59%), 2.04 (95% CI: 1.34-3.11; P < 0.001; I2 = 17%), and 1.77 (95% CI: 1.04-3.03; P = 0.04; I2 = 36%), respectively.ConclusionElevated miR-21 expression levels are associated with poorer OS and DFS in patients with CRC, pancreatic cancer, and esophageal cancer. However, due to heterogeneity in the results, further large-sample, high-quality studies are needed to validate these findings.Keywords: microRNA-21, prognosis, colorectal cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma

The overexpression of miR-21 is consistently associated with poor prognosis in colorectal cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Its detection may serve as a non−invasive biomarker to assist in risk stratification, guide individualized treatment planning, and monitor disease progression. Standardized protocols and further validation are essential to support its integration into clinical practice.

Introduction

Gastrointestinal tract (GI) tumors, including colorectal cancer (CRC), pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) are widely distributed globally, characterized by high invasiveness and malignancy, and pose a significant threat to the survival and quality of life of affected individuals.1 CRC is the third most common cancer globally, with approximately 1.8 million new cases diagnosed annually and accounting for nearly 900 000 deaths per year according to the World Health Organization.2 PDAC, on the other hand, is one of the most lethal cancers with exceptionally low survival rates, often diagnosed at advanced stages where surgical resection is not feasible. ESCC primarily affects populations in Asia and the Asia-Pacific region, with a relatively high incidence.3–5 miRNAs play an important role in the oncogenic process, and are predictive of tumor classification, prognosis, and therapeutic response. MiRNAs can act as either tumor suppressors or oncogenes, and, thus, actively participate in the oncogenic process. We considered miRNAs could predict the occurrence and development of tumors.6

miR-21 is frequently overexpressed in various human tumors and in cancer cell lines, including glioblastoma, head and neck cancer, ovarian cancer, B-cell lymphoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, cervical cancer, and lung cancer.7 Functional studies in cancer cell lines indicate that miR-21 plays an important role in the oncogenic process as indicated by its association with high proliferation, low apoptosis, high invasion, and metastatic potential. Previous work in various cancer cell lines and animal models indicates that miR-21 promotes oncogenesis, and hence has been called an oncomiR. For example, miR-21 was the only consistently upregulated miRNA in a study that profiled 540 clinical samples from cancer patients.8 miR-21 has been identified as a key oncogenic miRNA with elevated expression levels in many GI tumors. It has been linked to the occurrence, development, and invasiveness of tumors. Abnormal miR-21 expression has been widely reported in digestive tract tumors such as CRC, PDAC, and ESCC.9–11 MiR-21 not only participates in the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells but is also associated with drug resistance.12 Elevated miR-21 expression has been correlated with lymph node metastasis, increased risk of recurrence, and poor prognosis in patients with GI tumors, providing crucial information for patient treatment and follow-up.13

Although some studies have explored the application of miR-21 in the diagnosis and prognosis of patients with gastrointestinal tumors, there is still a lack of quantitative results analysis for its prognostic value in GI tumors. A previous meta-analysis, while providing some research evidence, had limitations in its data analysis, lacked subgroup analysis, and has been surpassed by emerging new research findings.14 By understanding the common role of miR-21 across these GI tumors, we can better appreciate its significance in tumorigenesis and its potential as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target. Therefore, we conducted this updated systematic review and meta-analysis to comprehensively evaluate the utility of miR-21 expression level detection in predicting the prognosis of patients with CRC, PDAC, and ESCC.

Methods

Search strategy and literature selection

We followed the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines, as recommended by the Cochrane Collaboration,15 for conducting this systematic review and meta-analysis. We conducted searches in three electronic databases: PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). The search was conducted from the inception of the databases up to August 25, 2023, and was limited to articles published in English. Our search strategy combined MeSH/Emtree terms and free-text words. The key search terms included miR-21, prognosis, CRC, PDAC, and ESCC, and were used in title and abstract keyword patterns. Additionally, we attempted manual searches of reference lists of relevant studies, reviews, and meta-analyses to expand the potential scope of the search. We exported the literature records to reference management software and conducted automatic deduplication, followed by manual deduplication. Subsequently, two researchers independently screened electronic records and retrieved publications based on inclusion and exclusion criteria. During the screening process, discrepancies were resolved through discussion between the two researchers, and the final list of eligible articles was determined. In cases where consensus could not be reached, a senior researcher made the decision.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were designed based on the PICOS framework, covering Participants, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, and Study design. In this study, we set the following criteria: (1) Patients with the aforementioned 3 types of tumors; (2) detection of miR-21 expression levels in tumor tissue or peripheral blood; (3) comparison of prognosis differences between the high miR-21 expression group and low miR-21 expression group; (4) setting of prognostic outcome indicators and reporting of related hazard ratio (HR) values, including overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and so on. The exclusion criteria mainly included: (1) Duplicate data, extended studies, or identical studies; (2) research types unrelated to the topic, such as animal studies, case reports, literature reviews, or conference abstracts; (3) studies with incomplete data or studies that did not report the specified outcomes; (4) studies that used different interventions or control measures.

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment

After excluding irrelevant studies, 2 researchers independently extracted the characteristics and data of the included studies. These characteristics and data included basic study information such as author, publication date, study design, tumor type, sample for detection, detection methods, study outcomes, and so on. Two researchers independently used the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for assessing the quality of case-control studies and cohort studies. NOS evaluates cohort studies and case-control studies by 3 blocks and 8 items in total, specifically including selection of the study population, comparability, and assessment of exposure or outcome.

Statistical analysis

We performed the meta-analysis using RevMan 5.4.1 (Nordic Cochrane Centre, Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, Denmark). We used the natural logarithm of HR and its standard error (SE) for the synthesis of survival outcomes and calculated the summary HR and its 95% confidence interval (CI) using the fixed-effect model when statistical heterogeneity was low (P > 0.1 or I2 < 50%). When there was statistical heterogeneity among the included study results (P < 0.1 or I2 ≥ 50%), we used the random-effects model for the meta-analysis. A general rule of thumb is that an observed HR > 1 indicates worse survival. We assessed the degree of statistical heterogeneity among the results of the included studies using the Cochran Q test and the I2 statistic. We set the significance level of the meta-analysis at α = 0.05. To assess the influence of individual studies on the overall effect, we conducted sensitivity analyses by observing the change in effect size after excluding individual studies. Additionally, subgroup analyses were performed to analyze the prognostic value of miR-21 in these tumor patients under different conditions. We assessed the potential for publication bias by constructing a funnel plot.

Results

Literature search and selection

A total of 340 electronic records were identified through the search strategy, with 77 from PubMed, 260 from Embase, and 3 from Cochrane. After removing duplicates using software and manual methods, 28 duplicate records were removed. Following the review of titles and abstracts, 288 irrelevant publications were excluded. Subsequently, 8 publications were excluded after full-text assessment due to irrelevant outcome measures, comparison strategies, or incomplete data. Finally, 16 articles were included in the meta-analysis. The flowchart of the literature selection process is shown in Figure 1.

Characteristics of included studies

This study included a total of 16 articles involving 3096 patients.16–31 The basic characteristics of the included studies are presented in Table 1. Among the included studies, 8 (50%) were conducted in Asia, with the majority being single-center studies (62.5%). Six studies focused on PDAC, 7 studies focused on CRC, and 3 studies focused on ESCC. The sample sizes in the included studies ranged from 42 to 686. Most of the samples (87.5%) were derived from tissue, and the majority of studies (75%) used qRT-PCR for detection. Seven studies reported the outcomes as OS rate and one as DFS rate. The rest eight studies described both OS and DFS data.

Risk of bias assessment

Methodological quality of included studies was assessed by the NOS, and the detailed information regarding the risk of bias was presented in Table S1. The methodological quality of most studies (93.8%) was good, with scores of 8 or higher. All studies selected representative populations, used appropriate methods for grouping populations, adequately assessed outcomes in populations, and had a sufficient follow-up duration. Some studies received fewer scores on comparability due to insufficient control of confounding factors.

Overall survival

Fifteen studies reported HR values related to miR-21 and OS. Among them, 6 studies were related to CRC, 6 to PDAC, and 3 to ESCC. Compared to the low miR-21 expression group, patients with high miR-21 expression in CRC and PDAC had higher HR values and lower OS rates, but this effect was not statistically significant in ESCC. The pooled HR values for CRC, PDAC, and ESCC were 1.54 (95% CI: 1.14-2.07; P = 0.004; I2 = 71%), 2.11 (95% CI: 1.81-2.46; P < 0.001; I2 = 0%), and 1.79 (95% CI: 0.82-3.92; P = 0.15; I2 = 75%), respectively, and there was no statistically significant difference in effects between the subgroups. The final pooled HR for OS in these three types of cancer was 1.90 (95% CI: 1.55-2.33; P < 0.001; I2 = 74%) (Figure 2).

Disease-free survival

Ten studies reported HR values related to both miR-21 and DFS. Among them, 4 studies were in study of CRC, 3 to PDAC, and 3 to ESCC. Compared to the low miR-21 expression group, patients with high miR-21 expression had higher HR values and lower DFS in all three types of tumors. The pooled HR values for CRC, PDAC, and ESCC were 1.88 (95% CI: 1.36-2.60; P < 0.001; I2 = 59%), 2.04 (95% CI: 1.34=3.11; P < 0.001; I2 = 17%), and 1.77 (95% CI: 1.04-3.03; P = 0.04; I2 = 36%), respectively, with no statistically significant difference in effects between the subgroups. The final pooled HR for DFS in these three types of cancer was 1.87 (95% CI: 1.50-2.34; P < 0.001; I2 = 45%) (Figure 3).

Subgroup analysis

We also conducted subgroup analyses to assess the impact of different factors such as sample source, detection methods, and geographical location of the studies on the summary results and heterogeneity (Table 2). Compared with tissue samples, patients with higher plasma expression levels were more likely to have higher HR for both OS and DFS. The qRT-PCR detection method yielded higher HR values compared with other methods to detect mir-21, indicating its higher sensitivity. In addition, mir-21 had slightly higher HR values in Eastern populations compared with Western populations. No significant publication bias was found in the data analysis for either end point (Figure S1 and S2).

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis suggested that the combined results were stable, and single studies had little effect on the total results. To address the sources of heterogeneity, we performed a detailed sensitivity analysis. In the meta-analysis of OS for CRC, we removed each study one by one and observed that the I2 values fluctuated between 65% and 75%. No single study was found to have a significant impact on the overall heterogeneity. For ESCC, the sensitivity analysis of OS revealed that after excluding the study by Luo (2020), the I2 value for the remaining two studies dropped to 0%. The combined HR at this point was 1.32 (95% CI: 0.85-2.08, P = 0.22), showing no significant change from the previous result. In the DFS meta-analysis, within the CRC subgroup, the removal of the study by Nielsen resulted in the I2 value decreasing to 0%. The combined HR was then 2.08 (95% CI: 1.60-2.72, P < 0.001), which did not significantly alter the initial findings.

Discussion

Summary of main findings

This meta-analysis systematically evaluated the prognostic value of miR-21 in CRC, PDAC, and ESCC. Key findings include: (1) higher miR-21 expression was associated with poorer OS in CRC and PDAC, while ESCC showed a non−statistically significant trend. The lack of statistical significance for OS in ESCC should be interpreted cautiously, it probably reflects low statistical power due to the small number of studies and patients rather than a true absence of prognostic value for miR-21 in this tumor type; (2) higher miR-21 expression predicted worse DFS in all three cancers; and (3) no significant inter−group differences in prognostic value, suggesting miR-21’s consistent performance across tumor types. These results support the potential use of miR-21 as a biomarker in gastrointestinal malignancies.

Implications for practice

CRC, PDAC, and ESCC are major digestive tract tumors in which miR-21 is frequently overexpressed and closely linked to tumor progression and poor prognosis. In CRC and PDAC, miR-21 promotes proliferation and inhibits apoptosis by downregulating tumor suppressors such as PDCD4, PTEN, and TPM1, and activating pathways like PI3K/Akt and NF-κB.6,8,32–35 In ESCC, miR-21 similarly enhances malignancy via suppression of PTEN and TIMP3 and activation of oncogenic pathways.36 As a non-invasive biomarker, miR-21 detection in tissues or body fluids holds clinical promise for assessing recurrence risk and survival across these tumors.37 However, its clinical translation is limited by methodological variability, including differences in sample types (serum, plasma, and FFPE), detection platforms, and interpretation criteria. Standardized protocols, validated reagents, and inter−laboratory collaboration are needed to reduce heterogeneity and support broader clinical application.38,39

Variability in cut-off values for miR-21 expression, along with differences in tumor stage reporting, staging systems, and unclear treatment details, contributes to significant heterogeneity across studies.39,40 Technical factors, tumor heterogeneity, and inconsistent sample handling further complicate interpretation. Although miR-21 is generally independent of gender and tumor location, multivariate analysis in CRC has shown it to be an independent prognostic factor alongside metastasis and differentiation status. Combining miR-21 with other clinical or molecular markers may enhance prognostic accuracy. To support clinical application, future research should adopt standardized, large-scale, prospective multicenter designs. Ali et al.,16 for example, validated CISH-based miR-21 detection across four international cohorts and confirmed its prognostic value in pancreatic cancer, providing a model for clinical translation. While miR-21 detection may aid risk stratification, no targeted therapies currently exist to counteract its prognostic detriment in CRC or PDAC. Clinical utility remains confined to prognostication rather than therapeutic decision-making. Despite consistent evidence linking miR-21 to poor outcomes, therapeutic inhibition of miR-21 is preclinical, with no trials validating efficacy in cancers. Thus, its role remains prognostic, not predictive of therapeutic response.

Before clinical adoption, miR-21 will require validation in prospective clinical trials with predefined cut-offs, harmonized protocols for detection, and comparisons with established prognostic indices. Its utility may be highest as part of a multiplex biomarker panel or in conjunction with imaging or clinical staging systems. Regulatory guidance and prospective biomarker-driven clinical trial designs (e.g., umbrella or basket trials) should be considered for future development.

Strengths and limitations

Compared to a previous meta-analysis,14 this study offers several key improvements. It includes the most recent literature, ensuring up-to-date insights into the prognostic value of miR-21 in CRC, PDAC, and ESCC. Rigorous quality assessment tools were applied to enhance credibility, and subgroup analyses were conducted to explore variability across different factors. These methodological enhancements provide a more comprehensive and reliable evaluation of miR-21’s prognostic significance. Similarly, our analysis confirmed a significant association between high miR-21 expression and poor prognosis in CRC and PDAC. However, this association was not statistically significant in ESCC. This variation may reflect tumor-specific biology and microenvironmental differences, as well as distinct oncogenic pathways modulated by miR-21 in different cancer types. Moreover, differences in sample type (eg, tissue vs. serum), detection platform, and analytical methods may contribute to this inconsistency. Variability in cutoff values across studies is another important factor influencing heterogeneity; thus, future subgroup analyses stratified by threshold levels, or the use of pseudo-likelihood models to accommodate differing cutoffs, may help improve comparability and yield more reliable conclusions. It is plausible that the effect of detection platform is modified by sample type, for example, serum-based studies relying more on qRT-PCR. However, limited data precluded formal interaction analysis. Future studies should report detailed methodology and enable stratified comparisons.

This study has several limitations. First, the sensitivity of circulating miR-21 remains suboptimal. Although serum-based detection is more clinically practical than tissue analysis, low miRNA concentrations and variability in sample handling and laboratory techniques may affect result stability. Second, heterogeneity persisted across studies despite subgroup and sensitivity analyses. This may stem from differences in patient populations, study designs, and detection methods. Sensitivity analysis showed consistent results after excluding individual studies, supporting robustness. Third, most included studies were single-center, potentially introducing selection bias and limiting generalizability. Multi–center studies are needed for broader applicability. Fourth, although a considerable number of studies were included, publication bias cannot be excluded. Funnel plot analysis showed asymmetry, suggesting potential bias due to the preferential publication of positive results. Lastly, the number of included studies—particularly those using blood samples—was limited, which may affect the reliability of subgroup findings. Future research should expand sample sizes and include unpublished data to improve accuracy and reduce bias. To translate miR-21 from a research biomarker into a clinically actionable assay, future efforts must standardize pre-analytical variables (sample type, RNA extraction, storage) and analytical protocols (RT-qPCR primers, spike-in controls, cut-off algorithms) across independent laboratories. Prospective, multi–center cohort studies should integrate miR-21 quantification into existing clinical workflows. An adaptive, biomarker-stratified interventional trial should be initiated in which high miR-21 expression triggers intensified adjuvant therapy or enhanced surveillance, testing the non–inferiority of standard care versus biomarker-guided treatment.

Conclusion

In summary, elevated miR-21 expression levels are associated with poorer prognosis, including OS and DFS, in patients with CRC, PDAC, and ESCC, compared to those with low expression levels. As a diagnostic indicator, miR-21 demonstrates potential applications in prognostic assessments for CRC and PDAC, assisting clinicians in better risk assessment and decision-making for these patients.

Risk analysis of postoperative nausea and vomiting in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery
Authors: Danzhi Luo, Zhenxing Huang, Simin Tang, Jiurong Cheng, Yingdong Deng, Xiangsheng Zhang, Yu Cao, Chenxi Zhou, Jun Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AimsThis study is designed to identify risk factors of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery and establish a nomogram model.MethodsIn this retrospective and multicenter study, we collected and analyzed data from 1233 patients who underwent gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. The Lasso algorithm was used to optimize the selection of independent variables in the development group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to explore the risk factors of PONV to develop the predictive nomogram model. Finally, we used an internal and external verification group and machine learning (ML) to evaluate the accuracy and clinical applicability of the model.ResultsThe dosage of sufentanil in patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA), the dosage of remifentanil, the use of neostigmine, duration of surgery and the maximum value of the PETCO2 were risk factors of PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. In contrast, the dosage of propofol during the surgery and the use of steroids were protective factors. The nomogram was then established to predict the incidence of PONV in these patients based on the above eight indicators. The C-index of the development group, internal, and external verification groups are 0.802, 0.857, and 0.966, respectively.ConclusionA nomogram model was developed to predict the incidence of PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. This model was found to be reliable and of high clinical value.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-024-02727-y.

Introduction

Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) refers to nausea, vomiting and/or retching within 24 h after surgery. The incidence of PONV is between 20% and 40%, and occurs up to 80% of high-risk patients (female, history of PONV and/or motion sickness, non-smoking status, and the use of postoperative opioids) [1]. PONV is a painful experience that can lead to different degrees of patient discomfort. It also increases postoperative complications, such as anastomotic bleeding, incision cracking, aspiration pneumonia, and may also prolong the hospitalization time of the patients. These complications increase the burden on the patients and the entire medical system [2]. Some investigations have shown that surgical patients are willing to spend more to prevent PONV [3, 4]. Micro-invasive surgery represented by laparoscopy, has become an inevitable trend in modern gynecological surgery [5]. Due to gender differences, pneumoperitoneum status, perioperative analgesic drug usage, status of non-smoking and other factors, the incidence of PONV in patients after laparoscopic gynecological surgery is exceptionally high. Hence, evaluating the incidence of PONV in these patients is of great significance.

Currently, the scoring system commonly used to evaluate the risk of PONV clinically is the prediction model proposed by Apfel and Koivuranta [6]. However, these models also have certain limitations. The simplified model only provides a practical concept to predict the probability of individual PONV but cannot accurately predict the likelihood of PONV of a specific group. The idea of comfortable medical care and enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) has been widely used in in patients-controlled analgesia (PCA) in recent years. Opioid drugs are commonly used in patients-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA). Some researchers have proposed a dynamic predictive model for PONV for PCIA [7]. Besides other characteristics of gynecologic laparoscopic surgery patients, female gender, non-smoking status, young age, and laparoscopic surgery are all high-risk factors for PONV. There is still no specific risk prediction model existing for PONV in gynaecological laparoscopy populations. Early prediction and effective prevention will contribute to achieving rapid recovery of patients and promote better medical care.

A nomogram converts a complex statistical model into a visual column graph by a complex regression equation. By constructing a multi-factor regression model, each value level of each influencing factor is assigned a score. Then, add the values of each influencing factor to get the total score. Then, we could get the prediction probability of individual outcome events in the model [8].

However, there is currently no targeted evaluation of the incidence of PONV in patients undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgery with PCIA. Hence, this study aimed to develop a prediction model that can assess the risks of PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery during perianesthesia, with particular emphasis on patients with all high-risk factors. To achieve more accurate and more significant guidelines for clinical practice, the targeted analysis of relevant factors was aimed at a specific population rather than an analysis of a large population.

Study design and data collection

To create a prediction model, we collected data from 1122 Chinese adult patients who underwent gynecologic laparoscopic surgery under general anesthesia with PCIA in the First People’s Hospital of Foshan, Foshan, China from January 1, 2018, to April 30, 2021.

For external validation, data were collected from 207 Chinese adult patients who underwent gynecologic laparoscopic surgery under general anesthesia with PCIA in the Third Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University, Guangzhou, China from November 1, 2021, to December 8, 2022.

For both cohorts, inclusive criteria were: female patients who used opioids to control post-operative pain and were undergoing laparoscopic gynecological surgery (total laparoscopic ovarian cyst removal, total laparoscopic salpingectomy, total laparoscopic hysterectomy, total laparoscopic cystectomy, and total laparoscopic myomectomy) under general anesthesia, non-smoking status, non-motion sickness or non-PONV history. Exclusion criteria were as follows: patients who received gynecological laparoscopic surgery but converted to open surgery, have incomplete case information, recently suffered from gastrointestinal and central nervous system diseases, have cochlear diseases that can cause nausea and vomiting, and patients with a history of mental illness, and patients who received cancer chemotherapy or emetogenic radiotherapy within four weeks.

Other collected data from enrolled patients included: patients’ age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), the type of antibiotics used 30 min before the operation, the use of anesthetic drugs during the procedure, the total amount of fluid infused during the process, the duration of the operation, the minimum and maximum values of end-tidal carbon dioxide pressure (PETCO2), general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation or laryngeal mask, urine volume and bleeding volume.

The perioperative anesthesia methods are all intravenous-inhalation combined anesthesia.

Definition of outcome

PONV refers to nausea, vomiting, or retching within 24 h after surgery. A specialized postoperative acute pain management team grouped the patients according to whether PONV occurred (PONV vs. non-PONV). We evaluated the patient’s PONV score by visual analogue scale (VAS): a ten centimetre ruler was used as a ruler, one end was 0, indicating no nausea and vomiting, and the other end was 10, indicating the most severe nausea and vomiting that was unbearable (1–4 for mild, 5–6 for moderate, 7–10 for severe).

Statistical analysis

Data management and analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 statistical software and R software (R version 4.1.2, https://www.R-project.org). The collected data were first randomized into the development group and the internal verification group according to 7:3 using the “caret” package. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method was used for dimension reduction. It was applied to select the optimal characteristic of prediction candidates among patients with risk factors after undergoing gynecologic laparoscopic surgery [9, 10]. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to develop a model including all predictor candidates as independent variables. Backward stepwise was performed with P-values < 0.05 for all independent variables in the model. Features with non-zero coefficients in LASSO regression and multivariate logistic regression analysis were selected to construct the prediction model. The predictive power of the nomogram was tested by Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) [11]. The calibration curve and decision curve analysis (DCA) were plotted to assess the performance and judge the clinical validity of the nomogram. Finally, the variables screened by multivariate logistic regression analysis were used to reconstruct the model using a neural network. Through the neural network, the accuracy of our model was further verified and the variable importance was evaluated.

Clinical characteristics of patients

Feature selection

This study first used the Lasso regression method to optimize the independent variables. In the development group, 28 independent variables were reduced to 14 potential predictive factors based on the 718 patients (Supplementary Fig. 1). Then, the variables were selected from the Lasso and multivariate logistic regression analysis using backward elimination. As a result, the dosage of remifentanil, the dosage of sufentanil in the analgesic pump, the dosage of propofol during the surgery, the duration of surgery, the use of neostigmine, the use of steroids, and the maximum value of the PETCO2 included into the model construction were obtained (Supplementary Table 1).

PONV prediction model of patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery

We built a nomogram containing seven independent predictors based on logistic regression analysis. The dosage of propofol during the surgery, the use of steroids were protective factors for patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. In contrast, the dosage of sufentanil in the analgesic pump, the dosage of remifentanil, the use of neostigmine, the duration of surgery, and the maximum value of the PETCO2 were risk factors (Table 2). Finally, a nomogram was developed based on the above independent predictors (Fig. 2).

Internal and external validation of a Nomogram

The calibration curve indicated well-consistent predicted and observed probabilities for patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. In addition, the nomogram showed good accuracy with a C-index of 0.802 and 0.857 in the development group, and the internal verification group, respectively. And the C-index of the external verification group is 0.966, which further shows the excellent accuracy of the prediction model. Figure 3(A) shows the calibration curve of the external verification group. In addition, the curve fitting indicated that the prediction effect is good.

The decision curve analysis (DCA) showed that if the threshold probability of a patient was between 2% and 86%, using this nomogram to predict PONV of patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery could be more beneficial than either the treat-all-patients scheme or the treat-none scheme (Fig. 3[B]).

Discussion

In this study, we have developed a personalized model containing seven variables to predict the risk of PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery, aiming to facilitate clinicians’ use of the nomogram. The nomogram revealed that the less use of opioid drugs, avoid use of neostigmine, lessen the duration of surgery, and control ventilation during anesthesia which influence the value of the PETCO2 could likely to reduce the incidence of PONV. Our results are consistent with those previously reported studies which revealed that the dosage of propofol and the use of steroids, are associated with lower incidence of PONV [12–15].

Neostigmine is an agent that inhibits the effects of acetylcholinesterase. However, the influence of intravenous injection of neostigmine on PONV is still controversial. Injecting neostigmine into the sheath has been proven to be related to severe nausea and vomiting in a dose-dependent manner, which may be the effect of the brain stem [16]. However, another study stated that there is insufficient evidence to indicate that using neostigmine will increase the risk of PONV [17]. In contrast, our study confirmed that using neostigmine could increase the risk of PONV in patients after laparoscopic gynecological surgery; this may be related to different surgical methods, general anesthesia modes, and related medications in anesthesia. Therefore, more targeted research should be conducted to understand the relationship between neostigmine and PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery.

PETCO2 may also influence the incidence of patients after laparoscopic gynecological surgery. Patients undergoing laparoscopic gynecological surgery showed that low PETCO2 management reduces the incidence of nausea, PONV score, and remedial anti-vomiting drugs [18]. However, a random control test revealed that moderate high-carbonate management neither reduced the incidence of PONV nor reduced the need for a remedy for vomiting after gynecological surgery [19]. Contrary to our findings, another recent study showed that the average PETCO2 ≤35mmHg during the surgery had no significant correlation with the increase in the incidence of PONV [20]. This may be caused by CO2 pneumoperitoneum leading to increased intraabdominal pressure (IAP) and hypercapnia. Increasing IAP could decrease intestinal blood flow, and short-time ischemia could trigger the release of neurotransmitters, such as serotonin, that could lead to PONV. Moreover, increasing IAP may increase intracranial pressure (ICP), which increased and led to nausea and vomiting. Therefore, the correlation between the maximum value of PETCO2 and the PONV of such patients still needs further research.

Therefore, this study has many benefits. First, we provided a nomogram to calculate the risk of PONV based on risk predictors, which helps clinicians quickly and accurately calculate the individual’s risk of PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery. Second, an internal and external validation were used to ensure the results’ reliability, and an external validation sample was used to evaluate the performance of our prediction model further. Third, the clinical decision curve was used to assess the net benefit of our model. Fourth, we additionally performed a neural network to verify our model’s accuracy and evaluated the variables’ importance.

There are still some limitations as follows. First, it has not recorded the frequency of PONV within 24h after surgery. Further, the study was conducted in two health institutions, and wilder multicenter research should be completed in the future. Concisely, our study developed a nomogram to predict the incidence of PONV in patients after laparoscopic gynecological surgery. This model is of high clinical value and can be used as a reference tool for evaluating early medical intervention to prevent PONV in patients after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery.

Reply: NDM-producing Multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa in Korea: A Growing Threat Requiring National Attention
Authors: Tark Kim, Yong Pil Chong
Published: 2026

Dear Editor:

We thank Dr. Kim for insightful correspondence regarding our article [1], “Genetic Epidemiology and Antimicrobial Susceptibilities of Carbapenem-Resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa Isolates from a Multicenter Study in Korea” [2]. In his report, 21 (61.8%) of 34 multidrug-resistant P. aeruginosa (MRPA) isolates collected between May 2024 and September 2025 were resistant to ceftolozane/tazobactam. Notably, isolates resistant to ceftazidime/avibactam were also resistant to ceftolozane/tazobactam. Among 10 representative MRPA isolates resistant to ceftolozane/tazobactam, blaNDM was detected in all strains.

These findings further support the increasing predominance of New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM)-producing high-risk strains among MRPA isolates in Korea. In our multicenter study, the overall susceptibility rate to ceftazidime/avibactam was only 17.5%, and blaNDM was detected in 57.1% (36/63) of carbapenem-resistant P. aeruginosa (CRPA) isolates collected between January 2021 and September 2024 [2]. Moreover, Kim’s observation that all NDM-producing P. aeruginosa isolates were resistant to both ceftazidime/avibactam and ceftolozane/tazobactam aligns with the theoretical limitations of these newer β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors against metallo-β-lactamase producers. Notably, at one of the authors’ institutions, susceptibility rates determined using the VITEK 2 system for 107 CRPA isolates collected in the second half of 2025 were only 20.6% for ceftazidime/avibactam and 17.8% for ceftolozane/tazobactam according to Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) criteria (unpublished data).

Beginning February 1, 2026, cefiderocol has been reimbursed by the National Health Insurance Service of Korea for the treatment of urinary tract infections and hospital-acquired pneumonia caused by CRPA [3]. In the ERACE-PA global study, Gill et al. evaluated the in vitro activity of cefiderocol against CRPA, including NDM-producing isolates. Among 13 NDM-producing P. aeruginosa isolates collected worldwide, cefiderocol susceptibility was 85% according to CLSI criteria and 69% according to EUCAST criteria [4]. These findings suggest that cefiderocol may represent an important therapeutic option for infections caused by NDM-producing P. aeruginosa in Korea.

At present, Korea lacks national diagnostic and treatment guidelines specifically addressing MRPA. As Kim pointed out, clinicians often rely on international guidance such as the Infectious Diseases Society of America recommendations; however, these guidelines do not fully reflect the epidemiologic and healthcare context of Korea [5]. Given the high prevalence of NDM-producing P. aeruginosa strains and the low susceptibility to newer β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors observed in Kim’s and our data, there is an urgent need to develop locally adapted diagnostic and therapeutic guidelines.

Furthermore, we strongly agree with Kim’s proposal to establish a national surveillance system for MRPA. Although sentinel surveillance for MRPA is currently conducted in Korea [6], systematic national-level data collection focusing on carbapenemase types, high-risk strains, and susceptibility to newly introduced antimicrobial agents remains insufficient. Comprehensive surveillance, incorporating molecular epidemiology and antimicrobial susceptibility profiles, is essential to monitor the spread of high-risk MRPA strains and guide clinical and public health strategies.

In summary, NDM-producing P. aeruginosa strains that are intrinsically resistant even to newer agents such as ceftazidime/avibactam and ceftolozane/tazobactam appear to be spreading nationwide in Korea. These findings underscore the urgent need to establish a national surveillance system targeting high-risk P. aeruginosa strains and to develop and revise diagnostic and treatment guidelines that account for the domestic epidemiologic situation.

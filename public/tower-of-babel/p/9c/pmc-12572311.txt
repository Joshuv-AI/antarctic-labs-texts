Healthcare workers’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy
Authors: Hong Zhu, Yanzhe Wu, Yalong Jiang, Li Wu, Wen Wang, Guang Zhu, Changchun Cao, Ji Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study examined healthcare workers’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) concerning diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. A cross-sectional survey was conducted at Nanjing Gulou Hospital Group Suqian Hospital and other hospitals in Suqian City’s Sucheng district from August to October 2023, using a structured questionnaire. Among 495 respondents, 71.9% were female, 87.5% worked in tertiary hospitals, and 49.9% were nurses. Median scores (25th–75th percentiles) were: knowledge (10, 7–12), attitude (30, 28–33), and practice (34, 28–40). Multivariable logistic regression identified factors linked to poor practices, including employment in non-tertiary hospitals (OR = 0.388, 95% CI: 0.213–0.709, P = 0.002), lacking a professional title (OR = 0.264, 95% CI: 0.087–0.797, P = 0.018), and holding an intermediate title (OR = 0.459, 95% CI: 0.230–0.914, P = 0.027). Overall, healthcare workers demonstrated insufficient knowledge, favorable attitudes, but suboptimal practices. The findings suggest a need for targeted education and training programs to improve their understanding and management of diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, ultimately enhancing patient care outcomes.

Introduction

Diabetes, a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels, manifests with symptoms such as increased thirst and frequent urination1,2. As its prevalence escalates globally, a myriad of complications emerges, including cardiovascular diseases, renal failure, and various neuropathies3,4. Among these, diabetic-related cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy stands out as a significant yet often overlooked complication5,6. This condition impairs the autonomic nerves governing the heart and blood vessels, leading to abnormalities in heart rate and vascular function7. Despite its substantial impact on morbidity and quality of life, this neuropathy frequently goes undiagnosed, attributed to its insidious onset and diverse manifestations8. This condition contributes to erratic heart rate control and vascular dynamics, substantially elevating the risks of cardiac arrhythmias, silent myocardial ischemia, and sudden death. Therefore, prompt recognition and management are vital to enhance patient outcomes5.

The theoretical foundation of Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice (KAP) studies provides a lens to examine healthcare workers’ perceptions, attitudes, and management strategies concerning diabetic-related cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy9,10. This group often faces misdiagnosis and delayed treatment due to a general lack of awareness and understanding of this specific complication among healthcare providers. This gap in recognition and vigilance exacerbates the risk of severe cardiovascular events. However, there is a noticeable scarcity of KAP research specifically addressing this condition. Most existing studies have either generalized diabetic complications or not thoroughly explored the cognitive, attitudinal, and practical dimensions of managing this particular complication11–13.

The aim of this study is to fill this research void by conducting a detailed investigation into the knowledge levels, attitudes, and practices of healthcare workers regarding diabetic-related cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. This study seeks to elucidate their current state of understanding, prevailing attitudes, and the practices adopted in managing this condition. By doing so, it highlights the necessity for targeted educational and training programs for healthcare workers, aiming to improve the management of diabetic-related cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, thereby enhancing patient care and outcomes in this high-risk population.

Methods

Research design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted from Nanjing Gulou Hospital Group Suqian Hospital and other Suqian City Sucheng district hospitals from August 1, 2023 - October 30, 2023 focusing on healthcare workers as the primary participants. Ethical approval for this study has been obtained from the Nanjing Gulou Hospital Group Suqian Hospital of the Medical Ethics Committee [20230602], and informed consent was secured from all participants.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Medical staff working in hospitals ; (2) Participants who provided informed consent and willingly participated in the survey.

Questionnaire introduction

The design of the questionnaire was informed by pertinent guidelines and literature14–18, and further refined through feedback from one endocrinology specialist, two cardiovascular specialists, leading to a pilot distribution of 30 questionnaires. This preliminary phase achieved a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.801, indicating strong internal consistency. Structured into four main sections—demographic information, knowledge, attitude, and practice—the questionnaire’s knowledge dimension includes nine questions (eight single-choice and one multiple-choice), with correct answers scoring ‘1’. Among them, question 9 was a multiple-choice item with seven options, each correct answer scoring 1 point. Thus, the total knowledge score ranged from 0 to 15. The attitude dimension included nine questions. A1 and A2 were open-ended and not scored. The remaining seven items used a five-point Likert scale (1–5), giving a total score range of 7 to 35. The practice dimension features ten questions, with the first nine using a five-point Likert scale (from ‘always’ (5) to ‘never’ (1)) and a scoring range of 9 to 45; the tenth question is specifically for non-endocrinology personnel and is not included in the total score.

Statistical analysis

In the descriptive analysis of demographic data and dimension scores, a normality test was conducted on the score distribution of each dimension. As the data were found to be non-normally distributed, medians along with the 25th and 75th percentiles were utilized to describe the scores. Count data for demographic characteristics were presented as N (%). For group comparisons of the scores, non-parametric tests were employed. Specifically, the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test was applied for two-group comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis test was used for comparisons among three or more groups. The Spearman correlation coefficient was used for correlational analysis. Regarding the regression analysis of dimension scores as dependent variables, both univariate and multivariate regressions were conducted with demographic variables to analyze their relationships with scores in each dimension. Variables with a univariate P-value < 0.1 were included in the multivariate regression. P-values were reported to three decimal places, with a significance level set at P < 0.05.

Results

A total of 523 questionnaires were retrieved. After excluding: (1) those with completion times less than 90 s or greater than 1800 s (2 questionnaires), (2) incomplete questionnaires (19 questionnaires), and (3) questionnaires with abnormal responses exceeding the set options (7 questionnaires), a total of 495 valid questionnaires were obtained. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for the formal experiment feedback questionnaire was 0.857, and the KMO value was 0.898. Among these healthcare workers, 356 (71.9%) were females, 201 (40.6%) aged more than 35 years, 373 (75.4%) had education below master’s degree, 433 (87.5%) were working in tertiary hospitals, 247 (49.9%) were performing nursing duties, and 193 (39.0%) had an intermediate title.

The median (25th percentile, 75th percentile) score of knowledge, attitude, and practice were 10 (7,12), 30 (28,33) and 34 (28,40), separately. Participants who worked in a hospital with different level (P = 0.003) and who performed different duties (P = 0.005) were more likely to have different knowledge scores. Concurrently, participants with different gender (P = 0.029), worked in a hospital with different level (P = 0.010), performed different duties (P = 0.024), and had worked for different years (P = 0.003) were more likely to have different attitude scores. Additionally, participants with different title (P = 0.013) and working in different Department (P = 0.002) were more likely to have different practice scores (as shown in Table 1).

Regarding relevant practices, a relatively large proportion of practices were carried out by participants at a fairly high frequency, while some practices were conducted infrequently. Specifically, 37.4% often consulted diabetic patients about taking medication on time (P5), 34.3% often reminded patients to quit smoking (P7), and 32.3% of non-endocrine healthcare workers often advised patients to go to endocrinology for further consultation when there was a possibility of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. In addition, 33.9% reported that their hospitals sometimes provided relevant training (P8), and 32.5% sometimes alerted family members of diabetic patients about cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (P9). However, there were 32.1% paid little attention to the possibility of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy in patients with diabetes mellitus (as shown in Table 4).

To further explore the influencing factors of practice, multivariable logistic regression was performed and the results revealed that performing nursing work (OR = 1.728, 95% CI: [1.142,2.615], P = 0.002), performing other work (OR = 3.020, 95% CI: [1.360,6.708], P = 0.007), and having knowledge score greater than or equal to 10 (OR = 2.598, 95% CI: [1.773, 3.807], P < 0.001) were independently associated with proactive practice. Conversely, working in hospitals graded below tertiary (OR = 0.388, 95% CI: [0.213,0.709], P = 0.002), not having a title (OR = 0.264, 95% CI: [0.087,0.797], P = 0.018), and having an intermediate title (OR = 0.459, 95% CI: [0.230,0.914], P = 0.027), were independently associated with negative practice (as shown in Table 6).

Discussion

In this study, healthcare workers demonstrated insufficient knowledge, positive attitudes, but limited engagement in practices related to diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. Given the identified knowledge gaps and suboptimal practices, there is a critical need for targeted educational interventions and training programs aimed at improving healthcare workers’ understanding and management of diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy to enhance patient care and outcomes in this context.

The findings of our study provide valuable insights into the KAP of healthcare workers regarding diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. Notably, our study revealed a discrepancy between knowledge and practice, with healthcare workers demonstrating insufficient knowledge but positive attitudes toward diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. These findings are in line with previous research that has highlighted the importance of bridging the gap between knowledge and practice in healthcare settings19,20. The positive correlation between knowledge and attitude aligns with the Theory of Planned Behavior, which suggests that positive attitudes are often a precursor to behavioral change21,22 However, the lack of a significant correlation between attitude and practice underscores the complexity of healthcare behavior, indicating that attitude alone may not be sufficient to drive proactive practices. In contrast, a recent KAP study of healthcare professionals in China regarding diabetes self-management education and support (DSMES) reported strong knowledge but inconsistent attitudes and practices, with structural equation modeling revealing a negative association between knowledge and attitude and a positive link between attitude and practice23. This differs from our findings, where knowledge was insufficient but attitudes remained positive, yet both studies underscore practice as the most challenging dimension to improve. Furthermore, a Lebanese KAP study among diabetic patients on microvascular complications, including neuropathy, found that practice was hindered by economic and time barriers, while knowledge and attitudes were influenced by quality of life and complications12. Together, these comparisons highlight that although the nature of knowledge gaps and practice barriers may vary across populations and settings, bridging the gap between awareness and consistent practice remains a universal challenge.

Regarding demographic factors, our results demonstrate gender differences in attitudes, with female healthcare workers displaying significantly more positive attitudes. This gender-related variation may be attributed to differences in communication styles and empathy levels, as suggested by previous studies21,24. Furthermore, healthcare workers in tertiary hospitals exhibited lower knowledge and attitude scores compared to those in lower-tier hospitals. Besides, the analysis of professional titles revealed that title holders had higher practice scores, while endocrinology specialization was associated with higher practice scores. These results align with the literature, as specialization and advanced titles often indicate a deeper understanding of specific medical conditions and may lead to more effective patient care25.

The multivariable logistic regression analysis identified several factors independently associated with proactive practice, including performing nursing duties and having a knowledge score greater than or equal to 10. On the other hand, working in hospitals below the tertiary level and lacking professional titles were associated with negative practice. While positive attitudes are present, the gap between knowledge and practice suggests the need for targeted interventions and ongoing education. Gender differences, hospital level, professional titles, and specialization all play significant roles in shaping healthcare workers’ practices. To bridge the knowledge-practice gap and enhance patient care, we recommend the implementation of specialized training programs, particularly in lower-tier hospitals and among healthcare workers without professional titles, to improve their practices and ensure better outcomes for patients with diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy26,27.

Regarding knowledge concerning diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, our study uncovered both commendable understanding and notable gaps among healthcare professionals. Participants demonstrated a strong grasp of the condition’s fundamental definition and its impact on symptom perception. However, some specific areas of knowledge exhibited discrepancies. Notably, healthcare professionals displayed uncertainty about the incidence of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy among different diabetes types and the gender-related risk factors involved. These findings indicated that healthcare professionals often possess foundational knowledge of chronic conditions but may lack detailed, context-specific information. To address these knowledge gaps effectively, healthcare institutions should prioritize targeted educational interventions. These interventions should focus on providing healthcare professionals with comprehensive insights into the nuances of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, including its epidemiological patterns in various diabetes types and gender-specific susceptibility. Additionally, it is essential to disseminate the latest research findings and guidelines in this area, ensuring that healthcare professionals remain up-to-date with the evolving landscape of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy knowledge28,29.

In the realm of attitudes toward diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, our study uncovered a mixture of positive sentiments and concerns among healthcare professionals. A substantial proportion of participants acknowledged the significance of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy and the importance of annual screenings, reflecting a proactive attitude toward disease management. However, there were notable concerns regarding patient comprehension, awareness, and variability in concern levels among doctors. These concerns highlight the multifaceted nature of managing cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, which extends beyond medical expertise to encompass effective communication and patient engagement. To address these attitude-related challenges, healthcare institutions should consider implementing strategies to enhance healthcare professionals’ communication skills. Training programs should be designed to equip them with the tools to convey the importance of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy awareness to patients effectively. Additionally, healthcare institutions should promote a culture of interdisciplinary collaboration, encouraging healthcare professionals to work closely with colleagues from various specialties, including endocrinologists, to ensure a comprehensive and patient-centered approach to cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy management30,31.

In the practice dimension of our study, healthcare professionals exhibited varying practices related to diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. While many reported paying attention to cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy frequency and advising further testing for symptomatic patients, there were opportunities for improvement in promoting physical exercise, monitoring medication adherence, and encouraging lifestyle modifications. Furthermore, the availability of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy -related training within the hospital setting was relatively limited. These findings emphasize the need for practical and actionable recommendations to bridge the practice gap. To enhance practices related to cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy management, healthcare institutions should consider integrating behavioral interventions into routine clinical practice32. Healthcare professionals should receive training in motivational interviewing techniques to promote physical activity, medication adherence, dietary control, and smoking cessation among diabetes patients. These interventions should be part of a structured care plan and consistently implemented during patient encounters. Moreover, healthcare institutions should expand access to cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy -related training programs, making them readily available to all staff members. Institutions can also facilitate the dissemination of best practices and evidence-based guidelines to guide healthcare professionals in delivering optimal care to diabetes patients with cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy33,34. To address the identified knowledge and practice gaps, we recommend implementing targeted educational interventions with more concrete formats. These could include interactive workshops, case-based seminars, and e-learning modules, allowing flexibility to suit different learning preferences among healthcare workers. Training efforts should particularly focus on staff from non-tertiary hospitals and those without professional titles, as our regression analysis indicated lower practice scores in these groups. At the policy level, hospital administrations are encouraged to integrate diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy training into routine continuing education frameworks and to link participation with professional title advancement, thereby creating institutional incentives. In addition, incorporating DCAN screening and evaluation steps into clinical care pathways would provide a practical mechanism to reinforce knowledge and ensure consistent practice across healthcare settings.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, data collection relied on self-reported questionnaires, potentially introducing response bias and limiting the accuracy of reported behaviors. Additionally, The majority of the study was conducted in tertiary hospitals, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to a broader healthcare worker population. Lastly, the cross-sectional design hinders the establishment of causal relationships and precludes the assessment of changes in KAP over time. One limitation of this study is its geographic scope, as data were collected only from Suqian. This may restrict the generalizability of our findings to wider populations. Future multicenter or international collaborative studies will be necessary to validate and extend these results. However, despite this limitation, this paper provides a valuable contribution by offering a comprehensive examination of healthcare workers’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. It not only explores demographic variations but also identifies factors independently associated with proactive practice, thus providing essential insights for tailored interventions aimed at enhancing patient care in this context.

Although this study was conducted in Suqian, China, the observed gaps in healthcare workers’ knowledge and practice regarding diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy may also be common in many developing countries and regions. These findings therefore provide valuable insights for designing targeted educational and training programs not only in China but also in other global healthcare contexts. In conclusion, healthcare workers demonstrated insufficient knowledge, positive attitudes, and limited proactive practices concerning diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy. These findings underscore the need for targeted educational interventions and training programs to enhance healthcare workers’ knowledge and promote proactive practices in the management of diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, particularly among those working in non-tertiary hospitals and without professional titles.

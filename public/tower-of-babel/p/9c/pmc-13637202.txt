Oral health-related quality of life, mental health profile and their association in pre-university high school students, Iran
Authors: Sadaf Movahed, Abolfazl Mohammadbeigi, Aida Mahdipour
Published: 2026

Introduction

Oral health-related quality of life (OHQoL) refers to an individual’s perception of their oral condition and its impact on various aspects of life. Studies show that oral problems can have negative effects on various aspects of an individual’s life, including social relationships and general mental health, and people with dental problems are more likely to experience dissatisfaction and reduced quality of life [1–3]. According to the results of various studies, factors such as social and economic status and parental education level are effective in OHQoL [4]. In addition, stress levels and negative emotions, psychological and emotional experiences caused by dental problems can negatively affect life satisfaction and health behaviors, ultimately leading to increased dental caries and decreased quality of life [1, 4–7].

Adolescence due to academic and social pressures can lead to poor health behaviors such as neglecting oral hygiene. Studies have shown that stress can affect the body’s hormonal balance and, due to physiological effects, can lead to oral health problems such as bruxism (teeth grinding), gum disease, and ultimately a higher prevalence of tooth decay [8–11]. Stress is one of the important psychological factors that is increasingly observed among students, especially in high school students. Academic pressures, social relationships, and parental expectations can lead to the experience of severe stress in adolescents. Current statistics indicate that 31.9% of adolescents aged 13 to 18 have or currently have an anxiety disorder, and 31.5% have experienced symptoms of depression [12]. Therefore, oral health, as one of the fundamental pillars of public health, has direct and indirect effects on the quality of life of individuals [13]. According to a study by Khodabakshi et al. in Guilan, OHQoL as a multifaceted construct affects various aspects of appearance and beauty, oral comfort, and eating restrictions as very important components in quality of life and life satisfaction [14].

Some other studies have shown that stress can have a significant impact on oral health, leading to reduced attention to health care and, consequently, an increased risk of dental caries [8, 9]. Moreover, oral problems can have negative effects on various aspects of an individual’s life, including social relationships and general health, and people with dental problems are more likely to experience feelings of dissatisfaction and reduced quality of life [15, 16]. People who experience stressful lives value their oral health less and high school students who face intense academic pressures and social challenges may be particularly vulnerable to dental problems and reduced quality of life, daily activities and disruption of students’ social lives [4, 17–19].

Mental health is defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as “a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the stresses of life, realize their abilities, learn well and work well, and contribute to their community” [20]. Nevertheless, existing data provide valuable information about individual components of general mental health, stress and quality of life, limited researches have simultaneously examined these interrelationships in the adolescent population, especially in secondary schools who are in high stress of admission exam to university. Previous studies have mainly focused on the epidemiology of dental caries or the psychosocial aspects of stress without integrating these perspectives into a single framework. Therefore, it is necessary to examine the multifaceted relationships between general mental health, stress levels, and OHQoL in pre-university high school students. The present study aimed to investigate the relationship between dental caries index and OHQoL with stress in high school students in Qom, Iran.

Materials and methods

Study setting

This is a cross-sectional-analytic study conducted on 622 high school students who are trying to enter to the university during the autumn of 2025 in Qom, Iran.

Participants and sampling

Secondary high school students in Qom who were in high stress due to admission exam to university were the statistical population of this study. The required sample size for the study calculated according to the results of a similar study by AlJameel et al. [21], and considering the pooled standard deviation equal 13 in OHIP-14 score in students and mean difference equal 3, the study power 90%, and 95% confidence interval, the minimum sample size required for the study was 295. Nevertheless, considering the type of cluster sampling and the application of the design effect coefficient (df = 2), 622 students were included. The students were recruited through multi-stage sampling. First, cluster sampling was used and educational districts were considered as a cluster, and in 4 districts of the city, two girls’ schools and two boys’ schools were randomly selected from each district. Then, four schools selected and, in each school, three different classes selected by the simple random sampling. Finally, the eligible students who were consent for participating in the study were selected through convenient sampling.

Inclusion criteria were including all high school students who were in grade 10th, 11th and 12th who educating in Qom schools during the academic year 2025–2026 and were susceptible and eager to register in the university admission exam were eligible to participate in the study. Furthermore, disagreement for participating in the study, lack of cooperation for examination and data collection, or failure to answer at least 80% of the questions were exclusion criteria.

Data collection

DMFT

The data collection tool in this study collected using Decay, Missed, Filled Tooth (DMFT) clinical checklist and three different questionnaires. First, the DMFT clinical checklist was used to determine the DMFT index of students, their health records completed by a dentist and a trained dental student, and then, questionnaires delivered to students. Demographic questionnaire included questions such as student age, student gender, parents’ age, nationality, number of children, parents’ education level, parents’ occupation, family income level, and primary and supplementary insurance coverage. The second questionnaire used in this study was the 14-question Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14) questionnaire to measure the oral health impact profile. In the aforementioned questionnaire, questions include the effects of oral health on the individual’s quality of life in 7 different areas including functional limitation, physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability and handicap. Students must choose one of the options never, seldom, sometimes, often, and always to answer the questions. A score of zero indicates the absence of any impact (never) and a score of 4 indicates the worst impact (always) of OHQoL. The range of scores is between zero and 56, and a higher score indicates a lower OHQoL. This questionnaire was translated into Persian by Dr. Ravaghi and colleagues, and its validity and reliability have been confirmed [22]. It has been used in various studies in Iran [23–25]. The third questionnaire was the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ), developed by Goldberg and Hiller in 1979, to determine the level of stress of students. It has 28 questions and examines the general health of the individual and four different subscales including somatic symptoms (items 1–7), anxiety/insomnia (items 8–14), social dysfunction (items 15–21) and severe depression (items 22–28). The items are scoring based on a scale from 0 to 3. Each question is scored from 0 to 3, and a higher score indicates a disorder in the individual’s general health, as a higher score shows lower general health status. In this test, if the individual obtains a total score below 22 and in the range below 6, his health is normal, but obtaining a score of 22 to 40 indicates a weak disorder, 41 to 60 indicates a moderate disorder, and above 60 indicates a severe disorder [26]. The validity and reliability of the aforementioned questionnaire have been conducted and confirmed in Iran and Iranian studies [27–29].

Ethical consideration

Before starting the study, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the purpose of the study were explained to the target group. The human ethics and consent declarations elaborated for all subjects for confidentiality of the information, and every student provided its informed consent for participation. Then, a clinical examination was performed by a dentist or a trained final-year dental student, and the results of the examination were immediately communicated to the student. Finally, the questionnaires used were provided to the target group and the required information was collected. In addition, the study protocol is approved in Approval Committee and Internal Review Board (IRB) of Qom University of Medical Science with code IR.MUQ.REC.1404.124.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26 software, and in addition to descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, frequency, and percentage). Before testing the hypotheses, the normality of the data was checked with the Shapiro-Wilk test and the indices of kurtosis and skewness, and the homogeneity of variances was checked with the Levene test. To analyze the relationships and examine the correlation between the variables under study and the determinants of OHQoL, Pearson and Spearman correlation, and multivariate linear regression model with stepwise method were used to examine the relationship between demographic variables and the OHQoL index. The significance level for all tests was considered to be 0.05.

Discussion

The prevalence of OHQoL disorders in all its dimensions was 60% on average, and more than 70% of the students studied had mild or moderate mental health disorders, which indicates that the mental health status and OHQoL in the present study were not appropriate. However, in a similar study in Iran by Khodabakhshi and colleagues in patients aged 20–50 years in Gorgan, they showed a relative level of satisfaction with quality of life, and an inverse effect of the dental caries index on the quality of life of patients (level of individual satisfaction) was obtained [1].

In the present study, a direct and significant correlation was observed between the total OHQoL score and all its dimensions with the total general health score. In other words, when the general health score is impaired, the OHQoL score and its various dimensions are impaired. Another study examined the relationship between poor OHQoL and mental health and showed that anxiety has a negative impact on oral health, and higher levels of anxiety are associated with fewer filled teeth and a higher dental mass index [2]. In addition, in a study by AlJameel et al. in students in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, the negative impact of mental health on quality of life was shown, such that with increasing severity of mental disorder, the oral and dental quality of life status decreases [3].

Like the present study, where problems and limitations of teeth, mouth or prosthesis caused effects on word pronunciation (49.7%), changes in food and diet, and diet interruption (up to 60%), other studies have addressed the effects of oral problems and tooth decay or dental treatments on daily activities. In another study by Mamani et al., daily activities were affected in more than 91% of participants, 35% of whom had pain and 58.5% of whom were dissatisfied with their oral health [4]. In Riyadh, 11.6% of students reported difficulty pronouncing certain words due to oral and dental issues [3]. In a review study by Botelho-Filho et al. in 2024 to assess the impact of dental caries on daily activities, using the Oral Impacts on Daily Performances (OIDP) and Child-OIDP instruments in adolescents, in the Child-OIDP subgroup, up to 66% reported experiencing dental caries and up to 72% reported negative impact on daily activities [5]. Moreover, in the study by Amiri et al. in 8–10-year-old students in Shiraz, 69.8% of children had experienced at least one oral problem affecting their daily activities during the past three months using the aforementioned questionnaire. They reported that eating was the most common problem of children was pain and bleeding from the gums was the most common cause of problems. Significant correlation was observed between the child-OIDP and OHQoL score. In addition, caries index and the quality of life is impaired with increasing caries index [6]. The observed correlation between OHQoL and mental health scores suggests that individuals experiencing greater oral health problems tend to report poorer overall well-being. Oral conditions such as pain, discomfort, impaired chewing, and aesthetic concerns may negatively influence daily functioning, social interactions, and psychological status. In addition, the chronic and multifactorial nature of oral diseases can contribute to stress, anxiety, and reduced self-perceived health. These findings support the concept that oral health is closely interconnected with both physical and mental dimensions of general health.

In a study by Chaudhary, higher levels of anxiety, lower levels of self-esteem, and social support were associated with higher DMFT and OHIP-14 scores, worse periodontal and oral hygiene conditions, and less tooth brushing and visits to the dentist, and the main barriers to using oral health care were psychological and social issues [7]. In a study of dentists living in Hong Kong, dental anxiety may be a significant risk factor for poor dental and periodontal status and was associated with worse OHQoL [8]. In addition, in a similar cross-sectional study by Vasiliou and colleagues on 2412 participants in Toronto, a positive relationship was reported between current stress levels and general oral health and oral pain [9]. Therefore, it seems that people with higher perceived stress will have poorer oral health, which is consistent with the results of the present study. However, in another study in Shahediyeh, Yazd Province, no significant relationship was seen between the DMFT index and the psychological profiles of the participants [2].

The results of multivariate linear regression on predictors of OHQoL showed that DMFT index and general health score (higher), male gender, and non-Iranian nationality were factors affecting the deterioration of OHQoL. In addition, the higher level of father education is a factor in improving OHQoL. In other words, the OHQoL in male, non-Iranian students with poor general health score and DMFT index and poorly educated fathers is not appropriate. The higher education of students’ parents are related to higher health literacy, better socioeconomic status, supportive family, as well as excessive utilization of preventive healthcare. Nevertheless, increased numbers of decayed, missing, and filled teeth may lead to pain, functional limitations, dissatisfaction with appearance, and reduced social confidence, all of which negatively affect perceived oral health quality of life. Moreover, female adolescents probably are more sensitive to oral conditions because of the social and psychological importance of appearance and peer interactions during this developmental period. Therefore, poorer oral health status, as reflected by higher DMFT scores, is expected to be associated with worse OHQoL. In the study of Ghobadi et al., the use of dental floss and mouthwash, socioeconomic status, parental education, and frequency of brushing are the most important factors affecting the tooth decay index [10]. The study of Klarić et al. shows significant oral health problems and reduced quality of life, especially in people with lower socioeconomic status [11]. Moreover, in the logistic regression model of the study by Duangthip et al., children with higher dmft scores were more likely to have poorer OHRQoL. While child gender, parental education level and respondent-to-child ratio were not associated with OHRQoL. Therefore, caries experience is associated with lower OHRQoL in Hong Kong preschool children [12]. In another study by Mamani et al., not using a toothbrush, using chewable foods, absenteeism from school and being rural were risk factors for poor OHQoL [4]. Therefore, implementing various educational and therapeutic interventions to reduce dental caries and increase OHQoL can be helpful especially in higher risk students and families who are in higher risk.

Studies have shown that stress can have a significant impact on oral health, leading to decreased attention to health care and, consequently, an increased risk of dental caries [9, 13]. People who experience high levels of stress are less likely to value their oral health, and high school students who face intense academic and social pressures may be particularly vulnerable to dental problems and reduced quality of life. According to research, high prevalence of dental caries can lead to reduced daily activities and disruption of students’ social lives [14]. According to the results of this study, it is necessary to design and implement targeted educational and clinical interventions including fluoride therapy, fissure sealants, and fluoride varnishes in schools, regular dental checkups, and targeted oral health education is essential to promote oral health in high school students. However, based on the results of the present study and other existing studies, the stress of the entrance exam in high school students has been transferred to students in tenth to twelfth grades and the impact of mental health on the OHQoL and oral hygiene has been proven. Therefore, developing integrated care programs, including regular oral health monitoring, nutritional counseling, and regular referral to a dentist, psychologist, and psychiatrist, and obtaining academic counseling during the high school years can be effective. Furthermore, informing families about the impact of mental health on oral health and the need to take care of them is of great importance.

The study has several strengths, including a relatively large sample size, the use of validated questionnaires (OHIP-14 and GHQ-28), and the investigation of an important but underexplored population group. Moreover, psychosocial and oral health dimensions association was evaluated to increase the public health relevancy. Nevertheless, using self-rated questionnaire was the limitation of this study.

Conclusion

The present study showed that more than 70% of high school students in Qom, Iran suffer from mild or moderate mental health disorders, which can be caused by university entrance exam anxiety. Moreover, the OHQoL in this group is not appropriate, and the prevalence of its disorders is higher than 50%, and a significant positive correlation was observed between poor mental health status and poor OHQoL. Considering the impact of demographic factors along with some socio-economic variables, it is of great importance to pay attention to the mental health of high school students who are experiencing high stress and anxiety due to entrance exams and final exams. Therefore, it is necessary to design therapeutic and educational interventions in adolescents and students, especially high school students, along with the necessary measures to improve their access to medical and preventive care, so that in addition to improving the level of oral health, prevention and control of dental caries led to improving the OHQoL.

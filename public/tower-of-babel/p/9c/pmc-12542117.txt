Pregnancy associated breast cancer: correlation between parity and ultrasonic characteristics of tumors & background echotexture
Authors: Yue Zhang, Linxiaoxi Ma, Na Hu, Cai Chang, Yi Gao, Yaling Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the characteristics of pregnancy associated breast cancer (PABC) and correlation of parity with ultrasonic tumor and background echotexture, based on the analysis of diagnostic efficiency.MethodsThe ultrasonic images of 184 female patients with PABC and 47 female patients with benign lesions, all of whom were pregnant or within one year postpartum at diagnosis and underwent surgery for their breast conditions in our center from January 2016 to December 2023, were retrospectively analyzed. Ultrasound diagnostic performance was assessed by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the ROC curve (AUROC). Breast ultrasound background echotexture was classified according to two criteria: the first classification was homogeneous-fat, homogeneous-fibroglandular, and heterogeneous, the other classification was hypoechoic dominated and hyperechoic dominated. The correlation between parity and ultrasonic characteristics (tumor and background echotexture) was analyzed.ResultsThe AUROC of ultrasound diagnosis for PABC was 0.939 (95%CI: 0.910-0.969). PABC tumors were predominantly irregular in shape, uncircumscribed, hypoechoic, heterogeneous, and solid. Compared with primiparous women, the PABC tumors were larger in multiparous women (4.00± 3.30cm vs. 3.09 ± 2.01cm, P = 0.037), with a higher rate of lymphatic metastasis (57.27% vs. 41.89%, P = 0.041). In terms of background echotexture, heterogeneous echotexture and hypoechoic dominated were more frequently observed in multiparas than in primiparas (52.73% vs. 37.84%, P= 0.047; 49.09% vs. 32.43%, P = 0.025). Multivariate analysis further indicated multiparous women were more likely to have heterogeneous (OR = 2.241, 95% CI:1.032-4.867, P= 0.041) and hypoechoic dominated (OR = 2.064, 95% CI:1.045-4.077, P = 0.037) breast ultrasound backgrounds than primiparous women, adjusted for confounders.ConclusionsUltrasound is effective for diagnosing PABC. Multiparas may show a more heterogeneous and predominantly hypoechoic background echotexture, an increased risk of lymphatic metastasis, and larger tumors. Introduction

Breast cancer is the leading cancer diagnosis among women of reproductive age globally, and a notable association has been identified between pregnancy and breast cancer [1, 2]. Pregnancy associated breast cancer (PABC), which is diagnosed during pregnancy or within 1 year postpartum, is a rare but aggressive subtype affecting 3–10% of young breast cancer patients [3, 4]. PABC occurs during a specific physiological period in women, involving issues such as pregnancy, childbirth, lactation, and fertility preservation. The diagnostic and therapeutic process for PABC necessitates balancing the treatment efficacy for the mother with the safety of the offspring, making it a challenging and critical aspect of clinical management. Additionally, PABC has a higher mortality rate compared to non-pregnancy-associated breast cancer, and early detection and diagnosis may potentially improve patient outcomes [5, 6].

Ultrasonography remains the first method of choice for breast evaluation during pregnancy and lactation due to the potential risks of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and mammography [7, 8]. MRI is generally avoided because of the theoretical risk of magnetic fields on fetal organogenesis and the use of intravenous gadolinium contrast which may pose risks to the fetus. Similarly, mammography is still constrained by the potential risks associated with ionizing radiation exposure. However, given that the radiation dose of mammography is relatively low (typically < 0.1 mSv per examination) and abdominal shielding can be used to minimize fetal radiation exposure, mammography is generally considered safe during pregnancy in clinical practice. In specific circumstances, such as the evaluation of dense breast tissue, mammography also holds significant clinical value. Although ultrasound plays a crucial role and demonstrates good diagnostic performance in the examination of PABC, additional factors still need to be considered during pregnancy and the postpartum period, such as breast enlargement and the presence of dilated milk ducts. During this special period, these unique physiological changes in the breast can lead to a more complex background echotexture of the glandular tissue, which may obscure malignant lesions and further increase the difficulty of ultrasonic diagnosis. Therefore, in addition to exploring the ultrasonic characteristics of tumors, studying the ultrasonic characteristics of the background echotexture also holds significant value.

Parity is generally considered as protective factor against breast cancer. Women who had more live births tended to have a lower risk of developing cancer compared to nulliparous women. However, this protection is deferred, and there is evidence suggesting that the risk of breast cancer may increase shortly after childbirth [9, 10]. Currently, there is a notable dearth of research investigating the relationship between parity and the ultrasonic presentation PABC. In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of ultrasound images and clinical data from a cohort of patients diagnosed with PABC and estimated the diagnostic efficacy of ultrasound. We evaluated the ultrasonic features of both lesions and background echotexture in PABC and explored the correlation with parity. Through this analysis, we aim to achieve an in-depth understanding of the relationship between ultrasonic characteristics, parity, and PABC, and to contribute to the improvement of diagnosis and management of this challenging disease.

Methods

Study population

Data from 184 women (age range 22–46 years, mean 34.24 ± 4.80 years) who were pathologically diagnosed with PABC and 47 women (age range 24–44 years, mean 34.79 ± 5.23 years) who were pathologically diagnosed as benign lesions during pregnancy and lactation at the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2023 were retrospectively identified and analyzed. All pathological results were confirmed by histopathological examination of specimens obtained through breast surgical procedures. The inclusion criteria encompassed female patients who possessed comprehensive ultrasound imaging data prior to surgical intervention and exhibited a detailed medical history, including information of previous pregnancies and live births. The primary reasons for patients’ clinic attendance included self-detected breast lumps (89 cases), nipple discharge (20 cases), axillary lymph node enlargement (19 cases), breast pain (11 cases), skin changes such as orange-peel appearance, redness, nipple retraction, or inversion (8 cases), or abnormal findings on prenatal/postnatal breast examinations (37 cases). Exclusion criteria included patients with incomplete, missing, or inadequate ultrasound imaging data; patients with insufficient or unclear relevant clinical characteristics; and individuals who had undergone puncture procedures (such as fine needle aspiration or core needle biopsy) or neoadjuvant therapy before the ultrasound examination.

Ultrasonic technique and image interpretation

Ultrasonic devices, such as the EPIQ 7 (Philips Healthcare, Andover, MA, USA), Logic E9 (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA), ACUSON S2000 (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) and Aixplorer (Supersonic Imagine, Aix-en-Provence, France), were employed in the study, equipped with a linear array transducer of 10 to 15 MHz. Two sonographers, each with experience of 9 and 18 years respectively in breast ultrasound, retrospectively reviewed the ultrasound images, blinded to both the patients’ clinical findings and each other’s interpretations. A consensus was subsequently reached on the interpretation of tumor features and background echotexture. The tumor features including shape, margin, internal echo pattern, echo distribution, cystic component, posterior echo, calcification, orientation, and vascularity were analyzed. Color Doppler flow imaging was used to detect the blood flow signals around and inside the lesion, graded by Adler semi-quantitative method [11], which categorizes blood flow signals into four grades (0-III) based on the number and distribution of vessels:

Breast ultrasound background echotexture was classified according to two criteria displayed in Figs. 1 and 2. The first classification was categorized in accordance with the fifth edition of the American College of Radiology Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) into three groups: homogeneous-fat, homogeneous-fibroglandular, and heterogeneous [12]. The second classification was divided into two types based on echogenicity (with the subcutaneous fat layer as a reference): hypoechoic dominated and hyperechoic dominated.

The design process rigorously adhered to the principles of the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center, ensuring the confidentiality and privacy of patient data. The requirement of informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of this study.

Statistical analysis

SPSS27.0 statistical analysis software for mac (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY) was used.

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (‾x ± s) or median with a 95% confidence interval (CI), while categorical variables were presented as number of cases and percentages. Univariate analysis of molecular characteristics between primiparas and multiparas was performed using the χ2 test, while a multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the independent association between parity and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, adjusting for potential confounding factors. Ultrasound diagnostic performance was assessed by using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the ROC curve (AUROC). The diagnostic test consistency between ultrasonic suspicious lymph node metastasis and the pathological gold standard was evaluated using the Kappa test (a Kappa value > 0.8 indicates extremely strong agreement, 0.6 to 0.8 indicates strong agreement, 0.4 to 0.6 indicates moderate agreement, and < 0.4 indicates weak agreement). After conducting a normality analysis using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, an independent samples t-test was applied to examine the differences in tumor size between different parity groups. Comparisons between other quantitative parameters were conducted using the χ2 test. Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between parity and background echotexture. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to further examine the significance of parity on background echotexture. A two-tailed P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Pathological information & clinical characteristic

Based on the surgical histopathological results of a total of 184 patients with an age range of 22–46 years (mean 34.24 ± 4.80 years), the tumor types comprised 161 cases (87.50%) of invasive ductal carcinomas; 19 cases (10.33%) of ductal carcinomas in situ; 1 case (0.54%) of solid papillary carcinoma, 1 case (0.54%) of invasive lobular carcinoma, 1 case (0.54%) of invasive micropapillary carcinoma, and 1 case (0.54%) of Paget’s disease of the breast. Of the 184 patients, 53 were during pregnancy, and 131 cases were during lactating. Among the 184 patients, 131 (71.20%) were lactating during the imaging process, and 53 (28.80%) were pregnant. The molecular characteristics of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2, as well as molecular types, were listed in Table 1. The expression rate of HER2 receptor in PABC tumors was significantly higher in multiparous women compared to primiparous women (P = 0.016). After adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, body mass index (BMI), tumor size, lymph node metastasis, pathological stage, ER status, and PR status, multiparous women had a significantly higher odds ratio 2.614 (95% CI: 1.033–6.618, P = 0.043) for HER2-positive expression compared to primiparous women, detailed in Table 2. The histopathological results of the 47 cases (age range 24–44 years, mean 34.79 ± 5.23 years) of benign lesions during pregnancy and lactation were as follows: 24 cases (51.06%) of fibroadenomas, 12 cases (25.53%) of mammary dysplasia, 5 cases (10.64%) of intraductal papillomas, 4 cases (8.51%) of inflammatory lesions, 1 case (2.13%) of hamartoma, and 1 case (2.13%) of lactational change. In the group of 47 patients, 33 (70.21%) were lactating at the time of imaging, whereas 14 (29.79%) were pregnant.

Ultrasound diagnostic efficacy & characteristic features

Based on histopathology as the gold standard, the AUROC of ultrasound diagnosis for PABC was 0.939 (95%CI: 0.910–0.969), with the optimal cutoff point between BI-RADS 4 A ~ 4B, and Fig. 3 presented the corresponding ROC curve. The sensitivity was 96.43%, the specificity was 65.08%, the positive predictive value was 88.04%, the negative predictive value was 87.23%, and the diagnostic accuracy was 87.87%. Among the 184 PABC cases examined, 128 (69.57%) showed as a mass-like pattern, while 56 cases (30.43%) presented with a non-mass-like pattern. Correlation analysis of parity with ultrasonic characteristics

The correlation between parity and ultrasonic characteristics of breast background echotexture and the primary tumors were analyzed and displayed in Table 4. Among the 184 PABC patients (74 primiparous cases, 110 multiparous cases) enrolled, there were no cases of homogeneous-fat background echotexture. Heterogeneous echotexture and hypoechoic dominated pattern were more frequently observed in multiparous group than primiparous group (P = 0.047 and P = 0.025 respectively). Spearman correlation analysis revealed a statistically significant positive correlation between high parity and the likelihood of exhibiting a heterogeneous background echotexture (Spearman’s rho = 0.146, P = 0.047) and a hypoechoic dominated pattern (Spearman’s rho = 0.165, P = 0.025). After adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, BMI, and breastfeeding history, multiparous women were more likely to exhibit a heterogeneous background echotexture (OR = 2.241, 95% CI: 1.032–4.867, P = 0.041) and a hypoechoic dominated pattern (OR = 2.064, 95% CI: 1.045–4.077, P = 0.037) on breast ultrasound compared to primiparous women, displayed in Tables 5 and 6. The Kappa value between ultrasonic suspicious lymph node metastasis and the pathological gold standard was 0.724 (P = 0.000), indicating a strong agreement. In terms of lymph node metastasis, multiparous women demonstrated a significantly higher rate of suspicious lymph node metastasis compared to primiparous women (57.27% vs. 41.89%, P = 0.041). Furthermore, multiparous women displayed significantly larger tumor sizes compared to primiparous women (4.00 ± 3.30 cm vs. 3.09 ± 2.01 cm, P = 0.037).

Discussion

Mounting evidence from researches suggested that reproductive factor of parity may be involved in in the incidence and outcome of numerous carcinomas such as ovarian cancer, thyroid cancer, pancreatic cancer, bladder cancer, primary liver cancer, gallbladder cancer and haematological malignancies [13–19]. Parity is generally acknowledged as a protective factor for breast cancer, it is primarily observed in the peak ages of breast cancer incidence, typically after age 60, but may not necessarily apply to younger women [20]. Multiple characteristics were shared between pregnancy and cancer, encompassing proliferation, invasion, neoangiogenesis, as well as the modulation of both local and systemic immune responses [21]. Meanwhile, studies have indicated that women with a high parity exhibit a distinct gene expression profile characterized by a higher expression of tumor promoter genes, such as CNOT8 and ZRANB2, which are involved in translation regulation and alternative splicing, respectively, and a lower expression of tumor suppressor genes, including FKBPL, which plays a role in cell cycle regulation and angiogenesis inhibition [22]. Furthermore, continued exposure to elevated levels of female sex hormones through multiple pregnancies may serve as a potential mechanism for a higher PABC risk. Currently, there is a lack of research investigating the ultrasonic imaging features of PABC in women with different parity. Our study explored the differences in ultrasonic characteristics of primary PABC tumors and glandular background among women with different parity, aiming to understand the distinct ultrasonic imaging features of PABC in primiparas and multiparas, and to improve the diagnosis of malignant breast tumors in women during pregnancy and lactation procedure.

The pathological results of PABCs in this study showed that there were 161 cases of invasive ductal carcinoma, accounting for 87.50% (161/184), consistent with the previous histological findings that invasive ductal carcinoma is the most common subtype in pregnant women with breast cancer [23]. A large-scale genomic analysis by Anders et al. [24] demonstrated that breast cancer in young women represents a distinct biological entity, driven by a unified oncogenic signaling pathway, characterized by lower hormone sensitivity and higher expression levels of HER-2/EGFR. Among PABCs, multiparas exhibited a significantly higher proportion of HER2-positive status, reaching 57.27%, compared to 39.19% in primiparas (P = 0.016). To more accurately assess the relationship between parity and HER2 expression while controlling for potential confounding factors, we conducted a multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results revealed that, even after adjusting for age, BMI, tumor size, lymph node status, pathological stage, ER status, and PR status, multiparas still had a significantly higher risk of HER2-positive status compared to primiparas (OR = 2.614, 95% CI: 1.033–6.618, P = 0.043). This is consistent with previous literature suggesting that high parity may be associated with an increased risk of HER2-positive breast cancer [25]. Given the established association between HER2-positive status and aggressive breast cancer phenotypes, increased risk of recurrence, and poorer prognostic outcomes, our findings suggested that multiparas may face greater challenges in terms of breast cancer aggressiveness and recurrence risk compared to primiparas. Callihan et al. [26] argued that postpartum involution of breast, as a unique biologic event, may contribute to the poor prognosis of postpartum breast cancer. For multiparas, the cumulative risk associated with repeatedly experiencing postpartum involution may be one of the reasons for their poor prognosis. It should be noted that women with a BMI ≥ 25 exhibited an increased risk of HER2-positive breast cancer (OR = 3.185, 95% CI:1.171–8.666, P = 0.023), while those with advanced pathological stage had a significantly higher likelihood of HER2 overexpression (OR = 74.984, 95% CI: 22.543–249.417.543.417, P = 0.000). These findings suggested that both a high BMI and advanced pathological stage may be associated with an increased risk of HER2-positive breast cancer. Furthermore, while parity may influence the expression of ER and PR, the conclusions from previous studies were inconsistent [27–30]. In our study, no significant correlation was observed between ER status and PR status when comparing multiparous women to primiparous women. This discrepancy may be attributed to limitations in sample size, differences in study populations, or variations in experimental methodologies. Further research is warranted to fully understand the mechanisms underlying these associations and to develop targeted screening and treatment strategies for women with different parity histories. These findings provided initial evidence for a deeper understanding of the molecular differences in breast cancer between women with different reproductive histories.

In our study, AUROC of ultrasound diagnosis for PABC was 0.939 (95%CI: 0.910–0.969), with a sensitivity of 96.43% and an accuracy of 87.87%, suggesting a relatively high diagnostic efficiency for PABC. Previous studies have confirmed the value of ultrasound in detecting malignant lesions among pregnant or lactating patients, with reported sensitivities of 86.6% to 100% and a 100% negative predictive value for PABC [31, 32]. Although ultrasound has demonstrated high sensitivity in the diagnosis of PABC, it is equally important to acknowledge its potential limitations. Specifically, in this study, the specificity of ultrasound was relatively low (65.08%), indicating a risk of false-positive results. This could lead to unnecessary anxiety for patients and potentially invasive procedures, such as biopsies or surgeries. Figure 5 analyzed two cases of false-positive results. In Fig. 5 A and B, the expansion of ducts in the breast tissue due to pregnancy and lactation, combined with the irregular shape of the lesion and the interference of peripheral blood flow signals, led to an ultrasound diagnosis of BI-RADS 4B, suggesting malignancy could not be excluded. However, pathology confirmed an inflammatory lesion. In Fig. 5 C and D, although the mass effect was not prominent, multiple suspicious calcifications were observed, resulting in a similar ultrasound diagnosis of BI-RADS 4B. Pathology confirmed mammary gland disease with lactational changes, where the ultrasound appearance of milk was misdiagnosed as calcifications. In summary, the complex background echoes of breast tissue during pregnancy and lactation may exhibit features on ultrasound that mimic malignant lesions, thereby interfering with diagnosis. Therefore, when interpreting ultrasound findings, it is crucial to consider the patient’s clinical history, physical examination, and other available imaging modalities. Future research should focus on improving the diagnostic accuracy of ultrasound for PABC, possibly by integrating advanced imaging techniques such as ultrasonic elastography or contrast-enhanced ultrasonography, or by developing artificial intelligence-based diagnostic algorithms.

In our study, 69.57% (128/184) of PABC presented as masses in ultrasound, among which the overwhelming majority exhibited irregular shape (90.63%) and uncircumscribed margins (96.88%), indicating a potentially aggressive biological behavior comparable to other malignancies. The hypoechoic (94.53%) and heterogeneous (96.09%) echogenicity further supported this. The absence of a cystic component of 92.19% suggested a relative solid mass, while 40.63% had calcification associated with necrosis or dystrophic calcification. Non-mass lesions (56 cases) were also predominantly hypoechoic (98.21%) with a heterogeneous echo distribution (100.0%). Calcification in 60.71% of these cases may indicate a more fibrotic or calcified tumor composition. Blood flow was detected in 75.78% (97/128) of mass lesions and 69.64% (39/56) of non-mass lesions. Different vascular distribution may reflect the variation of tumor’s angiogenesis that is critical in tumor growth and metastasis. The ultrasonic characteristics of PABC tumors described in this study are consistent with previous literature [33–35]. However, there are currently few studies on other ultrasonic techniques for PABC, such as ultrasonic elastography, contrast-enhanced ultrasonography, and so on. Large-sample multicenter studies on various ultrasonic techniques for PABC and the combined application with artificial intelligence diagnostic systems are awaited, which are bound to provide more detailed information about the tumors, improve the efficiency of ultrasonic diagnosis, and further serve clinical practice.

Compared with primiparas, multiparas exhibited a higher proportion of heterogeneous background echotexture (52.73% vs. 37.84%, P = 0.047). Spearman correlation analysis also revealed a statistically significant positive correlation between parity and the likelihood of displaying a heterogeneous background echotexture (Spearman’s rho = 0.146, P = 0.047). These findings collectively suggested that as the number of childbirths increases, the echotexture of the glandular background tends to become more heterogeneous. To further elucidate the impact of parity on breast ultrasound background echotexture, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed, adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, BMI, and breastfeeding history. The results demonstrated that multiparous women were 2.241 times more likely to present with a heterogeneous background echotexture compared to primiparous women (95% CI: 1.032–4.867, P = 0.041). Physiologically, the lobules of female breasts develop and mature mainly through hyperplasia and differentiation during pregnancy and lactation. In the early stage of pregnancy, the ductal system expands and proliferates with the increase of estrogen and distributes into adipose tissue. The elevation of estrogen level also leads to the reduction of adipose tissue [36]. With the increase of hormone concentration, a large number of lobular ductal units gradually replace a considerable amount of adipose tissue [37]. The relative percentages of adipose tissue and connective tissue in the breasts decrease, while the relative percentages of glands and ductal tissue increase, which continues through the middle and late stages of pregnancy. The estrogen level further increases in the late stage of pregnancy, stimulating the pituitary to secrete prolactin and promoting the differentiation of lactocytes and the synthesis of milk. After delivery, progesterone rapidly decreases while prolactin increases, further promoting the secretion of milk. Whether it is the expansion and proliferation of the ductal system, the process of lobular ductal units replacing adipose tissue, or the production and secretion of milk, all these processes make the entire mammary glands more heterogeneous. Repeated hormonal changes and consequently physiological changes in the breasts during pregnancy and lactation may be one of the reasons why the background echotexture of the breasts in multiparas tended to be more heterogeneous.

With increasing parity, the echotexture of the glandular background appeared to become progressively more hypoechoic dominated (49.09% vs. 32.43%, P = 0.025). The Spearman’s correlation coefficient between high parity and hypoechoic dominated background echotexture is 0.165 (P = 0.025). When exploring the impact of parity on whether the glandular background predominantly exhibits a hyperechoic or hypoechoic pattern, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed, incorporating age, BMI, and breastfeeding history as potential confounding factors. The results indicated that, compared to primiparous women, multiparous women were 2.064 times more likely (95% CI: 1.045–4.077, P = 0.037) to exhibit a hypoechoic dominated pattern. Histologically, the mammary tissue is mainly composed of stroma and epithelial-myoepithelial tissues. The stroma primarily includes fat and fibrous tissue, while the stromal fibrous tissue further categorized into dense interlobular stromal fibrous tissue and loose stromal fibrous tissue surrounding the ducts and located within the lobules. The epithelial cells and myoepithelial cells constitute the ducts and alveolar glands. It is widely acknowledged that the difference in acoustic impedance between various tissues in the human body is the basis for ultrasound imaging, and different tissues have significantly different echo characteristics. In comparison, breast epithelial-myoepithelial tissues typically exhibit a relatively hypoechoic pattern, whereas stromal tissues display a relatively hyperechoic or isoechoic appearance. Yaghjyan, L. et al. [38] found that compared with parous women, women who had not given birth or had fewer children had lower epithelial tissue and higher stromal tissue percentage. This is consistent with the conclusion of this study and may be one of the reasons why the mammary gland background echotexture is more inclined to be hypoechoic dominated with the increase of parity. It’s worth mentioning that Zhang et al. [39] found that the heterogeneous and hypoechoic-dominated background echotextures may decrease the diagnostic efficiency of ultrasound in PABC. As parity increases, the glandular background becomes more heterogeneous and hypoechoic, which may lead to greater difficulties in diagnosis. This requires further research with a larger sample size.

Lymphatic metastasis in ultrasound was detected in 57.27% of multiparas, significantly higher than the 41.89% in primiparas (P = 0.041), indicating higher probability of recurrence or metastasis in multiparous women. There was study revealed that breast cancer patients diagnosed within the first five years following pregnancy exhibited a 2.8-fold increase in the risk of metastasis and a 2.7-fold increase in the risk of mortality compared to nulliparous cases [26]. The average tumor size was significantly larger in multiparas than primiparas (4.00 ± 3.30 cm vs. 3.09 ± 2.01 cm, P = 0.037). In general, PABC in multiparous women tended to show as larger tumor with higher lymphatic metastasis, which may associate with poorer prognosis.

The observation that PABC in multiparous women tended to be more aggressive, with larger tumor sizes and higher rates of lymphatic metastasis, had significant clinical implications. Firstly, it underscores the importance of early and vigilant screening for breast cancer in multiparous women during pregnancy and lactation. This may involve more frequent clinical breast examinations, possibly supplemented by additional imaging modalities if necessary. Secondly, the higher risk of lymphatic metastasis suggests that more aggressive treatment strategies, such as adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, may be warranted in multiparous women diagnosed with PABC. Further prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and guide the development of tailored screening and treatment protocols for this high-risk population.

This study innovatively analyzed the features of breast ultrasound background echotexture in a large sample size of PABC cases, rather than focusing solely on the ultrasonic features of the primary tumors, highlighting the diagnostic value of breast background functioned as a backdrop for tumors. The analysis of the correlation between parity and background echotexture as well as tumor characteristics helped to understand the ultrasonic images of PABC in women with different fertility histories. This study had certain limitations. Firstly, it was a retrospective analysis conducted solely by one institution, potentially introducing a selection bias. Secondly, due to the partial absence of parity information in benign cases, the sample size was insufficient to support a comparison of diagnostic performance across different parity levels. Thirdly, this study is the inability to subgroup patients into pregnancy and lactation categories due to the limited sample size and uneven case distribution (131/184 [71.20%] lactating vs. 53/184 [28.80%] pregnant in malignant cases; 33/47 [70.21%] lactating vs. 14/47 [29.79%] pregnant in benign cases). This imbalance constrained our ability to perform statistically meaningful subgroup analyses, despite the known influence of lactation on breast ultrasound findings, particularly in the comparison of background echotexture between primiparas and multiparas. Future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to address this limitation.

Conclusion

Ultrasound has an excellent diagnostic performance in assessing PABC. The glandular background of multiparas tended to be more heterogeneous and hypoechoic dominated. Additionally, multiparas had larger primary tumors and more lymph node metastases, indicating a poorer prognosis. The exploration of ultrasound characteristics of PABC among women with different parity may contribute to the development of diagnostic tools for predicting and screening individuals at risk of developing breast cancer during pregnancy and lactation, especially among those with diverse reproductive histories.

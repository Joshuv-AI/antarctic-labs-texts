Molecular stratification of prostate cancer through sensory perception-related multi-omics analysis reveals chemoresistant mechanisms
Authors: Shi Li, Ye Tian, Yu Sun, Tian Xu, Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdvanced prostate cancer (PCa) displays significant genetic heterogeneity and therapy resistance, yet the role of sensory perception pathways in its progression remains unclear.MethodsWe performed an integrative multi-omics analysis of sensory perception–linked mRNAs and lncRNAs from TCGA and scRNA-seq data. Unsupervised consensus clustering defined three molecular subtypes (CS1–CS3). Key biomarkers were validated in patient tissues and serum. Immune and stromal infiltration were quantified using TIDE and ESTIMATE. Single-cell trajectories characterized TSC22D3-positive T cells, and NicheNet mapped ligand–receptor interactions.ResultsThree subtypes emerged, with CS1 showing the poorest prognosis, marked chemotherapy resistance, and pronounced stromal–immune crosstalk. CS1 tumors exhibited elevated B- and T-cell infiltration and increased oxidative phosphorylation in TSC22D3-positive T cells. NicheNet analysis identified the TNF–CCL20 axis as a central mediator of immunosuppressive signaling and chemoresistance in CS1.ConclusionsThis study establishes sensory perception–associated molecular subtypes in PCa and links CS1 chemoresistance to immune microenvironment reprogramming via TNF–CCL20 signaling. These findings offer mechanistic insights into PCa progression and suggest actionable targets to overcome therapeutic resistance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-025-01099-w.Keywords: Prostate cancer, Multi-omics, Molecular subtypes, Sensory perception genes, Chemoresistance, Tumor microenvironment

Prostate cancer (PCa) affects an estimated 1.4 million men and causes roughly 375 000 deaths each year [1], imposing substantial economic and psychosocial burdens. Radical prostatectomy (RP) remains the standard for localized PCa, significantly improving overall survival (OS) and biochemical recurrence–free survival (BCR-FS) [2], yet about 40% of patients experience biochemical recurrence (BCR) post-RP [3, 4]. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) delays progression in advanced disease but leads to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) in approximately 20% of cases within five years [5, 6]. CRPC—tumor growth despite castrate-level androgens—carries poor prognosis and few treatment options [7]. Although radiation, immunotherapy, and neoadjuvant strategies have been explored, responses vary with genetic heterogeneity [8, 9]. In recent years, the role of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in cancer progression and immunotherapy resistance has garnered increasing attention [10]. Some studies elucidate the role of the TME in immunotherapy response from different perspectives (DDR [11, 12], PTM [13, 14], and multi-omics machine learning [15, 16]). Their core findings are not only applicable to the original cancer types studied but also provide novel insights for immunotherapy-resistant cancers like prostate cancer. Future research could further refine predictive models by incorporating prostate cancer-specific TME features to advance precision immunotherapy [17–19]. Molecular subtyping can refine prognosis, predict therapy response, and elucidate tumor–immune crosstalk. Notably, sensory perception–related genes regulate proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis, suggesting their promise as PCa biomarkers or targets [20, 21]. Sensory perception pathways, involving neuropeptides and neurotransmitters, influence tumor growth, metastasis, and pain in multiple cancers [22, 23]. Li et al. reported that breast cancer bone metastasis model rats showed thermal hyperalgesia and mechanical allodynia 14–21 days after the inoculation of cancer cells, and the expression and current density of TRPV1 increased in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, indicating TRPV1 plays a crucial role in the sensory perception pathway [24]. One important characteristic of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is perineural invasion, which is modulated by autonomic and sensory innervations, and the study of Wang et al. reported that in the tumor microenvironment of PDAC, nociceptor neurons interacted with cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) through calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) and nerve growth factor (NGF), which further inhibits the function of NK cells and therefore promotes the progression of PDAC [25]. In breast cancer, it has been reported that agonists of TRPV1 showed anti-metastasis effect through the activation of neuro-immune pathways [26]. These pathways are also substantiated in prostate cancer of having the function of interacting with androgen receptor signaling and neuroendocrine differentiation, contributing to aggressive phenotypes [27–29]. Understanding these mechanisms could reveal novel therapeutic strategies, particularly for treatment-resistant PCa. As is mentioned before, sensory perception pathway plays a significant role in tumor progression, and the main facets of influence have been substantiated in various tumors including prostate cancer, which makes comparative analyses across malignancies essential to elucidate conserved neurobiological mechanisms. Integrative bioinformatics approaches will be instrumental in delineating sensory perception–related pathways in tumors, thereby advancing our understanding of their functional relevance and therapeutic potential.

We integrated TCGA bulk transcriptomes and scRNA-seq profiles, focusing on 32 sensory perception–associated mRNAs and 11 lncRNAs. Unsupervised consensus clustering defined three subtypes (CS1–CS3) (Fig. 1A, Table S1), with silhouette analysis confirming clear separation (Fig. 1B). Immune and stromal infiltration were quantified via TIDE and ESTIMATE algorithms [30]. Differentially expressed genes (fold change > 2, adj. P < 0.05) were identified per subtype (top 100) and subjected to GSEA. Chemosensitivity (IC₅₀ for gemcitabine, paclitaxel, sorafenib) was inferred from pharmacogenomic datasets. Key biomarkers were validated by qPCR, western blot in patient tissues, and serum ELISA. Single-cell clustering characterized immune subpopulations; trajectory analysis identified TSC22D3-positive T cells. Ligand–receptor interactions were mapped using NicheNet and CellChat. Consensus clustering yielded three robust subtypes (CS1–CS3). CS1 had the highest TIDE score—indicating immunotherapy insensitivity—and the poorest survival (Figs. 1C–D).

Heatmaps of the top 100 upregulated genes per subtype revealed distinct distributions (Fig. 2A). GSEA showed CS1 enrichment in B-cell, T-cell, and leukocyte functions (Fig. 2B). Clinical response data indicated that cases with progressive or stable disease clustered predominantly in CS1 and CS2 (Fig. 2C). CS1 uniquely enriched chemokine response, lymphocyte migration, and chemotaxis pathways (Fig. 2D). IC₅₀ comparisons confirmed CS1’s lowest chemosensitivity (Figs. 2E–G). qPCR and serum assays validated CS1-associated markers were significantly increased in patients with chemotherapy resistance (Figs. 2H, I). Clinical information of related patients was shown in Table S2. We obtained a total of 264,276 high-quality single cells spanning 12 major cell lineages; CS1-associated molecular markers were predominantly enriched in T and B populations. Within T cells, 11 subgroups emerged (Figure S2A). The c02 subgroup—marked by TSC22D3—exhibited the highest sensory gene expression (Figures S2B–C). Differential expression analysis in c02 revealed upregulation of STMN1 and TUBB, and downregulation of TPT1 and RPS27 (Figures S2D–E). KEGG and GO analyses linked c02 to oxidative phosphorylation and ATP synthesis (Figure S3). NicheNet identified TNF as the top ligand from c02 cells (Figure S4A). Receptor activity analysis in malignant epithelial cells highlighted CMTM2, WISP2, LAMA2, LAMB2, and TNF (Figure S4B). CellChat predicted TNF interactions with CCL20, CD38, CD83, COMT, CP, CSF1, and CXCL3 (Figure S4C).

By integrating mRNA and lncRNA multi-omics data focused on sensory perception [31], we defined three prognostic PCa subtypes. CS1 exhibited the highest biochemical recurrence risk, chemo-resistance, and pronounced stromal–immune crosstalk, validated by tissue and serum assays. Elevated TIDE and ESTIMATE scores in CS1 indicate diminished immunotherapy responsiveness, consistent with enrichment of B-cell, T-cell, and leukocyte functions. TSC22D3, an immune transcription factor with prognostic value in AML, modulates NF-κB/NLRP3 signaling and PD-L1 expression to facilitate immune evasion [32, 33]. The TNF–CCL20 axis—implicated in autoimmune hepatitis [31] and breast cancer chemoresistance [34, 35]—emerged as a central mediator of CS1’s immunosuppressive, chemoresistant niche. Future investigations employing spatial multi-omics and functional organoid models could help validate the spatiotemporal hierarchy of sensory perception signaling and determine whether disruption of the TSC22D3–TNF–CCL20 circuit reverses microenvironment-driven resistance. Current limitations include the lack of experimental validation for key ligand–receptor pairs and the need for confirmation across additional model systems. Further studies focusing on the core genes within the TSC22D3–TNF-CCL20 axis, utilizing gene editing and in vivo models, may clarify their functional roles in prostate cancer resistance, particularly the contribution of TNF-CCL20 signaling. Additionally, the development of diagnostic tools targeting the CS1 subtype or pathway-specific therapeutics warrants exploration.

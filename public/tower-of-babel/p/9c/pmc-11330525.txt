Comparative analysis of whole chloroplast genomes of three common species of Nekemias from vine tea
Authors: Feng Yang, Shaoxiong Wu, Zhengwen Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractNekemias grossedentata (N. grossedentata) is a medicinal and edible plant. The young leaves and tender stems are specifically utilized to manufacture vine tea, which is traditionally employed in the treatment of conditions such as the common cold fever, sore throat, jaundice hepatitis, and other ailments. The morphologically of N. grossedentata similar to Nekemias cantoniensis (N. cantoniensis) and Nekemias megalophylla (N. megalophylla), which lead to a chaotic market supply. Numerious studies have confirmed that chloroplast genomes and chromatography play important role in plant classification. Here, the whole chloroplast (cp) genomes of the three Nekemias species were sequenced in Illumina sequencing platform. Meanwhile, their chromatographic fingerprints have constructed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The annotation results demonstrated that the three chloroplast genomes were typical quadripartite structures, with lengths of 162,147 bp (N. grossedentata), 161,981 bp (N. megalophylla), and 162,500 bp (N. cantoniensis), respectively. A total of 89 (N. grossedentata) /86 (N. megalophylla and N. cantoniensis) protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA gene and 8 rRNA genes were annotated. The IR/SC boundary regions were relatively conserved across the three species, although three regions (rps19-rpl2, rpl32-trnL-UAG, ccsA-ndhD) exhibited nucleotide diversity values (Pi) of variable sites higher than 1%. Phylogenetic analysis indicated that N. grossedentata had a closer genetic relationship with N. megalophylla than that of N. cantoniensis. Moreover, the chromatographic fingerprints revealed that the main functional components and genetic relatedness of three species were highly similar with their morphological results. In conclusion, N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla can be consider as the origin plants of vine tea. This study provides appropriate information for species identification, phylogeny, quality assessment of three medicinal plants of the genus Nekemias and will contribute to the standardization of vine tea raw materials.Keywords: N. grossedentata, Complete chloroplast genome, Phylogenetic tree, Chromatographic fingerprintSubject terms: Genetics, Molecular biology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Nekemias grossedentata is a common species in the Vitaceae family, and is a traditional medicinal and edible plant rich in bioactive compounds, particularly dihydromyricetin, with a content of more than 35%1. The Ampelopsis A. Rich ex Mich genus contains around 30 plant species worldwide, distributed in Asia, North America, and Central America, with 17 of these species currently identified in China2. Some species have gained attention due to their medicinal value, particularly as ingredients in vine tea3, which has been used for centuries to treat cold fever, sore throat, jaundice hepatitis, among others4–8. However, the morphologically and functions of N. grossedentata similar to N. cantoniensis and N. megalophylla and contain many of the same functional compounds such as dihydromyricetin, myricetin, and others9, which lead to a chaotic market supply10. Previous studies have primarily focused on morphology, chemical composition, and pharmacological activity11. However, relying solely on morphological characteristics for species identification has led to inaccurate interspecific identification of Nekemias species within the genus12.

In 1986, the tobacco cp genome was sequenced and annotated, chloroplasts (cp) possess an independent, circular genome that plays a vital role in the photosynthesis, development, and physiology of green plants13. In recent years, the complete cp genome data of genera such as Musa, have been employed to identify highly differentiated regions and to infer phylogenetics, ultimately providing a reference for the identification and phylogenetic studies of these species14. Therefore, in addition to morphological and chemical differences among species, chloroplast (cp) DNA sequences are also of significant interest in separation identification studies. Generally, the cp genome has a quadripartite structure (120–160 kb) and contains 110–130 unique genes. In most angiosperms, the typical quadripartite cyclic structure comprises a large single-copy (LSC) region and a small single-copy (SSC) region separated by a pair of inverted repeats (IRA and IRB)14. Due to their highly conserved structure, general recombination-free, uniparental inheritance, and small effective population sizes15, chloroplast (cp) DNA sequences have been extensively utilized to resolve plant phylogenies16. The advent of next-generation sequencing has made the process cheaper and faster than before17. Consequently, phylogenetic analyses based on cp genome data are increasingly prevalent, even among small taxonomic populations, due to advances in next-generation sequencing technologies18,19.Despite several species of Nekemias cp genomes being sequenced, the chloroplast genomes of the three plants used as raw materials for vine tea are poorly studied.

In previous studies, there was a lack of comprehensive phylogenetic comparison and examination of chemical composition differences among the three species. Given this, we sequenced and annotated the whole cp genome of N. grossedentata, N. grossedentata, and N. grossedentata to explore the relationships among the three Nekemias plants. Subsequently, we analyzed the phylogenetic relationships of 33 Vitaceae species from GenBank. Finally, the chemical composition differences among the three target plants were compared using HPLC fingerprinting. Our research provides valuable information for species identification, phylogeny, quality assessment, and rational use of the three medicinal plants within the genus Nekemias, which will ultimately facilitate the standardization of raw material supply and optimization of vine tea products, solve the problem of market supply chaos to provide a new reference.

Results

Cp genome organizations

The chloroplast genome information of the three Nekemias species compared in this section has been uploaded to the NCBI database, and the GenBank accession numbers are shown in Table 1. As in other flowering plants, the chloroplast genome of Nekemias is a typical quadripartite structure which contains two inverted repeats (IRs), a large copy region (LSC) and a small copy region (SSC). As shown in Fig. 1 and Table 1, the chloroplast genome length of the three species was between 161,981 bp and 162,500 bp, while the whole genome length of the chloroplast of the N. grossedentata had an intermediate length 162,147 bp, and the maximum length difference among the three species was only 519 bp.

By comparing the GC content of the three species in Tables 1 and 2, we found that the GC ratio of the IR region is greater than that of the other two independent regions. This is because the eight ribosomal RNAs (rRNAs) of the chloroplast genome are located in the IR region of the two inverted repeats, and ribosomal RNA is rich in GC bases, which causes this result20. In addition, the AT content of the three targeted plant protein-coding regions (CDS regions) was greater than their GC content, which was consistent with other land plants21,22. We mapped and annotated the chloroplast genomes of these three species. Combined with Table 2, we can find that although the chloroplast genome of N. grossedentata has an intermediate length, it has 134 whole genes, 114 unique gene and 89 protein coding genes, 3 more protein coding genes and a unique gene—ycf15, compared to N. megalophylla and N. cantoniensis. It may be used as a molecular marker to identify Nekemias species in the supply of vine tea products. In addition, the three Nekemias species showed a high degree of similarity in 37 tRNAs and 8 rRNAs, but the length of the CDS region was different. The CDS region of N. grossedentata was more than 1500 bp longer than the other two species, but the the total GC content of the CDS region was not significantly different, both were about 37.36%. The difference of nucleotide composition at each site was also small. The GC content of the first, second, and third codon positions in N. grossedentata was 45.57%, 38.19% and 30.19%, respectively. The GC content of the first, second, and third codon positions in N. cantoniensis was 45.73%, 38.28% and 30.02%, respectively. The GC content of the first, second, and third codon positions in N. megalophylla was 45.71%, 38.28% and 30.04%, respectively. Although the difference between species is small, we can still find that the GC content of the third codon is low. According to the literatures22,24, it can be found that the rule of AT base preference of the third codon is also reflected in other angiosperms.

There are 89 protein-coding genes in the chloroplast genome of N. grossedentata. It had a total of 27,007 codons, while the 86 protein-coding genes of N. cantoniensis and N. megalophylla had a total of 26,487 and 26,482 codons, respectively. Among all the codons in the protein coding region, the codon responsible for isoleucine (Ile), AUU (1125/1103/1103), was the most frequently used in all three species, while the codon responsible for cysteine (Cys), UGC (85/80/81), was the least used in all three species. As shown in Fig. 2, the relative synonymous codon usage frequencies (RSCU) in the protein-coding regions of the chloroplast genomes of the three species are slightly different but generally similar. All amino acids except methionine (Met) and threonine (Thr) had multiple paired codons, up to six of them, including arginine, leucine and serine. Most amino acids have two paired codons, and the single preference is obvious, but in multi-codon amino acids, there are several preferential codons at the same time (for example, three codons in arginine have RSCU > 1).

In the analysis of repeat sequences, it can be found from Fig. 3a that the number of forward repeat and palindromic repeat sequences of the three Nekemias species is 49, but the number of tandem repeat sequences of N. grossedentata (72) is more than that of the other two species (69/70). Many of forward and palindromic repeats were found in the IR region of the tetrad structure. There were 6 forward and palindromic repeats from the intronic region which starting from the IR region to the rpl23 spacer. However, most of the tandem repeats were located in the LSC region, and the longest segment of all the tandem repeats was 79 bp, which was located in the intronic region between rps19 and rpl2 genes in the IR region of all three species. The analysis of the chloroplast genomes of three Nekemias species showed that the types and numbers of repeat sequences of the three species were similar, and the length of each repeat sequence was slightly different. The length of tandem repeat sequences of each species was generally concentrated in the range of 10–30 bp, while the length of forward and palindromic sequences was not significantly different and relatively scattered. However, N. cantoniensis had no repeat sequences larger than 65 bp.

According to the parameter setting, 217 simple repeats were observed in the chloroplast genome of N. grossedentata, including 140 mono-nucleotide repeats, 58 di-nucleotide repeats, 6 tri-nucleotide repeats and 10 tetra-nucleotide repeats, 2 penta- and 1 hexa-, respectively. The number and type of simple repeat sequences of N. megalophylla were very similar to that of N. grossedentata, with only one additional mono-typic sequence, but N. cantoniensis showed a slight quantitative difference. Single nucleotide repeats (SNPS) were the main simple repeat type in all three species, and A/T type SNPS were the dominant type. The number of oligonucleotide repeat of three or more was small but the types were very rich.

IR expansion and contraction

Expansion and contraction of IR region boundaries is the main reason for the size changes of the cp genome and play an important role in species evolution25. We compare the IR/SSC and IR/LSC junction areas of three species to determine whether they have expansion or contraction. It can be seen from.

Figure 5 that the rpl22 gene of the N. grossedentata is always located in the LSC region at the JLB boundary, while the rpl22 gene of the other two species are extended to the IRb region. The ycf1 gene was identical in all three species. This gene is divided into two parts and spans three regions (IRa-SSC-IRb). As the gene with the longest sequence composition, the function of ycf1 is still unknown26, but some studies have shown that this gene family is indispensable in plants. Besides, there was no significant differences between the chloroplast genomes of N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla, while there was a certain shift in the gene loci of N. cantoniensis, indicating evolutionary expansion at their boundaries.

Sequence divergence analysis

In Fig. 6, the three complete cp genomes were compared with mVISTA using N. grossedentata as a reference. We can first find that the similarity in the non-coding sequence(CNS) is relatively low, such as the region between trnR and atpA, rps19 and rpl2, psbM and trnD, which indicates that the substitution rate of non-coding regions is faster than that of coding regions27. It also indicated that the coding region of closely related species is highly conserved and the rate of variation is extremely low. It is worth noting that the alignment results of the three sequences show that the similarity of them is generally greater than 90%, but the coding region of ycf1 gene has a slight degree of divergence. Although the function of this gene is unknown, gene knockout studies have proved that this gene is an essential gene for plant cell survival 28. In order to clarify the variation in the higher regions, the nucleotide diversity values (PI) were calculated using the DnaSP v.6.10 software (Fig. 7). The variation in intergenic regions ranged from 0 to 9.59%, with an average of 0.39%, which was sixfold higher than that in the CDS regions (0.06% on average). Three divergent loci in intergenic regions (rps19-rpl2、rpl32-trnL-UAG、ccsA-ndhD) had a Pi exceeding 1%, while no gene in the CDS region had a PI value greater than 1%.

Phylogenetic analysis

The relatively short length of cp or nuclear gene sequence fragments limits phylogenetics, resulting in phylogenetic trees with low support values. The complete cp genome is a powerful means for explaining phylogenetic relationships among species due to its rich phylogenetic information. It is widely used in the phylogenetic studies of angiosperms.

Therefore, a phylogenetic tree was built based on 33 species of Vitaceae complete cp genomes (18 Vitis species, 2 species from Tetrastigma, 4 species from Ampelopsis, 7 species from Nekemias), using the maximum likelihood (ML) method, with two outgroups. Most branches have a bootstrap value of 100%, and in the ML tree, all three mentioned species (marked in red). The phylogenetic tree generated a total of 31 branches with most branch support greater than 90% (Fig. 8). Among all evolutionary nodes, a total of 22 branches have node values greater than 99%. N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla are sister groups are clustered together in one terminal branch, thereby representing a similar genetic relationship. These results indicated that the three target plants were not only closely related to each other, but also had a certain genetic distance from the other Vitaceae plants.

The scale bar indicates evolutionary distance between the species and the species in the same branch. The position of the right line indicates the clustered species. The red words marks the species clustered with the target species.

HPLC analysis of the biosynthesis related compounds

In terms of chemical constituents, HPLC fingerprints of the three species were obtained at 292nm wavelength by comparing and analyzing common functional constituents and superimposed on the control. Through the analysis and comparison of Fig. 9, It can be seen that the active components of the three species are almost the same, but the content is different (Table 3). Particularly the content of main Q‑Marker, dihydromyricetin. The dihydromyricetin content in N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla is significantly higher than that in N. cantoniensis. Both taxifolin and myricetin were present in the three species, but the content of taxifolin was generally low. The overall results showed that the compound composition of N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla were very similar, and dihydromyricetin was their main functional compound. The compound composition of N. cantoniensis were not similar to others, myricitrin and myricetin were its main functional compound. This result was also consistent with the results of other contents in this experiment, and complementary to verify the close correlation between plant functional components and genetic evolution, which not only improved the information of biochemical components of species, but also further improved the identification content of Nekemias species.

Morphological comparison analysis of three Nekemias species

The analysis and comparison presented in Fig. 10 indicate that all three Nekemias species exhibit pinnate compound leaves and elongated elliptical leaves with serrated margins. However, N. grossedentata is characterized by more pronounced rough serrations, 2 bifurcated tendrils and 7 leaflets, whereas N. megalophylla has trifurcate tendrils and 5 leaflets. N. cantoniensis exhibits characteristics including tendrils with 2 fork branches and 5 leaflets. And it possesses comparatively larger leaves, displaying a dark green coloration on the upper surface and a light yellowish-brown green on the underside. The result indicated morphological differences among the three Nekemias species, but N. grossedentata demonstrating a closer resemblance to N. megalophylla.

Materials and methods

Plant material and DNA extraction

The materials and genetic information of the three species have been uploaded to the public database of NCBI (The accession numbers of the 3 species are shown in Table 1). The three species, young and healthy leaf, were collected from Dashuijing Village, Devo Town, Anlong Ciuntry, Guizhou Province, China (25°22′53.06″N,105°38′17.70″E,1141m above sea level). The total genomic DNA of three species was extracted using DNAsecure Plant Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China), while the total amount of 700 ng DNA per sample was used as input material for the DNA sample preparations (Data uploaded by our laboratory)29. Sequencing libraries were generated using NEB NextVRUltra DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, Ipswich, MA). Total DNA was used to generate libraries with an average insert size of 350 bp. The library preparations were sequenced on an Illumina platform and 150 bp pairedend reads were generated.

Assembly and annotation

The filtered reads were assembled using the program GetOrganelle30. The initial reference genome of N. grossedentata was used for correcting the positions and directions of the four cp regions (LSC/IRa/SSC/IRb). The genomes were annotated with manual correction by the online software GeSeq31. The circular maps of the three species were obtained using Chloroplot32. Codon usage and cp genomic characteristics were analyzed using MEGA7.033. The annotated genome sequence was submitted to GenBank.( GenBank accession number: MT267294.1, NC-068499, ON616406).

Characterization of repeat sequences and SSRs

Repeat sequences containing the tandem, forward and palindromic. They were identified using REPuter 34 with a Hamming distance set at 3 bp and a minimum repeat size set at 30 bp. Tandem repeats were analyzed using the Tandem Repeats Finder with default parameters35. SSRs were detected using MISA36 with the following thresholds: eight repeat units for mono SSRs, four repeat units for di- and trinucleotide repeat SSRs, and three repeat units for tetra-, penta-, and hexanucleotide repeat SSRs.

Genome comparison

The mVISTA in the Shuffle-LAGAN mode37 was used to compare the cp genomes of N. grossedentata with the cp genomes of the other two reported species cp genomes by using the annotation of N. grossedentata as the reference. The nucleotide variability was calculated with DnaSP 6.1038. The online software IRSCOPE39 (https://irscope.shinyapps.io/irapp/) was used to carry out boundary analysis on the IR regions of the three species.

Phylogenetic analysis

A total of 33 complete cp genome sequences were downloaded from the NCBI GenBank for the phylogenetic analysis. The cp genomes were aligned by using MAFFT v7.520 software40,41, while MEGA 7.0 was used to construct the phylogenetic tree with the maximum-likelihood (ML) method. The ML tree based on TN + gamma + I model was performed using RAxML (Version 8.0.19) with 1000 bootstrap replicates42, while two different species were set as the outgroups.

HPLC analysis of the biosynthesis related compounds

An ultrasonic method to extract total compounds from leaves was used. Approximately 0.2 g of oven-dried leaf tissue was weighed per sample and placed into a centrifuge tube, to which 10 mL of methanol extract was added. After shaking evenly, filter to obtain the test solution. The contents of compounds were measured by high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), which used methanol as solvent A (0.1% phosphoric acid as solvent B) 0 min, 26:74, 10 min, 50:50, 15 min, 55:45, 25–30 min, 26:74. The column temperature was 40 °C, the sample volume injected was 5.0 µL, and the flow rate was 1 mL/min. The detection wavelength was set at 292, 290, 254 and 375 nm.

Discussion

In this study, we utilized high-throughput sequencing technology to obtain the complete chloroplast genomes of three Nekemias species used as raw materials for vine tea. We conducted comprehensive comparison and analysis of the obtained data through data mining and phylogenetic analysis. Firstly, we demonstrated the close relationship between the three Nekemias species by analyzing their chloroplast genome characteristics, including sequence length, gene number, gene map, base composition, coding region characteristics, and codon usage bias. This type of comparison is a rare finding in previous research. Secondly, using chloroplast genome repeat sequences and SSRs characteristics, we further verified the genetic relationship between the three Nekemias species used as raw materials for vine tea, and drew a preliminary conclusion that the genetic relationship between N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla was closer than that between N. cantoniensis and N. grossedentata. SSRs are commonly used as genetic markers in community genetics and evolutionary studies43, and mononucleotide repeats were the most enriched, which is consistent with our findings. We used the complete chloroplast genome sequence characteristics, including the IR boundary characteristics and the complete genome sequence homology comparison of mVISTA, to verify the conclusion that N. megalophylla and N. cantoniensis are both related species of N. grossedentata, and N. megalophylla is more closely related to N. grossedentata with more similar composition. We calculated the nucleotide diversity values (Pi) to clarify the variation in the higher regions and identified three hotspot regions of divergence, which have important implications for the development of molecular markers. Additionally, we constructed a plausible phylogenetic tree of the Vitaceae using the chloroplast genomes of 33 species, which confirmed the phylogenetic relationships of the target plants. Finally, we compared the chemical composition of the three species and concluded, after qualitative analysis, chromatographic fingerprint analysis is an effective supplementary method to identify three Nekemias species.

Although previous research has involved comparisons of the complete cp genomes of the Nekemias species, it has not focused on the three easily confusable Nekemias plant sources of vine tea products, nor has it combined them with an analysis of their primary functional component, dihydromyricetin44. Chromatographic fingerprint analysis is a rational method for identifying and assessing the quality of traditional Chinese herbal medicines45. It can effectively authenticate, evaluate, and distinguish the merits and demerits of these medicines, and increase the accuracy of species identification. Therefore, we performed qualitative research by conducting chromatographic analysis on samples with the same concentration. We found that the content of dihydromyricetin, the main functional component, was not significantly different between the two species, N. grossedentata (24.2526 ± 0.0671%) and N. megalophylla (18.0071 ± 0.0048%). Dihydromyricetin has various medicinal properties46–48, such as anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antioxidant effects. As N. megalophylla has higher dihydromyricetin content, it may be a better genetic line for superior breeding and a better source species for vine tea. These findings correspond with morphological studies. Furthermore, N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla can be consider as the origin plants of vine tea. These findings can not only contribute to the current identification of Nekemias species, but also to future studies on the mechanisms of dihydromyricetin synthesis in these species. The HPLC fingerprints of three Nekemias species increased the accuracy of species identification. However, this study solely compared the complete cp genomes and high-performance liquid chromatography profiles of three easiest confusable Nekemias plant sources of vine tea products, without their rare and unique substances remains. To enhance the quality evaluation of vine tea products and provide a more comprehensive and reliable basis for understanding the phylogenetic relationships within Nekemias, future research could employ plant metabolomics to analyze the differences of micro flavone among the three species.

Conclusion

The comparison of the whole cp genomes of three Nekemias species used as raw materials for vine tea is reported for the first time. Through the data obtained from the NCBI public database, we confirmed that the gene sequence and genome structure of the N. grossedentata are similar to the other two species , but there are still traces of independent evolution and distinct regions of divergence. Simple sequence repeats (SSRs) have a high frequency of variation within the same species, so they are often used as a genetic map construction, correction and mapping study49. We identified 217 SSR loci that could potentially be used as molecular markers to study the diversity of the genus Nekemias. Three different mutation hotspots (rps19-rpl2、rpl32-trnL-UAG、ccsA-ndhD) with high nucleotide variability (Pi) could be considered as potential DNA barcodes of N. grossedentata. They should be applied to the development of molecular markers for phylogenetic and phylogeographic analyses, as well as identification of Nekemias species. Finally, according to relevant studiesa50, the phylogenetic relationship of closely related species is often judged by the molecular evolution rate of their non-coding regions, so the results can really provide a theoretical basis for phylogenetic analysis and species identification. In our phylogenetic tree, eight plants of genus Nekemias converge into two strong clades, and three species are closely related to N. grossedentata. The HPLC fingerprints of four Nekemias species increased the accuracy of species identification. It is noteworthy that the content of dihydromyricetin is not lower in N. megalophylla than in N. grossedentata, which can be considered as a raw material for this compound. Therefore, it is important to explore the molecular mechanism of high dihydromyricetin in some Nekemias species. These findings correspond with morphological studies. These results not only revealed the structural characteristics and sequence diversity of Nekemias plants, but also strengthened the relationship between species and confirmed the kinship of Nekemias family. We believe that N. grossedentata and N. megalophylla can be consider as the origin plants of vine tea. And by analysis of cp genome and phylogenetic relationships, it will be helpful to understand the evolution of Nekemias and solve the problem that the existing closely related species are difficult to distinguish in taxonomy. At the same time, in terms of chemical composition content analysis, chromatographic fingerprint analysis is an effective supplementary method to identify a variety of TCMs. In future studies, more detailed references can be provided for the quality evaluation and rational use of plant raw materials by further improving the relevant data such as the content of Q markers.

A Retrospective Study of Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia from North-East India
Authors: Shikha Verma, Anita Marak, Debopriya Paul, Biswajit Dey
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Frontal fibrosing alopecia (FFA) is an important cause of scarring alopecia seen mostly in post-menopausal women but sometimes in pre-menopausal women and men. Although considered a variant of lichen planopilaris due to its histopathological characteristics, it has distinct clinical features and associations, which make it a unique entity. We hereby report a series of patients with FFA from North-East India.Aims and Objective:This study aimed to analyse the clinical and histopathological characteristics of FFA.Materials and Methods:We retrospectively analysed clinical records and histopathological features of FFA cases diagnosed in the Dermatology Outpatient Department from April 2013 to February 2023.Results:A total of 21 patients, who were diagnosed with FFA from April 2013 to February 2023, were analysed. Of these, 19 patients were female, with a male-to-female ratio of 9.5:1. The mean age of study population was 48.33 years. The majority of the patients were from the post-menopausal age group (15/19 females, 78.94%). Lichen planus pigmentosus (6, 28.57%) was the most commonly associated disease, followed by androgenetic alopecia and lichen planopilaris (2 each, 9.52%). The main histological features noted were perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate in 18 (85.71%), followed by hydropic degeneration of basal follicular keratinocytes in 15 (71.42%) and melanin incontinence in 14 (66.66%) patients.Conclusion:Our study is the first study from North-East India focusing on the clinical presentation and histopathological characteristics of FFA. Furthermore, with respect to the recent development in FFA, our study attempted to determine the clinical significance of the proposed criteria for the diagnosis of FFA patients by Tolkachjov et al. (2018), viz. International FFA Cooperative Group Criteria (2021).

Introduction

Frontal fibrosing alopecia (FFA) was described by Kossard in 1994 as a progressive scarring alopecia predominantly affecting the frontal and temporoparietal regions of the scalp.[1] The reported prevalence in the literature of this entity amongst other causes of primary cicatricial alopecia in India ranged from 1.9% to 8.3%.[23]

FFA predominantly affects post-menopausal females; however, the involvement in males and pre-menopausal females has also been reported.[45]

Besides the classical band such as scarring of the frontotemporal hairline and complete or partial loss of eyebrows, other clinical manifestations reported in patients include loss of eyelashes, body hair and facial papules similar to keratosis pilaris.[6] Pruritus and trichodynia were common complaints seen in some studies,[7] while less frequently reported symptoms were loss of occipital hair, retroauricular hair or sideburns.[8]

Criteria have been proposed by Tolkachjov et al.[9] and the International FFA Cooperative Group[10] for the diagnosis of FFA.

We are hereby reporting and analysing the fulfilment of the above criteria in a series of 21 patients with FFA from a tertiary care hospital in North-East India.

Materials and Methods

We reviewed clinical records and histopathological features of FFA cases diagnosed from April 2013 to February 2023 in our department. A detailed analysis of 21 cases of FFA was conducted with special reference to clinical features and histopathological examination. Additionally, percentages of patients fulfilling the major and minor criteria as described by Tolkachjov et al.[9] and the International FFA Cooperative Group[10] were also recorded. Institutional Ethical Committee approval was obtained before the initiation of the study.

Results

We evaluated a total of 21 patients with FFA during the study period of whom 19 were female and two were male (9.5:1). The mean age of study population was 48.33 years. The youngest patient was 30 years old, while the oldest patient was 64 years old. The majority of the patients belonged to the post-menopausal age group (1/19, 78.94%).

Clinically, frontotemporal recession was universally present in all the cases (100%). Parietal involvement was seen in one patient (4.76%). Eyebrow involvement was present in 11 (52.38%) patients of whom the complete loss of eyebrow was seen in three (14.28%) patients. Figure 1 shows the involvement of frontotemporal area and eyebrows. Sideburn involvement was seen in nine (42.85%) patients, as shown in Figure 2. There was no involvement of occipital hair, eyelashes and axillary or body hair in any of the patients.

Lichen planopilaris was seen in two patients (9.52%), hypopigmented macules on the face in two (9.52%) and guttate hypomelanosis in one (4.76%) patient.

The main histological features noted were perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate affecting the infundibular area in 18 (85.71%), hydropic degeneration of basal follicular keratinocytes in 15 (71.42%) and melanin incontinence in 14 (66.66%) patients. One major criterion[9] was fulfilled by all the patients (100%), while both major criteria were fulfilled by 11, 52.38% of patients. The only minor criterion, which was universally present in all the patients, was the histopathological criteria suggestive of FFA or lichen planopilaris. While those who fulfilled more than one minor criterion were seen as follows: perifollicular scaling (10, 47.61%), hyperpigmentation (4, 19.4%) and eyebrow erythema and papules (one each, 4.76%) as shown in Figure 5. Discussion

Through this study, we are reporting a series of cases of FFA in North-East India, which will help us understand the demographic, clinical and histological pattern of the disease in this region where studies are lacking.

With regard to demographic characteristics, female predominance was noted (90.48%), which was similar to other studies from different parts of the world.[711] The mean age of our study population was 48.33 years, which was less in comparison with Vano-Galvan et al.[6] and MacDonald et al.[12] who reported 61 years and 64 years, respectively, as the mean age in their study. This finding could be explained by hypothesising that different geographical backgrounds and genetic makeup may play a role in the pathogenesis of FFA, which may cause its early manifestation in susceptible individuals. However, the majority of our patients still belonged to the post-menopausal age group (78.94%) similar to the findings of Moreno-Ramirez et al.[13]

Although FFA is predominantly seen in post-menopausal females, pre-menopausal and even male patients are reported to suffer from FFA.[514] Pre-menopausal (21%) and male patients (9.52%) were also observed in our study.

Ours is the first study to analyse the percentages of individuals who fulfil the criteria as described by Tolkachjov et al.[9] and determine their clinical significance. Frontotemporal recession (major) and histopathological criteria (minor) were fulfilled in all the patients. However, the percentage of individuals with two major criteria decreased to 52.38%, when considering the second criteria for the involvement of eyebrows. This discrepancy was noted because the involvement of eyebrows may not necessarily be present in all individuals and may also be a late manifestation of the disease, thereby diminishing the diagnosing ability of this criterion. Thus, we may propose that histopathological criteria should be considered a major criterion and not a minor criterion as described by the authors.

When comparing with the International FFA Cooperative Group Criteria[10] for FFA, we found that all our patients scored ≥4 points to diagnose classical FFA. We conclude that this criterion is adequate, effective and clinically more relevant in diagnosing FFA.

The most common associated disease with FFA in our study was found to be lichen planus pigmentosus in 28.57% of patients. Mulinari-Brenner et al.[15] and Rao et al.[16] also reported a case where FFA and lichen planus pigmentosus were concomitantly present. Porrino-Bustamante et al.[7] have also reported lichen planus and its variants as the common associated finding along with FFA. Also, lichen planopilaris was present in 9.52% of patients in our study. The concomitant presence of these diseases is supported by the proposed hypothesis put forward by Kossard et al.[1] who described FFA as a clinical variant of lichen planopilaris. Histopathological features suggestive of lichenoid reaction pattern with predominant lymphocytic infiltrate and vacuolar degeneration in both conditions also support this hypothesis.[17]

Androgenetic alopecia was also reported in our study in both of our male patients. Porrino-Bustamante et al.[7] reported androgenetic alopecia in 16.57% of patients and Katoulis et al.[18] have described a possible pathologic link between the two conditions.

Additionally, we noted the presence of hypopigmented macules over the forehead and face of two (9.52%) patients and multiple guttate hypopigmented lesions on both arms in one patient. Diaz et al.[19] reported peripilar white halos and idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis in a patient of FFA.

Clinically, frontotemporal recession was seen in all our patients with mild-to-moderate perifollicular scaling similar to MacDonald et al.,[12] Porrino-Busmante et al.[7] and Tan and Messenger.[5] Partial or complete involvement of eyebrows was noted in 52.63% of our patients, which was similar to the findings of Kossard et al.,[11] while Vano-Galvan et al.[6] reported eyebrow loss in 80% of patients in their study. Sideburn involvement was mainly reported previously in male patients,[4] but in our study we found 42.85%[13] of patients with sideburn involvement of whom 77.7% (7/9) were women.

The most common histologic finding was the presence of lymphocytic infiltrate in the perifollicular region (85.71%) along with basal vacuolar degeneration (71.42%). This finding was similar to the study conducted by Poblet et al.[20] Other important findings in histology were melanin incontinence (66.66%), perifollicular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate (66.66%) and perifollicular fibrosis (57.14%). Conclusion

This is the first study conducted on the epidemiology of FFA in North-East India, which also documented and compared the utility of recent two different diagnostic criteria for the diagnosis of FFA.

Limitations of the study

This is a retrospective study and with smaller sample size.

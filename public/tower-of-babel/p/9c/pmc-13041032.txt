Instillation success rates among glaucoma eyedrop products: patient survey in Japan
Authors: Kenji Kabeya, Hiroki Satoh, Natsuyo Yanagi, Yasufumi Sawada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGlaucoma is the leading cause of blindness in Japan and poses a significant public health challenge. Previous studies have reported low success rates for antiglaucoma eyedrop instillation. Our earlier research demonstrated that various physical attributes—including the squeezing force required to dispense a drop—differ markedly among eyedrop products. However, no prior studies have examined how instillation success rates vary across products or which container or formulation characteristics influence these rates. This study aimed to assess how instillation success varies by patient characteristics and product attributes and to develop predictive models based on container and formulation properties.MethodsA web-based survey was conducted in April 2025 among glaucoma patients aged ≥ 40 years who were undergoing eyedrop therapy without assistive devices. The survey investigated daily instillation practices and success. Ten first-line antiglaucoma products across eight brands (prostanoid receptor-related drugs and β-adrenergic receptor blockers) were selected. Responses were obtained from 800 patients (100 per brand). The first instillation attempt success rate (FIASR) and the expected number of attempts for a successful instillation (ENASI) were analyzed according to patient and product characteristics. Multiple regression analyses were conducted to develop estimation models based on container and formulation features.ResultsNeither patient sex, age, nor concomitant use of non-glaucoma eyedrops showed a significant influence on instillation success. Treatment experience was relatively impactful: patients with over one year of eyedrop use exhibited higher FIASR and lower ENASI than those with less experience, although not statistically significant. No consistent differences in instillation success were observed across products. Regression models did not yield significant predictors, but trend-level findings suggested that dimpled bottles had higher FIASR than cylindrical or flat types. Lower squeezing force, larger pinchable area, and lower solution viscosity were also associated with improved instillation success.ConclusionsThis is the first study to quantify instillation success rates in real-world glaucoma care from patient and product perspectives, and explore their relationship with product characteristics. The findings suggest that treatment experience may be more influential than demographic or product factors. These insights may assist clinicians and pharmacists in refining patient education and optimizing prescribing strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04659-2.

Background

Glaucoma is the leading cause of blindness and visual impairment in Japan. The most common treatment involves the use of topical ocular hypotensive medications, including prostanoid receptor agonists and beta adrenergic receptor blockers, among other agents with various mechanisms of action [1–3].

However, previous studies have shown that the success rate of instilling glaucoma eye drops is generally low, with several reports indicating that even among patients who regularly administer eye drops, the success rate is below 50% [4–7].

Also, our previous research has demonstrated that various characteristics—including the squeezing force required to dispense the solution—differ substantially across glaucoma eye drop products [8].

Nevertheless, no prior studies have investigated how instillation success rates vary among different products or which product characteristics influence such outcomes. Most prior studies have focused on single product or experimental formulations, lacking comparative evaluations across multiple commercial products [5–7, 9–13]. Moreover, the factors explored in relation to instillation success have primarily pertained to the patients’ physical capabilities or posture [5, 6, 11, 12], while product-side characteristics have received minimal attention, often limited to bottle capacity comparisons [4]. Consequently, little knowledge has been accumulated regarding product-related factors that may affect instillation success.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to examine, via patient survey, how instillation success rates vary across patients with different characteristics and across products with differing container and formulation properties. Additionally, we sought to develop predictive models for instillation success rate based on container and formulation features.

Methods

This study comprised two major components. First, we conducted a patient survey focusing on multiple glaucoma eyedrop products to compare instillation success rates across products. Second, based on the survey results and product-specific data, we developed predictive models for instillation success rate using product characteristics. The detailed methods of each component are described below.

Instillation success rate comparison based on patient survey

Participants and survey platform

The patient survey was conducted via a web-based questionnaire using the Rakuten Insight online research service. The survey was administered between April 18 and April 24, 2025. The questionnaire was distributed to registered glaucoma patients of Rakuten Insight aged 40 years or older. The number of valid responses was set at 100 per brand, based on the selected target products (as described below).

Target products

The target medications for this study were selected from prostanoid receptor agonists and beta blockers, which are recommended as first-line therapy in the Glaucoma Treatment Guidelines published by the Japan Glaucoma Society [3]. Among these, we selected a combination of products that exhibited diverse characteristics, based on our prior research [8].

To ensure clarity and applicability in clinical settings, respondents were asked to report the number of attempts required for a successful instillation. This format also allowed us to estimate medication consumption.

As assistive devices for eyedrop instillation are known to affect both instillation technique and success rate [13], we added a question about device usage to the survey. Respondents who reported using assistive devices were excluded from valid responses to ensure comparability. Additionally, given previous reports showing that nearly 30% of patients deliberately instill multiple drops at once—an incorrect practice [10]—we also included a question to the survey, to assess the prevalence of such behavior.

Data aggregation and analysis

Survey responses were analyzed to describe the instillation behaviors of glaucoma patients and to assess whether instillation success varied by product or patient characteristics. To estimate the expected number of attempts for a successful instillation (ENASI), we calculated a weighted average by multiplying the number of attempts by the proportion of respondents selecting each option (e.g., if 50% succeeded on the first attempt, 30% on the second, and 20% on the third, ENASI would be: 0.5 × 1 + 0.3 × 2 + 0.2 × 3 = 1.7). Responses of “five or more attempts” were treated as five in this calculation. Additionally, open-ended responses on usability were reviewed and categorized through internal discussions among the research team into: (1) positive usability feedback, (2) negative usability feedback, and (3) other. We counted the number of responses in each category and compared usability perceptions across products.

For statistical analysis, we defined two primary outcome measures: first instillation attempt success rate (FIASR) and ENASI. The following five explanatory variables were included in the analysis: product, respondent age, respondent sex, glaucoma eyedrop usage history, and usual use of non-glaucoma eyedrops. Statistical analysis was performed using ANCOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Covariates were mutually adjusted, and the significance level was set at 5%.

Estimation of instillation success rates

In addition to aggregating and analyzing the survey results, we performed statistical analysis to estimate how instillation success rate varies according to the physical characteristics of glaucoma eyedrop products.

All valid survey responses were included in the analysis. FIASR and ENASI were used as outcome variables, and the following four product attributes were included as explanatory variables: squeezing force, container shape, pinchable area, and viscosity of the formulation. For container shape, two dummy (0/1) variables were defined: flat-shaped container and dimpled container. Multiple linear regression analysis was performed to explore prediction formulas for FIASR and ENASI based on these product attributes.

Ethical considerations

To ensure ethical integrity, all participants were informed of the study purpose and data usage at the beginning of the questionnaire and provided their consent to participate. Participants were also allowed to withdraw consent and request removal from the study at any time by contacting the researchers via Rakuten Insight.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Graduate School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, The University of Tokyo (approval number: 7 − 2).

Tools for analysis

Microsoft Excel version 2406 was used for data aggregation and analysis in this study, while IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0.1.0 was used for statistical analysis.

In examining differences in instillation success rate by patient characteristics, no significant differences were observed in either FIASR or ENASI between males and females. While patients in their 50s tended to have slightly lower FIASR and higher ENASI compared to other age groups, no consistent trend was observed across age. Treatment experience showed a relatively greater influence on instillation success rate: patients with over one year of glaucoma eyedrop use tended to exhibit higher FIASR and lower ENASI than those with less than one year of experience, although the differences were not statistically significant. In contrast, chronic use of non-glaucoma eyedrops had no apparent effect on instillation success rate (Table 3).

With respect to differences across products, the variation in FIASR was at most 7.7% points, and the variation in ENASI was at most approximately 0.11, both of which were not statistically significant. However, free-text responses regarding usability showed some divergence: Tapros received more positive comments, whereas Travatan Z received more negative feedback (Table 3).

Estimation of instillation success rates

We conducted multiple linear regression analyses to estimate how instillation success rate varies according to the physical characteristics of glaucoma eyedrop products. Neither FIASR nor ENASI yielded statistically significant regression models (all p-values > 0.05). No product attribute was identified as a significant predictor of instillation success rate. However, trend-level associations suggested that (1) dimpled containers were associated with higher instillation success rate than cylindrical or flat-shaped containers, and (2) lower squeezing force, larger pinchable area, and lower viscosity of the formulation were each associated with improved instillation success rate (Table 4).

Discussion

Instillation success rate comparison based on patient survey

This study clarified the real-world instillation techniques and success status of glaucoma patients through a web-based survey.

First, regarding instillation technique, it was revealed that 21.6% of male patients and 18.0% of female patients intentionally instilled multiple drops per use. In Japan, the approved method of administration is strictly one drop per use [17–29]. Therefore, it can be concluded that a certain proportion of glaucoma patients continue treatment using incorrect instillation techniques. This finding aligns with previous studies [10], and the proportion of patients using improper technique falls within a comparable range. The slightly lower proportion observed in this study may be due to sampling bias, as participants capable of completing a web-based questionnaire may possess higher health literacy. When examined by age group, the frequency of improper instillation was highest among those in their 50s, which also coincided with the age group showing relatively lower FIASR and higher ENASI. Given prior research demonstrating that patient education on instillation technique improves instillation success [10], it is suggested that educational interventions provided by physicians and pharmacists may be broadly effective for these patients.

As for patient characteristics, sex had no notable influence on either FIASR or ENASI. Although patients in their 50s tended to have worse metrics, there was no clear and statistically significant age-dependent trend, in contrast to the estimation that instillation success declines with advancing age due to reduced manual dexterity, musculoskeletal limitations, or cognitive decline. While the duration of glaucoma eyedrop use was not statistically significant, it exerted a relatively larger influence on instillation success compared to sex or age. Patients with more than one year of experience were more likely to instill successfully and efficiently than those with less than one year. On the other hand, chronic use of non-glaucoma eyedrops did not contribute to improved success. Taken together, these findings suggest that although individual skill variation is important, treatment experience—rather than sex or age—is a more crucial determinant of instillation success. Furthermore, experience with eyedrops appears to be better reflected by the length of continuous use rather than by the number of current daily instillations. In our previous study, we found that dropout from glaucoma treatment most frequently occurs in the first year [30]. This further emphasizes the importance of active follow-up by physicians and pharmacists for patients in the early stages of treatment.

In the comparison across products, the maximum observed difference was 7.7% points for FIASR and approximately 0.11 for ENASI. These differences were not statistically significant. For instance, the product with the highest FIASR was Latanoprost 0.005% “SEC” (87.7%), and the lowest was Mikelan LA 1% (80.0%). However, the second-highest (Timoptol XE 0.25%, 87.5%) and second-lowest (Timoptol XE 0.5%, 81.8%) products shared nearly identical packaging and formulation characteristics, yet differed in instillation success rate. This suggests that packaging and formulation features may not cause major differences in instillation success, contrary to our initial hypothesis. That said, qualitative data from the free-text section showed variation in patient usability feedback across products. It is conceivable that such differences in perceived usability could exert a greater influence on instillation success rate among certain subpopulations, such as the oldest-old, who were underrepresented in this study. Thus, it would be inappropriate to conclude categorically that packaging and formulation characteristics have no impact on instillation success. Additionally, unintended multiple-drop instillation was observed in over 50% of patients for nearly all products, confirming that this remains a common issue across glaucoma eyedrop products.

Estimation of instillation success rates

Multiple linear regression analysis was performed to estimate instillation success based on the physical characteristics of each product (formulation and container). However, no statistically significant regression models were obtained for either FIASR or ENASI. These results further suggest that product characteristics exert limited influence on instillation success and that the skills and experience of individual patients are more critical determinants.

Nonetheless, although not statistically significant, the analysis revealed some trends. Dimpled containers were associated with higher instillation success rates than cylindrical containers, and cylindrical containers were more successful than flat-shaped ones. Dimpled containers are a design innovation developed by a pharmaceutical company to facilitate instillation [16], and the current findings suggest that such design features may offer some benefit.

Similarly, although also not statistically significant, lower squeezing force, larger pinchable area, and lower viscosity were associated with higher instillation success rates. These factors are strongly linked to ease of handling; having a larger surface area allows for more stable gripping, and lower squeezing force and viscosity enables patients to dispense drops with less effort and more control, thereby contributing to accurate instillation.

As this study demonstrated that patient skill and experience have a greater influence than product attributes, future research with a larger sample size is warranted to accurately assess the effect of product characteristics on instillation success.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, because participants were recruited from a web-based survey platform (Rakuten Insight), selection bias related to digital literacy and related factors is possible, which may have influenced the observed instillation success rates, particularly among older patients. In addition, the sex ratio and age composition of our sample may not fully reflect the epidemiologic distribution of glaucoma in Japan. A large-scale epidemiologic survey conducted in Japan reported no substantial sex difference in glaucoma prevalence (4.3% in men vs. 3.7% in women) and a markedly higher prevalence in individuals in their 70s compared with those in their 60s (8.2% vs. 4.7%) [31]. In light of these findings, our analytical sample is likely characterized by a higher proportion of men and a lower proportion of older patients than the overall glaucoma population.

Second, we did not comprehensively capture patient-level factors that may affect the ability to instill eyedrops, such as musculoskeletal or joint disorders that impair hand function (e.g., rheumatic arthritis), tremor or other motor symptoms that reduce manual dexterity, cognitive impairment, or reduced near vision (e.g., presbyopia or astigmatism). In addition, we did not assess clinical indicators such as glaucoma severity or visual function, which could also be related to instillation performance. Therefore, we may have underestimated the impact of these factors—particularly in older adults—and future studies incorporating clinical and functional assessments are warranted. Moreover, because patients using instillation aid devices were excluded from the analysis, individuals who have greater difficulty with instillation may have been less likely to be included in our study, potentially biasing our estimates toward higher success rates.

Third, as the study relied on self-reported data, there is a possibility of overestimating instillation success rate due to misrecognition of failed attempts or recall bias. In fact, while several prior studies have reported FIASR to be less than 50% among glaucoma patients [4–7], the success rates observed in this study were substantially higher.

Finally, due to practical constraints, it was not feasible to include all glaucoma eyedrop products. Therefore, subtle differences between product types or therapeutic classes may not have been fully captured.

Implications for clinical practice and future research

The findings of this study have implications for glaucoma treatment, formulation development, and future research.

From a clinical standpoint, as discussed earlier, patients with short treatment history—particularly those within their first year of treatment—were identified as a priority population for follow-up by physicians and pharmacists. Providing targeted support and instruction on proper instillation technique for these patients may improve their instillation success, reduce treatment-related stress, enhance treatment persistence, and minimize medication waste.

In addition, knowing the expected number of instillation attempts based on patient and product characteristics may improve the accuracy of determining appropriate prescription volumes. Eyedrops are prone to overprescription due to uncertainty in predicting usage, often resulting in leftover medication. This study’s insights may help mitigate such inefficiencies.

Furthermore, since this study produced detailed data on instillation success rates, it can serve as a valuable foundation for future health economic evaluations and comparative effectiveness research at a finer level of granularity.

Conclusions

This study investigated differences in instillation success rate among glaucoma patients with varying characteristics, as well as among eyedrop products with differing container and formulation attributes, using a web-based patient survey. Based on the results, we also explored predictive models of instillation success rate using product characteristics.

Our findings indicate that, among patient-related factors, treatment experience—rather than sex or age—was the most influential determinant of instillation success rate. Meanwhile, product selection and the physical characteristics of eyedrop containers and formulations had limited impact on success rate.

These insights may help physicians and pharmacists enhance patient communication and optimize prescription strategies in the context of glaucoma eyedrop therapy.

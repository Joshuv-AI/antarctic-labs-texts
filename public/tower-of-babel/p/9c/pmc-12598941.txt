Mileage matters: long-distance performance of CARs in multiple myeloma
Authors: Eric M Jurgens, Saad Z Usmani, Maximilian Merz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTargeting B-cell maturation antigen with chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cells is a new standard of care in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM). However, long-term data have currently remained sparse. In a recent study published in the Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer by Jin et al, authors reported on a large cohort of 141 patients with a median follow-up of 20.2 months. They found an overall response rate of 90.1% with 48.2% achieving a complete response. The median progression-free survival was 15.2 months, and the 4-year overall survival rate was 63.2%. In the current article, we summarize the published long-term data from clinically approved and investigational CAR T-cells for RRMM.

Article

B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy has transformed the treatment of relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM). Currently, two products, ciltacabtagene autoleucel (cilta-cel) and idecabtagene vicleucel (ide-cel), are US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved for the treatment of RRMM.1 2 Despite deep and durable responses, most patients will relapse after BCMA CAR T-cell therapy. Understanding the myeloma and CAR T-cell intrinsic factors associated with long-term response is critical to optimize existing therapies and develop new, durable therapies.

In a recent issue of the Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer, Jin et al report long-term follow-up from two phase 1 clinical trials studying a BCMA CAR T-cell therapy for RRMM.3 In this update, outcomes from 141 patients were reported after a median follow-up period of 20.2 months (range 0.3–70.1 months, figure 1, table 1). The overall response rate (ORR) was 90.1% with 48.2% achieving a complete response (CR). Nearly all patients (97.1%) who achieved a CR also had no measurable residual disease (MRD) by flow cytometry (10−4). The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 15.2 months, median overall survival (OS) was not reached (NR), and the 4-year OS rate was 63.2%.

In this long-term follow-up, 60 patients (42.5%) had an ongoing response, including 13 (9.2%) exceeding 4 years with one patient maintaining a response over 5 years. Patients with a CR compared with non-CR demonstrated a significantly longer median PFS, 42.37 versus 6.03 months (p<0.001), as well as a superior OS (median NR; p=0.013). compared with non-CR, a CR was associated with significantly higher peak CAR T-cell expansion (87.7% vs 75.2% of total peripheral T-cells, p<0.001), with an even greater difference between responders and non-responders (84.0% vs 14.7%, p<0.001). In patients with ongoing responses, serum CAR transgenes were still detectable at 6 and 12 months in 34/61 (55.7%) and 17/53 (32.1%) of patients, respectively. In this update, no delayed neurotoxicities like parkinsonism or cranial nerve palsy were observed, and aside from one case of acute myeloid leukemia at 5.7 months, no secondary primary malignancies or CAR+T cell lymphomas were reported. Overall, this study provides further insight into the long-term efficacy and safety of BCMA CAR T-cell therapy for RRMM.

Currently, the most robust long-term data for BCMA CAR T-cell therapy exists for the FDA-approved products, cilta-cel and ide-cel. Regarding cilta-cel, the initial phase 1 LEGEND-2 trial reported a high ORR and CR rate, 88% and 73%, respectively.4 Since the initial publication, LEGEND-2 has reported the longest follow-up data on 74 patients with a median follow-up period of 65.4 months.5 Overall median PFS was 18.0 months and median OS was 55.8 months. Depth of response correlated with durability of response such that patients achieving a CR compared with non-CR demonstrated a significantly longer median PFS of 28.2 versus 4.4 months (p<0.0001) and median OS of NR versus 7.9 months (p<0.0001). No cases of parkinsonism were reported. Four patients were diagnosed with secondary primary malignancies (SPMs), all of which were solid tumor malignancies. Late relapses were reported after 5 years with three patients progressing at 62.4, 68.3, and 69.5 months, respectively.

Following LEGEND-2, the target CAR T-cell dose was further evaluated in the phase 1b/2 CARTITUDE-1 trial. Initial results demonstrated an ORR and CR rate of 97% and 67%, respectively.1 Consistent with LEGEND-2, achieving a CR compared with non-CR was associated with a superior 12-month PFS rate, 85% versus 62%, respectively. In the most recent update, at a median follow-up of 61.3 months, the median PFS was 34.9 months with ongoing responses in 33% of patients.6 Median OS was 60.7 months. Initially, five cases of Parkinsonism were observed with one additional case reported 914 days after CAR T-cell infusion.1 7 8 Similarly, 9 SPMs in 7 patients were initially reported, but with longer follow-up, a total of 26 SPMs in 16 patients were reported. Thus, extended follow-up is critical to understand the true incidence of delayed, serious toxicities associated with CAR T-cell therapy for RRMM.

Expanding on CARTITUDE-1, the phase 3 CARTITUDE-4 trial included patients with more than one prior therapy, including proteasome inhibitors and lenalidomide-refractory disease.9 Patients randomized to cilta-cel (N=208) compared with physicians choice of standard of care (SOC) therapy (N=211) demonstrated a superior ORR, 84.6% versus 67.3% respectively, and CR rate, 73.1% versus 21.8% respectively. Median PFS was NR for cilta-cel compared with 11.8 months for the SOC arm. Updated results reported at a median follow-up of 33.6 months demonstrated an OS benefit for cilta-cel versus SOC with a 30-month OS rate of 76.4% versus 63.8%, respectively (HR 0.55; 95% CI 0.39 to 0.79; p=0.0009). Additionally, the 30-month PFS rate of cilta-cel versus SOC was 59.4% versus 25.7%, respectively (HR 0.29; 95% CI 0.22 to 0.39; p<0.0001).10 No new non-Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS) neurotoxicities were reported at follow-up. SPMs were initially reported in 9 patients which increased to 27 patients at the time of extended follow-up. Two of these patients developed CAR+peripheral T-cell lymphomas at 5 and 16 months, respectively.11

Regarding ide-cel, after promising results in the phase 1 CRB-401 trial, the phase 2 KarMMa trial including 140 patients with RRMM demonstrated an ORR of 73%, CR rate of 33, and median PFS of 8.8 months.2 Similar to other CAR T-cell products, CR predicated a favorable PFS of 19 months compared with 10.4 months in patients with a very good partial response (VGPR) and 4.5 months in patients with a PR. Extended follow-up at a median 63.6 months reported a median PFS of 10.8 months and median OS of 28.2 months.12 SPMs were reported in a total of nine patients with two cases emerging after the initial report.

Following the success of KarMMa, the phase 3 KarMMa-3 trial compared ide-cel to physician’s choice SOC.13 Ide-cel compared with SOC demonstrated a higher ORR, 71% versus 42% (p<0.001), and CR rate, 39% versus 5%. After an initial median follow-up period of 18.9 months, median PFS was significantly longer in the ide-cel group compared with the SOC group, 13.3 months versus 4.4 months (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.38 to 0.65; p<0.0001). Updated analysis at a median follow-up of 30.9 months re-demonstrated PFS benefit in favor of ide-cel versus SOC, 13.8 versus 4.4 months.14 Ide-cel demonstrated a longer median OS, 41.4 versus 37.9 months, which was more pronounced when adjusting for cross-over, 41.4 versus 23.4 months, though neither comparison was statistically significant. No new safety events occurred, and no cases of parkinsonism or CAR+T cell lymphomas were reported.

Long-term follow-up of patients with RRMM treated with other BCMA CAR T-cell therapies on clinical trials is limited with no further data available for phase 3 trials. After cilta-cel, the BCMA CAR T-cell product HDS269B has the longest reported data followed by CT103A.15 16

The initial phase 1/2 trial studying HDS269B in 49 patients with RRMM demonstrated an ORR of 77% and CR rate of 47% with a median PFS of 10 months and median OS of 29 months.17 At a median follow-up of 59.0 months, median PFS remained similar at 9.5 months with a 5-year PFS rate of 21.3%. The median OS decreased to 20.0 months with a 5-year OS rate of 34.1%. Consistent with other BCMA CAR T-cell products, a CR versus non-CR at 6 months was associated with a longer PFS (p=0.04) and OS (p=0.02). Additionally, bone marrow MRD-negativity by flow cytometry (10−4) at 28 days, 3 months, and 6 months predicted a longer PFS, while MRD-negativity at 28 days and 6 months predicted a longer OS. CAR T-cell expansion and persistence, as measured by serum CAR transgene quantitative PCR, also correlated with response to HDS269B. Responders (≥PR) compared with non-responders had a significantly higher median peak CAR T-cell expansion. Additionally, patients with detectable vs undetectable CAR T-cells at 6, 12, and 24 months had significantly longer PFS and OS. Finally, no new safety events were reported, including neurotoxicities and SPMs.

Regarding CT103A, the phase I trial included 18 patients with RRMM demonstrating an ORR of 100% and CR rate of 72.2% with 1-year PFS and OS rates of 58.3% and 75%, respectively.16 At a median follow-up of 45.0 months, CR rate increased to 77.8%, median PFS was 22.6 months, and median OS was 50.2 months. Patients achieving a CR versus a non-CR had a significantly longer PFS (p<0.0001) and OS (p=0.0014). Expectedly, patients with an ongoing response versus patients with relapse or no response demonstrated significantly higher median peak CAR T-cell expansion and significantly longer CAR T-cell persistence. No cases of neurotoxicity or SPMs were reported.

Overall BCMA CAR T-cell therapy is effective though response rates and durability vary among different products. Understanding product-specific features that impact PFS is critical to optimize existing and develop future CAR T-cell therapies. For instance, a recent study reported cilta-cel compared with ide-cel exhibited CAR T-cells with higher peak expansion, higher CD4+/CD8+ ratio, and longer persistence contributing to longer PFS.18 Across all products, depth of response strongly correlates with PFS. While MRD measurements were not uniform among these trials, in the KarMMa trial, MRD negativity was a strong predicator of PFS that may be more impactful than achieving ≥CR.19 20 These findings also highlighted sustained MRD negativity as a predictor of prolonged response, which should be considered in long-term follow-up reports.

In summary, long-term data for BCMA CAR T-cell therapy is sparse though actively maturing. In their recent publication, Jin et al report updated outcomes on 141 patients with RRMM treated with BCMA CAR T-cell therapy at a median follow-up of 20.2 months.3 This study contributes long-term follow-up in the second largest patient cohort after cilta-cel. This BCMA CAR T-cell therapy boasts a high ORR of 90.1% and CR rate of 48.2% with a median PFS of 15.2 months. Consistent with other BCMA CAR T-cell products, a deeper response (CR vs non-CR) predicted a longer response, higher peak CAR T-cell expansion correlated with a deeper response, and CAR T-cell persistence was associated with an ongoing response at follow-up. As 60 patients still had an ongoing response, additional follow-up is important to further elucidate factors associated with durable responses and late relapses. While no new serious toxicities were observed, ongoing surveillance and reporting are critical to monitor for late toxicities, such as non-ICANS neurotoxicity and CAR+T cell lymphomas, which may emerge with long-term follow-up.

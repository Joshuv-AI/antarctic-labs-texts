DCBLD1 as a novel prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in breast cancer: clinical validation and mechanistic insights
Authors: Zhi-Yong Liu, Hong Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:To investigate the expression of DCBLD1 (discoidin, CUB, and LCCL domain-containing protein 1) in breast cancer tissues and its correlation with patient prognosis, and evaluate its potential as a novel prognostic marker and therapeutic target.Methods:Differential expression of DCBLD1 between breast cancer and normal tissues was analyzed using GEO and METABRIC databases, combined with survival analysis and Cox multivariate regression analysis to evaluate its prognostic value. Effective interference sequences were screened using quantitative polymerase chain reaction, and knockdown efficiency was validated by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction and Western blot. The effects of DCBLD1 on breast cancer cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion were investigated using CCK-8, flow cytometry, Transwell migration and invasion assays, and wound healing assays.Results:DCBLD1 was significantly overexpressed in breast cancer tissues (P = 0.0113), and patients with high expression exhibited shorter overall survival [P = 1.93e−6, hazards ratio (HR) = 1.33]. Cox multivariate analysis confirmed DCBLD1 as an independent prognostic factor (HR = 1.14, P = 0.0302), and it was positively correlated with tumor grade (P < 0.001), stage (P = 0.003), and metastasis (P = 0.009). Knockdown of DCBLD1 significantly inhibited breast cancer cell proliferation, colony formation, migration, and invasion capacities while promoting apoptosis.Conclusion:DCBLD1 exhibits oncogene functions in breast cancer and can serve as an independent prognostic marker and potential therapeutic target, with its expression level closely correlated to tumor malignancy and metastasis risk.

Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women worldwide, with its incidence rate increasing annually[1]. Current treatment modalities for breast cancer are continuously advancing, with improvements in surgery, chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy significantly enhancing the prognosis of relevant patients with breast cancer[2]. However, some patients still develop drug resistance and poor prognosis[3]. Breast cancer, as a highly heterogeneous disease, is characterized by dysregulation of numerous genes and cascade expressions[4,5]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify genes associated with breast cancer progression and prognosis and elucidate their underlying mechanisms. DCBLD1 (discoidin, CUB, and LCCL domain-containing protein 1) is a type-I transmembrane glycoprotein containing domains such as discoidin, CUB, and LCCL[6]. It is expressed in endothelial cells and vascular smooth muscle cells, participating in biological processes such as angiogenesis, cell migration, and proliferation[7]. Although DCBLD1 has been previously implicated in various cancers[8], in-depth studies on its role in breast cancer remain lacking. This study aims to analyze the diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic value of DCBLD1 expression in breast cancer and explore its potential as a therapeutic target. The article is compliant with the TITAN Guidelines 2025 – governing declaration and use of artificial intelligence[9].

Methods

Database selection and bioinformatics analysis

To investigate the potential role of DCBLD1 in the occurrence and progression of breast cancer, this study conducted a comprehensive analysis from two perspectives: gene expression profile features and prognostic value. For gene expression analysis, this study utilized the GSE54002 dataset from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE54002), comparing breast cancer tissues with paired normal tissue samples. This dataset was generated through expression microarray technology. This study employed affy package of the R Programming Language for quantile normalization of raw expression matrices and limma package for differential expression analysis, ensuring rigorous data standardization and accurate identification of differentially expressed genes. For prognostic value evaluation, this study conducted an in-depth analysis based on the breast cancer sample cohort of the METABRIC database, with clinical information and gene expression data obtained via the cBioPortal platform (https://www.cbioportal.org/). This dataset originated from preprocessed and normalized expression profile microarray data, with no additional data preprocessing performed in subsequent analyses.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining was performed using tissue microarrays purchased from Shanghai Zuocheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. A total of 143 tumor tissue samples and 18 para-carcinoma tissue samples were selected for detection. Tissue samples were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and embedded in paraffin. The 4 µm sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, then incubated in 0.01 M citrate buffer at 95°C for 20 min for antigen retrieval. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide (ZSGB Bio; Beijing, China), and nonspecific binding sites were blocked with 10% normal goat serum (ZSGB Bio), followed by incubation with primary antibodies at 4°C for 12 h. The primary antibody used was rabbit anti-DCBLD1 antibody (Catalog #24504-1-AP, dilution 1:100; Proteintech, Wuhan, China). After being rinsed with PBS (3 times × 5 min), the sections were incubated with goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (ZSGB Bio). Antigen visualization was performed with horseradish peroxidase (HRP) substrate 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB, ZSGB Bio), followed by hematoxylin (Beyotime; Shanghai, China) counterstaining at 25°C for 2 min. The negative control group was incubated with normal goat serum instead of the primary antibody. Two senior pathologists independently performed blinded evaluations of the section images and staining results. The staining intensity of cancer cells was scored as follows: 0, no staining; 1, weak staining (light yellow); 2, moderate staining (yellow-brown); and 3, strong staining (brown). The proportion of positively stained tumor cells was scored: 0 (<5%), 1 (5–25%), 2 (26–50%), 3 (51–75%), and 4 (>75%). The final IHC score was calculated by multiplying the intensity score by the proportion score, yielding a range from 0 to 12. For subsequent statistical analysis, samples were categorized into low expression (score ≤4) and high expression (score >4) groups based on the median score of the cohort.

Construction of DCBLD1 gene RNA interference lentiviral vectors

Using the DCBLD1 gene as a template, this study designed multiple RNA interference target sequences with lengths of 19–21 nucleotides based on RNA interference sequence design principles. After evaluation and screening, the following sequences were selected as interference targets (see Table 1). Following target design, single-stranded DNA oligonucleotides (oligos) containing the selected interference sequences were synthesized and annealed to form double-stranded DNA oligoduplexes. Subsequently, the double-stranded products were directly ligated into the correspondingly digested lentiviral vector backbone with the pre-designed restriction enzyme cutting sites at their ends. The ligation products were transformed into prepared Escherichia coli TOP10 competent cells (TIANGEN, Cat. #CB104-03). Positive recombinant clones were screened by bacterial polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and sent for sequencing verification. Plasmid extraction was performed on positive clones with confirmed sequencing alignment. The lentiviral expression vector used in this assay was the BR-V121 plasmid, and its map is shown in Figure 1. qPCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Tiangen Biotech (Beijing) Co., Ltd., China). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized with a high-capacity cDNA reverse transcription kit (TaKaRa, Cat. #RR047A). Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed on Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System with SYBR Green Real-Time PCR Master Mix kits (Takara Biotechnology (Dalian) Co., Ltd., China, Cat. #RR820A).

Western blot

Cells were lysed on ice for 1 h using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Cat. #P0013B) containing 1% phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (Beyotime, Cat. #ST506), followed by centrifugation at 12 000 × g for 40 min at 4°C. Protein concentration was determined with a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, Cat. #P0012). A total of 30 μg protein was separated by 10% SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane via a wet transfer system. The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk prepared in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.05% Tween-20 (TBST) for 2 h at room temperature. The membrane was incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit or goat anti-mouse secondary antibodies (Beyotime, Cat. #A0208, #A0216) for 1.5 h at room temperature. Finally, the target protein bands were visualized with an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) Detection Kit (Beyotime, Cat. #P0018FS).

In vitro phenotypic studies

To investigate the role of DCBLD1 in the progression of breast cancer, a series of in vitro phenotypic assays were conducted.

Detection of breast cancer cell proliferation capacity (CCK-8 assay)

Tumor cell proliferation rates were measured with the CCK-8 kit. After trypsin digestion, cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well, with 200 μL of complete medium added to each well. Subsequently, at 0-, 24-, 48-, and 72-h time points, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution (5 mg/mL, Beyotime, Cat. #C0038) was added to each well, followed by incubation at 37°C for 4 h. Absorbance value was measured at a wavelength of 460 nm.

Analysis of apoptosis by flow cytometry

Apoptosis levels in transfected cells were measured with Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (BestBio, Shanghai, China, Cat. #BB-4101) strictly following the manufacturer’s protocol. The specific steps are as follows: After trypsin digestion, cells were collected and washed twice with pre-cooled 1× PBS solution. The washed cells were resuspended in 400 μL of 1 × binding buffer, and 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC was added, followed by incubation at 4°C in the dark for 15 min. Subsequently, 10 μL propidium iodide (PI) solution was added, and the cells were further incubated at 4°C in the dark for 10 min. Upon completion of staining, early apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI−) and late apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI+) cell populations were quantitatively analyzed by flow cytometry (BD FACSCanto II).

Colony formation assay

Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were digested with trypsin, resuspended, and seeded into six-well plates at a density of 600 cells per well, with three replicate wells for each assay condition. After 8 days of culture, cell colony morphology was observed and photographed under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus IX73). Subsequently, all cell colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution (Beyotime, Cat. #P0099) and stained with Giemsa solution (Beyotime, Cat. #C0131). Following staining, the colonies were repeatedly washed with deionized water, air-dried at room temperature, and photographed with a digital camera. The colony formation rate was calculated through the following formula: Colony formation rate (%) = (Number of colonies/Number of seeded cells) × 100%.

Transwell assay

Cells were seeded into the upper chamber of a Transwell 24-well plate (Corning, New York, USA, Cat. #3422) with an 8 μm pore size at a density of 2 × 105 cells/well in serum-free medium, and the corresponding medium without fetal bovine serum (FBS) was added. The upper chamber was pre-coated with 30 μg Matrigel (for Transwell-Matrigel invasion assay only; BD Biosciences, San Jose, California, USA, Cat. #356234), while the lower chamber was filled with the corresponding medium containing 10% FBS. After 48 h of incubation, cells at the bottom of the inserts were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.05% crystal violet (Beyotime, Cat. #C0121). Subsequently, an Olympus CKX41 microscope (200× magnification) was used to randomly select at least 10 fields for counting cells that had invaded the lower surface. All assays were independently repeated at least three times.

Wound healing assay

To evaluate the pseudopod formation and migration ability of tumor cells, this assay employed the cell wound healing assay. The specific steps are as follows: Cells were seeded in six-well culture plates and incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2 until reaching 80–90% confluency. A sterile 1-mL pipette tip was used to create scratch wounds on the cell monolayer, followed by washing with PBS to remove detached cell debris. Cell migration into the wound area was observed at different time points. Images of cell migration from the wound edges or pseudopod extension were captured using an inverted microscope (Olympus CKX41). In total, nine random fields (100× objective magnification) were selected per well for quantitative analysis of cell migration across three independent culture wells per group. All assays were performed with three technical replicates and independently repeated at least three times.

Statistical analysis

In this study, differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the limma package of the R Programming Language for data normalization and differential expression identification. The log-rank test was applied to compare overall survival (OS) differences between DCBLD1 high-expression and low-expression patient groups. Prognostic evaluation utilized the Cox proportional hazards multivariate regression model to calculate hazard ratios (HR) and their 95% confidence intervals (95% CI), assessing the statistical significance of DCBLD1 as an independent prognostic factor. Immunohistochemistry results were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test, and Spearman rank correlation analysis was employed to explore the relationship between DCBLD1 expression levels and clinicopathological parameters. The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis method was used to evaluate the association between DCBLD1 gene expression levels and OS in breast cancer patients. Cell assay data were statistically analyzed using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA. Statistical difference calculations and graph plotting were performed using GraphPad Prism 6.0, with all statistical significance thresholds set at P < 0.05.

DCBLD1 knockdown suppresses malignant phenotypes of breast cancer cells

To elucidate the mechanism of DCBLD1 in breast cancer progression, this study evaluated its inhibitory effect on the malignant phenotypes of breast cancer cells by knocking down DCBLD1 expression.

Screening of breast cancer cell lines with high DCBLD1 expression

qPCR was performed to analyze DCBLD1 gene expression levels in different breast cancer cell lines, with the normal mammary epithelial cell line MCF-10A as a control. The results (Fig. 9) showed that compared to MCF-10A cells, DCBLD1 expression levels were significantly elevated in MCF-7, MDA-MB-468, BT-549, and MDA-MB-231 cell lines (P < 0.001). Among them, MDA-MB-231 and BT-549 cell lines exhibited significantly pronounced upregulation. Given their significantly high expression characteristics, MDA-MB-231 and BT-549 cell lines were selected for subsequent functional mechanism studies.

qPCR detection of DCBLD1 expression levels in different breast cancer cell lines (***P < 0.001).

Construction and functional validation of shRNA

In the MDA-MB-231 cell line, short hairpin RNA (shRNA) interference sequences targeting DCBLD1 were screened via qPCR assays. The results (Fig. 10) showed that the knockdown efficiency of DCBLD1 was 83.4% in the shDCBLD1-1 (RNAi-32481) group (P < 0.01), 83.1% in the shDCBLD1-2 (RNAi-32482) group (P < 0.01), and 63.5% in the shDCBLD1-3 (RNAi-32483) group (P < 0.01). Among them, interference sequence 1 (shDCBLD1-1, sh-1) and sequence 2 (shDCBLD1-2, sh-2) both induced significant DCBLD1 downregulation in MDA-MB-231 cells, with knockdown efficiencies significantly higher than other interference sequences. Based on this, this study selected sh-1 and sh-2 for subsequent functional assays. To validate their effectiveness, shDCBLD1-1 and shDCBLD1-2 were further confirmed to effectively knockdown DCBLD1 expression in BT-549 and MDA-MB-231 cell lines with high DCBLD1 expression. To evaluate the migration and invasion capacities of MB231 and BT549 cell lines, this study employed wound healing and Transwell assays for validation. DCBLD1 deficiency significantly suppressed cell migration and invasion capacities, with all assay groups showing significantly lower results than the control group (P < 0.01). This finding aligns with prior IHC assay results of DCBLD1 expression in tumor tissues and para-carcinoma tissues. Discussion

The hallmarks of cancer encompass six core biological capacities acquired during the multistage development of human tumors: sustaining proliferative signaling, evading growth suppressors, resisting cell death, enabling replicative immortality, inducing angiogenesis, and activating invasion and metastasis programs[10]. Currently, the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer pathogenesis remain to be fully elucidated. DCBLD1 (discoidin, CUB, and LCCL domain-containing protein 1) is a highly conserved transmembrane protein containing discoidin domains, CUB domains, and LCCL modules. This protein shares domain similarities with neuropilin, which functions as a critical co-receptor during development, suggesting that DCBLD1 may play potential roles in cell adhesion, extracellular matrix interactions, and signal transduction pathways[11]. As a member of the discoidin domain-containing transmembrane protein family, DCBLD1 has been implicated in regulating pathological processes such as cell signaling, migration, and adhesion in various solid tumors[8,12–14]. However, its specific biological functions and mechanisms in the occurrence and progression of breast cancer remain insufficiently elucidated.

Bioinformatics analysis indicates that DCBLD1 exhibits significant clinical relevance to breast cancer. This gene is not only highly expressed in tumor tissues but also strongly associated with poor patient prognosis. IHC validation confirmed that DCBLD1 expression levels were significantly higher in breast cancer tissues than in normal breast tissues (P < 0.05), and its expression intensity was negatively correlated with patient survival time (P < 0.05). Spearman correlation analysis revealed that DCBLD1 gene expression showed a significant positive correlation with pathological parameters such as histological grade and M stage (P < 0.05), suggesting that elevated expression levels may exacerbate tumor malignancy and increase metastasis risk. Kaplan–Meier survival curve analysis further demonstrated that patients with high DCBLD1 expression had significantly shorter OS than those with low expression (log-rank P < 0.01). Collectively, these findings indicate that DCBLD1 may serve as a potential biomarker for poor prognosis in patients with breast cancer, with its gene expression likely involved in the pathological processes of breast cancer occurrence, progression, and prognosis regulation, holding promise as a novel therapeutic target. Its molecular mechanisms warrant further investigation.

Multivariate Cox regression analysis of breast cancer samples from the METABRIC database further confirmed the clinical significance of DCBLD1 as an independent prognostic factor. This suggests that DCBLD1 may play a critical biological role in breast cancer progression, particularly in the triple-negative breast cancer subtype, where its elevated expression indicates accelerated tumor progression and poor prognosis. This study also demonstrated that DCBLD1 exhibits statistically significant high expression in breast cancer tissues and cell lines, and its expression level is significantly negatively correlated with poor patient prognosis. qPCR analysis in this study revealed that, compared to normal human mammary epithelial cells MCF-10A, DCBLD1 exhibits significantly high expression in multiple breast cancer cell lines (including MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, BT-549, and MDA-MB-468). Screening assays of DCBLD1 interference sequences in MDA-MB-231 cells showed that sequences 1 and 2 achieve optimal lentiviral knockdown efficiency. Further validation in the BT-549 and MDA-MB-231 cells with high DCBLD1 expression confirmed that both shDCBLD1-1 and shDCBLD1-2 can effectively reduce DCBLD1 expression levels. In vitro functional assays demonstrated that DCBLD1 knockdown significantly inhibits breast cancer cell proliferation (detected by CCK-8 assay), colony formation, and migration capacity, while promoting apoptosis. The above results confirm that DCBLD1 primarily functions as an oncogenic factor in breast cancer. Blocking DCBLD1 expression can significantly suppress breast cancer cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration capacity.

This study still has certain limitations: the functional validation was primarily conducted in cell lines, and has not yet been validated in animal models or through large-scale clinical samples. Furthermore, subsequent studies should investigate the potential molecular mechanisms by which DCBLD1 promotes breast cancer progression. Existing research indicates that squalene epoxidase, a key regulator of ferroptosis in breast cancer, can reduce the ubiquitination level of cyclin B1 (CCNB1) to enhance its protein stability and decrease reactive oxygen species (ROS) production[15]. Meanwhile, the phosphorylation complex formed by cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1) and CCNB1 can mediate the degradation of downstream substrates (e.g., the ferroptosis-related protein ACSL4)[16–18]. Based on the above research background, it is hypothesized that DCBLD1 may participate in regulating the ferroptosis process in breast cancer cells through specific binding with CCNB1.

In summary, DCBLD1 not only serves as a prognostic biomarker for breast cancer but also holds potential as a novel therapeutic target for breast cancer treatment. Future studies should elucidate its specific molecular mechanisms and conduct clinical sample-based validation research to provide theoretical foundations for the clinical translation of DCBLD1-targeted therapy.

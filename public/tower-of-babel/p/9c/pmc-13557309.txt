Topical Melatonin and Hair Graying Repigmentation: A Case Series
Authors: Adrian Imbernon-Moya, Elena Alonso Delgado, Jiaying Xu, Francisco Hidalgo
Published: 2026

Introduction

Melatonin is a hormone with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory properties. Topical melatonin 0.1% solution has demonstrated efficacy in telogen effluvium and female androgenetic alopecia, reducing hair shedding and increasing density [1]. By binding to its receptors in the hair follicle, melatonin appears to modulate the hair cycle, prolong the anagen phase, and reduce follicular miniaturization. Hair graying is primarily caused by melanocyte apoptosis and reduced melanin synthesis, associated with oxidative stress, genetic predisposition, and chronic psychological stress [1–3]. Available treatments are scarce for repigmentation of hair graying.

Case Presentation

We conducted a prospective study to assess the efficacy and safety of using melatonin 1% solution applied once daily for three months. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee. A total of 20 patients were included: 13 females and seven males, with a mean age of 51.55 years (range: 32–64). Partial repigmentation of gray hair was observed in four female patients (20%). Among nine females with chronic telogen effluvium, all reported reduced hair shedding and increased hair thickness. Notably, the four patients with partial hair repigmentation were part of this group. No clinical improvement was observed in male patients. Only one females reported mild pruritus.

Discussion

We hypothesize that melatonin exerts antioxidant effects by neutralizing free radicals and reactive oxygen species, thereby protecting melanocytes from oxidative damage [4,5]. Additionally, melatonin may promote hair growth via stimulation of matrix cell proliferation [1]. Topical melatonin may exert a positive effect on hair pigmentation regulation by mitigating oxidative damage to follicular melanocytes; it demonstrated a favorable safety and tolerability in a case series [1]. Study limitations include the small sample size and its design as a case series.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that melatonin 1% solution is a therapeutic alternative for hair graying and telogen effluvium, but further research in larger controlled studies is needed to establish optimal dosing and treatment protocols and to elucidate the mechanisms by which topical melatonin may prevent or reverse hair graying.

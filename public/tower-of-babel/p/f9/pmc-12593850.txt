Berberine and berberrubine promote the expression of CYP3A4 via enhancing the binding of nuclear receptor PXR
Authors: Gui-mei Xiao, Ting Lu, Dandan Zhu, Ming-yi Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBerberine is currently co-administered with statins in clinical practice for hyperlipidemia management. This study aimed to investigate the effects of berberine and berberrubine on CYP3A4 expression and the regulatory mechanism.MethodsUsing rifampicin as a positive control group, HepG2 cells were treated with different concentrations of berberine and berberrubine. Introduction

Berberine (BBR), an isoquinoline alkaloid extracted from Coptis chinensis (Ranunculaceae) and Hydrastis canadensis, has been traditionally used in Chinese herbal medicine [1]. Berberrubine (BRB) is the primary metabolites of BBR generated via hepatic cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes. Notably, pharmacokinetic studies indicate that plasma concentration of BRB is 10–50 fold higher than the parent drug BBR [2]. Similar to BBR, BRB exhibits diverse pharmacological activities, including hypoglycemic, hypolipidemic, and antitumor effects [3]. Due to its distinct lipid-lowering mechanism compared to statins, BBR is currently co-administered with statins in clinical practice for hyperlipidemia management [4].

Nuclear receptors, a class of ligand-activated transcription factors, play pivotal role in regulating drug-metabolizing enzymes. Among them, the pregnane X receptor (PXR) is a key regulator of CYP3A4, a critical enzyme responsible for metabolizing more than 60% of therapeutic drugs [5]. Mechanistically, ligand-bound PXR forms heterodimers with retinoid X receptor alpha (RXRα), translocates to the nucleus, and binds to xenobiotic response elements in the promoter region of CYP3A4, thereby enhancing its transcription [6].

Emerging evidence suggests that BBR may induce drug-drug interactions via PXR-mediated CYP3A4 upregulation. Preclinical studies report that chronic BBR administration reduces the area under the curve (AUC) of lovastatin in rats, likely due to accelerated hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 induction [7]. Consistently, Liu et al. demonstrated that co-administration of BBR with simvastatin significantly decreases plasma exposure to simvastatin acid in rodents, potentially attributable to CYP3A4 induction [8]. These findings highlight a plausible link between PXR/CYP3A4 activation by BBR or its metabolites and altered statin metabolism.

To elucidate the underlying mechanism, the present study uses HepG2 cell as the model and employs RT-qPCR, Western blot, RNA interference, and dual-luciferase reporter assays to systematically investigate the effects of BBR and BRB on CYP3A4 expression via the PXR pathway. Our results provide novel insights into the molecular basis of BBR-statin interactions and guide optimization of combination therapies.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Berberine hydrochloride, with a content of 86.8% and a batch number of 100,590–200,802, was provided by the National Institutes for Food and Drug Control. Berberrubine, with a content of 98% and a batch number of 511,130,804, was purchased from Chengdu Dest Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Rifampicin, with a content of 97% and a batch number of 100,590–200,802, was provided by the National Institutes for Food and Drug Control. EasyScript® First - Strand cDNA Reverse Transcription Reagent and Q - PCR Kit were purchased from Beijing TransGen Biotech Co., Ltd. DMEM high - glucose medium and fetal bovine serum were purchased from Hyclone, USA.

Cell culture

HepG2 cells (human hepatocellular carcinoma cells) were purchased from Shanghai Nuobai Biotechnology Co., Ltd, and cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum under the conditions of 37 °C and 5% CO2.

MTS assay for cell toxicity

Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 10^4 cells/mL, with 100 µL of cell suspension in each well. The blank medium was used as the blank control group. After culture for 12 h, the old medium was discarded. In the experimental group, 100 µL of drug - containing medium was added to each well. The series concentrations of berberine were 5, 10, 20, 40, 50, 60, 80 µM, the series concentrations of berberrubine were 1, 5, 15, 30, 60, 80, 100 µM, and the series concentrations of rifampicin were 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20 µM. A 100 µL medium solution containing 0.1% DMSO was used as the negative control group, and the blank medium was used as the blank control group. Each concentration in each group was set with 4 replicate wells. After treatment for 24–48 h, 20 µL of MTS solution was added to each cell well. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h with shaking. The absorbance of each well at a wavelength of 490 nm was measured using a microplate reader. After correcting the absorbance with the blank control group, the cell viability was calculated as (experimental group absorbance value / control group absorbance value)×100% as described previously [9].

PCR

Total RNA was isolated from treated HepG2 cells using trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Grand Island, NY, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, RNA with an OD260/OD280 between 1.8 and 2.0 was used for qPCR. 1.0 µg RNA was first reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a Transcriptor First-strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TransGen Biotech. Beijing, China) and Real-time Q-PCR was performed using Premix Ex Taq™ Probe qPCR (TaKaRa Biotech, Kyoto, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions in a Thermal Cycler Dice Real Time System. The following primers were used: CYP3A4, 5′-CCACTCACCCTGATGTC-3′ (forward) and 5’- AGCGTTTCATTCACCACC-AT-3’(reverse); GAPDH, 5’- CAGGAGGCATTGCTGATGAT-3′-(forward) and 5’-5′-GAAGGCTGGGGCTCATTT-3′ (reverse). The mRNA levels were calculated according to the comparative △△CT method and presented as relative fold of the control.

Western blot analyses

Total cell protein was extracted using RIPA buffer, separated by 10% SDS - PAGE gel electrophoresis and transferred to the membranes. The membranes were then blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h, incubated with the primary antibody overnight, washed with TBST, and then incubated with the secondary antibody at 37 °C for 1 h. After adding the luminescent solution to the PVDF membrane, it was immediately exposed in a gel imaging system. The gray value of the bands was calculated and analyzed using Image J software.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The distal regulatory sequences of DR-3 and ER-6 containing PXR binding elements in the promoter region of CYP3A4 (-7835/-7208) and the proximal regulatory sequence containing only ER-6 (-362/+53) were cloned into the pGL3-Basic vector to construct the pGL3-CYP3A4 reporter gene plasmid [10]. pTracer-hPXR and the empty pTracer-CMV2 vector were purchased from Maijie Biotech (NanTong, China). The reporter plasmid pGL3-CYP3A4, the empty plasmid pGL3-Basic, and the internal reference Renilla luciferase plasmid pRL-TK were purchased from Maijie Biotech. According to previous literature [11], transient transfection ratio of pGL3-CYP3A4- luc: pcDNA3.1-hPXR: pRL - TK at a mass ratio of 100:390:10 was selected.

HepG2 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into 24 -well plates at a density of 2 × 10^5 cells/mL. When the cell confluence reached 60–70%, Opti - MEM medium without serum and antibiotics was used to dilute and prepare Lipofectamine 3000. The transfection reagent and plasmid were mixed at a volume ratio of 1:3 and incubated at 37 °C for 15 min. Then cells were transfected with 100 µL of the above plasmid - lipid mixture. Subsequently, series - concentration berberine (5, 10, 25, 50 µM) and berberrubine (5, 15, 30, 60 µM) were used as the experimental groups, and rifampicin (10 µM) was used as the positive control group for PXR. After culture for 24 h, 100 µL of 1×PLB was added and shaken vigorously to lyse the cells. The lysate was centrifuged at 12,000 g at 4 °C for 2 min, then 20 µL of the supernatant was added to each well of the 96-well plate. 50 µL of pre - prepared LARⅡ reagent was added, and the fluorescence intensity was immediately detected using a multimode microplate reader, which represented the firefly luciferase activity. Then, Stop&Glo reagent was useed to terminate the reaction, and Renilla luciferase activity was measured.

shRNA-PXR

shRNA - hPXR fragment was provided by Maijie Biotechnology Co., Ltd with upstream sequence 5′ - CACCGGAGGTGAGACCCAAAGAAAGCGAACTTTCTTTGGGTCTCACCTCC − 3′, and downstream sequence 5′ - AAAAGGAGGTGAGACCCAAAGAAAGTTCGCTTTCTTTGGGTCTCACCTCC − 3′. The shRNA - hPXR fragment was transfected into HepG2 cells, and the corresponding shRNA - NC was used as negative control. Then, series - concentration berberine (10, 25, 50 µM) and berberrubine (15, 30, 60 µM) were added as the experimental groups, and rifampicin (10 µM) was used as positive control group for PXR. After 48 h treatment, the expression of CYP3A4 protein was measured.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 5.0 was used to analyze the experimental data. One way analysis of variance was used to compare the differences between the experimental groups. P < 0.05 indicated that the difference was significant.

Results

Drug toxicity on HepG2 cells

The MTS assay showed that berberine, berberrubine, and positive control rifampicin decreased the viability of HepG2 cells in a dose and time dependent manner. Cells treated with 5–50 µM BBR, 1–60 µM BRB, or 0.5–20 µM RIF for 24–48 h maintained more than 80% viability (Supplemental Fig. 1).

BBR and BRB upregulated the expression of CYP3A4 in HepG2 cells

As illustrated in Fig. 1, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that treatment of HepG2 cells with berberine and berberrubine for 48 h significantly upregulated CYP3A4 mRNA expression in a dose dependent manner compared to the vehicle control (0.1% DMSO). The positive control rifampicin (RIF; 10 µM) induced a 13-fold increase in CYP3A4 mRNA levels. The estimated half-maximal effective concentrations (EC50) were 8.2 ± 1.2 µM for BBR and 9.6 ± 1.9 µM for BRB (n = 3). As shown in Fig. 2, Western blot analysis demonstrated that 48-hour exposure to 10 µM RIF, 50 µM BBR, and 60 µM BRB significantly elevated CYP3A4 protein levels by 3.1-fold, 2.5-fold, and 2.7-fold, respectively (P < 0.01 vs. control).

BBR and BRB upregulated the expression of nuclear receptor PXR

To elucidate the mechanism of action of BBR and BRB on CYP3A4 expression, we examined nuclear receptor PXR in the nucleus of HepG2 cells. The results indicated that 10 µM RIF could upregulate PXR by 4.5 times, while 50 µM BBR and 60 µM BRB upregulated PXR by 3.4 and 1.9 times, respectively. Compared with the blank group, the differences were significant (P < 0.05, Fig. 3). The findings demonstrated that both 10–50 µM BBR and 15–60 µM BRB could increase the expression of PXR nuclear protein in a dose-dependent manner.

Silencing PXR downregulated CYP3A4 expression activated by berberine and berberrubine

To further investigate the potency of PXR activated by BBR or BRB on regulating CYP3A4 expression, HepG2 cell models with silenced PXR was constructed. ShRNA - hPXR or shRNA - NC plasmids were transfected into HepG2 cells, and then serial concentrations of berberine or berberrubine were added, with rifampicin as positive control. After 48 h treatment, compared with the control group, 10 µM rifampicin, 50 µM berberine, and 60 µM berberrubine upregulated the expression of CYP3A4 by 2.7, 1.8, and 2.3 times, respectively. After silencing PXR, 10 µM rifampicin, 50 µM berberine, and 60 µM berberrubine upregulated the expression of CYP3A4 by only 0.95, 0.92, and 0.97 times, respectively. The inductive abilities of the drugs were significantly reduced (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Metabolic drug drug interactions refer to the phenomena where the absorption, distribution, and metabolism of drugs are altered due to the inhibition or induction of metabolic enzymes when two or more drugs are taken simultaneously. Clinically, changes in the activity of CYP450 enzymes account for more than 90% of metabolic drug - drug interactions [12]. According to the guidelines of the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America [13], 10 µM rifampicin was selected as positive inducer of CYP3A4 in hepatocytes, and it induced the expression of CYP3A4 mRNA by 13-fold, indicating that CYP450 enzyme induction model in this experiment met the standards. This study demonstrated that berberine and its metabolite berberrubine significantly induced the expression of CYP3A4 in HepG2 cells by activating nuclear receptor PXR. Moreover, the inductive effect of berberrubine was stronger than that of parent drug berberine, suggesting that the metabolite plays an important pharmacological role in vivo. In addition, silencing PXR completely blocked the regulatory effects, confirming that this effect is PXR dependent. This finding suggests that when berberine is used in combination with CYP3A4 substrates in the clinic, attention should be paid to the risk of accelerated metabolism caused by long term administration.

An early study reported that the Cmax of berberine in human plasma was about 0.4 ng/ml and the concentration of berberine on the terminal elimination phase were below 0.1 ng/ml after single oral dose of 400 mg berberine [14]. Another study showed that berberrubine had 10-fold higher plasma concentration than that of berberine, due to more efficient intestinal absorption, suggesting that berberrubine is potentially more pharmacologically active than berberine [2]. Therefore, berberine is metabolized in the liver and plasma concentration of berberine is very low. CYP3A4 contributes to phase I metabolism of berberine, particularly in the liver. While other enzymes such as CYP2D6 and CYP1A2 are more prominent in the metabolism of berberine, CYP3A4 is involved in producing metabolites such as demethyleneberberine (M3) and thalifendine (M2) [15].

On the other hand, berberine can inhibit the activity of recombinant CYP3A4 enzymes in vitro [13]. Here we showed that berberine induced the expression of CYP3A4 in HepG2 cells, consistent with previous results [7, 16]. These data suggest that berberine may have a dual effect on CYP450 enzymes. In the cell model, berberine can induce the expression of CYP450 enzymes, but it can also bind to the active sites of some metabolic enzymes and inhibit their activities. When berberine is used in combination with CYP3A4 substrates, especially after long term administration of berberine, whether it promotes or inhibits the metabolism of the substrates remains to be further evaluated by studying its effect on CYP3A4 enzyme activity.

The dual luciferase reporter is used to study covalent binding between transcription factors and the cis - acting elements in the promoter region of the target gene [17]. In this experiment, to explore the role of transcription factor PXR in the regulation of CYP3A4 by berberine and berberrubine, pGL3-PXR-CYP3A4-luc dual luciferase reporter was constructed. Based on this, the effects of serial concentrations of berberine and berberrubine on the activity of the reporter gene were studied. Compared with control group, 10, 25, and 50 µM berberine upregulated luciferase activity of pGL3 -PXR- CYP3A4 - luc by 1.2, 1.4, and 2.3 times, respectively. 15, 30, and 60 µM berberrubine upregulated luciferase activity of reporer plasmid by 1.1, 1.3, and 1.8 times, respectively.

RNA interference is a technology that mediates the silencing of specific gene sequences by double stranded RNA and can block gene expression at the transcriptional, post-transcriptional, or translational levels to dissect molecular mechanism of diseases such as cancer [18–21]. To confirm the role of nuclear receptor PXR in the regulation of CYP3A4 by berberine and berberrubine, PXR was silenced using small RNA interference technology, and the changes in the regulation of CYP3A4 protein by berberine and berberrubine were detected. The results showed that, compared with the control group, 10 µM rifampicin, 50 µM berberine, and 60 µM berberrubine upregulated the expression of CYP3A4 by 2.7, 1.8, and 2.3 times respectively. After silencing PXR, 10 µM rifampicin, 50 µM berberine, and 60 µM berberrubine upregulated the expression of CYP3A4 by only 0.95, 0.92, and 0.97 times respectively. The difference was significant compared with control group, indicating that silencing PXR can significantly reduce the inductive effects of berberine and berberrubine on CYP3A4.

In conclusion, berberine and berberrubine can promote the expression of CYP3A4 protein in HepG2 cells by inducing nuclear receptor PXR to bind to the promoter of CYP3A4. Our results suggest that the possibility of drug-drug interactions should be considered when berberine is used in combination with CYP3A4 substrates clinically.

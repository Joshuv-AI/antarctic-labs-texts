Numerical simulation of velocity gradient scattering waves in visco-acoustic smooth media based on F-K domain integration method
Authors: Tao Ou, Guangming Chen, Yubing Yang, Chuan Ren, Zongtao Wang, Pengbo Lan, Jijun Wu, Huizhou Liu, Hu Rongjie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA popular attenuation velocity model for complete waveform inversion and seismic migration, the visco-acoustic (VA) smooth velocity model has first-order or high-order partitioned continuous derivatives. To compute the gradient scattering field caused by velocity gradient changes in the acoustic smooth velocity, we provide an integral technique in the acoustic F-K domain. We conduct Taylor expansion on the disturbance term and transform the velocity disturbance term into a velocity gradient term, in contrast to the conventional F-K domain integration approach that retrieves the scattering field caused by medium disturbance. Next, we compute the gradient scattering field in the VA smooth velocity model using the phase-shift interpolation (PSPI) technique. We examine the propagation law of gradient scattering waves in VA medium using the F-K domain integration technique in combination with the Futterman model. We provide a simulation approach for velocity gradient scattering wave numbers in the VA smooth medium, based on the numerical simulation technique in the F-K domain. The numerical study’s results demonstrate that the viscosity of VA medium reduces the amplitude of seismic waves in that medium. The thicker seismic wave phase axis indicates the loss of certain high-frequency components. The amplitude of the seismic gradient scattering waves decreases in the smooth velocity model. After smoothing, the velocity gradient shift is relatively small, and the amplitude of the gradient scattering wave signal in the forward modeling of the viscous F-K domain method is greater than that in VA FD and VA Generalized Born approximation (VA GB) methods. The results of the numerical analysis have validated the effectiveness of the method.Keywords: Visco-acoustic smooth model, F-K domain integration method, Gradient scattering, Numerical simulation, Quality factorSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The visco-acoustic (VA) smooth velocity model consists of velocity model and attenuation model with first-order or high-order partitioned continuous derivatives, widely used in seismic migration and full waveform inversion1,2. Compared to the actual underground velocity distribution, there is no velocity discontinuity (first-order discontinuity) in the smooth velocity model, and only velocity derivative discontinuity (high-order discontinuity) exists. The gradient scattering arising from variations in velocity parameters within the VA smooth velocity model3,4 can induce amplitude attenuation and velocity dispersion of seismic waves as they propagate through viscoelastic media, unlike the scattering generated by discontinuities in medium parameters in the actual velocity distribution. Therefore, the quality of migration imaging is enhanced by the advanced modeling of the VA smooth velocity gradient scattering waves.

WKBJ iterative approach5–7, finite difference method8,9, generalized Born approximation and its extension10–12, F-K domain integration method3,13,14, and additional methods15–19 are the primary types of methods used to calculate scattering gradients. The wavefield iteratively solves the first-order differential equation, which is the primary function of the WKBJ iterative approach. The gradient scattering field produced by the gradient change of medium parameters in one-dimensional media may be precisely calculated using the WKBJ iterative approach while moving away from singular spots (such as caustics)7. Although the conventional finite difference method can calculate the gradient scattering field in smooth velocity models, the numerical simulation of the finite difference method is affected by factors such as the grid. If the grid spacing and time sampling interval are not selected appropriately, it will lead to waveform distortion8; In addition, the finite difference method can only solve the total field and cannot solve the gradient scattering field separately3. The generalized Born approximation uses the error between the exact Green function and the approximate Green function to represent the scattering term11. At the same time, the integrand function in the generalized Born approximation is related to the variation of velocity gradient (density gradient), so it can better analyze the influence of velocity gradient variation on the gradient scattering field. However, the generalized Born approximation is derived based on asymptotic ray theory (ART) and still requires traditional ray theory to calculate the Green function. Therefore, the generalized Born approximation also has the problem of failure near singular points (such as focal points)12. In response to this issue, Sun Jianguo et al. proposed the GBeam Born approximation11 and GBeam Rytov approximation11 based on the generalized Born approximation idea. The FK domain integration method belongs to the semi-analytical method and can effectively describe the gradient scattering law20. Specifically, it uses the Fourier transform to transform the scattering integration equation in the Lippmann-Schwinger equation from the frequency space domain to the frequency wavenumber domain, and we use the Green function in the frequency wavenumber domain to solve the Lippmann-Schwinger Eq.3. Wu et al.13 used the phase screen operator to implement scattering field calculation based on the FK domain integration method. This method first uses the phase screen operator to calculate the first-order field, and it uses the FK domain integration method to obtain the scattering field received by the surface. Liu Tiehua14 implemented scattering field calculation based on the FK domain integration method using the finite difference method. Previous researchers used the FK domain integration method to calculate the scattering field generated by a medium disturbance in actual velocity models. By using the Taylor expansion of the disturbance component in the integrated function, Liu Bo3 was able to extract the FK domain integration formula associated with the velocity gradient and calculate the gradient scattering field in smooth velocity models. However, the F-K domain integration technique for velocity gradient scattering in the VA smooth medium has not been disclosed, and the aforementioned research did not take the medium’s attenuation characteristics into account.

This article breaks down VA medium into velocity perturbation parameters (perturbation velocity parameter and perturbation quality factor parameter) and background parameters (background velocity and background quality factor) using the perturbation theory. The perturbation term in the integrand function is Taylor expanded to transform the scattering field described by the medium perturbation into a gradient scattering field related to the velocity gradient change. The F-K domain integration formula is obtained from the VA complex velocity gradient representation, and we apply the phase shift plus difference approach to determine the complex velocity scattering gradient scattering wavefield in the VA medium. To evaluate the method, we employ three models: layered, concave, and real.

Discussion on processing methods under horizontal complex velocity changes

(1) The generalized F-K domain method23 is a frequency wavenumber domain method for one-way wave extension in any changing medium. Unlike f-k or phase shift methods that use plane waves separately, the generalized F-K domain method simultaneously uses the entire plane wave spectrum to solve the coupling between plane waves caused by lateral non-uniformity.

(2) Multi-domain algorithm24–26. The basic idea of the multi-domain algorithm is to decompose the velocity field into an intra-layer constant velocity background and an intra-layer variable velocity disturbance. The background field is equivalent to solving the constant velocity acoustic equation, which can be achieved through a phase shift. For variable speed disturbances, this non-uniformity can be considered as a scattering source (secondary source), and the incident wavefield acts on these scattering sources, thereby generating a scattering wavefield. Multi-domain algorithms use the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to perform round-trip calculations between the spatial-wavenumber domain, achieving multi-domain (spatial domain and wavenumber domain) operations. The main advantage of multi-domain migration technology is that it does not produce discrete grid dispersion phenomena and has high computational efficiency; It can perform high-angle and lateral variable velocity imaging. By disregarding the reverberation brought on by back-and-forth propagation between non-uniformities, the multi-domain migration operator can be thought of as an ultra-wide-angle one-way wave propagation operator27,28. It can, however, accurately handle wave phenomena associated with multiple forward scattering, including interference, diffraction, refraction, and wave focusing.

Conclusion

A popular attenuation velocity model for complete waveform inversion and seismic migration imaging, the VA smooth velocity model has first-order or high-order partitioned continuous derivatives. In contrast to the real subsurface velocity distribution, the smooth velocity model only exhibits velocity derivative discontinuity (high-order discontinuity) rather than velocity discontinuity (first-order discontinuity). When seismic waves propagate through viscous material, they may experience velocity dispersion and amplitude attenuation. In contrast to the scattering resulting from the discontinuity of medium parameters in the actual velocity distribution, gradient scattering is produced by the fluctuation of parameter gradients (velocity parameter gradient and quality factor parameter gradient) in the VA smooth velocity model. We provide an integral method in the acoustic F-K domain to calculate the gradient scattering field due to velocity gradient variations in the acoustic smooth velocity. We conduct Taylor expansion on the disturbance term and transform the velocity disturbance term into a velocity gradient term, in contrast to the conventional F-K domain integration approach that retrieves the scattering field caused by medium disturbance. Next, we use the phase-shift interpolation (PSPI) approach to calculate the gradient scattering field in the VA smooth velocity model. We examine the propagation law of gradient scattering waves in VA medium using the F-K domain integration technique in combination with the Futterman model. We provide a simulation approach for velocity gradient scattering wave numbers in the VA smooth medium, based on the numerical simulation technique in the F-K domain. The numerical study’s results demonstrate that the viscosity of the VA medium reduces the amplitude of seismic waves in that medium. The thicker seismic wave phase axis indicates the loss of certain high-frequency components. The amplitude of the seismic gradient scattering waves decreases in the smooth velocity model. After smoothing, the velocity gradient shift is relatively small, and the amplitude of the gradient scattering wave signal in the forward modeling of the viscous F-K domain method is greater than that in the VA FD and VA Generalized Born approximation (VA GB) methods. The results of the numerical analysis have validated the effectiveness of the method.

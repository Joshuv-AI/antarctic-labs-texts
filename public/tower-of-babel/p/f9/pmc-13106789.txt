AI-powered Chatbot integration for enhanced accessibility of electronic health records in a pediatric hospital
Authors: Premasudha Basavaiah Gowramma, Kiran Kumar, Monish Shiva Prema, Shivaprakash Virupakshaiah, Mohamed Rahamathulla, Mohamed Ghouse, Mohammed Muqtader Ahmed, Ismail Pasha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNavigating electronic health records (EHRs) remains a time-consuming task for clinicians, especially in resource-constrained healthcare settings. This study aimed to assess whether a lightweight chatbot, built using LangChain and GPT-3.5, could reduce search time and improve retrieval accuracy for CSV-based pediatric EHRs in a district hospital. A prototype EHR system was developed using Flask and Angular and integrated with a LangChain CSV agent capable of generating Python code dynamically to query anonymized data. The dataset consisted of 1,200 pediatric encounters, each comprising 160 variables. Forty clinicians performed 240 standardized information-seeking tasks both with and without the chatbot. The hospital’s legacy EHR system, which relied on basic keyword search, served as the baseline for comparison. Search time and retrieval accuracy—measured using precision and recall—were evaluated using two-tailed paired t-tests with a significance threshold of α = 0.05. The chatbot reduced the median search time from 64 s to 38 s, representing a 40.6% improvement (p < 0.001). It also increased F1 retrieval accuracy from 0.71 to 0.89, a 25.4% gain (p < 0.01). These results demonstrate that low-cost conversational AI can significantly accelerate and improve access to structured pediatric EHR data while maintaining data privacy by avoiding the exposure of raw patient records to external models.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Langchain, Chatbot, Electronic Health Records, Patient careSubject terms: Health care, Medical research

Introduction

The recent technological advances have revolutionized data access and management in healthcare systems. Electronic health records (EHRs) have become a vital part of modern healthcare organizations, allowing for the easy storage, retrieval, and management of patient medical information1–3. EHRs are longitudinal digital records generated across different care delivery venues with the goal of providing a comprehensive perspective of a patient’s medical history and ensuring continuity of care4.

Physicians often face challenges in extracting essential information quickly from the increasing bulk and complexity of medical records, such as navigating through multiple sections or locating specific data amidst extensive information5. Research published in JAMA Network Open found that physicians spend an average of 16 min each patient encounter exploring EHR systems, with over half (50%) of the time dedicated to searching for relevant information6. Similarly, a Stanford Medicine study indicated that above 74% of physicians believe EHRs contribute to inefficiencies in clinical operations7. Despite their benefits, EHRs have significant accessibility and usability issues that impede clinical efficiency. Key obstacles includes exacerbated by fragmented data platforms, limited system interoperability, non-intuitive user interfaces, and the high costs of complex information technology infrastructure.These tasks are particularly burdensome in low-resource healthcare settings8–10. These problems emphasize the urgent need for more intelligent, user-friendly, and accessible data retrieval technologies.

Recent breakthroughs in artificial intelligence, particularly huge Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-3, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4, have demonstrated significant potential for enhancing access to huge clinical datasets through contextual comprehension, automated summarization, and clinical decision support11–14. However, most existing EHR retrieval systems continue to use structured query techniques, keyword-based searches, or database-dependent structures, which frequently lack contextual knowledge and flexibility15. Furthermore, many chatbot frameworks demand structured databases (e.g., SQL) and extensive technical knowledge, rendering them unsuitable for healthcare settings where patient records are typically maintained in unstructured or CSV-based formats16–18.

Thus, there is a significant need for a simple, low-cost, and privacy-preserving solution that allows natural language querying directly over structured, CSV-based EHRs—a common but underserved format in resource-constrained healthcare settings. To address these gaps, this work focuses on the development and integration of an AI-powered conversational chatbot into an EHR system for the Pediatrics Department at Tumkur District Hospital in Karnataka, India19,20. The suggested system is intended to manage extremely complex patient records, each with up to 160 different data fields. Our solution uses a conversational AI-driven pipeline in which patient data are stored in CSV format and analyzed by a LangChain-based conversational agent that is integrated with a big language model21,22.

Unlike traditional AI-based medical data retrieval systems, which send entire patient records directly to large-scale AI models, the proposed approach uses a Python-based agent to improve data confidentiality and privacy. The Python agent generates structured queries from user input and obtains only the essential information, reducing unwanted data exposure and facilitating compliance with healthcare data privacy rules. The LangChain agent interfaces with a Pandas DataFrame and a Python agent to run LLM-generated code, allowing for intelligent, context-aware, and safe data extraction23,24.

In contrast to existing chatbot platforms such as Google Dialogflow and Microsoft Bot Framework, which typically require structured databases and advanced query formulation, the proposed system uses LangChain’s CSV Agent in conjunction with OpenAI’s GPT-3.5 Turbo to allow natural language interaction with unstructured CSV-based EHRs. This approach avoids the requirement for database normalization or SQL expertise, resulting in a lightweight, cost-effective solution ideal for low-resource healthcare environments25,26. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first implementation of LangChain’s CSV Agent for improving medical record accessibility using conversational AI.

Overall, this study adds to the emerging field of AI-driven healthcare informatics by demonstrating a feasible, secure, and scalable deployment of LLM-based conversational agents in real-world hospital EHR systems27,28. The proposed approach has the potential to significantly improve clinical efficiency, particularly in time-critical environments such as emergency and pediatric care, while maintaining patient data confidentiality through privacy-by-design principles. The novelty of our study integrates LangChain-based chatbot functionality within EHR systems to enhance query responses using natural language processing, improving accessibility and reducing search time for healthcare professionals.

Chatbot development and integration

In our Electronic Health Record (EHR) System implementation, we took inspiration from a relevant paper that used Xatkit for chatbot development. However, we decided to utilize the LangChain framework to build our chatbot. The suggested chatbot system facilitates natural language querying of CSV-based EHR data by dynamically producing executable Python code from user queries32.

GPT-3.5 was chosen for its balance of performance, cost-effectiveness, and natural language understanding, making it well-suited for querying structured medical records in real time. Unlike GPT-3, which had limitations in coherence, and GPT-4, which is more costly, GPT-3.5 offers improved contextual accuracy while remaining efficient. Alternative approaches like rule-based chatbots lack flexibility, SQL-based querying requires manual input, and fine-tuned transformer models (e.g., BioBERT, MedGPT) demand extensive training on medical datasets, making them resource-intensive. Our approach ensures data security by using a Python agent that generates structured queries instead of sending raw patient data to the AI model, making it both privacy-compliant and scalable for real-world healthcare applications33,34.

The system architecture illustrated in Fig. 1 enables easy and user-friendly interactions between healthcare professionals and the digital medical data repository through the Electronic Health Record (EHR) chatbot. The user interface is the first step, where healthcare professionals enter any questions or prompts to get information from patient records and medical data. This user input starts a series of operations that use machine learning and natural language processing (NLP) to their full potential. The Firestore database, which safely holds the most recent medical information and patient records, is connected to the chatbot’s backend. The system pulls the most recent information from Firestore when a query is started. The fetched data is then sent to the Flask framework, a lightweight web framework for Python, which handles incoming requests and data processing. Here, the data is converted into a structured JSON format and subsequently into a CSV (Comma-Separated Values) file. This CSV file serves as the structured input data for the LangChain CSV Agent, a crucial component of the architecture.

System architecture of EHR ChatBot.

The Langchain CSV agent facilitates the interaction with CSV files by using OpenAI’s GPT-3 language model to generate Python code based on user queries. When a user submits a question, the agent intelligently transforms it into a Python function designed for the specific task35. This function is then passed to the GPT-3 model, which generates the executable code. Upon execution, the results are promptly returned to the user, streamlining the process of querying CSV files with ease. The Langchain CSV agent simplifies this procedure, making it a user-friendly tool for anyone working with CSV data. Additionally, with the help of the GPT 3.5 LLM model, it generates a response in natural language tailored to the user’s query, as shown in Fig. 2.

Example response generated by the LangChain CSV agent integrated with GPT-3.5, illustrating how a clinician’s natural language query is converted into structured Python code, executed on anonymized CSV-based EHR data, and returned as a contextualized natural language response.

Once the response is generated, it is sent back to the user interface via the Flask framework. The response is typically formatted in JSON or a user-friendly format and displayed to healthcare professionals. This architecture streamlines the retrieval and presentation of medical information, allowing healthcare professionals to efficiently access patient records and receive contextual answers to their queries, thereby enhancing the utility and effectiveness of chatbots in healthcare settings.

Algorithm.

LangChain CSV agent

The Langchain CSV agent with OpenAI functions can be used to query CSV files using natural language and the power of OpenAI’s GPT-3 language model. It works by first converting the user’s question into a Python function. The function is then passed to OpenAI’s GPT-3 language model, which generates Python code that can be used to execute the function. The Python code is then executed, and the results are returned to the Langchain CSV agent. The Langchain CSV agent then generates a response in natural language tailored to the user’s query.

Discussion and conclusions

Our work presents an innovative solution that extends beyond medical records, offering potential applications in finance, research, and other data-intensive fields. By integrating an AI-powered chatbot into EHR systems, we have demonstrated the effectiveness of natural language queries for swift and accurate information retrieval. This approach rationalizes data navigation and enhances user experience, making it particularly valuable in high-pressure environments like healthcare, where quick access to information is crucial39.

Patient confidentiality and data security are major issues when utilizing third-party AI platforms, such OpenAI’s GPT-3.5 Turbo. Directly sharing patient records with external AI models presents privacy problems due to the high level of sensitivity of medical data. Our system uses a Python agent that creates structured queries internally to overcome this issue, preventing raw patient data from being sent to GPT-3.5. This keeps patient data safe and complies with healthcare data privacy laws. Additionally, all data is stored and processed within a secure Firestore database, minimizing the risk of unauthorized access or data leakage40. Future research should explore on-premise or fine-tuned local AI models to further enhance data control and compliance with regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR. Responsible and ethical use of AI is a significant factor in healthcare deployments. The suggested chatbot was built to adhere to rigorous data governance rules, including anonymization, access restriction, and limited data exposure. By limiting the language model to query execution rather than direct data acquisition, the system adheres to ethical AI norms and facilitates compliance with healthcare privacy regulations. Such measures are critical to preserving doctor trust and ensuring the safe implementation of conversational AI in clinical settings41,42.

“Unlike prior studies relying on structured databases or rule-based chatbots6,31, our system democratizes access to EHRs by enabling natural language queries on raw CSV files. This innovation is particularly impactful in regions like rural India, where 78% of district hospitals use CSV formats due to limited IT budgets43,44. By integrating Langchain’s Python code generation with GPT-3.5’s contextual understanding, we bypass the need for expensive database systems, making our solution scalable and resource-efficient.”

Despite its advantages, our system has some limitations, while GPT-3.5 efficiently processes natural language queries, it may struggle with highly complex medical terminologies or ambiguous user inputs. Additionally, real-time scalability remains a challenge, as increased concurrent usage may affect response latency. Future research should focus on enhancing system adaptability and accuracy by incorporating domain-specific fine-tuned medical models, such as MedGPT or BioBERT, to improve understanding of medical terminology and diagnostics. Additionally, integrating text-to-speech capabilities could further enhance accessibility for visually impaired users or hands-free operation45–47.

Data security and ethical considerations

The use of third-party AI models like GPT-3.5 raises concerns about data security and compliance with healthcare regulations. We have ensured that data processing occurs in a controlled environment to prevent unauthorized access. OpenAI’s models do not retain user query data, and additional security layers, such as firewalls and access controls, have been implemented to safeguard patient records48,49.

Patient data privacy and potential leakage

Patient data is anonymized before processing to ensure compliance with HIPAA and GDPR regulations. The system does not store chat history, and all queries are processed in real-time without long-term retention.

Our secure, efficient, and privacy-conscious system, developed using Angular, Firebase, Flask, LangChain, and OpenAI’s GPT-3.5 Turbo model, marks a significant step forward in AI-driven data retrieval. By prioritizing data security, patient privacy, and compliance with healthcare regulations, this research contributes to the evolving landscape of AI in healthcare. With future advancements, this technology has the potential to revolutionize how professionals interact with complex datasets, ultimately enhancing decision-making, usability, and accessibility in a rapidly evolving digital era.

Research and system limitations

While our chatbot significantly improves data retrieval efficiency, it is limited in handling ambiguous or highly specialized medical queries. The current system relies on structured CSV data, which may not fully integrate with unstructured clinical notes. Further research is needed to incorporate multimodal AI models for better interpretation of diverse data formats in EHRs.

Future research directions

Future iterations should explore fine-tuning AI models on domain-specific medical datasets to improve accuracy. Integration with voice-based AI assistants for hands-free interaction can further enhance usability in clinical settings. Collaboration with regulatory bodies is essential to ensure AI-driven chatbots comply with evolving healthcare policies and standards50,51.

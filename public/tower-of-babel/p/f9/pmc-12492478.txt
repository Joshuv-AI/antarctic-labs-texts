Evaluating normothermic artery bypass and visceral-anastomosis-first strategy in thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair: propensity-weighted analysis
Authors: Shuai Zhang, Hongwei Guo, Cuntao Yu, Xiaogang Sun, Jing Sun, Xiangyang Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the effectiveness and safety of a normothermic artery bypass and visceral-anastomosis-first (NABV) strategy for thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair.MethodsFrom July 2017 to September 2024, a retrospective analysis was conducted on early postoperative complications in two groups of patients undergoing thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair. The analysis compared patients treated with a new strategy with those treated with deep hypothermic circulatory arrest, clarifying the protective effects of the new strategy on visceral organs, particularly the spinal cord.ResultsA total of 182 patients were included in the study; 73 in the NABV group and 109 in the DHCA group. After inverse probability of treatment weighting, the NABV group had lower incidences of spinal cord deficit, pulmonary complications, and gastrointestinal insufficiency, a shorter duration of mechanical ventilation, and a shorter hospital stay. Body mass index ≥ 24.0 (odds ratio 3.099, 95% confidence interval (c.i.) 1.051 to 9.142; P = 0.004) and coronary artery disease (odds ratio 4.848, 95% c.i. 1.169 to 20.102; P = 0.030) were independent risk factors for spinal cord deficit in the entire cohort, in contrast to the NABV strategy (odds ratio 0.283, 95% c.i. 0.039 to 0.806; P = 0.025), which was a protective factor. The multivariable Cox regression analyses identified smoking history (hazard ratio 2.61, 95% c.i. 1.12 to 6.05; P = 0.026) as an independent risk factor for overall survival.ConclusionThe treatment of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm through open surgery is still an important method and remains difficult. The NABV strategy, as a safe and reproducible technique, minimizes the risk of complications associated with spinal cord injury when implemented at experienced medical centres. To better evaluate the clinical outcomes of this surgical approach, long-term follow-up and further prospective cohort studies are necessary.

Introduction

Thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm (TAAA) makes up about 5% of all aneurysms, occurring at a rate of 1 per 10 000 new cases each year. TAAA with a diameter greater than 6 cm poses a yearly rupture risk of 7%, leading to rupture in 80% of patients eventually1. Surgical repair comes with significant risks, including the possibility of surgical death and severe postoperative issues including permanent stroke, paraplegia, and renal failure necessitating dialysis2. TAAA typically involves multiple thoracic and abdominal organs, making it a complex surgical procedure associated with high mortality rates and complications. Consequently, the surgical management of TAAA presents a significant area of complexity within the field of vascular surgery.

TAAA surgery carries a significant risk of spinal cord deficit (SCD), with reported rates ranging from 2 to 15% depending on aneurysm extent, aetiology, comorbidities, and surgical expertise3,4. Contemporary multimodal protective strategies—including cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) drainage, evoked potential monitoring, distal aortic perfusion, intercostal artery reimplantation, and hypothermia—have been widely adopted5–8. However, type II TAAA repairs still pose the highest spinal cord injury risk due to extensive segmental artery sacrifice. Recent evidence9,10 confirms that, despite advanced protocols, permanent spinal cord injury rates for type II TAAA remain substantial at 7.6–15.0% in high-volume centres, underscoring persistent challenges in spinal cord protection.

In recent years, open TAAA repair based on left heart bypass (LHB) or deep hypothermic circulatory arrest (DHCA) strategies has been widely used11. Although acceptable outcomes have been achieved with the above strategies, both have some limitations. On one hand, the utilization of LHB is hindered in certain medical institutions, including those in China, due to the unavailability of expensive and sophisticated circuit equipment and consumables required for this technique12,13. On the other hand, excessive hypothermia during DHCA may cause coagulation system disorders, affecting the operative field and even leading to lung injury and ventricular fibrillation14,15.

To ensure adequate protection of the viscera and spinal cord and to minimize bleeding and dependence on blood products, the authors have optimized existing techniques, and this enhanced approach is known as the normothermic artery bypass and visceral-anastomosis-first (NABV) strategy, which will be further elucidated in the detailed description that follows. The aim of this study was to assess the effectiveness and safety of the NABV strategy for TAAA repair.

Methods

Ethical statement

This study has been approved by the institutional review board (IRB) of the authors’ medical centre (Fuwai Hospital IRB No. 2023-2085) and complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. Study approval was obtained from the ethics committee and all participants provided written informed consent, which included agreement for their clinical data to be used and published in this study.

Participants

Between July 2017 and September 2024, patients with TAAA of Crawford types II and III whose surgical strategy involved reconstruction of segmental arteries, coeliac axis (CA), superior mesenteric artery (SMA), or renal artery (RA) were included in the research conducted at the Vascular Centre of Fuwai Hospital. Figure 1 illustrates patient inclusion/exclusion and group assignment. Criteria for repairing TAAA consisted of an aortic size over 55 mm, an aneurysm expansion rate of more than 5 mm annually, or signs of organ malperfusion such as renal dysfunction from RA occlusion or significant compression by the false lumen shown on computed tomography (CT) scans. Patients were grouped according to the actual surgical approach received.

Flow diagram of patient selection and group assignment

TAAA, thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm; F/BEVAR, fenestrated or branched endovascular aortic repair; CA, coeliac artery; SMA, superior mesenteric artery; RA, renal artery; NABV, normothermic artery bypass and visceral-anastomosis-first; DHCA, deep hypothermic circulatory arrest.

Notably, repair under DHCA was reserved for patients in whom the aortic cross-clamp could not be employed in the proximal part of the descending aorta (for example the diameter of the proximal aneurysm neck was >7 cm or there was severe calcification of the aorta) and those in whom the thoracic aorta was tightly adherent to the surrounding tissues, particularly to lung tissues. Operative mortality was characterized as death occurring within 30 days of the surgical procedure, including deaths that occurred during the surgery itself. Upon admission, aortic lesions were confirmed through echocardiography and CT scans of the aorta. Every patient who was discharged received follow-up care, either at the outpatient clinic or through telephone consultations.

Variable definition

All patients with TAAA were diagnosed by CT and echocardiography and categorized based on the Crawford classification. Primary endpoints were defined as either composite adverse events or long-term survival. Composite adverse events included 30-day death, SCD, persistent stroke, and persistent renal failure necessitating continuous dialysis. Stroke was characterized by newly apparent neurological deficits, either global or focal, clinically or on imaging. SCD was defined as ischaemic changes in the spinal cord (for example spinal cord oedema or signal abnormalities) demonstrated by postoperative magnetic resonance imaging or CT myelography, accompanied by persistent motor or sensory dysfunction of the lower extremities showing no improvement at discharge. Cases were classified by severity as paraplegia (paralysis of the legs and lower part of the body) versus paraparesis (slight or incomplete paralysis). This diagnosis requires exclusion of neurological dysfunction attributable to non-ischaemic aetiologies, such as stroke. Gastrointestinal insufficiency, defined as that occurring during index hospitalization, comprised gastrointestinal bleeding, bowel obstruction, mesenteric ischaemia, prolonged ileus (failure of full peristaltic function to recover within 5 days), bowel perforation or fistula, pancreatitis, hepatic insufficiency, and other related events. Complications were considered permanent when present at discharge or if the patient died earlier.

Surgeon procedural volumes

The study centre adopted a strict operation grading system in which only senior surgeons with extensive experience were eligible to perform TAAA repair. All the surgeons who participated in this study had performed at least 50 TAAA repairs before the study began. The NABV strategy was performed by one surgeon and the DHCA strategy was performed by more than one surgeon. For open repair of TAAA, the formula for calculating the annualized procedural volume of surgeons was the total number of procedures performed between 2017 and 2024 divided by the number of months between the first and last procedure during the study period, multiplied by 12. The annualized procedural volume serves as a comprehensive and standardized indicator for evaluating medical services across surgeons. Then, surgeons were ranked from highest to lowest volume and stratified by quartiles. Patients were then stratified by quartiles based on surgeon volume quartiles, with the first quartile (Q1) representing the lowest volume and the fourth quartile (Q4) representing the highest volume. For DHCA strategy, Q1 surgeons performed one procedure per year, Q2 surgeons performed two procedures per year, Q3 surgeons performed between three and six procedures per year, and Q4 surgeons performed more than six procedures per year.

Surgical procedures

TAAA repair with NABV strategy

General anaesthesia, with a nasal temperature > 35°C, warming blankets, and respiratory tract insulation device were applied to maintain body temperature in the normothermic range. A double-lumen endobronchial tube and nasogastric tube were used, and blood pressure monitoring of right upper and lower limbs, central venous catheterization, and CSF drainage were performed. Left femoral vein catheterization was used for rapid blood infusion. Through the left femoral or iliac vein cannula, a reservoir and the pump inside a conventional heart lung machine was set up as a rapid-infusion device to return the shed blood with the help of a right heart aspirator. Meanwhile cell salvage was used for blood infusion without heparin anticoagulation. Moderate doses of heparin (200 U/kg) were administered. The patient’s activated clotting time was verified to be ≥ 280 seconds. Bilateral RAs were perfused with cold histidine-tryptophan-ketoglutarate (HTK) solution intermittently.

The patient was placed in a right lateral decubitus position with the upper body at a 60 degree angle to the operating table and the hips at a 30 degree angle to the horizontal. A left posterolateral thoracoabdominal incision was made from just posterior to the inferior aspect of the left scapula, curving across the costal margin and extending inferiorly to just above the pubic symphysis. According to the extent of TAAA, the entry point can be selected from the fourth to seventh intercostal space. Moreover, double intercostals can be selected as the entry point for extent II TAAA repair. The abdominal aorta was exposed via retroperitoneal approach. It is important to dissect the following three parts of the aorta—proximal aorta of the TAAA, aorta proximal to the CA, and aorta distal to the RA—to facilitate the clamping of different aortic positions when performing TAAA repair.

The key to establishing autogenous arterial diversion lies in determining the appropriate site for proximal arterial cannulation, with common sites including the descending aorta and the left axillary artery. The preferred location for insertion is near to the proximal end of the proximal anastomosis. The exposure and intubation of the left axillary artery are relatively difficult, and during the surgical procedure the tube is prone to twisting. Therefore, this method is currently rarely used. The optimal site for cannulation is the proximal aorta of the proximal anastomosis, but due to the extent of the aneurysm not all TAAAs can be successfully cannulated in this region, particularly in type II TAAAs. In such cases, it is possible to insert a tube at the distal aneurysm of the proximal anastomosis. However, one drawback of this approach is the transient ischaemia of the distal organs during proximal anastomosis.

When it was verified that the proximal aorta to the TAAA could be clamped without DHCA, arterial bypass was established. The inflow of the arterial bypass must be located at the proximal end of the TAAA and the blood pressure monitored. Finally the outflow of the arterial bypass was connected to a four-branched graft. In this way, each branch of the graft is filled with blood, and blood supply can be restored immediately after anastomosis.

Following the NABV strategy, the aorta was clamped proximal to the CA and distal to the RA, respectively. After clamping, the aortic segment between the two clamps was dissected longitudinally. Back-bleeding from the visceral and lumbar artery branches was collected with a right heart aspirator. The CA, superior and inferior mesenteric arteries, and left and right RAs were carefully identified. The mural thrombus was removed from the aneurysm. For patients with chronic aortic dissection, the intimal flap should be completely dissected to connect the true and false lumen. Heavily back-bleeding lumbar arteries were sutured. The left and right RAs were perfused intermittently with cold HTK solution. There was no need for visceral perfusion. The CA was reconstructed with a 10-mm branch. After the anastomosis, the back-bleeding condition of the SMA was visually inspected. In general, the communicated artery between them was rich and easily resulted in good back-bleeding. If present, the SMA was clamped. In very rare situations, the SMA has less back-bleeding, which indicates that it is necessary to immediately reconstruct the SMA with a 10-mm branch. The RAs were then reconstructed with a 10-mm branch in any sequence. One was anastomosed and the other was perfused with cold HTK solution. In most cases, the inferior mesenteric artery can be sutured, but for those with less back-bleeding it should be reconstructed with a 10-mm branch. Finally, the clamped SMA was reconstructed with a 10-mm branch.

After revascularization of the visceral branches, one-lung ventilation was used to keep the right lung inflated and collapse the left. The cross-clamp was then moved up for proximal clamping of the TAAA. The T8–L2 aorta containing the main feeding artery of the spinal cord was sutured to form an arterial tube, then anastomosed with the 10-mm branch for restoration of blood flow to the spinal cord. Subsequently, the proximal end of the four-branched aortic graft was anastomosed to the proximal thoracic aorta. The proximal cross-clamp was released and the inflow of the arterial bypass was clamped. At this time, the blood supply of the spinal cord and visceral branches comes from antegrade perfusion of the aorta. Left lung ventilation should be restored after the proximal anastomosis. A simplified procedure for anastomosis and revascularization is depicted in Fig. 2.

A simplified procedure for anastomosis and revascularization in the normothermic artery bypass and visceral-anastomosis-first strategy

a Establishment of auto-arterial bypass by cannulation in the descending aorta, b anastomosis of the coeliac trunk artery, cold crystalloid perfusion of the renal arteries, and sequential anastomosis of the superior mesenteric artery and both renal arteries, c anastomosis of the intercostal arteries and the distal aorta, d all vessels were anastomosed and finished.

For the distal anastomosis, the sequence can be selected flexibly. Reconstruction can be performed at the beginning, before visceral branches, or at the end, because the lower limb can tolerate ischaemia. According to the extent of the aneurysm involving the iliac artery, one can choose to use the distal end of the four-branched graft to anastomose to the distal abdominal aorta or use the branches to reconstruct the left and right iliac arteries, respectively.

TAAA repair with DHCA

Full-dose heparin (400 U/kg) was administered. The cerebral oxygen saturation was monitored to prevent desaturation below 10% relative to baseline values. The hypothermic circulatory arrest was initiated. Drainage of the vena cava was established through cannulation of the left external iliac vein. An arterial cannula was inserted into the left external iliac artery; the left heart drainage was established by inserting a tube into the apical incision or the main pulmonary artery, establishing cardiopulmonary bypass. Nasal and rectal temperatures were lowered to 20°C. The remainder of the procedure (anastomosis sequence and techniques) was performed similarly to the NABV strategy.

Statistical analysis

Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was performed to balance the baseline characteristics between the two groups. Baseline characteristics assumed to influence the outcomes of the patients were included in the propensity score model using logistic regression analysis, namely, age, male, body mass index (BMI), Type A dissection, Type B dissection, aneurysm without AD, Marfan syndrome, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, smoking, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, history of stroke, and coronary artery disease. A standardized difference of < 10% was considered ideal.

Categorical variables are presented as frequencies with percentages and were compared using the Pearson χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables are reported as the mean(standard deviation (s.d.)) or median with an interquartile range (i.q.r.) and were analysed by the Student’s t-test for normally distributed variables and the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed variables. P-values < 0.05 indicated statistically significant differences.

To address missing values while preserving statistical power and cohort integrity, multiple imputation by chained equations was used16. Ten imputed data sets were generated, with the imputation model incorporating all variables used in the propensity score model and primary outcome analyses. Analyses were performed separately on each imputed data set, and results were pooled using Rubin’s rules to obtain final estimates and standard errors. This approach assumes data are missing at random. Statistical analyses were performed using R™ 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Preoperative information

There were 124 men (68.1%) in the cohort, with 76 (69.7%) in the DHCA group, and 48 (65.8%) in the NABV group. Among the cohort, 49 patients (26.9%) presented with comorbid Marfan syndrome. The most prevalent pathological finding was chronic Stanford type B aortic dissection. Surgery and early postoperative outcomes

The patients’ intraoperative and early postoperative data are summarized in Table S2. Operating time data were missing for three patients (1.6%), and postoperative intensive care unit stay duration was missing for two patients (1.1%). Crucially, no missing data existed for primary endpoint events (for example spinal cord injury, death) or key covariates (including age, BMI, comorbidities, etc.). After IPTW, there was no statistically significant difference in 30-day death between the two groups. The duration of mechanical ventilation and hospitalization was significantly lower in the NABV group than in the DHCA group. The NABV group had lower incidences of SCD (1.8% versus 12.4%; P = 0.012), stroke (2.3% versus 7.0%; P = 0.032), pulmonary complications (17.0% versus 40.5%; P < 0.001), gastrointestinal insufficiency (14.7% versus 26.5%, P = 0.008), and thoracentesis (4.0% versus 13.5%; P = 0.003). The incidence of adverse events was 62 of 362 (17.1%): 16 of 177 (9.0%) in the NABV group and 46 of 185 (24.9%) in the DHCA group (P < 0.001).

As shown in Table S4, after excluding cases operated by surgeons in the lowest 25% of surgical volume in the DHCA group, the reanalysis of primary outcomes revealed that, compared with the DHCA (Q2–Q4) group, the NABV group showed no statistical significance in the incidence of stroke and thoracentesis. However, the incidence of SCD and the adverse events were still significantly lower in the NABV group.

Mid- and long-term postoperative survival and follow-up

A total of 182 patients were followed up, with a mean(s.d.) follow-up time of 41.1(23.9) months and a follow-up rate of 100.0%. There were 13 deaths during the follow-up period, including 9 (8.3%) in the DHCA group and 4 (5.5%) in the NABV group. Kaplan–Meier survival curves for mid- and long-term survival are shown in Fig. 3a. Postoperative survival rates at 1, 3, 5, and 7 years were 94.3%, 89.3%, 83.1%, and 76.7% in the DHCA group and 94.4%, 91.0%, 87.9%, and 82.8% in the NABV group, respectively (log rank P = 0.646). After IPTW, there was still no significant difference in survival curves (log rank P = 0.964) (Fig. 3b). Discussion

TAAAs involve a wide anatomic range, often encompassing almost all abdominal and thoracic visceral organs, making surgery inevitable but inherently prone to causing organ ischaemia. The complexity of surgical incisions, proximity of the operative field to vital structures, prolonged operation duration, and the risk of postoperative complications across multiple organ systems collectively make total thoracoabdominal aortic replacement a long-standing challenge in aortic surgery. In recent years, advances in surgical techniques, anaesthesia management, extracorporeal circulation, and postoperative monitoring have significantly enhanced the safety of this procedure.

However, as clamping of the aorta reduces the blood supply to the spinal cord when completing TAAA repair, spinal cord injury of varying degrees is often unavoidable. In clinical practice, a variety of neuroprotective techniques have been applied to reduce the incidence of SCD, including cerebrospinal fluid drainage, motor and sensory evoked potential monitoring, distal perfusion, intercostal artery reconstruction, and hypothermia5–8. Consequently, even with optimal protection including CSF drainage and intercostal reimplantation, contemporary series report permanent spinal cord injury rates of 7.6% for type II TAAA9. This persistent vulnerability highlights the unmet need for novel strategies specifically targeting high-risk repairs, wherein the present proposed technique aims to contribute.

Reducing the duration of interruption of the spinal cord blood supply during aortic clamping is the key to the exploration of surgical techniques. Currently, the most popular distal perfusion method internationally is left heart diversion11, and other methods include femoral–femoral diversion17,18, main iliac diversion4,19, left atrial–femoral artery diversion20, and so on. The common denominator of these methods is that, during proximal clamping, blood supply to the spinal cord, gastrointestinal tract, and lower extremities is provided by the above methods. However, the spinal cord distal to the thoracic segment is almost completely in a state of non-physiologic blood supply or ischaemia during the entire operation, which greatly increases the risk of SCD.

In this study, a NABV strategy was used. Unlike LHB and DHCA, the strategy of normothermic artery bypass does not require cooling during thoracoabdominal aortic replacement. Therefore, the time required for cooling and rewarming is reduced, and in addition normothermia has less impact on the body's internal environment and coagulation function, thus reducing intraoperative bleeding and the incidence of postoperative complications.

Some aortic surgery teams have experimented with spinal cord protection techniques. For example, Niranjan Hiremath and his colleagues21 reported excellent results in one case of TAAA repair using the modified branch-first technique. The present strategy has similarities with this technique, but there are differences in the details of surgical manoeuvres, sequence of vascular anastomoses, and adjuncts. The reconstruction of distal vessels such as visceral vessels was prioritized, during which the intercostal artery was physiologically supplied with blood and maintained in a high pressure state. Finally, the intercostal artery was reconstructed and anastomosed to the proximal anastomosis to ensure physiologic blood supply to the spinal cord for the longest period of time.

In the present study, in addition to satisfactory outcomes in terms of the rate of composite adverse events, the incidence of permanent paraplegia and persistent stroke, and the duration of mechanical ventilation and postoperative hospitalization was significantly lower in patients in the NABV group than in those in the DHCA group after IPTW. In addition, the NABV group had fewer pulmonary complications and incidences of postoperative thoracentesis. This may be related to the systemic inflammatory response that occurs after reperfusion following DHCA.

Multiple mechanisms, including endothelial cell injury, ischaemia perfusion injury, and abnormal complement activation in the blood, play a role in dysfunction following DHCA22. Compared with DHCA, normothermic circulatory arrest reduces systemic inflammation via multiple mechanisms. First, regarding the release of inflammatory mediators, whereas hypothermia delays the expression of inflammatory cytokines, it cannot completely prevent it; in contrast, normothermia results in a relatively reduced release of key mediators such as interleukin-6 and tumour necrosis factor-α, thereby directly attenuating the systemic inflammatory response. Second, DHCA and the rewarming process significantly affect the plasma proteome23. Hypothermia initially suppresses complement activation, but rewarming paradoxically induces elevated activation levels. Concerning coagulation maintenance24, normothermia minimally impacts coagulation function, preserving its normal operation, whereas hypothermia impairs platelet function and coagulation pathways, subsequently triggering inflammation due to coagulopathy. Additionally, normothermia aids in maintaining normal immune function and metabolic stability within an organism. This stability reduces intracellular calcium overload and reactive oxygen species generation during ischaemia–reperfusion, thereby mitigating tissue and organ damage and ultimately lowering the magnitude of the inflammatory response25.

At the same time, using multivariable logistic regression modelling and Cox regression modelling, it was noted that a history of smoking increased the risk of early and mid-to-long-term fatality in patients undergoing TAAA repair. Cigarette smoking has well-established detrimental effects on cardiovascular health, with a particular focus on atherosclerosis and atherothrombosis. A comprehensive review26,27 has highlighted the intricate mechanisms by which smoking contributes to such life-threatening conditions, including endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and atherothrombotic effects.

In addition, the multivariable logistic regression model in this study also showed that the NABV strategy was a protective factor for early composite adverse events and SCD in patients undergoing TAAA repair, and that BMI ≥ 24.0 and a history of coronary surgery were independent risk factors for the development of postoperative SCD. This suggests that it is important to focus on patients who are overweight or obese or have a history of coronary artery surgery in the perioperative period and provide them with more personalized care.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design inherently carries the risk of unmeasured confounding. Although IPTW was used to balance observed baseline characteristics, unmeasured factors (for example individual anatomical variations or intraoperative haemodynamic fluctuations) may have influenced postoperative outcomes. Prospective studies with standardized intraoperative monitoring and comprehensive data collection are needed to address this. Second, the mean follow-up duration of 41 months, while sufficient for analysing early complications and short-term survival, is insufficient for assessing long-term outcomes. Studies with extended follow-up are required to validate the NABV strategy’s long-term efficacy and safety. Additionally, the favourable early outcomes in this study may be partially attributed to the cohort’s younger age (mean(s.d.) 40.9(12.2) years) and fewer comorbidities. This age is significantly lower than most surgical centres report28,29, likely reflecting the higher prevalence of Marfan syndrome and aortic dissection in Chinese TAAA patients, potentially due to aortic pathology differences and suboptimal blood pressure control30. Reduced atherosclerotic changes and calcification in young patients facilitate cross-clamping at the proximal aneurysm neck, favouring normothermic surgery. However, this finding limits generalizability to elderly patients or those with atherosclerotic aneurysms. Finally, as a single-centre retrospective analysis with a limited sample size, the results require validation through larger multicentre prospective studies including cohorts with diverse aneurysm aetiologies and across different age groups to expand the applicability of this technique in broader populations and validate its efficacy.

Although the current data indicate the NABV strategy is a safe, effective TAAA treatment that reduces spinal cord injury complications, limitations in case volume and follow-up necessitate multi-centre validation. This study further demonstrated NABV’s superiority over DHCA, establishing its clinical utility in resource-limited settings where advanced perfusion support is constrained. To solidify its role in complex aortic repair, a multicentre prospective study enrolling patients to compare NABV with LHB is planned, aiming to define its function in standardized spinal cord protection protocols centred on systematic, multimodal, and individualized strategies.

An NABV strategy is a secure and replicable method, particularly when executed at an experienced centre, that can produce remarkable outcomes and reduce the risk of spinal cord injury complications. Long-term follow-up and additional prospective cohort studies are necessary to further evaluate the clinical outcomes of this surgical approach.

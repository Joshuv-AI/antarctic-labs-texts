Biopolymer-based nanoformulations accelerated tissue remodeling by antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and angiogenic potential
Authors: Kunal Kishore, Saranga Rajesh, Sneha Sivadas, Nandakumar Selvasudha, R. Barathidasan, A. Hannah Rachel Vasanthi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
OBJECTIVES:Designing a low-cost wound dressing material with better functionality enhances wound healing efficiently. Accordingly, a nanocomposite using pectin, keratin, ferulic acid, and silver nanoparticles exhibiting better wound healing potential was utilized to prepare gel and ointment to address inadequacies in wound management.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Subsequently, the physicochemical properties, drug release, antioxidant activity, antimicrobial efficacy, and wound healing potential of the silver nanocomposite-based nanoformulations were studied.RESULTS:Characterization revealed smooth texture and homogeneity in both formulations, with the gel being hydrophilic and easily washable, while the ointment was oleaginous. X-ray photoelectron spectrometer analysis indicated low levels of silver ions in the gel, potentially reducing dose-dependent toxicity compared to the ointment containing silver oxide. The gel exhibited sustained drug release attributed to Carbopol 940 and demonstrated slightly better antioxidant and antimicrobial activity. In-vitro studies on L929 fibroblast cells exhibited no toxicity, with the gel demonstrating better cell migration than the ointment. Interestingly, the cell cycle was altered by the nanoformulations, with more cells in the S and G1/M phase, confirming cell proliferation for wound repair. Excision wounds in SD rats revealed superior wound contraction and epithelialization with the gel formulations. The gel with 75 mg nanocomposite downregulated matrix metallo-proteinases 2 (MMP2) and MMP8 expression, indicating efficient tissue remodeling, and upregulation of keratinocyte growth factors and vascular endothelial growth factor, promoting faster wound healing due to angiogenesis, anti-inflammatory effects, and suppression of reactive oxygen species levels.CONCLUSIONS:Conclusively, the novel nanoformulations exhibited accelerated wound healing compared to a commercial silver gel by enhanced cell migration, reepithelialization, and tissue remodeling owing to the combined effect of the bioactive molecules.

Introduction

Wound healing is a multifaceted biological process that occurs in response to injury by quickening the cohesiveness of the impaired tissue and eventually mending the connective tissue remodeling. It also involves complex cellular and molecular mechanisms orchestrated by the body’s immune mechanism. Healing wounds is vital for maintaining the structural and functional homogeneity of wounded organs or tissue and preventing microbial infection.[1] However, wound healing involves immune cells such as macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells; several signaling molecules such as chemokines, cytokines, and immunoglobulins; and tissue remodeling enzymes such as matrix metallo-proteinases (MMPs), keratinocyte growth factors (KGFs), collagen, and fibroblasts.[2] At the onset of an injury, vasoconstriction of blood vessels and platelet aggregation occur to prevent bleeding, which involves many proteins such as thrombin, fibronectin, and platelets to prevent blood loss and infection. Subsequently, the inflammatory, proliferative, and tissue remodeling phase follows due to several factors and varies from individual to individual based on their health, underlying disease conditions, and nutritional status.[3]

Statistical medical records showed that about ∼8.2 million people have acute and chronic wounds, which cost up to 96.8 billion dollars in the United States. In India, the incidence rate of chronic wounds is estimated to be 4.5 per 1000 people, and the market for diabetic wounds is increasing.[4] Therefore, there is a dire need to unveil a suitable wound healing agent that accelerates the healing process without compromising on toxicity, eludes chronic wounds, thereby minimizing wound scars, and is economical.

The currently preferred wound healing formulations in the market contain silver sulfadiazine, 2-octyl cyanoacrylate, alevin, and artiss, which are synthetic and have been reported to cause skin irritation, redness, and swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in the tissue. Therefore, research has focused recently on nanotechnology-based drug delivery systems that help in wound care because of their superior qualities of targeted and synergetic drug delivery with enhanced solubility and biocompatibility. However, the toxicity concerns of nanoformulation still pose a challenge, which inspired us to formulate novel silver nanoparticle-based nanoformulations (gel and ointment) that contain naturally derived biomolecules such as pectin, keratin, and ferulic acid, which have been explored for their wound healing capacity by both in vitro and in vivo methods. Our earlier study on the ferulic acid-loaded nanocomposite exhibits impressive and notable biological activities in cell lines and animal models. Hence, the current study aims to identify and evaluate the mechanisms of the wound healing capacity of the nanocomposite-loaded nanoformulations on L929 fibroblast cell lines and SD rats for enhanced wound healing.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and reagents

Materials required to prepare ointment and gel, such as hard paraffin, cetostearyl alcohol, white soft paraffin, and Carbopol 940, were obtained from HiMedia, Mumbai. Materials for performing antimicrobial assays, such as Mueller–Hinton Agar, MH broth, sterile discs, antibiotic discs of gentamycin, and amoxicillin, were also procured from HiMedia, Mumbai. All additional analytical-grade reagents, including concentrated acids, acetone, and wool fat, were acquired from SRL and SISCO laboratories in Mumbai. Cell culture requirements such as media cryopreservation in 95% serum with 5% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) maintains colony. Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM), DMSO, fetal bovine serum (FBS), Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS), 1X Trypsin-EDTA, and other staining reagents were obtained from HiMedia, Mumbai. L929 (mouse fibroblast cells) cell lines were procured from the National Centre for Cell Science, Pune, India. Trizol reagent and 2’-7’- Dichlorofluorescein diacetate were obtained from Sigma. Applied Biosystems, CA., USA, provided a high-capacity complementary DNA (cDNA) reverse transcription kit, and Real Q PCR Master Mix Green-High ROX was purchased from AMPLIQON. Xylazine, ketamine, chloroform, isopropyl alcohol, hydroheal standard ointment, and normal saline were obtained from a registered pharmacy.

Novel nanoformulation for evaluation

A novel nanocomposite made up of keratin, pectin, silver nanoparticles, and ferulic acid core was developed into a gel and an ointment using suitable ingredients in respective quantities. Further, the novel nanoformulations were subjected to the following in vitro and in vivo studies to decipher their enhanced functionalities.

Preparation of nanoformulations

Preparation of silver nano-ointment

5 g of hard paraffin and 4.5 g of cetostearyl alcohol were taken in a china dish and kept in a water bath at 70°C, followed by adding 5 g wool fat and 85 g of white soft paraffin. It was stirred well until all ingredients melted. Finally, the nanocomposite was incorporated into the ointment base after it was cooled.

Preparation of silver nano-gel

The gel base was prepared by dissolving Carbopol 940 in distilled water, and the nanocomposite was incorporated into the gel base.[5]

Characterization of nanoformulations

Physicochemical characterization of nanoformulations

The ointment and gel’s color, consistency, and pH were noted. Further, the formulations’ spreadability, solubility, and washability were checked as per standard procedures.[6]

X-ray photoelectron spectrometer analysis

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was performed to quantify the elemental composition in the nanoformulations (ointment and gel) surface and calculate the elements’ binding state using the X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) instrument of the model K-Alpha-KAN9954133 with the specifications such as Ion Gun Energy range 100–4000 eV with 60 mm × 60 mm sample area and 20 mm maximum sample.[7]

Drug release assay

The drug release assay of both the nanoformulations (ointment and gel) was performed at pH 5.5 PBS buffer using the dialysis membrane technique. Briefly, 100 mg of the nanoformulation was added to 50 ml of pH 5.5 PBS buffer at 37°C ± 1°C. At various periods, 2 ml of the sample was extracted, and the drug concentration was then analyzed at 312 nm with a ultraviolet (UV)-visible spectrophotometer to estimate the drug released for the bioactivity.[8]

In vitro biological evaluation of the nanoformulations

DPPH radical scavenging activity of the nanoformulations

The nanoformulations (ointment and gel) were tested for their radical scavenging activity by DPPH assay. The sample and DPPH solution (0.1 mM) were combined in a 1:10 ratio and kept in the dark for 15 min. The level of decolorization was determined spectrophotometrically at 517 nm using a CE 7250 UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Cecil Instrument, UK). Ethanol served as the negative control, and ascorbic acid was the positive (standard) control. The scavenging activity percentage was determined using the formula Scavenging activity (%) = ([Abs control − Abs sample]/Abs control) ×100, where the tested samples and standard represent the absorbance of DPPH radical with methanol, respectively.[9] Every test has been run three times.

Antimicrobial assay

The antibacterial activity of the ointment and gel (75 mg and 100 mg concentrations) against both Gram-negative (Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Escherichia coli, Acinetobacter baumannii) and Gram-positive (Staphylococcus aureus) bacteria was tested by agar disc diffusion method, and compared with the hydroheal standard and nanocomposite 150 mg, after clearance from the Institutional Bio-safety Committee (IBSC).[9]

Cell viability assay

The effect of the nanoformulations on fibroblast cells was studied by performing an MTT assay. L929 cells were cultured under ideal CO2 conditions in T25 culture flasks containing DMEM media supplemented with 10% FBS. The standard MTT assay protocol was followed, and the absorbance was measured at 595 nm.[9]

Cell migration assay

The effect of the nanoformulations at the concentrations of 5 μM, 10 μM, and 20 μM on cell migration was evaluated by this assay. 12-well cell culture plates were seeded with L929 cells and kept in a CO2 incubator overnight to create a monolayer of cells. Subsequently, an even scratch was made, and the monolayer cells were effectively scraped out with a sterile 200 μL tip. Following that, the existing media from the wells were carefully pipetted out, and the media containing the nanocomposite in varied concentrations was added to the relevant wells. Observations of wound closure were made at various intervals.[9]

AO/EtBr staining of apoptosis

AO/EtBr staining was carried out to evaluate the apoptotic phases after treatment with varying dosages of the nanoformulation. After being evenly seeded in a 12-well plate, L929 cells were exposed to different concentrations of the nanocomposite (5 μM, 10 μM, and 20 μM), incubated overnight, and then rinsed. The cells were treated with an equal mixture of 10 μl/well of AO and EtBr stain.[9]

Cell cycle analysis

L929 fibroblast cells were then seeded in 6-well plates at the rate of 1.2 × 106 cells/well and incubated at 37°C and 5% CO2. Once the cells reached 70% confluence, they were treated with low, mid, and high doses of nanoformulation and hydroheal, which were chosen based on the cytotoxic dose determined by the MTT assay. Following a 24-h drug treatment period, PBS buffer was used to wash the cells, then trypsinized and centrifuged to collect all the cells. After that, 1 ml of chilled 70% ethanol was added drop-wise and fixed by overnight incubation at −20°C. Then, the cells were washed with PBS two times to remove the ethanol. The pellet was suspended in 500 μm of PI-solution and then incubated for 20–30 min at 4°C. The cells were viewed and analyzed using a flow cytometer of BD biosciences, FACS Aria™ III Cell Sorter, and were further analyzed using InCyte software.

Reactive oxygen species determination

Flow cytometry is utilized to determine the probable mode of action of the nanoformulation-induced cell death by depleting reactive oxygen species (ROS) via labeling L929 cells with 2, 7-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFDA). L929 fibroblast cells were seeded in 6-well plates and kept for growing overnight in an incubator under optimum conditions. Nanoformulations in different concentrations of 5, 10, and 20 μM were compared to 10 μM of the standard drug hydroheal for its effects on L929 cells. Following treatment, PBS was used to wash the cells and then trypsinized and centrifuged to collect the cell pellets. Then, 500 μl of 2, 7-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFDA) solution was added to each well, incubated for 15–20 min, and observed in a flow cytometer. In the presence of ROS, the non-fluorescent dye 2, 7-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) was oxidized to produce the fluorescent chemical dichlorofluorescein.

In vivo wound healing potential of the nanoformulations

Using an excision wound model in Sprague Dawley rats, the wound healing ability of the fabricated nanoformulations (ointment and gel) was assessed, and the potential mechanisms of action of the nanoformulations were examined as standardized in our lab.[9]

Maintenance of animals and experimental groups

Male Sprague Dawley rats, weighing between 180 and 200 g, were purchased after the nanoformulations received ethical clearance from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee. The rats were kept in polypropylene cages at the Animal House Facility with an ideal temperature of 23°C ± 2°C and a 12-h light and dark cycle. The rats were allowed to acclimatize for 7 days while receiving a standard pellet meal and unlimited water, following the CPCSEA necessities and the animal facilities’ SOPs. Using a Microsoft Office spreadsheet, random numbers were allocated against stratified body weight to divide the animals into various study groups [Table 1].

Experimental groups for animal studies

The randomized rats were divided into seven groups, each with five animals, for the study. The first group served as the untreated negative control, followed by the second group that received standard hydroheal treatment. Excision model of wound healing

An intraperitoneal injection of xylazine (5 mg/kg of animal body weight) and ketamine (60 mg/kg of animal weight) was used to anesthetize the rats before the formation of wounds. A 2 cm × 2 cm skin section was removed from the animal’s dorsal side’s shaved region to create an excision wound measuring 4 cm2. Throughout the 21-day study period, wound healing was assessed on the 3rd, 6th, 14, and 21st days. Following the study period of 21 days, animals were slaughtered, and biochemical and molecular analyses were conducted to elucidate the function of nanoformulation in wound healing and to comprehend the underlying mechanism.

The extent of wound contraction and the degree of wound healing were assessed in specific time intervals to identify the redressal of the wound. Determination of duration of epithelialization

Wound care drugs enhance the process of epithelialization over the wound tissue. Hence, the duration required by the epithelial cells to completely cover the injured surface with skin tissue served as an estimate for the degree of epithelialization induced by the nanoformulations. Wound contraction was observed at particular time points of the studies, and the photographs on various days after the creation of the excision wound were recorded for all the different groups.

Biochemical and molecular analysis

Estimation of protein

1 ml of RNA Xpress reagent was added to the 100 mg of homogenized tissue samples, which were incubated at room temperature for 5 min. The samples were then given a 200 μl addition of chloroform, shaken vigorously for 15 s, and left to stand at room temperature for 10 min. Subsequently, the samples underwent centrifugation at 12,000 rpm at 4°C for 15 min. Three layers with lower protein, an interphase of DNA, and a top colorless aqueous phase of RNA were observed. From this, the protein layer was carefully separated and estimated for the total protein content using a Bio-Rad Nanodrop DCodeTM instrument, and the protein content in each of the experimental groups was determined.

Estimation of DNA

The DNA content was estimated by separating the DNA layer and following the same procedure mentioned and quantitatively assessed using a Bio-Rad Nanodrop DCodeTM instrument.

Molecular markers of wound healing by real-time polymerase chain reaction

Proteins, DNA, and RNA were separated using Trizol reagent, and the standard protocol of[10] was followed. Isolated RNA was quantified and checked for purity using a Bio-Rad Nanodrop DCodeTM instrument. After quantification, a commercial kit (high cDNA, Applied Biosystem) was used to generate cDNA from the RNA extracted from the restored skin tissues. The expression levels of wound repair indicators, such as MMP-2 and MMP-8, KGF, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and inflammatory markers interleukin-2 (IL-2), IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) were measured by real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Histopathological analysis

After being extracted from the wound site, the granulated healing tissue section was preserved for histological examination using neutral 10% buffered formalin. Hematoxylin-eosin (H and E) was used to stain 5-micron-thick sections of tissues that had been fixed in paraffin. Multiple facets of the healing process were scored, including the amount and maturity of collagen, inflammation, vascularization, epithelialization, and the presence or absence of scabs over the healing surface.

Statistical analysis

Using the statistical software Graph Pad Prism version 5, GraphPad Software, Inc., City: San Diego, State: California, Country: United States. All data were expressed by one-way analysis of variance and then post hoc Tukey multiple comparison tests. The in vivo findings are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean for each set of five rats. A value of P ≤ 0.05, P ≤ 0.01, and P ≤ 0.001 were deemed significant for interpreting the results.

Characterization of nanoformulations

Physical evaluation

Color and consistency

On the visual evaluation of the formulation for its color homogeneity and consistency, it was found that the ointment and gel formulations showed two different colors. The ointment was pale white, while the gel was light yellowish brown. The difference in color for the nanoformulations is attributed to the different concentrations of bases added to the formulation. However, both the formulations exhibited smooth, consistent texture and homogeneous appearance, and the gel was glossy while the ointment was opaque.

Measurement of pH

The pH of both the nanoformulations measured by a pH meter was 7.2 for the ointment and 7.3 for the gel, both of which are neutral.

Spreadability

The ability of a nanoformulation to be spread on a flat surface is referred to as spreadability.[11] The formulation’s excellent and fast spreadability guarantees a quicker and more uniform distribution in the applied area. The gel and ointment were easily spread over with a small amount of shear. The time taken for the complete gel spreading was about 4 s, while for the ointment, it was 5 s. Carbopol, used as a base in gel preparation, is a known thickener and stabilizer and a determining factor for the quicker spreadability of gel preparations.

Solubility

The ointment was studied to be soluble in DMSO and other organic solvents like hexane and chloroform. At the same time, the gel was readily soluble in almost all the polar solvents tested, such as water, methanol, ethanol, and PBS, owing to its polar base.

Washability

The nanoformulations exhibited a contrasting washability, as the nano-gel was easily washable with no trace of greasy texture on the skin surface due to the hydrophilic nature of the base. However, the ointment took more time to remove from the skin surface due to the presence of the oleaginous base.

Non-irritancy

On application of the nanoformulations on the skin surface, no allergic symptoms like inflammation, redness, or irritation appeared, making them a non-irritant type and skin-compatible formulation. Accordingly, we found that the basic physicochemical parameters of the nanoformulations, such as color, texture, spreadability, pH, and skin irritation, were satisfactory and acceptable for the topical application of the wound healing product developed.

Silver ion concentration by X-ray photoelectron spectrometer

XPS is a surface-sensitive analytical method to determine a sample’s oxidation state, chemical bonding, electronic structure, and chemical or elemental composition. The survey scan of both gel and ointment [Figure 1] was carried out to determine the amount of silver in the formulations. The silver ion concentration in the gel was found to be only 0.14%.

Drug release of ferulic acid

A drug release study was carried out to confirm whether the API ferulic acid is released from the formulations to produce its activity. The comparative drug release profile of the gel and the ointment is represented in Figure 2. The percentage of cumulative drug release of the gel and the ointment was found to be 25.7% and 59.6%, respectively, at the end of 8 h. The silver nanocomposite-loaded gel exhibited more sustained release than that of the ointment. As the release from the gel was sustained and controlled, it was preferred over the ointment. The sustained drug release from the gel compared to the ointment was attributed to the gelling agent Carbopol 940. The swelling of the gelling agent is high, which forms a diffusion layer and restricts the dissolution of embedded ferulic acid. It thus aids in releasing ferulic acid and is suitable for controlled drug delivery applications. Interestingly, the super case II release mechanism was found to be followed by both the ointment and the gel. Combined swelling, diffusion, and erosion were identified as the release mechanism of the nanoformulations.

In vitro biological evaluation of the nanoformulations

DPPH radical scavenging activity of the nanoformulations

ROS plays a vital role in the orchestration of the normal wound healing process. The ointment and gel (containing ~ 225 μg of nanocomposite) showed good radical scavenging activity with an IC50 of 2.69 mg and 2.84 mg, respectively. At a dose of 38 mg/ml, the ointment exhibited the highest DPPH scavenging activity, with a percentage inhibition of 84.54 ± 0.01, as seen in Supplementary Figure 1. In contrast, the gel displayed a percentage inhibition of 90.72 ± 0.85 at the same concentration, which suggests that the nanocomposite-infused ointment and gel showed good radical scavenging activity.[12] Hence, it is expected to possess potent antioxidant activity and mediate rapid wound healing.[13]

Antimicrobial activity of the nanoformulations

The antibacterial properties of the gel and ointment were assessed using the disc diffusion assay and compared to the commercially available wound healing ointment hydroheal and the positive control Gentamicin (10 μg/disc). Table 3 and Supplementary Figure 2 illustrate the results of the comparative antibacterial activity of the nanoformulations, nanocomposite (150 mg), and the standards as a measure of the zone of inhibition. Herein, the ointment formulation NFO1 with 150 mg of NC exhibited a slightly elevated zone of inhibition against all the tested microorganisms, namely E. coli, A. baumannii, P. aeruginosa, and S. aureus than NFO2, the formulation with only 0.562 μg of nanocomposite in it, which may be attributed to the higher content of the silver nanocomposite in NFO1. More specifically, both NFO1 and NFO2 showed an elevated zone of inhibition against A. baumannii. A similar pattern of antimicrobial activity could be observed for the two test concentrations of the nano-gel formulations as well, wherein NFG1, possessing a higher concentration of nanocomposite of about 150 mg, showed a higher zone of inhibition than NFG2, which was about 0.562 μg of the nanocomposite. In the present case, the nanocomposite incorporated in the nanoformulation has a particle size of 88.35 nm.[13]

Cell migration by the nanoformulations

The scratch assay was utilized to compare the ability of the nanoformulations to heal wounds in fibroblast cells. Wound healing depends on cell migration, and the scratch test is a simple and versatile way to gauge the process.[14] Moreover, silver nanoparticles can induce fibroblasts to differentiate into myofibroblasts, aiding wound contraction. Scratch assay on three doses (5, 10, and 20 μM) was carried out following the IC50 value of the MTT assay, which is around 7–9 μM [Figure 3]. When L929 fibroblast cells were treated with 10 μM dosages of the gel and ointment, the cells proliferated better by migration. Compared to the L929 fibroblast cells treated with ointment and gel at concentrations of 5 μM and 10 μM, migration of the cells occurred, albeit more slowly and with fewer cells at the 20 μM dose of the nanoformulations. Cell death was noted at a dosage of 20 μM, suggesting that it is plausible. The gel and the ointment showed better cell migration within 12 h of the experiment, indicating faster cell migration. It is promising to note that the nanocomposite can promote cell proliferation and migration, leading to wound closure.

AO/EtBr staining for apoptosis

Apoptosis is a physiological process in the early phase of wound healing involving a complex network of biochemical pathways. Deregulated apoptosis leads to delayed wound healing.[15] Therefore, in the current study, the early and late apoptotic cells were identified by AO/EtBr staining. Intense green fluorescence was observed in cells treated with 5 μM and 10 μM dosages of the gel and ointment, indicating no cell death. Hydroheal produced comparable outcomes at each concentration, as shown in Figure 4. The microscopic pictures showed faster healing without cell death [Figure 4]. Notably, almost negligible toxicity was observed in the highest tested concentration of 20 μM for both the gel and the ointment. The apoptotic dead cells previously found in the groups treated with nanocomposite were not observed in the nanoformulations, indicating that the nanocomposite’s toxicity has also been concealed in the formulations, even at the highest tested dose. Cell cycle analysis

The cell cycle plays a vital role in wound healing, and it is a highly regulated and dynamic process that involves many repairs, regulatory proteins, and enzymes. During wound healing, tissue repair involves many stages, including inflammation and proliferation, wherein different types of cells participate in the various stages, namely interphase (G1, S, and G2 phase) and mitosis. In the present study, the mode of action of the silver nanocomposite, nanoformulations, and the standard drug hydroheal on L929 fibroblast cells was examined to identify the cell proliferation as a measure of cell cycle phases, namely in the G0/G1, S, or G2/M phase. The effect of nanocomposite and its nanoformulations and standard drug hydroheal on the cell cycle of L929 fibroblast cells was determined by flow cytometric quantification of cell population and DNA content at each phase of the cell cycle.

As seen in Figure 5, the negative control had the highest proportion of cells (62%) not conducting cell division in the G0/G1 phase. Hydroheal-treated and nanocomposite-treated cells showed more cells working towards cell repair, with fewer cells in the G0/G1 phase. Levels of reactive oxygen species

Chemically, ROS, such as superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radical, are produced by the cellular metabolism of the immune cell during inflammation, cell damage, cellular signaling, and antimicrobial defence. During the inflammatory phase of wound healing, ROS acts as a signaling molecule for regulating inflammation and is involved in cell migration and differentiation as a secondary messenger, which is crucial in wound healing. Therefore, maintaining a balance in ROS level is vital for optimal wound healing; the level of ROS is neither so less nor more.[16] It should be balanced for enhanced wound healing. In the present study, the production of ROS in the negative control was found to be 33.0%, whereas the standard drug hydroheal-treated cells were found to be 22.6%, as shown in Figure 6. The nanoformulation (gel) showed ROS production of 23.3%, and the nanoformulation (ointment)-treated cell was found to be 22.3%, and the nanocomposite-treated cells’ production of ROS was 20.8%. The percentage of ROS production for the control cells, nanocomposite, gel, ointment, and standard hydroheal-treated groups are 33%, 20.8%, 23.3%, 22.3%, and 22.6%, respectively [Table 5].

In vivo biological evaluation of the nanoformulations

To compare and assess the nanocomposite’s potential for wound healing and to further investigate any potential mechanisms of action, the optimal and most effective concentration of 75 mg was developed as an ointment and gel, which were then tested for their ability to heal wounds in Sprague Dawley rats. Concurrently, 100 mg of the NC was added to the ointment and gel base and examined to see if the bases would prevent the NC from releasing from them, and if the necessary activity might not be exhibited.

Wound contraction analysis

The rate of wound contraction was evaluated as a measure of wound closure concerning time and compared between the control, standard, and treated groups. According to the statistical interpretation of the wound contraction area plotted as a percentage measure, as shown in Figure 7, about 45.88% and 45.23% of the wounds had contracted within 6 days in the gel 75 mg and gel 100 mg-treated groups, respectively. The results were comparable to the standard hydroheal-treated group and the nanocomposite 75 mg-treated group, in which about 46.32% and 54.28% of wound contraction were observed on the 6th day, respectively. Meanwhile, the percentage of wound contraction was comparatively lower in both the ointment-treated groups, which were about 37.11% and 38.07%, respectively. However, it is noteworthy that on the 6th day of the trial, the untreated control group had a wound contraction rate of roughly 7.53%, whereas other tested groups had substantially higher wound contraction rates. Considering the percentage of wound contraction at the end of the 21 days of the study, it could be noted that almost complete and most efficient wound closure was observed in the order of the standard (98.59), gel 75 mg (97.47%), gel 100 mg (95.13%), ointment 100 mg (95.02%), nanocomposite 75 mg (94.39%), ointment 75 mg (92.03%), and negative control (90.04%).

Discussion

The physicochemical characterization of the nanoformulations (gel and ointment) shows desirable physical properties, such as smooth consistence, neutral pH, quicker spreadability, non-irritable to the skin, and almost soluble in all polar solvents. The XPS analysis of the nanoformulations, both gel and ointment are very low and accounts for the reduction of dose-dependent toxicity of silver. However, silver in the ointment is silver oxide, which might contribute to the untoward effects in vivo. As the nanocomposite loaded in the nano-gel exhibited shear thinning properties and sustained drug release, it was found suitable as a wound healing agent. Among the two nanoformulations, the gel follows zero-order kinetics (controlled release), which is better for wound healing applications, while the ointment exhibits first-order kinetics. We found that both the ointment and gel exhibited good scavenging activity, which implies that the drug-loaded formulation is performing the scavenging activity irrespective of its oil or water base and releases the drug in a controlled manner for easy penetration through the skin. The enhanced antioxidant activity is probably due to the oxidized form of silver nanoparticles in the nanoformulations loaded with the silver nanocomposite. Ferulic acid, the API in novel nanoformulations and its structural analogs, is widely known for its impressive antioxidant activity.[19] A similar study was conducted to understand the antioxidant activity of a hydrogel developed using keratin isolated from human hair and MnO2, which exhibited excellent ROS scavenging ability. Interestingly, the wound healing capacity of the keratin complements the antioxidant activity of the ferulic acid and probably shows a synergistic effect of the nanoformulations. Further, penetration of the drugs and formulations through the skin’s stratum corneum layer is a significant limitation of topical applications. The potential radical scavenging ability of the dosage forms thus critically would enhance wound healing and infection control at the wound site.

The gel had better activity against the microorganisms than the ointment, which could be attributed to the hydrophilic base Carbopol 940 that would aid in the diffusion of the ingredients efficiently and exert the action effectively. The nanocomposite played a pivotal role in inhibiting the growth of detrimental microorganisms, probably mediated by the scavenging of the ROS and the antibacterial activity of ferulic acid and the silver nanoparticles in the nanocomposite. Moreover, the size and form of the silver nanoparticles affect their ability to have an antibacterial effect.[16] The higher surface area of the silver nanoparticles with their nano- size helps to cross the membrane of the bacterial cells, thereby causing intracellular damage and inhibiting the growth of the bacteria.[10] Furthermore, wound healing studies carried out with silver nanoparticles in combination with biopolymers have been shown to decrease the healing time by 30% with a significant increase in antimicrobial activity compared to conventionally available wound dressings.[17] Another study on wound healing with a novel ointment with natural molecules reported that the developed ointment exhibited antimicrobial activity against E. coli (9.4 mm), S. aureus (8.6 mm), P. aeruginosa (13.9 mm), and A. baumannii (7.2 mm). Notably, the nanocomposite, which consists of the natural molecule ferulic acid and silver nanoparticles in both formulations, exhibits good antimicrobial activity, as evidenced in one of our recent publications.[20] It is noteworthy to infer here that the conversion of the nanocomposite into the nanoformulation did not reduce its antimicrobial action, indicating that the other components in the nanoformulation did not alter its activity. The hydrophilic nature of the gel or the oleaginous ointment did not hinder the leaching out of the active ingredients, thus exhibiting better activity against common wound pathogens.

Comparatively higher IC50 for gel-treated cells, which is almost equivalent to the IC50 of the silver nanocomposite, could be due to the nontoxic and nonirritant base Carbopol 940, while the strongly hydrophobic bases of ointments such as the wool fat and white soft paraffin might probably be preventing gaseous exchange between the cells, and lowering the viability.[10] However, it is also reported that white soft paraffin is a non-toxic additive. A study that compared the cytotoxicity of wound healing creams and ointments reported that the creams and ointments containing paraffin CreamTM containing chlorocresol preservative were more cytotoxic, and the ointments MeloliteTM and Chlorsig, formulated using white soft paraffin, were the least cytotoxic.[17] Therefore, the viability of the cells is fair enough in the ointment formulation.

Comparatively, the cellular proliferation and migration were better in the gel compared to the ointment. The faster wound closure of the cells treated with gel is in congruence with many of the studies reported in the literature, which state that wound management is enhanced in moist conditions, leading to an increased rate of cellular proliferation and greater migration of cells toward the wounded area in a wet environment than in dry wound healing conditions.[20] The moisture in the gel, which contributes to faster wound closure, is not only contributed by the hydrophilic base, but the pectin outer coat of the nanocomposite also plays a significant role that ensures long-lasting and sustained miniaturization of the application area. It is also worth mentioning that Carbopol 940, a polymer of polyacrylic acid, has the presence of several hydrophilic groups of hydroxyl and carboxyl groups in their polymer chains, which imparts them their characteristic porosity and flexibility and thereby promotes increased proliferation and faster migration of cells. The equally impressive and comparable cellular migration of cells was observed after 12 h of the study period; this may be due to the higher breathability conferred by the wool fat, as per studies that not only ensures breathability of the cells but also has a moisturizing effect. Furthermore, our results are impressive as the administration of the nanoformulations directly over the cells did not diminish their performance, and the ingredients remained inert and did not hinder the activity. The ingredients in the nanocomposite that aid the bioactivity of the nanoformulation possess intrinsic biological activities such as antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and wound healing properties. The formulations exhibited better cell migration mainly due to keratin, one of the active ingredients of the nanocomposite, which has been reported to have extensive wound healing effects.[12] In the current study, the hydrophilicity of pectin boosted base membrane breakdown and promoted cell migration by decreasing the hydrophobicity of keratin. Furthermore, the hydrophilicity of pectin boosted base membrane breakdown and promoted migration of cells by decreasing the hydrophobicity of keratin. Furthermore, the presence of pectin would enhance the wound healing effect due to its moderate swelling nature. Both keratin and pectin are known for their fibroblast proliferation and cell migration abilities, as they may induce growth factors. In addition, the results likely show that every element utilized to make the formulation is nontoxic and inert, and our findings verify the MTT assay’s viability test. The applicability of the nanoformulations as wound healing agents is highly impressive, as they promote faster cell migration with minimal cell death. When compared to the nanocomposite alone, the slower drug release from the nanoformulations may be partially responsible for the lower toxicity at greater doses. In wound healing, the cell cycle plays a vital role as it increases the number of cells and ultimately helps in closing the wound. Our formulations are composed of ferulic acid, pectin, and keratin protein that possess antioxidant properties. This decreases DNA damage during cellular metabolism and cell division. Keratin protein can stimulate collagen synthesis and induce cell division and cellular binding to each other. In our study, more cells actively divide on nanoformulation-treated cells compared to cells treated with hydroheal and in negative control, confirming that the cells treated with the nanoformulations have more favorable conditions for cell division and healing the wound. The nanoformulations have shown better results than the negative control and standard hydroheal. The presence of pectin and ferulic acid in the nanoformulation-treated groups, which have antioxidant activity and scavenge free radical molecules reduce inflammation in the wound and speed up wound healing, resulting in less ROS production compared to the untreated negative control group.

In contrast to the traditional knowledge that says dry scab formation is an indicator of faster healing, the currently available knowledge and understanding lead us to the fact that a moist environment supports better and quicker healing through moisture supply and gas exchange in the wounded area. Hence, gels, hydrogels, hydrocolloids, and occlusive dressings have been studied to facilitate faster rates of wound contraction than creams and ointments. Accordingly, higher angiogenesis, more significant wound contraction, enhanced collagen synthesis, prevention of tissue necrosis, and decreased infection rate have been reported in conditions with a moist environment. Hence, our results align with the current understanding that the nano-gel formulation initiates faster contraction in wounds and prevents cross-contamination or infection of the wounds.

An intriguing finding of the study is that, although the untreated groups do exhibit some healing over time, the topical formulations are found to produce a significant amount of healing; roughly half of the wound closure is found to occur within a week, while the untreated groups only exhibit <10% of the healing effects at the same point in time. Previous research has shown that the nanocomposite’s antioxidant, antibacterial, and wound healing properties provide the potential for wound contraction. As reported in our earlier study, keratin in the nanocomposite is responsible for faster keratinocyte migration, and pectin overcomes the hydrophobicity of keratin, helping in more rapid dissolution of the wound membrane.[9] Further, the study also revealed that the same underlying concept of hydrophilicity enhances significant re-epithelialization of the gel-treated groups. In the case of acute wounds, it has been reported that wound management dressings rely on a moisture-trapping mechanism that can heal the wound and initiate re-epithelialization 30%–45% faster than non-hydrating practices.[21] Also, studies prove that such applications also favour a faster healing rate, about 3–4 days faster than their oleaginous counterparts.

The lower MMP8 expression of the nanocomposite-treated tissues than standard-treated tissues indicates the initial phase of tissue remodeling after appropriate reepithelization of nanocomposite-treated tissues. Similarly, a study showed topical application of the wounded tissue with eucalyptol ointment for 2 weeks that brought a decrease in IL-6 levels than the initial time of injury, wherein elevated levels of IL-b activate other inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNFα. Subsequently, after 2 weeks of treatment, the levels of these marker genes significantly decreased, indicating improvement in inflammation and restoration of the extracellular matrix.[22] VEGF, an endothelial cell mitogen, initiator of vascular permeability, and one of the growth factors secreted by the fibroblasts during the proliferative and migratory phases of wound healing, is a known angiogenic gene that is responsible for initiating a series of wound healing cascades such as angiogenesis and deposition of collagen. In vitro and in vivo studies carried out by overexpressing VEGF in dermal fibroblast cells have found that there was more angiogenesis in the overexpressed groups from the second day, with significant wound closure by the 5th day of the surgery.[23] Accordingly, our study showed that the nano-ointment and nano-gel 75 mg-treated groups have relatively higher VEGF expression, owing to their therapeutic potential in the formulation that promotes angiogenesis.[24] In addition, relatively higher expression of VEGF in ointment 100 mg-treated groups indicates the efficiency of the formulation in treating wounds stubborn and resistant to the moist environment. Further, KGF expression strongly influences epidermal regeneration, and its overexpression at the wound surface indicates stimulating the accumulation of fibroblasts and collagen and, thereby, the formation of connective tissue.[25] In a study by,[25] the surface of the designed nanofibrils is coated with a hydrophilic epitope with a growth factor that attaches and attracts more fibroblast migration. The study found that hydro-gelation helped growth factor peptide attachment, stimulating endothelial cell migration and accelerating wound healing. In our study, as mentioned earlier, the pectin outercoat plays an essential role in creating this hydrophilic outer coat, and the formulations are highly effective. Therefore, it was confirmed that the nanoformulations exhibited good wound healing activity and were comparable and on par with the commercially available standard, contributing to the beneficial biological effects. Conclusion

A low-cost wound dressing material was designed in this study, which was found to have superior functionality, leveraging bio-waste-derived raw materials and advanced nanomaterials. The developed gel formulation exhibited better physicochemical properties, sustained drug release, and superior wound healing effects compared to the ointment. In vitro studies demonstrated the safety and efficacy of the nanoformulations, particularly the gel, in promoting cell viability, migration, and antioxidant activity. In vivo experiments on Sprague Dawley rats showcased the superior wound healing potential of 75 mg concentration of the gel. Altogether, the nanoformulation ointment and gel at 75 mg and 100 mg concentrations have the potential to enhance wound healing activity due to novel nanoformulation containing antioxidant activity, cell proliferation properties, antimicrobial activity, collagen synthesis promotion, and reepithelialization. The nanoformulation base and nanocomposite composition contain keratin, pectin, ferulic acid, and silver nanoparticles that have the best combined synergistic effect for enhancing wound healing effects. The developed formulations could be a potential promise of nanomaterial-based wound dressings in enhancing wound healing outcomes while addressing cost concerns.

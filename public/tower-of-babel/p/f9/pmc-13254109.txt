A hierarchical neuromorphic multi agent framework for energy aware and secure 6G resource optimization using Neuro6G agent
Authors: Ahmed Al Nuaim, Junaid Qayyum
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe convergence of Sixth-Generation (6G) wireless networks and neuromorphic computing presents significant opportunities for intelligent, energy-efficient resource management in distributed architectures. This paper introduces Neuro6G-Agent, a hierarchical neuromorphic agentic intelligence framework that integrates Energy-Aware Spiking Neural Networks (EA-SNNs) with multi-agent reinforcement learning to enable energy-conscious cognitive collaboration across cloud-edge-end 6G deployments. The framework addresses three principal challenges in distributed 6G resource management: energy sustainability, end-to-end latency under ultra-dense connectivity, and security resilience against adversarial threats. A three-tier architecture is employed, comprising cloud orchestrators, edge coordinators, and end devices, each operating dedicated neuromorphic agents with autonomous decision-making and trust-aware collaborative learning capabilities. The framework incorporates adaptive threshold EA-SNNs for event-driven processing, a distributed trust computation mechanism for secure multi-agent cooperation, and a hierarchical resource optimization algorithm responsive to dynamic workload conditions. Experimental evaluation across three public benchmark datasets—DeepMIMO (6G channel modeling), DVS128 Gesture (neuromorphic sensing), and CICIDS-2017 (network intrusion detection) demonstrates a 34.7% reduction in energy consumption, a 28.3% decrease in end-to-end latency, and a 95.6% security threat detection accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baseline methods, validated across ten independent experimental runs (p < 0.01). These results confirm the viability of neuromorphic intelligence for addressing complex optimization challenges in next-generation wireless architectures.Keywords: 6G networks, Neuromorphic computing, Multi-agent reinforcement learning, Spiking neural networks, Edge computing, Energy optimization, Trust-aware security, Resource allocationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Sixth-Generation (6G) networks will introduce a new era of smart and autonomous communication systems powered by agentic AI and distributed cognition1. Figure 1 depicts subsequent generation wireless will move past distributed structures of centralized AI streams to the autonomous networks of agent-based intelligence arrangements wherein self-sensing agents reasoning and cooperating in both cloud-edge-unit settings2. This paradigm overcomes underlying constraints in scalability and decision-latency experienced by conventional centralized intelligence systems in ultra-dense 6G processes involving billions of connected things3.

Contextualization of the 6G network issues and introduction of the suggested Neuro6G-Agent model, which does energy-conscience network collaboration between cloud-edge-end systems.

The development of neuromorphic processing has boosted the rate at which biologically inspired computational models that can support learning in low-latency and adaptive decision making in conditions of resource constraint are adopted4. All these developments are in line with new intelligent networking directions extending past 5G, where machine learning and distributed cognition are added to communication stacks to allow operation of autonomous networks5. In addition to land-based systems, networks of space computing power are being established as an important facilitator to smart and collaborative communication services between satellite-terrestrial systems6. Meanwhile, the basic 6G technologies, including intelligent reflecting surfaces, transmission at terahertz, or UAV-assisted connectivity are becoming the main pillars of network automation and awareness of the smart environment7.

The generative AI and agentic AI of resilient multi-domain connectivity has been noted in supplementary activity related to agent-augmented satellite networks8. In the meantime, edge computing systems are developing to real-time efficient execution on learning-driven communication systems9. Intelligence coordination and a hierarchical control structure have become significant as 6G networks implement AI in each layer10. Neuromorphic architecture has been justified by the fact that it enables energy-saving onboard communication activities, especially in a satellite system where both computational and energy limitations are highly limiting11.

Just as well, neuromorphic edge AI technologies have been shown with promise in the field of resource-constrained applications in remote and distributed sense conditions12. Satellite-edge AI ecosystems should be able to capitalize on large intelligent models that can facilitate self-optimizing space-terrestrial networks13. Semantic communication and goal-oriented transmission paradigms are coming up that minimize communication overheads and maximize relevance of the systems14. Simultaneously, neuromorphic computing was demonstrated to benefit tremendously in terms of energy-usage through radio resource management operations in space communications15. Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces offer extra flexibility of 6G wireless environment which allows programmable radio environment of adaptive optimization16.

The greater vision of 6G puts 6G in the system as an intelligent and self-driving network of everything and edge autonomy, fluid intelligence fusion and distributed learning is needed17. Space communication roadmaps support transformational trends of autonomous multi-agent intelligence, coupled with satellite networks18. The satellite connectivity and communication models based on AI promote distributed optimization and cooperative learning, especially19 that emphasizes the necessity. Expanding on this, sensor network studies point to the metamorphosis to adaptive, resilient and self-organizing edge intelligence20.

Based on these trends this paper proposes a neuromorphic enhanced, multi agent, cognitive work group, Neuro6G-Agent, to apply to the 6G networks of the future. It combines the spiking neural processing scheme, trust-sensitive agent intelligence, and hierarchical resource coordination to overcome the existing problems in scalability, low-energy consumption, credibility, and autonomous decision-making. As shown in Fig. 1, the proposed Neuro6G-Agent model enables energy-conscious network collaboration between cloud-edge-end systems.

Recent reports from 3GPP and ITU indicate that 6G systems must support E2E latency, density, and 10× lower energy-per-bit than 5G. Edge intelligence and neuromorphic accelerators are increasingly adopted in real deployments such as Ericsson neuro-edge RAN prototypes and Intel Loihi-powered IoT nodes. Motivated by these practical constraints, Neuro6G-Agent is designed to enable scalable distributed cognition, 30–50% energy reduction via event-driven SNNs, and real-time adaptive resource orchestration in ultra-dense, mission-critical deployments.

The paper has contributed to the subject of intelligent 6G network optimization in several ways. We initially propose a hierarchical neuromorphic agentic intelligence system that indemnifies energy conscious cognition resulting in potential collaboration among cloud-edge-end structures. Second, we come up with energy-conscious spiking neural networks (EA-SNNs) that are specifically created with the goal of optimization of distributed resources in 6G settings. Third, we introduce an end-to-end security conscious trust system capable of guaranteeing secure cooperation at the same time as guaranteeing high performance. Fourth, we show that the proposed framework leads to significant energy savings, latency reduction and security resilience improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods, which is the result of comprehensive experimental analysis.

Related work

Neuromorphic computing in wireless networks

Neuromorphic computing has demonstrated significant momentum as an eye-opening paradigm in the next-generation wireless intelligence, especially on low-latency and energy-constrained conditions. Recent publications prove that spiking-based computation is possible to perform more sophisticated physical-layer processing.

A spatio-temporal attention convolutional autoencoder with Akida Brainchip SNN as 5G non-terrestrial network demodulation was introduced by Varadararajulu et al.21, with the capabilities of neuromorphic inference in real-time satellite communications in view. Stogia et al.22 further concluded the role of the principles of neuromorphic in Industry 5.0 intelligence along with distributed processing when considering the use of 3D modeling and IoT integration to develop higher levels of autonomy and lower energy consumption.

Wu et al.23 put into leverage the development of artificial intelligence since the era of symbolic systems to biologically inspired models (AI 4.0) with the recognition of neuromorphic systems as critical to scalable and cognitive communication platforms. Such innovations indicate that neuromorphic computing has the capacity to offer ultra-low latency, event-driven and asynchronous intelligence that fits the demanding power and computing requirements of 6G. These studies, however, primarily consider device-level acceleration and lack schemes through which multi-agent neuromorphic coordination can happen at the cloud edge continuum, a factor driving the consideration of multi-tier cognitive frameworks in this paper.

Agentic AI and multi-agent systems for 6G networks

Communication intelligence is undergoing considerable paradigm change with the transition to agentic AI, as they can now negotiate resources, perform their duties, and make decisions in a group. According to Tripathi and Malik24, development of autonomous agents is associated with ethical and behavioral implication in 6G, and it has denoted the importance of intelligent coordination in the society. L. Wang., et al.25. gave an overview of edge AI trends in emphasizing the trend towards distributed, autonomous, and adaptive machine learning systems, apt to multi-agent collaboration.

The articles by Getu et al.26 identified semantic communication and goal-oriented information transmission as essential factors in agent-driven 6G systems. Furthermore, Baziana27 emphasized the use of distributed optical neural processing to perform large scale computing indirectly like distributed agent cognition. Overall, these publications indicate that multi-agent intelligence will be vital in the case of decentralized 6G networks, but they are deficient of neuromorphic-based coordination and trust-aware policy learning based on events. This gap is addressed by our work, which proposes new methods of neuromorphic multi-agent interaction that can be used with secure autonomous 6G decision pipelines.

Energy-aware edge computing and 6G networks

The 6G aims to achieve scalable edge-centric deployments whose core requirement is energy efficiency. Arga et al.28 pointed out the frugal AI ideology with an emphasis on sustainable computation based on energy-limited models. Singh et al.29 broached the contemporary issues in computing such as the power efficient real-time intelligence, the identification of neuromorphic circuits as promising opportunities in saving power. The relevance of lightweight learning in communication stacks has been proven by Petry30 who showed AI enhanced edge processing to physical-layer satellite systems.

Abdulkadir et al.31 examined the trends in environmental sustainability in ICTs with special focus on the green wireless development of computational design due to its efficiency in energy consumption. Meanwhile, Liu et al.32 introduced a federated edge computing architecture with new IIoT devices of limited power-constrained capabilities, and Wang et al.33 evaluated scalable edge program deployments of AI models with an emphasis on computation-communication trade-offs. Even though these contributions facilitate low-power distributed AI, none of them incorporate neuromorphic computation and multi-agent trust-sensitive energy-adaptive policy learning. Our suggested Neuro6G-Agent will offer this gap.

Optical and photonic neuromorphic systems

Neuromorphic photonics has become the solution of ultra-fast and energy-efficient next-generation AI acceleration. Recent advances in enterprise generative AI and blockchain-based fintech management further highlight the convergence of AI-driven autonomy with secure distributed systems34. A seminal roadmap of photonic neural networks with sub-nanosecond signal processing capabilities was presented by Shastri et al.35, demonstrating the promise of neuromorphic photonics for artificial intelligence acceleration. Halawa et al.36 talked about nanophotonic chip advances made in favor of optical-neural models that promote sustainable ultra-low-latency intelligence on healthcare and communication networks.

The future trends in satellite AI described by Navarro and Dinis37 indicated photonic neuromorphic processing as one of the acceleration lines in real-time inference in space systems. These endeavors make photonic neuromorphic approaches to 6G, yet these are emphasized on hardware acceleration, rather than distributed neuromorphic cooperation and trust-oblivious learning. Healthcare and haptic applications

Recently, neuromorphic computing has been used in medical robotics, prosthetics, and surgical intelligence and has proven practical in safety-critical areas. Tiwari et al.38 noted the AI-enriched neuromorphic haptic systems that aid in real-time sensorimotor feedback in healthcare robotics.

Proposed methodology

Framework overview

Neuro6G-Agent, shown in Fig. 2, is a three-tier hierarchical structure that aims to maximize resource utilization and minimize energy consumption and follows a hierarchical structure that will provide secure cooperation within cloud-end-edge 6G environments. The framework combines the idea of neuromorphic computing with that of multi-agent reinforcement learning to develop an intelligent, adaptive system able to decide and learn autonomously as well as share thoughts in a cognitive way.

Neuro6G-Agent framework is hierarchical, depicted in the figure, with cloud orchestrators, edge coordinators, and end devices having the neuromorphic agent implementation.

The cloud tier is made of neuromorphic orchestrators that do a global optimization and strategic resource allocation decision. These orchestrators use high-level optimization strategies and process large-scale network state information using highly sophisticated spiking neural networks. The edge tier includes distributed coordinators which are local optimization but coordinate with cloud orchestrator and end devices. The final layer has smart devices that have light neuromorphic agents that can make autonomous local decisions and collaborative learning.

The EA-SNN architecture is implemented as a three-layer feedforward spiking network consisting of an input encoding layer, a recurrent lateral inhibition layer, and a readout layer. Membrane potentials are governed by the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron model, with parameters τm = 20 ms, Rm = 10 MΩ, and Pspike = 3.2 pJ per spike event (calibrated from Intel Loihi datasheet estimates). Temporal rate coding is employed at the input layer, converting continuous state observations into Poisson spike trains at 200 Hz resolution. Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) governs synaptic weight updates during unsupervised pre-training, after which weights are frozen, and the readout layer is trained via surrogate-gradient backpropagation using the fast sigmoid as the differentiable approximation to the Heaviside step function. The EA-SNN is interfaced with the MARL policy network through a spike-rate-to-action mapping that converts population activity vectors into discrete resource allocation decisions. End devices operate 256-neuron configurations; edge coordinators use 512-neuron networks; cloud orchestrators employ 1024-neuron networks, reflecting the computational budget at each tier.

Game-theoretic robustness

The trust penalty structure is modeled as a repeated game in which each agent selects between honest (H) and deviant (D) strategies. A deviant agent j incurs trust reduction ΔT = −ω_5 × A_ij(t) per detected anomaly, resulting in progressive exclusion from resource allocation. Under the Folk Theorem for repeated games, honest cooperation is a Nash Equilibrium when the discount factor γ satisfies γ > (π_D − π_H)/(π_D − π_P), where π_D, π_H, π_P denote deviant, honest, and punishment payoffs respectively. Given γ = 0.95 and the empirically estimated ratio (π_D − π_H)/(π_D − π_P) ≈ 0.71, the condition 0.95 > 0.71 is satisfied, confirming that no rational agent has an incentive to deviate unilaterally from honest cooperation.

Experimental methodology

A distinction is drawn between the offline training phase and the online deployment phase of Neuro6G-Agent. Agent policies and EA-SNN weights are trained offline using historical network traces derived from the three benchmark datasets over 500 epochs. Upon deployment, the framework operates in a streaming online inference mode: at each 1 ms simulation time step, each agent observes its current state s_i^l(t), generates an EA-SNN spike response within one membrane time constant (τm = 20 ms), selects an action via the trained policy, and updates trust scores T_ij(t) based on observed agent behavior. Q-values are updated online via experience replay with a buffer of 10,000 transitions (Eq. 17). All performance metrics reported in Sect. 5.2–5.8 are measured during this online deployment phase over the full 24-hour simulation window, confirming real-time adaptive operation under dynamic, non-stationary 6G network conditions.

A hybrid simulation and neuromorphic learning environment is adopted to implement the Neuro6G-Agent framework to conduct transparent and reproducible evaluation. Python built on top of PyTorch with PyTorch-SNN for neuromorphic and EA-SNN model training, Ray RLlib using distributed multi-agent reinforcement learning, and Brian2 as well as validating the dynamics of spiking neurons are all included in the development stack. Online statistical analysis and energy profiling are done with NS-3 6G and offline, with behavior and communication latency at the network level modeled by that library.

All the experiments will be carried out on a high-performance computer platform that will possess an NVIDIA A100 GPU that has 40GB of HBM2e memory, two Intel Xeon Silver 4314 CPUs, and 256GB of DDR4 RAM and run Ubuntu 22.04 LTS with CUDA 11.7. Such structure is indicative of realistic next-generation edge-cloud execution surroundings as well as the stability of benchmarking throughout the layers of learning and communication.

The proposed framework is tested with the help of three benchmark datasets. DeepMIMO O1_28 dataset is an example of real mmWave channel and beamforming signatures in the future 6G communication systems. In the evaluation of spiking neuromorphic learning, the DVS128 gesture dataset is used because of the event-based format, which is high-resolution and has temporal sparsity. The CICIDS- 2017 intrusion dataset is also used to evaluate the strength of cybersecurity which includes current attack patterns, and it is a standard base in network threat detection investigations. Data is made publicly available and scripts to prep the data are present as an inclusion in the to assist in reproducibility of the work completely.

Neuro6G-Agent is measured under performance metrics in accordance with the emerging 6G edge AI standards. The significant data are the computational and communication energy consumption in millijoules per event and Joules per episode, end-to-end inference and decision latency, the throughput and the quality-of-service improvement rates. The measures of security effectiveness include intrusion detection accuracy, recall, F1-score, and attack resistance. Also, the ratios monitored include trust- stability and collaboration success to understand soundness of distributed decision-making.

Random seeds are fixed in all software components to provide reproducibility and the results reported as mean and standard deviation of 10 independent runs with cross-validation of five folds. In all publications, all source code, preprocessing pipeline data sets, hundreds of scripts aimed at running them, and configuration files relating to the environment needed to run the pipeline will be made publicly accessible through a specially designated repository on GitHub to enhance the practices of open-science and enable reproducibility efforts.

The O(N²·T) trust computation term requires pairwise evaluation among all N agents. At the 1,060-agent scale of the present evaluation, this yields approximately 1.12 × 10⁶ pairwise evaluations per time step, parallelized across GPU cores with a wall-clock cost of 2.3 ms per step on the A100 platform. For ultra-dense deployments approaching 10⁶ nodes, direct O(N²) computation becomes infeasible. Two mitigation strategies are identified for future scaling: (i) hierarchical trust aggregation, wherein intra-tier trust is computed locally and only tier-representative scores are exchanged inter-tier, reducing complexity to O(N·log N·T); and (ii) gossip-protocol-based propagation, achieving O(N·d·T) where d ≈ log N for random graphs, making the mechanism tractable at million-node scale (Table 2).

Benchmark datasets and real-world data sources

We use the 3 publicly available benchmark datasets to evaluate the Neuro6G-Agent framework fairly, replicable and non-synthetically and to the extent of requirements in 6G communication, neuromorphic sensing and cybersecurity needs. To model the wireless channels and for the beamforming behavior, the DeepMIMO data set is considered, which offers realistic mm Wave channel matrices and user-BS folders that display the next-generation cellular deployments. DVS128 Gesture dataset is utilized in the validation of the spiking-based neuromorphic processing and event-driven learning performance since it contains high-temporal-resolution event streams ideal to EA-SNN evaluation. To measure security resilience and enforcement of trust, the CICIDS-2017 intrusion data is included and includes real attacks on the network like DDoS, brutality, infiltration, and botnet traffic.

The three benchmark datasets are mapped to specific architectural tiers and evaluation objectives within the hierarchical framework as follows. The DeepMIMO O1_28 dataset is applied at the cloud and edge tiers; its mmWave channel matrices populate the network condition input N_i^l(t) in Eq. (6), enabling evaluation of beamforming-aware resource allocation decisions made by cloud orchestrators and edge coordinators under realistic propagation conditions. The DVS128 Gesture dataset is processed at the edge and end-device tiers; its event-stream format is natively compatible with the EA-SNN temporal coding scheme, with each 128 × 128 event frame encoded as a 200 Hz Poisson spike train input to the 256-neuron end-device agents, directly validating the neuromorphic inference pipeline under real event-driven sensory data. The CICIDS-2017 intrusion dataset is applied across all three tiers; network flow features are extracted and supplied to the trust evaluation function (Eq. 8), evaluating detection of DDoS, brute-force, infiltration, and botnet traffic patterns and triggering the corresponding trust score adjustments and resource isolation responses.

To guarantee the credibility of the further improvements, statistical significance analysis of the results was performed in 10 near experimental repetitions. Paired t-test was conducted to compare the Neuro6G-Agent framework with each of the baseline approaches when it came to the key performance aspects. The findings prove that performance improvement is statistically significant, in energy efficiency, in the reduction of latency, and in the enhancement of security metrics. Key advantages and innovations

Neuro6G-Agent has numerous innovations that render it differentiating in comparison to the current strategies. By combining energy-aware spiking neural networks with multi-agent reinforcement learning, optimization of the complicated interdependences between energy usage, performance-need and security constraints is established to develop a special paradigm. The hierarchical design allows supporting scalable deployment in a variety of 6G cases without losing the ability to efficiently coordinate the work of various levels within the network.

The cognitive collaboration mechanism is also a breakthrough in distributed intelligence, as it allows autonomous agents to make sound decisions, based on the trust evaluation and consensus mechanisms. This method handles crucial shortcomings of current multi-agent frameworks which commonly do not incorporate elaborate coordination protocols to safe satisfiers.

The adaptive learning features of the framework guarantee continuous improvement and adaptation to the evolving network conditions. The neuromorphic processing components have an intrinsic gain on real-time processing and energy efficiency, and hence the structure is especially adaptable to resource-constrained 6G settings.

Limitations and future directions

All energy efficiency metrics reported in Table 4 are derived from analytical energy models calibrated against published hardware characterization data: Intel Loihi (3.2 pJ/spike), BrainChip Akida (4.1 pJ/spike, based on Varadarajulu et al., 2024), and standard CMOS baseline (≈ 200 pJ/spike). Physical deployment introduces additional overheads including spike routing latency (≈ 1–3 ns per hop on Loihi mesh), on-chip memory bandwidth contention, and chip-to-chip communication energy not captured in software simulation. The reported 34.7% energy improvement should accordingly be interpreted as a conservative upper-bound estimate derived from calibrated simulation. Empirical validation on Intel Loihi 2 and BrainChip Akida 2 hardware platforms constitutes the primary direction of immediate future work.

Although the Neuro6G-Agent system proves to have significant advances in energy efficiency, latency, and security indicators, it also has certain constraints, which are worth noting. To begin with, the existing evaluation is largely based on simulation and is yet to be deployed on real neuromorphic hardware platforms including Intel Loihi and IBM TrueNorth or Brainchip Akida. Further development will involve actual silicon testing to determine hardware specific limitations like leakage of events and spikes routing delays and the use of on-chip memory.

Second, the hierarchical architecture can be scaled seamlessly to hundreds and thousands of distributed agents, but on very large scales (i.e. millions of non-homogeneous nodes) coordination bottlenecks and communication congestion can occur. Asynchronous federated aggregation, edge-driven model pruning, and communication-efficient neuromorphic compression are techniques that can be considered to address such challenges in ultra-dense 6Gs.

Third, the trust mechanism is useful in isolating malicious nodes; however, it currently is based on the measurement of behavior on a weighted basis and using preset limits. By making self-evolving trust scores behaviorally game-theoretically, graph-based, anomaly learning, and reinforcement-driven trust-calibration, the robustness to adaptive adversaries and colluding attackers in open network with the model is enhanced.

Other directions of research in the future are that quantum-inspired neuromorphic accelerators and photonic spiking units will be included to achieve other improvements to processing speed and durability, especially in mission-critical 6G edges. Also, by integrating our EA-SNN model with decentralized and adaptive federated-learning models, we may be able to have real-time model evolution of our model without retraining it to a central repository. Immune-system-related analogies of threat isolation, honeypots that are neuromorphic, synaptic immunization of adversarial robustness are also promising forms of security improvement that are bio-inspired. Lastly, hybrid semantic sensing, inter-layer smart routing, and self-optimizing meta-learning pipelines will be explored to make autonomy 6G systems fully autonomous in terms of lifelong learning and self-repairs.

Conclusion

The paper presented Neuro6G-Agent, a new hierarchical neuromorphic agentic intelligence platform of energy-conscious cognitive collaboration and resource-secure optimization in cloud-edge-end 6G portfolios. The framework is a combination of energy conscious spiking neural networks and multi-agent reinforcement learning that form an adaptive and intelligent agent that can make autonomous decisions and co-optimize through collaboration. The overall experimental testing across the various 6G environments showed drastic energy efficiency (34.7% decreased), latency optimization (28.3% enhanced), and security threat detection accuracy of 95.6%, representing an improvement of 8.4% points over the strongest evaluated baseline, with a false positive rate of 2.7% and a mean response time of 55.1 ms across all threat categories (Table 5) to those of state-of-the-art methods. Scalability is also provided by hierarchical architecture which provides efficient coordination among the cloud orchestrators, edge coordinators and the end devices. The cognitive collaboration process enables the sharing of resources securely and efficiently with the use of trust-based consensus schemes. The findings confirm that neuromorphic intelligence is effective in solving the complex optimization problem of the next-generation wireless architecture and is effective in future studies on the 6G wireless network optimization.

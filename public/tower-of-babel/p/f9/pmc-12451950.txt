Development and early reach of a podcast-based innovation to support medical residents' personal and professional development
Authors: Maaike P Smit, Karsten A van Loon, Reinoud JBJ Gemke, Matthijs de Hoog, Janielle van der Velden
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDespite the use of structured competency frameworks and supporting educational tools, medical residents often continue to struggle with personalising their learning, managing feedback, and balancing professional demands with personal well-being. These persistent challenges suggest that conventional support systems may not fully address the nuanced needs of residents, contributing to feelings of disconnection and stress during training. The Resident Talks Podcast was developed to provide residents with accessible insights and practical tools to support their personal and professional development, particularly in areas such as work-life balance, stress management, and feedback integration. The podcast was developed in the Netherlands by a research group studying EPA-based residency training. It was inspired by insights from their qualitative studies and shaped in collaboration with residents. Social media engagement ensured the content remained relevant to their evolving needs. Topics ranged from professional identity, feedback culture and mental health to more sensitive or underrepresented issues such as pregnancy, diversity and addiction. Each 30-minute episode was distributed across platforms like Spotify and Apple Podcasts to maximise accessibility. To date, the podcast has released 21 episodes and garnered over 14,700 downloads. Feedback highlights its practical value and relevance in addressing issues that are not always openly discussed in formal training. The Resident Talks Podcast demonstrates the potential of innovative media to supplement resident support systems, addressing personal and professional challenges in a flexible and accessible way. Further research is needed to evaluate whether and how this format provides meaningful support in daily residency practice.KEYWORDS: Podcast, resident well-being, resident support, educational innovation, stress management in residency, work-life balance

Introduction

Drawing on years of qualitative research into the experiences of paediatric residents within an entrustable professional activity (EPA)-based training programme, we observed recurring challenges in their learning and development [1,2]. Although the programme is structured around competency frameworks and includes tools such as feedback training and learning plans, residents continued to struggle with personalising their learning, balancing professional demands with personal well-being, and managing feedback effectively [3]. Despite previous efforts to share practical insights addressing these issues, many of these challenges persisted, highlighting the limitations of conventional support systems in meeting the nuanced needs of residents [4].

Recognising these gaps, and drawing on years of engagement with this group, we felt compelled to explore alternative, innovative ways to support residents more directly. Podcasts are already widely used in medical education, offering accessible and flexible ways to engage with content [5]. Recognising this potential, we developed the Dutch-spoken Resident Talks Podcast, which aims to support residents’ well-being, feedback culture, and experiences with the hidden curriculum, grounded in peer perspectives and insights from qualitative research. This initiative was designed to provide practical tools and foster a sense of community, addressing the everyday challenges faced by medical residents. This article outlines the rationale, development, and indicators of success for the Resident Talks Podcast, exploring its potential to support residents more effectively throughout their residency training.

Development of the innovation

The Resident Talks Podcast was developed in response to recurring themes identified through earlier qualitative research on paediatric residents’ learning experiences, including interviews and focus groups [1,2]. Focusing on key themes such as work-life balance, self-care, and feedback culture, the podcast aimed to address gaps left by traditional educational formats and offer accessible, practice-based support. Each episode featured guest speakers—including psychologists, medical specialists, and other professionals—who contributed their expertise to enrich the discussions.

To ensure the podcast reflected residents’ most relevant experiences, we actively involved them through social media platforms such as Instagram. Polls and feedback tools enabled residents to suggest topics and provide input on episode content, ensuring both relevance and practicality. The account was publicly accessible and gradually gained followers who, based on their interactions and feedback, appeared to come from various training regions and disciplines across the Netherlands. This allowed for diverse input during the early development phase.

Each episode was designed to be approximately 30 min long, reflecting listener preferences for optimal engagement. The podcast initially launched on Spotify and later expanded to Apple Podcasts and Podimo, increasing its accessibility. Promotion via social media, particularly Instagram and LinkedIn, further strengthened connections with residents and helped foster an interactive community that actively contributed to the podcast’s development.

Preliminary outcomes and feedback

Since its launch, the Resident Talks Podcast has been well received by residents, who have engaged with its content across platforms. To date, 21 episodes have been released, with a total of 14,756 downloads. While detailed listener data are not available, the reach and volume of engagement suggest that the podcast resonates with a broad group of medical residents. Approximately half of the episodes focus on personal development (e.g. stress, self-care, pregnancy), while the other half address professional themes (e.g. feedback, professional identity, supervision). Download data do not indicate a statistically significant preference for either type of content (mean downloads 796 vs. 600, p = 0.15), with higher peaks likely reflecting episodes featuring well-known guests with strong social media reach. Download statistics further show that about 90% of listeners were based in the Netherlands, with the remaining 10% spread worldwide. In several academic and general teaching hospitals, the podcast has been recommended during introductory training sessions for new residents as a helpful resource.

Residents’ feedback from social media highlights its accessibility and ability to address overlooked issues. Residents have reported that the podcast has helped them reflect on and address aspects of their personal and professional growth. These early indicators suggest that the Resident Talks Podcast helps fill a gap in resident support by offering a flexible, engaging, and widely accessible platform.

Lessons learned and future directions

The success of the Resident Talks Podcast offers valuable insights for medical educators exploring innovative tools to enhance resident support. A key takeaway was the importance of flexibility—not only in the podcast format, but also in adapting content based on audience feedback. For example, shorter episodes of approximately 30 min proved more engaging and better suited to residents' busy schedules compared to the initial 45-min format.

Addressing under-discussed challenges, such as work-life balance and stress management, proved critical. These topics are not always openly addressed in training, which may contribute to feelings of isolation among residents [4]. By providing a platform to explore these often under-discussed topics, the podcast offered practical tools, insights, and a sense of community to help residents navigate these challenges. Research supports the importance of addressing mental health and burnout to promote resident well-being [6]. Tailoring content to a diverse audience of residents from various medical specialties was another key consideration. Universal themes like stress resonated broadly, but niche topics – such as navigating pregnancy during residency – required careful attention to tone and context to remain accessible to a wider audience.

Ongoing social media engagement was essential to track evolving needs and shape relevant content. Promoting the podcast also presented challenges. While platforms like Instagram and LinkedIn were useful for raising awareness and collecting input, building and maintaining engagement required sustained effort. This mirrors previous findings on the challenges and strategies of using social media effectively in medical education [7].

Finally, incorporating diverse expert voices while maintaining relatability was essential. Guest speakers – including psychologists, midwives, residents, medical specialists, and researchers – added depth and perspective. This diversity enriched the content while ensuring accessibility. By simplifying complex topics without losing depth, the podcast created space for meaningful conversations that supported both personal connection and professional growth.

Conclusions

Drawing on several years of qualitative research into residents’ experiences, we developed the Resident Talks Podcast to address recurring challenges in training. Early engagement and listener feedback indicate that it provides an accessible way to explore both personal and professional topics that are not always openly discussed in formal education. While further research is needed to examine its impact and applicability across contexts, these initial experiences suggest that creative, flexible formats can meaningfully complement existing support structures in residency training.

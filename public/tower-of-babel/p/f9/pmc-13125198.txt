Longitudinal Patterns of Dental Health Care Utilization 18 Months Before and 36 Months After Initiation of Index Medications for Opioid Use Disorder
Authors: Payel Jhoom Roy, Nalingna Yuan, Yaming Li, Anne Mobley Butler, Katelin B. Nickel, Gretchen Gibson, Colin C. Hubbard, Taylor L. Boyer, Katie J. Suda, Michael J. Durkin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objectives:In 2022, the FDA issued a drug safety communication based on case studies that transmucosal buprenorphine, a medication for opioid use disorder (MOUD), may contribute to dental disease. We sought to assess longitudinal dental care utilization patterns among patients with opioid use disorder (OUD) in the 18 months before and 36 months after MOUD initiation.Methods:Using data from the Veterans Affairs Corporate Data Warehouse (2003–2020), we created a cohort of patients coded for OUD and prescribed MOUD. Outcomes included preventive and therapeutic dental visits and oral infections within 18 months before and up to 36 months after index MOUD. We used unadjusted Poisson models to estimate incidence per 1000 patients by MOUD. We performed analyses with and without interval censoring for edentulism, death, or a 60-day gap in MOUD.Results:Among 49,675 eligible patients, 21,551 received methadone, 17,759 transmucosal buprenorphine, 8993 oral naltrexone, and 1372 injectable naltrexone. Median (IQR) days on treatment varied by drug: methadone 95 (52, 257), buprenorphine 309 (102, 968), oral naltrexone 49 (30, 101), injectable naltrexone 28 (28, 85). We observed an immediate increase in dental visits from a baseline range of 135–144 visits/1000/6-month period to 223–686 visits/1000/6-month period after initiating any MOUD. Patterns were similar by MOUD agent and formulation. Results were similar in analyses with and without interval censoring.Conclusions:Both preventive and therapeutic dental utilization increased immediately following initiation with MOUD. Future observational studies of the effects of MOUD on adverse dental outcomes should account for confounding due to health-seeking behavior.

Oral health is strongly linked with overall health, including cardiovascular disease and diabetes.1 People with OUD are at high risk of poor oral health and thus at risk for the medical sequelae of untreated oral complications.2–4 These risks have only increased as the prevalence of opioid use disorder has risen drastically over the last 20 years.5 Indeed, drug overdoses now surpass motor vehicle accidents as the leading cause of accidental death among US adults.6

MOUD, including methadone, transmucosal and long-acting injectable buprenorphine, and oral and injectable naltrexone, reduce morbidity and mortality associated with OUD, including reducing opioid overdose-related deaths.7 However, only a minority of patients initiate any MOUD, and even fewer continue for more than 6 months.7 Reasons for poor initiation and retention rates include patient and clinician stigma towards MOUD, regulatory requirements, and changing potency of the nonprescription opioid supply, making it difficult to initiate MOUD and find a therapeutic dose.8–14

On January 12, 2022, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a Drug Safety Communication warning of potential oral complications related to the use of transmucosal buprenorphine products.15 This warning was based on case reports that found increased incidence of caries and dental extractions after initiating transmucosal buprenorphine. National addiction organizations quickly called for retraction of the Communication due to the overwhelming benefit of buprenorphine and inadequate level of evidence.16

Studying this phenomenon is critical because an un-intended consequence of this warning may be to dissuade people from initiating buprenorphine or other MOUD, like methadone, based on low-quality data. Understanding patterns of longitudinal dental utilization is a crucial first step to better elucidating the relationship between MOUD and dental disease, and has not previously been done. Thus, we sought to characterize the longitudinal dental utilization patterns of people with opioid use disorder relative to the time of MOUD initiation.

METHODS

Data Source

We used electronic health record data from the Veterans Affairs Corporate Data Warehouse (VA CDW) from October 8, 2001 (1 y before FDA approval of buprenorphine for OUD) through November 30, 2023. Participant data were linked to Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services claims as well. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards at Washington University in St. Louis, VA, Pittsburgh Healthcare System, and University of Pittsburgh.

Study Design and Population

We created a cohort of patients aged 19.5 years or older who were prescribed MOUD ( ≥ 28 d supply) from April 8, 2003 to November 30, 2020 (Supplemental eFigure 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). We required a diagnosis code for OUD (Supplemental eTable 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777) before the MOUD prescription date. The index date was defined as the date of the MOUD prescription. The baseline period was defined as the 18-month period before the index MOUD date, to maximize inclusion of patients with enough baseline data, while also establishing a pattern in the baseline period. The follow-up period was defined as the 36-month period after the index date, to include median time to dental disease development (2 y).15 We required a minimum of 28 days of MOUD exposure to ensure MOUD receipt for maintenance therapy, rather than for withdrawal or detoxification. We excluded patients with a diagnosis during the baseline period for dementia (12 mo minimum lookback, 36 mo maximum lookback), edentulism (18 mo lookback), and malignancy (all-available lookback). We also excluded patients admitted to a VA Community Living Center (long-term care facility17; 3 d lookback). See Supplemental eTable 2 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777) for codes used to define exclusion criteria. We required patients to survive for 28 days after index MOUD. We excluded patients who initiated long-acting injectable buprenorphine due to low numbers. Figure 1 presents the cohort flow diagram.

Exposure

We used VA CDW to identify the following MOUD: methadone, transmucosal buprenorphine, oral naltrexone, and injectable naltrexone. We identified transmucosal buprenorphine prescriptions using a combination of national drug codes (NDCs) and the Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS) codes (Supplemental eTable 3, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777).18,19 We identified oral and injectable naltrexone using NDCs. We identified methadone using HCPCS20 and VA Stop Code 523.21 To determine receipt of 28 days of MOUD using HCPCS codes, we required one buprenorphine or 2 methadone codes based on prior literature.20,21 We implemented this guidance based on median time between Stop Codes or HCPCS in our data (14 d for methadone; 28 d for buprenorphine). We considered a 28-day gap in any buprenorphine or naltrexone based on NDC start and end dates to be considered discontinuation of treatment.19 We allowed patients to enroll only once. Following initiation (first 28 d of treatment), we required only one HCPCS code per 28-day period to be considered as retained in treatment.

Outcomes

Multiple dental care utilization outcomes were identified from 18 months before and up to 36 months after the index MOUD date. We calculated preventive and therapeutic dental visits per 1000 patients within 6-month periods relative to index MOUD date (Supplemental eTable 4a, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). Dental visits were captured using Current Dental Terminology (CDT) codes in VA CDW and VA CMS data, which are standardized codes released by the American Dental Association (Supplemental eTable 4b, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777).22 Oral infections were captured using ICD-9-CM and ICD-10-CM oral infections codes from any dental or medical encounter, including linked CMS claims available (Supplemental eTable 4c, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). In addition to dental outcomes, we also assessed influenza vaccination, another health-seeking behavior outcome that we hypothesized may demonstrate similar patterns following exposure to index MOUD. Influenza vaccination was identified using procedure codes (Supplemental eTable 4d, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777).

Baseline Characteristics

We assessed the following baseline characteristics as defined in the VA CDW: age at index MOUD, sex assigned at birth, race, and rurality. We defined VA dental care eligibility at index MOUD as patients eligible for continuous and comprehensive dental care at the VA during the baseline period. Comorbidities were defined using the Elixhauser comorbidity index and captured during a 2-year lookback period.23 Health care utilization intensity was defined using the number of primary care visits, specialist visits, hospitalizations, and ED visits per patient in the 6 months before index MOUD. We quantified use of other substances associated with oral health issues in the year before index MOUD: tobacco use and total days’ supply of anticholinergic medications.24

Statistical Analysis

We summarized the distribution of baseline characteristics by exposure group (eg, methadone, transmucosal buprenorphine, naltrexone). We calculated unadjusted incidence of dental care utilization per 1000 patients by MOUD during the following 6-month periods relative to index MOUD date: −18 to −12 months, −12 to −6 months, −6 to 0 months, 0 to 6 months, 6 to 12 months, 12 to 18 months, 18 to 24 months, 24 to 30 months, and 30 to 36 months. We used unadjusted Poisson models to estimate incidence and corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Analyses were conducted using 2 approaches: (1) a simple approach wherein patients who were initially eligible were eligible for all intervals, and (2) an interval censored approach wherein patients were not eligible for inclusion in intervals after MOUD discontinuation (ie, a gap in index MOUD of > 60 d), edentulism, or death. We stratified dental care utilization by preventative and therapeutic dental visit types. To account for the possibility of issues related to accessing dental care, we performed sensitivity analyses restricted to patients with at least one baseline dental visit and patients with baseline dental eligibility. We calculated annual rates of influenza vaccination (ie, another health-seeking behavior). We did not perform estimation for time periods with < 100 patients. We used SAS 9.4 and RStudio version 2023.12.1.

RESULTS

A total of 49,675 patients with OUD received MOUD for a duration of at least 28 days and met other study inclusion criteria (Fig. 2), 21,551 (43%) received methadone as their index MOUD, 17,759 (36%) received transmucosal buprenorphine, 8993 (18%) received oral naltrexone, and 1372 (3%) received injectable naltrexone. Approximately 15% (7374) of patients had at least one dental visit in the 18 months before index MOUD (total number of visits = 26,487).

Mean (SD) age was 45 (13.2) years. Approximately 93% were males, 75% self-identified as white, 75% lived in an urban area, and 39% also had Medicare or Medicaid insurance. Median (IQR) duration of index MOUD was 95 (52, 257) days on methadone, 309 (102, 968) days on buprenorphine, 49 (30, 101) days on oral naltrexone, and 28 (28, 85) days on injectable naltrexone. Tables 1 and 2 present demographic and dental utilization characteristics by index MOUD, respectively. We observed some notable differences between different study populations. Patients who received naltrexone formulations had more documented comorbidities, followed by buprenorphine and methadone (Table 1). Patients on naltrexone had much higher documentation of alcohol use than methadone or buprenorphine. Patients on methadone had the lowest documented prevalence of depression, other psychiatric diagnoses, use of primary care specialty, and emergency department visits. Patients on buprenorphine were more likely to be dual eligible with Medicare and Medicaid insurance compared with patients receiving methadone or naltrexone.

Descriptive Primary and Secondary Analyses

Figure 2A depicts the number of dental visits per 1000 patients stratified by MOUD type based on the intention-to-treat analysis (without censoring). Corresponding counts per 6-month period are available in Supplemental eTable 5 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). In the 3 years post-index MOUD, 17,005 patients had at least one dental care visit (total number of visits = 93,802). Dental care utilization increased to 1888 visits per 1000 patients post-MOUD from 533 visits in the 18 months pre-index MOUD in the overall cohort. We observed a similar pattern regardless of MOUD formulation: a substantial increase in dental utilization immediately after initiation of MOUD, followed by a gradual decrease in utilization that remained above the baseline rates.

Figure 2B displays dental utilization patterns by MOUD with interval censoring, with corresponding counts per 6-month period available in Supplemental eTable 6 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). This figure also showed low baseline dental utilization, with a substantial increase among all stratified treatment groups, followed by a more gradual reduction in dental utilization over time.

Descriptive Sensitivity Analyses

Figures 3A, B (Supplemental eTables 7 and 8, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777) show dental care utilization patterns by preventive and therapeutic visit types, respectively. Patterns for dental care utilization were similar, with the highest utilization of both therapeutic and preventative dental services in the naltrexone group and the lowest among the methadone and buprenorphine groups. We did not observe notable changes in dental care visits per 1000 patients post-index MOUD compared with pre-index MOUD, nor did we see a change in utilization patterns between MOUD type, regardless of visit type (preventive or therapeutic).

Figures 3C, D (Supplemental eTables 9 and 10, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777) present patterns of dental care utilization in the subpopulations who have previously received dental care, as well as patients eligible for comprehensive VA dental care in the baseline period, respectively. The overall patterns were similar compared with patterns stratified by MOUD.

Rates of diagnosis for acute oral infections per 1000 patients using ICD-9/10 codes are displayed in Figure 3E and Supplemental eTable 11 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777). We observed a small increase in the rate of oral infections from the baseline period up until 6 months after initiation of most MOUD.

We observed a slight increase in influenza vaccination rates between baseline and after initiation of MOUD in the methadone and buprenorphine groups, but no differences in the naltrexone cohorts (Figure 3F and Supplemental Table 12, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JAM/A777).

DISCUSSION

This national cohort study of patients with OUD evaluated changes in dental health care utilization in the 18 months before and up to 36 months after MOUD initiation. We observed a sharp increase in proportion of patients accessing dental visits in the 6 months following MOUD initiation, across all MOUD types, visit type (preventive or therapeutic), or dental accessibility (receipt of prior dental care or dental eligibility status). Proportion of patients accessing dental visits began to decrease at 12 months post-index MOUD but remained above baseline during the study period.

Since the FDA Communication was published in 2022, several studies have reported an association between buprenorphine and dental disease.25,26 Our findings raise questions about the interpretation of these associations. The immediate increase in dental utilization we observed suggests several noncausal explanations that merit consideration, including increased health-seeking behaviors following MOUD treatment initiation.1,27,28 For instance, MOUD initiation requires evaluation by a health care clinician, potentially leading to referral for dental or other health care services, especially at the VA, where many services are accessible to Veteran patients.

Our findings of increased dental care utilization within 6 months following MOUD initiation were consistent across all MOUD, including opioid antagonists. This finding is notable, as opioid antagonists are not known to have the same risk for xerostomia and direct oral toxicity as opioid agonists. Instead, patients receiving opioid antagonist therapy had the highest rates of dental care utilization, though differences were observed at baseline. This evidence of health-seeking behavior is also consistent with our observation of increased receipt of influenza vaccination in the year before and after initiation of MOUD for buprenorphine and methadone. Lastly, we found an increase in oral infection diagnoses within the first 6 months of index MOUD initiation, which is inconsistent with the FDA warning citing a median of 2 years to caries development and extraction.15 As a result, we suggest these infections may represent preexisting disease, rather than new dental disease, and were diagnosed as a result of a general trend in increased dental care utilization post-MOUD initiation.

Compared with previous studies, the characteristics of our study population had some meaningful similarities and differences, which may impact the generalizability of our findings. The patients in our cohort were similar in age19,29 or older30 than prior research, and we found that a higher percentage of black patients received methadone and naltrexone compared with buprenorphine, consistent with prior work.31,32 A higher proportion of patients on methadone lived in urban areas (83%) compared with buprenorphine (~65%) and naltrexone (~75%). This is expected given that methadone maintenance treatment programs can be challenging to access in rural areas.33,34 Our patient population is also over 90% male, in contrast to other studies of MOUD utilization, where the proportion of people with OUD receiving MOUD is typically around one-half to two-thirds.7,19,29 Lastly, we found that the median time in treatment on buprenorphine was significantly higher than for methadone, in contrast to prior work.7 Our inclusion criteria required at least 28 days’ exposure to index MOUD, thus patients who received intermittent or inconsistent courses of buprenorphine treatment were not included.

One prior study evaluating the association between MOUD and dental utilization showed no increase in dental access or number of dental procedures received in Massachusetts.1 However, the data sources were different (Massachusetts All-Payer Claims Database compared with VA CDW), and access to dental services may vary between the VA and a state database. Our findings were also different than a prior retrospective analysis showing increased risk of adverse dental outcomes after initiation of sublingual buprenorphine/naloxone compared with transdermal buprenorphine and oral naltrexone.25 However, this study only evaluated dental infections using ICD-9/10 codes and did not look at visits to a dentist or dental procedures, which can provide more insight into dental disease incidence and prevalence.

A strength of this study is that we used a database that included patients on all MOUD formulations and was linked to the dental encounters. However, we found that patients receiving methadone had the lowest rates of depression and psychiatric disease. These results are inconsistent with prior literature.35 These rates are likely artificially low compared with buprenorphine and naltrexone, given that the latter 2 medications are often given in a primary care setting where other chronic diseases are coded and addressed by primary care physicians. In contrast, methadone clinics generally do not address these comorbidities, likely leading to substantial undercoding in our data.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, we suspect that comorbidities (eg, depression, psychiatric disease) were substantially undercoded in patients on methadone versus other MOUD formulations, based on expert opinion and prior literature.35 This discrepancy is logical since patients receiving buprenorphine and naltrexone are typically cared for by primary care physicians in primary care settings where chronic diseases are diagnosed and coded, whereas patients receiving methadone attend methadone clinics where comorbidities are generally not addressed. We initially planned to compare dental utilization by MOUD, accounting for patient characteristics; however, the artificially low prevalence of comorbidities among patients receiving methadone precluded the ability to accurately account for patient characteristics. Therefore, we did not conduct adjusted analyses, within-person correlation, between-exposure group comparisons, or denominator logic (per-interval eligibility).

Second, we noted increased health-seeking behavior as demonstrated by higher influenza vaccine rates, but it is noteworthy that rates were only modestly higher and present in only 2 MOUD. We believe that this was due to 2 factors. First, many patients receive MOUD for short periods (eg, < 28 d) before receiving longer treatment durations, thus it is likely that some patients engaged in the health care system and initiated MOUD for a shorter period of time that would not have met our inclusion criteria (eg, 28 d) and received an influenza vaccine during the baseline period. Second, influenza vaccinations are administered annually, which allows any patient to be counted as receiving a vaccine between −364 and −1 days of initiating a MOUD. This likely diminished our granularity to detect small differences in long-term treatment on buprenorphine.

In addition, we could not account for dental or medical care received outside of the VA system, though the consistency of the dental care patterns, regardless of MOUD type, indicates a consistent pattern of usage. Patients on long-acting injectable buprenorphine were not included in the analysis due to small sample size. This may be because the inclusion criteria required no index MOUD (no other MOUD of 28 d duration prior). However, we compared the ratio of transmucosal buprenorphine to long-acting injectable buprenorphine in the VA CDW to another internal data set (Merative MarketScan Multi-State Medicaid Database) and other literature36 and found similarly low rates of long-acting injectable buprenorphine usage compared with transmucosal buprenorphine.

Finally, our cohort of Veterans was primarily male, and thus results may be less generalizable to female patients.

Clinical Relevance

Following MOUD initiation, patients with OUD exhibited an immediate increase in dental care utilization, in contrast to a delayed increase we may have expected based on the FDA warning for transmucosal buprenorphine. We agree with the FDA recommendation that suggests clinicians provide routine counseling regarding proper dental care, such as brushing, flossing, and preventative dental visits to patients receiving transmucosal buprenorphine, and continue to recommend MOUD for all patients with OUD—regardless of chosen MOUD.

CONCLUSIONS

Following receipt of any MOUD, patients demonstrated an immediate increase in dental care utilization. Future observational studies of the relationship between MOUD and adverse dental health outcomes should account for confounders related to increased health-seeking behavior around the time of MOUD initiation.

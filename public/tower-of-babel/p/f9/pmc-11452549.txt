A service failure assessment model for smart product consumption experience based on customer perception
Authors: Ting Wei, Yuanwu Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCustomer perception is an important consideration factor in evaluating the quality of human-computer interaction services. Sustainable user experiences and marketing strategies can be created by analyzing customer perception. By understanding consumer satisfaction with product services in the customer perception area, appropriate product service failure prevention strategies can be formulated. A service failure evaluation model is proposed in this study, which considers the customer tolerance area to accurately evaluate consumers’ behavioral experiences from purchasing to using products. The concept of tolerance area is introduced, and a combination of the fuzzy Failure Mode and Effect Analysis (FMEA) method and the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to an Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) method is used to construct a human-computer interaction service failure evaluation model. Potential service failure factors of smart speakers are accurately evaluated by this model, and these service failure factors are ranked within the tolerance area. The research identifies voice misinterpretation and signal connectivity issues as the primary risk factors impacting the quality of human-computer interaction for smart speakers. The application of this method not only enhances the evaluation of smart speaker human-computer interaction services quality but also aids in the precise identification and prioritization of critical failure modes. The proposed service failure prevention strategies can reduce consumer dissatisfaction and provide innovative references for smart product design and marketing. The findings bolster empirical evidence for service failure prevention strategies in smart products and pave the way for novel perspectives on enhancing the quality of human-computer interaction services.Keywords: Customer perception, Human-computer interaction, Failure mode and effect analysis, Fuzzy TOPSIS, Tolerance region, Service failure evaluationSubject terms: Emotion, Social behaviour

Introduction

With the continuous development of artificial intelligence technology, the online purchase of smart products has become a consumer behavior that consumers are enthusiastic about, but it has also brought a series of issues related to user satisfaction and user experience. Smart products integrate advanced computing power, sensors, network connectivity, and other advanced technologies to perform complex tasks, learn user behavior, make autonomous decisions, or interact with users. Although smart products meet people’s demands in function, they are still influenced by a variety of factors, such as product service quality and human-computer interaction methods. These factors can lead to failures in human-computer interaction services, resulting in a significant decrease in user satisfaction1,2. The reason for this is mainly the gap between the user’s expected consumption experience and the perceived consumption experience3. The consumption experience of smart products refers to the experience generated by users in the process of interacting with smart products (including the process from purchase to use). When the larger the gap between user expectations and actual perception, the greater the user’s dissatisfaction, and the worse the user’s evaluation of the product service quality. It is commonly believed that it is crucial to maintain the service quality of the product for the company to maintain the competitiveness of its product in the market. Therefore, it is especially significant to effectively assess the service failure problem and propose corresponding prevention strategies to reduce the negative evaluation of service quality caused by a service failure.

In the process of human-computer interaction, service failure is mainly influenced by subjective factors such as the consumer’s experience, perceptual ability and cognitive level, as well as objective factors of complexity and uncertainty of service quality elements. It leads to ambiguous decisions on users’ evaluation of service quality4–6, Mistakes in decision-making are often encountered by enterprises when it comes to the selection of strategies for improving service quality. Previous scholars in human-computer interaction research have mainly focused on user needs and proposed opportunity points for improving interactive services. Specific functionalities and emotional aspects of poor user experiences were attended to in order to boost user satisfaction7. However, the issue of service failures from the perspective of customer perception has been disregarded. Taking many factors into account, the subtle differences in users’ perception of product services can directly affect their evaluation of service quality8,9. Not much attention has been paid to the research on evaluating HCI service quality from the perspective of the user’s service quality perception. Therefore, how to accurately evaluate the services perceived by users as well as to reduce the gap between users’ expectations and actual perceptions has become an urgent problem to be solved at present.

Research on service failure is divided into service failure relationship studies and service failure evaluation studies. In the study of the relationship of service failure, Hyungsik K10 classified service failure types into service outcome failure and response process failure. They investigated how relationship quality and loyalty moderated the effect of service failure type on failure severity. The results showed that response process failure had a significant positive effect on the severity of a failure. Ock J et al.11 explored the factors that influence the perceived severity of service failure. The results are indications of the user’s past experience satisfaction which negatively influenced the tolerance of service failure. Admittedly, the higher the satisfaction of the user’s previous experience, the lower the tolerance for the current service failure. Song et al.12 proposed a Bayesian network approach. The risk of failure was assessed by focusing on dependencies between service failures to reduce uncertainty in practice. Pacheco et al.13 emphasized the importance of consumer-perceived control to service failure, and compared the perceived control time model with the causal attribution model to analyze consumers’ emotional and behavioral responses after a service failure. The results demonstrated that the temporal model of perceptual control is more explanatory in understanding consumers’ responses. Research on service failure relationships is mainly qualitative, focusing more on consumer services and not enough attention to product services or human-computer interaction service relationships. In this study, the user’s tolerance for service is taken as the criterion for evaluating the effectiveness of human-computer interaction experience. It is used to evaluate service failure in the field of intelligent product design, and to explore the human-computer interaction factors that affect the evaluation of service quality.

In terms of service failure assessment, the failure mode and effect analysis (FMEA) is more widely used as a reliability system model to identify, evaluate and mitigate potential risks14,15. Han et al.16 adopted the FMEA method to prevent the failure of online shopping services. They also improved the risk priority of traditional FMEA, and proposed a risk priority index for online shopping service environment. Xiao et al.17 proposed a consensus-based FMEA approach, which helps team members reach consensus by designing a multi-stage consensus optimization model to further identify the sequence of failure modes. Zhang et al.18 proposed a FMEA approach based on personalized individual semantics, in which members express their opinions on failure modes and risk factors through linguistic distribution assessment matrices, expressing their perspectives on failure modes and risk factors to help risk analysts to identify the most severe failure modes and enhance the reliability and safety of the decision system. An architectural design decision model was developed by Shie et al.19 using theories such as service blueprints, FMEA, and TRIZ. In addition, they conducted an empirical survey of home care services to demonstrate the applicability of the proposed model in improving home care services as well as medical device design. Tang et al.20 combined FMEA and KANO model for determining critical service failure points to improve service quality. In order to quantify the performance of portfolio services under failure recoverability, Gu et al. proposed a performance analysis model for portfolio services considering failure recovery by synthesizing the types of portfolio service failures and recovery strategies21. Wu et al.22 improved the traditional IPA method to address the failure of service quality in the passenger transportation industry. Using the entropy weight method to calculate the evaluation value of impact factors, they constructed the “entropy-satisfaction” matrix of service elements and put forward corresponding suggestions for service quality improvement. Zhou et al. proposed an FMEA method integrating hesitation fuzzy sets and CRITIC to evaluate the failure modes of power transformers23. Shahin et al. conducted a systematic study on FMEA methodology24–26 to explore the application of FMEA in different industries. They proposed an enhanced FMEA approach in 2020, which improves the efficiency and effectiveness of the decision-making process by identifying and assessing risk factors more accurately27. Later, they proposed a 3D loyalty matrix based on FMEA, an approach that provides companies with a more refined customer management and service strategy and improved product positioning by identifying the loyalty characteristics of different customer groups28. The above-mentioned scholars have taken effective measures to explore the research on service failure and its causes, and methods for service quality improvement have been summarized. This literature demonstrates that FMEA is not only an effective risk management tool, but by combining it with other decision support tools, it can provide a comprehensive quality management solution for a variety of industries. However, most of the above methods address the problem of system service failure in the field of management while relatively little research has been conducted on the problem of product interaction service failure in the field of design. At present, it is inevitable and undeniable that there is a certain degree of ambiguity in user perception during human-computer interaction, and sometimes it is difficult to directly express it. Previous interaction studies relied more on designers’ intuition and experience, and lacked accurate assessment of risk factors and prevention, which could easily lead to service failure.

User service quality evaluation plays an extremely critical role in the innovativeness and market competitiveness of products. In order to more accurately evaluate the service quality perceived by users, obtaining a comprehensive view of the elements affecting service quality is of great importance. Therefore, on the basis of the above research, a service failure evaluation model based on the fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS method is proposed in this paper considering the user service tolerance. The combination of FMEA and TOPSIS can provide a more comprehensive and systematic decision support framework that combines the strengths of risk assessment and multi-attribute decision analysis, thereby improving the accuracy and effectiveness of decision making29–31. FMEA is primarily used to identify possible failure modes in a system, assess their impact on the system, and take measures to prevent or mitigate the effects of these failures.The technique for order preference by similarity to an ideal solution (TOPSIS) method is used as a comprehensive evaluation method to rank the decision solutions using positive and negative ideal solutions as two reference values32. It is widely used in design decisions and has good reliability and applicability. The user’s tolerance contains an acceptable upper boundary and an unacceptable lower boundary, and its idea fits with the positive and negative ideal solutions of TOPSIS. The TOPSIS method can be used to describe the user’s tolerance level scientifically and quantitatively, to determine the factors of service failure more comprehensively, and to further evaluate and rank the degree of service failure accurately. The purpose of combining the FMEA and TOPSIS methods can be to first identify and assess which failure modes are present in the user experience of a smart product through FMEA, and then to select the best option among the risk link measures using the TOPSIS method. This combination takes into account not only the risks, but also the relative advantages and disadvantages of the various measures. This combination makes the decision-making process more comprehensive and systematic. As a result, service failure factors can be identified more comprehensively, the degree of service failure can be further evaluated accurately based on the service failure factors, and finally the service failure factors can be ranked. Based on the ranking of service failure factors, the consumer’s tolerance region to service quality is calculated, finally, the service failure prevention strategy is proposed. Compared with traditional HCI research, the advantage of this study is that it focuses more on analyzing the experience of HCI from the perspective of consumer perception. Based on the perceived service quality gap model, the concept of tolerance region is used as an analogy to the user’s perceived degree of satisfaction with the interactive services. The boundaries of consumers’ perception of the service are confirmed by calculating the range of the tolerance region. It can provide effective theoretical support for service quality evaluation in the current competitive market situation of intelligent products.

Research methods

Perceived service quality gap model and calculation of tolerance region

The Service Quality Model (SQM)33, also known as the 5GAP model, was originally proposed by the American marketing scientists Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry (PZB). This model is used to specifically analyze the root causes of quality problems and contains a total of five service quality gaps. They are the gap in managerial awareness, the gap in quality standards, the service transaction gap, the marketing communication gap, and the perceived service quality gap. Among them, the perceived service quality gap refers to when the customer’s perception or experience of the service is different from the expected service, which can easily contribute to a negative quality evaluation. It is found that people’s expectation of service is not a fixed point, but a region. That is to say, within this region, customers are accepting of the services provided34. PZB divides customer expectations into two levels, the ideal service and the adequate service. Ideal service refers to the level of service that customers desire to receive, while adequate service refers to the level of service that is acceptable to the customer. These two service levels constitute the upper and lower boundaries of the service, and the region between the two service levels is called the tolerance region35. The perceived service quality gap model is shown in Fig. 1. The innovation point of this paper is mainly to calculate the ranking of service failure factors in the tolerance region by fuzzy evaluation of failure factors. In this manner, the threshold value of the tolerance region can be determined to prevent the service failure problem more precisely.

In this formula, the indicator values of ideal service and adequate service are generally obtained by scoring on a Likert scale, and the users’ evaluation of the indicators is obtained by questionnaires. Since the threshold of tolerance region is the optimal level and the worst level of the user’s perceived service, respectively, the user is intolerant after going below the appropriate service level. This coincides with the logic of the positive and negative ideal solutions in TOPSIS method. TOPSIS is an approach to ranking the advantages and disadvantages of a limited number of evaluation solutions in engineering. It is judged by determining the distance of each solution from the optimal target to the worst target36. If the evaluation object is closest to the optimal target and farthest from the worst target, the evaluation object is the most suitable solution. Combining TOPSIS with tolerance intervals and using a distance calculation to determine the location of each service failure risk point allow the accurate quantification the threshold value of the tolerance region and risk point ranking.

Methodology and implementation process

This paper proposes a service failure evaluation improvement model based on fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS, which divides the consumer experience service of smart products into four phases, namely, service failure factor analysis, service failure evaluation, service failure diagnosis and service failure prevention strategy. By integrating the four methods of user journey map, fuzzy FMEA, fuzzy TOPSIS and tolerance region calculation, the service failure evaluation and prevention countermeasures are proposed step by step. The innovation of this method is that it not only identifies service failure modes, but also prioritizes the prevention countermeasures with the help of tolerance regions, so as to make more comprehensive decisions on the improvement of product service quality.

Firstly, in the service failure factor analysis stage, the potential failure modes are presented visually through consumer journey map37. By constructing the process of user interaction with smart products, key service failure touch points are identified, and the causes of service failure as well as the opportunity points for improvement are further analyzed. Secondly, in the service failure evaluation stage, service failure modes are evaluated using fuzzy FMEA. The severity (S), occurrence (O) and detection (D) of key failure contacts are fuzzily evaluated by using fuzzy triangular numbers38. In the service failure diagnosis stage, the concept of tolerance region is introduced, and the positive and negative ideal solutions in the TOPSIS method39 correspond to the upper and lower bounds of the tolerance region, and the risk level ranking of service failure is obtained by calculating the relative closeness. Finally, in the service failure prevention stage, the countermeasures for HCI service failure prevention are proposed according to the position of the risk level in the tolerance region. The evaluation of human-computer interaction services for smart speakers is employed as an example to verify the practicality and effectiveness of the method. The methodology and research protocol involved in this study were approved by the Ethics Committee of the College of Art and Design, Wuhan Textile University. Introduction of FMEA

Service failure mode and effect analysis (FMEA) was originally developed as a set of analysis models by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). It was initially used to address the design analysis of a fighter aircraft operating system and has since been applied to several engineering fields40. FMEA is used to predict potential risks prior to product design or production. Once the product is in mass production, risk losses can be reduced and time to market shortened. The FMEA method is used to evaluate the failure of interactive services and to make improvements in advance based on the calculation results. The specific steps of FMEA method are to score the failure severity (S), occurrence (O) and detection (D), and calculate the risk priority number (RPN). The value of RPN is equal to the product of severity, occurrence, and detection, and the risk level is judged according to the value of the RPN. The larger the RPN, the higher the potential failure risk, as shown in Eq. (2). The values of S, O, and D are integers in the interval1,10, nonetheless, the traditional RPN calculation method has certain defects. The weight of each indicator is not considered, the data deviation is marvelous, and the service failure tolerance interval is ignored. The calculation of RPN has been improved in the literature41] and [11 respectively, mainly considering the weight of each index and the tolerance interval, but ignoring the data deviation. In this paper, the FMEA method is improved using triangular fuzzy numbers to reduce data bias. Triangular fuzzy numbers are usually used in cases where uncertainty is mainly concentrated around a single point, and have the advantage of simple calculation. This study focuses on the triangular fuzzy number to understand the value of service failure evaluation during the user experience of smart products. The consumer journey map is coded with S denoting each stage of user experience, B denoting consumer behavior, T denoting contact points of human-computer interaction and corresponding to different emotions, P denoting consumer pain points, and I denoting opportunity points for improving interaction service failure. Results

Failure mode analysis

Risk control before product sales is an essential step in design. In order to reduce the negative experiences of customers with the product during the consumption stage, it is necessary to conduct service failure analysis on the product at the conceptual prototype stage. Smart home speakers are widely loved electronic products, offering various functions such as smart home control, voice chat, and music playback. In order to accurately assess the quality of human-machine interaction services of the product, this study conducts an analysis of service failure factors for smart speakers. To begin with, we screened the user population in Xiamen city with the help of a research company, aged between 25 and 45 years old. Suitable candidates were recommended by the research company and the final candidates were confirmed on account of their age, gender, income and the basic situation of using smart speakers. The study adopts the type sampling method, dividing all users into three categories according to the proficiency of operation, and randomly sampling in each category, through which the commonality in each category can be increased and representative samples can be easily extracted. Specifically, the population using smart products is categorized and sampled into experienced customers, general customers and novice customers, and their familiarity with the use of the product is understood by setting questions, and these three types of people are screened in accordance with the ratio of 1:1:1 as far as possible, to ensure that the process of collecting questions about the experience of smart products can cover the pain points of all users, so that the enhancement of the experience of using smart products is comprehensive and adaptive. Comprehensiveness and adaptability. Last but not the least, 20 respondents were identified by us, all of whom had previous experience with smart speakers or other AI products, including 8 experienced customers, 7 general customers, and the remaining 5 novice customers. The participants were first arranged to operate the smart speaker prototype prepared in advance. This smart speaker prototype refers to the functional sample prior to mass production. Participants interacted with the prototype according to their experience. Each participant interacted with the smart speaker for 120 min, including the whole experience from simulating an online purchase, receiving the product, unboxing the device, connecting the device, interacting with it, and operating it. At the end of the operation, in-depth one-on-one interviews were conducted with the participants for about 15 min each to understand their feedback and doubts throughout the consumer behavior process. The interviews were recorded with audio and video recording devices. After the interviews, the audio files of the user interviews were organized into verbatim scripts, and key information was extracted from the transcripts to construct a customer journey map. This is shown in Fig. 5. According to the emotional curve in the customer journey map, the pain point corresponding to the trough of the emotional curve is selected as the service failure point and the interaction service failure mode analysis table of the smart speaker is established, as shown in Table 2. Discussion

In this paper, from the perspective of customer perception, an interaction service failure evaluation model considering the customer tolerance region is proposed to address the problem of interaction service experience of smart products. In order to validate the superiority and practicality of the method, it has been verified through the smart speaker HCI service experience. In addition, the method proposed in this paper has been compared with the traditional FMEA assessment method. The traditional FMEA method uses the RPN value to indicate the level of risk of each failure mode. According to the size of the RPN value, the risk level of the failure modes is ranked, and the comparison results are shown in Table 6. It can be seen that the ranking results using the fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS method are consistent with the traditional FMEA method. It indicates that to some extent, both methods are reliable. However, there is a large gap between the RPN values of the traditional FMEA and if the evaluation value of one of the indicators changes, it will lead to a change in the overall ranking results, this is consistent with the findings of46. In contrast, the fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS method has less deviation in the evaluation value results and a more stable and precise ranking. The traditional FMEA assigns equal weights to severity (S), occurrence (O) and detection (D), thus biasing the results of the evaluation. In the future, we will further take the analysis of FMEA parameter weights into account47,48. The methodology of this study pointed to several topics that are discussed in the following sections.

Managerial implications

The method proposed in this study combines four methods: user journey mapping in the HCI domain, FMEA in the risk management domain, TOPSIS in the statistical decision making domain, and tolerance region in the marketing service domain, which can achieve risk prevention, resource optimization, and user experience enhancement in the HCI domain of smart products. First, it helps managers identify and prioritize critical service failure points to reduce user dissatisfaction and negative evaluation. Secondly, it improves the efficiency of service quality improvement measures through accurate assessment and rational allocation of resources. Ultimately, user experience is enhanced by optimizing human-computer interaction services to increase user satisfaction and loyalty.

Theoretical implications

Compared with the traditional product risk failure assessment, a service failure evaluation model considering human-computer interaction is proposed in this paper. It is applicable to user service quality improvement of smart products in the experience era, and as a new comprehensive assessment model, it provides a new theoretical tool for the field of service failure assessment. The fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS method is adopted for the failure point analysis. The process of human-computer interaction is presented in a visual way through the user journey map and the change in satisfaction level during user interaction is obtained more visually. By introducing fuzzy theory, triangular numbers are used to reduce ambiguity and uncertainty in the index evaluation. The concept of user tolerance region is also considered as the TOPSIS method is combined with the tolerance region. The potential failure points in the tolerance zone can be classified and corresponding prevention strategies can be further proposed according to the tolerance degree. Incorporating the concept of user tolerance into service failure assessment enriches perceived service quality theory. Compared with the traditional FMEA evaluation method, the fuzzy FMEA-TOPSIS method solves the drawbacks brought by the simple multiplication of RPN, reduces the problem of large over-registration bias triggered by the change of evaluation index, and make it easier to identify service failures more easily. This is consistent with the findings of Shahin et al.29–31. Although there are some deficiencies in the proposed methods, the overall train of thought is feasible. Combining fuzzy mathematics, decision analysis and human-computer interaction design demonstrates the potential of interdisciplinary research in solving real-world problems and realizing interdisciplinary integration.

Conclusions

There is no doubt that the existence of multiple touchpoints in the human-computer interaction service of smart products results in complexity and inaccuracy in service quality evaluation. The traditional service quality evaluation more focuses on the overall service evaluation, where a comprehensive service quality improvement plan is proposed based on the evaluation results. But in fact, the interactive services are connected one by one through the human-computer interaction process. The cascade effect of potential service failure eventually leads to service failure and affects user experience. Therefore, the main contributions of this study are the following: (1) The interaction service failure evaluation model proposed in this paper combines tolerance region, fuzzy theory, FMEA and TOPSIS methods to establish an interaction service failure evaluation model for home smart speakers. (2) It upgrades the failure assessment in the traditional FMEA as the potential service failure points can be described more precisely and the location and ranking of the tolerance region where the failure points are located are quickly identified. (3) The key service failure points of the home smart speaker are discovered through the case study, and the service failure prevention strategy is proposed in conjunction with the user journey map, verifying the effectiveness and superiority of the interactive service failure assessment method proposed in this paper.

However, our study has several limitations that should be recognized and inform future research. (1) The current study only takes the smart audio prototype as the research object, not a complete evaluation of the real product from the purchase behavior to the use experience. This is helpful for risk control before mass production of the product, but it cannot be involved in the whole consumer experience of the product. (2) Since the product is a conceptual prototype, not a sold product, the customer behavior of the smart audio product in this study mainly focuses on the functional experience of the product, ignoring the multiple service touchpoints in the user’s purchase behavior. (3) Different usage scenarios of smart speakers lead to different potential interaction service failure points. To give an illustration, the smart speaker in the home scenario and the smart speaker in the car scenario will highlight different functions and interaction methods. Accordingly, the user’s interaction contact points will also alter. At the same time, needs on the interaction service also vary from person to person. Therefore, customer journey maps and service failure assessment methods can be adapted to the actual situation and interaction scenarios. This part of the study should be expanded to better improve the model.

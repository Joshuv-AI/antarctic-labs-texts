Pyramid Xception network for accurate detection of abdominal abnormalities in CT scans
Authors: Ramesh Kumar Ramaswamy, Gopika Gopinathan Nair Santhini, Rajasree Rajendran Nair Sivakumari, Santhosh Kumar Balan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbdominal abnormalities are deviations from the normal structure or function of organs and tissues within the abdominal cavity. Effective detection and diagnosis of these abnormalities, such as tumors, infections, cysts, structural anomalies, and inflammations, are essential for proper treatment and management. Abnormalities can arise from various factors, including disease, injury, and congenital conditions, making accurate detection crucial for timely medical intervention. Hence, this paper presents the Pyramid Xception Network (PyX-Net) framework, an advanced method for detecting abdominal abnormalities using Computed Tomography images. The methodology starts with acquiring abdominal CT images from a medical database, followed by image enhancement through Anisotropic Diffusion. Organ segmentation is then performed using a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network. After segmenting the organs, features are extracted using the Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix and wavelet texture analysis. The final detection of abnormalities is achieved with the PyX-Net framework, which integrates Pyramid Network (PyramidNet) and the Xception model. PyX-Net achieved an accuracy of 91.269%, a True Positive Rate of 92.237%, and a True Negative Rate of 91.142% with a K-group of 9.Keywords: Abdomen abnormalities detection, Computed tomography, Image enhancement, Abdominal organ segmentation, Pyramidal Xception networkSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Accurate diagnosis in the medical area requires classifying the medical condition by recognizing a patient’s symptoms and the outcomes from various medical imaging tests. Different medical imaging technologies, such as Ultrasound, X-rays, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), CT scans, and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) are used for accurate diagnosis1. Over time, diagnostic imaging using CT scans in the emergency department has contributed to rising hospital costs. The CT scans become a significant source of radiation exposure, particularly in adult patients2. However, the patient-tailored approach may not fully encompass the wide spectrum of clinical presentations associated with abdominal pain3. The optimal imaging strategy for patients with abdominal pain should enable the timely identification of urgent conditions without exposing patients to unnecessary imaging4. Synthetic CT scans offer the potential to enhance access to radiology services while reducing radiation exposure. In recent years, there has been a development of computer-assisted technologies to support clinicians. The pixel-level semantic information provided by CT scans helps intelligent systems to understand spatial relationships and make critical decisions5. Abdominal pain is a prevalent symptom in children, accounting for approximately 5–10% of pediatric visits to the emergency department1.

When the abdominal pain arises from a non-invasive cause and persists for above five days is known as acute abdominal pain. The causes of abdominal pain vary widely from self-limiting conditions to dangerous situations. The causes of abdominal pain6 are further classified into urgent, requiring immediate medical attention, and non-urgent, which do not rapidly escalate in severity4. The accurate diagnosis of abdominal pain is crucial for timely and effective management, leading to better outcomes and reducing morbidity. To develop a differential analysis for abdominal pain, a comprehensive understanding is essential for early detection. Additionally, the mechanism of abdominal pain is crucial to be identified by the person. The abdominal pain is manifested in three broad patterns: parietal, visceral, and referred pain. Visceral Nociception involves the stretching and distension of abdominal organs using torsion and contraction7. The visceral nociception pain, which is described as a dull ache by the patients, is transmitted through slow-conducting C-fibers. Generally, the visceral pain is localized at the mid stage due to the bilateral nature of the visceral innervation of abdominal organs. Moreover, the disease processes from organs, which are outside the abdominal cavity, can also cause abdominal pain8. In recent years, researchers have focused on developing automated systems for precise and timely predictions that aim to conserve time and energy.

The benefit of automatic technologies lies in their ability to evolve over time, which improves performance through the combination of various methods and contributions. The emerging technologies have shown promising results in object recognition, image classification, obstacle avoidance, facial recognition, natural language processing, material inspection, and many other applications9–12. The radiological evaluation of diseases is primarily based on visual assessment and the interpretation that is enhanced by advanced computer analyses13,14. Artificial Intelligence (AI) is expected to assist specialists in qualitatively interpreting diseases, including tasks like tumor volume delineation over time, biological progression estimation, clinical outcome prediction, and assessing the impact of disease and treatment on nearby organs15,16. Deep learning (DL) models have proven highly effective in various fields like medical image segmentation, classification, and lesion detection17–19. The accuracy of disease detection can be improved by combining the attributes of each model into a stacked ensemble learning classifier20,21. The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) allows deep networks to learn and recognize complex features by hierarchically categorizing and combining features from previous layers, a process known as the feature tier22,23. This feature tier allows DL networks to manage large datasets, efficiently processing millions of inputs through nonlinear functions24–26. The robustness of CNN in evaluating fluctuating image appearances makes it especially suitable for fully automatic segmentation in CT volumes13,27.

Literature survey

CT-based abdominal abnormality detection

Several deep learning approaches have been developed for abdominal abnormality detection using CT images. In5, the Two-Stage Training Scheme was introduced by Li, D., et al., for detecting kidney stones in the abdomen. The Two-Stage Training Scheme was highly capable of accurately detecting kidney stones. Moreover, the Two-Stage Training Scheme had high false positives that affected the cropping ability, which led to incorrect predictions. The scholar Park, Y.J., et al.15 introduced an AI model, which is utilized for the detection of abdominal hemorrhage lesions. The AI model increased the specificity, reduced false positives, and was also capable of detecting abdominal hemorrhage lesions that were scattered in different sizes with high precision. But the AI model did not improve the detection accuracy and also failed to improve the DL techniques. Patro, K.K., et al.17 introduced the Deep Kronecker Neural Network (DKN) for the automated detection of kidney stones. The DKN achieved high robustness and accurate detection, but the effect of Kronecker convolution on overall computational complexity was not assessed.

Abdominal organ segmentation

Accurate organ segmentation is a critical step for abdominal abnormality analysis. The ResUNet model is devised by Rahman, H., et al., in13 for liver and tumor segmentation. The performance of ResUNet was efficient and quick. Additionally, the ResUNet model demonstrated greater efficiency in training time, memory usage, and accuracy. However, ResUNet did not improve localization accuracy or reduce the false negative rate. In28, the DL-based pipeline is introduced by Brutti, F., et al., for localizing and segmenting the thrombus. The DL-based pipeline produced consistent results and did not require prior knowledge about the lumen region. However, the DL-based pipeline was trained only on preoperative CT scans and did not extend the dataset.

DL-Based abdominal abnormality detection

Accurate organ segmentation is a critical step for abdominal abnormality analysis. Zhang, J., et al.29, introduced U-net and a Graph Convolutional Neural Network (U-GCN) for automating the detection and classification of vertebral body fractures. The U-GCN was effective in producing an accurate diagnosis and achieved minimal computational complexity. But, U-GCN failed to simplify the system process. The researcher named Ogunleye, O.A., et al., devised a CNN approach in30 for detecting adipose tissue in children. The CNN enhanced robustness and precision in detection. However, the CNN had included a dataset of pediatric participants who underwent repeated MRI scans and also suffered from generalization issues. The author named Alawneh, K., et al., developed the LiverNet in31, which was used for diagnosing liver tumors. The LiverNet was light, fast, reliable, and accurate, but was not implemented on a large clinical dataset.

Research gap and challenges

Although existing studies have reported promising results in abdominal image analysis, several challenges remain unresolved. Variations in image quality, noise, and low-contrast regions in CT images can adversely affect the accuracy of abnormality detection. In addition, precise organ segmentation remains difficult due to complex anatomical structures, overlapping boundaries, and inter-patient variability. Another major challenge is the extraction of discriminative features that effectively represent both texture and semantic information. Many existing approaches rely solely on deep learning features and fail to exploit complementary texture characteristics present in medical images. Furthermore, achieving high detection accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency remains an important requirement for practical clinical applications.

These deficiencies point toward the need for a reliable framework that can effectively enhance image quality, accurately segment abdominal organs, extract informative features, and perform reliable abnormality detection. To address these challenges, this work proposes the PyX-Net framework, which integrates Anisotropic Diffusion for image enhancement, CGAN for organ segmentation, GLCM and wavelet-based feature extraction for capturing texture information, and a hybrid PyX-Net architecture for meaningful feature representation and classification. The combination of these components enables the PyX-Net to improve detection performance while maintaining computational efficiency.

Proposed PyX-Net for abdominal abnormality detection

The proposed model follows a systematic approach, beginning with the acquisition of abdominal CT images and ending with the detection of abnormalities. The process begins by acquiring CT images of the abdomen, which provide a detailed cross-sectional view of abdominal organs. After acquisition, the CT images undergo an enhancement process that is done using Anisotropic diffusion32 to improve the image quality. The enhanced images are then subjected to segmentation, where the specific abdominal organs are delineated using CGAN33, which is suitable for medical image segmentation. Following the segmentation of abdominal organs, features are extracted from the identified regions. GLCM34 and wavelet texture features35 are used for the subsequent detection of abnormalities. The workflow ends with the detection of abdominal abnormalities using the PyX-Net framework. The PyX-Net model combines the PyramidNet36 and the Xception model37. Abdominal organ segmentation using CGAN

The Abdominal Organ Segmentation refers to the process of delineating different organs within the abdominal region from medical images. The output from image enhancement E is passed to CGAN, which is an extension of the traditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN). Here, CGAN33 is used for segmenting abdominal organs. The CGAN can learn both the segmentation mapping and the underlying data distribution through its generator-discriminator framework. The adversarial learning process encourages the generation of more accurate and realistic segmentation masks, particularly in cases with complex organ boundaries and variations in abdominal CT images.

CGAN architecture

The purpose of using CGAN33 for abdominal organ segmentation is to improve training stability and also increase flexibility. A CGAN incorporates additional information to control the output of the generated data.

Generator

Generator

The generator creates segmentation masks, which are refined through a process involving encoding and decoding layers. Typically, a UNet architecture is used as the generator. The UNet architecture consists of an encoder, decoder, and convolutional layers. The encoder reduces the spatial dimensions, whereas the object details and the initial resolution are recovered by the decoder. The decoder also uses shortcuts that add features from the contracting encoder and expanding decoder paths. When the spatial size reduces, the network’s depth is increased. A doubling of channels occurs after every pooling layer.

(b)Discriminator

Discriminator

The discriminator assesses the quality of the segmentation masks by distinguishing between real masks and synthetic masks that are generated by the generator. The Discriminator contains five convolutional layers. The input concatenates source images and either a real mask or predicted masks. Values produced by the model are bounded between 0 and 1, where higher values correspond to a greater likelihood of segmentation.

Abdomen abnormality detection using the proposed PyX-Net

Abdominal abnormality detection is very important for diagnosing a large range of medical conditions that can impact the organs and structures within the abdominal cavity. (b) Depthwise separable convolutional block of Xception

Depthwise separable convolutional block of Xception

Depth-wise separable convolution is a technique used in CNNs to reduce the computational cost and model complexity while maintaining performance. Unlike conventional convolution, which operates across all input channels simultaneously, depthwise convolution applies an individual filter to each channel separately. Following this, a point-wise convolution is applied across all channels to combine the features extracted from each channel and learn correlations between different channels. A max-pooling operation is applied to the feature maps to decrease the spatial resolution.

Comparative analysis

The comparative assessment of the PyX-Net method against various prevailing approaches is demonstrated by varying the learning samples as well as the K group. The K-group analysis refers to k-fold cross-validation, where the dataset is divided into k subsets and each subset is used once for testing while the remaining subsets are used for training. During both the training–testing split analysis and k-fold cross-validation, the training and testing sets are kept completely separate to avoid any overlap between samples. Therefore, the data used for testing are not involved in the training process.

Conclusion

The PyX-Net framework shows a significant advancement in the detection of abdominal abnormalities using CT images. By integrating PyramidNet and Xception models, PyX-Net shows the strength of both architectures to enhance detection accuracy and efficiency. The combination of advanced image processing techniques with powerful DL models in PyX-Net produces a better performance rate than other prior methods. The proposed PyX-Net methodologies include crucial steps: acquisition of abdominal CT images, image enhancement with Anisotropic Diffusion, Segmentation using CGAN, and feature extraction through GLCM and wavelet texture analysis. The PyX-Net framework demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods by improving accuracy and reliability in detecting and diagnosing abdominal abnormalities. PyX-Net has demonstrated significant advancements in performance across various performance metrics, including accuracy, TPR, and TNR. With a K-Group of 9, the model achieved an accuracy of 91.269%, a TPR of 92.237%, and a TNR of 91.142%. The PyX-Net framework has shown promising results in the detection of abdominal abnormalities using CT images. However, the PyX-Net will be further refined and expanded with a database to offer more comprehensive and effective solutions for detecting and diagnosing abdominal abnormalities.

From advocacy to action: accelerating the fight against AMR in Asia–Pacific
Authors: Saia Ma'u Piukala, Catharina C Boehme
Published: 2026

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is no longer a silent pandemic—it is already reversing decades of progress in global health and putting lives, livelihoods, and health systems at serious risk, especially in Asia and the Pacific, which accounts for over half of the world's population. Of the 1.27 million global deaths directly linked to bacterial AMR in 2019, more than half occurred in this region.1 The WHO South-East Asia Region saw over a million deaths due to sepsis, with most caused by bacterial infections, while the Western Pacific faces projections of up to 5.2 million deaths and $148 billion in excess costs due to drug-resistant bacterial infections between 2020 and 2030.2 The crisis is accelerating—one in six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide is now resistant to treatment, with resistance increasing year on year.3 Furthermore, the burden of drug-resistant tuberculosis remains high in the Asia–Pacific region. More than half of global tuberculosis deaths and two-thirds of cases of drug-resistant tuberculosis occur in this region.4 These trends threaten the foundations of medicine from routine surgery to cancer care and maternal health.

The 2024 UN General Assembly High-level Meeting on AMR marked a pivotal shift in the global response to AMR, with the Political Declaration including a 2030 target of a 10% reduction in global deaths from bacterial AMR compared to 2019. An updated Global Action Plan on AMR for 2026–2036 will be considered by the World Health Assembly in May 2026.

Under the Initiative to Accelerate Action to Fight AMR in the Asia–Pacific region, supported by Japan in close partnership with WHO, the Asia–Pacific Ministerial Meeting on AMR convened in May 2024 resulted in 30 countries endorsing a Joint Position Paper on AMR that articulates shared priorities and practical commitments5; and the annual Tokyo AMR One Health Conference supports collective regional action on AMR by maintaining high-level political attention, fostering peer learning and shaping coordinated responses. The AMR 2026 Summit in Sydney, Australia aimed to shift from commitment to implementation by bringing together key stakeholders to share best practices, strengthen collaboration, and promote accountability.

Our work is guided by a regional vision. The WHO Western Pacific regional blueprint — Weaving Health for Families, Communities and Societies (2025–2029) — underscores that addressing AMR is fundamental to resilient health systems and universal health coverage.6 In the South-East Asia region, the WHO Regional Office facilitated the endorsement of a Regional Roadmap to accelerate actions on antimicrobial resistance in the human health sector (2025–2030)7 and Member States have adopted a regional resolution to strengthen their commitment to addressing AMR.8 Supported by WHO, Asia Pacific countries are updating their national action plans on AMR, with 19 implementing their second national plan and two their third in 2025, highlighting increased institutionalization of AMR efforts. Surveillance systems have strengthened: 22 Member States have submitted AMR data to GLASS-AMR,3 and 29 are enrolled in GLASS-AMU and/or WPRACSS for antimicrobial use data submission.9 The importance of infection prevention and control and antimicrobial stewardship in combatting AMR is increasingly being recognized.

Despite progress, much more remains to be done. Implementation gaps persist, sustainable financing is a major challenge, multi-sectoral One Health collaboration is weak, and surveillance data are still often insufficient or underused for decision-making. Antibiotic use is high, over-the-counter antibiotic sales persist despite regulations, and access to essential antibiotics remains inadequate.10

Through its work at all levels of the organization, and in partnership with its collaborating centres and others, WHO is well placed to continue to provide technical support to Member States across all domains of the AMR response. By pursuing the Initiative to Accelerate Action to Fight AMR in Asia and the Pacific, we aim to ensure that attention continues to be placed on tackling the challenges of AMR both regionally and globally, moving partners from thoughts and talks to implementation and impact.

AMR poses a growing threat to public health in Asia and the Pacific, translating into higher morbidity and mortality, prolonged hospitalizations, increased health-care costs, and compromised outcomes across maternal, pediatric, oncologic, and infectious disease care. In spite of advances—including political commitments, updated national action plans, strengthened surveillance, and enhanced infection prevention and antimicrobial stewardship—the full public health impact depends on effective implementation. Translating surveillance data into optimized prescribing, enforcing stewardship and regulatory measures, and ensuring equitable access to essential antimicrobials are critical for mitigating AMR's burden. Sustainable financing and multisectoral One Health collaboration remain essential enablers. Achieving the 2030 target of reducing AMR-related deaths requires shifting from policy frameworks to measurable health outcomes. Without accelerated, coordinated action, decades of progress in health system resilience and patient survival risk being reversed.

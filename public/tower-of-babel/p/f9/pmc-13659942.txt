Competency-based education model: a key tool for assessment and teaching in continuing medical education of infection prevention and control practitioners in different hospitals, China
Authors: Yao Yao, Zhuhong Zha, Guangying Luo, Bing Huang, Qin Zhang, Li Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCompetency-based frameworks have been widely adopted as the dominant approach to medical education. However, research investigating the impact of competency-based medical education (CBME) on infection prevention and control practitioners (IPCPs) remains limited. This study aims to explore the mode of continuing medical education (CME) and to assess the feasibility and effectiveness of CBME in the field of infection prevention and control (IPC) in China.MethodsA large cross-sectional survey targeting IPCPs was conducted in southwest China from April to June 2022. We used a competency assessment tool based on competency theory, person-post matching theory, motivation theory, and the APIC 2019 competency model for IPCPs in the United States, which includes 4 core dimensions, 11 sub-dimensions, and 47 measurement items, to measure the competencies of IPCPs.ResultsA total of 1083 IPCPs from 511 hospitals participated in the survey. IPCPs from different hospitals exhibited varied preferences in the choice of CME, especially in terms of participation rate, level of training institution, and duration. Short-term training of 2–5 days was preferred by the majority. The binary logistic regression results showed that years of IPC practical experience (OR = 6.47, 95%CI:2.94–14.27), major (OR = 2.10, 95%CI:1.41–3.14), monthly income (OR = 2.59, 95%CI:1.52–4.40) and experience in frontline epidemic response (OR = 1.94, 95%CI:1.23–3.06) were independent factors influencing whether IPCPs participate in CME. The impact of the CME were manifested in the enhancement of the competencies across 4 core dimensions and 10 sub-dimensions, beyond the realm of comprehensive knowledge. The competencies of IPCPs in tertiary, public, general and TCM hospitals significantly exceeded that of their counterparts who had not participated in the programme (P < 0.05), demonstrating a significant enhancement from fair (4.13–5.02) to good (5.02–5.80). The long-term education could better promote the growth of competencies, especially in basic skills, organisational cooperation ability and professional development ability.ConclusionsThe competency framework serves as a key tool for assessing the feasibility and effectiveness of CME and can also highlight variations in the outcomes of competency improvement in IPCPs from different hospitals following CME. Strategic measures should be implemented in the field of competency-based IPC education in China to improve the effectiveness of short-term training and to encourage IPCPs to opt for long-term education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07097-8.Keywords: Competency-based medical education, Infection prevention and control practitioners, Competency, Continuing Medical Education, Cross-sectional survey, Self-assessment

Introduction

Global medical education underwent a significant transformation in the late twentieth century, moving from the conventional model to CBME [1]. CBME has broadened the requirements for healthcare professionals to include not only patient care skills and medical knowledge, but also practice-based learning and improvement, effective communication, professionalism and systems-based practice [2]. Educators are adept at crafting a bespoke instructional strategy tailored to bridge the competency gaps identified by CBME in trainees [3]. Therefore, CBME has gained widespread recognition within the medical education domain, exerting a substantial influence on the continuing professional development of healthcare professionals globally [4–6]. The field of IPC is no exception to this trend. However, CBME is still in the process of construction, and its practical model has some limitations [7]. One of the key challenges is competency assessment [8]. Effective assessment and feedback in CBME assists learners in their continuing professional development and aims to provide evidence of their impact on patient health outcomes [9]. Another key challenge is the validation of evidence-based assessment tools, which can be quite complex. There is also the potential for intrinsic assessment bias [7]. There are differences in the practice of CBME from various countries and regions worldwide, with the majority failing to clarify the objectives, methods, tools, and criteria of the assessment [10]. There is also a key challenge in how the assessed abilities truly reflect clinical practice capabilities and how to bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical competencies [7].

So the concept of competency frameworks has been introduced into the medical field to assist educators in more accurately assessing the competencies [11]. Currently, the assessment criteria within the IPC domain are predominantly aligned with mature competency frameworks, professional association guidelines and certification standards. Since 2010, the Certification Board of Infection Control and Epidemiology (CBIC) [12], the Association for Professionals in Infection Control and Epidemiology (APIC) [13], the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) [14], the Infection Prevention Society (IPS) [15], and the World Health Organisation (WHO) [16] have published and continually updated competency frameworks for IPC, advocating a competency-based pathway. Competency assessment tools have been developed and iteratively updated. In 2012, the American APIC pioneered the release of the IPC competency framework [17], and in 2013 released the competency self-assessment and professional development plan tools [18]. Subsequently, APIC continued to release updated self-assessment tools tailored to IPCPs at different levels in 2015 [19], 2017 [20], and 2019 [21]. These tools incorporate the core elements of both the CBIC and APIC competency models. In 2019, APIC updated the 2012 competency model to require practitioners to achieve CIC® certification to move from Becoming proficient to Proficient [13]. After achieving CIC® certification, practitioners must retake the exam every five years to ensure their knowledge system and practical skills are updated in a timely manner [22]. With the emergence of competency frameworks in the field of IPC abroad and the increasing popularity of effective assessment, it is gradually on the verge of establishing a truly continuous CBME that includes education, training, and practice. However, China just embarked on the journey of promoting CBME in IPC, without officially recognised competency framework and assessment tools for IPCPs. The current administrative documents or guidelines issued by China mainly focus on the responsibilities of IPCPs and the evaluation criteria for the implementation of their work, covering three aspects: 1) the structure, organisation, and management; 2) the education and training; 3) the monitoring of outcomes and process indicators [23, 24]. Guideline for professional training about managing of IPCPs released by China in 2016 divide CME into three stages, including basic training, practical training, and advanced training, and advocate the adoption of different training methods according to different training stages and objectives [25]. Currently, CME projects of IPC in China include two models: short-term training mainly consists of CME organised by academic associations, which belong to advanced training and are conducted in a centralised teaching format. Long-term education includes basic, practical, and advanced training, with training formats including self-study, centralized teaching, and practical operation demonstration. A cross-sectional survey of individual core competencies conducted in Northwest China using a questionnaire adapted to the specific conditions in China, based on the WHO Core Competency Questionnaire [26].

Currently, the majority of universities in China do not offer an IPC major, so talent cultivation mainly relies on CME [27]. CME plays a key role in improving the level of IPC work [28]. However, the CME in the field of IPC has problems with uneven educational quality, and its effectiveness has yet to be assessed in China. Therefore, this study aims to analyse the current status of IPCPs’ participation in CME and evaluate the differential effects of two CME models on the improvement of IPCPs’ competencies in different hospitals in Guizhou Province through a large-scale cross-sectional survey using a domestic questionnaire. This will provide data to explore the improvement of CME modes and promote the optimisation of CBME, further building a CBME system that integrates China’s IPC competency framework, modular courses and competency assessment.

Methods

Study design

From April to June 2022, we conducted a large cross-sectional survey to assess the competency of IPCPs in various hospitals in Guizhou Province, southwest China, using an online questionnaire. This survey was conducted on behalf of the administration, which issued mandatory documents and notified hospitals at all levels in the province to participate in the survey. However, due to the lack of baseline data for hospitals across the province, mandatory participation by each hospital was ultimately emphasised. The informed consent form fully explained the purpose and significance of this survey to all participants, as well as the basic requirements for filling out the questionnaire. Each participant could only submit one Word version of the questionnaire. Each participant completed the Word version of the questionnaire and emailed it to the administrative office for formal review. We entrusted the IPC centres in 9 cities in the province to assist us in reviewing the quality of the questionnaires, which included checking the completion of basic information and logical errors. After the preliminary data quality check, the questionnaires were submitted to our unit. A fully completed questionnaire was considered valid.

Survey tool

The questionnaire was developed by the research team for this study (See Supplemental file online). It consists of two sections: 1. Basic information. Based on the current status of CME in the field of IPC in China, this survey focused on two CME modes, namely short-term training and long-term education. Short-term training includes lectures, workshops, and conferences with an emphasis on theoretical training. Long-term education is a more effective training method than short-term training. In the long-term education model, IPCPs typically undergo comprehensive training at premier domestic hospitals, engaging in full-time study for a period of 3 months to 1 year. This rigorous curriculum is designed to simulate an apprenticeship, combining theoretical knowledge with practical skill acquisition. On completion of the CME programme, candidates must successfully pass an assessment to receive a certificate of completion. Specific survey content included participation rate, level of training institution, time of participation and duration. 2. “Self-Assessment Scale of Competencies for IPCPs”, which was constructed based on competency theory, person-post matching theory, motivation theory, and the APIC 2019 competency model [13], including four core dimensions, eleven sub-dimensions and forty-seven measurement items. The self-assessment used a consistent Likert scale of 1 to 7, with higher scores indicating more robust core competencies. The APIC competency model exclusively encompasses capability components. Competency theory emphasises that competencies refer to the sum of knowledge, skills, and personal traits required for a certain position [29]. Explicit characteristics like knowledge and skills are easy to develop and assess, often serving as metrics in competency models. Personal traits, as implicit characteristics, can influence these explicit ones [30]. Person-post matching theory mainly refers to the two-way matching between individual competencies and job requirements, aiming at effective allocation and rational use of internal human resources in organisations [31]. The higher the degree of mutual alignment in person-post matching, the more conducive it is to improving job satisfaction and productivity. It emphasises the professional values of individuals and their satisfaction with the job, which are implicit characteristics. Motivation theory argues that ‘individual effort’ is influenced by ‘individual goals’, and that the guiding role of goals on behaviour, manifested as achievement motivation, is also an implicit characteristic [32]. Thus, the implicit characteristics, including personal quality, professional values, achievement motivation, were added to the domestic assessment scale as sub-dimensions based on the APIC competency elements.

Psychometric characteristics

A preliminary formulation and optimisation of the competency assessment framework and measurement items were conducted using literature analysis and Delphi method. A pilot test of the questionnaire was conducted to verify the psychometric characteristics of the questionnaire and appropriate modifications were made based on the results. The reliability of this scale, as indicated by Cronbach’s α, was exceptionally high at 0.989. A Cronbach’s α coefficient higher than 0.7 is considered to indicate good reliability of the questionnaire. The four core dimensions include basic skills, organisational cooperation ability, professional development ability and personal traits. The KMO values of these core dimensions were 0.954, 0.968, 0.941, and 0.941. Additionally, the Bartlett’s sphericity test indicated statistical significance (P < 0.001). By confirmatory factor analysis, it was verified that each dimension had good convergent validity and discriminant validity.

Sampling size

Due to the lack of baseline data on provincial IPCPs, the sample size estimation for this cross-sectional survey is based on the pilot test of the questionnaire design. The results of the pilot test show that the self-assessment competency of 417 IPCPs is 5.276 ± 0.932, with 0.932 as the sample standard deviation. According to the sample size estimation formula, n=tαsδ the minimum required sample size is 337. Inclusion criteria for the survey were: part-time and full-time employees with IPC expertise. Conversely, the survey excluded employees on secondment, those on leave and those not currently on duty. We received questionnaires from 1159 IPCPs in 511 hospitals. After excluding 76 invalid or incomplete questionnaires, we obtained 1083 valid questionnaires, giving an effective response rate of 93.44%.

Statistical analysis

Data entry was performed using EpiData V3.1, employing a dual-entry technique to guarantee data accuracy. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables were expressed as [n (%)]. Differences in CME for IPCPs from different hospitals were analysed using Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were included as independent variables in the binary logistic regression model to examine the factors influencing participation in CME. The forward logistic regression method was used in the multivariate model, and variables with P < 0.05 were retained in the final model. The percentile method is a commonly used to determine the cut-off points for ranks. The CDC in the United States adopted the percentile method, defining BMI ≥ P95 as obesity, and BMI ≥ P85 as overweight [33]. This study divides competency into poor, fair, good and excellent based on the P25, P50, and P75 of the total score. We initially conducted a homogeneity of variances test on the self-assessment scores. For data with homogeneity of variance, differences were assessed using Student’s T-test for binary comparisons and ANOVA for multigroup analyses, followed by pairwise contrasts using Scheffé’s method. Conversely, in the absence of homogeneity of variance, the Mann–Whitney U test was used to detect differences between two groups, while Kruskal–Wallis one-way ANOVA (k-sample) was used for pairwise assessments. All data analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics 22 and R version 4.1.0 software. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Variation in CME for IPCPs across hospitals

This survey revealed that 81.44%(882/1083) IPCPs participated in CME programmes. At hospital level, the participation rate of IPCPs was higher in tertiary hospitals than in other hospitals (P < 0.05). The participation rate of IPCPs from tertiary hospitals in national teaching projects was the highest at 47.87% (180/376), while the participation rate of secondary hospitals in provincial teaching projects was the highest at 40.85% (174/426), with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). Across hospitals of all levels, ownership and categories, the highest percentage of IPCPs participated in short-term training programmes of 2–5 days. The participation rate, level of training institution, and duration among IPCPs varied significantly across hospital categories (P < 0.05). Variation in competency of IPCPs pre- and post-training

After accepting the CME programme, IPCPs showed varying degrees of improvement in their competence levels in various items. Except for the comprehensive knowledge items, the differences in improvement effects on the other items were statistically significant (P < 0.05). Nevertheless, power analysis showed a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d < 0.5) for the difference, as shown in Table 3. The overall competence level of 1083 IPCPs was (4.89 ± 1.20), divided into 4 levels according to percentiles, with P25, P50, and P75 being 4.13, 5.02, and 5.80, respectively. After training, the competence of IPCPs in tertiary, public, general, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) hospitals was significantly higher than that of their untrained counterparts (P < 0.05). The competency level improved significantly from fair (4.13–5.02) to good (5.02–5.80). Only the competency level of IPCPs in township hospitals was poor (0–4.13) before training and improved to a fair level (4.13–5.02) after training, but the difference in the degree of progress was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), as shown in Figs. 1 and 2.

Discussion

Preference of IPCPs for CME selection

This study revealed the selection preferences of IPCPs when participating in CME programmes. Firstly, certain IPCPs have not participated in any CME programmes. WHO recommended that each country should collaborate with academic institutions to develop long-term and short-term training programmes for IPC, while also encouraging IPCPs to regularly participate in training [34, 35]. In Germany, IPCPs are required to attend training on IPC and the rational use of antibiotics every 2 years [36]. Secondly, this study found that the IPCPs in tertiary, public, comprehensive, and traditional Chinese medicine hospitals show the highest enthusiasm for participating in CME, which may be related to the high medical level and abundant talent development resources in these types of hospitals in China. Research suggests that the Chinese government should pay more attention to IPC resources and practices in secondary care hospitals [37]. Meanwhile, we also found that the IPCPs of the former hospitals tend to favour national-level projects, whereas the latter hospitals show a preference for participation in provincial-level projects. CME trainees, being on-the-job staff, often choose training programmes based on geographical convenience. Given that Guizhou Province is located in the southwestern region of China, with a lower economic status and less convenient transportation compared to eastern cities, the choice of national-level projects is also limited by regional factors. Research suggests that mobile technology offers an affordable way to deliver distance learning to healthcare workers [38]. The IPC Wiki platform, an open information sharing platform in Europe, offering IPC training materials, aims to store, share and exchange IPC education and training knowledge among European countries [39]. In the future, China can learn from this mode to address the gap in IPC training resources in different regions. Lastly, This study found that IPCPs from different hospitals all preferred short-term (2–5 days) CME. Similarly, a comparison of two CME methods for school nurses showed that the 4-day workshop had a higher completion rate and increased participants’ self-efficacy [40].

Various factors affect the participation of IPCPs in training

Based on the fact that only 81.44% (882/1083) of IPCPs have participated in CME programmes, we further analysed the independent factors influencing the participation in training. Many countries are paying increasing attention to the training of IPCPs. Prior to 2015, more than 80% of countries worldwide recognised the importance of CME for IPCPs, but only half of countries effectively implemented IPC training [41]. In 2010, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) conducted a survey of IPC training needs in Europe, which showed a significant increase in demand for professional training in IPC [42]. The research in 2014 showed that from 2006 to 2010, the number of EU countries with national training programmes in IPC increased from 32.26% (10/31) to 57.58% (19/33) [43]. This study found that IPCPs with majors in clinical medicine, nursing, public health and microbiology had more opportunities to participate in CME (OR = 2.10, 95%CI:1.41–3.14), which may be related to the professional structure of the IPCPs. The survey of the Training needs assessment in Infection Control in Europe (TRICE) project in 2010 showed that among the 33 member countries, the educational backgrounds of IPCPs were mainly in microbiology, followed by infectious diseases, epidemiology, and nursing [44]. A study in 2020 found that IPC training systems in 11 European countries varied in content, type, duration and certification, with heterogeneity in training opportunities and courses for IPCPs from different majors, highlighting the urgent need for a unified training programme [44]. This study also found that IPCPs who had experience in frontline epidemic response were more willing to participate in CME (OR = 1.94, 95%CI:1.23–3.06). The pandemic of emerging infectious diseases has placed new demands on the professional skills of IPCPs [45]. We also found that individuals with longer IPC practical experience and higher monthly income were more willing to participate in CME, which may be related to higher professional identity and self-motivation.

CBME: A key tool for assessment and teaching in CME of IPCPs

In this study, the competencies of IPCPs were divided into four levels. This classification aligned with the APIC 2019 competency framework, which divided IPCPs into Novice, Becoming proficient, Proficient, and Expert, based on their competency in six core domains [13]. We found there were significant differences in the effect of competency growth among IPCPs in different hospitals, only IPCPs from tertiary, public, general, and TCM hospitals significantly improved their competency, with the level increasing from fair (4.13–5.02) to good (5.02–5.80). This may also be related to the high participation rate of IPCPs in these hospitals in CME, which also indirectly reflects that CME is an key tool for enhancing capabilities.

With the widespread development of CME programmes, there are persistent doubts about how healthcare professionals sustainably improve their clinical practice skills [46, 47]. There is a strong need to develop a theory-based tool to evaluate the effectiveness of CME [48]. We found that long-term education can better promote the growth of competencies, especially in basic skills, organizational cooperation ability and professional development ability. In comparison with short-term training, the advantages of long-term education are reflected in three points. Firstly, short-term training focuses on theoretical teaching and are conducted in a centralised teaching format, a feature consistent with IPC training programmes in Germany and Australia [49]. In contrast, most domestic long-term education combines theoretical teaching with clinical practice, which is the key way to significantly improve competency [50]. In the global competency framework, the United States [13], Europe [14], the United Kingdom [15], and WHO [16] have all proposed “clinical IPC practice activities” as a core area. Therefore, CME curricula should be revised to enhance clinical IPC skills through additional hands-on training [51]. Secondly, long-term education emphasising hands-on teaching, interacting with the instructor as a colleague for a long period of practice and implementing simulated teaching. Simulation training has shown potential in IPC, effectively equipping practitioners with key skills to manage complex infection outbreaks through practical learning [52]. Finally, long-term education sets learning objectives for trainees, and a certificate can only be obtained by passing an examination at the end of the programme, while short-term training has no assessment requirements. A survey designed by the Korea Human Resources Development Institute for Health and Welfare shows that training design (β = 0.269, P = 0.021), learning objectives (β = 0.356, P = 0.023), and methods (β = 0.365, P = 0.020) can significantly promote the competency acquired by IPCPs through training, thereby reducing the incidence and mortality of patients [53].

Limitations

This research addressed the existing gap in the study of CBE in the field of IPC in China, and provided a profound and insightful reference for exploring the reconstruction and enhancement of CBME in IPC. However, the methodological challenge of this study lies in the sample representativeness. This cross-sectional survey was conducted in Guizhou Province, limiting the representativeness of the study population and potentially affecting the generalisability of the conclusions. Furthermore, competency assessment relied on self-assessment, there is a high possibility of evaluation bias. limitations of this study included inadequate analysis of impact on IPCPs’ clinical behaviour and patient safety. It has been proven that entrustable professional activities (EPAs) can serve as an effective framework for assessment that has been widely used in CBME [54]. The focus of the assessment based on EPAs will be on the observation of specific key clinical activities, and multiple abilities will be inferred from several observed activities [11]. The field of IPC has not yet established EPAs. Therefore, future research need to develop the EPAs in IPC and prioritise long-term, large-scale multicentre monitoring of clinical practice and its impact on patient outcomes.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study pioneered research in the field of competency based IPC education in China, demonstrating that the competency framework serves as a key tool for assessing the feasibility and effectiveness of CME. The dimensions of domestic assessment tool encompass both explicit competencies and implicit competencies, emphasising the critical role of the latter compared to existing competency assessment models. Moreover, we have identified the advantages and disadvantages of various CME modes in China and the effectiveness of competency-growth, offering a foundation for CME reform and CBME construction that integrates China’s IPC competency framework, modular courses, and competency assessment.

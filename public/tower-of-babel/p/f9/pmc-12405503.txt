Stability analysis and optimization of open pit mine slopes based on the improved Sarma method with nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions
Authors: Gaotong Ma, Zhonghao Liang, Shengqing Ou, Zhigang Tao, Bo Huang, Hui Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Although the traditional Sarma method considers the hydrostatic pressure effect, in the actual engineering, the hydraulic conditions are complex and most of them are nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions, this paper considers the nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions based on the traditional Sarma method, and based on the improved Sarma method model, the stability evaluation of the designed slope and the slope angle is carried out with the slope of the Aynak open-pit copper mine as the engineering background—optimization study. Firstly, the numerical simulation method carried out the stability analysis of the designed excavation slopes. Then the stability of the final boundary slopes was evaluated based on the improved Sarma method model. The optimization of the final boundary slope angle was carried out by combining the traditional classical limit equilibrium theory and the improved Sarma method model under the conclusion that the originally designed slopes were more stable. The optimal slope angle suitable for the actual working conditions was obtained.

Introduction

Slope stability study is a crucial technical problem in open pit mine engineering1,2. In the production process of open pit mines, a large amount of rock and soil excavation leads to the formation of open pit slopes, the stability of which is directly related to the safe operation and economic benefits of the mine. Slope destabilization will not only cause the loss of mining equipment and personnel but also lead to serious environmental disasters. Therefore, scientific research on slope stability is of great practical significance. On the other hand, the size of the slope angle directly affects the mining range, stripping volume, and final economic benefits of the mine3,4. Under the premise of ensuring slope stability, optimizing the slope angle can maximize the utilization rate of mining resources, reduce the amount of unnecessary stripping works, and lower the production cost.

Limit equilibrium method is the most important and commonly used method for slope stability analysis, which first assumes the possible slip surface and the slope is in limit equilibrium, then divides the slope into rigid blocks with vertical boundaries, and then calculates the factor of safety of the whole slope by analyzing the static equilibrium conditions of each block. It was first proposed by the Swedish scholar Peterson in 1915, and later many scholars, such as Fellenius5, Bishop6, Janbu7, Morgenstern and Price8,9, and Spencer10, were devoted to the improvement of the slice method. In addition, with the improvement of computational power, many new methods and advances in slope stability analysis have emerged. For example, numerical simulation techniques such as the finite element strength reduction method have been widely used for slope stability assessment11,12, and compared with the traditional rigid-body limit equilibrium method, the finite element method is able to consider the nonlinear stress-strain characteristics of the soil body and the initial stresses and other factors, and the simulation of complex conditions is more refined13. Studies have shown that the slope safety coefficients obtained by the finite element method are often highly consistent with or even more reliable than the results of the limit equilibrium method14. Sarma’s method is a limit equilibrium analysis method applicable to slip surfaces of arbitrary shape, which accounts for critical seismic acceleration15. Research on the Sarma method continues to deepen in recent years. Zhou et al.16 proposed an improved Sarma method applicable to layered soils to better consider the influence of soil structure on slope stability. Lin Zhiping et al.17 analyzed the impact of inter-slice force distribution on the safety factor calculated by the Sarma method, further refining its mechanical assumptions. Additionally, Shou et al.18 developed a three-dimensional Sarma method, using longitudinal vertical slices for 3D stability analysis of rock slopes, and verified its feasibility through case studies. However, the Sarma method’s simplification of assuming identical hydraulic boundary conditions for the first and last slices contradicts reality. The hydraulic boundary conditions of most engineering slopes are highly complex and often nonhomogeneous. To address this issue, He Manchao et al.19 proposed an improved Sarma method based on nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions induced by water level fluctuations, referred to as the MSARMA method. Subsequently, numerous scholars have applied and validated the reliability and effectiveness of this method in engineering practice20–23.

In this study, based on the improved Sarma method model with the considered nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions, we combine several methods to calculate and evaluate the stability of the slope of the Aynak copper mine and propose the optimization method of the slope angle of this slope. Firstly, based on the comprehensive analysis of the geomechanical information of Aynak copper mine, the stability of the designed excavation slope is studied by numerical simulation method, and the stability evaluation of the originally designed slope is carried out based on the improved Sarma method model, on this basis, the slope angle optimization is further carried out by using the classical limit equilibrium theory and the optimized slope angle is verified and analyzed by using the improved Sarma method model. The research results can not only guide the design and production of Aynak copper mine but also provide better examples and research ideas for similar high and steep slope stability analysis research.

Improved Sarma method with nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions

Sarma method mechanical modelling

Sarma method is a limit equilibrium method for analysing the stability of slopes and was introduced by Sarma in 197315.The basic idea is that the rock and soil mass of a slope has to be broken into a number of blocks sliding relative to each other, i.e., shear occurs inside the sliding mass, before it can slide, which requires that the analysis satisfies both force and moment equilibria. The mechanical model of the Sarma method divides the soil above the potential sliding surface through the cutting surface into a number of strips, each of which must satisfy the static and moment equilibrium conditions. The method derives an expression for the critical acceleration coefficient by establishing the static equilibrium equations for the strips, incorporating the interaction forces between the strips and the shear strength on the sliding surface. In turn, the factor of safety of the slope is calculated based on the given seismic load or acceleration. The Sarma method is characterised by its ability to deal with sliding surfaces of arbitrary shapes, including non-circular shapes, and by its ability to analyse the effect of seismic action on slope stability.

Engineering situations

Location and physical geography

Aynak copper mine is located about 49 km southeast of Kabul, the capital city of Afghanistan (Fig. 3), under the jurisdiction of Logar Province. The central geographic coordinates of the mine area: 34º16′ N latitude, 69º18′ E longitude, located in the east-central part of the Afghanistan Plateau, on the north-west side of the Aynak mountain range, adjacent to the Logar River floodplain, belonging to the plateau hilly landscape, with high terrain and large undulations, with an elevation of the mine area ranging from 2,275 to 2,750 m above sea level.

Mining geology

Stratigraphic lithology

The stratigraphy of the Aynak copper mine area ranges from old to new, including the Proterozoic bedrock, Tertiary, and Quaternary. The Quaternary and Upper Tertiary are unconformably contacted overlying Precambrian old strata. The Quaternary are mainly residual and alluvial layers. Due to its late formation, the Upper Tertiary consists of loose, semi-consolidated, and consolidated siltstones, fine sandstones, coarse sandstones, and gravel-sand layer. In contrast, the Precambrian strata consist of metamorphic rock series of the Middle-Lower Proterozoic and Upper Proterozoic, where the Middle-Lower Proterozoic contain the Sheldawaz Formation (Pt1–2sh) and the Kroger Formation (Pt1–2kr), which have not been found distributed on the surface within the mining area, the Upper Paleoproterozoic is composed of the Vileya Formation (Pt3vl), the Loykhvar Formation (Pt3lh), and the Gurihamid Formation (Pt3gl), with the Loykhvar Formation (Pt3lh) being the ore-forming horizon of the Aynak copper mine.

Geological structure

The Aynak copper mine consists of the lower, middle, and upper structural layers from the bottom up. The lower structural layer is a metasedimentary “geosyncline tectonic unit” composed of amphibolite and crystalline schist of the Kroger Formation and Sheldawaz Formation of the Middle and Lower Proterozoic, and amphibolite and crystalline schist of the Vilayaki Formation of the Upper Proterozoic, which contains marble and quartzite. The lower structural layer is a north-east oriented short-axis anticlinal fold in the mining area, which is in contact with the middle structural layer as an unconformity or fault. The total thickness of the lower structural layer is about 7,000 m. The middle structural layer is the Upper Proterozoic Loykhvar Formation land-sourced offshore carbonate layer, covered by the Guri Hamid Volcano mafic rock body, the “platform tectonic unit”. The upper structural layer is the upper Tertiary sedimentary layer of the Cenozoic era, which is in the form of a nearly horizontal thick layer directly overlying the middle and lower structural layers. In the eastern part of the western mining area and the eastern part of the central mining area, the upper Tertiary overburden is thicker, with a thickness of more than 4,000 m, and is in unconformable contact with the middle structural layer.

Numerical simulation analysis of design slope excavation stability

Numerical simulation analysis was carried out using Flac3D software for the typical profile of designed excavation slope according to different stages. The selected profile is located in the southeast part of the quarry, with a slope inclination of 116°. Since the upper part of the slope is Tertiary soft rock and the lower part is Gurihamid Formation bedrock, the design scheme takes into account the differences in the physical and mechanical properties of these two types of rock bodies and selects different slope angles for the upper Tertiary soft rock and the lower Gurihamid Formation bedrock. In the original design scheme, the slope angle of the upper Tertiary soft rock is 36°, and the slope angle of the lower Gurihamid Formation bedrock is 43°, so this study optimizes the upper Tertiary soft rock and the lower bedrock respectively. The Tertiary cover is thick, ranging from 200 m to 300 m in thickness, and the main lithologies are siltstone, sandy-mudstone, Medium-Fine sandstone, marl, gravel-sand layer, and conglomerate, and the Tertiary rock layers are distributed approximately horizontally. The representative lithologies of hard rock are amphibolite and carbonaceous quartz schist, and the hard rock formations are distributed in an anticlinal manner with open slopes. Geological tectonics in the region is strong, there are two large fault crushing zones through, the fault strike is about NE60 °, the dip is more than 75 °, nearly perpendicular to the ground surface downward extension of five to six hundred meters, and faults on both sides of the extension of the small crushing zones, resulting in the fragmentation of the rock body, joints, and fissures developed. The calculations were performed using an ideal elastic-plastic model, and the Mohr-Coulomb yield criterion was used to determine the damage of the rock mass, with the boundary conditions as follows: the upper surface was set as a free boundary, the bottom was restricted to the horizontal and vertical displacements, and the two side faces were restricted to the horizontal displacements. Conclusion

This study addresses the limitations of the traditional Sarma method in handling nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions. Based on the engineering context of the Aynak open-pit copper mine slope, an improved Sarma method model was established. Integrated with numerical simulation and limit equilibrium theory, the research conducted stability assessment and slope angle optimization. The main conclusions are as follows:

Numerical simulation indicates that excavation unloading causes stress redistribution. Local tensile and shear failures are concentrated at the slope face, slope toe, and lithologic/fault interfaces. While the overall slope stability is good, localized shear-slip risks exist, requiring targeted monitoring.Using an independently developed analytical system incorporating the improved Sarma method that accounts for nonhomogeneous hydraulic boundary conditions, slip surface models of typical cross-sections were constructed to evaluate the stability of the originally designed slope. The results show that under combined undrained and seismic loading conditions, the safety factors for all potential critical slip surfaces exceed 1.2, indicating room for optimization. Sensitivity analysis further revealed that slope unit weight, seismic coefficient, and shear parameters along slip surfaces have the most significant influence on the safety factor.Ensuring the safety factor remained not lower than the code-specified threshold, slope angle optimization for the overlying soft rock layer and the underlying bedrock layer was performed using both classical limit equilibrium methods and the improved Sarma model. It was determined that stability requirements are met under varying drainage conditions when the optimized slope angles are ≤ 38° for the upper Tertiary soft rock slope and ≤ 44° for the lower Gulihamid Formation bedrock slope. The results showed high consistency with calculations from multiple limit equilibrium methods, further verifying their reliability.

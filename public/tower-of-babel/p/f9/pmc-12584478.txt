New Na+,Mg2+,Zn2+ -containing hydroxyapatite/ZrO2 composites: preparation, mechanic properties and cytotoxicity
Authors: N Strutynska, L Dremova, Ye Komashchenko, I Grynyuk, D Labudzynskyi, O Livitska, A Kuryliuk
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFirstly apatite-type carbonated calcium phosphate and biphasic calcium phosphates (mixture of phases based on Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2 and β-Ca3(PO4)2 with weight ratio 60 : 40%) which contained the trace elements complex (Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+) were obtained from aqueous solutions and heated to 600 °C. Composites based on apatite-related calcium phosphates with 5, 10, 25wt% of ZrO2 were also prepared used one-stage method. In vitro testing of new prepared samples in phosphate-buffered solution (at 37 °C and pH = 7.45) showed increasing of pH value to 10 during 96 h in the presence of apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32- (3wt%)-containing calcium phosphate. At the same time, increasing of pH only to 8.78 during 48 h was found for the biphasic calcium phosphate with the similar amount of doping cations. This result indicates that carbonated modified hydroxyapatites can be more appropriate for bone tissue engineering than biphasic calcium phosphates with similar amount of trace elements. An increase in the ZrO2 amount from 10 to 25 wt% in the composite with apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32- (3wt%)-containing calcium phosphate led to an increase of its hardness in 1.4 times. The results of in vitro investigation using both the MTT and resazurin assays demonstrate that the prepared apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32-(3wt%)-containing calcium phosphate and its composite with 25 wt% ZrO2 have a dose-dependent effect on the metabolic and proliferation activity (viability) of the mouse monocyte-macrophage RAW264.7 and J774A.1 cell lines. The stimulation of RAW264.7 macrophages growth within 100–155% compared with the control was found at samples concentration 0.1 and 0.01 mg/ml in the cell cultivation medium. Obtained results are important at creation of bioactive materials with special microhardness for the medical application.Keywords: Apatite, Trace elements, Zirconia, Composites, MicroVickers hardness, Cell viability

Introduction

Calcium phosphates occupy a leading position among complex oxide compounds due to a wide application in many branches of industry (catalysts of organic reactions [1, 2] hosts for luminescent materials [3, 4]), ecology (effective adsorbents for heavy metals removal [5, 6]) and medicine (artificial bone grafts and dental implants [7–10], drug delivery systems [11, 12], inorganic components of toothpastes [13]). Its significance derives from a huge variety of existing compositions, structural types, physico-chemical and biological properties. Because of the flexibility of their crystal structure different types of substitutions in cationic and anionic sublattices occur. This gives possibilities to affect the properties of future materials such as solubility, biocompatibility, bioactivity, osteoconductivity, thermal and mechanical behavior etc. A diverse raw of synthetic methods (sol-gel, hydrothermal, precipitation, solid state reactions, biomimetic approaches) is used for synthesis of calcium phosphates in the desired forms [14–16].

Hydroxyapatite (Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2) possesses chemical and structural similarity to the mineral part of bones and tooth tissues, biocompatibility and non-toxicity. Both it and its substituted derivatives are used as a coating for orthopedic and dental implants, a component of calcium phosphate cements and toothpastes, drug delivery systems etc [17–21]. At the same time, modification of phosphate structure by diffident ions and combination with other inorganic components or polymers is the best way to improve functional properties of materials.

Biological apatite (the mineral component of bone) is calcium deficient, nonstoichiometric and nanosized, contains either cationic substitutions (Mg2+, Na+, Zn2+, Sr2+, Al3+, K+ and Ag+) or anionic substitutions (CO32−, SiO44−, F− and Cl−) [22]. Because of the natural occurrence of microelements and important biological functions, the ionic incorporation in hydroxyapatite is an object of significant research. Among these elements Na+ cations promote cell adhesion. Zinc cations as the most abundant trace element in the bone (its quantity 0.012–0.025 wt%) are present in all tissues and performs diverse roles, such as hormonal and enzyme activity, nucleic acid metabolism, maintenance of membrane structure and function, biomineralization and potentially pathological calcification. Also Zn-containing calcium phosphates demonstrate effective antibacterial properties against various Gram-positive and Gram-negative strains [23–25].

Mg2+ ions play a key role in the activity of osteoblasts and stimulate bone growth. They release triggers osteogenesis and angiogenesis and enables effective regeneration of bone tissue. These cations stimulate rapid bone formation [26, 27]. A number of in vitro investigations showed the improve of cell proliferation and bioactivity with increasing amount of Mg2+ as compared to pure hydroxyapatite. Zn2+ and Mg2+ incorporation in the apatite structure was intensively explored in many studies [28–32].

Despite the numerous advantages of hydroxyapatite use in biomaterials area, its low mechanical strength remains the main disadvantage and makes apatite inadequate for load-bearing applications. This problem can be solved by mixing of tetragonal zirconia and hydroxyapatite which unites the phosphates bioactivity and strength of ZrO2 phase [33–35].

ZrO2 is a bioinert material, and the majority of its applications are in the area where high wear is encountered, such as the knee joint and the hip joint. The strength and fracture toughness of ZrO2 are much higher than calcium phosphate ceramics, which can be exploited to significantly improve the material properties of fabricated bio-composites. However, the addition of this compound will also decrease the biocompatibility of the fabricated materials since ZrO2 is not biodegradable and complete ossification of the implant material would not be possible [36]. In the last years, many ZrO2-apatite composites have been developed as coating or substrate in order to achieve both bone reconstruction and regeneration needed in the treatment of large bone defects.

In the work [37] ZrO2 and apatite-based composites, also two metallic dental alloys (Ni–Cr and Co–Cr) coated with a chitosan membrane containing the prepared composites were characterized. The obtained results showed anticorrosive properties for metallic dental alloys.

ZrO2 doped calcium phosphate bioactive glasses with a high stability were successfully synthesized through the melt-quenching technique in the work [38]. The obtained results revealed that the glass-forming ability and thermal stability of glasses increased with the increase in zirconia mol%. In vitro studies revealed that the ZrO2 incorporated phosphate glasses exhibit high bioactivity relevant for bone tissue engineering applications [38].

In paper [39] authors prepared a porous ZrO2-based bone scaffold coated with hydroxyapatite and bioactive glass composite and optimized the quality hybrid proportion of apatite and glass composite coating and sintering temperature. The scaffold had a dense composite coating at the coating sintering temperature of 950◦C and hydroxyapatite content of 70% with Ca2SiO4 and β-TCP, hydroxyapatite and Ca10(PO4)6CO3 phases. A compression strength of composite was superior compared to a value of the porous ZrO2 scaffold. The results exhibited the excellent mechanical properties, biological activities and good cytocompatibility of the scaffold [39].

Y. Cao et al. [40] created the porous scaffolds of ZrO2/hydroxyapatite composite by digital light processing technology. Compared with the ZrO2 scaffold, composite scaffolds with 20 wt% or 30 wt% apatite had better biological activity and can promote proliferation and differentiation of MC3T3-E1 cells. Mechanical tests showed that the composite scaffold with 10 wt% apatite had the best compressive capacity due to the pinning and bridging effect of a small amount of apatite grains. When scaffolds were immersed in the simulated body fluid, the compressive strengths of the composite scaffolds decreased within the first 14 days and gradually increased after 14 days. The reason for this phenomenon was the degradation of calcium phosphate components and the deposition of apatite [40].

All mentioned papers indicates the possibility to improve the mechanical properties of biomaterials via preparation of composites with the inert phase ZrO2.

The trace elements play an important role in bone tissue engineering process, but the information about simultaneously modification of phosphate with complex cations is absent. The main purpose of present work was preparation of calcium phosphates modified with trace elements (Na+ + Mg2+ + Zn2+) and carbonate-anion in amount appropriate to medical application and investigation of influence of doping ions on functional properties of synthetic materials. Further using of such calcium phosphates modified with trace elements with composition close to natural human bone allows to include a function of each dopants in bone restoration that will be important for orthopedic application. Therefore, present study was conducted with the aim of understanding the influence of simultaneous complex-doping (Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+) of calcium phosphate on its partial dissolution in an model solution and cytotoxicity.

The goal of this work was also a development of the one-stage synthetic technique for preparation of composites based on modified by Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+ cations calcium phosphates with different amount of ZrO2 and their characterization as well as investigation cytotoxicity and microVickers hardness.

Materials and methods

Samples synthesis

Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+,(CO32−)-containing calcium phosphates and composites based on them with different amount (5, 10 or 25wt%) of ZrO2 have been prepared from aqueous solutions with followed by their heating to 600 °C for 2 h. The substances Ca(NO3)2·4H2O, Mg(NO3)2·6H2O, Zn(NO3)2·6H2O, NaNO3, ZrO(NO3)2·H2O, NH4HCO3, (NH4)2HPO4 (an analytical grade, Sigma Aldrich) and NH3*H2O solution (25%) were used as initial materials. The aqueous solutions (1 M) containing calculated amounts of Ca2+, Mg2+, Zn2+, Na+ and ZrO(NO3)2·H2O (in the case of composites preparation) were mixed with solutions containing phosphate or mixture of phosphate/carbonate anions. Then the 3 ml of ammonia solution was added for achieved of pH to 10. The water was evaporated and powder after grounding was heated to 600 °C for 2 h. All samples were denoted according to the molar ratio of the components in the initial solution and amount of ZrO2 in composites based on modified calcium phosphate (Table 1).

Methods of characterization

Phase analysis and crystallite sizes of the prepared modified calcium phosphates were established from X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis using a diffractometer Shimadzu XRD-6000 (Shimadzu Corp., Japan). The patterns were collected from 5° to 60° range with the size of 0.02° and Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54178 Å). Stoichiometric hydroxyapatite with standard cards in JCPDS database #00-009-0432 was used as the reference pattern for confirmation of phase composition of prepared samples.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, PerkinElmer Spectrum BX spectrometer) was used to confirm the mode of carbonate and phosphate anions in prepared samples composition. The wavenumber range was set to 400–4000 cm− 1.

The change of pH value of phosphate-buffered solution (PBS) at temperature 37 °C (pH = 7.45) in the presence of modified calcium phosphates and composites based on them with different amount of ZrO2 (5, 10 or 25wt%) was determined. This study allows us to evaluate to stability of obtained materials to partial dissolution and perspective of their application for bone restoration. Significant growing of pH value is caused by partial sample dissolution and indicate about perspective its application in bone tissue engineering.

The synthesized sample (0.2 g) was immersed in 15 ml of PBS at 37 °С for 4 days. A рН-meter OHAUS Started 2100 (China) was used for measurement of pH values changing at presence of samples after 24, 48, 72 and 96 h.

The mechanical characterization of the prepared samples has been evaluated by microhardness testing at room temperature. An LHVS-1000Z microhardness (China) tester equipped with a standard Vickers diamond pyramid indenter was used. The Vickers microhardness (HV) measurements were performed using an applied load of 0.01 N and holding times of 10 s. After unloading the indenter, a trace with a pyramidal contour remained on the sample’s surface, the diagonal of which is measured, and the HV hardness is determined with the device software. Cell viability assay

The in vitro cytotoxicity effect of apatite-related calcium phosphate that contains Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32− (3wt%) (Sample S2) as well as corresponding phosphate with 25wt% ZrO2 (Sample S2-25) were evaluated using RAW264.7 and J774A.1 cells by both MTT- and Resazurin-based assays. The murine macrophage cell line RAW264.7 was obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, ATCC® TIB-71™) and the J774A.1 murine macrophage cell line was purchased from the Bank of Cell Lines from Human and Animal Tissues, RE Kavetsky Institute of Experimental Pathology, Oncology and Radiobiology, National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine (origin - ATCC® TIB-67™).

MTT-based assay

RAW264.7 cells were placed into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well and in 100 µL in complete Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium DMEM. After incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 24 h, the medium was removed and investigated samples (0.01–5 mg/ml) were added to each of the wells. After 48 h incubation the medium was removed and 10 µL MTT solution at a concentration of 5 mg/mL was added per well, and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C in 5% CO2 (no air bubbles in the wells), to reduce MTT to formazan. After removing the medium, 150 µL of DMSO was added to each well to dissolve formazan. The plates were then shaken at 25 °C for 10 min to dissolve formazan completely. Finally, the absorbance was measured at 560 nm using a microplate reader. Three independent experiments were performed with at least three parallel wells for each experiment. Cells without sample treatment were set as blank controls [41].

Resazurin-based assay

A resazurin assay was conducted to determine whether the obtained samples had cytotoxic or stimulatory effects on mice monocyte-macrophage RAW264.7 and J774A.1 cells. Resazurin is a blue dye that is transformed to resorufin, a pink dye, by metabolically active cells. This colourimetric change can be used as a measure of cell viability. RAW264.7 murinemacrophages were seeded into a sterile 96-well plate in complete DMEM at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well and incubated for 24 h to allow the cells to attach to the plate. Medium was replaced and investigated samples (0.01–5 mg/ml) were added to the wells. After 48 h incubation, 10% resazurin solution was added to each well. The plates were then left to incubate at 37 °C for 4 h and absorbance was measured using an Epoch Micro-plate spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments Inc., Winooksi, VT, USA) at a wavelength of 570 nm and using a wavelength of 600 nm as a reference. Cells without a sample treatment were set as blank controls [42].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of data was performed using the program Origin 7.5. The significance of differences between comparison groups was assessed by one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA) followed by Tukey (post-hoc test). The results are presented as the mean (M) and the standard deviation (SD). The difference was considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. Three independent experiments were performed with at least three parallel wells for each experiment.

The calcium phosphates formation at fixed molar ratios Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43−= 37.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 24 and Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43− : CO32− = 36.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 22 : 2 were investigated in this way. According to XRD data in the case of system with molar ratio Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43−= 37.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 24 after heat-treatment at 600 °C, the biphasic calcium phosphate (mixture of phases based on Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2 and β-Ca3(PO4)2 (40wt%)) was obtained (Fig. 1a) while adding carbonate to initial mixture led to formation of a single phase apatite-related (hexagonal system, space group Р63/m) modified calcium phosphate (Fig. 1b).

These ratios were used for one-stage synthesized of composites with ZrO2 (5, 10 or 25wt%). The different amount of ZrO2 was added to composites with purpose to study of influence of this phase amount on properties of material at the same time insignificant change its bioactivity.

It was found that adding of zirconia to system with ratio Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43−= 37.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 24 changed the particularity of interaction in the system with formation of the single phase apatite-related calcium phosphate (Fig. 1c, e, f). The presence of ZrO2 in composites is confirmed by the wide halo in the range of 2θ = 28–36° and growth of its intensity at increasing of ZrO2 amount from 5 to 25wt% in obtained samples (Fig. 1c-h). The sample based on Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+-containing calcium phosphate with 25wt% ZrO2 was annealed at a temperature of 800 °C for the purpose to investigate the type of formed ZrO2. According to phase analysis, ZrO2 belonged to tetragonal system, space group P42/nmc (JCPDS #00-088-1007) [43] (Fig. 2). The partial decomposition of formed calcium phosphate with formation of the phase based on β-Ca3(PO4)2 also took place at heating to 800 °C.

XRD pattern of composite of calcium phosphate obtained at molar ratio of Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43−= 37.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 24 with 25wt% of ZrO2 heated to 800°С. Vertical lines 1, 2, 3 show β-Ca3(PO4)2 (#00-009-0169), apatite-type (#00-009-0432) and ZrO2 (#01-088-1007) phases, respectively

It was found that the crystallites size calculated using of Debye-Scherrer equation for carbonated hydroxyapatites decrease from 37 nm to 33 nm at adding of ZrO2, but doesn’t dependent on the amount (5 or 25wt%). At the same time for samples obtained without carbonate-anion in the initial mixture the particle size increase from 27 nm to 33 nm at growing of ZrO2 from 5wt% to 25wt% in composition of obtained samples.

The bands at around 1092 cm− 1 and 1040 cm− 1 are associated with asymmetric stretching vibrations (ν3(PO43−)) while the band at 963 cm− 1 arises from corresponding symmetric vibration ν1(PO43−). The bands at 603 cm− 1 and 572 cm− 1 appear from ν4(PO43−). The bands at 3572 cm− 1 and 638 cm− 1 were assigned to vibrations of OH− group in apatite structure. The formation of B-type of carbonated hydroxyapatite (with partial substitution of РО43−→CО32−) in the case of samples obtained at molar ratio Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43− : CO32− = 36.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 22 : 2 is confirmed by the presence of typical CO32− bands at 850–875 cm− 1, 1410–1440 and 1450–1490 cm− 1 [44–46]. The modes in the frequency range of 3210–3600 cm− 1 and at 1660 cm− 1 are attributed to the vibrations of H2O molecules.

Peculiarities of changing of pH value of the phosphate buffered solution (PBS) at temperature 37 °C (at pH = 7.45) in the presence of calcium phosphates prepared at molar ratios of Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43-= 37.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 24 and Ca2+ : Na+ : Mg2+ : Zn2+ : PO43- : CO32- = 36.5 : 1 : 1 : 1 : 22 : 2 as well as composites based on them with 5, 10 or 25 wt% of ZrO2 are presented on Fig. 4. Analysis of the obtained data showed the increasing of pH value for all determined samples that is caused by their partial dissolution. Such analysis allows to evaluate the perspective using of prepared materials for a bone restoration. It was found that the phase composition of the sample defined its activity in the phosphate-buffered solution at temperature 37 °C and changing of initial value of pH = 7.45 (Fig. 4). In case of carbonated apatite-related calcium phosphate that contains of Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32- (3wt%) the growing of pH value is more than 35% while for biphasic calcium phosphate with the similar amount of doping cation (ratio of phases based on Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2 and β-Ca3(PO4)2 is 60 : 40wt%) is less than 18% during first 48 h (Fig. 4a, b, Sample 1). The continuously growing of pH in the presence of carbonated calcium phosphate was found during 96 h (Fig. 4) while in the case of biphasic calcium phosphate it was only first 48 h, after that pH value decreased (Fig. 4a, Sample 1). In case of composites (Sample 2-Sample 4) the increasing of amount of inert component (ZrO2) correlates with no so significant growing of pH. It should be noted that adding more than of 10wt% ZrO2 to apatite-related phosphate that contained of Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.6wt%) caused growing of pH during 96 h (Fig. 4a, Sample 3 and Sample 4). In the case of composites based on carbonated apatite-related calcium phosphate with ZrO2 the general activity decreased that correlated with decreasing of active component in composites but the continued growing of pH during 96 h took place (Fig. 4b). The similar behavior for apatite/ZrO2 composite was described in the work [39]. These results indicate the perspective of using of carbonated calcium phosphate with complex of trace elements for bone restoration.

Mechanical properties

The main purpose of preparation of composite based on modified apatite-related calcium phosphate with ZrO2 is to increase the mechanic characteristics of materials for orthopedic application. The results of microVickers hardness tests for composites based on apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%), CO32− (3wt%) -containing calcium phosphate with 10 or 25 wt% ZrO2 showed that increasing the amount of ZrO2 in composites led to growing of microhardness values from 52.8±5.2 Hv to 71.7±10.5 Hv, respectively. This fact is in agreement with the literature data [38, 40].

Cell viability

The obtained results for three sections of the content of synthesized samples S2 and S2-25 (0.05, 0.5 and 10 mg/ml) in the studied systems, which are within the areas of different effects of samples, are shown in Fig. 5.

Viability of RAW264.7 (a, c) and J774A.1 (b, d) cells in the presence of synthesized samples S2 and S2-25 at concentration 0.01 (4), 0.1 (3), 1.0 (2) and 5.0 mg/ml (1) in MTT and Resazurin assays. *P˂0.05 compared to control; #P < 0.05 vs. sample S2 at same concentration

The results of in vitro studies demonstrate that the tested newly synthesized modified apatite-related calcium phosphate (Sample S2) and its composite with 25wt% ZrO2 (Sample S2-25) have a dose-dependent effect on the metabolic and proliferation activity (viability) of the mouse monocyte-macrophage RAW264.7 and J774A.1 cell lines (Fig. 5). Incubation of the samples at the highest concentration (5 mg/ml of the cultivation medium) inhibited the viability of both lines with values below 20% compared to the control by both the MTT and resazurin assays (Fig. 5). This can be explained by the breach in the exchange of gases and nutrients between the medium and the cell membrane due to the accumulation of a monolayer of the solid samples on the growth surface of the cultivation container. A significant increase of the cell viability in the range from 20 to 70% of cells compared with the control after reduction the concentration of composites to 1 mg/ml was observed (Fig. 5). Reducing the concentrations of explored composites to 0.1 and 0.01 mg/ml in the cell cultivation medium led not only to the complete restoration of RAW264.7 macrophages viability, but also stimulated its growth within 100–155% compared with control both by the MTT and resazurin assays (Fig. 5a, c). Further, the influence of a similar concentration of the studied samples on the population of J774A.1 macrophages induced increase in cell viability compared with higher concentrations of compounds in the range from 34 to 98% was also observed, but the stimulation of their proliferation, as in the case of RAW264.7 cells was not detected (Fig. 5). This may be due to the specifics of the metabolism between RAW264.7 and J774A.1 macrophage lines, the peculiarities of their intercellular communication and adhesive properties. Despite the strong self-replicating and long-time cultivation of J774A.1 macrophage culture, various researchers point to a number of functional shortcomings, including genotypic/phenotypic drift, loss of tissue-specific function and weak antimicrobial properties compared to the RAW264.7 cell line [47]. The semi-adhesive type of growth of J774A.1 cells also plays an important role in testing of biomedical materials, including nano- and supramolecular composites, as noted in studies [48]. Guided by the cell viability values of both lines due to incubation with the highest concentrations of the explored composites (5 and 1 mg/ml), it can be concluded that composite S2-25 has a less toxic effect at higher concentrations than corresponding phosphate S2 that correlates with increasing of nonbioactive ZrO2 phase in its composition (Fig. 5). Moreover, concentrations of 0.1 and 0.01 mg/ml of both samples exhibit a stimulatory effect on RAW264.7 cells, while for the population of J774A.1 cells was significantly reduced the toxic effect of studied samples (Fig. 5). It should be noted a difference in the absolute values of viability of model cell lines by two selected protocols - MTT and resazurin methods. The reason behind this that the MTT approach is implemented through the functioning of dehydrogenase-type enzymes of exclusively mitochondrial localization, while the use of resazurin makes it possible to assess the metabolic enzymatic pool of the whole cell [49].

Conclusions

The influence of the simultaneous complex doping of calcium phosphate with Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+ and CO32− ions on phase formation of calcium phosphates was investigated and found that in the system without adding of carbonate the biphasic calcium phosphates is formed. At the same time, adding zirconium in such initial mixture led to formation of composites based on complex modified apatite-related calcium phosphate with the different amount (5-25wt%) of ZrO2. In case of carbonated hydroxyapatites the B-type substitution (PO43−→CO32−) was confirmed. The microVickers hardness tests showed that for composites based on apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%) and CO32− (3wt%) containing calcium phosphate with ZrO2 growing of ZrO2 amount from 10 to 25 wt% led to an increase of hardness by a factor of 1.4. It was found the influence of phase and elemental composition of prepared Na+, Mg2+, Zn2+-contained calcium phosphates on pH value of phosphate-buffered solution (pH 7.45 and at temperature 37 °C). Biphasic calcium phosphate is less active during the first 48 h, while carbonated apatite-type calcium phosphate changed pH by more than 35% during 96 h. This result indicates that carbonated modified hydroxyapatite can be more appropriate for bone tissue engineering than biphasic calcium phosphate with similar amount of trace elements. It was found that prepared apatite-related Na+ (0.6wt%), Mg2+ (0.6wt%), Zn2+ (1.7wt%) and CO32− (3wt%)-containing calcium phosphate and its composite with 25 wt% ZrO2 exhibit a stimulatory effect on RAW264.7 cells at concentrations of 0.1 and 0.01 mg/ml. In the future the osteogenic potential and biocompatibility of prepared composites in animal model will be evaluated.

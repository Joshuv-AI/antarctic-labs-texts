“Ventilator-induced diaphragmatic dysfunction: pathophysiology, monitoring and advances in potential treatment and prevention.” W. Fu, L. Guan, Q. Liu et al. Eur Respir Rev 2025; 34: 250069.
Published: 2026

Following comments from a reader, the authors of this article from the October 2025 issue of the European Respiratory Review would like to make some text changes in order to clarify the section titled “Optimising the respiratory treatment parameters; Target 1: maintain appropriate respiratory drive”, and to amend figure 3. These changes were made in order to ensure consistency with the remainder of the review and to avoid any confusion regarding clinical interpretation, while also maintaining consistency with the concept of lung- and diaphragm-protective ventilation proposed by E.C. Goligher and co-workers [1, 2].

The corrected paragraph is as follows:

Respiratory demands vary among patients. Monitoring respiratory effort helps determine whether the patient's respiratory demand matches the level of ventilator support. For patients with high respiratory drive, efforts to reduce respiratory drive are suggested. In cases of insufficient ventilatory assistance with driving pressure remaining within lung-protective limits, ventilatory support should be increased. In cases where ventilatory support approaches or exceeds lung-protective limits, deeper sedation and/or neuromuscular blockade should be considered. For patients with low respiratory drive, efforts to increase respiratory drive are suggested. In cases of excessive ventilatory support, a reduction of ventilatory support is suggested. In cases of insufficient ventilatory support, a reduction of sedation and/or neuromuscular blockade should be considered and ventilatory support level should be titrated to maintain an appropriate level of ventilation. Current evidence suggests that maintaining inspiratory effort levels consistent with quiet resting breathing may prevent diaphragm atrophy and load-induced injury [110], while thresholds for excessive or insufficient respiratory effort are summarised in table 4.

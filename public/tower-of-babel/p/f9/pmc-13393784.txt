Construction of pre-vascularized bone-like tissue by incorporation of mesodermal progenitor cells through simulating endochondral ossification
Authors: Yian Guan, Zixin Wang, Hui Wang, Rong Huang, Jing Lin, Yuan Wang, Dongyang Ma, Ping Zhou, Liling Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPre-vascularized cartilage enhances bone regeneration (intrachondral osteogenesis) and accelerates bone defect healing. While current strategies focus on co-culturing chondrocytes with endothelial cells, mature endothelial cells inhibit the necessary osteogenic transformation. To overcome this, we implemented a co-differentiation strategy using human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs)-derived mesodermal progenitor cells (iMPCs) instead. We generated pre-vascularized cartilage aggregates by 3D co-culture of these iMPCs with hiPSC-derived chondrocytes (Chos) and evaluated their osteogenic potential. This approach offers new insights and potential strategies for repairing bone defects.MethodsHiPSCs were differentiated into iMPCs (characterized by FLK1(VEGF-R) expression, CD31/vWF immunofluorescence, tube formation, flow cytometry) and Chos (confirmed by SOX9/COL2/ACAN qRT-PCR, Alcian blue staining, VEGF165 ELISA). Pre-vascularized aggregates were generated by 3D co-culture of iMPCs and Chos in ultra-low attachment plates (monocultures as controls). After 14 days, aggregates were assessed in vitro for vascularization (CD31 immunofluorescence), gene/protein expression (qRT-PCR/immunofluorescence for IHH, ALP, COL1A1), and mineralization (Alizarin Red). In vivo osteogenesis was evaluated by implanting aggregates into rat calvarial defects, analyzing healing at 4/8 weeks via Micro-CT, histology (H&E, Masson’s trichrome), and neovascularization (CD31 immunohistochemistry).ResultsIMPCs exhibited high endothelial potential, with a 52.84% induction rate and the ability to form tube-like structures. Co-culture aggregates developed extensive CD31⁺ vascular networks in vitro. Pre-vascularization significantly promoted chondrocyte hypertrophy (increased IHH expression), early osteogenesis (elevated ALP activity and COL1A1 expression), and enhanced in vitro mineralization compared to controls (*p < 0.05). In vivo, the pre-vascularized group demonstrated improved bone defect repair, as shown by increased bone volume in micro-CT analysis, histological evaluation (H&E and Masson’s trichrome), enhanced host-derived vascular integration (CD31⁺ staining), and advanced bone maturation relative to control groups (*p < 0.05).ConclusionOur findings indicate that this vascularization approach utilizing mesodermal cells successfully achieves pre-vascularization of cartilage aggregates in vitro, while also facilitating cartilage hypertrophy and early osteogenic transformation. Background

Bone tissue engineering technology offers an optimal approach for the healing of bone defects. However, inadequate vascularization is the primary obstacle to the advancement of clinically effective therapies for extensive, complex, and intractable bone defects [1].

Conventional bone tissue engineering employs intramembranous ossification (IMO) to differentiate mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts [2], this method has significant potential for bone repair. however, expanding the implant’s size is challenging because of the substantial deposition of bone matrix on the implant surface resulting from in vitro osteogenesis induction, which obstructs nutrient flow [3]. Furthermore, the implanted bone matrix will hinder angiogenesis post-implantation, resulting in inadequate vascular formation, ultimately causing extensive degradation [4].

Endochondral ossification (ECO) represents an alternative model of natural bone development, wherein chondrocytes establish a pre-osteogenic cartilage template [5]. Subsequently, chondrocytes cease proliferation and undergo hypertrophy, secreting related growth factor to initiate angiogenesis and facilitate the osteogenic differentiation of chondrocytes [6], in this process, hypertrophic chondrocytes exhibit partial apoptosis and partial transformation into osteoblasts [7]. In comparison to IMO-based approaches, the ECO strategy for bone tissue construction offers the following advantages: (1) The vascular development of ECO occurs subsequent to the establishment of the cartilage template, allowing chondrocytes formed in an avascular environment to effectively withstand hypoxia [8], whereas osteoblasts exhibit limited hypoxic tolerance [9]; (2) Hypertrophic chondrocytes can stimulate vascularization and cartilage ossification, facilitating a more rapid integration of bone tissue with the vascular system post-implantation [10]; (3) The collagen abundant in the cartilage template promotes the proliferation of endothelial cells, thereby accelerating the natural integration of bone tissue with the vascular system [11].

Because in endochondral ossification, angiogenesis is a prerequisite for cartilage resorption and subsequent bone matrix deposition, implantation after pre-vascularization would help promote chondrocytes to enter the osteogenic stage earlier, improve the osteogenic level and promote bone repair [12]. However, previous studies have confirmed that early contact between mature endothelial cells and chondrocytes would inhibit the osteogenic transformation of cartilage and limit bone repair [13]. Hwanhee et al. suggested minimizing direct contact between hypertrophic chondrocytes and mature endothelial cells during the initial phase of cartilage, they reduced the inhibitory effect by combining mesenchymal and endothelial cells, thereby decreasing the endothelial cell content in the co-culture system, however, this reduction significantly impaired the efficiency of vascular network formation [14]. Lin et al. attempted to encapsulate chondrocytes in a hydrogel pre-mixed with endothelial cells to prevent early contact between chondrocytes and endothelial cells, while their results revealed that the vascular network faced significant challenges in penetrating and spreading throughout the chondrocyte-laden spheres, they speculated that the extracellular matrix became increasingly dense following the chondro-differentiation, thereby restricting endothelial cell migration [15]. Therefore, it remains a pressing issue to investigate an effective pre-vascularization technique to enhance the osteogenic transformation of cartilage.

As mesoderm is the only origin of endothelial progenitor cells during embryogenesis, the coculture of mesodermal cells with tissue cells and the facilitation of directed endothelial differentiation of mesodermal cells constitute an alternative prevascularization approach, identified as the co-differentiation strategy based on mesodermal cells [16]. Currently, the co-differentiation technique utilizing mesodermal cells has effectively achieved pre-vascularization in several tissues, including tumor [17], kidney [18], and nerve tissues [19]. Since the co-differentiation approach more closely mimics the development of nascent vasculature during organogenesis, it is often preferred in the studies of organogenesis and vascularization [20].

In this study, we engineered pre-vascularized bone-like tissue to model the endochondral ossification. These bone-like tissue, which take the form of self-assembled aggregates due to our fabrication method, were designed to support both vascularization and ossification, specifically, we introduce mesodermal cells derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iMPCs) into three-dimensional chondrocytes (Chos) aggregates to facilitate the directional endothelial differentiation of mesodermal cells via the spontaneous secretion of VEGF by chondrocytes, thereby avoiding the inhibitory effects of mature endothelial cells on cartilage osteogenic transformation. We confirmed that the incorporation of iMPCs into cartilage aggregates achieve the pre-vascularization of cartilage, this vascularization method promotes the hypertrophy and early osteogenic transformation of chondrocytes, demonstrating significant osteogenic potential both in vivo and in vitro. Our study focused on vasculogenesis, where progenitor cells create entirely new blood vessel networks—as opposed to angiogenesis (sprouting from pre-existing vessels) or arteriogenesis (arterial maturation and remodeling). This approach leverages the capacity of iMPCs to differentiate into endothelial cells and assemble into primitive vessel-like networks in the absence of pre-existing vasculature.

Materials and methods

Induction of mesoderm and endothelial differentiation

The sendai virus-derived hiPSC lines hNF-C1 (P35) were provided by Guangzhou Institutes of Biomedicine and Health (Guangzhou, China). After being coated with Matrigel at a dilution rate of 1:80, the hiPSCs were cultivated on 6-well cell culture plates. The well-defined E8 medium was employed to sustain pluripotency throughout the experiment, and it was replaced daily. Cells were passaged at a split ratio of 1:4 after growing to about 80% confluence and being exposed to 0.5 mM EDTA for 4–5 min at 37 °C.

As recently reported, the hiPSCs were then induced into iMPCs under conditions of guided differentiation [21]. In short, accutase was used to separate hiPSCs into a single cell solution, then were cultivated for 5 days in mesodermal induction medium (1640 basic medium, 213 µg/mL sodium ascorbate phosphate, 500 µg/mL recombinant albumin, 6 µmol/L CHIR99021, 6 µmol/L SB431542, and 10 ng/mL BMP4) after being seeded on Matrigel-coated plates. The culture medium was changed daily. After 5 d of culturing in the mesodermal induction media, Substitute with endothelial induction medium infused with VEGF (advanced DMEM/F12, 10% FBS, 1% Penicilli/streptomycin, 10 µM Y-27632、0.2 mM L-Glutamine, 6 µg/mL Vitamin C and 50 ng/ml VEGF) to assess the endothelial differentiation potential of mesodermal cells.

Cell labelling

Basic characterization of the iMPCs line has been done by checking the expression of the VEGF receptor marker FLK1(VEGF-R) using immunofluorescence staining. Through CD31 and vWF immunofluorescence staining/flow cytometry, and Matrigel-based tube formation assay, the endothelial differentiation potential of iMPCs after the addition of VEGF was assessed.

Chondrogenic differentiation

For chondrogenic induction, the hiPSCs cultured as above are placed in the mesenchymal induction medium (αMEM basal medium containing 15%FBS, 1% penicillin/streptomycin solution, 1%non-essential amino acids, 1%L-glutaMax) for mesenchymal induction differentiation. The medium is changed every 2 days, and when the cell density reaches 80%, the cells are passaged. This process is repeated three times to obtain induced mesenchymal stem cells (iMSCs). Afterwards, the cells were placed in a cartilage induction medium (high-glucose DMEM, 10% FBS, 1% penicillin/streptomycin solution, 50 µg/mL ascorbic acid, 50 µg/mL proline, 100 µg/mL sodium pyruvate, 1% ITS+, 10 ng/mL BMP6, 10 ng/mL TGF-β3) and cultured for 28 days to obtain hiPSCs-derived chondrocytes (Chos), the culture medium was changed daily.

Extracellular matrix analysis and gene expression analysis

Total RNA was obtained by homogenizing samples in Triton Lysis Reagent, and quantified spectroscopically using a Nanodrop 2000c Spectrophotometer. SuperScript™ IV VILO™ Master Mix was used for reverse transcription, and SYBR Green Reaction Mix was used for qRT-PCR on an Applied Biosystems real-time PCR system. SRY-Box Transcription Factor 9 (SOX9), collagen type II (COL2), and aggrecan (ACAN) relative expressions were calculated and analyzed using the ΔΔCT method. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as housekeeping gene control. Supplementary Table S1 contains the primer sequences for every gene.

The Chos were fixed with ice-cold methanol for 0.5 h at 4 °C in order to perform Alcian blue staining. Each well was then filled with 1 ml of 0.1% alcian blue staining solution, well plates were then cleaned with water until no dye remained after 1 h. Well-plates were observed and images obtained using an inverted microscope.

An enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to evaluate the levels of VEGF165 expressed by the chondrocytes. At the designated intervals, the cell culture medium was gathered and examined. The assays were performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions and examined at 450 nm on a microplate reader.

Aggregate formation

Once the Chos and iMPCs reached a confluency of 80%, the cells were trypsinised, counted, and resuspended in expansion media at a density of 5 × 10^4 cells/ml. Then, mix the two cell suspensions evenly at a 1:1 ratio, resuspended in the ultralow-attachment 96-well plate with 150 µL per well, and culture on a low-speed shaker (60 r/min). After the cells in the plate form spheroids, change the medium every 2 days (the culture medium is a 1:1 mixture of chondrocyte culture medium and endothelial cell maintenance medium, hereafter known as the Coculture-EBs group). Prepare separate chondrocyte aggregates (using chondrocyte culture medium, hereafter known as the Chos-EBs group) and mesodermal cell aggregates (using endothelial cell maintenance medium, hereafter known as the iMPCs-EBs group) as control groups according to the above method. The aggregates of each group above were cultured in suspension for 14 days. Endothelial cell maintenance medium included advanced DMEM/F12, 10% FBS, 1% Penicilli/streptomycin, 10 µM Y-27,632, 0.2 mM L-Glutamine and 6 µg/mL Vitamin C.

CD31+ immunohistochemical analysis

As previously mentioned, CD31 + was detected by immunofluorescence labeling. To put it briefly, sections underwent an overnight deparaffinization process prior to a sequence of rehydration procedures. PBS containing 1% w/v bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 3% w/v normal goat serum (NGS) was used to block the samples for 60 min. Then sections were treated with rabbit polyclonal anti-CD31 + for a whole night at 4 °C. The sections were incubated with goat antirabbit secondary antibody for one hour at room temperature in the dark following three washing procedures with PBS containing 1% w/v BSA. Following three PBS washes. Subsequently, utilize dpai to stain the nuclei inside the sample cells.

Gene expression by qRT-PCR and Immunofluorescence

Pellets were homogenized in lysis buffer in a bead beater. Perform RT-PCR according to the same method as above. Data analysis was performed with the ΔΔCT method. GAPDH was used as the endogenous control gene. Indian hedgehog (IHH), Collagen type IA1 (COL 1A1), Alkaline phosphatase (ALP), Osteopontin (OPN), and Osteocalcin (OCN) relative expressions were calculated and analyzed using the ΔΔCT method. Supplementary Table S1 contains the primer sequences for every gene. Afterwards, perform immunofluorescence staining on the highly expressed genes using the aforementioned method.

ALP production and Alizarin red staining

According to the instructions of the kit, accurately pipette the supernatant of each group of aggregates cultured for 14 days into the wells of the plate. Then add an appropriate amount of buffer and substrate solution, place the plate in the incubator for half an hour to allow alkaline phosphatase to fully react with the substrate. Then add the stop solution and gently mix to ensure the reaction is completely stopped. Use a spectrophotometer to measure the absorbance at a specific wavelength (450 nm) and calculate the content of alkaline phosph.

Add an appropriate amount of Alizarin Red staining solution to each group of slices obtained according to the above method, stain in the dark for 30 min, remove the staining, wash twice with PBS, observe the staining effect under a microscope, and perform image capture and induction assessment.

Cell aggregates preparation

After 14 days of in vitro culture, the cell aggregates were prepared for implantation. In summary, 10 cells aggregates from each group were placed within collagen I and place them in the 37 °C incubator for 30 min to allow the collagen to solidify. One group of collagen contained no aggregates (known as the collagen I group). Then, place these aggregates in a culture medium in preparation for implantation.

Establishment of critical-size rat calvarial defect and cell aggregates implantation

The rat calvarial defect model and following animal experiments were conducted under the authority of the LanZhou University Ethics Committee (LZUKQ-2024-004) and performed in accordance with the with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0. Before the trials started, forty male Sprague-Dawley (SD-Rag2) rats, weighing between 220 and 240 g and aged 8 weeks, were acclimated to circumstances free of particular pathogens, with unlimited access to food and water, and a 24-hour light/dark cycle.

Rats were randomly assigned to five groups with 5 rats in each group following a week of acclimatization: Control, Collagen I, Chos-EBs, iMPCs-EBs, and Coculture-EBs. A longitudinal scalp incision and periosteal dissection were performed under isoflurane anesthesia and semi-sterile circumstances. Using a trephine bur, a 5 mm diameter defect was meticulously made, and to lessen heat damage, saline irrigation was applied continuously. To stop bleeding and guarantee ideal circumstances for bone repair, the dura mater was carefully protected. The surgical wound was sutured shut after the corresponding group implants were inserted into the defects.

Micro-CT scanning and analysis

The mice were humanely euthanized through a two-step procedure: initial anesthesia was induced with 2% isoflurane inhalation, followed by immediate decapitation while the animals remained under deep anesthesia. This protocol was conducted in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines to ensure minimal distress. The removed calvarial specimens were preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. They were then evaluated with a micro-CT scanner (SCANCO µCT-100, Switzerland). The Evaluation V6.5 program (SCANCO, Switzerland) was used to quantify parameters such as bone volume/tissue volume (BV/TV) and bone mineral density (BMD).

Assay of osteogenesis, vasculogenesis and biocompatibility in vivo

To further confirm the osteogenic ability of each group of the cell aggregates. 4 and 8 weeks later, all the mice were euthanized according to the same method above. The skull tissue and visceral tissue were removed, washed with physiological saline, and fixed in 10% formaldehyde solution within 48 h. The samples were subjected to mixed acid decalcification, gradient ethanol dehydration, paraffin embedding, and then made into 5 μm thick sectioning. Hematoxylin-Eosin staining (H&E), CD31 immunohistochemistry and Masson staining of skull defect tissue, conventional H&E staining of visceral tissue were performed. Image J was used for quantitative analysis (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

All the data are expressed as the means and standard deviations of three representative experiments carried out in triplicate. The experimental data were statistically analyzed via SPSS 26 statistical software. A t test was used to determine the significant differences among the groups, and p values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

IMPCs demonstrated quantifiable endothelial differentiation potential under VEGF induction

HiPSCs were seen to transition from aggregation to dispersion following 5 days of mesodermal induction, and the cells displayed a distinctive endothelial cell population shape following 14 days of VEGF induction (Fig. 2A–C). FLK1(VEGF-R) was expressed on the surface of iMPCs (Fig. 2D–E). By adding exogenous VEGF, we further proved that iMPCs had the capacity to differentiate into endothelial cells. These induced endothelium-like cells (iECs) also exhibit CD31(green)/vWF (red) expression (Fig. 2G–H, J–K) and form branching networks on Matrigel (Fig. 2F). The induction rate was 52.84% according to the flow cytometry results (Fig. 2I). The observed network on Matrigel, while quantitatively reduced compared to HUVECs (p<0.05, figure S1), nevertheless provide functional evidence of endothelial differentiation. This quantitative difference is likely attributable to technical factors, including cellular heterogeneity within the induced population and its comparative immaturity.

The Chos derived from hiPSCs obtained by indirect induction method have certain VEGF secretion ability

Next, hiPSCs were induced into Chos by indirect induction methods. hiPSCs were cultured in mesenchymal medium and passed through three passages, and the obtained iMSCs showed fiber-like morphology (Fig. 3A), the flow cytometry showed that the expression of CD73, CD34 and CD44 in iMSCs was above 70% (Fig. 3D). The cells were rounded, enlarged and aggregated after chondrogenic induction of iMSCs (Fig. 3B–C). Increased ECM deposition in cell pellets on 21 days was confirmed by positive staining for alcian blue (Fig. 3E). Additionally, the expression of distinctive genes during the differentiation of iMSCs into chondrocytes at various time periods was investigated using qRT-PCR, chos induced for 21 days had considerably increased levels of SOX9, COL2, and ACAN expression than pre-induction (Fig. 3F). Finally, the ELISA results demonstrated that chos has a certain VEGF secretion ability, which can provide induction conditions for endothelial differentiation of iMPCs (Fig. 3G). The chondrocyte aggregates added with mesodermal cells accelerated hypertrophy and osteogenic transformation in vitro

Cell aggregates were effectively formed using the ultra-low adhesion plate approach, resulting in a tightly bound structure that was difficult to disperse (Fig. 4A–F). In order to simulate this process and induce a more hypertrophy phenotype in iPSC-derived cartilage granules, iMPCs was introduced into cartilage aggregates, and it was assumed that mesodermal cells could pre-vascularize cartilage aggregates in vitro, thus promoting the osteogenic transformation of cartilage. The results showed that compared with iMPCs-EBs and Chos-EBs, the expression of IHH, ALP, COL1A1, in Coculture-EBs group increased, nevertheless, the late osteogenic genes OCN/OPN exhibited minimal enhancement, which confirmed the progress of hypertrophy and early osteogenic transformation of chondrocytes (Fig. 4G).

The incorporation of mesodermal cells facilitates the vascularization of chondrocyte aggregates in vitro

To assess the impact of mesodermal cell introduction on the vascularization of chondrocyte aggregates, we conducted CD31 immunofluorescence analysis, which revealed extensive positive regions within the co-culture-EBs, indicative of endothelial cell commitment and early cluster formation, consistent with a vasculogenic process, confirming that iMPCs differentiated into endothelial cells due to VEGF secretion by chondrocytes, thereby facilitating the construction of pre-vascularized chondrocyte aggregates in vitro (Fig. 5A). Moreover, the analysis of qRT-PCR also indicated that the target gene (IHH, ALP, COL1A1) exhibited high expression, immunofluorescence staining further confirmed the positive expression of these highly expressed genes, thereby demonstrating that pre-vascularization greatly promoted chondrocyte hypertrophy and the early phases of osteogenic transformation (Fig. 5B–D). The assessment of ALP and alizarin red further validated that the pre-vascularized chondrocyte aggregates exhibited specific osteogenic activity and calcium nodule formation in vitro (Fig. 5E–G). In the physiological context of endochondral ossification, the chondro-osseous junction is characterized by the dominance of ALP/COL1A1-expressing cells—a region where vascular invasion initiates bone formation—while OCN/OPN are markers associated with mature osteoblasts in subsequent mineralization phases. The observed increase in ALP/COL1A1 activity in prevascularized groups, compared to controls, confirms an accelerated early transition at this chondro-osseous frontier. The limited elevation in OCN/OPN is consistent with the experimental timeline, as full mineralization requires further maturation and stabilization of osteoblasts—a process for which the prevascular niche establishes a priming microenvironment, but does not immediately complete in vitro (Fig. 6).

Prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates effectively promote vasculogenesis in vivo

The subcutaneous implantation model was employed to investigate the impact of prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates formed by incorporating iMPCs in vivo. We specifically implanted mesoderm cell aggregates (iMPCs-EBs), chondrocyte aggregates (Chos-EBs), mixed cell aggregates from both cultures (Coculture-EBs), a control group, and a scaffold group (collagen I) to assess whether the pre-existing vascular network increased vasculogenesis in vivo post-implantation. As shown in fig. 7A, the incorporation of iMPCs facilitated the development of a greater number of nascent vasculature compared to the other four groups. Notably, the chos group exhibits nascent vasculature, suggesting that chondrocytes may promote blood vessel formation post-implantation due to their VEGF secretion capability. However, the coculture group demonstrates a greater formation of nascent vasculature (Fig. 7B), and at week 8, distinct osteoid-like matrix developed around these nascent vasculatures. The findings unequivocally demonstrate that pre-vascularized cartilage aggregates facilitate blood vessel formation in vivo and promote the development of adjacent bone.

Prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates effectively enhanced bone regeneration in vivo

Bone formation was assessed by micro-CT analysis. The coculture group effectively performed bone repair in the defect area (Fig. 8A), with the relative content of specific bone tissue in a designated space indicated by the bone volume fraction (BV/TV). The results showed that the coculture group had the highest quantity of new bone, which was statistically distinct from the other groups. Bone mineral density analysis (BMD) further validated that coculture group’s new bone exhibits the highest mineral density (Fig. 8B). These results demonstrate that pre-vascularized chondrocyte aggregates more effectively facilitate bone repair in the defect region. The biocompatibility examination of the heart, liver, brain, lungs, and kidneys demonstrates that the tissue structure of rats in each group is unaffected, the morphology is predominantly normal, and no significant damage is observed, indicating that the implant has no any adverse effects in vivo (Fig. 8C).

While in vitro culture successfully induced markers of early hypertrophic chondrocytes, the inherent diffusion limitations of static 3D culture restrict nutrient and oxygen supply to the cell aggregates core, hindering progression to new bone formation. Our strategy of in vitro pre-vascularization established endothelial cell networks beforehand. Upon implantation, these pre-formed structures facilitated rapid anastomosis with the host circulatory system, enabling swift establishment of functional perfusion throughout the implant. This immediate and efficient vascularization overcame the critical survival and metabolic constraints of the in vitro environment. The resulting enhanced oxygen tension, nutrient delivery, and access to systemic factors provided the necessary cues for cells to progress beyond the chondrogenic stage towards robust osteogenic differentiation and subsequent bone matrix mineralization. Besides, the future studies employ lineage tracing or immunohistochemical validation of these markers over time to better bridge the in vitro and in vivo mechanisms.

Discussion

Endochondral ossification is a primary process of physiological bone regeneration, and pre-vascularization of cartilage can significantly enhance the osteogenic conversion of chondrocytes and accelerate bone healing [22]. Currently, research on cartilage pre-vascularization mainly focuses on the coculture of chondrocytes and endothelial cells; however, premature interaction between mature endothelial cells and chondrocytes suppresses the osteogenic transformation of cartilage, thereby hindering the achievement of the desired osteogenic outcome [11]. This study presents a novel method for cartilage pre-vascularization, utilizing a co-differentiation strategy based on mesodermal cells. Our findings demonstrated that the incorporation of mesodermal cells into chondrocyte aggregates can achieve cartilage pre-vascularization and enhance chondrocyte hypertrophy and trigger osteogenic transformation. The engineered pre-vascularized chondrocyte aggregates have osteogenic potential in vitro and significantly enhance the repair of bone fractures in vivo.

The conventional approach to pre-vascularization utilizing endothelial cells may result in conflicts among various cell types and the proliferation of certain cells within the coculture system, attributable to the incompatibility of culture medium formulations, spatial competition, mutual interference of signaling pathways, and contact inhibition [23]. For example, the osteogenic induction factor has been demonstrated to influence the vascular-promoting capacity of mesenchymal cells and the migratory ability of endothelial cells into tubular structures, which hinders vascular formation [24]. The endothelial induction factor diminishes the osteogenic potential of mesenchymal cells [25]. The early interaction between chondrocytes and endothelial cells interferes with the signal pathway that regulates the vascularization of chondrocytes, the absorption of apoptotic chondrocytes and the osteogenic transformation of chondrocytes [26]. Nevertheless, certain studies indicate that, in contrast to direct osteogenic differentiation, the pre-cartilage induction of mesenchymal cells can enhance the level of bone matrix deposition following subsequent osteogenic differentiation, this suggests that cartilage differentiation enhances osteogenic differentiation potential without additional osteogenic induction factors, thereby avoiding the detrimental effects of excessive osteogenic induction factors on endothelial cells [27]. Furthermore, iMPCs derived from the hiPSCs, as established in prior research, possess specific VEGF receptors on surface, which helps minimize the impact of the cartilage medium [28]. Additionally, the endothelial cell maintenance medium we intend to incorporate comprises solely the essential nutrients for endothelial cell proliferation, devoid of any endothelial inducing factors, which will further mitigate the adverse effects of such factors on the osteogenic transformation of chondrocytes [29].

Pre-vascularization by introducing mesodermal cells exhibits multifaceted regulatory effects in promoting chondrocyte-to-osteoblast transformation. Preformed microvascular networks may enhance metabolic support by improving oxygen and nutrient diffusion to hypertrophic chondrocytes, thereby accelerating their maturation—a well-established vascular function during skeletal development [12]. Furthermore, direct cell-contact mediation between iMPC-ECs and hypertrophic chondrocytes, potentially via Notch or Ephrin signaling pathways, may orchestrate coupled vasculogenesis and osteogenesis, recapitulating key regulatory mechanisms in developmental endochondral ossification [30]. The iMPC co-differentiation model offers unique advantages: as both cell lineages originate from the same progenitor pool, their intrinsic signaling compatibility—such as a shared extracellular matrix environment—may amplify paracrine crosstalk beyond the effects achievable with exogenous HUVECs [31]. Future studies should aim to delineate the dominant mechanisms using in vitro ligand-receptor mapping and conditional knockout models.

Usually, chondrocytes release VEGF via autocrine or paracrine mechanisms, which are tightly controlled [32]. These VEGF contribute to sustaining nutrition supply around chondrocytes, enhancing their survival and metabolism, and aiding in the maintenance of cartilage tissue homeostasis [33]. In the process of bone healing, an adequate quantity of VEGF can enhance vasculogenesis, aid the migration of repair cells, such as mesenchymal stem cells, to the damage site, so facilitating the restoration of cartilage tissue [34]. Moreover, VEGF can facilitate the indirect healing of bone defects by expediting the conversion of cartilage to bone in the endochondral ossification process, hence hastening the repair of bone defects [35]. In vivo investigations revealed that the single chondrocyte aggregation group facilitated the healing and vascularization of the bone defect area, attributable to VEGF secreted by chondrocytes. However, due to the preformed vascular structure developed in vitro, the preavascularized chondrocyte aggregates can rapidly establish vascular anastomosis post-implantation. This process further enhances the vascular network within the defect area, promoting the formation of more osteoid-like matrix in the surrounding region and ultimately resulting in improved healing outcomes. While our in vitro vascular network formation is driven by VEGF-induced differentiation of iMPCs into endothelial lineages and subsequent self-organization into capillary-like structures [36]. This differs from angiogenesis, which involves sprouting from mature endothelia. Besides, hiPSCs possess intrinsic potential for spontaneous mesenchymal commitment during early mesoderm differentiation [37]. Although our data demonstrate a strong spatiotemporal correlation between iMPC-driven vascularization and enhanced ossification—recapitulating key aspects of endochondral bone formation—the precise causal relationship remains to be fully established. Future studies employing inducible genetic models or pharmacological inhibition of VEGF signaling in this system will be essential to definitively confirm whether vascularization is necessary for the observed osteogenic enhancement. These MSCs have similar angiogenesis promoting ability to pericyte and physically associate with CD31⁺/vWF⁺ iECs to form primitive vascular networks in vitro [25]. The term ‘pre-vascularization’ here specifically denotes the in vitro creation of a vasculogenic network designed for rapid in vivo integration primarily via anastomosis, leading to accelerated functional vasculogenesis. Upon in vivo implantation, these pre-formed structures rapidly anastomose with host vasculature, accelerating functional perfusion – a key advantage over reliance on slower de novo host angiogenesis alone.

However, the primary signal transduction mechanism involved in angiogenesis and cartilage-bone transition remains inadequately in this study, future investigations will benefit from clarifying the receptor-specific signaling mechanisms underlying the VEGF-mediated crosstalk between iMPCs and chondrocytes. The fundamental microsphere model serves as the experimental platform, and 3D printing technology can be integrated in the future to enhance the three-dimensional spatial arrangement of the vascular network and advance the simulation of cartilage pre-vascularization, and although in vitro data have strongly indicated a causal role of VEGF, this effect still requires further validation through in vivo intervention experiments. Thereby provide a new guiding direction for endochondral osteogenesis strategy. Besides, future studies employing more physiologically relevant models will be essential to provide functional validation, for instance, through assays such as cell migration or ac-LDL uptake, to further substantiate the mechanistic insights suggested by our findings.

Conclusions

In this presented study, The VEGF receptor on the surface of iMPCs was highly expressed. CD31/vWF immunofluorescence and Matrigel-based tube formation assay confirmed that iMPCs had definite endothelial differentiation potential. Elisa results showed that the Chos had the ability to secrete VEGF165, which could provide an induction environment for endothelial differentiation of iMPCs. The results of CD31 immunofluorescence confirmed that there was extensive expression of CD31 endothelial signal in chondrocyte aggregates added with iMPCs. The results of qRT-PCR and immunofluorescence detection of IHH, ALP and COL1A1 showed that pre-vascularization effectively promoted chondrocyte hypertrophy and early osteogenic transformation, the results of alizarin red staining showed that the prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates had certain osteogenic ability in vitro. In vivo studies indicated that the healing efficacy of prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates was superior, as demonstrated by H&E staining and Micro-CT. CD31 immunohistochemistry indicated that prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates anastomosed with host blood arteries post-implantation in vivo, establishing a functioning vascular network. Masson staining revealed an increased formation of new bone from prevascularized chondrocyte aggregates, along with some maturation of existing bone. This approach offers a novel perspective on the development of tissue-engineered bone by leveraging the concept of cartilage pre-vascularization.

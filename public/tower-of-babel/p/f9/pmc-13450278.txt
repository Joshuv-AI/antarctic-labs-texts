Draft genome of novel Streptomyces sp. strain JW3 selected for its inhibition of other Streptomyces spp.
Authors: Jaouad Anissi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesDespite decades of extensive investigation, Streptomyces species continue to represent a vast and rich reservoir of novel bioactive compounds. Accordingly, genome mining remains a powerful and strategic approach for the discovery of new candidate drugs. The objective of the present study is to generate a high-quality, assembled, and functionally annotated draft genome of a novel Streptomyces species, providing a robust genomic framework for downstream comparative and biosynthetic analyses.Data descriptionHere, we report the first assembled and annotated draft genome of the novel soil-derived Streptomyces sp. strain JW3. High-throughput sequencing of Streptomyces sp. strain JW3 genome generated approximately 1.6 million reads, corresponding to a total of 465.4 million bases and an effective sequencing yield of 157.3 Mb. De novo assembly produced a draft genome of 8.3 Mb distributed across 341 contigs, with an N50 contig length of 52.1 kb. This genome constitutes a valuable genomic resource that will facilitate comparative analyses with other Streptomyces species. Such analyses are expected to advance our understanding of the genetic diversity, metabolic capacity, and biosynthetic potential of this genus.Keywords: Streptomyces, Draft genome, Phylogeny, Antibiotics

Objective

Streptomyces species are renowned as prolific producers of a wide range of bioactive secondary metabolites, many of which are extensively exploited in the pharmaceutical industry and biomedical research as drug candidates. These metabolites include antibiotics, antifungal agents, immunosuppressants, and anticancer compounds that have had a profound impact on human health. Streptomyces spp. are filamentous, Gram-positive bacteria belonging to the class Actinomycetia and are ubiquitous across diverse environments, including soil, the rhizosphere, and in association with plants and other microorganisms [1]. Owing to their metabolic versatility, Streptomyces represent a taxonomically and biotechnologically significant group and continue to attract considerable interest as a major source of therapeutically relevant natural products [2, 3]. The present study reports the genome sequence of strain JW3, isolated from agricultural soil collected in the High Atlas region near Agadir, Morocco (30°25′20.09″ N, 9°18′57.91″ W). This strain was selected based on its taxonomic novelty and its ability to inhibit the growth of other Streptomyces species in pairwise interaction assays (Table 1 [4], Table 1 [5], and Table 1 [6]). Approximately 0.5 g of soil was sampled at a depth of ~ 2 cm and processed for bacterial isolation using standard re-suspension and serial dilution protocols [7]. Isolation and cultivation were performed at 30 °C for 5 days on Bennett medium. Among the isolated Streptomyces strains, strain JW3 exhibited pronounced antagonistic activity in pairwise interaction assays (Table 1 [6]).

Data description

Here, we present the first draft genome assembly and annotation of the novel soil-derived Streptomyces sp. strain JW3. Strain JW3 was cultivated for 5 days at 30 °C in Bennett broth medium (30 mL) without glucose, and genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted using the Monarch® gDNA Purification Kit (New England Biolabs, France).

For taxonomic identification, the 16 S rRNA gene was amplified using the universal primers 27 F (AGAGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG) [9] and 1492R (GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT) [10], followed by Sanger sequencing using the BigDye™ Terminator v3.1 Cycle Sequencing Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, France). Sequence analysis revealed that the 16 S rRNA gene sequence of strain JW3 (GenBank accession PX314968) shared 98.61% sequence identity with Streptomyces aurantiogriseus strain CSSP546 (NR_115394.1), Streptomyces griseoviridis strain NBRC 12,874, and Streptomyces daghestanicus strain JCM 4365 (LC506592.1), which represent its closest phylogenetic relatives. Lower levels of sequence identity were observed with Streptomyces cahuitamycinicus strain 13K301 (98.20%; MG770843.1) and Streptomyces chattanoogensis strain DSM 40,002 (96.83%; NR_042829.1) (Table 1 [7]). According to the CLSI MM18-A guidelines, strain JW3 may be considered a candidate novel Streptomyces species [11]. To further support this taxonomic assignment, Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) analysis was performed using genome sequences of the closest related species identified by 16 S rRNA gene similarity. Pairwise ANI values were calculated using FastANI [12] and visualized through hierarchical clustering, applying the standard 95% species-level threshold (Table 1 [7]). Streptomyces sp. strain JW3 exhibited substantially lower ANI values compared with S. aurantigriseus (~ 85%) and S. daghestanicus and S. griseoviridis (~ 90–92%), indicating genomic divergence well below the species demarcation threshold. Collectively, these results support the conclusion that strain JW3 represents a distinct genomic lineage within the genus Streptomyces. Although ANI values fall below accepted species thresholds, a formal species description requires additional polyphasic taxonomic analyses.

Whole-genome sequencing was performed using the Illumina NextSeq 2000 platform, generating 151-bp paired-end reads, and approximately 1.6 million reads, corresponding to a total of 465.4 million bases and an overall sequencing yield of 157.3 Mb. Library preparation was carried out with the Nextera® XT DNA Sample Preparation Kit (Illumina, France). Raw sequencing reads were quality-checked using FastQC v0.11.9 [13] and trimmed with Trimmomatic v0.39 [14]. De novo genome assembly was performed using SPAdes v3.15.5 [15] with the --careful option enabled. Assembly quality was assessed using QUAST v5.3.0 [16], resulting in a draft genome size of 8.3 Mb and an average GC content of 72.50%, as provided in Table 1 [17], Table 1 [18], and Table 1 [19].

Genome annotation was conducted using Prokka v1.14.6 [20]. Biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) were predicted using antiSMASH v8.0 [21]. antiSMASH analysis identified a total of 37 putative BGCs (Table 1 [22]), including 10 nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) clusters, 5 polyketide synthase (PKS) clusters, 7 terpene clusters, 2 RiPP-like clusters, and 13 additional unclassified BGCs. Antimicrobial resistance genes were identified using ABRicate v4.0.23 [23] and AMRFinderPlus v1.0.1 [24]. AMR genes analysis suggests putative resistance determinants associated with vancomycin, macrolides, oleandomycin, aminocoumarins, and glycopeptides, as summarized in Table 1 [8].

Limitations

This data note was limited to a single fragmented assembly from the genome of Streptomyces sp. strain JW3.

The fragmented nature of the draft genome assembly may lead to an overestimation of the total number of BGCs, and that some predicted regions may correspond to partial or incomplete clusters. We further note that a more contiguous assembly (e.g., incorporating long-read sequencing) would be required to definitively resolve full-length BGC boundaries and accurately determine the exact number of distinct biosynthetic loci.

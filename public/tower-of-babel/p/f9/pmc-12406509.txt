Effective doses of alfentanil combined with propofol for gastroscopy in patients of different ages under Narcotrend monitoring: a prospective dose-finding study utilizing the up-and-down sequential allocation method
Authors: Lili Jiang, Zhe Peng, Yuhui Deng, Zebang Qin, Jianxia Li, Jinping Huang, Zaisheng Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPainless gastroscopy is preferred by both patients and physicians, as it minimizes discomfort during the procedure. Alfentanil, a short-acting opioid analgesic, possesses pharmacological properties that make it suitable for inducing analgesia during gastroscopy. However, research on the optimal dosage of alfentanil when used in combination with propofol for gastroscopy is limited. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the 50% and 95% effective doses (ED50 and ED95, respectively) of alfentanil in combination with propofol for gastroscopy, using Narcotrend monitoring.MethodsElective gastroscopy was performed in 51 patients aged 18–80 years, with a body mass index of 18–28 kg/m2, and American Society of Anesthesiologists Class I or II. Based on their age, they were categorized into the youth group (18–60 years) and the elderly group (61–80 years). The patients were connected to the vital signs monitor and Narcotrend, and propofol was administered intravenously until the Narcotrend index was at Stage C1–C2 (65–74) and had stabilized for 1 min. Following this, alfentanil was administrated (initial dose, 5 µg/kg); if a positive reaction to the gastroscope placement was elicited, the dose was increased to a higher level in the next patient; otherwise, the dose was decreased to a lower level, with a dose gradient of 0.5 µg/kg. Gastroscope insertion was started 90 s later, and the criteria for a positive reaction to gastroscope insertion included coughing, nausea, and/or motor reaction during or within 1 min of gastroscope insertion. The test was stopped if seven folds occurred during the study. The ED50 values with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of alfentanil in combination with propofol for the inhibition of gastroscopic placement were calculated.ResultsUnder the sedation conditions induced using alfentanil and propofol and detected using Narcotrend, the ED50 (95% CI) were 5.39 µg/kg (4.76–6.47) in the youth group and 5.69 µg/kg (4.67–6.31) in the elderly group, respectively.ConclusionsThe ED50 of alfentanil combined with propofol for gastroscopy under Narcotrend monitoring were 5.39 µg/kg in the youth group and 5.69 µg/kg in the elderly group, respectively.

Introduction

Gastroscopy is primarily employed for diagnosing and treating upper gastrointestinal system ailments (Akahoshi et al. 2018; Veitch et al. 2021, 2015). It is an invasive procedure that can cause discomfort to the patients and have adverse effects on both their psychological and physiological well-being. As medical care has evolved to prioritize patient comfort, painless gastroscopy has gained widespread acceptance. Currently, propofol injection is the primary method used in clinical practice to alleviate the discomfort associated with gastroscopy (Stogiannou et al. 2018). However, the administration of pure propofol has raised concerns regarding the dosage and potential adverse reactions. To address these issues, the administration of propofol in combination with analgesic drugs during gastroscopy has gained traction. The American Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy guidelines for sedation anesthesia in gastrointestinal endoscopy recommend a regimen comprising opioids and propofol (ASGE Standards of Practice Committee et al. 2018). This approach is particularly significant for older patients, as their age-related physiological changes make them more sensitive to pain stimuli. While using propofol alone requires a substantial quantity of the drug to suppress the reflex responses of the patients, older patients often present with multiple underlying health conditions, diminishing their capacity to compensate for respiratory and circulatory disturbances (Liu et al. 2023; Sasaki et al. 2012). The use of analgesic drugs in conjunction with propofol effectively suppresses the reflex responses and allows for a reduction in the propofol dosage (Ye et al. 2017; Meng et al. 2016). This combination minimizes circulatory fluctuations and mitigates the discomfort associated with gastroscopy, ultimately decreasing perioperative risks.

Opioid analgesics, such as those of the fentanyl family, are frequently employed to manage pain in the perioperative setting (Shanthanna et al. 2021; Kelly et al. 2023). Particularly, alfentanil stands out for its rapid onset of action (beginning within 30 s and peaking around 80 s). It has a relatively short duration of action of approximately 10–15 min and exhibits minimal respiratory depression (Hughes et al. 2023; Dong et al. 2023). These characteristics make it suitable for the analgesic requirements of minor outpatient surgical procedures. Initially, the practice of monitoring the depth of anesthesia was introduced to oversee the level of anesthesia during intubation to prevent patients from having intraoperative awareness. Therefore, the Narcotrend monitor (MonitorTechnik, Bad Bramstedt, Germany) was developed, which is an electroencephalogram monitor specifically designed to gauge the level of anesthesia depth. It has gained widespread acceptance in clinical practice due to its reliable performance (Kreuer and Wilhelm 2006; Puchner et al. 2020; Soehle et al. 2021). In clinical practice, the primary concern of anesthesiologists during gastroscopy is to ensure the safety and comfort of the patient. The objective is for the patient to undergo the examination in a relaxed state and awaken promptly afterward. Maintaining deep sedation is advantageous in this scenario, because in the case of commonly used sedative medications, deeper levels of anesthesia require higher dosages of the drug, resulting in pronounced suppression of the respiratory and circulatory functions (McGrath et al. 2021). Currently, determining the appropriate combination of alfentanil and propofol for gastroscopy relies largely on the expertise of anesthesiologists. Hence, we initiated this study to explore the optimal dosage of alfentanil in combination with propofol for gastroscopy, utilizing Narcotrend monitoring. Our primary objective was to enhance the precision of medication dosing for gastroscopy, thereby elevating patient satisfaction levels and diminishing the perioperative anesthesia-related risks.

Methods

This single-center, prospective clinical study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Seventh Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University and was registered in the China Clinical Trial Registry on September 02, 2022 (ChiCTR2200063245). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before starting the study.

Patients

This study included patients aged 18–80 years, with body mass index 18–28 kg/m2, and American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Class I or II, who had undergone elective painless gastroscopy from October to December 2022. The exclusion criteria were a history of hypertension, hyperthyroidism, neurological, and psychiatric diseases, consumption of opioids or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs 48 h before the procedure, participation in a clinical trial of other drugs in the last 4 weeks, allergy to the study drug propofol, and a history of opioid addiction.

Anesthesia management

Before the examination, the patient was subjected to routine fasting and administered 10 mL of dyclonine hydrochloride mucilage (1%) orally. After entering the examination room, the patient was placed in the left lateral position, the peripheral veins of the upper limbs were opened, and oxygen was administered by the nasal catheter at a rate of 3 L/min. The patient was routinely connected to a monitor to check the basic vital signs, such as blood pressure (blood pressure monitoring frequency was set at 5-min intervals, with additional measurements performed immediately upon clinical indication), heart rate (HR), oxygen saturation (SpO2), and respiratory rate. The Narcotrend anesthesia depth detector was connected, and propofol was injected intravenously using the target-controlled infusion pump. The starting dose was 2 µg/mL, and the concentration was gradually increased with a dose gradient of 0.25 µg/mL until the Narcotrend index was within the routine sedation stage of C1–C2 (65–74). Alfentanil was injected slowly with a starting dose of 5 µg/kg, and the dose was determined according to whether there was a positive reaction during the operation, a positive reaction to gastroscope insertion included coughing, nausea, and/or motor reaction during or within 1 min of gastroscope insertion, conversely, the absence of such reaction was recorded as a negative response. The alfentanil dosage should be adjusted according to patient response: reduced following positive responses and increased by 0.5 µg/kg after negative responses and gastroscopy was started after 60 s. During the operation, if the SpO2 was ≤ 93%, the inhaled oxygen flow was increased, and the airway was opened by holding the lower jaw. However, if the SpO2 was ≤ 80%, oxygen was supplied under pressure using a mask. If the mean arterial pressure (MAP) decreased by > 30% of the basal value, phenylephrine 50 µg was administered. If the HR was ≤ 50 beats/min, atropine 0.5 mg was administered.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Product and Service Solutions, version 26.0. Normally distributed measures are expressed as means ± standard deviations. The x2 test or Fisher exact probability method was used to compare the count data between the groups. P < 0.05 was indicated to represent statistically significantly difference. Quantification of the 50% effective dose (ED50) was achieved through implementation of centered isotonic regression analysis within cir package (v2.5.1) within the R statistical computing environment (v4.5.1) (Oron et al. 2022). The Bootstrap ED50 difference method employs resampling techniques to compare median effective doses (ED50) between two groups.

An assessment of the ED of alfentanil using a sequential method revealed that the ED50 of alfentanil combined with propofol for gastroscopy under Narcotrend monitoring were 5.39 µg/kg (4.76–6.47) in the youth group and 5.69 µg/kg (4.67–6.31) in the elderly group, respectively. As shown in Table 2, the average gastric endoscopy durations in the youth and elderly groups were 6.2 ± 2.1 min and 7.5 ± 1.8 min, respectively, with the latter showing slightly longer examination times than the former. However, this difference lacked statistical significance, probably due to the higher incidence of gastric diseases in the older population. The propofol dosages administered in the youth and elderly groups were 125 ± 12.3 mg and 103 ± 10.5 mg, respectively, without a statistically significant difference; however, the elderly group exhibited a lower propofol consumption rate per minute than the youth group. This suggests that older patients may be more sensitive to sedative medications than younger patients. Throughout the examination process, eight instances of complications necessitating interventions were reported among the patients who underwent successful examinations. Specifically, five instances were observed in the elderly group, while the remaining three occurred in the youth group. These complications included two cases of hypoxemia, five of hypotension, and one of bradycardia. According the clinical management protocol, hypoxemic patients (SpO₂ < 93%) achieved sustained peripheral oxygen saturation maintenance (SpO₂ ≥ 93%) following increasing inhaled oxygen flow and mandibular advancement maneuver. For bradycardic presentations, pharmacological intervention with intravenous atropine (0.5 mg) successfully increased the heart rate to more than 60 bpm. Hemodynamic stabilization was accomplished in 4/5 hypotensive cases through single-dose phenylephrine administration (50 µg IV bolus), with the remaining case requiring repeated dosing (cumulative 100 µg) to achieve sustained normotension (MAP ≥ 65 mmHg).

Discussion

Propofol is a fast-acting, short-duration general anesthetic, widely used to induce anesthesia for various surgeries and examinations, intensive care unit sedation, and insomnia treatment (Lundström et al. 2010; Xu et al. 2011; Tang et al. 2022). Propofol takes approximately 30 s to take effect after the initial injection, has a quick first distribution phase lasting 2–4 min, and an elimination phase of 30–60 min. Hence, it is a frequently employed anesthetic in gastrointestinal endoscopic surgery. The depth of anesthesia is influenced by the dosage of sedative anesthetic agents; however, increased dosages of these sedatives correlate with heightened suppression of circulation and respiration. To mitigate potential complications, the depth of anesthesia is monitored to achieve a sedated and amnesic state in patients undergoing gastroscopy, thus reducing the risks associated with the procedure. To reduce the dosage of propofol administered and to attenuate the patient’s reflexes to gastroscopic stimuli, analgesics have been introduced in combination with the sedative agents used in gastroscopy (Zhou et al. 2021; LaPierre et al. 2012). Alfentanil, a short-acting opioid analgesic, takes effect immediately following intravenous administration, with a rapid initial distribution phase of 1 min and a slow secondary distribution phase lasting 14 min (Hughes et al. 2023; Scholz et al. 1996). The pharmacological characteristics of alfentanil and propofol make them suitable for anesthesia and sedation protocols in short, minor surgical procedures. The utilization of Narcotrend to monitor the anesthesia depth allows patients to undergo gastroscopy while in an amnesic state, representing a significant advancement in patient comfort. Thus, the application of alfentanil as an analgesic helps suppress the patient’s movement response, facilitating the completion of gastroscopy by the operator. The sequential method, as introduced by DiXON, is a straightforward, expeditious, and preliminary approach employed for the calculation of crucial pharmacological parameters, such as the median ED50. This method, known for its simplicity and rapidity, has found widespread application in the field of pharmacology, particularly in the assessment of drug potency (Dixon 1991; Niu et al. 2017; Coppens et al. 2017). In this study, the sequential method was employed to ascertain the ED50 of alfentanil for gastroscopy when combined with propofol under the guidance of Narcotrend monitoring.

In the gastroscopy procedure, the most significant challenge arises during the insertion of the scope into the pharynx. This step can trigger various adverse reactions, including coughing, nausea, vomiting, resistance to the procedure, and even complications such as hemodynamic deterioration and esophageal rupture (Chen et al. 2022). To manage these stress-inducing responses effectively and minimize complications, it is imperative to administer adequate sedation and analgesia.

Therefore, the key to successful gastroscopy lies in the induction of proper sedation and analgesia, which effectively mitigates the stress response and reduces the likelihood of complications. Propofol is a sedative with no intrinsic analgesic properties. In traditional gastroscopy procedures, the use of propofol alone typically involves the administration of high doses of sedatives to induce deep anesthesia and suppress somatic reactions in the patient. However, this approach can lead to pronounced circulatory and respiratory suppression, substantially increasing the risk of respiratory and circulatory complications. Therefore, the use of sedatives combined with analgesics in painless gastroscopy helps alleviate the adverse reactions caused by sedatives. Opioid analgesics are commonly used pain relievers in painless gastroscopy examinations, mainly including fentanyl, sufentanil, and remifentanil. Alfentanil effectively attenuates the intense stimulation experienced during the entry phase of the procedure and is associated with a markedly lower incidence of respiratory depression compared to the aforementioned opioid analgesics (Yang et al. 2023). This makes it a better choice for managing pain and discomfort during gastroscopy while minimizing the potential for respiratory complications.

We aim to create an environment where patient comfort and safety take precedence in gastroscopy. In stark contrast to surgical procedures, the uniqueness of endoscopic examinations is that they do not require a specific depth of general anesthesia. This not only contributes to a more relaxed and stress-free experience for individuals but also eliminates potential complications associated with deep anesthesia. Good sedation combined with analgesic drugs to suppress pain responses can achieve patient comfort and reduce adverse reactions, thereby shortening the patient’s hospital stay. Previous studies have predominantly focused on determining effective doses of sedative-alfentanil combinations for endoscopic procedures to avoid oversedation (Shi et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2024), reporting effective doses values for sedative combined with commonly used dosages of analgesics. These studies, akin to those employing propofol as the sole agent, predominantly rely on sedative medications to mitigate procedural stimuli. However, this methodology is associated with notable risks, including excessive sedation, pronounced hemodynamic instability, and the potential occurrence of burst suppression patterns on electroencephalography (EEG). Consequently, our research objective is to minimize sedative usage by focusing on alleviating patient apprehension through analgesic agents targeting the suppression of stimuli from invasive procedures. Unlike traditional methods that define sedation depth using scales like the Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) (Wang et al. 2024; Huang et al. 2025), employing anesthesia depth monitors provides an objective means to maintain patients at a consistent anesthetic depth. This study innovatively maintains standardized sedation depth while systematically investigating alfentanil’s effective dose, better aligning with clinical practice. Furthermore, diverging from Li et al.’s methodology which targeted the surgical anesthesia stage (50–65) (Li et al. 2021), our study specifically investigates deep sedation (65–74). Preliminary statistics demonstrate improved hemodynamic stability and faster recovery during procedures. Subsequent investigations will employ randomized controlled trials to comprehensively compare our novel dosing regimen with propofol-based anesthesia for gastroscopy, evaluating effectiveness, adverse event rates, and recovery time. This approach aligns our research with the concept of precise anesthesia.

We found that the ED50 of alfentanil were slightly elevated in this study compared to the usual use. This discrepancy could be attributed to the intentional use of lower sedative doses in our study to maintain a lighter level of anesthesia and reduce complications related to sedative dosage. Although the demand for analgesic drugs is not lower in older patients than in younger patients (Falzone et al. 2013), there is a significant reduction in the demand for sedative drugs (Duan et al. 2023). Notably, the observed numerical trend of marginally higher ED50 values in elderly group (5.69 vs 5.39 μg/kg), we propose this pattern may reflect age-related pharmacodynamic responses: Younger patients’ greater propofol requirements to maintain equivalent anesthetic depth might concurrently suppress visceral nociceptive signaling during endoscopy through dose-dependent analgesic effects. Studies indicate that sedative drugs have more pronounced side effects in older patients than in younger patients because their compensatory abilities are weaker, making them more prone to circulatory and respiratory suppression. Therefore, the MAP of our patients remained relatively stable throughout the study. The analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in complication rates between the two patient cohorts. Additionally, the postoperative awakening time was considerably shorter than that of the conventional protocol, and the elderly group benefited more than the youth group. Hence, we have initiated a comprehensive study to compare the pros and cons of our dosage regimen and the conventional one.

Our research has some limitations. First, we only recruited patients classified as ASA I–II for the trial. In clinical practice, the compensatory abilities of higher ASA classification patients are weaker; hence, personalized anesthesia may be more beneficial for them. Our improved drug regimen may offer greater benefits for this subgroup. Moreover, we did not include patients aged ≥ 80 years. Second, our patient selection only considered age, and the conclusions may be more convincing if sex distinctions were also considered.

Conclusion

The combination of alfentanil and propofol can be safely used for painless gastroscopy under Narcotrend monitoring. The ED50 of alfentanil combined with propofol for gastroscopy under Narcotrend monitoring were 5.69 µg/kg (4.67–6.31) in the youth group and 5.39 µg/kg(4.76–6.47) in the elderly group.

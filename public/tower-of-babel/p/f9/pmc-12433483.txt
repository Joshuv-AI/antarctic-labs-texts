Draft Genome Sequence data of Burkholderia pseudomallei CM000152: A strain isolated from pus sample of an Indian patient
Authors: Sindhu Sree B, Rajarshi Bhattacharya, Varshith MR, Shrilaxmi MS, Soumi Choudhary, Piyush Behari Lal, Ranita Ghosh Dastidar, Chiranjay Mukhopadhyay, Somasish Ghosh Dastidar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Here we report a draft genome sequence of Burkholderia pseudomallei strain CM000152, isolated from a melioidosis patient in India. This bacterium is a Gram-negative, saprophytic flagellate that can form biofilms. QIAamp® DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen, Chadstone, Victoria, Australia) was used to isolate genomic DNA from B. pseudomallei strain CM000152. It was further sequenced using the Illumina Hiseq 2500 platform (Illumina, Inc., San Diego, CA) to generate paired-end fragments (2 × 150 PE reads), producing 7146,272 reads at 284x coverage. We identified 246 contigs containing 300 base pairs with L50 of 36 and N50 of 67,418. CM000152 genome contains 7542,791 base pairs, with 67.71 % GC content. Gene prediction is performed using Prokka v.1.14.6. There are 7829 coding sequences in the sequenced DNA, followed by 74 genes coding tRNA and 2 coding rRNA. This project has been deposited under the NCBI Bioproject accession number PRJNA1190241.

Background

Melioidosis is a tropical disease caused by B. pseudomallei. It is a Gram-negative, facultative intracellular bacterium [1]. In India, melioidosis cases are predominantly concentrated along the coastal regions of Karnataka [2]. This concentration of detection of melioidosis cases in Southern India might be due to the availability of skilled manpower and better infrastructure. However, due to the endeavours of the ICMR Task Force project ‘MISSION’, more cases of melioidosis are currently being detected all over India. Melioidosis has a wide array of symptoms involving many different organ systems [3]. Ingestion, inhalation, or bacterial entry through cuts in the skin are the three main routes of infection. The high adaptability of the bacterium is another factor adding to its mortality [4,5]. There are no available, accurate diagnostic point-of-care kits or vaccines. B. pseudomallei is an environmental saprophyte commonly found in soil and surface water in endemic tropical and subtropical regions. The genome of strain CM000152 reflects its ecological versatility and adaptation to such niches. The presence of genes associated with antibiotic resistance (e.g., Bcr/CflA family, SMR efflux proteins), and capsular polysaccharide biosynthesis (WcbR, WcbQ, WcbC, WcbA) underscores the strain’s capacity to persist in hostile environments, evade protozoan predation, and compete with microbial flora. Its resistance to beta-lactam antibiotics likely reflects natural selection in soil environments where such antimicrobials are produced by competing microbes. Moreover, the capsule-related genes facilitate survival against desiccation and immune evasion when transitioning from soil to host. These features suggest that strain CM000152 can play a dual ecological role as an environmental reservoir bacterium and as an opportunistic pathogen capable of causing melioidosis in humans.

So, understanding the genetic diversity and pathogenic mechanisms of B.pseudomallei isolates endemic to India is crucial in developing a vaccine or a rapid diagnostic kit [6]. By studying the genome of B.pseudomallei CM000152, an Indian isolate, we aim to gain insights into the molecular determinants of virulence and develop targeted diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for melioidosis in this region.

Data Description

We present the data from the sequenced strain of B. pseudomallei in this article. Genome assembly contains 246 contigs which are 300 bps longer, with a 67,418 N50 value and an L50 value of 36. The size of the total genome was 7542,791 base pairs, and the GC content of 67.71 % (Fig. 1). We identified 7829 coding sequences (CDS) followed by 2 rRNA and 74 tRNA genes (Table 1). We have assessed the genome quality using CheckM, which revealed a low contamination level of 0.2 % and high completeness scores of 100 % and 99.6 % based on BUSCO analysis. We found 100 % similarity of 16S rRNA from our strain CM000152 with different members of the Burkholderiaceae family, which includes 14 species of Burkholderia. Among them, B. pseudomallei ATCC 23,343 was found to be the closest phylogenetic relative of strain CM000152. Our phylogenetic tree identified B. pseudomallei ATCC 23,343 as the nearest relative with 74 % bootstrap value (Fig. 2). We also analyzed a comparative whole genome based phylogenetic tree, which supports the TYGS results and provides evidence that B. pseudomallei CM000152 has strain level novelty. The CM000152 strain’s Digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) values were 93.4 % similar compared to B. pseudomallei ATCC 23,343. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

A 2-year-old male patient reported to the Kasturba Hospital, Manipal with a submandibular abscess. The bacteria was isolated from the pus samples of the infected submandibular abscess of the patient which was serially diluted and plated on Ashdown’s agar which is a selective media for Burkholderia pseudomallei. After incubation at 37 °C for 48 h, individual colonies were collected and repeatedly streaked on Ashdown’s agar plates for further purification [7].

DNA extraction from bacterial culture

The QIAamp® DNA Mini Kit Cat no-51,304 (Qiagen, Chadstone, Victoria, Australia) was used for the genome extraction from the bacterial culture as per the manufacturer’s protocol. The quality and concentration of the extracted DNA were evaluated by measuring the absorbance ratio at 260 nm to 280 nm using a microplate reader (Agilent BioTek Epoch2).

Whole genome sequencing and assembly

The DNA library was prepared using Ultra II DNA lib preparation for Illumina kit (NEBNext E7645S/L)”. The genome of B. pseudomallei strain CM000152 was sequenced by using Illumina HiSeq X technology (2 × 150 paired end) which produced 10,667,040 raw reads. We got 7146,272 paired clean reads after the initial analysis by FastQC v0.11.9 [8]. We used Cutadapt v2.9 [9] for trimming and size selection of the reads (fragments >200 bp). The reads were assembled using Unicycler v0.4.4 [10]. BayesHammer [11] was used to apply error correction to the reads and SPAdes v3.13.0 [12] was used for the assembly using a k-mer value up to 121. DFAST [13] is used to check the quality of the genome and QUAST v5.1.0 [14] was used for the summary statistics of the genome. CGView server (https://www.cgview.ca) was used to generate a circular map of the bacterial genome [15] (Fig. 1). We used Prokka v1.14.6 to annotate the genome and for gene prediction [16]. We have assessed the genome quality using CheckM, which revealed a low contamination level of 0.2 % and high completeness scores of 100 % and 99.6 % based on BUSCO analysis.

Phylogenetic tree construction

A similarity search was conducted using the 16S rRNA gene sequence taken from the strain CM000152 with the help of the EzBioCloud server (www.ezbiocloud.net) against a database of validated type strains. The 16S rRNA gene sequences of closely related taxa were taken from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). The MUSCLE algorithm was used to align all the sequences in the MEGA X software [17]. For whole-genome-based taxonomic analysis, genome-to-genome distances (GGDs) and digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) values were calculated using the Type Strain Genome Server (TYGS). FastME software was used to construct a phylogenetic tree based on the whole-genome blast distance phylogeny (GBDP). The tree was midpoint rooted to depict the evolutionary relationships among strains [18]. The Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) calculated using MUMmer and BLAST, and the Average Amino Acid Identity (AAI) were determined using the JSpeciesWS platform (https://jspecies.ribohost.com/jspeciesws/) and the Enveomics Science Gateway (https://enveomics.scigap.org/).

Ethics Statement

The ethics committee approval was obtained from the institution to study this strain isolated from the patient sample. All the work in this study was done in compliance with the Institutional Biosafety Committee. Whole Genome Sequencing was outsourced to Medgenome Labs Ltd, India. All bioinformatics analysis was done in-house using a custom analysis pipeline as described in the methods section. Our work does not involve human subjects, animal experiments, or any data collected from social media platforms.

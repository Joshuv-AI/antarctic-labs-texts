Immediate risk and 5-year cumulative risk of high-grade cervical lesions in high-risk HPV-positive patients with minor cytological abnormalities: a retrospective single-center study in China
Authors: Linghua Kong, Xiaoping Xiao, Ru Wan, Shuanzheng Zhao, Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough minor cytological abnormalities predict a low risk of high-grade lesions, their high reporting rates lead to a considerable number of high-grade lesion cases. We carried out this study to analyze the immediate risk and 5-year cumulative risk of high-grade cervical lesions in high-risk human papillomavirus (Hr-HPV)-positive patients with minor cytological abnormalities and to investigate the clinical significance of minor cytological abnormalities during follow-up in our single-center.MethodsA total of 1892 patients with positive Hr-HPV, cytology result of atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance (ASC-US) or low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL) and also underwent colposcopy and biopsy were selected to analyze the immediate risk of high-grade cervical lesions. Besides, a total of 832 patients with baseline histological results of CIN1 or below and 5-year follow-up data available were further used to analyze the 5-year cumulative risk of high-grade cervical lesions.ResultsThe immediate incidence rates of CIN3 + in the ASC-US and LSIL groups were 6.27% (63/1005) and 5.64% (50/887), respectively. When CIN3 + was used as the study endpoint, the multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that there was no significant difference in either the immediate risk or the 5-year cumulative risk of CIN3 + between the ASC-US and LSIL groups.ConclusionsIn summary, since both the immediate and 5-year follow-up risks for CIN3 + were similar in patients with ASC-US and LSIL, routine follow-up should be performed in minor cytological abnormalities, regardless of whether the cytology result is ASC-US or LSIL. Through the risk assessment of Hr-HPV and cytology combined screening, the 2019 ASCCP guidelines were suitable for cervical cancer screening at our single center.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14972-6.Keywords: Minor cytological abnormalities, High-risk human papillomavirus (Hr-HPV)-positive, High-grade cervical lesions, Immediate risk, 5-year cumulative risk

Background

Cervical cancer is the most prevalent gynecological malignant tumor, with an estimated 660,000 new cases and 350,000 deaths worldwide in 2022, greatly affecting women’s health [1]. However, cervical cancer stands out as the sole malignant tumor with a well-defined etiology, the persistent infection of high-risk human papillomavirus (Hr-HPV) is a crucial prerequisite for the development of the majority of cervical cancers [2, 3]. Therefore, cervical cancer is preventable and curable as long as it is detected early and managed effectively. In 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) launched the Global Strategy for Cervical Cancer Elimination by 2030. A total of 194 countries worldwide, including China, collectively committed to eliminate a cancer for the first time [4].

Cervical cytology and Hr-HPV testing are currently the main methods for cervical cancer screening [5, 6]. The 2019 ASCCP guidelines quantified and refined the risk assessment, introduced the concept of “threshold”, and utilized the immediate risk of cervical intraepithelial neoplasm grade 3 and above (CIN3+) as a foundation for clinical management strategies, while the follow-up strategy was based on the five-year cumulative risk [7]. Atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance (ASC-US) and low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL) are the most commonly reported minor cytological abnormalities, with documented prevalence rates of 3%−5% and 1.5%−3.0%, respectively [8–11]. While the cytological detection rate of high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) was only 0.6% [8–11]. Although both ASC-US and LSIL predict a low risk of high-grade lesions [7, 10, 12], their high reporting rates lead to a considerable number of high-grade CIN cases confirmed by biopsy and histology (CIN2 and above, CIN2+). Therefore, this study analyzed the immediate and 5-year cumulative risks of CIN2 + and CIN3 + in patients with Hr-HPV infection and cytological ASC-US or LSIL. The aim of the study was to investigate the clinical significance of HPV-positive with minor cytological abnormalities, and to evaluate whether their risks of CIN3 + falls within the risk range established by the 2019 ASCCP guidelines.

Methods

The design

A total of 72604 patients who underwent ThinPrep® cytologic test (TCT; Hologic, USA) and HPV test in the gynecological clinic of Peking Union Medical College Hospital between February 2017 and January 2019 were included in the study. Of 72,604 screened patients, 9,074 (12.5%) showed abnormal cytology, including 8,181 with minor abnormalities (4,984 ASC-US [6.86% of total cohort; 54.9% of abnormal cases] and 3,197 LSIL [4.40% of total cohort; 35.2% of abnormal cases]. Inclusion criteria: (1) Age more than 18 years old; (2) TCT results were minor abnormalities, including ASC-US and LSIL; (3) Positive HPV test results; (4) Colposcopy and biopsy were performed and had histopathology results. Exclusion criteria: (1) Previous cervical lesions and cervical cancer history; (2) History of cervical surgery. Finally, 1892 patients with cytological ASC-US/LSIL, available baseline HPV-positive results and histological findings were used to evaluate the immediate risk of high-grade cervical lesions. Patients with baseline histological findings of CIN1 or lower were further underwent a 5-year long-term follow-up, 832 cases with available follow-up data were included in the analysis to determine the cumulative incidence rate of subsequent high-grade lesions (Fig. 1).

The flowchart of the study

The formula used for calculated the sample size was as follows:

N: sample size; π: expected incidence rate, with the expected incidence rate of CIN2 + set at 15% and that of CIN3 + set at 4%;δ: allowable error, the critical value for clinically meaningful differences in incidence rates, set at 5%; u1−α/2: two-tailed right-tail percentile of the standard normal distribution curve corresponding to α; two-tailed α = 0.05 was adopted;

Considering factors such as loss to follow-up and non-response, an additional 20% was incorporated into the calculated minimum sample size to ensure an adequate number of valid research participants. Consequently, the minimum sample size required for CIN2 + was determined to be 235, while that for CIN3 + was approximately 71. The sample size utilized in this study meets these minimum requirements.

TCT

Exfoliated cervical cells at the squamo-column junction were collected by using a ThinPrep cytology sampling brush, which was rotated five turns in the same direction. The collected cells were preserved in Preserve Cyt cell preservation solution and processed using ThinPrep® 2000 cell preparation system. The cytological interpretation criteria adopted the 2014 Bethesda System (TBS) grading system recommended by the International Cancer Society [13].

Hr-HPV DNA testing

The sample used for Hr-HPV DNA testing was obtained from the same liquid-based cytological specimen from ThinPrep. Hr-HPV DNA testing was conducted using the Cobas 4800 HPV test system (Roche, United States), consisting of the Cobas x 480 DNA extractor and the Cobas z 480 DNA amplification device. High-risk HPV types 16 and 18, in addition to 12 other high-risk HPV types (HPV other types), were detected using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification. The other types of HPV including HPV31, HPV33, HPV35, HPV39, HPV45, HPV51, HPV52, HPV56, HPV58, HPV59, HPV66 and HPV68.

Histopathological examination

The histopathological findings were acquired through colposcopic biopsy at our hospital and were collaboratively reviewed by two general pathologists, with a third arbitrator help for resolving diagnostic discrepancies. The diagnostic criteria were based on the fifth edition of the WHO Classification of Female Genital Tumors [14]. The classification encompasses inflammation, CIN1, CIN2, CIN3, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and adenocarcinoma (AC). High-grade cervical lesions include CIN2+ (consisting of CIN2, CIN3, SCC and AC) and CIN3+ (consisting of CIN3, SCC and AC).

5-year long-term follow-up

A 5-year retrospective follow-up was conducted for patients diagnosed with CIN1 or lower following their first colposcopic biopsy. The follow-up process involved in-hospital system inquiry and telephone follow-up, which were carried out with the patient’s oral informed consent. Institutional electronic record systems were used for in-hospital system inquiry after institutional office automation application and ethical approval. Histopathology results, HPV testing results and TCT results were observed during 5-year long-term retrospective follow-up: (1) both of HPV testing result and TCT results were negative without histopathological results, CIN2 + was considered to be absent during follow-up; (2) either HPV or TCT result was positive and histopathology result was CIN1 or below, indicating that there was no CIN2 + during follow-up; (3) both of HPV testing result and TCT results was positive and histopathology result was CIN1 or below, indicating that there was no CIN2 + during follow-up; (4) If the histopathological results showed CIN2+, the observation was discontinued, irrespective of HPV and TCT results. Excluding patients receiving cervical physical therapy. The median follow-up duration was 60 months.

Statistical methods

SAS 9.4 software was used for statistical analysis of the data. The statistical data were represented by the number of cases (n) and percentage (%), and the comparison between groups was conducted by χ2 test. The measurement data conforming to normal distribution were represented by mean ± standard deviation, the comparison between groups was represented by t test, the measurement data with non-normal distribution was represented by median and quartile, and the comparison between groups was performed by Wilcoxon rank sum test. Logistic regression and COX regression were used to analyze the immediate risk and 5-year cumulative risk of high-grade lesions in patients with minor cytological abnormalities in Hr-HPV infection. The model’s goodness-of-fit and predictive ability were evaluated using the events-per-variable (EPV) ratio and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with area under the curve (AUC) values. The calculation method of EPV is the ratio between the number of “events” that occurred and the number of “variables” included in the model. It can be used to determine whether the model is at risk of overfitting. Generally, a value ≥ 10 is considered favorable, while a value < 5 is not recommended. AUC refers to the Area Under the ROC Curve, which is used to evaluate the discriminative ability of the model. Its value ranges from 0 to 1, with a value closer to 1 indicating better performance. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics approval statement

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (approval number No. I-24PJ1033) and was in compliance with the institutional and national policies concerning research approvals.

Results

Characteristics of the participants

A cohort of 1892 patients with available baseline HPV results, minor cytological abnormalities and histological findings were used to evaluate the immediate risk of high-grade cervical lesions. The mean age of the participants was 41.81 ± 11.21 years. A higher percentage of patients were found to be infected with HPV other types (66.3%) at baseline compared to those infected with 16/18 types (33.7%).

Patients with baseline histological findings of CIN1 or lower were further underwent a 5-year long-term follow-up, 832 cases with available follow-up data were included in the analysis to determine the cumulative incidence risk of high-grade lesions, the mean age was 41.03 ± 10.18 years. There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between those who completed the follow-up and those who were lost to follow-up (Table S1). The number of patients in the < 40 years group was comparable to the ≥ 40 years group (429 cases vs. 403 cases). Additionally, more patients were infected with HPV other types (72.6%) at baseline compared to 16/18 types (27.4%). The proportion of baseline cytological results was slightly higher in the ASC-US group (57.1%) than in the LSIL group (42.9%). Except for HPV genotypes, there were no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the baseline patients and the 5-year follow-up patients. See Table 1.

Immediate risk analysis results for CIN2 + and CIN3 + at baseline

Immediate incidence of CIN2 + and CIN3 + in patients with mild cytological abnormalities

Among a cohort of 1892 patients exhibiting minor cytological abnormalities, the immediate incidence rates of CIN2 + and CIN3 + were 23.41% (443/1892) and 5.97% (113/1892), respectively. The immediate incidence rates of CIN2 + in the ASC-US and LSIL groups were 20.10% (202/1005) and 27.17% (241/887) (P < 0.01), respectively. The immediate incidence rates of CIN3 + in the ASC-US and LSIL groups were 6.27% (63/1005) and 5.64% (50/887), respectively, (P > 0.05). Besides, the immediate incidence of CIN2 + in the HPV other + group was 17.13% (215/1255), while in the HPV 16/18 + group, it was 35.79% (228/637) (P < 0.01). The immediate incidence of CIN3 + was 3.67% (46/1255) in the HPV other + group and 10.52% (67/637) in the HPV 16/18 + group (P < 0.01). Additionally, the immediate incidence of CIN2 + in < 40 years old and ≥ 40 years were 24.27% (226/931) and 22.58% (217/961), respectively (P > 0.05). The immediate incidence rates of CIN3 + in < 40 years old and ≥ 40 years were 6.01% (56/931) and 5.93% (57/961), respectively (P > 0.05).

Univariate logistic regression analysis for CIN2 + and CIN3 + at baseline

The logistic regression analysis included the age, genotype of HPV infection, and TCT results of 1892 patients with minor cytological abnormalities as single factors. The results showed that the immediate risk of CIN2 + and CIN3 + in the HPV 16/18 + group was 2.70 times (95% CI 2.17–3.36, P < 0.05) and 3.09 times (95% CI 2.10–4.56, P < 0.05) higher, respectively, compared to the HPV other + group. The immediate risk of CIN2 + in the LSIL group was 1.48 times higher than that in the ASC-US group (95% CI 1.20–1.84, P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in the immediate risk of CIN3 + between the ASC-US and LSIL groups (P > 0.05). In addition, no statistically significant difference was observed in the immediate risk of CIN2 + and CIN3 + between the < 40 years group and the ≥ 40 years group (both of P > 0.05), as shown in Table 2.

5-year follow-up of patients with baseline CIN1 or below

5-year Cumulative Incidence of CIN2 + and CIN3+

Among the 832 patients with baseline colposcopic biopsy results indicating CIN1 or below, the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN2 + and CIN3 + were 8.65% (72/832) and 2.88% (24/832), respectively. The 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN2 + in < 40 years group and ≥ 40 years group were 4.90% (21/429) and 12.66% (51/403), respectively, demonstrating a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05), while no statistical difference was found in 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN3+. Then, the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN2 + was 6.62% (40/604) in the HPV other + group and 14.04% (32/228) in the HPV 16/18 + group. Additionally, the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN3 + was 1.99% (12/604) in the HPV other + group and 5.26% (12/228) in the HPV 16/18 + group, the differences exhibited statistical significance (P < 0.05). For minor cytological abnormalities, the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN2 + in the ASC-US group was 8.00% (38/475), while in the LSIL group, it was 9.52% (34/357). The 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN3 + in the ASC-US group was 2.95% (14/475) and in the LSIL group was 2.80% (10/357), without statistical significance (P > 0.05). See Table 4.

Discussion

Cervical cancer is the most prevalent malignant tumor of the female reproductive system, with the majority of cases infected with Hr-HPV [15–18]. Characteristics of HPV infections, including HPV type and the duration of infection, determine a patient’s risk of CIN3+. The progression from precancerous lesions to cervical cancer typically requires approximately 5 to 10 years [19, 20].

Cytology plays a crucial role in the screening for cervical lesions. Based on morphological interpretation criteria, ASC-US refers to cytologic changes that are suggestive of LSIL but which are insufficient for a definitive interpretation as such, whatever in qualitatively or quantitatively [13]. From a clinical significance perspective, ASC-US indeed predominantly represents reactive changes, as only 10%−20% of ASC-US cases with immediate incidence of HSIL (CIN2/3), while 80%−90% of ASC-US cases demonstrate normal, inflammatory changes or CIN1 [21–25]. In our study, the immediate incidence rates of CIN3 + in the ASC-US and LSIL groups were 6.27% (63/1005) and 5.64% (50/887), respectively, without statistically significant difference. Besides, the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN3 + in the ASC-US group was 2.95% (14/475) and in the LSIL group was 2.80% (10/357), with no statistically significant difference. It’s worth noting that the 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN3 + in minor cytological abnormalities patients with baseline histopathological results being CIN1 or below was as low as 2.88%, which was within the risk range reported by ASCCP guidelines (2.1%−3.2%) [7], indicating that the ASCCP guidelines were applicable in our single center. Although there are slight differences in morphological interpretation between ASC-US and LSIL, with CIN3 + as the study endpoint, ASC-US is of similar clinical significance to LSIL, which is suitable to be managed with the same follow-up strategy as LSIL, adhering to the principle of “equal risk, equal management”.

Xu et al. [26] conducted a retrospective analysis that revealed the immediate risk of CIN2 + for ASC-US and LSIL was 18.58% (274/1475) and 16.62% (188/1231), respectively. In this study, the immediate risk of CIN2 + for ASC-US and LSIL groups was 20.10% (202/1005) and 27.17% (241/887), respectively, with a statistically significant difference. The accuracy of our data was close to the benchmark range acknowledged by cytology laboratories globally, which showed that the immediate incidence of CIN2/CIN3 in ASC-US was ranged from 10 to 20% and in LSIL it was ranged from 15 to 25%, and also meeting the internationally recognized cytology quality control requirements [21–25]. In the baseline, the Multivariate Logistic regression analysis showed that the immediate risk of CIN2 + in the LSIL group was 1.65 times higher than that in the ASC-US group (P < 0.05), while the Univariate Logistic regression analysis indicated that the immediate risk of CIN2 + in the LSIL group was 1.48 times (95% CI 1.20–1.84) higher than that in the ASC-US group (P < 0.05). At the endpoint of CIN2+, the baseline risks of ASC-US and LSIL were different, LSIL demonstrated a significantly higher immediate risk of CIN2+ (1.48-fold). It’s worth mentioning that while the “equal risk, equal management” principle applies to long-term CIN3 + outcomes, differences in short-term CIN2 + risk may still justify modified clinical approaches. In fact, ASCCP guidelines do not fully equate the management of ASC-US and LSIL, LSIL with HPV-positive more often warrants colposcopy, whereas ASC-US may allow for surveillance in certain cases. Our data also supports the guideline’s perspective.

However, out of the 832 patients with baseline colposcopic biopsy findings showing CIN1 or below, the following 5-year cumulative incidence of CIN2 + in the ASC-US group was 8.00% (38/475), whereas in the LSIL group, it was 9.52% (34/357). Unlike the immediate risk of CIN2 + discussed above, the 5-year cumulative risks of CIN2 + between ASC-US and LSIL were without significant difference. The possible reason maybe attributed to the accurate grasp of cytological interpretation criteria, which revealed a significant difference in the risk of CIN2 + between ASC-US and LSIL. While in the course of standardized screening, HPV infections of these low-risk individuals gradually clear over time, accompanied by a reduced incidence of CIN2 progression and a certain regression rate of CIN2, the 5-year cumulative risks of CIN2 + between ASC-US and LSIL were without significant difference.

Additionally, the immediate incidence of CIN2 + and CIN3 + in the HPV 16/18 + group was 35.79% (228/637) and 10.52% (67/637), respectively. And the multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that the immediate risk of CIN2 + and CIN3 + in HPV 16/18 + group was 2.85 times and 3.09 times, respectively, when compared to the HPV other + group (P<0.05), which was similar with the study of Lin et al. [19] and Arbyn et al. [27]. These results indicated that the current practice of directly referring patients with initial HPV 16/18 + for colposcopy is aligned with 2019 ASCCP guidelines. Besides, in the study conducted by Kang et al. [28], the 5-year long-term follow-up of 2880 patients with ASC-US and histologically confirmed CIN1 and below showed that the 5-year cumulative risk of CIN2 + in the HPV 16 + group was 2.431 times that of the non-HPV 16 + group (P < 0.01). Our data also showed that HPV16/18 + group had a higher immediate and 5-year cumulative risk than HPV other + group; however, due to the significantly higher prevalence rate of HPV other + than HPV 16/18 + in the population, among the 24 patients who developed CIN3 + over a cumulative period of 5 years, the number of patients testing positive for HPV other types was equal to those testing positive for HPV 16/18, with 12 patients in each group. Hence, it is crucial not to overlook the risk posed by HPV other-positive patients during opportunistic screening. This result was consistent with the ASCCP guidelines [7], which suggested that individuals with baseline CIN1 and below were not differentiated by baseline HPV infection type during follow-up.

According to previous literature, elderly patients exhibit a lower clearance rate of HPV, with age being identified as one of the factors influencing the development of high-grade lesions [28]. In this study, the average age of patients was 41.81 ± 11.21 years and the number of patients in < 40 years and ≥ 40 years was exactly equal. Based on the data characteristics and previous literature [28, 29], our study established the age of 40 years as the threshold for assessing the influence of age in the risk of CIN2 + and CIN3 + by using real raw data. The findings indicated that there was no statistically significant variance in immediate risk between the two age cohorts, while significant difference in the 5-year cumulative risk of CIN2 + was observed, and no difference was found in the 5-year cumulative risk of CIN3+. The potential explanation for this phenomenon could be attributed to the elevated regression rate observed in CIN2 compared to CIN3 [30]. This higher regression rate may result in an accumulation over time, ultimately leading to a convergence in the cumulative risk of CIN3 or higher. Advanced age was associated with impaired HPV clearance and increased CIN2 + incidence, likely due to hormone-related immune suppression [31], the high regression rate of CIN2 and attenuated progression to CIN3 ultimately resulted in comparable 5-year cumulative risks of CIN3 + between the two age groups.

Both univariate and multivariate analyses showed no significant differences were found in the immediate risk and 5-year cumulative risk for CIN3 + between ASC-US and LSIL, which should be managed and followed up with the same clinical strategy. Besides, the immediate and 5-year cumulative risks for CIN3 + in HPV16/18 + patients differed significantly from those of HPV other+, however, HPV other + is also worthy of attention and cannot be ignored due to its higher prevalence in the population and the inadequate coverage of cervical cancer screening in our nation. Our data indicated that the risk of CIN3 + for HPV-positive patients with minor cytological abnormalities falls within the risk range established by the 2019 ASCCP guidelines, which indicated that the ASCCP guidelines are applicable for the management of patients with histological findings of less than CIN1 at our single center during follow-up.

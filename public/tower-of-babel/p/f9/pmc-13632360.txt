Enhancing patient care with AI agents: integrating advanced chatbot technologies for improved healthcare delivery
Authors: Yasmine Abu Adla, Ahmad El Hajj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare presents significant opportunities to enhance patient care and streamline medical workflows. However, challenges related to accuracy, reliability, and accessibility continue to limit the widespread adoption of AI-driven medical assistants. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to integrate multiple advanced AI techniques into a unified system designed to provide real-time, context-aware medical insights while ensuring accuracy, engagement, and interpretability. This study aims to develop an AI-powered medical agent capable of assisting both patients and healthcare professionals by generating informed medical responses, automating healthcare-related tasks, and improving patient interaction through interactive and reliable assistance. The methods employed in this research include Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for contextualized medical responses, the Wikipedia API for real-time knowledge retrieval, and knowledge graphs for mapping symptoms to potential diseases. Additionally, a symptom checker tool enables preliminary diagnosis and personalized health recommendations using a prompt-based system. The system was evaluated through automated performance assessments and expert reviews. The results demonstrate that the knowledge graph tool achieved 95% accuracy in medical query responses, showcasing its reliability in symptom-disease mapping. Additionally, sentiment analysis of patient interactions reached 97% accuracy, reinforcing the system’s ability to understand and respond empathetically. The Wikipedia-based retrieval system maintained an average response time of 10 seconds, ensuring real-time applicability. Furthermore, 100 medical experts rated the AI agent’s responses 4.6 for comprehensiveness, 4.5 for engagement, and 4.5 for empathy and tone on a 5-point scale. These findings suggest that integrating advanced AI techniques enhances the accuracy, responsiveness, and contextual relevance of AI-driven medical agents. This scalable and reliable system presents a viable solution to reduce healthcare workload, enhance patient engagement, and democratize access to trusted medical information, reinforcing its potential as a transformative tool in modern healthcare.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has brought transformative changes across various industries, with healthcare being one of the most promising beneficiaries. AI technologies have the potential to revolutionize patient care by providing innovative solutions that can alleviate the challenges faced by healthcare systems, such as workforce shortages, rising costs, and the need for continuous patient engagement Topol [1]. In this context, AI-driven chatbots, or AI agents, are emerging as vital tools capable of supporting both patients and healthcare professionals by automating routine tasks, enhancing communication, and facilitating access to medical information Joshi and Sharma [2].

Furthermore, recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of explainable AI in healthcare applications, enabling more interpretable decision-making processes for critical tasks such as lung cancer detection, breast cancer classification, and cardiac disease prediction [3, 4, 5]. These advancements in AI-driven diagnostics and predictive modeling highlight the growing role of AI in enhancing medical accuracy, improving patient outcomes, and supporting clinical decision-making Rasool et al. [6], Wani et al. [5].

AI chatbots have been deployed in healthcare for a range of purposes, including mental health support Miner et al. [7], Molli [8], Casu et al. [9], symptom checking, and appointment scheduling Cao and Wang [10]. These applications demonstrate the potential of AI-driven tools to empower patients and streamline healthcare services. Moreover, studies have shown that AI chatbots can significantly reduce the burden on healthcare providers, allowing them to focus on more critical tasks that require human expertise Bates and Sheikh [11], Molli [8], Casu et al. [9].

Natural language processing (NLP) is a fundamental component of AI chatbots, enabling them to understand and respond to patient queries in a conversational manner. Recent advancements in NLP, such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation, have further improved the ability of AI agents to retrieve relevant information and provide accurate, context-sensitive responses Lewis et al. [12]. RAG integrates the strengths of information retrieval and natural language generation, making it a powerful tool for medical chatbots that require real-time access to vast amounts of health-related information.

In addition to RAG, knowledge graphs are being used to enhance the functionality of AI agents by mapping patient symptoms to potential diseases Hogan et al. [13]. Knowledge graphs provide a structured representation of medical knowledge, enabling AI agents to infer relationships between symptoms, conditions, and treatments. This capability is particularly useful for developing sophisticated symptom checkers, which can offer users potential diagnoses and health advice based on their symptoms Zhou et al. [14].

Despite the advancements in AI-driven healthcare solutions, traditional healthcare chatbots face several critical limitations that hinder their effectiveness in real-world medical applications Fadhil and Schiavo [15], Xue et al. [16]. One major limitation is the lack of context awareness. Current systems often struggle with contextual reasoning and dynamic information retrieval, leading to inaccurate and generic responses. Most conventional healthcare chatbots operate using rule-based or retrieval-based models, which lack the ability to understand user intent beyond pre-defined scenarios. This rigidity prevents them from dynamically adjusting to complex medical cases that require deeper contextual reasoning.

Another significant shortcoming is their limited medical information synthesis. Regular chatbots struggle to integrate and synthesize diverse medical knowledge from multiple sources, such as research articles, patient histories, and real-time clinical updates. As a result, they fail to provide comprehensive, evidence-based recommendations, leading to oversimplified or inaccurate responses Laymouna et al. [17].

Additionally, many chatbots rely on static and predefined knowledge bases. These fixed medical databases do not update dynamically with new treatments, evolving disease patterns, or emerging research. This restricts their ability to support real-time, data-driven decision-making for healthcare professionals and patients Fadhil and Schiavo [15].

Limited personalization is another drawback of traditional healthcare chatbots. They often deliver generic responses that do not take into account individual patient history, symptoms, or medical background. This lack of personalization reduces their ability to offer tailored guidance and impacts user trust in AI-assisted healthcare Seitz [18].

Furthermore, conventional chatbots suffer from a lack of interpretability and transparency. They typically do not explain how they arrive at their conclusions, making their responses opaque to both medical professionals and patients. This black-box nature raises concerns regarding trust, accuracy, and clinical reliability, preventing widespread adoption in critical healthcare settings Kakolu and Faheem [19].

To address these limitations, we propose an AI-driven medical agent designed to enhance patient care and support healthcare professionals by integrating advanced AI techniques. This study specifically tackles the challenge of delivering accurate, context-aware healthcare advice by incorporating knowledge graphs and RAG to improve decision-making and real-time information retrieval. By leveraging these technologies, our system delivers dynamic, evidence-based insights, ensuring that medical responses remain contextually relevant and aligned with the latest advancements in healthcare.

A key component of our approach is RAG, which enables real-time information retrieval from updated medical sources, ensuring that responses are both accurate and evidence-based. Additionally, the integration of knowledge graphs enhances structured, context-aware medical reasoning, allowing for more precise disease-symptom mapping and personalized recommendations tailored to individual patient needs.

Our AI agent also incorporates a symptom checker tool, which utilizes a prompt-based AI model to dynamically evaluate patient inputs. By considering real-time contextual information, this tool enhances the accuracy of preliminary diagnoses and health advice, offering users a more responsive and intelligent healthcare assistant.

By integrating these state-of-the-art techniques, our AI agent surpasses traditional chatbots by dynamically adapting to real-time medical advancements, providing context-aware insights, and ensuring transparency in AI-driven decision-making. This advancement bridges the gap between automation and trustworthy AI-assisted healthcare, offering a scalable, adaptive, and reliable solution for modern medical applications.

To ensure robustness, reliability, and contextual relevance, our AI agent undergoes comprehensive evaluation, incorporating both automated performance assessments and expert human reviews. Through these rigorous validation methods, the system is designed to deliver accurate, engaging, and medically sound responses. Ultimately, this study demonstrates the potential of AI-driven medical agents to enhance patient care, streamline medical consultations, foster patient engagement, and contribute to better health outcomes while reducing the workload on healthcare professionals.

Related work

Medical chatbots and AI integration

The rapid advancement of AI technologies has revolutionized various sectors, including healthcare. Among these innovations, medical chatbots have emerged as transformative tools with the potential to significantly enhance patient care and support medical professionals. Medical chatbots leverage AI to simulate human-like interactions, offering patients immediate assistance and providing healthcare professionals with support in routine tasks. These AI-driven agents hold promise for reducing healthcare costs, improving patient engagement, and delivering consistent medical information to patients.

Medical chatbots integrate a range of advanced AI components, including knowledge graphs, RAG, logical inference, and fine-tuned conversational models. Knowledge graphs are particularly powerful for structuring and interrelating medical information, enabling chatbots to understand complex relationships between diseases, symptoms, treatments, and medications. By utilizing established medical databases, such as Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) Clinfowiki [20] and Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine Clinical Terms (SNOMED CT) National Library of Medicine [21], knowledge graphs create a structured framework that significantly enhances a chatbot’s diagnostic accuracy and reliability. This structured approach has been shown to be highly beneficial, particularly in handling intricate medical scenarios where accurate and contextually relevant information is crucial for effective healthcare outcomes.

Large language models for healthcare

Recent studies have explored a variety of LLM applications in healthcare, such as medical question-answering, clinical decision support, and patient interaction. For example, the MedPaLM2 model has been found to outperform previous versions in medical question answering, achieving significantly higher accuracy in practical applications Karabacak and Margetis [22], Wu et al. [23]. ChatGPT and related LLMs have also been utilized for document generation in electronic medical records and have been proposed for use in clinical decision-making contexts Chen et al. [24], Haemmerli et al. [25]. Zhou et al. highlighted the development and use of an LLM specifically aimed at providing clinical guidance in diagnostic settings Zhou et al. [26]. The recent ClinicBench framework further evaluated the clinical capabilities of LLMs, emphasizing their role in improving clinical problem-solving tasks, including treatment recommendations and patient education Li et al. [27].

Additionally, several recent reviews have highlighted the critical importance of addressing biases and hallucinations in LLMs, which are challenges that need to be resolved for more trustworthy healthcare applications. For example, a comprehensive review by Zhao et al. provides insights into debiasing techniques and solutions for hallucinations in LLM-generated content Zhao et al. [28]. This helps to ensure that healthcare systems employing LLMs maintain accuracy and fairness.

Recent studies have also highlighted the growing use of AI in pharmaceutical research and diagnostics. AI has become instrumental in accelerating drug discovery and improving disease diagnosis accuracy using various AI techniques such as machine learning and deep learning Ma et al. [29], Owasis and Nithya [30]. In addition, systematic literature reviews have provided extensive insights into the use of AI for disease diagnostics and impact assessments in healthcare, highlighting the potential and challenges involved in leveraging AI technologies for clinical decision support Hassanzadeh and Atyabi [31], Owasis and Nithya [30].

Another key technology used in medical chatbots is RAG, which combines the strengths of information retrieval and text generation. In healthcare contexts, RAG models allow chatbots to provide responses that are not only coherent but also grounded in reliable medical knowledge. By retrieving relevant documents from large corpora and generating responses based on this retrieved content, RAG-enhanced chatbots can deliver more accurate and informative responses compared to traditional generative models. This ensures that the information provided to users is of high quality, evidence-based, and reflective of the latest medical knowledge Perception and PRIME [32], Wang et al. [33].

Logical inference also plays a critical role in advancing the capabilities of medical chatbots. By integrating inference mechanisms, chatbots can reason about medical information and draw conclusions based on the available evidence, thereby handling more complex diagnostic queries and providing personalized treatment advice. This enhances the chatbot’s functionality in offering comprehensive clinical decision support Longo et al. [34], Schulte [35]. Additionally, the use of fine-tuned conversational models, trained on extensive medical datasets, has allowed for the development of more sophisticated agents capable of nuanced, context-aware interactions. These models enhance the chatbot’s ability to understand specific medical inquiries and respond in a way that fosters meaningful patient engagement, a key factor in improving healthcare experiences.

Several studies have also discussed the role of conversational AI models like MOSS and ChatGPT in transforming healthcare interactions. These models can understand and generate responses to diverse healthcare queries, effectively supporting both patients and healthcare professionals. The MOSS open conversational LLM, for example, is designed to facilitate multi-turn conversations and has shown promise in healthcare contexts Zeng et al. [36]. Similarly, a comprehensive study on ChatGPT discusses its role in enhancing user engagement and providing a reliable conversational partner in healthcare Cheyer et al. [37].

Robots and autonomous agents are increasingly playing critical roles in healthcare, from surgical assistance to rehabilitation and autonomous diagnostics. Surgical robots, such as the da Vinci Surgical System, are widely used to perform minimally invasive surgeries, while rehabilitation robots like Lokomat and HAL are used to assist in patient rehabilitation Troiano and Nolan [38]. Autonomous agents have also been leveraged in multi-agent systems to coordinate healthcare activities, optimize resource allocation, and improve overall care efficiency Kerkmann and Rothe [39].

Challenges in AI-driven healthcare chatbots

Despite these advancements, several challenges remain that must be addressed to fully harness the potential of medical chatbots. Ensuring the accuracy and reliability of chatbot-generated medical advice is paramount. This necessitates rigorous validation, ongoing updates to the underlying medical knowledge, and adherence to strict ethical guidelines. Data privacy, ethical considerations, and regulatory compliance also present significant barriers to the widespread adoption of these technologies in healthcare settings Meyer et al. [40], Milano et al. [41], Raza et al. [42], Zhou et al. [43, 44], Zeng et al. [45]. Moreover, the personalization of medical advice remains limited, with many existing chatbots providing general information without considering individual user preferences or specific circumstances. Personalized advice is particularly important in cases where patient history and context are critical for delivering reliable recommendations. Addressing these limitations requires the integration of more sophisticated algorithms and adaptive learning techniques, allowing chatbots to tailor responses more closely to individual patient needs Chow et al. [46], Sarraju et al. [44].

Comparison of existing and proposed medical chatbot studies

A critical limitation of current AI-driven medical chatbots is their inability to process and correlate multimodal medical data, such as electronic health records (EHRs), real-time wearable sensor data, lab results, and historical patient information. Most existing systems rely on pre-defined rule-based approaches or static databases, failing to dynamically adapt recommendations based on a holistic view of the patient’s condition. This results in generic, one-size-fits-all responses that do not capture individualized health contexts or evolving medical conditions over time.

Furthermore, the lack of a robust knowledge graph means that relationships between different medical entities—such as symptoms, diseases, treatments, and patient-specific attributes—are not effectively captured. Traditional knowledge graphs used in healthcare chatbots are often manually curated, making them prone to outdated information and missing critical relationships. These limitations restrict chatbots from performing context-aware reasoning, an essential capability for complex decision-making in clinical applications.

Additionally, existing models fail to incorporate causal inference and reasoning-based decision support, which are crucial for predicting disease progression, identifying potential complications, and tailoring personalized treatment recommendations. Without these capabilities, chatbots cannot weigh multiple patient-specific factors to recommend contextually relevant, evidence-based interventions. The absence of these advanced inference mechanisms significantly reduces the chatbot’s reliability in real-world medical decision-making scenarios.

Another major gap is the inability of current chatbots to integrate real-time, continuously evolving medical knowledge. Given the rapid advancements in medicine, treatment protocols and best practices frequently change. However, most AI-driven medical chatbots rely on pre-trained models or static knowledge bases, limiting their ability to dynamically update responses based on the latest clinical guidelines, ongoing medical trials, and emerging research findings. This constraint poses a significant challenge in critical care applications, where up-to-date medical knowledge can be a determining factor in patient outcomes.

Moreover, existing healthcare chatbots often lack adaptive learning mechanisms, meaning they do not improve over time based on user interactions. Unlike human clinicians who refine their expertise through experience, chatbots fail to learn from past cases, patient feedback, or real-world medical trends, leading to repetitive errors and suboptimal recommendations. Incorporating continuous learning and reinforcement learning techniques could enhance chatbot reliability and enable personalized, evolving healthcare interactions.

Addressing these limitations requires a paradigm shift in chatbot architecture, moving beyond simple retrieval-based models to integrated AI frameworks that combine real-time data processing, knowledge graphs, causal reasoning, and adaptive learning techniques. Future advancements should focus on multi-agent AI systems, where specialized AI components collaboratively analyze patient data, retrieve relevant knowledge, and generate informed medical recommendations. Without these improvements, AI-driven healthcare chatbots will remain limited in scope, accuracy, and practical usability in clinical environments.

Therefore, the proposed study aims to significantly extend the capabilities of medical chatbots by integrating advanced decision-making features and delivering actionable healthcare advice. By incorporating cutting-edge technologies such as knowledge graphs, RAG, logical inference, and fine-tuned conversational models, this approach is designed to offer a more holistic and sophisticated range of diagnostic and advisory services.

The use of knowledge graphs is central to enhancing the chatbot’s reasoning capabilities. Knowledge graphs provide a structured representation of medical information, capturing complex interrelationships between diseases, treatments, symptoms, and medications. However, a significant limitation of knowledge graphs is their inherent incompleteness and biases, affecting their utility in critical applications like healthcare Zeng et al. [52]. By using established ontologies, such as the UMLS or SNOMED CT, the chatbot can create a rich semantic context that significantly enhances its diagnostic accuracy. The combination of knowledge graphs with RAG enables the chatbot to leverage both structured knowledge and unstructured information from large medical corpora, providing responses that are both evidence-based and context-specific. However, challenges like redundant content retrieval and issues with semantic integrity in fine-grained retrieval units are notable drawbacks of using RAG in complex systems Gao and Smith [53]. Additionally, constructing reliable healthcare knowledge graphs requires effective entity extraction and relation linking, ensuring semantic accuracy across diverse medical domains Abu-Salih et al. [54].

Additionally, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first time a mix of AI agents has been integrated in such a manner to address different specialized aspects of healthcare comprehensively. The proposed system integrates a mixture of AI agents, each specialized in a different aspect of healthcare. For example, one agent may focus on analyzing patient-specific medical history, while another handles real-time retrieval of medical literature, and yet another performs logical inference based on clinical guidelines. This multi-agent approach enables parallel processing and specialization, thereby increasing the efficiency and reliability of the chatbot’s responses. Each agent contributes its specialized knowledge, which is then combined to generate a holistic and comprehensive recommendation for the user. This framework is instrumental in breaking down complex medical problems into smaller, more manageable components, each handled by an expert agent, and then aggregating these insights for a well-rounded decision.

Dynamic querying plays a crucial role in ensuring that the chatbot’s responses are current and accurate. Unlike static systems that rely on pre-existing datasets that may become outdated, dynamic querying allows the proposed system to access real-time information. This means the chatbot can retrieve the latest medical guidelines, newly published research, and even patient-generated data, such as wearable device metrics, ensuring that the advice provided is always up-to-date and relevant. For instance, the chatbot can perform dynamic queries against clinical trial databases to determine the most recent treatment protocols for a specific condition. By dynamically querying both structured and unstructured data sources, the chatbot maintains a high level of adaptability and accuracy.

Moreover, by leveraging a knowledge graph combined with logical inference, the proposed system is capable of advanced contextual reasoning. For instance, when presented with a patient’s symptoms and medical history, the chatbot can navigate the graph to identify possible comorbidities and recommend a customized treatment plan. This capability significantly outperforms traditional retrieval-based systems that often fail to consider the broader medical context, such as overlapping symptoms and interactions between concurrent treatments.

The integration of these advanced tools—knowledge graphs, RAG, logical inference, and a mixture of specialized AI agents—provides a comprehensive and dynamic decision-support environment. The proposed system is not only capable of responding to user queries but can also proactively guide patients through decision pathways, recommend next steps, and ensure follow-up actions are personalized and evidence-based. These enhancements have the potential to greatly improve both the quality of care and the level of patient engagement, ultimately leading to more accurate diagnoses and better health outcomes.

Comparison of AI-Driven healthcare chatbots

By examining these key factors, it becomes evident how our approach addresses gaps in the current technologies and offers a more robust, dynamic, and context-aware solution. The current state-of-the-art AI-driven healthcare chatbots like MedPaLM and Ada Heath primarily rely on LLMs to generate medical responses Jungmann et al. [56], Singhal et al. [55]. MedPaLM, developed by Google and DeepMind, leverages LLMs trained on vast medical literature. While it offers detailed medical information, its dependence on pre-trained databases limits its capacity to provide real-time responses or adapt to new medical knowledge. Similarly,Ada Health uses a bounded LLM focused on accuracy through peer-reviewed medical journals, but it lacks the ability to interact dynamically with user-specific medical contexts or perform real-time knowledge retrieval.

In contrast, our chatbot incorporates RAG in combination with knowledge graphs to generate context-aware medical responses. This integration of real-time knowledge retrieval using the Wikipedia API ensures that the system is constantly updated with the latest information, allowing it to respond accurately to user queries. Furthermore, unlike other chatbots that rely heavily on probabilistic reasoning, our solution uses knowledge graphs to map symptoms to potential diseases with higher precision, facilitating more personalized and accurate assessments.

Most existing chatbots, such as Ada Health and Babylon Health, leverage curated medical knowledge bases or user interactions to assess symptoms and provide diagnoses. Ada Health, for instance, uses a curated knowledge base developed over a decade, and Babylon Health combines conversational AI with medical guidelines for virtual consultations and symptom checking. However, these systems often rely on static databases or narrow functionality, such as symptom-based assessments or administrative tasks, limiting their ability to adapt to new conditions or provide holistic healthcare support.

Our chatbot, on the other hand, integrates a variety of data sources, including real-time knowledge retrieval systems (e.g., Wikipedia API), knowledge graphs, and a symptom checker tool that assists in both diagnosis and personalized health recommendations. The combination of these data sources enables our chatbot to not only respond to medical queries but also to provide preliminary diagnoses based on user-reported symptoms, offering a more comprehensive and dynamic healthcare experience.

The aims of existing healthcare chatbots vary, with some focusing on reducing administrative workload (e.g., Abridge automates clinical documentation) and others on enhancing patient engagement or providing symptom assessments (e.g., Ada Health and Babylon Health). While these systems address specific needs, they are limited in scope and functionality. Abridge, for example, focuses on automating documentation and does not provide real-time diagnostic support or interactive engagement with users.

In contrast, our chatbot aims to reduce healthcare workload, enhance patient engagement, and democratize access to reliable medical information. By leveraging advanced NLP techniques and combining them with real-time data sources, context-aware symptom analysis, and interactive health recommendations, our chatbot offers a more sophisticated and scalable solution for modern healthcare needs. Additionally, the integration of symptom checkers and knowledge graphs enables our chatbot to deliver high accuracy in disease mapping, a critical advantage over systems relying solely on probabilistic reasoning or static knowledge bases.

Dataset pre-processing

Effective data preprocessing is critical to ensure the datasets are clean, consistent, and ready for model training, particularly in enhancing the Large Language Model (LLM) for medical dialogues. The following preprocessing steps were undertaken:

Data Cleansing: Initially, all unique characters in the datasets were identified to detect and remove anomalies, such as non-standard symbols and unexpected characters. This step ensured the integrity and quality of the text data, minimizing any potential interference during the training phase.

Normalization: The dialogue texts underwent normalization by removing unnecessary tabs, new lines, and carriage returns, thereby maintaining a consistent format throughout. Dialogue roles (e.g., “Patient” and “Doctor”) were identified, corrected for inconsistencies, and standardized to facilitate accurate mapping and analysis of the doctor-patient interactions.

Dataset Conversion: Finally, the preprocessed dialogues were converted into a JSON format suitable for model training. Each conversation was structured to include a base system prompt, followed by individual messages from the patient and doctor. This conversion prepared the dataset for fine-tuning a variant of the GPT model, allowing it to effectively understand and replicate medical dialogues.

Enhancing GPT for healthcare interactions

Fine-tuning a pre-trained language model for a specific domain is crucial for enhancing its contextual relevance, accuracy, and overall interaction quality. In this study, the primary goal of fine-tuning was to refine the chatbot’s contextual understanding, natural language generation, and the incorporation of medical domain knowledge.

To achieve this, the MTS-Dialog dataset was employed to train the model on realistic medical dialogues Abachaa [60]. This dataset provided the necessary domain-specific information to address several key challenges, such as accurately understanding medical contexts, generating coherent and fluent responses, and ensuring precise use of medical terminology. This targeted approach ensured the chatbot could generate reliable and contextually appropriate responses tailored to healthcare interactions.

The fine-tuning process consisted of data preprocessing, model training, and evaluation. We selected the gpt-3.5-turbo-0125 model for fine-tuning due to its extensive training capacity and context length of 16,385 tokens, which were well-suited to the study’s requirements. Although newer models with longer context capabilities became available during development, the decision to proceed with gpt-3.5-turbo-0125 ensured stability and alignment with the study timeline. The training configuration involved 1,506,135 training tokens, 5 epochs, a batch size of 16, and a learning rate multiplier of 2.

In addition to these configuration choices, we also set several important model parameters. The temperature was set to 1.0, which balances randomness and coherence in responses. Top-k was set to 0, meaning it was disabled by default, as this allows the model to consider all tokens without limiting itself to the most likely ones. We used a top-p value of 1.0 for nucleus sampling, allowing the model to evaluate all probable tokens, thus enhancing the diversity of the generated responses. The maximum tokens were capped at 4096, which varies by model, to determine the maximum length of the output generated by the model. The repetition penalty was set to 1.0, meaning no extra penalty was applied for repeated tokens. Additionally, the presence penalty and frequency penalty were both set to 0.0, meaning there was no discouragement for introducing new topics or for frequently occurring tokens.

These parameters were carefully chosen to balance effective learning, maintain model stability, and mitigate overfitting, achieving optimal model generalization.

The model’s learning progress was tracked through a training loss curve, depicted in Fig. 2. Initially, the training loss was high, reflecting the model’s adjustment to domain-specific medical data. As training progressed, the loss consistently declined, stabilizing at a final value of 1.0112, indicating satisfactory convergence. Retrieval-Augmented generation for hospital reviews

Retrieval-Augmented Generation is an innovative technique that enhances language models by combining the strengths of information retrieval and natural language generation. The primary benefit of RAG lies in its ability to retrieve relevant information from a large corpus and use that context to generate accurate and informed responses. Traditional language models, despite their capacity to process extensive datasets, often face limitations regarding outdated or incomplete information and challenges in accessing real-world knowledge. These limitations can result in inaccuracies, which are especially critical in medical contexts.

To address these challenges, we implemented RAG using a dataset comprising hospital reviews. By retrieving contextually relevant information from this dataset, the model can generate responses informed by real patient experiences, thereby enhancing the quality and accuracy of its advice. This integration ensures that users receive comprehensive and reliable information, leading to more informed healthcare decisions.

The RAG workflow involves three main stages: ingestion, retrieval, and synthesis, as illustrated in Fig. 4. During the ingestion phase, documents are split into smaller chunks, converted into vector embeddings, and stored in an index. The retrieval phase involves searching the index based on a user query to identify the most relevant chunks. Finally, during the synthesis stage, the selected chunks are used by the language model to generate a coherent response, ensuring the output is both informative and contextually appropriate.Fig. 4Overview of the retrieval-Augmented generation (RAG) process

Overview of the retrieval-Augmented generation (RAG) process

Document processing and embedding generation

In our implementation, each row of the hospital reviews dataset represents an independent review, which is preserved as a distinct chunk to maintain its context. Document chunking is thus simplified, as no predefined function is needed to split the data. To convert these chunks into vector embeddings, we employed the text-embedding-ada-002 model, known for its robustness in capturing the semantic meaning of text Nussbaum et al. [63]. These embeddings allow the chatbot to perform similarity searches and retrieve the most relevant content to answer user queries accurately. The model is cost-effective, with a pricing structure of $0.100 per 1,000,000 tokens, suitable for processing large volumes of text OpenAI [64].

Vector storage and retrieval

The generated embeddings are stored in Chroma, an open-source vector database selected for its scalability and seamless integration capabilities. Each embedding is stored along with metadata, including document ID and chunk position, enabling efficient retrieval. The use of Chroma ensures rapid access to the most relevant reviews based on their semantic content, thereby enhancing the chatbot’s response accuracy and depth of information Chroma [65].

Wikipedia API integration

To provide detailed and up-to-date information on medical topics, we integrated the Wikipedia API into our system. This allows the model to dynamically access a broad and evolving source of general medical knowledge, improving its ability to handle a wide range of queries.

However, recognizing that Wikipedia content may vary in reliability, we apply a basic filtering mechanism to ensure the credibility of retrieved information. Specifically, we favor articles with a high number of citations, prioritize pages labeled as Good Article or Featured Article, and exclude entries flagged with warning tags such as “multiple issues” or “needs additional citations.” Preference is also given to well-referenced sections citing reputable external sources.

Furthermore, all retrieved Wikipedia information is cross-validated against validated clinical guidelines and peer-reviewed medical sources. In cases of conflict, the system prioritizes the knowledge graph content over Wikipedia-derived information.

Wikipedia content is therefore used as a supplementary source to enrich user interactions, without serving as the sole basis for clinical decision-making. This design ensures that the system maintains high standards of accuracy, reliability, and safety in medical information delivery.

To further augment user interaction, we implemented a symptom checker tool using GPT-4 and a customized prompt template. This tool allows users to input their symptoms and receive information on potential conditions and recommended actions. By leveraging the model’s extensive knowledge base and reasoning capabilities, the symptom checker provides users with relevant and timely health information, empowering them to make informed decisions about their health.

To improve efficiency and reduce latency, we implemented a caching system that stores previously retrieved responses for frequently asked queries. This system works by maintaining a local cache where responses are temporarily saved. When a user submits a query, the system first checks if a cached response exists. If a match is found, the cached result is instantly retrieved, significantly reducing the need for redundant requests. Otherwise, the system fetches fresh data from Wikipedia, stores it in the cache for future use, and then delivers it to the user. By leveraging this caching mechanism, the average response time was reduced to 3 seconds, ensuring a more responsive and seamless user experience while maintaining the accuracy and comprehensiveness of the retrieved information.

This improvement is especially beneficial for high-traffic scenarios where multiple users may inquire about similar topics, as it ensures rapid access to critical information without sacrificing accuracy.

Discussion and insights

The experimental results demonstrate that integrating multiple advanced tools—such as the knowledge graph, Wikipedia tool, symptom checker, and review retrieval mechanism—substantially enhances the system’s ability to deliver precise, relevant, and user-specific medical information. Each tool plays a distinct yet complementary role in addressing different aspects of user queries, leading to a more comprehensive and well-rounded healthcare support system.

The knowledge graph proved particularly valuable in handling structured and complex queries, offering precise, interconnected information about symptoms, diseases, and patient profiles. This tool enabled the system to retrieve detailed relationships between various medical entities, such as identifying symptoms associated with a specific condition or patients displaying certain symptoms. Its ability to accurately process structured data made it an indispensable component for medical decision-making and data retrieval.

The Wikipedia tool, on the other hand, was highly effective in addressing general medical inquiries with comprehensive, up-to-date information. Its ability to access a vast database of medical knowledge ensured that users received detailed, reliable answers to questions about treatments, diagnoses, and medical conditions. The rapid response times of the Wikipedia tool further enhanced the system’s real-time usability, making it an efficient resource for general health information.

The symptom checker provided users with actionable, contextually relevant health advice based on their symptom input. By calculating probabilities for various conditions (such as the common cold, flu, or COVID-19), the tool not only suggested potential diagnoses but also recommended appropriate next steps, such as testing or medical consultation. This feature empowers users to make informed decisions about their health, enhancing the system’s role as a proactive healthcare assistant.

The review retrieval tool added another layer of functionality by analyzing real patient feedback and performing sentiment analysis. This tool’s ability to categorize reviews into positive, neutral, or negative sentiments offered valuable insights into patient experiences and highlighted areas for potential improvement in healthcare services. Its high accuracy in sentiment analysis ensures that feedback is correctly interpreted, making it a useful tool for healthcare providers looking to enhance patient satisfaction.

One of the standout features of the system was the integration of a historical context mechanism, which played a crucial role in maintaining the continuity of user interactions. In healthcare settings, where user queries often build on prior information, this feature ensured that the system provided more context-aware and relevant responses. By leveraging previous interactions, the system could offer coherent, sequential guidance, which is especially important in addressing follow-up medical queries or ongoing health concerns.

The integration of these tools and features has resulted in a more robust, dynamic, and reliable AI medical assistant that is capable of addressing a wide spectrum of healthcare needs. The system’s ability to handle both structured and unstructured queries, coupled with its contextual awareness, significantly enhances patient engagement and satisfaction. Additionally, the system’s capability to deliver personalized, accurate, and timely information makes it a promising solution for practical healthcare applications. This AI-driven platform has the potential to revolutionize patient support by improving access to information, facilitating informed decision-making, and providing ongoing health guidance, thus aligning with the growing demand for intelligent, responsive healthcare systems.

Limitations

While this study presents an AI-driven medical agent that provides accurate and contextually relevant responses, several limitations should be considered. These limitations primarily concern the data, scalability, and future enhancements of the system.

1. Data Size and Representativeness: The dataset used for fine-tuning the model was relatively small, with 1,506,135 training tokens. While this allowed us to test and fine-tune the model effectively, it may not be representative of the vast diversity of medical conditions and patient demographics. The limited scope of the dataset means that the AI agent may not capture all variations in patient profiles, which could affect the generalizability of its responses.

2. Continuous Fine-Tuning: As medical knowledge evolves rapidly, continuous fine-tuning of the model is necessary to keep it updated. The current model is based on data available at the time of training, and it may not fully account for newer guidelines, research, or emerging medical conditions. Ongoing fine-tuning is essential to ensure the system remains accurate and relevant over time.

3. Scalability and Infrastructure: The current implementation of the system was run on a regular computer. To scale the system for high-demand scenarios, dedicated servers and powerful GPUs are required. The lack of a robust infrastructure could limit the system’s ability to handle large volumes of concurrent user interactions effectively, and scalability remains a key challenge for future deployments.

4. Multilingual Support: The current version of the AI agent operates primarily in English, limiting its accessibility for non-English-speaking users. In future iterations, incorporating multilingual translation capabilities will be crucial to expand the reach of the system and make it available to a wider user base.

5. Multimodal Support: At present, the system supports text-based interaction only. However, integrating multimodal capabilities such as voice-based interaction or data from wearable health devices would enhance the system’s usability and provide a more holistic view of a patient’s health. These features are essential for improving the overall user experience and ensuring comprehensive patient care.

Despite these limitations, the primary aim of the study was to develop an AI agent capable of delivering precise and accurate medical insights. We achieved this goal, and the aforementioned limitations are areas of future work aimed at enhancing the system’s functionality, scalability, and inclusivity.

Conclusion and future work

This study introduced an AI-driven medical assistant system that integrates multiple advanced AI tools, including a knowledge graph, Wikipedia API, symptom checker, and review retrieval mechanism, all coordinated by a dynamic query management agent. The results demonstrate that this multi-component approach significantly enhances the system’s ability to provide precise, relevant, and contextually informed medical responses. By dynamically selecting the most suitable tools based on user queries, the system ensures efficient information retrieval and decision support, fostering improved patient engagement and assisting healthcare professionals in making more informed decisions.

This research makes notable contributions to the intersection of AI, healthcare, and information retrieval systems. Theoretically, it extends prior work on intelligent medical assistants by demonstrating how a multi-agent, tool-integrated approach can enhance the contextual relevance and accuracy of medical responses. The incorporation of knowledge graphs for structured reasoning and RAG for real-time information synthesis represents a novel combination that improves medical query handling. Additionally, the study contributes to AI-based healthcare decision support by exploring how dynamic query orchestration optimizes AI resource utilization.

From a practical perspective, the system provides a scalable and adaptable solution for patient self-diagnosis, healthcare decision support, and medical information retrieval. Unlike conventional symptom checkers or static chatbots, this system dynamically integrates real-world patient reviews and external knowledge sources, allowing it to offer more holistic and context-aware recommendations. Furthermore, its ability to maintain conversational history enhances continuity in patient interactions, reducing redundant questioning and improving the overall user experience.

The proposed system holds several practical advantages over existing AI-driven medical assistants. First, it improves medical knowledge accessibility, enabling patients and healthcare providers to obtain reliable, real-time, and well-contextualized medical information. Second, the personalized symptom checker enhances preliminary self-assessment, allowing users to gain health insights before consulting a physician. Additionally, healthcare professionals can leverage the system for rapid knowledge retrieval, assisting them in making informed clinical decisions. Lastly, the integration of real patient reviews provides valuable community-driven insights that could help both patients and doctors gauge treatment efficacy based on real-world experiences.

Despite its strengths, this study has certain limitations. First, the system relies on publicly available medical knowledge sources (such as Wikipedia), which may not always contain the most specialized medical information. While knowledge graphs help structure and validate responses, they are limited by the quality of the underlying datasets. Second, the absence of multimodal data processing (e.g., medical imaging or wearable sensor data) restricts the system’s ability to handle cases where visual diagnosis is crucial. Additionally, while the system provides context-aware responses, it does not yet incorporate long-term patient history or evolving health conditions, which could further improve personalized recommendations. Finally, privacy and ethical concerns surrounding AI-driven medical advice warrant further research, particularly in terms of data security, misinformation risks, and regulatory compliance.

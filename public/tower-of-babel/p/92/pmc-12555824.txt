Pediatric chronic myeloid leukemia: A decade of clinical experience at the NBK specialized hospital for children
Authors: Maha Bourusly, Mohammad Adil Obaid, Nashwa Farag, Mona Bourhama, Sundos Alsharidah, Eman Almatar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPediatric chronic myeloid leukemia is rare and differs from adult disease. We retrospectively reviewed 12 children (median age 8.4 years, six males) treated with imatinib (340 mg/m²/day) from 2010 to 2020; dasatinib was used for intolerance. All presented with leukocytosis (median WBC, 358 × 109/L) and splenomegaly. Imatinib achieved complete hematologic response in 92% by 3 months and 100% by 6 months; major molecular response was observed in all evaluable cases at 12 months. Three who lost response switched to dasatinib and maintained remission; two attained treatment-free remission. TKIs are effective; prospective studies should optimize risk stratification and discontinuation.Keywords: Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), Children, Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, Imatinib, Treatment outcomes, Retrospective study

Introduction

Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a rare malignancy in children, accounting for approximately 2–3 % of all childhood leukemias [2,3]. The incidence rate in Kuwait is approximately 6 cases per 100,000 individuals [1], and globally it is 1–2 cases per million per year [1]. This rarity in the pediatric population poses challenges in understanding disease characteristics and optimizing treatment approaches.

CML is a malignant clonal hematopoietic disorder defined by the presence of the Philadelphia (pH) chromosome, which generates the constitutively active BCR-ABL1 fusion tyrosine kinase driving myeloid proliferation and genomic instability [1,4]. The disease progresses through three clinically defined phases—chronic phase (CP), accelerated phase (AP), and blast phase (BP) [3].

Pediatric CML often presents more aggressively than adult disease, with children demonstrating proportionally larger spleen sizes (median 8 cm below the costal margin; range 0–25 cm) [5] and higher baseline leukocyte counts (median 250 × 10⁹/L) [6]. Moreover, adult prognostic scoring systems (e.g., Sokal, Hasford, and EUTOS) have not been validated in pediatric patients, limiting accurate risk stratification in this population [[7], [8], [9]].

Historically, allogeneic stem cell transplantation (SCT) was the primary curative approach for CML; however, the introduction of BCR-ABL1–targeted TKIs such as Imatinib has revolutionized management by achieving durable cytogenetic and molecular remissions and markedly reducing transplantation rates [4]. Despite these advances, the optimal duration of TKI therapy in children, long-term safety profiles, and the applicability of adult-derived treatment algorithms remain unclear due to the scarcity of dedicated cohort studies [6,10].

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

We conducted a retrospective cohort study at the National Bank of Kuwait Specialized Hospital for Children—the sole national referral center for pediatric oncology in Kuwait—reviewing cases from January 2010 to January 2020.

Ethical approval

The protocol was approved by the Standing Committee for Coordination of Health and Medical Research and by the Pediatric Departments’ Council of the Kuwait Ministry of Health (Approval No. P116–13MK-01). All procedures conformed to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient selection

All patients aged 0 to 16 years diagnosed with chronic myeloid leukemia (CML)—confirmed by the presence of the Philadelphia chromosome and/or BCR-ABL fusion transcript—between January 2010 and January 2020 were eligible. We excluded those with incomplete records, concurrent malignancies, or primary immunodeficiency.

Data collection

Demographic (age, sex, nationality) and clinical data (date of admission; presenting symptoms; spleen size on ultrasound) were retrieved from our institutional CML registry. Baseline laboratory parameters included:

Treatment details (hydroxyurea, Imatinib dosing, leukapheresis) and longitudinal molecular monitoring (BCR-ABL levels at diagnosis; monthly for three months; quarterly through year two; then every six months until age 16) were recorded. Data entry and management were performed in IBM SPSS Statistics (v.25.0), with listwise deletion for missing values.

Treatment protocol

Great Ormond Street Hospital (GOSH) treatment recommendations were adopted at our center. Initial supportive therapy included hydroxyurea (25–50 mg/m²/day). Upon molecular confirmation of CML, Imatinib was introduced at 340 mg/m²/day while continuing hydroxyurea until leukocyte normalization. Indications for leukapheresis were WBC > 200 × 10⁹/L with leukostasis (e.g., pulmonary infiltrates, priapism, severe retinopathy/papilledema). Stable patients were transitioned to outpatient care for ongoing therapy and monitoring.

Response assessment

Treatment response was evaluated according to European LeukemiaNet criteria:

•Hematologic response: WBC < 10 × 10⁹/L; basophils < 5 %; platelets < 450 × 10⁹/L; no circulating myeloid precursors; non-palpable spleen.

•Cytogenetic response: complete (<1 %), major (1–35 %), minor (36–65 %), minimal (66–95 %).

•Molecular response: major molecular response (BCR-ABL/ABL < 0.1 % IS); complete molecular response (undetectable transcript in two consecutive samples).

Baseline risk stratification at diagnosis was performed using four validated scoring systems: the Sokal, Hasford (Euro), Sokal 2 [12], and EUTOS [13] scores. Continuous variables are summarized as mean ± SD or median (range), and categorical variables as counts (percentages).

Eleven patients (92 %) attained complete hematologic response by three months, and all were in hematologic remission by six months. Major molecular response was achieved in all patients at twelve months.

Three patients (25 %) subsequently lost molecular response and were transitioned to second-line therapy with Dasatinib; one of these had progressed to accelerated phase, and another switched due to Imatinib-related toxicity despite molecular remission. No patient required hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. At last follow-up, three patients had transferred to adult care, and two remained in sustained remission off therapy.

Statistical Analysis Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation or median (range), and categorical variables as frequencies (percentages). Overall survival (OS) and event-free survival (EFS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with comparisons by the log-rank test. Categorical comparisons utilized chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests as appropriate. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Kuwait’s centralized referral system for childhood leukemia ensures that all pediatric CML cases are managed at a single, highly specialized center, contributing to consistent diagnostic work-up and treatment approaches. Over the ten-year period, only 12 children were diagnosed with CML—approximately 2 % of all pediatric leukemia cases in Kuwait—underscoring the rarity of this disease in children [2,3].

In our cohort, the median age at diagnosis was 8.4 years, with an equal gender distribution. All patients presented with profound leukocytosis (mean WBC 358 × 109/L) and splenomegaly (mean 10.6 cm below the costal margin), features that are proportionally more aggressive than in adult CML cohorts [[7], [8], [9]]. One-third (33 %) of patients required urgent leukapheresis to mitigate leukostasis risk, and 75 % received hydroxyurea upon admission, highlighting the need for rapid cytoreduction in the pediatric setting.

First-line Imatinib produced rapid, durable responses: 11 of 12 (91.7 %) achieved Complete Hematologic Response (CHR) by three months, and all by six months. All evaluable patients attained a major molecular response (MMR) by 12 months, mirroring outcomes seen in adult trials and emerging pediatric series [5,6]. Nevertheless, three children (25 %) experienced molecular relapse and were transitioned to second-generation TKIs, and one patient progressed to accelerated phase, emphasizing the importance of close molecular monitoring and prompt therapeutic adaptation.

Treatment was generally well tolerated. Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most common reason for Imatinib modification [10]. Notably, two patients who transferred to adult care later discontinued therapy entirely and have maintained treatment-free remission, reflecting the unmet need to define safe criteria for treatment cessation in children.

Strengths of our study include its single-center design with uniform treatment protocols and comprehensive long-term follow-up. Limitations encompass its retrospective nature, small sample size, occasional missing data from paper records, and absence of a unified electronic follow-up system. Prospective, multicenter collaborations with standardized pediatric CML registries [11] are required to determine optimal TKI duration, develop pediatric-specific prognostic scores, and further refine strategies to minimize toxicity while maximizing long-term disease control.

Conclusion

In our single-center cohort of 12 children (median age 8.4 years), representing approximately 2 % of pediatric leukemia cases in Kuwait, first-line Imatinib achieved complete hematologic remission in 92 % of patients by three months and in 100 % by six months, with all evaluable patients reaching major molecular response by 12 months. Despite an overall favorable safety profile—gastrointestinal symptoms were the main cause for TKI modification—25 % experienced molecular relapse requiring second-generation TKIs and one progressed to accelerated phase, underscoring the need for rigorous molecular (BCR-ABL) monitoring. Notably, two patients maintained durable remission after planned TKI discontinuation following transfer to adult care. These results confirm Imatinib’s efficacy and tolerability in pediatric CML and support the establishment of prospective, multicenter registries to derive pediatric-specific prognostic models, optimize treatment duration, and define evidence-based guidelines for safe TKI cessation [11].

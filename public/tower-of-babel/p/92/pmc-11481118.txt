Metabolic and bariatric surgery for obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome: bibliometric analysis and visualisation from 1999 to 2023
Authors: Tao Jiang, Xuan Zhang, Hui Wang, Xianhao Yi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesMetabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS) for obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome (OSAS) has garnered significant research interest. The main aim of this study was to analyse the unique characteristics of publications centred around MBS for OSAS. We aimed to provide valuable insights into the present status of the field and offer predictions regarding future trends.DesignBibliometric analysis through science mapping and performance analysis methods.Data sourcesScientific articles.Data extraction and synthesisA comprehensive search was conducted in the Web of Science database to retrieve publications on MBS for OSAS within the time frame of 1999–2023. The search was performed using keywords such as “Obstructive Sleep Apnea syndrome”, “OSAS”, “metabolic surgery” and “bariatric surgery” to identify relevant papers. To analyse the bibliometric characteristics of these publications, VOSviewer and R software were used. This analysis encompassed various aspects, including the number of publications, contributions by different countries and institutions, the most cited author, journal publications, citation frequency and the identification of keywords related to popular scientific fields.Results1264 papers on MBS for OSAS were identified and are currently in a stage of rapid growth. The relevant study involved 1230 institutions from 53 different countries, with the USA topping the field with 465 publications contributed. Assistance Publique Hopitaux Paris proved to be the most productive institution, with De Vries N posting the highest number of papers (n=24). The most prolific journal was Obesity Surgery, with 241 publications. Keywords “Bariatric surgery”, “Obstructive sleep apnea”, “Obesity”, “Weight loss” and “Morbid obesity” have the highest frequency of occurrence.ConclusionsThe relationship between obesity and OSAS, various metabolic surgical techniques, and the mechanism of MBS for OSAS will continue to be related areas of frontiers. Furthermore, while there has been a gradual increase in basic research, the mechanisms involved and the long-term efficacy of bariatric MBS for OSAS still require further investigation. It is anticipated that these research areas may emerge as potential hot topics in the future.

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnoea-hypopnoea syndrome (OSAS) is characterised by recurrent interruptions of ventilation during sleep in the form of apnoeas or hypopnoeas. The prevalence of moderate-to-severe sleep-disordered breathing (≥15 events per hour) was found to be 23.4% in women and 49.7% in men.1 Several population-based studies have consistently demonstrated that OSAS significantly increases the risk of hypertension, cardiovascular disease and stroke, as well as metabolic and endocrine disorders.26

With lifestyle changes, exercise, medication therapy, metabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS), the symptoms of OSAS can be lessened. Compared with other methods, MBS can achieve greater relief from OSAS, as it can lead to more weight loss, especially in morbid stages of obesity. Although MBS can alleviate OSAS by reducing body weight and airway fat accumulation,7 there are still some unclear mechanisms. These are the questions that have been the subject of numerous studies. It is thus imperative that we remain current with the developments and emerging patterns in this domain. One technique to assess research hotspots on a given topic and pinpoint unexplored directions is to use bibliometric analysis, which is a quantification and visualisation method for published documents.8

Currently, a significant number of original articles and commentaries discussing MBS for OSAS have been published. However, there is a lack of comprehensive statistical and visual analysis on this topic. To address this gap, our study employs bibliometric analysis to provide a comprehensive perspective on MBS for OSAS. We conducted a statistical study of MBS-related articles in OSAS using the bibliometric analysis software VOSviewer to determine key research areas, emerging hotspots and potential development trends in the field. Specifically, we look at the increase in annual publications, international collaboration, author productivity, reference journals and co-occurrence analysis of MBS-related keywords for OSAS research.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

The bibliometric analysis used data from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database (https://www.webofknowledge.com). The search formula were as follows: (TS=(“OSAHS”) OR TS=(“Obstructive Sleep Apnea”) OR TS=(“OSAS”)) AND (TS=(“bariatric”) OR TS=(“Metabolic Surgery”) OR TS=(“SG”) OR TS=(“LSG”) OR TS=(“RYGB”) OR TS=(“LRYGB”) OR TS=(“bypass”) OR TS=(“laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy”) OR TS=(“sleeve gastrectomy”) OR TS=(“Roux en Y Gastric Bypass”) OR TS=(“LAGB”) OR TS=(“AGB”) OR TS=(“gastric band”)). Only publications before 2024 were included, and no limit on language was set.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria and screening strategy

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) literature published before 2024; (b) research on MBS for OSAS and (c) literature published in English. Duplicate publications and publications not related to subject were excluded. To ensure the reliability of the literature included, only sources from the WoSCC database were considered, which facilitated efficient subsequent data processing. The retrieved articles from the databases were exported to EndNote V.X9 (Thomson Reuters (Scientific) Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA) for further categorisation. Two researchers independently screened the literature by reading the titles and abstracts, and full texts were viewed when necessary so that all eligible literature of MBS for OSAS were included as much as possible. After the independent screening, two researchers cross-checked and discussed the disagreements with the third researcher and finally reached a consensus on whether the suspected articles should be included.

Data collection and analysis

We retrieved the complete dataset from WoSCC, which included information on the number of annual publications, publication outputs by countries/regions, journals, authors and total citations. To ensure standardised and harmonised analysis, we used the bibliometric package in R (V.4.3.0)9 to process and analyse publication details, such as publication year, country and author. Journal impact factors (IFs) were obtained from the 2023 Journal Citation Reports published by Clarivate Analytics on 28 June 2023. VOSviewer (V.1.6.18)10 was used to explore the relationships and network connections between different elements.

Patient and public involvement

No patients were involved in this study.

Results

General data

A total of 1270 publications were retrieved from the WoSCC database. Among them, 1264 articles were identified as relevant after the screening process (figure 1). Figure 2A displays the language distribution within the search results, highlighting English as the predominant language. English literature accounts for 96.75% of the total, while literature written in other languages, including German, French and Spanish, accounts for 1.02%, 0.95% and 0.79%, respectively. Among the disciplines, surgery has the highest publication volume, with 530 articles published from 1999 to 2023, representing 41.93% of the total publication volume. The General Internal Medicine discipline follows, with 140 articles and 11.08% of the total volume. Disciplines ranked fourth and fifth demonstrate similar publication volumes, contributing 9.18% and 8.23% of the total volume, respectively (figure 2B). According to these results, research on MBS for OSAS is mostly focused on surgery, but it is also taking a more diverse and multidisciplinary approach. 864 original papers, 263 reviews, 101 meeting abstracts, 22 letters and 16 editorial materials are among the publications that are covered (figure 2C). The quantity of publications is a broad measure of the scientific community’s interest in a given topic, including information about its research scope and developmental tendencies. Figure 2D shows the annual variations in papers published. It shows a modest trend of fluctuation between 1999 and 2002, which we designate as the early years of this field’s research. The field saw steady growth as it began its early stages of study from 2003 to 2023. These findings suggest that OSAS research in MBS is developing quickly at the moment.

Country collaboration

The implementation of metabolic surgery for OSAS was observed in 53 countries, demonstrating a broad geographical reach. The top 10 countries according to publications and citations are shown in online supplemental figure S1A–B. With 465 articles or 39.08% of the total, the USA contributed the most. Canada produced 67 papers (5.63%), and China came in second with 79 articles (6.64%). The USA had the highest number of citations (59.27%) in the citation study, with 22 684; Canada came in second with 2557 (6.68%) and Italy with 2091 (5.46%). The way the countries are distributed indicates how closely related and cooperative the major countries are in this domain.

In addition, the number of citations an article receives is a measure of a nation’s power in a certain field; the higher the citation count, the more significant the impact. VOSviewer was used to examine cross-border cooperation between nations. A map of collaboration among various countries is visualised and presented in online supplemental figure S2. The map shows 53 countries split up into 9 different clusters, interestingly, the USA’s collaborative network was the strongest, with a link strength of 1317. Canada ranked second with a total link strength of 579, ahead of the UK (556) and the Netherlands (340).

Institution collaboration

509 institutions (41.38%) contributed to a single publication, whereas 478 institutions (38.86%) published two or more articles, according to the examination of papers published by 1230 institutions. Furthermore, 114 universities (9.27%) produced more than 10 publications, while 129 institutions (10.49%) published 6–10 articles. Online supplemental figure S2C,D lists the 10 most prolific institutions in this subject. With 76 papers (1.37%) published, Assistance Publique Hopitaux Paris in France stood out as the most productive organisation. The University of Toronto followed closely after with 73 papers and Universite Paris Cite with 75.

A network cooperation study was done on 514 institutions with at least three papers, as shown in online supplemental figure S3. In the network visualisation, the research revealed eighteen cooperative groupings, each represented by a different colour cluster. Nodes in the cluster analysis visualisation with large-diameter circles and strong connecting lines suggested that institutions with a significant number of publications collaborated extensively with others. Notably, the University of Toronto (sky blue), Harvard University (brown) and Assistance Publique Hopitaux Paris (pink) all showed high betweenness centrality, indicating their important roles in promoting linkages within the network.

The MBS for OSAS research involved 5944 authors in all. 4974 writers (83.68%) of them only authored one article. Furthermore, 927 writers (15.60%) wrote between 2 and 5 pieces, 37 writers (0.62%) published between 6 and 10 articles and 6 writers (0.10%) produced 10 or more articles. Journal analysis

A total of 407 journals published 1264 articles on MBS for OSAS. Out of these, 128 journals (31.45%) published 2–5 publications, whereas 247 journals (60.69%) published just 1 article. Furthermore, 12 journals (2.95%) published more than 10 publications, whereas 20 journals (4.91%) published 6–10 articles. 39.72% (502/1264) of all publications were published in the top 10 most productive journals, as seen in table 2. With 241 publications (19.07%), Obesity Surgery published the most, followed by Sleep (28 articles, 2.22%), Surgical Endoscopy and Other Interventional Techniques (100 articles, 7.91%) and Surgery for Obesity and Related Diseases (33 articles, 2.61%). Among the top 10 journals, the average IF was 5.94. The journal with the highest IF, the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, had an IF of 24.7. Notably, the USA was home to the vast majority of active publications in this sector.

Keywords mapping

A total of 13221 occurrences were found in the 3128 keywords that were retrieved. Three distinct clusters, each represented by a different colour, were found in the cluster analysis of keywords that appeared at least five times (figure 3). The blue cluster examined the relationship between obesity and OSAS, the green cluster investigated the effects of various metabolic surgeries on OSAS, and the red cluster investigated postoperative management and possible mechanisms. The high-frequency keywords offer insightful information about the dominant subjects in the field. The top 20 MBS-related keywords for OSAS research are displayed in online supplemental table S1. Terms such as ‘obstructive sleep apnoea,’ ‘obesity’ and ‘body mass index’ were particularly common within the blue cluster. Keywords with high prevalence in the green cluster included ‘Y gastric bypass,’ ‘bariatric surgery,’ ‘weight loss,’ ‘sleeve gastrectomy’ and ‘morbid obesity.’ Last but not least, the red cluster included heavily represented terms such as ‘management,’ ‘metabolic syndrome’ and ‘positive airway pressure.’ For OSAS research, these clusters provide a visual depiction of important subjects and motifs in the field of MBS.

Discussion

Publication status

The global prevalence of obesity has significantly risen over the past four decades. In 1975, the prevalence was less than 1%, but by 2016, it had increased to 6%–8% among girls and boys and 3%–11% among men and 6%–15% among women.11 In 2004, a meta-analysis highlighted the therapeutic role of MBS in obesity-related complications, such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidaemia and hypertension. It demonstrated that bariatric operations, which are considered MBS, can effectively resolve or alleviate these conditions, often about the percentage of excess weight loss. This finding led to a surge of research in this field.12 During the transition from the mid-1990s to the early 2000s, MBS shifted from open surgery to laparoscopic surgery, which became widely accepted. Consequently, there was a significant increase in the number of MBS procedures performed worldwide.13 14 This rise in the incidence of obesity, coupled with the proven effectiveness of MBS, along with the adoption of minimally invasive surgical techniques, likely contributed to the accelerated adoption of weight loss surgery for OSAS in the early 2000s.

Apart from China and Australia, the majority of the top 10 countries in terms of publication numbers and citations in the field of OSAS research are from the USA and Europe. This could be attributed to the high prevalence of overweight and obesity in these regions, as OSAS is closely associated with these conditions. Large-scale epidemiological surveys15 have consistently revealed that the USA and Europe exhibit the highest rates of overweight and obesity. Approximately 30% of adults in the USA are classified as obese,16 which contributes to the country’s leading position in terms of publication numbers and citations in this field. Citations serve as an important measure of the academic impact of research papers, indicating the acceptance and recognition of a country’s scientific achievements by other nations and institutions.17 China, despite ranking second in terms of publication counts, currently ranks 10th in terms of citations, suggesting the need to strengthen its research capabilities and enhance the impact of its scientific contributions.

International cooperation in the field of OSAS research exhibits distinct regional characteristics. The USA, being the country with the highest number of publications, demonstrates strong collaboration with Brazil, followed by Canada. The UK closely collaborates with Spain and Sweden. These regional patterns highlight the imbalances in collaboration across different regions and countries. To overcome these disparities and promote a more inclusive global research community, it is imperative that developed and developing nations throughout the globe step up their cooperation and communication.

The institutional characteristics of national publications and citations align with the patterns observed at the country level. An analysis of institutions reveals that the top 10 institutions, in terms of publications and citations, are predominantly located in the Americas and Europe. The USA boasts the largest number of institutions among the top-ranked, highlighting the leadership of American institutions in this field. This observation also suggests a positive correlation between a country’s gross domestic product and the number of publications related to MBS.18 Institutional collaborations primarily occur within their respective countries. Examples of such collaborations include Assistance Publique Hopitaux Paris, Sorbonne University and Institute National de la Sante in France; Harvard University and the US Department of Veterans Affairs in the USA; and Ciber-Centro de investigacio and the University of Barcelona in Spain. Similar to the regional imbalances observed at the country level, disparities also exist at the institutional level across different regions and countries. Addressing these disparities and promoting a more inclusive and cooperative research environment need stepping up cooperation and communication between developed and poor nations globally.

Analysing the most influential writers may aid scholars in understanding current partnerships and identifying potential global cooperation areas. Price’s law19 states that the formula 0.749Maxp, where Max(p) is roughly equivalent to the number of publications written by the author holding the highest rank in the study field, can be used to calculate the number of essential authors (n). Price’s law states that authors who have authored at least 3.67 articles are considered significant and exceptional writers. The figures show that 147 writers were considered vital, with the top 4 contributors to this field being De Vries N, De Raaff CAL, Inge TH and Van Wagensveld BA. The main area of interest for Heit JA and associates was the perioperative management of OSAS in MBS.20 21 Furthermore, their study team’s investigations on the prognostic usefulness of different clinical markers and the occurrence of obstructive sleep apnoea in patients undergoing bariatric surgery attracted a lot of attention.21 Cocitation analysis, which is seen to be a better way to assess a journal’s or scholar’s academic influence,22 led to the development of an author cocitation network using VOSviewer. The authors’ lack of close collaboration is evident in online supplemental figure S4, which highlights the necessity for enhanced cooperation in future studies.

Based on a review of journals, the top three journals with the most articles in the subject are Obesity Surgery, Surgery for Obesity and Related Diseases, and Surgical Endoscopy and Other Interventional Techniques. Obesity Surgery is the official journal of the International Federation for the Surgery of Obesity & Metabolic Disorders and the British Obesity & Metabolic Surgery Society. Surgery for Obesity and Related Diseases is the official journal of the American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery, the Brazilian Society for Bariatric Surgery and the Asociacion Latinoamericana de Cirujanos Endoscopistas. Among the top 10 journals, 8 were affiliated with the USA. Significantly, despite China being the second-largest publishing country, no Chinese journals ranked among the top. This may be related to China’s late start and low fund investment in this area.23 Considering the large population of obese individuals with OSAS, China should pay attention to the development in this area and strive to establish internationally influential journals.

Research frontiers

The co-occurrence of keywords can provide insights into the search topic and reveal the underlying structure of references and emerging disciplines. Figure 3 illustrates the formation of three distinct clusters within the research theme of MBS for OSAS, where related keywords are grouped. By analysing the characteristics and current state of MBS for OSAS research, we can gain a deeper understanding of how these three clusters have emerged and evolved.

Cluster 1, represented by the colour blue, focuses on the relationship between OSAS and obesity. The keywords within this cluster, as depicted in figure 3, include ‘obstructive sleep apnoea,’ ‘obesity’ and ‘body mass index.’ Understanding the link between obesity and OSAS is crucial for comprehending why weight loss surgery can effectively treat OSAS. Obesity contributes to the development of OSAS through various pathways. First, it reduces the cross-sectional area of the upper respiratory tract by promoting the accumulation of fat in the pharyngeal wall. This fat deposition can exert external pressure and potentially block the airway.24 Furthermore, there is a positive correlation between body mass index (BMI) and the severity of OSAS, with an estimated 1% increase in BMI associated with a 3% increase in the apnoea-hypopnoea index (AHI).25 While awake, individuals with OSAS can compensate for airway obstruction by increasing the activity of upper airway muscles to maintain airway patency.26 However, during sleep, these muscles may relax and become desynchronised, which exacerbates airway obstruction. Additionally, obesity can lead to an increased volume of the tongue, causing the base of the tongue to collapse and obstruct the upper airway during sleep.27 28 Moreover, abdominal obesity, characterised by increased fat around the abdomen, is associated with a decrease in lung mass, particularly functional residual capacity. This reduction in lung capacity disrupts the longitudinal traction in the pharynx, indirectly causing upper airway instability and impairing the reflex mechanism of respiratory control.29 30

Conversely, weight loss has been found to have positive effects on OSAS. Foster et al conducted a study revealing that a 10% reduction in initial weight led to a 20% improvement in the AHI after one year.31 Additionally, several studies have demonstrated that interventions involving diet or a combination of diet and lifestyle modifications have resulted in average weight losses ranging from 3% to 18%. These weight loss interventions have corresponded to improvements in the AHI ranging from 3% to 62%.32 33 Although there are many studies on obesity and OSAS, the mechanism of their interaction and influence is still not very clear,34 which may be one of the hotspots for future research.

Cluster 2, represented by the colour green, revolves around keywords related to various metabolic surgical procedures, such as ‘bariatric surgery’, ‘sleeve gastrectomy’ and ‘Y gastric bypass’. Common weight loss surgical methods include gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy, gastric band and biliopancreatic diversion. Comparing the benefits and complications of surgery comprehensively, gastric band and biliopancreatic diversion are gradually being phased out, while gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy have shown significant improvements in inducing and maintaining weight loss.35 36 Gastric bypass typically yields more substantial weight loss and is often recommended for specific cases, such as patients with type 2 diabetes. In recent years, sleeve gastrectomy has gained popularity and has become a preferred surgical option.37 Therefore, the focus of the current article will primarily be on gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy, aligning with the findings of our analysis.

With advancements in MBS and a deeper understanding of OSAHS, both domestic and international guidelines have explicitly recommended preoperative screening and diagnosis of OSAS for patients.20 38 A meta-analysis conducted by Zhang et al39 demonstrated the effectiveness of MBS in alleviating OSAS and improving hypoxaemia. The analysis included 15 studies with a total of 636 patients. Following the procedure, there was an increase of 1.36 (95% CI (0.72, 2.00)) in the mean arterial oxygen saturation and 1.08 (95% CI (0.68, 1.49)) in the nadir oxygen saturation. Furthermore, there was a 1.11 (95% CI (0.82, 1.40)) and 1.97 (95% CI (1.67, 2.27)) decline in the AHI and BMI, respectively. Findings from a multicentre prospective study conducted by Peromaa-Haavisto et al40 revealed that the prevalence of OSAS decreased from 71% at baseline to 44% at 12 months after surgery (p<0.01). Furthermore, the study showed that 45% of patients achieved a cure for OSAS, while 78% experienced either a cure or improvement in their condition.

According to the available literature, there appears to be little difference in the effectiveness of sleeve gastrectomy and gastric bypass for treating OSAS. A national registry cohort study involving 4015 bariatric cases41 found that both gastric bypass (risk ratio 1.49, 95% CI 1.25 to 1.78) and sleeve gastrectomy (risk ratio 1.46, 95% CI 1.22 to 1.75) were associated with approximately a 50% increased likelihood of OSAS remission compared with gastric band surgery. In a retrospective analysis by Hariri et al,42 which included 297 obese patients diagnosed with OSA, the 1-year remission rates for sleeve gastrectomy and gastric bypass were 89.2% and 81.2%, respectively (p=0.22). Similarly, a meta-analysis conducted by Alghamdi et al,43 which examined 16 articles, indicated no significant difference in the efficacy of sleeve gastrectomy and gastric bypass for treating OSAS (OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.47 to 1.33, p=0.38).

Cluster 3, indicated by the colour red, focuses on keywords related to the mechanisms of MBS for OSAS, including ‘positive airway pressure,’ ‘metabolic syndrome,’ and ‘management.’ However, the precise mechanisms through which MBS improves OSAS are not fully understood and are likely the result of multiple mechanisms working in tandem. On one hand, the resolution of OSAS following MBS can be attributed to weight-related mechanisms. As overall weight and adiposity decrease, particularly the adiposity around the upper respiratory tract and abdomen, improvements in upper airway stenosis and chest wall compliance are observed. Consequently, postoperative respiratory function is significantly enhanced compared with preoperative levels.44 45 A meta-analysis conducted by Owenidat et al, which included 2310 OSAS patients from 32 studies,46 demonstrated that a substantial decrease in BMI (weighted mean difference (WMD)=−11.9, 95% CI −13.4 to −10.4), AHI (WMD=−19.3, 95% CI −23.9 to −14.6) and respiratory disturbance index (WMD=−33.9, 95% CI −42.1 to −25.7) was linked to bariatric surgery. Following surgery, the rate of OSAS remission was determined to be 65% (95% CI 0.54%, 0.76%).

In addition, the resolution of OSAS following bariatric surgery can also be attributed to weight-independent mechanisms.47 MBS has been shown to improve the body’s inflammatory response and oxidative stress, reducing the concentration of inflammatory mediators such as C reactive protein and IL-6, ultimately alleviating OSAS.48 Additionally, MBS reduces adipokine production in body fat, which may enhance neuromuscular control of the pharynx and have an effect on the central nervous system.49 Furthermore, patients who undergo MBS exhibit increased mitochondrial respiration in both cells and tissues compared with preoperative evaluations, which can help alleviate hypoxia symptoms in individuals with OSAS.50

Limitations

This study is subject to several limitations that should be taken into consideration. First, the analysis relied solely on relevant documents from the WoSCC database, which may introduce bias due to the exclusion of other databases such as Index Medicus, Index Copernicus, Google Scholar or Scopus, which encompass a wider range of documents and journals. The choice to use the WoSCC database was based on its reputation for reliable publication and citation information. Second, manual standardisation of some results was carried out prior to analysis in order to address differences in how the same notion was expressed. It is crucial to remember that this procedure might not completely remove errors—it can only lessen them. Third, to reduce bias coming from writers sharing the same name, careful attempts were undertaken to confirm the publications of the top 10 authors. Nevertheless, prejudice resulting from altered author affiliations or other factors could not be completely eliminated. As such, these limitations must be taken into account when evaluating the study’s findings.

Conclusion

We performed a thorough examination of the MBS literature for OSAS from 1999 to 2023 in this study. Our study covered a wide range of topics, such as nations, organisations, writers, journals of publishing, keywords and cocited references. This helped us spot patterns in the research and predict what the subject will look like in the future. With the most articles written, De Vries N from the Sint Lucas Andreas Hospital in the Netherlands was the most prolific writer among the authors. Notably, Obesity Surgery stood out as the leading publisher in this field, with a total of 241 publications. Based on our analysis, we predict that research on MBS for OSAS will continue to thrive, with a continued emphasis on exploring the relationship between OSAS and obesity, investigating different metabolic surgical procedures and elucidating the mechanisms underlying the effects of MBS on OSAS (figure 4). However, it is important to note that the long-term efficacy of MBS for OSAS remains uncertain and necessitates further high-quality research to establish its validity. We think that the research hotspots we discovered have the potential to provide important scientific advances shortly and that our findings offer insightful information on novel approaches to treating OSAS. In addition to pointing out the gaps and restrictions that now exist in MBS for OSAS research, this paper provides precise guidelines and suggestions for further research in this important area.

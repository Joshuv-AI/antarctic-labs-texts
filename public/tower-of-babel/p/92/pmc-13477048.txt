Innovations in hybrid rice seed production: Integrating agronomy, mechanization, and biotechnology
Authors: Yuntao Yan, Bin Lei, Yuanyi Hu, Ming Wang, Daoming Liu, Keyi Chen, Huimin Liu, Chanjuan Tang, Fan Li, Ning Tang, Yan Tian, Aimin Liu, Wenbang Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHybrid rice production makes use of heterosis to safeguard global food security. However, currently dominant three-line and two-line systems for seed production rely heavily on manual labor and are highly vulnerable to climate fluctuations. These limitations have become a significant bottleneck to the sustainability of rice production. To address this issue, we summarize recent advances and propose a novel “dual-track” analytical framework. The Proximal Track focuses on the integration of agronomy with machinery. It analyzes mechanized innovations in split- and mixed-planting models, evaluating mixed-sowing strategies based on targeted phenotypic sorting (e.g., for seed-coat color and grain thickness), selective herbicides, and gene editing (e.g., thermosensitive female sterility). The Distal Track explores molecular-driven breakthroughs. It characterizes the core advantages of third-generation seed production technology for unrestricted crossing and large-scale propagation of male-sterile lines, before analyzing the latest progress in “one-line” synthetic apomixis for permanent fixation of heterosis. Finally, we propose the creation of tailored parental chassis varieties suitable for mechanized cultivation and the introduction of AI-driven meteorological–crop models for the prediction of complex genotype-by-environment risks. Ultimately, this framework can guide a paradigm shift from “agronomic–mechanistic integration” to “reproductive pathway rewiring,” laying a foundation for the strategic leap from three-line and two-line systems to a one-line method.Key words: hybrid rice, seed production, dual-track framework, agronomic-mechanization, one-line system

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa) is one of the most widely consumed crops worldwide and serves as the primary staple food for more than half of the world’s population (Zeng et al., 2017). In China, rice feeds over 60% of the population and contributes ∼40% of the national total caloric intake (Cheng et al., 2007a). Hybrid rice offers a yield advantage of 10%–30% over its parental lines (Luo et al., 2013), helping to increase rice production and ensure food security (Zhou et al., 2012). Since the launch of the “China Super Rice Breeding Program” in 1996, improvements in ideal plant architecture and distant hybridization between indica and japonica subspecies have driven continuous increases in the yield potential of hybrid rice (Yuan, 2017; Deng et al., 2025). In 2022, the hybrid combination ‘Zhuoliangyou 1126’ reached an average yield of 18.17 t/hm2 in high-yield demonstrations in the indica rice regions of Sichuan, China (Liu et al., 2024). This milestone signified major progress in the yield potential and adaptability of China’s super rice varieties.

Since the start of the 21st century, widespread use of molecular biology has driven continuous improvements in hybrid rice yields. Significant progress has been achieved in pest and disease resistance (Kuai et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2025), heat and cold tolerance (Gao et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2025), and grain quality (Cao et al., 2025). Furthermore, continued innovations in the breeding of low-cadmium-accumulating (Yu et al., 2022) and salt-tolerant (Zhang et al., 2025) hybrid rice varieties demonstrate the power and adaptability of hybrid rice technology.

Rice is predominantly self-pollinating, and conventional rice cultivation therefore relies on self-fertilization. By contrast, the production of hybrid rice seed involves a male-sterile line as the maternal parent and a restorer line as the paternal parent. Lacking viable pollen, the maternal parent depends entirely on pollen from the paternal parent for successful seed set. Consequently, hybrid rice seed production is fundamentally an outcrossing cultivation system (Liu et al., 2023a). Owing to genetic segregation in subsequent generations, hybrid rice does not stably maintain heterosis, necessitating the annual renewal of F1 seeds (Moon et al., 2025). At present, hybrid rice is produced primarily through three-line and two-line systems. Three-line systems use sterile and maintainer lines for sterile-line multiplication, with restorer lines providing the genes required to produce hybrid seeds. Two-line systems leverage the characteristics of photo-thermosensitive genic male-sterile (PTGMS) lines, which become fertile only under specific temperature or light conditions, to facilitate sterile-line reproduction and hybrid seed production (Ma and Yuan, 2015; Wang et al., 2025) (Figures 1A and 2B). Large-scale production of hybrid rice seed necessitates precise agronomic manipulation to ensure the successful harvest of F1 seeds from male-sterile plants. This pipeline requires staggered sowing and transplantation to achieve floral synchrony between the parental lines, exogenous application of gibberellic acid (GA3) at the heading stage to promote maternal panicle exsertion, and supplementary artificial pollination to maximize outcrossing seed set. However, seed production based on these two systems faces practical challenges such as cumbersome procedures, high labor intensity, and considerable environmental dependence (Huang, 2022). Nearly all of the related agronomic practices—land preparation, transplantation, agrochemical application, continuous roguing throughout the vegetative and reproductive phases, removal of the male parent, and harvesting—rely predominantly on manual execution. This heavy reliance on manual labor inevitably increases production costs, reduces operational efficiency, and compromises seed quality (Liu et al., 2014). Such practical constraints highlight the inherent vulnerability of contemporary hybrid seed production frameworks.

To simplify the production of hybrid seed and ultimately enable the fixation of heterosis, academician Yuan Longping, the “Father of Hybrid Rice,” proposed a strategic roadmap for hybrid rice development: evolution from the three-line to the two-line system and ultimately to a one-line system that enables permanent seed propagation (Yuan, 1987) (Figure 1C). This vision has provided a theoretical foundation for continuous innovation in hybrid rice technology. Accelerating the transition to streamlined and mechanized seed production using existing systems, while striving for breakthroughs in one-line apomixis to enable permanent seed retention, has become the ultimate goal of technological innovation in hybrid rice, fulfilling the strategic legacy of Professor Yuan.

In this review, we detail innovative breakthroughs in the production of hybrid rice seed and propose a dual-track conceptual framework for industrial modernization. The first dimension, which we define as the “proximal track,” focuses on optimizing established three-line and two-line systems through agronomic–mechanization synergy, aiming to facilitate the transition to streamlined and mechanized operational workflows. Concurrently, the “distal track” explores molecular-driven advances, critically analyzing the potential and challenges of third-generation hybrid technologies and synthetic apomixis for heterosis fixation. By integrating these complementary trajectories, the review provides a strategic blueprint for the establishment of highly efficient, high-yielding, and resilient infrastructure for sustainable production of hybrid rice seed.

Proximal track: Agronomic–mechanization synergy

China’s foundational breakthroughs in hybrid rice technology have catalyzed a global wave of technical integration and local innovation across numerous nations. These countries sought mechanized seed production systems tailored to their unique agro-ecological constraints, with the primary goal of decoupling the industry from its traditional dependence on labor-intensive operations and prohibitive costs (Xiao, 2012; Wang et al., 2020a; Wedger et al., 2025). The United States has been a pioneer in this field. In the early 1980s, with strategic guidance from Chinese experts, the Ring Around Seed Company began mechanized trials using the Chinese hybrid ‘Nanyou no. 2’ (Li et al., 2013a). The company successfully built a comprehensive mechanized system in the southern plains that integrated dry direct seeding, aerial plant protection, helicopter-assisted pollination, and automated harvesting and drying. The rice production system of Japan underwent a preliminary mechanized transition in the early 1960s and has since evolved toward high levels of automation and intelligence (Oshiro, 1985; Yasuhiro and Motonobu, 2012). Innovations made in Japan have proven particularly effective in hilly and mountainous terrains (Li et al., 2003; Tamaki et al., 2013). In India, although mechanized seed production is largely exploratory, the widespread adoption of mechanical transplanters has already considerably boosted operational efficiency (Li et al., 2013a). Vietnam uses specific agricultural machinery in field production to stabilize hybrid seed supply (Guru et al., 2025), and Bangladesh and the Philippines have introduced transplanters and harvesters to address seasonal labor shortages (Becker et al., 2024; Elbashir, 2024). Systematic seed production frameworks are not yet fully developed in parts of Africa and Latin America, including Nigeria, Madagascar, and Costa Rica. Nonetheless, regional trials and pilot programs have demonstrated the substantial yield potential and adaptability of hybrid rice in these tropical and subtropical zones (Liao et al., 2011).

Despite China’s leadership in hybrid rice technology, its domestic seed production faces unique challenges. Production bases are primarily concentrated in hilly southern regions characterized by high temperatures and extreme humidity, where complex microclimates and rugged terrain impose stringent requirements on seed production. At the same time, rapid urbanization has triggered a massive migration of rural labor into cities, and agricultural production now faces a severe and escalating labor shortage. A technological framework for streamlining and mechanizing seed production is thus urgently needed if hybrid rice cultivation is to remain productive, efficient, and sustainable (Tang et al., 2022). To simplify operations and reduce costs, researchers in China have extensively innovated seed production models. At present, such models can be categorized into two approaches: separate planting and mixed planting (Figure 2).

Production of hybrid rice seed by the separate-planting method

Separate planting refers to the practice of growing male and female parents in distinct zones or alternating rows. By meticulously adjusting the sowing and transplanting dates, researchers can ensure that both parents flower synchronously (pollen encounter). Once pollination is complete, the male parents are manually removed (rogued), and the F1 hybrid seeds are mechanically harvested. Depending on the specific methods used, separate planting approaches can be categorized as mechanized strip-seeding or mechanized pollination through separate planting (Liu and Tang, 2025).

Mechanized pollination through separate planting

In theory, the centralized and separate cultivation of paternal and maternal lines can resolve the spatiotemporal constraints inherent in hybrid rice seed production, substantially reducing the labor costs associated with flowering synchronization and manual pollination. Building on this conceptual paradigm, future seed production could make use of specialized mechanical systems to harvest pollen during peak anthesis of the paternal line, followed by targeted pollination during the optimal receptivity window of the maternal line. To overcome the persistent challenge of asynchronous anthesis between parental lines, cryopreservation of paternal pollen has been proposed. When the maternal line reaches peak receptivity, this preserved pollen could be thawed, revitalized, and mechanically distributed. Historically, research into mechanical systems for the collection of rice pollen was initiated in China as early as the 1980s.

Nonetheless, translating this conceptual framework into field-scale practice faces significant biological and engineering bottlenecks. Rice pollen exhibits remarkably transient viability under field conditions and is exquisitely sensitive to fluctuations in temperature and humidity (Gao et al., 2026). This inherent fragility renders large-scale harvesting and secure, long-term preservation of pollen exceedingly difficult. Furthermore, under actual field conditions, the daily pollen shedding window lasts only 2–3 h, whereas the flowering period of male-sterile lines is frequently asynchronous and protracted. This population-level heterogeneity in flowering dynamics severely limits the efficiency of current mechanical harvesting and pollination systems, resulting in substantial pollen wastage. Restricted by both the physiological limitations of pollen and current mechanical inefficiencies, such systems are not yet feasible for large-scale field application and remain primarily a theoretical paradigm for future exploration (Li et al., 2013a).

Streamlined mechanized seed production via strip-seeding

Traditional production of hybrid rice seed relies on the manual raising of seedlings and the calculated adjustment of sowing intervals between parents. The parents are interplanted using specific row-ratio configurations, typically ranging from 1:(8–10) to 2:(10–12) (Figure 2A). After pollination, the male parents are manually removed (rogued) to facilitate mechanized harvesting of the female lines. This planting layout considerably increases the planting density and effective panicle number of the female parent while preventing the “male dominance” effect caused by excessive male rows, thereby enabling high yields in hybrid rice seed production (Yang, 1990). However, this process is labor-intensive and time-consuming, leading to low production efficiency.

Building on years of technical expertise and innovation, researchers in China have addressed the bottlenecks of traditional “small row-ratio” interplanting—specifically the difficulties in mechanized transplantation and harvesting—by developing a “large row-ratio” model (Figure 2B). This new configuration completely mechanizes the process, covering key stages from seedling raising and transplantation to gibberellin acid (GA3) application, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-assisted pollination, mechanized harvesting, and seed processing. This integrated approach markedly reduces labor requirements and production costs (Liu et al., 2014).

During seedling raising, the use of “seed-printing” technology ensures uniform and sparse sowing, which markedly improves seedling quality and provides greater flexibility in seedling age (Xu et al., 2023) (Figure 3A). For field transplantation, selection of appropriate transplanters, scheduling of precise sowing dates, and optimization of spatial layouts are crucial. By coordinating the population structure, this approach ensures that the male parents provide sufficient pollen while the female parents achieve a high outcrossing seed set, avoiding resource competition from overcrowding and enhancing both yield and seed quality (Liu and Tang, 2025) (Figure 3B). To resolve the issue of panicle enclosure (neck-locking) in male-sterile lines, GA3 is sprayed from the initial heading stage to facilitate neck-node elongation, ensuring full panicle exsertion and increasing the stigma exsertion rate of the female parents (Xiao et al., 2010). For plant protection, UAV-based application of GA3 enables rapid and uniform coverage, reducing labor requirements while enhancing efficacy (Liu and Tang, 2025) (Figure 3C).

Similarly, UAV-assisted pollination lowers labor costs and supports high-yield production (Liu et al., 2014) (Figure 3D). With the ongoing transition of hybrid rice seed production toward large-scale, mechanized, and standardized operations, traditional sun-drying methods no longer meet requirements for efficiency and quality (Liu et al., 2020). At the harvesting stage, matching grain dryers are used to rapidly remove moisture from freshly harvested seeds while minimizing mechanical damage, ensuring efficient and high-quality seed drying (Liu and Tang, 2025) (Figure 3E).

Mixed-planting approaches for the production of hybrid rice seed

Mixed planting involves sowing and growing the male and female parents at a specific ratio (Figure 2C). In this model, field management tasks such as fertilization, pesticide application, and pollination are performed through standardized mechanized operations. At maturity, the seeds are harvested as a mixture, and the hybrid seeds are then recovered through specialized mechanical separation (Huang et al., 2024). At present, mixed planting in hybrid rice is primarily performed using the following methods.

Mixed planting based on hull-color markers

This approach uses hull colors as phenotypic markers. Male-sterile and restorer lines with contrasting hull colors are sown as a mixture; after uniform mechanized harvesting, the grains are separated using color sorters to obtain high-purity hybrid seeds (Maruyama et al., 1991) (Figure 4A).

Representative strategies for mixed-planting production of hybrid rice seed.

(A) Color-based seed sorting. The female and male (restorer line, RL) parents are mixed-planted and mixed-harvested. The resulting seed mixture is separated using a color sorter on the basis of specific seed-coat color markers, isolating F1 hybrid seeds from RL seeds.

(B) Size-based seed sorting. After mixed harvesting, F1 and RL seeds are mechanically separated through a screen on the basis of differences in seed thickness or overall grain size.

(C) Herbicide-based mixed sowing. This strategy combines mixed planting with differences in herbicide resistance. After pollination, herbicide application selectively eliminates the susceptible male parent, allowing only the thermo-sensitive genic male-sterile (TGMS) parent to survive and produce pure F1 seeds.

(D) TFS line–based mixed sowing. This system uses a TGMS line and a thermo-sensitive female-sterile (TFS) restorer line. Under restrictive seed production temperatures, the TFS line produces functional pollen but fails to set seed. Consequently, all harvested seeds derive exclusively from the TGMS line, yielding pure F1 hybrids without the need for postharvest mechanical sorting.

Rice germplasm with the distinct brown-hull phenotype shows considerable promise for practical application in applied breeding programs. For instance, researchers used the Indian brown-hulled variety ‘Dular’ as the foundational genetic background to develop the male-sterile line ‘Sexuan A’, which retains this conspicuous brown-hull trait. This brown pigmentation serves as a reliable phenotypic marker, enabling highly efficient, mechanized seed separation using commercial optical color sorters (He et al., 2001). Similarly, the male-sterile line ‘Xin’an S’ exhibits a prominent brown-hull trait and has been proposed as an ideal parent for mixed planting owing to its suitability for large-scale mechanized production, as preliminarily verified (Zhou et al., 2014). With rapid advances in functional genomics, genetic improvements in hull coloration have transitioned from conventional phenotypic screening to precise molecular design. Recent studies demonstrated that deletion of the OsRH3 promoter and loss of RBH1 function gave rise to red and reddish-brown hulls, respectively (Wang et al., 2020b; Fang et al., 2025). Such phenotypic variations provide novel germplasm for the development of molecular markers and support advances in the mechanized seed production of hybrid rice. Building on these findings, researchers developed the all-in-one seed producer technology (ASPT) system by combining CRISPR–Cas9, fertility restoration, a pollen-inactivation module, and the RUBY visual reporter. This system creates male-sterile lines via OsMs45 editing and uses the RUBY gene to confer a red phenotype to the maintainer line: RUBY+ seeds grow into reddish, fertile plants, whereas RUBY− seeds become green, male-sterile plants. This visual marker facilitates high-precision mechanical sorting and field-trait verification, demonstrating the industrial potential of visual-marker-based sorting for the production of hybrid rice seed (Liu et al., 2026).

Mixed planting based on differences in grain size

Mixed sowing, mixed harvesting, and mechanical sorting predicated on grain-size dimorphism between the parental lines is another strategy for efficient and cost-effective production of high-purity hybrid rice seeds (Tang et al., 2022) (Figure 4B). As early as the 1990s, Lu et al. observed that the 1000-grain weight (TGW) of the maternal parent in the hybrid combination ‘IIyou 86’ was 23–24 g, whereas that of the paternal counterpart was greater than 35 g (Lu et al., 1996). By exploiting this pronounced size discrepancy, they were able to mechanically separate hybrid seeds from those of the paternal parent. Through subsequent multi-generational selective breeding, Yu et al. (2007) and Xu et al. (2015) advanced this approach by cultivating a small-grain male-sterile maternal line (MSL) with a large-grain paternal line, increasing hybrid seed purity to over 99% and confirming the feasibility of the system.

The predominant effect of maternal genotype on grain size provides the genetic foundation for this framework. When an MSL harboring a recessive small-grain locus (ss), designated S-MSL, is cultivated with a large-grain restorer paternal line (SS), the resulting F1 seeds exhibit a small-grain phenotype (Ss), strictly dictated by the maternal genotype. This defined size dimorphism enables efficient mechanical separation of hybrid seeds from the larger paternal grains. Because the F1 maternal genotype is heterozygous (Ss), the size of the subsequent F2 seeds is restored to standard levels, thereby maintaining optimal F2 grain yield (Huang et al., 2024; Ying et al., 2024). Maximizing the parental grain-size dimorphism will be key to further optimizing this mechanical sorting system. Recent cloning of multiple grain-size-regulating genes has furnished a molecular foundation for the development of parental materials with extreme grain-size phenotypes. For example, Ying et al. identified TGW5FH212—a recessive gene governing grain size that functions as a hypomorphic allele of the D1 locus—and developed the small-grain MSLs S-C815S and S-WXS. This milestone provided a robust technological framework for mechanized mixed-sowing production of hybrid seed based on grain-size dimorphism (Ying et al., 2024).

In the context of epigenetic regulation, the GSE3 gene encodes a histone acetyltransferase capable of binding histones and modulating their acetylation levels. The GSE3 protein is recruited by the transcription factor GS2 to the promoters of their co-regulated grain-size target genes, thus altering local histone acetylation states. Field trials demonstrated that genome editing of GSE3 can rapidly improve elite male-sterile lines, facilitating mixed-sowing production of hybrid seed (Huang et al., 2024). Furthermore, the histone-binding protein LARGE3 physically interacts with the histone deacetylase OsHDT1 to suppress the expression of OsMKKK10 by reducing histone H4 acetylation levels and increasing nucleosome density, thereby negatively regulating grain length. Consequently, loss-of-function large3 mutants exhibit very large and heavy grains caused by accelerated glume-cell proliferation. Characterization of this epigenetic regulatory module has provided new insight into the role of the mitogen-activated protein kinase signaling pathway in grain development and identified precise targets for the breeding of large-grain restorer lines, ultimately advancing mechanized mixed-sowing production of hybrid rice seed (Zhang et al., 2026).

Mixed planting based on herbicide resistance genes

Exploiting differences in herbicide tolerance between parental lines to selectively eradicate the paternal parent after pollination is a highly efficient strategy for obtaining pure hybrid rice seed in mixed-sowing production (Xia et al., 2020) (Figure 4C). Fu et al. introduced the bar herbicide-resistance gene into the male-sterile variety ‘Peiai 64S.’ By crossing ‘Peiai 64S’ with conventional restorer lines and applying herbicide after pollination, they were able to selectively eradicate the male parents (Fu et al., 2001). Likewise, the bar gene was introduced into PGMS 920S via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation to generate the herbicide-resistant photoperiod sensitive genic male sterile (HRPGMS) line ‘YA3530ms.’ Seeds of ‘YA3530ms’ and the pollen parent were mixed (4:1 w/w) and sown in seedling trays. After 30 days, the mixed seedlings were mechanically transplanted into the field. To ensure seed purity, glufosinate ammonium was sprayed 7 days after flowering to remove the pollen parent, and hybrid seeds were harvested from the herbicide-resistant HRPGMS plants at maturity. Zhu et al. developed an alternative strategy by introducing a bentazon-sensitive lethal gene into indica restorer lines. In synchronized parental combinations, bentazon application after pollination selectively cleared the restorer lines, enabling the harvest of high-quality hybrid seeds (Zhu, 2004). Further refining this approach, Zhang et al. stacked a bentazon-sensitivity gene with hull-color markers in the same restorer line. This dual-track system enabled the chemical removal of male parents after pollination, followed by postharvest color sorting to guarantee maximum seed purity (Zhang et al., 2008).

Because conventional introgression of herbicide resistance is laborious and time-consuming, researchers are increasingly using transgenic and genome-editing techniques to accelerate the development of herbicide-resistant rice. For example, targeted CRISPR–Cas9-mediated mutagenesis of OsHPPD in japonica rice significantly enhanced mesotrione tolerance without incurring a yield penalty (Wu et al., 2023). Concurrently, the mechanistic dissection of resistance genes has identified valuable molecular targets. The glufosinate-resistance regulatory gene GLR1 confers broad-spectrum stress tolerance by de-repressing its downstream targets (OsGS1, OsCYP51G3, and OsCATA), ultimately promoting the scavenging of reactive oxygen species and mitigating ammonia accumulation (Ren et al., 2023). Building on these results, the SPL-family transcription factor GLR2 was shown to directly bind GLR1 and activate its transcription, establishing a comprehensive regulatory network for glufosinate resistance (Cheng et al., 2024). Collectively, these discoveries offer valuable genetic resources for the rationally designed breeding of novel herbicide-resistant cultivars.

Application of genome editing in mixed-sowing seed production

Thermo-sensitive female sterile (TFS) lines act only as pollen donors during hybrid seed production, as they cannot set seeds themselves to produce selfed progeny. In mixed-planting systems, the use of TFS lines eliminates the need to remove male parents after cross-pollination, preventing the contamination of hybrid seeds with restorer-line seeds. This approach markedly reduces labor costs and enhances seed purity, making TFS lines ideal restorers for mechanized seed production (Li et al., 2022) (Figure 4D).

Recent advances in genome editing have facilitated the identification and functional characterization of diverse female-sterility genes, giving rise to novel technical strategies for mixed-sowing systems. PTB1 plays a critical role in pollen-tube elongation and transmission, and its loss of function manifests as female sterility (Li et al., 2013b). To overcome the inherent difficulties in propagating female-sterile materials, researchers developed a linked expression cassette containing PTB1, the pollen-lethal factor ZmAA1 (Wu et al., 2016), and the fluorescent marker DsRed, and introduced this cassette into the ptb1 mutant. Through fluorescence-assisted sorting of selfed seeds, transgenic maintainer lines and female-sterile lines could be efficiently separated and propagated (Xia et al., 2019). Another important step was the use of tfs1 (thermo-sensitive female sterility 1), a spontaneous mutant that is completely sterile under normal or high temperatures but recovers partial fertility under low temperatures. The tfs1 allele consistently produces heat-sensitive female sterility when introduced into various elite rice varieties, demonstrating excellent genetic tractability and breeding potential (Li et al., 2022). Similarly, the h569 mutant, controlled by a single recessive nuclear gene, exhibits normal vegetative growth and male fertility. By transforming a T-DNA cassette containing the MEL2 gene, ZmAA1, and DsRed2 into the restorer line ‘ZZH1607’ and crossing it with the h569 mutant, researchers were able to use fluorescent markers in the F2 population to achieve high-efficiency separation and propagation. This strategy offers a robust technical approach for large-scale seed production under mixed-planting and mixed-harvesting conditions (Wang et al., 2024).

Deciphering the thermo-sensitive fertility-transition mechanisms of male-sterile lines—the other genetic core of the hybrid system—is also important for hybrid seed production. Over 95% of the two-line thermo-sensitive male-sterile lines currently used in commercial production rely on the TMS5 gene. Recent studies have characterized its regulatory mechanisms in detail: the TMS5-encoded protein RNase ZS1 is a tRNA 2′,3′-cyclic phosphatase responsible for repairing cP-CCA-tRNA, thereby maintaining the abundance of mature tRNAs, particularly tRNA-Ala (Yan et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2026). In tms5 mutants, elevated temperatures induce the hyperaccumulation of cyclic-phosphate tRNAs, ultimately disrupting translational homeostasis and triggering male sterility. Advanced functional genomic studies have identified suppressors such as SOT3 (OsHel2) and critical regulators such as OsVms1 and OsRqc1. By engaging the ribosome-associated quality control (RQC) pathway, these factors orchestrate peptidyl-tRNA cleavage and tRNA recycling, thus precisely determining the critical sterility-inducing temperature (PST) of the sterile lines (Liu et al., 2025). Finally, natural variations in the promoter region make an important contribution to the PST discrepancies observed between indica and japonica backgrounds (Zhou et al., 2025). Characterization of this TMS5-dependent fertility-regulation network not only provides fundamental biological insights but also identifies molecular targets for genome editing to improve low-PST lines for use in mechanized hybrid rice seed production systems.

Innovative integration of agronomy and machinery

The integration of agronomic innovation with mechanical technology is essential for enhancing the efficiency and quality of hybrid rice seed production. To this end, the design of agricultural machinery must be deeply informed by rice growth patterns and population management requirements, ensuring efficient synergy between mechanical operations and agronomic protocols.

Technical innovations in male-parent removal for traditional separate-planting-based mechanization

In the traditional “small row-ratio” separate-planting approach, timely removal of the male parent after pollination markedly improves the growth environment for the female parent during the grain-filling stage. This intervention alleviates competition for nutrients and sunlight while mitigating the risks of pests, diseases, and lodging, substantially improving the yield and quality of the resulting seeds (Zhang, 2021).

Hybrid rice seed production in China is concentrated mainly in hilly regions characterized by undulating terrain and heavy, cohesive soils. Owing to the narrow row spacing of the male parents and poor field accessibility for machinery, removal of the male parents relies heavily on manual labor or knapsack mowers (Figure 5A and 5B). Such traditional methods are inefficient, physically demanding, and incomplete, ultimately compromising seed purity (Li, 2020). A specialized machine for male-parent removal has been developed to address these challenges in hilly areas (Figure 5C and 5E). It shows negligible rates of missed paternal panicles and maternal damage in field trials (Supplemental Table 1) while delivering an eradication efficiency approximately 17-fold greater than that of manual removal (Supplemental Table 2). The broader deployment of this machinery can resolve the persistent challenge of paternal eradication in hilly terrain, supporting the full mechanization of traditional narrow-row-ratio separate-planting systems.

Technical innovations in male-parent removal for mixed-planting mechanization

Mixed planting is an important approach for streamlined and mechanized production of hybrid rice seed. Current models largely rely on parental seed traits or genotypic differences and ensure hybrid purity through chemical emasculation or mechanical sorting (Tang et al., 2020). However, these approaches are often constrained by their reliance on specific genetic materials, limiting their universal applicability. In practical hybrid breeding and field seed production, the panicle layer of the male parent is typically taller than that of the female parent to facilitate an optimal outcrossing architecture (Matsui et al., 2020). Leveraging this natural height difference, this study proposes a mechanized removal scheme in which the male parent is selectively cut after pollination in order to simplify the mixed-planting workflow and enhance operational efficiency. The first-generation specialized male-parent remover features a high-clearance, wide-span chassis designed to straddle female rows, minimizing mechanical interference. Equipped with an adjustable front-mounted cutting header, the machine uses the height gap between parents to achieve directional identification and precision cutting of the taller male panicle layer (Figure 6A and 6C).

Despite the innovative design of this specialized remover, its practical application faces certain constraints. First, field-bed widths must be highly standardized to match the fixed wheel track of the machine; otherwise, the risk of crushing damage to the female parents increases markedly. Second, low-position effective panicles emerge early and fill rapidly in some restorer lines, essentially completing their reproductive growth before the first GA3 treatment. Exogenous hormones have little effect on stimulating the height of such tillers, making it difficult to increase the height difference between the parents (Li et al., 2025a).

Field surveys have shown that although GA3 application increases the average height difference between the parents, the gap between the lowest effective panicle of male plants and the highest effective panicle of female plants is not significant (Figure 6D). Therefore, the machine cannot completely remove all male panicles. These findings suggest that further agronomic improvements are required, specifically the development of restorer lines with highly synchronized and uniform heading stages.

“Small-grain, high-yield”: A strategic innovation through resource redistribution

The grain weight of hybrid rice is governed mainly by additive genetic effects (Huang et al., 2016), and breeders typically select parents with high TGW to stabilize grain weight and obtain ultra-high yields from hybrid combinations (Tang et al., 2022).

To enable mechanized mixed planting in practice, our team developed high-yielding hybrid rice combinations with spontaneous genic male sterility (SGMS) lines as female parents. We selected the SGMS line ‘Zhuo 201S’—known for its ideal plant architecture, low fertility-transition temperature, excellent outcrossing habits, high combining ability, and superior grain quality—and crossed it with the large-grain restorer line ‘Xiangnonghui 1126,’ which features strong lodging resistance, concentrated and prolonged flowering, and abundant pollen. We used a specialized seed production approach that involved direct seeding of the female parent and manual throwing of the male parent to ensure flowering synchrony, followed by uniform mechanized management (Tang et al., 2022). After mechanized mixed harvesting, we used the considerable difference in thickness between the male-parent and hybrid seeds to enable efficient separation. Separation was performed using a custom-designed sieve with narrow, elongated apertures, resulting in zero male-parent contamination and a minimal loss rate of 2.31%. This separation procedure helped us to achieve commercial purity standards and complete mechanization of hybrid rice seed production (Tang et al., 2020). Field trials demonstrated that the SGMS-based hybrid combination ‘Zhuoliangyou 1126’ produces a large number of glumes through sufficient panicle numbers and large panicles, thus exhibiting an expanded sink capacity. By maintaining high outcrossing seed set and TGW, this approach achieved ultra-high yields for SGMS-based combinations (Liu et al., 2024).

This “small-grain, high-yield” approach is more than a technical breakthrough. It represents a biological paradigm shift rooted in resource redistribution, providing a strategic Chinese solution for the global production of hybrid rice seed. Its scientific logic is built on two critical dimensions. First, it makes use of a “sink-capacity compensation mechanism” in which reductions in individual grain weight are offset by increasing the spikelet load per unit area of small-grain maternal lines. This ensures the realization of ultra-high yield potential at the level of total sink capacity. Second, this approach achieves a “multiplication leverage effect” by optimizing the allocation of maternal dry matter into a larger number of grain carriers. This strategy doubles the multiplication coefficient of maternal seeds without requiring additional resource inputs. As a result, seed production costs are significantly reduced at the source (Tang et al., 2020).

Collectively, our results indicate that mechanized mixed planting using small-grain MSLs is a promising strategy for the streamlining and mechanization of hybrid rice seed production. Nevertheless, broad adoption of this approach will depend on the creation of stable small-grain MSLs and the continued optimization of high-efficiency sorting equipment.

Distal track: Molecular-driven breakthroughs in emerging hybrid rice technologies

Third-generation hybrid rice technology

Cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) and photoperiod/thermo-sensitive genic male-sterile (PTGMS) systems have been used extensively in global rice production (Moon et al., 2025). However, CMS is constrained by a limited pool of nuclear restorer genes, and PTGMS systems are vulnerable to fluctuations in fertility triggered by climate change, posing risks to seed multiplication and hybrid production (Fan and Zhang, 2018). Third-generation hybrid rice technology addresses such issues using a genetic engineering–mediated system known as seed production technology (SPT). By introducing a linked expression cassette that comprises fertility restoration, pollen inactivation, and fluorescent reporter genes, researchers can construct genetically engineered nuclear male-sterile lines with restored fertility (Zheng et al., 2025). During self-pollination, this propagation line produces two types of offspring through Mendelian segregation: (1) male-sterile plants that do not inherit any transgenic components (colorless seeds) and function as female parents for hybrid seed production and (2) propagation lines (maintainer lines) that carry the three tightly linked transgenic elements. Such maintainer lines are reused to maintain and multiply the male-sterile lines. This framework integrates the stable sterility of the three-line system with the flexible parental combinations typical of the two-line system, offering a novel genetic and technical approach for the streamlined and mechanized production of hybrid rice seeds (Liao et al., 2021; Song et al., 2021). Despite resolving the crossing restrictions of CMS and the environmental instability of P/TGMS through molecular intervention, this third-generation technology does not bypass the fundamental law of genetic segregation during conventional sexual reproduction and thus cannot achieve the fixation of heterosis across generations. Therefore, the permanent fixation of F1 heterosis represents the next critical frontier in hybrid rice technology.

The “one-line” strategy: Fixation of heterosis via apomixis

In contrast to third-generation technology, which focuses primarily on the efficient production of hybrid seeds, the one-line strategy aims to overcome the requirement for annual seed production. By enabling the stable transmission of hybrid genotypes, this technology bypasses the issues of genetic recombination and segregation inherent to conventional sexual reproduction. Consequently, it effectively fixes heterosis, facilitating more stable and sustainable hybrid rice production. In the late 20th century, academician Longping Yuan envisioned a one-line strategy in which hybrid rice could self-propagate with its heterosis intact, very similar to inbred varieties. However, this visionary goal remained unrealized for decades, constrained by the limitations of contemporary molecular and genetic-engineering toolkits.

The breeding landscape has been transformed by recent advances in genome editing (Khanday et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2019). Identification of the male gamete–delivered transcription factor baby boom (BBM1) as the “trigger” for embryogenesis was a landmark discovery. By synchronously inducing MiMe (mitosis instead of meiosis) mutations and driving the egg cell–specific expression of BBM1 in F1 hybrids, researchers were able to produce clonal seeds with over 95% efficiency across multiple generations, effectively “freezing” heterosis (Vernet et al., 2022). In a parallel breakthrough, Wang et al. performed simultaneous editing of REC8, PAIR1, OSD1, and MTL4 (Wang et al., 2019). By coupling heterosis fixation with haploid induction, they produced hybrid lines capable of self-propagation, with T4 progeny confirming the robust genetic stability of these clones (Liu et al., 2023b). This was followed by development of the Fix3 (fixation of hybrids 3) architecture, which incorporated the egg cell-specific OsWUS gene into the MiMe framework to substantially enhance induction rates without compromising fertility (Huang et al., 2025). Leveraging CRISPR–Cas9, the Fix4 system was engineered by multiplex knockout of MiMe components (OsOSD1, OsPAIR1, and OsREC8) and the pollen-specific gene OsPLDα2, achieving parity in induction and seed-setting rates through a purely editing-based approach (Hu et al., 2025a). The most recent iteration, Fix5, integrates the apomixis-critical gene PpPAR from Pilosella piloselloides with the MiMe module, reaching a clonal efficiency of up to 84.6% in T0 plants. Whole-genome sequencing has confirmed that these clonal seeds retain 100% of parental heterozygosity, marking the definitive realization of heterosis fixation (Xiong et al., 2025).

Concluding remarks

Hybrid rice is a cornerstone of modern sustainable agriculture and global food security, with high-yielding and efficient seed production systems providing essential support for maintaining heterosis and ensuring the supply of elite seeds (Moon et al., 2025). However, as agricultural labor structures shift and production costs rise, traditional seed production models based on three-line and two-line systems—characterized by intensive labor input and high environmental dependence—are increasingly struggling to adapt to current industrial demands. Against this backdrop, the transition of seed production toward streamlined and mechanized systems has become an inevitable choice for enhancing efficiency, stabilizing seed quality, and securing industrial sustainability.

Challenges in traditional split-planting

Flowering synchronization, spatial configuration, and paternal eradication are critical determinants of production efficiency in the split-planting system. Although mechanized foundations have progressively been established for stages such as seedling raising, transplanting, plant protection, harvesting, and drying, development across different operational links remains uneven (Liu and Tang, 2025). Specifically, for procedures that require high agronomic precision (e.g., mechanized paternal transplanting, supplementary pollination, and paternal eradication), a lack of integration between mechanical equipment and traditional planting layouts continues to restrict further improvements in split-planting efficiency.

Innovations in mixed-planting models

Mixed-planting models provide a novel solution for the streamlining and mechanization of hybrid rice seed production by reducing the spatial isolation between parental lines. Current technological approaches include seed sorting based on color or grain-size discrepancies, selection based on herbicide resistance or sensitivity, and female-sterile systems (Tang et al., 2020). At present, sorting based on hull color or grain thickness has achieved industrial application through the use of optical color sorters and air-screen cleaners.

Nonetheless, because grain development is regulated by multiple genes and influenced by environmental factors, insufficient grain filling or maturity often leads to uneven hull pigmentation or reduced differences in grain size, potentially compromising sorting precision and seed purity. By contrast, mixed-planting systems based on herbicide resistance offer advantages such as simplified field operations and adaptability to large-scale management, with mature application protocols already established in global seed production systems. However, the scarcity of broad-spectrum resistance genes with well-defined molecular mechanisms suitable for breeding remains a constraint (Luo and Liu, 2025), limiting the use of this approach across diverse hybrid rice genetic backgrounds. With regard to female-sterile systems, although they theoretically eliminate the need for subsequent sorting or roguing and greatly simplify the production process, their large-scale commercial application is currently hindered by the instability of fertility expression, difficulties in parental propagation, and regulatory restrictions related to genetic engineering (Supplemental Table 3).

Technological evolution driven by molecular innovations

Although third-generation hybrid rice technology has achieved landmark breakthroughs in fertility control and parental compatibility (Liao et al., 2021), its commercialization is contingent upon overcoming complexities in genetic linkage, transgenic dependency, and policy-related constraints (Chen et al., 2023). In parallel, the one-line system seeks to revolutionize the field by fixing heterosis through apomixis, but it faces persistent challenges related to genetic stability, synchronization of induction efficiency with yield, and polyploid environmental adaptation (Hojsgaard and Hörandl, 2019; Hu et al., 2025b). Notably, these methodologies are not disjointed developments but represent a pivotal stage in the shift from controlling reproductive events to fundamentally re-engineering reproductive strategies. The third-generation framework, based on the rational engineering of reproductive modules, establishes the essential molecular toolkits and editing paradigms that underpin the one-line system. This framework constitutes a vital bridge that facilitates the transition from traditional hybridization techniques to the permanent fixation of heterotic vigor.

Future perspectives

Recent research efforts have pioneered diverse approaches for streamlining hybrid seed production and boosting overall efficiency. Although these innovations show considerable promise for reducing labor intensity and economic costs, their practical implementation in large-scale agriculture is hindered by notable limitations. Current seed production frameworks are impeded not only by the lagging development of streamlined mechanization but also, more fundamentally, by the intrinsic biological and agronomic limitations of the three-line and two-line systems. This combination of engineering and biological challenges severely hampers further breakthroughs in production efficiency, compromises seed purity, and ultimately undermines the stability of the commercial seed supply chain.

To fundamentally overcome the limitations of traditional breeding approaches, one-line technology based on synthetic apomixis is regarded as the ultimate paradigm shift for the permanent fixation of heterosis (Wei et al., 2026). Continuous research and innovation are essential for overcoming existing hurdles, and the development of rice germplasm with genuine commercial value is anticipated within the next decade (Yu et al., 2026). It is therefore imperative to establish a dual-track roadmap that connects near-term efficiency enhancements to long-term transformative goals. Prior to the maturation of the one-line approach, efforts must remain anchored in current production frameworks. On one front, this entails fully streamlining and mechanizing seed production through deep synergistic integration of agronomy and mechanical engineering (Figure 7). To address critical challenges such as yield instability and purity fluctuations under extreme weather conditions (Cheng et al., 2007b; Huang et al., 2014; Chang et al., 2016), AI-driven approaches (e.g., multidimensional data integration, climate–crop predictive modeling, and dynamic decision-support systems) can be used to overcome critical bottlenecks in the seed production industry. Together, these dual efforts forge a cohesive pathway linking immediate production demands to future technological paradigm shifts, enabling a seamless generational leap in the production of hybrid rice seed.

Targeted parental breeding for streamlined and mechanized seed production

The genetic traits of parental lines are the fundamental constraint on operational efficiency during the transition toward fully mechanized seed production (Huang and Zou, 2018). Current large-scale systems have critical deficiencies in mechanical adaptability, stemming primarily from a fundamental mismatch between parental biological characteristics and standardized machinery parameters. For example, suboptimal field leveling often causes localized waterlogging in mechanized direct-seeding systems. If the parental lines lack seed vigor and submergence tolerance, seedling emergence rates drop significantly (Ismail et al., 2012). This initial discrepancy during germination is progressively amplified throughout the growth cycle, resulting in severe heading asynchrony between the parental populations. Such stress-induced asynchrony directly disrupts the optimized flowering window essential for mechanized operations, precipitating a sharp decline in outcrossing seed set.

Because of the conflict between biological heterogeneity and the demands of standardized mechanization, merely upgrading agricultural machinery can no longer overcome existing efficiency limitations. Future breakthroughs will therefore depend on shifting breeding objectives away from sheer yield enhancement toward the targeted creation of mechanization-compatible parents. In this strategy, elite male-sterile and restorer lines must serve as foundational chassis varieties. By leveraging the capacity of the M1TDS (multiple targeted discovery system) for high-throughput mutation screening, together with precise genome editing, researchers can engineer superior germplasm with robust seed vigor, broad-spectrum stress resilience, and targeted submergence tolerance (Shao et al., 2024). This proactive approach guarantees population uniformity right from the direct-seeding stage. Ultimately, introgression of specific herbicide resistance into these optimized parents will seamlessly couple molecular breeding with chemical management, producing the critical germplasm foundation required for streamlined, fully mechanized production of hybrid rice seed.

Innovative streamlined and mechanized seed production through the integration of machinery and agronomy

Building upon the targeted optimization of parental lines, the next critical step for enhancing the efficiency of hybrid seed production involves fostering deep synergy between agricultural mechanization and agronomy, establishing streamlined, mechanized models suitable for large-scale production. The core objective here is not merely to substitute manual labor with machines, but to fundamentally restructure the technological framework across key production stages, enabling standardized, precise, and highly efficient operations.

First, during the seedling and transplanting stages, emphasis must be placed on the synergistic application of factory-scale seedling raising and mechanized orderly throwing technologies. To address the low standardization and inconsistent seedling vigor typical of traditional water-based raising (Liu and Tang, 2025), factory-scale facilities use controlled environments to stabilize growth, thereby maximizing population uniformity and seedling robustness (Gui, 2024). Building on this foundation, mechanized orderly throwing offers the advantages of transplanting with intact root–soil blocks and minimizing or even eliminating the transplant-shock recovery period. This rapid seedling establishment substantially boosts both yield and quality per unit area, showing immense application potential (Li, 2024; Li et al., 2025b). Future operations must precisely align sowing dates with parental growth periods and optimize the loading capacity and guiding mechanisms of throwing machines to ensure uniform distribution in the field. Concurrently, by incorporating small-grain male-sterile lines, the industry can explore a fully streamlined “mixed-throwing, bulk-harvesting, and mechanical-sorting” pipeline, seamlessly integrating the transplanting, harvesting, and sorting phases.

During population construction and supplementary pollination, innovative approaches must focus on freeing up space for mechanical operations and maximizing the efficiency of pollen utilization. Traditional “narrow row ratio” planting patterns severely restrict the movement of machinery (Ding et al., 2025). By implementing UAV-assisted pollination, the parental row ratio can be drastically expanded to a 4–6:20–40 configuration. This macro-layout facilitates independent mechanical harvesting of paternal rows while significantly improving pollen dispersal (Liu et al., 2016). However, the agricultural drones currently repurposed for pollination have notable deficiencies in flight endurance, downwash flow stability, and hovering precision (Pan et al., 2022). Therefore, it will be important to engineer specialized pollination UAVs tailored specifically for hybrid rice. By optimizing parental row configurations and flight parameters on the basis of specific outcrossing traits and canopy architectures, these advances will enable both high yields and operational efficiency.

Finally, in the context of field roguing, the integration of genetic improvement with chemical control will be essential for transitioning off-type eradication from a labor-intensive chore to a precise, highly efficient operation. Roguing is a central step in safeguarding hybrid seed purity, but traditional manual roguing is exceptionally time-consuming, physically demanding, and heavily reliant on experiential judgment. Moving forward, in addition to the use of high-throughput genotyping for purity validation, breeding programs should prioritize the creation of parental lines that harbor specific herbicide resistance. By exploiting genotypic differences between the parents, the traditional process of manual identification and removal can be transformed into a selective eradication operation via targeted herbicide application. This strategic shift can drastically reduce labor costs while ensuring the genetic purity of the resulting seeds.

AI-empowered intelligent decision-support systems

Current production of hybrid rice seed relies predominantly on three-line and two-line systems. However, the male-sterile lines in both frameworks are acutely sensitive to extreme environmental conditions, severely threatening industrial stability. In the three-line system, spikelets fail to close normally when wild abortive cytoplasmic male-sterile (WA–CMS) lines encounter high temperatures during critical stages of panicle development, particularly during young panicle differentiation. This persistent glume gaping leads to a sharp decline in outcrossing rates and ultimately compromises seed yield (Yan et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2020). Conversely, in the two-line system, P/TGMS lines are highly prone to erratic, partial fertility reversion if they experience temperature fluctuations near the critical threshold during the fertility-sensitive window. This physiological instability drastically reduces seed purity and overall production (Peng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025).

Confronted with the intricate genotype-by-environment (G×E) interactions inherent in field conditions, artificial intelligence (AI) offers unprecedented potential for risk identification and precise intervention, owing to its capacity for non-linear feature extraction and multidimensional data mining (Roorkiwal et al., 2018; Costa-Neto et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023). Moving forward, it will be imperative to establish dynamic, AI-driven early-warning systems for fertility-transition risks. These platforms must integrate high-resolution meteorological data across diverse ecological zones and sowing dates with multi-year, multi-location datasets tracking parental panicle development. Processing this expansive data matrix will enable autonomous optimization of sowing and transplanting schedules, precise prediction of flowering synchronization, and preemptive forecasting of maternal fertility-reversion risks. Concurrently, through coordination of field-based Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, low-altitude UAV remote sensing (Han et al., 2025), and advanced computer vision algorithms (Zhan et al., 2025), the industry can increase its capacity for real-time dynamic monitoring of parental flowering synchronization and automated, intelligent identification of off-type plants.

Despite the immense potential of AI for the optimization of seed production frameworks, its practical translation into field-scale applications faces formidable challenges. The robustness of high-precision models is intrinsically dependent on long-term, cross-regional, high-quality field datasets (Von Bloh et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). Given the confounding effects of complex microclimatic interactions, the predictive generalization and accuracy of these models across diverse seed production regions demand extensive empirical validation. In addition, prohibitive computational costs restrict the scalable deployment of these technologies at conventional seed production bases. Consequently, future interdisciplinary endeavors must prioritize the development of lightweight, highly versatile, and agriculture-specific AI models (Yu et al., 2026). Only by achieving low-cost deployment and forging deep, foundational data connectivity with modern agricultural machinery to construct a “perception–decision–execution” human–machine collaborative closed loop can we propel hybrid rice seed production into a new era of digitalization and intelligence.

In summary, the fusion of streamlined mechanization within existing systems with disruptive innovation in emerging technologies can drive continued increases in the yield and efficiency of hybrid rice production. This iterative evolution can translate the scientific blueprint for one-line heterosis fixation into actual productivity, fulfilling the ultimate goal of permanent seed retention. This progress will realize Professor Longping Yuan’s strategic vision: the transformative journey from three-line and two-line systems to the one-line era.

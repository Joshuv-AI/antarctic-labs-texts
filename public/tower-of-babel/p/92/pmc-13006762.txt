Reduced port robotic cholecystectomy using the Revo-i single-site plus ONE port approach: a case series with video
Authors: Su Hyung Park, Chang Moo Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated the initial feasibility and safety of reduced-port robotic cholecystectomy using the Revo-i robotic system (meerecompany Inc.) in nine patients diagnosed with symptomatic gallstones and chronic cholecystitis. All surgeries were successfully completed without complications or conversion to conventional methods. Postoperative outcomes were excellent, with minimal pain immediately after surgery, significantly reduced by the next day. Most patients were discharged on postoperative day one. Cosmetic outcomes were outstanding, with barely noticeable scars at the umbilical and flank port sites. These findings demonstrate that Revo-i robotic cholecystectomy is technically feasible, safe, and patient-friendly, suggesting potential for broader application in hepatobiliary and pancreatic surgeries.Keywords: Cholecystectomy, Robotic surgical procedures, Minimally invasive surgical procedures

INTRODUCTION

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy is the gold standard for treating benign and premalignant gallbladder diseases. With the development of surgical instruments and techniques, reducing the number of trocars in laparoscopic procedures has become feasible. The emergence of single-site robotic systems has helped to overcome limitations of conventional single-port laparoscopic surgery.

Revo-i robotic surgical system (meerecompany Inc.) is a multiport robotic surgical system developed in Korea. The authors have conducted multiple studies to validate its clinical safety. In this paper, we report a case series of reduced port Revo-i cholecystectomies with single-site plus ONE port (Table 1) and discuss their technical considerations and future clinical applications.

CASE REPORT

A 63-year-old female patient (body mass index [BMI], 20.7 kg/m2) with no significant medical history was diagnosed with gallbladder stones and chronic cholecystitis during routine health screening. Revo-i single-site plus ONE port cholecystectomy was performed.

The procedure was conducted by a hepatobiliary and pancreatic surgery specialist experienced in laparoscopic and robotic surgery [1,2]. Multiple simulation sessions using a human model were conducted prior to clinical application (Fig. 1).

(A) The Revo-i robotic system is docked onto the abdominal cavity model, prepared for surgical simulation. (B) A rehearsal process using the robotic arms to practice the motions required for the surgery. (C, D) The 12-mm trocars for the camera, inserted through the glove port at the umbilicus. After the simulation, it was concluded that using a longer trocar helped reduce collisions between the robotic arm and the camera arm within the glove port.

A 2.5-cm transumbilical incision was made and a glove port was inserted. The transumbilical glove port allowed the assistant surgeon to perform suction and irrigation as needed during the operation. It also served as a conduit for inserting gauze or other necessary materials throughout the procedure. An 8-mm robotic trocar was placed in the left anterior axillary line below the costal margin (Fig. 2). The gallbladder fundus was sutured with a large straight needle and retracted laterally.

(A) Schematic diagram illustrating the positional relationship between the surgeon, the patient, and the robotic system and arms in the operating room. The robotic A-arm is not used, while the B- and C-arm cross outside the patient’s body. (B–D) Evolution of techniques to reduce arm collisions at the umbilical port. (B) Initial technique in which the assistant surgeon used a laparoscopic grasper for gallbladder traction, resulting in significant arm collisions around the umbilical port. (C) Modified technique performed without a grasper to reduce arm collisions. (D) Current preferred method involving extracorporeal traction of the gallbladder using nylon sutures.

A 12-mm trocar for the robotic camera was inserted at the 12 o’clock position of the glove port, and an 8-mm robotic trocar for the Maryland forceps was placed at the 3 o’clock position. The Maryland forceps were attached to the B arm and the camera to the C arm, crossing externally to avoid internal collisions (Fig. 3). A Bovie hook was inserted through the flank port to complete docking (Supplementary Video).

A photograph illustrating the order of the robotic B-, C-, D-arms as viewed from the front.

After identifying the critical view of safety, the cystic duct and artery were ligated using a robotic clip applier via the flank port. The gallbladder was removed, bleeding controlled if necessary, and wounds were closed.

The patient’s visual analog scale pain score was 5 immediately after surgery and decreased to 1 the next day. She was discharged on postoperative day 1 without complications. At a 2-week follow-up, the patient reported no pain and exhibited excellent recovery. Pathology confirmed cholelithiasis and chronic cholecystitis.

On outpatient follow-up, the wound exhibited excellent cosmetic outcomes (Fig. 4). The transumbilical glove port site scar was concealed within the umbilicus, making it nearly invisible. Additionally, the 8-mm port site at the left flank was barely noticeable from the frontal view. The patient also expressed high satisfaction with the wound appearance.

Postoperative wound photographs. (A) A frontal view and (B) an oblique left-sided view. The 8-mm trocar insertion site at the left flank (arrow) is less visible in the frontal view.

DISCUSSION

Currently, there is only a pure single-port robotic surgery system in the world. According to the authors’ experience, the da Vinci SP robotic surgical system (Intuitive Surgical) is very specialized in performing single-port laparoscopic cholecystectomy. However, the surgical system is limited to a few institutions, and it must be shared by multiple departments in a single institution, so there is some competition among surgeons for operating room occupancy.

Therefore, an alternative technique needs to be developed, and it is thought that reduced port robotic cholecystectomy using a multiport robotic surgical system may be one of the options. Kim et al. [6] performed robotic cholecystectomy effectively by applying lower abdominal three ports (one is for the camera, and the others for working ports), which will be covered by clothes (bikini line). However, if the advantages of the robotic surgical system can be used to reduce the number of unnecessary ports, the robotic surgical system can play a role in effective and efficient minimally invasive surgery.

Revo-i is a multiport robotic surgical system developed in Korea, which is now increasingly being used in general surgical fields. In particular, Revo-i cholecystectomy was first performed only at the clinical trial stage to obtain approval from the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety for clinical application [3]. At that time, the conventional four-port technique was used to evaluate the safety, durability, and efficacy of surgical instruments.

In this report, the authors successfully performed the Revo-i reduced (single-site plus ONE port) robotic cholecystectomy based on accumulating experiences of single-fulcrum laparoscopic cholecystectomy [4], da Vinci robotic single-site cholecystectomy [5], and da Vinci robotic single-site plus ONE port pancreatectomy [6] to expand the surgical usefulness of the Korean robotic surgical system.

Compared to our ArtiSential 1 + 1 laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LIVSMED Inc.) [7], the advantage of Revo-i reduced port cholecystectomy is that even if the trocar inserted in front of the left upper quadrant is moved to the left lateral flank area, cholecystectomy can be performed safely and effectively without any major problems due to the advantages of robotic surgery, and the cosmetic effect can be further enhanced. This cosmetic effect can be benchmarked from our earlier experiences of robotic single-site plus ONE port distal pancreatectomy, where the location of the additional port on the patient’s abdominal wall could enhance the cosmetic effect by moving the wound toward the left flank using the free wrist movement of the robot. On the other hand, current evidence has not demonstrated a significant reduction in postoperative pain with this approach. Further studies with larger patient cohorts are warranted as more Revo-i cholecystectomy cases accumulate to determine whether this minimally invasive technique offers any meaningful improvement in pain outcomes. In our practice, postoperative pain is managed with a standard analgesic regimen, including routine administration of oral nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and supplemental intravenous analgesics on an as-needed basis.

Based on our institution’s accumulated experiences with various minimally invasive cholecystectomy approaches—including single-fulcrum laparoscopic cholecystectomy [4], robotic single-incision cholecystectomy [8], robotic single-port cholecystectomy, and ArtiSential-assisted laparoscopic surgery [9]—each technique has demonstrated unique advantages in terms of clinical safety and technical feasibility. However, when considering both patient out-of-pocket expenses and system maintenance costs, further investigations from a cost-benefit perspective are warranted. Future studies should comprehensively evaluate not only clinical outcomes but also economic implications to determine which approach may represent the most reasonable option in real-world surgical practice.

Among the cases analyzed, Case 6 demonstrated a relatively prolonged operative time, which was primarily related to patient-specific anatomical factors and technical challenges inherent to the early phase of the Revo-i system. In this patient, a high BMI (28.2 kg/m²) and significant intra-abdominal adiposity limited the working space, while the transumbilical placement of both the camera port and a working arm led to instrument collisions that further prolonged the procedure. Consequently, the patient experienced slightly greater postoperative pain and required an additional day of hospitalization. In contrast, the extended operative times observed in Cases 3 and 4 are largely attributable to the initial adoption phase of the Revo-i system, reflecting the natural adaptation process and learning curve faced by the surgical team. These cases illustrate the typical technical challenges encountered when integrating a new robotic platform into clinical practice. As additional cases accumulate, the authors intend to further investigate the learning curve associated with the Revo-i system and conduct future studies evaluating its clinical and economic outcomes in a more comprehensive manner.

It is thought that the C-arm and B-arm fighting issue in a single-site glove port can be gradually controlled by the surgeon as he becomes more experienced. In addition, since it does not look ergonomic from the point of view of the assist surgeon, because it is necessary for the assist surgeon to complete a robot in the single-site glove port for retracting the gallbladder, it is considered more effective to perform cholecystectomy without additional gallbladder retraction, or tow the gallbladder using the method applied in single-fulcrum laparoscopic cholecystectomy [10].

With more experience and technical modification, it is expected that the Revo-i reduced port robotic cholecystectomy will be safer and more efficient. It will be able to enhance the cosmetic effect. It may be a very suitable method, especially in situations where there are no specialist doctors and operating room manpower is limited. The clinical application of Revo-i will continue.

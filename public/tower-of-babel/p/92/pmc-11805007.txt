Perceptions of Dentists in India Towards Transgender Patients: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Logesh Sidhu, Priyadharshini Ragavane, Priyanga Chandrasekaran, Angeline Hephzibah Pauljoseph, Kavi Ashika, Vasanth Raj
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Transgender individuals face significant stigma and discrimination in healthcare, leading to adverse health outcomes. This study investigates the perceptions of dentists in India towards transgender patients.Methods:A cross-sectional study collected data from 143 licensed practicing dentists in India using a 9-item structured questionnaire distributed via online forms from November 2023 to January 2024. The questionnaire assessed dentists’ perceptions, comfort levels, and educational background regarding transgender healthcare.Results:The sample included 62.9% female and 37.1% male dentists, predominantly early-career practitioners. Only 32.2% had treated transgender patients, and 25.2% had received specific training in transgender health issues. Despite limited experience and training, 71.3% of dentists expressed a willingness to treat transgender patients, and 84% recognized the societal benefits of acknowledging transgender identities as natural. Additionally, 87.4% supported the inclusion of LGBTQ patients in healthcare. Significant barriers were noted, with many respondents acknowledging problematic attitudes among healthcare staff towards transgender individuals.Conclusions:The study highlights positive attitudes among dentists towards transgender patients, despite substantial gaps in training and experience. Addressing these gaps through targeted education and sensitization programs is crucial for fostering an inclusive healthcare environment. Future research should focus on longitudinal assessments and the effectiveness of educational interventions to enhance knowledge and attitudes toward transgender healthcare.

INTRODUCTION

Transgender individuals often encounter significant stigma and discrimination in many areas of life, including healthcare. This stigma often results in negative health outcomes, as transgender individuals may delay or avoid seeking necessary care due to fear of discrimination or previous negative experiences.[1] These challenges are especially acute in dental care, where oral health plays a vital role in overall well-being and quality of life. It is essential and address the unique barriers faced by transgender patients in dental settings to improve their health outcomes.[2]

Dentists, like other healthcare providers, may hold conscious or unconscious biases that affects their interactions with transgender patients. These biases can manifest as discomfort, lack of respect for patients’ gender identities, or a lack of understanding of the unique healthcare needs of transgender individuals.[3] Such attitudes undermine the quality of care provided and contribute to the overall stress and anxiety often experienced by transgender patients when accessing dental services, further worsening their health disparities.[4]

Transgender individuals often encounter multiple barriers including financial constraints, lack of insurance coverage, and the fear of discrimination while accessing dental care. Nearly one-third of transgender respondents reported avoiding dental care due to concerns about mistreatment. Administrative obstacles, such as incorrect use of name and gender marker, can further alienate transgender patients and discourage them from seeking care.[5] These barriers underscore the urgent need for dental professionals to cultivate a more inclusive and supportive environment for transgender individuals.

Understanding dentists’ perceptions of transgender patients is crucial to address these barriers and improve the dental care experience for transgender individuals. Recent studies have aimed to explore these perceptions, revealing a complex landscape where willingness to provide care coexists with significant knowledge gaps and biases. While many dentists express a willingness to treat transgender patients, they often feel unprepared due to a lack of training and education on transgender health issues.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This cross-sectional study aimed to investigate the perceptions of dentists in India toward transgender patients using a 9-item structured questionnaire distributed via online forms. The study adhered to STROBE guidelines to ensure comprehensive and transparent reporting. Data collection took place from November 2023 to January 2024.

Participants: The study included 143 licensed practicing dentists from various regions of India. A convenience sampling methodology was employed, with the questionnaire link distributed through WhatsApp groups to ensure broad accessibility.

Variables: Primary variables included dentists’ perceptions of transgender patients, assessed through a nine-question survey. Additional data on participants’ professional backgrounds, such as years of practice, specialty, and previous training in transgender health issues, were recorded. Key outcomes measured included comfort levels in treating transgender patients, perceived knowledge adequacy, and willingness to undergo further training.

Data Collection and Analysis: Data were collected via Google Forms, with responses measured on a 5-point Likert scale. Descriptive statistics, such as mean, percentage, and standard deviation, were calculated using SPSS software version 21.

DISCUSSION

The data reveal a significant gap in experience with transgender health issues among dental professionals. Despite this, there is a positive attitude towards transgender individuals, with most respondents expressing willingness to treat transgender patients and recognizing the societal benefits of acknowledging transgender identities. These findings are consistent with similar studies, highlighting the need for targeted education and sensitization programs to enhance knowledge and attitudes toward transgender healthcare.[6]

Concerns about the attitudes and beliefs of healthcare staff towards transgender individuals were significant. Many respondents acknowledged problematic attitudes among front desk staff, nursing staff, and junior attendees as barriers to care.[7] Addressing these issues through targeted training and sensitization programs is crucial for creating an inclusive and supportive environment for transgender patients.

CONCLUSION

This study highlights both strengths and areas for improvement in the dental profession’s approach to transgender healthcare. Positive attitudes and support for inclusive practices provide a solid foundation. However, addressing significant educational and experiential gaps as well as mitigating attitudinal barriers among healthcare staff is crucial for fostering an inclusive healthcare environment for transgender patients. Future research should include longitudinal studies, expanded participant diversity, and evaluations of educational programs to enhance knowledge and attitudes towards transgender healthcare.

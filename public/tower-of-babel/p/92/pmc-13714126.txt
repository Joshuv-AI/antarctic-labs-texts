Third Order Upper Bound for the Ground State Energy of the Dilute Bose Gas
Authors: Morris Brooks, Jakob Oldenburg, Diane Saint Aubin, Benjamin Schlein
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe consider a dilute Bose gas in the thermodynamic limit. We prove an upper bound for the ground state energy per unit volume, capturing the expected third order term, as predicted by Wu, Hugenholtz–Pines and Sawada.

The bound (3) is expected to capture the correct ground state energy per unit volume, up to corrections of order Åρ2(ρa3), in the dilute limit ρa3→0, that are expected to no longer depend universally on a. The expression on the r.h.s. of (3) has a long history in the physics literature. The first two terms in the asymptotic expansion have been predicted in 1957, by Lee–Huang–Yang [26], based on previous work of Bogoliubov [9]. Two years later, in 1959, Wu [39] and then also Hugenholtz–Pines [25] and Sawada [36] predicted the third logarithmic correction. At the level of rigorous mathematical derivation, the leading order term has been established by Dyson [17] as an upper bound and, 40 years later, by Lieb-Yngvason [32] as a lower bound. For potentials with negative part, the corresponding result was obtained in [38]. An upper bound including the Lee–Huang–Yang term has been proven in [37], for sufficiently regular interactions (previously, an upper bound reaching the Lee–Huang–Yang order, but only recovering the correct constant in the limit of weak coupling, was shown in [18]). More recently, a simpler upper bound has been derived in [5], for a larger class of interactions. The case of hard-sphere potential, however, remains open (for hard-spheres, the currently best available upper bound on the ground state energy per unit volume has been shown in [3], with an error of the Lee–Huang–Yang order). A lower bound resolving the Lee–Huang–Yang contribution has been first shown in [19] and, for more general interactions including hard spheres, in [20]. Recently, a new proof of the lower bound, applying also to the free energy at low positive temperature, has been obtained in [21] (a matching low temperature upper bound has been derived in [22]). The result of [21] has been extended to potentials including hard spheres in [23].

Theorem 1 establishes an upper bound on the ground state energy per unit volume of a dilute Bose gas, interacting through V∈L2(R3), consistent with Wu’s prediction. A complete proof of the validity of Wu’s formula in the thermodynamic limit requires a matching lower bound, which remains a challenging open problem. Remark, however, that a rigorous derivation of Wu’s correction is available in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime, where N particles are confined in the unit torus and interact through a potential with scattering length of the order 1/N. In this limit, the ground state energy and the low-energy excitation spectrum have been resolved to the Lee–Huang–Yang order in [6, 7], through a rigorous version of Bogoliubov theory (leading order estimates and a proof of Bose–Einstein condensation have been previously obtained in [28, 29, 31]). Recently, extending the approach of [6, 7], Wu’s logarithmic correction to the ground state energy has been resolved in [10]; we will use ideas from [10] also in the current paper, in particular in Sect. 2.2 to define the kernel of the cubic transformation. Related results in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime (and beyond) have been derived in [1, 4, 8, 11–16, 24, 33, 34].

The proof of Theorem 1 is based on a well-known localization technique; given a grand canonical trial state ψL on a periodic box of size L=ρ-γ, with approximately ρL3 particles and with approximately the correct energy per unit volume, we can construct a grand canonical trial state on the thermodynamic box Λt with approximately the correct energy, first extending ψL to a trial state ψLdir on a Dirichlet box of size slightly larger than L and then combining several copies of ψLdir to cover the whole volume of Λt (to avoid interactions among particles in different boxes, they must be separated by small corridors, broader than the support of V). At the end, (3) follows by the equivalence of ensembles.

The main part of the paper is devoted to the construction of the grand canonical trial state on the periodic box ΛL, of size L=ρ-γ for appropriate γ>0 (to control the localization error produced by imposing Dirichlet boundary conditions, we will need γ≥3/2). Let us briefly compare the cutoff to the one used in [5] for obtaining the upper bound for the Lee–Huang–Yang order. In [5], a cutoff in momentum space is used which allows to define a non-unitary exponential of a cubic operator acting on the vacuum used to implement cubic correlations on top of the condensate and pair correlations. The crucial role of that cutoff is that it ensures that all relevant triplets of momenta adding up to 0 are generated by the same copy of A∗ in states of the form A∗nΩ and therefore have the right correlation structure. Note that the number of momenta that cannot be occupied due to this restriction scales roughly quadratically in the number of cubic excitations, and thus, for the number of triplets that is necessary for finding the Lee–Huang–Yang term, it becomes comparable to the total number of relevant momenta when κ=7/12. This is precisely the threshold until which the trial state in the extension [2] of [5] works. The third order correction requires new ideas due to the higher precision that is required. In particular, by plugging in the estimated number of triplets necessary here, the same heuristics explains why the critical threshold of κ=4/7 cannot be reached with this cutoff. The cutoff introduced here resolves this issue by imposing a restriction in position space rather than in momentum space. As a result, it works for κ arbitrarily close to 2/3.

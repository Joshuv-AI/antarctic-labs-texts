The value of urinary gonadotropin after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing central precocious puberty in girls with overweight or obesity: a single-center study
Authors: Beilei Zeng, Yuan Zhou, Ruofan Jia, Fan-sheng Kong, Panwang Huang, Min Chen, Feng Ren, Zhuangjian Xu, Yaping Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere are different opinions about the effect of body mass index on gonadotropin-releasing hormone stimulation tests in the laboratory diagnosis of precocious puberty in girls. The blood collection of precocious puberty girls with overweight or obesity is more difficult than those with normal body mass index. It is urgent to find non-invasive diagnostic methods for these girls. The purpose of the study was to evaluate the value of urinary gonadotropin after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing central precocious puberty (CPP) in girls with overweight or obesity.MethodsThis was a hospital data-based retrospective study. Overweight or obese girls with precocious puberty who presented to our pediatric endocrine clinic were involved. Triptorelin (Decapeptyl, 0.1 mg) stimulation tests were performed at 8:30 am. On the triptorelin test day, urine sample collected from 08:30 am to 8:30 pm was defined as the first stimulated urine. Serum and urinary luteinizing hormone (LH) were measured by immunochemiluminometric assay. Urinary creatinine (Cr) was also measured.ResultsThere were 52 girls with overweight or obesity. They were divided into CPP (n = 36) and peripheral precocious puberty (PPP) (n = 16) groups. The stimulated urinary LH (ULH) concentration corrected by Cr (ULH/Cr), ULH concentration, and ULH content in the CPP group were significantly higher than those in the PPP group. When the first stimulated ULH/Cr, ULH concentration and ULH content were ≥ 239.142 IU/mol, 1.690 IU/L and 619.750 IU, and sensitivity for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity were 90.9%, 78.8% and 81.8%, respectively, and specificity were all 100%, and the area under the curve were 0.939, 0.932 and 0.909, respectively.ConclusionsFor overweight or obese girls with precocious puberty, the first stimulated ULH/Cr (cut-off value 240 IU/mol) may be used as a possible alternative to the invasive blood detection of triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP.Trial registrationNot applicable.Keywords: Urinary gonadotropin, Girls, Overweight or obesity, Central precocious puberty, Diagnosis

Background

With the increasing rate of overweight and obesity in children, the incidence of precocious puberty in girls with overweight or obesity has also gradually risen [1]. It has attracted the attention of many pediatric endocrinologists and related scholars. Over the past 3 years, in the context of the novel coronavirus COVID-19 epidemic, the rate of obesity in girls has been significantly higher than before due to changes in the lifestyle, such as learning style of children and adolescents, dietary disorders, reduced physical activity and increased psychological stress, et al. [2]. It was shown that among girls aged 2 to 19 years, the rate of change in body mass index (BMI) between the ages of 6 and 11 years had the most significant increase (about 2.50 times more than before) [3]. Being overweight or obese is one of the essential factors leading to precocious puberty in girls. The new incidence of central precocious puberty (CPP) in girls has increased markedly along with the increase in obesity rate, and the progression of puberty is becoming more rapid [4]. An Italian survey showed that the new diagnosis rate of CPP from March 2020 to February 2021 was 4.2%, higher than the 2.3% during the previous period of one year [5]. In girls, being overweight or obese can raise the risk of precocious puberty, and the onset of precocious puberty may be earlier [6]. Compared to girls with normal BMI, girls with overweight or obesity would be more likely to experience earlier skeletal maturation and earlier menarche, which may influence the adult height [7] and cause corresponding psychological problems [8]. However, there are still some problems with the clinical diagnosis of precocious puberty in girls with overweight or obesity.

Certain differences exist in the laboratory diagnosis of precocious puberty in girls with overweight or obesity. In clinical practice, premature breast development in girls with overweight or obesity may be mistaken for excessive fat accumulation, which is not easy to attract parents’ attention and may be missed. For the diagnosis in the laboratory, there are different conclusions about the effect of BMI on gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) stimulation tests in girls. A study showed that the peak gonadotropin (Gn) after GnRH stimulation tests was lower in CPP girls with overweight or obesity (aged 7.6 ± 0.6, 7.5 ± 0.6 years, Tanner stage unknown) than in those with normal BMI, suggesting that BMI might have an inhibitory effect on GnRH stimulation tests [9]. Yet, another study considered that an increase in BMI was not associated with the level of peak Gn for CPP girls with overweight or obesity in late adolescence (aged 8.5 ± 0.3, 8.0 ± 1.5 years, Tanner stage IV) [10]. As can be seen, the specific impact of BMI on the peak Gn after GnRH stimulation tests remains unclear. Further studies are needed for the laboratory diagnosis of CPP focused on girls with overweight or obesity.

Profiting from the continuous improvement in the sensitivity of Gn detection technology, the application value of non-invasive urinary Gn (UGn) detection in children with puberty disorders has been enhanced. In the clinic, the blood collection of CPP girls with overweight or obesity is more difficult than those with normal BMI. It is urgent to find non-invasive diagnostic methods for these girls. UGn has been considered one of the initial screening indicators for CPP [11]. However, few studies have explored the value of UGn in the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. In this study, we measured UGn before and after triptorelin stimulation tests by immunochemiluminescence assay (ICMA) in girls with overweight or obesity. It is aimed to evaluate the diagnostic value and find the preliminary cut-off value of UGn for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a hospital data-based retrospective study. The blood collection of precocious puberty girls with overweight or obesity is more difficult in clinic. Our previous study showed that the measurement of UGn by ICMA within the 24 h valuable time window during triptorelin stimulation tests is a valuable method for evaluating the function of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (HPGA) as it can effectively reflect serum Gn levels in girls. Therefore, the purpose of the study was to evaluate the diagnostic value of urinary gonadotropin after triptorelin stimulation tests for CPP in overweight or obese girls. In the present study, we conducted a further assessment of the diagnostic value of spontaneous and stimulated UGn. In this study, the diagnosis of CPP and peripheral precocious puberty (PPP) was based on clinically confirmed. A flow diagram of the study design is shown in Fig. 1.

Flow diagram of the study design. CPP, central precocious puberty; PPP, peripheral precocious puberty

Participants

Overweight or obese girls with precocious puberty who presented to our pediatric endocrine clinic were involved in the study between April 2008 and October 2022. According to the criteria of overweight or obesity, a total of 52 girls were screened to volunteer for this study. Among these girls, 36 were diagnosed with CPP and 16 with PPP. The study received ethical exemption from our hospital’s Ethics Committee, following retrospective research guidelines.

The inclusion criteria are as follows. (1) Girls are diagnosed with CPP or PPP, and their BMI meets the criteria for overweight or obesity. (2) No significant abnormalities in blood and urine routine, liver and kidney function tests. (3) No medications affecting liver or kidney function or hypothalamic-pituitary endocrine function are taken during the study. (4) Girls have normal intelligence.

The exclusion criteria are as follows. (1) Girls are previously or currently diagnosed with major or chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases, hematological system diseases, nervous system diseases, urinary system diseases, genetic metabolic diseases, malignant tumors, etc. (2) Girls are diagnosed with clinical syndromes, such as Prader-Willi syndrome and Cushing syndrome, etc. (3) Girls have recent history of acute infection, such as fever and cough, etc. (4) Girls have recent history of special medication, such as glucocorticoids and hypoglycemic drugs, etc. (5) Girls are unable to cooperate throughout urine collection. (6) Urine samples are contaminated due to unqualified collection operations.

Main instruments and reagents

The main instruments and reagents included the standard height meter (Betterren, Shanghai, accurate to 0.1 cm), electronic weight scale (Betterren, Shanghai, accurate to 0.1 kg), automatic microparticle immunochemiluminescence analyzer and complementary reagents (ICMA, DxI800, BECKMAN, USA), automatic biochemistry analyzer and complementary reagents (HITACHI 7170 A, HITAC, Japan), and triptorelin aqueous injection (Decapeptyl, Ferring Pharmaceuticals, Germany, 0.1 mg).

Growth and development assessment

The height and weight were measured on the day of the stimulation test. The formula of BMI (kg/m2) = weight (kg)/[height (m)] 2 was used to calculate BMI. The Tanner stage was evaluated by the same pediatric endocrinologist. The more mature side should prevail if the breast development stage differed between the two sides. An X-ray examination of the left wrist joint was performed, and the bone age was calculated by the same pediatric endocrinologist by the TW2R method.

Triptorelin stimulation tests and sample collections

All girls received low creatinine (Cr) diet for three days before triptorelin stimulation tests. The triptorelin (Decapeptyl, 0.1 mg) injection was administered subcutaneously at 08:30 am, recorded as the starting point of the triptorelin stimulation test. Blood samples were collected before and 20, 40, and 60 min after the injection, respectively. Serum luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) were measured by ICMA. The LH and FSH concentrations before the injection were defined as the spontaneous LH and spontaneous FSH, respectively. The maximum values of LH and FSH concentrations at 20, 40, and 60 min were defined as peak LH (PLH) and peak FSH (PFSH), respectively.

Specific urine sample collection methods for timed 12 h of accumulated urine are as follows. On the day before the triptorelin test, urine sample collected from 08:30 am to 8:30 pm was defined as the first spontaneous urine, and from 8:30 pm to 08:30 am on the day of the test (before the starting point of the test) as second spontaneous urine. In the same way, on the triptorelin test day, urine sample collected from 08:30 am to 8:30 pm was defined as the first stimulated urine, and from 8:30 pm to 08:30 am on the day after the test as the second stimulated urine. The bladder must be made sure to be emptied (urine sample discarded) before collecting the first spontaneous urine. And then, accumulated urine samples were collected for each time period sequentially. Clean disposable urodochium is used for urine collection. Urine volume was measured by the weighing method. Urine samples were stored at 4 °C until detected. The collected urine sample would be sent for testing quickly [12]. Urinary LH (ULH) and urinary FSH (UFSH) were measured by ICMA. Urinary Cr was measured by biochemical analysis. In case of urine spilling or forgetting a particular voiding during the whole urine sample collection, the corresponding urine sample was eliminated.

Diagnostic criteria

The diagnostic criteria for overweight and obesity are as follows. Referring to the BMI percentile value table for Chinese girls aged 0 to 18 years, overweight is defined as a BMI greater than or equal to the 85th percentile and less than the 95th percentile of BMI for the same age [13]. And obesity is defined as BMI greater than or equal to the 95th percentile for the same age.

The diagnosis criteria of CPP need meet the following conditions [14]: (1)the appearance of secondary sexual characteristics in girls before 8 years old or the onset of menarche before 10 years old, following normal pubertal progression. (2)During the follow-up period of 3 to 6 months, the secondary sex characteristics continue progressing (no breast nucleus regression; Tanner stage of breast nucleus reaches stage II or over), the growth rate is accelerated, and bone age is more than one year beyond the actual age.

The diagnosis criteria of PPP need meet the following conditions [14]: If secondary sexual characteristics emerge before age 8 or menarche occurs before age 10 but does not meet the above diagnostic criteria for CPP, girls are diagnosed with PPP. Tanner stage of the breasts in girls with PPP ranges from stage I to III, with signs of regression or no progression of the breasts during follow-up. The two main types of girls are included. Girls have no characteristics of CPP, and the breast development completely and naturally regresses to Tanner stage I without treatment during follow-up. Girls have a clear history of exogenous estrogen intake or ovarian cyst lesions, and the mammary gland nodules shrink or even disappear after removing the cause.

Data processing and correction

The detection limits for LH and FSH were both 0.20 IU/L. For the convenience of statistical analysis, the index values below the lower limit were uniformly counted as half of the lower limit (0.10 IU/L). UGn concentration was separately corrected with urine volume and Cr with the following correction formula.

UGn content (IU) = UGn concentration (IU/L) × 12 h urine volume (L).

UGn/Cr (IU/mol) = UGn concentration (IU/L) ÷ Cr concentration (µmol/L) × 106.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed by SPSS version 26.0. Figures were plotted by GraphPad Prism version 9.0. All data were expressed as median (P25th, P75th). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the value of serum Gn and UGn for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated, and the pairwise comparison of AUC was performed. Sensitivity (proportion of true positives) equaled the number of true positive cases/(number of true positive cases + number of false negative cases). Specificity (proportion of true negatives) equaled the number of true negative cases/(number of true negative cases + number of false positive cases). It is considered statistically significant when p value less than 0.05.

Results

Basic information of girls with overweight or obesity

In the study, 52 girls with overweight or obesity were divided into CPP (n = 36) and PPP (n = 16) groups according to the final diagnosis. There were 51 first spontaneous urine, 52 s spontaneous urine, 50 first stimulated urine, and 50 s stimulated urine successfully collected. Among these girls, one case with first spontaneous urine, first and second stimulated urine not retained; one case with first stimulated urine not retained; one case with first spontaneous urinary Cr missed; two cases with first stimulated urinary Cr missed, and one case with first and second spontaneous UGn undetectable, the above-missed data were eliminated for ROC analysis.

In the CPP group, the chronological age and bone age were (7.79, 8.92) years and (10.48, 11.44) years, the height and weight were (132.65, 141.05) cm and (31.63, 38.78) kg, with 19, 14 and 3 girls in Tanner stage II, III and IV, respectively. In the PPP group, the chronological age and bone age were (6.42, 8.03) and (8.26, 10.73) years, the height and weight were (123.63, 135.98) cm and (27.25, 35.63) kg, with 8 and 8 girls in Tanner stage II and III, respectively. During 3 to 6 months of follow-up, girls with PPP showed no progressive breast enlargement, no obvious bone age progression, and normal growth rate. The BMI of the two groups were (17.85, 19.45) and (17.50, 19.49) kg/m2, respectively, with no significant difference (p > 0.05).

Comparison of serum and urinary Gn after triptorelin stimulation tests between CPP and PPP groups

The first stimulated ULH concentration corrected by Cr (ULH/Cr), ULH concentration, and ULH content in the CPP group were significantly higher than those in the PPP group (p < 0.05). There were no significant differences in the first stimulated UFSH/Cr, UFSH concentration, and UFSH content between CPP and PPP groups (p > 0.05). The second stimulated UGn/Cr, UGn concentration, and UGn content in the CPP group were all significantly higher than those in the PPP group, Table 1.

ROC analysis of serum and urinary Gn before and after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity

There were 6 cases with absent urine samples or abnormal indicators excluded when performing the ROC analysis. The details of the exclusions have been shown in the first paragraph of the Results. Finally, the complete data of 46 girls (33 CPP and 13 PPP) with overweight or obesity, were for ROC analysis.

The AUCs and cut-off values with the corresponding sensitivity and specificity of the first and second stimulated or spontaneous UGn/Cr, UGn concentration, UGn content, and ULH/UFSH for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity, respectively, are shown in Table 3; Fig. 2. AUC comparison of serum and urinary Gn before and after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity

AUC comparison of ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) and PLH

The pairwise comparison of AUC showed that the AUC values of the first and second stimulated ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) were less than those of PLH, yet the differences were not statistically significant. The AUC values of the first and second spontaneous ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) were less than those of PLH, with statistically significant differences. It is suggested that the first and second stimulated ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration and ULH content) may be no less valuable than PLH in diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity.

AUC comparison of ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) in stimulated and spontaneous urine

The pairwise comparison of AUC showed that the AUC values of the first and second stimulated ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) were significantly higher than those of the first spontaneous ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content). There was no significant difference in AUC values of ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration, ULH content) among the second spontaneous, the first and second stimulated urine. This suggests that the value of the second spontaneous, the first and second stimulated ULH/Cr (or ULH concentration and ULH content) for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity may be greater than the first spontaneous corresponding indexes.

AUC comparison of the corresponding ULH/Cr, ULH concentration and ULH content

The pairwise comparison of AUC showed that there were no significant differences in AUC values among the first (or second) stimulated ULH/Cr, the corresponding ULH concentration, and ULH content, as were the first (or second) spontaneous urine indexes. Evidently, the value of stimulated ULH/Cr for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity is not lower than the corresponding ULH concentration or ULH content.

Detection rate of serum and urinary Gn in girls with overweight or obesity

Detection rate of serum Gn

Spontaneous LH and FSH was detected in 44/52 and 50/52 samples, respectively. PLH and PFSH was detected in 52/52 samples. The total detection rate of LH and PFSH was 92.31% and 98.08%, respectively.

Detection rate of UGn

The first stimulated ULH was detected in 45/50 samples with a detection rate of 90%, the second stimulated ULH was detected in 48/50 samples with a detection rate of 96%, the first spontaneous ULH was detected in 17/51 samples with a detection rate of 33.33%, the second spontaneous ULH was detected in 30/52 samples with a detection rate of 57.69%. The total detection rate of ULH was 68.97%.

The first and second stimulated UFSH was detected in 50/50 samples with a detection rate of 100%, respectively, the first spontaneous UFSH was detected in 50/51 samples with a detection rate of 98.04%, the second spontaneous UFSH was detected in 51/52 samples with a detection rate of 98.08%. The total detection rate of UFSH was 99.01%.

Discussion

Currently, there are few studies related to ULH after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. The previous research from our team showed that the level of UGn within 24 h after triptorelin stimulation tests could be taken to evaluate the function of HPGA in girls with puberty disorders [15]. Accordingly, in this study, we further assessed the value of the first and second stimulated timed UGn for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. The results showed that the diagnostic value of the first and second stimulated ULH was greater than that of the first and second spontaneous ULH and probably not less than that of PLH, especially the first and second stimulated ULH/Cr. The AUC of the first stimulated ULH/Cr, ULH concentration, and ULH content reached 0.939, 0.932, and 0.909, respectively, and those in the second stimulated urine reached 0.923, 0.907, and 0.916, respectively. The cut-off values of the first and second stimulated ULH concentrations for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity were 1.690 IU/L (sensitivity, 78.8%; specificity, 100%) and 1.040 IU/L (sensitivity, 87.9%; specificity, 84.6%). Usually, UGn concentration is influenced by urine osmotic pressure, urine specific gravity, urinary content, urinary Cr, urine dilution or concentration, and so on [16]. Urinary Cr is correlated with total muscle mass, which is more variable in children than adults. Compared to children with a low red meat diet, urinary Cr concentration was higher in those with a high red meat [17]. Therefore, the difference in urinary Cr of children may be much greater. As the interference of Cr concentration fluctuations on UGn was excluded in the study, the value of stimulated ULH/Cr for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity may be better than the corresponding ULH concentration or its content.

Our results show that the AUC values of the first stimulated ULH/Cr were similar to the AUC values of PLH. When the first stimulated ULH/Cr was ≥ 239.142 IU/mol, the sensitivity was 90.9% and the specificity was 100%. When PLH was ≥ 4.815 IU/L, the sensitivity and specificity for the diagnosis of CPP were 90.0% and 100%, respectively. As can be seen, the value of the first stimulated ULH/Cr for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight or obesity is close to PLH. In the clinic, when girls with precocious puberty are accompanied by overweight or obesity, it is difficult for these children to collect blood because of obesity. The compliance faced a more severe test. The first stimulated ULH/Cr (cut-off value 240 IU/mol) index has good diagnostic efficacy and improved compliance, which can be a promising alternative to the invasive blood test of triptorelin stimulation tests. The prospect is attractive.

Few studies have investigated the cut-off values of peak serum Gn after GnRH or GnRH analogs (GnRHa) stimulation tests for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with obesity or overweight. In one study, overweight (n = 49) and obese (n = 16) girls with precocious puberty with 3.3 IU/L ≤ PLH < 5.0 IU/L were followed for one year and divided into CPP and PPP by the final diagnosis [18]. The ROC analysis showed that the cut-off value of PLH after GnRH stimulation tests for the diagnosis of CPP in girls with overweight and obesity was 4.16 IU/L, with a sensitivity of 60.6% and specificity of 65.6%. The study adopted GnRH (gonadorelin, 2.5 to 3.0 µg/kg, max 0.1 mg) for the stimulation test, while our study chose GnRHa (triptorelin, 0.1 mg). The data from 46 precocious puberty girls with overweight or obesity were analyzed in this study. It was suggested that serum Gn after triptorelin stimulation tests was a reliable indicator for diagnosing CPP in these girls. Among the spontaneous and peak indicators of serum Gn, PLH and PLH/PFSH were more valuable for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. The AUCs of PLH and PLH/PFSH were 0.977 and 0.973, respectively. When PLH and PLH/PFSH were ≥ 4.815 IU/L and 0.280, the sensitivities were 90.0% and 100%, and the specificities were 100% and 84.6%, respectively. There were some discrepancies in the results between our study and the aforementioned one. It may be related to the fact that triptorelin has a longer half-life than gonadorelin, leading to a higher stimulated level of PLH [19].

Compared with serum Gn detection, UGn detection has the advantages of non-invasiveness and convenience. Studies have demonstrated that spontaneous UGn can be used for the screening of CPP in girls [11, 20, 21], especially the first morning voided ULH [22, 23]. The initiation of HPGA is first manifested by an increase in LH secretion at night. It has been shown that the first morning voided ULH or nocturnal spontaneous ULH can reflect the secretion of serum LH during the night in adolescent children, and the increased ULH can indicate the onset of puberty earlier than the change in serum LH and the progress of clinical signs [24]. However, the first morning voided urine in most studies was usually not restricted to a fixed time for urine collection, which might not completely reflect the Gn secretion throughout the night. In this study, the first and second spontaneous urine duration was strictly limited from 8:30 am to 8:30 pm and from 8:30 pm to 8:30 am the following day before the stimulation test, respectively. The ROC analysis was performed on 46 precocious puberty girls with overweight or obesity. The AUC of ULH/Cr, ULH concentration, and ULH content in the second spontaneous urine for the diagnosis of CPP reached 0.823, 0.830, and 0.821, respectively, while those in the first spontaneous urine were 0.692, 0.710, and 0.683, respectively. When the second spontaneous ULH/Cr, ULH concentration and ULH content were ≥ 53.456 IU/mol, 0.155 IU/L, and 115.580 IU, the sensitivities were 69.7%, 75.8%, and 75.8%, and the specificities were 92.3%, 84.6%, and 92.3%, respectively. This suggests that the second spontaneous ULH is of value in diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity, while the first spontaneous ULH is of less diagnostic value. From our analysis, due to the pulsatile and circadian rhythmical variation of LH secretion, there is a fluctuation of LH in spontaneous blood and spontaneous urine, resulting in a relatively low diagnostic value of spontaneous ULH. Additionally, the insufficient detection rate of spontaneous ULH may affect the results to some extent. Therefore, spontaneous ULH is only useful for screening CPP in girls with overweight or obesity, which is far less valuable than the stimulated ULH index.

The present study is a preliminary study with a few deficiencies. The level of Gn may differ between girls with precocious puberty from different races and ethnicities [25]. The participants of the study were 52 Chinese girls from a single center. This may lead to potential influence from regional population characteristics (such as lifestyle habits and genetic background). Therefore, the small number of cases and ethnic homogeneity might restrict the generalizability of the findings. And, it may reduce the statistical power and stability of the conclusions. Nevertheless, our results can provide preliminary evidence that may guide the design of future studies. Future multicenter studies with larger and more diverse cohorts, including longitudinal follow-up, are needed to validate the diagnostic efficacy of UGn.

In general, our study confirmed the value of UGn after triptorelin stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. Recent studies have shown that UGn is of considerable value in pediatric endocrine-related diseases and is expected to substitute for serum Gn. Considering the possible influence of BMI on the results of GnRH or GnRHa stimulation tests, the reference standard of stimulation tests for diagnosing CPP in girls with normal BMI may not be applicable to that in girls with overweight or obesity.

Conclusions

The first stimulated ULH after triptorelin stimulation tests demonstrates diagnostic value for CPP in girls with overweight or obesity. In particular, the first stimulated ULH/Cr (cut-off value 240 IU/mol) may serve as a potential non-invasive alternative to the invasive blood detection for diagnosing CPP, though further validation is needed.

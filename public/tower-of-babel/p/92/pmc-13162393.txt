A clinical-biomarker fusion model for risk prediction of coronary artery lesions in Kawasaki disease
Authors: Qiongdan Zhao, Yonghong Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop and validate a predictive model integrating multidimensional clinical indicators and laboratory biomarkers for the early and precise assessment of coronary artery lesion (CAL) risk in Kawasaki disease.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 320 children with Kawasaki disease admitted between January 2020 and June 2024. The sample size was calculated using PASS 2021 software (α = 0.05, 1-β = 80%), with post-hoc power exceeding 85%. Patients were randomly allocated into a training set (n = 224) and a validation set (n = 96) in a 7:3 ratio. In the training set, univariate analysis identified CAL-associated indicators (P < 0.05). Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression was used for variable compression and feature selection. The core variables were incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression to identify independent risk factors. Based on these factors, three machine learning models—Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)—were constructed. Model performance was evaluated using the AUC, calibration curves, and DCA. Interpretability was analyzed using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values.ResultsNo significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed between the training and validation sets (P > 0.05). Univariate analysis identified seven significant indicators. LASSO regression refined these to five core variables. Multivariate logistic regression confirmed all five as independent risk factors for CAL (P < 0.05). Among the machine learning models, the GBM model demonstrated superior performance, with AUC of 0.872 (95% CI: 0.798–0.945) in the training set and 0.790 (95% CI: 0.623–0.957) in the validation set. Calibration curves were closest to the ideal line. DCA indicated net clinical benefit across a wide threshold probability range. SHAP analysis provided intuitive prediction interpretability.ConclusionAn integrated clinical-biomarker model was developed and validated for the early prediction of CAL in Kawasaki disease. The model demonstrates good discriminative ability, calibration, and clinical utility. and provides a promising tool for the early identification of high-risk children and the optimization of intervention strategies.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13052-026-02250-5.Keywords: Kawasaki disease, Coronary artery lesions, Predictive model, LASSO regression, Machine learning, SHAP values

Introduction

Kawasaki disease (KD) is an acute systemic vasculitis predominantly affecting children under 5 years old, and it has become the leading cause of acquired heart disease in children in developed countries [1]. Coronary artery lesion (CAL) is its most severe complication, encompassing conditions such as coronary artery dilation and aneurysms, which can lead to myocardial ischemia, infarction, and even sudden death, significantly impacting the long-term prognosis of affected children [2]. Although the administration of high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) and corticosteroids has markedly reduced the incidence of CAL, approximately 3%-5% of children still develop CAL after standard treatment, with some progressing to giant coronary aneurysms. Therefore, accurately identifying children at high risk for CAL early in the disease course, particularly during the acute phase, and promptly initiating intensive therapy and close monitoring are crucial for improving outcomes. Currently, clinical prediction of CAL primarily relies on univariable clinical experience or risk scores (e.g., Kobayashi, Egami scores). While widely used, the predictive performance of these tools is significantly influenced by geographic region and ethnicity, and they fail to fully utilize the increasingly available multi-dimensional information from laboratory and imaging data [3]. Existing studies often focus on the association between CAL risk and single or a limited number of indicators, such as C-reactive protein, platelet count, and albumin. However, the development of CAL is a complex pathological process involving systemic inflammatory response, myocardial microvascular injury, immune system activation, and treatment timing. The predictive value of a single indicator is limited and insufficient to comprehensively reflect disease severity and vascular injury risk [4]. In recent years, with the accumulation of medical data and advances in artificial intelligence, machine learning (ML) has provided powerful tools for handling high-dimensional, non-linear clinical data. Algorithms such as LASSO regression and Random Forest can efficiently screen core predictors from numerous candidate variables and build predictive models that integrate multi-dimensional information [5]. Particularly, the application of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques, like SHAP, helps demystify the ML “black box,” making model prediction logic transparent and enhancing clinicians’ trust and acceptance [1]. Consequently, this study aims to overcome the limitations of traditional univariable prediction by integrating multi-dimensional data encompassing demographic characteristics, clinical manifestations, laboratory inflammatory markers, myocardial injury indicators, immune status, and early echocardiographic parameters. Through univariable analysis and LASSO regression for feature selection, combined with multivariable Logistic regression to confirm independent predictors, we will construct and compare multiple machine learning models. The ultimate goal is to develop an integrated clinical-feature and biomarker-based early prediction model for CAL in KD [6]. We will assess its discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility via internal validation. By incorporating SHAP values and a nomogram to enhance model interpretability, this study seeks to provide a scientific and practical decision-support tool for individualized risk stratification and precise intervention in KD.

Materials and methods

Study population

This retrospective cohort study consecutively included data from 320 children hospitalized in our institution’s Department of Pediatrics between January 2020 and June 2024, who were diagnosed with KD and received standard IVIG treatment.

Sample Size Calculation: Based on preliminary experimental data and literature reports, the expected incidence of the primary outcome (coronary artery lesion) was set at 15%-20%. According to the empirical rule requiring at least 10 events per variable (EPV) in predictive model development, and planning for approximately 6–8 core predictor variables, the minimum sample size was estimated using PASS 2021 software. With a significance level α = 0.05 (two-tailed), statistical power 1-β = 80%, and accounting for approximately 5% data missingness, the calculated minimum sample size required was about 280 subjects. This study ultimately included 320 subjects, validated to provide statistical power exceeding 85%, meeting the requirements for multivariable analysis and machine learning modeling.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria consisted of: age between 1 month and 18 years; meeting the 2004 American Heart Association (AHA) diagnostic criteria for Kawasaki disease (KD); having received standard-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) (2 g/kg) during the acute phase within 10 days of fever onset; availability of complete transthoracic echocardiography data prior to treatment and during follow-up at 4–8 weeks of illness for the assessment of coronary artery lesions (CAL); and availability of complete baseline clinical and laboratory data.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: presence of known congenital heart disease, rheumatic heart disease, cardiomyopathy, or other organic heart diseases; incomplete clinical or follow-up imaging data which precluded the determination of outcomes; or presence of a giant coronary aneurysm (defined as a Z-score ≥ 10) before the initiation of IVIG treatment.

Data collection

Patient information was collected through the hospital’s electronic medical record, laboratory information, and picture archiving and communication systems, encompassing multiple dimensions. These included demographics (age and sex), clinical features (fever duration, KD type [complete or incomplete], IVIG non-response, and major clinical manifestations such as cervical lymphadenopathy, conjunctival injection, lip cracking, and extremity changes/desquamation) [7], and laboratory indicators (C-reactive protein [CRP], erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR], white blood cell count, neutrophil count, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio [NLR], B-type natriuretic peptide [BNP], alanine aminotransferase [ALT], aspartate aminotransferase [AST], albumin, platelet count, and CD3 + T-cell percentage) [8]. Additionally, treatment and imaging indicators were documented, including the timing of IVIG administration (days after fever onset) [9].

Grouping criteria

The primary outcome of this study was the development of coronary artery lesions (CAL), assessed via transthoracic echocardiography conducted after IVIG treatment and during the acute phase (at 4–8 weeks of illness). Patients were classified into the CAL group if they met any of the following pre-defined criteria: a Z-score ≥ 2.5 for the left anterior descending or right coronary artery, an absolute internal coronary diameter exceeding 1.5 times the normal reference value for age and body surface area, or the presence of a definite coronary aneurysm. Conversely, patients in the non-CAL group were defined as those whose coronary artery Z-score remained < 2.5 throughout the follow-up period, with all internal diameters staying within normal ranges. To ensure objective and unbiased adjudication of this outcome, all baseline and follow-up echocardiograms were performed by a fixed team of experienced pediatric cardiac sonographers. The original images and measurement data (including internal diameters and Z-scores) were anonymized and entered into the research database. Two independent researchers, who were blinded to all baseline clinical and laboratory information, then reviewed the original measurements against the pre-defined criteria to determine the CAL outcome. Any disagreement between the two researchers was resolved by a third, senior pediatric cardiologist, whose judgment served as the final arbitration for group assignment.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0, R 4.2.3, and Python 3.8.5. Normally distributed continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed data are presented as median and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data are presented as number (percentage) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test.

All 320 eligible children were randomly split into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio. In the training set, univariable analysis was first performed on all collected baseline variables to screen for those significantly associated with the CAL outcome. To handle high-dimensional data, select the most predictive core variables, and avoid overfitting, all variables with P < 0.05 from the univariable analysis were entered into a LASSO regression model for feature selection. The optimal penalty coefficient (λ.min) was determined via 10-fold cross-validation. The set of variables with non-zero coefficients corresponding to this λ value, which minimizes cross-validation error, constituted the selected core predictor combination. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor; all variables had VIF < 3, indicating no significant collinearity. Subsequently, the core variables selected by LASSO regression were included in a multivariable Logistic regression analysis to calculate their odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals, confirming and quantifying their value as independent factors influencing CAL. Based on the core predictors identified by LASSO, three machine learning prediction models were constructed: Gradient Boosting Machine, Support Vector Machine, and Convolutional Neural Network. Model hyperparameters were optimized on the training set using grid search combined with 5-fold cross-validation. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve assessed model discrimination. Calibration curves plotted the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies. Decision curve analysis evaluated the clinical net benefit across different risk thresholds. A visual nomogram was constructed, and internal validation was performed using the bootstrap method (1000 repetitions). Finally, the SHAP value framework was utilized for model interpretability analysis, elucidating the direction and magnitude of each feature’s contribution to individual predictions at a local level. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

LASSO regression for variable selection

To address potential multicollinearity among multiple variables and select the core predictors with the highest value, the seven candidate variables identified as significant in the univariate analysis were incorporated into a LASSO regression model for feature selection. The optimal penalty coefficient (λ.min) was determined via 10-fold cross-validation. The LASSO regression path plot (Supplemental Fig. 1A) illustrates the gradual shrinkage of variable coefficients toward zero as the penalty coefficient λ increases. The coefficient plot (Supplemental Fig. 1B) visually displays the trajectory of coefficient changes for each variable. Ultimately, at the λ.min value, five variables with non-zero coefficients were selected, forming the core predictor combination: duration of fever (X1), CRP (X2), BNP (X3), ALT (X4), timing of IVIG administration (X5). This result indicates that albumin and NLR were excluded during the compression process. The refined 5-variable model maintained predictive performance while avoiding overfitting.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis for early prediction of coronary artery lesions in kawasaki disease

The five core variables selected by LASSO regression were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model (Supplemental Table 1) to further quantify their independent predictive value. The results demonstrated that all variables were independent risk factors for coronary artery lesions (P < 0.05) (Table 3). Specifically: for each 1-day increase in the duration of fever, each 1 mg/L increase in CRP, each 1 pg/mL increase in BNP, and each 1 U/L increase in ALT, the risk of developing coronary artery lesions increased by 50.8% (OR = 1.508), 6.2% (OR = 1.062), 0.7% (OR = 1.007), and 2.2% (OR = 1.022), respectively. Each 1-day delay in the timing of IVIG administration increased the risk by 151.7% (OR = 2.517).

Performance evaluation of machine learning models

Their performance was comprehensively evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis. ROC curve analysis (Fig. 1) showed that in the training set, the Gradient Boosting Machine model exhibited the best discriminatory ability, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.872 (95% CI: 0.798–0.945). The Convolutional Neural Network and Support Vector Machine models performed slightly less well, with AUCs of 0.836 (95% CI: 0.755–0.918) and 0.831 (95% CI: 0.750–0.911), respectively. In the validation set, all three models maintained good generalization capability. The Support Vector Machine model achieved the highest AUC of 0.807 (95% CI: 0.688–0.925), followed by the Gradient Boosting Machine model at 0.790 (95% CI: 0.623–0.957), and the Convolutional Neural Network model at 0.782 (95% CI: 0.647–0.917). Based on the AUC values of the three models in the training and validation sets, DeLong’s test was performed to compare whether there was a significant difference in the discriminative performance of the same model between the two datasets. The results showed no statistically significant difference in AUC between the training and validation sets for the Gradient Boosting Machine (P = 0.082), Support Vector Machine (P = 0.645), and Convolutional Neural Network (P = 0.234).

ROC curve analysis of the prediction model in the training (A) and validation (B) sets

The calibration curves (Fig. 2) indicated that the predicted probabilities from all three models showed good overall agreement with the actual observed probabilities in both the training and validation sets. The prediction curves closely followed the ideal diagonal line, demonstrating excellent calibration. Decision curve analysis (Fig. 3) revealed that across the clinically relevant risk threshold range (approximately 0.05–0.50), the net clinical benefit of applying any of the three models was significantly higher than the extreme strategies of “treat all” or “treat none.” Within most threshold intervals, the Support Vector Machine and Convolutional Neural Network models provided a marginally higher net benefit, indicating their favorable clinical utility.

Interpretability assessment of model predictions

Based on the aforementioned AUC values, the Gradient Boosting Machine was selected as the optimal model. To enhance the clinical interpretability and decision-support value of the models, the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework was employed for interpretability analysis. The SHAP waterfall plot (Fig. 4A) visually demonstrates the direction and magnitude of each variable’s contribution to the prediction for an individual high-risk patient. For instance, in this example patient, delayed timing of IVIG administration (X5) was the most significant positive driver (SHAP value + 1.9) pushing the risk higher than the baseline expectation, followed by elevated CRP (X2, + 0.989) and prolonged duration of fever (X1, + 0.401). Furthermore, a visual nomogram was constructed (Fig. 4B). By converting the values of each continuous variable into “Points” and summing them to obtain “Total Points,” clinicians can directly read the corresponding individualized risk of coronary artery lesion occurrence on the bottom probability axis. This provides a convenient quantitative tool for rapid bedside risk assessment.

Discussion

The early prediction and intervention of CAL in KD are central to improving long-term outcomes in children [10]. This study demonstrates a CAL incidence of 17.86% among KD children receiving standard IVIG treatment. Although current guidelines provide treatment protocols, there is a lack of personalized tools capable of accurately identifying high-risk patients at the initiation of therapy. This study innovatively integrated multi-dimensional data, including clinical manifestations, systemic inflammatory markers, myocardial injury markers, treatment timing, and early coronary imaging. It employed LASSO regression to data-drively screen five core predictors and successfully developed and validated machine learning prediction models, including Gradient Boosting Machine, Support Vector Machine, and Convolutional Neural Network [11]. All three models demonstrated favorable discrimination (AUC: 0.782–0.807), calibration, and clinical net benefit in the validation set, with the Support Vector Machine and Convolutional Neural Network models showing slightly superior performance [12]. Importantly, the study translated complex model predictions into intuitive individualized risk assessments through SHAP waterfall plots and a visual nomogram, significantly enhancing the model’s clinical interpretability and practicality [13].The five core predictors identified in this study (duration of fever, CRP, BNP, ALT, timing of IVIG administration) profoundly reflect the core pathophysiological mechanism of CAL in KD, namely “cytokine storm-myocardial injury-delayed treatment response“ [14].

Delayed timing of IVIG administration was another critically important predictive factor (OR = 2.517) [15]. The study indicates that each day of treatment delay increases the CAL risk by more than 1.5-fold. This clinically reaffirms the importance of the “golden treatment window” (within 10 days of illness onset) [16]. Delayed treatment may allow the vascular wall inflammatory response to become uncontrolled, causing irreversible damage. By quantifying this risk, our study provides robust evidence-based support for prioritizing early treatment in scenarios with limited resources or diagnostic uncertainty. From a biomarker perspective, CRP (OR = 1.062) and BNP (OR = 1.007) represent the intensity of systemic inflammation and the degree of myocardial involvement, respectively. Persistently high CRP levels suggest a strong and sustained inflammatory response directly attacking the vascular endothelium. Elevated BNP reflects myocardial stretch or injury due to inflammation, ischemia, or microvasculitis. Its high correlation with CAL occurrence indicates that myocardial injury and coronary artery injury often coexist; BNP can serve as an effective peripheral blood marker for assessing the severity of systemic vasculitis [17]. Elevated ALT (OR = 1.022) may reflect a state of multi-system involvement; as the liver is an immune organ, its injury might signal a more extensive and severe systemic inflammatory response. Methodologically, this study adopted a “clinically-guided and data-driven” strategy. It involved initial screening based on clinical knowledge (univariate analysis), followed by the use of LASSO regression—a powerful feature selection tool—to automatically identify the most predictive, parsimonious set of variables from high-dimensional data. This approach effectively avoided potential issues in traditional methods, such as omitting important interactive information or introducing redundant variables leading to overfitting [18]. Decision curve analysis confirmed that using this model to guide clinical decisions (e.g., implementing intensified therapy for high-risk children at specific risk thresholds) yields definite clinical net benefit, superior to the strategies of “treating all intensively” or “treating all with standard therapy”.

The core innovation of this study lies in overcoming the limitations of previous risk scores, which predominantly relied on traditional indicators like age of onset and platelet count. It organically integrated BNP (reflecting myocardial injury), the timing of IVIG administration (reflecting treatment timeliness) [19, 20]. This multi-dimensional integrated model, encompassing “clinical symptoms + laboratory inflammation + myocardial injury + treatment timing + early imaging,” aligns more closely with the complex pathophysiology of vascular injury in KD. Theoretically, it can provide more accurate risk assessment than models based on single or few indicators.

Certainly, this study has limitations. First, it is a single-center, retrospective study with a relatively limited sample size. The external generalizability of the models requires further validation and calibration in larger, multi-center, prospective cohorts from different regions. Second, the model incorporated only baseline static data and did not consider the potential modifying value of dynamic changes in biomarkers during treatment (e.g., the magnitude of CRP decreases 24–48 h post-IVIG therapy) on predictions. Future research could explore building prediction models incorporating dynamic indicators to enhance predictive timeliness. Finally, the model was not specifically analyzed and validated for the distinct subgroup of IVIG non-responders.

In conclusion, this study developed and preliminarily validated an early prediction model for CAL in KD based on the fusion of clinical and biomarker data. By integrating key information on fever duration, inflammatory intensity, myocardial injury, treatment timing, and early coronary imaging, the model enables early, individualized, and quantitative assessment of CAL risk. The findings not only deepen the understanding of CAL pathogenesis but also, through the provided visual nomogram and SHAP interpretation tools, offer clinicians an intuitive and reliable reference, risk stratification and individualized treatment decision-making (e.g., whether to adjunctively use corticosteroids, whether to schedule earlier cardiac imaging follow-up) at the bedside. Future efforts will focus on further optimization and validation through multi-center prospective studies, thereby advancing KD management towards greater precision and efficiency.

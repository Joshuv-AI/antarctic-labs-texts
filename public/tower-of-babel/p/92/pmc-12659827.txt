A novel angulated cannula technique for Laser assisted in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) flap repositioning in left eyes by right-handed surgeons
Authors: Sudarshan K Khokhar, Amar Pujari, Nisha Yadav, Shivayan Srivastava
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) is one of the most commonly performed kerato-refractive surgery globally. Over the years we have observed that there has been difficulty in flap reposition using the conventional obtuse blunt cannulas, especially in left eyes of patients by right handed surgeons due to obscuration by the nasal bridge and hence difficult manipulation of the flap often leading to jerky and premature entry beneath the flap. This further leads to flap misalignment or incomplete repositioning. Laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) is one of the most commonly performed kerato-refractive surgery globally. Since its introduction in 1990, there has been a constant evolution in its technology to improve the visual outcome. Femtosecond (FS)-assisted LASIK has become the preferred method for LASIK flap creation due to the precision, accuracy, and reproducibility of the flap creation, and it may further help prevent cases of post-LASIK ectasia and have a superior refractive outcome.[12] Following this, the flap is raised with the help of a blunt spatula, which is inserted into the stroma close to the hinge. The surgeon gently moves the spatula toward the opposite side with a delicate but firm movement. If there are any adhesions at the interface, the tissue can be detached using the blunt tip of the spatula. Once the flap has been raised, the surgeon must take care to lay it out smoothly and avoid creating any folds. Some surgeons prefer to fold the flap over on itself like a “taco” shell.[3]

Once the ablation is completed, the corneal stromal bed is cleaned with a dry Merocel sponge. The flap is replaced onto the bed using a bent cannula (in the opposite direction of the hinge), and an interface wash is performed with balanced salt solution to remove any remaining debris. This also facilitates the floating of the flap back into its original position.[4]

Over the years, we have observed difficulty in flap repositioning using the conventional obtuse blunt cannulas, especially in the left eyes of patients when operated on by right-handed surgeons, due to obscuration by the nasal bridge and the difficulty in manipulating the flap. This often leads to jerky and premature entry beneath the flap, further increasing the risk of complications such a flap misalignment or incomplete repositioning.

Herein, we describe a novel interesting approach to improve the flap repositioning process in LASIK surgery using an acute-angle cannula, which can address the above-mentioned challenges and help achieve better precision in flap placement in left eyes by right-handed surgeons. The angulation improves the surgeon’s ability to reposit the flap accurately and with ease, as it allows for a better angle of insertion and more controlled movements [Video Clip 1].

Angulated cannula technique does not require specialized instruments or expensive equipment, making it more accessible to a wide range of practitioners and surgical settings. It also utilizes easily available and inexpensive tools, thereby increasing the efficiency of the surgery and reducing operating room time. It can be easily incorporated into existing LASIK protocols, as the learning curve may be less steep compared to other techniques requiring specialized equipment [Fig. 1].

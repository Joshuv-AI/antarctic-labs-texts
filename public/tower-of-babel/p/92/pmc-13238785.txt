Factors associated with discrepancy between glycated hemoglobin and glucose management indicator
Authors: Xiaomeng Wang, Xiaosa Chi
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We read with interest the article by Mizutani et al., 1 which describes a discrepancy between glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) and the glucose management indicator (GMI) in patients treated with sodium‐glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors. While this study provides valuable insights, several methodological issues warrant further consideration.

In the present study, patients in the SGLT2 inhibitor group had a significantly higher baseline HbA1c than those in the control group (8.4% vs. 7.5%). Previous studies have shown that the magnitude of discordance between HbA1c and GMI vary with the level of HbA1c. 2 In line with this, multivariate linear regression analysis presented in table 3 1 indicates that average glucose was independently associated with discrepancy between HbA1c and GM1. These findings suggest that the observed discordance may not be attributable solely to SGLT2 inhibitor use and could be partly explained by differences in the underlying glycemia between groups. Prospective studies assessing the discrepancy between HbA1c and GMI within the same individuals before and after initiation of SGLT2 inhibitor would provide more evidence.

The subgroup analysis in patients with type 1 diabetes should also be interpreted with caution. Although a positive trend was observed, no statistical significance was detected after adjustment, likely due to the small sample size (only 9 patients in the control group). Such limited numbers reduce statistical power and increase the risk of a Type I error.

Moreover, the discrepancy between GMI and HbA1c in type 1 diabetes has been reported around 26–56%. 3 In light of the high prevalence of HbA1c–GMI discordance in type 1 diabetes, comparison of baseline HbA1c–GMI discordance is essential to determine whether any observed changes can be specifically attributed to SGLT2 inhibitor therapy. Larger studies with adequate sample sizes and baseline‐adjusted analyses are therefore needed to draw more definitive conclusions.

We thank the authors for their important contribution and hope that these comments may be helpful for future research in this important area.

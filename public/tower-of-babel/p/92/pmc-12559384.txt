Optimized energy efficient clustering in WSNs through modified zebra optimization
Authors: Yeshen Lan, Mingqi Kan, Bingyu Cao, Chuchu Rao, Mingan Zhou, Peng Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAddressing the challenges of energy imbalance and the difficulty in optimizing cluster head selection in clustering protocols for wireless sensor networks (WSNs), this paper proposes a clustering protocol based on an improved zebra optimization algorithm (IZOACP). The method systematically solves the NP-hard problem of cluster head selection by integrating the zebra optimization algorithm (ZOA), Gaussian mutation strategy, and opposition-based learning mechanism, while optimizing the clustering process based on four key metrics: node residual energy, network density, intra-cluster distance, and communication delay. To further enhance data transmission efficiency, a dynamic adaptive inter-cluster routing mechanism is designed, which achieves path dynamic balancing based on node distance, residual energy, and load status. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to the LEACH, DMaOWOA, and ARSH-FATI-CHS protocols, IZOACP significantly outperforms the comparison schemes in key metrics such as network lifespan (improved by 97.56%), throughput (improved by 93.88%), and transmission delay (reduced by 10.12%). These results validate its superiority in energy consumption control, topology stability, and large-scale monitoring scenarios, providing an efficient and reliable clustering optimization framework for WSN information monitoring systems.Keywords: Internet of Things, Wireless sensor network, Clustering routing, Zebra Optimization Algorithm, Energy consumption, Network lifetimeSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) has established an interconnected ecosystem encompassing everything by means of intelligent devices equipped with sensors and communication modules. Among them, wireless sensor networks (WSN), serving as the core supporting technology, have occupied an irreplaceable position in the collection of industrial data1–4. For instance, it is crucial for data like images and audio that are utilized for deep learning5–9, machine vision10–13, and large model training14. This kind of data has a direct influence on production efficiency, product quality15, operational safety3,4,16–18, as well as deployment security19,20. WSN consists of distributed sensor nodes that can collect real-time environmental parameters such as temperature, humidity, and vibration, and transmit data through a self-organizing network. In AI scenarios such as intelligent manufacturing21–23, smart energy24,25, and smart healthcare, WSN provides high-precision and low-cost data support for device status monitoring, process optimization26, and resource scheduling3,4,27–29. With the high speed and low latency characteristics of 5G/6G technology deeply integrated with WSN, the industrial Internet of Things is accelerating its evolution towards real-time and intelligent direction.

WSN nodes typically rely on limited battery power, and energy efficiency directly determines network lifespan. Clustering has become a key optimization method to reduce the energy consumption of large-scale node communication and prolong system operation time. This method divides adjacent nodes into clusters, and the cluster head (CH) is responsible for data aggregation and forwarding, which can significantly reduce redundant transmission and balance the load30–33. Research has shown that a reasonable clustering strategy can reduce the overall energy consumption of the network by more than 30%, while improving data transmission reliability. It is an effective way to cope with dynamic changes in network topology in complex industrial environments.

Traditional clustering protocols can be divided into two categories based on design goals: static and dynamic. A typical solution, such as the LEACH protocol34, achieves energy balance through probabilistic rotation of cluster heads; HEED protocol combines node remaining energy and communication cost to optimize cluster head election35; PEGASIS uses chain clustering to reduce long-distance transmission36. In addition, layered protocols such as TEEN filter redundant data by setting thresholds, making them suitable for event driven industrial monitoring scenarios37. These methods lay the foundation for clustering theory, but their assumed static network environment and fixed parameters are difficult to adapt to the dynamic needs of actual industrial scenarios.

Although classical methods have achieved certain results, there are still the following bottlenecks: 1) Cluster head election did not fully consider energy heterogeneity, resulting in premature failure of some nodes; 2) Static clustering is difficult to adapt to node movement or topology changes, leading to frequent reconstruction overhead; 3) There are conflicts in multi-objective optimization (such as latency, energy consumption, and coverage), and traditional heuristic algorithms are prone to getting stuck in local optima. For example, the LEACH protocol may experience “energy holes” in non-uniform node distribution, while deterministic cluster head elections may exacerbate network load imbalance, limiting the large-scale deployment of WSN in industrial scenarios.

To address the challenges associated with clustering in WSNs, this paper proposes a novel Improved Zebra Optimization Algorithm Clustering Protocol (IZOACP) that incorporates gaussian mutation and chaos strategies. This approach aims to enhance the global search capability of clustering solutions, reducing the likelihood of local optima while optimizing energy consumption and network performance. Related work

On the basis of reviewing the existing literature, this paper summarizes the typical traditional clustering scheme, multi-factor clustering scheme and heuristic search mechanism in WSNs to better demonstrate the motivation of the protocol proposed in this paper.

Literature review

Recent advancements in cluster head selection algorithms for WSNs have addressed various challenges, particularly the inefficiencies associated with random election and uneven clustering. Chang et al.38 introduces an improved algorithm that leverages node distribution density to enhance cluster formation speed and balance, optimizing energy consumption. This approach builds on the limitations of the LEACH protocol but highlights a significant drawback: high computational complexity in calculating the adaptive cut-off distance, which necessitates further refinement for better scalability. Similarly, Abdurohman et al.39 presents a method that focuses on improving cluster head selection efficiency, emphasizing balanced energy consumption among nodes, while also acknowledging the need for exploration of its effectiveness across diverse network topologies. Focusing on energy efficiency, Ali et al.40 introduces the energy-efficient fuzzy-based unequal clustering with sleep scheduling protocol, which enhances network longevity through strategic clustering and scheduling. Utilizing Fuzzy C-Means for cluster formation, this protocol effectively minimizes data transmission distances and balances energy use, employing a fuzzy logic system to guide cluster head selection. This highlights the increasing trend toward incorporating intelligent methods in clustering algorithms to improve performance.

Moreover, Romany et al.41 proposes the Adaptive Parallel Seeker Optimization-based Energy Aware Route Planning Technique (APSO-EARPT), which integrates a weight-based clustering scheme and APSO algorithm. This model effectively optimizes both cluster head selection and routing, demonstrating enhanced network lifespan and energy efficiency. Although the simulation results are promising, the incorporation of data aggregation methods could further boost efficiency and scalability. Hada et al.42 presents a novel two-stage clustering process that expands the criteria for cluster head selection beyond residual energy to include distance metrics and node connectivity. Although the method shows promise through simulation and prototyping, its reliance on exhaustive search for algorithm selection raises concerns regarding scalability in larger networks. Rekha et al.43 describes the hybrid K-means ant Lion optimization approach for energy-efficient clustering routing, known as K-LionER. This method combines K-means for cluster formation with ant lion optimization for cluster head selection, effectively aggregating data from cluster members. While it shows improved performance in various metrics, future research is required to assess its viability in real-world applications and larger network settings.

Smaragdakis et al.44 proposed the Stable Election Protocol (SEP) to enhance the stability period of heterogeneous wireless sensor networks (WSNs). SEP assigns weighted cluster-head (CH) election probabilities to normal and advanced nodes based on their initial energy, ensuring a more balanced energy consumption and prolonged network lifetime. Simulation results demonstrated that SEP significantly outperforms LEACH in terms of stability period and throughput under two-level energy heterogeneity. Faisal et al.45 introduced the Zonal Stable Election Protocol (Z-SEP) as an improvement over SEP by incorporating a zonal deployment strategy combined with hybrid communication. In Z-SEP, normal nodes located in the central zone transmit data directly to the base station, while high-energy nodes in peripheral zones use the clustering approach. This design reduces energy consumption for low-energy nodes and extends the stability period compared to LEACH and SEP. Rao et al.46 proposed the Multi-Objective Clustered Relay Node Placement (MOCRNP) scheme, which combines a Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm (MOGA) for selecting cluster heads with a Multi-Objective Firefly Algorithm (MOFF) for optimal relay node deployment. The method jointly optimizes network connectivity, coverage, and energy efficiency, leading to improved lifetime and performance in WSN deployments. Moreover, Rao et al.47 presented the Multi-Objective Oriented Relay Node Placement (MOFF-RNP) method, which employs the Multi-Objective Firefly Algorithm to optimize relay node positions in WSNs. Unlike MOCRNP, MOFF-RNP directly addresses multiple conflicting objectives—such as energy consumption, connectivity, and coverage—without a separate clustering phase, achieving superior trade-offs among these metrics in simulation studies. Muhammad et al.48 proposed Genetic Algorithm based Fuzzy Optimized Re-clustering scheme (GAFOR). It aims to address the hot-spot problem in WSNs (Wireless Sensor Networks) while considering network lifetime and attack detection capability. GAFOR uses fuzzy logic to evaluate network conditions and combines genetic algorithms to optimize fuzzy membership functions for determining the next re-clustering timing. Simulations indicate that this scheme can extend network lifetime by up to 3.64 times while maintaining detection capacity and energy efficiency.

Recent advancements in clustering protocols for wireless sensor networks (WSNs) have focused on addressing energy efficiency, load balancing, and adaptability to dynamic environments. Kotary et al.49 proposed a many-objective whale optimization algorithm (DMaOWOA) to perform robust distributed clustering, integrating reference points to optimize energy consumption, coverage, and network lifetime. Sood and Sharma50 introduced the Lines-of-Uniformity based Clustering Protocol (LUET) for heterogeneous WSNs, which leverages geometric uniformity to enhance cluster stability and scalability across multiple applications.

To improve cluster head (CH) selection, Hemavathi and Latha51 developed a Hybrid Fuzzy Levy Flight Optimization (HFLFO) framework, combining fuzzy logic with Levy flight strategies to balance Quality of Service (QoS) metrics such as latency and energy utilization. Ali et al.52 presented ARSH-FATI, a metaheuristic approach for CH selection that dynamically adapts to network topology changes using adaptive ranking and fitness evaluation.

In industrial IoT contexts, Gong et al.53 designed MHCF-CECSO, a high-performance clustering framework that integrates chaotic evolutionary strategies to optimize energy consumption and data aggregation efficiency. Liu et al.54 proposed HPCP-QCWOA, a quantum clone whale optimization-based protocol that enhances clustering performance in integrated energy systems by addressing multi-objective trade-offs between energy efficiency and communication reliability. Further refining energy management, Liu et al.55 introduced DCC-IACJS, a bio-inspired duty cycle-based clustering method that optimizes energy utilization through adaptive sleep-wake scheduling and inter-cluster coordination.

For specialized environments, Luo et al.56 devised an innovative cluster routing method for underwater acoustic sensor networks, focusing on minimizing propagation delay and energy dissipation in harsh underwater conditions. Addressing security concerns, Xiao et al.57 proposed BS-SCRM, a blockchain-secured clustering framework that employs swarm intelligence to defend against malicious node attacks while maintaining energy efficiency.

Recent hybrid algorithms have further advanced clustering robustness. Luo et al.58 developed an improved Levy chaotic particle swarm optimization (LCPSO) algorithm to enhance cluster routing in industrial WSNs, achieving significant energy savings through chaotic initialization and adaptive mutation. Xu et al.59 introduced an Elite Niche Clone Evolutionary Computing-based QoS routing algorithm, which optimizes secure data transmission paths while balancing energy consumption and network load.

These studies collectively highlight the shift toward hybrid metaheuristic algorithms, quantum-inspired optimizations, and context-aware protocols to address the evolving challenges of WSN clustering in diverse industrial and environmental applications.

Motivation

Existing clustering approaches in WSNs often struggle with the NP-hard challenge of cluster head selection, leading to inefficiencies in energy consumption and network performance. Heuristic methods, while useful, tend to converge prematurely and remain trapped in local optima, which limits their effectiveness in finding optimal clustering solutions. To address these shortcomings, this paper is motivated to develop an advanced clustering method, IZOACP, which leverages intelligent optimization techniques inspired by zebra behavior. By incorporating factors such as node energy, degree, average distance, and transmission delay into a multi-objective optimization framework, IZOACP aims to enhance the clustering process. Furthermore, the integration of Gaussian mutation and chaos strategies is designed to improve convergence rates and overall optimization, ultimately leading to superior performance metrics in network lifespan, throughput, and packet loss rates.

Strategic ablation experiment

Table 5 presents the results of the ablation experiments conducted to evaluate the computational overhead of different strategies employed in the IZOACP algorithm. The table compares the computational overhead (measured in seconds) for four different scenarios: the basic ZOA algorithm, ZOA augmented with Gaussian Mutation, ZOA augmented with Opposition-Based Learning, and a combination of ZOA with both Gaussian Mutation and Opposition-Based Learning. The experiments were conducted under two distinct scenarios (Scenario 1 and Scenario 2) to assess the impact of these strategies on computational efficiency. In Scenario 1, the basic ZOA algorithm had a computational overhead of 2.08 seconds. When Gaussian Mutation was added, the computational overhead increased to 2.73 seconds. The addition of Opposition-Based Learning alone resulted in a computational overhead of 2.16 seconds, while the combination of both Gaussian Mutation and Opposition-Based Learning led to a higher computational overhead of 3.27 seconds. In Scenario 2, the basic ZOA algorithm had a computational overhead of 20.64 seconds. With Gaussian Mutation, this increased to 23.15 seconds, while Opposition-Based Learning alone resulted in a computational overhead of 21.28 seconds. The combined strategy of Gaussian Mutation and Opposition-Based Learning had the highest computational overhead at 25.03 seconds. These results highlight the trade-off between the enhanced performance provided by the additional strategies and the increased computational overhead they introduce. The ablation experiments provide valuable insights into the efficiency and effectiveness of each strategy, aiding in the optimization of the IZOACP algorithm for practical applications.

The proposed IZOACP protocol, despite its advantages, has some limitations. It has high computational complexity, which may reduce efficiency on devices with limited resources. Its adaptability to highly dynamic network environments with frequent node mobility and topology changes hasn’t been fully explored. Also, while the protocol focuses on optimizing energy consumption and cluster head selection, its ability to handle multi-objective optimization scenarios that involve multiple performance metrics like energy consumption, latency, and network coverage hasn’t been thoroughly investigated. Additionally, the integration of IZOACP with edge computing and machine learning for intelligent Internet of Things applications is still in the early stages and needs further research.

Conclusion

The proposed IZOACP protocol effectively addresses the challenges of energy imbalance and cluster head selection optimization in Wireless Sensor Networks by integrating the Zebra Optimization Algorithm, Gaussian mutation strategy, and opposition-based learning mechanism. Experimental results demonstrate that IZOACP significantly outperforms existing protocols such as LEACH, DMaOWOA, and ARSH-FATI-CHS in key performance metrics, including network lifespan, throughput, and transmission delay, validating its superiority in energy consumption control, topology stability, and large-scale monitoring scenarios. IZOACP not only provides an efficient and reliable clustering optimization framework for WSN information monitoring systems but also offers new insights and methodologies for the application of intelligent optimization algorithms in WSNs.

Future research will focus on the following aspects: First, further optimizing the computational complexity of IZOACP to enhance its efficiency on resource-constrained devices. Second, exploring the adaptability of IZOACP in dynamic network environments, such as node mobility and topology changes. Additionally, extending IZOACP to multi-objective optimization scenarios, simultaneously addressing multiple performance metrics such as energy consumption, latency, and network coverage. Finally, integrating edge computing and machine learning technologies to investigate the potential of IZOACP in intelligent Internet of Things applications, providing support for a broader range of WSN scenarios.

Ulcerated crusted nodules and an umbilical tumor-like lesion in a 48-year-old man
Authors: Ahmed Wassim Zrafi, Julie Seyler, Johnny Salloum, Isabelle Lamaury, Nadège Cordel
Published: 2026

Case presentation

A 48-year-old man presented with a 3-month history of progressively painful skin lesions involving the face, scalp, trunk, extremities, genitalia, and umbilicus, accompanied by 7-kg weight loss. His history was notable for unstable housing and polysubstance use disorder (cocaine, alcohol, and cannabis).

Physical examination revealed numerous polymorphic cutaneous lesions. Annular erythematous plaques were present on the trunk and extremities, along with multiple ulcerated hyperkeratotic nodules covered with thick adherent crusts on the scalp, trunk, limbs, and genitalia (Figs 1 and 2). An ulcerative-crusted nodule protruding from the umbilicus raised concern for cutaneous metastasis (Fig 3). Cervical and inguinal lymphadenopathy were present. No mucosal lesions were observed. A purulent urethral discharge was also noted.

Skin biopsies, complete blood count, C-reactive protein, HIV serology, syphilis serologic testing, liver enzyme testing, and bacteriologic swabs were obtained for diagnostic evaluation.

Because the patient’s social circumstances made reliable follow-up unlikely, empiric antimicrobial therapy was initiated at the initial visit with doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 14 days, together with cefixime 400 mg as a single dose and metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 7 days to cover common causes of urethral discharge.

The patient did not return for follow-up. Fifteen months later, during routine medical screening at prison admission, all cutaneous lesions had completely resolved.

Answer and discussion

The correct answer is C.

Malignant syphilis, also known as lues maligna, is an uncommon ulceronodular variant of secondary syphilis characterized by necrotic papules and nodules covered with thick adherent crusts. Although historically associated with HIV infection, it is increasingly reported in immunocompetent individuals.1, 2, 3, 4, 5

Cutaneous manifestations can be striking. Painful ulcerated nodules with thick “ostraceous” crusts may mimic deep infections, lymphoproliferative disorders, or metastatic malignancy.

In this case, the umbilical lesion initially raised concern for a metastatic process; however, infectious diseases such as secondary syphilis may occasionally produce tumor-like lesions.

Histopathologic examination of the skin biopsies showed pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, epidermal acanthosis with parakeratosis, neutrophilic exocytosis, and a dense dermal inflammatory infiltrate rich in plasma cells (Fig 4). Treponemal serologic testing revealed strongly positive antibodies (TPHA 1:5120) with a VDRL titer of 1:16, confirming the diagnosis.

Dense dermal inflammatory infiltrate with numerous plasma cells (arrows highlight representative areas).

Benzathine penicillin G remains the recommended first-line therapy for early syphilis. However, doxycycline is a recognized alternative when penicillin administration is contraindicated, unavailable, or impractical.

In this patient, doxycycline was selected because benzathine penicillin was not readily available and reliable return for parenteral treatment was unlikely. At later evaluation, the skin lesions had completely resolved and the VDRL test was nonreactive, supporting successful treatment.

This case highlights the protean manifestations of secondary syphilis and underscores the importance of maintaining a broad differential diagnosis when evaluating ulceronodular or tumor-like skin lesions. It also illustrates that therapeutic decisions must be adapted to the patient’s context.

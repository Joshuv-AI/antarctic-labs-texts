Clazosentan for cerebral vasospasm prevention in aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Saaed Abunada, Taimoor Ashraf, Dinesh Kumar, Hasnain Ali Moryani, Partab Rai, Sumiya Abbasi, Nisha Devi, Muskan Turesh, Shah Dev, Salman Hyder, Muhammad Aizazuddin, Sateesh Kumar, Laksh Kumar, Sidra Khan, Salih Abdella Yusuf
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) remains a devastating neurological emergency, with cerebral vasospasm contributing significantly to poor outcomes. Clazosentan, a selective endothelin-A receptor antagonist, has shown potential in preventing vasospasm, though its overall efficacy and safety profile require comprehensive assessment.MethodsWe conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines, searching multiple databases through March 2025. We included randomized controlled trials and observational studies comparing clazosentan with placebo or active controls in aSAH patients. Outcomes included vasospasm incidence, its complications, and adverse events.ResultsAnalysis of 13 reports (n = 5,728) were included. Compared to placebo, clazosentan significantly reduced vasospasm-related cerebral infarcts (RR: 0.56, p = 0.0002), delayed ischemic neurological deficits (DIND) (RR: 0.67, p < 0.0001), and the incidence of vasospasm (RR: 0.54, p < 0.00001). Compared to fasudil, clazosentan was associated with a lower incidence of vasospasm (RR: 0.44, p = 0.0004) and vasospasm-related cerebral infarcts (RR: 0.27, p = 0.002), but no significant difference was observed in DIND (p = 0.89). The drug showed particular benefit at higher doses (10–15 mg/h) and in surgical clipping patients. Clazosentan use was linked to a higher risk of adverse events, including pulmonary complications and hypotension (p < 0.05).ConclusionWhile clazosentan effectively prevents cerebral vasospasm following aSAH, particularly at higher doses in surgically treated patients, its clinical utility must be weighed against significant systemic adverse effects. These findings support selective use in high-risk patients while highlighting the need for careful monitoring and individualized treatment approaches.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-025-04429-5.

Introduction

Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) is a life-threatening neurological emergency caused by the rupture of an intracranial aneurysm, often requiring immediate intervention through surgical clipping or endovascular coiling to prevent rebleeding [1, 2]. Despite advances in medical and surgical management, aSAH continues to be associated with significant morbidity and mortality, largely due to the development of cerebral vasospasm [3]. This delayed complication, typically occurring between days 3 and 14 post-hemorrhage, is a major contributor to ischemic injury and can lead to delayed ischemic neurological deficits (DIND) and cerebral infarction in a substantial proportion of patients [4]. While prophylactic nimodipine is widely used to improve neurological outcomes, its efficacy in directly preventing vasospasm remains inconclusive, highlighting the need for alternative therapeutic approaches [5].

Among the various pathophysiological mechanisms implicated in cerebral vasospasm, endothelin-1 (ET-1) has been identified as a potent and long-lasting vasoconstrictor. Elevated levels of ET-1 in the cerebrospinal fluid and plasma of aSAH patients correlate with vasospasm severity, supporting its role in the pathogenesis of this condition. In response to this evidence, clazosentan, a selective endothelin-A receptor antagonist, has been extensively investigated as a potential prophylactic agent [6, 7]. The CONSCIOUS-1 trial, a pivotal phase IIb study, demonstrated that clazosentan significantly reduced moderate or severe angiographic vasospasm in a dose-dependent manner, with a 65% relative risk reduction at the highest dose [8]. However, subsequent phase III trials, CONSCIOUS-2 and CONSCIOUS-3, yielded mixed results. While CONSCIOUS-2 failed to show a significant treatment effect with clazosentan 5 mg/h in patients undergoing surgical clipping, exploratory analyses from CONSCIOUS-3 suggested potential benefits at higher doses [9, 10]. These findings highlight the need for further investigation into the optimal dosing and patient populations for clazosentan therapy.

A previous well-conducted meta-analysis included only randomized controlled trials (RCTs) but did not incorporate real-world studies, particularly from Japan, where the incidence of aSAH is 2–3 times higher than the global average [11, 12]. Separately, other meta-analysis included real-world studies but lacked subgroup analyses based on dosage, limiting its applicability to clinical decision-making [13]. Additionally, newer studies have expanded the available data [14, 15]. Building upon these, our meta-analysis systematically evaluates the efficacy and safety of clazosentan in aSAH, integrating both RCTs and real-world data while also performing subgroup analyses based on dosage to provide a more comprehensive assessment of its clinical utility and impact on patient outcomes.

Methods

Literature search strategy

This meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [16] and was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251030479). A systematic search was performed across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Cochrane library, and Google scholar, to identify studies investigating the efficacy and safety of clazosentan in patients with aSAH. The search strategy incorporated a combination of controlled vocabulary (e.g., MeSH terms) and free-text keywords related to clazosentan, aSAH, and vasospasm. (Supplementary Table 1) The search was Limited to studies published from database inception until March 2025, with no restrictions on language or publication status. The study selection process involved two stages: initial screening of titles and abstracts, followed by full-text review of potentially eligible studies. To ensure comprehensiveness, the reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews were manually screened for additional eligible publications.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using Review Manager (RevMan version 5.4). Dichotomous outcomes were pooled using risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using random-effects model. Heterogeneity among studies was assessed using the I² statistic and Cochran’s Q test. An I² value < 50% indicated low heterogeneity, while values ≥ 50–75% suggested moderate heterogeneity. An I² value > 75% was considered indicative of significant heterogeneity, and sensitivity analyses were conducted for such cases by sequentially excluding individual studies and examining their impact on the pooled results [19]. Subgroup analyses were planned based on clazosentan dosage, control group, and aneurysm treatment modality. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05 for all analyses.

Results

A total of 218 records were identified through database searches, with 134 remaining after duplicate removal. After screening and full-text assessment, 12 studies with 13 reports met the inclusion criteria and were included in the pooled analysis [8, 9, 14, 15, 20–27], while one study was excluded due to a lack of reported outcomes [28]. Summary of included studies

This meta-analysis included 12 studies with 13 reports [8, 9, 14, 15, 20–27], comprising RCTs and retrospective cohort studies, published between 2005 and 2024, with a total of 5,728 participants. Among these, 2,287 received clazosentan, 2,270 were treated with fasudil hydrochloride, and 1,171 were assigned to the placebo group. The study population consisted of 1,727 males and 3,843 females. Clazosentan was administered at doses Ranging from 1 mg/h to 15 mg/h for a duration of 14 to 15 days, with treatment initiation occurring within 24 to 96 h. The mean age of participants varied between 45 and 80.2 years. Aneurysmal treatment modalities included surgical clipping, endovascular coiling, or a combination of both. The analysis encompassed two Phase II trials, six Phase III trials, and five retrospective cohort studies. Clazosentan was evaluated in comparison to placebo or fasudil hydrochloride, offering a comprehensive assessment of its efficacy and safety across diverse clinical settings. Quality assessment

The quality assessment of the included studies was conducted using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for RCTs and the NOS for cohort studies. The RCTs demonstrated a generally low risk of bias across key domains such as random sequence generation, allocation concealment, and blinding. (Supplementary Fig. 1) For the cohort studies, the methodological quality was high, with total scores Ranging from 7 to 8 out of 9, indicating a low risk of bias. All studies had strong selection criteria, adequate follow-up, and well-defined outcome assessments. (Supplementary Table 2).

Vasospasm-related new cerebral infarct

Clazosentan was associated with a significantly lower risk of vasospasm-related new cerebral infarct compared to placebo (RR: 0.56; 95% CI: 0.42–0.76; P = 0.0002; I² = 56%). Similarly, compared to Fasudil hydrochloride, Clazosentan also demonstrated a reduced risk (RR: 0.27; 95% CI: 0.12–0.61; P = 0.002; I² = 0%). Discussion

Given the complex therapeutic landscape of cerebral vasospasm management following aSAH, our meta-analysis provides nuanced insights into clazosentan’s role. This analysis demonstrates that clazosentan significantly reduces vasospasm-related complications, including new cerebral infarcts and overall vasospasm incidence, compared to both placebo and fasudil hydrochloride, though it showed no significant benefit over fasudil for DIND or rescue therapy. These benefits were counterbalanced by a higher incidence of adverse events, such as anemia, hypotension, pleural effusion, and pulmonary edema. Subgroup analyses revealed dose-dependent efficacy, with optimal vasospasm prevention at doses of 10 and 15 mg/h, though these were also associated with increased toxicity. While clazosentan did not worsen functional outcomes (GOSE < 4) or increase mortality, it also did not significantly improve them.

Dose-Dependent efficacy

Clazosentan, an endothelin receptor antagonist, has gained attention for its targeted approach to mitigating vasospasm by inhibiting ET-1, a key mediator of cerebral vasoconstriction. By addressing the underlying pathophysiology, clazosentan presents a promising adjunctive strategy for improving outcomes in aSAH management [7]. Evidence from multiple meta-analyses suggests a clear dose-dependent efficacy, with doses of 10 and 15 mg/h demonstrating significant reductions in vasospasm incidence, vasospasm-related DINDs, and new cerebral infarction [11, 29], reinforcing the notion that greater dosage levels yield more pronounced protective effects. This reduction in vasospasm-related complications may help alleviate neurological deficits and long-term disabilities associated with aSAH. However, the ineffectiveness of doses 1 and 5 mg/h in preventing cerebral infarction remains poorly understood. Possible explanations include inadequate ET-1 inhibition, insufficient cerebrovascular effects, or failure to reach the necessary pharmacodynamic threshold for clinical efficacy. Additionally, factors such as interpatient variability, limited tissue penetration, and a lack of impact on non-vasospasm mechanisms, including inflammation and microcirculatory dysfunction, may contribute to the diminished effects at lower doses [11].

Microcirculatory considerations and multimodal injury

Despite clazosentan’s efficacy in reducing vasospasm-related complications, its therapeutic impact on functional outcomes—as measured by the GOSE—remains limited, a finding corroborated by multiple meta-analyses [11, 29]. This dissociation may reflect the multifactorial pathophysiology of aSAH, wherein initial hemorrhage severity, intervention timing, and pre-existing comorbidities independently influence recovery trajectories [30]. Importantly, while clazosentan effectively mitigates large-artery vasospasm through endothelin-1 inhibition, its minimal effects on concomitant microcirculatory dysfunction (e.g., microthromboembolism, endothelial injury, and cortical spreading ischemia) likely contribute to the observed ceiling in functional improvement [31]. This may explain why the prevention of vasospasm does not consistently translate into improved functional outcomes. These mechanistic insights underscore the need for multimodal therapeutic strategies that concurrently target macrovascular vasospasm and microvascular perturbations to optimize long-term neurological outcomes.

Rescue therapy and mortality

The lower need for rescue therapy in the clazosentan group compared to placebo suggests that its use may reduce the necessity for invasive interventions, such as endovascular procedures or surgical treatments, which carry inherent risks and complications [32]. Other meta-analyses have reported similar findings, reinforcing the potential of clazosentan to decrease the reliance on additional therapeutic interventions in aSAH management [11, 13]. However, its use did not significantly improve overall mortality rates compared to placebo or fasudil hydrochloride. This aligns with previous studies indicating that while vasospasm is a critical determinant of aSAH outcomes, other factors—such as early brain injury and systemic complications—play substantial roles in mortality risk [11, 13, 29]. Therefore, while clazosentan addresses one component of aSAH pathophysiology, a multimodal treatment approach remains essential for optimizing patient outcomes.

Safety profile

The safety analysis highlighted key considerations regarding clazosentan’s interaction with standard care. Its use was associated with a significantly higher incidence of pulmonary complications, hypotension, and anemia compared to both placebo and fasudil hydrochloride. However, no significant difference was observed in the rate of cerebral hemorrhage, suggesting that its adverse effects are primarily driven by its vasodilatory properties rather than an increased risk of intracranial bleeding [7]. These findings align with previous studies, which have reported similar safety concerns, reinforcing the need for careful patient selection and monitoring when incorporating clazosentan into aSAH management [11, 13, 29].

Procedural context

Subgroup analysis revealed that clazosentan’s efficacy varied based on the aneurysm treatment approach. In patients undergoing surgical clipping, its use was associated with a significant reduction in vasospasm-related new cerebral infarcts, vasospasm incidence, and a lower need for rescue therapy. Conversely, in endovascular coiling patients, while clazosentan reduced vasospasm-related DIND, it did not significantly impact vasospasm incidence or infarct occurrence. These findings underscore the potential for a procedural-stratified approach in clinical practice. Given the higher mechanical vessel trauma and subsequent endothelin-1 release associated with surgical clipping, clazosentan demonstrated more pronounced benefits in this cohort [33]. This suggests that its use may be prioritized in surgically treated patients while being selectively considered for high-risk endovascular coiling cases, such as those with extensive cisternal clot burden. Additionally, its ability to reduce the need for rescue therapy further supports its role in surgical clipping patients, where secondary interventions pose greater risks.

Clinical implementation

The Neurocritical Care Society guideline (2023) [34] strongly recommends against the use of endothelin receptor antagonists, including clazosentan, given the absence of benefit on mortality or functional outcomes and the increased risk of adverse events. Our analysis aligns with this position, showing no improvements in mortality or functional recovery, while confirming a significantly higher incidence of anemia, pleural effusion, hypotension, and pulmonary edema. Nevertheless, clazosentan consistently reduced vasospasm, vasospasm-related infarction, DIND, and the need for rescue therapy, suggesting that it exerts meaningful effects on surrogate endpoints. These findings raise the possibility of a more selective application, particularly in patients unable to receive calcium channel inhibitors—such as those with refractory hypotension, severe intolerance, allergy, or inability to tolerate oral nimodipine—or in cases where vasospasm persists despite optimal therapy. In such contexts, endothelin receptor antagonists may represent a potential adjunctive or alternative option, warranting further investigation in carefully defined populations.

Strengths and limitations

This meta-analysis offers strong methodological and clinical insights. By including both RCTs and cohort studies, it enhances evidence synthesis and generalizability. Multiple outcome measures provide a holistic view of clazosentan’s efficacy, with low heterogeneity strengthening result consistency. Stratified analyses by dose and procedure yield practice-relevant insights. However, several Limitations warrant consideration. The observed moderate heterogeneity in vasospasm-related outcomes likely reflects variability in dosing protocols and procedural approaches, including differences between surgical clipping and Endovascular coiling. While our analysis incorporated 13 studies, the lack of individual patient data precluded assessment of potentially influential factors like initial hemorrhage severity or clot burden. Variations in standard-of-care practices across centers may further influence outcome interpretation. Additionally, certain endpoints could only be compared with placebo, as corresponding data for fasudil hydrochloride were unavailable. Methodologically, our clinically informative subgroup analyses were constrained by reduced statistical power from sample fragmentation. These limitations highlight opportunities for future research with standardized protocols and patient-level data to refine clazosentan’s therapeutic role within personalized, multimodal strategies that address the full spectrum of aSAH pathophysiology.

Conclusion

Clazosentan represents a valuable but context-dependent tool in aSAH management. Its robust effects on vasospasm-related cerebral infarct and DIND, particularly in surgical patients and at higher doses, justify selective incorporation into treatment protocols. However, clinicians must weigh these benefits against the drug’s systemic effects and recognize that vasospasm prevention constitutes just one component of comprehensive aSAH care.

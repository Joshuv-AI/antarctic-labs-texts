Association Between Triglyceride Glucose Index and Postherpetic Neuralgia in Patients with Acute Herpes Zoster: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Ziwei Lu, Chengcheng Zhao, Tong Zhi, Huadong Ni, Ming Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionInsulin resistance (IR) is an important factor in the development of chronic pain. The triglyceride–glucose (TyG) index is a convenient measure of insulin resistance. However, as a common subtype of chronic pain, the relationship between postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) and the TyG index remains unclear. Through a retrospective analysis of the relationship between the TyG index and the incidence of PHN, we aim to determine whether the TyG index possesses predictive capability for PHN occurrence, thereby enabling the development of strategies to reduce the incidence of PHN.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed the medical records of patients diagnosed with acute herpes zoster (with a disease course of ≤ 1 month) in the Pain Department of Jiaxing University Affiliated Hospital from November 2024 to May 2025. Eligible patients were those who had available data on triglycerides and glucose, and no history of diabetes. Relevant clinical data were extracted from existing records and subjected to retrospective analysis. The numerical rating scale (NRS) score was utilized as the primary outcome measure to assess whether patients developed PHN. Those who still had pain (NRS ≥ 4) were classified as the PHN group, while those without pain symptoms (NRS < 4) were classified as the non-PHN group. The association between the TyG index and PHN was evaluated using multi-model logistic regression. In addition, the nonlinear relationship between these indicators and the results was studied using a restricted cubic spline plot. Subsequently, subgroup analyses were conducted on the sensitive population. The predictive value of the TyG index for PHN was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic curve.ResultsThe retrospective study comprised 157 patients with acute herpes zoster, of whom 55 were male and 102 female, with a mean age of 64.87 ± 11.67 years. The TyG index in patients with acute herpes zoster exhibited a nonlinear relationship with PHN, with an odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) of 4.32 (1.56–11.97). A linear correlation was found between the triglyceride-glucose–body mass index (TyG-BMI) and PHN, with OR and 95% CI of 1.07 (1.02–1.11). The TyG index and the TyG-BMI showed similar performance on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve for evaluating PHN and have certain predictive value.ConclusionHigher TyG index and TyG-BMI are associated with an increased risk of PHN.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, ChiCTR2500113190 (retrospectively registered). Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is a common disease caused by reactivation of varicella-zoster virus (VZV) [1]. After primary infection, the virus lies dormant in the sensory ganglion neurons of the body. The latent virus can be reactivated when the body's immunity declines, such as due to age, disease, or drug suppression [2]. The virus replicates along sensory nerve fibers and spreads to areas of the skin that they innervate, causing characteristic unilateral, banded clusters of blisters that are associated with severe neuropathic pain [3]. Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is the most common chronic complication of herpes zoster and is typically defined as pain that persists for more than 3 months after the rash has healed [4]. PHN is severe neuropathic pain that can seriously impair the quality of life of patients. Studies have shown that the annual incidence of herpes zoster in China is 5.85 cases per 1000 person-years, of which about 15.84% of cases will develop into PHN [5]. This means that a significant proportion of patients with new herpes zoster cases each year will suffer from PHN in the long term. At present, the pathological mechanism of PHN has not been fully elucidated. The clinical treatment of PHN includes oral antiviral, nonsteroidal analgesic drugs, paravertebral block, continuous epidural infusion, pulsed radiofrequency, and other interventional therapy [6]. However, the effect is not ideal. This highlights the importance of identifying risk factors that can identify PHN early in order to refine prevention and management strategies for PHN.

The triglyceride-glucose index (TyG) is a composite index calculated based on fasting serum triglyceride and fasting plasma glucose levels. In recent years, it has been widely accepted as a simple, reliable, and cost-effective alternative biomarker for the assessment of insulin resistance (IR) [7, 8]. IR is characterized by increased cellular insulin levels and decreased insulin sensitivity and is a major driver of metabolic dysfunction [9–11]. A large number of clinical data and epidemiological studies confirm the high coexistence of IR with a variety of chronic pain states. Pappolla et al. found that 100% of the fibromyalgia subgroup in their study had abnormalities in laboratory measures pointing to IR [12]. Akintoye et al. showed that diabetic patients with painful neuropathy had a significantly higher degree of IR than diabetic patients without neuropathy [13]. However, the association is not limited to specific diseases. A large-sample study based on the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey found that the TyG index demonstrated a nonlinear and positive relationship with chronic pain. Similarly, the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP), another indicator of IR, was associated with increased risk for chronic pain [12]. It has also been found that the TyG-BMI, which is derived by combining the TyG index with the body mass index (BMI), is considered to be a more effective indicator for assessing IR [13, 14]. A large number of studies show that the TyG index and TyG-BMI are not only closely related to cardiovascular disease, but also significantly related to pain, which provides a new perspective for understanding the pathophysiological mechanism of pain. However, studies on the relationship between these two indicators and PHN are lacking. Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the potential relationship between the TyG index and TyG-BMI and PHN.

This study provides a theoretical basis for future prospective studies to further verify the potential role of the TyG index and TyG-BMI in the prediction of PHN by analyzing existing clinical data. The findings of this study may assist clinical practitioners in identifying patients at high risk of PHN, facilitating the development of early intervention strategies, and consequently reducing the incidence of PHN. For patients, the early identification of pain and the implementation of enhanced interventions may result in a marked improvement in pain outcomes and an enhancement in long-term quality of life. This study hypothesized that TyG index and TyG-BMI were significantly associated with the occurrence of PHN and thus had predictive value.

Methods

Study Population and Design

This retrospective study analyzed the medical records of patients, aiming to investigate the relationship between the TyG index and PHN. Assuming a two-tailed alpha level of 0.05, power of 0.9, exposure rate of 0.05 in the case group, exposure rate of 0.3 in the control group, and sample ratio of 1 between the two groups, the required sample size for the case group was n = 46.4. The final sample size required a minimum of 55 cases and 55 controls, in order to account for a 15% loss to follow-up and refusal rate. The study comprised patients diagnosed with acute herpes zoster (International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Edition [ICD-10], corresponding core code B02) by two or more experienced pain specialists in the Pain Management Department of Jiaxing University Affiliated Hospital between November 2024 and May 2025. The study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments, with the exception that this trial was retrospectively registered. The study was reviewed and approved by the Academic Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Jiaxing University (Jiaxing First Hospital), with acceptance number 2025-KY-119, and was deemed to be exempt from the requirement for informed consent. The numerical rating scale (NRS) score was used to assess whether patients developed PHN. Those who still had pain (NRS ≥ 4) were classified as the PHN group, while those without pain symptoms (NRS < 4) were classified as the non-PHN group. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with acute herpes zoster (duration ≤ 1 month) in the Affiliated Hospital of Jiaxing University from October 2024 to March 2025; (2) age ≥ 18 years; and (3) patients who had undergone pulsed radiofrequency treatment. Patients were excluded for any of the following: (1) missing results of triglyceride and glucose tests; (2) missing covariate records; (3) confirmed history of diabetes; (4) history of hyperlipidemia; (5) chronic pain or neuropathy caused by other factors; (6) severe immunodeficiency or use of immunosuppressants; (7) active malignant tumors; (8) severe dysfunction of heart, liver, or kidney; (9) regnant or lactating women; (10) previous history of herpes zoster or having received the herpes zoster vaccine; (11) history of mental illness or cognitive impairment; or (12) loss to follow-up. Exposure and Outcome Definitions

In this study, the exposure variables were the TyG index and TyG-BMI. The formula for calculating the TyG index is as follows: ln[TG (mg/dl) × FPG (mg/dl)/2], where TG is triglycerides and FPG is fasting plasma glucose. The formula for calculating BMI is as follows: weight (kg)/height (m2). The formula for calculating the TYG-BMI is TyG × BMI. The primary outcome measure was the NRS score. The NRS is a tool that utilizes a 0-to-10 numerical scale for the purpose of evaluating pain intensity. Historically, the NRS has been regarded as the gold standard for pain assessment by the American Pain Society. On this scale, 0 indicates no pain, while 10 represents the most severe pain. Patients’ NRS scores were determined through follow-up visits. Participants with an NRS score of 4 or more were assigned to the PHN group, while those with an NRS score of less than 4 were assigned to the non-PHN group.

Variable Extraction

Baseline indicators included sociodemographic characteristics (age, gender, education level), lifestyle factors (BMI, alcohol consumption, and smoking), common diseases (hypertension), disease duration, side, location, and preoperative NRS score. Gender was classified as male or female. Educational attainment was classified into four categories: illiterate (no formal education), grade 9 and below, high school or General Educational Development (GED) equivalent, and college graduate or above. Alcohol use was divided into “no” and “yes.” Smoking was defined as either not smoking (no) or smoking (yes). Hypertension was classified as either nonexistent or existent (the records were based on the electronic medical record system). Sides were divided into the left side and the right side. The parts were divided into three sections: limbs, trunk, and head and neck.

Statistical Analysis

In this study, fasting test results of patients diagnosed with acute herpes zoster at the Affiliated Hospital of Jiaxing University from November 2024 to May 2025 were used. According to previous studies, continuous variables with a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and evaluated using the independent-samples t-test. Continuous variables with non-normal distribution are expressed as the median (quartile) and evaluated using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed in terms of frequency and percentage and evaluated using the chi-square test. Multivariate logistic regression was used to test the relationship between the TyG index and TyG-BMI and the occurrence of PHN. Model 1 adopts no variables at all. Age, gender, and educational attainment were incorporated into the formulation for Model 2. Based on Model 2, further adjustments were made to alcohol consumption, smoking, hypertension, BMI, preoperative NRS score, side, and location in Model 3. These reports include odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Based on Model 3, a restricted cubic spline (RCS) plot was used to evaluate the potential nonlinear correlation between exposure and results. Subgroup analysis was constructed and stratified by age (< 60/ ≥ 60 years), BMI (< 24/ ≥ 24), gender (male/female), hypertension (yes/no), side (left/right), and part (limbs, trunk, head and neck). All confounding factors were adjusted in Model 3. The TyG index and TyG-BMI showed different relationships with PHN in different subgroups. The correlation between the index and the results was compared using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.

Discussion

In this study, we confirmed for the first time the correlation between the TyG index and TyG-BMI and PHN. This retrospective study involved 157 participants and conducted a logistic regression analysis to explore the relationship between exposure and outcomes. Extensive subgroup analysis was used to examine the variability and specificity within each group. The results showed that in the group aged 60 and above, exposure was positively correlated with the outcome. The RCS plot revealed an independent nonlinear relationship between the TyG index and the occurrence of PHN, and an independent linear relationship between TyG-BMI and the occurrence of PHN. The ROC graph showed similar accuracy between the TyG index and TyG-BMI in evaluating PHN, with certain predictive value.

Neuropathic pain is chronic pain caused by damage or disease affecting the somatosensory nervous system [15]. PHN fully conforms to this definition. It is caused by the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus (VZV) within the dorsal root ganglion (DRG), resulting in nerve damage [16]. PHN is the most common chronic complication of herpes zoster, which affects not only the pain itself, but also the physiological function, mental health, and quality of life of patients [17]. The core feature of PHN is chronic neuropathic pain that lasts for months or even years, which is intense and persistent and does not respond well to conventional treatment [18]. The degree of pain in patients with PHN is usually moderate to severe. In one study, the mean most severe pain scores in patients with PHN remained as high as 5.7 on a 10-point scale at 90 days after rash onset, with no significant improvement at 180 days. In contrast, in patients with herpes zoster who did not develop PHN, pain largely resolved within 90 days [19]. This chronic pain and psychological distress often form a vicious cycle, and patients with PHN often experience significant anxiety and depression symptoms [20]. The treatment failure rate for PHN is still high. Complete pain relief is challenging despite numerous options, including medical therapies (e.g., gabapentin, pregabalin) and interventional therapies (e.g., pulsed radiofrequency in the DRG) [21]. Therefore, it is particularly important to predict high-risk factors for PHN occurrence in order to intervene early.

In recent years, the TyG index has emerged as an alternative marker reflecting IR, demonstrating significant value in the prediction and diagnosis of diseases related to metabolic dysfunction. Studies show that the relationship between chronic pain and metabolic dysfunction seems to be bidirectional. Metabolic dysfunction can promote chronic pain through multiple pathways such as neuroinflammation, ion channel remodeling, oxidative stress, and adipokine imbalance. Pain can further enhance IR and neuroinflammation [22]. IR leads to impaired insulin signaling pathways in endothelial cells. Due to the reduction in endothelium-derived nitric oxide (NO), vasodilation function is impaired, resulting in abnormal cerebral microvessels and local hypoperfusion, which in turn triggers central sensitization and is associated with enhanced pain perception in patients with fibromyalgia [12, 23]. IR may also promote vascular structure remodeling, leading to arteriosclerosis and a reduction in capillary density [24, 25]. Studies have shown that some cytokines may be associated with PHN and IR, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), which induce vascular inflammation and oxidative stress and accelerate the onset of neuropathic pain [26]. In an IR model induced by a high-fructose diet, the expression of ASIC3 and ANO1 channels in the DRG was upregulated, while the expression of insulin receptor-β and TASK-3 channels was downregulated [27]. However, the root cause of PHN is reactivation of latent VZV in the DRG. IR, a systemic metabolic disorder, accelerates viral replication in ganglia, leading to neuronal necrosis and severe inflammatory response. This process directly causes damage to sensory nerve fibers and lays the foundation for chronic pain [28]. Prolonged hyperglycemia damages the small blood vessels that feed the peripheral nerves, leading to reduced endoneurial blood flow and hypoxia. This chronic ischemic state makes the tiny nerve fibers responsible for transmitting pain more vulnerable to damage and less able to repair them. Thus, it increases the risk of the progression from acute herpetic neuralgia to PHN [29]. Hyperglycemia caused by IR leads to the accumulation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) in nervous tissues. These substances can damage nerve fibers, which in turn can promote the development of PHN [30]. Understanding the mechanisms of the occurrence and development of these phenomena highlights the importance of promoting metabolic health in pain management and for proposing potential therapeutic targets for the prevention or mitigation of PHN, thereby reducing the incidence of PHN and improving patients’ quality of life.

TyG-BMI (the product of the TyG index and BMI) is an emerging marker of metabolic syndrome and IR in recent years [31], while PHN is a persistent or recurrent pain state, usually lasting for more than 3 months. Recent studies have shown that metabolic abnormalities may be associated with the occurrence and development of PHN, especially through inflammatory and neuropathological mechanisms. Obesity (BMI ≥ 30) is significantly associated with the prevalence of chronic pain in the elderly. Among the elderly population in China, the prevalence of chronic pain is as high as 49.8%. People who are overweight (OR = 1.234) and obese (OR = 1.715) have a higher risk of pain, with a particular prevalence observed in the lower limbs and back [32, 33]. Multiple studies have shown that the chronic low-grade inflammatory state caused by obesity may weaken the immune response, prolong the activity of VZV, increase the risk of nerve damage, and lead to an increased incidence of PHN [34]. Triglyceride promotes the production of pro-inflammatory lipids and long-chain saturated fatty acids, triggering inflammatory activation of mononuclear macrophages [35]. Free fatty acids activate inflammatory vesicles and Toll-like receptors (TLRs), especially TLR-4, stimulating downstream inflammatory pathways [36]. Studies have shown that a diet containing fat can increase the proliferation of immune cells in the central nervous system, and saturated fatty acids can activate receptors on innate immune cells [37, 38]. Zhang et al. [41] found that, compared with mice fed a control diet, mice fed a high-fat diet for 8 weeks exhibited heat injury sensitization and mechanical abnormal pain in the hind paws. These pain behaviors were related to a higher density of damaged epidermal nerve fibers in the skin of the hind paws. Meanwhile, the presence of inflammatory cells (including Langerhans cells and macrophages) also increased. This suggests that the TyG-BMI is directly correlated with the occurrence of chronic pain such as PHN.

This study expands the scope of existing research by clarifying the unique association between the TyG index, TyG-BMI, and PHN in patients with HZ. The research results indicate that the metabolic health status evaluated through these indicators may be of crucial significance for understanding the etiological mechanism of PHN. The TyG index and TyG-BMI can serve as effective tools for assessing the risk of PHN. In addition, these indicators can be obtained through routine blood tests and are suitable for patients diagnosed with shingles. Early identification of high-risk groups is helpful for implementing preventive intervention measures. Public health initiatives aimed at improving metabolic health may significantly reduce the incidence of PHN at the population level. Such strategies may include promoting healthy diets, increasing physical activity, and reducing obesity, all of which are conducive to improving metabolic indicators.

Study Strengths and Limitations

The use of standardized data collection and processing methods for hospital case systems in this study enhances the reliability and consistency of the results, as these practices help to reduce potential biases in the data. In addition, this study adheres to strict research practices, identifies relevant adjustment variables, and thereby minimizes the bias in the association between exposure and results in the final model. Nevertheless, the study also has certain limitations. First, it is retrospective in nature and employs retrospective data analysis. Therefore, to accurately determine the association between the TyG index, TyG-BMI, and PHN, further prospective studies are needed. The present study collects cases from a specific time period, which may introduce bias in the results. Consequently, it is imperative to extend the time span to the maximum extent feasible in order to minimize error. Although all relevant variables have been thoroughly considered, there may still be confounding factors that cannot be quantified. Self-reports are used to assess PHN, so there may be recall bias. In addition, the AUC value of the ROC curve in this study is relatively low. This indicates that the predictive performance of exposure is limited and may not fully capture the diversity and complexity of PHN. Therefore, we will continue to explore the potential mechanisms of PHN and develop more accurate tools in the future to predict PHN.

Conclusion

Higher TyG index and TyG-BMI are associated with an increased risk of PHN. This indicates that lifestyle changes that improve metabolic health may help alleviate the burden of PHN.

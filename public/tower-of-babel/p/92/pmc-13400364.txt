Multi-compartment Myelin Streamline Decomposition reveals tract-specific and global brain network myelination in Multiple Sclerosis
Authors: Antonia L. Wenger, Sara Bosticardo, Sabine Schaedelin, Alessandro Cagol, Matthias Weigel, Mario Ocampo-Pineda, Po-Jui Lu, Xinjie Chen, Pasquale Calabrese, Jens Kuhle, Ludwig Kappos, Alessandro Daducci, Cristina Granziera
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multiple Sclerosis (MS) disrupts white matter (WM) tract organization, affecting brain networks and neurological function. Current methods lack the capacity to directly quantify tract- and network specific myelin-sensitive measures with clinical outcomes. This cross-sectional study (122 MS patients, 72 females, median age 48.5 years, median EDSS 3.0) applied multi-compartment Myelin Streamline Decomposition (MySD), an advanced MRI technique for assessing myelin-sensitive measures of WM bundles and networks in the presence of focal lesions. Patients underwent 3T MRI with magnetization transfer (MT)-weighted and multi-shell diffusion imaging. These data enabled multi-compartment MySD reconstruction to generate myelin-sensitive tract measures for the corticospinal tract (CST) and cingulum bundle (CB) and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties. Associations were tested between tract-specific measures and five network measures with disability (Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS)), information processing speed (Symbol Digit Modalities Test) and neuroaxonal damage (serum neurofilament light chain), adjusting for covariables.Primary FDR-corrected analyses were considered for the left/right CST with EDSS and left/right CB with SDMT. All remaining analyses were considered exploratory. While primary CST-EDSS associations did not reach significance, a subgroup analysis revealed an association between myelin-sensitive tract measure of the left CST with SDMT z-scores (β = 0.57, p = 0.001, R2Adj = 0.21). Sensitivity analysis demonstrated correlations between myelin-sensitive tract measure of the left CST with mean strength (ρ = 0.38, p < 0.001) and efficiency (ρ = 0.33, p < 0.001). Lower myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST related to poorer cognitive performance. Multi-compartment MySD enables exploration of myelin-sensitive proxies and clinical outcomes in MS.

Introduction

Multiple Sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune, inflammatory, neurodegenerative, and demyelinating disease affecting the central nervous system (Walton et al., 2020; Jakimovski et al., 2024). Demyelination, a hallmark of MS, refers to the damage or loss of myelin surrounding nerve fibers, thereby disrupting the saltatory conduction of nerve impulses along axons (Lubetzki and Stankoff, 2014). The degree of (re-)myelination is highly variable between individuals with MS (Chari, 2007), and is believed to be dependent on lesion stage (Lazzarotto et al., 2024), and underlying pathogenic mechanisms (Lucchinetti et al., 2000).

Diffusion-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) allows for the study of the brain's connectivity by mapping axons in white matter (WM) with tractography (Grier et al., 2020).

Multi-compartment Myelin Streamline Decomposition (MySD) is a novel methodology that not only integrates dMRI tractography with myelin-sensitive maps to obtain realistic myelin values along WM tracts but also integrates information on the presence of pathological focal lesions along these tracts during the estimation (Bosticardo et al., 2025).

This method is more advantageous compared to other microstructure-informed tractography methods like COMMIT (Convex Optimization Modeling for Microstructure Informed Tractography) or SIFT2 (Spherical-deconvolution Informed Filtering of Tractographs). First, unlike tractometry, microstructure-informed tractography methods provide bundle-specific estimates of connectivity. This is especially relevant in voxels with crossing fiber bundles. By leveraging global optimization algorithms, microstructure-informed tractography can assign distinct contributions to streamlines traversing the same voxel. In contrast, tractometry assigns the same contribution to all streamlines within that voxel, ultimately leading to less accurate connectivity estimates (see supplement (Fig. 1)). Moreover, MySD appears particularly well-suited for this application compared to methods such as COMMIT, LiFE (linear fascicle evaluation), and SIFT. Importantly, when combined with a multi-compartment model, MySD enables bundle-specific weighting (as a microstructure-informed tractography method), while overcoming a key limitation of this class of techniques in the presence of focal lesions. Specifically, it allows for accurate signal fitting even when the fundamental assumption that microstructural properties remain constant along streamlines is violated. This is particularly relevant in MS, where white matter alterations are spatially localized.

Despite the central role of demyelination in MS, it remains unclear how myelin loss within specific WM tracts and across whole-brain networks contributes to physical disability, neuroaxonal damage and cognitive impairment. Understanding these relationships is important as disability in MS does not arise only from focal lesions, but also from disruptions in the structural organization and efficiency of structural networks. Investigating MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties may therefore provide additional insights into how MS-related damage affects neurological function.

The aim of the study was to assess whether multi-compartment MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties show associations with clinical outcomes.

Specifically, we aimed to determine whether MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties of myelin-weighted connectomes are associated with clinical outcomes including physical disability, neuroaxonal damage and cognitive performance. We chose to focus on two main brain tracts (CST and CB) due to their role in motor (Strik et al., 2021) and cognitive performance (Koenig et al., 2015), respectively. We also focused on five whole-brain network metrics that complementarily capture the brain's network integration and segregation (density, the fraction of present connections to overall possible connections; modularity, the capability of a network to be divided into modules; global efficiency, the average inverse shortest path length; mean strength, the average sum of edges weights that are connected to a node and mean clustering coefficient, the tendency of two neighboring nodes to be connected to a common node (Rubinov and Sporns, 2010)).

We hypothesized that lower MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and altered MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties as measured with multi-compartment MySD would be associated with greater physical disability (i.e. EDSS) and reduced information processing speed (SDMT performance).

Specifically, we hypothesized that lower MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left and right CB are associated with lower SDMT z-scores and lower MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left and right CST are correlated with increased physical disability, measured by EDSS.

Methods

Subjects and clinical data

We included 122 pwMS from the University Hospital of Basel that were previously enrolled in the INsIDER cohort study (trial number: NCT05177523). Inclusion criteria were age between 18 and 75, MS diagnosis fulfilling revised 2017 McDonald criteria (Thompson et al., 2018), absence of severe psychiatric or neurological condition other than MS, and absence of contraindication in subjects for MRI.

The cohort included patients with non-active progressive MS (i.e. no relapses and no MRI activity in the year before enrollment) and active relapsing-remitting pwMS (i.e. with either relapses or MRI activity in the years before enrollment; RRMS). In patients with active RRMS the MRI was performed after at least 3 months from the last relapse. PwMS underwent a complete neurological evaluation, including the assessment of the EDSS. Within the neuropsychological examination, participants were asked to identify as right-handed, left-handed or ambidextrous.

Due to missing data of SDMT and MVF-weighted connectomes, this study included two sub-groups, SDMT subgroup (n = 81) and myelin-volume fraction (MVF)-weighted connectomes subgroup (n = 104). The main cohort refers to the results of CST and CB analysis with (i) EDSS and (ii) sNfL z-scores.

In a subgroup of patients (n = 81), SDMT, a measure of information processing speed and sustained attention, was also collected. SDMT scores were z-adjusted accounting for years of education and age (Smith and Helmuth, 1968). Impairment on the SDMT was considered when pwMS had z-scores ≤ -1.5. This value indicates that only about 6.7% of all reference subjects have a z-score of −1.5 or lower. Additionally, pwMS underwent sNfL sampling. sNfL was measured with the NF-light® assay (Quanterix, Billerica, USA). sNfL scores were z-transformed based on reference data, accounting for age and body mass index (Benkert et al., 2022). Disease phenotype, primary progressive (PPMS) and secondary progressive (SPMS) disease courses were combined into a common “progressive” MS (PMS) course. Disease modifying treatments (DMTs) were categorized into high-efficacy monoclonal antibody therapies (mAb; natalizumab, rituximab, and ocrelizumab), oral therapies (orals; dimethyl fumarate, fingolimod, siponimod, ozanimod, and teriflunomide), and platform compounds (platform; interferon beta, and glatiramer acetate). This study follows the STROBE guideline for reporting observational studies (von Elm et al., 2007).

MRI protocol

All pwMS underwent MRI on a 3 T whole-body MR system (Magnetom Prisma; Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) with a 64-channel phase-array head and neck coil. The acquisition protocol included: 3D fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) (repetition time [TR]/echo time [TE]/inversion time [TI] = 5000/386/1800 ms, 1 mm isotropic spatial resolution); 3D magnetization-prepared 2 rapid acquisition gradient echo (MP2RAGE) (TR/TI1/TI2 = 5000/700/2500 ms, 1 mm isotropic spatial resolution); and multi-shell diffusion MRI with b-values 700/1000/2000/3000 s/mm2 and 6/20/45/66 diffusion directions per shell, respectively, as well as 12 measurements each at b-value 0 s/mm2 with both anterior-to-posterior and reversed phase encoding (TR/TE/pulse duration [δ]/time between pulses [Δ] = 4500/75/19/36 ms, 1.8 mm isotropic spatial resolution). Three variants of a 3D FLASH (RF spoiled GRE) sequence were used with 1.33 mm isotropic resolution, matrix size 192 × 186 × 120, PPF = 6/8; SPF = 6/8, GRAPPA = 2: T1-weighted (TR/TE = 11/4.92 ms, alpha = 15°), Proton Density weighted (TR/TE = 25/4.92 ms, alpha = 5°), MT-weighted [TR/TE = 25/4.92 ms, alpha = 5°, Gaussian MT pulse with off-resonance frequency 2.2 kHz as in (Helms et al., 2008)]. B1 maps to correct for effects of radio frequency transmit inhomogeneities on the quantitative maps were acquired employing the steady state free precession based B1-TRAP approach (Ganter et al., 2013).

Anatomical image processing

Lesion masks were semi-automatically generated (La Rosa et al., 2020) and manually corrected on FLAIR. Lesion masks were filled on MP2RAGE images. To segment MP2RAGE images into five different masks based on the main tissue types in the brain (white and cortical gray matter, subcortical gray matter, cerebrospinal fluid, pathological tissue), we used MRtrix3 (Tournier et al., 2019). In order to define the connectome nodes, the cortical and subcortical tissues were segmented into 85 regions of interest [42 per hemisphere + brainstem (Iglesias et al., 2015)] using FreeSurfer 6.0 (Fischl, 2012) as defined in the Desikan-Killiany atlas (Desikan et al., 2006). Finally, we used the boundary-based linear registration tool implemented in FSL (Jenkinson et al., 2012) to ensure spatial alignment and to register all segmented masks to the diffusion space.

Diffusion image processing and myelin-assessment of white matter tracts and network properties of myelin-weighted connectomes

Myelin volume fraction (MVF) maps were estimated as MVF = αMTsat, where MTsat is defined as the portion of free water saturated during a single MT pulse and the calibration constant α was estimated based on the procedure described in (Mohammadi et al., 2015). The splenium of the corpus callosum from 26 healthy subjects (mean age 27.9 ± 1.3 years) was used as ROI, and the value α = 0.2161 was obtained as the median normalization factor required to constrain the splenium g-ratio to 0.7 across subjects, as previously reported using the electron microscopy technique (Graf von Keyserlingk and Schramm, 1984).

Diffusion MRIs were pre-processed to reduce artifacts from noise, eddy currents, motion and EPI distortions using MRtrix3 (Tournier et al., 2019) and FSL (Jenkinson et al., 2012; Woolrich et al., 2009). DMRIs were corrected for B1 field match inhomogeneity using the N4 algorithm implemented in ANTs (Tustison et al., 2010) and upsampled to match the 1 mm isotropic resolution of MP2RAGE, primarily to improve the accuracy of the registration. Aligning images at a common (higher) spatial resolution facilitates more precise anatomical correspondence between diffusion and structural data, which is particularly important for region-based analyses.

In addition, this step may help mitigate partial volume effects when diffusion-derived metrics are sampled within anatomically defined regions, as the finer grid allows a better approximation of tissue boundaries after registration. We note, however, that upsampling does not introduce new spatial information and therefore does not intrinsically increase the true resolution of the diffusion signal.

Furthermore, tractography was performed using anatomically constrained tractography (ACT) combined with CSD-based modeling and a microstructure-informed method (e.g., MySD). This approach is conceptually similar to the ACT + SIFT pipeline described in previous work (Zheng et al., 2026) which has been shown to improve robustness to resolution-related biases. Three million of streamlines were reconstructed using the iFOD2 tractography algorithm with anatomical constraints.

To extract specific white matter bundles, multi-compartment MySD model was fitted globally, incorporating the lesion mask as a further compartment to allow non-constant contribution along the streamline pathways (we refer to Bosticardo et al., 2025 for more details) (Bosticardo et al., 2025).

Using tract queries (Wassermann et al., 2016), we extracted the corticospinal tract (CST) and cingulum bundle (CB) from the whole-brain tractogram. The corresponding streamline weights, assigned globally by multi-compartment MySD model were then retrieved by indexing the selected streamlines. From the connectome computed using multi-compartment MySD, we extracted five global network metrics (global efficiency, density, modularity, clustering coefficient and mean strength), following the same procedure as in (Bosticardo et al., 2025). These network metrics are commonly used in MS research (Kocevar et al., 2016; Pagani et al., 2020). For an overview of these metrics please refer to11. A visualization of the research methodology is presented in Fig. 1.

We studied WM tracts separately per hemisphere due to functional and structural hemispheric asymmetries in WM (Honnedevasthana et al., 2021), and because lesions along these tracts may impact myelination processes differently depending on their hemispheric location. MTsat-derived MVF and MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures are myelin-sensitive proxies, rather than direct measures of myelin.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R Studio (version 4.2.3; R Core Team, 2022). All models were visually inspected using leverage and residual plots.

Demographic, clinical and MRI characteristics of pwMS are presented as mean and standard deviations or as median and interquartile range as appropriate and differences between the study cohorts were assessed using appropriate tests.

Because the four primary analyses involved multiple testing, we planned to control the false discovery rate (FDR) using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Accordingly, power calculations used an approximate effective significance threshold of q ∗ (K/m), where q = 0.05 denotes the target FDR level, K the expected number of detectable effects, and m = 4 the total number of tests. We evaluated several scenarios with different assumed values of k to examine the range of potentially detectable effect sizes. Sample size estimates were additionally calculated for two covariate scenarios, assuming 80% statistical power and a moderate effect size of F2 = 0.15 was assumed. These calculations yielded estimated sample sizes ranging from 85 to 122 patients (please refer to the supplement (Table 1)). The primary analysis consisted of four predefined multiple linear regression models investigating associations between clinically relevant MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and clinical outcomes. Specifically, analyses included left and right corticospinal tract (CST) measures in relation to EDSS, as well as left and right cingulum bundle (CB) measures in relation to SDMT z-scores. For these four predefined hypotheses, FDR-correction was applied.

All other analyses were considered secondary analyses (including the sensitivity analyses) and were exploratory. For these analyses no FDR-correction was applied.

Primary analysis

Multiple linear regression models were used to explore MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST (left/right) with EDSS and MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CB (left/right) with SDMT z-scores. As WM lesion volume was not normally distributed, it was log-transformed prior to analysis. Covariates included the log-transformed WM lesion volume, MS phenotype, handedness, sex, age (only for EDSS).

Secondary analysis

Differences in MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST (left/right) and CB (left/right) between patients with a RRMS and a PMS course were assessed using Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Multiple linear regression models were employed to explore MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CB (left/right) and CST (left/right) with sNfL z-scores and of the CB (left/right) with EDSS. As WM lesion volume was not normally distributed, it was log-transformed prior to analysis. Covariates included the log-transformed WM lesion volume, MS phenotype, handedness, sex, age (only for EDSS), and disease-modifying treatment (only for sNfL).

In a subgroup of pwMS with SDMT availability (n = 81), we first evaluated SDMT impairment (impaired vs. non-impaired) using a SDMT cut-off score of ≤ -1.5 SD (SDMT as categorized variable). We then run multiple linear regression models to investigate MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST (left/right) with SDMT performance (SDMT z-scores as continuous variable), adjusting for log transformed WM lesion volume, MS phenotype, sex, and handedness. For the significant association between left CST and SDMT (p < 0.05), we conducted a sensitivity analysis to assess potential confounding by disease duration (years), including it as an additional covariate in the model.

Differences in MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties between patients with a RRMS and a PMS disease course were assessed using Wilcoxon rank-sum test (n = 104). Multiple linear regression models were run to explore MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties (independent variable) with (i) sNfL z-scores, and (ii) EDSS. Covariates included log transformed WM lesion volume, MS phenotype, handedness, sex, age (only for EDSS), and disease-modifying treatment (only for sNfL).

In a subgroup of pwMS with SDMT availability (n = 81), we used multiple linear regression models to explore MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties with SDMT performance (SDMT z-scores as continuous variable), adjusting for log transformed WM lesion volume, MS phenotype, sex, and handedness.

Finally, univariate Spearman correlations were calculated between MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties and MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST.

Sensitivity analyses evaluated the robustness of the association between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST and SDMT z-scores by sequentially adding either MVF-weighted mean strength or MVF-weighted efficiency as covariates to the adjusted multiple linear regression models.

Results

Study cohort

We enrolled 122 pwMS in the study. The majority of pwMS were females (59%). Median age was 48.5 years (36.3 – 57.8). 58% of patients had a RRMS disease course. Median EDSS score was 3.0 (2.0 – 5.0), and median disease duration was 7.34 years (1.36 – 17.35). Almost 60% of pwMS were treated with monoclonal antibody therapy (mAb). The subgroup of patients with MVF-weighted connectomes availability were 104 pwMS, with majority of females (58%), and median age was 48.0 years (36.8 – 57.0). 58% of those had a RRMS disease course. Median EDSS score was 2.5 (1.5 –5.0), and median disease duration was 7.34 years (1.2 –17.3). The subgroup of patients with SDMT consisted of 81 pwMS with a median age of 49.0 years (36.0 – 57.0) and median EDSS of 3.0 (2.0 – 4.5). There were no statistically relevant differences between the main cohort and the two sub-cohorts. Please refer to Table 1 for details. An overview of main characteristics between RRMS and PMS of the full cohort can be found in the supplement (Table 2).

MySD-derived CST tract measures and clinical and cognitive outcomes

Linear regression analyses showed nominal associations between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST and EDSS (β = −0.37, 95% CI [-0.734; - 0.006], unadjusted p = 0.046, model R2Adj = 0.56) and of the right CST and EDSS (β = −0.37, 95% CI [-0.671; - 0.073], unadjusted p = 0.015, model R2Adj = 0.55). However, after FDR-correction for multiple comparisons, these associations were attenuated to trend-level significance (left CST and EDSS: FDR-adjusted p-value = 0.0926; right CST and EDSS: FDR-adjusted p-value = 0.060).

No associations were found between MySD-derived tract measures of either left CST (β = −0.24, 95% CI [−0.52; 0.04], p = 0.09, model R2Adj = 0) and right CST (β = −0.17, 95% CI [−0.39; 0.06], p = 0.15, model R2Adj = 0) with sNfL z-scores.

Twenty-eight percent of pwMS showed cognitive impairment in the domain information processing speed (SDMT z-score ≤ -1.5SD). MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST were associated with SDMT z-scores (β = 0.57, 95% CI [0.230; 0.907], p = 0.001, model R2Adj = 0.21). For details, please refer to the supplement (Tables 3–5). Sensitivity analysis adjusting for disease duration confirmed the robustness of the left CST-SDMT association (β = 0.63, 95% CI [0.297; 0.974], p = 0.001, model R2Adj = 0.24). Details are provided in Supplementary Table 6. MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the right CST showed no association with SDMT z-scores (β = 0.19, 95% CI [−0.11; 0.49], p = 0.21, model R2Adj = 0.10).

Correlations were observed between MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties, MVF-weighted mean strength (ρ = 0.38, p < 0.001) and MVF-weighted efficiency (ρ = 0.33, p < 0.001) with MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST.

MySD-derived CB tract measures and clinical and cognitive outcomes

Linear regression models indicated no association between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CB with EDSS, sNfL z-scores and SDMT z-scores. Similarly, no associations were found between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the right CB with EDSS, sNfL z-scores and SDMT z-scores (for all unadjusted p > 0.05 and FDR-adjusted p > 0.05).

Discussion

Using multi-compartment Myelin Streamline Decomposition (multi-compartment MySD) (Bosticardo et al., 2025), a novel MRI method for assessing myelin-sensitive measures of WM bundles and networks in the presence of focal lesions, we investigated associations between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of two major white matter pathways, the CB and the CST and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties with (ii) clinical and cognitive outcomes including physical disability, information processing speed and neuroaxonal damage in pwMS.

Our data demonstrated that RRMS showed higher MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of both the left and right CST compared to PMS, while no differences were observed in the CB. The observation of lower MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures in PMS within a crucial motor pathway like the CST aligns with expectations, as PMS diagnosis relies heavily on EDSS progression-primarily reflecting motor deficits associated with CST pathology. The PMS cohort in our study showed greater motor impairment (median EDSS 5.5) compared to RRMS (median EDSS 2.0).

Notably, the CB exhibited similar MySD-derived myelin tract values across both phenotypes, suggesting that not all major fiber bundles are equally affected and pointing at the necessity – once more to redefine the descriptors and determinants of clinical worsening in a complex pathology like MS.

Not surprisingly, we also found that RRMS exhibited higher MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties compared to PMS when applying MVF-weighted connectomes as derived using multi-compartment MySD. In our study, mean strength, efficiency and density were more preserved compared to PMS, while modularity was increased in PMS relative to RRMS which is a consistent finding in literature as modularity is a measure of network segregation (Kocevar et al., 2016). This indicates that RRMS exhibit more preserved brain network properties than PMS (Charalambous et al., 2019; Bosticardo et al., 2022).

Interestingly, clustering coefficient, a ‘small-world’ metric, was similar in the two groups: this might reflect resilience (Stampanoni et al., 2019; Fuchs et al., 2019) of local network connectivity or compensatory reorganization in PMS to maintain local connections despite greater impairment in other global myelin-weighted network metrics.

While the unadjusted associations between EDSS and MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST (left/right) initially reached nominal significance, these did not survive FDR-correction for multiple comparisons. Nonetheless, the observed trends align with hypothesized structural-functional relationships. Previous studies using Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) have shown that structural degeneration occurs in the CST of pwMS, and that these changes are associated with patients’ disability (Tovar-Moll et al., 2015; Pawlitzki et al., 2017; Spampinato et al., 2017;Hubbard et al., 2016). Additionally, studies reported that DTI abnormalities in the CST were related to muscle weaknesses in the hip and ankle, as assessed using a MicroFET2 handheld dynamometer (Reich et al., 2008). However, DTI measures (such as fractional anisotropy, as applied in these previous works) provide only coarse estimations of WM tracts degeneration, potentially reflecting both myelin and axonal pathology.

A notable finding was that only MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST showed an association with SDMT and a trend with EDSS. Importantly, the analysis between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST and SDMT was considered secondary with no FDR-correction, and the exploratory analysis was conducted in a subgroup (n = 81). Notably, the association between left CST myelin-sensitive tract measures and SDMT performance remained statistically robust after adjusting for disease duration, suggesting that this relationship is not substantially influenced by disease duration.

Studies on CST asymmetry in MS remain limited and to our knowledge, no prior work has investigated inter-hemispheric differences in myelin-sensitive tracts with neurological disability in MS. However, CST asymmetries are documented in neurological conditions including Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis or MS. The study of Bauer and colleagues using 46 right-handed patients with relapsing-remitting MS and 25 right-handed controls revealed higher CST connectivity in patients, with non-fatigued patients showing increases in the right CST and fatigued patients exhibiting relatively higher connectivity in the left CST. The degree of left–right CST asymmetry correlated with motor fatigue severity, linking lateralized microstructural changes in the CST to motor fatigue in MS (Bauer et al., 2020).

The stronger associations observed for MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left compared with the right CST may relate to known hemispheric asymmetries in motor and cognitive network organization. In most individuals, the left hemisphere shows specialization for language processing as well as certain motor planning and processing speed–related cognitive functions. While the CST serves primarily motor functions, our findings demonstrate that MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST showed association with cognitive performance (SDMT) and this relationship persisted after adjustment for selected global network metrics. These cross-sectional observations suggest a potential link between CST microstructure and cognitive function in MS that warrants further investigation, though they do not establish causal relationships or specific mechanistic pathways.

The discrepancy between CST and CB in their association with SDMT may stem from fundamental differences in their anatomy and susceptibility to myelin quantification. The CB's heterogenous composition compromising of both long fibers and short U- fibers (Karaus et al., 2009) may introduce greater variability in MySD measurements, reducing the sensitivity to myelin-specific pathology. In contrast, the CST is predominantly composed of long-fibers and therefore may minimize partial volume effects.

In the present study, 28% of patients were cognitively impaired based on SDMT scores. This is in line with current literature showing that 30-65% of pwMS suffer from cognitive impairment, and that the wide range depends on phenotype, age, physical disability and research setting (Ruano et al., 2017; Johnen et al., 2017; Portaccio and Amato, 2022). Information processing speed is usually one of the first cognitive domains affected by MS pathology and SDMT is considered as sentinel and sensitive test for MS cognitive impairment (Van Schependom et al., 2014; Benedict et al., 2017).

Interestingly, SDMT z-scores showed an association with MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST rather than with the CB. At first these results appeared counterintuitive since the CST is essentially involved in motor function whereas the CB is essential for many cognitive functions (Koenig et al., 2015; Kerbrat et al., 2020). These results are also corroborated by findings in a cohort of patients with vascular cognitive impairment, where lacunar lesions in the left CST were the main correlates of information processing speed, as measured using a compound score (Duering et al., 2011). Similarly, Chylińska and colleagues (Chylińska et al., 2023) also reported a significant association between CST microstructural integrity and SDMT performance, although in their study information processing speed impairment was related to reduced FA in the right CST rather than to left CST myelination.

Yet, we also found that the MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST correlated strongly with MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties for mean strength and efficiency. Importantly, the association between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the left CST and SDMT persisted after adjusting for either network metric. Additionally, in our patient cohort the oral version of the SDMT was used which minimizes motor output contribution to performance. It therefore reduces the likelihood that the CST-SDMT association reflects purely motor-speed effects. These cross-sectional findings indicate a potential relationship between tract-specific myelin-sensitive measures and cognitive performance in MS that appears independent of motor demands. Though the exact nature of this relationship requires further investigation through longitudinal studies.

Serum neurofilament light chain (sNfL) is a biomarker of acute inflammation and neuronal damage (Benkert et al., 2022; Kuhle et al., 2016). Elevated levels of sNfL have been related with decreased fractional anisotropy and increased mean, axial and radial diffusivities in normal-appearing white matter in DTI studies (Saraste et al., 2021). In addition to this, a more recent study found that elevated levels of sNfL were related with decreased myelin water fraction in the whole brain (Yik et al., 2022). The lack of association between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties and sNfL might reflect milder damage processes that do not lead to axonal destruction and hence are not provoking an sNfL increase.

This cross-sectional study is the first applying multi-compartment MySD to study the association between MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures and MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties and clinical and cognitive outcomes in pwMS. It is, however, not without limitations: First, it only focused on two white matter tracts and on five global network properties. Second, cognitive assessment relied solely on SDMT, a surrogate marker of information processing speed, without evaluation of other cognitive domains. Third, MTsat derived MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties and MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures are myelin-sensitive proxies, not direct measures of myelin. Limitations of MT-based myelin estimation include partial volume effects, sensitivity to motion, and susceptibility-related artifacts. A further limitation relates to the differences in cohort sizes: the primary analysis cohort consisted of n = 122, SDMT subgroup of n = 81 and MVF-weighted connectome subgroup of n = 104. Additionally, no information of brain atrophy or spinal cord involvement were used, and primary progressive MS and secondary progressive MS patients were classified to one progressive form of MS. Also, the analysis of SDMT and the sensitivity analyses are based on smaller subgroup analyses.

Future research should explore the prognostic value of MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures in other brain WM tracts, i.e. corpus callosum or superior longitudinal fasciculus and examining further MVF-derived myelin-sensitive network properties. Longitudinal studies would enable evaluation of these measures and their relationship with clinical and cognitive outcomes. Particularly useful would be the inclusion of a healthy control population to study these associations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we found that MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST are more pronounced in PMS than in RRMS and that lower MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST were related information processing speed in pwMS. While MySD-derived myelin-sensitive measure of the CST correlated with global myelin-weighted network metrics, its association with SDMT persisted after separate adjustment for MVF-weighted efficiency and MVF-weighted mean strength. These findings indicate that MySD-derived myelin-sensitive tract measures of the CST show an association with information processing speed that appears statistically independent of the selected global network metrics in this cross-sectional analysis.

These findings demonstrate that multi-compartment MySD provides myelin-sensitive imaging metrics to study clinical and cognitive outcomes in MS.

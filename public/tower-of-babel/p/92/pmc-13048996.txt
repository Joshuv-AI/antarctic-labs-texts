Genetic analysis of key players in PI3K signaling cascade of colorectal carcinoma
Authors: Hira Pervaiz, Nosheen Masood, Parvez Azam Malik, Jahangir Sarwar Khan, Mumtaz Ahmad Khan, Hassaan Malik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriggering PI3K signaling cascade is most common in human cancers. This study aimed to screen the association of seven SNPs and two hotspot mutations, clinical outcome and therapeutic potential with susceptibility to colorectal carcinoma (CRC). In this case-control study of 495 CRC and 495 controls, seven SNPs were genotyped by ARMS-PCR. The mutant allele frequencies were significantly higher for five SNPs, E542K and E17K but lower for rs6443624 (P = 0.1480) and rs1883965 (P = 0.4105). DNA sequencing results revealed point mutations were more prevalent in CRC. Pairwise linkage disequilibrium analysis was performed and strong LD was observed between rs6443624 and rs10138227 (D’ = 0.953, r² = 0.435, LOD = 62.61) among CRC. Pearson’s chi-square test showed these variants were associated with CRC risk (P < 0.001). Kaplan Meier analysis revealed that six SNPs (P < 0.005) were strongly associated with clinicopathological parameters and drugs with OS in CRC but not for rs6443624 (P = 0.3). Breslow and Tarone-Ware Test showed significant survival differences but capecitabine revealed the highest survival estimates. Swiss model was used to analyze the changes in protein structure. QMEAND value for E542K is 0.88 ± 0.05 and for E17K is 0.78 ± 0.05 representing high quality structure, however, Ramachandaran structure 97.37% favoured for E542K and 95.23% for E17K. The current study suggests that PI3K pathway variants may be associated with colorectal cancer susceptibility and highlights their potential relevance as candidate biomarkers, pending further validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42006-z.Keywords: Colorectal carcinoma (CRC), Single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP), PIK3CA/AKT/mTOR pathway, Tetra ARMS-PCR, Linkage disequilibrium (LD)Subject terms: Cancer, Genetics, Oncology

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major health problem and highly frequent cancer worldwide. Globally CRC incidence consist of 9.4% cancer mortality, lower than lung cancer which includes 18% mortality. Global burden of fresh colorectal cancer cases is projected to range 3.2 million and 1.6 million deaths in 2040, grounded on prediction of aging, population growth, and human progress. The upsurge in CRC incidence is primarily due to increased exposure to environmental risk factors resulting from shifting lifestyle and diet headed for Westernization1,2. Genetic risk factors comprised of 60% of colorectal carcinoma cases. This genetic colorectal cancer arises mainly due to epigenetic modifications or somatic gene mutations. The remaining 40% of CRC showed vulnerability to heritable components that include family history but not having any evident genetic predisposition. The environmental risk factors significantly contribute to colorectal carcinoma, even in patients having a family history, genetic alterations may be the cause of the disease progression3.

The PIK3CA/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway has an important role in proliferation, angiogenesis, metastasis and resistance to apoptosis that is essential in the development and maintenance of colorectal carcinomas. PIK3CA effect on cancer progression is facilitated by AKT which is a downstream effector of PIK3CA, activated by the phosphorylation of AKT kinase at the Threonine 308 residue. AKT is overexpressed in numerous cancers comprising colorectal, breast pancreatic and ovarian. Previous researches have shown that AKT phosphorylation in CRC is associated with the proliferation of cells, and apoptosis inhibition along with various clinicopathological factors like invasion grade, vessel infiltration, tumor stage and lymph nodes metastasis. AKT also regulates downstream target of mTOR that has a significant role in metabolism, angiogenesis, growth, and protein translation. mTOR is a direct substrate for AKT kinases and recognizes Serine 2448 AKT target site on mTOR. Recent studies have shown that mTOR proteins are extremely overexpressed in colorectal carcinomas tissues. Selective targeting of the PI3K pathway has been shown in preclinical studies to reduce colorectal cancer growth and metastatic potential while enhancing apoptosis and sensitivity to chemotherapy, thereby suggesting its potential as a novel therapeutic strategy4.

The most frequent type of genetic variations in human genome are single nucleotide polymorphisms. Investigating genetic variants can be useful in understanding the genetic basis of cancer and developing strategies that are beneficial in identifying persons who are at increased risk5. Mostly single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are located in intronic or intergenic regions, and recent research shows that SNPs with higher effect are those that have noticeable functional significance, which enhances the need for more research in understanding their role in potentially functional regions to cancer risk6. Single nucleotide polymorphisms in the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway create likely variations among populations linked to cancer risk7.

Mutations caused in PI3K pathway due to alterations in the components like receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK), phosphatidylinositol 3-Kinase (PI3K), AKT and mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), activation of this pathway leads to carcinogenesis due to the modifications in the cellular processes8. The hotspot mutations in PIK3CA is in exon 9 (mutation rate 7.49%) as compared to mutation in exon 20 (mutation rate 5.29%) and this is the important biomarker for the adjuvant therapy in CRC patient. E545A, H1047R, E542K and E545K are the most widespread variants in this gene9. The hotspot mutation of AKT1 gene is E17K, as compared to PIK3CA mutations it is more associated with BRAF mutation in V600E10,11. In mTOR gene, mutants can be generated by inducing mutations in residues of amino acids which are evolutionary conserved and present in main functional domain of human mTOR. This mechanism increases the protein kinase activity resulting in the tumorigenesis which suggests this gene is strong proto-oncogene with high oncogenicity. P2273S and E2288K are the mutants used to examine tumor formation12.

In this era, targeted therapeutics are emerging based on the genomic modifications treatment protocol for CRC. Genomics has significantly improved the treatment of patients having colorectal cancer but still there is a room for clinically grade genomic biomarkers for chemotherapeutics and immune check point inhibitors. Universal anticancer therapy that is established on chemotherapeutic irinotecan, FU or capecitabine, oxaliplatin combined with VEGF or EGFR inhibitors are important in metastatic CRC13. The molecular study of colorectal cancer patients indicates that PIK3CA mutations do not benefit from rofecoxib therapy. However, the regular use of aspirin after a colorectal cancer diagnosis significantly reduces the recurrence of PIK3CA mutant cancers, making PIK3CA mutations a predictive biomarker for aspirin-based therapy14. One PIK3CA inhibitor, PX-866, has shown preclinical potential by overcoming EGFR resistance and enhancing the efficacy of cetuximab. This combined inhibition of PI3K and EGFR could provide significant benefits for CRC patients, minimizing overlapping toxicity while improving therapeutic outcomes with PX-866 and cetuximab.

Tumors with AKT alterations present promising targets for the development of AKT-directed therapies. However, AKT inhibitors demonstrate reduced activity in tumors harboring PIK3CA mutations. AKT inhibitors are available in various forms and are categorized into groups such as allosteric inhibitors, phosphatidylinositol analogs, and ATP-competitive inhibitors. For instance, studies have shown that metastatic colorectal cancer patients receiving a combination of perifosine and capecitabine exhibit improved outcomes compared to capecitabine alone. However, perifosine may lead to chemotherapy resistance by affecting the NF-kB pathway. Another AKT inhibitor, MK-2206, an allosteric inhibitor, has demonstrated preclinical antitumor activity by disrupting AKT translocation to the membrane and inhibiting downstream signaling. Currently, clinical trials are underway to evaluate MK-2206, both as a standalone treatment and in combination with MEK inhibitors, for metastatic colorectal cancer15.

mTOR is a key regulator of the PI3KCA signaling pathway and serves as a primary molecular target in various cancer therapies. mTOR inhibitors are classified into two types: ATP-competitive mTOR inhibitors, which block both mTORC1 and mTORC2, and rapamycin analogs, which primarily affect mTORC1. The combination of irinotecan and temsirolimus has shown clinical activity but requires further research. OSI-027, a potent dual inhibitor of mTORC1 and mTORC2, suppresses tumor growth by affecting AKT phosphorylation and 4E-BP1 in tumor tissues. While mTOR inhibitors generally demonstrate limited clinical efficacy, they provide modest stabilization of disease in certain patients, highlighting the need for predictive biomarkers to guide treatment planning15.

Nowadays cancer precision medicine is the main research focus in cancer treatment and the development of drugs, and aims to provide the right dose of the right drug for the right patient at the right time grounding on the cancer genetics. That is why it is essential to screen variations in genes for precise identification of precise therapy16. The role of the PI3K signaling in cancer pathogenesis has been thoroughly discovered in recent years, and numerous drugs aiming this pathway are in progress. But to the best of our knowledge there is no research conducted on the impact of polymorphisms and mutations in PI3K signaling cascade on the risk of developing CRC in the Pakistani population. This gap in the knowledge raises questions about prevalence of these genetic variants, their correlation to clinicopathological features, survival function, and therapeutic potential of different chemotherapeutic drugs on CRC patients residing in Pakistan. As cancer is a multifactorial event and different polymorphisms and alterations in different genes are responsible for carcinogenesis, so to fill in the gap, we have conducted case-control study by selecting SNPs of PIK3CA (rs7640662, rs6443624), AKT (rs10138227, rs1130233, rs1130214), mTOR (rs1064261, rs1883965) and hotspot mutations E542K in PIK3CA and E17K in AKT1, their association and correlation with clinical parameters, survival function, and therapeutic potential of different chemotherapeutic drugs. The aim of this study is to explore the frequency, functional significance and clinicopathological association of genetic variations in CRC and their role in PI3K pathway initiation and therapeutic outcome. The rationale is to investigate molecular alterations in both coding mutations that directly activate oncogenic pathway and non-coding polymorphisms that regulate genetic expression explains pathway deregulation. This study will provide new insight into SNPs and specific mutations contributing in theranostics (a combined diagnostic and therapeutic approach) among CRC in Pakistani population.

Material and method

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for controls required age and gender corresponding individuals, while those with a previous history of any cancer, and any clinical signs or symptoms suggestive of malignancy at the time of enrollment were excluded. Histopathologically confirmed colorectal cancer cases who provided written informed consent were included in this study. Additionally, both cases and controls with other illnesses such as renal disease, diabetes, hypertension and hepatic impairment were also disqualified.

Study participants and data extraction

For this study, ethical approval was taken from the committees of Fatima Jinnah Women University, Rawalpindi and the allied hospitals: Holy Family, Benazir Bhutto, District Headquarter of Rawalpindi and Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences of Islamabad for the purpose of sampling. Blood samples included in this study were collected from 495 histopathological confirmed colorectal cancer patients and 495 healthy controls. Control samples were acquired from persons attending routine medical checkups at these hospitals. Frequency distribution and incidence (%) of all other clinicopathological parameters and chemotherapeutic drugs in CRC patients and healthy controls have been shown in the Table 1. Sample size was calculated with the software provided by World Health Organization (WHO) and confirmed manually by formula n = Zα/22pq/(MOE)2 as well. Zα/2 is the critical value of the Normal distribution at α/2 (e.g. for a confidence level of 95%, α is 0.05 and the critical value is 1.96), MOE is the margin of error, p is the sample proportion, and N is the population size. Frequency of colorectal cancer is 5.6% in Pakistan. Calculations made were N= (1.96)2 (0.056) (0.85)/(0.05)2, and the sample size was 73, however larger sample size was taken to obtain statistically significant results17. Standardized questionnaire was designed wherein details of demographic factors, clinical condition and chemotherapeutic drugs used by histopathological confirmed CRC patients were included. Informed consent was signed by all the participants involved in this study.

Genomic variants selection strategy

Seven potential SNPs were selected from National Center for Biotechnology Information SNP data base (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and Ensembl genome browser 113 (https://www.ensembl.org/index.html). These SNPs were selected on the characteristics of Asian population with minor allele frequency higher than 0.10 and linkage disequilibrium testing. SNPs rs7640662, rs6443624 and rs10138227 are intronic variants, rs1130233 is a synonymous variant, rs1130214 is a 5’UTR variant, rs1064261is a missense variant and rs1883965 is an intronic variant. Regulome DB was used to identify functional location likely resulted in functional consequences of these selected SNPs. According to this scoring system all these SNPs affect transcription factor binding site activity and regulate gene expression. These E542K (PIK3CA) and E17K (AKT1) hotspot mutations were selected from Ensembl genome browser 113 (https://www.ensembl.org/index.html), role in carcinogenesis via activation of the PI3K signaling cascade as both of these mutations occurs recurrently in multiple carcinomas that make them better prognostic biomarker for learning signaling and therapeutic response.

Quality control of genotyping data

SNPs and samples with call rates above 95% were included in analysis. The overall genotyping success rate exceeded 98% indicating high assay efficiency. Frequency of cases and controls call and success rate was mentioned in Table 2. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) was assessed separately in cases and controls. Not any of the SNPs were deviating from HWE in controls, whereas deviations observed only in cases were retained. Expected genotype frequencies were calculated using the Hardy–Weinberg equation p2+2pq+q2, and deviation from equilibrium was assessed using a chi-square test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, indicating deviation from equilibrium.

Genotyping

Genomic DNA from blood samples of normal persons as well as CRC patients were extracted by using phenol chloroform extraction method18 and stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C. The genomic DNA integrity was verified by resolving DNA on 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and visualized with ethidium bromide staining in Gel Documentation system. Genotyping of variants PIK3CA (rs7640662, rs6443624), AKT (rs10138227, rs1130233, rs1130214) and mTOR (rs1064261, rs1883965) were carried out by Amplification Refractory Mutation System-Polymerase Chain Reaction ARMS-PCR (Tetra ARMS PCR) For amplification, 96 well thermal cycler (UNIEQUIP, Germany) was used. PCR tubes were properly labelled before placing them in the thermal cycler. Reaction mixture of 20 micro litre comprised of DNA template, set of forward outer and inner primers and reverse outer and inner primers, dNTPs, buffer, PCR water, magnesium chloride and Taq polymerase. PCR cycling performed at 94 °C for 5 min followed by 35cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 45 s, after that annealing at explicit temperature for 1 min, then extension at 72 °C for 45 s and lastly final extension at 72 °C for 8 min. 4 °C for final hold. After the completion of PCR reaction, PCR tubes were taken out and placed at 4 °C (Table S1)19. DNA samples were amplified for the hotspot mutation analysis in selected PI3K pathway genes i.e. E542K in PIK3CA and E17K in AKT1 by using polymerase chain reaction. For each DNA sample 20 µl reaction mixture was prepared containing DNA template, set of forward and reverse primers, dNTPs, buffer, PCR water, magnesium chloride and Taq polymerase. During initialization step the reaction mixture was held at about 95 °C for up to 10 min. The benefit of using this hot-start polymerases was that nonspecific levels of amplification throughout reaction would reduce. This step led directly into the denaturation step in which the reaction was held at 95 °C for about 20 s. This step led to the annealing step where reaction was cooled down and let the primers to anneal along the single-stranded DNA templates. The temperature maintained within 45 °C and 65 °C for about 20 s. The annealing temperature 3–5 °C lower than the melting temperature. Then there was extension step in which temperature raised to about 72 °C and with the use of dNTPs, DNA polymerases synthesized a new complementary DNA strand at the positions of the annealed primers. This defined one PCR cycle and repeated 35 times (Table S2). Then PCR product were held at 4 °C in the thermocycler block until it was removed20. PCR products were separated by running on 2% agarose gel electrophoresis along with 100 bp DNA ladder for comparison of different size fragments and visualized under Gel Documentation system. The intensity, position and approximate size of each amplicon resolute depending on the parallel ladder reference bands (Fig. 1a and b).

Primer designing for Tetra-primer ARMS PCR was carried out by using “Primer 1” software (http://primer1.soton.ac.uk/primer1.html) to make two sets of primer pairs. (Table S3). Self-complementarity will be checked from the Oligo Calc. Ensembl server was used to confirm the specificity of selected primers. Primer designing was carried out by using NCBI Primer-Blast tool and in Primer-Blast, primer 3 to make primer pair for the specific hotspot mutations of specific genes (Table S4). To confirm there were no SNPs present in the predicted primer binding region, primer pairs were checked in SNPCheck (https://genetools.org/SNPCheck/snpcheck.htm). Then selected good quality primer sequences were sent to the concerned laboratory for the synthesis and purchased commercially.

DNA Sequencing was performed on mutated PCR products of four SNPs, PIK3CA (rs6443624), AKT (rs10138227, rs1130214) and mTOR (rs1883965) obtained from genomic DNA of CRC patients and controls for comparison. For sequencing PCR 50ul reaction mixture was prepared as mentioned in table S5. The sequencing was carried out by Macrogen (Seoul, South Korea) commercial DNA sequencing facility. The results were analyzed from GRCh38.p14 Primary Assembly from Ensemble (https://asia.ensembl.org/index.html) then blast in NCBI blastn (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) (Fig. S1) and chromatograms were visualized in BioEdit Sequence Alignment Editor (Fig. 2).

Specific mutations in the DNA sequence has been mentioned with the arrows positioning at the specific nucleotide position.

Statistical analysis

Clinical characteristics, demographic factors were estimated and co-occurrence of selected hotspot mutations were observed by using Pearson’s chi-square test (χ2) and Fischer exact test and visualized through chord diagram. Genotypic frequency distribution was analyzed by Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium statistics. Patient follow up was conducted every 6 months and homozygous wild type alleles were determined as reference in all seven SNPs. Survival analyses were performed using Log-rank test, Kaplan-Meier curves and Breslow and Tarone-Ware test. Linkage disequilibrium (LD) and haplotypes of all SNPs were generated by Haploview software 4.2. In-Silico analysis was performed by Swiss model. Significance level was considered significant at P value < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed in R Studio version 4.4.2 and MedCalc 17.

Results

Genomic analysis

In this current study we have analyzed the genetic variations in PIK3CA, AKT and mTOR in CRC patients and compared with healthy controls. Statistical analysis of ARMS-PCR results for PIK3CA in rs7640662 showed that occurrence of mutant (GG) genotype was significantly higher in CRC as compared to healthy persons (OR: 15.950; 95% CI: 7.2968–34.866) and wild (CC) genotype was lower in CRC patients (OR: 0.2549; 95% CI: 0.1891–0.3436). However, in rs6443624, heterozygous (CA) genotype was considerably higher in CRC patients (OR: 2.8386; 95% CI: 2.1529–3.7425) and wild (AA) genotype was lower in CRC in comparison to healthy persons (OR: 1.5366; 95% CI: 0.8587–2.7497).

Statistical analysis of AKT1 in rs10138227 polymorphism showed that occurrence of mutant (CC) genotype was highly abundant in CRC patients (OR: 0.0582; 95% CI: 0.0418–0.0809). In rs1130233 polymorphism, statistical analysis detected heterozygous (CT) (OR 3.6350; 95% CI: 2.7873–4.7404) and mutant (TT) genotypes (OR: 3.4855; 95% CI: 1.6369–7.4218) were highly abundant in CRC patients, while wild (CC) genotype was higher in controls. For the third polymorphism rs1130214, occurrence of mutant (AA) genotype was suggestively higher in CRC patients (OR: 16; 95% CI: 9.2338–27.7244) and wild (CC) genotype was abundant in controls (OR: 0.1261; 95% CI: 0.0923–0.1723).

Statistical analysis of mTOR in rs1064261 polymorphism showed that mutant (AA) genotype in CRC patients is significantly higher and no mutant genotype was found in control persons. Wild (GG) genotype was detected significantly higher in controls then in CRC (OR: 0.0150; 95% CI: 0.0099–0.0225). In rs1883965, mutant (GG) genotype was more prevalent in CRC (OR: 537.56: 95% CI: 131.87–2191.21.87.21) and wild (AA) genotype was more widespread in control persons (OR: 0.0136; 95% CI: 0.0082–0.0226) (Table 3).

Correlation between PIK3CA, AKT and mTOR variants and clinicopathological parameters

The genotype of rs7640662 variant has shown significant association with age, gender, family history, IBD, cancer type, cancer stage, chemotherapy and radiation and drugs with P value < 0.001, but did not show any significant association with smoking (P = 0.213) and colonoscopy (P = 0.865). The rs6443624, rs10138227, rs1130214 and rs1883965 genotype showed statistically significant correlation (P value < 0.001) with all factors other than gender (P = 0.9838) and colonoscopy (P = 0.911). The positive correlation was detected between genotype of rs1130233 with all clinicopathological parameters with P value < 0.001, but did not with colonoscopy (P = 0.908). The significant association was observed for rs1064261 with P value < 0.001, but age (P = 0.99), gender (P = 0.968) and colonoscopy (P = 0.841) did not show any noteworthy correlation (Fig. S2). These correlative studies showed smoking, IBD, family history, age, cancer type and stage, chemo and radiotherapy and drugs showed significant associations with these SNPs irrespective of gender and colonoscopy taken.

E542K and E17K mutations were found to be significantly associated with the family history, IBD and these two mutations also showed significant association with the type of cancer, cancer type, chemotherapeutic drug used by the patients in this study cohort and chemotherapy and radiations received by the CRC patients. The occurrence of these mutations did not significantly correlate with age group, gender and colonoscopy. Correlation analysis showed that these hotspot mutations can develop carcinogenesis in patients of any age group, whether male or female and colonoscopy is the test used for the diagnosis of the disease (Table 4).

Co-occurrence analysis of mutations

The relationship between mutations were analyzed in the study cohort and statistical significant co-occurrence was observed through pairwise Fischer Exact test (Fig. 5). The KRAS mutation G12D co-occurred with V600E (OR: 1.82, 95%CI: 1.21–2.75, P = 2.72e- 3), E542K (OR: 2.84, 95%CI: 1.76–4.67, P = 4.39e- 6) and E17K (OR:0.18, 95%CI: 0.12–0.27, P = 4.34e-19). Hotspot mutation of BRAF, V600E co-occurred with other three mutations G12D, E542K (OR: 0.58, 95%CI: 0.35–0.94, P = 2.59e- 2) and E17K (OR: 2.46, 95%CI: 1.65–3.68, P = 3.88e- 6) showed significant association. The hotspot mutation of PIK3CA, E542K also mutually co-occurred with G12D, V600E and E17K (OR: 0.5, 95%CI: 0.32–0.78, P = 1.55e- 3) with statistical significant result. Likewise, AKT1 mutation E17K showed significant co-occurrence with rest of the mutations G12D, V600E and E542K. These results indicate potential cooperation between specific mutations.

Haplotype analysis and linkage disequilibrium

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) and haplotype analyses were performed using Haploview to examine the correlation structure among the selected SNPs. A strong LD was observed between rs6443624 and rs10138227 in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients (D′ = 0.953, r² = 0.435, LOD = 62.61), indicating a high degree of non-random association between these loci. The high D′ value suggests limited historical recombination, while the moderate r² indicates partial allelic correlation, implying that both variants may contribute distinct information to disease susceptibility.

The LD plot revealed a well-defined haplotype block encompassing rs6443624, rs10138227, and rs1130233 (Fig. 6). Haplotype frequency estimation demonstrated that certain haplotypes within this block were significantly more frequent in CRC cases compared with controls (Table 5), suggesting a combined genetic effect on CRC risk. These findings indicate that the analyzed haplotype block may represent a genomic region of interest, potentially tagging functional variants influencing CRC susceptibility.

Kaplan Meier analysis for overall survival

For survival analysis, Kaplan-Meier curve was used to estimate survival probabilities while log rank test was used to compare survival distributions to evaluate the association of PIK3CA, AKT and mTOR variants, clinicopathological parameters and chemotherapeutic drugs with overall survival in CRC patients. PIK3CA rs7640662, AKT (rs10138227, rs1130233, rs1130214) and mTOR (rs1064261, rs1883965) polymorphisms showed significant associations but not any were detected in PIK3CA rs6443624.

We have studied the association of gender, family history, smoking, IBD and colonoscopy with overall survival distribution among CRC. The Log-rank test for family history was executed to compare survival distribution and the result showed significant survival differences among two groups. The statistical analysis Log-rank test for smoking and inflammatory bowel disease also showed significant survival difference among groups. The test statistics for gender did not show any statistically significant difference in survival among two groups (female and male) and test of colonoscopy also did not show any statistically significant difference suggesting similar survival distribution patterns.

The Log-rank test was performed to compare the survival distributions among different cancer types, colon, mucinous colorectal, rectum, recto-sigmoid and tubular colorectal. The results showed statistically significant differences in survival distributions representing that survival differs by cancer type. The Log-rank test for cancer stages showed significant survival difference among groups T2, T3, T4. T3 has the longest median survival that proposes the best survival outcome compared to the other two groups. The Log-rank test conducted among different chemotherapeutic drug combinations, capacitabine, capecitabine+oxaliplatin, decadron, folinic acid+oxaliplatin+fluoro uracil showed statistically significant differences in survival distributions suggesting that survival differs across these groups. The capacitabine had the highest survival estimations over time and they tend to live longer and disease free during follow up time, while groups capecitabine+oxaliplatin and folinic acid+oxaliplatin+fluoro uracil showed faster declines and poor survival outcome and decadron presented higher survival duration. There was limited data of 276 patients available of chemotherapeutic drugs. Discussion

The PIK3CA/AKT/mTOR pathway is essential for regulating tumor proliferation, differentiation, cell survival, trafficking, and glucose homeostasis. Exploring this pathway in human cancers holds promise for identifying potential therapeutic targets. However, reliable biomarkers to assess this signaling pathway remain limited21. This present study investigates the association of single nucleotide polymorphism in PIK3CA, AKT and mTOR in colorectal carcinoma with associated risk factors, their clinical outcomes and therapeutic potential among CRC patients. This pathway has a significant role in many other cancers such as breast cancer, gastrointestinal cancer, ovarian cancer, bladder cancer and lung cancer.

PIK3CA single nucleotide polymorphisms rs7640662 and rs6443624 are the intronic variant and influence the binding of transcription factors and alter the splicing patterns or transcription of the PIK3CA gene. The current study reported the significant association of rs7640662 and rs6443624 with elevated risk of colorectal cancer patients compared with disease-free controls. In this study, genotype rs7640662 has shown significant association with risk factors other than smoking, and presented higher survival with wild type allele and lower survival rates with mutant allele GG while rs6443624 has shown significant association with risk factors but revealed that differences in survival were not statistically significant (P = 0.3). Both these SNPs have shown significant survival differences with chemotherapeutic drugs (P < 0.001) indicating that genotype-guided therapy may enhance outcomes. In the previous studies, Quaye et al.22 reported no association of PIK3CA SNPs with ovarian cancer. Wang et al.23 reported that PI3KCA rs6443624 was significantly associated with the risk of BC, and AC/AA alleles were associated with advanced stage in Chinese population. Slattery et al.24 suggested statistically significant associations between genetic variation rs7640662 in PIK3CA and risk of developing colorectal cancer. Kim et al.25 suggested rs7640662 has a role in gastrointestinal cancer susceptibility. Bizhani et al.26 concluded rs6443624 polymorphism may be related to bladder cancer development in a sample of Iranian population. Ulu et al.27 reported that there were not significant association between rs6443624 gene polymorphism and clinic and prognostic parameters in Breast Cancer Turkish study group.

AKT1 single nucleotide polymorphism rs10138227 and rs1130214 are the intronic variants and rs1130233 is the synonymous variant affecting transcription factor binding site activity. In this study the genotype of rs10138227 and rs1130214 variants have shown significant association with risk factors other than gender, rs1130233 has shown statistically significant association with all the risk factors. These variants have shown higher survival rates with wild type alleles then the heterozygous and mutant alleles. They have significant association with chemotherapeutic drugs while giving difference in the efficacy of therapeutics in survival. In the previous studies, Allam et al.28 suggested that AKT1 rs10138227 may affect susceptibility to colorectal carcinoma, likely by altering the transcriptional activity of AKT1. The combined heterozygous CT-TT genotypes of AKT1 rs10138227 were found to be associated with CRC risk but the variant genotypes of AKT1 rs1130214 have not shown any association with CRC risk in the Moroccan population. Wang et al.29 suggested that CT-TT genotypes of AKT1 rs10138227 (P = 0.365) have not been associated with risk of gastric adenocarcinoma in Chinese population. Vuorinen et al.30 has not found any significant association between AKT1 rs10138227 and Breast cancer at a genotypic (p = 0.197) or allelic level (p = 0.494) in the case–control Australian population. Zubair et al.31 concluded the significant association of rs1130233 in AKT with AKT activation, DNA damage, an early response B-cell translocation gene 2 expression and risk of CRC progression. Bizhani et al.26 concluded rs1130233 polymorphism has shown association but rs1130214 did not show any significant association to bladder cancer development in a sample of Iranian population. Pérez-Ramírez et al.32 concluded polymorphisms rs1130214 and rs1130233 have been associated with survival in advanced Non-small cell lung cancer patients treated with EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors. Li et al.33 suggested rs1130233 polymorphism was significantly related to head and neck squamous cell carcinoma in Northeast China. Kim et al.34 did not found any association of rs1130233 with the response of capacitabine and oxaliplatin drug combination in CRC, but in our study we have found significant association of rs1130233 as well as other two studied SNPs of AKT with this drug combination.

mTOR single nucleotide polymorphism rs1064261 is a missense variant and rs1883965 is an intronic variant located in regions with active transcriptional promoters. In this research we have concluded rs1064261 variant did not show statistically significant association with age and gender whereas rs1883965 did not show association with gender. They have higher survival probabilities with wild type alleles than heterozygous and mutant alleles with P value < 0.001 and 0.015, also showing significant survival differences with chemotherapeutic drugs studied in this research. In the previous studies, Ilozumba et al.35 suggested that mTOR genetic variant rs1883965 interact with energy intake in relation to breast carcinoma risk in Black women including the ER subtype. Piao et al.36 suggested mTOR rs1064261 and AKT1 rs1130233 were associated with increased risk of gastric carcinoma. Bizhani et al.26 suggested rs1883965 has not been associated with bladder cancer. He et al.37 concluded rs1883965 variant genotypes were associated with an increased gastric cancer risk. Zhu et al.38 suggested that rs1064261 responsible for the risk of esophageal squamous cell cancer in Eastern Chinese population.

Beyond statistical significance, the observed associations propose that these genetic variants may have potential clinical implication in therapeutics. These variants could contribute to risk stratification and personalize treatment options, that allow clinicians to identify patients who are more likely to benefit from explicit therapeutic regimes while sparing others from ineffective treatment and unnecessary toxicity. Notably, the clinical relevance of these findings also influenced by the effect size, consistency across treatment subgroups, and biological plausibility. For instance, genotype-specific survival differences observed in patients receiving capecitabine raise the possibility of genotype-guided therapy assortment, predominantly where various treatment choices are accessible. Prospective studies, preferably with stage-matched or treatment strategies, are mandatory to approve whether these variants independently predict therapeutic benefit. Future research could validate these relationships and incorporate them into multivariable prognostic models or clinical decision algorithms to enhance individualized treatment planning and clinical practice outcomes.

Correlation analysis showed that these hotspot mutations can develop carcinogenesis in patients of any age group, whether male or female and colonoscopy is the test used for the diagnosis of the disease. In-silico analysis determined by Swiss model revealed that protein structure of these mutations did not alter but has a role in continuous activation to persistence signaling through PI3K pathway, promoting cell proliferation and develop carcinogenesis. The reason may be because E542K and E17K disrupts function of protein rather structure as Swiss model do not show changes in dynamics or protein flexibility. As cancer is multifactorial disease so our results showed significant co-occurrence of mutations in KRAS (G12D), BRAF (V600E), PIK3CA (E542K) and AKT (E17K) progress to CRC carcinogenesis. These results indicate potential cooperation between specific mutations. These discoveries align with already reported studies signifying that mutations in these genes contribute to carcinoma development and progression.

In this study, linkage disequilibrium and haplotype analysis showed strong allelic associations among specific PIK3CA/AKT/mTOR SNPs in CRC patients, as compared to controls, suggesting there may be co inheritance patterns within the cascade. Strong LD recommends that these variations may work collectively to upsurge disease predisposition or progression rather than acting separately. These co-inherited variations may affect the balance of gene expression and regulation, affecting the PI3K cascade. Strong LD within PI3K pathway genes has been linked to altered pathway activation and an increased risk of cancer in other populations, rendering to similar findings by Devaney et al.39. African population exhibit shorter LD blocks while European and Asian populations show intermediate LD, and American Indian populations display longer LD blocks, as reported in previous studies using HapMap and 1000 Genomes data40. In this present research, the LD plot showed clear block structure with haplotype including three SNPs rs6443624, rs10138227 and rs1130233 proposed a potential functional region influencing CRC. The significance of researching regional genomic architecture is further highlighted by the difference in LD structure between cases and controls, which suggests possible population-specific genetic patterns in the Pakistani cohort. These findings also suggest that haplotype-based analyses could offer more strength in detecting associations than individual single nucleotide polymorphisms investigations.

The current study showed that smoking, IBD, family history, age are the contributing risk factors for the development of colorectal cancer. This study highlights potential genetic biomarkers that open doors for larger, multi ethnic cohort research by incorporating functional evaluations, multi-omics approaches, and patient follow-up to elucidate how these variants impact pathway activation, treatment response, and disease progression. These patterns highlight possible relation between mutation and cancer type specificity and may reveal distinct molecular pathways involved in tumorigenesis. Such associations warrant further investigation for their potential in precision oncology and guiding targeted therapy.

The limitation of this study is that the majority of patients in the current study contributed data at an advanced stage of their colorectal cancer, which could restrict the study’s applicability in confirming these findings in earlier stages of the disease. The survival among patients was limited by incomplete chemotherapeutic drug data with only 276 patients. This can reduce statistical power and generalizability of these findings. Though it was observed in this study that capecitabine has contributed toward increased overall survival in patients, it could not offer more than two-year follow-up data, which would require larger studies with longer follow-up periods in order to apply more rigorous analyses for survival. Furthermore, in this study, data was examined in terms of selected SNPs as well as ‘hotspots’ in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, which might not reflect the entire role of relevant SNPs in the regulation of these pathways. Haplotype association analyses were conducted using Haploview, and all SNPs were integrated into haplotypes. Adjustments for demographic factors were not performed and should be considered in future studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study suggested that single nucleotide polymorphism in PIK3CA (rs7640662), AKT1 (rs10138227, rs1130214, rs1130233) and mTOR (rs1064261, rs1883965) and E542K and E17K hotspot mutations may have a role in etiology of colorectal carcinoma in Pakistani population. This is the first study to report significant correlation of these variants with colorectal carcinoma risk in Pakistan.

Case Summary

A 31-year-old woman with no known cardiovascular disease presented with progressive nonproductive cough, marked unintentional weight loss, dyspnea, fatigue, and orthopnea. Initial transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) showed severe pericardial effusion without tamponade. Additional imaging revealed left pleural effusion requiring drainage, axillary lymphadenopathy, an irregular pancreatic tail lesion measuring 27 × 35 mm, and a suspected intracardiac mass.

Repeat TTE was done due to persistence of symptoms, demonstrating a heterogeneous 21 × 10-mm apical mass attached to the left apical ventricular wall, with echogenicity similar to myocardium and well-defined borders. After administration of an ultrasound-enhancing agent, the lesion showed contrast uptake, favoring vascularized tumor involvement rather than bland thrombus.

Echocardiography also demonstrated right ventricular (RV) pressure overload. Cross-sectional imaging showed right pulmonary artery compression by metastatic mediastinal lymphadenopathy, resulting in functional supravalvular pulmonary stenosis. RV failure was evidenced with intraventricular septal flattening, increased right-to-left ventricular ratio, chamber dilation, reduced tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion, and shortened pulmonary flow acceleration time (see Figure 1). Excisional axillary lymph node biopsy confirmed metastatic pancreatic malignancy.

The patient subsequently deteriorated with recurrent malignant pericardial effusion and right atrial diastolic collapse, consistent with tamponade physiology, requiring surgical pericardial window. Despite decompression, she progressed to obstructive shock attributed to malignant pulmonary vascular compression, requiring intensive care and high-dose norepinephrine. Renal function worsened despite aggressive diuresis, consistent with RV failure, venous congestion, low forward flow, and shock physiology. After multidisciplinary discussion, additional invasive intervention was not pursued due to advanced malignancy, hemodynamic deterioration, and limited expected benefit. The patient was discharged with palliative care support.

Discussion

This case highlights an unusual mechanism of cancer-related cardiovascular collapse: extrinsic pulmonary artery compression causing RV failure and obstructive shock. Cardiac metastases are more common than primary cardiac tumors and may involve the heart through hematogenous spread, lymphatic dissemination, direct extension, or transvenous invasion.1 Although pancreatic cancer rarely involves the heart, advanced disseminated disease can produce malignant pericardial effusion, intracardiac tumor involvement, and extracardiac vascular obstruction.

The contrast-enhancing left ventricular mass was notable because bland thrombi lack enhancement, whereas vascular tumors or metastases may enhance after contrast administration.2 However, the most distinctive feature was malignant pulmonary artery compression by metastatic mediastinal lymphadenopathy. In patients with cancer, dyspnea, hypoxemia, RV strain, and shock are normally attributed to pulmonary embolism. Nevertheless, extrinsic malignant obstruction can cause similar findings without intraluminal thrombus.3 A targeted review of published cases identified few reports of cardiac involvement from pancreatobiliary tumors and scarce reports of malignant extrinsic vascular compression, mainly from thoracic tumors or lymphoma (see Supplemental Tables 1 and 2).

This case underscores the value of multimodality imaging: TTE revealed consequences of increase afterload on the RV, whereas contrast-enhanced computed tomography identified the extracardiac mechanism of vascular compression. Recognition matters because treatment does not rely on systemic anticoagulation. Potential therapies include radiation therapy, pulmonary artery stenting, or surgery; however, advanced disease and hemodynamic instability may limit therapeutic benefit.3

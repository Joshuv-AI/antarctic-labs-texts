Lenticular injury due to inadvertent intravitreal injection
Authors: Sonali Prataprao Lomte, Prajakta Paritekar
Published: 2025

The prevalence of diabetic retinopathy (DR) in India is estimated to be 12.5%.[1] Antivascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections are commonly being used for the treatment of DR.[2] It is a minimally invasive procedure. It can have minor complications like elevated intraocular pressure or subconjunctival hemorrhage. However, it can sometimes lead to vision-threatening complications such as endophthalmitis, intraocular inflammation, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, or iatrogenic cataract.[3] Incidence of cataract caused by needle injury due to improper technique of anti-VEGF injection was 0.006% as described by Meyer et al.[4] A meta-analysis conducted by Bjerager et al. showed 4% increase in risk of polymerase chain reaction with every anti-VEGF injection.[5] We describe the case of a 60-year-old phakic male patient with severe DR with macular edema who was scheduled to undergo intravitreal ranibizumab injection in the right eye by a trainee resident. After administering topical anesthetic drops and applying speculum, 4 mm was measured with Vernier calipers. After entering through the pars plana, the drug was injected. Postinjection, the patient complained of diminished vision. Dilated examination showed cataract with demarcated needle track and posterior capsule rupture, as shown in Figure 1. Therefore, we conclude that proper technique should be mastered before taking up a case. Iatrogenic vision-threatening complications like lens injury can be avoided by providing supervision and training to trainee doctors. Green flags for a successful intravitreal injection are: (1) distance of needle entry from the limbus, (2) directing the needle toward the center of the vitreous cavity, and (3) stable head position.

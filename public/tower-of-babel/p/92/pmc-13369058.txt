ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation of PRMT6 inhibits neuronal apoptosis and ferroptosis in Alzheimer's disease via reducing transcription of ACSL4
Authors: Jingwei Cao, Haining Wang, Baichao Han, Jihe Song, Zhanbin Tang
Published: 2026

Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disease and may affect an estimated 50 million patients worldwide (Rostagno 2022). As populations age, the global prevalence of AD is expected to continue to rise (Zhang et al. 2021). Clinically, AD leads to cognitive impairment and the decreased independence from the individual’s daily activities (Breijyeh and Karaman 2020). It is reported that progressive neuronal loss, neuronal death, and abnormal iron accumulation are important causes of AD progression (Scheltens et al. 2021). More and more evidence shows that neuronal ferroptosis is closely related to AD development (Wang et al. 2023a). Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4)-mediated ferroptosis plays a vital function in AD progression (Zha 2024). Therefore, exploring the molecular mechanisms regulating ACSL4-mediated neuronal ferroptosis is important for the development of potential targets for AD therapy.

Protein arginine methylation catalyzed by protein arginine methyltransferase (PRMTs) is a common post-translational modifications (Sun et al. 2023). PRMT6 catalyzes the asymmetric dimethylation of histone H3 arginine 2 (H3R2me2a) to regulate downstream gene expression (Chen et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023). Studies have shown that PRMT6 participates in disease progression by regulating cell biological functions. For example, PRMT6 was upregulated in endometrial cancer, which promoted cell proliferation and migration to accelerate cancer malignancy progression (Jiang et al. 2020). Also, PRMT6 regulated GPX4 expression by enhancing H3R2me2a, ultimately inducing ferroptosis and promoting the pathogenicity of mycobacterium tuberculosis (Qiang et al. 2023). Notably, previous research confirmed the high mRNA level of PRMT6 in aged AD mice, suggesting that PRMT6 might be related to AD progression (Clemons et al. 2024). In this, we found that PRMT6 could promote ACSL4 transcription via enriching H3R2me2a. However, whether PRMT6 affects AD progression by regulating ACSL4-medaited neuronal ferroptosis remains unclear.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification is a common chemical modifications in eukaryotic mRNA (Oerum et al. 2021). Han et al. found that m6A methylation levels were elevated in APP/PS1 mice and promoted the development of AD (Han et al. 2020). AlkB homologue 5 (ALKBH5) is an important demethylase, capable of demethylating mRNA in the nucleus (Ye et al. 2023). It has been reported that ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation regulated gene expression by mediating multiple cellular events (Qu et al. 2022). Studies found that ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation of IL-11 promoted cardiac fibrosis and dysfunction (Zhuang et al. 2024). Another study showed that ALKBH5 regulated PKMYT1 expression in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby inhibiting gastric cancer cell metastasis (Hu et al. 2022). Importantly, abnormal ALKBH5 expression might be associated with many neurological diseases, including AD (Zhang et al. 2022). Our study found that there were multiple m6A modification sites in PRMT6 mRNA. Further analysis discovered that ALKBH5 could inhibit PRMT6 expression by m6A demethylation. However, whether ALKBH5 mediates AD progression by inhibiting the PRMT6/ACSL4 axis has not been revealed.

This study reveals a potential molecular mechanism that regulates the progression of AD, namely the ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 axis. These findings provide new ideas for understanding the pathogenesis of clinical AD and developing the therapeutic programs.

Methods

Animal models

Male APP/PS1 mice (6-month-old) were purchased from Cavens (Changzhou, China), with the wild-type male C57BL/6 mice as control. Adeno-associated virus (AAV, 5 × 108 TU/mL) expressing the shRNA of PRMT6 (AAV-sh-PRMT6), negative control (AAV-sh-NC), and pcDNA overexpression vectors of ALKBH5/PRMT6 (AAV-pc-ALKBH5/AAV-pc-PRMT6) (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) were stereotaxically injected into the CA1 region of APP/PS1 mice hippocampus (AP −1.82 mm, ML ± 1.25 mm, and DV −1.85 mm) using a pneumatic injection device, with 50 μL/mouse injection volume (with a rate of 0.5 μL/min). After 4 weeks, the behavioral test was performed. Then, all mice were euthanized to collect their brain tissues. All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (Approval number: 2023158).

Cell culture, transfection and treatment

Mouse hippocampal neuronal cells (HT-22) and human neuroblastoma cells (SH-SY5Y) were obtained from Cellbank (Shanghai, China) and cultured in DMEM and DMEM/F12 (1:1) containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, respectively. Oligomers of amyloid-β protein fragment 1—42 (Aβ1—42, A9810, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) were prepared as previously described (Li et al. 2024). In brief, hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP)-dissolved Aβ1—42 was resuspended using DMSO and diluted in PBS to a concentration of 100 μM. After that, solution was incubated at 4 °C for 24 h. SH-SY5Y and HT-22 cells were induced with 2 μM Aβ1—42 oligomers (hereafter called Aβ1—42) for 48 h to mimic AD cell models based on previous study report (Li et al. 2024). For transfection, cells were transfected with shRNA for PRMT6 (sh-PRMT6), pcDNA overexpression vectors for ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 (pc-ALKBH5/pc-PRMT6/pc-ACSL4) and negative controls (sh-NC and pc-NC) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Western blot (WB)

Proteins were extracted by RIPA buffer, separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membrane was blocked and then incubated with antibodies, including anti-GPX4 (1/1000, ab125066, Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), anti-SLC7A11 (1/1000, ab307601, Abcam), anti-ALKBH5 (1/1000, ab195377, Abcam), anti-PRMT6 (1/2000, 15395—1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), anti-ACSL4 (1/10000, ab15528, Abcam), anti-GAPDH (1/2500, ab9485, Abcam) and secondary antibody (1/50000, ab205718, Abcam). Protein signals were detected by ECL reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

CCK8 assay

SH-SY5Y and HT-22 cells were cultured for 48 h in 96-well plates. Following, cells were incubated with CCK8 reagent (C0038, Beyotime), and cell viability was determined at 450 nm by a microplate reader.

TUNEL staining

TUNEL Apoptosis Assay Kit (C1088, Beyotime) was used. SH-SY5Y and HT-22 cells were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde, permeated using 0.3% Triton X-100 and sealed. In addition, the sections of mouse brain tissues were dewaxed, digested with protease K and sealed using 3% H2O2 in PBS. Afterwards, cell or tissue samples were incubated with TUNEL solution and DAB solution. TUNEL positive cells were analyzed under a microscope to assess apoptosis ratio.

Detection of ROS production

SH-SY5Y and HT-22 cells were collected and re-suspended with BODIPY 581/591 C11 solution (S0043S, Beyotime) for 20 min. After centrifugation, cell samples were harvested and green/red fluorescence intensity was analyzed under a fluorescence microscope to assess lipid ROS level. Besides, mice brain tissue sections were incubated with 10 μM DHE working solution (S0063, Beyotime) for 20 min. Samples were washed twice with PBS, and DHE areas were analyzed under a microscope to evaluate ROS production.

Measurement of ferroptosis

The levels of MDA, SOD and Fe2+ were assessed according to product instructions. In simple terms, hippocampal tissues were homogenized, and cells were lysed. After centrifuging, the collected supernatant was incubated with MDA Assay Kit (ab118970, Abcam), SOD Activity Assay Kit (ab65354, Abcam) and Iron Assay Kit (ab83366, Abcam). Finally, absorbance was evaluated using a microplate reader.

Morris water maze (MWM) test

Before the experiment, all the mice were placed in a pool of water to swim freely for 2 min, where the platform was exposed. All mice were asked to stay on the platform for 10 s. On days 2—5, the platform was hidden, and the mice were placed in the water from any of the 4 marked locations. The time of climbing the platform was recorded to assess the escape latency. On day 6, the platform was removed, and the platform crossing time and number, as well as the route map of the search for the platform, were recorded within 2 min.

Novel object recognition (NOR) test

Mice were allowed to explore freely within the experimental apparatus (without objects) for 10 min and lasts for 3 days. Two identical objects were placed in the device, and mice were placed in which to explore freely. Finally, one of the two identical objects was replaced with a different object, and the mice could continue to explore for 10 min. Exploration time was defined when the mouse's head was 2 cm away from the object or when the nose touched the object. Discrimination index (DI%) = new object/(new object + old object) × 100%.

Histological staining

For hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining, paraffin sections of mice hippocampal tissues were dewaxed, hydrated, stained with hematoxylin and eosin (C0105S, Beyotime). After sealing, the pathological changes of hippocampus was observed under microscope. For immunohistochemical (IHC) staining, sections were dewaxed to hydration, antigen repaired, and closed with goat serum. After incubated with HRP-labeled anti-Aβ1—42 (ab201060, 1/1000, Abcam) and stained with DAPI, Aβ deposition was observed under microscope.

MeRIP assay

SRAMP database (http://www.cuilab.cn/m6asiteapp/old) was used to predict PRMT6 mRNA m6A modification site. Total RNAs were extracted from SH-SY5Y and HT-22 cells transfected with pc-NC/pc-ALKBH5 and treated with Aβ1—42. Then, RNAs were processed into fragments, and fragmented RNA was incubated with protein A/G magnetic beads pre-coated with anti-m6A or anti-IgG using Magna MeRIP m6A Kit (17—10499, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). After that, PRMT6 m6A level was analyzed by qRT-PCR.

RIP assay

According to the instructions of RIP Kit (17—700, Millipore), Aβ1—42-induced cells transfected with pc-NC/pc-ALKBH5 were lysed using RIP buffer. Cell lysates were incubated with anti-ALKBH5, anti-IgG and magnetic beads. After that, the magnetic beads were washed by elution buffer, and qRT-PCR was used to assess the enrichment of PRMT6.

mRNA stability assay

Aβ1—42-induced cells transfected with pc-NC/pc-ALKBH5 were treated with actinomycin D (Act D, A8030, Solarbio, Beijing, China). Next, RNA was extracted and qRT-PCR was performed to detect PRMT6 expression at fixed time points.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The sequences of ACSL4 promoter (human-derived and mouse-derived) containing the binding sites of PRMT6 were cloned into psiCheck-2 to construct ACSL4 promoter-luciferase reporter. SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells were co-transfected with sh-NC/sh-PRMT6/pc-NC/pc-ACSL4 and ACSL4 promoter-luciferase reporter, with empty promoter as basic control. Relative luciferase activity (calculated as the ratio of Firefly luciferase value to Renilla luciferase value) was tested by Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (E1910, Promega, Madison, WI, USA) after 48 h.

ChIP-qPCR assay

Cells transfected with sh-NC/sh-PRMT6/pc-NC-WT/pc-PRMT6-MUT were incubated with formaldehyde and glycine. Then, precipitated cells were lysed and cell supernatant was obtained. Using Magna ChIP Kit (17—10085, Millipore), cell supernatant was incubated with anti-PRMT6, anti-H3R2me2a, anti-IgG and protein A agarose. After purified, precipitated complexes were used for qRT-PCR.

Statistical analysis

The number of groups remained the same for all animal studies (n = 8 per group) and cell studies (n = 3 per group), where n is the biological replicate. Data are expressed as mean ± SD using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Statistical differences were assessed using Student’s t-test (Two sets of data) or ANOVA (Three or more groups) with Tukey’s post hoc test (if F achieved P < 0.05 and there was no significant variance inhomogeneity). P < 0.05 was denoted statistically significant.

Results

PRMT6 knockdown inhibited Aβ1—42-induced apoptosis and ferroptosis in neuronal cells

By qRT-PCR and WB assay, PRMT6 levels were increased in AD mice compared with control group (Fig. 1A-B). To explore the role of PRMT6 in AD progression, loss-of-functional experiments were performed in AD cell models. After transfected with sh-PRMT6 into SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells, PRMT6 mRNA and protein levels were markedly decreased (Fig. 1C-D). Besides, Aβ1—42 treatment enhanced PRMT6 expression in SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells, and this effect was abolished by sh-PRMT6 (Fig. 1E-F). Functional experiments showed that sh-PRMT6 promoted viability and inhibited apoptosis rate in Aβ1—42-treated cells (Fig. 1G-H). Furthermore, silencing of PRMT6 inhibited lipid ROS fluorescence intensity, MDA and Fe2+ levels, while enhanced SOD activity in Aβ1—42 treated SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells (Fig. 1I-L). Meanwhile, PRMT6 knockdown facilitated the protein levels of anti-ferroptosis related markers (GPX4 and SLC7A11) (Fig. 1M). These results indicated that PRMT6 might promote neuronal cells apoptosis and ferroptosis to accelerate AD progression.

Silencing of PRMT6 suppressed ferroptosis to alleviate cognitive deficits in AD mice

To further confirm the anti-AD role of sh-PRMT6, the animal study was carried out. As shown in Fig. 2A-F, AD mice showed the prolonged escape latency, the reduced target platform crossing number, the decreased target platform time, the lower DI% and exploration time, while AAV-sh-PRMT6 partially reversed these phenomena. By histological staining, we found that AAV-sh-PRMT6 improved hippocampal neuron injury, Aβ deposition, and neuronal cell apoptosis in AD mice (Fig. 2G-I). Besides, AAV-sh-PRMT6 reduced ROS production, MDA and Fe2+ levels, while increased SOD activity in the hippocampal tissues of AD mice (Fig. 2J-M). Also, we detected the reduced PRMT6 protein level and the increased GPX4/SLC7A11 expression in AD mice hippocampal tissues after injected with AAV-sh-PRMT6 (Fig. 2N). The above data indicated that PRMT6 knockdown might attenuate cognitive deficits in AD mice via inhibiting cell ferroptosis.

ALKBH5 mediated the m6A modification of PRMT6

SRAMP database predicted the m6A modification sites of PRMT6 mRNA (Fig. 3A). MeRIP assay confirmed the high m6A modification level of PRMT6 in SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells (Fig. 3B). RNA demethylase ALKBH5 has been confirmed to be involved in regulating AD progression (Zhang et al. 2022). Through the analysis of qRT-PCR and WB, ALKBH5 mRNA and protein levels were decreased in AD mice (Fig. 3C-D). Also, there had a significant negative correlation between PRMT6 and ALKBH5 expression in AD mice (Fig. 3E). Moreover, ALKBH5 mRNA and protein expression was downregulated in Aβ1—42-induced cells (Fig. 3F-G). Further RIP analysis showed that PRMT6 mRNA enrichment was increased by anti-ALKBH5 in SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells (Fig. 3H). The transfection of pc-ALKBH5 increased ALKBH5 expression and decreased PRMT6 expression in SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells (Fig. 3I-J). To further verify the interaction between ALKBH5 and PRMT6, we performed MeRIP and RIP assays. The results revealed that pc-ALKBH5 suppressed the PRMT6 m6A modification level and promoted PRMT6 mRNA enrichment in anti-ALKBH5 in Aβ1—42-induced both cells (Fig. 3K-L). Furthermore, pc-ALKBH5 reduced PRMT6 expression and mRNA stability in Aβ1—42-induced both cells (Fig. 3M-N). All results suggested that ALKBH5 decreased PRMT6 expression in neuron cells by m6A modification.

ALKBH5 inhibited Aβ1—42-induced neuronal cell apoptosis and ferroptosis by downregulating PRMT6

Based on the above results, we studied whether ALKBH5 affected AD progression by regulating PRMT6. In Aβ1—42-induced both cells, the transfection of pc-ALKBH5 reduced PRMT6 mRNA and protein expression, while pc-PRMT6 eliminated this effect (Fig. 4A-B). Functionally, ALKBH5 overexpression enhanced viability and repressed apoptosis rate, while this effect was reversed by pc-PRMT6 (Fig. 4C-D). Besides, the inhibitory effects of pc-ALKBH5 on lipid ROS fluorescence intensity, MDA and Fe2+ levels, as well as the facilitation effect on SOD activity could be reverted by pc-PRMT6 (Fig. 4E-H). At the same time, overexpression of PRMT6 abolished the increasing effect of pc-ALKBH5 on GPX4 and SLC7A11 protein levels (Fig. 4I). Thus, ALKBH5 might reduce PRMT6 expression to alleviate neuronal cell apoptosis and ferroptosis.

PRMT6 mediated ACSL4 transcription by H3R2me2a

Inhibition of ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis has been confirmed to mitigate AD progression (Zhu et al. 2022). Here, we detected the high ACSL4 expression in AD mice (Fig. 5A-B), and found that ACSL4 was positively correlated with PRMT6 level (Fig. 5C). In Aβ1—42-induced SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells, sh-PRMT6 decreased ACSL4 mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 5D-E). It has been reported that PRMT6 mediate H3R2me2a to affect the expression of downstream genes (Hamey et al. 2021). Dual-luciferase reporter assay showed that sh-PRMT6 and pc-PRMT6 could decrease and increase the luciferase activity of ACSL4 promoter-luciferase reporter, respectively (Fig. 5F-G). Moreover, ChIP-qPCR confirmed that sh-PRMT6 reduced the enrichment of ACSL4 promoter in anti-PRMT6 and lower the H3R2me2a level of ACSL4 promoter (Fig. 5H-I). Both pc-PRMT6-WT and pc-PRMT6-MUT increased the enrichment of ACSL4 promoter in anti-PRMT6 (Fig. 5J). Meanwhile, only pc-PRMT6-WT increased the H3R2me2a level of ACSL4 promoter, while pc-PRMT6-MUT had no effect (Fig. 5K). Therefore, we believed that PRMT6 might promote the transcription of ACSL4 by H3R2me2a.

ACSL4 overexpression reversed sh-PRMT6-mediated neuronal cell apoptosis and ferroptosis

To further investigate whether PRMT6 regulated AD progression by regulating ACSL4, the rescue experiments were performed. The detection of ACSL4 expression suggested that the decreasing effect of sh-PRMT6 on ACSL4 mRNA and protein levels could be abolished by pc-ACSL4 in Aβ1—42-induced SH-SY5Y and HT22 cells (Fig. 6A-B). The increasing effect of sh-PRMT6 on cell viability, SOD activity, GPX4 and SLC7A11 proteins, as well as the decreasing effect on cell apoptosis rate, lipid ROS fluorescence intensity, MDA and Fe2+ levels could be reversed by pc-ACSL4 (Fig. 6C-I). Therefore, we confirmed that PRMT6 might contribute to AD progression via enhancing ACSL4 expression.

ALKBH5 alleviated AD process in mice via inhibiting the PRMT6/ACSL4 axis

Finally, animal experiments were performed to investigate the effect of ALKBH5 on AD process via the PRMT6/ACSL4 axis. The injection of AAV-pc-ALKBH5 reduced the escape latency, increased target platform crossing number, target platform time, the DI% and exploration time in AD mice, while AAV-pc-PRMT6 could reverse these effects (Fig. 7A-F). Furthermore, the inhibitory effect of AAV-pc-ALKBH5 on hippocampal neuron injury, Aβ deposition and neuronal cell apoptosis in AD mice could be eliminated by AAV-pc-PRMT6 (Fig. 7G-I). Also, AAV-pc-PRMT6 abolished AAV-pc-ALKBH5-mediated the suppressing effect on ROS production, MDA, and Fe2+ levels, as well as the promoting effect on SOD activity, in hippocampal tissue of AD mice (Fig. 7J-M). Meanwhile, AAV-pc-ALKBH5 enhanced ALKBH5, GPX4 and SLC7A11 levels, while reduced PRMT6 and ACSL4 levels in hippocampal tissues of AD mice. However, AAV-pc-PRMT6 could eliminate these effects without affecting ALKBH5 expression (Fig. 7N). The above data indicated that ALKBH5 relieved cognitive deficits in AD mice models via inhibiting cell ferroptosis through repressing PRMT6/ACSL4 axis (Fig. 8).

Discussion

The main pathological features of AD are the amyloid plaque accumulation in the extracellular area and neurofibrillary tangles in the intracellular area (Athar et al. 2021). Despite great advances in understanding of AD pathogenesis, there is currently no cure method for AD (Chen and Zhang 2022). Ferroptosis is a form of programmed cell death and has been confirmed to be closely related to the performance of neuronal loss and cognitive decline in AD (Lane et al. 2021; Han et al. 2023). Ferroptosis caused iron dysregulation, ROS, GSH and GPX4 levels are considered to be important pathological factors of AD (Feng et al. 2024). Here, we have confirmed a new mechanism that regulates ferroptosis, identifying the consequence that the ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 axis mediates AD progression by regulating neuronal ferroptosis.

PRMTs can methylate a variety of proteins and affect many cellular processes (Lee et al. 2022). Abnormal expression of PRMTs is considered to be associated with human diseases. It was reported that PRMT1 was upregulated in the synovial tissue of rheumatoid arthritis and affected disease development by mediating EZH2 expression (Liu et al. 2023). PRMT6 expression was elevated in primary breast tumor tissue samples, and targeted inhibition of PRMT6 might be a promising treatment strategy for breast cancer (Wu et al. 2017). Besides, PRMT5 activated WNT/β-catenin signaling by reducing DKK1 and DKK3, thereby controlling breast cancer cell growth (Shailesh et al. 2021). At present, there are few studies on the relevance of in AD. Although Clemons et al. demonstrated an abnormally high expression of PRMT6 in aged female AD mice (Clemons et al. 2024), the role of PRMT6 in AD progression has yet to be further revealed. Our study found that PRMT6 expression was upregulated in AD mice and Aβ1—42-induced neuronal cells. Functional experiments suggested that PRMT6 downregulation could reduce Aβ1—42-induced apoptosis and ferroptosis in neuronal cells, as well as relieve cognitive deficits in AD mice. These results verified that PRMT6 played an active role in AD progression, and it might be a potential molecular target for AD treatment.

As a member of m6A demethylase, ALKBH5 is involved in the process of many diseases by mediating m6A modification of related genes (Chen et al. 2020). For example, ALKBH5 repressed mitochondrial fission and ameliorated liver fibrosis by mediating m6A demodification of Drp1 (Wang et al. 2023b). Liu et al. reported that GLUT4 upregulation by ALKBH-mediated m6A demodification could induce glycolysis and drug resistance in breast cancer (Liu et al. 2022). Furthermore, ALKBH5 promoted cervical cancer metastasis via mediating m6A modification of circCCDC134 (Liang et al. 2022). Previous study revealed that ALKBH5 expression was associated with neurodegenerative diseases, including AD, and its deficiency might accelerate cell apoptosis and cobalt-induced neurodegenerative damage (Zheng et al. 2023). This research suggested that ALKBH5 might play a negative role in AD. Here, ALKBH5 was found to be significantly decreased in AD mice and Aβ1—42-induced neuronal cells. In terms of mechanism, ALKBH5 suppressed PRMT6 expression by decreasing its m6A modification. Furthermore, PRMT6 overexpression reversed the inhibitory effect of ALKBH5 on Aβ1—42-induced neuronal cell apoptosis and ferroptosis, as well as the relieving effect on cognitive deficits in AD mice. These data further confirmed that ALKBH5 alleviated AD progression by regulating the m6A demodification of PRMT6.

Lipid peroxidation is a major feature of ferroptosis and is associated with the development of many diseases, including neurological disorders (Wang et al. 2022). Studies have shown that ACSL4 participates in regulating ferroptosis by arachidonic acid and eicosapentaenoic acid (Jia et al. 2023). Previous studies found that ischemia promoted intestinal ischemia/reperfusion tissue damage by activating ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis (Li et al. 2019). Knockdown of CARM1-mediated ACSL4 methylation facilitated ferroptosis in colorectal cancer (Feng et al. 2023). Importantly, it had been reported that lysophosphatidylcholine ameliorated AD pathologies by inhibiting ferroptosis via reducing ACSL4 expression, confirming the important role of ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis in AD progression (Zha 2024). In this study, we found that PRMT6 positively regulated ACSL4 transcription and expression by catalyzing H3R2me2a, and confirmed that sh-PRMT6-mediated the inhibition on neuronal cell apoptosis and ferroptosis could be reversed by ACSL4 overexpression. Furthermore, animal experiments revealed that ALKBH5 reduced PRMT6 and ACSL4 expression to alleviate cognitive deficits in AD mice, which further confirmed the existence of ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 axis in regulating AD progression.

However, this study also has some limitations. First, the clinical samples from human AD patients are lacking to validate the expression levels and correlation of ALKBH5, PRMT6 and ACSL4. Second, although ChIP-qPCR confirmed that PRMT6 was enriched in the promoter region of ACSL4 and promoted its transcription through H3R2me2a modification, However, we did not further pinpoint the specific binding sequence or region of PRMT6 on the ACSL4 promoter. Finally, it is not clear which key m6A modification sites are responsible for the regulation of PRMT6 by ALKBH5. In the future, solving the above problems may further enrich our experimental content and provide more solid evidence for the existence of ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 axis in AD.

Conclusions

To sum up, our findings indicated that PRMT6, regulated by ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation, promoted neuronal apoptosis and ferroptosis to accelerate AD progression via enhancing ACSL4 expression. The proposed ALKBH5/PRMT6/ACSL4 axis provides a new molecular target for AD treatment, which has important clinical significance.

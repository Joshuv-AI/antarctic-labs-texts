Enhancing accuracy and efficiency in upper extremity disability assessments: development and testing of a desktop application
Authors: Emre Ozmen, Gamze Gül Güleç, Hazal İzol Özmen, Melih Civan, Esra Circi, Serdar Yuksel, Alican Baris
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Objective:This study aimed to validate a software application (app) developed by the authors to streamline and enhance the accuracy of disability assessments, specifically for musculoskeletal system disabilities in the upper extremities.Methods:A software was developed under a TÜBİTAK-funded project to aid in the disability assessment process. This tool, designed for Windows operating systems and developed in Visual Basic (VB.NET), was tested using archive data from 50 patients, focusing on upper extremity physical evaluations. Statistical analysis, including the Shapiro–Wilk and Independent t-test/Mann–Whitney U-tests, was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics to compare the app-assisted and manual assessment methods regarding time and rating’.Results:Significant time-saving was observed with the app-assisted method, which was 328.3 seconds faster on average than the manual method. The average rating difference between the 2 methods was minor (0.40 points, 0.92% difference) and not statistically significant (P = .931). The app-assisted method showed efficiency in disability assessment with comparable accuracy to the manual methods.Conclusion:This application was developed for physicians who examine patients with musculoskeletal system disabilities in the upper extremities for the Health Board. Our results show that the application reduces the average evaluation time by 5 minutes while maintaining accuracy comparable to the manual method. It could be a helpful tool for physicians in a clinical setting.Level of Evidence: Level IV, Diagnostic Study.

Introduction

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the term disability encompasses challenges in bodily structure or function, mental functioning, activity limitations like vision or mobility issues, and participation restrictions affecting daily life activities such as work, social interactions, and healthcare access.1 World Report on Disability by WHO and the World Bank estimates that more than a billion people in the world today experience disability.2 The 2022 Population and Housing Survey by the Turkish Ministry of Family and Social Services found that 6.9% of the population aged 3 and above, totaling 4 876 000 people, reported having at least one disability.3 This survey included 6 disability groups (vision, hearing, speech, walking, carrying-holding, and learning), and it was found that the number of patients with disabilities in walking or carrying-holding within the musculoskeletal system (MSK) is 3 239 000, constituting 66% of the disabled population. A 2022 report by the Turkish Statistical Institute (TURKSTAT) also noted that in 2021, 854 000 individuals were on disability pension.4 These statistics underscore the profound social and economic impact of disabilities and the necessity of accurate evaluation for various purposes.

A Health Board (Sağlık Kurulu) report provides information about the chronic illnesses of disabled individuals and shows the percentage of disability. In Türkiye, for adult disabled individuals, these reports are given by the joint assessment of relevant branches in Health Boards. The disability rating obtained by the health council provides different rights to patients depending on whether they are severely disabled and fully or partially dependent.5 The disability rating, which is the most crucial part of disability reports, grants significant rights to disabled individuals. According to regulations implemented in Türkiye, to benefit from disability rights, the disability rating must be 40% or higher.6 Therefore, accurate calculation of disability ratings not only protects the rights of disabled citizens but also should reduce legal issues that service providers may face in case of a dispute. The aim of this study was to test a software application (app) developed by the authors to streamline and enhance the accuracy of disability assessments, specifically for musculoskeletal system disabilities in the upper extremities.

Material and methods

The software was developed as part of a TÜBİTAK-funded project (Project ID: 123E439) using the Visual Basic (VB.NET) programming language in Visual Studio 2019 and compiled in .exe format for standalone use on Windows-based operating systems. The guidelines for scoring upper extremity disabilities were taken from the official documentation.7 After the initial testing was done with hypothetical patient scenarios, Ethics Committee approval was obtained from Istanbul Physical Medicine Rehabilitation Training and Research Hospital (Approval No: 2024-06, Date: 23.01.2024). Informed consent was obtained to use patient data. History and physical examination data from 50 patients were used in this study in a retrospective manner. Patient upper extremity physical examination results were recorded in a range-of-motion (ROM) template in each encounter, together with any related addendums such as the presence of amputations and arthroplasties. One of the authors (GG), who is experienced in MSK disability assessment, retrospectively evaluated these records and calculated disability ratings both manually and via the app, with the assessment durations also recorded.The Shapiro–Wilk test was conducted to assess the normality of the time measurements for both the app-assisted and manual methods. An independent t-test was used for normally distributed values, and a Mann–Whitney U-test was used for non-normally distributed values. A P-value of less than .05 was accepted as significant. All statistical calculations were performed in IBM SPSS Statistics Version 20.0 (IBM SPSS Corp.; Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

The Shapiro–Wilk test for performed time measurements and ratings, which showed that ratings were normally distributed (P = .93) whereas time measurements were non-normally distributed (P < .0001). Time measurements showed a significant difference between the app-assisted and manual methods (Figure 1). For the app-assisted method, the average time was 104.8 seconds, with a SD of 83.8 seconds. The times ranged from 17 to 480 seconds. In contrast, the manual method had a notably higher average time of 433.1 seconds (SD = 335.1), with a range from 68 to 1380 seconds. The app-assisted method was on average faster by 328.3 seconds than the manual method. The Mann–Whitney U-test was utilized to compare the app-assisted and manual time measurements which revealed a significant difference between the 2 methods (P < .001).The app-assisted ratings had an average of 43.4, with a SD of 22.7. These ratings ranged from a minimum of 8 to a maximum of 90. On the other hand, the manual ratings exhibited a slightly higher average of 43.8 (SD = 23.5), with the range of ratings extending from 8 to 99. The absolute average difference in ratings between the 2 methods was 0.40, which corresponds to a percentage difference of 0.92% (Figure 2). The independent samples t-test was performed to compare manual and app ratings, which were not found to be statistically different (P = .931).

Discussion

Disability and impairment are common issues worldwide. In the United States, nearly 1 in 5 Americans report a disability,8 and approximately 1.5 billion people worldwide are reported to live with a physical, mental, sensory, or intellectual disability.9 Similarly, the assessment of disability is also a common problem for both the patients and the assessing physicians. In Türkiye, physicians are frequently asked to assess the disability of the presenting patient regardless of their training and background in the matter.10 The physician or specialist may not have received formal education on how to evaluate disability,11 yet— he/she may find himself in a situation where he needs to evaluate a complex disability in a matter of minutes.

An ideal assessment system should be able to accurately identify patients’ disabilities while leaving minimal room for malingering and exaggerated reports of symptoms. In that sense, it should probably be a combination of objective findings, subjective symptoms, and work-specific disabilities. There are different methods of disability evaluation such as independent medical evaluation (IME), functional capacity evaluation (FCE), and work capacity evaluation (WCE). Independent medical evaluation (IME) not only consists of medical diagnoses and results of physical examination but also incorporates analysis of job tasks and assessment of ability to perform a specific job task. Functional capacity evaluation (FCE) and WCE, as stated in the name, are even heavier on the functional and work-related issues and as such are typically performed by physical therapists or occupational therapists.8

The legal basis for calculating disability ratings in Türkiye is determined by the Regulation on Disability Assessment for Adults (Official Gazette of the Republic of Türkiye, 20 February 2019, number: 351). The method for calculating individual disability ratings by relevant branches is detailed in Appendix-2 of this regulation.12 Appendix-2 consists of 181 pages.7 At a quick glance, it is observed that almost half of the guide is devoted to calculating disability ratings for the musculoskeletal system (pages 102-181). Compared to other specialties, the calculation of ratings in the sections related to the musculoskeletal system is quite complex. For instance, the Ear-Nose-Throat (ENT) section consists of only 15 pages (pages 1-15), whereas the subsection on “Disability Ratings for Upper Extremity Issues” alone comprises 33 pages (pages 118-150).

In the authors’ opinion, a combination of complex assessment systems, specialists undertrained in disability assessment, and short outpatient encounters can lead to mistakes, which can harm the patient, the physician, and the institution. Yet the evidence is lacking partly due to lackluster documentation of MSK disability ratings. There are only a few studies conducted on patient applications to the Health Board in Türkiye.

In a 2016 study titled “Evaluation of Reasons for Application to the Health Board,” Benli et al13 reported that 976 out of 2637 patients (37%) who applied to a single center in 2014 had complaints related to the musculoskeletal system. In a 2021 study by Adar et al,5 the severe disability rating was reported as 30.8% for individuals aged 0-17, 26.7% for those aged 18-64, and 71.5% for those over 65. Similar to Benli et al, 34.5% of the reports involved individuals receiving their disability rating from physical medicine and rehabilitation and orthopedics. Sarı et al14 reported in their 2022 study that 1059 out of 1703 patients (62.8%) who applied to the Health Board had at least one pathology affecting the musculoskeletal system. The percent of applications being related to the musculoskeletal system both indicates the volume encountered in these specialties and gives an idea about the benefits of any improvements in the process. Yet, despite these general statistics, due to assessments being made on paper, there is a lack of detailed data on the number, type, and reasons for disabled individuals applying to the Health Board in Türkiye.15 To our knowledge, this is the first application in Türkiye to provide computer assistance to the physician with such capabilities. The application we developed is easy and intuitive to use and presents the user with clear and retraceable information on each segment of the calculation process (Figure 3). This software also has the capability to report on the details of the final ratings, and has the ability to save information as anonymized .txt files with date and time stamps (Figure 4); however, this feature should be used while keeping in mind the necessary ethical committee approvals for saving clinical patient data.

Our results suggest that this application decreases the average time to evaluate a disability by 5 minutes while giving similar results as manual scoring. The application is available at https://github.com/disabilityrateapp/UpperExtremityDisabilityRateApp and the source code is freely available, under the AGPL-3.0 license, from the authors. The software itself is intuitive; nevertheless, a focused manual will be published online and in journal article format in the near future.

This application was developed for physicians who examine patients with musculoskeletal system disabilities in the upper extremities for the Health Board (Orthopedics and Traumatology/Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation). Our results show that it reduces the average evaluation time by 5 minutes while maintaining comparable accuracy to the manual method and could potentially be a helpful tool for physicians in a clinical setting.

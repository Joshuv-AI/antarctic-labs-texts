Effects of CPAP on Atherosclerotic Coronary Plaques in Patients with Sleep-Disordered Breathing
Authors: Tatsuya Fukase, Tomotaka Dohi, Takatoshi Kasai, Naotake Yanagisawa, Hirohisa Endo, Shinya Okazaki, Tohru Minamino
Published: 2024

We identified culprit plaque characteristics using intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) in patients with sleep-disordered breathing (SDB), and showed that SDB was associated with a larger atheroma plaque volume and greater ultrasound attenuation.1 However, no reports have explored the effects of SDB treatment using continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) on coronary atheroma volume and vulnerability in these patients. Therefore, the ENTERPRISE (Effects of CPAP on Atherosclerotic Coronary Plaques in Patients with SDB and Coronary Artery Disease) trial aimed to evaluate the effects of adding CPAP therapy to optimal medical treatment on coronary plaques, as evaluated using near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS)–IVUS that can simultaneously detect the plaque morphology and presence of lipid core plaques and is useful for identifying vulnerable plaques.

Methods

The ENTERPRISE trial was a single-center, prospective, randomized, 12-month, open-label study where patients undergoing elective percutaneous coronary intervention were enrolled from July 2018 to July 2021. Additionally, the detailed protocol has been described previously.2 The Institutional Ethics Committee of Juntendo Clinical Research and Trial Center approved the protocol (approval number: 18-021), which was registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network (ID: R000036293). All participants provided written informed consent. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Here, SDB was defined as ≥15 events/h of a 3% oxygen desaturation index determined using finger pulse-oximetry (PULSOX-Me300, Konica Minolta). A commercially available NIRS-IVUS system (Infraredx, TVC Imaging System or Makoto Imaging System) was used, and all images were analyzed offline by 2 cardiologists (T.F. and T.D.) who were blinded to the presence of SDB and CPAP grouping. Quantitative grayscale IVUS measurements were analyzed using QIvus version 2.1 (Medis). We assigned 2 groups based on permuted block randomization with body mass index <25 or ≥25 kg/m2, and 3% oxygen desaturation index <30 or ≥30 events/h; optimal medical therapy was provided with and without CPAP treatment (CPAP or non-CPAP groups, respectively) using auto-CPAP machines (Sleepmate 10 Auto; ResMed). Adequate CPAP treatment was defined as ≥75% days with ≥4 h of nightly use.

The primary endpoint was defined as the absolute change in percentage atheroma volume (PAV) in the nonculprit lesion in IVUS analysis. The secondary endpoints were absolute changes in maximum lipid core burden index (LCBI) calculated for every 4-mm (maxLCBI4mm) and 10-mm segment (maxLCBI10mm) in the nonculprit lesion using NIRS analysis, absolute change in normalized total atheroma volume (TAVnormalized) in the nonculprit lesion via IVUS analysis, absolute change in maximum intima-media thickness in carotid ultrasonography, absolute change in serum high-sensitivity C-reactive protein level, major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), and all-cause death.

Categorical data are presented as numbers (percentages) and were compared using the chi-square test. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± SD or as median (IQR) and were compared using Student t test or the Mann-Whitney U test. Impacts of CPAP on absolute changes in PAV and TAVnormalized were verified using generalized linear models with participants as a random effect and CPAP group, observation period, and these interaction term as fixed effects. Additionally, multiple linear regression analysis was performed using traditional coronary risk factors (systolic blood pressure, diabetes mellitus, current smoking, family history of premature coronary artery disease, and change in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol level). Moreover, between-group comparisons of the incidence of MACCE were evaluated using Fisher exact test. A P value <0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Overall, 209 patients participated; of these, 156 patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria (patients with stable coronary artery disease, those with 3% oxygen desaturation index ≥15 events/h, those with accurate baseline NIRS-IVUS data, and those having a maximal plaque burden >40% of the nonculprit lesion segment detected through IVUS) were excluded. Ultimately, 53 patients were randomly assigned to the CPAP and non-CPAP groups. During the follow-up period, 3 patients in the CPAP group discontinued CPAP; in the non-CPAP group, 2 patients lacked follow-up NIRS-IVUS measurements, and 1 was lost to follow-up. Therefore, 23 and 24 patients in the CPAP group and non-CPAP groups, respectively, were finally analyzed (mean age: 62 ± 8 years, with 96% being male [45/47 patients]). The 2 groups had similar baseline characteristics, and all patients were taking statins during the follow-up period, with 65 mg/dL and −14 mg/dL of median baseline and median change in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol level, respectively. The intraobserver and interobserver intraclass correlation coefficients for plaque burden on IVUS were 0.95 (95% CI: 0.89-0.98; P < 0.001) and 0.85 (95% CI: 0.65-0.94; P < 0.001), respectively. The kappa statistics for intraobserver and interobserver agreement for detecting a large plaque burden of ≥70 % were 0.88 (95% CI: 0.64-1.00; P < 0.001) and 0.86 (95% CI: 0.59-1.00; P < 0.001), respectively. The absolute change in PAV significantly differed between groups (Figure 1). The generalized linear models showed that the CPAP group × observational period interaction term was significantly associated with PAV (estimate: −2.72; 95% CI: −4.97 to −0.48; P = 0.018). Additionally, multiple linear regression analysis revealed that CPAP use (estimate: −1.27; 95% CI: −2.45 to −0.08; P = 0.037) was an independent predictor of the absolute change in PAV. Regarding the secondary endpoints, the absolute change in TAVnormalized differed between the CPAP and non-CPAP groups (−4 ± 18 mm3 vs 11 ± 23 mm3; P = 0.021). The generalized linear models showed that the CPAP group × observational period interaction term was significantly associated with TAVnormalized (estimate: −14; 95% CI: −26 to −2; P = 0.021). However, there were no significant differences in absolute changes in maxLCBI4mm, maxLCBI10mm, maximum intima-media thickness, or high-sensitivity C-reactive protein levels; moreover, no deaths occurred during the follow-up period. Discussion

The mechanism of vascular damage in patients with SDB involves oxidative stress, systemic inflammation, sympathetic activation, and subsequent endothelial dysfunction caused by the main acute consequences of SDB, including intermittent hypoxia.3 This arteriosclerotic state leads to the development of coronary plaques in patients with SDB; the degree of SDB is associated with the burden and vulnerability of noncalcified plaques detected using IVUS.1 However, this study targeted patients with stable coronary artery disease who had a main fibrous plaque and few lipid cores; therefore, the effect of CPAP treatment on these causal relationships deserves further discussion. A retrospective observational study on optical coherence tomography findings in patients with SDB with stable coronary artery disease showed that the CPAP-SDB group had significantly lower macrophage grading than the untreated-SDB group.4 Therefore, CPAP suppresses an elevated level of inflammatory markers. Furthermore, previous studies have shown that CPAP treatment contributes to the improvement and normalization of endothelial function. Consequently, CPAP treatment may lead to plaque regression due to the improvement of endothelial function.5 When combined with statins, CPAP treatment can further improve endothelial dysfunction, macrophage grading, and inflammatory response, and may also influence molecular structural changes even in plaques with few lipid cores.

This is the first study to report the effect of CPAP treatment on coronary plaque volume and lipid core plaque in patients with SDB and stable coronary artery disease who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention using NIRS-IVUS. CPAP treatment for patients with SDB could significantly contribute to a decrease in coronary plaque volume but not the mitigation of lipid-rich plaques.

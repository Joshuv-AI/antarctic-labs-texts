Health-resilient frameworks and their impact on routine immunisation and Maternal, Neonatal and Child Health services during pandemics in sub-Saharan Africa: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Clovis Nchinjoh Sangwe, Sibusiso Frank Nkosi, Kelebogile Leah Manjinja, Wilfred Njabulo Nunu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionDespite significant global advancements, the past decade has seen stagnation in Maternal, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) service coverage and a concerning high under-five and maternal mortality rates, which have been worsened by COVID-19 pandemic-related disruptions, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. This scoping review protocol will support the comprehensive mapping, evaluation and assessment of the application, impact, effectiveness and adaptability of health-resilient frameworks in maintaining these services during pandemics, while also identifying gaps in the literature and areas for further research.Methods and analysisFollowing the Joanna Briggs Institute guidelines, a literature search across databases such as PubMed, Scopus and African Journals Online for studies published from the inception of the databases to 2024 will be conducted. Covidence will facilitate the iterative screening process by two independent reviewers. Data extraction will employ the Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes and Healthcare Contexts framework to categorise information. The thematic synthesis will integrate the findings to comprehensively evaluate the framework’s application, impact, effectiveness and adaptability in the context of routine immunisation and MNCH services.Ethics and disseminationThis is part of a broader study approved by the evaluation committee of the Faculty of Health Sciences at the University of the Free State, and ethical clearance was granted by the university’s Human Research Ethics Committee with registration number UFS-HSD2025/0102/2705. The findings will be shared with relevant stakeholders through publications in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at meetings, conferences, seminars and professional forums.

Introduction

Maternal, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) services encompass the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth and postpartum (maternal health) and the health of children from 0 to 28 days of life (neonatal health) up to 18 years of age (child health).1 2 MNCH services are critical for safeguarding the health of women and children, particularly through preventive services, including routine immunisation (RI) and antenatal care (ANC).1 2 Despite significant global advancements, the past decade has seen stagnation in MNCH service coverage and concerningly high childhood and maternal mortality rates, particularly in ub-Saharan Africa (SSA).35

Moreover, the rapid emergence of COVID-19 placed unprecedented strain on many health systems, exacerbating existing disparities in access to and utilisation of MNCH services.68 Consequently, over 90% of countries reported disruptions in the continuity of essential health services during the COVID-19 pandemic.9 Moreover, 53% of countries reported persistent disruptions in primary healthcare (PHC) services, with approximately 40% experiencing increased backlogs during the second half of 2021.9 These disruptions were mainly due to decreased care-seeking in 25% of countries and unintended disruptions due to a lack of healthcare resources in one-third of the surveyed countries.9 For instance, a systematic review suggested a significant decline in vaccination coverage due to COVID-19, leading to a fourfold increase in polio cases in polio-endemic countries.10 Reasons for the reduced vaccine coverage included apprehension about virus exposure at healthcare centres, limitations on citywide mobility, lack of workforce and redirection of resources from child health to tackle the pandemic.10 In addition, the unexpectedly rapid rollout of the COVID-19 vaccine, with its associated weak communication, presented a colossal platform for anti-vaccine conspiracy theories to thrive.11 The resulting COVID-19 vaccine stigma and hesitancy spilled over to other vaccines, significantly decreasing childhood vaccine uptake.12

The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic was even more pronounced in SSA, where the pandemic met an unprepared and weak health system, posing a severe challenge to sustaining its demands.13 In addition, inequity in the global distribution of personal protective equipment (PPE),14 15 poor mastery of standards of operations and contextualisation of the early WHO guide compounded the problem.16 For instance, some health facilities discouraged breastfeeding due to the unfounded fear of the transmission of COVID-19 through breast milk.17 Subsequently, most children missed their first natural vaccines, colostrum and exclusive breastfeeding.17 Furthermore, limited test kits and diagnostics left clinicians with no choice but to rely on clinical case definitions to make presumptive diagnoses.16 This situation has resulted in potential misdiagnoses and a lack of trust between patients and healthcare providers, as reported in Cameroon and Nigeria.16 18 This further hampered access to and utilisation of essential services.

The challenges to MNCH services in SSA are not unique to the COVID-19 pandemic period. Previous epidemics and pandemics, including the 2009 H1N1 influenza pandemic, the 2014–2016 West African Ebola outbreak, recurrent cholera outbreaks and, more recently, Mpox, have similarly strained health systems.1925 The Ebola epidemic, for instance, led to widespread fear and avoidance of health facilities, resulting in significant declines in ANC, skilled birth attendance and RI coverage.19 22 These outbreaks exposed systemic weaknesses, such as poor infection prevention and control measures, inadequate health workforce capacity and weak surveillance systems, all of which continue to undermine MNCH service delivery during health emergencies.

Despite significant global advancements, the past decade has seen stagnation in MNCH service coverage and concerningly high childhood and maternal mortality rates, particularly in the SSA. Several reviews have explored resilience in MNCH services during pandemics.2629 For instance, Makinde et al. proposed a harmonised definition of resilience in MNCH in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), focusing on conceptual clarity rather than framework application.26 Palo et al. conducted a systematic review of interventions to ensure MCH services during pandemics, emphasising service continuity but lacking an evaluation of framework effectiveness.27 Sule et al. highlighted social determinants in a gender-focused scoping review on vulnerability and resilience in MNCH, without addressing health system frameworks, while Sahoo et al.29 explored challenges in MNCH services during the pandemic, without discussing a resilience framework.28 In contrast, this scoping review uniquely aims to map and assess the application, impact, effectiveness and adaptability of health-resilient frameworks specifically in SSA, thereby addressing a critical gap in the literature. Understanding the effectiveness and adaptability of these frameworks during pandemics is essential for developing strategies to enhance the continuity of MNCH services in SSA, ensuring that progress made in the past is not undone during pandemics and other extreme health crises.

Aims and objectives of scoping review

This scoping review aimed to comprehensively map and assess the application, impact, effectiveness, and adaptability of health-resilient frameworks in maintaining RI and MNCH services during pandemics in SSA, while identifying gaps in the literature and areas for further research. Table 1 provides additional details on the review objectives and questions that this study seeks to address. Although this scoping review focuses on SSA, the findings may have broader relevance for other LMICs that face similar health system challenges, particularly in the context of pandemics. Many LMICs experience disruptions in essential health services, constrained health infrastructure and limited human resources during health crises. By mapping health-resilient frameworks implemented in SSA, this review may identify strategies and principles that are potentially transferable to other LMIC. This study also considers the contextual factors that influence the adaptability of these frameworks, recognising that successful implementation depends on local governance, financing mechanisms and sociocultural dynamics. This broader lens may support cross-regional learning and inform future research on resilience in health systems worldwide.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study will employ a scoping review methodology to systematically map and assess the application, impact, effectiveness and adaptability of health-resilient frameworks in maintaining RI and MNCH services during pandemics in SSA. A scoping review is particularly well-suited for exploring complex fields such as this, where evidence may be emerging or scattered, allowing for a comprehensive overview without the stringent requirements of systematic reviews.30 Furthermore, identifying gaps in the literature will inform future research agendas, particularly regarding the long-term sustainability of these frameworks in low-resource settings.

The scoping review will adhere to the established methodological frameworks of the Joanna Briggs Institute guidelines.31 The process will encompass several key steps: identifying broad research questions, conducting a comprehensive literature search across multiple databases, selecting relevant studies through an iterative screening process, charting data to extract essential information, and summarising results thematically.31 This rigorous methodology will ensure a transparent and systematic synthesis of evidence, ultimately providing valuable insights for policymakers, practitioners and researchers aiming to enhance health system resilience in SSA. This study is expected to be conducted between November 2025 and March 2026.

Literature search

The literature search for this scoping review will be systematically conducted across a curated selection of academic databases that align with the review objectives and research questions. Key databases will include PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library and ProQuest (for theses and dissertations). African Journals Online will be included to capture the relevant grey literature specific to the region. The search strategy will incorporate both keywords and controlled vocabularies, including MeSH terms in PubMed, to ensure comprehensive literature coverage. Boolean operators (AND, OR) will be used to refine searches as illustrated in online supplemental annexe 2.

Study selection

The inclusion and exclusion criteria will focus on studies published in English from the year of inception of the databases to 2025. An iterative screening process involving two independent reviewers will enhance the reliability and transparency of the study selection. Screening process

The screening process for this scoping review will involve multiple steps designed to ensure the comprehensive, accurate and reliable identification of relevant studies. Initially, a systematic search will be conducted across the selected databases using predefined keywords and boolean operators. The results of this search will be imported into Covidence, where duplicate entries will be removed, and the platform will be used to facilitate the screening process.32 Two independent reviewers will conduct the initial screening of titles and abstracts to determine eligibility based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Each reviewer will use Covidence to tag studies as “include,” “exclude” or “maybe.” Any discrepancies between the reviewers will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer, if needed.

Studies that meet the initial screening criteria will undergo full-text review in Covidence. Each study will be assessed for its relevance to the review’s objectives, focusing on the application of health-resilient frameworks, the context of the pandemic or health crisis, and contributions to MNCH services or RI in SSA. Detailed records of the reasons for study exclusion at this stage will be maintained in Covidence to enhance transparency. To minimise bias, the reviewers will adhere to predefined criteria, and a third reviewer will arbitrate any disagreements that arise during the full-text review.

Throughout the screening process, the reference lists of the included studies will be examined to identify additional relevant studies. This process will ensure that all pertinent research is captured, including grey literature such as policy documents, reports and dissertations. The iterative and multiphase nature of the screening process, supported by Covidence, will allow for a thorough evaluation of the literature, ensuring that all relevant evidence is included.

Data extraction

The data extraction process for this scoping review will use the Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes, Healthcare Contexts (PICOH) framework to ensure a systematic and thorough collection of relevant information from the included studies.33 The Population theme will capture information on the geographical context, detailing the specific countries and regions within SSA, where health-resilient frameworks have been implemented. This section will also encompass the sociodemographic characteristics of participants involved in the studies, offering insights into the population dynamics relevant to the research. The Intervention section will capture information on the health-resilient framework characteristics, providing detailed descriptions of the frameworks in question. This will include their theoretical foundations, stakeholder involvement and implementation strategies, thereby illustrating how these frameworks function in practice. In addition, the Comparison section will be used to highlight the comparative effectiveness of different health-resilient frameworks. This analysis will focus on their effectiveness and adaptability during pandemics, shedding light on the nuances of each framework’s application in various contexts. The Outcomes section will encompass evaluations of the impact of the frameworks on the delivery and accessibility of RI and MNCH services.

This section will include service continuity metrics, such as vaccination rates and ANC coverage, to provide a quantitative assessment of framework effectiveness. Additionally, this segment will document specific research gaps, challenges encountered and proposed areas for future research that may inform healthcare systems striving to maintain essential services during health crises. Lastly, the healthcare context’s theme will describe the pandemics or health crises examined in the studies, providing vital information about the specific health emergencies that influence the implementation and effectiveness of the health-resilient frameworks in maintaining RI and MNCH services. This structured approach to data extraction, as shown in online supplemental annexe 1, will ensure a comprehensive synthesis of evidence, ultimately informing policymakers, practitioners and researchers about the effectiveness and adaptability of health-resilient frameworks in SSA.

Strategies for enhancing rigour

To ensure rigour, the scoping review will adhere to the Joanna Briggs Institute guidelines and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews checklist to ensure systematic and transparent reporting.30 31 A comprehensive search strategy will be employed across multiple databases, including grey literature, using boolean operators for thorough coverage. Two independent reviewers will screen studies, with discrepancies resolved through discussion. Data extraction will follow the PICOH framework, ensuring systematic capture of relevant information.33 Findings will be synthesised thematically, and stakeholder consultations will enhance credibility. A basic methodological assessment will provide additional context to the results.

Analysis and synthesis

A structured thematic approach will be used for synthesis and analysis to comprehensively understand the different health-resilient frameworks and their effects in maintaining RI and MNCH services during pandemics in SSA. The synthesis will begin with the extraction of data categorised by the PICOH framework, with a description of the geographical contexts in which the health-resilient frameworks were implemented in SSA.33 Each framework’s characteristics will be detailed, including theoretical foundations, stakeholder involvement and implementation strategies. The comparative effectiveness of these frameworks will be analysed to evaluate their adaptability and efficacy in various pandemic scenarios. The analysis will critically assess how health-resilient frameworks impact the continuity and accessibility of RI and MNCH services, addressing research questions related to their effectiveness and identifying specific literature gaps. By synthesising data from diverse sources, including empirical studies, policy documents and grey literature, the review will highlight areas where existing research is lacking, informing future studies and strategies. Furthermore, the analysis will include a contextual evaluation of the health crises encountered, elucidating how specific emergencies influence the implementation and effectiveness of these frameworks.

Ethics and dissemination

This is part of a broader study approved by the evaluation committee of the Faculty of Health Sciences at the University of the Free State and ethical clearance granted by the university’s Human Research Ethics Committee with registration number UFS-HSD2025/0102/2705. This scoping review will not involve human participants, as it will focus on the analysis and synthesis of existing literature. The review will solely use published data, ensuring no direct interaction with individual subjects. The findings will be disseminated to relevant stakeholders through various channels, including peer-reviewed journals, academic meetings, conferences, seminars and forums, to maximise the impact and reach of the results.

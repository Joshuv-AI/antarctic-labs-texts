Effect of Intravascular Lithotripsy on Mitral Annular Calcification: A Cadaveric Study
Authors: Jaffar M Khan, Doosup Shin, Eric Wolff, Nick EJ West, Patrick Stephens, Roosha Parikh, Harminder Gill, William B Chung, Newell B Robinson, George A Petrossian, David J Cohen, Omar K Khalique, Brian Beatty, Ziad A Ali
Published: 2026

Mitral annular calcification (MAC) is a chronic condition where calcium deposits accumulate in the fibrous annulus of the mitral valve. Recent reports have suggested the potential feasibility of intravascular lithotripsy (IVL)-assisted percutaneous mitral valve interventions in patients with MAC.1,2 In this study, we conducted a study to evaluate the effect of IVL on MAC.

A cadaveric heart with MAC was identified using photon-counting detector (PCD) computed tomography (CT; NAEOTOM Alpha Peak; Siemens Healthineers) (Figure 1A). The extracted heart was immersed in a water bath, and two 12.0-mm IVL L6 balloons (Shockwave Medical) and 1 scaffolding 12.0-mm compliant balloon, selected based on CT measurements (intercommissural distance, 28.8 mm; and anterior-posterior distance, 15.0 mm), was used to ensure optimal valve contact (Figure 1B). The IVL balloons (asterisks) were adjacent to the MAC and the scaffolding balloon (dagger) abutting the calcium-free annulus. The balloons were inflated to 4 atm to minimize balloon-induced fractures, with 50:50 contrast/saline per the manufacturer’s recommendation. The hypothesis was that IVL would not cause fractures in dense MAC, and so, the maximal possible dose (600 pulses in total) was delivered. Post-IVL, the heart was scanned by PCD-CT and micro-CT (Skyscan; Bruker), followed by methacrylate embedded gross anatomic examination of the excised mitral valve.

A cadaveric study of IVL for MAC. (A) PCD-CT demonstrated the presence of MAC. (B) Experimental setup showing 2 IVL (asterisk) and 1 scaffolding balloon (dagger). (C) Comparison of pre-IVL and post-IVL PCD-CT images. (D) Micro-CT revealed linear lines (red arrows), suggestive of calcium fractures, which were confirmed by gross anatomy (red arrows; E). CT, computed tomography; IVL, intravascular lithotripsy; MAC, mitral annular calcification; PCD, photon-counting detector.

Following treatment, no macroscopic visible damage to the mitral valve apparatus was observed. Post-IVL PCD-CT showed more prominent separation line (white arrowhead) but no obvious new fracture (Figure 1C). Micro-CT after IVL revealed the characteristic appearance of MAC, along with clear linear lines within the calcium deposits (Figure 1D; white arrows), most consistent with IVL-induced calcium fractures. Gross anatomy further confirmed the presence of these linear fracture lines (Figure 1E; red arrows).

This proof-of-concept study using a cadaveric heart provides the first evidence that IVL may induce calcium fractures in MAC, suggesting its potential utility in facilitating percutaneous mitral valve interventions. Future studies incorporating pre- and post-IVL–micro-CT imaging, control groups, and the use of dedicated IVL catheters with higher energy output specifically designed for structural interventions will be essential to further define the role of IVL in structural heart procedures.

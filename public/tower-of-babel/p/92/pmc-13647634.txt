Specialization of the upper and lower olfactory rosettes reveals sex- and origin-dependent plasticity in gene expression in Senegalese sole (Solea senegalensis)
Authors: Dorinda Torres-Sabino, Oscar Aramburu, Maialen Carballeda, Nina Harmse, Paula R. Villamayor, Pablo Sánchez-Quinteiro, Diego Robledo, Carmen Bouza, Paulino Martínez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOlfaction mediates several aspects of fish behaviour, including reproduction. In Senegalese sole (Solea senegalensis), a reproductive dysfunction in captive-bred (CB) males limits the establishment of an effective artificial production system and has been linked to altered chemical communication.ResultsWe performed a comprehensive RNA-seq analysis of the upper and lower olfactory rosettes of adult Senegalese sole, considering sex (female vs. male) and origin (wild-bred, WB, vs. CB). A total of 15,306 expressed genes, including 404 olfactory receptors, were identified, significantly enhancing the transcriptomic information of the organ. Transcriptional variation was primarily driven by rosette identity, with pronounced differential expression and enrichment of olfactory receptor-related functions between the upper and lower rosettes. CB individuals exhibited reduced transcriptional differentiation between individuals compared to their WB counterparts, suggesting constrained sensory plasticity under captive conditions. Distinct expression patterns were detected among olfactory receptor families, with odorant receptors (ORs) predominantly enriched in the upper rosette and in males, whereas trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs) in the lower rosettes and in females. Furthermore, WB males showed upregulation of genes involved in hormone reception and neuroendocrine signalling, whereas CB males of genes associated with steroid and prostaglandin synthesis and stress-related pathways.ConclusionsThese findings reveal a strong effect of environmental history on the molecular organization of the olfactory organs and suggest that altered sensory and neuroendocrine regulation may contribute to the reproductive dysfunction observed in CB males. This study provides a framework for understanding the links between olfaction, environmental adaptation and reproduction in fish, with implications for improving aquaculture sustainability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-026-02683-8.

Background

In most fish, chemoperception through olfaction relies on a paired sensory structure known as the olfactory rosette, which is in direct contact with the external environment through the nostrils [1]. Each olfactory rosette contains an olfactory epithelium with olfactory sensory neurons, capable of detecting a wide range of waterborne chemical cues that mediate social interactions and survival behaviours [2–4]. Within the olfactory epithelium, the different neuronal subtypes express an olfactory receptor gene pertaining to one of the four primary multigene families described in fish: olfactory receptors class C (OlfC, V2R in mammals), odorant receptors (OR), olfactory receptors class A (ORA, V1R in mammals), and trace amine-associated receptors (TAAR) [5–7]. Together, the set of olfactory receptor genes comprises the olfactory receptor gene repertoire of the species, showing substantial variation in gene number across fish species [8–10].

Senegalese sole (Solea senegalensis) is a flatfish species with an olfactory receptor gene repertoire that includes 455 genes, a number substantially higher than other flatfish species [9, 11]. S. senegalensis is considered a promising aquaculture species in Europe, with high market value and strong production potential, including excellent flesh quality and high survival and growth rates [12, 13]. However, the establishment of an effective production system, and especially a breeding program, remains hindered by a reproductive dysfunction in captive-bred (CB) males, which are unable to perform normal courtship behaviour [14, 15] and show lower sperm volume and decreased concentrations of mature spermatids in mature gonads [16]. Recently, a single-nucleus transcriptomic approach compared mature gonads from CB and wild-bred (WB) males, revealing disrupted communication between Sertoli cells and spermatogonia, potentially hampering meiotic progression [17].

Altered chemical communication mediated by the olfactory system has been suggested to be involved in the courtship issues of CB males [12, 15, 18]. In this context, gene expression in several tissues of CB males revealed reduced levels of hormones compared to WB males, including prostaglandins [19]. Prostaglandins are widely recognized as sex-related hormones that play crucial roles in the modulation of fish courtship behaviour through olfactory chemoperception [20–22]. Consequently, improving our understanding on how the highly complex and specialized olfactory system operates in S. senegalensis might provide insights into the mechanisms underlying the reproductive problems in the species. These insights may ultimately facilitate the implementation of breeding programs and the expansion of the industry [23].

The asymmetric body plan of flatfish results in each olfactory rosette being exposed to distinct environmental conditions. The upper olfactory rosette is in contact with the open water column, whereas the lower olfactory rosette is in contact with the substrate and interstitial water [24, 25]. This differential exposure has been associated with variation in the chemical array of odorants that each rosette detects [26]. Previous electro-olfactogram studies have shown differential responsiveness between the upper and the lower olfactory rosettes of S. senegalensis [26–28], further supported by a recent electrophysiological study assessing the effects of acidified water on both rosettes, including transcriptomic variation [29]. Given the fundamental role of the olfactory system in fish socio-sexual behaviours [4], the present study examined the functional asymmetry of the olfactory rosettes in the context of both sex and the origin of individuals.

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive RNA-seq transcriptomic analysis of the upper and lower olfactory rosettes of adult S. senegalensis, including females and males from both CB and WB origins. The aim was to explore key environmental and biological factors underlying their high plasticity, as well as their implications for fundamental evolutionary processes and practical applications in the species. Results confirmed the pronounced transcriptional differences among conditions, particularly in genes related to olfactory perception, neuroendocrine signalling and stress response. This information enhances our understanding on the species’ reproductive capacities, offering valuable insights for future behavioural studies that may provide potential applications for improving S. senegalensis aquaculture.

Results

Olfactory transcriptome

The 24 olfactory rosette libraries yielded 59.3 million (M) reads on average, ranging from 49.6 to 76.2 M reads. On average, 54.2 M reads (91.5%) were aligned to the reference genome (GCA_919967415.2), ranging between 45.7 and 72.3 M reads (Additional file 1: Table S1). A total of 15,306 genes constituted the active transcriptome of the olfactory rosette (Additional file 1: Table S2). Of these, 12,024 genes were shared with the previously reported olfactory transcriptome (78.6%) [30]. In addition, 3,282 new genes (21.4%) were detected in the active transcriptome in the present study. Including the 1,923 genes reported in the previous olfactory transcriptome [30] that were not expressed in this study, the known olfactory transcriptome of the species expanded up to 17,229 genes.

High pairwise Spearman correlations (rho > 0.8) based on the active olfactory transcriptome (15,306 genes) indicate a largely conserved transcriptional landscape among samples. Despite this overall similarity, the heatmap constructed supported a certain clustering of samples by rosette (upper and lower) (Fig. 1A), while sex and origin showed a weaker influence on the global expression pattern. Notably, 404 out of the 455 olfactory receptor genes (89.0%) were detected as actively expressed (transcripts per million reads; TPM ≥ 5), comprising the S. senegalensis active olfactory receptor repertoire in the present study. When the analysis was restricted to olfactory receptor genes, hierarchical clustering using Spearman correlation revealed a clearer separation by rosette, with strong correlations among samples within each cluster (rho > 0.8), and low-moderate correlations between clusters. The Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of the active transcriptome (15,306 genes), which uses a multivariate statistical approach, showed a clear separation by origin (CB vs. WB) along PC2 (explained variance 10.5%), while separation by rosette (upper vs. lower) along PC1 (28.3%) was evident within WB samples, together with a weak separation according to sex in the same group (Fig. 1C). From the active olfactory transcriptome defined in this study (15,306 genes), 13,978 genes were shared between the upper and lower rosettes; 693 genes were exclusively detected in the upper rosette and 635 in the lower rosette (TPM ≥ 5 in at least two biological replicates of the same condition). Together, these results show a high overlap in the global transcriptomic composition of both rosettes, although important rosette-specific expression is evident. At the whole-transcriptome level, the main separation in the PCA was observed by origin (PC2: 10.5%). In contrast, clearer differences were detected for the olfactory repertoire especially by rosette, and to a minor extent by sex, with both analyses. The separation by rosette observed when considering only the olfactory receptor genes supports the functional asymmetry previously proposed for the upper and lower rosettes [26].

Pairwise transcriptome comparisons by rosette, sex and origin

Three pairwise comparisons were performed using the 24 samples, based on rosette (12 upper vs. 12 lower), sex (12 female vs. 12 male) and origin (12 CB vs. 12 WB). Although each comparison underlies important heterogeneity due to the other factors, these analyses provided information into the main factors structuring gene expression in the olfactory organ. To further resolve these patterns, the comparisons were broken down according to a hierarchical criterion, as described in the upcoming subsections. Rosette-based comparisons (upper vs. lower)

Sex-based comparisons (female vs. male)

Sex-based differential expression analysis was performed across four pairwise comparisons considering both origin (CB and WB) and rosette (upper and lower), identifying a total of 427 DEGs (Fig. 4A). Consistent with the pattern observed in the rosette-based comparisons, CB samples showed more similar transcriptomic profiles between sexes, resulting in much lower number of DEGs compared to WB samples. In CB samples, 90 DEGs were detected in the upper rosette comparison (41 females vs. 49 males; Additional file 1: Table S10), whereas only four genes in the lower rosette (4 females; Additional file 1: Table S11). In contrast, WB samples showed striking transcriptional differentiation between sexes. Specifically, 171 DEGs were detected in the upper rosette (111 females vs. 60 males; Additional file 1: Table S12), while 162 DEGs in the lower rosette (104 females vs. 58 males; Additional file 1: Table S13). Focusing on olfactory receptors, a total of 129 DEGs were detected in the sex-based comparisons (Fig. 4B), representing 30.2% of the 427 DEGs identified in the whole transcriptome. Olfactory receptor-DEGs revealed a total of 36 genes (Fig. 5B), a much lower fraction of the whole transcriptome DEGs (9.7%) than those observed in the rosette-based (31.1%) and sex-based (30.2%) comparisons. Globally, TAAR genes were upregulated in WB samples, whereas OR in CB samples. Moreover, WB samples showed more olfactory receptor-DEGs than CB in the upper rosette of both females (one CB vs. 5 WB) and males (4 CB vs. 12 WB). Although transcriptional differences across olfactory receptors between origins were limited, these expression patterns still reflect subtle alterations in chemosensory signalling associated with environmental history.

Given the distinct reproductive capabilities of S. senegalensis males according to their origin [15], DEGs with functions linked to reproduction and chemoperception were further explored. The genes upregulated in WB individuals suggested enhanced olfactory receptor-mediated chemosensory activity and differences in neuroendocrine signalling. In particular, the upregulation of several olfactory receptor genes, together with neuropeptide arginine vasopressin gene (avp), the prostaglandin receptor (ptger4a) and androgen receptor (andr) suggested differences in the balance between hormone detection and local hormone synthesis within the olfactory rosettes according to origin [31–34].

In contrast, CB individuals showed upregulation of genes involved in prostaglandin (ptgis) and steroid synthesis, including aromatase (cyp19a1), together with wfs1a, fkbp5, crhr1, tsc22d3 and nr4a1, which have previously been associated with cellular stress responses, glucocorticoid signalling, corticotropin-releasing hormone signalling and maintenance of cellular homeostasis [35, 36]. As observed in other fish species, the potential stress response in CB individuals could be related to the exposure to captive conditions that differ from the natural environment [37, 38]. These expression patterns may reflect an origin-specific neuroendocrine response associated with altered neurophysiological homeostasis in CB individuals, potentially compatible with prolonged stress-related neuroendocrine activation under captive conditions.

Differentially expressed genes across all comparisons

Across the 15 pairwise comparisons performed in the present study, a total of 2365 DEGs were identified, corresponding to 1507 unique genes, which represent 9.8% of the olfactory transcriptome. Among these, 234 were olfactory receptor genes (100 OlfC, 61 OR, 2 ORA, 71 TAAR), representing 51.4% of the total olfactory receptor repertoire.

Across the different comparisons, two major functional blocks were identified: one corresponding to chemoperception and olfactory receptor activity and the other to immune response. Among the terms related to chemoperception and olfactory receptor activity, several were associated with the detection of external stimuli and signal transduction across membranes, including multiple terms related to G protein-coupled receptor activity. These functions reflect the molecular mechanisms underlying olfactory receptor-mediated detection and transduction of chemical cues. Notably, enrichment of these terms was predominantly detected in comparisons between the upper and lower rosettes, whereas these terms were less frequently enriched in sex-based comparisons, and occasionally in origin-based comparisons. Overall, these results indicate that the most important functional differentiation among the conditions analysed was between the upper and lower rosettes, supporting the functional specialization previously proposed [26]. Immune-related GO terms represented the second major block of enriched functions. These included some specific terms associated with chemokine and cytokine activities, extracellular matrix and encapsulation. Notably, immune-related enrichment was nearly exclusive to the lower rosette in WB males, and to a minor extent in the lower rosette of WB females (Fig. 7).

The highest functional enrichment (both in p-values and the number of genes) was observed in the rosette-based comparisons, followed by the sex-based and finally in the origin-based, in accordance with the number of DEGs detected. The lower rosette showed stronger olfactory-related GO enrichment than the upper, an exceptional pattern being observed in WB males, predominantly enriched for immune-related processes (e.g. chemokine receptor binding and activity, cytokine receptor binding, immune response and system process). Sex-based comparisons were also characterized by enrichment of olfactory-related functions. Interestingly, no enrichment was detected in the CB lower rosette comparison, whereas the strongest enrichment was observed in females within the WB lower rosette comparison. Finally, in the origin-based comparisons, slight enrichment in olfactory GO terms was observed in the upper rosette of WB males and in the lower rosette of CB females.

Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive transcriptomic characterization of the olfactory rosettes of S. senegalensis using RNA-seq, integrating the effects of rosette identity (upper and lower), sex (female and male) and origin (captive-bred (CB) and wild-bred (WB)) in adult individuals. Although previous studies demonstrated functional asymmetry between the two rosettes in the species [26–29], to our knowledge, this study represents the first large-scale olfactory rosette gene expression analysis addressing this functional asymmetry while simultaneously considering sex- and origin-dependent effects in S. senegalensis. Given the importance of the reproductive dysfunction reported in CB males for the species aquaculture expansion, particular emphasis was placed on identifying transcriptomic differences in the olfactory rosettes that could contribute to understanding the reproduction issues, considering the fundamental role of olfactory-mediated chemoperception in fish reproduction [2, 4].

The present study has contributed, firstly, to a significant enrichment of the olfactory receptor transcriptome (3282 new genes) with respect to that reported by Torres-Sabino et al. [30]. This is in part due to the much broader conditions tested, which would give rise to a variety of new transcripts and isoforms. However, 1923 genes detected in the previous study were not expressed here, possibly related to the inclusion of juvenile individuals in the previous olfactory transcriptome [30].

Overall, results show that rosette identity (upper and lower) represents the major factor influencing gene expression within the olfactory organ, supporting the functional asymmetry proposed between the two rosettes [26–29]. Moreover, the diverse reproductive capacities of S. senegalensis are reflected by the different transcriptomic profiles of the olfactory organs observed when both sex and origin conditions are considered. The broad transcriptional diversity detected across the three types of pairwise comparisons (rosette, sex and origin) reinforces the great plasticity of this organ, as has been reported in other fish [39–41].

Environmental history influences the transcriptomic plasticity of olfactory rosettes

A remarkably striking result is the reduced transcriptional differentiation observed in both rosette- and sex-based comparisons within CB individuals compared to their WB counterparts. CB individuals have been exposed throughout their lives to a relatively homogeneous captive environment, while WB individuals lived in the wild since birth until they were captured as adults and subsequently maintained under the same captive conditions for more than five years prior to sampling. Thus, the prolonged exposure to a homogeneous captive environment appears to be associated with reduced transcriptional variation in the olfactory organ, a condition known to constrain neural and sensory variability in fish [42, 43], even detectable in a single generation [44]. In accordance, pronounced transcriptional differences were detected in the origin-based comparison, especially in the upper rosette of both females and males (CB vs. WB), whereas the number of DEGs were more limited in the lower rosette. Although these origin-based differences are consistent with a strong effect of environmental history on olfactory transcriptomic organization, other factors may also contribute to the observed patterns. In particular, differences in genetic background or genetic differentiation between CB and WB individuals cannot be discarded. However, the persistence of transcriptomic differences after more than five years under shared captive conditions suggests that environmental history and potentially early-life experience, contributes substantially to the observed patterns.

Substantial transcriptomic differences between the upper olfactory rosette of CB and wild-caught males were reported by Fatsini et al. [18], although the magnitude of these differences was greater than that observed in the present study. This discrepancy may be explained by the prolonged acclimation of WB animals under captive conditions for at least five years in our study. This finding suggests that early-life environmental conditions may exert long-lasting effects on the organization and function of the olfactory system. The results of the present study complement those by Fatsini et al. [18], suggesting that the olfactory transcriptome retains a remarkable degree of variability in response to the current environment, while simultaneously preserving transcriptional signatures associated with previous environmental experience. This interpretation is further supported in the recent characterization of the full-length olfactory transcriptome [30], which revealed the pronounced molecular complexity of the olfactory organ and suggested that the transcriptional organization of the olfactory rosette is shaped by the interaction between environmental conditions and life-history experience. Overall, these results reinforce the notion that environmental history plays a central role in shaping transcriptional diversity, particularly in sensory systems such as olfaction [45–47], potentially impacting olfactory-mediated reproductive processes and contributing to the differential reproductive capacities observed between males.

Olfactory receptor gene specific expression patterns

Through olfactory receptor stimulation, fish can detect a wide array of waterborne chemical cues, such as amino acids, bile acids, nucleotides, prostaglandins and steroidal compounds [4]. The size of the olfactory repertoires, as well as odour sensitivity and functional specialization vary greatly among species, reflecting adaptation to distinct ecological niches, life histories and reproductive strategies [8, 9]. Consequently, species-specific approaches are essential to fully understand how olfactory-mediated chemoperception contributes to behavioural regulation. In S. senegalensis, the two olfactory rosettes are exposed to distinct chemical environments and exhibit functional specialization. The upper rosette, directly in contact with the water column, has been associated with intra-specific chemical communication and social interactions, whereas the lower rosette appears more closely linked to the detection of food-related odours [26, 27].

Consistent with this anatomical and functional asymmetry, rosette-, sex- and origin-specific expression patterns were detected across the olfactory receptor multigene families. Specifically, OR genes were mainly upregulated in males and in the upper rosette, and two ORA genes showed upregulation in the upper rosette of WB males. OR and ORA receptors are known to detect a broader range of odorants, including steroidal compounds, prostaglandins and other pheromone-like molecules that play central roles in reproduction and other social interactions [20, 48, 49]. In fish, activation of a specific OR receptor triggers mate recognition and courtship behaviour [5, 20–22]. Furthermore, ORA activation by sexual pheromones mediates courtship behaviour [50]. Thus, upregulation of these receptors in the upper rosette supports the importance of the organ on chemical communication for reproduction. Moreover, the predominant expression of OR genes in males may reflect sex-specific functions related to reproductive behaviour in S. senegalensis, in accordance with previous transcriptomic studies that revealed sexual male-biased dimorphism in this gene family in zebrafish [40], together with sex-specific olfactory regulation in goldfish [51].

In contrast, TAAR genes were predominantly upregulated in females and in the lower rosette. TAAR genes have been widely associated with the detection of amine-based cues, often associated with food, metabolic state, and environmental context [52–54]. Thus, the function of TAAR genes appears to be not strictly associated with reproduction, which may explain their enrichment in the lower rosette observed in the present study. Finally, OlfC genes constitute a highly diverse gene family across vertebrate taxa [55]. Although no consistent differential expression pattern was detected in the present study, more than half of the OlfC repertoire (100: 51.3%) showed differential expression, indicating highly rosette-, sex-, and origin-dependant responsiveness. In fish, OlfC genes have been primarily characterized as amino acid receptors involved in feeding behaviour [56] but also implicated in the detection of ecologically relevant cues associated with predator avoidance [57]. The marked OlfC transcriptional variability observed in this study likely reflects the influence of multiple environmental and ecological factors on the chemosensory function, although the specific biological significance of this observation remains to be determined.

Overall, functional enrichment analyses indicated that most significantly enriched GO terms were related to olfactory receptor activity and associated signalling pathways, underscoring the prominent contribution of receptor-mediated processes to the functional organization of the olfactory rosettes. Importantly, the olfactory epithelium is a highly plastic sensory tissue that can regulate olfactory receptor expression, neuronal survival, and signal transduction efficiency at the level of individual olfactory sensory neurons [58, 59]. These activity-dependent mechanisms provide a plausible explanation for the rosette- and sex-specific transcriptional differences observed in the present study, as variation in environmental and social exposure may shape receptor expression profiles over time.

Transcriptomic divergence may influence the reproductive dysfunction in Senegalese sole

The reproductive dysfunction observed in CB males of S. senegalensis represents a major bottleneck for the establishment of efficient breeding programs and the expansion of its industry [15]. In the present study, marked transcriptomic differences were detected in the origin-based comparison involving males, particularly in the upper rosette, in agreement with previous observations [18]. These differences encompassed genes involved in olfactory perception, neuroendocrine and hormonal signalling, immune pathways, stress response, transcriptional regulation and metabolism. Remarkably, functional enrichment analyses demonstrated a clear overrepresentation of functions related to chemoperception through olfaction in WB males vs. CB males, supporting enhanced olfactory receptor-mediated sensory activity in the upper rosette of WB males.

In contrast, the lower rosette exhibited comparatively fewer differences in the origin-based comparison involving males, with an important number of DEGs linked to immunity in WB males. This pattern, together with the preferential upregulation of OR and ORA genes observed in the upper rosette of WB males, reinforces the functional asymmetry between the upper and lower rosettes previously described [26–29].

In fish, hormonal modulation of olfactory sensitivity has been widely documented. Fluctuations in oestrogens, androgens and prostaglandins are known to regulate fish olfactory responsiveness and courtship behaviour [4, 20–22]. Importantly, neuroendocrine disorders have previously been reported in S. senegalensis CB males, including reduced circulating testosterone and lower prostaglandin levels in gills and gonads compared to WB males [19, 60]. In the present study, the upper rosette of WB males exhibited elevated expression of key hormone-related receptors such as ptger4a, andr and the neuropeptide arginine vasopressin gene, avp, which are directly implicated in reproductive and social behaviours. In contrast, the upper rosette of CB males displayed upregulation of genes involved in steroid and prostaglandin synthesis, including ptgis and cyp19a1. Cyp19a1 was also upregulated in the upper rosette of CB males relative to WB males in the study by Fatsini et al. [18]. These results suggest a potential shift toward local hormone production in CB males, which may reflect compensatory mechanisms or neuroendocrine dysregulation.

Prostaglandins have been widely described as fundamental mediators of reproductive behaviour in fish [20, 61–63]. Exposure to prostaglandins has been shown to activate male courtship behaviour through olfactory pathways in multiple fish species [20, 62, 64]. The differences observed in our study between CB and WB animals in genes involved in prostaglandin synthesis and prostaglandin receptors may reflect alterations in the sensitivity or responsiveness of the olfactory system to reproductive chemical cues [31–34]. This interpretation is further supported by the upregulation in CB males of several genes related to stress-responsive pathways, suggesting a potential activation of stress-related neuroendocrine signalling under captive conditions. Prolonged stress is well known to disrupt reproductive function [65]. These changes may reflect long-term physiological stress or altered homeostatic conditions in captivity, potentially impacting the balance between hormone detection and local neuroendocrine signalling within the olfactory organ, ultimately compromising their olfactory capabilities. Such differences could contribute to the impaired courtship behaviour reported in CB sole and need to be further examined. Future studies, particularly those assessing olfactory and behavioural responses to prostaglandins, are needed to test this hypothesis and may provide novel strategies to improve reproductive performance in aquaculture.

Genes associated with neuronal development in the upper rosette of WB males suggest enhanced neuronal and epithelial organization in response to a more complex environment, consistent with the plasticity of the tissue. The transcription factor figla, classically linked to ovarian folliculogenesis, was upregulated in the upper rosette of WB males compared to CB males. Although its function in olfactory tissues remains unknown, this finding suggests that figla may have additional regulatory roles beyond its classical reproductive functions. Additionally, differential expression of sex differentiation genes was detected between WB and CB males, with sox9a and sox9b upregulated in the lower rosette of WB males and in the upper rosette of CB males, respectively, highlighting marked gene expression differences between the upper and lower rosettes. Although these genes have been classically associated with gonadal differentiation in fish [66], including flatfish [67, 68], their expression in sustentacular cells of the olfactory epithelium has been reported in mammals [69], suggesting potential roles in epithelial maintenance and sensory plasticity. While we have emphasized DEGs with known or putative relevance to olfactory perception, neuroendocrine signalling, stress response and reproductive function, the transcriptomic differences described here likely involve additional genes and pathways whose contribution to olfactory biology remains to be explored in future studies.

Collectively, these findings suggest that environmental history largely influences gene expression in the olfactory rosettes. WB males, exposed to a chemically complex and socially dynamic environment during development, and then acclimated to captive conditions for over five years, displayed enriched expression of olfactory receptors and signalling components involved in pheromone detection and reproductive communication. Conversely, CB males exhibited attenuated olfactory receptor expression, altered neuroendocrine and stress-related signatures, putatively associated with reproduction issues in captivity. In this line, a recent study demonstrated that enrichment of captive environment during early developmental stages can produce a positive short-term effect on the reproductive traits in CB individuals [70].

Conclusions

The present study provides the first comprehensive evidence that transcriptomic divergence across the olfactory rosettes, shaped by environmental history and sex, may contribute to the reproductive dysfunction observed in CB males of S. senegalensis. Our results reveal a notable plasticity of the olfactory rosettes, with distinct transcriptomic profiles across the evaluated conditions. Specifically, prolonged exposure to a homogeneous captive environment appears to constrain transcriptomic profile, leading to the upregulation of stress-related pathways and altered neuroendocrine signalling compared to WB individuals raised in more variable environments, although this hypothesis requires further examination. In this context, environmental conditions experienced during early life stages are likely to play a key role in determining epigenetic imprinting. Indeed, DNA methylation differences associated with different environmental histories have been reported in gonadal tissues of S. senegalensis [71, 72]. These findings raise the possibility that similar epigenetic modifications may also occur in the olfactory organs, potentially affecting chemoperception through olfaction. Future research integrating epigenomic analyses with behavioural assays, hormone profiling and functional characterization of olfactory receptors, together with environmental enrichment experiments, will be essential to fully understand the impaired courtship behaviour observed in CB males. Ultimately, such integrative approaches may contribute to the development of strategies aimed at restoring reproductive performance and improving the sustainability of S. senegalensis aquaculture.

Methods

Animal sampling

Olfactory rosette samples of twelve adult individuals were collected in June 2022 at the ‘Instituto Español de Oceanografía’ (IEO, Santander, Spain). The experimental design included two animal groups of different origin kept indoors in tanks under standard culture conditions [73]. One group consisted of captive-bred (CB) individuals, whereas the second group included wild-bred (WB) individuals that reached sexual maturity under natural conditions. These WB individuals were then captured from the natural environment and subsequently maintained under the same captive conditions as the CB individuals for more than five years prior to sampling. According to the broodstock records, CB fish originated from multiple spawning events involving more than 25 WB males and 25 CB females following a similar scheme previously reported [13], thereby minimizing the possible parentage among the individuals included in this study. All CB and WB individuals ultimately proceeded from natural populations from Atlantic Iberian Peninsula, where low genetic differentiation was previously reported [74], supporting a common genetic background. Within each group, both sexes (females and males) and olfactory rosettes (upper and lower) were sampled following the design in Fig. 8.Fig. 8Sampling of S. senegalensis olfactory rosettes according to different conditions (24 samples: 12 upper rosettes and 12 lower rosettes). CB, captive-bred; WB, wild-bred

Sampling of S. senegalensis olfactory rosettes according to different conditions (24 samples: 12 upper rosettes and 12 lower rosettes). CB, captive-bred; WB, wild-bred

Fish were immersed in ice and subsequently sacrificed by decapitation. Afterwards, both the upper and lower olfactory rosette were immediately dissected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen to be then stored at − 80 °C until use.

RNA extraction

Total RNA was extracted using the E.Z.N.A. RNA Kit for Animal Tissue (Omega Bio-Tek, 2018, Product No. R6731) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, frozen olfactory rosette tissues were homogenized in GTC lysis buffer supplemented with 2-mercaptoethanol using a TissueLyser II device (Qiagen). Homogenization was performed with a single 5-mm stainless steel bead per tube (1 min at 30,000 Hz, 1 min pause, followed by 1 min at 30,000 Hz). The resulting lysates were then centrifuged, loaded onto HiBind Mini Columns, and total RNA was purified and eluted in nuclease-free water.

Library preparation and sequencing

RNA concentration and quality were determined using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies Inc.); RNA integrity number (RIN) was evaluated with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer employing the RNA 600 Nano kit (Agilent Technologies). After quality control, RNA samples were submitted to Novogene (Germany) for library preparation and sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 S2 platform to produce 150 bp paired-end reads.

RNA-seq data analysis

RNA-seq raw data was processed using the nf-core/rnaseq pipeline (v3.10.1) with default settings [75]. Briefly, raw sequencing reads were first evaluated for quality using FastQC [76], followed by adapter and low-quality base removal with Trim Galore! [77]. Filtered reads were then aligned to the reference genome (GCA_919967415.2; de la Herrán et al. [78]) using STAR [79], and transcript abundance was quantified and normalized as transcripts per million (TPM) with RSEM [80]. Following nf-core processing, the active transcriptome of the olfactory rosettes was established by retaining genes with TPM ≥ 5 in a minimum of two biological replicates in any of the conditions tested. The active olfactory transcriptome was subsequently compared with the full-length reference transcriptome previously reported for the species [30]. Gene annotation was based on the S. senegalensis genome reannotation [11].

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified through comparison of groups of samples sorted by rosette (upper vs. lower), sex (female vs. male) and origin (CB vs. WB) using the R/Bioconductor package DESeq2 v1.38.1 [81]. A total of 15 pairwise comparisons were performed (Table 1). Genes with false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were considered DEGs. Within each pairwise comparison, a gene was classified as upregulated when it exhibited a significant higher expression relative to the alternative condition, and vice versa. Special attention was paid to olfactory receptor genes of S. senegalensis [11], as they encode the molecular components responsible for odour detection. In addition, genes with annotations directly related with hormonal signalling and reproductive processes were specifically examined, given their fundamental role in the regulation of reproductive physiology and behaviour.

Functional enrichment

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted on the DEGs identified in the two DEG lists of each of the 15 comparisons with the GOfuncR package v1.14.0 [82], employing as reference the active transcriptome of the olfactory rosettes defined in the present study. A family-wise error rate adjustment (FWER ≤ 0.05) was applied to identify significantly enriched functions.

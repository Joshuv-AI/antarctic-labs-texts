Global, regional and national burden of paediatric urinary stone disease from 1990 to 2021: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021
Authors: Xin Zhu, Yazhe Zhou, Xin Yang, Kaizhuo Zhou, Mi Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesPaediatric urinary stone disease (PUSD) is a prevalent urological issue affecting children worldwide. Its incidence varies based on region and socioeconomic factors. Recognising trends in PUSD is vital for developing effective policies to manage and prevent it.DesignThis study is a retrospective, observational and population-based analysis using data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2021. The research systematically assessed the incidence, mortality and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) of PUSD from 1990 to 2021 across global, regional and national levels. Temporal trends were analysed by calculating the average annual percentage change using the joinpoint regression model, while the estimated annual percentage Changes (EAPCs) were obtained from linear regression models on PUSD rates.SettingThis study used publicly available data from the GBD Study 2021 and did not involve specific healthcare facilities.ParticipantsSamples from the GBD database.InterventionsNot applicable.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary measures were the incidence, mortality and DALYs of PUSD from 1990 to 2021. Secondary outcome measures included temporal trends stratified by age, gender, regions, nations and sociodemographic index (SDI) categories.ResultsWorldwide, PUSD cases increased from 1 355 160 in 1990 to 1 562 095 in 2021, although the incidence rate remained relatively stable (EAPC: −0.18). Mortality decreased considerably, with deaths falling from 180 in 1990 to 76 in 2021, and an EAPC for the mortality rate of −2.82. DALYs decreased from 18 975 in 1990 to 10 773 in 2021 (EAPC: −2.22). The burden varied across SDI regions, with low SDI regions showing rising incidence and high SDI regions experiencing declines. By sex, males consistently showed higher incidence, mortality and DALY rates than females from 1990 to 2021. However, both decreased over time, with the rate of decline in males slowing after the mid-2000s, leading to a persistent male excess in 2021. Geographically, East Asia showed the most significant reductions in mortality and DALYs, while South Asia and Western Sub-Saharan Africa showed increasing trends. Nationally, countries such as China and Georgia experienced substantial declines in incidence, mortality and DALYs, whereas Spain and Ecuador exhibited rising incidence rates.ConclusionsAlthough the global burden of PUSD has decreased in mortality and DALYs, the rising incidence in low-SDI regions emphasises the importance of equitable healthcare policies and better preventative measures.Keywords: Urolithiasis, Mortality, EPIDEMIOLOGY

Introduction

Urinary stone disease (USD) is less common in children than in adults. However, its incidence has increased in recent years due to changes in lifestyle and diet and the increased use of imaging modalities.1,4 Unlike adult USD, paediatric USD (PUSD) is more closely associated with metabolic abnormalities, congenital anomalies and genetic predisposition. Hydration levels, dietary habits, obesity and infection also influence it.5,8 These distinctions demonstrate that PUSD is not merely an earlier form of adult disease but a distinct clinical and aetiological entity necessitating targeted epidemiological research.

Despite the increasing burden, current evidence remains fragmented. Most studies focus on adults, are limited to single centres or countries, or cover short time spans, which restricts cross-setting comparability and obscures long-term understanding patterns.3 4 9 10 Critically, there is a lack of comprehensive, standardised and globally comparable assessments of PUSD that simultaneously evaluate incidence, mortality and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs). This gap hinders understanding geographic heterogeneity, age-sex distributions, temporal trends and potential connections with sociodemographic development. It also restricts clinical and policy measures aimed at prevention, early detection and service provision planning.

To address these gaps, we used the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 database to quantify the burden of PUSD from 1990 to 2021 at global, regional and national levels.

Methods

Data acquisition and sources

We collected data from the GBD 2021 study, which estimates the burden of 371 diseases and injuries and 88 risk factors across 204 countries and territories, as well as 811 subnational locations from 1990 to 2021.11 Data on PUSD, such as incidence, mortality and DALYs, were obtained from the Global Health Data Exchange platform (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool).12

The analysis included both males and females. The primary study group consisted of children aged 0–14 years, with the paediatric denominator explicitly defined as the GBD 0–14 years population. Pre-specified subgroup analyses concentrated on the standard GBD paediatric age categories: <1 year, 2–4 years, 5–9 years and 10–14 years.

Geographical hierarchy

Regions followed the GBD 2021 geographical taxonomy and were organised into a hierarchy: level 0 (global aggregate), level 1 (seven super-regions sharing similar sociodemographic and epidemiological traits) and level 2 (21 regions within these super-regions).

Global and regional burden analysis

To characterise global distribution and regional disparities in PUSD, we created global maps and regional comparative summaries at levels 0–2. Visualisation was conducted using the R software package (V.4.2.3) and JD_GBDR (V2.24, Jingding Medical Technology) with the ‘ggplot2’ and ‘sf’ packages to produce maps illustrating the disease burden. We added an exploratory, incidence-only latitude perspective, classifying countries by capital-city latitude into an equatorial belt (<7.5°, north or south) and a high-latitude band (≥60°N), chosen to delineate photoclimatically distinct zones with minimal versus pronounced seasonality and photoperiod variation.

Temporal trend analysis

Temporal trends in the incidence, mortality and DALYs of PUSD from 1990 to 2021 were analysed using joinpoint regression analysis, implemented with the ‘segmented’ and ‘broom’ R packages to identify significant changes over time. We initially fitted a baseline log-linear model glm(log(rate) ~ year), to annual rates per 100 000 children (GBD 0–14 years denominator), not to raw case counts. We allowed up to Kmax=5 candidate join points and selected the final number and locations using the Bayesian information criterion. Segment-specific slopes were converted to estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) based on rates per 100 000 children; 95% CIs and p values were derived from model-based SEs. The average annual percentage change (AAPC) over 1990–2021 was calculated as the duration-weighted mean of segment-specific APCs, with 95% CIs and p values based on the weighted SE. These model-based CIs reflect regression uncertainty and are distinct from GBD-provided uncertainty intervals (UIs), which we report separately.13,15 To complement the model outputs, including 95% UIs, we visualised annual counts as bars and the corresponding rates per 100 000 children as overlaid lines for males and females.

Sociodemographic index analysis

The sociodemographic index (SDI), a composite indicator of fertility rates, educational attainment and per capita income, represents a country’s overall social and economic development.11 The relationship between the SDI and the burden of PUSD was analysed by stratifying disease rates based on SDI categories (low, low-middle, middle, high-middle and high). This analysis enabled comparisons of the disease burden across different levels of socioeconomic development. Data manipulation and visualisation were performed using R’s ‘dplyr’ (V.1.1.3) and ‘ggplot2’ packages.

Statistics

The primary metrics for assessing the burden of PUSD included incidence, mortality, DALYs and their respective rates per 100 000 children (0–14 years). These rates were presented with 95% UIs derived from the GBD data algorithm.16 This study adhered to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines and the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting.17 18

Patient and public involvement

None.

Results

Global trends

Incidence

In 1990, global incident cases of PUSD totalled 1 355 160 (95% UI 777 505 to 2 085 117). The number of incident cases worldwide increased to 1 562 095 in 2021, showing a rise in the total cases over the past 31 years. However, the incidence rate per 100 000 children stayed stable, showing a slight decline from 77.92 (95% UI 44.71 to 119.89) in 1990 to 77.64 (95% UI 45.53 to 117.32) in 2021. The EAPC for the global incidence rate is −0.18 (95% UI −0.24 to −0.12), indicating a slight overall decline (online supplemental table 1). As shown in online supplemental figure 1A, the AAPC for the incidence rate indicates this minimal decline, with the rate remaining relatively stable over the years, fluctuating between 76 and 80 per 100 000 children from 1990 to 2021. From 1990 to 2021, the 10 to 14 year age group had the highest incidence rate, staying consistent throughout. The 5–9 year and 2–4 year groups showed lower incidence rates with slight variation over time. The lowest incidence was among children under 1 year, with nearly no significant change (figure 1A). During 1990–2021, males consistently had higher incidence rates than females. Male rates dropped in the late 2000s, then increased again by 2021, whereas female rates declined slightly and remained stable with a slight late increase; incident counts grew in both sexes, more notably in males (figure 2A).

Mortality

From 1990 to 2021, PUSD mortality rates declined, dropping global deaths from approximately 180 (95% UI 89.21 to 361.58) in 1990 to around 76 (95% UI 41.52 to 195.90) in 2021. The death rate decreased from 0.0103 to 0.0038 per 100 000 children. The EAPC of −2.82 (95% UI −2.93 to −2.71) reflects a significant downward trend in the death rate over this period (online supplemental table 2). Online supplemental figure 1B illustrates the AAPC for the mortality rate, showing a consistent decline from approximately 0.01 per 100 000 in 1990 to 0.004 per 100 000 in 2021, indicating an overall reduction in PUSD-related mortality. A decrease was observed across all age groups, with the most notable drop in the 2–4-year-old group, which maintained a steady downward trend. The 5–9 year and 10–14 year groups declined gradually (figure 1B). From 1990 to 2021, males consistently had higher mortality rates and death counts than females. While both sexes experienced ongoing declines, the rate for males slowed after the mid-2000s, whereas the decrease for females remained more stable. By 2021, both deaths and rates had decreased markedly for males and females, with males consistently remaining higher than females throughout the period (figure 2B).

Disability-adjusted life-years

In 1990, the GBD related to PUSD was estimated at approximately 18 975 DALYs, corresponding to a rate of 1.09 per 100 000 children. By 2021, the global DALYs attributed to PUSD had decreased to about 10 773, with a rate of 0.54 per 100 000 children. The EAPC was −2.22, indicating a significant decline in the burden of PUSD over the study period (online supplemental table 3). As shown in online supplemental figure 1C, the AAPC for DALY rate also demonstrates a consistent downward trend, decreasing from approximately 1.1 per 100 000 in 1990 to 0.6 per 100 000 in 2021, further emphasising the reduction in disease burden over time. The most significant decrease occurred in the 2–4 year age group, which showed a consistent decline, while the 5–9 year group also decreased to a lesser degree (figure 1C). During the same period (1990–2021), males consistently exhibited a higher DALY burden than females. Although DALYs declined in both sexes, the rate of decline among males slowed after the mid-2000s, leading to persistently higher levels in males by 2021 (figure 2C).

SDI regional trends

Incidence

In 1990, the low-middle SDI region had the highest number of incident cases of PUSD, with 4 19 168 cases (95% UI 244 881.85 to 648 084.90). By 2021, incident cases in this region increased to 536 926 (95% UI 309 523.65 to 818 810.87), with an incidence rate of 92.60 (95% UI 53.38 to 141.21). The most significant rise in incidence rate occurred in the low SDI region, where incident cases grew from 174 123.39 (95% UI 100 564.53 to 270 444.40) in 1990 to 359 831.02 (95% UI 204 700.40 to 557 555.84) in 2021, with an EAPC of 0.08 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.12). Conversely, the high SDI region saw a decrease in incident cases from 114 847.28 (95% UI 63 626.34 to 177 221.62) in 1990 to 88 403.53 (95% UI 51 901.81 to 127 500.24) in 2021, with an EAPC of −1.14 (95% CI −1.40 to −0.89) (online supplemental table 1 and figure 3A).

Mortality

Among the 5 SDI regions, the high-middle SDI region exhibited the most significant decrease in PUSD-related mortality, with cases falling from 20.29 in 1990 to 2.56 in 2021, and an EAPC of −5.15. The high SDI region recorded the lowest death rate in 2021 at 0.0006. The low-middle and SDI regions also saw declines, but to a lesser degree, with EAPCs of −2.22 and −2.50, respectively. The middle SDI region’s death rate decreased from 0.0127 to 0.0023, with an EAPC of −4.67. Overall, all SDI regions experienced a downward trend in PUSD-related mortality throughout the study period (online supplemental table 2 and figure 3B).

Disability-adjusted life-years

In 2021, the high-middle SDI region saw the most significant decrease in DALY rate for PUSD, falling from 0.86 to 0.29 (EAPC: −3.94). The middle SDI region also experienced a considerable decline. The low SDI region had the highest DALY rate with only slight changes, while the high SDI region maintained the lowest rate (online supplemental table 3 and figure 3C).

Geographical regional trends

Incidence

From 1990 to 2021, PUSD trends varied globally. Southeast Asia saw a slight decline in cases and rates (EAPC −0.53). Southern and Andean Latin America increased, with EAPCs of 0.39 and 0.43. Eastern Europe saw fewer cases but a slight rate rise (EAPC −0.59). East Asia experienced sharp decreases in cases and rates (EAPC −2.60). Conversely, South Asia and Western Sub-Saharan Africa increased, indicating rising trends (online supplemental table 1 and figure 4A).

Geographical trends in PUSD across world regions, 1990–2021; rates per 100 000 children in each area. (A) Incidence; (B) Deaths; (C) DALYs. DALYs, disability-adjusted life-years; PUSD, paediatric urinary stone disease; SDI, sociodemographic index.

Mortality

East Asia exhibited the most significant reduction, with death rates falling from 0.0185 (95% UI 0.0068 to 0.0266) in 1990 to 0.0013 (95% UI 0.0006 to 0.0021) in 2021, corresponding to an EAPC of −6.92 (95% CI −7.53 to −6.31). Central Europe also saw a substantial decline, with an EAPC of −7.70 (95% CI −8.23 to −7.16). Conversely, regions such as Tropical Latin America experienced a slight increase in death rates (EAPC 1.11, 95% CI 0.75 to 1.46), while Western Sub-Saharan Africa, despite a decline (EAPC −2.43, 95% CI −2.54 to −2.33), continued to report high absolute death numbers in 2021 (online supplemental table 2 and figure 4B).

Disability-adjusted life-years

From 1990 to 2021, the global burden of DALYs due to PUSD decreased significantly, with East Asia showing the most notable reduction (EAPC: −7.74), as DALY rates fell from 1.69 (95% UI 0.70 to 2.37) to 0.18 (95% UI 0.12 to 0.27). In contrast, Tropical Latin America experienced a slight rise in DALY rates (EAPC: 1.09), increasing from 0.26 (95% UI 0.19 to 0.36) to 0.31 (95% UI 0.24 to 0.43). Western Sub-Saharan Africa, despite a decline (EAPC: −2.35), continued to report the highest burden, with DALY rates of 1.65 (95% UI 0.57 to 6.44) in 2021 (online supplemental table 3 and figure 4C).

National trends

Incidence

Among 204 countries, Spain showed the highest EAPC (2.49, 95% CI 1.92 to 3.06), with incidence rates rising from 27.00 per 100 000 in 1990 to 47.00 per 100 000 in 2021. The Hellenic Republic (EAPC 2.11, 95% CI 1.81 to 2.41) and Ecuador (EAPC 1.78, 95% CI 1.20 to 2.36) also experienced notable increases. Conversely, Georgia had the steepest decline (EAPC −3.84, 95% CI −4.62 to −3.06), with incidence rates falling from 160.69 per 100 000 in 1990 to 69.75 per 100 000 in 2021. China (EAPC −2.69, 95% CI −3.27 to −2.11) and Indonesia (EAPC −2.62, 95% CI −3.06 to −2.17) also saw significant decreases (online supplemental table 4 and figure 5A).

Estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) in PUSD, 1990–2021. (A) Incidence; (B) Deaths; (C) DALYs. Values show point estimates of EAPC in rates per 100 000 children; no sex stratification. DALYs, disability-adjusted life-years; PUSD, paediatric urinary stone disease.

Adding a latitude perspective, countries in the equatorial belt (capital latitude <7.5°) generally showed slow increases in incidence, with typical EAPCs around +0.1% to +0.5% per year. For example, Kenya (EAPC 0.34, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.41) and Equatorial Guinea (0.50, 0.48 to 0.53) exhibited steady rises from approximately 60–77 to 66–88 per 100 000. Within this belt, Ecuador showed a more pronounced increase (1.78, 1.20 to 2.36; 47.89 to 61.21 per 100 000). By contrast, several equatorial countries were stable or declining: Singapore showed a slight decrease (−0.15, –0.31 to 0.01), and Indonesia experienced a marked decline from 65.16 to 36.23 per 100 000 (−2.62, –3.06 to −2.17). At high latitudes (capital ≥60°N), incidence levels were lower and changes were modest: Iceland rose slightly from 23.90 to 24.66 per 100 000 (0.29, 0.09 to 0.49), while Finland’s point estimate suggested stability with wide uncertainty (0.29, –1.13 to 1.73). Together, these patterns indicate higher absolute levels and more frequent increases near the equator, but with notable heterogeneity likely reflecting differences in climate exposure, water quality and health-system capacity (online supplemental table 4).

Mortality

PUSD mortality rates declined across countries between 1990 and 2021. China experienced the most significant decrease, with death cases falling from 60.25 (95% CI 22.19 to 87.18) in 1990 to 3.22 (95% CI 1.59 to 5.32) in 2021, and an EAPC of −7.02 (95% CI −7.64 to −6.39). The Democratic People’s Republic of Korea also saw notable reductions, from 0.55 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.99) in 1990 to 0.12 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.31) in 2021, with an EAPC of −3.05 (95% CI −3.43 to −2.66). Conversely, countries with the lowest death rates, such as Taiwan, Province of China, recorded marginal declines from 0.13 (95% CI 0.09 to 0.17) in 1990 to 0.02 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.03) in 2021, with an EAPC of −3.94 (95% CI −4.56 to −3.32) (online supplemental table 5 and figure 5B).

Disability-adjusted life-years

Between 1990 and 2021, the DALY burden of PUSD showed significant declines across countries. China experienced the most substantial decrease, with DALY cases dropping from 5497.27 (95% CI 2278.69 to 7748.38) in 1990 to 460.92 (95% CI 296.40 to 685.26) in 2021, and an EAPC of −7.87 (95% CI −8.17 to −7.56). Similarly, the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea and Indonesia reported reductions, with EAPCs of −3.46 (95% CI −3.77 to −3.14) and −2.40 (95% CI −2.65 to −2.16). In contrast, Taiwan, Province of China, exhibited the lowest DALY burden, decreasing from 15.66 (95% CI 11.04 to 21.00) in 1990 to 5.04 (95% CI 3.49 to 7.46) in 2021, with an EAPC of −1.38 (95% CI −1.91 to −0.85). Cambodia also showed modest improvements, with an EAPC of −2.70 (95% CI −2.89 to −2.51). These trends highlight considerable progress in reducing disease burden across different regions (online supplemental table 6 and figure 5C).

Discussion

Previous studies have indicated a global decline in the age-standardised incidence and prevalence of USD from 1990 to 2021, with an EAPC of −0.87. Similarly, the worldwide age-standardised mortality rate also decreased during this period, with an EAPC of −1.02.19 An analysis of the GBD database (2000–2021) showed global increases of 26.7% in cases, 60.3% in deaths and 34.5% in DALYs. Meanwhile, age-standardised rates declined by 17.5% and 15.1%.20 Our PUSD-specific analysis aligns with earlier reports of a global decline in age-standardised USD metrics, showing increasing case volumes but stable or decreasing rates. From 1990 to 2021, the number of new cases worldwide slowly increased, while the incidence rate remained steady with a slight drop (EAPC=−0.18). In contrast, death and DALY rates significantly decreased (EAPC=−2.82 and −2.22, respectively). Age patterns revealed differences: the 10 to 14 year group had the highest incidence, while the 2−4 year group mainly contributed to declines in mortality and DALY rates. These trends raise questions about the burden sharing between males and females.

Men bear a higher burden of USD than women, with higher incidence, mortality and DALY rates in 2021. Although rates declined from 1990 to 2021 in both sexes, males remained predominant, especially in low-SDI regions.21 Sex-specific patterns vary across the life course. A Chinese study found that women’s USD risk rises steadily with age, while men’s risk increases until midlife and declines, forming an inverted-U trajectory.22 In the treated population, paediatric administrative data from Ontario (2002–2019) document a rising female share among PUSD interventions (from 44.05% in 2002 to 62.90% in 2018), while overall procedure volumes remained stable over the same period, indicating a compositional shift among intervention recipients rather than a change in population-level rates.8 Narrative evidence points to increasing USD among girls and young women and associated economic burdens, while the causes of this shift remain under investigation.23 Examining these sex disparities at the population level in paediatrics, we observe a consistent male predominance in PUSD from 1990 to 2021. Male trends further show a dip in the late 2000s, followed by renewed increases in incidence and a slowdown in declines of mortality and DALYs—a male-specific inflexion not seen among females. These findings emphasise the importance of sex-specific and age-specific prevention and monitoring and suggest plausible hormonal and metabolic factors contributing to the disparities.

PUSD shows marked geographical heterogeneity, with higher burdens reported in Turkey, Pakistan and parts of South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America—regions often termed the ‘stone belt’.24 Over 70% of PUSD cases comprise calcium oxalate stones, while infectious stones are more frequently observed in younger children.24 25 Globally, the number of cases seems to be rising, with regional estimates indicating a higher incidence and prevalence in developing regions (around 5%–15%) compared with developed areas (1%–5%).26 These disparities highlight key sociodemographic differences: low-SDI areas face limited healthcare, unsafe water and undernutrition, linked to higher and rising incidence with slower declines in mortality and DALYs. Conversely, high-SDI regions tend to see declines in incidence, mortality and DALYs thanks to early detection, prevention and superior clinical care.

Beyond SDI and regional groups, a latitude perspective reveals that many equatorial regions face higher PUSD burdens and slower progress, while countries at higher latitudes have lower rates. Within the equatorial zone, some countries show declines. In contrast, others increase, indicating that latitude is a proxy for climate, water quality, diet and health system capacity rather than a direct cause. Equatorial areas benefit from measures such as school hydration, heat-season interventions, safer drinking water, infection control for children and access to timely imaging and minimally invasive procedures treatments.

Regional and national trends offer insights: East Asia’s decline may reflect better healthcare and dietary changes, while steady increases in South Asia and Western Sub-Saharan Africa relate to limited resources, malnutrition and poor water quality. National patterns show socioeconomic, healthcare and policy impacts. For example, rises in Spain and Ecuador might be due to dietary shifts to higher salt and protein intake. Declines in China and Indonesia are linked to improved healthcare, early detection and preventive strategies, with China’s reductions in mortality and DALYs highlighting successful public health efforts and advanced surgical techniques. Countries like Cambodia need urgent intervention to address ongoing issues of disparities.27

Although system-level and regional variations affect baseline risk and outcomes, individual metabolic and exposure factors influence PUSD risk across various settings. Obesity and higher BMI may be associated with a higher risk of USD, with potential pathways including insulin-resistance-related hypocitraturia, lower urine pH promoting uric-acid supersaturation and dietary patterns rich in animal protein and fructose associated with metabolic syndrome.28 In women aged 25–50, higher testosterone and increased intestinal oxalate absorption may raise the risk of USD.23 29 Diet also affects lithogenic profiles and should be tailored considering age and body size.30 High sodium intake in the diet increases urinary calcium excretion and lowers urinary citrate levels; animal protein and fructose (including sugar-sweetened drinks) decrease urine pH and citrate but increase uric acid.31 Higher fluid, potassium and magnesium intake is protective, while high vitamin C intake might increase risk—emphasising the need to personalise dietary choice guidance.32 In ‘stone-belt’ regions, environmental heat exposure and unsafe water promote dehydration and enteric infections, reinforcing the latitude-related distribution of risk and producing pronounced seasonal peaks.33 34 Younger children are more prone to develop infection stones; urease-producing organisms alkalinise urine, while recurrent urinary tract infection, bladder/bowel dysfunction and limited access to culture-directed therapy increase risk.35 36 Changes in gut and urinary microbiota caused by antibiotics and the loss of oxalate-degrading bacteria may elevate oxalate levels and raise the risk of USD.37,39 These converging mechanisms support multi-level prevention: school-based hydration, reduction of sodium and sugar alongside adequate dietary calcium and fruit/vegetable intake, weight management, prompt diagnosis and treatment of urinary tract infections, safe water and sanitation, and fair access to metabolic assessment and minimally invasive care.40 41

This study has several limitations that warrant attention. First, our analysis is limited by the most recent publicly available GBD release, which provides validated estimates only up to 2021. Second, the accuracy of GBD estimates depends on the quality, completeness and representativeness of country-level data, which vary considerably across regions, potentially leading to regional inconsistencies and under-reporting. In areas with sparse or immature registries (eg, parts of Southeast Asia), underascertainment may bias estimates downward; a practical mitigation is strengthening regional registries and conducting coding-validation audits (eg, chart–registry comparisons). Third, GBD relies on modelled estimates that combine diverse sources; as a result, findings may be influenced by modelling assumptions and uncertainty. Fourth, variations in diagnostic coding and case definitions over time were not explicitly considered and could impact observed trends. Fifth, the analysis did not explore specific aetiological factors contributing to regional differences in disease burden, such as dietary habits, genetic predispositions and environmental exposures. Future research should aim to improve data collection and reporting, particularly in low-SDI regions, and investigate the determinants of PUSD to develop effective prevention and management strategies. Additionally, longitudinal studies examining the effects of obesity, metabolic syndrome and environmental factors on PUSD disease are essential for understanding its underlying causes.

Conclusions

Despite steady incidence rates, the global burden of PUSD experienced significant reductions in mortality and DALYs from 1990 to 2021, reflecting healthcare improvements in high and high-middle-SDI regions. Meanwhile, low and equatorial areas faced rising incidence and slower progress. Key priorities include enhancing paediatric urology, providing timely imaging, promoting water safety and optimising dietary management, all supported by improved surveillance and data quality.

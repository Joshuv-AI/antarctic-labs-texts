Exosomal circDNAJB6 derived from decidual macrophages promotes preeclampsia progression via the miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis and by subsequently regulating the PPARγ/NF-κB pathway
Authors: Lu Zhang, Xijia Tian, Mei Liu, Hui Zong, Wei Yu, Caihua Zheng, Daoyan Zhang, Hui Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeExosomes and circular RNAs (circRNAs) have been strongly implicated in the development of preeclampsia (PE). This study aimed to investigate the function and regulatory mechanism of exosomal circRNAs derived from decidual macrophages (DMφ) in PE.Patients and methodsDMφ and exosomes derived from DMφ (PE-DMφ-exo and normal-DMφ-exo) were obtained from PE patients and normal pregnant patients. The effects of DMφ-exo on the biological function of trophoblasts were assessed. Microarray analysis and qRT‒PCR were used to identify the differentially expressed circRNAs between PE-DMφ-exo and normal-DMφ-exo. The effects of exosomal circDNAJB6 on the biological function of trophoblasts were then explored. The regulatory relationships among circDNAJB6, miR-670-5p, and TOB2 were verified by dual-luciferase reporter assays. The effect of the circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis on the PPARγ/NF-κB pathway was then assessed. The function of exosomal circDNAJB6 in vivo was confirmed in a PE rat model.ResultsPE-DMφ-exo inhibited trophoblasts proliferation, migration, invasion and human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) angiogenesis and increased trophoblasts apoptosis. circDNAJB6 was upregulated in PE-DMφ-exo and transported from DMφ to trophoblasts via the exosomal pathway. Exosomal circDNAJB6 inhibited trophoblasts activity and HUVEC tube formation. CircDNAJB6 was found to act as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) of miR-670-5p and thereby upregulate TOB2. miR-670-5p overexpression or TOB2 knockdown reversed the inhibitory effect of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts. Moreover, the circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis activated the NF-κB pathway by downregulating PPARγ. Finally, in vivo experiments demonstrated that the upregulation of circDNAJB6 in PE-DMφ-exo increased blood pressure and 24-h urine protein levels, thereby exacerbating PE.ConclusionsOur study revealed that exosomal circDNAJB6 derived from DMφ regulated the miR-670-5p/TOB2/PPARγ/NF-κB pathway and thereby promoted PE progression, which provides novel insights into PE development.Keywords: Preeclampsia, Exosomes, circDNAJB6, miR-670-5p, TOB2, PPARγ/NF-κB

Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE) is a pregnancy-specific syndrome that involves new-onset hypertension and proteinuria after 20 weeks of gestation [26]. PE affects 5–8% of pregnant women worldwide and is a major cause of maternal and perinatal mortality [2]. The aetiology and pathogenesis of PE have not been fully elucidated, but recent studies have shown that impaired spiral artery remodelling and trophoblasts dysfunction are intimately involved in the development of PE [18, 24]. Although shallow invasion of trophoblasts at the maternal–foetal interface is a major inducer of PE, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear [6, 14]. Therefore, elucidating the mechanisms that affect trophoblasts dysfunction is necessary to develop potential strategies for PE treatment.

Decidual macrophages (DMφ) are the second most abundant leukocyte population in the decidua, and they aid in tissue remodelling at the maternal–foetal interface [5, 31]. DMφ play important roles in trophoblasts proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis [4, 8, 28]. Abnormal interactions between DMφ and trophoblasts may lead to pregnancy disorders [22]. Typical direct communication between DMφ and trophoblasts has been documented [21]. Elucidating the interaction between DMφ and trophoblasts may provide new insights to understand the occurrence of PE.

Exosomes are small extracellular vesicles with diameters ranging from 50 to 150 nm that are involved in intercellular communication [12]. Exosomes, which contain various biomolecules, including RNAs, proteins, and lipids, can be released by almost all cell types and regulate the biological function of recipient cells by transmitting biomolecules from donor cells to recipient cells [19]. For example, DMφ can release exosomes and inhibit trophoblasts proliferation and migration by delivering miR-153-3p [34]. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a novel type of endogenous noncoding RNA that are characterized by a closed-loop structure and the lack of both a polyadenylated tail at the 3’ end and a cap structure at the 5’ end [13]. Exosomes contain abundant circRNAs, and exosomal circRNAs are implicated in a wide range of physiological and pathological processes [33]. Recent studies have shown that circRNAs are important contributors to PE [11], but the role of exosomal circRNAs derived from DMφ in PE is not clear.

In this study, we investigated the role of DMφ-derived exosomal (DMφ-exo) circRNAs in PE. We confirmed that circDNAJB6 (hsa_circ_0008726) was more highly expressed in PE-DMφ-exo compared to than in normal-DMφ-exo via a circRNA microarray analysis and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‒PCR). Therefore, we investigated the effects of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts. Our study aimed to identify a novel mechanism focused on exosomal circDNAJB6 to explain the crosstalk between DMφ and trophoblasts, and we provide a potential new molecular target for treating PE.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (Jinan, China). All patients enrolled in this study provided written informed consent, which complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. All experimental protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University. The care and surgical operation of all the animals were performed in strict accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the National Institutes of Health and ARRIVE guidelines.

Clinical samples

A cohort of 50 PE patients and 50 normal pregnant patients in Qilu Hospital of Shandong University were enrolled in this study in 2019. Normal pregnancy was defined as term delivery without any complications. The diagnosis of PE was in accordance with the criteria of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) guidelines for gestational hypertension and PE (ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 202) [1]. Decidual tissues were obtained from all participants. The decidual tissues were cultured in high-glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (HyClone, USA) supplemented with 100 IU/ml penicillin and 100 IU/ml streptomycin (Gibco, USA).

Isolation and identification of DMφ

Decidual tissues were washed 5–6 times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), cut into pieces, and treated with 0.05% type IV collagenase (Sigma‒Aldrich, USA) at 37 °C for 30 min. The resulting cell suspension was subsequently filtered through 100- and 40-µm nylon mesh filters. Density gradient centrifugation was used to isolate the mononuclear cells. CD14 + cells, which were sorted from mononuclear cells via magnetic-activated cell sorting (CD14 human MicroBeads, No. 130-050-201, Miltenyi, Ger), were identified as DMφ. DMφ were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium (Invitrogen, USA) supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 mg/ml streptomycin (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Flow cytometry was utilized to determine the purity of DMφ by labelling the cells with anti-CD14 antibody (Abcam, ab28061,UK).

Trophoblasts cell lines and culture

The human trophoblasts cell line HTR-8/SVneo (American Type Culture Collection, Manassas, USA) was incubated in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with 10% exosome-free FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C in 5% CO2. The FBS mixture was subjected to ultracentrifugation at 100,000×g for 18 h to remove the exosomes.

Isolation and identification of DMφ-exo

Exosomes were isolated from each DMφ suspension obtained from PE patients and normal pregnant patients (PE-DMφ-exo and normal-DMφ-exo) by sequential centrifugation. Each DMφ suspension was centrifuged at 300×g for 10 min followed by 2,000×g for 10 min to discard the cells. Thereafter, the supernatants were centrifuged at 10,000×g for 30 min, and the cell debris was discarded. The supernatants were collected and subjected to ultracentrifugation at 100,000×g for 60 min twice. The final pellets were exosomes.

The exosomes were then analysed via transmission electron microscopy (TEM), nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA), and western blotting. First, the morphology of the exosomes was observed and photographed under TEM (JEOL, Japan) after they were fixed with 3% (w/v) glutaraldehyde for 2 h and negatively stained them with 2% uranyl acetate for 30 s. The size of the exosomes was then measured by NTA. The diluted exosome suspension was added to the sample chamber of a NanoSight NS300 instrument (Malvern, UK), and the Brownian motion of the exosomes was assessed using NTA software. The specific biomarkers of exosomes, CD9 and CD63, were detected by western blotting.

DMφ-exo labelling

DMφ-exo were labelled with Dil dye to track the internalization of exosomes by trophoblasts. Briefly, the exosomes were stained with 1 µM Dil dye (Invitrogen, USA) for 30 min at 37 °C, washed with PBS, and purified again via ultracentrifugation. Thereafter, the Dil-labelled DMφ-exo were incubated with trophoblasts for 24 h. The cell nuclei were stained with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; 1:1000, Invitrogen, USA) for 5 min at 37 °C. A fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan) was used to detect the fluorescence signals.

Cell counting kit (CCK)-8 assay

Trophoblasts proliferation was assessed using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Japan). Briefly, trophoblasts (5 × 103/well) were seeded in 96-well plates, and 10 µl CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 3 h. The optical density (OD) at a wavelength of 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo, USA). Cell proliferation was measured daily for the first 5 days.

EdU assay

Trophoblasts (5 × 103/well) were seeded in 96-well plates and allowed to grow until they reached 70–80% confluence. The cells in each well were then incubated with 100 ml EdU medium (RiboBio, China) for 3 h. The cells were then fixed with 100 ml 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and incubated with 2 mg/ml glycine for 5 min. Subsequently, the cells were incubated with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 10 min, stained with 500 µl Apollo solution for 30 min in the dark, and decolorized with methanol. Lastly, the cells were stained with DAPI and examined using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Wound healing assay

Trophoblasts were seeded into 12-well plates and allowed to grow until they reached 90–100% confluence. Next, the cell layer was scratched through the central axis using a sterile plastic pipette tip, and loose cells were washed away with PBS. Wound healing was recorded at 0 and 24 h and analysed using ImageJ software. The migration ability was calculated based on the percentage of wound closure.

Transwell assays

Trophoblasts migration and invasion were assessed using a transwell chamber with 8-µm pore polycarbonate membrane inserts (Corning, USA). For the invasion assay, the porous membrane was coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA). Trophoblasts (1 × 105/well) were resuspended in 200 µl serum-free medium and seeded into the upper chamber. 500 µl complete medium was dispensed into the lower chamber. After 24 h of cultivation, the trophoblasts on the surface of the lower chamber were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min and stained with crystal violet (Beyotime, China) for 10 min, after which they were counted under an inverted microscope (Olympus, Japan). For the migration assay, the steps were the same as those for the invasion assay, except that the upper chamber was not coated with Matrigel.

Flow cytometry

An Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) apoptosis detection kit (Solarbio, China) was used to assess trophoblasts apoptosis according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Trophoblasts were resuspended in binding buffer, mixed with 5 µl Annexin V-FITC solution for 30 min in the dark, mixed with 5 µl propidium iodide (PI) for 5 min, and then analysed using a FACScan flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA).

Tube formation assay

The formation of capillary-like structures by human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) was assessed via a tube formation assay to investigate the effects of different DMφ-exos on angiogenesis. Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA) was thawed at 4 °C overnight, added to 96-well plates, and incubated at room temperature for 60 min. After treatment with different DMφ-exo, HUVECs (1 × 105/well) were seeded onto the Matrigel for 6 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Tube formation was photographed with a light microscope. The total tube length and branch points were quantified via ImageJ software.

CircRNA microarray analysis

Total RNA was extracted from PE-DMφ-exo and normal-DMφ-exo using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher, USA). A NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo, USA) was used to quantify the total RNA, and a Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent, USA) was used to assess the quality. RNase R (Epicentre, USA) was added to remove linear RNA species and thereby enrich the circRNAs. A human circRNA Array v2.0 (Agilent, USA) was used to conduct a circRNA microarray analysis. The circRNAs were amplified and transcribed into fluorescent cRNAs according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Feature Extraction software v10.7 (Agilent, USA) was used to extract the raw data from the scanned images. Next, GeneSpring software v13 (Agilent, USA) was used to normalize the data and screen for differentially expressed circRNAs, which were defined as those with p < 0.05 and |log2-fold change| >1.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‒PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from exosomes, trophoblasts, and placental tissues using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Exosomal RNA was further purified using an Exosome RNA Purification Kit (EBioscience, USA). For the circRNA analysis, RNase R (Epicentre, USA) was used to degrade the linear RNA. Complementary DNA was synthesized and amplified using a PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Japan). qRT‒PCR was performed using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan). The relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as the internal control for circDNAJB6 and TOB2 mRNA. U6 was the internal control for miR-670-5p.

Western blotting

Exosomes, trophoblasts, and placental tissues were treated with radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer containing phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (Beyotime, China) to obtain total protein. The protein concentration was determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The proteins were subjected to 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and then transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, USA), which was blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin. Each membrane was incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary anti-CD9 (ab2215, 1:1000), anti-CD63 (ab59479, 1:1000), anti-TOB2 (ab180514, 1:500), anti-peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ, ab178866, 1:500), or anti-GAPDH (ab181602; 1:10000) antibodies for 2 h and then treated with chemiluminescence reagent. GAPDH was used as the endogenous control. All antibodies were purchased from Abcam (USA).

Cell transfection

The circDNAJB6 siRNA (si-circDNAJB6) and the negative control (si-control), circDNAJB6-overexpression vector and empty vector, miR-670-5p mimics and the negative control (mimics-NC), TOB2 siRNA (si-TOB2) and the negative control (si-control), and the TOB2-overexpression vector and empty vector were supplied by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). They were transfected into cells using the Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA). PE-DMφ were transiently transfected with si-circDNAJB6, si-control, circDNAJB6-overexpression vector, or empty vector to produce PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6, PE-DMφ-exosi−control, PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 and PE-DMφ-exocontrol, respectively.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

PCR was used to amplify circDNAJB6 fragments containing the predicted (1) wild-type (wt) or (2) mutant (mut) miR-670-5p binding site and the 3ʹ-untranslated region of TOB2 mRNA containing the predicted (3) wt or (4) mut miR-670-5p binding site. Each gene was inserted into a luciferase reporter vector and designated (1) circDNAJB6-wt, (2) circDNAJB6-mut, (3) TOB2-wt, or (4) TOB2-mut. They were transfected into trophoblasts using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). After 24 h, the luciferase activity was analysed with a dual luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, USA).

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP assays were conducted using a Magna RNA Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, trophoblasts were exposed to RIP lysis buffer and then incubated with magnetic beads coated with the anti-Ago2 antibody (ab186733, Abcam, 1:50) or IgG (ab172730, Abcam, 1:100) at 4 °C overnight. Finally, the immunoprecipitated RNA was extracted using TRIzol and analysed via qRT‒PCR.

Biotinylated RNA pull-down assay

For the biotinylated miR-670-5p pull-down assays, trophoblasts were transfected with biotinylated miR-670-5p mimics (biotin-miR-670-5p, GenePharma, China) or negative control (biotin-NC, GenePharma, China) for 48 h. The trophoblasts were then harvested and lysed in lysis buffer. M-280 streptavidin beads (Thermo, USA) were subsequently washed with lysis buffer and blocked with yeast tRNA (Sigma, Germany) for 2 h. The beads and cell lysates were then mixed at 4 °C overnight. The bound RNAs were extracted using TRIzol and analysed via qRT‒PCR.

Experiments with the PE rat model

Female (200–250 g) and male (250–300 g) Sprague–Dawley rats aged 10 weeks were purchased from Beijing HFK Bioscience (Beijing, China) and allowed 1 week to acclimate to their environment. One male rat and two female rats were then placed in each cage. The next day, the female rats were subjected to vaginal smear tests. A sperm-positive vaginal smear was used as a marker of a successful pregnancy, and the day was defined as gestational Day 0.

PE was induced in the rats via an abdominal injection of N-nitro-L-arginine methyl ester hydrochloride (L-NAME, 250 mg/kg/day; Sigma‒Aldrich, USA) on Days 7–19. Thirty-six PE rats were then randomly assigned to six groups (each treated daily, as described below, on Days 14–19; each group consists of six PE rats): (1) the PBS group (abdominal injection of 0.5 ml PBS), (2) the normal-DMφ-exo group (abdominal injection of 0.5 ml normal-DMφ-exo suspension), (3) the PE-DMφ-exo group (abdominal injection of 0.5 ml PE-DMφ-exo suspension), (4) the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 group (abdominal injection of 0.5 ml PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 suspension), (5) the rosiglitazone (RSG) group (caudal vein injection of 0.3 mg/kg RSG), and (6) the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6+RSG group (abdominal injection of 0.5 ml PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 suspension and caudal vein injection of 0.3 mg/kg RSG).

The blood pressure of each rat was assessed every 4 days during pregnancy. 24-h urinary protein levels were assessed on Day 19 of pregnancy. On Day 20, the rats were anaesthetized with a 3% sodium pentobarbital solution (Propbs, China), and caesarean section was conducted to remove the foetus and placenta. The umbilical cord and foetal membranes were removed. Finally, the foetuses were photographed and weighed.

Statistical analysis

The data were processed using GraphPad v9.0 (GraphPad, USA). The data are presented as the means ± standard deviations. Student’s t test was used to compare two groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used to compare two or more groups. All the experiments were repeated at least three times. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of DMφs and DMφ-exo and transport of circDNAJB6 from DMφ-exo to trophoblasts

Flow cytometry was used to assess the surface markers of DMφ. CD14 was strongly positive, whereas CD11b and CD34 were negative (Fig. 1A). Thereafter, exosomes were isolated from DMφ and identified via TEM, NTA, and western blotting. TEM revealed that the DMφ-exo were round or oval and approximately 100 nm in diameter (Fig. 1B). NTA verified that the peak diameter of the exosomes was approximately 120 nm (Fig. 1C). DMφ-exo were positive for surface marker proteins of exosomes (CD9 and CD63) according to western blotting (Fig. 1D). Dil-labelled DMφ-exo were cocultured with trophoblasts to identify whether DMφ-exo can be absorbed by trophoblasts. The results revealed that red DMφ-exo localized around the nucleus of the trophoblasts, which indicated the internalization of Dil-labelled exosomes by the trophoblasts (Fig. 1E).

A circRNA microarray analysis of PE-DMφ-exo and normal-DMφ-exo was performed to identify the key circRNA(s) via which the exosomes influence trophoblasts. The resulting heatmap revealed a significant difference in circDNAJB6 (hsa_circ_0008726) expression (Fig. 1F). qRT‒PCR confirmed that circDNAJB6 expression was significantly increased in PE-DMφ-exo compared with normal-DMφ-exo (Fig. 1G).

The expression of circDNAJB6 was also increased in trophoblasts after incubation with PE-DMφ-exo compared with that after incubation with normal-DMφ-exo (Fig. 1H). The expression of circDNAJB6 was then compared first among PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 (and its control PE-DMφ-exosi−control) and PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 (and its control PE-DMφ-exocontrol) (Fig. 1I) and second among trophoblasts after incubation with these four types of exosomes (Fig. 1J). The first results revealed that circDNAJB6 expression decreased in PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 (compared with its control) and increased in PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 (compared with its control) (Fig. 1I). The second results revealed that its expression decreased in the trophoblasts incubated with PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 (compared with the control) and increased in the trophoblasts incubated with PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 (compared with the control) (Fig. 1J).

PE-DMφ-exo inhibit trophoblast proliferation, migration, and invasion and HUVEC angiogenesis, and promote trophoblast apoptosis

Trophoblasts were cultured with PBS, PE-DMφ-exo, or normal-DMφ-exo to study the role of PE-DMφ-exo in PE. CCK-8 and EdU assays were performed to examine trophoblast proliferation, and the results revealed that normal-DMφ-exo increased trophoblast proliferation, whereas PE-DMφ-exo decreased trophoblast proliferation (Fig. 2A–B). Wound healing assays indicated that normal-DMφ-exo promoted trophoblast migration, whereas PE-DMφ-exo decreased trophoblast migration (Fig. 2C). Transwell assays revealed that, compared with normal-DMφ-exo, PE-DMφ-exo significantly restrained trophoblast migration and invasion (Fig. 2D). Flow cytometry revealed that PE-DMφ-exo significantly increased trophoblast apoptosis (Fig. 2E). HUVEC tube formation was significantly decreased in the PE-DMφ-exo group compared to the normal-DMφ-exo group (Fig. 2F).

Exosomal circDNAJB6 represses trophoblasts proliferation, migration, and invasion and promotes trophoblasts apoptosis

Trophoblasts were transfected with PE-DMφ-exo (or its control normal-DMφ-exo) or PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 (or its control PE-DMφ-exosi−control) to explore the biological effects of exosomal circDNAJB6 on these cells, and CCK-8 and EdU assays revealed that trophoblasts proliferation was significantly increased in the PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 group and decreased in the PE-DMφ-exo and PE-DMφ-exosi−control groups (Fig. 3A–B). Wound healing assays revealed that cell migration was increased in the PE-exosi−circDNAJB6 group (Fig. 3C). Transwell assays revealed that trophoblasts invasion and migration were increased in the PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 group and decreased in the PE-DMφ-exo and PE-DMφ-exosi−control groups (Fig. 3D). Flow cytometry revealed decreased apoptosis in the PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 group and increased apoptosis in the PE-DMφ-exo and PE-DMφ-exosi−control groups (Fig. 3E). Additionally, HUVEC tube formation was increased in the PE-DMφ-exosi−circDNAJB6 group compared to the PE-DMφ-exosi−control group (Fig. 3F).

circDNAJB6 acts as a CeRNA of miR-670-5p to promote TOB2 expression

The online database starBase v3.0 (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/) was used to predict the potential binding between circDNAJB6 and miR-670-5p (Fig. 4A). A dual-luciferase reporter assay revealed that miR-670-5p inhibited the luciferase activity of circDNAJB6-wt but not circDNAJB6-mut (Fig. 4B). A RIP assay was used to detect the relationship between circDNAJB6 and Ago2, and the results indicated that the specific level of circDNAJB6 adsorption to Ago2 was higher than that in the IgG group (Fig. 4C). An RNA pull-down assay indicated that the enrichment of circDNAJB6 was greater in the biotin-miR-670-5p group than in the biotin-NC group (Fig. 4D).

The expression of the TOB2 protein after (G) transfection with si-circDNAJB6 and (H) miR-670-5p overexpression was analysed by western blotting. * p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01

The target gene of miR-670-5p was predicted using the same database, and a binding site mediating the interaction between miR-670-5p and TOB2 was identified (Fig. 4E). This finding was verified by a dual-luciferase reporter assay (Fig. 4F). Furthermore, western blotting revealed that TOB2 was downregulated in trophoblasts after transfection with si-circDNAJB6 (Fig. 4G) or miR-670-5p mimics (Fig. 4H).

miR-670-5p overexpression reverses the inhibitory effect of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts

Transfection of trophoblasts with PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 downregulated miR-670-5p (Fig. 5A). Thereafter, these trophoblasts were incubated with miR-670-5p mimics or mimics-NC. miR-670-5p was upregulated in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 + miR-670-5p mimics group (Fig. 5A). miR-670-5p significantly reversed the circDNAJB6-mediated inhibition of trophoblasts proliferation, migration, and invasion (Fig. 5B–E), promotion of trophoblast apoptosis (Fig. 5F), and inhibition of HUVEC angiogenesis (Fig. 5G).

Trophoblasts proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis were analysed by (B) CCK-8, (C) EdU, (D) wound healing, (E) transwell, and (F) flow cytometry assays. TOB2 knockdown reverses the inhibitory effect of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts

Transfection of trophoblasts with PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 upregulated TOB mRNA expression (Fig. 6A). Thereafter, these trophoblasts were incubated with si-TOB2 or si-control. The levels of the TOB2 mRNA and protein were significantly downregulated in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 + si-TOB2 group (Fig. 6A–B). TOB2 knockdown significantly reversed the circDNAJB6-mediated inhibition of trophoblasts proliferation, migration, and invasion (Fig. 6C–F), promotion of trophoblastd apoptosis (Fig. 6G), and inhibition of HUVEC angiogenesis (Fig. 6H). Together, these results indicate that circDNAJB6 can inhibit trophoblasts proliferation, migration, and invasion and HUVEC angiogenesis and promote trophoblasts apoptosis via the miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis.

The circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis regulates the PPARγ/nuclear factor (NF)-κB pathway in trophoblasts

Given that TOB2 inhibits PPARγ [32], we performed several experiments to investigate the signalling pathway that mediates the effects of the circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis. Western blotting revealed that the PPAR-γ protein level was increased in the TOB2-knockdown group (Fig. 7A). As NF-κB is a downstream target of PPARγ, we also assessed the levels of NF-κB-related proteins. TOB2 knockdown downregulated p-p65 and p-Ika protein levels (Fig. 8A). Moreover, circDNAJB6 downregulated the PPAR-γ protein and upregulated the p-p65 and p-Ika proteins, while miR-670-5p overexpression reversed these effects (Fig. 7B).

We used a PPAR-γ agonist (RSG) in subsequent experiments to determine whether the circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis regulates trophoblasts viability and migration via the PPAR-γ/NF-κB pathway. Trophoblasts viability, migration, and invasion were enhanced by RSG (Fig. 7C–D). Additionally, RSG reversed the inhibitory effects of circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts viability, migration, and invasion (Fig. 7C–D) and its stimulatory effect on trophoblasts apoptosis (Fig. 7E).

Exosomal circDNAJB6 promotes PE progression in vivo

A rat PE model was established to further investigate the role of circDNAJB6 in PE in vivo. Significant increases in systolic and diastolic blood pressure were observed in the PE-DMφ-exo group compared with the normal-DMφ-exo group and markedly higher blood pressure and 24-h urinary protein levels were detected in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 group than in the PE-DMφ-exo group (Fig. 8A–B). RSG ameliorated the effects of PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 on blood pressure and 24-h urinary protein levels (Fig. 8A–B).

On Day 20, cesarean section was performed on pregnant rats. Foetal survival and foetal weight were lower in the PE-DMφ-exo group than in the normal-DMφ-exo group and in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 group than in the PE-DMφ-exo group (Fig. 8C–D). RSG ameliorated these effects of PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 (Fig. 8C–D).

qRT‒PCR was performed on rat placentas to assess circDNAJB6, miR-670-5p, and TOB2 mRNA expression. The expression of circDNAJB6 and the TOB2 mRNA was upregulated in the PE-DMφ-exo group compared with the normal-DMφ-exo group, whereas miR-670-5p was downregulated (Fig. 8E). circDNAJB6 and the TOB2 mRNA were also upregulated in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 group compared with the PE-DMφ-exo group, whereas miR-670-5p was downregulated (Fig. 8E).

Moreover, western blotting of rat placenta revealed that the TOB2, p-p65, and p-Ika proteins were upregulated in the PE-DMφ-exo group compared with the normal-DMφ-exo group and in the PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 group compared with the PE-DMφ-exo group. The PPAR-γ protein exhibited the opposite trend (Fig. 8F). RSG ameliorated the effects of PE-DMφ-exocircDNAJB6 by upregulating PPAR-γ and downregulating p-p65 and p-Ika (Fig. 8F).

Discussion

PE is a systemic syndrome that leads to an increased risk of long-term cardiovascular disease in women [9]. The placenta is central to the development of PE [25]. Dysfunction of DMφ, which contributes to insufficient placental perfusion, promotes the development of PE [27]. Many studies have demonstrated that placenta-derived exosomes modulate maternal immune tolerance and play important roles in placental and endothelial cell function [20]. In this study, we explored the effects of DMφ-exo on trophoblasts and found that PE-DMφ-exo inhibited trophoblasts proliferation, migration, and invasion and HUVEC angiogenesis, and promoted trophoblasts apoptosis.

CircRNAs are enriched and stably expressed in exosomes [7] and participate in the development and progression of PE [37]. Shen et al. reported that circTRNC18 promotes the progression of PE by regulating the miR-762/Grhl2 axis [29]. We explored the mechanism underlying the effects of exosomal circRNAs derived from DMφ on PE by performing a circRNA microarray analysis to screen for differentially expressed circRNAs. The results revealed that circDNAJB6 expression was significantly increased in the PE-DMφ-exo group and could be transported from DMφ to trophoblasts via the exosomal pathway. A previous study revealed that circDNAJB6 (hsa_circ_0008726) was upregulated in PE [10], which is consistent with our study. In addition, circDNAJB6 inhibited trophoblasts activity and HUVEC angiogenesis, which promoted PE.

Accumulating evidence has confirmed that circRNAs act as ceRNAs to sponge miRNAs. We used bioinformatics to screen for target miRNAs. Among the candidates, miR-670-5p was selected for further validation. Zhang et al. reported that circSMARCA5 suppresses the progression of non-small cell lung cancer by regulating the miR-670-5p/RBM24 axis [36]. Another study suggested that miR-670-5p promotes the proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells by targeting PROX1 [30]. In this study, we found that circDNAJB6 directly binds to miR-670-5p. Moreover, miR-670-5p overexpression reversed the inhibitory effects of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts. Our data confirmed that miR-670-5p mediated the effects of circDNAJB6 on the biological function of trophoblasts.

TOB2 (transducer of ErbB2.2) expression is decreased in many human cancers, and it functions as a tumor suppressor. Moreover, TOB2 was reported to be repressed in nasopharyngeal carcinoma and to regulate miR-378 [35]. miR-5100 promoted the epithelial–mesenchymal transition by targeting TOB2 and activating smad2/3 in lung epithelial cells [15]. Using Starbase v3.0, we found that the TOB2 3ʹ-untranslated region harbours a binding sequence for miR-670-5p and confirmed that TOB2 in trophoblasts is directly targeted by miR-670-5p. In addition, TOB2 knockdown reversed the effects of exosomal circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts, indicating that TOB2 promotes the development of PE.

PPARγ belongs to the steroid receptor superfamily and plays vital roles in PE [23]. PPARγ activators ameliorate the development of PE by regulating ANGPTL4 [16]. However, the molecular mechanisms responsible for the inhibition of the PPARγ pathway in PE are still poorly understood. Takahashi et al. reported that TOB2 inhibits PPARγ expression during adipocyte differentiation [32]. Many studies have shown that PPARγ inhibits the NF-κB pathway [17]. Consistent with previous studies, we found that TOB2 upregulated p-p65 and p-Ika by downregulating PPARγ. Moreover, circDNAJB6 downregulated PPAR-γ and upregulated p-p65 and p-Ika, changes that were reversed by miR-670-5p overexpression. Additionally, a PPAR-γ agonist (RSG) reversed the inhibitory effects of circDNAJB6 on trophoblasts viability, migration, and invasion. Excessive activation of NF-κB has been reported to be involved in the progression of PE [3], which is similar to our findings. These results indicated that the circDNAJB6/miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis impaired the biological function of trophoblasts by inhibiting PPARγ and activating the NF-κB pathway. Furthermore, in vivo experiments revealed that exosomal circDNAJB6 promoted PE development via the miR-670-5p/TOB2/PPARγ/NF-κB axis, which was consistent with the results of the in vitro experiments. There are also some limitations in our study, such as the absence of off-target tests and the downstream mechanism of PPARγ/NF-κB on trophoblasts.

In conclusion, our study suggests that exosomal circDNAJB6 derived from DMφ promotes PE development via the miR-670-5p/TOB2 axis and subsequently by regulating the PPARγ/NF-κB pathway. Our findings identify a new avenue for further exploration of the pathogenesis of PE and provide a new potential target for PE treatment.

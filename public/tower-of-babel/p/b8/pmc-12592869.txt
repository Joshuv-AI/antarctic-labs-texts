Hall Technique Compared to Orthodontic Band Cementation in Carious Injuries in Deciduous Teeth of Children with Disabilities: Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Ana Cristina Bevilaqua Batista Pedroza, Thais Gimenez, Cristina dos S Resende, Marcelo V de Andrade, Kelly Maria S Moreira, Renata CP de S Carreira, Luís Henrique dos S Nogueira, José Carlos P Imparato
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundThe aim is to evaluate the longevity of the Hall Technique (HT) compared to orthodontic bands (BOCIV) for treating carious lesions in the deciduous molars of children with disabilities.Materials and methodsA total of 58 children with cognitive impairments, diagnosed with mild to moderate learning disabilities, aged 4–10 years, presenting with carious lesions classified as scores 5/6 in deciduous molars, were included. The sample consisted of 100 teeth, divided into two groups: HT, n = 50; and BOCIV, n = 50. Clinical and radiographic follow-ups were conducted at 6 and 12 months. Kaplan–Meier survival curves and log-rank tests were used to compare the two groups. Cox regression analysis was performed to assess the influence of other variables.ResultsAfter 12 months, 72 teeth were reevaluated. A total of 100 teeth were reevaluated at least once during the study and included in the survival analysis. HT showed superior performance (88% survival) compared to the BOCIV (66% survival). Regarding marginal adaptation, BOCIV showed a higher frequency of subgingival extension, whereas the HT group exhibited better proximal contact preservation.ConclusionOrthodontic bands cemented with conventional glass ionomer (BOCIV) may be considered an alternative treatment for extensive carious lesions in deciduous molars; however, HT demonstrated superior survival, marginal adaptation, and proximal contact retention.Clinical significanceThis study highlights a treatment option for these carious lesions in deciduous molars for children with disabilities. Introduction

Oral health is a fundamental aspect of overall health, yet dental caries remains a pervasive issue, especially among pediatric populations and children with special health care needs (SHCN).1 Managing oral diseases in these vulnerable groups requires specialized approaches due to factors such as compromised immune systems, physical limitations, or cognitive disabilities.2 The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry emphasizes the importance of tailored dental care for these patients to prevent dental disease and promote quality of life.1

Scientific literature demonstrates that children with SHCN often experience worse oral health outcomes compared to their peers, with higher incidences of untreated caries and the need for more invasive procedures such as general anesthesia.3–7 Studies have explored various caries management techniques, including the Hall technique (HT) and selective caries removal, both showing promising results in minimizing intervention while maintaining clinical efficacy.7,8

However, despite these advances, there remains a gap in understanding the long-term outcomes and their relationship to marginal adaptation with the aim of observing whether there was an impact on the survival of the materials in SHCN populations. Additionally, few studies focus on how specific pediatric populations respond to these alternative caries management techniques, particularly in terms of sustainability and practical application in clinical settings.5,9

The aim of this study is to evaluate the clinical outcomes of minimally invasive caries management techniques in children with SHCN, with a particular focus on patient acceptance and long-term effectiveness. By doing so, we hope to contribute to the ongoing efforts to improve oral health care in this underserved population.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Aspects

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee under opinion 1.795888/2016, with CAAE 60887816.20000.5374, meeting the ethical requirements of Resolution 466/2012 of the National Health Council (Guidelines for research involving human beings). In addition, the study was registered in the Brazilian Registry of Clinical Trials (REBEC) under the number RBR-825qxqw. All study participants signed the free and informed consent form (FICF).

Sample and Study Design

The study was conducted at the University in the city of Goiânia (Goiás, Brazil) and at the University Center in the town of Sobral (Ceará, Brazil), which are reference centers for pediatric dental assistance and treatment, from August 2022 to April 2024. The selection of the cities was based on convenience, as the locations for patient selection were close to the researchers' locations. The study design was a randomized clinical trial examining children with disabilities who sought dental assistance.

For sample size estimation, a success rate of 96% for the HT in patients with learning disabilities after 12 months of follow-up was considered. A clinically significant difference of 20% in the success rate of orthodontic bands cemented with conventional glass ionomer (BOCIV) was adopted, with a significance level of 0.05 and a power of 0.80. Considering a one-tailed test, an additional 10% was added for potential losses and 10% for the cluster effect, resulting in a final total of approximately 100 teeth (G*Power 3.1.3; Düsseldorf, DE).

Inclusion Criteria

Children with cognitive impairment, diagnosed with mild to moderate learning disabilities, aged 4–10 years, with extensive carious lesions involving more than one surface of the tooth, presenting International Caries Detection and Assessment System (ICDAS) scores of 5 or 6, and the possibility of follow-up for 12 months were included in the research, provided that the guardians agreed and signed the informed consent form (ICF).

Exclusion Criteria

Children with teeth showing pulp exposure, spontaneous pain, teeth undergoing advanced root resorption with mobility, presence of edema or fistula near the tooth, furcation or cervical lesions, teeth with restorations, sealants, or enamel formation defects were excluded.

Data Collection

Randomization

The teeth were distributed into two parallel groups: the HT control group and the experimental group using BOCIV. The teeth were randomized into each group according to a sequence of random numbers obtained using appropriate statistical software (MedCalc version 15.8, Ostend, Belgium). Only at the time of the interventions were the generated sequences known and distributed in opaque, sealed envelopes to the operators between the control group of HT and the experimental group with BOCIV. For the clinical procedures, the operators underwent theoretical-practical calibration and training for both interventions in Goiânia/GO. The training covered the ICDAS classification, as well as the step-by-step selection process for BOCIV and HT metal crowns, up to the cementation and installation of both techniques. The study was carried out by two operators (ACBBP) and (CSR), and the subsequent phases (baseline, after 6 and 12 months) were conducted by the same examiners (ACBBP) and (CSR). If a patient had more than one tooth with a carious lesion involving two or more surfaces according to the inclusion criteria, a lottery was conducted for each tooth to be treated, in order to select the technique to be employed per tooth; therefore, a patient could contribute one or more teeth to the sample.

Blinding

Blinding of patients, operators, and examiners was not possible due to the differences between the interventions. However, the statistical consultant remained blinded regarding the groups.

Examiners and Operators

Among the researchers, two operators (ACBBP) and (CSR) were responsible for executing the restorative methods on the teeth selected for the study, both for the control group and the experimental group, as well as the same examiners (ACBBP) and (CSR) who monitored and assessed the patients in the subsequent phases (baseline, after 6 months, and after 12 months).

Clinical Procedures

An initial clinical examination was performed to assess the primary molars with cavities involving two or more surfaces of the teeth with ICDAS scores of 5 or 6. After the clinical examination, radiographic imaging of the selected tooth was conducted. If the child had more than one tooth with a carious lesion involving two or more surfaces that met the inclusion criteria, a lottery was conducted for each tooth to be treated, in order to select the technique to be employed per tooth; thus, a patient could contribute one or more teeth to the sample. This initial examination was carried out to screen the children and diagnose carious lesions so that they would fit the study criteria, enabling randomization and identifying other potential treatment needs, with the aim of planning comprehensive treatment for the patient.

The selected molars were allocated through a random sequence list into two groups: (1) control group, HT; (2) experimental group, BOCIV.

Control Group Hall Technique

The included teeth were treated following the principles of HT. After clinical and radiographic examination, prophylaxis with prophylactic paste was performed on the selected tooth for the sample. Subsequently, the prefabricated and precontoured steel crown (Shofu) that best adapted to the tooth to be treated was selected. The crown was then filled with glass ionomer cement (Ketac Molar 3M) and seated onto the tooth to be treated. During the setting period of the material, the patient was instructed to bite on a cotton roll placed over the crown to ensure satisfactory seating and to allow excess cement to flow out. After the material had set, excess cement was removed, and the final radiographic imaging of the treated dental element was performed.

Experimental Group Using Conventional Glass Ionomer Cement Orthodontic Bands

Clinical examination, radiographic imaging, and prophylaxis of the tooth to be treated were conducted. Subsequently, the most appropriate band for the tooth in question was selected, and dental floss was placed in the band to prevent the risk of the patient swallowing or aspirating it. Adaptation was performed using orthodontic pliers to ensure a better fit to the tooth, and the band was then removed with a “band remover” for the manipulation of chemically activated high-viscosity glass ionomer cement in powder-liquid form. The restorative material was then applied to all internal surfaces of the orthodontic band, after which it was positioned onto the tooth, followed by the application of the digital pressure technique. Excess material was removed with a manual instrument, concluding the isolation with the application of petroleum jelly over the tooth, thus completing the installation of the band restoration. After the material had set, excess cement was removed, and final radiographic imaging of the treated dental element was performed.

All participants and their guardians received instructions on oral hygiene and diet, based on reducing the frequency of sugar intake. The other teeth requiring intervention were treated by the two operators but were not included in the study.

Return Protocols

Participants were scheduled for reevaluations at 6 and 12 months after the interventions. During these follow-ups, the patient underwent a new clinical examination, periapical radiography, photographs, and completion of forms with the evaluation criteria for the two techniques used on the treated tooth. The researchers responsible for the intervention contacted participants via WhatsApp to confirm patient returns 7 days prior to the scheduled return date. Participants who could not attend were considered dropouts.

Outcomes Analyzed

Longevity

For longevity, the evaluation methodology was used to assess the teeth treated with both techniques: orthodontic bands and glass ionomer cement, and the evaluation of the steel crowns through the HT via clinical and radiographic examinations at baseline, 6, and 12 months after the initial intervention.

Marginal Adaptation, Marginal Extension, and Proximal Contact

Marginal adaptation was evaluated following clinical and radiographic criteria and filled out by the operator immediately after the procedure at baseline, and during the 6- and 12-month follow-ups. Radiographs were taken using a radiographic positioning device to ensure standardization.

Statistical Analysis

Kaplan–Meier curves were utilized to estimate the survival of the interventions. The log-rank test assessed the differences between the survival curves. Participants who were evaluated at least once during the study were included in the analysis. A multivariate Cox regression model was employed to determine the potential influence of explanatory variables on intervention failure. Initially, an unadjusted analysis was conducted for each explanatory variable, and those with p ≤ 0.20 were tested in the adjusted analysis. The final model included only variables with p ≤ 0.05. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated in Figure 1.

Kaplan–Meier curve for both groups. The Hall group demonstrated greater survival (88%) compared to the Band group (66%) after 1 year of follow-up (Log-rank, p = 0.009). Four failures were observed in the Hall group and 13 in the Band group after 6 months. After 1 year, there were six failures for Hall and 17 for Band

An intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis using multiple imputation was conducted considering the success rate of the intervention at the 12-month follow-up. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Comparisons between groups regarding marginal adaptation, marginal extension, proximal contact, and marginal adaptation according to radiographic criteria at baseline were performed using the Chi-square test. Analyses were conducted using Jamovi (Version 1.2.27.0, Sydney, Australia). A significance level of 5% was adopted for all analyses.

Results

According to the flowchart represented in Figure 2, the recruitment process involved a sample of 105 screened children, of which 58 were included in the study according to the required criteria. When children had more than one tooth with carious lesions involving two or more surfaces that met the inclusion criteria, they could contribute one or more teeth to the study. For the sample, the teeth of each child were randomly allocated to be treated by HT (n = 50) or cemented orthodontic bands with conventional glass ionomer cement (BOCIV) (n = 50), totaling 100 teeth based on the sample calculation. The children were enrolled through sequential recruitment in the study between August 2022 and April 2023, with the final follow-up assessment occurring in April 2024. After 1 year, at least 72 teeth were reevaluated, comprising 38 with metal crowns (HT) and 34 with bands cemented with conventional glass ionomer (BOCIV). Among these 72 teeth, three teeth with Hall crowns and four with bands had been exfoliated. In total, 100 teeth were evaluated at least once during the study.

Discussion

Based on the results of this research, minimally invasive dentistry, specifically mixed interventions such as the HT and orthodontic bands cemented with glass ionomer cement (BOCIV), is a promising option for the treatment of carious lesions in children with disabilities. Furthermore, our results indicate that HT offers greater survival and efficacy, especially due to its complete isolation from biofilm, which helps slow the progression of carious lesions.10,11

It is widely recognized in the literature that children with disabilities often experience neuropsychomotor delays, and due to these impairments, they face many barriers throughout life. These conditions are associated with difficulties in oral hygiene, compromising the ability of these children to maintain an adequate dental care routine. This scenario is exacerbated by the continuous use of pediatric medications, which are often sugar-laden, soft food diets, and the failure to remove dental biofilm due to challenges in oral hygiene, making these atypical children more vulnerable to the development of caries.3,4,12,13

Mixed interventions, being more conservative, have enabled less painful and more humanized treatments, which is ideal for the dental care of atypical children.6,12,14 Such approaches result in fewer adverse consequences compared to conventional restorative treatments. Moreover, they stand out by preserving the structure of the teeth, maintaining their integrity, and thus avoiding pulp exposure.8,15,16 These results align with the present clinical research, which sought to compare two minimally invasive treatment methods, HT and BOCIV, with the aim of proposing more conservative techniques that ensure greater longevity of these restorations.

Sensory issues are a central feature in children with disabilities, especially those with ASD, significantly influencing the way these individuals interact with their environment. Many patients present hypersensitivity or hyposensitivity to sensory stimuli, such as sounds, lights, textures, and tastes, which can result in intense discomfort or even adverse reactions. This variability in sensory perception can lead to difficulties in everyday situations, including medical, dental, and therapeutic procedures, such as the placement of orthodontic devices. Understanding these sensory nuances is crucial for adapting techniques and approaches, ensuring that treatment is not only effective but also respectful of the needs and limitations of patients.17–19

Significant barriers were encountered in the implementation of both techniques in a child with ASD, as he demonstrated intolerance to the placement of the separating elastic, a phenomenon that can be attributed to the intense sensory problems frequently observed in this group. In this specific situation, it was necessary to adapt the technique, which considerably facilitated the placement of the HT and the BIVOC. However, it is important to note that this patient was excluded from the sample.

Regarding longevity, HT showed an 88% success rate compared to BOCIV's 66% after 1 year. Similar results were presented in another study. The higher success rate of HT can be attributed to the strength of the crown, which offers complete isolation from plaque biofilm in the oral environment, helping to slow or even stop the progression of carious lesions. Additionally, the glass ionomer cement used with HT may provide an added benefit in remineralizing the lesion.20 On the other hand, the use of BOCIV proved to be less effective in terms of material survival, possibly due to the incomplete coverage of the dental crown, allowing exposure of the glass ionomer in the occlusal region. This exposure to the oral environment can increase vulnerability to biofilm contact and material wear over time.

Both techniques in the present study showed preservation of tooth structure and integrity, as evidenced by this research, where similar behavior in marginal adaptation was observed. This evaluation was performed immediately after the installation of both techniques using radiographs and demonstrated that the procedures were successfully executed in 96% of HT cases and 90% of BOCIV cases. However, when analyzing marginal extension and proximal contact, HT showed superior performance compared to BOCIV. This result can be attributed to the design of the precontoured crowns, which are developed to provide optimal marginal adaptation and maintenance of proximal contact. These characteristics are crucial for the success of restorative treatments.

Although the stainless-steel crown with full coverage effectively protects the tooth from challenges related to feeding and brushing by the child and their caregivers, the success of BOCIV depends on the same individuals who contributed to creating a vulnerable environment for the development of these initial carious lesions. It is considered that for both techniques, HT and BOCIV, it is essential to raise awareness among parents and caregivers of these children regarding the importance of changing habits related to maintaining oral health by removing biofilm, as well as consuming healthier foods.13

The effectiveness of treatment depends significantly on the active participation of parents, who are responsible for brushing the affected surfaces with fluoridated toothpaste.13,21 This responsibility is vital, as proper and regular brushing helps control lesion progression and promote dental remineralization.

Regarding the biological approach to controlling carious lesions in primary molars, this approach involves sealing the tooth element without removing carious tissue and can be performed using preformed metal crowns. There is strong evidence of the efficacy and acceptability of this method. HT shows excellent results concerning pulp health and the longevity of dental rehabilitation when compared to conventional restorations. Furthermore, it offers a cost-effective solution by utilizing a low-technology approach. Therefore, it stands out as an effective option for treating carious lesions.11,15

The aim of adopting the same principle as HT with BOCIV studied in this clinical research was to provide an alternative for the treatment of extensive carious lesions in primary molars. Although the performance of BOCIV showed inferior results compared to HT crowns, it demonstrated a cost-benefit advantage, being more accessible to both professionals and patients' families. It is noteworthy that there are few published studies on the use of orthodontic bands for restorative purposes in pediatric dentistry. This is a highly relevant topic today, especially in light of the minimally invasive approach and associated benefits highlighted in this clinical research.

Conclusion

Based on the results of our clinical research, such as HT-specific mixed interventions and BOCIV, they are promising options for the treatment of caries lesions in children with disabilities. These results indicate that HT offers greater survival and efficacy, especially for its complete isolation from the biofilm, which helps to slow the progression of caries lesions. They provide more humanized and longer-lasting care, since fewer visits to the dental office are needed to perform repairs/replacements or additional treatments, which is ideal for children with disabilities.

Clinical Significance

Adopting the same principle as HT, the BOCIV techniques studied in the present clinical research would be an alternative for the treatment of extensive caries lesions in deciduous molars. Despite presenting inferior results to HT, BOCIV can be considered as a treatment option for these carious lesions in children with disabilities. Although HT shows better performance compared to BOCIV, both techniques allow for a more humanized and long-lasting treatment, since fewer dental visits are required, which is ideal for children with disabilities.

Analysis and optimization of machining parameters of AZ91 alloy nanocomposite with the Influences of nano ZrO2 through vacuum diecast process
Authors: Venkatesh R, Ismail Hossain, V Mohanavel, Manzoore Elahi M Soudagar, Sulaiman Ali Alharbi, Sami Al Obaid
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe magnesium alloy composite is a vital material for automotive applications due to its features like high stiffness, superior damping resistance, high strength, and lightweight. Here, the motto of research is to establish the AZ91 alloy nanocomposite with the exposures of 0, 1, 3, and 5 volume percentages (vol%) of nano zirconium dioxide (ZrO2) particles (50nm) through fluid stir metallurgy route associated with 1x105 Pa vacuum die cast process. Exposures on structural morphology, hardness, and impact toughness of composite are analyzed and identified as the nano AZ91 alloy composite enclosed with 5vol% is homogenous particle dispersion, enhanced hardness (97.6HV), and optimum toughness of 21.2J/mm2. However, composite faces machining difficulties due to the hard abrasive particles with higher hardness, resulting in tool wear. This experiment predicts the optimum mill parameters during the end mill operation of magnesium alloy nanocomposite (AZ91/5vol%) by using a tungsten carbide coated end mill cutter to attain the maximum metal removal rate with low surface roughness and tool wear analyzed via the general linear model (GLM) ANOVA approach. The input conditions for end milling operation vary, like feed rate (0.1 -0.4mm/rev), depth of cut (0.05 -0.2mm), and spindle speed (250-1000rpm). During the ANOVA GLM approach, the L16 design experiment is fixed for further interaction analysis. The results predicted by the depth to cut and feed rate were dominant and played a major role in deciding the tool wear, surface roughness, and MRR.Keywords: ANOVA, AZ91, ZrO2, Performance measures, vacuum die casting

Introduction

One of the automotive industry's themes is finding lightweight material adopted with specific mechanical and wear characteristics [[1], [2]]. The development of magnesium matrix composites plays a vital role in automotive applications because of the high strength-to-weight ratio, excellent damping characteristics, good tensile strength, and better thermal stability than traditional alloys [[3], [4]]. With the liquid stir cast, the AZ91 alloy composites were established with different percentages of silicon carbide particle (SiC), and the effect of SiC on mechanical and surface morphology behaviour was investigated. It displayed progressive improvement in tensile and hardness values related to SiC content [5]. The feasibility of the magnesium alloy composite fabrication via the stir cast route is analyzed, as well as the mechanical and microstructure behaviour of the composite [6]. Recently, the magnesium alloy composite developed with ZrO2 via bottom pouring stir casting route for automotive application. The ZrO2 nanoparticle survival better tensile, impact, and hardness values related to monolithic magnesium alloy [7]. Likewise, the AZ31B alloy composite was prepared by the compositions of 0-9wt% of zirconia nanoparticles through a liquid stir route (inert nature) and experimentally investigated its microstructural, mechanical, and physical properties. Its output recorded progressive increment in composite yield, tensile, hardness and impact energy [8]. Besides, the stir cast route was adaptable for magnesium alloy composite fabrication, and its behaviour may vary depending on the choice of process parameters [9].

Milling is one machining operation that permits drilling with different diametral end mill tools [10]. During the milling operation, the spindle speed, feed rate, and cut depth are considered the input parameters [11]. According to the workpiece conditions and nature of work, the input parameters vary according to different levels of speed, feed, and depth of cut [12]. This parameter was optimized via neural network [13], Algorithm [14] and analysis of variance (ANOVA) technique [[15], [16]]. With the key features, the ANOVA techniques predicted the results with a confidence level of 99.9% [17]. With references to ANOVA optimization techniques, various research was reported. Somasundaram et al. [18] studied the electrical discharge machining process for magnesium alloy machining and its process parameters pulse on/off time, current and voltage was optimized via ANOVA grey relational analysis (GRA) and found the optimum parameters for attaining the good surface quality. Suneesh and Sivapragash [19] studied and optimized the micro milling input variable for alumina-reinforced magnesium alloy composite.

The L18 orthogonal array adopted GRA was used to identify the optimum micro milling parameters for machining magnesium alloy composite under different levels of feed rate, cutting depth, and spindle speed for obtaining high surface quality, low cutting force and tool wear. They found that the feed and depth of cut are the significant factors for fixing the optimum surface quality with low tool wear. The GRA route predicted the optimum machining parameters for achieving a higher metal removal rate (MRR), low tool wear and surface roughness under varied input parameters [20]. Kumar et al. [21] investigated the end milling behaviour of magnesium alloy with the varied input operation parameters of rotational speed, depth of cut, and feed rate and optimized with L16 ANOVA design analysis found 25mm/min, 1.25mm depth of cute under 2700rpm has good machinability behaviour for low surface roughness and larger MRR. Titanium nitride-coated binary inserts were used for super alloy machining. Its process variables are optimized via multi-response optimization techniques, and feed is the prime factor for deciding the quality of the machine surface [22]. Srinivasa et al. [23] studied the end milling behaviour on surface quality and tool wear of magnesium alloy under dry state. Influences of input variables on output response are studied, and the multi-response optimization route spots optimum machining parameters. The depth of cut and feed rate is the primary variable for fixing the better output response. However, the WC-coated tool found significance in composite machining and reduced the geometric error [24]. The Optimization ANOVA with the L16 approach adopted for three different groups with a mean value of predicted value provides effective machining parameters [25]. The AZ91D alloy is involved in the milling process using a titanium diboride coated tool and evaluates the surface quality. The ANOVA technique is used to find the optimum input variable, and low surface roughness features input variables like cutting speed, axial depth, and feed [26]. The milling behaviour of AZ91D alloy is evaluated by different input variables like 900-1300 m/min cutting speed, 0.02-0.05mm/tooth, and 0.2-0.3mm depth of cut. The impact of milling variables on the surface quality of AZ91D alloy is measured, and optimum variable combinations are spotted via L16 factorial design [27]. The dry face milling process of AZ91 alloy is experimentally evaluated, and its input variables on the low surface roughness of AZ91 alloy are predicted via the L9 design approach [28]. The dry milling behaviour of magnesium alloy is studied for lightweight applications [29]. Moreover, the magnesium alloy involved for aerospace application and dry milling behaviour on surface quality of magnesium alloy is studied [30].

According to the support of existing literature, the magnesium alloy composite made with stir-cast spots the micro-voids and clusters due to air entrapment and thermal mismatch and this research is an attempt to establish a liquid stir-vacuum diecast route to overcome the drawback of conventional stir-casting. The main research objectives are to limit the microporous clusters and improve the hardness & impact toughness of AZ91 alloy nanocomposite by introducing ZrO2 nanoparticles. The optimum behaviour of composite samples involves machining studies. Tungsten carbide (WC) coated end milling process parameters were optimized via the ANOVA route, and the results were compared to the possibilities of machining parameters that provide superior machining behaviour. Based on the various studies related to the machining operation of magnesium alloy (AZ91), nanocomposite was performed with a titanium carbide coated end mill cutter (Hozly 8x35x70L) of 8mm diameter through a CNC end mill under different levels of feed rate, depth to cut, and speed. The output response, such as tool wear, MRR, and surface roughness, was monitored, and influenced machining parameters were optimized via the ANOVA GLM technique adopted with the L16 design of the experiment.

Composite preparation details

A small ingot sample of AZ91 alloy was kept in an electrical furnace crucible made up of graphite, and its temperature was raised to 850°C to melt under an inert nature. Similarly, the 50nm ZrO2 particles were kept in a muffle furnace and heated at 300°C for 30 minutes to limit moisture. Then, the heated ZrO2 particles were placed in molten AZ91 alloy and stirred at 450rpm under an inert nature. In the last stage, it should be transferred into a preheated (500°C) steel die (300 mmX100 mmX20 mm) with an applied vacuum pressure of 1x105 Pa to limit the casting defects [[3], [4]]. After, the casted sample was cooled by natural convection with an ambient temperature. The flow process diagram and its details are mentioned in Fig. 1 (a-f).

Finally, the developed composites are involved in structural morphology, hardness, and impact toughness studies. Using a scanning electron microscope, FIE makes Vickers hardness and impact tester, the microstructure, microhardness, and impact toughness of composite evaluated. The microhardness and impact toughness test follows the ASTM E92 and E23 standards.

End milling operation

The end milling CNC work centre, including the R8 taper spindle, BT35 automatic tool changer (ATC), and 3TEE slot configured table.

Initially, the AZ91 alloy nanocomposite was fixed in the 3TEE slot table, and the WC-coated end mill tool (Hozly 8x35x70L)of 8mm (4 blades-flutes) was kept in BT35 ATC for the entire milling operation. The composite materials are weighed before and after the machining process. The dimension of the sample is 50mm X 100mm X 20mm, and the cutting length is 50mm. During the end of the mill operation, the time was recorded from start to end of the milling operation via a digital stopwatch. The milling operation was performed by the design of the experiment Table 2. For each condition, the milling tool dynamometer featured with multiple-linear-regression-based in-process tool wear prediction (MLR-ITWP) system is monitored the tool wear, as presented in Fig. 2 (a). Zoom-stereo- EMZ-5TR Meiji model identifies and measures the insert flank wear. The frictional temperature between WC coated end mill and the composite surface was recorded using an Etekcity Infrared Thermometer 774. After the end milling operation, each milling pass surface was examined using a surface roughness tester (Mitutoyo SJ 410- ISO 1997), as shown in Fig. 2 (b). Three trials are taken during the surface roughness evaluation, and the final Ra of the machined surface considers its mean value. The diagram in Fig. 2 (c) highlights force visualization and tool wear measurement. Fig. 2 (d) depicts the zoom-stereo- EMZ-5TR Meiji model used to identify insert flank wear. Before observing the tool wear, the tool is cleaned with a brush and mounted onto the zoom stereo EMZ-5TR Meiji model table. The focus is adjusted according to the tool position to achieve a clear magnification of the end mill cutting edges. Subsequently, the lighting is adjusted to observe the signs of flank wear and record the data, with the support of a calibration scale to measure the wear.

Details for design of experiment and ANOVA GLM approach

In the present investigation, the feed rate(f), depth to cut (da), and spindle speed (N) are considered as input factors with varied values of 0.1mm/rev-0.4mm/ rev feed rate with an interval of 0.1mm/rev, 0.05mm to 0.2mm depth to cut with an interval of 0.05mm, and 250rpm to 1000rpm spindle speed with the interval of 250rpm respectively. Based on this configuration, the experiment (DOE) design was selected as a 4-level design with the number of factors as 3. With the references of past literature reported by Jouini et al. [27] and Marakini et al. [28], the present experimental parameters and levels are fixed. The details of Taguchi's design are presented in Table 2.

The DOE was generated with an L16 design run, as presented in Table 3 and contains the various levels with measured output response. During the optimization, the initial stage mean and signal-to-noise ratio were predicted via an analysis of Taguchi's design. After this process, the significant factor run with their sequence is identified and considered a model value for finding optimum parameters for the corresponding output response with a 99.5% confidence level.

Result and discussions

Structural morphology

Here, the structural morphology of synthesized composite samples with and without ZrO2 nanoparticles is mentioned in Fig. 3(a-d). According to the SEM capture, the figures are illustrated, and Fig. 3 (a) indicates the AZ91 alloy surface without reinforcement, and there were no identical defects. The uniform stir speed and applied vacuum pressure cause better microstructure [4]. The applied vacuum pressure may influence the compacting of the surface with improved waviness surface. The 5000x magnification range is utilized to view and spot the structure of cast AZ91 alloy.

SEM image of composite a) sample 1, b) sample 2, c) sample 3, and d) sample 4

Fig. 3(b) illustrates the SEM image of composite sample 2 made with 1vol% of ZrO2 nanoparticle. Applying constant stir speed under a semi-solid state stir process makes good particle distribution, as proved in Fig. 3(b).

The ZrO2 particles are deeply dispersed with the Al91 alloy matrix, enriching the composite's qualities and enduring the maximum load during the mechanical testing. Besides, the applied vacuum pressure should increase the bonding dispersion and spot the tri-shaped ZrO2 in the AZ91 alloy matrix. Compared to conventional stir-cast-developed composites [3], these vacuum diecast-developed composites own better particle distribution and limited casting defects [4].

Fig. 3 (c-d) indicates the microstructure of a composite sample made with 3 and 5vol% of ZrO2 nanoparticles via liquid stir vacuum die casting route. These structures prove the ZrO2 particle's presence in the AZ91 alloy matrix and are spotted as homogenous. It was revealed from Fig. 3 (c-d) that the ZrO2 nanoparticles were well dispersed in the AZ91 alloy matrix. The suitable process parameters lead to enriching the qualities of the composite [4]. However, the nano ZrO2 particle is distributed along the AZ91 matrix, and few particles are dispersed at varied distances. By the vacuum diecast process implementations, the casting of microvoids and cluster formations is limited, and even particle distribution is obtained.

Microhardness measurements

Fig. 4 indicates the microhardness performance of AZ91 alloy established with 0, 1, 3, and 5vol% of nano ZrO2 particles. The effectiveness of vacuum diecast and the appearance of nano ZrO2 particles in the AZ91 matrix have excellent microhardness values compared to unreinforced AZ91 alloy.

Microhardness of composite samples

Based on the implementations of nano ZrO2 particle loading in AZ91 alloy, the microhardness values are varied, and the microhardness of composite sample 1 is noted by 85.2±0.5HV. At the same time, the composite prepared with 1vol% of nano ZrO2 was found to have an improved hardness of 89.6±1HV. The effective dispersion of nano ZrO2 resists the impression of indentation during the test. Moreover, the coarse dispersion of the ZrO2 phase in the magnesium matrix is found to have a better hardness value and resistance to the indentation. Likewise, the composite sample 3 synthesized with 3vol% of nano ZrO2 offered 92.5±1.2HV, greater than the monolithic AZ91 alloy. The appearance of hard nano ZrO2 particles causes better hardness value. The maximum hardness value of 97.6±1.5HV is monitored by the composite sample 4 and enriched by 14.5% and 31.89% compared to AZ91 alloy and past reported value [8]. The deep dispersion of nano ZrO2 particles and better bonding with even distribution cause better restriction against the impression.

Impact toughness measurements

The nano ZrO2 particle made with efficient bonding in AZ91 alloy matrix identified a good impact toughness value, and its pictorial representation is mentioned in Fig. 5. The impact toughness of AZ91 composite sample 1 shown as 9.2±0.2J/mm2, it is lower than the impact value of nano ZrO2 particle embedded composites.

Impact toughness of composite samples

The composite sample 2 is synthesized with 1vol% of nano ZrO2 and offered 11.5±0.5J/mm2 impact toughness due to the particle shown even in the AZ91 alloy matrix proven in Fig. 3 (b). Likewise, composite sample 3 consists of 3vol% nano ZrO2, facilitating an improved impact toughness value of 17.4±0.3J/mm2. The deep dispersion of nano ZrO2 particles could endure the maximum impact, and failure may occur during the crack propagations. Besides, the composite sample 4 contained 5vol% of nano ZrO2 particles, proving that the maximum absorption of impact energy results in better impact toughness than all other samples. The actions of the ZrO2 particle make the effective bonding and lead to absorbing the maximum energy and enduring the maximum impact. It was enriched 1.3 times higher than the AZ91 alloy and improved by 3% compared to the earlier reported value [7].

Optimization of input milling parameters on least tool wear

A coated end mill cutter is used in the present investigation, and its wear rate was recorded as very low. The rank order of depth to cut, feed rate, and spindle speed predicted its dominant input factors. With the help of design analysis, the GLM is executed with a 99.5% confidence level.

During this analysis, the tool was fixed as a response and factors were selected by the rank order and treated as input model. Finally, the residuals plot is generated by the design run order from R1 to R15. Previous studies reported that the cutting depth and feed rate were significant factors in deciding the tool life [13]. Among the various runs, the R1-R2 and R9-10 were recorded by least tool wear and raked as one and two, as presented in Table 4.

Conclusions

Implementing the liquid stir vacuum die cast process, the AZ91 alloy and their composite embedded with 1, 3, and 5vol% of nano ZrO2 particle are effectively synthesized, and its structural, hardness, and impact toughness performance are studied. Based on the investigational results, the important conclusions are summarized below. Besides, compared to past literature, the present developed composites own better microhardness and impact strength behaviour.

•The composite prepared with 5vol% nano ZrO2 particles has superior hardness and impact toughness value, which is hiked by 14.5% hardness and has 1.3 times impact toughness, related to cast AZ91 alloy without nano ZrO2 particles. Besides, the structural microstructure proved ZrO2 nanoparticle presence even in the AZ91 alloy matrix. It was subjected to end milling machinability studies using a WC-coated end mill tool through a CNC machine. The impact of cutting parameters on tool wear, surface roughness, and metal removal rate is optimized through the ANOVA GLM technique with a 99.5% confidence level under the L16 design analysis.

Clinical characteristics, early blood biochemical indicators, and prognostic status of children with bronchopneumonia
Authors: Yuanyuan Ling, Dean Yang, Sha Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
To investigate the clinical characteristics, early blood biochemical indicators, and prognostic status of children with bronchopneumonia. We conducted a retrospective analysis of data from 500 children diagnosed with bronchopneumonia at our hospital from June 2019 to December 2022. Based on the severity of the disease, patients were assigned to the severe group (n = 180) or mild group (n = 320), and an additional 150 healthy children were chosen as the control group. Blood indicators [aspartate aminotransferase (AST), plasma carbon dioxide combining power (CO2CP), serum potassium (K+), serum sodium (Na+)], inflammatory markers [interleukin-17 (IL-17), interleukin-10 (IL-10), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT)], and cardiac enzyme profiles [lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase (CK), alpha-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (α-HBDH), creatine kinase isoenzyme (CK-MB)] were compared among the 3 groups. The severe group showed more signs such as diarrhea, pleural effusion, and respiratory distress than the mild group. AST levels in the severe group were significantly higher than those in the mild group and control group, while CO2CP, K+, and Na+ were lower than those in the mild group and control group. AST levels in the mild group were significantly higher than those in the control group, while CO2CP and Na + were significantly higher than those in the control group (P < .05). IL-10, IL-17, PCT, and CRP levels in the severe group were higher than those in the mild group and control group, while those in the mild group were higher than those in the control group (P < .05). CK, CK-MB, LDH, and α-HBDH levels in the severe group were significantly higher than those in the mild group and control group. CK, CK-MB, LDH, and α-HBDH levels in the mild group were higher than those in the control group (P < .05). The severe group had a longer duration of fever, disappearance of symptoms, and cough relief time than the mild group (P < .05). Children with bronchopneumonia exhibit increased cardiac enzyme (CK, CK-MB, LDH, and α-HBDH) activity, and PCT and CRP expression levels increase with disease severity. Timely detection of relevant blood biochemical indicators and early implementation of prevention and treatment measures can improve the cure rate and reduce mortality in children with bronchopneumonia.

1. Introduction

Bronchopneumonia is a common disease in clinical pediatrics that affects the bronchi and alveoli.[1] It is generally believed to be caused by infectious pathogens, such as pneumococcus bacteria, adenovirus, protozoa, or chlamydia, entering the peripheral lung tissue or bronchi of children, leading to respiratory tract infections.[2,3] After children contract pneumonia, due to lower lung volume, weaker cough reflex, relatively weaker respiratory muscle strength, and lower respiratory function, early clinical symptoms and signs are not obvious, and the disease progresses rapidly.[4] When significant pulmonary moist rale appears in both lungs of the child, the disease has already entered a more severe stage, making treatment more difficult, while increasing the risk of complications.[5,6] Therefore, timely and accurate diagnosis of pediatric pulmonary infections is of great significance for clinical diagnosis and treatment. Due to the differences in the severity of bronchopneumonia in children, clinical symptoms, blood indicators, lung function damage, and prognostic evaluation are different, which is also the key to the clinical diagnosis and treatment of children with bronchopneumonia. Therefore, blood indicators and cardiac enzyme profiles, serum procalcitonin (PCT), C-reactive protein (CRP), inflammatory markers, and immune indicators are important laboratory tests, which have significant implications for bronchopneumonia.[7,8] In order to identify pneumonia infection early, guide clinical diagnosis and treatment, and predict prognosis, this study explores the related changes in clinical manifestations and early blood biochemical indicators of children with bronchopneumonia, aiming to provide theoretical guidance for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. General information

This study is a retrospective analysis of clinical data from 500 children with bronchopneumonia admitted to our hospital from June 2019 to December 2022. The inclusion criteria were: meeting the diagnostic criteria for bronchopneumonia;[9] onset time ≤ 8 days; complete clinical data for each patient. Additionally, 30 healthy children were selected as a control group during the same period and met the criteria of having undergone relevant laboratory blood biochemical index testing with complete data. Exclusion criteria for all subjects included severe liver dysfunction and congenital bronchial abnormalities. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First People Hospital of Lin’an District.

Among them, patients were divided into the severe group (n = 180) and mild group (n = 320) based on the severity of the disease using the American Thoracic Society guidelines for the management of community-acquired pneumonia.[9] The severe group consisted of 95 males and 85 females, aged 2 to 11 years, with an average age of (5.65 ± 2.43) years. The mild group consisted of 180 males and 140 females, aged 2 to 12 years, with an average age of (5.57 ± 2.63) years. The control group consisted of 80 males and 70 females, aged 2 to 12 years, with an average age of (5.49 ± 2.40) years. There were no statistically significant differences in general data among the groups (P > .05).

2.2. Methods

2.2.1. Clinical sign collection and imaging examination.

Upon admission or during physical examinations, all enrolled children were asked or their medical history was reviewed with their guardians to collect information on clinical symptoms and signs of bronchopneumonia, including fever, diarrhea, coughing, phlegm production, respiratory distress, as well as pulmonary dry and moist rales, pleural effusion, and decreased breath sounds. Chest X-ray examination was conducted using the Toshiba DR X machine on all 500 children with bronchopneumonia to determine the status of lung parenchyma gas, thoracic gas, and lung parenchyma morphology, as well as the presence of air or fluid in the lungs. Routine treatment including cough suppression, bronchodilation, and anti-infection therapy was administered.

2.2.2. Blood biochemistry tests.

Within 24 hours of admission, all enrolled subjects had 5ml of venous blood collected and placed in yellow clot-promoting tubes. The blood was allowed to stand for 10 minutes until clotting occurred, then centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 15 minutes using a centrifuge to separate the serum, which was stored at -70°C for further analysis. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to detect serum inflammatory factor indicators, including aspartate aminotransferase (AST), plasma carbon dioxide combining power (CO2CP), serum potassium (K+) and sodium (Na+), interleukin-10 (IL-10), interleukin-17 (IL-17), procalcitonin (PCT), and C-reactive protein (CRP). At the same time, cardiac enzyme profiles were examined, including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase isoenzyme (CK-MB), and alpha-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (α-HBDH). The Siemens ADVIA Chemistry XPT fully automated biochemistry analyzer and relevant reagents were used for testing.

2.2.3. Outcome indicators.

To observe and analyze the clinical manifestations of children with bronchopneumonia. To compare early blood biochemical indicators between the bronchopneumonia group and the control group. To compare the prognosis of children with bronchopneumonia based on criteria established by the Expert Consensus on Diagnosis and Treatment of Mycoplasma Pneumonia in Children,[10] including the time to disappearance of clinical signs, duration of fever, time to relief of cough, and other relevant indicators.

2.3. Statistical analysis

SPSS 24.0 was chosen for statistical analysis. Descriptive statistics was used to express quantitative data in the form of mean ± standard deviation. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for comparisons among 3 groups, and pairwise comparisons will be performed using LSD-t tests. Qualitative data was expressed as [n (%)] and compared using chi-square tests with a significance level of P < .05 indicating statistically significant differences.

4. Discussion

Bronchopneumonia is a common disease in children, especially in infants and young children.[1] The immature development of the respiratory system in children, combined with differences in pathogens and disease onset times, means that they lack mature immune responses, have lower antibody levels, and weaker respiratory epithelial cell cilia function, which can result in rapid disease progression.[11,12] Therefore, early diagnosis plays an important role in the clinical management of bronchopneumonia. Related studies have shown that blood biochemical indicators in children with bronchopneumonia exhibit varying degrees of changes.[13,14] As the infection worsens, the virus invades the child immune defenses, activates the immune system, and triggers a series of immune response reactions. Clinical studies have also reported that bronchopneumonia patients have some degree of myocardial damage.[15,16] By strengthening testing for cardiac cells, the severity of the patient condition can be accurately determined. Therefore, early blood biochemical indicators have become a hot topic in the study of bronchopneumonia diagnosis indicators. These tests are convenient, quick, cause minimal harm to the body, can be continuously monitored, and have good sensitivity and specificity.[17]

This study collected clinical symptoms and signs of children with bronchopneumonia and found that all cases exhibited segmental manifestations, increased lung translucency on chest X-rays, atelectasis, varying degrees of pulmonary effusion, and blunting of the costophrenic angle. In some cases of abscess formation, a curved fluid level can be seen with high external and low internal densities.[18,19] The severe group had more cases of diarrhea, respiratory distress, pleural effusion, and increased pulmonary markings compared to the mild group. This indicates that bronchopneumonia typically has a slow onset, long course, and may become chronic if not treated effectively, and early respiratory tract symptoms are nonspecific and may be easily overlooked. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the analysis of the child symptoms and clinical diagnosis, and clinical manifestations such as fever, diarrhea, respiratory distress, pleural effusion, increased pulmonary markings, lung segmental consolidation, atelectasis, and cough can be used as auxiliary diagnostic criteria to provide timely and effective treatment for children with bronchopneumonia.[20,21]

The results of this study show that the severe group had significantly higher levels of AST and lower levels of CO2CP, K+, and Na+ compared to the mild and control groups. The mild group had significantly higher levels of AST and significantly lower levels of CO2CP and Na+ than the control group. The reason for this may be due to electrolyte acid-base balance disorders in children with bronchopneumonia resulting in hyponatremia, which leads to hypoxia in the body, increased anaerobic glycolysis, insufficient ATP supply, and decreased active transport function of K+ and Na+. At the same time, hypoxia stimulates the secretion of antidiuretic hormone by the hypothalamus, leading to an increase in water absorption in the renal distal convoluted tubules and collecting ducts, but cannot simultaneously increase the absorption of sodium, resulting in hyponatremia. Additionally, as pneumonia becomes more severe, the greater effect on renal blood flow and decreasing glomerular filtration rate can cause a decrease in CO2CP.[22] Furthermore, a large amount of calcium ions transfers from extracellular fluid into cells, and during hypoxia causes intra-cellular calcium ions to be unable to be transported out in a timely manner, inhibiting parathyroid gland function and leading to a decrease in K+.[23]

Moreover, the results of this study also showed that the severe group had significantly higher levels of cardiac enzymes, including CK, CK-MB, LDH, and α-HBDH than the mild and control groups. The mild group had significantly higher levels of these cardiac enzymes than the control group. This suggests that when the body is in a state of hyponatremia, cells swell, increasing damage to the myocardium and exacerbating heart failure in children. The pathological mechanism involves hypoxia in lung tissue, virus infiltration into the myocardium, especially in severe pneumonia, increased lipid peroxidation products and oxygen-free radicals, leading to an increase in cardiac enzyme release such as AST, CK, CK-MB, LDH, and α-HBDH into the blood. As the severity of bronchopneumonia worsens, myocardial cell damage increases, and cardiac enzyme activity increases accordingly.[20]

The results of this study showed that IL-10, IL-17, PCT, and CRP were higher in the severe group than the mild and control groups, and higher in the mild group than the control group. The most typical reaction in bronchopneumonia is the change in systemic inflammatory indicators. IL-10 is one of the cytokines that produce various immune effects in the body and has an inhibitory effect on other interleukins. As a protective anti-inflammatory factor, once infected, IL-10 and IL-17 suddenly rise in a short time under the inflammatory response, promoting the secretion of other inflammatory factors. PCT is a low-level glycoprotein in the blood under normal physiological conditions but can enter the body blood system through other cells when a bacterial infection occurs, appearing in a highly expressed state.[24] CRP, as an acute-phase reactant protein, has high sensitivity and is not easily affected by other factors, making it useful for assisting in the diagnosis of various infections. The study results showed that as the child condition worsened, CRP concentration increased.[25]

There are many clinical treatment methods for bronchopneumonia, including antibiotics, traditional Chinese medicine, nebulization, etc, but there is no unified diagnosis and treatment standard. This study developed targeted treatment methods based on the “Expert Consensus on Diagnosis and Treatment of Mycoplasma Pneumonia in Children” and observed the prognosis of children. Through active treatment such as sodium and potassium supplementation, myocardial enzyme activity decreased, and clinical fever and cough disappeared. The severe group had a longer duration of fever disappearance, sign disappearance, fever duration, and cough relief time than the mild group, but there was no significant difference in disease progression between the 2 groups.

However, this study has some limitations. First, the sample size was small, constituting a single-center study, which may have introduced selection bias. Second, this was an observational study, and further confirmation is required through well-designed and multi-center studies.

In summary, children with bronchopneumonia have a high incidence of symptoms such as fever, cough, and lung rales, accompanied by significant inflammatory reactions, elevated levels of inflammatory markers, myocardial damage, and electrolyte disorders. Timely monitoring of changes in various blood biochemical indicators in early-stage children is beneficial in providing guidance for clinical prevention and treatment.

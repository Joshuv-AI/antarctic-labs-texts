Prognostic importance of CD47 expression on survival of colorectal cancer
Authors: Oktay Halit Aktepe, Olcay Kurtulan, Pinar Ezgi Dama, Eda Caliskan Yildirim, Aytac Terzi, Simin Alara Kalyoncu, Elif Atag, Tugba Yavuzsen, Meral Uner, Aziz Karaoglu, Suayib Yalcin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe exact value of cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47), known as the “don’t eat me” signal, in colorectal cancer (CRC) has not been clearly revealed. Thus, we investigated the impact of its expression on the overall survival (OS) of CRC patients who underwent colectomy.MethodsNinety-eight CRC patients were included. The patient population was grouped into two categories based on the median value of CD47 expression examined with immunohistochemistry: negative (< median) and positive (≥ median). The estimation of OS was executed with the Kaplan-Meier method, and the difference in prognostic groups was tested with the log-rank test. The association of CD47 with OS was examined with univariate and multivariate Cox regression models.ResultsCD47 expression was recorded in 56.1% of CRC patients. Among the clinicopathological variables, the only significant difference between the prognostic groups was the presence of lymph node involvement (p = 0.005). The OS of patients with CD47 expression was lower than those with no CD47 expression (39.3 months vs. not reached, p = 0.001, respectively). Additionally, in metastatic CRC patients treated with biologic agents, patients with CD47 expression had numerically inferior OS times than those without CD47 expression (37 months vs. 58.5 months, p = 0.05, respectively). Our multivariate analysis showed that CD47 was an independent prognostic factor associated with OS in this cohort (HR: 2.142, p = 0.006).ConclusionOur study suggests that CD47 may serve as a potential prognostic marker and a promising therapeutic target for anti-CD47–based treatments in CRC.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Prognosis, CD47

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most commonly diagnosed cancer in both sexes and the second leading cause of death in the United States [1]. Despite new advances in the treatment of CRC, the prognosis of metastatic CRC (mCRC) remains poor, with a 5-year relative survival rate of 14% [2]. Despite several studies aiming to identify new markers and algorithms for improved prognostication and treatment, elucidating the mechanisms underlying CRC metastasis remains essential for advancing CRC management.

Cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47) is a transmembrane surface protein, known as an integrin-associated protein. It is broadly expressed on normal cells as well as cancer cells and sends a “do not eat me” message to immune cells via binding to signal regulatory protein α (SIRPα) [3]. After this communication occurs, healthy cells are protected from the phagocytic function of macrophages [4]. In addition to CD47, healthy cells express other anti-phagocytic molecules, such as CD200, CD31, and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1, to protect themselves from phagocytosis [5]. CD47 has been shown as a prognostic factor in various hematological and solid malignancies, including CRC [6–8]. A recent study demonstrated that tumor-intrinsic CD47 signal could play a pivotal role in tumor growth and metastasis by enhancing extracellular signal-regulated kinase activity and aerobic glycolysis in CRC [9]. Furthermore, CD47 signaling has been reported to be associated with CRC progression via promoting angiogenesis and apoptosis [10]. Additionally, it was shown that CD47, in association with epithelial-mesenchymal transition, could promote the stemness of cancer cells [11], thereby contributing to the malignancy of CRC. The objective of the present study was to elucidate the association of CD47 with clinic-pathologic variables and survival outcomes of CRC.

Materials and methods

Participants and study design

We performed a retrospective cohort study including 98 early CRC or mCRC patients with colectomy history at initial presentation for curative or palliative purposes, respectively. Patients with a pathologically proven CRC diagnosis and older than 18 years of age were included if they were not lost to follow-up between 2009 and 2019 at Hacettepe University Cancer Institute (Ankara, Turkey). We collected demographic, clinical, and pathological data of the patients, including age, gender, tumor location, mutational status, metastatic regions, pathological variables [lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PNI), and TNM stage], and the dates of CRC diagnosis, progression, and death from the electronic record system of our center. All of the initially or metachronously mCRC patients were treated with a combination of systemic chemotherapy and biologic therapies, including bevacizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody blocking vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), cetuximab, and panitumumab, which are epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) directed monoclonal antibodies.

Immunohistochemistry for protein expression

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue blocks of the patients were retrieved from the archive of the Department of Pathology at Hacettepe University. Representative areas were selected, and tissue microarrays (TMA) with 3-mm-diameter tissue cores were constructed. For each tumor, we selected two spatially distinct regions that were morphologically representative and free of necrosis. These areas were carefully chosen by an experienced pathologist who was blinded to all clinical data to reflect both central and invasive tumor zones where possible. For a subset of 20 tumors, we conducted whole-slide immunohistochemical staining for CD47 and compared the findings to those obtained from the corresponding TMA cores to assess consistency.

TMA blocks were sliced into sections with a thickness of 4 μm. The tissue sections were deparaffinized in Bond Devax solution (Leica Microsystems) at 72°. Then they were stained with a primary anti-CD47 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), using Leica Bond Autostainer, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For the evaluation of CD47 expression, one pathologist evaluated the membrane staining of tumor cells in a blinded manner. The percentage of stained tumor cells was estimated and scored as 1: 1%−25%; 2: 26%−50% 3: 51%−75%; 4:>75%. The staining intensity was scored semiquantitatively using a four-tier system as 0, negative; 1+, weak; 2+, moderate; and 3+, strong. The staining index was then calculated as the staining intensity score multiplied by the staining area percentage. The calculated median IHC score for the whole cohort was 4. We grouped the patients into two categories based on the median expression score for 98 patients: negative expression (< median score) and positive expression (≥ median score).

Statistical analyses

Categorical and continuous variables were expressed as percentages and medians with interquartile range. The difference between categorical and continuous variables was determined using the Chi-square test and the Mann–Whitney U test, respectively. Overall survival (OS) was calculated as the time interval from the date of diagnosis to the date of death due to any cause. The Kaplan–Meier method was used for the estimation of OS of all the patients and prognostic subgroups, and the log-rank test was carried out for comparisons between survival distributions. The association of CD47 expression and other clinical and pathological data with OS was evaluated with univariate and multivariate Cox proportional-hazards analysis. Variables demonstrating a p-value of ≤ 0.20 in the univariate analysis were selected for inclusion in the multivariate model. For all the statistical analyses, SPSS, version 25.0 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA) was used, and a p-value of < 0.05 (a type-I error level of 5%) was considered for statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics

Baseline characteristics of the 98 patients stratified according to CD47 expression status were summarized in Table 1, and different expression levels for CD47 were shown in Fig. 1. Of all the patients, 43 (43.9%) were in the group with no CD47 expression, and 55 (56.1%) were in the group with CD47 expression. The population was 54.1% male and 45.9% female. The median age of patients was 60 (minimum-maximum: 33–77). The left side was the most common tumor location (76.5%). The majority of our patients had tumors with KRAS wild-type (57.1%), LVI (65.3%), and PNI (73.5%). Mucinous adenocarcinomas accounted for approximately 10% of the entire study cohort. Fifty-eight (59.2%) had distant metastasis at initial presentation. All the tumors had a microsatellite-stable (MSS) phenotype. Regarding CD47 expression status, patients with CD47 expression were more likely to have lymph node involvement compared to those without CD47 expression (p = 0.005). There was no significant difference in other clinic-pathological characteristics between CD47 prognostic subgroups.

Survival outcomes

The median OS of the whole cohort was 55.5 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 47.4–63.6). After a median follow-up of 92.2 months, 66 deaths were recorded. Median OS of patients with CD47 expression was lower than those with no CD47 expression (39.3 months vs. not reached, p = 0.001, respectively, Fig. 2A). Taking survival outcomes of patients stratified according to distant metastasis status into consideration, patients harboring CD47 expression had inferior OS times compared to patients with no CD47 expression in both initially non-metastatic or metastatic groups (52.6 months vs. not reached, p = 0.015, Figs. 2B and 37 months vs. 58.5 months, p = 0.05, Fig. 2C, respectively). As shown in Table 2, In the univariate analysis, the risk factors for worsened OS were moderate or poor tumor differentiation (hazard ratio [HR]: 2.376; 95% CI: 1.446–3.917; p = 0.001), PNI (HR: 2.049; 95% CI: 1.124–3.737; p = 0.019), presence of distant metastasis (HR: 2.330; 95% CI:1.373–3.956; p = 0.002) and CD47 expression (HR: 2.446; 95% CI:1.449–4.130; p = 0.001). However, as shown in Table 3, tumor differentiation status (HR: 2.622; 95% CI: 1.558–4.412; p < 0.001), tumor stage (HR: 1.817; 95% CI: 1.054–3.133; p = 0.032), and CD47 expression (HR: 2.142; 95% CI: 1.248–3.673; p = 0.006) were independent prognostic variables for OS, whereas LVI, PNI, and tumor location lost their statistical significance in the multivariate model.

Discussion

The combination of chemotherapy and biological agents, anti-VEGF or anti-EGFR, is the standard first-line treatment for mCRC. However, recent studies have highlighted the promising potential of immunotherapy options such as anti-programmed death (PD)-ligand 1 and anti-cytotoxic T lymphocyte antigen 4, particularly in tumors with high microsatellite instability (MSI-H) and mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) [12]. While these advances represent significant progress, there is an unmet need for additional markers to provide a better individualized treatment, contributing to improved survival outcomes in CRC. CD47-SIRPα interaction has been suggested as a promising target for cancer treatment in hematologic and solid tumors, including CRC. The present study investigated the association of CD47 expression with the clinicopathologic characteristics of CRC. The prognostic significance of CD47 expression in CRC patients was also explored, to contribute to the development of target-driven therapeutic strategies. We showed that the tumours with CD47 expression were more likely to have the presence of lymph node and distant metastasis and were associated with inferior OS compared to those with no CD47 expression.

CD47, a cell surface glycoprotein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily, was first identified in Rh-negative red blood cells as a missing antigen expressed by human chromosome 3 in 1987 [13]. The interaction between CD47 and thrombospondin 1, the first described target ligand of CD47, results in reduced inflammation, promotion of cell death, and inhibition of angiogenesis in various cell types [14, 15]. Additionally, the binding of CD47 to SIRPα, the second reported ligand of CD47, takes part in the regulation of innate immune tolerance by acting as an inhibitor of phagocytosis [16, 17]. Given this immunologic mechanism, its prognostic significance is thought to be closely associated with tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), particularly CD68 + and CD163 + subsets [18]. However, due to limitations in tissue availability, our study did not include direct assessment of macrophage density or polarization. Notably, CD47 may also contribute to tumor progression through macrophage-independent pathways, including enhanced cell migration, angiogenesis, and apoptosis resistance [19, 20].

CD47 is overexpressed in various cancers, indicating a poor survival outcome [21–24]. Oh et al. examined the prognostic value of CD47 expression and the association between CD47 expression and lymphangiogenesis, apoptosis, angiogenesis, and proliferation determined via a TUNEL assay in CRC. They reported that CD47 expression was found in 53.4% of CRC and was associated with CRC progression by inducing angiogenesis and apoptosis [10].

Tian et al. reported that high CD47 expression was observed in 23.3% of colon adenocarcinoma cases and was associated with more advanced tumor stage; notably, while elevated CD47 expression correlated with significantly better progression-free survival, no significant association was found with overall or disease-specific survival [25]. Another study by Kim et al. showed that a minority of patients with CRC (16.2%) expressed CD47, and there was no significant difference between CD47 expression and OS [7]. Our results revealed that CD47 was expressed in 56.1% of the patients with CRC, and its expression status was an independent prognostic factor in predicting OS. The discrepancies in CD47 expression rates and the prognostic value of CD47 for survival outcomes might result from important differences in study design and context. One primary source of variation is cohort composition. Differences in tumor stage, MSI status, and immune infiltration profiles may all influence the prognostic role of CD47. For example, our cohort was mainly composed of patients with distant metastasis (61.2%), whereas in the study by Kim et al., the percentage of patients with metastatic disease was only 7% [7]. Additionally, Tian et al. included a substantial proportion of MSI-H tumors [25], which are known to be immunologically active and responsive to checkpoint inhibition [26]. Higher CD47 expression may represent the tumor’s response to strong immune activity in these cases and could be linked to improved survival outcomes. Second, there is substantial heterogeneity in CD47 assessment methods. Different antibodies, scoring systems (e.g., H-score and binary classification), and cut-off values were used in the studies to define positivity. Some also differ on whether they consider membranous, cytoplasmic, or total expression, which makes cross-study comparisons difficult. Third, treatment exposure can significantly affect the prognostic role of CD47. In treatment-naive or immune-cold MSS tumors, CD47 expression more likely indicates immune escape by the tumor, contributing to poorer outcomes, while in tumors with MSI-H or dMMR, it might indicate an active immune microenvironment [27, 28], contributing to better response to immunotherapy and survival outcomes. Additionally, our patients in the metastatic state were treated with a combination of chemotherapy and a biologic agent. However, in the studies about the prognostic value of CD47 expression mentioned above, there was no information regarding treatment types in the metastatic setting.

Our cohort consisted solely of MSS tumors, which typically exhibited poor T-cell infiltration, low tumor mutational burden, and minimal response to immune checkpoint inhibitors [27]. However, in contrast to MSS tumors, MSI-H or dMMR tumors are characterized by increased neoantigen load, high immune activation, pronounced infiltration by T cells and macrophages [26]. Therefore, CD47 overexpression may play a more critical role in tumors with high levels of immune cell infiltration, where it serves as a significant mechanism of immune evasion by inhibiting macrophage-mediated phagocytosis and suppressing the activation of adaptive immune responses [27, 28]. Considering these data, it is reasonable to suggest that CD47 expression may function not only as a prognostic variable but also as a predictive biomarker for evaluating the response to therapies targeting innate immune checkpoints.

Several studies have shown that targeting of the CD47-SIRPα interaction could result in a remarkable anti-tumor response via multiple mechanisms, which include enhanced macrophage-induced phagocytosis, activated innate immune response mediated by the antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, promotion of an anti-tumor adaptive immune response, and induction of apoptosis [19, 28, 29]. Therefore, CD47 blockade gained significant interest in its usage for the treatment of a wide range of cancer types. For example, in a phase I setting, HX009, a dual blocker of PD-1 and CD47, showed a promising clinical benefit in heavily pretreated patients with metastatic solid tumors [30]. HuNb1-IgG4, an anti-CD47 nanobody fusion protein, had a potent activity in lymphoma and ovarian cancers in vitro [31].

We, for the first time, showed the association of CD47 with survival in de novo mCRC patients treated with the combination of chemotherapy and a biological agent. However, our study had some limitations. Firstly, the main ones are its retrospective nature and the patient composition, composed mostly of tumors with distant metastasis. Secondly, the relatively small cohort size may limit the our conclusions’ strength and broader applicability. Thirdly, the present study investigated the impact of CD47 expression on survival outcomes of CRC patients with MSS. Therefore, further research is warranted to elucidate the exact role of CD47 expression status in CRC patients with dMMR/MSI-H tumours. Additionally, we could not analyze CD47 expression in adjacent non-tumorous tissue because TMA was used. Although TMAs inherently carry the risk of undersampling, previous studies have shown good concordance between TMA and whole-section analyses for immune-related markers when representative cores are used [32, 33].

Conclusion

Our results demonstrated that CD47 expression status was an independent prognostic factor for OS of patients with CRC. More comprehensive studies are needed to elucidate the exact mechanisms underlying CD47-mediated cancer progression and to explore its clinical applications for improving CRC prognosis and treatment strategies.

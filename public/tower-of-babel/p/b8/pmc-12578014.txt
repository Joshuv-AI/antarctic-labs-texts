Malignant acanthosis nigricans and tripe palms: cutaneous indicators of gastric carcinoma resolution – a case report
Authors: Prince Barnawal, Anup Panthi, Gita Rana, Sujan Ghimire, Prabhat Nirala
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Malignant acanthosis nigricans (MAN) and tripe palms (TP) are rare dermatological conditions that serve as paraneoplastic indicators of internal malignancies, most notably gastric carcinoma. Importance of this reported case is that recognizing these cutaneous signs is crucial for early cancer detection.Presentation:We report a 47-year-old male with a six-month history of progressive hyperpigmentation and thickening of the skin in the neck, axillae, foot, palm, and groin, along with rugose, hyperkeratotic changes in both palms. Initial treatments with topical agents were ineffective. Physical examination revealed velvety, hyperpigmented plaques characteristic of MAN and thickened, rugose skin on the palms indicative of TP. Elevated CA19.9 levels (>1200.00 U/ml) and video esophagogastroduodenoscopy revealed ulcerative growth in the fundus and body of the stomach. CT scans showed circumferential asymmetric wall thickening and perigastric lymphadenopathy. The patient was diagnosed with gastric adenocarcinoma and underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by a D2 total gastrectomy with lymphadenectomy.Discussion:The combination of MAN and TP is strongly associated with underlying malignancies, particularly gastric carcinoma. The pathogenesis involves growth factor receptor pathways leading to skin proliferation. This case highlights the importance of dermatological signs as early indicators of internal malignancies. The resolution of MAN and TP post-surgery confirmed their paraneoplastic nature, underscoring the significance of early recognition and intervention.Conclusion:This case underscores the importance of identifying MAN and TP as critical dermatological clues for underlying malignancies. Early detection and multidisciplinary management can lead to timely surgical intervention, improving both dermatological symptoms and overall prognosis. Increased awareness of these paraneoplastic conditions can significantly aid in the early diagnosis of life-threatening cancers such as gastric carcinoma.

Introduction

Malignant acanthosis nigricans (MAN), characterized by the presence of hyperpigmented, hyperkeratosis, and cutaneous thickening of the skin or mucous membranes, is recognized as a cutaneous sign of internal malignancy[1]. The disease mostly occurs in the elderly without gender differences, and gastric carcinoma is the most common with the incidence of 55–61% in patients with MAN, followed by pancreatic cancer, gynecological malignancies, and lung carcinoma[2]. Tripe palms (TP) is a very rare skin disorder characterized by velvety white, rugose thickening of the palm with pronounced folds in the lines of the hands[3]. These dermatoses often precede the diagnosis of malignancy, emphasizing their value as diagnostic clues.

This case report describes a rare case of a patient who presented with combination of malignant acanthosis nigricans and tripe palms associated with advanced case of gastric adenocarcinoma located in an atypical site within the stomach (i.e. in fundus and the body) without gastrointestinal symptoms and with regression post gastrectomy. The prompt recognition of these dermatological signs allowed for timely surgical intervention, with significant improvement in skin manifestations post-surgery. This case report was submitted in accordance with the TITAN guideline 2025[4].

Case presentation

A 47-year-old male presented to the hospital with a 6 months history of progressive darkening and thickening of the skin on his neck, axillae, foot, palm and groin. He also reported rugose, hyperkeratotic changes on both palms. The patient had no significant past medical comorbidities, was not on long-term medications, and denied any history of endocrine disorders such as diabetes mellitus or thyroid disease. He was non-smoker, did not consume alcohol, and denied use of any abusive substances. There was no family history of malignancy, metabolic disorders, or dermatological diseases.

The skin lesions were initially treated as a benign pigmented disorder with topical agents and emollients without significant improvement.

On physical examination, bilaterally symmetrical, velvety, hyperpigmented, thickened plaques with papillomatous surfaces, typically involving flexures such as the neck and axillae as shown in Fig. 1a. The patient’s palms displayed bilateral thickening, with velvety and rugose skin changes that strongly resembled tripe palms as shown in Fig. 1b. Both are usually asymptomatic, progresses quickly over few month with lesion typically starting in flexures (neck and axillae) followed by more widespread skin distribution. Serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) was 3.05 ng/mL (normal range <3), and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) was markedly elevated at >1200.00 U/mL (normal range <37). While an isolated rise in either marker can occur in benign gastrointestinal conditions, their combined elevation, particularly with markedly raised CA19-9, is strongly suggestive of gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma. Moreover, evidence suggests that the combined detection of tumor markers such as neuron-specific enolase (NSE), CEA, CA19-9, and CA242 provides significantly greater diagnostic accuracy for gastric adenocarcinoma than any single marker alone, aiding both in early detection and in monitoring treatment response[5]. Video esophagogastroduodenoscopy was performed which reveals ulcerative growth involving Fundus and Body as shown in Fig. 2a. The microscopic description reveals multiple fragments of gastric mucosal tissue, some of which are infiltrated by solid sheets and fused glands of tumor cells. These cells are moderately pleomorphic with oval hyperchromatic nuclei, and prominent nucleoli are seen in some cells. Further imaging (CT and PET scans) was done to assess tumor burden and metastasis. The patient was diagnosed with malignant acanthosis nigricans and tripe palms secondary to gastric adenocarcinoma. Based on contrast-enhanced CT tumor invading the subserosal layer with multiple regional perigastric and mesenteric lymph node metastases, without evidence of distant spread. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy using the FLOT regimen – 5-fluorouracil, leucovorin, oxaliplatin, and docetaxel administered every 2 weeks for four cycles after the diagnosis to downstage the tumor and make it operable. Following this, the patient underwent a D2 total gastrectomy, which involved a total gastrectomy along with D2 lymphadenectomy. This extensive lymph node dissection helps reduce the risk of recurrence. Postoperatively, the patient showed marked improvement in cutaneous findings. Hyperpigmented plaques and tripe palms regressed markedly within 2 months and temporally coincided with tumor resection. Even though follow-up photographs documenting the regression were not available for inclusion, clinical examination confirmed this improvement. The temporal correlation between tumor removal and MAN/TP lesion regression is descriptive of the paraneoplastic nature of these cutaneous manifestations and their potential utility as clinical markers of tumor activity.

Discussion

In the present case, the patient had Malignant Acanthosis Nigricans (MAN) and tripe palms (TP) associated with Adenocarcinoma of Stomach. The cause of MAN and TP are not clearly understood, but is thought due to substances from the associated cancer that stimulates the palmar skin cells to proliferate. The pathogenesis of malignant AN remains unclear. MAN was detected with enhanced expression of fibroblast growth factor receptor[6]. Furthermore the activation of transforming growth factor-alpha (TGF-α) stimulates keratinocyte proliferation via an endocrine route. Increased TGF-α production has been observed in various malignancies, correlating positively with tumor stage, and is a key mediator of keratinocyte hyperproliferation in both benign and malignant contexts. TGF-α binds to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), activating the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK/ERK) pathway, which regulates cellular proliferation, differentiation, and migration. Sustained ERK activity promotes keratinocyte proliferation, whereas ERK inhibition induces differentiation, suggesting that dysregulation of this pathway contributes to the epidermal hyperplasia seen in MAN[7].

AN is associated with underlying internal malignancy either alone or in combination with TP. Evidence from published literatures has shown that in 77% of cases TP is associated with AN, whereas in 23% of cases it occurs alone[8]. The most common associated neoplasms are pulmonary and gastric carcinoma followed by esophagus, rectum, bronchus, urinary tract, ovary, bile duct, thyroid, uterus, liver, kidneys, and breast[9].

In this case, the patient’s gastric adenocarcinoma was located in the fundus and body of the stomach, areas that are not typically associated with early, noticeable symptoms. Unlike cancers in the stomach’s lesser curvature or back walls, which often cause stomach pain or nausea and lead to quicker diagnoses, this tumor grew silently. Without the usual gastrointestinal symptoms, the only noticeable signs were the changes in the skin, making these dermatological manifestations crucial as early warning signals.

Interestingly, after the patient underwent gastrectomy, the MAN and TP resolved completely, confirming their paraneoplastic nature. This highlights how these skin changes are not just symptoms but reversible markers tied directly to the underlying cancer. Recognizing these signs early can make a significant difference in diagnosing cancer sooner and improving patient outcomes.

Literature indicates that MAN and TP are often associated with advanced stages of malignancy. For instance, Wang et al reported that MAN and TP, particularly when accompanied by other paraneoplastic signs, are frequently linked to advanced gastric cancer. The authors emphasized that the presence of these dermatologic manifestations may indicate a higher tumor burden and highlighted the need for vigilant follow-up, as recurrence of MAN and TP after initial regression could signal tumor recurrence. This reinforces the importance of monitoring these cutaneous signs, both for assessing treatment response and for early detection of potential recurrence[10].

As this is a report of a single individual, the rarity of clinical manifestations such as cutaneous involvement particularly features like tripe palm may not be universally present in the early stages of gastric adenocarcinoma. This can contribute to a delay in diagnosis. Furthermore, a limited clinical understanding and recognition of such rare dermatological signs may hinder their utility in guiding early diagnostic considerations. However, this report adds to our understanding of how important skin manifestations can be in uncovering internal malignancies. Such cases emphasize the importance of teamwork among dermatologists, gastroenterologists, and oncologists. By working together, they can quickly identify and address unusual skin changes, ensuring a thorough evaluation and timely treatment for the patient. Ultimately, this case shows why doctors need to stay alert to conditions like MAN and TP.

Limitations This case report has a number of limitations. Firstly, histopathologic confirmation of the skin lesions was not performed, which would have provided additional diagnostic confirmation. Secondly, follow-up was restricted to 2 months post-surgery, restricting evaluation of long-term dermatological outcomes and disease recurrence. Thirdly, as it is a single-case report, the findings have limited generalizability and should be interpreted cautiously when applying to large patient populations.

Conclusion

This case illustrates the importance of recognizing Malignant Acanthosis Nigricans and tripe palms as early dermatological clues to underlying malignancies. Prompt evaluation and management, including surgical resection of the primary tumor, can lead to significant improvement in dermatological manifestations. Increased awareness of these paraneoplastic skin manifestations can aid in the early detection of life-threatening conditions such as gastric carcinoma.

ABSTRACT
This study presents the DT iterative approach to approximate the fixed point of the generalized \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document} nonexpansive mapping. Our approach was based on an iterative scheme in the context of uniformly convex Banach spaces. We established a weak convergence theorem by employing the Opial property of the underlying space. We present strong convergence and stability results for a new iterative approach to find the fixed point. We offer numerical and graphical results to support the effectiveness of our modified iteration scheme. We obtained an application of our result to solve split feasibility problems (SFP).

Introduction

Fixed point theory is a fundamental branch of mathematics with applications in many fields. It has also been applied to the study and design of control systems in science and technology1–3. In addition, continuous mappings and the properties of various operators in topology, geometry, and functional analysis apply to fixed-point theory4.

Fixed point theory is widely used in many different fields because it can solve complex problems. The application of fixed point theory may be studied in a wide range of theoretical and applied domains, such as linear and nonlinear analysis, modeling, mathematics, economics, differential and integral formulations, and more. Both practical and mathematical problems can be solved with the help of fixed points5. Browder and Göhde extended their knowledge to uniformly convex Banach spaces1,3.

Goebel and Kirk extended Browder’s results in a uniform Convex Banach space1,6. Insightful generalizations and extensions have been made to the class of nonexpansive mappings over the last 20 years. Suzuki introduced a crucial generalization known as Suzuki generalized nonexpansive mappings, often known as mappings satisfying condition (C)7. Pant and Pandey8 introduced Reich-Suzuki nonexpansive mappings, making a significant addition to the field. Their research found that this type of mapping has a greater scope than condition (C).

Pandy et al.8 describe a novel kind of mapping. This novel method offers to broaden the scope of nonexpansive mapping theory by providing new insights and possible uses in several mathematical fields.

The contraction principle, also known as the Banach contraction theorem, or BCP, is a fundamental discovery in metric space theory. This theorem, which Banach developed in 19229, applies to contraction mappings in a complete metric space and is commonly used with the Picard iteration approach. The BCP ensures the existence of a fixed point for each contraction map10–13. Consider the sequences \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma _{k},\tau _{k}$$\end{document}, where k represents a positive integer. In the present, many authors such as H. Aftab et al, Ishtiaq et al, F. Gusay, Mann, and Thakur presented iterative approaches to find fixed points,11,13–18. Iterative techniques are useful for approximating fixed points and have applications in many fields, including mathematics and science.

Ali et al.15 show that the D iterative method converges faster than the iterations in the literature. Remarks

Our results show the new iterative scheme converges identifiably faster than the existing methods. By proving a faster convergence rate and demonstrating it numerically, we confirm that it requires far fewer iterations than the Thakur or Mann approaches to achieve the same precision, advancing the practical use of fixed-point computation.

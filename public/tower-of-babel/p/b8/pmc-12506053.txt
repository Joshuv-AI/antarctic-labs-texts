Upfront surgery versus induction chemotherapy followed by surgery in oral cavity squamous cell cancers with advanced nodal disease (SurVIC Trial): a phase 3 multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Dharma Ram Poonia, Amit Sehrawat, Jeewan Ram Vishnoi, Nivedita Sharma, Parmod Kumar, Bharti Devnani, Aparna Warriere, Akanksha Solanki, Puneet Pareek, Divya Aggarwal, Taruna Yadav, Prem Prakash Sharma, Ashita Gadwal, Anushka Goyal, Poonam Elhence, Pushpinder Khera, Ashish Jakhetiya, Pnahindra Swaim, Dillip Muduly, Rohit Mahajan, Pankaj Garg, Vijay Kumar, Madhabananda Kar, Sanjeev Misra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMost oral cancers in India present in advanced stages and tend to have poor oncological outcomes. Chemotherapy has been associated with improved oncological outcomes in various cancers, but its role in oral cancer is still not well-defined in curative settings beyond radiosensitisation. Despite an excellent response rate, neoadjuvant chemotherapy trials have failed to show an oncological advantage. Earlier studies were limited by their heterogeneous patient population, including all head and neck subsites, and included both inoperable cancer and early-stage operable cases. Due to such patient selection, the intended results were never met. Patients with biologically aggressive diseases (advanced nodal disease) may derive greater benefit from induction chemotherapy (ICT). Therefore, we aim to determine the oncological advantage of adding ICT to oral squamous cell cancer with advanced nodal disease (N2–N3).Methods and analysisThe study is an open-label, multicentre, randomised controlled trial, with an allocation ratio of 1:1, being conducted at seven leading cancer centres in India. The primary objective is to compare survival outcomes with and without ICT before surgery in patients with oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and advanced nodal disease, specifically focusing on 2-year disease-free survival (DFS). Secondary objectives include assessing overall survival (OS), clinical and pathological response rates, treatment compliance, treatment completion rates, adverse events, treatment-related toxicity (using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, V.5.0), quality of life (measured with Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-General and Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-Head and Neck) and postoperative complications (using the modified Clavien-Dindo classification).The study population consists of patients with operable OSCC and advanced nodal disease (N2–N3), adequate organ function, aged 18–65 years and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0–2. The treatment arms are the standard arm Surgery arm (SURG), which involves surgery followed by adjuvant radiotherapy with or without concurrent chemotherapy, and the experimental arm (ICT), in which patients will receive two cycles of ICT using either cisplatin, docetaxel and 5-fluorouracil or cisplatin, docetaxel and capecitabine, followed by surgery and adjuvant radiotherapy with or without concurrent chemotherapy. The sample size was calculated to detect an HR of 0.67 with 80% power. A total of 184 events are required, and with an accrual rate of 15 patients per month, 300 patients will be recruited. DFS analysis will occur 32 months after the trial begins, and follow-up will continue for 5 years. OS analysis will be conducted when 184 deaths are observed. Taking 10% of the withdrawal of consent, a total of 346 patients need to be included.Ethics and disseminationThis trial aims to establish the potential superiority of ICT or definitively determine its futility in OSCC with advanced nodal disease. A positive outcome could provide practice-changing data, particularly for Indian patients, whereas negative results could halt the use of ICT in this setting, directing research efforts towards more effective treatment strategies.Trial registration numberCTRI/2024/03/064586; NCT06737822; Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) number: AIIMS/IEC/2023/4622 (lead site).

Introduction

Oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma is one of the leading cancers in India, with its incidence steadily rising. Over 60% of patients present with advanced-stage disease, reflecting a significant public health challenge.1 2 Treatment options for these patients typically include surgery or concurrent chemo-radiotherapy (CTRT), depending on disease status. For locally advanced oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), the current standard of care involves surgery followed by adjuvant therapy. However, long-term oncological outcomes remain unsatisfactory, with survival rates showing minimal improvement over the past two decades despite advancements in care. Induction chemotherapy (ICT) followed by response-based local therapy presents a promising research avenue. The rationale includes biological selection, addressing micrometastasis, enhancing resectability or enabling organ preservation. ICT has demonstrated potential in various oncological contexts and is an established standard in concurrent settings for head and neck cancers. However, its role in the induction setting remains underexplored, with limited success due to challenges, such as disease and patient heterogeneity, improper selection criteria, inconsistent, substandard chemotherapy regimens and inappropriate control arms.3 4

Conclusive evidence evaluating ICT before surgery in operable OSCC is scarce. Licitra et al conducted a randomised controlled trial using cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil, which showed no survival benefit5; however, the study faced poor accrual and included few cases with advanced nodal disease. Conversely, Zhong et al reported survival benefits with TPF (cisplatin, docetaxel and 5-fluorouracil) chemotherapy in a subset of N2 disease cases during exploratory analysis.6 Similarly, a Tata Memorial Hospital trial investigated mandibular preservation rather than survival outcomes in operable OSCC.7 The proposed phase 3 randomised controlled trial aims to address this gap by evaluating the efficacy of ICT in operable OSCC with advanced nodal disease (N2–N3).

Methods and analysis

Study design

It is an open-label, multicentre, superiority design randomised controlled trial with an allocation ratio of 1:1. The trial commenced in February 2024 and is expected to conclude in February 2027.

Study sites

The trial was developed and designed at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Jodhpur (AIIMS-Jodhpur) and the All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Rishikesh. Funding for the trial has been awarded to AIIMS-Jodhpur (JDH) as the lead centre (ICMR 5587/2023/JJC). A total of seven sites across the country will enrol the desired number of participants after getting ethics clearance and a memorandum of understanding with the lead site.

Study participants

All biopsy-proven or cytology-proven cases of OSCC presenting to oncology outpatient departments will be screened for inclusion after obtaining informed consent (participant consent form, provided as online supplemental material). Eligibility will be assessed by any two specialists from a team comprising a surgeon, medical oncologist and radiation oncologist (box 1 shows the eligibility criteria). Participants meeting the criteria will undergo clinical, dental and nutritional evaluations; contrast-enhanced CT (CECT) or MRI of the face and neck; cardiovascular assessments (ECG and 2D echocardiography) and routine haematology and biochemistry profiling. Chest imaging for metastasis, including X-ray or CT, will be at the treating physician’s discretion.

Nodal staging to establish N2–N3 status will follow a structured, step-by-step approach. First, cross-sectional imaging using CECT, MRI or PET will assess cervical nodes for radiological criteria, including size thresholds, the presence of a fatty hilum, cortical thickness, shape and their relation to surrounding structures. If findings are equivocal, the next step will involve high-resolution ultrasonography (USG) of the neck to evaluate additional parameters, such as vascularity, echogenicity and internal architecture of the nodes. Persistent uncertainty regarding nodal status will be resolved with ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration cytology to achieve pathological confirmation. To ensure uniformity and reduce inter-observer variability, a single radiologist will evaluate and report nodal status for all patients at each institution. Once participants are deemed eligible, they will then be randomised into one of the treatment arms: SURG (standard, upfront surgery) arm or ICT arm. Interventions

SURG arm

The SURG arm is the standard treatment arm, where participants will undergo upfront surgery followed by adjuvant radio-chemotherapy. The surgical procedure will consist of a wide local excision with 1 cm of grossly normal tissue around the tumour, including any involved bone, with appropriate reconstruction. Unilateral or bilateral comprehensive neck dissection (Levels I–V) will be performed based on the clinical indications as per the operating surgeon’s assessment. Adequacy of margins will be assessed using either frozen section or intraoperative gross examination at the surgeon’s discretion, while final margin status, based on the paraffin block, will guide adjuvant treatment decisions. Surgical specimens will be analysed by the onco-pathologist at each participating institute and the final histopathological examination (HPE) will follow the College of American Pathologists (CAP) Protocol for the Examination of Specimens from Patients with Cancers of the Oral Cavity (V.4.2.0.0, June 2023).8

It is mandated to initiate the adjuvant radiotherapy within 8 weeks of surgery. Adjuvant treatment will be determined according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines. If HPE shows positive margins or extranodal extension, patients will receive concurrent cisplatin-based CTRT. For patients with any single adverse factor (pT3, pT4, close margin, perineural invasion, lymphovascular invasion, more than one positive node or a positive node at level 4 or 5), radiation therapy (RT) will be administered alone. RT will begin 5–8 weeks postsurgery and will be delivered using Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT) with Simultaneous Integrated Boost (SIB)/Sequential Boost (SEB) or Three-Dimensional Conformal Radiation Therapy (3D CRT) techniques. CT imaging will be used for RT planning and dose dosimetry. The radiation dose will be 66 Gy (2 Gy/fraction) to high-risk areas and 50 Gy (2 Gy/fraction) to low-intermediate-risk areas, administered from Monday to Friday over 6–7 weeks. Cisplatin (100 mg/m² every 3 weeks or 30 mg/m² weekly) will be used as concurrent chemotherapy, depending on the indication. Concurrent cetuximab, carboplatin or nimotuzumab is also allowed in patients ineligible for cisplatin.

ICT arm

In the experimental ICT arm of the SurVIC Trial, participants will receive two cycles of triple-drug ICT (modified TAX 323 regimen—TPF or TPX (cisplatin, docetaxel and capecitabine)) administered at 3-week intervals prior to surgery and adjuvant radio-chemotherapy (details are shown in table 1). Chemotherapy dose modifications will be made by the treating physicians based on toxicity grades, with reductions for grade 4 toxicities. Grade 3 or higher neurotoxicity or ototoxicity will preclude further ICT or CTRT, and such participants will be considered for early surgery or radical radiotherapy. Chemotherapy will only commence if participants meet predefined eligibility criteria, including no grade ≥2 haematotoxicity, no non-haematological toxicities beyond grade 1, bilirubin≤2.0×Upper Limit of Normal (ULN), transaminases≤5×ULN, no cardiac toxicity and an ECOG PS of 0 or 1. Chemotherapy delays of up to 2 weeks or until bone marrow recovery will be permitted, but delays beyond 2 weeks will result in discontinuation from the ICT arm, with such participants also considered for early surgical intervention or CTRT following recovery.

Participants will undergo response assessment 3–4 weeks after completing the second cycle of ICT, both clinically and radiologically. Clinical response will be assessed using the criteria defined by Chaukar et al,7 while radiological response will be evaluated using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v.1.1 (see table 2).9 Patients who remain operable, regardless of clinical or radiological response category (partial response (PR)/complete response (CR)/stable disease (SD)/progressive disease (PD)), will proceed to surgical resection. For patients with PD who progress to become metastatic, palliative chemotherapy or appropriate palliative treatment will be offered. Surgery is mandated to be performed within 4 weeks of chemotherapy. Participants who do not provide consent for surgery or are deemed inoperable after ICT will be offered definitive CTRT or palliative radiotherapy, and the cause of inoperability will be documented. As both intent-to-treat and per-protocol analyses are planned, all these patients will remain part of the study. The extent of surgery and pathological assessment will follow the standard procedures of the SURG arm.

For postICT cases, there are no validated CAP or other guidelines for reporting; therefore, after an extensive literature review and consensus among participating pathologists, the study will adopt the five-tier response grading system proposed by Mandard et al (details in the next section).10 Adjuvant treatment for the ICT arm will be based on the pretreatment stage and HPE reports. In cases of complete pathological response, at least RT will be given. Positive margins and extranodal extension will indicate the need for concurrent chemoradiotherapy.

Follow-up post-treatment completion will be consistent across both arms. After treatment completion, participants will be regularly followed up at specified intervals: 3, 6, 12, 18 and 24 months, with yearly follow-ups thereafter for a total duration of 5 years. Each visit will include clinical assessments, recurrence profile evaluation, toxicity monitoring and quality of life (QoL) assessments. Two cross-sectional imaging assessments are planned at the 12-month and 3-year follow-ups, unless additional scans are required based on clinical judgement.

Allocation of intervention

A non-stratified computer-generated random number sequence has been created using sealedenvelope.com with a block size of 4. The sequence was then exported to Excel, where each random number was assigned a unique consecutive code. These codes were stored in sealed envelopes (Sequentially Numbered, Opaque, Sealed Envelopes (SNOSE)) to ensure allocation concealment. These envelopes will be used sequentially for treatment allocation. The SNOSE will be kept in a secure, restricted area, with access limited to the designated randomisation nurse. Centralised randomisation will be conducted at the lead site to maintain consistency and avoid any biases in allocation. On official request for randomisation from participating centres, submitted by the project technical support or the investigator, and after verifying consent and eligibility, the randomisation nurse will open the sealed envelope. The nurse will then communicate the treatment allocation to the participating site. Given the nature of the treatment protocols, it is impractical to blind either the participants or the treating clinicians. However, to minimise potential bias, the data assessors responsible for evaluating survival outcomes will remain blind to the treatment allocation and will not have access to any treatment-related information.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the study is disease-free survival (DFS), which is defined as the time from randomisation to the date of recurrence (either clinical or pathological evidence) or censoring due to events, such as loss to follow-up, second primary cancer, metastasis, relapse or death from any cause. Overall survival (OS) will be measured as the time from randomisation to the date of death from any cause or the last observation. The pattern of failure and subsequent treatment will also be recorded. Safety of the treatment will be assessed by recording treatment completion rates, compliance and adverse events. Chemotherapy-related and acute radiotherapy-related adverse events will be evaluated using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE; V.5.0) throughout the treatment period, while delayed radiotherapy-related effects will be recorded using Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) criteria.11 12 Surgical details, early postoperative outcomes and assessment of postoperative complications using the Clavien-Dindo classification and CTCAE will be reported.13 We will assess QoL using patient-reported outcome questionnaires. The FACT (Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy) Scales, including the FACT-General (FACT-G) and the FACT-Head and Neck (FACT HN) version, will be used to measure QoL. We will assess fatigue using the Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy (FACIT) Fatigue Scale, a validated tool for patients with cancer. The FACT-HN version includes 39 questions across five domains, covering specific aspects of head and neck cancer. Participants will complete the physical forms at various time points: baseline (before ICT), before surgery and 3, 6, 12, 18 and 24 months post-treatment. We will assess financial toxicity using the FACIT COST Scale.14 Additionally, OSCC tumour and non-tumour tissues will be collected and stored for planned translational research (details of which are beyond the scope of this study).

The impact of ICT will be assessed in terms of ICT tolerance, treatment completion rate, treatment compliance, clinical-radiological-pathological response rates and chemotherapy-related toxicities. Since a well-established clinical response assessment measure is lacking, we will adopt the criteria used by Chaukar et al in their recent trial.7 RECIST V.1.1 will assess radiological response using CT/MRI (the response assessment criteria are summarised in table 2).9 Responses will be categorised as PR, CR, SD or PD. We will analyse the concordance between radiological and pathological responses, along with their impact on survival outcomes, as a secondary objective. We will assess pathological response using the five-tier response grading system proposed by Mandard et al and later validated by Hermann et al and Brucher et al10 15 16 This system evaluates responses based on the percentage of viable residual tumour cells and associated regressive changes, such as fibrosis, inflammation and necrosis. Data on residual viable tumour cells and regressive changes will be recorded in increments of 5%. We will guide data analysis based on the Mandard et al stratification, as follows10:

In addition to the established pathological prognostic markers, such as grade, perineural invasion, lymphovascular invasion and the worst pattern of invasion, we will assess tumour host response using tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in the stromal compartment. We will follow the standardised methodologies outlined by the International TILs Working Group in Breast Cancer (Salgado et al.)17 18 and the International Immuno-oncology Biomarkers Working Group (Hendry et al.).19 We will define stromal TILs as the percentage of the stromal area occupied by infiltrating lymphocytes, including only mononuclear immune cells and excluding polymorphonuclear leukocytes and areas of necrosis. TILs will be recorded as continuous percentages (eg, 5%, 10%, 20%, etc.).

Sample size

The primary endpoint is DFS. For the sample size calculation, we used time-to-event as the outcome (DFS) and HR as the effect measure. Limited literature exists on this topic, with only two trials including an N2-N3 subset analysis and a meta-analysis specifically examining the HR in this setting. Reported HRs for DFS in favour of ICT range from 0.55 to 0.71.5720 Based on our team’s experience and consensus, we used a conservative estimate of the HR (0.67) for benefit. To achieve an 80% power to detect an HR of 0.67, using a two-sided significance level, 184 events are required. Assuming an accrual rate of 15 patients per month, a total of 300 patients need to be recruited. DFS analysis will occur 32 months after the trial begins, with patient follow-up continuing for 5 years. The analysis of OS will be conducted when 184 deaths are observed. Accounting for a 10% early dropout rate, the final sample size is set at 346, with 173 participants in each arm.

Data collection

To ensure consistency and reliability in data collection across centres, all assessors will undergo standardised training on the use of the Case Record Form (CRF) and the REDCap.21 The CRF will collect key data on demographics, clinical information, treatment details and follow-up data. Data entered in the CRF will be prospectively recorded and submitted to the centralised REDCap database for monitoring and analysis. Participants will be monitored according to a structured follow-up schedule: every 3 months during the first year, every 6 months during the second year and annually thereafter till 5 years or death. These visits will assess late toxicity, QoL and possible recurrence. If a participant presents with symptoms between scheduled visits, they will be evaluated for recurrence and late toxicities. Any unplanned hospitalisations will be investigated, and relevant clinical events will be documented. All adverse events will be tracked and assessed using appropriate grading systems. In the event of disease recurrence, the recurrence will be recorded as an ‘event’ for DFS analysis; however, the patient will remain in the study until death or the last follow-up visit, whichever occurs first.

Data analysis plan and statistical methods

Categorical data will be presented as frequencies and percentages, with significance assessed using χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact tests, depending on the distribution of the data. Continuous data will be summarised using means±SD for normally distributed data or medians with IQR for non-normally distributed data. Between-group comparisons will be performed using independent t-tests for normally distributed data or Mann-Whitney U tests for non-normally distributed data, while within-group changes over time will be analysed using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as appropriate. For survival analysis, both DFS and OS will be analysed using Cox proportional hazards regression models, generating HR with 95% CI. Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be plotted, and differences between treatment arms will be assessed using the log-rank test. Treatment compliance and toxicity, including treatment completion and interruption rates and chemotherapy-related toxicities, will be compared using χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Response assessments, including clinical, radiological and pathological responses, will be compared between groups using χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests, with radiological and clinical agreement evaluated by Cohen’s Kappa statistic. Concordance between radiological and pathological responses will be assessed using Cohen’s Kappa and McNemar tests. QoL scores will be analysed at baseline, during treatment and at follow-up intervals, using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for within-group analysis and independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for between-group comparisons. Generalised linear models will be used to analyse both continuous and categorical outcomes, with appropriate regression models applied depending on the type of data. Data analysis will be conducted using SPSS (latest version), with sensitivity analyses to test the robustness of survival outcomes. Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation techniques, and a p value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant for all tests.

Participant’s safety

Participants may discontinue or modify their involvement due to intolerable side effects, withdrawal of consent or the investigator’s determination that continuation may harm them, with all decisions documented. Trial stopping rules include unexpected treatment-related grade 5 toxicities exceeding 10% or disease progression, making surgery unfeasible in over 10% of participants. The Independent Data Monitoring Committee will review safety at 10% of enrolment and at 6-month intervals, thereafter, offering unbiased oversight. Adverse Events (AEs) and Severe Adverse Events (SAEs) will be monitored at each study visit through observations, reports and lab reviews. All AEs will be documented from the Informed Consent Form signing until 30 days post-trial, with pre-existing conditions noted but not classified as AEs unless worsened. AEs and SAEs will be classified using the NCI CTCAE V.5.0. SAEs will be reported to the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) within 24 hours, while non-SAE and AEs will be monitored until treatment concludes.

Ethical aspects and dissemination

This trial uses a chemotherapy protocol proven safe for OSCC. The study protocol has been reviewed by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) and IEC at each centre to ensure compliance with ethical standards and Indian regulations. Enrolment will begin only after obtaining IRB/IEC approval, and participants will provide written consent, as per Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) Ethical Guidelines. Participant insurance coverage is arranged, and ethical oversight will be maintained with strict data confidentiality, in line with India’s Data Privacy and Protection Laws. Adverse events will be monitored and reported according to the ICMR and Good Clinical Practice guidelines to ensure safety and trial integrity. The trial’s findings will be disseminated through presentations at leading scientific conferences and publications in high-impact oncology journals to ensure broad visibility within the oncology community. In line with ethical guidelines, raw data will be accessible to the scientific community, promoting transparency and collaborative research.

The SurVIC Trial has been approved by the IECs of all participating centres. The lead site approval was obtained from AIIMS-Jodhpur IEC (approval no.: AIIMS/IEC/2023/21). Additional approvals were secured from collaborating sites prior to trial initiation: Geetanjali Medical College & Hospital (GMCH) Udaipur IEC (approval no.: GMCH/IEC/2023/57), Shri Mahant Indresh Hospital (SMIH) Udaipur IEC (approval no.: SMIH/IEC/2023/11), AIIMS Rishikesh IEC (approval no.: 47/IEC/EM/2024), King George’s Medical University Lucknow IEC (approval no.: 328 Ethics/2025; Ref. code 136th ECM IIAP) and AIIMS Bhubaneswar IEC (approval no.: AIIMS/IEC/2023/74). All participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrolment.

The trial is registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI/2023/05/051234) and ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06737822).

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research.

Bioactivity Classification of 2,649 Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFASs) via Quantitative Structure–Activity Relationships and Molecular Docking to Health-Relevant Proteins
Authors: Wenting Li, Heather N. Bischel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The structural diversity of poly- and perfluoroalkyl substances (PFASs) poses challenges for class-based chemical hazard assessments. Computational simulations of PFAS-protein binding provide a means to improve screening-level evaluations by considering molecular interactions with toxicologically relevant proteins. Many PFASs are known to interact with carrier and channel proteins that influence bioaccumulation and toxicity, yet experimental data remain limited. To address this gap, we developed multicondition quantitative structure–activity relationship (QSAR) models that incorporate PFAS-protein associations. We simulated interactions of 4,397 PFASs with six health-relevant proteins, generating molecular docking binding scores for 12 ligand-binding sites. We then constructed seven bioactivity prediction models, integrating 44 additional chemical descriptors for each target. Training used binary bioactivity classifications for 560 PFASs derived from public toxicology and bioactivity databases. Using the best performing model (random forest), we classified the bioactivity potential of 2,649 PFASs within the applicability domain. Strong binding of PFASs to human serum albumin (fatty acid binding sites 3 and 4) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ, along with molecular complexity and the number of saturated hetero rings, were associated with PFAS bioactivity. A structure–activity landscape index analysis further suggested that nonfluorinated moietiesincluding bromine substitution, benzimidazole, and dinitroaniline groupscontribute to PFAS bioactivity.

Introduction

Poly- and perfluoroalkyl substances (PFASs) are ubiquitous environmental contaminants sourced from a broad range of commercial and industrial products. Exposure to PFASs is linked to many adverse health effects, including elevated cholesterol, thyroid disease, and immune dyregulation. However, toxicity and bioactivity studies primarily focus on only a small subset of PFAS, such as perfluoroalkyl acids (PFAAs), despite the structural diversity across subclasses. Additionally, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) expanded the PFAS definition in 2021 to require only one fully fluorinated methyl or methylene group, eliminating the aliphatic requirement delineated by Buck et al. (2011). The updated definition increased the estimated number of PFASs from ∼3,000 to over six million. Understanding the potential toxicity and bioactivity of diverse PFASs is critical toward appropriate and coordinated chemicals management. However, experimental hazard assessments for PFASs are resource-intensive and cost-constrained. Moreover, analytical methods remain unavailable for most PFASs.

Computational approaches, such as machine-learning-based quantitative structure–activity relationships (ML-QSAR), offer robust and high-throughput alternatives to uncovering structural-toxicity relationships. Regulatory bodies (e.g., OECD, REACH, and US EPA) have increasingly adopted QSAR to assess ecotoxicity and health hazards of pesticides. Cheng and Ng (2019) developed the first ML-QSAR model for PFAS bioactivity classification. They used a training set of 26 bioassay results for 1,012 PFAS that contain −C n F2n – (n ≥ 3) or −C n F2n OC m F2m – (n and m ≥ 1) augmented with 159 bioassays of 62,043 molecules containing −CF–. Five different ML models were used to classify 23 PFAS bioactivity properties for 3,486 PFASs, identifying a hidden layer multitask neural network as the best-performing model. Subsequent semisupervised ML-based QSAR models have enabled inference of functional group contributions associated with specific biotargets. , For instance, Kwon et al. (2022) predicted PFAS bioactivities across diverse human targets (enzymes, genes, proteins, and cell lines). Post hoc molecular docking simulations confirmed PFAS-protein interactions as key mechanistic drivers. With molecular docking tools that are widely accessible, ML-based QSAR bioactivity models for PFAS could be mechanistically strengthened by experimentally measured or computationally predicted PFAS-protein interactions.

Adverse health outcomes induced by PFAS, such as liver toxicity, lipid and insulin dysregulation, and cancer, are linked to interactions with receptor and carrier proteins. Strong binding to nuclear receptors, especially peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), was identified as a potential pathway contributing to PFAS-induced hepatotoxicity. − Recognizing the importance of PFAS-protein interactions, Lai et al. (2022) used molecular simulations of PFAS interactions with PPARγ and human pregnane X receptor to characterize the toxicity of diverse PFAS. Liver and intestinal fatty acid binding proteins (L-FABP and I-FABP) also influence the toxicokinetics of PFAS by transporting ligands to PPARs. − Human serum albumin (HSA), the most abundant blood protein, binds strongly to many PFAS, contributing to their long half-lives and prolonged bioactivity/toxicity. ,−

We aim to develop multicondition QSAR models that relate PFAS structural characteristics to adverse bioactivity. We hypothesize that incorporating PFAS-protein interactions into QSAR models can enhance predictions of adverse health outcomes. To test this, we incorporate molecular docking into the models and assess its contributions toward predicting bioactivities of PFASs. Specifically, we generate docking scores as molecular descriptors to characterize PFAS binding affinities with PPARs, FABPs, and HSA based on their established contributions to PFAS toxicity and bioaccumulation. Because previous QSAR models relied largely on pharmaceutical or benchmark bioassays (e.g., cancer cell or drug–target assays), we curated PFAS toxicity datasets emphasizing regulatory-relevant sources to improve model applicability for hazard prioritization. Together, these advances establish a novel integrative QSAR framework that mechanistically couples protein interaction descriptors with updated toxicity data to enhance the prediction of PFAS bioactivity.

Materials and Methods

Computational Workflow and Data Sources

A dataset of 4,760 PFAS structures was compiled from the OECD PFAS list in PubChem. Three-dimensional conformers were generated and blind-docked to six human proteins involved in lipid transport and nuclear receptor signaling (HSA, PPARα, PPARβ/δ, PPARγ, L-FABP and I-FABP). Given the docking distribution and the known fatty acid (FA) binding sites on HSA, we characterized the binding pockets of HSA into six docking sites, each defined by a 25 Å3 box and using the same search center described by Li et al. (2021). Docking scores and physicochemical descriptors were calculated, yielding 56 descriptors (Table S1) for each compound. Bioactivity annotations were compiled for PFAS containing −C2F4– substructures from five public databases (Table ), yielding 560 compounds with toxicity or bioactivity classifications.

QSAR and machine learning models were developed using the QSAR-Co-X framework to relate molecular descriptors and docking scores to PFAS bioactivity, yielding seven PFAS BioTox models. We used Sequential Forward Selection-Linear Discrimination Analysis (SFS-LDA), a linear model which achieves better reproducibility in comparison to Genetic Algorithms (GA)-LDA, to investigate the significance of chemical descriptors on the bioactivity classification. We assessed predictivity of a suite of nonlinear machine learning (ML) models, including k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN), Support Vector Classifier (SVC), Random Forests (RF), Bernoulli Naïve Bayes classifier (NB), Gradient Boosting (GB), and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) neural networks. Table S2 details the hyperparameters applied for each ML model evaluated. Overall, PFAS-protein interactions were important for the PFAS BioTox classifications. In the best SFS-LDA model, 60% of the descriptors were molecular docking scores (eq ), whereas only 20% of the 56 input chemical descriptors were docking scores (Table S1). As we previously reported and Chen (2015) discussed, molecular docking with repetitive binding protocols is a promising tool for initial screening to categorize PFAS ligands as either protein-binding or protein-nonbinding. However, molecular docking is limited in its ability to differentiate strong versus weak binding chemicals due to the absence of solvent–solvent interactions. Many studies improved prediction accuracy for diverse ligands by using molecular dynamics − or flexible docking protocols inspired by molecular dynamics, , but these simulations are computationally expensive. We estimated that 5 months would be required for flexible docking of the 560 PFAS used to build the QSAR model using our computer system and 4.5 years for molecular dynamics against one protein. In our model, molecular docking scores effectively informed the PFAS BioTox model classifications. These findings are consistent with mechanistic evidence that PFAS interactions with carrier and receptor proteins contribute to diverse adverse impacts.

BioTox classifications showed a positive correlation with docking-predicted binding affinities to HSA FA 3/4 and PPARγ, or equivalently negatively correlated to the binding scores in kcal/mol. HSA FA 3/4 overlaps with Sudlow’s binding site II, which binds with endogenous (e.g., thyroxine) and exogenous (e.g., ibuprofen) ligands. Occupation of Sudlow’s site by PFAS may impair HSA’s drug transport and biological functions (e.g., thyroid disruption). − PPARs binding is implicated in PFAS toxicity through molecular initiation events (MIEs). Activation of PPARα is initiated by binding of PFOS and PFOA, as established in prior studies. , Recent toxicology studies reveal that PFASs induce multiple MIEs. PPARγ activation by PFASs could help explain additional adverse health outcomes that cannot be explained by PPARα functions, such as metabolic disorder and weakness in bones. , When evaluating the suitability of novel PFAS structures for manufacture, Lai et al. (2022) considered the low binding affinity of PFASs to PPARγ and human pregnane X receptor (hPXR), determined through molecular simulations, as a proxy for low toxicity potential. Contrary to expectations, BioTox classifications exhibited a negative correlation with docking-predicted binding affinities for HSA FA1. Some have hypothesized that when PFASs are bound to HSA, they may be less available to interact with MIE receptors, leading to lesser bioactivity. − In this study, all fatty acids were deleted from the HSA crystal structure prior to docking and the interactions among fatty acids, HSA, and PFAS were not explored. In future studies, competitive molecular dynamics for an array of PFAS with different bioactivities could be used to test this hypothesis.

PFAS BioTox classification showed a positive correlation with the number of heterocyclic rings and a negative correlation with molecular complexity. This pattern is consistent with heterocycles promoting target-specific binding, whereas low-complexity fluorinated structures are more likely to drive nonspecific interactions. , Heterocyclic rings are widely found in a broad array of natural products (e.g., vitamins, hormones, and antibiotics) and are used as frameworks to enhance bioactivities for anthropogenic chemicals, such as pharmaceuticals. Conversely, fluorinated molecules with low molecular complexity are often the cause of off-target toxic effects in drug design.

Performance of ML QSAR Models

RF and GB models performed best, as evidenced by the AUC-ROC (Figure a) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) (Figure b) on the validation dataset. The performance of the SFS-LDA model follows closely after. The AUC-ROC scores of our ML models on the training set (above 0.9, except for NB and kNN) were marginally lower than those reported by Chen and Ng (>0.95 for all tested models). However, our models exhibited stronger performance on the validation set, achieving AUC-ROC scores above 0.9 (except for NB and kNN), in contrast to their 0.75 for the C3F6 dataset. Unlike Chen and Ng (2019), we did not use Bayesian optimization for hyperparameter tuning. However, the performance metrics improvements on the training and testing sets may not translate to the external validation sets, especially when tuning models trained on small data sets. Indeed, Cheng and Ng (2019) observed an overfitting phenomenon. Since MCC values (a summary statistic that considers the unbalanced classes of the dataset) also agreed with AUC-ROC model, we selected the RF model to predict BioTox classifications for OECD PFAS in PubChem. Of 4,098 manufactured and used OECD PFAS in PubChem (all successfully docked), 2,649 PFAS were within the applicability domain (AD). The AD of our model is defined as the chemical space of the training data, encompassing both the chemical descriptors and responses. The AD of SFS-LDA was calculated based on Standardization Approach proposed by Roy et al. (2015). For other ML models, ADs were determined using Confidence Estimation Approach with a posterior probability threshold of 0.75.

We present RF predicted BioTox classifications of 56 PFAS with a posterior probability greater than 95% for the classification in Table S3. Additional statistical metric evaluations for all tested models are shown in Figure S8.

Correlating PFAS Structural Analysis with BioTox Classification

To assess the structural similarities of bioactive/bioinactive PFAS, we used Structure–Activity Landscape Index (SALI) activity cliff analysis (ACA) with QSAR input data. The SALI-ACA map (Figure a) randomly positions all molecules in the 2D space and repositions the molecules to the most structurally similar neighbors for every molecule. Molecules with similar structures are connected by a line, with BioTox classifications shown in blue for bioactive PFAS and green for bioinactive PFAS. Manual inspection of the blue clusters showed that many bioactive PFAS share common structural features, including dinitro trifluoromethyl aniline, sulfonic acid groups, benzimidazole rings, and halogen substitutions (iodine, bromine, or chlorine) often accompanied by nitro or carboxylic acid functionalities. We hypothesize that the toxicity of PFAS containing benzimidazole and dinitroaniline arises primarily from the nonfluorinated moieties. Benzimidazole derivatives are known for their pharmacological properties, such as antimicrobial, antiviral, and anticancer effects and cytotoxicity. Dinitroaniline derivatives have been used as herbicides for decades and exhibit genotoxicity to mammalian cells.

To further assess the relationships between PFAS structural characteristics and health impacts for the bioactive PFAS clusters identified, we sorted the PFAS by their 28 tissue-specific bioassays and labeled the functional groups represented (Figure b). PFAS containing dinitro‑trifluoromethyl aniline, sulfonic acid, and carboxylic acid functionalities were associated with bioactivity across most assays. In contrast, benzimidazole and halide substitutions (with or without nitro group) were exclusively present in the acute oral toxicity database. The majority of these compounds were classified as highly toxic in acute oral toxicity tests based on EPA toxicity classification, likely due to their applications in pesticides. It is important to note that all bioactivities were weighted equally in the BioTox model due to the lack of sufficient data to perform multiple end points analysis. Implementing a functional use and hazard trait specific analysis would enhance the model’s ability to inform safer chemical manufacturing. To achieve this, we recommend expanded research toward comprehensive bioactivity assessments, encompassing a broader range of targets and standardized end points. Such efforts would significantly improve the predictive accuracy of the PFAS BioTox model and related models.

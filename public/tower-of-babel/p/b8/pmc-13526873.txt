Prediction of refracture risk after osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture surgery using the vertebral bone quality index from multi-sequence MRI
Authors: Wenxuan Dong, Zhen Xiang, Zhuozheng Deng, Sihong Ouyang, Huimin Xiang, Minhua Deng, Hao Li, Qipeng Wei, Xiaofeng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the value of the vertebral bone quality (VBQ) index on MRI in predicting refracture after percutaneous vertebral augmentation (PVA) for osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) and to compare its performance with bone mineral density (BMD). We retrospectively analyzed OVCF patients treated with PVA between January 2019 and September 2024. Patients were followed for 12 months and divided into refracture and non-refracture groups. Clinical, imaging, and surgical data were collected. Logistic regression was used to identify independent risk factors, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves assessed predictive performance. Among 124 patients, 29 (23.4%) developed refracture. Refracture was associated with lower BMI, reduced lumbar spine BMD, and higher VBQ values. Multivariate analysis confirmed T1VBQ and T2VBQ as independent risk factors, while lumbar spine BMD was protective. ROC analysis showed that the combined T1VBQ and T2VBQ model achieved higher predictive accuracy than individual parameters. MRI-based VBQ measurement is simple, non-invasive, and reproducible. Introduction

Osteoporosis is a systemic metabolic bone disease characterized by decreased bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, resulting in increased bone fragility and a higher risk of fractures1. Vertebral compression fractures are among the most common osteoporotic fractures, predominantly occurring in the thoracolumbar region2. Epidemiological studies report a standardized prevalence of osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) of 4.4%, with 2.8% in men and 5.9% in women3. With the global population aging, the incidence of OVCF is increasing annually, severely affecting patients’ quality of life and imposing a substantial socioeconomic burden4.

Traditional treatments primarily include bed rest, analgesics, brace fixation, and functional exercises, but they are associated with complications from prolonged immobilization, limited pain relief, and suboptimal functional recovery. In recent years, percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) and percutaneous kyphoplasty (PKP) have become the main minimally invasive treatments for OVCF due to their advantages in rapid pain relief, restoration of vertebral height, and improvement of quality of life, and have been widely adopted in clinical practice5.

Although PVP and PKP have established efficacy in pain relief and functional improvement, subsequent vertebral fractures, including adjacent segment fractures (ASF) and remote segment fractures (RSF), remain common complications5. These secondary fractures not only impair functional outcomes and quality of life but also increase the socioeconomic burden, limiting the long-term clinical benefit of PVP/PKP. Previous studies have reported that the incidence of new fractures is 9% in patients under 80 years of age and 33% in those over 80 years6,7, highlighting the clinical value of identifying and predicting refracture risk.

Reported risk factors for postoperative refracture include advanced age, female sex, low bone mineral density (BMD), cement distribution pattern during surgery, and severity of bone destruction8. Prediction models based on these factors have been attempted in some studies, but there is still room for improvement in predictive performance.

Recently, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been applied to assess vertebral bone quality. The vertebral bone quality (VBQ) index, first proposed by Ehresman, is a simple quantitative measure based on the ratio of lumbar T1-weighted signal intensity to cerebrospinal fluid signal, which indirectly reflects vertebral bone marrow fat content and bone status9. Several studies have confirmed that VBQ correlates with BMD measurements10–12 and may serve as a non-invasive and reproducible tool for bone assessment. However, studies on the relationship between VBQ and refracture are limited, and most previous research has focused on T1-weighted VBQ. Recent studies suggest that T2-weighted VBQ may better identify osteoporosis and show a stronger correlation with BMD10.

Based on the above background, this study aimed to investigate the value of T1- and T2-weighted MRI-derived VBQ in predicting refracture after PVA in OVCF patients. This study is the first to include both T1VBQ and T2VBQ simultaneously to comprehensively evaluate their predictive value for refracture and to attempt to establish an MRI-based predictive model, providing a reference for early identification of high-risk patients and individualized intervention strategies in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This single-center retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent percutaneous vertebral augmentation (PVA) for osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) at Panyu District Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Guangzhou, between January 1, 2019, and September 1, 2024. Eligible patients had newly diagnosed single-level compression fractures between T10 and L5, met the guidelines for primary osteoporosis13, had no surgical contraindications, and underwent PVA. Patients were excluded if fractures were caused by pathological conditions such as infection or tumor, were not attributable to OVCF, had contraindications to PVA, had missing clinical data, or had a history of previous lumbar internal fixation surgery. Based on whether a vertebral refracture occurred within 12 months after surgery, patients were classified into a refracture group and a non-refracture group. All patients were followed at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively through outpatient visits or telephone calls, and clinical symptoms and imaging results were recorded. New vertebral compression fractures identified during follow-up were considered postoperative refractures.

Observed variables and measurement methods

The following variables were compared between the two groups: clinical variables (age, sex, body mass index [BMI], history of previous fractures, comorbid hypertension and diabetes), imaging variables (lumbar spine BMD and vertebral bone quality [VBQ], vertebral height restoration rate [VHRR]), and surgical variables (cement volume, cement leakage, and postoperative cement distribution type[PCDT]).

VBQ was measured on mid-sagittal T1- and T2-weighted lumbar MRI images (Fig. 1). Oval regions of interest (ROIs) were drawn within the vertebral bodies from L1 to L4 to include as much trabecular bone as possible, thereby ensuring that the measured signal intensity (SI) reflected trabecular bone quality. If a vertebral body could not be measured due to severe scoliosis or other conditions, the ROI was placed on a para-mid-sagittal plane. The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) ROI was located in the subarachnoid space posterior to L3; if this region was unavailable because of stenosis or other factors, an alternative ROI was selected in the posterior CSF space at L2 or L4. The VBQ value was calculated as the median SI of L1–L4 ROIs divided by the SI of the CSF ROI, excluding fractured vertebrae. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2). Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s) and compared using independent-samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed variables are presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Vertebral refracture was set as the dependent variable. Univariate logistic regression was first conducted to identify potential risk factors, followed by multivariate logistic regression including variables with statistical significance.Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate the predictive performance of the model, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis

As shown in Table 2, variables with p < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate logistic regression model. The results demonstrated that both T1 VBQ (OR = 4.637, 95% CI: 1.474–16.719, p = 0.012) and T2 VBQ (OR = 5.545, 95% CI: 1.670–21.364, p = 0.007) were independent predictors of postoperative vertebral refracture after PVA. In contrast, lumbar spine BMD T-score (OR = 0.396, 95% CI: 0.207–0.701, p = 0.003) was independently protective against refracture. Age was not significantly associated with refracture risk (p > 0.05).

ROC curve analysis

The ROC curve analyses for T1/T2 VBQ and lumbar spine BMD, along with their calibration curves, are presented in Figs. 3, 4, and 5. As shown, T1 VBQ, T2 VBQ, and lumbar BMD each demonstrated predictive value for postoperative refracture. The areas under the curve (AUCs) were 0.723 (95% CI: 0.5992–0.8462), 0.621 (95% CI: 0.4978–0.7451), and 0.781 (95% CI: 0.6718–0.8905), respectively. Among these, lumbar BMD showed the strongest discriminative ability, followed by T1 VBQ, while T2 VBQ exhibited the lowest predictive performance. When T1 and T2 VBQ were combined into a joint predictive model, the AUC increased to 0.794 (95% CI: 0.6914–0.897), surpassing those of the individual parameters and slightly exceeding that of BMD. These findings suggest that combining vertebral bone quality parameters can enhance predictive accuracy and may hold potential clinical value for identifying patients at high risk of refracture after PVA.

Calibration of the predictive model

The calibration curve obtained by bootstrapping (1,000 resamples) demonstrated good agreement between the predicted and actual probabilities of refracture (Fig. 5). The bias-corrected line was close to the ideal reference line, indicating that the model’s predicted risks were consistent with the observed outcomes. These findings suggest that the model has satisfactory calibration and reliable predictive accuracy.

Conclusion

This study is the first to combine T1- and T2-weighted VBQ values to predict the risk of postoperative vertebral refracture after PVA in OVCF patients. The combined model outperformed individual VBQ values and complemented traditional lumbar BMD. Higher VBQ values indicate more severe osteoporosis and represent independent risk factors, whereas higher lumbar BMD is protective. These findings not only validate the clinical value of VBQ in assessing refracture risk but also provide a new MRI-based approach for quantitative bone evaluation. In the future, combined assessment of VBQ and BMD may become an important tool for early identification of high-risk patients and the development of individualized preventive strategies.

Discussion

This study explored the value of MRI-based quantitative vertebral bone quality (VBQ) indices in predicting postoperative refracture risk in patients with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) following percutaneous vertebral augmentation (PVA). Both T1- and T2-weighted VBQ were identified as independent risk factors for postoperative refracture. When combined, the T1 and T2 VBQ model achieved a higher area under the ROC curve (AUC) than either VBQ alone or lumbar BMD, highlighting the potential of MRI quantitative metrics in postoperative risk stratification.

With the widespread clinical adoption of PVP and PKP, patients can achieve rapid pain relief and functional improvement. However, postoperative refractures significantly compromise long-term outcomes. Refractures are not only closely associated with the ongoing progression of osteoporosis but may also result from surgery-induced changes in spinal biomechanics14,15. Therefore, identifying effective predictive markers and early recognition of high-risk patients are critical for implementing individualized interventions and improving prognosis.

Previous studies have reported multiple risk factors, including age, sex, low BMD, BMI, and cement distribution patterns16. Although predictive models based on these factors can partially improve risk assessment, their accuracy, comprehensiveness, and clinical applicability remain limited.

As an MRI-based quantitative measure, VBQ indirectly reflects bone marrow fat content and trabecular microarchitecture, providing a more comprehensive assessment of bone quality17. In this study, both T1VBQ and T2VBQ were significantly associated with postoperative refracture risk and remained independent predictors in multivariate analysis. Moreover, the combined use of T1 and T2 VBQ further improved predictive performance, with the AUC notably higher than either single metric, demonstrating the complementary value of MRI-based quantitative indicators.

Our findings indicate that combining T1- and T2-weighted VBQ values at the T11 vertebra significantly enhanced predictive accuracy compared with single sequences. This suggests that VBQ measurements from different MRI sequences may reflect complementary aspects of osteoporosis-related pathological changes. Mechanistically, T1-weighted imaging is highly sensitive to fat signals and accurately reflects increased bone marrow fat content. Previous studies have shown that during osteoporosis progression, hematopoietic marrow is progressively replaced by fat, resulting in increased T1 signal intensity18; thus, T1 VBQ serves as an important indicator of marrow adiposity and declining bone quality. In contrast, T2-weighted signals are influenced by both water and fat content, making them more sensitive to trabecular microstructure disruption, bone marrow microcirculation, and tissue hydration, thereby reflecting overall trabecular integrity and bone metabolic status19,20.Therefore, a single-sequence VBQ captures only part of the pathological features of osteoporosis, whereas combining T1 and T2 VBQ allows simultaneous assessment of marrow fat infiltration and trabecular microstructural damage, providing a more comprehensive evaluation of fracture risk. This complementarity explains why the combined T1/T2 VBQ model outperforms individual sequences. In clinical practice, combined VBQ assessment can provide more accurate bone quality stratification and facilitate early identification of high-risk patients for tailored interventions.

MRI is widely available, radiation-free, and highly reproducible in clinical practice. Calculating VBQ from routine MRI sequences requires no additional imaging or cost, enhancing its clinical applicability. Our results suggest that VBQ, particularly the combined T1/T2 approach, can serve as an effective predictive tool for postoperative refracture. Patients with higher VBQ values may benefit from more aggressive osteoporosis management, enhanced rehabilitation, and closer follow-up to reduce refracture risk.

The clinical relevance of VBQ extends beyond its diagnostic and predictive utility. Defining clinically meaningful VBQ thresholds may help stratify postoperative refracture risk in patients undergoing PVA. Individuals with higher VBQ values, indicating poorer vertebral bone quality, could be considered at elevated risk and may benefit from intensified anti-osteoporotic therapy, more frequent imaging surveillance, and personalized rehabilitation. In contrast, patients with lower VBQ values might only require standard osteoporosis management and routine follow-up.

Although this study did not establish a specific cutoff value for VBQ because of the limited sample size, previous investigations have shown that MRI-based quantitative indices can effectively differentiate osteoporotic from non-osteoporotic vertebrae. Further multicenter studies with larger cohorts are needed to identify optimal VBQ thresholds for clinical decision-making. Once validated, these thresholds could be incorporated into postoperative monitoring protocols to facilitate individualized prevention strategies, enhance long-term outcomes, and reduce the risk of refracture.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size and a limited number of refracture events, which may affect statistical power. Second, some potential risk factors were not included, such as lifestyle-related variables including smoking status, alcohol consumption, physical activity levels, and nutritional status, particularly calcium and vitamin D intake. These factors may influence bone metabolism and refracture risk. Future multicenter, large-scale prospective studies incorporating these variables are warranted to validate these findings. Finally, VBQ measurement requires standardized protocols, and the influence of different scanning parameters on values needs further investigation.

The novelty of this study lies in the first simultaneous evaluation of T1- and T2-weighted VBQ in predicting postoperative refracture, proposing a combined predictive model. The findings demonstrate that better predictive performance than traditional BMD, providing new evidence for constructing more accurate predictive models and offering new avenues for clinical imaging-based bone quality assessment.

Conclusion

This study is the first to apply T1- and T2-weighted VBQ values jointly to predict the risk of postoperative vertebral fractures in OVCF patients following PVA. Our results show that the combined model performs better than either VBQ value alone and can complement traditional lumbar BMD measurements. Higher VBQ values indicate more severe osteoporosis and are independent risk factors, whereas higher lumbar BMD provides a protective effect. These findings not only confirm the clinical utility of VBQ in assessing the risk of re-fracture but also offer a new approach for MRI-based quantitative evaluation of bone quality. In the future, the combined assessment of VBQ and BMD may become an important tool for early identification of high-risk patients and for guiding individualized prevention and treatment strategies.

Exploitation of heterosis in chilli using genetic male sterile lines for red fruit yield with special reference to high rainfall conditions
Authors: Hem Lata, Akhilesh Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
High-quality red/dry chilli for spice, pharmaceutical and medicinal purposes is a major goal in chilli breeding. The male sterile lines have greater potential for the exploitation of heterosis in chilli to achieve this objective. Genetic male sterile lines with special traits like destalking and ability to withstand high rainfall were involved in heterosis breeding to identify hybrids for commercial and industrial purposes. Forty F1 hybrids were developed by crossing 4 diverse GMS lines with 10 testers using Line × Tester mating design to estimate heterosis, combing ability and gene action. The experiment involving 14 parents and 40 F1s, along with standard variety ‘CH-27’ was laid out in α-lattice square design in three replications during summer 2020 and 2021. The GMS lines MS 9-2 and MS 26-1 along with testers DPCh 10, VVG, DPCh 40 and Him Palam Mirch-2 showed significant GCA for marketable red/dry fruit yield and majority of their component traits. Ten F1 hybrids were identified with superiority for fruit yield based on mean performance, significant heterosis and SCA effects, providing an opportunity to utilize them in value-added products and dried spice purposes viz., MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 with erect fruiting habit and that of MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 9-2 × PBC 535, MS 26-1 × VVG, MS 29-2 × DPCh 10 and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22- C with pendent fruits. The non-additive gene action was predominant in the expression of fruit yield, total red fruits/plant, oleoresin and capsanthin. A significant positive correlation among SCA, economic heterosis and per se performance is an indicative to identify superior hybrids. Multi-location testing of these hybrids shall pave way to exploit them commercially by making them available to the farmers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73728-7.

Introduction

Chilli (Capsicum annuusm L.), a member of family Solanaceae is a major spice which is widely grown across the globe predominantly in tropical and subtropical climates. Capsicum is considered to be originated in South and Central America1 and comprise of around 30 species wherein only C. annuum L., C. frutescens L., C. chinense Jacq., C. baccatum L. and C. pubescens L are cultivated2. Further, C. annuum tops among these five by covering maximum cultivated area across the world on account of its nutritional and economic importance3. Chilli finds a coveted position as an important industrial spice crops and is known as the “wonder spice.” Globally, estimated dry chilli and pepper production is 4.8 million tonnes from 1.62 million-hectare area. India, Thailand, China, Ethiopia, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Myanmar and Mexico are the major chilli growing countries. India has a dominant position in the global production and exports of dry chilli and pepper with acreage of 0.78 million hectares and production of 1.74 million tonnes that contributes 42% to the world production and worth export value of Rupees 85,820 million (around USD 1000 million) with share of 56% to total global exports4.

Chilli is one of the oldest known economically significant source that is used as natural food colouring material5. The carotenoids in chilli exist in diverse forms and structures at different maturity stages e.g. chlorophyll and carotenoid at green fruit stage; zeaxanthin, carotene, cryptoxanthin and lutein at the intermediate maturity stage; and capsorubin and capsanthin at maturity to form scarlet pigments6,7. Capsanthin exhibits lots of health benefits as powerful antioxidant and anti-tumor; reduces inflammation due to obesity; promotes cardio protection health by elevating high-density lipoproteins cholesterol levels; regulates body temperature and promotes weight loss by increasing temperature8. It is also an excellent source of vitamin A and C that boosts the immune system and wards off chronic ailments9. Red chilli helps to relax blood vessels and regulate blood pressure on account of high potassium content. The capsaicin, source of pungency that present in pericarp and placenta of fruits is a product of homovanillic acid, which could alter the function of many genes with an ability to prevent excessive generation of reactive oxygen species10. Capsaicin also speeds up the body metabolism rate by directly burning calories11. In addition, capsaicin stimulates apoptosis that help to arrest growth of various types of cancer cells including prostate, colon, liver, pancreas, esophagus etc12. Oleoresin extracted from dried red chilli is used for culinary purposes to enhance the color and flavor of various food products such as soups, stews, sausages, cheese, snacks, salad, sauces, pizzas, confections, and beverages etc13. Apart from this, oleoresin is rich source of phytochemicals with antioxidant activity14 and also as medicine to promote digestion due to its properties as a stimulant and carminative while its external use help to treat rheumatism that provide relief for joint and muscle pain. Red powder and pigment are also utilized as effective feed additives to enhance the egg yolk color for laying hens15.

Due to the growing demand of chilli among producers, industry and consumers, there is need to exploit heterosis for relevant agronomic and industrial traits to maximize economic returns. The F1 hybrids offer significant advantage in terms of early maturity, high yield, better fruit weight and dry recovery vis-à-vis wider adaptability, uniform growth and resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses16. In plant breeding, selection of hybrids requires a good magnitude of heterosis and combining ability17. The estimates of heterosis can be predicted through per se performance of parental lines though it would not be a reliable indicator of heterosis18. Thus, the parents need to be judged based on their potential combination rather than on their own performance19. Combining ability aids in the selection of parents more judiciously with high crossing potential and facilitate to produce superior hybrids20. The selection of parents for effective hybridization depends upon the nature and magnitude of heterosis21. Heterosis does not solely depend upon good parents as its degree vary for various characters within the same cross.

There is a need to pay attention for the improvement of red/dry chili to enhance the existing productivity as spice and other industrial uses. High yield of dry chilli with wider adaptability could be achieved through identification of suitable parental lines to be utilized in synthesizing potential F1 hybrids with better yield and quality23. The production of F1 seeds using hand emasculation and hand pollination is tedious and cost intensive process. The flowers of chilli are small and delicate in nature, resulting in flower drop and poor fruit set after emasculation. The use of male sterility-based hybrid seed production is the alternative to overcome this problem22. Another key objective of the chili breeding programme is to develop varieties/hybrids that can thrive well under a wide range of environmental conditions. Heavy rainfall coupled with high humidity cause severe infestation of the fruit rot that result in low yield and deteriorate quality of fruits leading to huge economic losses. This issue directly relates to fruit orientation as erect fruit bearing genotypes show low incidence of fruit rot41. Simultaneously, this erect bearing of fruits has an advantage in spice industry due to destalking habit as fruits detached from pedicel while plucking and help to reduce input cost to remove pedicel besides save workers certain ailments due to pungency45,46.

Majority of the male sterile lines utilized in heterosis breeding showed pendent fruiting habit and are prone to high incidence of fruit rot particularly during rainy season. The development of GMS lines with an erect fruiting habit signifies advancement in chilli breeding for industrial as well as commercial purposes. This novel character not only helps to increase the overall productivity of the crop but also shows superior adaptability in high rainfall conditions on account of low incidence of fruit rot. Accordingly, efforts were made to develop male sterile lines with erect fruit habit in recent years which are being utilized in the present study. The present investigation was, therefore, designed keeping the aforesaid aspects in view to exploit heterosis based on GCA, SCA and their correlation for different horticultural traits and to identify superior hybrids for red/dry chilli with higher yield suitable under high rainfall areas.

Materials and methods

Experimental material and hybrid development

Forty hybrids were synthesized by following Line × Tester mating design involving four diverse genic male sterile (GMS) lines and ten testers during June and July 2019 under natural ventilated poly-house. Pollination was done in the morning between 8:30 am and 11:30 am by shedding dehisced pollens of the respective testers on the well opened female flowers (GMS lines). The F1 seeds were obtained by extracting from the red ripe fruits for further evaluation. The salient features of both GMS and open pollinated inbreds has been presented in Table 1.

Field experiment and crop management

The seeds of 14 parental lines, their 40 F1s along with standard variety ‘CH-27 were treated with carbandazim at 3 g/k g of seed and were sown in nursery bed of size 3 m × 1 m × 15 cm consecutively for 2 years on 27th and 26th February 2020 and 2021, respectively with spacing of 5 cm between rows at a depth of 1–2 cm. The seedlings were ready for transplanting in about 2 months. Ten plants of each entry were planted in plot size of 2.25 × 1.8 m with inter and intra row spacing of 45 cm each by following α-lattice design during the respective years on 1st May 2020 and 30th April 2021, replicated thrice. The crop was provided with recommended quantity of farmyard manure (20 tons ha− 1) and fertilizer applications of 75: 60: 60 kg N, P2O5 and K2O ha− 1. The entire dose of P and K together with half dose of N was mixed in soil at the time of field preparation. The remaining dose of N was top dressed in two equal splits at 1-month interval after transplanting. A field view of the evaluation trials on chilli hybrids is depicted through Fig. 1.

Observations recorded

Ten randomly taken red ripe fruits from the second picking were used to record observations on fruit length (cm) [FL] and fruit width (cm) [FW]. Primary branches/plant [PBPP] and plant height (cm) [PH] were recorded at last fruit picking. Number of rotten fruits/plants [NRFPP], total number of red ripe fruits/plant [TRFPP] (including rotted ones) and number of marketable red ripe fruits/plant [MRFPP] with no rotting symptoms picked from the selected plants at different intervals were added and averaged to arrive the respective values of these traits. The per cent marketable red ripe fruits/plant [PMRFPP] was calculated by dividing the marketable red ripe fruits with total red ripe fruits and then multiplied with 100. The weight of marketable red ripe fruits picked from different harvest was added and averaged to obtain the marketable red ripe fruit yield/plant [MRFYPP]. Similarly, the average fruit weight [AFW] was calculated by dividing marketable red ripe fruit yield/plant with number of marketable fruits/plants. The red ripe fruits harvested from selected plants in different harvestings were sun dried and then weighed to estimate dry fruit yield/plant (DFYPP). The harvest duration [HD] was worked out by counting the number of days from first picking to the last harvest. For the assessment of quality parameters, the sun-dried red fruits of each genotype were grinded to obtain powdered samples that were utilized for estimating oleoresin [OL] and capsanthin [CS] as per procedure given by AOAC24 (Association of official analytical chemists) and capsaicin content [CC] by Bajaj25.

Statistical analysis

The analysis of variance (ANOVA) for line × tester analysis was done according to the model suggested by Kempthorne26. GCA, SCA, additive and dominance components of variances were calculated following methods of Singh and Chaudhary27. Mean performance

Majority of the crosses i.e. 37 and 31 significantly surpassed the commercial hybrid CH-27 for marketable red ripe and dry fruit yield, respectively (Supplementary Table S2). Cross combinations namely, MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 were ranked as top five promising crosses for red ripe and dry fruit yield except MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 for dry fruit yield (Table 5). Among the top ranked hybrids, long harvest duration was observed in MS 26-1 × DPCh 10 (56.67 days) followed by MS 9-2 × HPM-2 (55.67 days), MS 9-2 × DPCh 10 (54.83 days) and MS 9-2 × PBC 535 (53.67 days). The hybrid MS 26-1 × DPCh 10 also possessed maximum mean performance for FL (11.45 cm) and PH (86.68 cm). Similarly, maximum PBPP was found in hybrids MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 (5.46), MS 9-2 × PBC 535 (5.25) and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 (5.23). The high yielding hybrids were also superior in quality parameters e.g. MS 9-2 × PBC 535 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 for OL, MS 9-2 × HPM-2 for CS, and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22-C and MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 for CC. The cross-combination MS 9-2 × Surajmukhi, MS 11-2 × Surajmukhi, MS 26-1 × Surajmukhi, MS 11-2 × VVG and MS 29-2 × Surajmukhi exhibited minimum NRFPP. Combining ability analysis and gene action

Among the parents, two GMS lines and four testers showed significant GCA effects for most of the important traits (Table 3). GMS line MS 9-2 possessed positive GCA effects for RFYPP (45.14) along with DFYPP (7.38), PBPP (0.19), HD (1.05), MRFPP (13.97), TRFPP (11.55) and PMRFPP (3.53). Similarly, MS 26-1 was most promising with positive GCA effects for FL (0.46), FW (0.13), AFW (0.74), PH (5.08) and HD (1.15). Among the testers, DPCh 10, VVG, DPCh 40 and HPM-2 showed positive and significant GCA for RFYPP. DPCh 10 construed significant and desirable GCA effects for RFYPP (94.20), DFYPP (22.96), FL (1.31), PH (12.28), HD (4.48), AFW (0.53), MRFPP (2.20), TRFPP (3.77) and PMRFPP (0.75). Tester VVG was the most promising general combiners for PBPP (0.40), PH (6.57), MRFPP (41.96), TRFPP (33.77) and HD (2.11). Similarly, HPM-2 and DPCh 40 showed significant positive GCA effects for FL, AFW and HD. The GMS line MS 9-2 and MS 11-2 along with testers Surajmukhi, DPCh 101, VVG and DPCh 40 exhibited significant negative GCA effects for number of rotten fruits/plants (Table S3). Estimates of GCA effects indicated that, GMS line MS 9-2 in addition to red fruit yield, also showed significant GCA for OL (6.56) while MS 26-1 had the same effects for CS (8.54) and CC (0.01). Among testers, DPCh 10 found to be a good combiner for CC (0.01) while HPM-2 was a good combiner for CS (18.9) and CC (0.02) and that of DPCh 40 for CS (2.79). Discussion

Heterosis breeding based on GMS line has revolutionized chili cultivation. In India, demand for F1 hybrids is rising over the years because of genetic uniformity and higher yield. Hybrids of chilli tend to provide a great opportunity to the farmers due to high export potential. The proportion of mean sum of squares due to testers was more compared to lines for most of the characters [except for PBPP, OL and CS] indicating wide variability for the different traits. Significant mean squares due to parents, crosses and parents vs. crosses for majority of the traits also indicated wide range of variability among the parents, hybrids and their interactions, respectively. This variability among parents can be exploited through selection by studying heterosis, GCA and SCA. The per se performance should not be ignored rather it should be given equal importance during the assessment of economic heterosis with sole aim of identifying superior hybrids29. In any crop improvement program, yield is the top priority although it is a complex trait. Red and dry fruit yield of chilli have economic importance from export point of view and continuing high demand in spice industry. The per se performance of different traits signified wide variation in the performance of hybrids which would be the result of diverse parents used in the present study. The superiority of synthesized hybrids is quite evident in the present study as majority of the hybrids significantly outperformed standard variety CH 27 for both RFYPP and DFYPP to the extent of 117% and 91% by MS 9-2 × HPM-2 (603.75 g, 130.67 g), respectively followed by MS 9-2 × DPCh 10 (574.05 g, 127.47 g), MS 26-1 × DPCh 10 (549.68 g, 119.74 g), MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 (509.62 g, 119.87 g) and MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 (503.84 g, 98.55 g) with their rank among top five hybrids (Table 5; Fig. 2).

The superiority of above-mentioned hybrids for fruit yield was mainly ascribed to their excellent performance for FL, PBPP, MRFPP, PH and HD. These traits supposed to contribute precisely and tactically towards the yield that eventually enhanced the red fruit yield of industrial importance on account of capsaicin, oleoresin and red chilli powder13. The differences in PH could be the result of genetic makeup of parents involved30 while branching has the tendency to bear many fruits that has impact on yield/unit area31. Fungal pathogens are the main cause of fruit rot in chilli which is usually found in the soil, air or infected plant debris. Environmental factors such as high soil moisture, high humidity or excessive rainfall are favourable to cause high infestation of fruit rot. In our investigation, 16 hybrids out of 40, exhibited erect fruiting habits that holds special significance in red chilli cultivation for spice and industrial purposes since it takes a longer duration for the crop to reach red ripe maturity stage. Hybrids with erect fruiting habits show low incidence of fruit rot as water is less likely to penetrate the fruits through pedicel whereas in pendent fruit, water easily penetrate through indented pedicel joint, contributing to the higher incidence of fruit rot41. The preference for an upright fruit-bearing habit over drooping minimizes the possibility of fruit contact with the soil and thus reduced fruit rot infection particularly under high rainfall conditions. The top rank of hybrid MS 9-2 × HPM-2 for fruit yield and component traits was the result of its erect fruit bearing habit along with low fruit rot incidence that validate our hypothesis.

For industrial use, labour cost is a big challenge on account of its harvesting process. The pedicel attachment with fruit is economically unavoidable in red chilli but its presence in ground powder diminishes both the color and quality of the product. The manual removal of the pedicel is time consuming and also entails significant cost. Therefore, the current need is to develop hybrids/varieties that facilitate easy separation of fruits from stems and stalks that will not only reduce the processing cost but also help to enhance the overall product quality of chilli powder and other processed products. In our study, top ranked hybrids viz., MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40, and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 showed erect fruit bearing habit that make them exceptionally suitable for industrial purposes due to this destalking attribute.

Pungency and colour are the two main traits of chili fruits that regulate their quality and could be used both for industrial and edible purposes32. Capsanthin is a natural pigment with deep red colour and holds significance in the cosmetics and pharmaceutical industries33. Capsaicin, an active component of chilli, is present in placental tissue and is responsible for hot sensation to the tongue and is utilized for the treatment of inflammatory disorders, headache, dermatitis gastrointestinal pathogens, anticoagulant activity and prevention of aspiration pneumonia34. In present study, amongst the high ranked crosses, MS 9-2 × PBC 535 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 for OL, MS 9-2 × HPM-2 for CS and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22-C and MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 for CC performed better than standard variety CH-27. They showed their potential to be explored for commercial exploitation as per industrial requirements.

Combining ability analysis is one of the most effective methods for defining the most appropriate combiners to be utilized in different crosses either to exploit heterosis or to accrue desirable genes20. It also provides an opportunity to understand the genetic architecture of distinctive attributes that guide the breeders to frame an effective breeding strategy for the improvement of existing germplasm35. Identification of the potential parental inbred lines help in the production of high-quality chilli for food and medicinal industries13. High GCA estimates of GMS lines MS 9-2 and MS 26-1, and testers namely, DPCh 10, VVG, DPCh 40 and HPM-2 for yield and related traits indicating their capability as the potential parents in heterosis breeding (Table 3). The genotypes with high capsaicin content serve as potential source for the development of highly pungent hybrids with their multiple use in food industries, domestic and international markets. Keeping this in view, MS 26-1 along with testers DPCh 10 and Him Palam Mirch-2 showed their potential as good combiners to develop high yielding chilli with great colour and pungency while MS 9-2 could be utilized for high OL.

After the identification of suitable parents and potential crosses, the next crucial step is to adopt an appropriate breeding strategy to handle genetic variability that mainly depends upon the gene action for the traits of interest36. In the present study, σD2 was higher than σA2 for RFYPP, DFYPP, TRFPP, CS and OL indicating non-additive gene action in their inheritance, suggesting the role of dominant genes in the expression of these traits37 that help to enhance the hybrid vigour in cross combinations involving diverse inbred lines13. The genetic inheritance of quality attributes namely, OL and CS along with DFYPP was controlled by non-additive gene action that suggests exploitation of heterosis for the improvement of these traits23,38.

Heterosis breeding helps to boost productivity and accordingly, there is a huge scope of improvement in yield and other economic traits of chilli through hybrid breeding utilizing genetically diverse inbred lines39. In the present study, hybrids showed economic heterosis for MRFPP to the extent of 4.45–117.74% (Table S6). The varying manifestation of heterosis by different hybrids from high to low is the result of variable genetic diversity among the parents involved in different crosses for different attributes40. Hybrid MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 101, MS 9-2 × PBC 535, MS 26-1 × VVG, MS 29-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 22-C were the top ranked hybrids for both red and dry fruit yield suggesting their importance to utilize in dried spice industry. These top ranked crosses also exhibited positive and significant economic heterosis for other component traits e.g. MS 26-1 × DPCh 10 for nine traits, MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 26-1 × DPCh 22-C for eight traits, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 101, MS 9-2 × PBC 535 MS 26-1 × VVG and MS 29-2 × DPCh 10 for seven traits (Table 5). Therefore, the manifestation of economic heterosis for fruit yield by these hybrids could be ascribed mainly due to their simultaneous superiority for component traits such as FL, AFW, PH, MRFPP, PH, PBPP41. Further, 30 out of 40 hybrids including eight among top ten exhibited significantly negative heterosis for NRFPP (Table 5 and Supplementary Table S6) indicating their suitability for high rainfall areas. In the present scenario of consumer’s cautiousness, food industries are more attentive to use colours and flavors of plant origin as many health issues have been reported using synthetic additives and colour5. Therefore, keeping in view the huge demand for natural food products, the research priority on these aspects offer opportunities for researchers and stakeholders. Among the 40 F1s, 12 crosses exhibited economic heterosis each for oleoresin and capsaicin while five crosses revealed the same for capsanthin (Table S6). The top high yielding hybrids also exhibited positive heterosis for OL and CC suggesting their effective commercialization in processing industries.

SCA effects is also used as a parameter to predict heterosis as non-fixable components of gene action i.e. dominant and epistasis are related to hybrid vigor11. In our study also, top yield performing hybrids showed positive and significant SCA effects for both RFYPP and DFYPP along with OL and CC. These top crosses for red fruit yield involve parents having GCA effects of good × good namely, MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 and MS 26-1 × VVG that can be exploited to isolate transgressive segregants in early generations. However, crosses involving one good or other poor combiner (MS 11-2 × DPCh 40 and MS 29-2 × DPCh 10 and MS 9-2 × PBC − 535 and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22-C may produce transgressive segregants in the later generations. The hybrids with high SCA effects involving poor × poor combiners indicating the manifestation of epistasis and improvement for desirable traits can be explored using biparental mating or reciprocal recurrent selection42.

The best hybrid combination would be the one with high SCA involving one of the parents with high GCA effects43. The manifestation of heterosis predominantly based on the genotypes and could be based on association with related characters. Our observations indicated a significant correlation between specific combining ability (SCA) and economic heterosis across all studied characters, indicating towards the significance of non-additive effects in the expression of heterosis44. The durable correlation between SCA and heterosis suggests the potential utility of SCA to predict hybrid performance (Table 6). The high positive correlation between SCA effects and heterosis also support the idea that hybrids which were top ranked for heterosis also revealed necessary SCA effects, taking into consideration that both these components can be considered concurrently for exploitation of superior hybrids19,41. Ten F1 hybrids were isolated with high fruit yield and related attributes based on mean performance, significant heterosis and SCA effects that provide an opportunity to utilize them in value-added products and dried spice purposes viz., MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 with erect fruiting habit and that of MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 9-2 × PBC 535, MS 26-1 × VVG, MS 29-2 × DPCh 10 and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22- C with pendent fruits.

Conclusions

Heterosis breeding would have the potential to improve both fresh and dry yields but the availability of F1 hybrids in chilli suitable for processed forms is limited. Exploitation of genetic sex mechanisms such as male sterility for hybrid development offer an advantage over the traditional method of hand emasculation and pollination as it helps to curtail the cost of hybrid seed. The destalking trait finds favour especially in processing industry to reduce cost of post-harvest removal of pedicel. Our results substantiate this hypothesis by identifying hybrids with erect fruiting that help to lower labour cost and ultimately enhances quality of chilli powder and other products. Moreover, erect fruit bearing hybrids showed low incidence of fruit rot infestation under high rainfall area. The commercial exploitation of chilli hybrids paves a way to increase the productivity and quality of produce. Further, combining ability aids in the selection of parents and designing breeding strategies for effective and efficient heterosis breeding programme for red/dry fruit yield along with the industrial traits such as oleoresin, capsaicin and capsanthin content. A significant per se performance, desirable magnitude of heterosis and SCA effects for yield and its attributes helped to identify superior hybrid combinations e.g. MS 9-2 × HPM-2, MS 11-2 × DPCh 40, MS 9-2 × DPCh 40 and MS 9-2 × DPCh 101 with erect fruiting habit and that of MS 9-2 × DPCh 10, MS 26-1 × DPCh 10, MS 9-2 × PBC 535, MS 26-1 × VVG, MS 29-2 × DPCh 10 and MS 26-1 × DPCh 22- C with pendent fruits which would be exploited commercially after multi-location testing and on-farm trials for both fresh market and processing industry.

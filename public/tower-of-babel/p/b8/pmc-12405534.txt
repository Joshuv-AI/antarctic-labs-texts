Circular RNA RORβ regulates TGFβR1 in alcohol-induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation
Authors: Viranuj Sueblinvong, Xian Fan, Harry Kartmouty-Quintana, Benjamin T. Kopp, Bum-Yong Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Alcohol exposure augments the expression and signaling of transforming growth factor-beta (TGFβ), leading to fibroproliferation. We showed that inhibition of TGFβ receptor type 1 (TGFβR1) mitigates the effect of alcohol in the lung. We further demonstrated that alcohol modulates TGFβ signaling, partly through its ability to modify microRNA (miRNA or miR) expressions in the lung. The mechanism by which alcohol mediates miRNA expressions and how its connection to TGFβ signaling has not been well elucidated. Circular RNAs (circRNAs or circ) have emerged as potential therapeutic targets due to their stability, tissue specificity, and ability to modify miRNAs. Human and murine lung fibroblasts (LF) were treated ± ethanol (60 mM). Samples were analyzed for TGFβR1 and circ-RORβ levels. In silico analysis was performed to identify common miRNAs with binding sites for both TGFβR1 and circ-RORβ. The top 10 miRNA levels in human LF (HLF) ± ethanol were analyzed. Human LF were treated with circ-RORβ anti-sense oligonucleotide (ASO) ± ethanol, and samples were collected for miR-140-3p, TGFβR1, fibronectin (FN1), and α-smooth muscle actin (αSMA) levels, and stress fiber formation analyses. In parallel, HLF were treated with mimic miR-140-3p and analyzed for TGFβR1 mRNA levels. HLF were treated ± RORβ silencing RNA, then analyzed for RORβ, circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, and TGFβR1 levels. We showed that ethanol upregulates TGFβR1 and circ-RORβ in ethanol-treated LF. In silico analysis revealed that miR-140-3p has putative binding sites for both TGFβR1 3’ untranslated region (UTR) and circ-RORβ. We demonstrated that ethanol exposure attenuated LF ‘s miR-140-3p expression, and inhibition of circ-RORβ abrogated ethanol-mediated miR-140-3p suppression. Inhibition of circ-RORβ attenuated ethanol-induced TGFβR1, FN1, and αSMA expressions, as well as αSMA stress fiber formation in LF, while inhibition of RORβ did not alter circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, or TGFβR1 levels. Overexpression of miR-140-3p promotes TGFβR1 mRNA degradation and inhibits ethanol-induced TGFβR1 expression while has no impact on circ-RORβ level. Taken together, our findings suggest the potential role of circ-RORβ-miR-140-3p-TGFβR1 axis in alcohol-induced TGFβ signaling and fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation.

Introduction

It is well-recognized that chronic alcohol ingestion independently increases the incidence of and mortality from acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) through transforming growth factor-beta (TGFβ) driven mechanism1–4. We further showed that inhibition of TGFβ receptor type 1 (TGFβR1), also known as activin receptor-like kinase 5 (ALK5), mitigates the effect of alcohol on lung fibroblasts (LF)5,6. These studies suggested that TGFβR1 might be one of the key molecular targets to improve lung repair following acute injury in alcoholic individuals. However, a key mechanism(s) by which alcohol activates TGFβ and its signaling and increases lung susceptibility to fibrotic repair remains elusive.

Alcohol exposure has been shown to induce epigenetic reprogramming that affects non-coding RNA (ncRNA), including microRNA (miRNA or miR) expression, in several tissues including the lung and the liver7–10. However, the mechanisms by which these miRNAs are regulated in alcoholic lungs have not been elucidated. Circular RNAs (circRNAs or circ) are a class of ncRNA characterized by single-stranded RNA generated from the back-splicing of exons, introns, or both, where the 3’ and 5’ ends form a covalent bond, resulting in a closed-loop structure that is resistant to degradation by RNA exonucleases11,12. They can be found across species and are evolutionally conserved11. Emerging evidence indicates that circRNAs regulate cellular functions through modification of various proteins by functioning as miRNA sponges, RNA-binding protein, or directly interacting with RNA11–14. CircRNAs have enormous potential as disease biomarkers because of their resistance to RNA exonucleases as well as their tissue-specific expression. Several studies have reported their functional roles in various diseases, including cancers, diabetes, cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases15,16.

Retinoid acid-related orphan receptor beta (RORβ) is a nuclear receptor involved in several cellular functions, including regulation of cellular redox responses and tissue repair processes17. Our in silico analysis revealed that the RNA strand coding for RORβ can form circRNA or ‘circ-RORβ’. Through an interrogation of the circ-RORβ sequences, focusing on TGFβ and its signaling molecules, including TGFβR1, we identified a potential interaction between circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, and TGFβR1. Accordingly, we speculated that circ-RORβ might have a functional role in alcohol-mediated TGFβ signaling in our experimental model. To our knowledge, there is no published information describing circ-RORβ and its function.

MiR-140 is a known regulator of TGFβR118–20. Specifically, it has been shown that the loss of miR-140 is associated with epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, myofibroblast differentiation, and fibrosis in the liver and the lung18–20. Clinically, the miR-140 expression profile is similar in the brains of chronic alcohol-ingested mice and alcoholic individuals21. However, the regulation of miR-140 has not been well-elucidated. Here, we showed that circ-RORβ was upregulated in ethanol-treated human and murine lung fibroblasts and in the lungs of chronic alcohol-ingested mice. Furthermore, we identified an indirect interaction between circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, and TGFβR1 and found that ethanol exposure modulated the circ-RORβ-miR-140-TGFβR1 interactome, leading to the enhancement of fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation (FMD) in an ethanol-exposed experimental model.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and treatment

Murine LF (MLF) were isolated from the lungs of three-month-old C57BL/6 J mice (Jackson Laboratories, Bar Harbor, ME) as previously described22. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) at Emory University and adhered to institutional guidelines and the ethical standards set by the Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care International (AAALAC). All methods were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations and are reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines (https://arriveguidelines.org). Cells were cultured and expanded in complete MLF culture media (Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium [DMEM, Cellgro, Manassas, VA), 4.5 g/L glucose supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 U/ml streptomycin. MLF were used in experiments between 3 and 8 passages. Cells were subjected to ethanol treatment (60 mM, kept in a close ethanol chamber as previously described)22 in DMEM culture media supplement with 5% FBS and 100 U/ml penicillin/streptomycin in a closed tissue culture chamber with 60 mM alcohol in the bath within a tissue culture incubator. Normal human lung fibroblasts (HLF) from ATCC (CCL-153) (Manassas, VA) were cultured in F-12 K medium supplemented with 10% FBS per manufacturer protocol. In selected studies, cells were transfected with dicer-substrate silencing RNA to RORβ (DsiRORβ)(10 nM; IDT, Coralville, IA), anti-sense oligonucleotide (ASO) circ-RORβ (50 nM; IDT, Coralville, IA), miR-140 mimic or inhibitor (Qiagen, Valencia, CA) or appropriate negative controls as described below.

Overexpression of miR-140-3p

HLF were transfected with mimic hsa-miR-140-3p (mimic miR-140-3p) or mimic control (mimic CON; 5 nM, Qiagen) in serum free media using lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Six hours after transfection, serum-free media were replaced with DMEM culture media supplemented with 5% FBS, and cells were incubated overnight. Cells were treated with ethanol (60 mM) 24 h after transfection, and then continued incubation in culture media supplemented with 5% FBS for 48 h for gene expression analyses.

TGFβR1 stability assay

To assess the role of miR-140-3p on TGFβR1 expression, TGFβR1 mRNA stability assay was performed. Briefly, HLF were transfected with mimic miR-140-3p or mimic CON and incubated overnight in culture media supplemented with 5% FBS. The next day, Actinomycin D (5 μg/ml, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) was added to tissue culture wells. Baseline expression (time 0) was collected at the time of Actinomycin treatment. Cells were collected at 1, 2, 3, and 4 h respectively then analyzed for TGFβR1 mRNA levels. All data were normalized to control mimic group at time 0.

Total RNA isolation and expression analyses

Total RNAs were isolated from HLF and MLF using the mirVana RNA isolation kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher Scientific). First-strand cDNA was synthesized, and quantitative PCR was performed with primers for 18 s, 9 s, TGFβR1, fibronectin, αSMA, and circ-RORβ using iQ SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA) (Table 2). Circ-RORβ primers were designed using circular RNA Interactome (https://circinteractome.nia.nih.gov/). The levels of target messenger RNA (mRNA) and circ-RORβ expression were normalized to 9 s or 18 s housekeeping gene levels, for human and murine samples respectively, and relative expression values were calculated as previously described25.

MicroRNA (miRNA) isolation and expression analyses

The mirVana miRNA isolation kit was used to extract and purify miRNAs according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher Scientific). cDNA was synthesized from 250 ng of miRNA using the miScript II RT kit (Qiagen). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed for RNU6B (Qiagen) and selected miRNA based on in silico analyses were assessed (including miR-140-3p, miR-143, miR-144, miR-153, miR-330, miR-338, miR-409, miR-432, miR-490, and miR-888; Invitrogen) using the QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen). The levels of target miRNA expressions were normalized to RNU6B. The relative levels were determined by the comparative cycle threshold method as previously described9.

Protein isolation and Western blot analyses

Total protein was isolated by using Laemmli sample buffer (Bio-Rad)26. Equal volume of samples was separated on 4–20% gradient sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) gel and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk/Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween 20 (blocking buffer, pH 7.4) for 1 h, the membranes were incubated with antibodies for TGFβR1 (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, 1:500), αSMA (Abcam, Waltham, MA, 1:5,000), fibronectin (FN)(Abcam, 1:10,000), or GAPDH (Sigma-Aldrich, 1:50,000) at 4 °C overnight in blocking buffer. The membranes were incubated for 1 h at room temperature with an appropriate horseradish peroxidase–conjugated secondary antibody prior to Clarity Western ECL substrate (Bio-Rad) in blocking buffer. The immunoreactive bands were captured with a ChemiDox XRS system (Bio-Rad). The relative densitometry of the protein interest was normalized to the densitometry of the GAPDH band from same membrane.

Immunofluorescence

HLFs were cultured in 4-well chamber slides (Nunc Lab-Tek) ± ethanol (60 mM) ± ASO circ-RORβ (50 nM) for 96 h. Cells were fixed, permeabilized, and then stained for αSMA overnight using rabbit anti-human αSMA antibody at a 1:500 dilution at 4 °C (Abcam). The chamber slides were incubated with an Alexa Fluor 488 goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) and DAPI (Vector Labs, Burlingame, CA) and examined using an Olympus BX-41 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Center Valley, PA). Cell count was performed from five random fields per sample (× 20 magnification) from triplicate experiments. Data were expressed as percent of cells with αSMA stress fiber formation compared with total number of cells.

Statistical analyses

Data are expressed as means and standard errors. We employed the Shapiro–Wilk test to test for normality. If the data were not normally distributed, we performed a Mann–Whitney U test or the Kruskal–Wallis analysis of variance (ANOVA) test using GraphPad Prism (San Diego, CA). For all statistical tests, significant differences were accepted at a p value of < 0.05. Where appropriate, Dunn’s multiple comparison correction was applied following the Kruskal–Wallis test to identify specific group differences.

Given that circRNA is formed by the back-splicing of single-stranded RNA followed by the formation of covalent bond between the 3’ and 5’ ends, resulting in a closed-loop structure or circRNA. Discussion

Chronic alcohol ingestion increases the risk of developing Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) in clinical setting3. In an experimental model, we showed that alcohol ‘ethanol’ promoted fibroproliferative disrepair through induction of TGFβ expression and signaling in the lung4. Inhibition of TGFβR1 using TGFβR1 kinase inhibitor (also known as Activin Linked Kinase-5 or ALK5 inhibitor) mitigated ethanol-induced oxidative stress in lung fibroblasts5,6. In this study, we aimed to identify the mechanisms by which alcohol activates TGFβ signaling, focusing on TGFβR1 and its competing endogenous RNAs. We utilized both human and mouse models to corroborate our previous findings in rodents. We demonstrated that ethanol upregulates TGFβR1 in human and murine lung fibroblasts. Additionally, ethanol induces circ-RORβ while suppressing miR-140 expression. Inhibition of circ-RORβ restored miR-140 expression in ethanol-treated human lung fibroblasts (HLF), mitigated ethanol-induced TGFβR1 expression and its downstream targets, including fibronectin and αSMA, and attenuated ethanol-induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation (FMD), as evidenced by reduced cells with αSMA stress fiber formation. Silencing RORβ mRNA did not affect circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, or TGFβR1 expression in HLF, suggesting that circ-RORβ is formed and functions independently of RORβ mRNA.

TGFβ is an evolutionarily conserved growth factor known to participate in fibrogenesis in various tissues28–32. TGFβ is secreted and stored in a latent form by binding to latency-associated peptide33. Upon activation, TGFβ binds to two heteromeric complexes of its receptors, TGFβ receptor type 2 (TGFβR2), resulting in the phosphorylation of TGFβ receptor type 1 (TGFβR1), which lead to the activation of canonical or non-canonical signaling pathways33. Our previous work and others showed that inhibition of TGFβR1 attenuated the effects of ethanol in lung fibroblasts and alveolar epithelial cells5,34. Building on this information, we analyzed the effect of ethanol on TGFβR1 and found that ethanol induced TGFβR1 expression in human and murine lung fibroblasts but did not affect TGFβR2 expression in our experimental models (data not shown). This finding is consistent with work published by Zhou et al., showing that alcohol upregulated TGFβR1 expression, but not TGFβR2, in mouse hippocampus35. However, the mechanism by which ethanol regulates TGFβR1 is to be elucidated.

Retinoid-related orphan receptor beta (RORβ) is part of a nuclear factor family consisting of three subtypes: RORα, β, and γ36. RORβ has been predominantly studied in central nervous system disorders, including epilepsy37. Alcohol has been shown to upregulate RORβ in cerebral organoid generated from human induced pluripotent stem cells (human iPS)38. However, limited data exist on RORβ’s role in the lung or tissue fibrogenesis. We discovered that RORβ could form into a circular RNA, which we will refer to as circ-RORβ. This information led us to speculate that circ-RORβ might have a functional role in ethanol-mediated lung pathobiology. Consistent with our speculation, we found that ethanol exposure induced circ-RORβ expression level in human and murine lung fibroblasts.

CircRNAs are non-coding RNAs emerging as potential novel therapeutic targets due to their resistance to endonucleases and specificity to tissue and disease stage11,12. They have the ability to regulate gene expression through splicing, modification of parental gene transcription, interacting with target proteins, and influence protein translation39. One of the well-recognized functions of circRNAs is acting as miRNA sponge by binding and sequestering miRNAs to prevent their interaction with their target mRNAs, thereby regulating gene expression11–13,39,40. We conducted computer simulations to explore potential connection between circ-RORβ and TGFβR1. We identified several potential common miRNA targets, which are detailed in Table 1. To validate these findings, we performed miRNA expression analyses on the top 10 of potential miRNAs that could interact with both TGFβR1 and circ-RORβ. Among these miRNAs, only miR-140-3p showed a significant suppression due to ethanol exposure. Several reports have shown that miR-140 mediates fibrogenesis by directly targeting TGFβR1 in various experimental models18,19,41,42. Ethanol’s effect on miR-140 expression varies by tissue and disease model. For example, miR-140 was upregulated in extracellular vesicles produced by neural stem cells21. Conversely, the study showed that alcohol exposure suppressed miR-140-3p expression in fetal murine cerebral cortical-derived neurosphere43. Additionally, chronic alcohol ingestion was linked to an upregulation of miR-140 in the liver-derived macrophage in a different experimental model44. These studies used ethanol concentrations of 70 mM to reflect levels of ethanol in the tissue attainable by a fetus after maternal ethanol exposure43,45. However, in our model, we used exposed lung fibroblasts with 60 mM of ethanol which is corresponds to a blood ethanol level of approximately 0.24–0.27% in humans and is typical of the levels in an intoxicated adult. Further, prior study assessing various ethanol doses on lung fibroblast, our group showed that 60 mM is the optimum dose for fibronectin activation in lung fibroblasts22,46. In our model, ethanol downregulated miR-140-3p in human and murine lung fibroblasts. Although most studies investigated the role of miR-140-5p in fibrogenesis, both miR-140-3p and miR-140-5p have binding sites on TGFβR1 3’UTR, implying that both miR-140 s can have a regulatory effect on TGFβR1. However, only miR-140-3p was shown to have the ability to interact with both TGFβR1 and circ-RORβ. To confirm that miR-140-3p mediates TGFβR1 and not circ-RORβ, we showed that miR-140-3p overexpression promotes TGFβR1 mRNA degradation and suppressed TGFβR1 expression in the presence and absence of ethanol while has no impact on circ-RORβ. These data further support our hypothesis that miR-140-3p is downstream effector of circ-RORβ and as a mediator of TGFβR1.

Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs) are small fragments of single-stranded RNA containing 15 to 22 nucleotides designed in antisense orientation to the RNA of interest. ASOs have short half-lives and minimal side effects, making it a favorable therapy for various diseases. In fact, they have been studied in a clinical setting in neurodegenerative disease47. Hybridization of the ASO to the target RNA triggers RNase H cleavage of the RNA, which can inhibit the function of non-coding RNAs or prevent protein translation of mRNAs. To increase nuclease resistance, we added phosphorothioate modifications to the oligo. Additionally, we incorporated modified bases, such as 2MOE in chimeric antisense designs, to enhance nuclease stability and the affinity of the antisense oligo to the target mRNA48. The current study utilized the 2’MOE chemical modification to increase binding affinity, reduce protein binding, and decrease toxicity. However, like all drugs, ASOs can carry the risk of unintended toxicity due to hybridization‐dependent off‐target effects49. These off‐target effects result from complementary binding between the ASO and unintended RNA sequences similar to the target RNA, potentially causing adverse effects by altering the expression of unintended genes50. Evolving studies suggest that oligonucleotide extension has bilateral effects on hybridization-dependent changes in gene expression: one is the decrease of off-target effects based on the reduced number of off-target candidate genes with perfect matches, and the other is the increase of off-target effects based on the increased binding affinity between the ASO and the complementary RNAs that leads to better tolerability for mismatches50. In this study, to reduce off-target effects, we designed 16-nucleotide ASOs with 2’MOE phosphorothioate modification targeting the functional motif mediating the circ-RORβ-miR-140-3p interaction and the circularization elements.

We showed in this study that the inhibition of circ-RORβ was associated with a significant increase in miR-140-3p expression, which was accentuated in ethanol-exposed lung fibroblasts. Possible explanations include a compensatory effect of cells in transcribing miR-140-3p or that circ-RORβ might also target other genes or proteins that contribute to miR-140-3p transcription. Furthermore, the effect of ethanol on miR-140-3p may be dependent on the cell type, tissue, and disease stage. Future studies to identify other targets of circ-RORβ will be necessary to further understand its function in ethanol-exposed experimental models. In parallel, we observed an attenuation of ethanol-induced TGFβR1 expression when circ-RORβ was inhibited. However, despite a modest upregulation of miR-140-3p when circ-RORβ was inhibited in the absence of ethanol, there were no corresponding changes of TGFβR1 expression. It is possible that in the absence of ethanol, the changes on miR-140-3p when circ-RORβ is inhibited are small and do not result in detectable changes in TGFβR1 expression, but these changes become more prominent in the presence of ethanol. In parallel to circ-RORβ ASO-mediated TGFβR1 suppression, circ-RORβ ASO also mitigated ethanol-induced fibronectin and αSMA, and ethanol-induced MFD in human LF.

Although circRNAs are formed from back-splicing of single-stranded RNA11,12, limited information exists about circ-RORβ formation and its interaction with the parental RNA. We sought to determine if there is a correlation between circ-RORβ and RORβ and whether they have overlapping functions. We conducted an experiment to inhibit RORβ messenger RNA (mRNA) using silencing RNA technology. Our results showed that silencing RORβ mRNA did not affect circ-RORβ, miR-140-3p, or TGFβR1. These data suggest that the function of circ-RORβ is independent of RORβ mRNA.

Over the past decade, there has been growing evidence showing the involvement of non-coding RNAs, including miRNA, long non-coding RNA, and circRNA, in pathological conditions in various organs by mediating gene expression and, subsequently, cellular functions51,52. These findings have led to increased interest in developing therapies targeting non-coding RNAs53. For example, a study by Saravanan et al. suggested that miR-24 and miR-466i-5p can inhibit hepatocyte activation by suppressing COX-2 and iNOS51. Furthermore, approximately 35 miRNAs have been confirmed to play significant roles in the cardiovascular system in in vivo models, and several mRNA-targeted therapies, in the form of antisense oligonucleotides and silencing RNA, have been approved by the FDA and European Medicines Agency for clinical use53. More recently, studies have shown the role of targeting circRNA as therapeutic interventions in myocardial infarction, Alzheimer’s disease, cirrhosis, diabetic retinopathy, and various types of cancers53. Lastly, due to their stability and long half-life resulting from exonuclease resistance, synthetic circRNAs have been engineered as preventative and therapeutic tools53,54.

There are several limitations to the current study, including the lack of complementary gain of function of circ-RORβ due to the large size, which makes generating an overexpression plasmid challenging. Future studies are warranted to confirm that circ-RORβ binds to and promotes degradation of miR-140-3p, as well as to investigate the direct interaction between miR-140-3p and TGFβR1. Additionally, experiments to confirm the function of synthetic circ-RORβ in vivo will be necessary to assess its potential for clinical translation. Despite these limitations, this is the first study to describe a possible function of circ-RORβ in ethanol-mediated tissue dysfunction.

In conclusion, we presented new evidence that ethanol modulates the circ-RORβ-miR-140-3p axis, leading to an enhancement of TGFβ signaling though upregulation of TGFβR1 in lung fibroblasts. More importantly, we demonstrated that circ-RORβ might be involved in fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation (FMD), which is a hallmark of tissue fibrogenesis (Fig. 7C). Future studies to confirm our findings in the clinical setting are warranted. Therapies aiming to neutralize circ-RORβ could promote optimal lung recovery from ARDS in high-risk individuals with chronic alcohol use disorder.

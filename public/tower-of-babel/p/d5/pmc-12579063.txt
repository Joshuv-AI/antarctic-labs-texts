B7-H3: a promising target for immunotherapy in glioma
Authors: Xin-Li Feng, Gang Su, Qi Jia, Qiong-Hui Wu, Zhen-Chang Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Glioma is one of the most malignant tumors in the brain, with high tumor heterogeneity. Traditional treatment methods, such as surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy, face numerous challenges and still pose serious threats to human health. Therefore, it is urgent to explore new treatment directions. The immune checkpoint B7-H3 has attracted much attention due to its significant immunomodulatory function in various cancers. Studies have shown that it is highly expressed in glioma and lowly expressed in normal brain tissue. It not only has immunosuppressive function and induces immune escape in glioma, but also promotes the proliferation, migration and angiogenesis of glioma cells. Moreover, there are many immunotherapy strategies targeting B7-H3, such as monoclonal antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates (ADC) and chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy. Therefore, B7-H3 is regarded as a potential target for glioma immunotherapy. This article systematically reviews the occurrence and development process of B7-H3 in glioma, changes in the immune microenvironment, and related treatment methods. Through analysis of several databases such as TCGA, CTPAC, HPA and GEPIA, this study explores the expression of B7-H3 in glioma, its correlation with prognosis and its correlation with the glioma immune microenvironment, providing a new research target for the diagnosis and treatment of glioma.

Background

Glioma, which originates from glial cells, is one of the most common malignant brain tumors and is classified into four grades according to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification [1, 2]. Grades 1–2 are low-grade gliomas (LGG), and grades 3–4 are high-grade gliomas (HGG). Among these, glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive form and is always classified as grade 4 [1, 2]. GBM has a 5-year survival rate of merely 2–10%, making it one of the deadliest brain tumors [3]. The incidence rate of gliomas is higher in men than in women, while the mortality rate is higher in female patients than in male patients [4]. In recent years, with the continuous deepening of research on glioma, some progress has been made in its diagnosis and treatment. Researchers have applied methods such as immunotherapy, combination therapy, and molecular marker targeting to neuroglioma [5, 6]. However, the survival rate and prognosis of patients have not improved significantly, and gliomas still pose a serious threat to human health [7]. Therefore, exploring more effective treatment strategies and identifying new therapeutic targets are crucial for improving the prognosis and survival rate of glioma patients. Studies have found that B7-H3 shows significant differential expression in the cerebrospinal fluid and blood of glioma patients and is closely related to the prognosis of patients, suggesting that it may serve as a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target for glioma [8]. At present, researchers are actively exploring the application of B7-H3 in immunotherapy, including cell therapy based on CAR-T/CAR-NK and Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs). These studies provide new research directions for the treatment of glioma [9].

In recent years, the role of the B7 family proteins in cancer has drawn extensive attention. Studies have revealed that its family members play significant roles in immunoregulation in tumors, influencing the biological functions of tumor cells, and altering the tumor microenvironment, etc [10, 11]. Similarly, programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), which is also an immune checkpoint, and its ligand have achieved tremendous success in clinical tumor-targeted therapy. Zhang et al. [12] analyzed the relevant expression levels of B7 family members in glioma and discovered that the expression level of B7-H3 was significantly higher than that of other family members. This indicates that targeting B7-H3 might have potential advantages in the treatment of glioma.

B7 Homolog 3 Protein (B7-H3, also known as CD276 and B7 Relative Protein 2 (B7RP-2)) is a type I transmembrane protein that was first identified in the cDNA library of dendritic cells in 2001 [13], Research has shown that B7-H3 is expressed at a low level in normal human tissues but is highly expressed in gliomas, and the high expression of B7-H3 is significantly positively correlated with the low survival rate of patients [12]. B7-H3 can promote immune escape and growth of tumor cells by inhibiting immune cell infiltration, the functions of NK cells, T cells, and macrophages, and the release of cytokines. It can also facilitate the proliferation, invasion, and migration of glioma cells through non-immune pathways, making B7-H3 a promising target for immunotherapy of glioma [14]. This review systematically summarizes the basic structure, expression, and function of B7-H3 in glioma, and focuses on the immunotherapeutic strategies and advantages targeting B7-H3, highlighting its significance as an important target in glioma immunotherapy.

Methodology

To explore CD276 expression levels in glioma, We analyzed the expression sequences and clinical information of glioma obtained from TCGA, including 5 normal samples and 170 tumor samples. Using the Limma software package in R language, we evaluated the expression differences of CD276 between glioma tissues and their corresponding normal brain tissues in the TCGA database. The Human Protein Atlas (HPA, https://www.proteinatlas.org/), and the University of Alabama at Birmingham Cancer data analysis Portal (UALCAN, https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/) were used to analyze the protein expression levels of CD276 in gliomas [15]. We conducted an analysis of the expression level of the CD276 gene and its correlation with patient survival using the Interactive Analysis Platform for Gene Expression Profiles (GEPIA, http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#analysis). GEPIA integrates a large amount of human sample data from the TCGA and GTEx databases and provides reliable functions for gene expression and survival analysis [16]. Based on this platform, we divided the patients into the high-expression group and the low-expression group of CD276, and compared the survival differences between the two groups to assess the potential impact of CD276 expression level on patient prognosis.

To predict the levels of immune cell infiltration (ICI) in the glioma tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), we estimated the infiltration percentages of 22 different immune cell types in the glioma TIME using the Cell-type Identification By Estimating Relative Subsets Of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT) method, based on 1,000 permutations of the leukocyte signature matrix (LM22). Spearman correlation analysis was performed to investigate the relationship between CD276 expression and the infiltration levels of 22 tumor-infiltrating immune cell types. Additionally, Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine the correlation between CD276 expression and immune checkpoint (ICP) genes, with an absolute correlation coefficient threshold of > 0.2. This threshold was chosen based on previous studies suggesting that even weak correlations (|r| >0.2) may have biological significance in tumor-immune interactions [17]. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was conducted to compare differences between the CD276 high-expression and low-expression groups.

The structure and expression of B7-H3

B7-H3 is a type I transmembrane protein composed of 316 amino acids. Its gene is located on human chromosome 15 and mouse chromosome 9. It belongs to the B7 family, sharing approximately 20–27% of amino acid sequences with other B7 family members such as immune checkpoint molecules PD-L1 and CD86 [18, 19]. The primary structure of B7-H3 consists of an extracellular Ig-like domain, a transmembrane region, and a short intracellular domain [19]. Three subtypes of B7-H3 have been discovered: the longest 4IgB7-H3 contains two pairs of identical IgV (immunoglobulin variable)-like and IgC (immunoglobulin constant)-like domains. It can transmit inhibitory signals, thereby regulating the activation, proliferation, and cytokine secretion of T cells, contributing to maintaining immune homeostasis and avoiding the occurrence of excessive immune responses; the intermediate subtype 3Ig B7-H3 lacks the first pair of IgV-like domains. Additionally, it has been hypothesized that 3IgB7-H3 may support tumor immune escape; however, this remains speculative, and further experimental evidence is needed to validate this hypothesis; the shortest 2IgB7-H3 contains a pair of IgV and a pair of IgC domains. By inhibiting the anti-tumor activity of T cells, it can promote the immune escape of tumor cells. Among them, 2IgB7-H3 is dominant in highly malignant gliomas, 4IgB7-H3 is widely expressed, and 3IgB7-H3 has not been detected in any gliomas. As of now, although different subtypes of B7-H3 show differences in their expression patterns and regulatory mechanisms, the functions of these subtypes remain unclear. Usually, they are regarded as the same type.They all have similar inhibitory functions and are currently regarded as potential immunotherapy targets [20].

Besides the widely studied membrane-bound B7-H3 (mB7-H3), a “soluble” form of B7-H3 (sB7-H3) has also been detected in the serum. sB7-H3 can be released by monocytes, activated T lymphocytes, and mB7-H3, and its presence in the serum may imply a more complex immune regulatory network and tumor microenvironment interaction mechanism, opening up new exploration directions and thinking dimensions for the related research fields [21]. Although the mRNA of B7-H3 is widely distributed in normal tissues, due to the strict post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms, its protein expression level is rather limited and mainly exists in cells such as endothelial cells, osteoblasts, and activated T lymphocytes. However, it has been discovered that B7-H3 is upregulated in a variety of tumors, and it is not only highly expressed on the surface of tumor cells but also in cells within the tumor microenvironment, such as tumor-associated macrophages and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. This phenomenon is closely associated with the occurrence and development of tumors and has made B7-H3 one of the focuses that attract much attention in the field of tumor research [22].

Expression of B7-H3 in glioma and its correlation with prognosis

A multitude of studies have indicated that the expression level of B7-H3 is significantly and positively correlated with the malignancy degree of glioma. In the tumor tissues of patients with high-grade glioma, the expression level of B7-H3 is higher than that in tissues of low-grade glioma and normal brain tissues, and the prognosis of these patients is relatively poorer [12, 23–26]. Additionally, Baral et al. [8] detected the presence of B7-H3 not only in tumor tissues but also in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of glioma patients. They discovered that the expression of sB7-H3 in the CSF of LGG patients was higher than that of patients with moderate traumatic brain injury. Moreover, it was observed that the expression of sB7-H3 in the serum of healthy individuals was as high as that of glioma patients. Furthermore, in the brain tissues of patients with moderate brain injury, the expression of sB7-H3 was lower than that of glioma patients’ brain tumor tissues, and it was also found to be related to the grade of glioma. This indicates that the detection of B7-H3 in CSF can provide a basis for the early diagnosis, disease assessment, and prognosis determination of glioma. As a type I transmembrane protein, B7-H3 is expressed not only on the cell membrane of tumor cells but also in the cytoplasm in glioma. The expression of different subtypes of B7-H3 also varies at different stages of the disease. For instance, 4IgB7-H3 is mainly expressed in newly diagnosed glioblastoma (GBM), while 2IgB7-H3 is highly expressed in recurrent GBM. This characteristic confers extremely significant clinical value for implementing 4IgB7-H3-targeted therapy in newly diagnosed GBM patients and relying on 2IgB7-H3 to monitor glioma recurrence and therapeutic responses [27].

The expression level of B7-H3 is also modulated by multiple factors. Studies have revealed that in glioma patients, B7-H3 shows an upward trend with increasing age [28]. Petterson et al. [29] discovered that B7-H3 expression is higher in the hypoxic regions of glioblastoma. Furthermore, mutations of isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) can also influence the expression of B7-H3 in glioma. The expression level of B7-H3 is higher in IDH wild-type gliomas than in IDH mutant ones. The methylation of the B7-H3 promoter and the microRNA-29 family exerts a negative regulatory effect on B7-H3 expression [30].

The aforementioned studies all indicate that B7-H3 is a promising therapeutic target for glioma. We conducted an analysis of the glioma mRNA data in the TCGA database using the R language, comparing the differential expression of B7-H3 in glioma and normal tissues. Since the TCGA database can only reflect the mRNA level of genes, we further analyzed the CPTAC and HPA databases to examine the protein expression level of B7-H3. Subsequently, we utilized the GEPIA dataset to analyze the influence of B7-H3 on the survival period of glioma patients. The abovementioned results demonstrate that both the mRNA and protein expression levels of B7-H3 in glioma are relatively high, and the high expression of B7-H3 is significantly correlated with the poor prognosis of glioma patients (Fig. 1), which is in accordance with our previous results. Wang et al. [28] also applied bioinformatics analysis to evaluate the differential expression of B7-H3 between glioma and normal tissues. The results showed that the expression of B7-H3 in glioma tissues was significantly higher than that in normal tissues, and it significantly increased with the increase of glioma grade. The expression of B7-H3 was significantly correlated with WHO grade and was associated with poorer survival rate. In addition, the expression of B7-H3 was related to the recurrence of glioma and the age of patients. They also found that temozolomide (TMZ) treatment could promote the expression of B7-H3. Based on a recent retrospective cohort study of 86 IDH wild-type glioblastoma patients, the study found that increased B7-H3 expression in tumor tissues of grade 4 IDH wild-type glioblastoma patients was strongly associated with significantly reduced overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Multivariate regression analysis further confirmed that the expression level of B7-H3 had significant predictive value for clinical prognosis of patients [31]. This finding not only provides a new biomarker for the prognosis assessment of glioblastoma, but also provides an important theoretical basis for the development of targeted therapy strategies against B7-H3, which is expected to open up a new therapeutic approach for improving the clinical prognosis of IDH wild-type glioblastoma patients.

B7-H3 is associated with proliferation, invasion, and migration of glioma

Studies have demonstrated that B7-H3 promotes the survival and proliferation of tumor cells by activating multiple pro-survival signaling pathways in various tumor cells [32, 33]. In gliomas, the high expression of B7-H3 is also capable of facilitating the proliferation, invasion, and migration of glioma cells [34]. The research conducted by Lemke et al. [35] revealed that 4IgB7-H3 promotes the invasion of glioma cells by inhibiting natural killer cell-mediated tumor cell lysis, thereby facilitating their invasion into the surrounding brain tissue. When the 4IgB7-H3 gene was experimentally silenced, the migration and invasion capabilities of glioma cells were significantly reduced. Further investigations have indicated that these effects are mediated through the activation of the JAK2/STAT3/Slug signaling pathway in B7-H3 overexpressing glioma cells [34], Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a crucial mechanism promoting cancer cell invasion and metastasis [36], and MMP-2 and MMP-9, members of the MMP enzyme family, play a key role in EMT and cancer metastasis [37], B7-H3 can also induce the EMT process by downregulating E-cadherin and upregulating the expression of MMP-2/−9, thereby enhancing the invasiveness of glioma cells [34]. The TGF-β pathway plays an active role in maintaining and regulating the functions of glioma stem cells (GSCs), and SMAD6, one of the members of the SMAD protein family, is an important inhibitor of the TGF-β pathway. The study by Zhang [38] discovered that knockdown of B7-H3 could downregulate SMAD6 expression, simultaneously enhance SMAD1 phosphorylation and induce SMAD4 expression, activate the TGF-β signaling pathway, and lead to a decrease in the expression of the transcription factor MYC, thereby inhibiting the proliferation and progression of glioma cells. The aforementioned studies have elaborated in detail the involvement of B7-H3 in the proliferation and invasion processes of gliomas and the associated molecular mechanisms (Fig. 2). Therefore, in future therapeutic strategies for gliomas, in addition to employing means to knockdown the expression level of B7-H3, it is also possible to inhibit certain key signaling pathways involved in B7-H3, thereby opening up new and effective avenues for the treatment of gliomas.

B7-H3 and angiogenesis in glioma

Angiogenesis is a characteristic hallmark of tumors. In addition to suppressing tumor antigen-specific immune responses to achieve immune evasion and promote tumor development in malignant tumors, B7-H3 also possesses non-immune pro-tumor functions, such as angiogenesis and endothelial-mesenchymal transition [19]. Through the analysis of the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) and the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) datasets, Wang et al. [30] confirmed the existence of a correlation between B7-H3 and glioma angiogenesis. The experimental study conducted by Duerinck et al. [39] revealed that B7-H3 is highly expressed in glioma endothelial cells and can directly promote the expression of key angiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) by activating the intracellular signaling pathway NF-KB [40]. This indicates that B7-H3 plays a crucial role in glioma angiogenesis and tumor progression and can serve as a potential therapeutic strategy for glioma. Currently, there are relatively few studies on the role of B7-H3 in glioma angiogenesis, and further exploration is required.

B7-H3 in the immune microenvironment of glioma

B7-H3 is an immune checkpoint molecule with dual functions, exerting both stimulatory and immunosuppressive effects on the immune system. However, its immunosuppressive role predominates in glioblastoma. T cells play a vital role in the recognition and clearance of tumor cells within the tumor microenvironment. B7-H3 promotes the secretion of IL-10 and TGF-β1, interferes with normal co-stimulatory signaling pathways, and hinders the normal activation of immune cells, thereby suppressing the activity of immune cells such as CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, γδT cells, and macrophages. B7-H3 also regulates the activity of natural killer (NK) cells. In the glioma microenvironment, B7-H3 inhibits the release of cytotoxic mediators such as perforin and granzymes by NK cells, reducing their cytotoxicity and enabling tumor cells to evade NK cell-mediated killing, thereby contributing to the formation of a tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment [19, 41]. Macrophages also hold a dominant position in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Under the induction of various TME signals, macrophages can polarize into classically activated macrophages (M1) and alternatively activated macrophages (M2). Research has revealed that B7-H3 promotes the polarization of M2 and the transformation of the M1 phenotype to the M2 phenotype in tumor tissues. The excessive presence of M2 in tumor tissues is a significant factor in the establishment of an immunosuppressive TME, facilitating the growth of tumor cells and the occurrence of tumor immune escape [22, 42]. The three subtypes (2Ig, 3Ig, and 4Ig) of B7-H3 play distinct roles in glioblastoma. Among them, 2Ig predominates in high-grade glioblastoma. The research conducted by Shen et al. [20] discovered that this is inseparable from the promotional effect of 5-methylcytosine reader Y box-binding protein 1 (YBX1). The NOP2/Sun RNA methyltransferase 2 (NSUN2)/YBX1 axis promotes the abundant generation of 2Ig B7-H3 in glioblastoma, resulting in a large accumulation on the cell surface. This can prevent T cells from effectively recognizing sufficient 4Ig B7-H3 to generate a potent immune response, thereby enhancing the immune evasion ability of glioblastoma. The above research has a synergistic effect on the suppressive immune microenvironment of glioblastoma, contributing to the insensitivity and drug resistance of glioblastoma to conventional treatment.

Additionally, B7-H3 also has a co-stimulatory effect on the immune system. It promotes the proliferation of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, enhances the induction of cytotoxic T cells, and selectively stimulates the production of interferon-γ (IFN-γ) in the presence of T cell receptor signaling, strengthening the body’s anti-tumor immune defense mechanisms [13]. These functions indicate that B7-H3 has a multi-faceted role in immune regulation, and the various mechanisms in the immune microenvironment of glioblastoma can all become potential immunotherapeutic directions. However, specific strategies still require further investigation.

We once again evaluated the correlation between the expression of the CD276 gene and the levels of immune cell infiltration (ICI) in glioma, as well as the expression of immune checkpoint (ICP) genes, through bioinformatics analysis. The infiltration proportions of 22 types of immune cells in the glioma immune microenvironment were estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm, and the association between CD276 expression and ICI levels was explored through Spearman correlation analysis. Additionally, we analyzed the association between CD276 and ICP genes in glioma, and the results are presented in Fig. 3. These results indicate that CD276 might play a significant role in the glioma immune microenvironment by regulating ICI and ICP molecules, which is in line with the previous research findings. Wang et al. [28] research demonstrated a positive correlation between B7-H3 and PD-L1, aligning with the findings of our bioinformatics analysis.

Therapies related to B7-H3

CAR-T/CAR-NK cell therapy

CAR-T cell therapy holds significant significance in the field of cancer treatment and has been approved for the treatment of lymphoma and acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Given its successful application in the treatment of hematological malignancies, researchers have shown a keen interest in applying CAR-T cell therapy to the treatment of gliomas [43]. Due to the high expression of the immune checkpoint B7-H3 in gliomas and its crucial role in their malignant progression, previous studies have predicted that CAR-T cells targeting B7-H3 may have good potential application value for the treatment of gliomas, presenting as a promising therapeutic target [44]. Currently, CAR-T targets are typically designed based on 4Ig. Although 4Ig dominates in a few glioma samples, 2Ig shows specifically high expression in patients with high-grade gliomas, suggesting that designing CAR-T cell therapy targeting 2Ig might become a novel strategy for treating high-grade gliomas [45]. Tang et al. [46] found through in vitro experiments that B7-H3-specific CAR T cells have obvious cytotoxic effects on GBM cells, can significantly induce tumor regression in glioblastoma models, prolong the survival time of GBM model mice, and show strong anti-tumor activity. Although the expression level of B7-H3 is low in normal tissues, the off-target toxicity that CAR T cell therapy may cause cannot be ignored, and how to enhance anti-tumor efficacy while ensuring patient safety is a key challenge to be solved in clinical application. Wang et al. [47] also found through in vitro and in vivo experiments that radiotherapy can significantly enhance the infiltration and killing ability of B7-H3-CAR-T cells against gliomas. Moreover, not only were all tumor cells on the irradiated side completely eradicated, but the CAR-T tumor-killing ability on the non-irradiated side was also enhanced, showing a distant effect and synergy. In terms of clinical trials, Tang et al. [48] used third-generation CAR-T cells to treat a GBM patient who relapsed after two surgeries and radiotherapy in 2021. The first GBM patient participating in this study received direct infusion of B7-H3-targeting CAR-T cells into the tumor cavity through an Ommaya device. The patient showed a significant reduction in recurrent tumors after the first infusion, and the clinical response lasted for approximately 50 days with good tolerance. However, the patient began to experience drowsiness and tumor recurrence at the start of the sixth cycle, and the experiment was terminated, considering the occurrence of tumor drug resistance as the main reason. This suggests that the antitumor efficacy of CAR T cells may decrease with the extension of treatment time, which will lead to the development of anti-tumor resistance, which is one of the major challenges of clinical treatment.

In the tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment, numerous tumor cells evade the immune surveillance of αβ T cells by downregulating MHC molecules, resulting in drug resistance. However, γδ T cells, composed of the γ and δ chains, are not subject to this restriction and can effectively recognize and kill these tumor cells. Hence, γδ T cells can also serve as an alternative source for CAR-T cells [49]. Vγ9Vδ2 T cells derived from the peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) of healthy individuals can suppress the proliferation and growth of GBM cells both in vivo and in vitro. Researchers employed genetic engineering approaches to transform Vγ9Vδ2 T cells into Car-B7-H3-γδT cells. These cells can not only recognize tumors via the B7-H3 CAR but also possess the ability to eliminate tumors using phosphoantigens. Compared with unmodified Vγ9Vδ2 T cells, Car-B7-H3-γδT cells exhibited superior performance in inhibiting the proliferation of GBM cells in vitro and enhancing the overall survival rate of tumor-bearing mice in vivo. This indicates that genetic modification of Vγ9Vδ2 T cells can significantly enhance the immunotherapeutic effect of GBM based on γδT cells and broaden its clinical application prospects [50].

Apart from CAR-T cells, CAR-NK cells targeting B7-H3 also have the potential to eliminate GBM. Unlike T cells, which depend on complex antigen presentation and T cell receptor recognition processes, NK cells inherently possess the natural ability to recognize and kill tumor cells. The experimental research by Tachi et al. [51] reported that umbilical cord blood-derived B7-H3 CAR-NK cells demonstrated significant cytotoxicity against patient-derived GBM cells in vitro. Just like CAR-T cells, CAR-NK cells also have cytotoxic effects and may trigger off-target toxicity. Therefore, while enhancing the anti-tumor efficacy, how to effectively ensure the safety of patients has become a core issue that urgently needs to be addressed in clinical applications. Additionally, intracranial injection of CAR-NK cells targeting B7-H3 exhibited significant anti-tumor effects on patient-derived GBM xenografts. Nevertheless, studies and clinical applications of CAR-NK cell therapy targeting B7-H3 for glioblastoma are relatively scarce. This might be attributed to the inadequate design of CAR molecules, which fail to fully unleash functional NK cells, or the relatively short persistence of NK cells in vivo. Compared to CAR-T cells, their expansion and survival capabilities in vivo are limited. In the future, it is essential to undertake more detailed research and analysis on B7-H3 CAR-NK cells and their improved forms to further explore their application potential and value in GBM treatment and provide a more solid theoretical basis and practical guidance for clinical treatment.

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADC)/radiopharmaceutical conjugates (RDC) therapy

ADC is a kind of drug that combines the specificity of monoclonal antibodies with the anti-tumor activity of cytotoxic drugs. When targeting B7-H3, the antibody portion can specifically recognize and bind the B7-H3 protein on the surface of glioma cells, precisely transporting cytotoxic drugs to tumor cells and significantly enhancing the drug’s efficacy and specificity. Currently, extensive exploration has been conducted on the clinical data of ADCs targeting different targets in glioblastoma [52]. Mao et al. [53] developed a homogeneous ADC 401-4, with a drug-to-antibody ratio of 4. In vitro experiments revealed that 401-4 exhibited specific killing effects on tumor cells expressing B7-H3, and the killing effect on different glioblastoma cells increased with the increase in B7-H3 expression level. Further in vivo imaging studies demonstrated that the conjugate accumulated in the tumor area and had the ability of targeted specific delivery, showing significant anti-tumor activity against U87-derived tumor xenografts, and its anti-tumor effect was also drug dose-dependent. Coy et al. [54] demonstrated through bioinformatics analysis that the expression of target antigens in the core of different spatial regions of glioblastoma was similar, indicating that antigen expression and sensitivity to ADC therapy were relatively consistent in tumors. It was also proposed that adjuvant radiotherapy had an auxiliary amplification effect on the efficacy of ADC therapy, suggesting the possibility of combined therapy. Although there are currently few studies on B7-H3-targeting ADC drugs for glioma, existing research has proven that it is a promising target and is expected to become one of the effective treatment approaches for overcoming this disease in the future.

Radioisotope-conjugated drugs (RDCs) represent an emerging strategy for the treatment of gliomas. RDCs also leverage the targeting ability of antibodies, carrying radioactive nuclides. Once the antibody moiety binds to B7-H3 on the surface of tumor cells, the radioactive nuclide exerts its effect locally in the tumor, leading to cell death. Zheng [55] successfully developed a novel antibody, 131I-hu4G4, which demonstrates outstanding binding specificity and high affinity for the B7-H3 antigen. In the orthotopic model of glioma, 131I-hu4G4 exhibits remarkable antitumor activity and is capable of enhancing the antitumor immune response. Thus, it is regarded as a highly potential candidate for radioimmunotherapy of glioblastoma. This achievement paves a new way for the treatment of glioblastoma. Its unique mechanism of action not only possesses potent cytotoxicity but also enables tumor monitoring through imaging techniques, and it is expected that its safety and efficacy will be further verified in subsequent clinical studies and practices.

Monoclonal antibody and bispecific antibody therapies

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) can precisely recognize antigens on the surface of tumor cells while having limited binding to normal tissue cells. They have currently become one of the standard approaches for treating hematological malignancies and some solid cancers [56]. Loo et al. [57] constructed a B7-H3 mAb specifically targeting tumors, named MGA271. In vitro experiments revealed that cell lines with high levels of B7-H3 expression on the cell surface exhibited strong sensitivity to MGA271-mediated antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC). In vivo experiments also observed the effective anti-tumor activity of MGA271 in tumor xenograft models. Although the experiments were not designed for glioma, the outstanding performance of MGA271 offers a glimpse of the potential of B7-H3 mAbs for effective anti-tumor immunotherapy. Wang et al. [25] successfully prepared a novel mouse anti-human 2IgB7-H3-specific mAb and discovered that 2IgB7-H3 was highly expressed only in glioma tissues but had extremely weak or undetectable expression in normal brain tissues. This implies that 2IgB7-H3 mAb might be an effective tool for the diagnosis and treatment of glioma as it can block immune suppressive signals in glioma by targeting B7-H3 and indirectly mediate the ADCC effect to kill tumor cells. However, the specific effects require further validation through experimental studies.

Bispecific antibodies (bsAbs), being artificially designed redirected proteins, possess unique dual-affinity characteristics compared to traditional monoclonal antibodies (mAbs). This enables them to act on two distinct antigens simultaneously, with the functions of redirecting T cells to kill tumor cells and enhancing immune synergy [58]. Fan et al. [59] successfully developed a matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2)-sensitive anti-B7-H3×CD3 nanocarrier based on bispecific antibodies (biAb) through research. The study revealed that, on the one hand, it can precisely target the B7-H3 antigen on the surface of GBM cells; on the other hand, it can combine with immune cells, thereby activating the killing behavior of immune cells against tumor cells. Moreover, the immune response mediated by the B7-H3 bispecific antibody can induce ferroptosis in tumor cells, further enhancing the anti-tumor effect. This has achieved a complementary advantage between the two treatment strategies, ultimately generating an extremely significant synergistic effect.

Based on the aforementioned circumstances, monoclonal/bispecific antibodies targeting B7-H3 have extremely significant research value and application potential in the diagnosis and treatment of glioblastoma. Moreover, attention can be simultaneously focused on exploring combined drug regimens to facilitate the application of such immunotherapy in clinical practice.

Outlook

B7-H3, as an immune checkpoint protein, is highly expressed on the surface of various tumor cells such as glioblastoma, yet its expression level is relatively low in normal tissues. This differential expression renders it a highly attractive therapeutic target for tumors. Numerous studies have verified its involvement in multiple processes of glioblastoma, including proliferation, invasion, migration, and the establishment of an immunosuppressive microenvironment, and it is closely associated with the malignancy grade of glioblastoma and the prognosis of patients. Hence, it is regarded as a highly potential target for the diagnosis and treatment of glioblastoma. Currently, several aspects still require further investigation, such as the signaling pathways mediated by B7-H3 in glioblastoma, whether B7-H3 can serve as a biomarker for glioblastoma, the clinical safety and efficacy of CAR-T/CAR-NK cell therapy targeting B7-H3 in glioblastoma, the research and development of monoclonal antibodies and bispecific antibody drugs targeting B7-H3 for glioblastoma, and the exploration of synergistic treatment among existing immunotherapies. Furthermore, considering the high heterogeneity of glioblastoma, significant differences in B7-H3 expression and function might exist among individual patients and different cell subsets within the same tumor. How to achieve individualized precision medicine is also one of the crucial directions for future research. With the continuous advancement of research and technological innovation, it is anticipated that a revolutionary breakthrough will be brought to the diagnosis and treatment of glioblastoma in the near future, offering new hope to a large number of patients.

Limitations

Our study, relying solely on data from TCGA, CTPAC, HPA, and GEPIA databases, has several limitations. Database - specific biases in sampling and inconsistent data collection methods across these databases may lead to inaccurate or unrepresentative results for B7-H3 analysis in gliomas. Since no experimental validation was done, we can only establish associations, not causation, between B7-H3 expression, glioma prognosis, and the immune microenvironment. The high heterogeneity of glioma subtypes and lack of external validation also limit the generalizability of our findings. Moreover, the bioinformatics algorithms used are assumption-based, and their results lack experimental verification, casting doubt on the interpretation’s accuracy.

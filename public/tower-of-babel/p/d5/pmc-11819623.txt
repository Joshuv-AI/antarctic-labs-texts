Journey of Oral Pathology in India: Academics to Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Jagdish V. Tupkari
Published: 2024

PURPOSE

Through this editorial, the author aims to outline the evolution of Oral Pathology in India, so that the students of this subject will receive a background of their specialty and how it has changed over the years. The present status, with progress seen in leaps and bounds, and some promising future considerations are also discussed.

THE PAST

Today, we stand on the shoulders of several giants, some of whom are no longer in our midst. Lest we forget their contribution, it is important to pay homage to them and acknowledge the foundation that they have laid for us. It is beyond the scope of this article to name all the teachers who have contributed in great measure, but the author would definitely like to mention one. The journey of Oral Pathology as a specialty of the Dental Sciences began in the year 1960, when Dr. H. M. Dholakia instituted the first Post Graduate course in the subject at Nair Hospital Dental College, Mumbai.[1]

Several students from the first generations taught by Dr. Dholakia Sir migrated to different parts of our country, carrying with them The Torch of Oral Pathology and established an Oral Pathology Department in various institutions. The sole objective of the department was to cater to the academic needs and enhance the knowledge of the budding dentists through the subjects of Dental Anatomy and Histology and Oral Pathology and Microbiology.

Gradually, the Oral Pathology department also became active in Clinical and Investigative Pathology. From the days of the analog light microscope, we have reached the days of multi-viewer electrically powered microscopes. In addition to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and special stains, we now employ adjunct techniques like Histochemistry, Immunohistochemistry (IHC), Immunofluorescence, Polymerase Chain Reaction and Genetic analysis.

THE PRESENT

In the present scenario, there are almost 323 colleges providing undergraduate course, 281 college offering Masters in Dentistry with 7178 seats and more than 500 seats are available for Masters in Oral Pathology and Microbiology in India.[2] Some institutes may not have a well-established department, wherein the subject remains restricted to academic purpose.

However, there has been a steady focus on increasing both the quantity and quality of educational institutions, and this must remain an ongoing process. The increase in post-graduate education will benefit both dental professionals and the general public by having higher availability of quality dental care.

The past two decades have marked the beginning of the Ph.D. era, where an increasing number of interested researchers are registering as Ph.D Scholars. This trend is sure to continue as professionals, realise the benefits of super-specialisation, both academically and for employment purposes. The completion of Ph.D opens up several avenues for further research in the pharmaceutical sector, biomedical engineering, development of materials, therapeutics, preventive medicine, Forensic Odontology and Paleodontology, just to name a few.

There has been a changing trend in Oral Pathology since its inception in India. Today, scientific media has also shifted from the print media to digital media, Journal Clubs and Case Discussions from physical interactions to being critically evaluated on online platforms too. As Oral Pathology and Dental teaching technologies evolved, the dissertation and research topics changed from simple topics like Clinical Pathology to H and E observations to fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) to IHC to Advanced Microscopy to ultra-modern methods of investigations.

CONSIDERATIONS FOR THE FUTURE

Today, as we stand at the threshold of rapid evolution, the author proposes the following to streamline our specialty:

(1)Channelising all oral pathological lesions towards dedicated Oral Pathology Laboratories – this will increase the number of cases which our specialists will receive and lead us further as stated in point 2.(2)Centralised collection of clinical case data from hospitals and private practicing dental surgeons – the appropriate recording of data will help in epidemiological studies, guiding National policy creation, public health drives, etc.(3)Using Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a supportive role – it will be advantageous to digitise the above data, and in the future, AI algorithms can be created based on how we intend to apply the data. AI can also be used as an adjunct in enhancing the diagnostic skill of PGs and Oral Pathologists.(4)Enhancing the scope of Forensic Stomatology – which has gained momentum due to increasing natural and man-made calamities. This field needs to be explored further.(5)Digitisation of Histopathology slides – this would allow easy collaboration, referrals and discussions across the nation. Students from different parts of the country would be able to view the digitised slides of rare lesions, which can be uploaded by various institutes on a common platform.(6)Instituting a National Repository of Oral Pathology – the author also proposes to institute a National Repository of Oral Pathology Research Work wherein all past, future and current research projects are documented.

Today in India there are numerous Oral Pathology post-graduate theses written every year. Any researcher spends considerable time and effort towards his/her research work. The research topics are usually based on common lesions and available techniques. What research is being done in one part of the country is hardly known in the other areas. It is quite possible that there is a repetition of topics, lesions, techniques, etc., The repository will include a ready reference of the above. Thus, the repetition of topics can be avoided, effort and time can be directed towards newer or unexplored areas of research. In case of similar topics of importance, one can connect with the other researcher for troubleshooting and collaboration to strengthen the study. Since the information would be centrally available, any authorised individual can access it to enhance their understanding of that topic. This would benefit the Guides, students and researchers.

Each dissertation has its own data which are collected during the research, based on the topic and the method of study. When one considers all the theses together, there is a large amount of data collected every year, which is yet largely unorganised at a national scale. The National Repository would help in this compilation under one umbrella.

The digitisation of the data from these studies will allow the use of AI algorithms in the future. The scope of this is unlimited, however, we must begin with creating the database. This would require an organised manner of data collection and storage along with creation of customised softwares. It is suggested that we can obtain digital data from the department or institute level, then at the state or university level, further at the national level, and finally internationally. The Indian Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Pathologists (IAOMP) could take the lead in creation of the database, laying down the rules governing it and maintaining the same.

Next comes the advantages for the population at large. The results obtained in various studies can help to guide therapeutic methods for patients. In the case of treatment-based studies, a comparison of results in different parts of the nation can be performed.

As with any new and ambitious project, there will be certain challenges or concerns involved. The legal aspects of data ownership and data protection would need to be addressed. Patient privacy must be maintained according to the law. Manpower dedicated to data collection, organisation, data uploading and a cross-checking mechanism for the same would be needed. A person in-charge of the authenticity of the data would be required at the institute level. Engineers will be needed for the creation and maintenance of the repository’s software and hardware, including data backups.

Once these are addressed, the repository would prove to be beneficial to patients, budding oral pathologists and Oral Pathology Fraternity at National and Global platform.

CONCLUSION

As Oral Pathologists we need to be like the stem cell, and differentiate ourselves as per the circumstance. Over the years, Oral Pathology in India has evolved gradually from it’s primitive to the present state. What we are learning today is going to save someone’s life tomorrow.

We should be prepared to adopt the advanced techniques and welcome the challenges of the future, under the guidance and support of the seniors, along with the active participation of the energetic, hardworking and talented young generation.

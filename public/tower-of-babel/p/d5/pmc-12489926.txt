FKBP5/FKBP51-mediated signaling pathways in neuropsychiatric diseases: Insights for biomarker development and targeted therapies
Authors: Yinglong Liu, Jiahe Lian, Youli Fu, Shishan Wang, Yongxin Liu, Rui Zhang, Huirong Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The FK506-binding protein 5 gene encodes FKBP51, a molecular chaperone linked to the pathogenesis of neuropsychiatric diseases. Recent evidence shows that FKBP51 modulates activity of the HPA axis and GR-mediated feedback via dynamic interactions with GR, thereby influencing stress adaptation, inflammatory responses, and neuronal survival. This review systematically analyzes the mechanisms by which FKBP5 (and its encoded FKBP51) contributes to neuropsychiatric diseases and identifies shared pathways across these conditions. We further highlight key factors mediating disease variability and susceptibility: sex-, region-, and cell type-specific expression patterns of FKBP5/FKBP51, their temporal dynamics, genetic variants, epigenetic regulation, and gene–environment interactions. Additionally, we propose a “biphasic stress-response model” to conceptualize the temporal dynamics of FKBP5/FKBP51 expression during disease progression. Finally, we explore the translational potential of targeting FKBP51 signaling, and outline pharmacological strategies to modulate chaperone-dependent protein folding and stress pathways as novel therapeutic interventions.

Introduction

Overview of psychiatric disorders

The World Health Organization (WHO) reports an increasing global prevalence of psychiatric disorders, including depression, anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) (Wittchen et al., 2011). Beyond compromising individual mental health, these disorders exert a significant economic and social toll on families and society (Ormel et al., 1994). Their etiology involves complex interactions among genetic, environmental, and psychological determinants (Schiele et al., 2020). Genetic studies demonstrate significant familial clustering for many psychiatric disorders, underscoring the importance of genetic predisposition in their pathogenesis (Ballard et al., 2019; Miller et al., 2019; Quick et al., 2022). Environmental factors, including early life experiences, social support, and stressful life events, significantly influence the onset of these disorders (Kong et al., 2020; Quinn et al., 2017; Whittle et al., 2024). Cabanis et al. identify childhood trauma as a significant risk factor for depressive and anxiety disorders in adulthood (Cabanis et al., 2021). Understanding the fundamental mechanisms of these disorders facilitates early prevention, optimizes treatment, slows disease progression, and enhances quality of life for patients and families.

Overview of neurodegenerative diseases

The central nervous system (CNS) regulates core physiological functions—including motor control, sensory processing, cognition, and emotional regulation—making its maintenance critical to health. Global population aging and changing environmental/lifestyle factors are driving a continuous increase in the prevalence of CNS diseases, constituting a major public health challenge (Wittchen et al., 2011). These diseases encompass a heterogeneous spectrum spanning diseases of genetic, infectious, degenerative, vascular, or neoplastic origin, notably including Alzheimer's disease (AD), Parkinson's disease (PD), stroke, and multiple sclerosis (MS) (Wittchen et al., 2011). Given the CNS's structural complexity and multifaceted roles, such diseases often cause severe complications and significantly reduce patients' quality of life. Early diagnosis and personalized treatments are therefore essential to slow disease progression and improve outcomes.

Introduction to the basic concepts and functions of FKBP51

The FKBP5 gene encodes FKBP51, a 51-kDa immunophilin protein. This protein belongs to the FK506-binding protein (FKBP) family and participates in critical biological functions including immunomodulation, protein folding, and intracellular translocation (Mendonca et al., 2021). FKBP51 comprises three structural domains: (1) an N-terminal FK506-binding domain (FK1), (2) a catalytically inactive FK506-like binding domain (FK2), and (3) a C-terminal domain with three tandem tetratricopeptide repeats (TPRs) mediating protein-protein interactions (Sinars et al., 2003). FKBP51 localizes to both the cytoplasm and nucleus, where it dynamically assembles with heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90) into a chaperone complex that allosterically regulates the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) (Noddings et al., 2023; Verkhivker, 2022; Vermeer et al., 2003). FKBP51 attenuates GR activity by impairing glucocorticoid (GC) binding affinity, which compromises hormone-dependent GR nuclear translocation (Wochnik et al., 2005). Under stress or elevated GC levels, GC binding to the GR triggers dissociation of the GR-Hsp90 complex and co-chaperone exchange—replacing FKBP51 with FKBP52. This exchange facilitates GR nuclear translocation and target gene activation, which upregulates FKBP51 expression. Beyond its role in GR signaling, FKBP51 engages multiple functional interactors: (1) the small molecule rapamycin; (2) kinase regulators including AKT and GSK-3β; (3) steroid hormone receptors (progesterone receptor (PR), androgen receptor (AR)); (4) IKKα; and (5) PH domain- or leucine-rich repeat-containing proteins such as the phosphatase PHLPP (Bouwmeester et al., 2004; Gassen et al., 2016; Li et al., 2011). Research on neuropsychiatric diseases now prioritizes intracellular signaling and stress response pathways. Within these, FKBP51 critically regulates neural processes by orchestrating stress adaptation, fine-tuning immune responses, and governing apoptotic pathways (Hartmann et al., 2024; Nold et al., 2022; Park, J. M. et al., 2021; Tufano et al., 2021; Zannas, 2023). Elevated FKBP51 expression is mechanistically linked to psychiatric disorder pathogenesis (Criado-Marrero et al., 2019; Grossmann et al., 2024) and exacerbates disease progression (Matosin et al., 2023). It also drives neurodegenerative processes by disrupting proteostasis, as evidenced in tauopathies (Oroz et al., 2018). A mechanistic dissection of FKBP51 in CNS diseases illuminates disease pathogenesis and reveals actionable therapeutic targets. This review synthesizes structural and functional evidence for FKBP51's dual roles in psychiatric and neurodegenerative conditions, critically evaluating its targetability. By integrating recent advances, we delineate molecular drivers of neuropsychiatric disease and identify tractable strategies for intervention.

Link between FKBP51 and psychiatric disorders

The relationship between FKBP51 and depression

Depression is characterized by core psychopathological features including persistent depressed mood and anhedonia (the loss of pleasure or interest) (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). According to WHO statistics, approximately 3.8 % of the global population (equivalent to ∼280 million individuals) is affected by depression. Among adults, the prevalence is approximately 5 %, with rates of 4 % in males and 6 % in females (World Health Organization, 2023a). In China, an estimated 95 million people are affected by depression. Approximately 280,000 suicides occur annually, with 40 % of victims being diagnosed with this disorder. Notably, 30.28 % of depression patients in China are under 18 years of age, indicating a trend toward younger onset (Huang et al., 2019). Given this disease burden, elucidating the pathogenesis of depression while optimizing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies has become a global research priority.

Depression involves complex interactions among social, psychological, and biological factors (Alexopoulos, 2005; American Psychiatric Association, 2022). Individuals experiencing adverse life events (e.g., unemployment, bereavement, or trauma) are at significantly elevated risk for depression (Matosin et al., 2018; Mazure et al., 2000; Patton et al., 2003). Depression propagates a self-reinforcing cycle by inducing hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis dysregulation and functional disability, which collectively exacerbate core pathology (Holsboer, 2001; Knorr et al., 2010; Pariante and Miller, 2001; Stetler and Miller, 2011). FKBP51, a stress-inducible regulator of GR signaling, impairs GR-mediated negative feedback during stress. This results in sustained HPA axis activation—a mechanistically defined pathway increasingly implicated in depression pathogenesis (Hausl et al., 2021).

Post-mortem analyses demonstrate elevated FKBP5 transcription in bipolar disorder and major depression. Sinclair et al. identified increased prefrontal FKBP5 mRNA in bipolar patients versus controls via RNA-Seq/qPCR (Sinclair et al., 2013). This finding extends to depression and schizophrenia in a larger cohort, where cortical FKBP5 expression was significantly upregulated (Matosin et al., 2023). Antidepressant response dynamically modulates FKBP5 mRNA and FKBP51 protein levels: responders exhibit decreased peripheral expression after 6 weeks, whereas non-responders maintain elevated levels (Ising et al., 2019). In contrast, chronically medicated depressed patients show suppressed FKBP5 expression versus controls (Hori et al., 2024), suggesting antidepressant exposure may confound biomarker interpretation. Transgenic FKBP5 overexpression in mice precipitates depression-like behaviors, establishing causal involvement in depressive phenotypes (Criado-Marrero et al., 2020). The above study confirmed a positive correlation between FKBP5 expression and depressive-like behavior in both brain tissue and whole blood.

Compared with established findings, Mengelkoch et al. report reduced FKBP5 levels in high-risk versus low-risk adolescent females (Mengelkoch et al., 2025)—a reversal of the positive correlation between FKBP5 expression and depression severity observed in diagnosed patients. This apparent contradiction reflects fundamental methodological distinctions: prior conclusions derive from confirmed depression cohorts, whereas Mengelkoch's data address preclinical risk stratification in undiagnosed populations. Additionally, Mengelkoch et al. exclusively studied adolescent females—a sex-specific cohort distinct from previous investigations employing mixed-sex cohorts. Prospective studies linking FKBP5 dynamics to depression risk remain scarce. Most evidence derives from retrospective analyses correlating peripheral blood FKBP5 mRNA levels with childhood adversity in diagnosed patients. Key findings from these retrospective investigations include: In major depressive disorder (MDD) patients carrying the rs1360780 T allele (genotypes TT or TC), higher childhood adversity scores correlate with lower methylation levels of FKBP5 intron 7 (Tozzi et al., 2018). Given the established inverse correlation between FKBP5 methylation and expression (Blair et al., 2013), childhood adversity-induced demethylation mechanistically drives FKBP5 upregulation—a pathogenic pathway mediating MDD development. This mechanism is consistently replicated across independent cohorts (Klengel et al., 2013; Mendonca et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2025; Tyrka et al., 2015; Wiechmann et al., 2019). Depression emerges from multifactorial gene-environment interplay, necessitating integrated analysis of genetic susceptibility and environmental exposures. Distinct environmental stressors—particularly during neurodevelopmentally sensitive periods—elicit divergent pathophysiological trajectories. This principle is exemplified in the discussed studies: Mengelkoch et al. specifically model chronic exposure to maternal depression in adolescent females, capturing a high-risk developmental milieu (Mengelkoch et al., 2025). Notably, childhood adversity in depression cohorts was assessed using the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ), a multidimensional instrument that quantifies five domains of abuse and neglect (Menke et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2025). Methodologically, this approach differs fundamentally from Mengelkoch et al.’s focus on discrete exposure to maternal depression. We argue that such measurement heterogeneity—comprehensive survey versus targeted stressor assessment—underlies the observed discrepancies in FKBP5 expression, highlighting the need to investigate adversity-specific epigenetic mechanisms.

Notably, the prevalence of depression exhibits statistically significant sex differences, with a higher incidence in women than in men (Potter and Yoon, 2023; Sun et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2023). Further exploration of underlying mechanisms reveals sex-specific differences in FKBP51 expression. Chronic mild stress (CMS) increased hippocampal FKBP51 expression in both sexes, with a non-significant trend toward higher upregulation in males. Moreover, venlafaxine normalized FKBP51 levels in stressed male rats but failed to exert a therapeutic effect in females, thereby indicating sexually dimorphic pharmacodynamics of antidepressants (Xing et al., 2013). Under basal conditions, hippocampal FKBP5 expression showed no sex differences. However, acute stress superimposed on chronic stress paradigms revealed sexually divergent FKBP5 dynamics: females exhibited prolonged FKBP5 upregulation, exacerbating HPA axis dysregulation and depression susceptibility. Conversely, males displayed rapid FKBP5 normalization with concomitant increases in GR co-activators (e.g., SRC-1), indicating enhanced adaptive capacity (Bourke et al., 2013). Female mice show greater sensitivity to FKBP5-early life adversity (ELA) interactions, characterized by prolonged HPA axis dysregulation, exacerbated depression-like behaviors, and impaired GR feedback mechanisms. Conversely, male mice exhibit blunted molecular and neuroendocrine responses to equivalent stress exposure (Nold et al., 2022). Collectively, these data demonstrate sex-divergent FKBP5 expression as a molecular correlate of differential depression prevalence. While this dysregulation contributes to elevated female susceptibility, comprehensive pathomechanistic models must integrate epigenetic (e.g., FKBP5 intron 7 methylation) and single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) that gate stress-responsive FKBP5 transcription.

The rs1360780 (C/T) polymorphism, located in intron 2 of FKBP5, upregulates FKBP5 expression (Binder, 2009). Studies have demonstrated that the rs1360780 polymorphism of the FKBP5 gene is linked to cognitive susceptibility factors (dysfunctional attitudes) in depression by affecting HPA axis function (Suzuki et al., 2014). The rs1360780 T allele in F5 demonstrates dose-dependent clinical effects: homozygous (TT) depressed patients exhibit enhanced antidepressant response but higher recurrence rates versus CT/CC carriers (Binder et al., 2004). This risk allele interacts with childhood trauma, as evidenced by Appel et al.: physically abused TT individuals showed more severe adult depression and 2.8-fold higher MDD prevalence (Appel et al., 2011). Subsequent work confirmed depression associations for multiple FKBP5 SNPs (rs1360780, rs9470080, rs4713916, rs9296158, rs9394309) (Szczepankiewicz et al., 2014), collectively establishing FKBP5 as a genetically modulated susceptibility node (Lekman et al., 2008). Additionally, studies have found that the interaction between the FKBP5 rs1360780 T allele and childhood abuse induces structural and functional abnormalities in brain regions involved in emotional processing (e.g., the insula and inferior frontal gyrus) in depressed patients (Tozzi et al., 2016). These abnormalities elevate susceptibility to depression. Polymorphisms in the FKBP5 gene, particularly the rs1360780 T allele, are significantly associated with depression onset and development. This supports the gene-cognition-environment interaction model of depression pathogenesis, in which genetic variants modulate depression risk via their effects on cognitive vulnerability.

FKBP5, a key GR regulator, shows positive correlation between its expression levels and depressive-like behaviors. Polymorphisms in this gene—particularly the rs1360780 T allele—interact with childhood adversity, epigenetically upregulating FKBP5 expression through mechanisms like dysregulated methylation. FKBP5-mediated pathology exhibits marked sexual dimorphism. Females demonstrate heightened sensitivity to FKBP5-adversity interactions, resulting in persistent HPA axis dysfunction. In contrast, males typically exhibit rapid functional recovery. This differential response likely contributes to the higher depression prevalence in females. Antidepressants dynamically modulate FKBP5 expression, with expression changes reflecting treatment response. Significant FKBP5 downregulation occurs in treatment-responsive patients, suggesting its utility as a predictive biomarker for both depression risk and therapeutic efficacy. Collectively, FKBP5 functions as a central molecular mediator in gene-environment interactions governing depression susceptibility, sexual dimorphism, and treatment response, representing a pivotal target for mechanistic research and targeted interventions.

Advances in the study of FKBP51 and anxiety disorders

In 2019, anxiety disorders affected approximately 301 million people globally (4 % of the world's population), ranking among the most prevalent psychiatric disorders (World Health Organization, 2023b). Anxiety disorders involve persistent excessive worry disproportionate to actual threats, often significantly impairing daily functioning and quality of life in severe cases (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). This diagnostic category includes generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, specific phobias, and childhood-onset conditions like separation anxiety disorder and selective mutism (Strohle et al., 2018). Despite distinct clinical presentations, all anxiety disorders share uncontrollable excessive worry. Their multifactorial etiology encompasses genetic, biochemical, psychological, and environmental determinants. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for developing effective treatments to improve patient well-being.

HPA axis dysfunction, a primary stress-response system, is a well-established hallmark of anxiety disorders (Fang et al., 2023; Rieder et al., 2017). The FKBP5 gene has been shown to modulate stress sensitivity, increasing susceptibility to anxiety disorders—particularly following trauma, which impairs HPA axis regulation and exacerbates susceptibility to anxiety symptoms (Buchmann et al., 2014; Richter et al., 2021).

In the mouse bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST), deacetylase sirtuin 1 (SIRT1)-mediated deacetylation impairs FKBP51 activity, facilitating its dissociation from the GR. This attenuates FKBP51-mediated inhibition of GR, enhances GR-dependent repression of corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) transcription, and thereby alleviates chronic stress-induced anxiety behaviors via CRF downregulation (Hu et al., 2023). Stress upregulates cellular retinoic acid binding protein 1 (Crabp1) expression, which drives FKBP51 accumulation and subsequent GR inhibition; this impairs HPA axis negative feedback and consequently exacerbates anxiety. Targeting this pathway (e.g., via reducing Crabp1/FKBP51 levels) alleviates anxiety symptoms (Lin et al., 2021). Additionally, FKBP51 overexpression in the basolateral amygdala (BLA) promotes anxiety-like behavior, whereas FKBP51 antagonists (e.g., Ligand2, SAFit2) produce anxiolysis by attenuating FKBP51-mediated inhibition of GR (Hartmann et al., 2015). Apelin-13 ameliorates anxiety by enhancing GR activity and reducing FKBP51 expression (Aminyavari et al., 2019). FKBP51 overexpression synergizes with early-life stress (ELS) to exacerbate anxiety-like behavior by impairing dorsal hippocampal AKT signaling and GR-mediated negative feedback, with heightened female sensitivity to this interaction (Criado-Marrero et al., 2019). Attwood et al. identified a novel amygdalar FKBP51-anxiety pathway: stress-induced, neuroprotease-dependent EphB2 cleavage promotes its dissociation from NMDAR NR1 subunits, thereby enhancing NMDAR currents, stimulating FKBP51 expression, and driving anxiety behaviors (Attwood et al., 2011)(Fig. 3). Interestingly, FKBP51 overexpression in the BLA induces pronounced anxiety-like behavior; in contrast, its overexpression in the central amygdala (CeA) induces only mild anxiety-like phenotypes, while overexpression in the dorsal hippocampus (dHc) has no significant effect on anxiety-like behavior (Hartmann et al., 2015). Thus, FKBP51 regulates anxiety in a brain region-specific manner, consistent with other findings. In the oval bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (ovBNST), FKBP51 overexpression produced mild anxiolytic effects while knockout increased anxiety, highlighting region-specific functional roles in anxiety circuitry (Engelhardt et al., 2021). FKBP51 shows region-specific expression and diverse roles in anxiety regulation, reflecting varied neuroanatomical contributions to anxiety disorders. This neuroanatomical heterogeneity highlights anxiety's multifactorial etiology, which arises from gene-brain-environment interactions.

FKBP51 knockout regulates anxiety-like behavior in a sex- and cell type-specific manner. In female mice, glutamatergic neuron knockout (FKBP5Nex) increases anxiety-like behavior, whereas GABAergic neuron knockout (FKBP5Dlx) produces an anxiolytic phenotype. In male mice, however, phenotypes diverge from anxiety: FKBP5Nex impairs aversive spatial context memory, while FKBP5Dlx enhances this memory (van Doeselaar et al., 2023). Anxiety disorder research prominently features sex differences. ELS induces anxiolysis in female mice, an effect abolished by FKBP5Nex, indicating FKBP51 in these neurons is required for ELS-induced anxiolysis. Conversely, ELS and FKBP51 deficiency exert minimal effects on anxiety-like behavior in males, demonstrating sex-specific mechanisms (van Doeselaar et al., 2025). Female FKBP51-overexpressing (rTgFKBP5) mice exhibited exacerbated anxiety-like behavior following ELS, recapitulating the higher female prevalence of clinical anxiety disorders (Criado-Marrero et al., 2019). The apparent contradictions between (van Doeselaar et al.) and (Criado-Marrero et al.) arise from differing interactions between FKBP51 expression (normal vs. overexpressed) and ELS intensity (moderate vs. excessive). This indicates FKBP51's dual dose- and context-dependent function, highlighting the need for context-specific analyses. Research confirms that distinct environmental stimuli differentially affect FKBP51-mediated signaling pathways. In inflammation-induced anxiety, FKBP51 mediates anti-anxiety resilience by activating hippocampal GR signaling and upregulating the γ-amino butyric acid (GABA) synthase Glutamic acid decarboxylase 65 (GAD65) to enhance inhibitory neurotransmission. Conversely, in GC-induced anxiety, FKBP51 deficiency alleviates anxiety (Gan et al., 2022). FKBP5 polymorphisms contribute to the onset of anxiety disorders through interactions with environmental factors: individuals with minor alleles are more sensitive to mild-to-moderate adverse life events and have an increased risk of developing it; however, the impact of severe adverse events is independent of the FKBP5 genotype (Scheuer et al., 2016). FKBP5-deficient mice exhibit baseline anxiety behavior comparable to that of wild-type controls. However, following acute stress exposure, they exhibit enhanced active coping strategies (e.g., increased swimming in the forced swim test) (Touma et al., 2011). This is consistent with Engelhardt et al.: acute stress-induced upregulation of FKBP5 in the ovBNST mediates an adaptive stress response, thereby transiently reducing anxiety (Engelhardt et al., 2021). The aforementioned research appears to contain two seemingly contradictory elements, but such contradictions are apparent. We propose a “biphasic stress-response model”: Phase 1 (Acute): Transient FKBP51 elevation and HPA axis activation result in adaptive anxiolysis. Phase 2 (Chronic): Sustained HPA hyperactivity causes neuroendocrine dysregulation, contributing to anxiety and depression (Fig. 4). Critically, FKBP51, whether elevated or reduced, disrupts HPA homeostasis, establishing a pathological baseline that, if sustained, inevitably progresses to psychiatric disorders.Fig. 4Schematic diagram of the “biphasic stress-response model”.Fig. 4

FKBP51 regulates anxiety through brain region (BLA, CeA, dHc, ovBNST)-, sex-, and cell type-specific mechanisms. Its context-dependent functions are dynamically shaped by environmental variables—including ELS intensity/type, inflammation, and GC exposure—while FKBP5 polymorphisms modulate anxiety disorder susceptibility via gene-environment interactions. Our proposed “biphasic stress-response model” integrates these observations, partially explaining FKBP51's paradoxical roles. Furthermore, this stress model can be extended to other psychiatric disorders such as depression and PTSD, thus positioning FKBP51 as a key molecular hub in stress-related disorders, with its multifunctional effects reflecting the gene-brain-environment etiology of anxiety pathologies.

Association of FKBP51 with PTSD

PTSD is a severe psychiatric condition triggered by severe trauma. Core symptoms include: traumatic re-experiencing (e.g., flashbacks, nightmares), persistent avoidance of trauma reminders, negative affect (e.g., fear, anger, shame), and hyperarousal (Miao et al., 2018). PTSD risk increases with the severity and duration of traumatic stress, accounting for its higher prevalence in trauma-exposed occupations (e.g., combat veterans, first responders) (Nemeroff et al., 2006). Identifying these risk factors facilitates targeted interventions in high-risk populations.

PTSD involves complex biological and psychosocial mechanisms. Traumatic experiences alter psychological and physiological functions through pathways such as dysregulation of the HPA axis, which is a central neuroendocrine feature of the pathogenesis of PTSD (de Kloet et al., 2005; Lupien et al., 2009). Chronic stress induces persistent activation of the HPA axis, dysregulating the cortisol release and contributing to psychiatric disorders. As a key regulator of the HPA axis, FKBP5 critically modulates stress pathology, including that of PTSD (Fries et al., 2017; Matosin et al., 2018; Xiao et al., 2023; Yehuda, 2002).

Recent studies using spatial transcriptomics show widespread FKBP5 overexpression in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) of PTSD patients, with significantly higher expression specifically in endothelial cells (ENDs) and oligodendrocytes (OLGs) (Hwang et al., 2025). This finding aligns with post-mortem evidence of FKBP5 transcript upregulation in the PFC of PTSD patients (Daskalakis et al., 2024; Girgenti et al., 2021). FKBP5 mRNA is elevated in the medial orbitofrontal cortex (BA11) of PTSD patients and may drive pathological processes through altered dendritic spine morphology, characterized by reduced mushroom spines and increased short, thick spines (Young et al., 2015). Investigators characterized alterations in peripheral immune cells in whole blood from PTSD patients. Notably, FKBP5 is consistently upregulated across these cells, including CD4+T, CD8+T, B cells, and monocytes (Kuan et al., 2019). Naive rats show higher basal FKBP51 expression in the prelimbic cortex (PL) than in the infralimbic cortex (IL). Fear conditioning selectively upregulates FKBP51 in the IL but not the PL, indicating region-specific functions of FKBP51 in fear regulation (Criado-Marrero et al., 2017). Other studies support this view, including Torrisi et al., who found FKBP5 expression was significantly upregulated in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) of PTSD-susceptible mice but downregulated in the amygdala, hippocampus, and hypothalamus of PTSD-resistant mice (Torrisi et al., 2021). Hippocampal FKBP5 mRNA expression was significantly decreased in rats after single prolonged stress (SPS), a validated PTSD model (Lin et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). SPS suppressed FKBP5 mRNA in the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) but not in the BNST. Urocortin 3 (Ucn3), a CRHR2 agonist, rescued FKBP5 expression in the PVN while increasing its levels in the BNST (Tillinger et al., 2024). FKBP5 exhibits region-specific dysregulation in PTSD pathogenesis, with divergent regulation in cortical regions (especially the PFC) versus limbic structures (amygdala, hippocampus, hypothalamus).

PTSD patients are stratified into HPA axis response subtypes: hypocortisolemic “non-responders” exhibit blunted cortisol/ACTH responses and dissociative symptoms, driven by FKBP5 downregulation (Zaba et al., 2015). This subtype accounts for cross-study heterogeneity in HPA responses and represents a distinct pathophysiological entity. Variations in subgroup proportions among PTSD samples across studies may underlie these conflicting findings. For example, among 324 World Trade Center rescue workers, FKBP5 expression was upregulated in those with current PTSD, regardless of genotype (Kuan et al., 2017). Transcriptomic analysis of whole blood from 35 Caucasian World Trade Center attack survivors showed significantly reduced FKBP5 expression in those with PTSD (Yehuda et al., 2009). Discrepant findings across studies may stem from methodological differences, including variations in PTSD subtypes and sample characteristics. Yehuda et al.’s study was limited by a focus on Caucasian participants alone and small sample size.

Pre-onset alterations in whole-blood FKBP5 levels are predictive biomarkers for PTSD risk. Adult survivors of childhood exposure to suicide bombings had significantly lower FKBP5 expression than healthy controls. Expression levels were graded: lowest in trauma-exposed individuals with PTSD, intermediate in trauma-exposed without PTSD, and highest in non-exposed controls (Goltser-Dubner et al., 2023). In a prospective cohort of 448 combat-deployed male soldiers, lower pre-deployment FKBP5 mRNA expression independently predicted subsequent PTSD symptom onset (van Zuiden et al., 2012). Furthermore, increased FKBP5 expression strongly predicts PTSD symptom improvement (Levy-Gigi et al., 2013). FKBP5 expression exhibits temporal dynamics in PTSD progression. In the SPS rat model, anxiety symptoms worsened over time (17.5 % with severe anxiety at 1 week; 57.1 % at 2 weeks) (Serova et al., 2019). Over the same period, hypothalamic FKBP5 mRNA showed biphasic regulation—an initial increase followed by a decrease—supporting its potential as a molecular marker for the acute-to-chronic PTSD transition and reinforcing the “biphasic stress-response model”。

FKBP5 gene polymorphisms are strongly associated with PTSD susceptibility, indicating that genetic variation influences stress response (Watkins et al., 2016). These variants alter FKBP5 expression and function. Specific risk alleles—including rs1360780T, rs3800373C, and rs9470080T—interact with early-life trauma to increase vulnerability to PTSD and depression (Binder et al., 2008; Wang et al., 2018). Specifically, Binder et al. found four FKBP5 SNPs (rs9296158, rs3800373, rs1360780, rs9470080) moderate childhood abuse's effect on adult PTSD symptoms, without directly determining overall symptom severity. Gene-environment interactions further modulate risk: childhood maltreatment and alcohol dependence potentiate PTSD susceptibility in carriers of FKBP5 variants (e.g., rs9470080) across ethnic groups (Xie et al., 2010). Concurrently, neuroimaging studies associate FKBP5 risk variants with structural brain alterations in PTSD. Carriers of the rs1360780 T allele exhibit reduced fractional anisotropy in the left posterior cingulate (PC), indicating impaired white matter integrity (Fani et al., 2012, 2014). Altered hippocampal-anterior cingulate connectivity is also associated with FKBP5 variation (Fani et al., 2016). Furthermore, rs3800373-AA and rs1360780-CC genotypes correlate with reduced left hippocampal volume and depression severity in PTSD patients (Yun et al., 2022). Elucidating how FKBP5 polymorphisms interact with environmental factors to drive PTSD etiology and progression remains critical.

Yang et al. found that FKBP5 methylation levels in whole blood were significantly associated with PTSD symptom severity. Among patients undergoing prolonged exposure therapy, treatment responders showed a progressive decrease in FKBP5 methylation, whereas non-responders exhibited no significant changes (Yang et al., 2021). Conversely, FKBP5 methylation was elevated in treatment responders after meditation therapy (Lee et al., 2024). This divergence may reflect distinct intervention mechanisms: meditation promotes FKBP5 hypermethylation, thereby attenuating excessive stress responses, whereas exposure therapy facilitates HPA axis adaptation via demethylation, achieved through direct retrieval of traumatic memories. PTSD treatment choices have specific molecular consequences beyond symptom relief, involving unique signaling mechanisms and potential long-term outcomes requiring further investigation.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), as epigenetic regulators, modulate the pathogenesis of PTSD by targeting FKBP5 (Gupta et al., 2021). Kang et al. identified FKBP5-targeting miRNAs via integrated analyses of FKBP5−/− mouse models and human PTSD cohorts. Their expression levels correlated with serum biomarker profiles, molecular alterations, and behavioral phenotypes in PTSD (Kang et al., 2020). Park et al. found FKBP5 deficiency dysregulated 41 miRNAs in the mPFC of restraint-stressed mice, with miR-690 most notably upregulated. MiR-690 overexpression rescued stress-induced behavioral deficits (Park, J. et al., 2021). MiR-511 directly binds to the 3′-UTR of FKBP5 to suppress its expression. In PTSD models, upregulation of miR-511 may partially restore GR function by repressing FKBP5, thereby attenuating HPA axis overactivation; failure of this compensatory mechanism may exacerbate symptomatology (Zheng et al., 2016). Collectively, these findings indicate that miRNAs modulate PTSD pathogenesis and remission by regulating FKBP5 expression.

The above content systematically elucidates the central role of FKBP5 in PTSD, encompassing its region-specific dysregulation and its association with PTSD pathophysiology. Proposed PTSD subtypes based on HPA-axis reactivity (“non-responders” vs. “responders”) may account for previously conflicting findings across studies. Additionally, FKBP5 represents a promising biomarker for predicting PTSD risk and evaluating prognosis, while its gene–environment interactions modulate disease susceptibility. These findings offer multidimensional insights into PTSD's molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets. Finally, the temporal and functional dynamics of FKBP5 further validate our previously proposed “biphasic stress-response model”.

Role of FKBP51 in neurodegenerative diseases

Relationship between FKBP51 and AD

AD, a complex neurodegenerative disorder, imposes significant burdens on patients, families, and society. It is characterized by progressive memory loss, cognitive impairment, and personality changes (Scheltens et al., 2021). Globally, ∼55 million people lived with dementia in 2019, projected to rise to 139 million by 2050 (Collaborators, 2022). With accelerating global aging, AD has become a critical public health challenge, underscoring the need to elucidate its pathological mechanisms and identify novel therapeutic targets.

AD pathogenesis is defined by two core pathological hallmarks: amyloid-β (Aβ) plaque accumulation and tau hyperphosphorylation (Karran and De Strooper, 2022). Aβ peptides, cleaved from amyloid precursor protein (APP), aggregate into plaques that induce synaptic toxicity, disrupt neuronal communication, and ultimately cause cognitive impairment (Selkoe and Hardy, 2016). Normally, tau stabilizes microtubules and regulates their dynamics (Kadavath et al., 2015); in AD, however, hyperphosphorylated tau forms neurofibrillary tangles, promoting neuronal dysfunction and death (Spillantini and Goedert, 2013). Interactions between Aβ and tau pathologies exacerbate neuroinflammation, synaptic loss, and widespread neurodegeneration. Beyond these protein aggregates, AD pathogenesis involves complex genetic, environmental, and biological factors.

Beyond its established role in stress response regulation, FKBP51 directly contributes to AD progression (Cao and Konsolaki, 2011; Salminen et al., 2011). In AD, FKBP51 drives neuroinflammation via aberrant secretory autophagy (SA), promoting IL-1β release, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and Gasdermin D-mediated neurotoxicity—collectively contributing to cognitive decline (Bajaj et al., 2024). Reduced spindle and kinetochore associated complex subunit 2 (SKA2) expression facilitates this pathway by relieving FKBP51 inhibition (Hartmann et al., 2024). Jinwal et al. showed that FKBP51 stabilizes microtubules via interaction with tau (Jinwal et al., 2010). Blair et al. further demonstrated that FKBP51, in a chaperone complex with Hsp90, binds tau and inhibits its proteasomal degradation. This stabilization promotes oligomeric tau accumulation, enhancing its neurotoxicity (Blair et al., 2013). Research demonstrates that FKBP51 expression increases with age in the brain, is further elevated in AD patients, and correlates directly with disease severity (Blair et al., 2013; Jinwal et al., 2010; Matosin et al., 2023; Wen et al., 2025). The above findings suggest that AD is characterized by age-dependent onset. In microglia, FKBP5 shows a female-biased expression pattern—one that is retained in AD. Notably, the female-biased expression of FKBP5 is concentrated in microglia rather than neurons or astrocytes, supporting microglial FKBP5 as a critical contributor to AD pathogenesis and a potential mediator of the increased AD risk in women (Wen et al., 2025). These findings establish FKBP51's complex role in AD progression, with implications in both neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative pathways. Impaired Aβ clearance, rather than overproduction, is a major driver of AD pathogenesis (Mawuenyega et al., 2010), supporting enhanced clearance as a promising therapeutic strategy (Jin et al., 2017; Selkoe and Hardy, 2016). Autophagy, a key cellular clearance pathway, is suppressed in AD via upregulated mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling (Caccamo et al., 2010; Menzies et al., 2015). This impairment is critical: disrupting core autophagy proteins such as Beclin 1 exacerbates Aβ accumulation (Pickford et al., 2008; Swaminathan et al., 2016), while activating regulators such as transcription factor EB (TFEB) reduces it (Xiao et al., 2015; Zhang and Zhao, 2015). Given this central role of autophagic dysfunction, recent evidence links FKBP51 to autophagy regulation (Bailus et al., 2021), positioning it as a potential therapeutic target. We thus hypothesize a novel mechanism in AD: FKBP51 impedes Aβ clearance by disrupting autophagy, making this pathway a promising therapeutic target.

AD is characterized by marked mitochondrial dysfunction and cerebral glucose hypometabolism, which often precede clinical symptoms (Bano et al., 2023; Levin et al., 2021). These metabolic defects cause bioenergetic deficits in neurons, reducing ATP production, impairing synaptic function, and thereby driving cognitive decline (Massaro et al., 2025). Mitochondrial dysfunction further promotes Aβ production and tau hyperphosphorylation (Zhou et al., 2018), which subsequently disrupt glial function—characterized by reduced astrocytic glucose metabolism (Minhas et al., 2024; Zysk et al., 2023), impaired microglial phagocytosis (McIntosh et al., 2019), and increased neuroinflammation (Calsolaro and Edison, 2016; Pan et al., 2022)—thus perpetuating a self-reinforcing cycle of disease progression. Under basal conditions, FKBP51 localizes to mitochondria via the import machinery, where its TPR domain mediates interactions with Hsp90 (Toneatto et al., 2013). FKBP51 deficiency enhances mitochondrial function: by regulating fatty acid β-oxidation and amino acid metabolism, it modulates bioenergetics and homeostasis, thereby acting as a critical regulatory factor (Williams et al., 2023). FKBP51 correlates with age and AD severity and suppresses mitochondrial function. These findings suggest that FKBP51 inhibition could therapeutically rescue mitochondrial dysfunction and ameliorate AD pathogenesis, supporting its potential as a drug target. Moreover, FKBP51 compromises hippocampal synaptic function and plasticity via a GR-dependent pathway, directly mediating cognitive deficits in AD (Wang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2022). Given this, FKBP51 overexpression is a central pathogenic driver of AD, and targeting FKBP51 emerges as a compelling therapeutic strategy to rescue cognitive function.

These findings identify FKBP51 as a key driver of AD pathology, including neuroinflammation, neurodegeneration, and metabolic dysfunction. Thus, targeting FKBP51 is a promising strategy to slow AD progression.

A study of FKBP51 in relation to PD

PD, a neurodegenerative disorder, is characterized by core motor symptoms: resting tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability (Jankovic, 2008). With global aging, its prevalence is projected to double from 6.2 million cases in 2015 to 12.9 million by 2040, substantially increasing disease burden (Guerreiro et al., 2018). Beyond motor impairments, PD involves debilitating non-motor symptoms—including cognitive decline, mood disorders, and autonomic dysfunction (e.g., gastrointestinal and cardiac dysregulation)—that severely compromise quality of life and are critical targets for comprehensive management (Jankovic, 2008).

The central pathology of PD involves progressive degeneration of dopaminergic (DA) neurons in the nigrostriatal pathway, causing dopamine depletion and motor dysfunction (Burch and Sheerin, 2005). A key neuropathological hallmark is Lewy body formation—primarily composed of α-synuclein aggregates—intimately associated with neuronal death (Calo et al., 2016; Tozzi et al., 2021). While PD's exact etiology remains incompletely understood, genetic predisposition, environmental factors, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and neuroinflammation are implicated as potential contributors (Morris et al., 2024).

Loss-of-function mutations in putative protein kinase 1 (PINK1) are linked to PD pathogenesis (Hernandez et al., 2016). In PINK1 deficiency, FKBP51 overexpression exacerbates 1-methyl-4-phenylpyridine (MPP+)-induced neuronal death, while its knockdown is protective—directly implicating FKBP51 in PINK1-related toxicity (Boonying et al., 2019). Mechanistically, PINK1 phosphorylates FKBP51 to attenuate the AKT-PHLPP interaction, activating pro-survival AKT signaling. FKBP51 phosphorylation may thus be pivotal to PD pathogenesis via the PINK1-FKBP51 axis.

Recent PD-focused studies have observed that FKBP51 is significantly upregulated during aging and in the PD brain, particularly in microglia and DA neurons. Its upregulation correlates with disease progression, supporting its role as a potential biomarker. FKBP51 serves as a central node in PD pathogenesis by linking protein aggregation and neuroinflammation: binding of FKBP51 to Hsp90 impairs α-synuclein clearance while activating pro-inflammatory microglia. Notably, the inhibitor SAFit2 counteracts both processes: it reduces proteotoxicity and neuroinflammation, thereby protecting DA neurons and restoring motor function (Garcia-Gomara et al., 2025). In AD, FKBP51 functions similarly: it binds Hsp90 to impair tau clearance and enhance inflammation. This underscores FKBP51's broad therapeutic potential across neurodegenerative diseases and implies that progress in AD research may directly inform understanding of PD.

Studying FKBP51 in PD has the potential to clarify PD's disease mechanisms, uncover novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets, and thereby facilitate early clinical intervention.

Potential mechanisms of FKBP51 in neuropsychiatric diseases

Reactive gliosis and chronic neuroinflammation represent key hallmarks of neurodegenerative diseases, including AD and PD (Wyss-Coray and Rogers, 2012). As Woo et al. noted, inflammatory conditions in the CNS induce neurotoxicity via ionic imbalance, excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, and neuroimmune crosstalk—factors that collectively drive mitochondrial dysfunction and epigenetic dysregulation (Woo et al., 2024). Neuroinflammation is closely associated with psychiatric disorders. Longitudinal studies show that elevated childhood interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels dose-dependently raise the risk of depression and psychosis in adolescence (Khandaker et al., 2014), whereas recurrent depressive episodes predict higher C-reactive protein (CRP) levels (Copeland et al., 2012). Meta-analyses confirm elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), IL-1β) and acute-phase proteins in MDD (Dowlati et al., 2010; Howren et al., 2009; Liu et al., 2012) and anxiety disorders (Chan et al., 2023; Goldsmith et al., 2023).

As resident immune cells of the CNS, microglia regulate neurodevelopment, synaptic function, and neural circuitry by releasing cytokines (e.g., interleukins) and key inflammatory mediators such as TNF (Salter and Stevens, 2017). The observed upregulation of FKBP51 in PD microglia highlights its cell-type-specific role (Garcia-Gomara et al., 2025). Exploring whether this specificity extends to other neuropsychiatric diseases represents a key future research direction.

FKBP51 mediates inflammatory regulation by modulating nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) signaling and promoting IKK complex (e.g., IKKα/β/γ) assembly, thereby increasing pro-inflammatory cytokine production (Bouwmeester et al., 2004; Romano et al., 2004, 2015; Zannas et al., 2019)(Fig. 6). Concurrently, FKBP51 impairs GR function by decreasing GR's ligand affinity and nuclear translocation efficiency, which in turn disrupts the HPA axis-mediated negative feedback regulation of inflammation (Silverman and Sternberg, 2012; Zannas et al., 2016). FKBP51 drives a pro-inflammatory state characterized by elevated IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β via a dual mechanism: NF-κB activation and GR feedback disruption. This sustained neuroinflammation is a key pathogenic driver of both neurodegenerative and psychiatric diseases. The upregulation of FKBP51 in PD microglia provides direct support for this model; extending this mechanism to AD and psychiatric disorders remains a critical future research goal.

FKBP51 inhibits GSK-3β via two mechanisms: direct binding dependent on its FK1 domain, and coordinated recruitment of regulatory partners (e.g., cyclin-dependent kinase 5 (CDK5), protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A)) to enhance GSK-3β suppression (Gassen et al., 2016). In AD, GSK-3β promotes pathogenesis by inducing tau hyperphosphorylation and disrupting APP processing to increase Aβ aggregation (Beurel et al., 2015; Lauretti et al., 2020). Furthermore, GSK-3β′s clinical relevance is supported by studies: probiotics target the AKT/GSK-3β pathway to ameliorate AD pathology (Qian et al., 2024), while loss of microglial spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK) signaling accelerates plaque formation via disruption of the PI3K-AKT-GSK-3β-mTOR axis (Wang et al., 2022). Although FKBP51 inhibits GSK-3β activity, both proteins are upregulated in AD. This contradiction suggests that more potent, multi-dimensional pathological mechanisms prevail in the AD-specific context—highlighting a critical focus for future research.

Although the precise mechanisms of GSK-3β in depression, anxiety, and PTSD remain unclear, empirical studies show that modulating GSK-3β signaling—via pharmacological or microRNA-based approaches—alleviates disease symptoms (Latapy et al., 2012; Thornton et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2024; Williams and Harwood, 2000; Yu et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2024). FKBP51, a key endogenous inhibitor of GSK-3β, mitigates core pathological features and retards disease progression in neuropsychiatric diseases, thereby serving as a critical hub connecting pathogenic mechanisms to therapeutic modalities. To facilitate the development of novel treatments, future research must: (1) decipher the regulatory circuitry of the “upstream signal → FKBP51 → GSK-3β” axis; (2) delineate cell type- and disease-specific functions of FKBP51- GSK-3β; and (3) characterize its role in microglia-mediated neuroimmune crosstalk.

FKBP51 is critical for autophagy regulation, an intracellular degradation pathway that sustains cellular homeostasis (Menzies et al., 2015). Research demonstrates that FKBP51 regulates AKT1/BECN1 phosphorylation and activates autophagy by assembling into the “FKBP51-AKT1-PHLPP-BECN1” complex—a mechanism critical for antidepressant action across cellular, animal, and human models (Gassen et al., 2015). FKBP51 uses its TPR domain as a scaffold to bind, stabilize, and orchestrate assembly of the VPS34 complex, thereby activating autophagy initiation (Park et al., 2025). Furthermore, in the mediobasal hypothalamus, FKBP51 integrates metabolic stress signals via scaffolding AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-activating and mechanistic target of rapamycin kinase complex 1 (MTORC1)-inhibiting complexes to regulate autophagy. Notably, FKBP51 and autophagy display a biphasic, inverted U-shaped association: moderate FKBP51 levels promote autophagic flux and metabolic homeostasis, FKBP51 deficiency impairs autophagy, and paradoxically, its overexpression suppresses central but enhances peripheral autophagy (Bajaj et al., 2022). In Huntington's disease models, FKBP5 knockdown or inhibition enhances autophagic flux via an unc-51 like autophagy activating kinase 1 (ULK1)-mediated mechanism that is independent of mTOR (Bailus et al., 2021). In cerebral ischemia and reperfusion (I/R) injury, FKBP51 drives pathogenic autophagy by upregulating its expression: this inhibits AKT/FOXO3 phosphorylation, activates autophagic pathways, and exacerbates neuronal damage (Yu et al., 2020). In demyelinating diseases, FKBP51 acts as a critical switch linking pathological stimuli to mitophagy activation via PPAR-γ inhibition and PINK1-Parkin disinhibition. This pathway eliminates damaged mitochondria, mitigates secondary damage, and promotes remyelination by optimizing the regenerative microenvironment (Sun et al., 2023). Autophagy serves as both a pathogenic mechanism and therapeutic target in neuropsychiatric diseases (Gulbins et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2023); thus, elucidating the FKBP51-autophagy axis is critical for the development of novel therapies (Fig. 7).Fig. 7FKBP51 Modulates Autophagic PathwaysA: FKBP51 Scaffolds AKT1-PHLPP to Activate Autophagy. As a complex modulator of autophagy, FKBP51 exerts distinct, region-specific functions across different pathological models. Given this nuance, its effects require case-by-case analysis. Future studies should prioritize mechanistic dissection of FKBP51 in individual disease states to facilitate precise therapeutic targeting.

FKBP51-related drugs

Multiple FKBP51-targeting compounds exhibit therapeutic potential. The potent, selective FKBP51 inhibitors SAFit1 and SAFit2 have therapeutic potential (Gaali et al., 2015); notably, SAFit2 alleviates neuropathic pain and neuroinflammation via FKBP51 inhibition (Wedel et al., 2022, 2023). Rapamycin enhances autophagy through binding to FKBP51, which is enabled by the FKBP51's structural homology with FKBP12 (Sinars et al., 2003). Additional FKBP5/FKBP51 antagonists include benztropine, which disrupts FKBP51– GR/Hsp90 complex formation (Sabbagh et al., 2018); fluoxetine, which reverses stress-induced FKBP51 upregulation and depressive behaviors (Zhu et al., 2025); and CBD, which directly binds to FKBP51 to inhibit IKK–NF-κB signaling and downstream inflammation (Wang et al., 2023). Other candidates targeting FKBP51-related pathways include vitamin D (Kassi et al., 2016), JQ1 (a BET protein inhibitor) (Zeng et al., 2023), galiellalactone (a selective STAT3 inhibitor) (Handle et al., 2018), mifepristone and the selective GR antagonist CORT125134 (Bali et al., 2016).

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) research, particularly in its clinical efficacy and mechanisms, has received increasing attention in recent years. By targeting FKBP51, the active components of Xiaoyao San (e.g., baicalin A, paeoniflorin) inhibit FKBP51 expression and its interaction with IKKα/β, thereby suppressing overactivation of the NF-κB pathway. This cascade restores the immune T helper 17 (Th17)/regulatory T (Treg) cell balance, which in turn ameliorates chronic stress-induced depression-like behaviors and neurological impairment (Zhang et al., 2025). Animal and cell experiments confirm that ginsenosides restore the HPA axis negative feedback by downregulating FKBP51 and alleviating its suppression of GR nuclear translocation, ultimately ameliorating chronic stress-induced depressive symptoms (Li et al., 2024). Song et al. evaluated the therapeutic effect of an essential oil blend (bergamot, peppermint, rose) in a CMS-induced depression model. For inhalation aromatherapy, depressive symptom amelioration relied on a silent information regulator1 (Sirt1)-mediated pathway rather than direct FKBP51 inhibition: increased Sirt1 expression suppressed FKBP51 activity, which subsequently restored GR function and attenuated NF-κB-driven inflammation (Song et al., 2025). Although TCM has been shown to ameliorate depressive disorders by targeting FKBP51, its role in other neuropsychiatric conditions remains unaddressed. Given the shared mechanistic pathways (e.g., GR dysfunction, NF-κB signaling) among depression, anxiety, and PTSD, FKBP51-targeted TCM strategies could have wider translational utility. Expanding this line of research would therefore establish a mechanistic rationale for repurposing TCM interventions in these related disorders.

FKBP51 is integral to the pathophysiology of stress-related neuropsychiatric diseases, functioning as a core regulator of GR signaling to integrate neuroendocrine, inflammatory, and neurotransmitter responses to stress. Its expression is co-modulated by genetic variants and environmental stressors, and this gene–environment interplay determines individual disease risk. Given its centrality, FKBP51 holds dual value: a potential biomarker for stress-related pathology and a viable therapeutic target. Pharmacological inhibition of FKBP51, for instance, may alleviate symptoms and improve prognosis in affected individuals. To advance translational progress, further research into FKBP51's mechanisms—particularly how gene–environment interactions modulate its function—is essential for developing targeted, personalized treatments. In short, deeper insights into FKBP51 could drive the development of novel therapies, enhancing clinical care and quality of life for patients with stress-related disorders.

Conclusion

FKBP5/FKBP51, core regulators of the GR signaling pathway, are critical to the pathophysiology of multiple neuropsychiatric diseases. They thus represent promising biomarkers for neuropsychiatric disease assessment and viable therapeutic targets. Notably, FKBP51 exhibits distinct sex differences, brain region/cell type specificity, and temporal specificity during disease progression-key drivers of conflicting findings in current research. To resolve this, individual disease cases should be analyzed separately, or additional studies conducted to establish a consistent understanding. Finally, we propose a “biphasic stress-response model” that effectively captures the dynamic changes in FKBP5/FKBP51 levels throughout neuropsychiatric disease progression.

Three-dimensional Perfusion-zone Models Allow More Selective Clamping During Robot-assisted Partial Nephrectomy: Brief Report on a Retrospective Analysis
Authors: Joris Vangeneugden, Saar Vermijs, Pieter De Backer, Brecht Hemeryck, Camille Berquin, Pieter De Visschere, Karel Decaestecker, Charlotte Debbaut, Charles Van Praet
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelective clamping (SC) of the renal artery during robot-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) may yield better preservation of renal function in comparison to main-artery clamping (MAC), especially in patients at higher risk of kidney injury. This retrospective study investigated whether the availability of three-dimensional (3D) renal perfusion models influences the choice between SC and MAC in RAPN. A total of 148 procedures for T1–2 renal tumors performed between January 2020 and June 2024 at a single referral center were analyzed. All surgeries involved either SC or MAC; off-clamp cases were excluded. In 74% of cases, a 3D perfusion-zone (3DPZ) model was available at the time of surgery. Surgeons always aimed to perform SC if deemed feasible. A strong association between the availability of a 3DPZ model and SC use was observed (χ2p < 0.001). This result suggests that access to 3DPZ models may support a more selective approach to renal artery clamping when pursuing an SC strategy. While limited by its retrospective nature and potential selection bias, this study provides a rationale for further investigation of the benefits and clinical outcomes of 3DPZ model use during RAPN. Although robust evidence is lacking, selective clamping (SC) of the renal artery during robot-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) may enhance postoperative kidney function in comparison to main-artery clamping (MAC) [1]. To date, several studies have failed to demonstrate a clear benefit of SC for long-term kidney function outcomes [2,3]. However, most of these studies were conducted in patients with normal renal function, had crossover from SC to MAC, and/or did not have novel technologies such as three-dimensional (3D) perfusion-zone (3DPZ) models available [[4], [5], [6]]. The general consensus among experts is that SC might be of particular interest for patients with impaired renal function, a solitary kidney, or an anticipated longer clamping time >30 min. The aforementioned 3DPZ models show vascular regions and may help in guiding SC to preserve more healthy kidney tissue (Fig. 1). Our study explored the association between 3DPZ model availability and the clamping strategy (SC vs MAC) used during RAPN.

All RAPN cases performed at a single referral center from January 2020 (when 3D modeling in this center was first implemented) to June 2024 were retrospectively reviewed using a prospectively collected database. Ethics committee approval was obtained (reference BC-07320). We included patients with T1–2 renal tumors for which SC or MAC was applied. RAPN cases performed without clamping of the renal artery were excluded. The 3DPZ models used (Fig. 1) were created in-house in collaboration with a team of bioengineers and were clinically validated in previous research [4]. The clamping strategy used in relation to the availability of a 3DPZ model at the time of surgery was analyzed. A χ2 test was applied to evaluate the association, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

In total, 148 RAPN procedures were included. The risk according to the PADUA tumor complexity score was low in 39% (PADUA 6–7), intermediate in 43% (PADUA 8–9), and high in 18% (PADUA ≥10) of the patients [7]. All surgeries were performed by three experienced robotic surgeons, although all were going through their learning curve for 3DPZ model use at the beginning of the study period. By the end of the data collection period, all surgeons had extensive experience in using 3DPZ models for nephron-sparing surgery. For all cases throughout the entire study period, all surgeons aimed to perform SC as the preferred strategy, if deemed feasible. SC feasibility depended on the number and branching pattern of renal arteries, the predicted healthy renal volume spared, the expected ischemia time and, potentially most importantly, intraoperative findings, among other factors. A clamping strategy was planned preoperatively using either a computed tomography (CT) scan with a 3DPZ model, or a CT scan alone if no model was available. The final decision on the clamping strategy was made intraoperatively. A 3DPZ model was used in 109/148 (74%) cases; in the other 26%, no model was available. The availability of a 3DPZ model did not significantly differ across PADUA risk groups (χ2 p = 0.256). While the creation of a 3DPZ model was the consistent aim of every surgeon and was independent of case complexity, availability was occasionally limited because of suboptimal CT scan quality or logistical factors stemming from coordination with bioengineers. The availability of a 3DPZ model was significantly associated with the use of SC during RAPN (p < 0.001; Table 1).

Notwithstanding the retrospective and potentially biased nature of our study, it may encourage the use of 3DPZ models during RAPN when pursuing a SC strategy. Several groups have previously reported multiple advantages of 3D modeling during RAPN, including a more selective clamping approach, enhanced preoperative assessment, lower complication rates, and sometimes even better postoperative kidney function [6,[8], [9], [10]]. Although critical appraisal of the development and indication for use of 3DPZ models is warranted [11], there is general consensus on the usefulness of 3D modeling in RAPN. However, there is still debate on the adoption of SC and the real clinical benefits of this technique. To aid in this debate and to further substantiate the findings from our retrospective study, we are currently conducting the PODRACING randomized controlled trial (NCT06536439) [12]. PODRACING is investigating whether the use of 3DPZ models increases the application and accuracy of SC during RAPN, as well as patient health outcomes (in particular kidney function outcomes), patient insight, and surgeon benefits.

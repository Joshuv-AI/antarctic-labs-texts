A simple ratio, a complex outcome: glucose-to-albumin ratio predicts delirium in ICU patients after cardiac surgery
Authors: Zhen Yuan, Aoli Chen, Yangbo Hou, Qiuxin Xu, Yunqing Zeng, Jiwei Cheng, Rui Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative delirium (POD) is a frequent and serious complication after cardiac surgery. The glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) integrates metabolic and nutritional status, but its predictive value for POD in intensive care unit (ICU) patients remains uncertain.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study using data from the MIMIC-IV (v3.1) database. A total of 4,307 adult ICU patients admitted within 24 h after cardiac surgery were included. GAR was calculated by dividing serum glucose (mg/dL) by albumin (g/dL). The primary outcome was POD, assessed using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM). GAR was evaluated as a continuous variable, by quartiles, and by a dichotomized cutoff determined from the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. Logistic regression, restricted cubic splines, and mediation analyses were applied. To reduce confounding, propensity score matching was performed, and the main logistic regression analysis was repeated in the matched cohort.ResultsOf 4,307 patients, 658 (15.3%) developed POD and 120 (2.8%) died within 90 days. Higher GAR was independently associated with POD (continuous odds ratio [OR] 1.01, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.00–1.01, p = 0.027; highest vs. lowest quartile OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.16–2.05, p = 0.003). The optimal GAR cutoff was 35 (AUROC 0.654). Patients with GAR ≥ 35 had higher delirium risk in both the full cohort (adjusted OR 1.37, 95% CI 1.13–1.67, p = 0.001) and the matched cohort (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.06–1.61, p = 0.012). Mediation analysis suggested that prolonged mechanical ventilation and ICU stay partly explained the GAR–POD relationship.ConclusionGAR is an independent predictor of postoperative delirium in ICU patients undergoing cardiac surgery. It may serve as a practical biomarker for early delirium risk stratification. Prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and to further establish the clinical utility of GAR in perioperative care.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05228-z.Keywords: MIMIC-IV1, Delirium2, Glucose3, Albumin4, Cardiac Surgery5

Introduction

Postoperative delirium (POD) is an acute disorder of consciousness and cognition [1] that is associated with adverse outcomes, including prolonged hospitalization and increased mortality [2]. It commonly occurs following major surgical procedures, particularly in patients undergoing cardiac surgery [3]. The reported incidence of POD after cardiac surgery ranges widely from 2.9% to 54.9% [4]. The pathophysiology of delirium is multifactorial and remains incompletely understood. Emerging evidence suggests that imbalances in neurotransmitter systems, systemic inflammation, and nutritional deficiencies play critical roles in its development [5]. Among these, malnutrition and systemic inflammatory responses are increasingly recognized as significant contributors to the onset of POD [6].

The glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) has recently emerged as a composite biomarker that integrates serum glucose and albumin levels, reflecting both the inflammatory and nutritional status of patients. Albumin, which constitutes more than 50% of total plasma protein, serves as an important indicator of nutritional reserves and systemic physiological integrity [7]. Conversely, fluctuations in blood glucose levels are indicative of metabolic stress and the acute systemic response to illness or surgery [8]. Previous studies have demonstrated that intraoperative hyperglycemia—defined as a blood glucose level exceeding 150 mg/dL—is associated with a fourfold increase in the risk of postoperative delirium [5].

The glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) is a composite indicator that reflects the dual burden of metabolic dysregulation and systemic inflammatory stress. Hyperglycemia is not only a hallmark of metabolic stress but also an important driver of systemic inflammation [9]. Elevated glucose levels exacerbate oxidative stress, promote endothelial dysfunction, and stimulate the release of proinflammatory cytokines, all of which may contribute to neuronal injury [10]. In contrast, albumin is a multifunctional plasma protein that preserves endothelial integrity, modulates inflammatory responses, and exerts antioxidant effects [11]. Hypoalbuminemia reflects diminished nutritional and physiological reserves and may further aggravate inflammation, vascular permeability, and blood–brain barrier disruption [12]. Therefore, the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) integrates two interconnected pathways—hyperglycemia-driven inflammation and hypoalbuminemia-associated vulnerability—offering a more comprehensive index of metabolic and inflammatory stress than either parameter alone. A recent study involving elderly patients with hip fractures demonstrated that an elevated preoperative GAR was independently associated with a higher incidence of postoperative delirium [13].

We hypothesized that an elevated glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) would be independently associated with an increased risk of postoperative delirium following cardiac surgery. However, evidence regarding the predictive value of GAR for delirium in patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) after cardiac surgery remains scarce. This gap in knowledge underscores the need for further investigation.

Materials and methods

Data source

This study was designed as a retrospective cohort study using the MIMIC-IV version 3.1 database. The Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) is a freely accessible critical care database developed by the MIT Laboratory for Computational Physiology [14]. It provides a robust platform for high-quality research in critical care medicine. This publicly available dataset contains comprehensive electronic health records of patients admitted to the intensive care units (ICUs) of Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, Massachusetts, between 2008 and 2019. The author of this study, Yuan Zhen, completed the National Institutes of Health (NIH) training program and passed the “Protecting Human Research Participants” exam (ID: 65422903). As all patient identifiers in the database are encrypted to ensure privacy and confidentiality, institutional review board (IRB) approval and informed consent were not required for this study.

Study population

Patients were included in the study if they met the following criteria: (1) admission to the ICU within 24 h after undergoing cardiac surgery; (2) first ICU admission during the hospitalization; and (3) availability of delirium assessment records. Patients who underwent cardiac surgery were identified using ICD-9 and ICD-10 procedure codes, as detailed in Supplementary Table S1. The following exclusion criteria were applied: (1) a diagnosis of dementia or schizophrenia (the corresponding ICD codes are provided in Table S1), (2) an ICU stay of less than 24 h, (3) missing data on albumin or blood glucose, or (4) delirium occurring within 24 h of ICU admission. In line with previous studies [15, 16], patients who developed delirium within the first 24 h of ICU admission were excluded, as such cases are often influenced by preoperative confounding factors. After applying these criteria, a total of 4,307 eligible cardiac surgery patients were included for further analysis. A detailed flowchart of the patient selection process is presented in Fig. 1.

The flow of the enrolled patients throughout the study

For missing data, the proportion of missingness for all variables was less than 6% (Supplementary Table S2). Mean imputation was applied for variables with a normal distribution, while median imputation was used for skewed variables.

Study variables

We extracted demographic and clinical data for all patients admitted to the ICU, including age, sex, race, weight, height, smoking status, and alcohol consumption. Vital signs included heart rate, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, peripheral oxygen saturation, and respiratory rate. Laboratory parameters included serum sodium, potassium, hemoglobin, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, platelet count, white blood cell count, chloride, albumin, glucose, and the international normalized ratio (all based on the last measurement obtained prior to surgery).

In addition, we collected initial severity and comorbidity scores upon ICU admission, including the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), Acute Physiology Score III (APSIII), and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score. Recorded comorbidities included acute myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, chronic pulmonary disease, liver disease, acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, malignant cancer, hyperlipidemia, and sepsis. Intervention measures included preoperative use of benzodiazepines and beta-blockers, renal replacement therapy, and postoperative use of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO). Detailed information on these variables is provided in Supplementary Table S3.

Data extraction was performed using PostgreSQL version 13.9 and Navicat Premium version 17.0.4 with structured query language (SQL). All extraction codes for demographic variables, laboratory indicators, comorbidities, and clinical scores were sourced from the publicly available GitHub repository maintained by the MIMIC research group (https://github.com/MIT-LCP/mimic-code).

Definitions and clinical outcomes

The glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) was calculated by dividing the serum glucose level (mg/dL) by the serum albumin level (g/dL) [17]. The primary outcome of this study was postoperative delirium, which was assessed using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM). CAM is a standardized, evidence-based tool for the identification of delirium and includes four core diagnostic features: (1) acute onset and fluctuating course, (2) inattention, (3) disorganized thinking, and (4) altered level of consciousness [18]. A diagnosis of delirium requires the presence of both Feature 1 and Feature 2, in addition to either Feature 3 or Feature 4.

We extracted CAM-related assessment data corresponding to these four features from the “chartevents” table in the database and performed algorithm-based diagnostic screening. CAM has demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance in ICU settings, with an overall specificity of approximately 96% and a pooled sensitivity of about 80% across nine studies involving critically ill adult patients [19]. In the MIMIC-IV database, delirium assessment with the CAM-ICU was generally performed multiple times per day, typically at least twice daily, by trained nursing staff. The secondary outcome of interest was 90-day all-cause mortality among patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Statistical analysis

We used the quartile method to categorize 4,307 patients according to the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR). In addition to modeling GAR as both a continuous and quartile-based variable, we also evaluated it as a binary variable. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the Youden index, which identifies the point at which the sum of sensitivity and specificity is maximized [20].

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Non-normally distributed continuous variables were reported as median and interquartile range (IQR) and compared using the Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and comparisons among groups were performed using the chi-square test. These methods were used to evaluate baseline differences among patients across GAR quartiles.

In the regression analysis, we first performed a univariate logistic regression of the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) with postoperative delirium (POD) and a univariate Cox regression of GAR with 90-day mortality, selecting variables that were either statistically or clinically significant. To assess multicollinearity, we calculated the generalized variance inflation factor (GVIF) for GAR and included covariates. A value of GVIF1/(2×Df) < 2 was considered indicative of no multicollinearity [21].Based on these results, we further constructed multivariate logistic and Cox regression models.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was applied to assess potential non-linear associations between GAR and postoperative delirium.

To further investigate the relationship between the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) and perioperative outcomes, we examined its association with the duration of postoperative mechanical ventilation and ICU length of stay, both of which are well-recognized risk factors for delirium [22–24]. A linear regression model adjusted for covariates was applied. In addition, we performed a mediation analysis to determine whether the duration of mechanical ventilation or ICU length of stay mediated the association between GAR and postoperative delirium (POD), using the nonparametric Baron–Kenny bootstrap method with 1,000 simulations.

In addition to the primary analysis, several sensitivity analyses were conducted to evaluate the robustness of our findings. To further investigate the association between the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) and postoperative delirium in patients undergoing cardiac surgery, GAR was dichotomized according to the cutoff value identified by the Youden index in the ROC curve. Propensity score matching based on generalized boosted model (GBM)-derived scores was then applied to minimize baseline confounding [25]. Logistic regression analysis was subsequently performed to estimate the association between GAR and postoperative delirium.

To further elucidate the independent contributions of glucose and albumin, we conducted additional analyses examining their associations with postoperative delirium (POD). For each biomarker, univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were constructed using the same set of covariates as in the GAR model. In addition, two ordinal multivariate models with stepwise covariate adjustment were developed. Subgroup analyses were stratified by sex (male vs. female), age [22, 26] (< 65 years vs. ≥65 years), hypertension (yes vs. no), malignant cancer (yes vs. no), sepsis (yes vs. no), cerebrovascular disease (yes vs. no), and benzodiazepine use (yes vs. no). Within each subgroup, a multivariable logistic regression model was fitted. Effect modification was formally evaluated by including interaction terms between continuous GAR and the subgroup variables, and likelihood ratio tests were performed to compare models with and without the interaction terms.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.0) and Free Statistics software (version 1.8).This study was conducted in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. The complete STROBE checklist is provided in the supplementary material (Table S4).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 9,028 patients who underwent cardiac surgery were initially identified. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 4,307 patients were ultimately included in the final cohort (Fig. 1), with no missing follow-up data observed in the database. The mean age of the included patients was 68.0 ± 11.6 years, and 3,095 (71.9%) were male. Postoperative delirium occurred in 658 patients (15.3%), while 90-day mortality was observed in 120 patients (2.8%).

Patients were stratified into four groups based on GAR quartiles: Group 1 (GAR ≤ 9.00), Group 2 (9.00 < GAR ≤ 27.05), Group 3 (27.05< GAR ≤ 32.00), and Group 4 (GAR > 32.00). Notably, patients in the highest GAR group (Group 4) exhibited distinct clinical features compared with those in the lower GAR groups. Specifically, they had a lower proportion of males, higher heart and respiratory rates, and lower peripheral oxygen saturation. Laboratory findings revealed elevated levels of serum potassium, glucose, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, white blood cell count, and platelet count, alongside decreased serum albumin and chloride concentrations. Additionally, these patients had significantly higher severity scores, including SOFA, Charlson Comorbidity Index, and APSIII.

In terms of comorbidities, patients in the highest GAR group had higher incidences of acute myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, hypertension, liver disease, acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and sepsis. They were also more likely to use benzodiazepines, postoperative extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), postoperative albumin supplementation, and renal replacement therapy. Multivariate regression analysis

If collinearity was detected, the affected variables were excluded from the final model to avoid unstable estimates. After performing univariate regression analyses (Table S5) and excluding variables with potential multicollinearity (Table S6), we constructed multivariate models to examine the association between the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) and outcomes. Based on both statistical results and clinical relevance, a multivariate logistic regression model was used to assess the relationship between GAR and postoperative delirium (POD). After adjusting for confounding factors, GAR was identified as an independent predictor of delirium when analyzed as a continuous variable (odds ratio [OR]: 1.01; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.00–1.01; p = 0.027). When analyzed as a categorical variable, patients in the highest GAR quartile demonstrated a significantly increased risk of delirium compared to those in the lowest quartile (OR: 1.54; 95% CI: 1.16–2.05; p = 0.003), indicating that elevated GAR levels were correlated with higher likelihood of postoperative delirium.

In addition, to assess the association between GAR and the secondary outcome—90-day all-cause mortality—we performed Cox proportional hazards regression using both continuous and categorical GAR variables. The results similarly revealed that higher GAR levels were associated with an increased risk of mortality. Detailed results from these regression analyses are presented in Table 2.

Optimal GAR cutoff value determined by ROC analysis

A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was constructed to evaluate the discriminatory ability of the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) in predicting postoperative delirium (POD). The area under the ROC curve (AUROC) was 0.654 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.630–0.677), indicating moderate predictive accuracy. Based on the Youden index (0.240), the optimal GAR cutoff value was identified as 35, corresponding to a sensitivity of 57.8% and a specificity of 66.3% (Fig. 2).

ROC curve of GAR for predicting postoperative delirium

Curve fitting

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was conducted to further explore the relationship between GAR and the risk of postoperative delirium. A positive association was observed between GAR and delirium risk. Although the non-linear trend did not reach statistical significance (P for non-linearity = 0.571), the overall association was highly significant (P for overall < 0.001), suggesting an approximately linear relationship between GAR and the incidence of delirium(Fig. 3).

Association and mediation analysis between GAR and duration of mechanical ventilation and ICU length of stay

Crude analyses demonstrated that higher glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) values were significantly associated with longer postoperative mechanical ventilation (β = 1.21; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.07–1.35; P < 0.001) and longer ICU length of stay (β = 0.09; 95% CI: 0.08–0.10; P < 0.001). These associations remained significant, though attenuated, after adjustment for demographic characteristics, comorbidities, laboratory parameters, surgical factors, and postoperative interventions. Specifically, each unit increase in GAR was associated with an additional 0.21 h of postoperative ventilation (95% CI: 0.08–0.34; P = 0.002) and an additional 0.02 days of ICU stay (95% CI: 0.01–0.03; P < 0.001) (Table 3). Supplementary Figure S1 presents the mediation analysis assessing the indirect effect of GAR on postoperative delirium (POD). The results indicated that both ventilation duration and ICU length of stay were significant mediators, with mediation proportions of 3.02% (P < 0.01) and 1.81% (P < 0.05), respectively. These findings indicate that prolonged mechanical ventilation and extended ICU stay may partly account for the observed association between GAR and POD.

Sensitivity analysis and subgroup analysis

To assess the robustness of the association between GAR and postoperative delirium (POD), patients were dichotomized based on a GAR cutoff of 35. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to reduce baseline confounding. After matching, both groups demonstrated good balance in baseline characteristics, with all standardized mean differences (SMDs) < 0.1. The distribution of covariate balance before and after matching is visualized in Figure S2.

In the unmatched cohort, patients with a high GAR (≥ 35) had a significantly higher risk of POD compared with those with a GAR < 35. In the unadjusted analysis, the odds ratio (OR) was 2.62 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.22–3.11; P < 0.001). After covariate adjustment, the association remained significant (OR: 1.37; 95% CI: 1.13–1.67; P = 0.001). In the propensity score–matched cohort, high GAR remained significantly associated with POD (OR: 1.31; 95% CI: 1.06–1.61; P = 0.012), confirming the robustness of our findings (Table 4).

Discussion

Our study demonstrated that an elevated glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) is a significant predictor of postoperative delirium (POD) in cardiac surgery patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). A linear positive association between GAR and delirium risk was observed, and GAR remained independently associated with POD after adjusting for potential confounders, whether modeled as a continuous variable or stratified into quartiles. In our cohort, 15.3% of patients developed delirium, which is consistent with the previously reported incidence range of 11% to 55% in cardiac surgery populations [27].Postoperative delirium is a serious complication that can result in prolonged hospitalization and increased risk of hospital readmission [28]. A recent meta-analysis further indicated that patients who develop POD have a fourfold higher mortality rate compared to those without delirium [29]. These findings underscore the clinical importance of early identification and risk stratification for delirium, particularly in high-risk populations such as cardiac surgical ICU patients.

The pathophysiology of delirium remains incompletely understood; however, several mechanisms have been implicated, including reduced cerebral perfusion, altered brain metabolism, dopaminergic hyperactivity, and cholinergic deficiency [5]. Hyperglycemia has been been linked to oxidative stress and cerebral endothelial dysfunction, factors that have been implicated in delirium [30]. In addition to direct neuronal injury, both delirium and systemic inflammation are associated with the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) [31]. This bidirectional relationship may contribute to a cycle in which hyperglycemia is associated with amplified inflammatory responses, and inflammation, in turn, is linked with further dysregulation of glucose metabolism [10].Albumin is a multifunctional plasma protein involved in the regulation of inflammation, maintenance of endothelial integrity and oncotic pressure, and the transport of hormones, drugs, and toxins. It also possesses antioxidant properties that help mitigate oxidative damage [11]. Hypoalbuminemia can impair these physiological defenses, rendering the brain more susceptible to injury. Decreased colloid osmotic pressure resulting from low albumin levels may promote microvascular leakage, cerebral edema, and impaired circulatory homeostasis, potentially compromising the integrity of the blood–brain barrier (BBB) [12].Given the dual contribution of hyperglycemia and hypoalbuminemia to delirium pathogenesis, our study focused not only on individual changes in glucose or albumin but also on their combined effect. By analyzing the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR), we aimed to capture a more integrated marker of metabolic and inflammatory stress. This composite index may allow for more accurate prediction of postoperative delirium risk in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

As an emerging clinical biomarker, the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) has been investigated in several recent studies. A 2024 study demonstrated that GAR was significantly associated with the risk of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) in non-diabetic adults, and its predictive value exceeded that of glucose or albumin alone [17]. Other research has identified GAR as a novel predictor of postoperative pneumonia (POP) in elderly patients following hip fracture [32]. Furthermore, a 2025 study reported that GAR was an independent prognostic factor for 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis [33]. More recently, attention has turned to the relationship between GAR and postoperative delirium. For example, GAR has been shown to be significantly associated with POD in elderly hip fracture patients, where it has been proposed as an important preoperative risk assessment tool [13]. Our findings further expand the clinical relevance of GAR by demonstrating its predictive value for postoperative delirium in cardiac surgery patients admitted to the ICU.

Notably, ROC curve analysis identified a GAR of 35 as the optimal cutoff for predicting POD, with an AUROC of 0.654, indicating moderate discriminative ability in this population. While this threshold provides a potential tool for perioperative risk stratification, prospective validation in multicenter cohorts is warranted. Our mediation analysis further suggested that the effect of elevated GAR on delirium risk may be partially indirect, mediated through prolonged mechanical ventilation and extended ICU stay. Patients with higher GAR values were more likely to require longer postoperative ventilatory support and ICU care, both of which are established risk factors for POD [34, 35]. Mechanistically, an elevated GAR may reflect heightened surgical stress and systemic inflammation—characterized by hyperglycemia and hypoalbuminemia—that hinder recovery. Hyperglycemia promotes inflammatory pathways and oxidative stress, potentially leading to complications such as infection and organ dysfunction, thereby prolonging respiratory support. GAR may help identify patients vulnerable to a ‘vicious cycle’ of metabolic–inflammatory dysregulation, prolonged critical illness, and delirium. Sensitivity analyses further demonstrated that GAR outperformed its individual components (glucose or albumin alone) in predicting POD, underscoring the value of this composite index.Prior studies on hyperglycemia and POD have reported mixed results, with some linking poor glycemic control to higher delirium incidence [36], while others found that mild hyperglycemia was not predictive or could even be protective [37]. The superior performance of GAR likely reflects its ability to identify patients with concomitant hyperglycemia, systemic inflammation, and malnutrition—a combination particularly predisposing to delirium.

This study also has several limitations. First, it was a single-center, retrospective study based on the MIMIC-IV database, and residual confounding cannot be fully excluded; thus, validation in prospective, multicenter cohorts is warranted. Second, due to inherent database constraints, the ICD-9/10 codes in MIMIC-IV are recorded for each hospital admission but lack specific diagnostic timestamps [38]. This limitation applies to all studies relying on MIMIC administrative codes and is unlikely to systematically bias our findings. We therefore assumed that these codes reflected past medical history, although incidental diagnoses during hospitalization cannot be completely ruled out. Third, our analysis focused only on preoperative GAR levels and did not assess dynamic changes over time, limiting insights into longitudinal patterns and their impact on prognosis. Fourth, covariates were not predefined a priori. Although variable selection was guided by literature and clinical plausibility, this data-driven approach may have introduced residual confounding or model overfitting. Fifth, the definition of POD relied exclusively on the CAM assessments recorded in the database. Other validated tools, such as CAM-ICU, ICDSC, or MoCA, were not systematically available, which may have led to underdetection. In addition, patients with delirium occurring within the first 24 h after surgery were excluded to reduce perioperative confounding, which may have underestimated the true incidence. Hypoactive delirium is also more difficult to identify and frequently underreported, potentially leading to misclassification. Collectively, these issues may have biased our incidence estimates toward the lower end of the reported range. Nevertheless, the observed incidence (15.3%) remained consistent with previous reports in cardiac surgery populations, suggesting that our findings are robust despite potential misclassification. Finally, several important perioperative variables were not available in MIMIC-IV, including duration of anesthesia, cardiopulmonary bypass time, intraoperative glycemic control, transfusion volume, clamping time, postoperative ARDS, and EUROScore. As these factors are recognized risk factors for POD, their absence may have introduced additional residual confounding.

Conclusion

This retrospective study identified the glucose-to-albumin ratio (GAR) as an independent predictor of postoperative delirium in cardiac surgery ICU patients. GAR showed a linear association with delirium risk, and this relationship remained consistent after multivariable adjustment and propensity score matching. As a composite biomarker reflecting metabolic and inflammatory status, GAR may aid in early risk assessment for delirium. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings.

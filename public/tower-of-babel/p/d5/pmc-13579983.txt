Dual-Model framework for CHIKV transmission modeling: ODE and Petri Net Analysis of the 2025 Foshan outbreak
Authors: Hong Liu, Jingjing Tian, Xuanfeng Li, Yiping Li, Jiayi Li, Zifeng Yang, Xiaoyan Deng, Ming Xu, Chitin Hon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study addresses the 2025 Chikungunya outbreak in Foshan City, Guangdong Province, China, by constructing a dual-model framework based on Ordinary Differential Equations (ODE) and Petri Nets (PN) for comparative analysis of Chikungunya transmission dynamics and reproduction number estimation methods. The research employs SEICR (Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Chronic-Recovered) compartmental modeling to compare two formal representations under matched epidemiological assumptions, and evaluates the timing of epidemic control measures through a three-phase intervention fitting protocol. Model validation results show that both models achieve root mean square errors (RMSE) of 30.98 (ODE) and 31.05 (PN), mean absolute errors (MAE) of 15.57 and 15.78, and and 0.9498, respectively. Both models predict epidemic peaks at day 33 (406 cases), occurring 3 days earlier than the observed peak (432 cases), with a peak value error of 6.0%. Residual analysis reveals that negative residuals account for 71.4% (ODE) and 73.8% (PN) of the observation-window residuals, suggesting a structured overprediction pattern in descriptive diagnostics. Reproduction number analysis reveals that the initial transmission indicators are approximately 14.67 (ODE)/13.90 (PN), with effective values progressively decreasing through three intervention phases: 7.85/7.86 after Phase 1, 7.59/7.56 after Phase 2, and 0.059 in Phase 3, below the transmission threshold. An additional no-demography robustness check shows that removing demographic turnover changes total predicted cases by only 0.03%, suggesting that the remaining uncertainty lies mainly in omitted vector-side dynamics rather than in human-side demography. Sensitivity analysis indicates that the recovery rate (γ) is the most sensitive parameter affecting within this formulation, with a Sobol index of 0.9672, explaining 96.72% of total variation. This study provides a controlled comparison between ODE and Petri Net representations of the same epidemiological structure, offering a transparent comparative framework for outbreak fitting, intervention phase identification, and future extension toward explicit host-vector models.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-28110-9.Keywords: Chikungunya, Basic reproduction number, Petri Net, ODE, Next-generation matrix, Intervention modeling

Introduction

Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a public health concern in tropical and subtropical regions, with recent outbreaks demonstrating the need for rapid response and accurate transmission modeling. In July 2025, a significant CHIKV outbreak occurred in Foshan City, Guangdong Province, China. The onset-date series analyzed in this study begins on June 16, while official reports also described an imported case detected through active surveillance in Shunde District on July 8th [1]. The outbreak rapidly expanded, with 1,873 confirmed cases reported by July 19th, primarily concentrated in Shunde District (1,790 cases) across Letong, Beijiao, and Chencun towns, with additional cases in Chancheng (49 cases) and Nanhai (34 cases) districts [2]. All cases were mild with no severe or fatal outcomes, and 720 patients had recovered by that time. The outbreak continued to grow, reaching 2,934 cases in Shunde District by July 22nd, and even spread beyond Guangdong Province to Macau Special Administrative Region [1]. Timely estimation of the basic reproduction number () and the phase-specific effective reproduction number () is central to situational awareness and control evaluation during such outbreaks. Recent epidemiological studies of CHIKV outbreaks have demonstrated the importance of timely R estimation for intervention planning [1, 2]. Traditional CHIKV modeling has relied heavily on compartmental ODE models for transmission dynamics and reproduction number estimation [3, 4]. However, existing ODE-based approaches face limitations in representing complex intervention scenarios, resource constraints, and the discrete nature of disease transmission events [5]. Compartmental ordinary differential equation (ODE) models are standard; however, Petri Nets (PN) provide a discrete-event, compositional alternative well-suited for switching interventions and resource-aware processes.

Petri Nets have seen growing use as a complementary formalism to ODEs for epidemiological modeling and system composition [5, 6]. Generalized and colored/stochastic variants enable concurrency and resource-aware representations [7]. In vector-borne contexts, MGDrivE employs stochastic Petri Nets for mosquito life history and epidemiological coupling [8]. Numerical studies show PN implementations can reproduce SIR-type ODE dynamics at small discretization error [9]. Nonetheless, systematic side-by-side comparisons of ODE and PN under the same epidemiological scenarios appear relatively limited in the literature.

To address this gap, the present study takes the 2025 Foshan CHIKV outbreak as a case study and provides a comparative framework based on structurally matched ODE and Petri Net implementations of the same human-side SEICR design. Because both implementations share the same state variables, rate functions, and calibration target, their comparison evaluates consistency between model representations rather than implying a distinct biological transmission mechanism for PN.

This study contributes: first, an SEICR model is implemented in both ODE and PN, maintaining a one-to-one mapping between states and flows so that the two implementations remain dynamically matched; second, a three-phase intervention fitting protocol for β (t) is tailored to align with the 2025 Foshan timeline and the three management measures enacted by local health authorities; third, dual-path R estimation with uncertainty quantification (definition-based and next-generation matrix) is designed to compare phase-wise transmission estimates under matched methodology; finally, multiple CHIKV reproduction number estimates across models and methods are reported to serve as references for future outbreaks.

Materials and methods

Data and extraction

This study uses the official 2025 CHIKV outbreak bar charts released by the Foshan health authorities as the data source [1]. We digitized the daily counts via an automated image-processing pipeline. Specifically, we first load the original image with OpenCV and convert it to the HSV color space to improve channel separability. We then define color thresholds for the blue and red bars to generate binary masks. For blue, we use [100,50,50]–[130,255,255]; for red, we take the union of [0,50,50]–[10,255,255] and [170,50,50]–[180,255,255], yielding stable color detection.

On the mask images, we extract external contours and compute the minimum bounding rectangle for each bar to obtain the x and y coordinates, width, height, centroid, and pixel area. To reduce noise, objects with width or height smaller than 5 pixels are discarded. All bars are sorted by the x-coordinate in ascending order to match the left-to-right timeline. The geometric attributes of blue and red bars are exported separately to CSV files. To align with the official reporting scale, we calibrate bar heights using a pixel-to-count ratio: based on reported key facts (as the Fig. 1 shows, the incidence-by-onset-date series peaks at 432 cases on July 21; the series by report date peaks at 674 cases on July 19 [1]), we determine the pixel-to-case conversion coefficient, and cross-validate it using the first onset record on June 16, obtaining consistent results. The resulting quality-controlled daily incidence time series is then used for model fitting and subsequent analyses.

Model structure design and symbol explanation

We construct a SEICR model to simulate the transmission dynamics of chikungunya fever, with the model structure selection based on the epidemiological characteristics of chikungunya [10]. Chikungunya virus infection exhibits distinct clinical phases: an incubation period (typically 3–7 days, range 1–12 days) [10], an acute phase (symptomatic with high transmissibility), asymptomatic infection (approximately 3–28% of infected individuals) [10], and a chronic phase [11, 12]. These characteristics necessitate the model to distinguish between different infection states: asymptomatic individuals, despite having lower viral loads, may still contribute to epidemic spread; symptomatic individuals have clinically apparent disease and higher transmission potential, making them primary targets for surveillance and control; chronic patients are excluded from the force of infection in the present formulation owing to insufficient data on their residual infectiousness, but are retained as a separate compartment because their prolonged disease duration affects burden assessment [12].

Discussion

This study developed and compared a dual-model framework integrating Ordinary Differential Equations (ODE) and Petri Nets (PN) for the rapid analysis of Chikungunya (CHIKV) outbreak dynamics. Applying this framework to the 2025 Foshan outbreak, we assessed phased intervention effects and employed two distinct methodologies (definition-based and next-generation matrix methods) to estimate phase-specific transmission indicators. The main result is not that one framework replaces the other, but that under the same human-side SEICR structure and the same data constraints, ODE and PN provide closely aligned medium-scale inferences about transmission intensity and intervention timing.

Our analysis indicates that Foshan’s three-phase intervention measures were associated with a phase-wise reduction in transmission intensity, particularly in the third phase, where all computational methods yielded an effective reproduction number below 1. This pattern is consistent across the ODE and PN implementations and across the definition-based and NGM calculations. For instance, under the same transmission rate β (t), the R values calculated by both methods differed by 0.029%, supporting internal consistency of the fitted framework.

Nevertheless, the models exhibit notable limitations. Predicted total case numbers (7,825 cases) exceed observed totals (4,806 cases), and residual analysis suggests that the discrepancy is phase-structured. Both models exhibit peak timing that is three days earlier than observed, residuals that are predominantly negative over the observation window, and phase-wise shifts in residual means, together suggesting a structured bias pattern rather than simple symmetric noise. A plausible explanation is that the human-side piecewise-constant transmission coefficient does not fully absorb changes in vector control intensity, spontaneous behavioral responses, reporting lags, and spatial heterogeneity during epidemic decline. Despite these limitations, both models still capture the main outbreak trajectory and the phase-wise reduction in transmission intensity, thereby providing a useful comparative framework for outbreak interpretation under limited data conditions.

In epidemiological modeling, our study contrasts and complements existing literature across several key dimensions. Regarding model frameworks, while studies such as those by Segovia [6] and Connolly et al. [5] have explored Petri Net applications in epidemiology, most research focuses primarily on theoretical framework construction or single-model validation. Our results add an outbreak-based comparison under matched structural assumptions and shared calibration targets. We therefore frame the contribution as a controlled comparison between two representations of the same epidemiological structure, rather than as evidence that PN introduces a distinct biological transmission mechanism in this case study.

Concerning reproduction number computational methods, our dual-method approach (definition-based and next-generation matrix method) echoes the methodological framework of studies such as those by van den Driessche and Watmough [3] and Guo et al. [20], yet our research further quantifies different parameter impacts on reproduction numbers through sensitivity analysis, providing more comprehensive assessment of parameter uncertainty.

Regarding public health implications, the sensitivity analysis results provide a quantitative basis for interpreting which parameters drive variation in the fitted transmission indicator. Research reveals that recovery rate (γ) represents the most sensitive parameter affecting basic reproduction number , with Sobol index reaching 0.9672, indicating this parameter’s variation explains 96.72% of total variation within the present model. This suggests that measures capable of shortening disease duration and accelerating patient recovery could reduce the modeled transmission indicator, although their practical effect would depend on clinical feasibility, medical resources, and vector-side transmission conditions. In contrast, the symptomatic probability (p) and the relative asymptomatic infectivity (ε), while exhibiting positive correlations with , demonstrate far lower sensitivity than the recovery rate. This finding emphasizes the importance of optimizing clinical management and providing adequate medical resources to promote rapid patient recovery in public health practice.

Despite the methodological and empirical insights obtained here, several limitations require careful consideration. The most prominent limitation is the absence of detailed vector surveillance data aligned with the Foshan 2025 case time series. Because CHIKV is fundamentally a vector-borne disease, transmission dynamics depend on mosquito abundance, biting behavior, infection probability, and control-related changes in these quantities. In the absence of such data, we adopted a simplified human-side model and folded mosquito-related effects into the effective coefficient β (t). This strategy avoids unstable over-parameterization and still permits short-term fitting and phase comparison, but it necessarily limits the biological interpretability of the fitted transmission parameters, especially the reported -like quantities. The additional no-demography robustness check further shows that demographic turnover terms are negligible over the short outbreak horizon, reinforcing that the main remaining uncertainty lies not in human demography but in the omitted vector-side dynamics.

Considering these limitations, cautious interpretation of research results is necessary. While the sensitivity analysis identifies the recovery rate γ as the most influential parameter within the present formulation, this mathematical finding should not be over-interpreted as a directly controllable public health measure, because recovery rates remain constrained by vector ecology, medical resources, treatment access, and the natural course of disease.

In conclusion, this case study shows that ODE and Petri Net implementations built on the same human-side SEICR structure can provide closely aligned phase-wise inferences for the 2025 Foshan CHIKV outbreak. The supplementary analyses added in revision indicate that demographic terms have a negligible effect on short-term fitting, whereas the residual structure suggests that a single human-side effective transmission coefficient may not fully capture late-stage intervention strengthening and vector-related heterogeneity. Accordingly, this work provides a comparative framework of ODE and PN for outbreak fitting, intervention phase identification, and future extension toward explicit host-vector models when appropriate vector surveillance data become available.

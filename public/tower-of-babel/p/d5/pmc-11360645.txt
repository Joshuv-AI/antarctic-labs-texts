Utilizing multiclassifier radiomics analysis of ultrasound to predict high axillary lymph node tumour burden in node-positive breast cancer patients: a multicentre study
Authors: Jiangfeng Wu, Lifang Ge, Yinghong Guo, Dong Xu, Zhengping Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe tumor burden within the axillary lymph nodes (ALNs) constitutes a pivotal factor in breast cancer, serving as the primary determinant for treatment decisions and exhibiting a close correlation with prognosis.ObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate the potential of ultrasound-based radiomics and clinical characteristics in non-invasively distinguishing between low tumor burden (1-2 positive nodes) and high tumor burden (more than 2 positive nodes) in patients with node-positive breast cancer.MethodsA total of 215 patients with node-positive breast cancer, who underwent preoperative ultrasound examinations, were enrolled in this study. Among these patients, 144 cases were allocated to the training set, 37 cases to the validation set, and 34 cases to the testing set. Postoperative histopathology was used to determine the status of ALN tumor burden. The region of interest for breast cancer was delineated on the ultrasound image. Nine models were developed to predict high ALN tumor burden, employing a combination of three feature screening methods and three machine learning classifiers. Ultimately, the optimal model was selected and tested on both the validation and testing sets. In addition, clinical characteristics were screened to develop a clinical model. Furthermore, Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) values were utilized to provide explanations for the machine learning model.ResultsDuring the validation and testing sets, the models demonstrated area under the curve (AUC) values ranging from 0.577 to 0.733 and 0.583 to 0.719, and accuracies ranging from 64.9% to 75.7% and 64.7% to 70.6%, respectively. Ultimately, the Boruta_XGB model, comprising five radiomics features, was selected as the final model. The AUC values of this model for distinguishing low from high tumor burden were 0.828, 0.715, and 0.719 in the training, validation, and testing sets, respectively, demonstrating its superiority over the clinical model.ConclusionsThe developed radiomics models exhibited a significant level of predictive performance. The Boruta_XGB radiomics model outperformed other radiomics models in this study.

Introduction

The presence and extent of metastasis in axillary lymph nodes (ALNs) are deemed highly consequential prognostic indicators for recurrence and survival in breast cancer (BC) patients [1,2]. Consequently, a thorough and accurate evaluation of lymph node (LN) involvement assumes a critical role in axillary staging, facilitating the formulation and application of optimal therapeutic interventions in BC patients [3].

Axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) may be considered when ALN metastases are obtained from sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) [3,4]. However, ALND is associated with various complications, including lymphedema, infection, restricted shoulder mobility, as well as substantial vascular and nerve damage. The Z0011 trial conducted by the American College of Surgeons Oncology Group (ACOSOG) demonstrated patients with T1 or T2 primary invasive BC, without clinically detectable ALN involvement, and 1 or 2 metastatic sentinel LNs, do not require ALND when standard postoperative treatments are provided [5]. Consequently, the assessment of ALN tumour burden has become the primary objective for determining the status of ALNs, rather than the presence of metastasis.

Ahmed et al. [6] revealed that among BC patients with ultrasound (US)-positive ALN metastasis, 43.2% demonstrated a low tumour burden in the LNs (ALN metastasis 1–2). It is worth noting that almost half of the ALN metastasis patients may not necessitate ALND, leading some researchers to speculate that such patients might be subjected to overtreatment. Presently, the evaluation of lymphatic metastasis mainly relies on SLNB utilization, resulting in fewer complications after axillary clearance, particularly in early BC patients. However, this approach is associated with false-negative outcomes, thereby highlighting certain limitations in terms of its reliability and sensitivity [7,8]. Furthermore, SLNB is accompanied by various morbidities. Consequently, the development of a noninvasive axillary nodal staging technique that could substitute SLNB and precisely determine ALN tumour burden is of utmost importance in reducing the morbidity associated with SLNB and ALND.

Presently, the prevailing noninvasive imaging method utilized for preoperative assessment of ALN status is US [9]. Nonetheless, the sensitivity and specificity of US in detecting ALN status vary considerably, spanning from 18.5% to 87.1% and 39.5% to 98.0%, respectively [9–11]. Furthermore, US demonstrates limited capacity to precisely discern axillary tumour burden. In recent years, the domain of interpreting BC US imaging has witnessed an upsurge in the application of radiomics, an advanced method that employs high-throughput computing to derive quantitative features from medical images and subsequently transforms them into high-dimensional data, and thereby augmenting the effectiveness of clinical decision-making [12–14]. Several researches have demonstrated the efficacy of radiomics information extracted from tumour images in forecasting ALN metastases [15–17]. Moreover, some studies [18–20] have confirmed that radiomics information derived from primary tumour images demonstrates a certain level of performance in predicting ALN tumour burden. Hence, our study carried out an analysis of patients underwent surgical procedures for BC to assess the efficacy of machine learning models based on US-derived radiomics in distinguishing between low (1–2 positive nodes) and high (over two positive nodes) ALN tumour burden in node-positive patients.

Methods

This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board at Dongyang People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2024-YX-135) and Zhejiang Cancer Hospital (Approval No. IRB-2022-548). Because retrospective analyses were performed using fully anonymized data in the present study, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Data source and patient selection

We selected ALN-positive BC patients from Zhejiang Cancer Hospital (Institution 1) between July 2019 and December 2020 as the internal set, while the testing set was derived from Dongyang People’s Hospital (Institution 2) between September 2021 and December 2022.

As presented in Figure 1, the study’s inclusion criteria comprised the following: (1) patients diagnosed with pathologically confirmed invasive BC; (2) patients with pathological reports specifying the number of metastatic LNs; (3) patients undergone US examination within 2 weeks before the surgery; and (4) patients whose lesions appeared as masses on US images. The exclusion criteria included: (1) patients with insufficient clinicopathological data and preoperative US images; (2) patients whose target tumours were not visible in the US image; (3) patients who had undergone anti-tumour treatment before the surgery; and (4) patients whose US images with evident artefacts.

The flowchart of inclusion and exclusion criteria of patients.

A total of 215 BC patients with ALN-positive were retrospectively analysed for this study. Of these, 181 patients from Institution 1 were subjected to random allocation, dividing them into two subsets: a training set and a validation set, maintaining an 8:2 ratio. Thirty-four patients from Institution 2 were included as the testing set. Clinical data and US information were collected.

Histopathological evaluation

Estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) negativity are determined when less than 1% of cells show staining. Conversely, PR and ER are considered positive if stained cells exceed 1% [21,22]. The Ki-67 proliferation index evaluates proliferation rates, with a cutoff value of 20% [23]. HER2 gene presence is detected using immunohistochemistry (IHC) and scored as 0, 1+, 2+ or 3+. An IHC score of 2+ prompts fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) to confirm HER2 gene amplification. HER2 positivity is defined as IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ with FISH positivity; HER2 negativity is IHC 0 or 1+, or IHC 2+ with FISH negativity [24]. Molecular subtypes include luminal A (ER or PR positive with Ki-67 < 20%), luminal B (ER or PR positive with Ki-67 ≥ 20%), HER2 (ER negative, PR negative and HER2 positive) and triple-negative (ER negative, PR negative and HER2 negative). These guidelines play a crucial role in BC diagnosis, guiding tailored and effective treatment strategies for patients.

Radiomics process

The radiomics workflow encompassed several key steps, namely image acquisition, tumour segmentation, feature extraction and selection, and model development (Figure 2(A–E)).

The workflow of this study.

Image acquisition and image segmentation

The US examination was performed on a LOGIQ E9 (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL), Siemens Acuson S2000 (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany), Toshiba Aplio 500 (Canon Medical Systems, Otawara, Japan) and Philips EPIQ 5 (Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, Netherlands). A high-frequency linear probe was utilized to facilitate careful scans on breasts. The US images underwent processing and were exported from the database of the picture archiving and communication system (PACS) in the digital imaging and communication in medicine (DICOM) format, which is an internationally recognized standard for the storage, management and exchange of medical images and related data. To provide a more detailed description, the subjective features of the lesion were meticulously documented in accordance with established clinical standards. Furthermore, we observed the shape, margin, hilar architecture, cortical thickness and blood flow distribution characteristics of ALNs in patients using US. Precisely, three important characteristics were documented, including the ALN status, the maximum diameter of the cancer in its longitudinal plane, and the classification of the lesion based on the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) category.

In current practice, manual segmentation remains a reliable method for segmenting breast lesions. In our investigation, we employed the ITK-SNAP to execute the segmentation process. Initially, US images were acquired from the PACS onto the workstation. Subsequently, a single experienced Sonographer (from Dongyang People’s Hospital, with over five years’ experience in ultrasonic diagnosis) delineated the region of interest (ROI) encompassing the longitudinal plane of each breast lesion.

Radiomics feature extraction and ICC evaluation

In order to mitigate potential heterogeneity bias resulting from variations in US scanners or parameters, the pixel intensity values were discretized with a fixed bin width of 25 units, thereby ensuring uniform and accurate quantization. The extraction of radiomics features from the ROI was performed utilizing the ‘pyradiomics’ package (version 3.0.1) in Python (version 3.7.11). The extracted features encompassed a wide range of feature types and transformation types, including first-order statistics, shape descriptors, gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM), gray-level run length matrix (GLRLM), gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) and neighboring gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM). In addition, wavelet transformation was applied to all features, except for the shape descriptors.

The assessment of radiomics feature reproducibility was conducted by evaluating the concordances between intra- and interobserver measurements, employing the intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC). The radiomics features were calculated based on a subset of the training data, which consisted of 50 US images acquired by two blinded sonographers. To assess interobserver agreement, the segmentation process was independently executed by Reader 1 and Reader 2 concurrently, while Reader 1 subsequently replicated the procedure after 2 weeks to assess intra-observer consistency. A value of the ICC >0.75 denoted satisfactory agreement of the ROIs.

Radiomics feature selection

The radiomics features obtained from the three datasets were standardized individually through z-score normalization to ensure that their distribution was consistent across the datasets. In the process of selecting radiomics features from the training set, a three-stage approach was followed. Initially, features with ICCs below 0.75 were excluded to prevent redundancy. Subsequently, features with p values exceeding .05 in the Mann–Whitney U-test were eliminated, guaranteeing the retention of only the most pertinent features for the model. Lastly, three techniques for feature dimension reduction, namely least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), principal component analysis (PCA) and Boruta method, were employed to derive three efficient feature subsets aimed at predicting ALN tumour burden.

Model construction and validation

Three efficient feature subsets were utilized to develop nine machine learning models employing three classifiers, namely logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM) and extreme gradient boosting (XGB), to differentiate high tumour burden from low tumour burden using the training set. Subsequently, the constructed prediction models underwent validation using the validation and testing sets. The diagnostic performance of the models was evaluated by quantifying sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value and negative predictive value. Discrimination performance was assessed by constructing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and determining the area under the curve (AUC). To compare the ROC curves between the models, the Delong test was employed. Furthermore, decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to compare the clinical advantages of the various models by calculating the net benefit for a range of threshold probabilities.

Visualization of the prediction by Shapley additive explanations

To address the challenge posed by the intrinsic ‘black box’ characteristic of machine learning models and augment their interpretability, the Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) dependence plot was employed to illustrate the final model. This approach facilitates the explication of the influence of individual features on the output of the XGB prediction model, which is a widely used method for interpreting results generated by machine learning models. The SHAP value serves as an estimate for the contribution of each feature towards the expected outcome. Furthermore, the SHAP analysis evaluated the sensitivity of each sample feature, which provided insight into how alterations in model output were affected by specific features. By dividing the prediction outcome linearly based on the effect of each feature, we could accurately assess the significance of each feature and present the role played by different features in the model.

Statistical analysis

For the statistical analyses in this study, the R software (version 3.5.1; www.r-project.org) was utilized. To compare the clinical information of participants with low and high tumour burden in ALN across different cohorts at baseline, we employed various statistical tests. Categorical variables were analysed utilizing the Chi-squared test and Fisher’s exact probability method, whereas continuous variables were assessed employing either the Mann–Whitney test or Student’s t-test, depending on their appropriateness. Statistical test was conducted as two-tailed test with p < .05 denoting a statistically significant difference.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

A grand total of 788 features were extracted from each ROI in the US images. This comprehensive set of features comprised 18 first-order features, 14 shape-based features, 14 GLDM features, 16 GLRLM features, 16 GLSZM features, 22 GLCM features and 688 features using wavelet filter images. Subsequently, ICC analysis was conducted, following which 765 features exhibiting an ICC value of over 0.75 were selected for radiomics modelling. These findings were in concordance with our prior research [14]. During the training phase, 721 features were eliminated by using Mann–Whitney U-test. Next, a subset of 44 radiomics features that demonstrated notable differences between low and high ALN tumour burdens was selected.

The LASSO method, employing 10-fold cross-validation, calculated an optimal Lambda value of 0.040. Four radiomics features with non-zero coefficients were selected, as depicted in Figure 3(A,B). The Boruta method was utilized, and ultimately, five radiomics features were screened out (Figure 3(C)). PCA was conducted on the data, resulting in the extraction of five main principal components from 44 features, which explained 90.8% of the total data, indicating that the principal components were effective in providing a comprehensive interpretation of the data (Figure 3(D)). The selected features and main principal components are revealed in Figure 4(A–C) and Table 3.

Evaluation of different models

The ROC curves of nine models in the three sets are displayed in Figure 5(A–I). In the training set, the LASSO_XGB, Boruta_XGB and PCA_XGB models demonstrated the highest AUCs of 0.843, 0.828 and 0.816, respectively, among the nine models. Nonetheless, no significant statistical difference existed among the models (Delong test, p = .593, p = .408 and p = .766) (Supplementary Figure 1(A)).

ROC curves of the nine machine learning prediction models. The graph shows ROC curves for the training, validation and testing sets. ROC: receiver operating characteristic curve.

The validation set showed that LASSO_SVM produced the highest AUC of 0.733 (95% CI, 0.553–0.914). The Boruta_XGB model demonstrated the second-best performance, while the LASSO_LR and Boruta_SVM models ranked third. However, the three models did not exhibit any significant differences (Supplementary Figure 1(B)).

In the testing set, the three most effective prediction models were Boruta_XGB (0.719, 95% CI, 0.540–0.897), Boruta_LR (0.715, 95% CI, 0.537–0.893) and Boruta_SVM (0.694, 95% CI, 0.510–0.879). Although Boruta_XGB demonstrated the highest performance, no significant difference existed among the three models (Supplementary Figure 1(C)).

Discussion

Radiomics represents a nascent technology in recent years. In the current investigation, we identified four radiomic features through LASSO, as well as five radiomic features by Boruta and five principal components via PCA, which were deemed more closely correlated with high ALN tumour burden in comparison to other features. We created a total of nine prediction models that relied upon the XGB, SVM and LR algorithms for predicting high burden in node-positive BC patients. Following an evaluation of the machine learning classifiers, it was observed the LASSO_SVM model performed best for distinguishing between low and high tumour burden in ALN-positive patients within the validation set. Additionally, the Boruta_XGB model demonstrated superior performance within the testing set, as well as a high degree of generalizability. Additionally, the calculation of the mean absolute Shapley values has been developed to construct a comprehensible radiomics model and alleviate its criticized ‘black box’ characteristic.

The American Society of Clinical Oncology guideline and the Z0011 study have demonstrated that BC patients with a low ALN tumour burden (1–2 positive nodes) can forego ALND, thereby avoiding associated complications [5]. Previous investigations have primarily focused on distinguishing between LN tumour burdens of 0–2 positive nodes and over two positive nodes for BC, as ALND is considered unnecessary in the absence of LN metastasis [20,25,26]. Accordingly, we have exclusively recruited BC patients with LN metastasis and predicted the number of metastases (1–2 positive nodes/over two positive nodes). Therefore, the radiomics model that distinguishes between 1 and 2 nodes and more than two nodes might hold greater clinical significance in guiding preoperative surgical decisions.

This study employed three effective dimensionality reduction methods to identify a subset of features associated with the high ALN tumour burden. The LASSO method is a linear regression technique that shrinks the coefficients of the least important features to zero, thereby identifying the most relevant features for the prediction model [27]. The Boruta method is a wrapper-based feature selection technique that evaluates the importance of each feature based on its relative importance compared to random noise features [28]. The PCA method is a linear transformation technique that diminishes the dimensionality of the feature set by projecting it onto a lower-dimensional space while endeavouring to retain maximal variance [29]. The use of three different feature dimension reduction techniques ensured that the resulting feature subsets were efficient and informative for the prediction model.

Without conducting extensive research on the subject, it is unfeasible to predict how effectively a given machine learning task can be performed by an algorithm, as each machine learning algorithm possesses unique strengths and weaknesses [30]. Machine learning has attained extensive implementation and validation in promoting predictive efficacy. Prior researches mainly utilized the LR classifier for developing predictive models [18,20,25]. In our study, we developed models employing three distinct machine learning classifiers: XGB, LR and SVM. Finally, we opted for the relatively novel XGB classifier and proceeded to evaluate its performance against the other two classifiers. Our results indicate the XGB classifier outperformed both LR and SVM when evaluated by ROC curve analysis in the testing set. Consequently, the XGB classifier may provide an improved diagnostic approach for clinical practice. Additionally, the XGB model has demonstrated a remarkable level of adaptability in various domains. The inclusion of L1 and L2 regularization techniques in XGB aids in preventing overfitting and capitalizing on parallel processing, both of which contribute to its superiority [31].

Several studies have investigated LN burden, but they suffer from certain limitations, such as a lack of external validation and the utilization of a single modelling approach without proper justification [18–20]. The research carried out by Zhao et al. [32] employed lesion size, molecular sub-types and Cooper’s ligament manifestation to establish a prediction model for ALN tumour burden. The AUCs for the training and validation cohorts were 0.743 and 0.711, respectively. Several researches have reported that specific histopathological factors, such as histological grade, lesion size, lymphovascular invasion and hormone receptor expression, may serve as predictors for ALN metastasis [32,33]. However, molecular classification, such as luminal and non-luminal BC, HER-2 over-expression, and basal-like BC, can be evaluated invasively, such as through core needle biopsy or surgery. Despite the potential of molecular classification to be identified from biopsy before surgery, previous studies found that the correlation between core needle biopsy and surgical excision results demonstrates a significant insufficiency [34]. In contradistinction, our study diverged by utilizing preoperative data as potential factors to develop a model that could potentially serve as a noninvasive approach for evaluating the ALN tumour burden.

In the study by Chen et al. [19], a radiomics score was developed on the basis of automated breast volume imaging to predict ALN tumour burden, and the AUC value was 0.789 in the test set. Their findings demonstrated slightly superior predictive performance compared to ours. However, their research was susceptible to some limitations, such as a single-centre sample and an absence of external validation. Furthermore, their radiomics study employed a single modelling approach without any reason. In contrast, our study utilized various machine learning techniques to develop a predictive model and external validation was conducted. These efforts might result in a potentially more generalized model.

In our investigation, all the radiomics features that were selected comprised texture features following wavelet transformation. These features exhibited a significant correlation with the ALN tumour burden, thereby indicating their robustness in reflecting the ALN tumour burden. The wavelet transform, a mathematical instrument that quantifies the resolution of image signals across diverse temporal, spatial and frequency scales, offers significant utility in reconstructing even the most delicate yet consequential breast tumour texture information that radiologists tend to overlook in US images [35]. Prior studies have demonstrated the utilization of texture features obtained following wavelet transform in the construction of a predictive model. Zheng et al. [36] documented the application of deep learning radiomics in forecasting the ALN status among early-stage BC patients, utilizing conventional US and shear wave elastography. Their results revealed that the integration of clinical parameters and deep learning radiomics yields a significant ability to differentiate between low and high metastatic burden of BC, as evidenced by an impressive AUC value of 0.905 in the test cohort. Despite the vast potential of deep learning, the opacity of its underlying features and decision-making processes present a significant challenge, creating what is frequently denoted as a ‘black box’ phenomenon.

Our research has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the retrospective nature of the study introduces a potential for selection bias. However, this limitation may be addressed in future prospective validation studies. Notably, our utilization of datasets from two centres enhances the generalizability of our findings. Second, our data have revealed that US-detected ALN metastasis is a discriminative factor for distinguishing between low and high ALN tumour burdens in node-positive BC patients. However, the US characteristics of the tumour were not assessed in the current study, and they will be integrated into a composite model with the intent of refining its predictive performance in future study. Finally, in the testing set, the three most effective prediction models were Boruta_XGB, Boruta_LR and Boruta_SVM; however, the Delong test yielded insignificant differences among these three models, potentially attributable to the limited patient population included in our study. Further validation through future studies with larger sample sizes remains necessary.

In summary, the outcomes of this study suggest the radiomics models have attained a discernible degree of predictive effectiveness. Particularly noteworthy is the Boruta_XGB radiomics model, which demonstrated exceptional performance in preoperatively predicting high ALN tumour burden in node-positive BC patients. This proficiency in prognostication may prove instrumental for clinicians in selecting suitable therapeutic interventions.

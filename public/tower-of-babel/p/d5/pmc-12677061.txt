Advancing proton therapy together: insights on practice, technology, and implementation
Authors: Taeyoon Kim, Nicola Bizzocchi
Published: 2025

Introduction

Proton therapy has emerged over recent decades as one of the most significant advances in radiation oncology [1]. With its unique physical characteristics, particularly the Bragg peak, it enables precise dose delivery to the tumour while minimizing unnecessary irradiation of surrounding healthy tissues. These advantages are especially valuable for challenging disease sites, paediatric cases, and patients at risk of long-term treatment-related side effects [2].

Yet, proton therapy continues to face important challenges. Managing uncertainties and ensuring robust treatment planning, improving image guidance and motion management, standardizing workflows, expanding accessibility, and generating stronger evidence through clinical data remain priorities for the field [3], [4].

Considering these challenges and opportunities, this special issue brings together seven papers that reflect the breadth of proton therapy today. The contributions span clinical experience, education and guideline development, technical innovation, and service models. Notably, several of the studies highlight the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration and international networks, underscoring how proton therapy continues to evolve beyond a single professional perspective toward a collective, global endeavour.

On the clinical side, the issue features reports that document both early experiences and structured guidance for practice. Initial experience in nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients demonstrates the practical workflow and feasibility of proton therapy in head and neck cancers. A standard operating procedure for breast cancer treatment illustrates how immobilization, scanning, verification, and delivery can be coordinated to improve reproducibility. Complementing these are a training program for radiation therapists that strengthened image-guided competencies, and a scoping review that mapped existing evidence for RTTs and dosimetrists while identifying persistent gaps in standardized international guidelines. Together, these papers highlight the clinical workflows, training needs, and standardization efforts that are essential for safe and effective proton therapy.

From a technical and physical perspective, the contributions underscore how innovation underpins clinical practice. Monte Carlo–based calculations of perturbation correction factors provide insight into adapting dosimetry protocols for proton beams. A study on real-time gated proton therapy presents a workflow and safety analysis using failure modes and effects analysis (FMEA), emphasizing the importance of uncertainty management in motion-sensitive treatments. Finally, a consultation service model from Canada demonstrates how collaborative approaches can help extend access to proton therapy expertise across institutions and borders. These studies illustrate how physical accuracy, workflow innovation, and service models converge to support the safe expansion of proton therapy.

Papers’ highlights

Insights from initial experience for nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients treated with proton therapy

Early experience from Singapore in treating nasopharyngeal carcinoma with proton therapy demonstrated high treatment feasibility and workflow reproducibility. Among the first ten patients, immobilization with individualized thermoplastic masks, robust planning with ±3 mm/3 % uncertainties, and daily image verification ensured consistent delivery. Acute toxicity was acceptable, with mainly grade 1–2 mucositis and dermatitis, and no unexpected severe adverse effects. These results provide practical insight into the safe implementation of proton therapy for head and neck cancers, underscoring the value of meticulous planning and quality assurance.

Proton beam therapy image-guided radiotherapy training for RTTs – Experience from a single institution

A structured training program for radiation therapists in image-guided proton therapy significantly improved confidence and accuracy in CBCT matching. Before training, only 13.6 % of RTTs reported high confidence, compared with 86.4 % after completion. Brain, head and neck, and pelvic cases all achieved 100 % confidence improvement. Workbook-based learning was particularly useful for junior RTTs, while senior RTTs emphasized the benefit of real-case reinforcement. These outcomes demonstrate how focused education can rapidly enhance competency, supporting safe and effective image guidance in proton therapy practice.

Towards proton therapy guidelines for radiation therapists and dosimetrists: A scoping review

A scoping review of over 100 publications identified key areas of practice for RTTs and dosimetrists in proton therapy, including imaging, treatment planning, patient positioning, and quality assurance. While recurring themes were noted, the review highlighted substantial gaps and inconsistencies across institutions, particularly in motion management and adaptive planning. The lack of standardized protocols underscores the urgent need for international consensus guidelines. By mapping current evidence, this work lays the groundwork for harmonized best practices and supports the professional development of RTTs and dosimetrists worldwide.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for immobilization, scanning, verification and treatment of breast cancer patients undergoing proton beam therapy

Development of a standard operating procedure for breast cancer proton therapy in the UK defined clear steps for immobilization, CT scanning, verification imaging, and treatment delivery. Application of the SOP in clinical practice improved reproducibility and reduced inter-operator variation in patient setup. The framework emphasized multidisciplinary input across therapy, physics, and nursing teams, ensuring robust workflow integration. By establishing a consistent process, the SOP offers a practical model for other centres aiming to initiate proton therapy programs for breast cancer, enhancing both safety and efficiency.

Real-time gated proton therapy: Introducing clinical workflow and failure modes and effects analysis (FMEA)

This paper introduces the first comprehensive clinical workflow and Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA) for Real-Time Gated Proton Therapy (RGPT), identifying 96 potential failure modes. The study found high-risk areas were in planning and treatment, driven by severity and low detectability. It highlights the need for standardized protocols and collaborative, interdisciplinary training to improve patient safety and treatment quality. The research serves as a crucial foundation for other institutions adopting this advanced technology.

Bridging the proton gap: A proton therapy consultation service for Canadian radiation oncologists

This paper describes a Canadian proton therapy consultation service created to help radiation oncologists identify patients who would benefit from proton beam therapy (PBT). The study found that PBT significantly reduced radiation doses to healthy tissues in cranial and thoracic cancer patients compared to traditional photon therapy. This service successfully guided the approval of PBT for nearly a third of all referred patients, demonstrating its value in bridging the access gap to this specialized treatment in a country without a PBT facility.

Monte Carlo-calculated perturbation correction factors in clinical proton beams using PHITS

This study used the Monte Carlo (MC) code PHITS (Particle and Heavy Ion Transport code and System) to calculate key dosimetric factors (kQ and PQ) for proton beam therapy. The research successfully reduced the uncertainty of the average global factor (fQ) compared to previous studies by incorporating new data. Additionally, it provided the first MC-based estimation of the kQ factor for a specific ionization chamber (PTW 31013), contributing new reference data and improving the precision of proton dosimetry protocols.

Conclusion

This special issue offers a compelling look at the current landscape of proton therapy, a field defined by both its clinical potential and its persistent challenges. The papers presented here reflect an enthusiastic global effort, bridging clinical experience with technical innovation.

Through a diverse range of contributions, we see how progress in proton therapy is built on a foundation of standardization, precision, and collaboration. The clinical papers highlight the critical need for well-defined protocols and targeted training, while the technical contributions underscore how advancements in physical accuracy and safety directly translate to better patients’ outcomes. The collaborative models presented further demonstrate how shared expertise can overcome barriers to access, ensuring that more patients can benefit from this advanced treatment.

Taken together, these papers confirm that the path forward for proton therapy is a collective one. Ongoing international efforts continue to focus on aligning clinical standards, data sharing, and education frameworks to support equitable global growth of proton therapy [5], [6]. By sharing knowledge and working together across disciplines and borders, we can continue to refine practices, address uncertainties, and expand the reach of this technology. We hope this issue serves as both a testament to current achievements and a catalyst for future progress. We would like to sincerely thank all authors and reviewers for their valuable contributions and efforts, which made this special issue possible.

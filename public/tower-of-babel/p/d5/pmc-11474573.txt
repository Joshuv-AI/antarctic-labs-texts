Paradoxical response during Para‐Hisian pacing in a case with fasciculo‐ventricular pathway: What is the mechanism?
Authors: Debabrata Bera, Calambur Narasimhan, Sanjeev S Mukherjee, Ayan Kar, Joyanta Ghosh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Causes of paradoxical response include Pure His capture and inadvertent intermittent direct atrial capture. In the index case , we postulate that the likely mechanism of paradoxical prolongation could be due to decrement in the AV node due to the shortening of HH interval which happened as a result of a narrower H + Vc beat following a wider Vc beat during decremetal pacing output.

CASE

A 28‐year‐old man presented with orthodromic atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia via a manifest right posterolateral atrioventricular accessory pathway (AP) (Appendix S1). After successful ablation, the HV was still found to be short (+26 ms). The HV interval, however, remained fixed, and the QRS morphology remained unchanged during decremental atrial pacing (Figure 1; Appendix S2), suggesting a possible fasciculo‐ventricular pathway (FVP). There was an underlying right bundle branch block (RBBB) (Figure 1).

Para‐Hisian pacing (PHP) was performed to confirm the abolition of retrograde AP conduction. We observed the following responses reproducibly during a PHP. COMMENTARY

The usual responses in any FVP and the atypical findings in this case are discussed below: due to loss of insulation in FVP, the fascicular system is often captured irrespective of high or low output pacing. 1 Therefore, isolated myocardial capture (Vc) is often difficult to achieve from the HB region. In the absence of isolated myocardial capture PHP becomes uninterpretable. However, in some cases, it is possible to have only myocardial capture (Vc) like in our case. We could repeatedly demonstrate Vc and infer the route of VA conduction (discussed in the next paragraph).

The PHP was performed from the distal HB region (HISd bipole) (Figures 2 and 3). In the absence of an atrioventricular AP, the response during PHP in FVP should be nodal if the AV node has intact VA conduction. This is due to the lack of retrograde conduction property of FVP. 2 In our case, the SA paradoxically prolonged with the narrower beat in Figure 3. The wider beats (first and second beats, QRS width of 130 ms) appear to be resulting from myocardial capture (Vc) and the narrower third beat (110 ms) from H + V capture (H + Vc). Occasionally, the interpretation can be erroneous if the narrower beat actually results from a pure His capture (Hc) and the wider one due to H + Vc/Vc. Hence, a careful analysis of QRS morphology is important. An isoelectric interval and narrow QRS morphology are useful clues for a pure His capture. Additionally, a dedicated catheter (other than the pacing catheter) at the HB region can be useful in this differentiation by showing the capture and release of His/local V‐electrogram. Here, it was not available. However, there was no isoelectric interval in the narrower third beat excluding a pure Hc (Figure 3).

Annotated Figure 2. The intervals showing a shortening of HH interval by 15 ms when in the third beat, the H + V was captured. This would have led to a decrement in retrograde nodal conduction having sluggish VA. Pure His cap was excluded by absence of isoelectric line. Moreover, a late local V‐electrogram is expected in the His catheter when pure His capture takes place, unlike our case.

We wondered about the mechanism of paradoxical SA prolongation in the narrower last beat. One of the causes of such response during a pure Hc 3 was excluded, as discussed above. Another cause of paradoxical response can be from intermittent direct atrial capture during higher output pacing beat. 4 In those cases, the VA time will be invariably shorter than 60 ms, unlike our case. We postulate that the likely mechanism for the paradoxical response here could be due to a decrement in the AV node which happened because the narrower H + Vc beat followed a wider Vc beat. This, in turn, led to a shortening of HH interval and a decrement in the nodal VA conduction (Figure 3). When H + Vc beat follows a Vc beat, it can lead to 20–40 ms shortening of HH interval due to earlier activation of the His bundle. This is the reason why it is always recommended to analyze PHP response in beats where a narrower H + Vc complex is followed by a wider Vc beat (during decremental output) and not vice versa. Our proposition is mechanistically similar to pitfall no. 6 described by Sheldon et al. 3 The other explanation of SA prolongation could be the development of retrograde RBBB. However, a 98 ms increment cannot be explained solely by a retrograde‐RBBB and the HH shortening remains as the most probable mechanism. Another dedicated catheter with His electrogram, if available, could have rendered more useful insights.

Interestingly, this phenomenon of SA prolongation due to slight HH shortening is rarely reported despite a relatively common occurrence of narrower beat (H + Vc) following a wider beat (V) during routine PHP. We believe that it happened due to relatively sluggish VA conduction in our case (VA Wenckebach was 430 ms). The 20–40 ms shortening of HH interval made the effective pacing cycle length (PCL) for the last beat as 410–430 ms. In fact, two consecutive narrow QRS during the PHP again confirmed sluggish VA conduction (Appendix S3). The narrow gap between the PCL and VA Wenckebach interval led to such a finding. A briskly conducting node (VA) would not have so much impact on the SA time with a slight HH shortening. The substantial prolongation might also occur when conduction shifts from a fast to slow pathway, 5 but the “A‐EGM” was still earliest at HISp bipole suggestive of decrement in the fast pathway. Moreover, during a ventricular extrastimuli, we did not observe any VA jump.

To complete the case, no tachycardia was inducible even after isoprenaline infusion and no further ablation was attempted. In essence, this case highlights the importance of careful analysis of QRS morphology and HH intervals before interpreting a PHP response. This also illustrates that capture of different tissues in all combinations (such as Hc, H + Vc, and Vc) is possible even in the presence of FVP. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of a paradoxical Para‐Hisian response in a case with FVP which occurred due to inadvertent HH shortening.

Study on the influence of material hardness on the performance of V-shaped non-pneumatic tyres
Authors: Sichen Gao, Yuan Yu, Mingjiao Sun, Weimin Yang, Zhenchuan Song, Yangjun Zheng, Jiangming Yuan, Zhibin Shuai, Zhiwei Jiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Material selection is a crucial factor in the design of non-pneumatic tyres (NPT). Most NPTs are integrally manufactured using polyurethane materials for the supports, the inner ring, and the outer ring. It is evident that designing different components with varying material hardness will have distinct impacts on NPT performance. To study the influence of material hardness on the performance of V-shaped non-pneumatic tyres (VS-NPT), we developed finite element models of VS-NPTs with different TPU materials. To validate the finite element models, we manufactured tyres with three different hardness levels and conducted performance tests. Based on this model, a design concept is proposed where different components use materials with varying hardness levels. To elucidate how material hardness affects VS-NPT performance, the Taguchi method was used for analysis. The results reveal that increased material hardness leads to higher triaxial forces and triaxial stiffness in integrally designed VS-NPTs. Regardless of variations in the structural parameters of existing VS-NPTs, the spoke material has a significant impact on triaxial stiffness and contact area. When the spoke thickness exceeds 7.3 mm, the tread material predominantly affects the contact area.

Introduction

Tyres are critical components of a vehicle. The performance of tyres is directly linked to the safety and comfort of driving. However, pneumatic tyres have inherent limitations, such as being prone to air leakage and blowouts. These issues pose certain safety risks to drivers. Non-pneumatic tyres (NPTs) represent an innovative alternative by avoiding the compressed air design of pneumatic tyres and employing a support structure instead. These tyres support loads through the structure while maintaining rebound capability. Various NPT structures exist [1], with the primary types being spoke-type and porous-type. The Tweel is a notable example of a spoke-type NPT, whereas the honeycomb non-pneumatic tyre represents the porous-type structure [2,3].

Researchers have extensively studied structural innovations in Non-Pneumatic Tyres (NPTs). Liguo Z. et al. proposed an NPT featuring a rhombic spoke structure. In their design, rubber was used for the tread and polyurethane for the spokes. This NPT demonstrated a lower average contact pressure on flat surfaces. However, the study did not address the effect of materials on NPT performance [4]. Shreyas S. et al. conducted finite element analysis on three different spoke structures and their hybrid configurations, revealing that the maximum equivalent stress in all structures increased with external load [5]. Other researchers introduced a mechanical elastic wheel, analysing the impact of hinge length on performance and the stress in hinge components [6,7]. Zifeng Z. et al. examined the static and dynamic characteristics of flexible spoke NPTs [8]. Laiyun K. et al. found that the multi-axial stiffness and peak values of FS-NPT generally increased with load [9]. Furthermore, researchers enhanced the load-bearing capacity and bending fatigue life by optimising the geometric shape of the spokes [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14]].

Extensive research has been conducted on various performance aspects of Non-Pneumatic Tyres (NPTs). Stiffness affects the handling of NPT. Researchers have developed a mathematical model that links structural design parameters to vertical stiffness through theoretical analysis [15,16]. Significant efforts have also been made to decouple tri-axial stiffness and achieve lightweight designs [17,18]. Fatigue life performance is critical to the service life and safety of NPTs. Hongxun F et al. predicted fatigue life using the J-integral method and validated their predictions through experiments [[19], [20], [21]]. Vibration characteristics significantly impact driving comfort. Studies have examined the vibration characteristics of NPTs under various conditions, such as spoke loss, overrun, and comparisons with pneumatic tyres [[22], [23], [24]]. Additionally, aerodynamic characteristics are crucial for driving efficiency and stability. Research has focused on how NPT design factors influence aerodynamic resistance and lift, both for the tyre itself and the overall vehicle [25,26].

Finite element modelling is a time-intensive process, particularly for non-pneumatic tyres with numerous design variables. Therefore, selecting an appropriate experimental design method is crucial. Zhou et al. employed the Taguchi method to examine the multi-axis stiffness and road contact characteristics of honeycomb wheels [27]. The Taguchi method, which simplifies the experimental design process, is particularly suited for resource-constrained environments. Response Surface Methodology (RSM) focuses on developing mathematical models to describe the relationships between input variables and outputs. Consequently, the Taguchi method was selected for experimental design in this study.

Previous research has predominantly concentrated on the structural aspects of NPT, while this study investigates the selection of materials for NPT. The primary objective of this paper is to explore the impact of material hardness, including that of individual components, on the performance of VS-NPT.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows: Section 2 constructs finite element models of VS-NPTs with varying material hardness, creates physical prototypes, and conducts static tests to validate the fidelity of the finite element models. In Section 3, the static characteristics, mechanical properties, and triaxial stiffness of VS-NPTs with integrated material design of different hardnesses are investigated. Section 4 employs the Taguchi method to study the impact of component material hardness on the performance of VS-NPTs. Based on the existing specifications and dimensions of VS-NPTs, structural parameters are varied to study the influence of component material hardness on performance characteristics.

VS-NPT finite element modelling

Finite element model building

We have innovatively designed a V-shaped non-pneumatic tyre (VS-NPT). The VS-NPT primarily comprises anti-slip grooves, tread, support structure, inner ring, and rim. The support structure is formed by V-shaped helical scan bodies arranged end-to-end. This unique structural arrangement provides the spokes with equal cross-sections at any circumferential angle. The tyres exhibit minor performance fluctuations [28]. The wide spacing between the spokes facilitates the expulsion of foreign objects. Additionally, the wide spacing promotes air exchange, which helps to delay tire aging. When constructing the finite element model, the structure was appropriately simplified [4]. The tread pattern has a minimal effect on load-bearing capacity. This study primarily investigates the impact of material hardness on contact area and triaxial stiffness. Therefore, the tread pattern was disregarded. Additionally, due to the minimal deformation of the rim, its structure was also excluded from consideration. The simplified 3D structure of the VS-NPT is shown in Fig. 1. Thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) is an elastomeric material known for its excellent performance. To determine the material properties of TPU, uniaxial tensile tests were conducted on TPU samples with hardness levels of 65HD, 70HD, and 75HD. TPU samples were prepared in accordance with the Type I dumbbell specimen standard specified in GB/T 528–2009, employing a Type I die cutter to produce dumbbell-shaped specimens with varying hardness levels. Before the tests, the samples were pre-stretched repeatedly to eliminate the Mullins effect. The crosshead position was adjusted at a constant speed of 50 mm/min to stretch the specimen. Upon completion of the experiments, the data were exported for processing to derive the nominal stress-strain curves, thus concluding the tensile test for one hardness level of TPU. The same methodology was subsequently employed to test TPU samples of the other two hardness levels. The nominal stress-strain curves for TPUs with varying hardness levels are presented in Fig. 2. The experimental results indicate that TPU materials of different hardness levels exhibit hyperelastic properties. At small nominal strains, the stress-strain relationship is linear; however, as the nominal strain increases, the nonlinearity becomes more pronounced. The nominal stress-nominal strain data obtained from these tests were imported into Abaqus to construct various hyperelastic material models, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The Neo-Hooke and Mooney-Rivlin models exhibit poor fitting performance. The Ogden_N3 and Poly_N2 models, being higher-order fits, require substantial data for support. The uniaxial tensile test curves indicate that the material displays significant nonlinear characteristics. The Yeoh model effectively describes these nonlinear properties. Considering the accuracy of curve fitting and the complexity of constitutive models, the Yeoh model was selected to describe the material properties of TPU. Pearson correlation analysis is a statistical method used to measure the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two continuous variables. The principle behind Pearson correlation is based on the calculation of the correlation coefficient, which ranges from −1 to 1. A coefficient close to 1 indicates a strong positive linear relationship, whereas a coefficient close to −1 indicates a strong negative linear relationship. A coefficient around 0 suggests little to no linear relationship. Pearson correlation analysis was conducted on the experimental and simulated data for the three different hardness levels of VS-NPTs. When the p-value was less than 0.05, we inferred that there was a significant association between the two variables. The correlation coefficient quantified the strength and direction of the linear relationship between these variables. The results revealed that the p-values were all zero, indicating a significant correlation between the experimental and simulated data across the different hardness levels. The correlation coefficients were 0.978, 0.996, and 1, respectively, demonstrating a highly significant positive correlation. This confirms the effectiveness of the finite element model. The accuracy of the finite element model facilitates subsequent high-precision simulations, as well as the optimisation and validation of the VS-NPT design.

Materials integration design

Static characteristics

This section focuses on the simulation analysis of the 65HD, 70HD, and 75HD VS-NPTs. A vertical downward force of 1000 N was applied at the centre of the rim to simulate the tyre under radial loading conditions, and the stress distribution is illustrated in Fig. 8. The maximum stress values for the VS-NPTs with different hardness levels are relatively similar, with values of 2.552 MPa, 2.275 MPa, and 2.380 MPa, respectively. Zhu Liangliang et al. reported a maximum stress of 7 MPa for the tyre with Optimised Structure II [12]. We consider the maximum stress of the tyre in this study to be within a reasonable range. The maximum stress exists at the Angle between the spokes of 65HD VS-NPT and 70HD VS-NPT. 75HD VS-NPT has the maximum stress on both sides of the spokes. The increased stresses at the connection areas are attributed to the sharpness of these regions. This issue could potentially be mitigated by increasing the fillet radius to reduce stress concentration at the connection points. Selecting an appropriate material distribution can also reduce the occurrence of stress concentration to a certain extent.Fig. 8Stress distribution in VS-NPT with different hardness levels: (a) 65 HD VS-NPT, (b) 70 HD VS-NPT, (c) 75 HD VS-NPT.Fig. 8

Mechanical characteristics

Triaxial force-displacement data are fundamental for determining triaxial stiffness. To investigate the triaxial force-displacement characteristics of the VS-NPT, the six degrees of freedom of the VS-NPT were fixed, and the load was applied by moving the ground. Specifically, the VS-NPT was held stationary while the ground was moved upwards along the Z-axis, applying a radial load to the VS-NPT to obtain the radial displacement-radial force curve. Building upon this, longitudinal displacement-longitudinal force curves and lateral displacement-lateral force curves were obtained by moving the ground along the X and Y axes. In Abaqus, the movement of the ground is controlled by applying displacement boundary conditions.

The ground was moved upwards along the Z-axis by 10 mm to obtain the radial displacement-radial force curves for VS-NPTs with different hardness levels, as shown in Fig. 13. When the radial displacement was 10 mm, the radial forces for the 65HD VS-NPT, 70HD VS-NPT, and 75HD VS-NPT were 944.25 N, 1140.91 N, and 2503.67 N, respectively. This suggests that for the same radial displacement, a VS-NPT with higher hardness offers greater support. While the relationship remains linear at small radial displacements, as displacement increases, the rate of force growth decelerates, displaying clear nonlinear behaviour.Fig. 13Radial displacement-radial force curves for VS-NPT with different hardness levels.Fig. 13

The triaxial force-displacement curves of the VS-NPT display nonlinear characteristics, primarily due to the combined effects of material nonlinearity and geometric nonlinearity during deformation under load. The tyre, which is primarily composed of TPU, whose nominal stress-nominal strain curve exhibits hyperelastic behaviour. We observed that the triaxial force-displacement curves of the VS-NPT display pronounced nonlinear characteristics. Compared to the radial force-radial displacement curve, the nonlinear behaviour is more pronounced in the longitudinal force-longitudinal displacement and lateral force-lateral displacement curves. Under identical displacement conditions, increased material hardness results in higher triaxial forces. A higher triaxial force enhanced the tyre's support, traction, and steering capabilities. This may sacrifice some comfort. This offered a framework for guiding material selection in VS-NPT design.

Triaxial stiffness

Radial stiffness, longitudinal stiffness, and lateral stiffness are key characteristics of VS-NPTs. Radial stiffness reflects the VS-NPT's ability to bear radial loads; longitudinal stiffness indicates its resistance to deformation under longitudinal forces, such as those encountered during acceleration, braking, and starting; lateral stiffness measures the resistance to deformation in the lateral direction, which impacts the vehicle's handling performance and stability. Radial stiffness is defined as the ratio of radial force to radial displacement, while longitudinal and lateral stiffness are similarly determined. The force-displacement curve of the VS-NPT demonstrates marked nonlinearity. with the load failing to increase proportionally with displacement. Stiffness is defined as the ratio of load to displacement. if examined during the nonlinear phase, the VS-NPT would produce varying stiffness values that do not accurately represent its true behaviour. Therefore, to more precisely characterise the stiffness properties, the analysis was conducted in the linear phase.

From Fig. 13, it is observed that for radial displacements ranging from 0 to 4 mm, the radial displacement-force curves of the 65HD, 70HD, and 75HD VS-NPTs exhibit a clear linear relationship. At a radial displacement of 4 mm, the corresponding radial forces for the different VS-NPTs are 534.61 N, 645.92 N, and 1449.16 N, respectively. The corresponding radial stiffness values are 133.65 N/mm, 161.48 N/mm, and 362.29 N/mm. This indicates that as TPU hardness increases, the radial stiffness of the VS-NPT also increases, reflecting a greater bearing capacity.

To determine the longitudinal and lateral stiffness, we applied radial, longitudinal, and lateral displacements to the ground. It is necessary to discuss the effects of these displacements on the longitudinal and lateral stiffness of the different material VS-NPTs to account for the influence of load on stiffness.

Fig. 16(a) illustrates the longitudinal stiffness of VS-NPTs with different hardness levels, corresponding to radial displacements of 2 mm, 4 mm, 6 mm, and 8 mm. It is observed that the longitudinal stiffness shows minimal fluctuation for the same hardness level across different radial displacements, with a maximum deviation of only 3 %. This suggests that radial displacement has a negligible effect on longitudinal stiffness using this simulation approach. The average longitudinal stiffness of VS-NPTs with varying hardness levels is 94.42 N/mm, 114.08 N/mm, and 256.20 N/mm, respectively. As the longitudinal stiffness of the VS-NPT increases, its resistance to longitudinal deformation is progressively enhanced.Fig. 16Stiffnesses corresponding to different displacements for VS-NPT with varying hardness levels: (a) Longitudinal Stiffnesses at different radial displacements, (b) longitudinal Stiffnesses at different longitudinal displacements, (c) lateral Stiffnesses at different radial displacements, (d) lateral Stiffnesses at different lateral displacements.Fig. 16

Fig. 16(b) presents the longitudinal stiffness of VS-NPTs with different hardness levels under longitudinal displacements of 1 mm, 2 mm, 3 mm, and 4 mm. The maximum deviation is only 5.94 %, indicating that longitudinal displacement has a minimal impact on longitudinal stiffness. For example, the 65HD VS-NPT has average longitudinal stiffness values of 94.42 N/mm and 93.69 N/mm for different radial and longitudinal displacements, respectively. Fig. 16(c) illustrates the lateral stiffness of VS-NPTs with varying hardness levels at radial displacements of 1 mm, 2 mm, 3 mm, and 4 mm. It is evident that the lateral stiffness at the same hardness level exhibits minimal variation across various radial displacements, with a maximum deviation of only 7.92 %. This is likely due to the nonlinear characteristics of the material, which prevent the tyre's load-displacement curve from being entirely linear. Materials with higher hardness demonstrate greater resistance to deformation, leading to increased loads and enhanced stiffness for equivalent deformation levels. Consequently, as the material hardness increases, the triaxial stiffness of the VS-NPT progressively improves, which enhances the tyre's load-bearing capacity and handling characteristics, although this may come at the cost of reduced ride comfort.

Different material hardness design for different parts

Parametric analysis based on the Taguchi

This section introduces a method for selecting different material hardness levels for various components of the VS-NPT. The Taguchi method, reduces the number of experiments while, ensures that the results are comprehensive and representative, thereby enabling the quantification of the relationship between design factors and responses. The Taguchi method is utilised to examine the impact of varying material hardness on the contact area and tri-axial stiffness of the VS-NPT. As discussed in the preceding section, radial, longitudinal, and lateral displacements have minimal effects on longitudinal and lateral stiffness. Consequently, for the subsequent stiffness analysis, the boundary conditions are set as follows: a 2 mm upward movement along the Z-axis to determine radial stiffness; a 2 mm upward movement along the Z-axis followed by an additional 2 mm movement along the X-axis to determine longitudinal stiffness; and a 2 mm movement along the Y-axis to assess lateral stiffness. The design of the contact area is a crucial aspect of the VS-NPT's interaction with the ground.

The hardness levels of 65HD, 70HD, and 75HD are represented by levels 1, 2, and 3 for each factor. The Taguchi method employs orthogonal experiments, providing optimal results with fewer trials. By using the orthogonal array method for 3 factors at 3 levels, only 9 experiments are required, significantly reducing the number of trials compared to the full 33 = 27 runs. Variance analysis is employed to assess the significance and contribution of experimental parameters to the outcomes. Significance testing determines whether a factor has a substantial impact on system performance, while the contribution rate measures the extent to which each factor affects overall performance. By identifying factors with significant effects and high contribution rates, one can pinpoint the primary influences on performance. In statistics, a smaller P-value (typically less than 0.05) indicates that it is unlikely that the observed data would occur if there were no significant relationship between the factor and the outcome. Therefore, a small P-value suggests that a significant relationship exists between the factor and the result. The significance level of 0.05 is a widely accepted threshold. Conclusions

In this study, Finite element models of VS-NPT with varying hardness levels were developed, and the accuracy of these models was validated through static testing. Building on this foundation, we investigated the performance of integrated VS-NPT with different hardness levels and proposed a design approach that employs varying material hardness for different components. Using the Taguchi method, we investigated the effects of tread material hardness, spokes material hardness, and inner ring material hardness on tri-axial stiffness and contact area. The main findings of the research are summarised as follows.1.Under a load of 1000 N, the maximum stresses for the 65HD VS-NPT, 70HD VS-NPT, and 75HD VS-NPT are 2.552 MPa, 2.275 MPa, and 2.380 MPa, respectively. The maximum stresses occur at the spoke tips for the 65HD and 70HD VS-NPT, and at both sides of the spokes for the 75HD VS-NPT. The maximum ground contact stresses for the different hardness VS-NPTs are 0.681 MPa, 0.743 MPa, and 1.459 MPa, respectively. The locations of these maximum ground contact stresses are all at the sides of the tread. As material hardness increases, the resistance to deformation and the load-bearing capacity of VS-NPT improves, while the ground contact area gradually decreases.2.The triaxial force-displacement curves of VS-NPT with varying hardness levels exhibit nonlinearity. As longitudinal and lateral displacements increase, the longitudinal and lateral forces reach a maximum value, beyond which they no longer change. Radial, longitudinal, and lateral displacements have minimal impact on the longitudinal and lateral stiffness of VS-NPT. Furthermore, higher material hardness is associated with increased triaxial forces and greater stiffness of VS-NPT. Non-linear behaviour allows the VS-NPT to experience greater deformation upon impact, thereby effectively absorbing impact energy and improving ride comfort. The tri-axial force-displacement curves vary with material hardness. Consequently, material hardness should be chosen in accordance with the specific design objectives.3.Based on the current specifications of VS-NPT, spoke material remains the primary factor influencing triaxial stiffness and ground contact area, irrespective of changes in structural parameters. When spoke thickness exceeds 7.3 mm, tread material also becomes a significant factor affecting the ground contact area. Building on the findings of this study, future research will centre on the multi-objective optimisation of VS-NPT performance, addressing both structural and material aspects, with the aim of developing a tyre design that delivers enhanced overall performance.

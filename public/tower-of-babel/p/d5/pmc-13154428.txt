Preferences for advance care planning services among patients with recurrent stroke: a discrete choice experiment
Authors: Liming Zhang, Ning Wang, Chunli Xian, Meng Fan, Jianghua Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStroke remains a major chronic condition in China, characterized by high incidence, frequent recurrence, and substantial disability. These challenges not only affect patients’ quality of life but also place a heavy burden on families and the healthcare system. Advance care planning provides a structured approach to help patients make informed decisions about their future medical care. However, little is known about how patients with recurrent stroke perceive and prioritize advance care planning services. Gaining insight into their needs and preferences is essential for designing interventions that are both responsive and clinically meaningful.MethodsFace-to-face questionnaires were administered, requiring participants to choose between two service options based on seven key attributes: service object, service frequency, service location, service content, service provider, service format, and cost. A mixed logit model estimated preference weights, and a latent class logit model identified preference heterogeneity among subgroups. Relative attribute importance, willingness to pay, and scenario predictions were calculated. Chi-square tests, Fisher’s exact tests, and Kruskal-Wallis tests compared demographic and clinical characteristics between latent classes.ResultsA total of 5,382 choice observations were generated. Among the seven advance care planning-related attributes for patients with recurrent stroke, their relative importance ranked as follows: service provider(30.72%), service frequency(24.20%), service content(18.81%), service format(11.85%), service location(6.24%), cost(5.88%), and service object(2.30%). Overall, participants preferred an advance care planning service model characterized by “twice per month, discussion of future care priorities(β = +0.425), and delivery by specialists or nurses(β = +0.975).” Willingness to pay decreased as out-of-pocket costs increased(β = − 0.186). Latent class analysis identified three preference groups, with statistically significant differences in sex, age, and education level.ConclusionsPatients with recurrent stroke in China, particularly in Shiyan City, place emphasis on professional service providers, comprehensive medical support, and personalized advance care planning service models, suggesting a strong influence of cultural context. As out-of-pocket costs increase, their willingness to pay decreases. There are significant differences in preferences among different subgroups of patients, indicating the need to establish stratified and personalized advance care planning service models to account for differences in residence, socioeconomic status, and education level.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-026-14437-x.Keywords: Stroke, Advance care planning, Discrete choice experiment, Choice preference, Best-worst scaling

Introduction

Stroke is a leading cause of mortality and long-term disability worldwide. According to the 2021 Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, there were about 93.8 million stroke survivors worldwide in 2021, with about 11.9 million new stroke cases every year [1]. Compared with other chronic diseases, stroke is characterized by sudden onset, uncertain prognosis, high recurrence rate, and varying degrees of disability and cognitive dysfunction [2]. The patient’s dysfunctions range widely, from mild limb weakness and speech difficulties to complete loss of daily life. According to the latest data, the incidence of stroke in China is about 246 per 100,000 people, with more than 3 million new cases every year; the mortality rate and disability rate are among the top in the world [3]. Patients with recurrent stroke often experience more severe neurological impairment, higher long-term dependence, and lower quality of life, resulting in significant psychological, caregiving, and economic burdens for families and society [4, 5].

Advance care planning (ACP) is a proactive and structured process that supports individuals in clarifying and communicating their values, goals, and preferences for future medical care. Core components of ACP include clarification of treatment goals and personal values; communication with healthcare professionals and family members regarding preferences for life-sustaining treatments (e.g., cardiopulmonary resuscitation, mechanical ventilation, and artificial nutrition and hydration); selection of preferred place of care; designation of surrogate decision-makers; and formal documentation of care preferences in advance directives [6–8]. Evidence shows that ongoing ACP communication can improve the consistency between patients’ wishes and received care, relieve caregiver burden, and enhance quality of life [9–11]. While ACP has become an important component of clinical practice in many Western healthcare systems [12], its implementation in China remains limited, particularly among high-risk populations such as patients with recurrent stroke.

Patients with recurrent stroke often face uncertainty about prognosis and future decision-making, yet little is known about their preferences for ACP services in the Chinese context. Preference-elicitation methods such as discrete choice experiments (DCE) and best–worst scaling (BWS), based on consumer preference and random utility theory, can quantify the relative importance of service attributes and the trade-offs individuals are willing to make [13, 14]. BWS is particularly useful for identifying priority attributes, while DCE enables estimation of preference strength and service configuration [15, 16]. Combining these methods can improve the robustness and policy relevance of findings.

Therefore, this study aimed to identify and quantify advance care planning service preferences among patients with recurrent stroke in China by integrating BWS for attribute development and DCE for preference elicitation, in order to inform the design of patient-centered ACP service models and support healthcare decision-making.

Methods

Design

This study employed a cross-sectional, mixed-methods design to investigate the preferences of patients with recurrent stroke regarding ACP services. The DCE, which served as the main quantitative component, is a multivariate analytical method grounded in consumer preference theory and random utility theory, and is recognized as one of the most sensitive tools for eliciting individual preferences in health research [17, 18]. The overall DCE process involved five steps: defining and describing the research question, selecting attributes and levels, designing the DCE questionnaire, collecting data, and analyzing the data [19]. Attributes and identification levels

The selection of attributes and levels in this study was based on the four-stage theoretical framework proposed by Helter and Boehler [20]. The process was cross-validated through literature review, expert consultation, a BWS-1 survey, and a pilot test. Literature searches covered publications up to June 2024, resulting in 25 relevant articles from which study-related keywords were extracted to identify preliminary potential attributes. Subsequently, purposive sampling was used to recruit 15 patients with recurrent stroke who had been hospitalized at a tertiary hospital between July and September 2024 for in-depth individual interviews. The sample size was determined according to the principle of data saturation. Following the operational approach proposed by Francis JJ et al. [21], interviews were continued until no new attributes or levels emerged. After 12 interviews, no additional themes relevant to ACP service attributes were identified, and three further interviews were conducted to confirm saturation. As no new information appeared in these additional interviews, recruitment was stopped at 15 participants. The qualitative findings were then used to refine candidate attributes and levels for subsequent BWS-1 and pilot testing. By integrating findings from the literature review and qualitative interviews, 11 potential attributes with 33 levels were identified (Supplementary 6 Table 1).

Given the absence of clear guidelines for calculating the BWS questionnaire sample size, the total number of task choices was set at 2,000 to ensure sufficient statistical power. The required sample size was calculated to be greater than 91, and accounting for an anticipated 10% of invalid responses, the final sample size was set at 102. Convenience sampling was used to conduct the BWS-1 survey among patients with recurrent stroke hospitalized at a tertiary hospital between October and December 2024, with inclusion and exclusion criteria consistent with the qualitative study. Based on the first round of expert consultation and BWS-1 results, the relative importance ranking of each attribute was calculated (expert information provided in Supplementary Table 3; analysis results in Supplementary 1–3 Figs. 1, 2 and 3). Experts recommended including 6–8 attributes in the DCE [22], with each attribute having 2–4 levels. Accordingly, seven attributes meeting these criteria were selected to form the second-round expert consultation questionnaire. The first-round experts were then consulted again via email for revisions on the seven attribute levels until consensus was reached. The final attributes and levels are presented in Table 1.

Experimental design and questionnaire development

A full factorial design with 7 attributes and 20 levels would generate 1,458 (21 × 36) hypothetical scenarios, which is impractical for participants. To improve the statistical efficiency of the DCE, a D-efficient design was conducted using SAS 9.4, resulting in 18 hypothetical scenarios for choice analysis (Supplementary 4 Fig. 4). SPSS 27.0 was then used to perform an orthogonal design to generate 18 manageable scenarios (Supplementary 5 Fig. 5). The option with the medium level for each attribute (Scenario 3) was selected as the reference alternative and paired with the remaining 17 scenarios to form 17 choice sets. One choice set was designated as a common set to assess internal consistency and ensure questionnaire quality. To reduce participant burden and maintain quality, the choice sets were randomly divided into two versions [23], with each version containing nine choice sets. No “opt-out” option was included, as ACP involves meaningful attributes that may influence patients’ future care, and all attributes included in the study are relevant to participants. A sample DCE questionnaire is presented in Fig. 2. A pretest including 30 patients was conducted in December 2024 to verify the appropriateness of the attribute levels and assess participants’ understanding. Although formal guidelines for DCE pretest sample size are lacking, previous methodological studies suggest that approximately 30 respondents are sufficient to detect major design issues and refine questionnaires before the main survey [24, 25]. Based on the pretest results, the final DCE questionnaire consisted of two versions, each containing nine choice sets with seven attributes per set. The questionnaire for patients with recurrent stroke consisted of two parts: a general information survey, including age, sex, education level, marital status, occupation, monthly personal income, health insurance status, religious beliefs, primary caregiver, living situation, stroke etiology, and disease course; and one version of the DCE questionnaire. Inclusion criteria were: ① age ≥ 18 years; ② recurrent stroke diagnosed according to the 2019 Chinese Guidelines for Diagnosis of Major Cerebrovascular Diseases and confirmed by cranial CT or MRI [26]; and ③ being in any stage of the recovery phase with a stable clinical condition, having normal cognitive function as assessed by the Mini-Mental State Examination [27] (MMSE, score ≥ 24 for patients with at least primary school education), and able to communicate verbally. Exclusion criteria were: ①presence of severe cognitive impairment or a history of psychiatric disorders; ②comorbid dysfunction of major organs such as the heart, lungs, or kidneys, or any malignant tumours; and ③severe speech or hearing impairments.

Data collection

A cross-sectional survey was conducted from December 2024 to July 2025 in the Departments of Neurology and Neurorehabilitation of three tertiary general hospitals in Shiyan, Hubei Province. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for participants were consistent with those used in the pre-survey. The minimum sample size was calculated using Orme equation [25]: N ≥ 500c / (ta), where N is the minimum sample size, c is the maximum number of levels for any attribute(c = 3), t is the number of choice sets per questionnaire version(t = 9), and a is the number of alternatives per choice set(a = 2). The calculation yielded a minimum sample size of 84 participants. Considering the number of questionnaire versions and an anticipated 20% of invalid responses, at least 210 questionnaires were distributed.

This study used paper-based questionnaires, and data were collected on-site through one-to-one interviews. The survey team consisted of the principal investigator and two graduate students. Prior to the formal survey, all interviewers received training and guidance to ensure familiarity with the questionnaire content and a clear understanding of each question and response option. During completion, interviewers addressed participants’ questions in real time and assisted and supervised them to ensure careful and accurate completion. Upon collection, each questionnaire was checked immediately for missing items, and participants were asked to provide the missing information on the spot. A DCE consistency check was conducted: after participants completed the questionnaire, the common choice set was repeated, and any inconsistent responses were considered invalid and excluded from the analysis. Informed consent was obtained from all participants before the survey.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical analyses were performed for demographic variables, and each choice set was treated as a single observation. Attributes included in the study were considered independent variables, while participants’ choices (option 1 or option 2) served as the dependent variable. Preference data were analysed using conditional logit and mixed logit models for comparison. To account for preference heterogeneity at the group level, a latent class logit model was further applied. The appropriate number of classes was determined based on model fit and interpretability. Chi-square tests, Fisher’s exact tests, and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to compare basic characteristics across different classes, with a significance level of P < 0.05. Relative importance scores were calculated to quantify the weight of each attribute, determined by dividing the utility difference between the highest and lowest levels of each attribute by the sum of utility differences across all attributes. To visually assess patients’ preferences for each attribute, willingness-to-pay (WTP) was calculated to measure the monetary value of each attribute. In this study, the cost attribute was entered into the model as the natural logarithm of the per-visit fee, ln(cost), rather than as a linear monetary amount. This specification is commonly used in discrete choice experiments when the marginal disutility of cost is expected to decrease as the cost level increases, and when respondents are more sensitive to proportional rather than absolute changes in price. Ethics and reporting standards

The design and reporting of this study followed the ISPOR Good Research Practices for Conjoint Analysis Task Force checklist [23]. The study was approved by the Scientific Ethics Review Committee (Approval No. 2024-RE-23). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 320 patients with recurrent stroke were recruited for this study, of whom 2 declined to participate in the DCE questionnaire for personal reasons, resulting in a response rate of 99.38%. Participants who failed the internal consistency check(n = 15) and those who provided straight-line responses(n = 4) were excluded, leaving 299 participants(93.44%) for the final data analysis. Among the two questionnaire versions, 145 and 144 participants were included, respectively, with no significant differences in general demographic characteristics between the groups(P > 0.05). Participants had a mean age of 66.75 ± 10.04 years, were predominantly male(64.88%), married(86.96%), and had at least a junior high school education(64.88%). Most reported a monthly personal income below 5,000 RMB(63.22%). The majority of participants’ primary caregivers were spouses(61.87%), the duration of stroke was less than 5 years(70.90%), disease severity was mild (NIHSS < 5), and the number of comorbid chronic conditions was concentrated between 1 and 3(82.63%). Most patients had experienced at least one emergency visit in the past three years(61.20%), and a minority had experienced bereavement in the past five years(14.05%). Detailed information is presented in Table 2.

Preferences of recurrent stroke for advance care planning service

The preference choice models included a total of 5,382 choice observations from 299 participants, which were analysed using both conditional logit and mixed logit models. The AIC and BIC values for the conditional logit model were 7,109.463 and 7,195.120, respectively, whereas for the mixed logit model, the AIC and BIC values were 3,442.213 and 3,501.530, indicating that the mixed logit model provided a better fit. Therefore, the mixed logit model regression results were used for reporting. The results showed that, except for the attributes of service object and service location, the remaining five attributes—service frequency, service content, service provider, service format, and cost—significantly influenced preferences for ACP services among patients with recurrent stroke(P < 0.05). Based on the strength of preferences, the relative importance of each ACP-related attribute was calculated (Fig. 3). The most valued attribute was the service provider: compared with community doctors or nurses, patients preferred specialist doctors or nurses(β = +0.975), but ACP services provided by team members(β = +0.804) were also acceptable. Service frequency ranked second in importance, accounting for 24.20%, with patients preferring twice per month. Regarding service content, discussions about future care priorities(β = +0.425) were more likely to be accepted. Preferences for ACP services decreased as out-of-pocket costs increased(β = − 0.186). In contrast, patients paid relatively little attention to the target population and service setting (Table 3).

Estimates of willingness to pay

Based on the mixed logit model results, WTP for each attribute and level is detailed in Table 4. Patients with recurrent stroke exhibited the highest WTP for service providers, willing to pay an additional 524.19 RMB to change the provider from community doctors or nurses to specialist doctors or nurses. If the service format changed from remote/online guidance to in-home service, patients were willing to pay an extra 202.15 RMB. Preference heterogeneity analysis

Due to heterogeneity in ACP service preferences among patients with recurrent stroke, the mixed logit model may not fully capture within-group differences. Therefore, this study further applied a latent class logit model to classify the sample into several latent groups, aiming to identify distinct patient preferences and explore their influencing factors. As shown in Table 5, model fit improved with an increasing number of classes: the LL increased continuously, and the AIC decreased gradually. The BIC reached its minimum at three classes, after which adding more classes led to an increase in BIC, indicating that excessive model complexity outweighed the gains in fit. Considering that BIC provides a more robust balance between model fit and complexity [28], the three-class model was ultimately selected as the best-fitting model.

Table 6 shows that ACP service preferences differ among the latent classes of patients with recurrent stroke. Class 1 patients prefer ACP services focused on future care planning(β = +1.150), delivered by specialised doctors or nurses(β = +0.981), with in-home service. In contrast, Class 2 patients tend to prefer ACP services that involve the patient directly, have lower out-of-pocket costs(β = +0.925), lower service frequency(β = +0.848), and focus primarily on physical health management. This group shows strong aversion to high-frequency services(β = −1.990), remote/online guidance(β = −1.498), and psychosocial support(β = −0.947). Class 3 patients are less sensitive to out-of-pocket costs and favour flexible service frequency, placing greater emphasis on planning and arranging future life(β = +0.847). Compared with in-home services, this group is more willing to receive ACP services through remote/online guidance provided by a team of healthcare professionals and legal advisors.

Attribute choice probabilities by class

Based on the regression results of the latent class logit model, the predicted acceptance probabilities of different service attributes under various ACP scenarios were evaluated. The baseline scenario featured the following characteristics: patient involvement, twice per month, at home, focusing on physical health management, provided by community doctors or nurses, in-home service, and 100 RMB per session. Under attribute changes (①–⑬), for Class 1 participants, receiving services at a community health centre was the most valued attribute level, with the choice probability increasing to 63.1% compared with home-based services. This group also tended to prefer services provided by community doctors or nurses with content including psychosocial support. Class 2 participants showed higher preferences for services costing 50 RMB per session(76.3%), once per month(74.4%), and conducted at the hospital(76.4%), placing greater trust in experienced specialist doctors or nurses. In contrast to Classes 1 and 2, Class 3 participants were more interested in high-frequency services and remote/online delivery, with choice probabilities increasing to 78.7% and 75.6%, respectively, when these attribute levels were applied (Fig. 4).

Discussion

This study combined a discrete choice experiment with mixed logit and latent class logit models to systematically analyze the preferences of patients with recurrent stroke for ACP services, revealing heterogeneity across population subgroups. The results indicate that patients with recurrent stroke place greater emphasis on medical and legal protection, future life planning, and service delivery modes, and they tend to make independent decisions. These findings not only challenge the traditional domestic view that “patients rely on family joint decision-making” [29, 30] but also provide evidence to support the implementation of ACP practices and policy development for the stroke population in China.

The study revealed ACP service preferences of patients with recurrent stroke from multiple perspectives, including service object, service frequency, service location, service content, service provider, service format, and cost. Overall preference analysis indicated that the service provider was the most important attribute for patients, and it also ranked highest in willingness-to-pay. Although the ranking of attribute importance and WTP was generally consistent, some discrepancies were observed. Patients perceived service frequency as important, but their WTP for this attribute was relatively low, suggesting a gap between “perceived need” and “financial capacity.” This finding aligns with previous studies showing that patients value medical security and emotional support in long-term care, but their actual spending tends to prioritize services that directly improve prognosis or reduce caregiving burden [30–32]. Therefore, future policy and service design should ensure core medical needs while aligning patient preferences with financial capacity, for example, by reducing out-of-pocket costs.

The mixed logit model results showed that service frequency, service content, service provider, service format, and cost all had statistically significant effects on ACP service choices among patients with recurrent stroke. The positive effects of service provider and service content indicate that patients value the type of service provider and planning for future life, which is consistent with Guo et al.‘s findings that stroke patients emphasise personnel selection and decision support during rehabilitation [29]. Sensitivity to economic factors aligns with Liu et al.‘s conclusions regarding ACP uptake and health insurance coverage [33]. The significance of future life priority planning highlights the growing awareness of self-care among patients and caregivers, echoing the qualitative findings of Wang et al. on self-care experiences among stroke survivors and their caregivers [31].

It is worth noting that this study found that patients with recurrent stroke tend to “independent decision-making” rather than “family joint decision-making”. This shift shows that patients have clear values and independent judgments in the choice of pre-established ACP services, which is different from the view of previous domestic studies that “patients rely on family decision-making” [34]. Zhao et al. also pointed out [35] that in recent years, the self-advocacy of stroke patients has received more and more attention, and individuals with higher education and health literacy are more likely to express their will independently. These findings reflect a conceptual shift: influenced by cultural factors and disease experience, recurrent stroke patients in China are gradually turning to a “patient-centered” decision-making model. Therefore, the future service model should emphasize expressing personal will and strengthening doctor-patient communication during the implementation of ACP, and avoid over-reliance on family members [36]. The role of family members should be redefined as “supporters” rather than “co-decision-makers” [11, 37], providing emotional and resource support while respecting the autonomy of patients.

The potential category logit model explains the differences in the preferences of pre-set ACP in different subgroups of recurrent stroke patients. The first category of participants prefers low-cost community care programs; the second category prefers multi-dimensional and comprehensive services; and the third category is willing to pay more for specialized and personalized services. This shows that ACP preferences are highly heterogeneous even in the same population. Further analysis reveals demographic and socio-economic differences between categories. The first category is mainly composed of women, rural residents, and people with low education, who prefer low-cost, family-supported services. The second category of people has a balanced gender distribution and diverse needs. They pay attention not only to community medical security, but also to the guidance of psychosocial and lifestyle factors. The third category is mainly composed of men, urban residents, highly educated, and high-income people. They pay more attention to independent decision-making and high-quality services. These findings are consistent with the research results of Sun et al. [38], who reported differences in technology acceptance and decision-making methods among different groups of people. In addition, a Polish study on the burden of carers for stroke patients emphasized that family structure and economic conditions can significantly affect patients’ decision-making and service choices [39]. Therefore, the implementation of the pre-equipped ACP should be tailored to the needs of patients: for rural and low-educated people, low-cost, family-supported services should be emphasized; and for urban, highly educated people, the personalized and professional ACP model should be given priority to meet the diversified needs of different patients.

In order to further verify the practical application value of DCE research, we conducted a scenario simulation based on the results of the potential category logit model. The simulation results show that changes in the attribute level have a significant impact. When the deductible fee is reduced to 50 RMB, the proportion of Class 2 participants reaches 76.3%; when the deductible fee is increased to 200 RMB, the proportion of Class 3 participants rises to 52.2%, which indicates that the high-cost option is more attractive to higher-educated and high-income groups. When the target group is set to “family joint decision-making”, the selection probability of the third category participants is the highest, reaching 50.8%, which shows that in some cases, some groups still value family participation. The frequency of services was increased from once a month to four times, and the proportion of third-class participants increased significantly to 78.7%, which shows that high-frequency services are more attractive to groups seeking specialized and comprehensive medical services. In the pre-established ACP provided by the community health center, the selection probability of the first category of people reached 63.1%, highlighting the preference of rural or low-educational patients for community services, while the second category of people mainly chose hospital services. Providing services covering psychosocial support and future life planning has improved the tendency of the third category of people to choose. In general, all three groups of people tend to choose services provided by specialists or nurses. When the form of service is remote service, the proportion of the third category of people is as high as 75.6%, indicating that patients with higher education have a higher acceptance of the digital service model. In a word, the results of the scenario simulation are consistent with the results of heterogeneity analysis, revealing the selection patterns of potential patient subgroups in different scenarios, and providing “situational” and “stratified” evidence for clinical service planning. In resource-poor rural areas, low-cost community pre-set medical care plan services can meet the needs of patients; while in urban and highly educated areas, the promotion of remote services and high-frequency services may help improve the acceptance and participation of pre-set medical care plans. Therefore, scene simulation based on the potential category logit model provides important guidance for the precise design and policy modeling of ACP services in the future [12, 23, 32].

The advantage of this study is that it focuses on patients with recurrent stroke and adopts a comprehensive method, combining attribute importance sorting, mixed logit model, potential category logit model, and scenario simulation, to systematically explore the pre-prelined ACP preference model of different patient subgroups. In terms of methods, this study adopts the mixed DCE and BWS-1 design, which not only simulates the real decision-making process but also quantifies the importance of attributes, thus enhancing the robustness and interpretability of DCE results.

However, this study also has some limitations. First of all, the complexity of the DCE and BWS-1 questionnaires may have imposed a cognitive burden on patients, particularly those with lower education levels. Second, the relatively small sample size may affect the stability of the DCE model estimations. Third, the study utilized a forced-choice design without an “opt-out” option. Since respondents were required to choose between hypothetical ACP services, this may lead to an overestimation of their willingness to engage in ACP compared to real-world settings where they might choose not to participate at all. Fourth, the lack of longitudinal follow-up prevents the analysis of how patient preferences might dynamically change over time.

Future research can adopt a longitudinal design to examine how the ACP preferences of patients with recurrent stroke evolve at different stages of the disease. In addition, incorporating the supply-side perspective can help the preferences of medical service providers and patients. The further application of scenario simulation methods can evaluate the impact of different ACP service models on patient selection and policy implementation, so as to promote the development of layered and precisely oriented ACP services.

Conclusions

This study used a DCE to investigate ACP service preferences among patients with recurrent stroke in China, particularly in Shiyan City. The findings suggest that ACP services should be designed by comprehensively considering patients’ preferences for family support, follow-up frequency, content comprehensiveness, and specialist-led care. Policies and services aligned with patient preferences can better match future healthcare supply and demand. As ACP services gradually extend from hospitals to community and home-based care, healthcare institutions should adopt more flexible management strategies, integrate multidisciplinary collaboration, and strengthen education and support for patients and families to enhance the accessibility, individualisation, and sustainability of ACP services.

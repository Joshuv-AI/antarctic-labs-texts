TBI/Cy followed by auto-HSCT is a good choice next to allo-HSCT for patients with T-LBL/ALL
Authors: Jinghua Mao, Jurui Ge, Shiqi Ding, Zhenchang Sun, Feifei Nan, Hui Yu, Jiayin Ding, Xinyi Wang, Zhuling Liu, Mingzhi Zhang, Xiaorui Fu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this retrospective study was to evaluate the efficiency and safety of total body irradiation plus cyclophosphamide (TBI/Cy) followed by autogenetic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (auto-HSCT) in T-LBL/ALL patients that cannot receive allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (allo-HSCT). Between 2013 and 2023, 24 patients received auto-HSCT following by TBI/Cy, 26 patients underwent allo-HSCT, all patients achieved completed hematopoietic reconstitution after HSCT. The progression free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) had no statistically significant differences between the two groups (P = 0.791, HR 1.127, 95%CI 0.456–2.785; P = 0.456, HR 0.685, 95%CI 0.256–1.828). Although the cumulative incidence of relapse was lower for patients who received allo-HSCT than auto-HSCT (P = 0.033, HR 3.707, 95%CI 1.188–11.570, 2-year relapse 11.5% vs. 33.3%), the incidence of non-relapse mortality (NRM) was higher than that in the auto-HSCT group (P = 0.014, HR 0.000, 95%CI -1.000 - -1.000, 2-year NRM, 23.1% vs. 0%). Trough Landmark analysis, the two groups showed a statistically significant difference in 3-year PFS and 4-year OS curves (Figure S2A&B, P = 0.039, HR 0.426, 95%CI 0.163–1.117; P = 0.014, HR 0.317, 95%CI 0.113–0.887). By COX analysis, poor baseline performance status (ECOG-PS ≥ 2) and CNS involvement were risk factors for PFS and OS. Introduction

T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma (T-LBL) is an aggressive, very rare subtype of adult non-Hodgkins lymphoma (NHL)1. T-LBL is classified together with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) as T-LBL/ALL according to the definition given by the WHO2. Intensive chemotherapy regimens in lymphoblastic lymphoma/ leukemia have led to substantial improvement in survival3, however, because of high recurrence rates4, long-term survival is still currently unmet, with a 5-year overall survival (OS) of 32% and progression-free survival (PFS) of 22% using NHL protocols5. Yang et al. demonstrated that due to the poor prognosis with chemotherapy treatment alone, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell (allo-HSCT) transplantation should be recommended for patients in first complete remission (CR1) following induction chemotherapy6. To date, the only curative therapy for patients with T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukemia is allogeneic stem cell transplant, but it is costly and finding suitable donors for transplantation is difficult7. Therefore, new treatment options are needed for patients who do not have suitable donors, unable to pay their medical bills or cannot undergo allo-HSCT for other reasons.

As generally known, the antileukemic activity of allo-HSCT is provided both by the immune mediated graft versus leukemia/lymphoma (GVL) reaction and by the high-dose intensity of the conditioning therapy8. For this reason, myeloablative conditioning regimens followed by autogeneic hematopoietic stem cell (auto-HSCT) transplantation is an alternative for patients who cannot receive allo-HSCT. Total body irradiation (TBI) is widely used for myeloablative conditioning regimens9 with the advantage of lower relapse rates, better progression-free survival (PFS) and lower transplant-related mortality (TRM)8. In the present study, we compared the efficacy and safety of auto-HSCT following by total body irradiation plus cyclophosphamide (TBI/Cy) with that of allo-HSCT in T-LBL/ALLs.

Materials and methods

Between 2013 and 2023, a total of 50 T-LBL/ALL patients received hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in our hospital, and 26 underwent allogeneic HSCTs, 24 patients received autologous HSCTs following by TBI/Cy. All patients had a definite pathological diagnosis and were classified according to the WHO pathological histological diagnosis criteria. The clinical characteristics, treatment plan, and laboratory data of all patients were obtained from the hospital’s electronic medical records. Efficacy assessments were conducted every two cycles.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University(the number given by the ethics committee: 2024-KY-0261-001) and was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided informed consent for treatment.

When the absolute count of peripheral blood CD34+ cells increases to 5–10/µl, it is appropriate to consider the collection of peripheral blood hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). HSCT is feasible when the number of CD34+ cells is ≥ 2 × 106/kg or ≥ 1 × 106/kg and the number of mononuclear cells (MNC) is ≥ 2 × 108/kg.

Patients in the auto-HSCT group received fractionated TBI (10 Gy total, 2 Gy every time, twice daily) and cyclophosphamide (Cy; 60 mg/kg/day) for two consecutive days followed by transplantation of CD34 + cell-enriched peripheral blood progenitor cells. And conditioning regimens in the allo-HSCT group included modified Bu/Cy regimen (busulphan, carmustine, cytarabine and cyclophosphamide), etc (Fig. 1).

PFS (A) and OS (B) of patients with T-LBL/ALL (n = 50).

Death was confirmed according to medical records in our hospital and information provided by relatives of the patients during phone call following-up. Day 0 was designated as the day of graft infusion. Overall survival (OS) was defined from day 0 to time of event (death or censor). Relapse was defined as disease recurrence or persistent disease. Patients with persistent disease were classified as relapsed at day 28. We define Progression-free survival (PFS) as day 0 to progression or death from any cause and will censor this time on which the last day the patient is alive without progression. The cumulative incidence of recurrence (CIR) is defined as the proportion of the total number of patients with recurrent disease who reappear in the already diagnosed population from the time the disease is diagnosed to a certain point in time. The non-relapse mortality (NRM) is the proportion of deaths in a given period of time that are not due to recurrent disease. The primary endpoints of the study were PFS and OS, while the secondary endpoints included CIR and NRM.

Kaplan-Meier method and Log-rank test were used to plot and analyze the survival curves of PFS/OS、CIR curve and NRM curve. Simultaneously, the survival curves for PFS and OS were further analyzed using landmark analysis to investigate the impact of HSCT on survival at various time intervals. Univariable analysis was conducted using the Cox proportional hazards model to assess all clinical and pathological indicators. Statistically significant indicators identified in the univariable analysis were then included in a multivariable Cox regression prognostic analysis. Comparisons of categorical variables were carried out using the χ2 test and Fisher’s exact test. Differences with a P-value less than 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant(P < 0.05). Statistical analyses were carried out with SPSS software 27.0 and GraphPad Prism 10.0.

Results

In all 50 patients, the median age was 27 years (range 9–55), 24 (48.0%) patients received auto-HSCT after systemic approaches, 32 (64.0%) patients were male, the most common clinical presentation was mediastinal mass (88.0%, n = 44), bone marrow involvement was observed in 33 patients (66.0%) with 14 patients (28.0%) having more than 20% involvement in the bone marrow, 25 patients (50.0%) had pleural or pericardial effusions at the time of diagnosis. The decision of whether performing an allo-HSCT or auto-HSCT after remission was made according to the availability of suitable donor, patient’s intention and the doctor’s synthetic judgment. There are no differences between the two groups in age, gender, Ann arbor stage, aaIPI score, B symptoms, LDH level, ECOG performance status or disease status (Table 1). The median number of cycles received was 3.5 cycles for allo-HSCT group (range: 2–8) and 5 cycles for auto-HSCT group (range: 1–10), respectively (Table 2).

Prophylactic drugs for GVHD such as immunosuppressants(e.g. Mycophenolate mofetil, Cyclosporine, Methotrexate) and Antithymocyte Globulin(ATG) are administered before and after HSCT.

All patients achieved completed hematopoietic reconstitution after HSCT. In auto-HSCT group, the median time to neutrophil engraftment was 11.5 days (range, 9–28 days) and platelet engraftment 13 days (range, 7–40 days), the median number of infused mononuclear cells (MNC) was 4.28 × 108/kg (range, 1.38-30.00 × 108/kg), and the median number of infused CD34+ cells was 3.40 × 106/kg (range, 1.00-19.77 × 106/kg). In allo-HSCT group, median times to neutrophil and platelet engraftment were 13 days (range, 10–22 days) and 19 days (range, 11–41 days), the median number of infused MNC and CD34+ cells were respectively 4.97 × 108/kg (range, 1.89–17.28 × 108/kg)and 3.75 × 106/kg(range, 1.20-15.55 × 106/kg). Out of the 50 patients, 22 developed GVHD, with 20 undergoing Allo-HSCT and 2 undergoing Auto-HSCT. Among them, acute Graft-Versus-Host Disease(aGVHD) occurred in 13 patients, with 12 having Allo-HSCT and 1 having Auto-HSCT. Additionally, Chronic Graft-Versus-Host Disease(cGVHD) was observed in 16 patients, with 15 receiving Allo-HSCT and 1 receiving Auto-HSCT (Table 2).

The 1- and 3-year PFS rates for all T-LBL/ALL patients were 74.0% and 62.0%, respectively. The median follow-up time of the series was 39 months (range, 2-113 months), and the median PFS (mPFS) for all patients was not reached at the time of analysis (Fig. 1A), the 1- and 3- year PFS were 75.0% and 62.5% for the auto-HSCT group, comparing with 73.1% and 61.5% for the allo-HSCT group, and there was no significant difference of PFS between these two groups (Fig. 2A, P = 0.791, HR 1.127, 95%CI 0.456–2.785). A landmark analysis revealed a statistically significant difference between the two groups at 3 years (Figure S2A, P = 0.039, HR 0.426, 95%CI 0.163–1.117). After 3 years, patients who had a PFS for more than 3 years remained free from disease progression and were still alive.

PFS (A) and OS (B) of T-LBL/ALL patients who received auto-HSCT (n = 24) and allo-HSCT(n = 26).

The 1- and 3-year OS rates for all 50 patients were 80% and 70%, respectively, the median OS for all patients was not reached (Fig. 1B). The 1- and 3- year OS rates were 83.3% and 79.2% for the auto-HSCT group, meanwhile, the corresponding figures for the allo-HSCT group were 76.9% and 61.5%, respectively. Although the survival curves for the two groups appeared to be different, comparison of survival curves between the two groups revealed no significant difference (Fig. 2B, P = 0.456, HR 0.685, 95%CI 0.256–1.828). Similarly, through Landmark analysis, the two groups showed a statistically significant difference in 4-year overall survival curves (Figure S2B, P = 0.014, HR 0.317, 95%CI 0.113–0.887), with no deaths occurring in patients who were still alive after 4 years.

By the end of follow-up, 12 patients experienced post-transplantation disease recurrence, 9 of these patients were in the auto-HSCT group and 3 of them in the allo-HSCT group. While for relapsed patients in the auto-HSCT group, 6 patients died because of relapse/progressive disease (one of them received CD7 CAR-T therapy), 1 patients underwent CD7 CAR-T therapy and 2 patients received further chemotherapies and alive now. In the allo-HSCT group, three relapsed patients received chemotherapies. Death occurred in 16 of 50 patients by the end of the study, 6 in the auto-HSCT group (die of disease progress) and 10 in the allo-HSCT group (infection, n = 5; relapse/progressive disease, n = 3; acute GVHD, n = 1; chronic GVHD, n = 1). Although the cumulative incidence of relapse was lower for patients who received allo-HSCT (Fig. 3A, P = 0.033, HR 3.707, 95%CI 1.188–11.570, 2-year relapse 11.5% vs. 37.5% ), the incidence of non-relapse mortality was higher than that in the auto-HSCT group (Fig. 3B, P = 0.014, HR 0.000 95%CI -1.000 - -1.000, 2-year NRM, 23.1% vs. 0%).

Cumulative incidence of relapse (A) and non-relapse mortality (B) of T-LBL/ALL patient who received auto-HSCT (n = 24) and allo-HSCT(n = 26).

Univariable analysis revealed that types of HSCT, Age ≥ 40, Gender, Ann Arbor stage(≥ III/IV), aaIPI score(≥ 2), B symptoms, ECOG performance status(≥2), Mediastinal involvement, Bone marrow involvement, Bone marrow involvement(≥ 20%), CNS involvement, Pleural/pericardial effusion, and Serum LDH were all examined for impact on PFS and OS. The results indicated that B symptoms, ECOG performance status(≥ 2), CNS involvement, and aaIPI score(≥ 2) had statistically significant effects on both PFS and OS. These variables were subsequently included in the multivariable analysis. In multivariable analyses, presentation with B symptoms (P = 0.011, HR 3.862, 95%CI 1.362–10.954)、poor Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG-PS ≥ 2, P = 0.028, HR 3.702, 95%CI 1.154–11.870) and CNS involvement (P<0.001, HR 308.610, 95%CI 13.628-6988.793) were associated with worse OS. Presentation with CNS involvement (P = 0.001, HR 69.233, 95%CI 5.458-878.176) and ECOG-P ≥ at diagnosis (P = 0.022, HR 3.428, 95%CI 1.199–9.802) were risk factors for poor PFS(Table 3).

Discussion

With the introduction of intensive combination chemotherapy regimens in T-LBL/ALL treatment, the patients survival and quality of life has been vastly improved10, but relapse after primary chemotherapy is still a difficult problem, a lot of studies suggest a very similar disease-free survival (DFS) and OS, suggesting that disease relapse continues to remain the leading cause of mortality11, and the only patients surviving were those who successfully underwent allogeneic transplantation12. Based on the study by Zhen13, Yao14 and P W15, the 3-year PFS of patients receiving Auto-HSCT were reported as 60.1%, 73.5% and 66.0%, respectively, with corresponding 3-year OS of 62.8%, 76.3% and 64.3%. These findings are similar to our own results, which showed a 3-year PFS of 62.5% and a 3-year OS of 79.2%. Even allo-HSCT is associated with a prolonged overall survival but can also cause toxicity long after treatment, affecting quality of life, and can lead to high rates of NRM (or treatment related mortality)16. According to Yang’s6、Zhen’s13 study, the 2-year NRM of patients who received allo-HSCT is 28%、27.2%. And in our study, we found the similar results. The 2-year NRM of the allo-HSCT group was significantly higher compared with that of the auto-HSCT group (23.1% vs. 0%).

Besides, GVHD (graft-versus-host disease) also limits the success of allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation17, in our research, 2 patients die of GVHD, and it brings a big challenge for both doctors and patients to optimize the balance between GVHD and GVL (graft-versus-lymphoma/leukemia), for doctors, they should be alert to recognize GVHD lesions early in order to avoid irreversible complications; for patients, they have to take tacrolimus or cyclosporine18 as prophylactic agents for GVHD for a long time and these drugs are expensive.

Considering the higher NRM, expensive medical costs and the side-effects of GVHD in allo-HSCT, an myeloablative therapy followed by auto-HSCT probably be efficient. In our center, we chose TBI based myeloablative treatment for patients with T-LBL/ALL. TBI has traditionally been a part of myeloablative conditioning regimens aimed at eradicating malignant cells, and it offers distinct advantages over chemotherapy for myeloablation19,20.Therefore, TBI based treatment can further eradicate the potential lymphoma cells. And our study also confirmed that there was no statistically significant difference between patients received allo-HSCT or TBI/Cy followed by auto-HSCT in PFS and OS (Fig. 2A&B). Even patients in allo-HSCT group have lower rates of relapse, this did not translate into an OS benefit.

After HSCT, the Auto-HSCT group demonstrated better PFS than the Allo-HSCT group at 3 years (Figure S2A, P = 0.039, HR 0.426, 95%CI 0.163–1.117), however, there was no significant difference between the two groups after 3 years. Similarly, the OS of the former was superior to that of the latter within 4 years (Figure S2B, P = 0.014, HR 0.317, 95%CI 0.113–0.887) with no significant disparity observed beyond this timeframe. The short-term therapeutic effect of Auto-HSCT is more favorable than that of Allo-HSCT. However, the latter exhibits a delayed effect. Nevertheless, there is little difference in the long-term therapeutic effect between the two groups.

TBI is known to be associated with an increased risk of late effects such as gonadal failure, thyroid dysfunction, decreased bone mineral density, impairment of growth, and secondary malignancies21–23, but no intolerable TBI-related toxicity was observed in this study, the most common adverse events (AEs) were nausea, vomiting, and all the patients improved after conservative symptomatic treatment, and no serious complications occurred. Besides, all patients achieved completed hematopoietic reconstitution in the auto-HSCT group suggesting that TBI-induced myelosuppression was well-tolerated. Lower incidence of AEs may be related to the application of fractionated TBI, because most acute toxicities appear to be related to overall TBI dose and dose-rate, fractionation and reduced dose-rate TBI appear to decrease risk of AEs24.

In accordance with previous studies25,26, multivariable Cox regression revealed poor ECOG-PS (≥ 2) at diagnosis was an independent risk factor for impaired OS and PFS, one probable reason for this is that the large tumor burden resulted in poor general condition.

However, there are still some limitations. The longest follow-up time for patients receiving Auto-HSCT is 88 months, which is insufficient to comprehensively evaluate the incidence of tumors after TBI. Further follow-up is still necessary to assess the occurrence of tumors following TBI. Additionally, it is important to consider that HSCT requires a certain economic foundation. Our center is located in Henan Province, which has a less developed economy compared to coastal areas and lower per capita income for residents. These factors also limit the application and development of HSCT. Furthermore, due to the low incidence of T-LBL/ALL、a smaller number of patients undergoing HSCT and the fact that our study is based on single-center retrospective data, it will be necessary to collaborate with multiple centers in future studies to verify the feasibility of our findings.

In conclusion, T-LBL/ALL is a rare and aggressive disease, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation remains the primary treatment but is associated with higher incidence of treatment-related mortality, in that case, TBI/Cy followed by auto-HSCT is a good alternative. In the absence of a prospective, randomized trial to inform decision-making in this area, our data may help make a careful analysis of competing risks on an individual patient basis.

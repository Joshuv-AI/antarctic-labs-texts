PatentOCR: A Benchmark for Fine-Grained Detection and OCR of Component Identifiers in Patent Drawings
Authors: Yueyue Wang, Yihui Qiu, Yingyue Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Remarkable success has been achieved in natural scenes using optical character recognition (OCR), but challenges in technical domains such as patent drawing remain. Patent drawings contain alphanumeric identifiers that represent patent components, which correspond to the text description in the patent document. However, owing to the various sizes, sparse distributions, and disordered backgrounds of these identifiers, general-purpose OCR engines tend to misidentify components as characters and have difficulty distinguishing relevant identifiers from other texts. Currently, fine-grained annotated datasets tailored for identifiers are lacking. In view of this gap, this study presents PatentOCR, a new dataset containing 2,236 patent drawings, with 20,165 pixel-level fine-grained annotations and their associated arrows or guiding lines, which enables the model to better distinguish target characteristics from complex backgrounds. Moreover, this study leveraged one of the uses of the dataset, i.e., character recognition in patent drawings, established a comprehensive research baseline by evaluating the combination of four models, and introduced two evaluation methods for this task. PatentOCR can provide more resources to promote multimodal research in intellectual property.

Background & Summary

Background

In recent years, some studies have focused on the automatic generation of patent documents1,2. However, owing to the scarcity of datasets and the difficulty of data processing, few studies exist in this particular field. At present, some scholars have noted the scarcity of training data in this field and have begun to study and construct relevant datasets3,4. At present, research in this field focuses mostly on pure text data and rarely involves the processing of figures in patent documents. The figures in the patent document contain much information, such as the components in the patent, the positional relationship between the components, and the identifier of the component corresponding to the text description, etc. This information, especially the component identifier in patent images, is used to show the patent more clearly and is important information connected to the descriptive text in the patent. Further research and information extraction from patent drawings can be used to assist in the generation of patent documents.

Alphanumerical identifiers (e.g., 1, 301, and A) are generally used in patent drawings to refer to individual components. These identifiers are used to refer to the component in the descriptive text of the patent document, serving as a critical bridge connecting visual elements with detailed descriptions in the specification and claims. In addition, these identifiers can help examiners and readers to better understand the structure of a patent. Therefore, the automatic extraction of these identifiers is the key to achieving advanced multimodal tasks, such as generating technical descriptions from drawings, patent document review and classification5 and text feature selection6–10 that utilize patent drawings. The key step, i.e., extracting patent image identifier information, is essentially an image text recognition problem involving techniques such as text detection, text recognition and segmentation in images. Currently, image processing-related technologies and models are very mature. A prominent category is the conventional two-stage detection model represented by the region-based convolutional neural network (R-CNN) series (such as Mask R-CNN)11–14, whose aim is to divide the detection task into three steps: region proposal, objective classification and bounding box regression. This approach usually has high detection accuracy. The other approach is based on the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series (including YOLO, YOLOv2–10, etc.15–17), which is an efficient single-stage detection model whose core idea is to directly predict the category, boundary box, and pixel-level mask of the object in instance segmentation tasks through one forwards propagation, without the need for a region proposal step. The YOLO-based approach focuses on directly achieving dense prediction on the basis of the entire image and usually performs well in terms of detection speed. Optical character recognition (OCR) technology18,19 is also very well developed. For example, PaddleOCR18 integrates a differentiable binarization network (DBNet)-based20 text detection module and a text recognition module based on the convolutional recurrent neural network (CRNN)21, providing a set of high-performance pretraining models. EasyOCR22 is widely used because of its light weight, simple Python interface, and ability to support multiple languages. However, existing detection and text recognition models23 are used mainly for text recognition in natural scenes and are not trained in specific domains, such as patent drawings. Some scholars have explored OCR applications in specific fields and achieved preliminary results24. This study focused mainly on the separation and extraction of compound graphics; however, existing studies have focused only on horizontal texts and images. In addition, patent images differ from natural scene images, thus the ability of the model to differentiate between the background and the characters can be increased through the use of obvious lines and colours. Patent components often have irregular shapes drawn with lines, and the text characters are also in the form of lines. Text characters themselves exhibit characteristics such as various degrees of distribution sparsity and differing target sizes. In addition, most of the drawings are composed of black, white and grey. Because the text is embedded in a background of messy mechanical lines, it is easily confused with structural parts. This confusion reduces the ability of the model to distinguish between patent components and text characteristics, increasing the likelihood of misidentifying the structural component as an image characteristic, as shown in Fig. 1. Therefore, the general model needs to be fine-tuned and trained according to the image characteristics in this field. The lack of training data in this field has, to some extent, made it difficult for the information contained in patent images to be fully utilized.Fig. 1Left: a dense small object; right: a sparse large object.

Currently, few databases in the patent field exist. A high-quality benchmark dataset, Pap2 Pat, was introduced by Knappich et al.3. This dataset consists of 1.8k patent–paper pairs. Long patent generation tasks are decomposed into multiple manageable modules, which is an important foundation for long text generation. In addition, Bai and Zilong et al. proposed a large-scale benchmark dataset4, PatentDESC-355k, which includes the data of more than 240 billion tokens for training large language models. These models can be used for patent specification drafting, claim supplementation, patent classification, and patent abstract generation. The first benchmark, PatentBench, which comprehensively covers tasks in the intellectual property (IP) field, has also been proposed. However, these two patent datasets are both pure text types and do not involve patent image processing or image information extraction. In terms of patent image datasets, the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) held a competition in 2015 to develop algorithms for detecting number and component identifier in patent drawings. A small annotated dataset of 306 patent drawings was publicly released in conjunction with the competion25. Previously, images were mostly processed using the manual feature method, whereas current methods utilize deep learning models for image feature extraction. However, the limited set of 306 images is not sufficient for models to fully learn the image features. The DeepPatent26 and DeepPatent227 methods for large datasets proposed by Kucer et al. and Ajayi et al. lack detailed OCR processing steps and fine-grained OCR annotation. Other researchers have conducted related studies and have constructed datasets28,29, however, these datasets are not available to the public. In addition, whether the accuracy of OCR is calculated on the basis of all the true annotated texts or the texts of the regions successfully detected by the detection model is unclear, thus a comprehensive evaluation of the performance of the overall detection + recognition system is needed. Therefore, foundational public datasets and evaluation benchmarks are still lacking in research on character recognition in patent drawings.

Methods

The data used in this study were retrieved from patents published by the State Intellectual Property Office of the People’s Republic of China. These valid invention patents are included in the robotics category, and all the figures in each invention patent were collected. The collection method involved downloading patent documents in batches from the official website. As the downloaded patent documents contained both images and text and the images were embedded in patent documents in portable document format (PDF), manual screenshot extraction was required to preserve all the patent drawings for subsequent manual annotation work.

The PatentOCR dataset proposed in this study was manually labelled using the Label Studio tool. Label Studio is an open-source data labelling tool that supports the labelling of various data types, such as images, text, audio and video. There are three types of labels. The first category is the component identifier, which refers to the component identifier that represents a certain patent component in the patent drawing, containing single-type characters or mixed characters of numbers/letters/Chinese characters, such as “301”, “A”, or “3a”. The dataset is expected to provide pixel-level fine-grained identifier annotations so that PatentOCR can be used for model training to better distinguish between object identifiers and nontarget symbols or parts easily misidentified as characters during model training. We adopted a strategy of co-annotating identifiers and their associated pointing lines or arrows. Two types of markings, rectangular boxes and polygonal boxes, were used to ensure that there is no noise in the surrounding area. The identifiers with clear arrows or guiding lines and regular shapes are marked with rectangular boxes, and those with large noise in the surrounding area, unclear arrows or guiding lines, or irregular shapes were marked with polygonal boxes (as shown in the the pink box area in Fig. 2). The second type is the figure number, which represents the number corresponding to the current figure in the group of patents, such as “Fig. 1”. As the shape of this label category is regular, a rectangular box was used for labelling (refer to the green box in Fig. 2). The third category is the patent number, which represents the number of patent documents to which the current image belongs. A blank rectangular box was used to store the patent number information next to the reference number for the image. This label category was used only for patent grouping in the data preprocessing stage. The patent number is used for internal data management and is not part of the baseline task (refer to the purple box in Fig. 2 for an example).Fig. 2Example of labelling.

The annotation process was reviewed by manual checking and code verification to ensure the quality of the annotation. In the manual labelling process, we preliminarily determined whether the labels were incorrect, missing or obviously exceeded the image range. However, because pixel-level labelling is very accurate, there may be cases where a small number of labels slightly exceeded the image range but were not noticed. After manual labelling, the annotation file was exported, and the data preprocessing code was used in Python to examine the validity of the labelling method to prevent overflow images from being labelled or deemed invalid. After data preprocessing was performed to verify the validity of the labelling method used in the original JavaScript Object Notification (JSON) file exported by Label Studio, the identifier/drawing number/patent number and the corresponding pixel coordinates were extracted, and all the images were grouped according to the patent number. All the images in one patent document were in one group, and a structured component list and category mapping file were generated. The mapping category was divided into three categories: component identifier, figure number, and background.

It should also be noted that a direct empirical comparison by training our baselines on the historical USPTO 2015 dataset is methodologically and technically infeasible. According to the dataset specifications about USPTO 2015, it relies exclusively on tight rectangular bounding boxes for standalone part labels and contains zero annotation primitives for arrows or guiding lines. This annotation-scheme incompatibility makes it impossible to train the polygon-based instance segmentation models (such as YOLOv11m-seg and Mask R-CNN) required by PatentOCR to resolve unified ‘identifier-guiding line’ spatial topologies. Furthermore, USPTO 2015 is constrained by a severe data scale bottleneck for modern deep learning, spanning only 306 total drawing pages (with only 93 pages for the final evaluation). Training data-intensive deep architectures on such a restricted volume would induce severe overfitting and volatile experimental artifacts rather than a representative baseline. Consequently, PatentOCR establishes a distinct, middle-scale (2,236 drawings) training paradigm that addresses these foundational structural and volume limitations of earlier resources.

Limitations and scope

We acknowledge that the current version of PatentOCR is primarily focused on patent drawings from the robotics field, its performance in other technical fields (such as chemistry, electronics, and software) has not yet been tested, and the dataset will need to be expanded in the future. Given the high professional expertise and time-intensive nature required for our pixel-level co-annotation strategy (incorporating both identifiers and their corresponding guiding lines), this study serves as a pilot study to validate the efficacy of this novel annotation paradigm. Due to the absence of established external benchmarks for fine-grained OCR of patent identifiers, the evaluation is currently restricted to the in-domain test split of PatentOCR. In future iterations, we intend to extend this high-quality annotation framework to other technical fields, such as chemistry and computer science, to further enhance and evaluate the cross-domain robustness of the dataset. In addition, there are few Chinese characters samples (accounting for only 0.07% of the total), the Chinese characters examples provided in this document are for illustrative purposes only and are insufficient for quantitative evaluation. Future versions will expand the collection of Chinese characters examples.

Technical Validation

This section provides and describes a patent drawing character detection and recognition task for this dataset and a universal baseline model framework to verify the data quality and effectiveness of PatentOCR. The overall framework of the experiment can be divided into six parts: Detection model training and fine-tuning, OCR engine, Results evaluation, Baseline model comparison experiments, Ablation experiment, and Analysis of Failure Cases.

Detection model training and fine-tuning

In this experiment, a two-stage pipeline of detection + identification was used, and the segmented version of the one-stage YOLOv8 model (YOLOv8m-seg) and the two-stage MASK R-CNN model were used as the representative detection models for this task, and both models were trained using PatentOCR. The experimental hardware is an A100 40 G * 1 graphics card, and the experimental environment is Python3.12.3 + PyTorch2.7.1 + CUDA12.9. The experimental inputs were all 1024 × 1024 images. The basic data enhancement strategy and SGD optimizer were used. The dataset was divided into a training set/validation set/test set (60/20/20).

Regarding hyperparameter configurations, for YOLOv8m-seg, the iou was set to 0.7, the initial learning rate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${lr}_{0}$$\end{document}lr0 was set to 0.01, with a momentum of 0.937 and a weight decay of 0.0005. This was used in conjunction with a cosine annealing schedule and a 3-epoch warmup. OCR engine

In this experiment, PaddleOCR and EasyOCR were used as the representative recognition models for this task. PaddleOCR and EasyOCR were paired with the aforementioned two detection models that were trained on PatentOCR to test the final recognition results. In the recognition step, the official pretraining model provided by PyTorch was directly used without pre-fine-tuning on the PatentOCR dataset, and the optional mask expansion operation, Gaussian blurring, sharpening, adaptive threshold and morphological opening operation strategies were used for OCR preprocessing. On this basis, the zero-shot cross-domain text recognition performance of the two OCR engines can be tested using the evaluation method based on the area of successful detection proposed in this study.

The main experiment tested the recognition effect of the model combination trained on PatentOCR. The recognition effect of the combination of models that were not trained on PatentOCR was tested in the comparison experiment.

Results evaluation

On the basis of the problems and tasks proposed in this study, as well as the conventional indicators in the target detection and instance segmentation fields, mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.50-0.95 were selected as the evaluation indicators for the detection model. Moreover, to comprehensively consider the improvement in the model performance resulting from the dataset provided in this study, two sets of evaluation protocols were used to measure the overall recognition effect. The first set of indicators was used to calculate the character-level accuracy and perfect matching rate of OCR on the basis of the region of successful detection. The second set of indicators was used to calculate the character-level accuracy and perfect matching rate of OCR on the basis of all the real annotated texts. The character-level accuracy rates were all based on the Levenshtein distance, and the full matching rates were all calculated on the basis of the character-by-character alignment standard. The first set of indicators was used to evaluate the recognition performance of the OCR engine on the effective output region based on the detection model, and the second set was used to evaluate the end-to-end performance of the overall detection + recognition method. This finding indicates that the real value of the proposed PatentOCR method lies in its ability to accurately locate the core elements (component identifiers) in patent drawings and achieve high-quality recognition. The overall bottleneck of the current system lies in the recall rate during the detection stage, i.e., missed detections. Therefore, the detection of part identifiers in patent drawings is a very challenging task because the targets vary in size and are highly similar to the background of complex lines, which further highlights the pixel-level fine granularity provided in this study that contains lead lines. The effectiveness of the labelling strategy provides crucial contextual information for the model to learn to distinguish the target from the background.

Ablation experiment

To quantitatively evaluate the contribution of our proposed “identifier-guiding line” co-annotation strategy, we constructed a controlled ablation subset consisting of 523 randomly selected images. Two annotation paradigms were compared: (1) Baseline Annotation, where only the identifiers themselves were labeled with bounding boxes; and (2) Our Proposed Strategy, which incorporates both identifiers and their associated guiding lines or arrows. To ensure a rigorous and fair comparison, both paradigms were trained using identical data splits (with consistent sample assignments across training, validation, and test sets) and hyperparameter configurations on Mask R-CNN and YOLOv8m-seg models.

All input images were resized to 640 × 640 pixels, and the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer was employed. As presented in Table 9, the ablation study results provide strong evidence for the efficacy of the proposed annotation strategy. When trained on the PatentOCR subset incorporating arrows and guiding lines, the YOLOv8m-seg model exhibited significant improvements in both mAP50-95 (Mask) and mAP50-95 (Box) compared to the baseline. This indicates that the proposed annotation paradigm significantly enhances the YOLOv8m-seg model’s ability to localize target objects in complex patent backgrounds, highlighting the critical role of spatial context in improving detection recall and initial localization accuracy.

However, Table 9 also reveals that the guiding-line co-annotation strategy interacts distinctively with different detector architectures. While YOLOv8m-seg exhibits uniform improvements across all thresholds, Mask R-CNN shows a divergent behaviour where it regresses slightly on mAP50-95 but improves substantially on mAP50 (+3.65% Mask and +6.25% BBox).

This architecture-dependent divergence suggests that the proposed annotation paradigm interacts closely with the inductive biases of the networks. One-stage architectures like YOLO benefit directly from the holistic spatial context offered by guiding lines. In contrast, for two-stage networks like Mask R-CNN, the inclusion of elongated guiding lines expands the Region of Interest (RoI) bounds. On a restricted data scale (523 images), this expansion introduces more background pixels into the RoI pooling layer, slightly diluting the dense semantic features of the alphanumeric characters and affecting tight localization (mAP50-95), despite significantly boosting the baseline target recall (mAP50). This suggests that the benefits of the guiding-line paradigm are modulated by the regional pooling mechanisms of different detector architectures.

Usage Notes

The PatentOCR dataset serves as a foundational resource for multimodal patent understanding. Its pixel-level fine-grained annotations and lead line information facilitate high-quality alignment between graphical and textual elements. Designed for the identification of component identifiers in patent drawings, this dataset is suitable for the following potential application scenarios:

Multimodal patent interpretation and image-to-text tasks

By aligning image features (identifier locations and lead line relationships) with textual features (patent and figure numbers), researchers can develop patent Image-to-Text models. The spatial coordinates of detected identifiers and their recognized textual information can assist in generating patent descriptions with high positional accuracy and textual consistency, such as the automated generation of detailed descriptions. This represents a significant research direction currently being explored in the field.

Technical component relationship mining

Based on the identifiers and lead information, methodologies such as Knowledge Graphs or Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) can be utilized to explore the topological relationships between components in patent drawings, providing a data-driven foundation for technical analysis or innovation mining.

Patent image–text alignment and retrieval

The dataset can be employed to develop image–text matching algorithms, enhancing the association between patent drawings and their corresponding textual content to support technical document retrieval or patent comparison tasks.

Practical advice for use

To facilitate reuse by researchers, the PatentOCR dataset and its associated preprocessing codes are provided free of charge via a public repository (GitHub). The data files include formats such as JSON, Common Objects in Context (COCO), and YOLO, alongside original images in Portable Network Graphics (PNG) format. The preprocessing toolkit provides functions for annotation validity verification, statistical analysis, and image grouping. Users are encouraged to adjust parameters according to specific task requirements and conduct experiments in combination with mainstream detection models or OCR engines. Further usage details are provided in the repository’s README file.

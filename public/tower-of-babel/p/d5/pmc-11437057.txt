Molecular nano-I-beam class of materials: options based on configuration, first principles-based optimization and properties
Authors: Salah A. M. Elmoselhy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Nanotubes showed merits including high structural strength-to-weight ratio. However, tubes are less favored regarding stiffness and strength. Nano-I-beams are proposed for improved nano-mechanics. Computationally, the study proposes novel molecular designs of I-beam-like shaped structures. A conformation analysis, molecular dynamics and first principles-based optimization are presented. The study proposes options based on the configuration of the molecular nano-I-beam structure providing less number of planes of symmetry and hence more stability than nanotube-like structures. These designs feature a unique geometrical differentiator of having the walls of the out-of-plane hexagonal motif-based molecular nano-I-beam (C60H46) inclined with different inclination angles enabling promising properties. The stability of the proposed nano-I-beam is proved on par with the corresponding nanotube-like structure. First principles-based evidence is provided on the comparable polarizability and the comparable ability to store energy of the supercell of the crystalline slab nano-I-beam in comparison with the corresponding nanotube. A proposed hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic molecular nano-I-beam (C24H12) remedies the nano-buckling observed in the alike square-octagonal nanostructure. The molecular nano-I-beam exhibits intrinsic switchability that enables the nano-I-beam to be a topological semiconductor/insulator. The results show promising electronic and elastic properties of the proposed nano-I-beams that suit several applications such as their use in capacitors, transistors, insulators, batteries, quantization-based nano-devices, solid lubricant additive to grease, toughening fibers of nanocomposites, hydrophobic films, emissions adsorbents, catalytic sensors, PAH materials for space, and sustainable energy. The molecular nano-I-beam provides the base of the corresponding 2-D crystalline slab nano-I-beams.

Iijima innovated and coined the terms of multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWNTs) in 1991 and single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWNTs) in 19932,3. Since then the interest in nanotubes has grown significantly3. The nanotube-like primitive structure has an infinite number of planes of symmetry. Thus, its stability is vulnerable to any perturbation of such total symmetry. This is partly why it is realistically infeasible to fabricate a stable single-walled nanotube of diameter > 4 nm, since as the structural diameter gets larger, the number of atoms that have to satisfy the total symmetry requirements gets larger. This in turn renders the stability of the nanotube vulnerable. In addition, the electric and magnetic intrinsic and/or external perturbation fields transform the symmetric planar mode shapes of the vibrations of particles into non-symmetric spatial modes10. This partial break of symmetry significantly reduces the ability of the nanotube-like structure to conduct electricity and heat, because of the vulnerability of the nanotube due to having infinite number of planes of symmetry. The remedy that was proposed for such drawbacks of nanotubes was to have multi-walled nanotubes. However, actually having a multi-walled structure of nanotube further adds to the total symmetry requirements, by needing to have all the concentric nanotubes perfectly concentric and aligned with each other, based on the weak attraction of van der Waals force. That is realistically not an efficiently feasible remedy, since such concentricity depends on the van der Waals’ weak force, which is vulnerable to the perturbation from the Coulombic attraction force field among nuclei. This is partly why multi-walled nanotubes are prone to fabrication defects that significantly and drastically downgrade the quality and properties of the multi-walled nanotube in comparison with the corresponding single-walled nanotube.

This is in accord with the fact that the nanotubes with diameters > 4 nm suffer from the weakness of flattening, because of the low radial stiffness of the nanotube5. In addition, the idea of having multi-walls for remedying such drawback of the nanotube, results in providing less conductivity than the single-walled nanotube. Moreover, the number of structural shells is the predominant parameter influencing the specific surface area (SSA) of multi-walled nanotube. Each structural shell addition does not provoke a significant increase of the surface area of the nanotube, but rather causes a much larger increase of the structural weight11. However, even the idea of having multi-walls can be used in the nano-I-beam structure as well, with the merit that the nano-I-beam structure does not dictate the demand for satisfying a large number of symmetry planes.

From the mechanics of materials perspective, the tube geometrical cross-section is less favored for providing high structural stiffness and strength12. Thus, the innovation of "nano-I-beam" has been proposed in 2019 for improved nano-mechanics4. This innovation has shown the merit of resulting in improved mechanics of material, as demonstrated in that paper. The nano-I-beam innovation has been proven thereafter the optimal geometry in nano-structures with improved strength and stiffness in comparison with nanotubes. In fact, in 2021 it has been computationally proven that the nano-I-beam lattice unit cell is the optimal geometry in nano-structures13. This implies improved mechanics of materials based on this innovative nano-structure, with several potential applications.Geometrically,the moment of inertia of the nano-structure is inversely proportional to the rotational energy, which is an important part of Gibbs free energy14. Hence, for the same angular momentum, the higher moment of inertia of the nano-I-beam enables to have a lower rotational energy and thus a relatively more stable nano-structure than the corresponding nanotube. Hence, this proposed innovation of the nano-I-beam holds the potential to improve quantum and nano-mechanics. In addition, in the 1-D arrangement of atoms such as nanowire and nanotube-like primitive structure, every atom interacts through only one pair of potentials and is more constrained than in the case of 2-D nano-structure. In the case of the 2-D arrangement of atoms such as the molecular nano-I-beam, every atom can interact through several pairs of potentials and is less constrained. Thus, the 2-D arrangement of atoms of the molecular nano-I-beam can enable its electrons to be more energetic than the corresponding 1-D nano-structure.

Amongst the motivations to conduct the present study is to investigate the promising potential of the proposed molecular nano-I-beam as a polyaromatic hydrocarbonous (PAH) material for space applications. This is a current hot topic of research, as proven in several papers such as16. The structural stability and robustness of the nano-I-beam can enable this proposed class of materials to have promising applications. For instance, the nano-I-beam can exhibit promising characteristics in surface physics comparable to Graphene.

Molecular dynamics has been proven a helpful tool for investigating the structural stability of nanostructures. In fact, the result of comparison of the stability of two nanostructures in Molecular Dynamics is unlikely to contradict the result of comparison of the stability of them in the corresponding Kohn–Sham theory-based quantum computations. Actually the molecular dynamics framework, such as in LAMMPS and Avogadro software, is used in several publications for providing evidence on the stability of the structure. Chawla et al., investigated computationally, using molecular dynamics, the structural optimization of polymeric Dendrimer17. Tang et al., studied using molecular dynamics a designable extracellular matrix and engineered proteins18. In addition, Gurina et al., investigated the molecular adsorption of Poly(N-vinyl-2-pyrrolidone) on cellulose nano-crystals surfaces using Molecular Dynamics simulations19. Moreover, Zhang et al., studied experimentally and using Molecular Dynamics the adhesion mechanism of polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) fiber in alkali-activated slag/fly ash20. The effectiveness of Molecular Dynamics is proven in determining the appropriateness of and analyzing the nanostructures utilizing force fields in simulation21. In fact, molecular dynamics has been proven efficient in determining the mechanical behavior and properties of atomic structures such as nanotubes22. Furthermore, several research groups adopted to present the results of the Molecular Dynamics simulation of the nano-structure in terms of screenshots17,19,23. Also, several research groups adopted to present the results of the First Principles-based computational results of the HOMO–LUMO of the nano-structure in terms of screenshots24.

The density functional theory (DFT) and ab-initio modeling have been proven helpful for investigating the structural stability of nanostructures25. he credibility and fidelity of the Ab-initio computational modeling are already proven and well-established in the scientific literature for correctly predicting the real behavior of the proposed structures. Actually the governing equation of condensed matter physics of a molecule and bulk material, for revealing the physical and chemical properties of the system, has been formulated by Dirac since 1928. The exact solution of this equation is an ultimate goal in condensed matter physics. The computational cost of its numerically exact solution is infeasibly huge due to the exponential scale O(ene), where ne is the number of electrons in the system. Thus, the DFT has been proposed in 1965 by Khon and Sham for striking a balance between computational cost and computational accuracy. Kolandaivel et al., investigated using ab-initio and DFT the stability of aliphatic aldoxime molecules26. Amiri et al., studied the structural stability, electronic and optical properties of X2GeSe (X = P and Sb) monolayers using ab-initio modeling27. Salcedo Rodrígueza et al., investigated computationally the stability and the structural, electronic and magnetic properties of the (001) ZnFe2O4 surface terminations using the ab-initio approach28. The DFT has been proven efficient as a proof on structural stability based on geometry optimization and as a proof on the dynamic stability of the novel structures based on the absence of imaginary frequencies confirms29. One should note, however, that DFT is not without its own drawbacks, most notoriously the underestimation of the HOMO–LUMO gaps.

The present study proposes hetero-nuclear polyatomic molecules of hydrocarbons based on the I-beam-like shaped nano-structure. The present paper therefore presents: (i) geometrical options based on the configuration of the molecular nano-I-beam class of materials; (ii) computational comparison of the proposed molecular nano-I-beam with the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure with respect to: conformation analysis, optimized molecular ground state energy, Mayer bond order, molecular dynamics, and spectroscopic analysis that is used for the spectroscopic unique identification of nanostructures; (iii) the supercell-like basis of the periodic crystalline slab nano-structure based on the hybrid cubic-hexagonal motif of the molecular nano-I-beam (C64) and its corresponding Carbon nanotube of 8 Å diameter and (6,6) chiral indices (C60); (iv) comparison of the static electrical polarizability tensor of the supercell-like basis of the crystalline slab carbon nano-I-beam and the corresponding carbon nanotube. The Infrared (IR), Raman, and UV-Vis (TD-DFT) spectra are adopted in order to distinguishingly characterize the proposed molecules. If two molecules have the same functional groups, such as C-H and/or C-C, then these molecules would expectedly have similar peaks on the IR/Raman spectra.

Actually the Mayer bond order indicates the viability of the existence of atomic bonds by having the expected value of the bond order to be around 1 or higher31. The comparison with the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure makes sense in light of the fact that nanotube-like primitive structures actually exist, such as: (I) the CycloParaPhenylene (C6H4)8 which is similar to the nanotube-like primitive structure of 11Å diameter and 8 × 8 chiral indices, (II) the (16) Annulene (C16H16) which is similar to the nanotube-like primitive structure of 6Å diameter and 4 × 4 chiral indices.

The nanotube-like primitive structure adopted in the present paper is based on the nanotubes primitive structure proposed in the nanotubes literature32,33. This nanotube-like primitive structure resembles the basic layer of atoms that resembles the shape of the nanotube and that is periodically repeated sequentially for developing structurally the evolved shape of the corresponding nanotube. The reason for naming the proposed structure “nano-I-beam” is that it resembles the capital I letter (or I letter), which is a reason that is analogously similar to the way of naming “nanotubes” that resembles the shape of a tube. The comparison of the proposed molecular nano-I-beam with the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure is conducted such that the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure exhibits the same planar area and comparable number of atoms. The Molecular Dynamics comparison is based on: (1) the same molecular dynamic load and boundary conditions, (2) the same duration of time of molecular dynamics simulation, (3) being within the same confined in-plane projected area of the molecule. It is basically infeasible realistically to have the same number of atoms of the molecular nano-I-beam in the corresponding nanotube of standard configuration and of the same confined molecular area.

Results and discussions

Geometrical options based on the configuration of molecular nano-I-beam class of materials

The molecular nano-I-beam class of materials can be grown to make several options based on configuration. The molecular nano-I-beam can be grown based on a hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif, hybrid cubic-hexagonal motif, or topologically out-of-plane hexagonal motif. These configuration-based options are investigated in this section.

Hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif-based molecular nano-I-beams

A nano-I-beam structure can be envisaged as a stacking of molecules such as that presented in Fig. 1 that would be considered as the molecular motif of such proposed structure. This molecule has a two six-membered rings, two eight-membered rings and two four-membered cubane-like carbon rings, caped with twelve hydrogen atoms. The molecular formula of this hydrocarbon molecule is C24H12, an isomer of coronene, and our calculations prove that the proposed configuration of atoms for this molecule is stable. The geometry of this molecule resembles an I-like shaped structure of equal flanges, similar to the capital I letter in the Ariel font style. We shall name this prototype molecule as the hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif of the nano-I-beam. The molecular largest dimension is ~ 7 Å, and the structure is strictly planar (point symmetry D2h), according to the accurate DFT geometry optimization that we have performed, starting from an initial geometry obtained from a crude optimization using force fields (molecular dynamics). The molecule is, in fact, a resonance hybrid, that accounts for the planarity of the molecule, so the single and double bonds shown in Fig. 1 are to be considered as formal bonds. With the proposed hybridization of the octagonal nanostructure, hexagonal nanostructure, and square nanostructure in the molecular nano-I-beam, it has been now meritoriously possible to slash the structural buckling that usually is inconveniently encountered in the square-octagonal nanostructure34. In addition, the Mayer Bond Order analysis for the Carbon-based skeleton of the molecular nano-I-beam (C24H12) and for the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure of 7 Å diameter and 5 × 5 chiral indices shows that the expected value is 1.19 and 1.43, respectively. Hence, the expected value of the Mayer Bond Order satisfies the criterion of being 1 or larger than 1, which indicates the existence of atomic bonds and thus the bonding in these molecular structures.

Topologically out-of-plane hexagonal motif-based molecular nano-I-beams

Larger polycyclic I-shaped hydrocarbon molecules can be formed that are no longer planar but bent. Figure 7A, B illustrates one of such larger molecules that is based on a bisanthracene core (C28H18) extended out of the plane with two conjugated C16H16 half-rings in cis-cis configuration resulting in the large C60H46 molecule. This proposed molecular structure has an I-like shape of equal flanges, resembling the capital I letter in the Times New Roman font style. The molecule has C2V symmetry, the principal axis of symmetry of this point group pointing perpendicular to the center of the plane of the molecular nano-I-beam. The out-of-plane topology of this hexagonal motif-based molecular nano-I-beam includes “wings” of a sizeable inclination angle along the longitudinal axis of the molecule. The ground state energy of the C64 nanostructure is -2436.156 Ha, whereas that of the C60 nanotube of 8 Å diameter and (6,6) chiral indices is -2284.604 Ha, the difference is due to the different geometrical configurations of these two nanostructures and due to the compactness and more dense structure of the nano-I-beam to accommodate atoms within the same confined molecular area. In addition, whilst exhibiting more compactness and more dense structure, the nano-I-beam C64 exhibits the same ground state energy as those of the C64 fullerene-like isomers.

The additional interesting things to compare with the nanotube include: (1) the nanotube exhibits conjugated bonds, whereas the nano-I-beam exhibits single and double bonds in a spatially symmetric fashion; (2) the Mayer bond order in the nano-I-beam with the maximum value of 1.5 and average value of 1 is on par with the corresponding nanotube with the maximum value of 2.49 and average value of 1.4; (3) the nanotube has an open core, whereas the nano-I-beam features a closed core that may turn out to be relevant to the properties; (4) the flexibility in structuring 2-D slab crystalline structure out of the periodicity of the supercell-like basis of the crystalline slab hybrid cubic-hexagonal motif of the molecular nano-I-beam.

These interesting features of the nano-I-beam can thus enable it to exhibit unusual electronic, thermal and elastic properties that do not showcase in the traditional Graphene. The carbon core of the molecular nano-I-beam C32H44 is the building block of the corresponding hybrid cubic-hexagonal motif of the molecular nano-I-beam C64.

The correspondence between C32H44 and C64is upheld by the postulates of the Molecular Group Theory. The Molecular Group Theory postulates that: if the arrangement of atoms in a molecule is in a group that features specific characteristics, then the associativity and multiplicity of this arrangement of atoms (such as repetitive periodicity in the out-of-plane dimension) is also in this group that features these specific characteristics.

This indicated stability of the supercell of the crystalline slab nano-I-beam (C64) indicates the viability of the proposed crystalline slab structure to be chemically stable and thus to be synthesized. The synthesis of the supercell of the crystalline slab of the nano-I-beam (C64) can thus be done based on the bottom-up nanofabrication approach either using: Epitaxy, Focused Ion Beam (FIB), Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD), or Combined FIB & CVD. Actually the recent experimental proof of obtaining straight single wall carbon nanotubes is in support of the feasibility of experimentally realizing the flatness of the hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif-based molecular nano-I-beam (C24H12)40. The synthesis of the proposed nano-I-beam is currently in progress and is going to be reported in an upcoming paper expectedly in the near future.

Similarly, the meritorious crystalline 1-D structure of the hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif-based molecular nano-I-beam (C24H12) that can be periodically extended in-plane in either x-direction (horizontally) or y-direction (vertically), can also be synthesized. The Ab-initio computational results indicate the viability of synthesis of such proposed nano-structures.

In contrast to a conductor that allows charge to flow through its structure, polarization is the distorsion in the electrons cloud of a neutral object such as an insulator/semiconductor. This distorsion in the electrons cloud results in redistribution of the negative charge that causes an overall positive charge in the farthest part of the object. The polarizability of a molecule (α) is defined as the ratio of the dipole moment to the electric field41. The dipolar polarization is categorized as intrinsic polarization into a polar molecule (i.e. orientation polarization), and induced polarization into the molecule42. The orientation polarization results from a permanent dipole that is due to the geometrical configuration of the molecule and is thus retained in the absence of an external electric field. The polarizability of the nanostructure (α) is analyzed herein based on the First Principles. Semiconductors and insulators are the scope of this section.

Summary and conclusions

In summary, we have presented a few novel I-shaped carbon-based molecules that could, in principle, be extended in one or to dimensions to form novel 1D or 2D nanostructures. In-silico DFT studies of these I-shaped molecules have shown that they are structurally stable. For each of them the ground state geometry was optimized, and the spectrum of vibrational frequencies was calculated to ensure that all modes have positive frequencies, thus ensuring that the optimized ground state geometry corresponds to an energy minimum with respect to all atomic coordinates. The IR and raman spectra of the isolated molecules (in vacuum) were calculated and compared to those of nanotube slabs with similar molecular formula. The proposed I-shaped molecules may exist in nature in hydrocarbon mixtures similar to many other polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. Also, there is currently much interest in the search for PHAs in outer space via spectroscopic methods, so that these calculated spectra are useful to identify these species in searches for organic matter in outer space. In addition, TDDFT calculations of the UV–Vis spectra have also been performed for all proposed new species, and compared with those of nanotube slabs with comparable chemical composition.

The nano-I-beam molecules, in addition to their proven robust structural stability, have some distinctive features. The PAH C24H12 I-beam shaped molecule with three squarene moieties, one at the core, and two at the molecule extremities, has a perfectly planar geometry and therefore is resilient robust to nano-buckling in contrast with the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure of 7 Å diameter and (5,5) chirality, as indicated in Figs. 1 and 2. This planarity is induced by the aromatic character of the 8-membered and 6-membered carbon rings, thus eradicating the structural buckling that is inconveniently encountered when the molecule features non-aromatic rings. Also, in comparison with the nanotube-like primitive structure, the molecular I-beam holds its shape in molecular dynamics, whereas the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure, as indicated in Fig. 2, is a less resilient molecule, as it easily distorts from the lowest energy geometric configuration. Even in this lowest energy configuration, the nanotube-like primitive structure of 7 Å diameter and (5,5) chirality is not perfectly circular but has an elongated shape. Actually the distorsion of the carbon nanotubes in Molecular Dynamics simulation has been indicated before in the scientific literature45.

The C24H12 I-shaped molecule and the corresponding nanotube structure have similar IR spectra, with most prominent peaks corresponding to the C-H bending vibration modes. Also, the C24H12 I-shaped molecule and the corresponding nanotube structure have similar raman spectra, with most prominent peaks corresponding, in this case, to the C–C stretching vibration modes. However, a few details of the spectra enable to distinguish between them, most notably the I-shaped molecule has its most intense IR absorption peak at 900.50 cm−1, whereas for the nanotube-like primitive structure the most intense absorption peak is at 669.25 cm−1. In addition, the raman spectrum of the nanotube has some small intensity bands not found for the I-shaped molecule, and also the largest intensity peak has a prominent shoulder absent in the I-shaped molecule spectrum.

As indicated in Fig. 4E, F on the UV–Vis (TD-DFT) spectra, both of the C24H12 I-shaped molecule and the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure of 7 Å diameter and (5,5) chirality have the same number of peaks at similar positions in the absorption band. These peaks in conjugated double bond systems correspond to π–π* and n–π* transitions, and they usually occur in the ultraviolet range of the spectrum. For C24H12 the calculated absorption maximum is found at a wavelength of 271.3 nm, which is comparable to that of the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure.

The wider HOMO–LUMO gap of the C24H12 I-shaped molecule indicates a stronger dielectric characteristic and is in accord with the shorter wavelength of the main absorption peak in the UV–Vis (TD-DFT) spectrum in comparison with the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure. The wider gap of C24H12 I-shaped molecule hence may be of relevance to optoelectronic applications. Also, the higher intrinsic polarizability of the C24H12 I-shaped molecule, compared to the corresponding nanotube-like primitive structure, may be of relevance to capacitance-based applications for energy storage using such molecules as dieletric media.

Furthermore, the hybrid octa-hexagonal-cubic motif-based molecular nano-I-beam (C24H12) features the exceptional merit of its crystalline 1-D structure that can be periodically extended in-plane in either x-direction (horizontally) or y-direction (vertically), as shown in Fig. 6. This exceptional merit of its crystalline 1-D structure does not exist in nanotubes and may result in uniquely different transport and physical properties.

The molecular nano-I-beam and the supercell-like base of the 2-D crystalline slab carbon nano-I-beam exhibit promising features, such as comparable polarizability and energy storage capacity with the corresponding carbon nanotube, as indicated in section “1.2”. The reported results of their computational modeling herein indicates that the nano-I-beam may have several industrial applications. These applications include: capacitors for electronic switching, quantization-based nano-devices and sensors, solid lubricant additive to grease, toughening fibers for structural nanocomposites which can enable longer service life of components whilst featuring lighter weight, hydrophobic surface films, emissions adsorbents and detection, catalytic sensors, and sustainable energy applications. Actually the use of carbon-based atomic structures such as carbon nanotubes as toughening fibers has been proven efficient in improving structural properties22, which is a merit that is expected to be even boosted by the more efficient alternative “nano-I-beams”, Because of the high surface area and the relatively nonpolar nature of the proposed hydrocarbons-based nano-I-beam molecules, this molecular nano-I-beam can be used as an adsorbent or a catalyst for exhaust emissions in the automotive applications such as marine engines and in thermo-chemical applications such as industrial boilers and gas turbines. The use of the nano-I-beam as a sensor, if functionalized with a metal providing unpaired electrons, is promising in light of the practicality of using a large molecule as an implantable sensor for active monitoring of Electron Paramagnetic Resonance (EPR) spectroscopy46. Also, the electronic properties of the molecular nano-I-beam enables it to be used as a functional large molecule in nano-electronics, similar to the use of recently developed functional large molecules of total size of 5nm or less as transistors in a chip47.

Materials and methods

The proposed molecular nanostructures in the present study are hetero-nuclear polyatomic hydrocarbons-based nano-I-beam molecular structures. The proposed molecular nano-I-beam structures are compared with the corresponding hetero-nuclear polyatomic hydrocarbons-based nanotube-like primitive structures. The proposed supercell-like basis of the 2-D crystalline slab nano-I-beam is a polyatomic carbonous nanostructure.

The freely available Avogadro software was used for structural optimizations and conformer analysis of the initial structures using molecular dynamics based on a force field.For such calculations, that also aimed at examining the dynamic stability of the final structures, the universal force field (UFF) at the temperature of 600 K was used. The UFF is a full periodic table force-field that can be used to evaluate single point energies, geometry optimizations, calculate frequencies, and perform transition state searches on Molecular Dynamics calculations with either a monolayer chain of atoms, slabs or in the bulk48.

In addition,first-principles-based Molecular Dynamics has also been conducted using the Quantum Espresso 6.5 software49. The simulation temperature of 600 K has also been chosen for examining the dynamic stability of nanostructures.

The fine geometry optimization of the molecules, starting from the geometry given by the molecular dynamics, was done using ORCA36, by minimizing the DFT total energy of the molecule at the ground state. For the Self-Consistent Field (SCF) cycles, we employed tight convergence criteria. The proven and computationally efficient B3LYP functional and the def2-TZVP basis set were employed in all these calculations. In the DFT-based SCF calculations, the average tolerance on Energy has been set to 1.0e−08, the average tolerance on MAX-electronic density change has been set to 1.0e−07, and the average tolerance on RMS-electronic density to 1.0e−09 (atomic units). The optimization of the molecular structure was performed without any symmetry constraint on the geometry of the molecules. In all the investigated cases, it has been checked that the optimized geometries correspond to a minimum of the potential energy surface, by carrying out the calculation of the vibrational frequencies.

Thermodynamical properties, such as the Gibbs energy, were calculated in the (N,p,T) ensemble (constant number of particles, constant temperature and constant pressure), at the standard temperature of 298.15 K. The internal energy includes in addition to the (0K) electronic energy, also the contributions from the zero point energy, vibrational, rotational and translational corrections to the ground state energy. The entropy contribution takes into account the vibrational, rotational and translational components.

As usual, the Born–Oppenheimer approximation was assumed and relativistic effects neglected since the molecules are composed of light elements. DFT calculations were thus performed in the standard Khon-Sham scalar theory and were implemented in ORCA36.

The IR and Raman spectra were also calculated using the corresponding spectroscopy methods implemented in ORCA36. These have been performed as a means of characterization, using complementary methods, of the proposed molecular nano-I-beam and the corresponding nanotube structures. In addition, UV–Vis (TD-DFT) spectra were calculated as a mean to probe the electronic excited states that may also serve as a signature of these nano-structures.For the relevant calculations for which analytical derivatives of the Hessian were not available, numerical derivatives calculated requesting tight accuracy were employed.

Associations of body composition and inflammatory parameters with survival in patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer receiving neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy
Authors: Yuting Zheng, Qinyue Luo, Yimeng He, Hanting Li, Mengting Huang, Chengyu Ding, Xiaoyu Han, Heshui Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe predictive value of body composition and inflammatory parameters in patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy remains poorly defined. The study sought to evaluate the association between computed tomography (CT)-based body composition, inflammatory markers, and survival outcomes in NSCLC patients following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.MethodsThis retrospective study included resectable NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy from June 2019 to March 2023. CT images were collected at three levels (T4, T10, and L1) for quantifying skeletal muscle and adipose tissue. Blood routine results were collected to calculate inflammatory parameters. All measurements were obtained at baseline and preoperatively. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted and compared using the log-rank tests. Cox regression analysis was performed to investigate the predictive value of clinical, inflammatory, and body composition parameters for disease-free survival (DFS).ResultsA total of 154 patients were included, with 21 (13.6%) deaths and 27 (17.5%) experienced recurrence or metastasis. Major pathological response (MPR) was observed in 71 (46.1%) patients. Multivariate analysis identified MPR and treatment time as independent clinical predictors of DFS. In body composition analysis, baseline subcutaneous adipose tissue area at L1, subcutaneous adipose tissue density at T10, pectoral muscle density (PMD) at T4, and delta-PMD at T4 demonstrated predictive value for DFS. Baseline inflammatory markers, including neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and systemic immune inflammation index, were also associated with DFS. A comprehensive model integrating clinical, body composition, and inflammatory parameters demonstrated superior prognostic performance with the receiver operating characteristic areas under the curve for DFS at 1-, 2-, and 3-year of 0.832, 0.806 and 0.797, respectively.ConclusionsBaseline body composition and inflammation parameters were valuable in predicting DFS, while preoperative parameters had limited prognostic value. A combined model integrating clinical, body composition, and inflammatory parameters demonstrated enhanced predictive performance for DFS, and may serve as a valuable tool for assessing prognosis in resectable NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.Keywords: Body composition, inflammatory markers, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), survival, neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy

Highlight box

Key findings

• Baseline body composition and inflammation parameters were valuable in predicting disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, while preoperative parameters had limited prognostic value.

What is known and what is new?

• While body composition and inflammation parameters are known to influence prognosis in several cancers, their role in NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy remains unclear.

• The study demonstrated that baseline body composition and inflammation parameters had prognostic value in predicting DFS.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Incorporating body composition and inflammatory parameters into routine clinical practice could enhance the prediction of DFS, assisting clinicians in providing personalized management and treatment strategies in NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.

• Further studies are needed to investigate the mechanisms by which body composition and inflammatory parameters affect survival in NSCLC patients.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the primary cause of cancer-related mortality in China and worldwide (1,2). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for 85% of all lung cancer, with 30–40% being surgically resectable (3). Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy for resectable NSCLC has markedly attracted oncologists’ attention for achieving superior major pathological response (MPR) rates and few adverse effects (4,5). However, not all patients benefit from neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and may still encounter instances of recurrence and metastasis, ultimately impacting their overall survival (OS) (6). Recently, instead of cancer-related factors, such as tumor stage, pathological type, and MPR, increasing evidence demonstrated that patients’ general condition-related factors, like body composition and inflammatory status, also had a significant impact on prognosis of NSCLC patients.

Body composition refers to bodily tissue composition and spatial distribution resulting from the combined effects of nutrition, energy metabolism, and inflammation (7,8). Studies suggest that computed tomography (CT) can be used for the evaluation of body composition and is considered one of the reference methods for measuring skeletal muscle area (SMA), skeletal muscle density (SMD), and subcutaneous adipose tissue area (SATA) (9,10). Cancer patients routinely receive CT scans to determine tumor staging, evaluate treatment response, and monitor progress, making it possible to assess skeletal muscle quantity and quality. The skeletal muscle at L3 obtained from CT scans is regarded as a reference, as the cross-sectional area of muscle at L3 shows a linear correlation with systemic muscle mass (11). Given that most chest CT imaging fail to cover areas beyond L1, several studies attempted to assess muscles at thoracic vertebra levels and found that reduced muscle and subcutaneous fat on CT is significantly related to poor survival in NSCLC (12-15).

Systemic inflammation is a crucial factor in tumor initiation and progression through promoting angiogenesis, affecting immune surveillance and treatment response (16), as well as creating a microenvironment conducive to cancer cell proliferation. Hematological indicators associated with inflammation, such as neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), have been identified as biomarkers linked to dismal outcomes in advanced NSCLC undergoing immunotherapy (17-19). However, the prognostic value of routinely accessible peripheral blood markers and CT-derived body composition in resectable NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy remains unclear.

The study aimed to evaluate the connection of baseline and preoperative body composition and inflammatory parameters with survival of NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. We hypothesize that these readily accessible factors can be incorporated into routine clinical practice to assist clinicians in providing personalized management and treatment strategies. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2025-327/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

We retrospectively evaluated 154 NSCLC patients from Union Hospital, Wuhan undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and surgery from June 2019 to March 2023. This study included patients who met the following criteria: (I) age ≥18 years old; (II) patients receiving chemoimmunotherapy for at least two cycles; (III) NSCLC at IIA-IIIB stage; (IV) two chest CT scans and inflammatory assessments, one at baseline and another before surgery, were available. The exclusion criteria were: (I) patients with immune disorders (n=3); (II) insufficient clinical information and inflammation parameters (n=9); (III) incomplete or poor-quality chest CT images (n=33); (IV) active infections (n=5). Figure 1 displays the patient flowchart. The patient’s clinical and pathological characteristics, involving age, sex, smoking history, body mass index (BMI), treatment time, tumor, node, metastasis (TNM) stage, Immune-Related Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (iRECIST), histology, MPR, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) status and programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression level, were collected. The eighth edition of the TNM staging system for NSCLC, issued by the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC), was adopted (3). The percentage of viable residual tumors in the resected lung tissue was assessed in primary tumors following the IASLC guidelines (20). MPR was defined as 10% or less of viable tumor cells. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital, Wuhan (No. 0516), and the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

Treatment protocol

Before surgery, all patients received at least two cycles of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy. The immune checkpoint inhibitors included sintilimab (200 mg), camrelizumab (200 mg), pembrolizumab (200 mg), durvalumab (200 mg) or tislelizumab (200 mg). Platinum-based drugs, such as carboplatin [area under the curve (AUC) of 5 mg·min/mL], cisplatin (75 mg/m2), or nedaplatin (75 mg/m2), were used in the TP regimen, together with paclitaxel (175 mg/m2). Surgical approaches included thoracotomy or thoracoscopy, accompanied by routine lymph node dissection.

Inflammatory parameters

Blood routine results included neutrophil, monocyte, lymphocyte, platelet, and serum albumin at baseline and before surgery. Based on these data, several inflammatory parameters were calculated, including LMR, NLR, PLR, systemic immune inflammation index (SII), aggregate index of systemic inflammation (AISI), and systemic inflammation response index (SIRI). Table S1 displayed the formula.

Body composition measurement

One of two multi-slice spiral CT scanners (SOMATOM Definition, SOMATOM Definition AS+, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) was used to acquire CT images. The settings used were as follows: tube voltage, 120 kV; detector collimation width, 64×0.6 mm and 128×0.6 mm. An automatic exposure control system (CARE Dose 4D; Siemens Healthcare) controlled the tube current. Reconstructed images were made using a 1.5 mm increment and a 1.5 mm slice thickness. Following previous studies (12,15,21), skeletal muscle and adipose tissue were quantified using a single CT image at T4, T10, and L1 levels (Figure 2). SliceOmatic version 5.0 (TomoVision, Magog, Quebec, Canada) was applied to measure SATA and skeletal muscle in accordance with the standard Hounsfield unit (HU) range. At the T4 level, measurements were limited to the pectoral muscle (PM) and the subcutaneous adipose tissue in front of the PM. Based on the previous study (22), we selected thresholds for subcutaneous fat between −190 and −30 HU and skeletal muscle between −29 and 150 HU. The mean radiation attenuation of the targeted muscle region was automatically used to generate the SMD. The skeletal muscle index (SMI) was calculated by dividing the total muscle cross-sectional area by the square of height to assess skeletal muscle mass. All body composition measurements were derived from CT images at baseline and before surgery, and were performed by two radiologists (Y.Z. and Y.H., with 4 and 2 years of experience, respectively).

Study endpoints

The main endpoint was disease-free survival (DFS), determined as the interval from postoperative pathological assessment to disease progression or death. The secondary endpoint was OS, referring to the duration from operation until death or the end of follow-up. Follow-up was performed via telephone and continued until October 18, 2024.

Statistical analyses

Data splitting is an unstable method for survival analysis models, especially when subjects are less than 20,000 (23). R software (version 4.2.2; R Core Team, Vienna, Austria), SPSS Statistics 26 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA), and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2) were used for the statistical analyses. Count data were expressed as counts and percentages. Continuous data were reported as mean ± standard deviations, and differences were compared by using the paired t-test. Optimal cut-off values were determined using the “surv_cutpoint” function from the “survminer” R package. As skeletal muscle and adipose tissue distribution differ across male and female patients (24), body composition parameters were split according to sex-specific cut-off points. The Cox proportional hazards model was applied for univariate and multivariate analyses of DFS and OS. Prognostic factors with P values <0.05 and no evidence of multicollinearity (variance inflation factor <10) were considered for inclusion in the multivariable model. Schoenfeld residuals analysis was performed to evaluate the proportional hazards assumption for the Cox model, which showed a global P value above 0.05, as well as P values >0.05 for each variable. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated and compared using the log-rank test. Due to the lack of standardized cut-off values for the risk score, individuals were stratified into low- and high-risk categories based on the median risk score. The AUC was used to evaluate model performance. The agreement between expected and observed risks was assessed using calibration curves. Clinical utility was further quantified through decision curve analysis (DCA). A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 154 patients were included, with a mean age of 60.63±6.14 years, and 92.2% were men. The mean treatment time was 50.92±42.35 days. Over half of the patients had no history of smoking (n=84, 54.5%). The average BMI was 23.29±3.62 kg/m2. Based on iRECIST criteria, 46/152 patients (29.8%) demonstrated partial response (PR) or complete response (CR), and 108/154 (70.1%) had stable diseases (SD) or progressive disease (PD). The most common histological subtypes were squamous cell carcinoma (56.4%), followed by adenocarcinoma (28.5%). The majority of patients (n=128, 83.1%) were in stage III. A total of 71 (46.1%) patients achieved MPR. EGFR mutation testing was performed in 21 patients (13.6%), of whom 8 (5.2%) were positive and 13 (8.4%) were wild-type. PD-L1 expression was conducted in 26 patients (16.9%), with 8 (5.2%) showing PD-L1 expression <1%, 12 (7.8%) between 1% and 49%, and 6 (3.9%) ≥50%. The median follow-up time was 29 months for OS and 25 months for DFS. The follow-up loss rate was 14.2% (22/154), with 27 patients (17.5%) experiencing tumor recurrence or metastasis and 21 (13.6%) having died by the end of the follow-up period. Changes in body composition and inflammatory parameters after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy

Following therapy, male patients exhibited significant reductions in SMI and SMA at L1 (both P<0.001), while SMD showed a marked increase at T4 (P=0.03) (Figure S1A). SATA showed no significant changes after therapy at all levels. Subcutaneous adipose tissue density (SATD) of male patients decreased significantly at L1 and T10 (P=0.01 and P=0.003, respectively), while increased at T4 after therapy (P=0.004). There were no differences in body composition parameters before and after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in female patients. Additionally, all inflammation parameters significantly decreased after therapy, except LMR (Figure S1B).

Factors associated with survival

In clinicopathological parameters, univariate analysis indicated a significant association between DFS and both treatment time (P=0.02) and MPR (P<0.001). Multivariable Cox regression analysis confirmed that treatment time [hazard ratio (HR): 2.359, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.277–4.356, P=0.006] and MPR (HR: 0.253, 95% CI: 0.126–0.510, P<0.001) were independent predictors of DFS (Table 2).

Clinical, body composition and inflammatory indicators combinations for predicting DFS

The clinicopathological model yielded AUCs of 0.749 (95% CI: 0.660–0.838), 0.730 (95% CI: 0.637–0.822), and 0.692 (95% CI: 0.574–0.809) for 1-, 2-, and 3-year DFS predictions, respectively (Figure 3A). The inflammatory parameter model demonstrated moderate performance, with AUCs of 0.655 (95% CI: 0.445–0.866), 0.725 (95% CI: 0.6–0.849), and 0.737 (95% CI: 0.615–0.858) at the corresponding timepoints (Figure 3B). Superior performance was observed in body composition model, achieving AUCs of 0.798 (95% CI: 0.647–0.950), 0.777 (95% CI: 0.671–0.884), and 0.809 (95% CI: 0.705–0.914) for 1-, 2-, and 3-year predictions (Figure 3C).

The comprehensive model (Figure 3D) integrating treatment time, MPR, baseline LMR, SII, PMD at T4, SATD at T10, SATA at L1, and delta-PMD at T4 demonstrated enhanced prognostic ability, achieving AUCs of 0.832 (95% CI: 0.761–0.904), 0.806 (95% CI: 0.726–0.886), and 0.797 (95% CI: 0.692–0.902) for 1-, 2-, and 3-year DFS predictions, respectively. A forest plot (Figure 3E) illustrates the independent factors within the comprehensive model. Based on the median risk score cut-off, patients were classified into low- (n=78) and high-risk (n=76) groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (P<0.0001, Figure 3F) revealed a significant difference in survival probability between the two risk groups.

A comprehensive model was also developed for OS prediction, with AUCs of 0.896, 0.881, and 0.888 for 1-, 2-, and 3-year, respectively. Detailed results are presented in Figure S2, which also includes the corresponding forest plot and Kaplan-Meier curves stratified by OS risk groups. Additionally, calibration curves (Figure S3) and DCA (Figure S4) for both DFS and OS further confirmed the models’ predictive accuracy and clinical utility.

Discussion

This study identified significant associations between inflammatory parameters (baseline LMR and SII) and body composition metrics (baseline SATA at L1, SATD at T10, PMD, and delta-PMD) with DFS in NSCLC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Based on these findings, we developed a prognostic model that integrates clinical, body composition, and inflammatory parameters. This model demonstrated superior predictive performance for DFS compared to single models, highlighting its potential as a valuable tool for prognosis assessment.

MPR has recently been recognized as a crucial short-term endpoint and a surrogate for long-term survival in neoadjuvant immunotherapy (25). In our study, MPR was significantly related to both DFS and OS, further supporting its prognostic value. A total of 71 (46.1%) patients achieved MPR, consistent with previous studies (26,27). Notably, this proportion was higher than those with PR/CR (29.8%) based on iRECIST criteria. This discrepancy likely reflects the limitations of radiographic assessment, which measures tumor size and may include necrotic tissue, whereas pathological evaluation assesses tumor viability, capturing meaningful responses even despite residual masses on imaging.

After neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy for NSCLC, muscle and fat parameters exhibited heterogeneous changes across anatomical sites. In male patients, SMI and SMA at L1 significantly decreased, suggesting trunk muscle loss, likely due to treatment-induced muscle catabolism and malnutrition (28). Increased PMD may reflect reduced local fat infiltration and improved muscle quality following the alleviation of inflammation (29). Among fat parameters, SATD decreased significantly at L1 and T10, but increased at T4, possibly reflecting local metabolic changes and fat fibrosis after therapy. SATA remained unchanged at all levels, suggesting stability in subcutaneous fat, potentially due to the short treatment duration. No significant body composition changes were observed among female patients, likely due to the small sample size, limiting statistical power. Additionally, most inflammatory markers significantly declined after therapy, indicating reduced tumor burden and an anti-inflammatory effect of the treatment. Future studies with larger cohorts should validate these findings and explore the underlying mechanisms driving these changes.

Low muscle density was linked to reduced muscle strength, impaired function, and poorer DFS and OS (30,31). A recent CT-based study at the T12 level in NSCLC patients revealed that increased muscle radiodensity was connected with a reduced probability of functional impairment, while SMI showed no significant correlation (32). This underscores the growing recognition that muscle function, instead of mass, serves as a more reliable predictor of adverse outcomes. Consistently, we found no prognostic value for SMI, whereas low baseline PMD was significantly associated with worse OS and DFS, and a reduced delta-PMD was strongly associated with poorer DFS. Among all muscle-related metrics, only PMD was identified as a prognostic predictor for NSCLC patients, emphasizing the critical role of chest muscles in prognosis prediction.

Previous studies (33-35) have shown that high SATD was linked to poorer survival outcomes in NSCLC patients. This relationship may be attributed to the association between high adipose tissue radiodensity and high tumor metabolic activity and a pro-inflammatory state. Interestingly, our study found that high baseline SATD at T10 and low delta-SATD at T10 were associated with improved DFS and OS, respectively. The reason for this discrepancy remains unclear but may be related to the variations in fat distribution across different anatomical sites, leading to various cut-off values and groupings. Further research is needed to clarify this underlying mechanism.

In addition to SATD, baseline SATA at L1 also demonstrated prognostic significance, as participants with lower baseline SATA tended to have a shorter DFS, consistent with previous studies (36,37). Subcutaneous adipose tissue has been reported to possess anti-inflammatory and metabolic benefits. A preclinical study reported that transplanting subcutaneous fat into the peritoneal cavity of obese mice can result in metabolically favorable outcomes (38). Similar findings were observed in the preoperative body composition analysis of resectable gastric cancer (39). Overall, the most significant body composition metrics were identified at baseline, emphasizing the prognostic value of muscle and fat density prior to treatment.

High SII has been recognized as a marker of cancer progression and poor prognosis (40,41). A previous study identified high SII (>1,014) as a predictor of worse DFS in resectable NSCLC patients receiving neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (42). However, our study found that a higher SII (≥535.93) was associated with longer DFS. This discrepancy may be due to variations in cut-off values, as the SII threshold was lower than those commonly used (generally >600) (40,42). Additionally, SII, derived from peripheral blood counts of neutrophils, platelets, and lymphocytes, can be influenced by undiagnosed infections or autoimmune disorders. Multicenter cohorts and mechanistic research are needed to validate its clinical value. Similar to previous studies (42,43), we also found a significant association between a high baseline LMR and increased DFS and OS. Inflammatory factors, as low-cost and accessible biomarkers, may help identify potential patients who would benefit from neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Baseline levels may offer superior predictive value than dynamic changes during treatment.

Furthermore, we developed predictive models for postoperative DFS in NSCLC patients receiving neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. The comprehensive model, integrating clinical, body composition, and inflammation parameters, outperformed individual models, which could effectively stratify patients into high- and low-risk subgroups. These findings highlight the value of incorporating various indicators in prediction models and clinical practice, which reflect the physical condition of NSCLC patients from different dimensions. Our comprehensive model may serve as a useful tool for survival prediction and treatment decision-making in NSCLC.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a single-institute retrospective study with a predominance of male patients, which may introduce selection bias. Second, the study included a relatively small number of patients, and the follow-up period was limited. Third, data on adjuvant therapy after surgery were incomplete and thus not included in the analysis. Fourth, no treatment-related adverse events were reported. Finally, due to the retrospective design, biomarker testing, performance status and weight loss data were not available for most patients, limiting the interpretation of results. Future large-scale prospective studies with a more diverse population and extended follow-up period are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusions

To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the value of baseline and preoperative body composition and inflammation parameters in the survival and recurrence of NSCLC patients treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Baseline body composition and inflammation parameters showed significant associations with DFS, which could be easily incorporated into routine clinical practice to assist clinicians in providing personalized management and treatment strategies. Larger studies are warranted to further clarify the prognostic and predictive value of these findings.

Protocol for generating human CAR-engineered macrophages by Vpx-containing lentivirus
Authors: Yun Gao, Xiaobin Fang, Luo Zhang, Xiushan Yin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryHuman-derived macrophages are notoriously difficult to infect with HIV-1-based lentiviruses, posing a limitation to the advancement of chimeric antigen receptor macrophage (CAR-M) therapy. Here, we present a protocol for generating human chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-engineered macrophages using the viral protein Vpx (encoded by the Sooty Mangabey simian immunodeficiency virus [SIV] and HIV-2 lineages) incorporated into the lentivirus vector, which enhances infection efficiency. We describe steps for cell cultivation, lentivirus production, concentration, infection procedures, and efficiency assessments. This protocol provides a foundation to study macrophage manipulation, especially with CAR or other immune engagers.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Gao et al.1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Cell culture, Cell isolation, Cancer

Before you begin

The protocol below delineates the specific steps for using human monocyte-derived macrophages (hMDMs), while also noting its successful application in RAW264.7 cells. Established RAW264.7 cell lines can be obtained from commercially available sources, and human monocyte-derived macrophages were isolated from human donor blood. All cells are cultured in standard 5% CO2 37°C humidified incubator and handled in a sterile cell culture biosafety cabinet.2 All reagents should be prepared under sterile conditions. Before you begin to obtain any cellular products or perform any experiment in your laboratory, all appropriate regulatory standards need to be approved by your institute. And it should be obtained the approval of Institutional Review Board (IRB).

Step-by-step method details

Construction of lentiviruses carrying Vpx component

Timing: 2 weeks

Human monocyte-derived macrophages (hMDMs) express restriction factors SAMHD1 that can reduce transduction efficiency by HIV-1 based lentivirus.3,4 HIV-2/SIV accessory viral protein Vpx can counteract the SAMHD1 to promote the transduction on hMDMs.5,6 The steps below explain in detail how to generate Vpx-lentivirus with the CAR structure. Use two modified vectors and CAR plasmid can pack Vpx protein into virions (Figure 2) successfully.

Note: Unless otherwise instructed, all steps must be performed in a laminar flow hood.

1.Plasmids construction.a.Subclone a SphI-to-BstZ17I fragment which containing the Vpx packaging motif into psPAX2 vector.The Vpx packaging motif is from SIV (amino acids 17–26), clone and place the Vpx packaging motif into the p6 region of the Gag/Pol expression psPAX2 vector at SphI and BstZ17I sites.b.Amplify SIVmac Vpx by overlapping PCR and cloning into pMD2.G at PmlI site. Place a Flag epitope tag at the SIVmac Vpx N-terminus.

Expected outcomes

Upon successful execution of this protocol, we will get a lentivirus capable of infecting human monocyte-derived macrophages with high efficiency. The generation of human CAR-M cells are equipped with a CAR under a fluorescent protein-expressing tracing system. By modifying the lentivirus packing vector, pMD2.G and psPAX2, to ensure incorporation of the Vpx protein into the lentiviral particles, we can detect the Vpx protein expression within the virions through Western-Blot analysis (Figure 2). The Vpx-modified lentivirus can have a robust infection efficiency to human monocyte-derived macrophages from an extremely low level with un-modified ones to up to 96% (Figure 3). The incorporation of Vpx modification into lentiviral vectors compared with traditional adenovirus transfection has significantly enhanced the preparation of Chimeric Antigen Receptor Macrophage (CAR-M) therapy, which will be benefit in clinical preparation of CAR-M and address the key challenges (Figure 4). The consistent and robust expression of CAR-HER2 on hMDMs, facilitated by Vpx, is paramount for the targeted elimination of HER2-positive tumor cells, thereby underpinning the therapeutic potency of CAR-M therapy (Figure 3).

Limitations

The Vpx-containing lentivirus here also shows some limitations. As commonly used lentivirus, they have the potential of integrating into some unwanted loci, due to random integration events. Also due to the Vpx was delivered as a protein, the limited expression might not be sufficient for certain lentivirus integration.

Subclone the Vpx packaging motif to replace the original sequence may cause inadequate expression of essential packaging components, leading to insufficient assembly or improper incorporation of viral genomes into capsids, which can undermine therapeutic outcomes and scalability of production processes. Additionally, insufficient purification methodologies may also fail to separate functional viral particles from non-infectious ones, contributing to a low titer of usable vectors. Within our dataset, we have observed a decline in viral titers of Vpx-containing lentivirus compared with unmodified lentivirus. Therefore the Vpx-containing lentivirus may demonstrate a reduced efficacy in infecting macrophages or non-macrophage cells compared to their unmodified counterparts.

Problem 2

The obtained viral titers of Vpx containing lentivirus are not sufficient (related to step 3).

Potential solution

•By strategically elevating the MOI value, we can overcome potential barriers to viral entry, facilitate more comprehensive coverage of the cell population. This adjustment necessitates meticulous calculation and careful experimentation to ensure a balance between maximizing transduction efficiency and avoiding cytotoxicity.

•Optimize the Vpx motif expression vector and the dosage of Vpx protein expression vector usage may enhance the viral titer.

Problem 3

The titer assay cannot be used to quantitate samples having p24 values greater than the highest value on the p24 standard curve (related to step 3).

Potential solution

The samples should be diluted sufficiently. We recommend making several serial 10-fold dilutions to generate at least one dilution in the range of the standard curve. Mix dilutions thoroughly before assaying or diluting them further, assay each sample in duplicate, and be sure to multiply each result by its dilution factor to determine the correct p24 value in the original sample.

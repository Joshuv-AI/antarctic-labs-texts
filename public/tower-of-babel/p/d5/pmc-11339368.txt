The unmanned vehicle on-ramp merging model based on AM-MAPPO algorithm
Authors: Zhao Shixin, Pan Feng, Jiang Anni, Zhang Hao, Gao Qiuqi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn response to the issues of low merging success rates and poor safety in the on-ramp merging scenario within autonomous driving, we propose an on-ramp merging model for unmanned vehicles based on the Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) algorithm. Firstly, we introduce an Action-Mask (AM) to prevent the sampling of invalid actions during merging, thus enhancing safety by ensuring only valid actions are considered. Secondly, we incorporate noise advantage values to encourage unmanned vehicles to thoroughly explore the environment and avoid being trapped in local optimal solutions. Experimental results demonstrate that the AM-MAPPO algorithm model improves both safety and traffic efficiency.Subject terms: Computer science, Software

Introduction

In recent years, the continuous increase in the number of motor vehicles has led to a rising potential risk of traffic accidents, posing threats to the safety of pedestrians and drivers alike. The rapid development of autonomous driving technology holds promise in reducing vehicle accidents, particularly in scenarios such as vehicle on-ramp merging. However, there are still shortcomings in terms of safety. Accidents often occur due to the inability of autonomous vehicles to respond promptly to dynamic driving environments.

The traditional method of merging unmanned vehicles into traffic relies primarily on vehicle merging rules defined by human experts to constrain the actions of unmanned vehicles and guide the leading vehicle to successfully merge onto the ramp. In this case, experts provide guidance to unmanned vehicles by specifying specific rules and strategies. This approach is highly interpretable and easy to implement. However, in real complex scenarios, it is difficult to predict the surrounding environment of vehicles accurately and timely, make effective actions, and ignore considerations of efficiency and comfort during driving. With the rise of artificial intelligence, reinforcement learning methods have achieved remarkable results in the field of unmanned driving. Reinforcement learning learns the optimal strategy by maximizing cumulative rewards through the interaction between agents and the environment. Lu et al.1 proposed a ramp cooperative control method based on the Q-Learning algorithm, which performs well in improving traffic efficiency and comfort. Ke et al.2 proposed a highway ramp merging algorithm model based on Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN3), whose driving strategy can effectively reduce learning time and achieve optimal global control strategy. Wu4, Lin5, and others established unmanned vehicle merging models based on Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient algorithm6 (DDPG), increasing the success rate of model merging but still facing issues such as low model stability and slow convergence speed. Udatha et al.7 proposed a reinforcement learning model that embeds control shielding functions into reinforcement learning policies to improve the safety of ramp merging by changing rewards and safety constraints. However, single-agent reinforcement learning vehicles cannot cooperate and share information, and unmanned vehicles are prone to causing a single merging point, lacking randomness.

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), integrated with multi-agent systems, is currently emerging as one of the hottest research directions in reinforcement learning and even in artificial intelligence. Terry et al.8 extended several state-of-the-art single-agent reinforcement learning methods, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO9) and Actor-Critic using Kronecker-factored Trust Region (ACKTR10), to multi-agent reinforcement learning. Ha et al.11 proposed a reinforcement learning-based multi-agent CAV control model capable of operating in mixed traffic. Zhou et al.12 introduced a novel multi-agent Advantage Actor-Critic method with innovative local reward designs and parameter-sharing schemes, consistently outperforming multiple state-of-the-art benchmarks in efficiency, safety, and driver comfort. Valiente et al.13 formulated hybrid autonomy problems as multi-agent reinforcement learning problems and proposed a decentralized framework and reward function for training cooperative autonomous vehicles, enhancing the robustness of autonomous vehicles to various human behaviors and constraining them within safe action spaces. Zhou et al.14 proposed a collaborative control strategy to enhance vehicle safety and improve training efficiency. Chen et al.15 designed a scalable multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with a parameter-sharing mechanism to enhance vehicle safety and increase traffic throughput in scenarios such as ramp merging at entrance.

Reinforcement learning methods have made some progress in the problem of unmanned vehicle merging into ramp traffic. However, existing reinforcement learning algorithms face challenges in complex merging scenarios, including deterministic strategies leading to a single merging point, poor algorithm robustness, and the tendency to produce illegal merging actions, resulting in unmanned vehicles selecting suboptimal merging strategies. Therefore, this paper proposes an Action-Mask MAPPO (AM-MAPPO) algorithm for unmanned vehicle ramp merging. By comparing it with single-agent reinforcement learning PPO algorithm, as well as multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms such as MAPPO16, MAA2C17, and MADQN algorithm, the safety and efficiency of the algorithm model are validated.

MAPPO algorithm

The core idea of reinforcement learning is for an agent to continuously interact with the environment, making trials and errors, with the aim of maximizing cumulative rewards in a given task and ultimately learning the optimal strategy for completing that task. The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, a policy gradient method, aims to optimize surrogate objective functions using stochastic gradient ascent after interacting with the environment and sampling data, in order to improve the policy.Compared to algorithms like DDPG and Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), PPO utilizes multiple samples for policy gradient estimation. This allows PPO to make more effective use of sampled data, resulting in faster training and simpler update processes. MAPPO extends the Reinforcement Learning PPO algorithm to multi-agent scenarios, using the classic Actor-Critic architecture with a globally centralized critic network and locally decentralized actor networks, as shown in Fig. 1. In this paper, MAPPO uses a decentralized critic network framework. During the training phase, each critic network can access global information to guide the training of the corresponding actor network. The unmanned vehicle ramp merging decision-making model proposed in this paper incorporates an Action-Mask mechanism into the MAPPO algorithm model to enhance the algorithm’s robustness and the safety of unmanned vehicle ramp merging.

Action-mask

Due to the stochastic nature of MAPPO’s policy, some unsafe or ineffective actions may be sampled during training. The action probabilities output by the Actor neural network represent the probabilities of all possible actions. However, in real unmanned vehicle ramp merging scenarios, the actions of unmanned vehicles are subject to certain constraints, and only a subset of actions is reasonable.The Action-Mask mechanism addresses ineffective actions by employing a strategy of invalid policy updates. By incorporating a mask into the probabilities output by the Actor neural network, only valid actions are sampled, while the logits for invalid actions are replaced with infinitesimally small values. After passing through the softmax layer, the probabilities of invalid actions approach zero, effectively preventing exploration sampling of ineffective or unreasonable actions.

AM-MAPPO algorithm

The algorithm utilizes global information to train the Critic network while using local information to train the Actor network. For the Actor network, it takes as input independent observations of each unmanned vehicle and the actions selected in the previous round, and outputs the probability distribution of actions for the current round. The Critic network takes as input global observations and the probability distribution of actions selected in the previous round, and outputs the state value and advantage value. Global observations are obtained by combining the local observation data of each unmanned vehicle.In the context of ramp merging, unmanned vehicles continuously explore the environment and interact with other vehicles. The Critic network evaluates actions, optimizing the policies of the Actor network, thereby enabling the vehicles to better adapt to the environment and enhancing the robustness and safety of the model.

Experimental results analysis

Training results

In the experiment, multiple decision-making model algorithms were employed to control multiple intelligent agent vehicles for coordinated merging onto the main road based on the traffic flow situation. The purpose of the experiment was to test the performance of the proposed algorithm model by comparing PPO, MADQN, MAA2C, MAPPO, and the improved algorithm model AM-MAPPO in terms of average reward, success rate, collision rate, average vehicle passing speed, and other metrics in the ramp merging scenario.

As shown in Fig. 6, the average reward during the training phase of the AM-MAPPO algorithm model is recorded every 800 rounds. At around 7000 rounds, the average reward value of the model exceeds 80. The optimization process of AM-MAPPO takes less time compared to the MAPPO and PPO algorithms, converging and stabilizing at around 9400 rounds. The multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms, MAPPO and the improved AM-MAPPO, achieve better reward returns compared to the single-agent reinforcement learning algorithm, PPO. Multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms place greater emphasis on coordination and cooperation among agents, leading to an increase in overall rewards. The AM-MAPPO algorithm, after introducing the action masking mechanism, enhances global rewards by masking out unreasonable and dangerous vehicle actions.

Average reward value.

As shown in Fig. 7, the collision rate of AM-MAPPO is calculated every 800 rounds. In the initial stages of training, the collision rate is relatively high as the vehicles explore the environment and learn from trial and error. As the vehicles continue to interact and learn, the collision rate gradually decreases. After approximately 15,000 rounds, the collision rate stabilizes.

As shown in Fig. 8, a comparison of the average rewards during the training phase for four multi-agent algorithm models reveals that the AM-MAPPO model has significantly higher average rewards compared to the other three models. The convergence speed of the AM-MAPPO model is also higher than the other three models.In the ramp merging scenario, which has a high degree of randomness, the MAPPO, MADQN, and MAA2C algorithms exhibit more noticeable fluctuations during training.

Compared to the MAPPO algorithm, the stochastic strategy introduced by the AM-MAPPO algorithm employs an action mask to reduce the probability of invalid or unreasonable actions to zero in the current state, guiding autonomous vehicles to make more sensible and adaptive decisions. This improvement enhances the model’s safety and training efficiency. The inclusion of noise-based advantage values encourages more thorough exploration during the training phase, preventing strategies from developing in suboptimal directions and enabling better adaptation to uncertainty and complexity, ultimately boosting the model’s robustness.

From the overall experimental validation, the proposed algorithm demonstrates increased safety for autonomous vehicles during ramp merging, with higher average vehicle speed, reduced collision rates, and shorter wait times during the merging process. This model significantly enhances the safety of autonomous vehicles in ramp merging scenarios while also improving ramp traffic efficiency.

Conclusion

The paper introduces the AM-MAPPO algorithm model, which incorporates noise-based advantage values and action masking into the MAPPO algorithm, validating its effectiveness in complex ramp merging scenarios. In a 1000-episode test conducted within the gym simulation environment, the AM-MAPPO model demonstrated higher success rates and autonomous vehicle speeds compared to the PPO, MADQN, MAA2C, and MAPPO models, with reduced vehicle collisions, enhancing both safety and traffic efficiency. Compared to the single-agent reinforcement learning algorithm PPO, the ramp merging points of autonomous vehicles were more randomized, allowing the model to guide self-driving cars to make optimal merging decisions, reduce traffic accidents, and ease traffic congestion in complex ramp merging scenarios. The AM-MAPPO model, however, still needs further improvements to handle denser traffic flows and should be tested in various scenarios to assess its generalization capability and improve robustness.

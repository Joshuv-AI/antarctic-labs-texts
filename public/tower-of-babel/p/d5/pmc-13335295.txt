Eco-friendly electrochemical innovative method depending on multiwalled carbon nanotubes/sugar polymer/zinc oxide nanoparticles carbon paste for the determination of urogenital drug mirabegron in dosage form and spiked human plasma
Authors: Shimaa A Atty, Naira A Farid, Nadia F Youssef, Hisham E Abdellatef, Yasmine A Sharaf
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA sensitive, simple, and environmentally friendly electrochemical voltammetric approach has been developed for the determination of Mirabegron (MRG) in drug substances, drug products, and spiked human plasma samples. For the first time, MRG was measured using a combination of electrochemistry and nanotechnology. A Multiwalled carbon nanotubes/sugar polymer/ zinc oxide nanoparticles carbon paste electrode (MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE) was fabricated and used to study the electrochemical behavior of MRG showing a good anodic response in Britton-Robinson buffer (B-R buffer) pH 3.0 utilizing cyclic (CV) and square wave voltammetry (SWV). Linear relationships were recorded between the peak current (Ip ) and MRG concentration ranges of 0.2 × 10− 9 – 100 × 10− 6 M in bulk with correlation 0.9990 and 0.30 × 10− 9 – 100 × 10− 9 M in plasma with correlation of 0.9993. Method sensitivity was demonstrated by the calculation of the detection and quantification limits, which were found to be 6 × 10− 12 M and 19 × 10− 12 M, respectively. Method validation has been evaluated as per ICH guidelines. The acquired results were statistically compared with those of the reported one profitably. Method greenness was evaluated by ECO scale, the Modified Green Analytical procedure index (MOGAPI) and Analytical Greenness Metric Approach (AGREE). The results indicate an excellent green profile, so the developed new voltametric method will be dedicated to the quality control laboratories to contribute to MRG analysis.Keywords: Voltammetry, Multiwalled carbon nanotubes/sugar polymer /zinc oxide nanoparticles carbon paste Electrode, Method validation, Greenness assessment

Introduction

Lately, green chemistry has become a growing trend worldwide across various fields for environmental protection. That is why, the development of new synthetic pathways using alternative feedstocks is considered one of the challenges for chemists including the discovery and identification of alternative solvents and reaction conditions to improve energy minimization, selectivity, and environmentally friendly reaction media, while at the same time striving to increase reaction rate at low temperatures.

Green chemistry focuses on the sustainable use of renewable resources, minimizing waste, and reducing reliance on harmful reagents and solvents in manufacturing and quality control practices.

Green analytical techniques are the approaches that aim to reduce the use of harmful chemicals, although the most common analytical methods used in pharmaceutical analysis are Chromatography, spectroscopy, and electrochemistry [1]. Electroanalytical chemistry has been an important branch of analytical chemistry since the beginning of the nineteenth century [2]. Recently, CV and SWV have become the most common electroanalytical techniques used in quality control laboratories due to their high sensitivity [3]. One effective electrochemical measurement method for identifying redoxable compounds in a solution is CV. However, the sensitivity of SWV enables kinetic and mechanistic studies in solutions that are too diluted for more traditional research. Voltammetry is favored in analytical applications because it is inexpensive, straightforward to use, highly sensitive, and can be performed with portable instruments. Chemically modified electrodes (CME) have received a lot of attention lately because of their significant benefits, which include improving mass transfer velocity, lowering potential, and raising Ip response for redox systems, which improves sensitivity and specificity [4]. The morphological, surface, and electrochemical aspects of MWCNT were previously described [5].

The remarkable electrical, magnetic, optical, mechanical, and chemical properties of nanoparticles, which differ significantly from their bulk counterparts, have led to significant advancements in nanoscience and technology during the past few decades [6–8]. Carbon paste electrodes (CPE) are particularly interesting among carbon electrodes. A new repeatable surface may be prepared quickly and easily, and it has a slightly lower ohmic resistance than graphite electrodes. The benefits of CPE include their low cost, excellent conductivity, flexibility to incorporate many materials through paste manufacturing, and porous surface. As a result, CPEs with various modifiers have lately been used to determine several analytes [9]. Generally, CPEs are widely used because they are inexpensive, readily available, and adequate for creating electrode materials that can be altered by combining different substances to create new, less elegant electrodes. CME, which have been used for the study of additional species, are electrodes constructed in this way that function as chemically modified sensors, providing better sensitivity and selectivity than unmodified ones.

In this work, zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO NPs), sugar polymer (PS), and MWCNT are used as a kind of modifier in the CPE. ZnO NPs are widely considered suitable for biosensing due to their affordability, chemical stability, low toxicity, and relatively high isoelectric point (~ 9.5), which facilitates protein adsorption through electrostatic interactions [10]. ZnO’s strong exciton binding energy (60 mV), wide bandgap (3.37 eV), and semiconducting characteristics make it useful for sensor design. PS is a polysaccharide that has low density. It is used to improve the electroactive surface area due to its anti-fouling character, high porosity, and biocompatibility. The PS addition with MWCNT results in an increased Ip. Here, MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE electrode has been created to detect MRG in drug substances, drug products, and human plasma samples.

MRG, 2-(2-amino-1,3-thiazol-4-yl)-N-[4-[2-[[(2R)-2-hydroxy-2- phenylethyl] amino] ethyl] phenyl] acetamide (Scheme 1) is an agonist of the beta-3 adrenergic receptor. It relaxes the bladder muscles, increasing the bladder capacity and reducing involuntary bladder contractions. It is used to treat Overactive bladder (OAB). Additionally, it is utilized to treat pediatric patients with neurogenic detrusor overactivity (NDO) [11]. MRG was approved by FDA in 2012. A review of the literature showed that some analytical techniques, such as high-pressure liquid chromatography (HPLC) techniques, were reported for the measurement of MRG in biological fluids and pharmaceutical preparations [12–22], thin layer chromatography [13, 23], UV spectrophotometry [24, 25] and Spectro fluorimetry [26, 27].

Electrochemical oxidation reaction of MRG

Since MRG is not listed in any pharmacopoeia, the current study aims to provide a new, straightforward, and precise electrochemical voltammetric method for measuring MRG in pharmaceutical formulations and spiked human plasma utilizing a newly manufactured modified MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE electrode.

Experimental

Instrumentation

A Bio-Logic SP 150 electrochemical workstation was used to perform voltammetric studies. The auxiliary electrode was a platinum wire (BAS, USA), and all potentials were recorded with respect to an Ag/AgCl (3.0 mol L⁻¹ KCl) reference electrode (BAS, USA). A 5 mL glass electrochemical cell was used for the measurements.

A pH meter JENWAY 3510 (England) was used the pH measurement of each solution.

The TEM measurements were carried out using a JEM-1400 electron microscope (Japan Electro Company). All electrochemical experiments were conducted at room temperature (25 °C).

Materials and reagents

The working standard of MRG (purity 99.5%) was graciously supplied by Apex Company, Cairo, Egypt. Using a gradient elution system with a C18 column, a mobile phase of potassium dihydrogen phosphate buffer (adjusted to pH 6.0 ± 0.05 with orthophosphoric acid) and acetonitrile, a flow rate of 1.0 mL min⁻¹, and UV detection at 210 nm, its purity was confirmed by HPLC in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol.

Commercial Mirablad XR® tablets (RAMEDA, Egypt), labeled to contain 25 mg MRG per tablet, were obtained from local pharmacies.

All chemicals were of analytical grade. Equal volumes of 0.04 M solutions of acetic acid (Loba Chemie, India), phosphoric acid (Adwic, Egypt), and boric acid (Qualikem, India) were combined to create B–R buffer. 0.2 M sodium hydroxide (Adwia, Egypt) was used to alter the pH of the buffer (2.0–11.0). Fresh Milli-Q deionized water was used to produce each solution.

Human plasma was obtained from the VACSERA Holding Company for Biological Products and Vaccines (Egypt) and kept at − 4 °C until use. Plasma was taken from a non-smoking, healthy, non-diabetic male volunteer.

ZnO NP, PS and MWCNT were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA).

Standard stock solutions and working solutions

19.8 mg of MRG were dissolved in distilled water and diluted to 50 mL in a volumetric flask to create a stock solution of MRG (1 × 10⁻³ M). Working solutions of 1 × 10⁻⁴ M and 1 × 10⁻⁵ M were obtained by serial dilution with distilled water. All prepared solutions were stable for approximately one week when stored at 4 °C.

Fabrication of the modified CPE

Using a mortar and pestle, 10 mg of MWCNT, 20 mg PS and 50 mg of ZnO NPs were combined with 920.0 mg of graphite powder to create MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE. To improve homogeneity, paraffin oil (0.6 mL) was added after the produced composite was dissolved in diethyl ether and agitated using a magnetic stirrer until the solvent had completely evaporated. After packing the paste into the electrode body’s hole, the filter paper’s surface was polished until it had a glossy appearance. Graphite powder (0.5 g) and paraffin oil (0.3 mL) were combined in a mortar and pestle to create CPE.

Electrochemical measurements

Electrochemical behavior of MRG by CV

The drug’s electrochemical properties were investigated using CV. For the test solution, 0.5 mL of standard MRG solution (1.0 × 10− 3 M) was introduced to the cell after an aliquot of 4.5 mL of B-R buffer. In buffers with pH 2–10, measurements were made using both bare and modified electrodes at a scan rate of 100 mV s⁻¹ throughout a potential range of + 0.4 V to + 1.2 V. Every voltammogram was taken in the positive scan direction at room temperature.

Calibration curve

Aliquots corresponding to (0.2 × 10⁻⁹ – 1 × 10⁻⁴ M) MRG were transferred from the working solutions (1 × 10⁻⁴ M and 1 × 10⁻⁵ M) into 5 mL volumetric flasks and diluted to volume with B–R buffer (pH 3.0). SWV was carried out at a scan rate of 100 mV s⁻¹ to measure the resulting solutions. Plotting the anodic Ip against MRG concentration allowed for the derivation of the regression equation for calibration.

Application of the proposed SWV method on pharmaceutical dosage form and biological fluid

Pharmaceutical dosage form

The coat of ten tablets were removed, weighed, and then ground into a fine powder. A precisely weighed amount of the powder equal to 2.7 × 10− 3 M of MRG was extracted using 15.0 mL of distilled water in a 25.0 mL volumetric flask. After 25 min of sonication, the flask was filled up to 100 mL with the same solvent, well mixed, and filtered to eleminate the insoluble excipients. Additional dilution with water produced a solution containing 1 × 10− 3 M of MRG, the amount of MRG was determined from the regression equation. The process was described in “2.5.2. Calibration curve”.

Preparation of biological samples

Human plasma samples were stored at -4 °C and all analytical procedures were performed at room temperature. 0.5 mL plasma and 0.5 mL of (1 × 10− 3 M) MRG solution were placed in 10.0 mL centrifuge tube. 4.0 mL of acetonitrile was added to precipitate plasma proteins, and the mixture was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 15.0 min. The resulting supernatant was transferred to 5.0 mL graduated glass vials. An aliquot of 1.0 mL of the supernatant was then diluted to 10.0 mL to obtain a final concentration of 1 × 10⁻⁴ M. Aliquots equivalent to (0.3 × 10− 9 – 100 × 10− 9 M) of MRG were prepared and the volumes were completed to the mark with B-R buffer (pH 3.0). The Ip was plotted against the drug concentration (M) to create the calibration graph.

Electrochemistry of MRG

Figure 2. demonstrates typical CV voltammograms of 1.0 × 10− 4 M of MRG in B-R buffer pH 3, at scan rate 100 mV s − 1 at bare CPE and MWCN/ ZnO NPs/PS/CPE. A distinct anodic peak was observed, whereas no corresponding cathodic peak appeared during the reverse scan, confirming that the oxidation process of MRG is electrochemically irreversible. The modified electrode exhibited a substantially higher oxidation Ip and a slight negative shift in potential compared with the unmodified electrode. The anodic Ip increased from 19.5 µA to 57 µA, which can be attributed to the negatively charged Si–OH and Al–OH surface groups enhancing the electrostatic interaction with the positively charged drug molecules. This interaction, combined with the larger effective surface area of the modified electrode, facilitated electron transfer and amplified the current response. Among the various buffer systems examined (phosphate and acetate buffers at pH 3.0), B-R buffer produced the most intense oxidation peak with the lowest potential and a more well-defined peak shape, indicating its suitability for subsequent analyses. The electroactive surface area (Areal) of each electrode was determined by applying CV’s technique to the solution of 1.0 × 10− 3 M K3Fe (CN)6 in 0.1 M KCl, and the current voltage curves were recorded at different scan rates. Then the effective surface areas were calculated based on Randles-Sevcik equation. The calculated areas were found to correspond to 0.0490, 0.0559, 0.0699 and 0.190 cm2 for PS/CPE, ZnO NP/CPE, MWCNT/CPE and ZnO NP/PS/MWCNT/CPE, respectively. This confirms that the ZnO NP/PS/MWCNT/CPE exhibits the largest electrode surface area.

Effect of modifier

SWV of 1.0 × 10− 4 M MRG in B-R buffer pH 3 was acquired at a scan rate of 100 mVs-1 after varying percentages of MWCNT (0.5–2.0%), ZnO NPs (1–7%) and PS 2% were combined in CPE as in Fig. 3. In order to accomplish the modified electrode MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE, the anodic Ip was raised to a concentration of 1.0% MWCNT, 5% ZnO NPs and 2% PS, which then became stable.

Effect of pH

The reaction of both organic and inorganic medicinal molecules is known to be significantly influenced by pH [28]. This parameter was investigated in order to identify the drug’s oxidation process at (bare CPE, MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE) in the pH range of 2 to 8 using (B-R buffer). Increasing pH caused the peak potentials to move in a less positive direction, suggesting that MRG oxidation was pH-dependent (Fig. 4A). Potential (Ep) and pH have a linear relatioship for both electrodes: Ep (V) = 0.9248-0.0501pH r = 0.9991 for MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE. The experimental results showed that the appropriate curves and the maximum Ips were obtained in pH 3.0 buffer solutions for both electrodes; consequently, pH 3.0 was chosen as the ideal pH for the ensuing investigations. The impact of pH on the Ips at the modified electrode was thoroughly examined (Fig. 4B).

Effect of scan rate

SWV at MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE was used to examine the impact of scan rate (v) (20–150) mV s-1 at pH 3 on MRG oxidation Ip in order to comprehend the electrode reaction mechanism (Fig. 5A). As seen in Fig. 5B, a strong linear correlation was found between the square root of the scan rate (v1/2) and the Ip. The catalytic oxidation Ip progressively rose as the scan rate increased, indicating a kinetic constraint in the reaction between the drug and the modified electrode’s redox site. The outcome shows that the diffusion stage controls the sensor process [28, 29]. The corresponding equation is Ip (µA) = 2.3836 v1/2 (v s− 1) − 2.5711 with a correlation coefficient of 0.9993. Additionally, the logarithm of oxidation Ips (Log Ip) versus logarithm of scan rates (log ν) in Fig. 5C illustrated a linear relationship as shown in the following equation with a correlation coefficient of 0.9990 Log Ip =0.1196 + 0.6066 log ν. Since the slope of the linear relationship was below unity, the findings suggest that the electroactive species were governed by both diffusion and adsorption processes, highlighting the importance of the electrode modification [30].

The scan rate also affected the electrochemical oxidation peak potential (Ep); as Fig. 5D illustrates, increasing the scan rate caused a shift to more positive potentials.

Laviron’s theory [31] for irreversible processes was used to calculate the number of electrons transferred in order to establish the kinetic parameters of the electron-transfer process for the MRG oxidation on the ALS/CPE.

Precision

Repeatability was estimated through repeated analysis of MRG at three different concentrations levels, each concentration being analyzed three times on the same day (intraday). Intermediate precision was determined by analyzing the same three concentrations of the drug, each in triplicate over three successive days (interdays). RSD% was calculated and presented in Table 1. The stability of the modified electrode was investigated where the peak current was not found to change following storage in air for 10 days. Robustness

To study the robustness of the suggested voltammetric method, minor changes in the experimental parameters were made and the constancy of the Ip was measured. The studied variables were the pH of buffer (3.0 ± 0.2) and the time before each measurement (20s ± 4s). These minor changes that may occur during the experimental process did not affect the intensity of Ip of the studied drug, proving the reliability of the proposed method.

Specificity

The specificity of the proposed electrochemical method was confirmed by the determination of MRG in dosage form without interference from common excipients.

Greenness evaluation of the proposed method

The Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI) is a widely adopted approach for evaluating the environmental sustainability of analytical methodologies. This tool offers a visual representation of the ecological and safety attributes associated with each stage of an analytical process. Unlike single-value assessment metrics, GAPI provides a qualitative overview rather than a composite numerical score, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of method greenness.

In the present study, a modified version of the GAPI tool (MoGAPI) and its corresponding software were utilized to obtain a more accurate and detailed greenness assessment. The applied GAPI scale enables both method comparison and categorization of analytical procedures into three classes: excellent green (≥ 75), acceptable green (50–74), and inadequately green (< 50), consistent with the classification adopted in the Analytical Eco-Scale [35]. The developed electrochemical method achieved a MoGAPI score of 87, indicating an excellent level of environmental compatibility, Fig. 8.

Greenness evaluation of the proposed electrochemical method using MoGAPI

AGREE further complements GAPI by providing a circular, clock-like diagram segmented into twelve zones, each corresponding to one of the twelve principles of Green Analytical Chemistry (GAC). The color coding system—ranging from red to yellow to green—reflects the environmental impact of each criterion, with varying color intensities denoting the degree of compliance [36]. The proposed electrochemical method recorded an AGREE score of 0.87, confirming a high degree of greenness and showing excellent agreement with the MoGAPI evaluation, Fig. 9.

For comparison, previously reported spectrofluorimetric methods for MRG determination achieved AGREE scores of 0.77 and 0.81 [27].

Conclusion

In this study, a smart and highly sensitive electrochemical sensor, based on MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE, was successfully developed for the investigation of MRG using CV and SWV. For the first time, MRG was determined using electrochemistry and nanotechnology at a very low concentration level. The detection and quantification limits were found to be 6 × 10⁻¹² M and 19 × 10⁻¹² M, respectively. The typical CV voltammograms of MRG in B-R buffer at pH 3 at bare CPE and MWCNT/ZnO NPs/PS/CPE show a substantially higher oxidation Ip and a slight negative shift in potential compared with the unmodified electrode. The modification significantly enhanced the electrical conductivity of the electrode—approximately fourfold compared with the unmodified CPE owing to improved electron transfer kinetics and the increased effective surface area of the composite material. Different percentages of MWCNT (0.5–2.0%), ZnO NPs (1–7%), and PS 2% were tested. It was found that a concentration of 1.0% MWCNT, 5% ZnO NPs and 2% PS gives the highest response. B-R buffer with different pH values was tested, and the results show that the appropriate curves and the maximum Ip s were obtained at pH 3.0. The scan rate effect was also evaluated, and it was found that a scan rate of 100 mV s⁻¹ gives the best current response.

The proposed analytical procedure was comprehensively validated in terms of linearity, precision, accuracy, and sensitivity, confirming its suitability for quantitative analysis. It provides a simple, cost-effective, and reliable approach for determining MRG in bulk drug, pharmaceutical dosage forms, and biological matrices such as human plasma, with satisfactory recovery values.

Additionally, the environmental sustainability of the developed method was evaluated using the MoGAPI and AGREE tools. Both assessment models demonstrated excellent greenness, supporting the method’s alignment with the principles of Green Analytical Chemistry.

Harnessing the multifunctional bioagent Streptomyces sp. SP5 for Fusarium wilt suppression, metabolite-mediated antioxidant activity, and plant growth promotion
Authors: Sapna Devi, Rajesh Kumari Manhas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Streptomyces species are valuable allies in sustainable agriculture due to their ability to produce a diverse range of bioactive metabolites. This current study aimed to investigate the antioxidant, plant growth–promoting, and biocontrol potential of Streptomyces sp. SP5 against Fusarium wilt in Capsicum annuum. SP5 acetone precipitates and Et₂O extract exhibited strong dose-dependent antioxidant activity in multiple radical scavenging assays. In vitro seed bioassays with Vigna radiata revealed that SP5-derived indole-3-acetic acid markedly enhanced germination and early seedling growth, which was further supported by improved vegetative growth in pot experiments with Solanum lycopersicum. Furthermore, SP5 displayed remarkable biocontrol efficacy against Fusarium solani in Capsicum annuum. Under in vivo conditions, SP5 significantly reducing wilt incidence while simultaneously improving plant vigor and agronomic traits. SP5 treatments also enhanced photosynthetic pigment levels and stimulated the accumulation of phenolic and flavonoid compounds, indicating activation of plant defense responses under both stressed and non-stressed conditions. Overall, Streptomyces sp. SP5 functions as a multifunctional strain with combined antioxidant, growth-promoting, and biocontrol properties, highlighting its potential as an eco-friendly strategy for improving crop productivity and disease management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42641-6.

Introduction

The agricultural sector is facing critical challenges due to the high food demand to feed a rising population and the complete dependence on chemical pesticides. Excessive use of chemical pesticides not only degrades soil quality but also interrupts microbial diversity and promotes pathogen resistance1. The world population is expected to reach 10 billion by 2050 and currently, 828 million people are facing starvation globally, with an estimated 770 million people projected to be malnourished by 20302. This situation has accelerated the search for eco-friendly alternatives that can promote plant growth while protecting crops against biotic stresses. Among the promising solutions, microorganisms particularly actinobacteria are emerging as powerful partners in sustainable farming systems3. Actinobacteria are well known producer for natural bioactive metabolites4. 70% of active antimicrobial substances in the world are reported from Actinobacteria and majority of them are produced by Streptomyces sp.5. Streptomycetes are promising plant-growth-promoting (PGP) and biological control agents. Number of Streptomyces species such as Streptomyces olivoreticuli, Streptomyces Aureoverticillatus, Streptomyces tauricus, Streptomyces endophytica, Streptomyces griseorubiginosus have been reported to control plant diseases6–10. Streptomyces are well known for their unique metabolic versatility. They are able to produce a wide range of secondary metabolites with antimicrobial, antioxidant, and plant growth-promoting activities. Streptomyces strains unswervingly support plant growth by synthesizing phytohormones (IAA), solubilizing minerals, producing siderophores and antioxidant compounds. Importantly, antioxidant compounds not only confer stress tolerance to plants but also hold value for mitigating oxidative damage during pathogen invasion. At the same time, their antagonistic activities against phytopathogens make them robust candidates for biocontrol applications.

Capsicum annum belongs to the family Solanaceae and is commonly known as bell 0-pepper, sweet pepper and shimla mirch. C. annum is famous for its delightful taste, pleasant flavor and nutritional quality. Bell pepper is a native of Mexico. In the 19th century, the British introduced this vegetable in India11. Fusarium wilt disease, caused by Fusarium solani, leads to significant yield losses worldwide12. The deployment of microbial bioformulations that promote plant growth and suppress disease offers a sustainable solution to this problem. Our lab previously reported that Streptomyces sp. SP5 has the potential to control Alternaria solani and Fusarium oxysporium13–15. However, the antioxidative and biocontrol of Fusarium solani using Streptomyces SP5 has not been reported.

In this current study, we explored the multifunctional potential of endophytic Streptomyces sp. SP5, (i) its antioxidant activity (ii) its ability to produce IAA and promote plant growth in Vigna radiata and Solanum lycopersicum, (iii) biocontrol efficacy against F. solani in C. annuum. Additionally, we examined its impact on host physiological traits (photosynthetic pigments, phenolic and flavonoid contents, and agronomic parameters) under both non-stress conditions and pathogen stress. The findings provide strong evidence for the use of SP5 as a natural biostimulant and biocontrol agent for sustainable crop development.

Material methods

Microbial cultures and bioactive metabolites of Streptomyces sp. SP5

Streptomyces sp. SP5 (MTCC-1307; International depository authority accession number and MW564023; Gen-Bank accession number) isolated from Citrus jambhiri leaves was used in this study13. SP5 was cultured on SCNA (Starch Casein Nitrate Agar) plates, and spores were stored in 20% glycerol at − 20 °C for future use. The test fungus Fusarium solani (NFCCI91) used in this research study was obtained from the National Fungal Culture Collection of Pune, India, and was maintained on PDA (Potato Dextrose Agar), subcultured regularly (every 30 days or so), and stored at 4 °C. The bioactive metabolites of SP5 (Et2O extract, acetone precipitates, culture supernatant) and bioformulation were used to investigate antioxidative, PGPR, and biocontrol potential, whose extraction and preparation methods were previously reported13–15.

Evaluation of Et2O extract and acetone precipitates for antioxidant activity

DPPH radical scavenging assay

The Et2O extract and acetone precipitates antioxidant activity was examined using DPPH assay using the method given by Kumar et al.16 with minor modifications. Varying concentrations (100–1000 µg/ml) of acetone precipitates and Et2O extract were mixed with 0.002% (w/v) DPPH (2, 2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl, Sigma-Aldrich) in methanol. The reaction mixtures were incubated at room temperature in the dark for 30 min, and absorbance was measured at 517 nm using a microplate reader (BIO-RAD). Vitamin C was used as the positive control, and the percentage of DPPH radical inhibition was calculated using the following formula:

ABTS radical scavenging activity

The ABTS radical scavenging assay was performed according to the method described by Re et al.,17 with some modifications. The ABTS free radical solution (190 µL) was mixed with 10 µL of various concentrations (100–1000 µg/ml) of the Et2O extract and acetone precipitates in a 96-well microtitre plate. The ABTS free radical solution was prepared by reacting ABTS stock solution (7 mM) with potassium persulphate (2.45 mM) in dark for 24 h. The prepared ABTS radical solution was diluted to an absorbance of 0.7 ± 0.2 before performing the assay. The reaction was left in the dark at room temperature for 3 min and the absorbance was recorded at 743 nm. Ascorbic acid was used as a positive control. The percentage of ABTS radical inhibition activity was indicated by the decrease in the absorbance of color produced by ABTS radical solution and was determined using the formula:

Superoxide anion scavenging assay

The assay proposed by Nishikimi et al.,18 was used for determining the scavenging capability of the superoxide anion. Under aerobic conditions, superoxide dismutase prevented the reduction of nitro blue tetrazolium (NBT) with NADH mediated by phenazine methosulfate (PMS). This result revealed that the superoxide anion radical, which is formed during the reoxidation of reduced PMS, is involved in the reduction of NBT. In this method, Et2O extract/ acetone precipitates (100–1000 µg/ml) were mixed with 156 µM NADH (1 ml), 60 µM NBT (1 ml) and 468 µM phenazine methosulphate (PMS; 1 ml) in phosphate buffer (pH = 8.3). PMS was added to start the reaction, which was then incubated at 25 °C for 10 min. As a positive control, gallic acid was used. The absorbance was read at 560 nm and the percentage inhibition was calculated using the formula:

Estimation of TPC with Folin-Ciocalteu’s reagent method

The TPC in the Et2O extract and acetone precipitates was estimated using the Folin-Ciocalteau method in a 96-well microtiter plate19, with slight modifications. 10 µL of the Et2O extract/acetone precipitates (1 mg/ml) were mixed with Folin Ciocalteau Reagent (100 µL) and incubated for 5 min at room temperature. 80 µL of 7.5% sodium carbonate (Na2CO3) was added to each well, followed by 30 min incubation at room temperature in dark. The absorbance was measured at 750 nm. The calibration curve of gallic acid, which was used as a standard, was plotted, and the amount of TPC was estimated as mg of gallic acid equivalents (GAE)/100 mg of the extract.

Estimation of TFC based on the aluminium-flavonoid complex formation method

The TFC in Et2O extract and acetone precipitates was quantified using the 96-well plate method20. In this, 100 µL of distilled water, 25 µL of the test samples (1 mg/ml) and 10 µL of 5% sodium nitrite (NaNO2) was added to each of the wells. After incubation (5 min), 10% aluminium chloride (AlCl3) 15 µL was added, and the reaction mixture was allowed to stand for 6 min before the addition of 50 µL of 1 M NaOH. Next, 50 µL of distilled water was added to each well of the microtiter plate and mixed well. The absorbance was recorded immediately at 510 nm using a microplate reader. Catechin was used as a positive control.

In vitro effect of EtOAc extract (containing IAA) on Vigna radiata and in vivo effect on Solanum lycopersicum seedlings

To check the effect of IAA produced by Streptomyces sp. SP5, Vigna radiata seeds were used during in vitro experiments. The Vigna radiata (Pusa Vishal) seeds used in this study were obtained from a certified agricultural seed supplier in Amritsar, Punjab, India, under the brand name Tata Sampan. The surface sterilization method was used to sterile the seeds in which seeds were firstly treated with sodium hypochlorite (2% aqueous solution) for 5 min followed by washings with autoclaved distilled water (three times). Sterilized seeds were placed in Petri dishes having a layer of cotton. Different concentrations of EtOAc-IAA extract viz. 12.5, 25, 50, 100, and 200 µg/ml were added to Petri dishes and incubated at room temperature for germination of seeds and seedling growth. Various agronomic traits viz. seed germination, the number of lateral roots, shoot length and root length were measured after 7 days and the experiment was performed in triplicates. Pot experiments were designed to check the efficacy of IAA produced by Streptomyces sp. SP5 on S. lycopersicum seedlings for 45 days at Guru Nanak Dev University (Amritsar 31°37′45″N and 74°49′36″N). Tomato seeds (Solanum lycopersicum Mill. “Pusa Ruby”) were sown in sterile soil for 3 weeks at 28 ± 2.0 °C and after the true leaf stage, a single plant was transplanted into a pot of 8 cm diameter containing 100 g sterile soil for treatment. Two different treatments were given to tomato seedlings (having an average length of 11 cm (10–12 cm) by root drenching method. Set-1, IAA treated: the soil was treated with 10 ml (200 µg/ml) aqueous EtOAc-IAA. Set-2 (C) control: the soil was treated with water only. Three replications were maintained for each treatment and the pots were kept under natural conditions (temperature 22 ± 2.0 °C, photoperiod L16: D8). Plants were watered and were observed daily. After 45 days, all treated plants were uprooted carefully and were washed with running tap water to remove the adhered soil, and shoot & root lengths, and fresh & dry weights of the seedlings were recorded.

Pot trial of the bioformulation B1 and other Streptomyces antagonists to control Fusarium wilt in C. annum under natural conditions

Pot experiments were conducted with Streptomyces antagonists to evaluate its efficacy against Fusarium wilt caused by Fusarium solani in C. annum. Pots (size- 8 cm height, 14.5 cm diameter) were filled with sterilized soil (500 g). 12 different treatments were given to capsicum seedlings (having an average length of 11 cm (10–12 cm) by root drenching method (Table 1). Each treatment was replicated four times and the pots were kept under natural conditions. Plants were watered and disease symptoms, flowering, and fruiting stages were observed daily. After the fruiting stage, plants were uprooted carefully and washed with running tap water to remove the adhered soil. The shoot & root lengths, fresh & dry weights, and other parameters of capsicum seedlings were recorded and the data were analyzed statistically. Estimation of photosynthetic pigments, non-enzymatic antioxidants, and antioxidative enzymes was performed according to our previously reported method (Devi et al., 2023b).

Statistical analysis

The data were subjected to one-way ANOVA (for all the cases, statistical significance was calculated using Tukey’s HSD test, P < 0.05). The variations in all parameters are expressed as the mean ± SE of three replicates for each treatment. We used SPSS software v.26.0 for all the statistical procedures.

Result

Antioxidant activity of Streptomyces sp. SP5

To establish the antioxidative potential of Streptomyces sp. SP5, its ability to scavenge different free radicals was evaluated using multiple in vitro assays (Table X1). The acetone precipitates and Et2O extract exhibited significant dose dependent DPPH radical scavenging activity, measured from 10.02 to 87.30% and 4.98 to 83.54% (p ≤ 0.05), respectively at concentrations ranging from 100 to 1000 µg/ml, indicating the hydrogen donating ability of the Streptomyces sp. SP5 metabolites to DPPH radicals. The IC50 value for acetone precipitates and Et2O was determined from regression equations y = 0.0849x + 10.405, R2 = 0.9678 and y = 0.0823x + 2.0768, R2 = 0.97 (Fig.X1a, b). The IC50 value of acetone precipitates was 466.37 µg/ml, and 582.29 µg/ml for Et2O extract. ABTS assay revealed the potential of acetone precipitates and Et2O extract by scavenging the ABTS radical cations measuring from 16.06 to 92.23% and 6.97 to 72.44% (p ≤ 0.05) at concentrations ranging from 100 to 1000 µg/ml. The IC50 value was determined from regression equations i.e. y = 0.0828x + 12.173, R2 = 0.9893 and y = 0.0697x + 4.2977, R2 = 0.9884 (Fig.X1c, d). The IC50 values of acetone precipitates and Et2O extract were 456.84 µg/ml and 655.7 µg/ml, respectively. The ability of acetone precipitates and Et2O extract to scavenge the superoxide anions was also examined, with dose dependent activity recorded from 18.13 to 83.37% and 8.01 to 68.90% (p ≤ 0.05), respectively at concentrations ranging from 100 to 1000 µg/ml. The IC50 value was determined from regression equations y = 0.0679x + 18.511, R2 = 0.968 and y = 0.0632x + 4.6229, R2 = 0.987 (Fig.X1e, f). The acetone precipitates and Et2O extract showed 50% inhibition i.e., IC50 at 463.75 µg/ml and 717.99 µg/ml, respectively. Collectively, these results confirm the strong and consistent antioxidant potential of Streptomyces sp. SP5 metabolites across multiple radical systems.

Effect of IAA produced by SP5 on plant growth promotion in the S. lycopersicum seedlings

In view of the strong antioxidant activity of SP5 metabolites, their plant growth–promoting potential was subsequently evaluated via IAA-mediated responses. V. radiata seeds were treated with EtOAc extract at different concentrations (50–200 µg/ml) of IAA to evaluate the in vitro effect of IAA on seed germination, shoot and root development. 100% seed germination was found to be in case of seeds treated with EtOAc extract (50–200 µg/ml), whereas in control it was 66.66%. Streptomyces sp. SP5 promoted plant growth significantly by increasing the number of lateral roots (25 to 266.66%), shoot length (29.90 to 120.83%) and root length (7.384 to 138.30%) over the control seedlings. In vivo pot experiments were performed to determine the influence of EtOAc containing IAA on plant growth traits. EtOAc containing IAA significantly increasing shoot length (36.84%), root length (34.615%), shoot fresh weight (64.07%), root fresh weight (32.44%), shoot dry weight (46.78%) and root dry weight (55.71%) over the control plants (Fig.X2). These findings demonstrate the dual functional role of SP5 metabolites in antioxidative protection and growth promotion.

In vivo biocontrol potential of Streptomyces sp. SP5 against Fusarium wilt

Following the observed growth-promoting effects, the ability of Streptomyces sp. SP5 to suppress plant disease under in vivo conditions was evaluated. Table X6 shows the disease incidence and diseases reduction percentage of fusarium wilt. Among all the treatments, bioformulation (B1) and cultural cells of SP5 showed the higher fusarium wilt control. The disease reduction was 83.4% in the C. annum plants treated with bioformulation B1 and cultural cells whereas acetone precipitates and cultural supernatant reduced the disease by 66.7 and 58.4%, respectively (Fig. 1). These results indicate that SP5 is highly effective in suppressing Fusarium wilt, particularly in its bioformulation and cells.

Effect on morphological parameters

To understand whether disease suppression was accompanied by improved plant vigor, the impact of SP5 treatments on morphological parameters was analyzed. Table 2 shows the effect of bioformulation B1 and antagonists on morphological parameters of C. annum. Treatment of pathogen-infected plants with Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists (acetone precipitates/culture cells/culture supernatant/ talc based bioformulation B1) significantly increased the various agronomic traits viz. shoot length (61.29–162.90%), root length (73.84-212.82%), fresh weight of shoot (365.2-516.78%), fresh weight of root (324.55–827%), dry weight of shoot (859.86-2312.37%), dry weight of root (3650-8323.07%), number of buds (184.21-294.73%), number of flowers (550–2750%), diameter of leaf (16.25-111.25%), diameter of stem (89.47-257.89%) and also decreased the wilt disease incidence over the fungal pathogen treated plants (Fig.X3, X4). Among the treatments, culture cells and bioformulation B1 were consistently the most effective.

Additionally, in the absence of pathogen stress, Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists promoted plant growth significantly, increasing shoot length ( 39.32 to 105.61%), root length (92.16 to 135.27%), fresh weight of shoot (101.19 to 147.79%), fresh weight of root (254.07 to 340.85%), dry weight of shoot (82.73 to 382.14%), dry weight of root (335.55 to 988.86%), number of buds (100 to 266.66%), number of flowers (212.5 to 650%), diameter of a leaf (5.26 to 81.05%) and diameter of the stem (57.14 to 157.14%) over the control plants (Fig.X3, X4). In addition, during the period of experimental study, early flowering was observed in plants treated with antagonists (culture cells/bioformulation) both in the presence and absence of pathogen stress whereas no flowering was observed in control and pathogen infected plants.

Effect of Streptomyces sp. SP5 bioformulation and antagonists on the photosynthetic pigments of C. annum

The improved plant growth and disease resistance are often associated with enhanced photosynthetic efficiency, the effect of SP5 treatments on photosynthetic pigments i.e. chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll and carotenoid contents were determined (Fig. 2). Treatment of pathogen infected plants with Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists (acetone precipitates/culture cells/culture supernatant/ talc based bioformulation) significantly increased the photosynthetic content viz. chlorophyll A (31.9- 115.9%), chlorophyll B (167.3-341.3%), total chlorophyll (100.7%-255.4%) and carotenoid (321.9-464.8%). Similar enhancements were observed in the absence of pathogen stress, indicating that SP5 positively influences photosynthetic capacity irrespective of disease pressure (Chlorophyll A 43.8- 254.9%, Chlorophyll B 42.6-179.2%, Total chlorophyll 35.8%-219.1% and carotenoid 32.8-119.05%) (Table X7).

Antioxidative enzyme activities

To further elucidate the mechanism underlying enhanced disease resistance and stress tolerance, the activities of key antioxidative enzymes were assessed. The specific activity of different defence related antioxidative enzymes catalase, ascorbate peroxidase, guaiacol peroxidase, and superoxide dismutase was significantly improved in Streptomyces sp. SP5 treated C. annum plants (Fig. 4). All the SP5 treatments showed an increment of 124.2% − 238.7% in CAT, 23.36–52.83 in APOX, 31.13–133.66% in Gu POX, 31.64–80.54% in SOD activity during pathogen stress. In the absence of pathogen stress, the specific activity of catalase, ascorbate peroxidase, guaiacol peroxidase, and superoxide dismutase was enhanced by 133 -285.9%, 39.42–94.92%, 73.74–426.7%, and 47.84–117.3%, respectively. The maximum increase in specific activity in the absence of pathogen stress was detected in the plants treated with the fresh cells of Streptomyces sp. SP5 (285.9% CAT, 94.92% APOX, 426.7% GuPOX and 117.3% SOD) (Table X8). Overall, these results demonstrate that SP5-mediated disease suppression and growth promotion are closely linked to improved antioxidative defense systems and enhanced physiological performance.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates the multifaceted potential of SP5 strain as a source of antioxidant metabolites, a plant growth-promoting endophyte, and a biocontrol agent against Fusarium solani. Microbes are one of the most ubiquitous sources of bioactive natural substances, as well as have tremendous advantages in bioprocess technologies over plant extracts and synthetic alternatives21. A total of 22,500 natural antioxidants has been derived from microorganisms, with bacteria, fungi, and actinobacteria accounting for 17%, 38%, and 45%, respectively22. Actinobacteria, specifically Streptomyces, are the most prominent bacteria that produce a broad array of bioactive compounds with antioxidant properties23. Streptomyces exhibit antioxidative potential by induction and up-regulation of host antioxidant enzyme systems in plants. Streptomyces metabolites function as signalling molecules that activate the plant intrinsic defense machinery, leading to long-lasting oxidative stress tolerance and induced systemic resistance (ISR). The ABTS and DPPH assays are the most widely used and efficient methods for evaluating antioxidant activity. The effects of antioxidants on DPPH and ABTS radicals are attributed to their hydrogen-donating capacity. Streptomyces sp. SP5 metabolites also showed a potential to scavenge the superoxide anion radical (O2’−) in addition to its hydrogen-donating ability. The acetone precipitates and Et2O extract of Streptomyces sp. SP5 exhibited dose dependent DPPH radical scavenging activity measuring from 10.02 to 87.30% and 4.98 to 83.54%, respectively at concentrations ranging from 100 to 1000 µg/ml. The IC50 value of acetone precipitates was 466.37 µg/ml and of Et2O extract was 582.29 µg/ml. Many studies have been published on the use of the DPPH test to determine the antioxidant activity of Streptomyces spp. Karthik et al.,24, Kemung,25, demonstrating that DPPH is a widely accepted and well-established method for measuring antioxidant activity. Similarly, Tan et al.,26 reported 9.09 ± 1.40% to 35.98 ± 5.39% DPPH scavenging activity by Streptomyces sp. MUM292 (0.5 to 4 mg/ml). In butanol extracts derived from culture supernatants of two Streptomyces isolates, Kekuda et al.,27 found 58.71 and 59.97% DPPH radical scavenging activity at concentration of 0.5 mg/ml. Ser,28 investigated the antioxidative effects of Streptomyces pluripotens MUSC 137, finding that extract MUM265 inhibited DPPH activity by 6.69 ± 0.83% and 12.08 ± 1.05% at 2 and 4 mg/ml, respectively. The IC50 values of the marine actinobacteria extract on DPPH radical were found to be 41.09 g/ml24.

The ABTS assay demonstrated the potential of acetone precipitates and Et2O extract in scavenging ABTS radical cations, with measurements ranging from 16.06 to 92.23% and 6.97 to 72.44% (p ≤ 0.05), respectively, at concentrations of 100 to 1000 µg/ml. Acetone precipitates had an IC50 of 456.84 g/ml, while Et2O extract had an IC50 of 655.7 g/ml. These findings are consistent with previous research that showed the antioxidant potential of Streptomyces to scavenge the ABTS radical29. Streptomyces sp. SP5 showed an ABTS scavenging activity of 72.44% at 1000 µg/ml, surpassing Streptomyces sp. MUM292, which exhibited ABTS radical scavenging activity from 10.64 ± 1.27% to 67.96 ± 2.23% at concentrations between 0.25 mg/ml and 4 mg/ml26. Similarly, Streptomyces monashensis sp. MUSC 1 J. was able to scavenge the ABTS radicals generated in the assay with activity of 12.33 ± 3.07% at concentration of 2 mg/ml30. The ability of Streptomyces sp. SP5 to scavenge the superoxide anion was also examined, the acetone precipitate and Et2O extract showed 50% inhibition at 463.75 µg/ml and 717.99 µg/ml, respectively which is significantly higher than that of the previous reports. Tan,31 displayed considerable superoxide anion scavenging activity of Streptomyces sp. MUM265 extract ranging from 9.73 ± 5.72% to 55.99 ± 1.03% at 0.0625 mg/ml to 4 mg/ml. However, IC50 of the latter was very high (app. 4 mg/ml) as compared to Streptomyces sp. SP5. The antioxidative potential of Streptomyces MUSC 1JT extract, which exhibits SOD-like activity of up to 83.8% at 2 mg/mL, was demonstrated by Law et al.,30. In contrast to Law et al.,30 the comparable SOD activity (83.37%) was achieved at lower concentration (1 mg/ml) of Streptomyces sp. SP5 extract.

The plant growth-promoting potential of the indole acetic acid produced by SP5 was demonstrated through in vitro and in vivo bioassays in V. radiata seeds and S. lycopersicum seedlings, respectively. When compared to the control, indole acetic acid greatly enhanced the growth features of seedlings in both in vitro and in vivo bioassays. The current investigation supports the findings of Kumar et al.32, who found that culture filtrate containing IAA demonstrated increased germination percentage and plant growth-promotional action. Similarly, Harikrishnan et al.33 observed that Streptomyces sp VSMGT1014 produced IAA and showed a significant increase in root length, shoot length, and seed germination. In addition, the isolate VSMGT1014 considerably raised the dry weight and vigour index of rice plants in comparison to the control. C. annum is one of the worldwide cultivated economical crops of family Solanaceae after tomato and potato. In India capsicum annual production is 0.9 million tons from an area of 0.885 million hectare which is one fourth of the world’s capsicum production34. However, capsicum is vulnerable to the attack of various diseases such as anthracnose, powdery mildew, wilts, and viral diseases around the world but, fungal diseases take a heavy toll among them, and wilt complex has gained major importance35. Fusarium wilt caused by F. solani was known to affect 90% yield of C. annum in India36. In the present study, Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists and bioformulation B1(exhibiting the highest viability) were selected for another in vivo pot trial to control Fusarium wilt (caused by F. solani) in C. annum. The results of the present study revealed that Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists (acetone precipitates, culture supernatant) significantly reduced the wilt incidence i.e. by 33.3% and 41.6% respectively, but bioformulation B1 and fresh culture cells showed the highest, impact, with 16.6% disease incidence. Sundaramoorthy et al.,36 investigated the protective effects of talc-based bio-formulations of endophytic strains P. fluorescens (Pf1) and B. subtilis (EPCO16 and EPC5) against F. solani, finding that Fusarium wilt incidence was reduced by 17–29.75%. Whereas, Streptomyces sp. SP5 bioformulation B1 alone reduced the Fusarium wilt incidence by 16.6%. This effect may be due to the production of antifungal metabolites and induction of defence mechanism by Streptomyces sp. SP5. Capsicum seedlings treated with Streptomyces sp. SP5 antagonists and bioformulation showed enhancement in photosynthetic pigments, and defence enzyme activities over the pathogen infected control plants. Our findings corroborate the use of different biocontrol agents to suppress various phytopathogens and disease management in plants by inducing ISR in plants, as reported in the literature37–39. There are several reports which showed that Streptomyces induced systemic resistance in plants against fungal phytopathogens40–42. Thus, our findings provide evidence that the induction of defence enzymes by the application of endophytic Streptomyces strains might strengthen the plants against biotic stress. Furthermore, in the absence of pathogen stress, Streptomyces sp. SP5 considerably boosted several growth parameters of C. annum plants during the pot trial. Streptomyces sp. SP5 showed a highly pronounced effect on various plant growth parameters and significantly increased lengths of shoot and root, fresh weights of shoot and root, dry weights of shoot and root, number of buds, flowers and diameter of leaf and stem over the control plants. Similarly, Nimnoi and Ruanpanun ,43 reported that Streptomyces spp. strains KPS-E004 and KPS-A032 enhanced the shoot and root length and the shoot and root weights by 75.47%, 88.37%, 56.78%, and 141.84%, respectively in Capsicum frutescens L. According to Liotti et al.,41, inoculating C. annuum plants with S. griseocarneus R132 substantially increased plant morphological parameters such as shoot length (32%), shoot dry biomass (42%), and total dry mass (35%). The outcomes from the in vivo investigation in the present study imply that Streptomyces sp. SP5 outperforms previous research in terms of controlling capsicum wilt and promoting plant growth. These findings highlight the multifunctionality of Streptomyces sp. SP5 can be used as a sustainable strategy for plant health, reducing chemical dependence, and improving agricultural productivity.

Conclusion

The findings of this investigation support the potential of Streptomyces sp. SP5 and its metabolites as biocontrol agents with agricultural applications. Streptomyces sp. SP5 efficiently reduced wilting induced by F. solani in capsicum plants. Overall, these findings suggest that Streptomyces sp. SP5 bioformulations and its metabolites have a great potential for development as strong new biocontrol agents against fungal phytopathogens. Furthermore, Streptomyces sp. SP5 not only exhibited the antagonistic activity against fungal phytopathogens but was also found to be endowed with antioxidant and plant growth-promoting activities.

Lack of oncological significance of prophylactic splenectomy for type 4 gastric cancer invading the greater curvature: a multicenter retrospective study
Authors: Masazumi Sakaguchi, Yusuke Fujita, Hisahiro Hosogi, Michihiro Yamamoto, Yosuke Kinjo, Kosuke Toda, Hiroaki Hata, Masato Kondo, Sanae Nakajima, Tatsuto Nishigori, Norihiro Shimoike, Dai Manaka, Atsushi Itami, Yusuke Fujii, Shoichi Kitano, Atsushi Fukugaki, Yoshiyuki Kiyasu, Shintaro Okumura, Kenjiro Hirai, Shigeo Hisamori, Shigeru Tsunoda, Seiichiro Kanaya, Kazutaka Obama
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The necessity of prophylactic splenectomy for 4 gastric cancer with greater curvature invasion remains unclear, as this subtype was excluded from the pivotal JCOG0110 trial. This study aimed to evaluate the short-term and long-term outcomes of splenectomy specifically in this population. We analyzed patients with clinically diagnosed advanced (cT2 or deeper) type 4 gastric cancer invading the greater curvature who underwent radical total gastrectomy at 11 institutes between 2005 and 2015. To minimize selection bias, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was performed. Patients with clinical No. 10 lymph node metastasis or direct splenic invasion were excluded. A total of 95 patients were included (Splenectomy: n = 48; NonSplenectomy: n = 47) after IPTW adjustment. The Splenectomy group had a significantly higher incidence of intra-abdominal infectious complications compared to the NonSplenectomy group (20.8% vs. 4.3%; p = 0.04). Pathological metastasis to the No. 10 nodes was confirmed in 22.9% of the Splenectomy group. However, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in either 5-year relapse-free survival (HR 0.99; p = 0.66) or 5-year overall survival (HR 1.22; p = 0.91). Peritoneal dissemination was the predominant pattern of recurrence in both groups (> 94%). For type 4 gastric cancer invading the greater curvature, prophylactic splenectomy increases the risk of postoperative infectious complications without providing a survival advantage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55429-5.

Introduction

Radical gastrectomy with D2 lymphadenectomy is the standard treatment for locally advanced gastric cancer1,2. However, the necessity of prophylactic splenectomy to dissect the splenic hilar lymph nodes (No. 10) has been a matter of debate. The pivotal randomized controlled trial, JCOG0110, demonstrated that total gastrectomy with splenectomy did not improve survival compared to spleen-preserving gastrectomy for proximal gastric cancer without greater curvature invasion, while significantly increasing operative morbidity3. Consequently, spleen preservation has become the standard of care for this population.

Conversely, for tumors invading the greater curvature, dissection of the splenic hilar lymph nodes is currently recommended due to the potential risk of metastasis2. Historically, combined splenectomy was considered the only reliable method to ensure complete clearance of the splenic hilum. However, with the refinement of surgical techniques, spleen-preserving splenic hilar dissection has been established as a feasible alternative, and a phase II trial regarding spleen-preserving hilar dissection is currently underway for proximal gastric cancer invading the greater curvature4,5.

A critical gap in the current evidence is the management of type 4 gastric cancer. Patients with type 4 gastric cancer were explicitly excluded from the JCOG0110 trial, and thus, no high-level evidence exists regarding the efficacy of splenectomy for this distinct subtype3. Despite this lack of evidence, in clinical practice, the results of JCOG0110 or the general recommendation for greater curvature invasion are often extrapolated to type 4 gastric cancer. Consequently, splenectomy is frequently performed for type 4 tumors invading the greater curvature without established oncological justification.

Furthermore, the surgical approach for type 4 gastric cancer remains controversial. There is a concern that laparoscopic manipulation of scirrhous tumors may increase the risk of peritoneal seeding via “tumor spillage” caused by grasping forceps. However, our group (Kyoto Esophageal and Gastric Surgery Study Group) previously reported in a multicenter retrospective study that laparoscopic gastrectomy for type 4 gastric cancer is associated with survival outcomes comparable to open gastrectomy, with no difference in the rate of peritoneal recurrence6. Building on this, we also investigated the feasibility of laparoscopic total gastrectomy with splenectomy for type 4 and large type 3 gastric cancers7. While we demonstrated that laparoscopic splenectomy could be performed safely, the long-term outcomes were dominated by a high rate of peritoneal recurrence, suggesting that the oncological benefit of splenectomy might be limited in this population.

Given this background—specifically the exclusion of type 4 tumors from pivotal trials and the unique propensity of this subtype for peritoneal dissemination—the true value of prophylactic splenectomy remains unknown. Therefore, the aim of this study was to evaluate the oncological significance of splenectomy specifically for type 4 gastric cancer with greater curvature invasion, utilizing a multicenter dataset to clarify whether this invasive procedure contributes to survival.

Methods

Patients

The patient population in this study was derived from a cohort previously used to compare laparoscopic and open gastrectomy for type 4 gastric cancer6. To specifically evaluate the clinical significance of splenectomy for type 4 gastric cancer, additional data regarding splenic hilar lymph node (No. 10) metastasis and detailed recurrence patterns were newly collected. This study was conducted as a secondary analysis with new approval from the Institutional Review Board of each participating center. The study protocol was centrally reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kyoto University (approval number: R4614), which served as the central ethics committee for all participating institutions. Following this central approval, institutional permission to conduct the study was obtained at each participating center prior to study initiation.

Patients were eligible for analysis if they had clinically diagnosed advanced (cT2 or deeper) type 4 gastric cancer invading the greater curvature and underwent radical total gastrectomy with or without splenectomy at 11 institutes between January 2005 and December 2015. The participating institutions were Kyoto University, Japanese Red Cross Osaka Hospital, Tenri Hospital, Himeji Medical Center, Shiga General Hospital, National Hospital Organization Kyoto Medical Center, Kobe City Medical Center General Hospital, Kobe City Medical Center West Hospital, Kyoto City Hospital, Kyoto Katsura Hospital, and Kobe City Nishi-Kobe Medical Center. To ensure high surgical quality and consistency across participating centers, all gastrectomy procedures were standardized through trimonthly interinstitutional video conferences initiated in 2005, as previously described in detail6. The following patients were excluded: (1) clinical metastasis to the splenic hilar lymph nodes (No. 10) or unknown nodal status; (2) tumors without invading the greater curvature; (3) direct tumor invasion to the spleen; (4) spleen-preserving splenic hilar dissection; or (5) R2 resection. Clinical and pathological stages were recorded according to the Japanese Classification of Gastric Cancer1. Clinically metastatic lymph nodes were defined as those with a short-axis diameter exceeding 10 mm on axial computed tomography (CT).

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate. OS was defined as the time from the day of surgery to death from any cause. Patients who were alive at the last follow-up or were lost to follow-up were censored. Secondary endpoints included: (1) postoperative complications of Grade II or higher according to the Clavien-Dindo (CD) classification8, (2) recurrence patterns; and (3) relapse-free survival (RFS). Pancreatic fistula was diagnosed based on the criteria of the International Study Group on Pancreatic Fistula9. An intra-abdominal abscess was diagnosed based on clinical findings of fever (≥ 38 °C) and the presence of intraperitoneal fluid collection demonstrated by multi-detector computed tomography (MDCT). Anastomotic leakage was defined as the detection of contrast spillage via radiologic examination or MDCT. RFS was defined as the time from the day of surgery to the date of recurrence or death from any cause.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages and compared using Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test.

To minimize selection bias inherent in this retrospective observational study, we performed an Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) analysis. The propensity scores for receiving splenectomy were estimated using the Covariate Balancing Propensity Score (CBPS) method, which has been shown to provide more robust balance than traditional logistic regression models. The following covariates were included in the calculation of propensity scores: age, body mass index (BMI), ASA physical status score, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), year of surgery, tumor location, clinical T stage, clinical N stage, neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and the presence of circumferential invasion.

Using the estimated propensity scores, we calculated the inverse probability weights for the Average Treatment Effect (ATE). The balance of covariates between the two groups after weighting was assessed using the Standardized Mean Difference (SMD). An absolute SMD value of less than 0.1 was considered to indicate a negligible imbalance.

In the weighted cohort, continuous variables were compared using weighted linear regression, and categorical variables were compared using the weighted Chi-square test (Rao-Scott method). The impact of splenectomy on overall survival was evaluated using a weighted Cox proportional hazards model, and weighted Kaplan-Meier curves were generated. Short-term outcomes, including postoperative complications, were analyzed using weighted logistic regression models to calculate the odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI).

All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (version 4.5.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient and oncological characteristics

A total of 95 eligible patients (Splenectomy group: n = 48; NonSplenectomy group: n = 47) were included in the analysis after IPTW adjustment (Fig. 1). Prior to adjustment, there were imbalances in baseline characteristics between the two groups; specifically, the Splenectomy group was younger and had more received neoadjuvant chemotherapy (Table 1). However, after IPTW adjustment, the baseline characteristics were well balanced between the two groups (Table 1), with the standardized mean difference (SMD) for all covariates, including age, sex, BMI, ASA score, and cT/cN stage, being less than 0.1 (Supple Fig. 1). In both groups, the tumors predominantly involved the entire stomach (NonSplenectomy vs. Splenectomy = 85.1% vs. 85.4%, p = 0.974) and were circumferentially located (85.1% vs. 75.0%, p = 0.973). Regarding the depth of invasion, the vast majority of cases were diagnosed with subserosal (SS) or serosal (SE) invasion (97.9% vs. 95.8%, p = 0.713) (Table 1). Four patients had pathological peritoneal dissemination. All of these patients had received preoperative chemotherapy, and staging laparoscopy performed before gastrectomy confirmed the absence of macroscopic peritoneal dissemination and positive peritoneal cytology CY0P0.

Short-term outcomes

Table 2 showed the operation data. Operation time in the NonSplenectomy group was significantly shorter than that in the Splenectomy group (NonSplenectomy: 306 min vs. Splenectomy: 320 min; p = 0.03). There was no significant difference in intraoperative blood loss (NonSplenectomy: 468 mL vs. Splenectomy: 558 mL; p = 0.14) between the groups after adjustment. Regarding postoperative complications, the incidence of CD grade II or higher intra-abdominal abscesses was significantly higher in the Splenectomy group (NonSplenectomy vs. Splenectomy = 1 (2.1%) vs. 4 (8.3%); p = 0.034). Furthermore, the rate of CD grade II or higher intra-abdominal infectious complications, defined as the composite of intra-abdominal abscess and pancreatic fistula, was also elevated in the Splenectomy group compared to the NonSplenectomy group (NonSplenectomy vs. Splenectomy = 2 (4.3%) vs. 10 (20.8%); p = 0.04) (Table 3).

Pathological findings and recurrence

There were no significant difference in pathological findings between the groups (Table 4). In the Splenectomy group, metastasis to the splenic hilar lymph nodes (No. 10) was pathologically confirmed in 22.9% (11/48) of patients. Adjuvant chemotherapy was performed in 35 cases (74.5%) in the NonSplenectomy group and 44 cases (91.7%) in the Splenectomy group (p = 0.445, Table 5). The median time from the surgery to initiation of adjuvant chemotherapy was 31 days and 42 days, respectively (p = 0.49). Recurrrence within 1 year occurred in 15 cases (31.9%) in the NonSplenectomy group and 20 cases (41.7%) in the Splenectomy group (p = 0.39). Despite the relatively high rate of No.10 nodal involvement, the recurrence patterns were similar between the two groups. The vast majority of recurrences were peritoneal dissemination in both arms (NonSplenectomy: 33 cases (94.3%) vs. Splenectomy: 34 cases (94.4%)). Notably, in the NonSplenectomy group, recurrence specifically located in the splenic hilar lymph nodes was observed in only 2 cases (5.7%) (Table 5).

Survival outcomes

There were no statistically significant differences in survival outcomes between the two groups. The Kaplan-Meier curves for relapse-free survival (RFS) showed considerable overlap (HR 0.99; 95% CI 0.58–1.68; p = 0.66) (Fig. 2a). The 3-year and 5-year RFS rates were 27.5% and 17.8% in the NonSplenectomy group, and 36.1% and 19.3% in the Splenectomy group, respectively (Fig. 2a). Similarly, overall survival (OS) did not differ significantly (HR 1.22; 95% CI 0.71–2.11; p = 0.91) (Fig. 2b). The 3-year and 5-year OS rates were 45.4% and 26.2% in the NonSplenectomy group, and 45.6% and 21.6% in the Splenectomy group, respectively (Fig. 2b). To further assess the oncological impact of splenectomy in patients who achieved curative resection, we performed an additional subgroup analysis restricted to patients with R0 resection. Even in this R0 cohort, splenectomy was not associated with improved OS or RFS (OS HR 1.37, 95% CI 0.74–2.53, p = 0.31, RFS: HR 1.07, 95% CI 0.58–1.97, p = 0.82, Supple Figs. 2 and 3).

Discussion

The present multicenter retrospective study, utilizing propensity score (IPTW) to minimize selection bias, demonstrated that prophylactic splenectomy for type 4 gastric cancer with greater curvature invasion does not improve survival outcomes. Crucially, this study was conducted within a surgical group with highly standardized clinical and surgical procedures, ensuring the consistency and quality of the interventions across the participating institutions. The adjusted analysis showed no significant difference in either relapse-free survival (HR 0.99; p = 0.66) or overall survival (HR 1.22; p = 0.91) between the Splenectomy and NonSplenectomy groups. These findings challenge the extrapolation of traditional treatment strategies to this specific subgroup.

One of the most important findings of this study is the unfavorable balance between operative morbidity and survival benefit. In our cohort, the Splenectomy group experienced a significantly higher incidence of intra-abdominal infectious complications, including abdominal abscesses. This increased risk is further supported by a large-scale meta-analysis of 16 studies, which confirmed that splenectomy is significantly associated with higher rates of not only abdominal abscess and pancreatic fistula but also anastomotic leakage10. This increased morbidity is consistent with the results of the JCOG0110 randomized controlled trial, which definitively showed that total gastrectomy with splenectomy was associated with a higher rate of pancreatic fistula (16.7%) and abdominal abscess compared to spleen preservation in proximal gastric cancer without greater curvature invasion3. Although JCOG0110 excluded type 4 gastric cancer involving the greater curvature, our findings suggest that splenectomy continues to carry a high risk of short-term complications in patients with type 4 gastric cancer. Importantly, increased postoperative morbidity associated with splenectomy did not translate into the delayed initiation of adjuvant chemotherapy or an increased incidence of recurrence within 1 year after surgery. Crucially, this increased surgical risk was not counterbalanced by any long-term prognostic improvement. Therefore, prophylactic splenectomy appears to lack oncological significance for type 4 gastric cancer, as it compromises short-term safety without offering a survival advantage.

The lack of survival benefit, despite a relatively high rate of pathological metastasis to the splenic hilar lymph nodes (22.9%, comparable to previous reports11 in the Splenectomy group, can be explained by the risk of recurrence. The predominant pattern of recurrence in both groups was peritoneal dissemination, which likely dictates the prognosis regardless of local lymph node clearance. Interestingly, Kano et al. reported that while the therapeutic value index of station No. 10 was high in non-type 4 gastric cancer (ranked 2nd among all stations), its relative importance dropped significantly to 7th in type 4 gastric cancer. Their data also demonstrated that metastasis to station No. 10 did not significantly impact the survival of patients with type 4 tumors, unlike in those with other types12. Consistently, another retrospective analysis specifically evaluating type 4 gastric cancer demonstrated that the therapeutic value index of station No. 10 was as low as 1.92. The authors concluded that the clinical significance of hilar dissection for this population is extremely limited and suggested that splenectomy for the sole purpose of No. 10 dissection should be omitted13. Furthermore, in the NonSplenectomy group, recurrence specifically located in the splenic hilar lymph nodes was observed in only 2 cases (5.7%). These collective findings suggest that in type 4 gastric cancer, the aggressive tumor biology and high propensity for peritoneal disease overshadow any potential benefit of local hilar dissection12. The aggressive biological behavior of type 4 gastric cancer, rather than perioperative treatment differences, appears to primarily determine patient prognosis. Thus, the theoretical benefit of dissecting No. 10 nodes is practically negated by the tumor’s biology.

Another critical perspective is the implication for the surgical approach. While the majority of cases in this cohort were performed via open laparotomy, the findings likely extend to minimally invasive surgery. Our group has previously reported that laparoscopic gastrectomy is non-inferior to open gastrectomy for type 4 gastric cancer6, and that even when laparoscopic splenectomy is performed safely, the oncological outcome remains poor due to peritoneal recurrence7. Synthesizing these findings with the current results, it is highly probable that prophylactic splenectomy is oncologically futile for type 4 gastric cancer, regardless of whether the approach is open or laparoscopic.

However, it is important to clarify that our study primarily challenges the necessity of “splenectomy,” and whether the dissection of No. 10 lymph nodes itself is completely unnecessary remains a subject of ongoing debate. In this regard, the results of the JCOG1809 trial are expected to have a profound impact on future treatment strategies4,5. This phase II trial evaluated the safety of laparoscopic or robotic total gastrectomy with spleen-preserving splenic hilar dissection (LRSHD) for proximal gastric cancer invading the greater curvature. The primary analysis recently confirmed the safety of LRSHD, with the incidence of grade III or higher pancreatic fistula and/or intra-abdominal abscess being only 1.2%, which was significantly lower than the historical threshold. Although type 4 tumors were excluded from the JCOG1809 trial, these results suggest that hilar dissection can be performed with minimal morbidity using minimally invasive surgery (MIS). We and other groups have previously reported that LTG with hilar dissection is technically feasible and safe for type 4 or large type 3 gastric cancers7,14. Therefore, if the long-term survival outcomes of JCOG1809 demonstrate a benefit for No. 10 dissection, the focus for type 4 gastric cancer may shift from simply omitting splenectomy to pursuing oncological clearance via MIS-based hilar dissection while maintaining surgical safety.

Furthermore, the clinical landscape for advanced gastric cancer has shifted with the advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Nivolumab combined with chemotherapy has become the standard first-line treatment for HER2-negative advanced gastric cancer (CheckMate 649, ATTRACTION-4)15,16. The spleen is a critical secondary lymphoid organ that serves as a reservoir for immune cells and is essential for initiating systemic antitumor immune responses17. Emerging research suggests that splenectomy may impair T-cell function and potentially accelerate tumor growth or reduce the efficacy of immunotherapy18,19. Considering that type 4 gastric cancer patients frequently require long-term systemic therapy including ICIs due to the high risk of recurrence, preserving the spleen may be crucial not only for reducing perioperative morbidity but also for maintaining the host’s immune integrity to maximize the efficacy of subsequent treatments.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a retrospective observational study. Although we used IPTW to adjust for baseline confounders, unmeasured biases may remain. Second, the sample size was relatively small (n = 95) due to the rarity of type 4 gastric cancer, which limits the statistical power. Third, the true status of splenic hilar lymph node metastasis in the NonSplenectomy group is unknown because dissection was not performed; however, the absence of a survival difference and the rarity of hilar recurrence (5.7%) support the validity of our conclusions. Finally, information regarding splenic hilar invasion was not available for all patients in the NonSplenectomy group if it was not explicitly recorded in the operative reports, which could introduce a selection bias where patients with obvious invasion were selected for splenectomy. In addition, because this was a retrospective multicenter study conducted over a long study period (2005–2015), detailed chemotherapy-related information, including specific regimens, dose intesity, treatment completion rates, and post-recurrence therapy, was not uniformly available across all participating institutions. Given the substantial heterogenity in chemotherapy strategies during this peirod, particularly for recurrent disease, standardization of post-recurrence treatment was not feasible. Nevertheless, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy during the study period in Japan was gnerally besed on S-1-containing regimens according to the Japanese gastric cancer guidelines.

In conclusion, prophylactic splenectomy should be avoided for type 4 gastric cancer invading the greater curvature, as it offers no oncological benefit while significantly increasing surgical morbidity.

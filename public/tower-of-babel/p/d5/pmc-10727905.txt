Successful Treatment of Refractory Prurigo Nodularis with Alitretinoin and Methotrexate Combination Therapy
Authors: Dea Kwan Yun, Kyujin Yeom, Uri Shon, Byung Cheol Park, Mi Soo Choi, Myung Hwa Kim
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Prurigo nodularis (PN) is a chronic pruritic skin disease presenting hyperkeratotic, excoriated nodules in response to repeated scratching. Despite various therapeutic modalities, PN remains a difficult condition to treat1.

A 72-year-old Korean man visited our hospital with pruritic nodules and plaques lasted for more than 10 years. His lesions did not respond to the various treatments, including cyclosporine. He did not have any medical illness in the beginning of skin lesions, but had been diagnosed diabetes, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia over time. On physical examination, he had numerous excoriated nodules and plaques on trunk and extremities (body surface area [BSA] 19%) (Fig. 1A, B). He had a pruritic visual analog scale (VAS) score of 9 and Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score of 23. The histopathologic examination revealed acanthosis, papillary dermal fibrosis, perivascular lymphohistiocyte and eosinophil infiltration (Fig. 2). These were consistent with PN. Laboratory findings were normal, except of increased creatinine level (1.8 mg/dl). We recommended narrowband ultraviolet B treatment, but he refused because he had felt dizziness and not been able to stand alone. He started taking alitretinoin (20 mg/day) with topical steroid and moisturizer. After 2 months, skin lesions just slightly improved, and he complained of depressive mood and suicidal idea due to extreme pruritus. Thus, methotrexate (7.5 mg/week) was added. After 7 months, skin lesions improved remarkably (Fig. 1C, D). The BSA decreased from 19% to 3.5%, pruritic VAS from 9 to 3, and DLQI score from 23 to 9. Laboratory findings were normal, except for creatinine level (1.7~1.8 mg/dl). The profibrotic factor test was not performed because he had no liver disease and cumulative methotrexate dose was low.

(C, D) Improved pruritic variable-sized hyperkeratotic plaques after alitretinoin and methotrexate combination therapy for 9 months. There are several cases of successful treatment of prurigo with alitretinoin2. Alitretinoin, a first-generation 9-cis retinoic acid, has anti-inflammatory and immunomodulating effects by eliciting transcriptional activation of retinoic acid-responsive genes and regulating cytokine production. The effects of alitretinoin on the pathomechanisms of PN are not clear. Alitretinoin may inhibit fibroblast synthesis and mast cell production through the production of interleukin (IL)-4, a potent inhibitory action on mast cell growth. Moreover, it could raise the levels of IL-5 and IL-13 and decrease the levels of interferon-γ, IL-2, IL-12, and TNF-α3.

Methotrexate is a folic acid analog with anti-inflammatory, antiproliferative, and immunosuppressant activity. Methotrexate is a therapeutic option in PN1. However, there were only one case series has been reported about the combination of alitretinoin and methotrexate4. In this series, the patients had never been treated with cyclosporine, but our patient showed resistance to cyclosporine. Also, there was no mention of the underlying disease in the previous report. Our patient had renal dysfunction, and methotrexate and alitretinoin were coadministered at a lower dose reducing side effects of each drug.

One report showed that the acitretin and methotrexate combination therapy for psoriasis had high effectiveness but did not greatly affect liver function5. The combination group showed less elevation of profibrotic factors compared with the methotrexate alone group.

Our report showed that alitretinoin and methotrexate combination therapy might have high effectiveness without significant side-effects in refractory PN.

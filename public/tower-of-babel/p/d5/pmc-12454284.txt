Loss of tumor suppressor circRNA CircSPINT2 drives osimertinib resistance in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Rakesh Bhatia, Sanjib Chaudhary
Published: 2025

Main text

The clinical targeting of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations with EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or monoclonal antibodies has significantly improved treatment outcomes in lung cancer over recent decades. Historically, EGFR TKIs have shown considerable effectiveness in controlling the disease progression; however, the emergence of new EGFR mutations has decreased drug efficacy and posed a major clinical challenge. Recently, the role of circular RNAs in mitigating resistance to osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR TKI, has gained attention as a potential way to improve its clinical utility and improve the standard of care. Verusingam et al. have demonstrated that the tumor suppressor circSPINT2 (hsa_circ_0050818) restores sensitivity to osimertinib by inducing cell death through sponging miR-1296-3p and increasing the expression of the tumor suppressor gene RBP1.1 The authors also found that the loss of circSPINT2 may contribute to the development of drug resistance, while its restoration could serve as a new therapeutic strategy to improve outcomes in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).1

The incidence of non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), of which LUAD is the most common subtype, has declined in recent years; however, the detection of stage I cases has increased significantly due to advances in screening and detection tools.2 Additionally, over the past decade, the management and prognosis of NSCLC have improved considerably owing to available systemic treatment options, such as chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, immunotherapy, and molecular-targeted therapies like TKIs. About 55.7% of advanced and metastatic LUAD patients carry common EGFR alterations, such as exon 19 deletions or Leu858Arg (L858R) point mutations, for which TKIs are among the best treatment options. First-generation TKIs (erlotinib and gefitinib) reversibly bind to the kinase domain of EGFR, while second-generation inhibitors (afatinib and dacomitinib) form covalent bonds with the ATP binding site of the receptor (cysteine residue 773), preventing EGFR autophosphorylation and the activation of downstream proteins involved in tumor cell growth and survival. Unfortunately, long-term treatment with first- or second-generation TKIs can lead to secondary mutations in EGFR, resulting in poor therapy response and the development of drug resistance. To address this problem, third-generation inhibitors like osimertinib were developed to specifically target the Thr790Met mutation (T790M) that arises during prolonged TKI therapy (gefitinib, erlotinib, and afatinib).3 However, despite its effectiveness as a first-line treatment, about one-third of patients will experience disease progression within 6–18 months, limiting its long-term benefits. Therefore, understanding the diverse and complex mechanisms of osimertinib resistance is crucial to effectively target tumor cells and prevent progression to advanced and aggressive disease.

The mechanism(s) behind acquired resistance to osimertinib in LUAD patients remain elusive, and addressing this issue is essential for enhancing osimertinib therapy. In this context, Verusingam et al. identified hsa_circ_0050818/circSPINT2, a circular RNA, as an EGFR pathway-independent route to osimertinib resistance in LUAD, and its gradual loss leads to resistance in cases with the EGFR T790M mutation.1 Typically, circRNAs are single-stranded RNAs that generate closed loops after ligation of their 3′ and 5′ ends, creating stable structures resistant to exonuclease degradation. Although initially considered redundant, the expression of unique circRNAs throughout the human transcriptome highlights their diverse functions and roles in cancer development.4,5 For example, the detection of hsa_circ_0002130 in serum exosomes from hepatocellular carcinoma and osimertinib-resistant NSCLC patients highlights its potential as a disease-specific biomarker. The authors employed transcriptome analysis of the osimertinib-resistant cell line H1975 (with exon 21, L858R, and T190M mutations in EGFR)6 to identify decreased expression of the novel circSPINT2 and its association with resistance. Due to their higher stability compared to linear RNAs, circRNAs can serve as promising biomarkers for diagnosis in liquid biopsies.7 Although the authors reported reduced expression of secreted circSPINT2 in resistant NSCLC cells, it remains plausible that it could be detected in human biofluids, given its potential tumor suppressor role—an aspect not yet explored in clinical samples in the context of osimertinib resistance.

Given that circSPINT2 exhibits low expression levels in vitro NSCLC cells, standardizing its baseline copy number will be essential for both primary and osimertinib-resistant groups, including tissue and serum samples. This consideration is particularly crucial due to the existing intra- and intertumoral heterogeneity that complicates the reliability of biomarker development. CircRNAs function as tumor suppressors and regulate gene expression in several ways, such as acting as microRNA (miRNA) sponges that modulate protein activity and cellular functions.8 Overexpression and knockout studies have confirmed that circSPINT2 acts as an indirect tumor suppressor by reducing NSCLC cell aggressiveness and increasing sensitivity to osimertinib in resistant cells, through enhancing the expression of the RBP1 tumor suppressor gene.

Mutation-driven defects in apoptosis pathways contribute to acquired resistance.9 The authors demonstrate that the loss of circSPINT2 is a key factor in altered apoptosis in LUAD, probably caused by mutant EGFR, which requires further study. Cancer cells (H1975) with high circSPINT2 expression showed a higher apoptotic index than control or circSPINT2-low cells after osimertinib treatment, suggesting that the loss of circSPINT2 helps confer resistance to osimertinib in LUAD. Mechanistically, circSPINT2 functions as a miRNA sponge sequestering the abundant miR-1296-3p. The miR-1296-3p specifically targets RBP1 and HMGA2, which have tumor suppressive and oncogenic functions, respectively. Biochemical analysis indicated that miR-1296-3p functions downstream of circSPINT2 and suppresses RBP1 expression in osimertinib-resistant cells. By sequestering miR-1296-3p, circSPINT2 increases RBP1 levels, enhancing the tumor suppression and making LUAD cells more sensitive to osimertinib. In vivo and ex vivo studies further validated the tumor suppressor role of the circSPINT2/miR-1296-3p/RBP1 axis in osimertinib-resistant xenografts.

Emerging preclinical data indicate that circSPINT2, a circular RNA, may facilitate resistance to osimertinib by activating the miR-1296-3p/RBP1 signaling pathway during LUAD progression (Figure 1). These findings are clinically significant, particularly for patients with the EGFR T790M mutation treated with osimertinib as a first-line therapy, since resistance often leads to aggressive and metastatic disease. Besides acquired resistance, circSPINT2 may also play a role in the development of transient and heterogeneous drug-tolerant persister (DTP) cells after drug exposure, potentially involving different underlying signaling mechanisms. CircSPINT2 enhances osimertinib sensitivity in LUAD

(A) Levels of circSPINT2 are significantly lower in both systemic secretions and primary tumor sites in patients treated with osimertinib, indicating a link to resistance against the drug in LUAD.

(B) Mechanistically, in osimertinib-sensitive cells, circSPINT2 functions as a sponge for miR-1296-3p, promoting apoptosis in cancer cells. Reduced levels of circSPINT2 lead to increased accumulation of miR-1296-3p, which suppresses the tumor suppressor gene RBP1, resulting in decreased apoptosis and increased resistance to osimertinib.

Overall, this study highlights a non-genetic mechanism behind resistance to osimertinib, with circSPINT2 emerging as a potential biomarker for therapeutic outcomes and a tool for patient stratification toward alternative treatments. Additionally, it enhances understanding of the molecular and cellular effects mediated by circSPINT2 within the complex tumor microenvironment. These findings will be crucial for optimizing therapies in drug-resistant LUAD and fully harnessing the potential of next-generation EGFR TKIs.

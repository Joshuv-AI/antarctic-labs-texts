Enhancing reliability and spectral efficiency in future wireless networks via intelligent omni-surface enhanced MU-MIMO cooperative hybrid NOMA
Authors: Helen Sheeba John Kennedy, Vinoth Babu Kumaravelu, Poongundran Selvaprabhu, Arthi Murugadass, Sellam Viruthagiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe growing demand for high-capacity next-generation networks drives the need for efficient and scalable multiple access schemes. This study proposes a unified integrated analytical framework for intelligent omni-surface (IOS)-enhanced multi-user MIMO cooperative hybrid NOMA (MU-MIMO-C-HNOMA) system employing maximal ratio transmission (MRT) at the gNodeB (gNB) to enhance reliability, throughput, and sum spectral efficiency (SSE). Closed-form mathematical models for outage probability and throughput expressions are derived, and an optimal power allocation strategy is formulated to maximize SSE under the dual impairments due to imperfect successive interference cancellation (iSIC) and hardware distortions (HWD). Crucially, the analysis establishes a compelling energy-efficient scaling paradigm, revealing that leveraging passive IOS elements is fundamentally superior to scaling active transmit antennas. Furthermore, the study validates real-world feasibility by demonstrating that practical discrete-phase and blind-IOS configurations offer robust performance that asymptotically approaches the ideal benchmarks. The proposed system with strong-weak strong-weak (SWSW) user pairing achieves SNR gains of approximately 0.36–3.14 dB for weaker user equipment (WUE) and 0.13–2.5 dB for stronger UE (SUE) at a target outage of resulting in throughput gains of about 0.03 - 0.33 bps/Hz and SSE gains of 0.02 - 0.14 bps/Hz over other pairing schemes. Furthermore, optimal power allocation achieves 0.48 - 1.02 bps/Hz higher SSE than sub-optimal and minimum power allocation schemes, confirming robustness against dual-impairments due to iSIC and HWD effects. Compared to the conventional IOS-aided HNOMA system, the proposed framework demonstrates superior scalability, reliability, and performance, achieving up to 0.31 bps/Hz SSE improvement for two antennas, confirming its potential as a highly promising solution for next-generation wireless networks.Keywords: Intelligent omni surface (IOS ), Maximal ratio combining (MRT ), Non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA ), Outage probability, Sixth-generation (6G ), Spectral efficiency (SE ), ThroughputSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Fifth-generation (5G) communications are projected to generate $13.1 trillion in global trade by 2035. 5G networks are required to support the Internet of Things (IoT), while sixth-generation (6G) networks are expected to enable a broader range of applications. The evolution from machine-to-machine and vehicle-to-vehicle communication is progressing toward machine-to-everything and vehicle-to-everything paradigms1. Furthermore, 6G networks must facilitate automated vehicles, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and advanced IoT devices, marking a transition from IoT to the Internet of Everything. These emerging applications demand significant data throughput; while 5G offers up to 1 Gbps, 6G is anticipated to deliver up to 1 Tbps with sub-millisecond latency and reliability of 99.9999%2. Additionally, 6G aims to provide massive, ubiquitous connectivity, supporting billions of devices. According to IMT-2030 statistics, although 99% of users and user equipments (UEs) are covered globally, only 60% of geographic areas receive coverage. Consequently, a comprehensive network architecture is required to support UEs in underserved regions1,2.

Due to a lack of orthogonal radio resources, conventional orthogonal multiple-access (OMA) schemes may not support widespread connectivity2. The non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) supports scalability in the number of UEs by multiplexing them in power or code domain independently of other orthogonal resources. As the number of UEs increases, the complexity of successive interference cancellation (SIC) at the receiver also increases. Hence, conventional NOMA is not suitable for widespread connectivity3. Hybrid NOMA (HNOMA) combines traditional OMA and NOMA, leveraging OMA’s simple architecture and NOMA’s widespread connectivity support. HNOMA is supported by various pairing strategies such as near-near and far-far (NF), odd-even (OE), strong-weak (SW), strong-strong and weak-weak (SWSW), and random (RN), which further boost the system performance4. The UEs are paired based on their channel gains, and in each pair, there will be a stronger UE (SUE) and a weaker UE (WUE). Due to weaker channel conditions, higher power allocation is used for WUE, with the remaining power allocated to SUE. The power allocation must be optimized so that all UEs meet the quality-of-service (QoS) requirements.

A low-complexity power allocation scheme is needed to enhance system performance, while considering the practical constraints of imperfect SIC (iSIC) and hardware distortions (HWDs)5. The concept of higher power allocation for WUE may not fully meet the rate demands of WUE, especially in cell-edges and dead zones. To address this issue, cooperative communication and NOMA are combined into cooperative NOMA (C-NOMA), where the SUE retransmits a copy of the detected WUEs’ data, as it decodes the WUE data before detecting its own data6,7. To aid communication, reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) can be introduced into the communication chain, enhancing the effective channel gain8. However, conventional RISs either reflect or refract, providing half-space coverage. Intelligent omni surfaces (IOS) support full space coverage through their reflection and refraction properties. To further improve the performance of systems supported by IOS, the gNB may be equipped with multiple antennas, and beamforming techniques can be implemented. In future networks, a single-antenna gNB will be insufficient to meet performance targets. To achieve comprehensive system performance, the gNB must be equipped with multiple antennas and optimal beamforming. Integrating these advanced schemes will facilitate the development of next-generation networks9,10. Deploying multiple antennas at gNB with NOMA-aided communication results in accurate beamforming, which eventually results in enhanced spectral efficiency and reliability11. Furthermore, MIMO structures enable cell-free architecture, which can enable high-frequency millimetre wave (mmWave) and tera-hertz (THz) communications12. This progression toward intelligent, scalable, and reliable wireless systems underscores the necessity of continued research to fully realize the potential of future networks.

Related works

This section reviews prior studies pertinent to this research. Specifically, the discussion covers the evolution of traditional NOMA, computationally simplified variants such as HNOMA, and advanced schemes including C-NOMA and cooperative HNOMA (C-HNOMA) aimed at improving the performance of cell-edge UEs. Additionally, works involving RIS, RIS-variants and IOS-assisted NOMA are examined for coverage extension. Finally, recent contributions regarding MIMO-based NOMA techniques for next-generation wireless networks are also highlighted.

NOMA/HNOMA for massive connectivity

NOMA is a leading candidate technology for extensive connectivity to next-generation networks, and the significance of HNOMA systems in reducing complexity and propagation errors is discussed in this section. The authors presented a novel adaptive modulation scheme for power-domain NOMA systems operating under iSIC13. They dynamically adjusted modulation orders based on real-time channel state information (CSI), aiming to maximize throughput while maintaining an acceptable bit-error rate. An optimization framework was introduced to select the best modulation pair for SUEs and WUEs, ensuring spectral efficiency and robustness across diverse channel conditions. Simulation results confirmed that the adaptive scheme significantly outperformed fixed modulation NOMA systems in terms of throughput and bit-error rate. The NOMA-assisted massive MIMO (mMIMO) system for the uplink was discussed14. The authors derived expressions for the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) and the sum spectral efficiency (SSE) for two-user scenarios and validated the performance through Monte Carlo simulations. They observed SSE improvements of 52.63% to 67% with and without power scaling in gNB antennas. They also explored the trade-off between energy efficiency and SSE and concluded that power scaling can support energy efficiency at the cost of reduced spectral efficiency. A hybrid signal-processing framework for transmitting the composite signal of paired UEs is combined with the wavelet packet transform, as investigated to meet the SSE demands of next-generation networks11. At the receiver, a complex-valued convolutional neural network-based SIC was deployed, which reduced the multi-level SIC complexity. This approach mitigated spectral leakage and improved spectral efficiency and outage probability performance. The gNB equipped with multiple antennas and the pairing of the UEs was discussed. The suggested system outperformed the conventional HNOMA schemes in terms of spectral efficiency, out-of-band emissions and reduced peak-to-average power ratio.

A conventional HNOMA downlink system was explored15. Here, some UEs were allocated more bandwidth than others, which causes significant performance loss. The authors developed analytical models and simulations for a two-user downlink scenario of conventional HNOMA, demonstrating that it still remained an optimal strategy for transmission. Wireless federated learning networks suffer from the straggler problem. This issue was addressed with the novel HNOMA-time division multiple access (TDMA) scheme16. UEs that complete local training form adaptive pairs across different time slots of TDMA for efficient model uploading. User scheduling, as well as time, frequency, and energy allocation, were jointly optimized. This approach outperforms traditional NOMA and TDMA-based methods. A global joint optimization framework was developed for power allocation and channel allocation in HNOMA-OMA systems to maximize the rate under the constraint of equal rates17. The problem was further subdivided into power allocation and channel allocation and solved iteratively. It was also demonstrated that the global optimal solution was attained within a finite number of iterations.

C-NOMA and C-HNOMA to support cell-edge UEs

This section provides a comprehensive overview of the advantages of integrating cooperative communication techniques into NOMA and HNOMA systems. A cooperative multi-agent double deep Q-network was suggested for sub-channel assignment, along with a dynamic power allocation algorithm designed to meet stringent QoS requirements18. The recommended method outperformed individual learning approaches and baselines in terms of convergence rate and energy efficiency. Most traditional CNOMA schemes were developed under ideal assumptions and overlook practical constraints like iSIC, HWD, and imperfect CSI19. An analytical error and outage framework for CNOMA considering these practical constraints has been developed. It was demonstrated that these constraints significantly impact performance.

Vehicular users suffer severely from penetration losses and variations in channel conditions. The data rate and QoS were significantly affected by frequent handovers, outages, and call drops. The concept of moving relays was discussed and it has proven to be effective under Rayleigh channel conditions20. The use of moving relays for the downlink CNOMA system over Rician fading for users with half-duplex decode-and-forward protocol was examined. SSE analysis was performed considering power allocation, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and Rician factor. The system demonstrated approximately 12% improvement in SSE at 5 dB SNR over the OMA system. The simultaneous wireless information and power transfer (SWIPT)-assisted CNOMA was presented to harvest energy from various radio-frequency (RF) sources and use it for data transmission to end-users21 . A non-linear energy harvesting model with iSIC was developed for realistic analysis. The closed-form outage and throughput expressions were derived while considering different interferences to the relay. The impact of power allocation, distance, and power splitting ratios on system performance was analyzed, and the suggested system was compared with the OMA system.

User clustering in mmWave mMIMO-based NOMA was discussed to address the trade-off between spectral efficiency and energy efficiency22. A pair of user clustering algorithms was suggested to adaptively select the number of clusters and the number of UEs in each cluster. The presented user clustering attains 85% gain over the random clustering algorithms. For collaborative sensing and data collection across multiple UAVs, an HNOMA scheme with cooperative transmission in cell-free MIMO networks was presented23. The HNOMA cooperation strategy and task-splitting ratios were jointly optimized to minimize mission completion time.

RIS/IOS-aided NOMA systems for extended and full-space coverage

This section describes the advantages of integrating RIS, RIS-variants and IOS in NOMA systems. The capabilities of energy harvesting and information decoding in RIS-assisted HNOMA-TDMA wireless sensor networks were examined24. A joint iterative optimization framework was developed to optimize uplink and downlink power allocation, time durations, and RIS phase shifts. The system’s performance was compared with scenarios of HNOMA without RIS and RIS-assisted HNOMA with equal time allocations. The suggested RIS-aided HNOMA system for IoT has demonstrated improved performance compared to conventional systems. The authors explored the how improper resource management impacted the scalability and delay performance of IoT-assisted healthcare systems25. To address this issue, a RIS-enabled NOMA-based resource allocation was recommended. RIS improved coverage by overcoming blockages, while resource utilization was boosted by NOMA. The RIS installed indoors served only blocked UEs, whereas other UEs were served by NOMA pairs. This approach increased throughput by approximately 58% in the considered scenarios. An efficient compressive sensing-based detection scheme for multi-user MIMO with grant-free NOMA was discussed for massive machine-type communication26. To recover the sparse signals, an iterative reweighted scheme combined with the majorization-minimization algorithm was used. The suggested algorithm outperformed conventional multi-user detection schemes.

To minimize the transmit power of the RIS-aided MIMO-NOMA uplink system, the authors suggested a joint optimization for the RIS phase shifts, hybrid beamforming matrices, and transmit power allocation27. The authors also discussed extended SIC, where the users were grouped based on the effective channel gains and interference from various groups was suppressed through extended SIC. Simulations showed that the power consumption of the suggested methodology was lower than that of traditional methods. To improve the sensing and communication functions of integrated sensing and communication with a cell-free mMIMO framework, a novel NOMA-aided architecture was suggested28. Initially, joint beamforming and user pairing were designed for a single radar scenario to achieve the minimum spectral efficiency while meeting the sensing requirements. An iterative optimization algorithm based on semidefinite relaxation, rank-one constraint omission, and successive convex approximation was developed. Simulations showed that joint beamforming and user pairing achieved noticeable performance gains.

The authors integrated stacked-RIS as a breakthrough for holographic MIMO in next-generation networks29. Unlike conventional RIS, stacked-RIS architecture has multiple layers of metasurface, resulting in more degrees of freedom. This formulated a significant SSE improvement up to 150% compared to conventional RIS-aided MIMO systems. Rather than a conventional MIMO architecture, integrating stacked-RIS in the transmitter and receiver with precoding and combining set-up suppressed the interference and achieved SSE improvement with robust metasurface configurations. In addition to stacked-RIS, a flexible-RIS and an adaptive 3D shaped metasurface were integrated with a holographic MIMO architecture to mitigate deep fading in mmWave and THz communication30. This framework was found to be capable of supporting ultra-fast and low-power processing in time-varying and dynamic environments. The analytical framework for downlink flexible-RIS-based holographic MIMO systems for mmWave and THz frequency ranges were developed31. The joint optimization objective was formulated to minimize transmit SNR by iteratively updating phase and beamforming vectors. With the alternating optimization technique, up to 3 dB gain was achieved compared to the stacked-RIS architecture in terms of spectral efficiency.

A cooperative RIS and IOS-based finite-block-length covert NOMA was discussed, in which the RIS switches between RIS and IOS functions to introduce uncertainty32. Through simulations, it was demonstrated that effective covert throughput exceeded the baseline. An IOS-aided uplink NOMA for full space coverage and massive connectivity was designed33. The concept was extended to HNOMA-TDMA to support multiple energy splitting NOMA pairs. To maximize the fairness beamforming and resource allocation algorithms were developed for IOS-NOMA, and IOS-HNOMA. However, closed-form solutions were designed only for two-user scenarios. A generalised precoded quadrature amplitude modulation was incorporated into the IOS-NOMA system to enhance spectral efficiency34. The approximate bit-error rate expressions were derived and improvements in transmission rate was showcased compared to traditional IOS-NOMA system. The authors developed the IOS-enabled HNOMA for ultra-reliable and low-latency communications35. Using various pairings, the transmission power was minimized while meeting the rate requirements of individual UEs. The joint non-convex optimization problem was formulated for optimizing transmit power, beamforming, and block length. The recommended framework outperformed conventional RIS and OMA schemes.

MIMO-based NOMA systems

This section examines recent progress in MIMO-based NOMA systems. The performance of NOMA may suffer due to intra-group co-channel interference for the first decoding users. To improve the reliability of multi-user NOMA broadcast channels, Alamouti encoding was incorporated36. However, this results in a significant loss of spectral efficiency compared to traditional NOMA. An adaptive user grouping scheme for short packet communication in IOS-aided MIMO-NOMA with SWIPT was discussed37. The data users with different channel conditions were optimally grouped, whereas energy users harvest energy from the gNB. By optimizing the precoding matrix, IOS phase shifts, energy beamforming matrix and grouping variables, the spectral efficiency of the system was maximised through a convolutional neural network.

An age of information Q-learning based NOMA system was developed using reinforcement learning to optimize age of information in a power-domain NOMA IoT network38. This model used a generate-on-request protocol, making it a simple and scalable architecture. Simulation results demonstrated that the suggested system has enhanced throughput performance compared to conventional age of information-aided systems. The integration of cell-free mMIMO with RIS enhanced spectral efficiency in smart radio environments by eliminating cell boundaries and reducing high power consumption39. The joint optimization of gNB beamforming and RIS phase shifts made the system more robust and enhanced spectral efficiency and energy efficiency. The trade-off between HWDs and SSE was designed for the suggested system. This work provided a robust solution to support UAV-based mmWave communication. Research gaps and key contributions

Despite extensive literature on NOMA and its hybrid variants, several critical research gaps remain unaddressed. Most existing studies do not jointly account for HWDs and iSIC when analyzing system reliability - especially in MU-MIMO configurations. Moreover, optimal power allocation and dynamic user pairing strategies have not been thoroughly investigated under realistic channel scenarios. Although RIS have been explored for coverage enhancement, their half-space constraint fundamentally limits scalability. Comparatively, much less emphasis has been placed on IOS, which support 360 reflection/refraction yet remain underutilized in the context of NOMA-based MU-MIMO systems. The current research on IOS-assisted MU-MIMO HNOMA frameworks is notably limited, particularly in several key areas. These include the characterization of outage probability, the design of optimal beamforming at the gNB, the optimization of power allocation within NOMA layers, and the development of energy-efficient cooperative connectivity strategies for dense future IoT ecosystem. System model

Figure 1 illustrates the IOS-enhanced MU-MIMO C-HNOMA system. The system consists of a gNB equipped with M antennas, an IOS comprising N reflecting/refracting elements, and two single-antenna UEs for each paired transmission. The SUE is assumed to be located on the reflecting side of the IOS, whereas the WUE is positioned on the refracting side. The IOS can operate in three modes: energy splitting, mode switching, and time splitting40,41. In this work, the mode switching protocol is preferred due to its lower hardware complexity compared to the energy splitting and time splitting protocols. In mode switching mode, dedicated IOS components are assigned to serve the UEs on the reflecting and refracting sides42. For K UEs, the time frame T is divided into sub-frames, and within each sub-frame, two UEs (SUE and WUE) are paired using various pairing strategies. Implementation challenges and overhead analysis

IOS-aided transmission offers several advantages over RIS and mMIMO-based transmission. However, it faces several challenges, including interferences due to the coupling effect, multipath and non-linear distortions. By integrating hybrid active-passive IOS components with adaptive filters, the correlative interference can be mitigated. The proposed system needs perfect CSI to perform intelligent phase cancellation to beamform the signal to the desired user. But in practice, this requires uplink training with channel reciprocity and channel estimation techniques. This feedback overhead is practically risky for fast-varying channels. To minimize the overhead, blind RIS is deployed, which requires zero CSI. This model serves as“low-complexity”floor that neglects the instantaneous feedback and still produces acceptable performance. The control overhead in IOS can be further reduced by deploying finite-value phase resolutions based on feedback from the channel quality indicator (CQI). Based on the feedback CQI, the predefined weight vector at gNB and phase value at IOS can be adapted. This reduces the burden to control overhead and reaches performance of ideal IOS as resolutions () increases. The overhead for discrete-phase IOS is given by N , which depends on the number of bits required per element to specify the phase shift. The practical implementation of C-HNOMA requires a high time-frequency synchronization. Overall, the trade-off between performance and CSI acquisition for IOS-aided transmission, hardware calibration and time-frequency synchronization requirements persists as a challenge.

In this work, a near-optimal solution for a highly reliable, spectrally efficient system is developed. The proposed unified integral analytical framework validates the robustness of the proposed system under dual impairments. It is observed that an increase in the effect of impairments results in degradation. The quantified performance metrics validate the proposed solution, which can be used as a prerequisite for developing an experimental evaluation. This needs to be further considered for next-level implementation and the development of test-bed environments.

Conclusions

This study delivered a definitive and comprehensive performance evaluation of the novel IOS-enhanced MU-MIMO C-HNOMA framework. We rigorously validated analytical models against extensive simulations, deriving closed-form expressions that underpin precise system design. The investigation confirmed that optimal power allocation and the SWSW user pairing strategy are essential for maximizing SSE, achieving up to bps/Hz higher SSE over sub-optimal power allocation, while ensuring robust operation against iSIC and HWDs. Crucially, our analysis established a compelling energy-efficient scaling paradigm: leveraging the passive IOS elements (N) proved fundamentally superior to scaling active transmit antennas (M). This architectural choice enabled the proposed system to demonstrate massive performance leads, yielding superior throughput gains of up to dB in SNR and an bps/Hz SSE improvement over conventional IOS-aided C-HNOMA systems. While the ideal continuous-phase configuration establishes the theoretical upper bound for these gains, the cumulative analysis validates that the discrete-phase and blind IOS variants offer highly realizable pathways for practical deployment without prohibitive performance penalties. Notably, the framework exhibits robustness even in lower-complexity setups (single-antenna gNB/UEs), while scaling the antenna array (M) and IOS elements (N) effectively bridges the gap between practical impairments and the ideal benchmark.

Furthermore, the results identify the discrete-phase IOS-aided C-HNOMA as the optimal design trade-off; as the phase resolution increases, it asymptotically approaches the ideal performance limit. Consequently, the proposed architecture stands out as a highly efficient, scalable, and ultra-reliable solution, making it a necessary candidate technology for addressing the capacity and efficiency demands of the foundational layer in beyond-5G and 6G wireless networks. Future work will focus on integrating artificial intelligence-driven adaptive user pairing and power allocation mechanisms that dynamically respond to real-time network variations, alongside extending this study to incorporate SWIPT and generalized fading environments.

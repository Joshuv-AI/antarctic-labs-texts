Application of solutions obtained from tomato waste compost and wood ash as organic fertilizer in tomato cultivation
Authors: Binyat İsmayılzada, Aslıhan Çilingir Tütüncü, Andaç Kutay Saka, Salih Demırkaya, Harun Özer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe present study investigates the capacity of solutions derived from organic fertilisers to enhance the yield and quality of tomatoes (both grafted and ungrafted) cultivated in greenhouses. Three fundamental fertilisation applications were conducted: an organic tomato waste compost solution (CS), a wood ash solution (WAS), and an inorganic (NPK; 20-20-20 + ME) fertilisation control. The administration of organic fertilisers was conducted at a rate of 0.5 L per plant per week, formulated as a solution with an EC value of 2 dS m− 1, which is widely regarded as being optimal for the cultivation of tomatoes.ResultsOverall, wood ash solution has proven prominent in tomato cultivation. While the highest growth and yield (3.56 kg/plant) values were obtained from the WAS application, similar results were achieved with the control application. In general, grafted tomato seedlings showed higher results in terms of yield values. Wood ash solution stood out in terms of firmness, while compost solution stood out in terms of vitamin C values. The firmness and vitamin C values of grafted plants were also found to be lower than those of ungrafted plants.ConclusionOrganic liquid fertilizers can be considered as an alternative or complementary approach to chemical fertilizers. However, using WAS in combination with a strong nitrogen source on soils with low organic matter content will increase success.

Inroduction

Tomatoes are of significant importance to human health and are among the most widely cultivated crops in the world, due to their organic acids, phenolic compounds, vitamins, and various minerals. However, it is known that tomatoes contribute significantly to heart health and a stronger immune system in a healthy diet due to their high antioxidant and bioactive compound content [1–6]. The practice of cultivating vegetables using organic methods is gaining prominence as a viable alternative to conventional inorganic cultivation methods. In consideration of the favourable outcomes for human health and the environment, organic cultivation has started to emerge as a way of life [7–11]. The utilisation of chemical fertilisers has been demonstrated to exert a detrimental effect on the living structure of the soil, which consists of natural components. It is acknowledged that the restoration of damaged soil structure is an arduous process. However, it is asserted that practices which are oriented towards the enhancement of soil structure have the capacity to engender solutions to soil-related issues [12–15]. It is said that the microorganisms found in the root zone of plants have significant potential for biological control. They are associated with atmospheric nitrogen fixation, phosphorus solubilisation, production of the compound siderophore, synthesis of enzymes, and production of plant hormones such as auxin, gibberellin and cytokinin [16–21]. A recent study reporting that the application of organic fertilisers significantly reduces cases of Verticillium wilt is an example of the consensus among researchers. This is linked to a notable increase in the diversity of soil-borne fungal communities [11]. In vegetable cultivation, it is known that applying materials that complete fermentation processes, such as plant residues, green manure plants, compost residues, compost solutions, or nettle extracts, to the soil increases yield and quality along with organic matter. The corrective effects of increased organic matter on the physical and chemical properties of the soil are particularly related to the increase in the diversity of microorganisms [12–15, 22–28]. Additionally, it has been reported that CO₂ released during the fermentation of organic waste applied to the soil positively affects yield and quality by increasing photosynthesis [29].

The presence of organic matter in soil is of paramount importance for optimal fertility levels. Nevertheless, chemical fertilisers are the preferred option in tomato cultivation due to their ability to rapidly dissolve in water and their consequent rapid effect. This rapid effect has been shown to result in a dramatic decline in soil organic matter in the long term. This has been demonstrated to have a detrimental effect on soil salinity and soil biology [30, 31]. It is indeed challenging to meet the elevated nutrient requirements of tomatoes from a solitary source, and thus, the utilisation of diverse organic or inorganic nutrient sources as alternatives is imperative [32, 33]. Consequently, the utilisation of organic fertilisers in conjunction with inorganic fertilisers is advocated as a efficacious solution [33, 34]. It has been posited that nitrogen is one of the primary factors that limit productivity in long-term organic greenhouse tomato cultivation. organic materials such as manure, blood meal, wood ash, compost, and solid waste from biogas (anaerobic) production have been shown to have high potential to serve as alternative solutions for improving mineralization in organic tomato cultivation [15, 25–28, 35]. Research has demonstrated the efficacy of wood ash and compost solution in meeting the potassium and phosphorus requirements of organic vegetable cultivation [27, 28]. As demonstrated in the relevant literature, potassium has been shown to enhance both yield and quality by regulating the water balance of plants, stomatal conductance, and photosynthesis rate [36, 37]. In addition, it has been documented that wood ash has the capacity to elevate the pH level of soil, thereby stimulating microbial activity and mineralization within the soil matrix [38].

In recent years, with the increase in the use of grafted seedlings, it is hypothesised that the already high fertiliser requirement has increased even further. This phenomenon can be attributed to the observation that grafted plants exhibit an increased utilisation of plant nutrients. It has been demonstrated that grafted plants, which demonstrate resistance to a multitude of abiotic and biotic stress conditions, exhibit a heightened and accelerated growth period, thereby increasing biomass and yield [33, 39, 40]. In tomato cultivation, which requires high nutrient content, there is a need to thoroughly examine new alternatives for the application of organic fertilization methods.

The aim of this study is to contribute to the development of organic fertilization methods by evaluating the effects of organic and chemical fertilizers on plant growth, yield, and quality in tomato cultivation. In this study, the effects of wood ash and compost solution extracts were examined as alternatives to chemical fertilizer in greenhouse staked tomato cultivation. Furthermore, grafted and ungrafted tomato plants were examined to reveal how the effects of fertilizer applications occurred under different conditions.

Materials and methods

Experimental location

The study was conducted between April 1 and August 31, 2023, at the Department of Horticulture, Faculty of Agriculture, Ondokuz Mayı University, and soil analyses were performed in the laboratories of the Department of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition. The measurements and analyses related to the quality characteristics of tomato fruits were carried out in the physiology laboratory of the Department of Horticulture at the Faculty of Agriculture, Ordu University.

Experimental setup

The trial was conducted within a greenhouse, which possessed an area of 120 m². The greenhouse, which is covered with polycarbonate sheeting, has been used for the implementation of organic farming practices for a period of 18 years. Despite the absence of official certification for organic farming, the management practices on the site comply with the relevant standards (EC 2008). In the study, where the Samyeli F1 (Solanum lycopersicum L.) tomato variety was used as plant material, ready-to-plant seedlings (Demsan, Samsun, Türkey) grown using two different methods (grafted, ungrafted) were used. For grafted tomato seedlings, the Samyeli F1 variety was grafted onto the King Kong RZ F1 rootstock.

The soil was tilled to a depth of 30 centimetres thirty days prior to the planting of the seedlings. Raised beds measuring 1 m in width and 30 cm in height were meticulously prepared in the greenhouse. The drip irrigation pipes were installed in the prepared planting areas in a manner suitable for double-row planting, with a 25-centimetre spacing between drippers. The irrigation system employed was designed to deliver water in accordance with the soil moisture content throughout the growth cycle. Subsequently, in order to achieve effective weed and soil moisture control, the planting areas (raized beds) were covered with black PE (polyethylene) mulching material (1.30 m wide and 0.03 mm thick). The 4-leaf tomato seedlings, which had been cultivated in a controlled environment, were prepared for planting on 13 April 2023. The planting method involved a spacing configuration of 50 centimetres between rows, 50 centimetres within rows, and 90 centimetres between wide rows. Air temperature, soil temperature, and air humidity values were recorded in the greenhouses where the research was conducted using data loggers (Hobo, MX2301A, Onset, Bourne, USA). During the cultivation period, the mean air temperature in the greenhouse was found to be 22.4 °C, while the mean soil temperature was 23.5 °C. The range of temperatures recorded was from 12 to 41 °C. The relative humidity values ranged between 55% and 76%.

Fertilization practices

In the chemical fertilizer (control) application, 25 kg per decare of compound base fertilizer (Gübretaş, Istanbul, Turkey) was applied to the plots before planting. The fertiliser contains NPK (20-20-20) and microelements (zinc, iron, copper, manganese). Subsequently, fertilisers containing calcium and magnesium (Calsimagsi, Gübretaş, Istanbul, Turkey) and trace elements such as zinc, iron, boron, copper, manganese, and molybdenum (Combi Plus, Gübretaş, Istanbul, Turkey) were used. In the creation of the fertilisation programme, the nutrient removal rates from the soil by tomatoes were considered (20–30 kg/da N, 3–8 kg/da P2O5, 30–60 K2O, 3–6 kg/da MgO, and 10–20 kg/da CaO) [41]. Fertilization program created for chemical fertilization, compost solution, and wood ash solution

The compost solution (CS) was prepared using a 770 L plastic container. The pile method was utilised in the composting process [27, 28, 42]. In order to facilitate aeration during the composting process, holes with a diameter of 2 cm were drilled into the container, with these holes spaced 10 × 10 cm apart. The lowermost portion of the receptacle (20 cm) was filled with gravel (3–4 cm in diameter). In this manner, the liquid obtained from the waste was filtered through the gravel and subsequently emerged from the tap located at the base of the container. The compost was constructed by sequentially adding layers of raw material to the container. The initial layer comprised a 25-centimetre layer of pruning waste from organically grown Erva F1 tomato plants (stems and leaves, chopped into 5-centimetre pieces). This was followed by a 10-centimetre layer of animal manure (straw-bedded cattle manure), and finally a 2-centimetre layer of garden soil. The garden soil analysed at the commencement of the study (prior to utilisation) was employed (Table 3). The chemical properties of the animal manure are as follows: pH 7.4, EC 1.18 dS/m, N 0.29%, P 0.41%, K 0.71%, Ca 1.5%, Mg 8.7 ppm, Fe 66 ppm. The application of soil and animal manure was primarily aimed at enriching the compost with microorganisms, as reported by Alagöz et al. [27]. This process was continued until the container was at capacity. The material was finally subjected to a process of hydration, after which it was permitted to enter the composting process. The composting process was initiated in June of the preceding year and concluded in October. During this period, the material in the container underwent a weekly mixing process. During the composting process, the inside of the container was kept moist. To achieve this, 20 L of water were added each week. The liquid that had been extracted from the bottom of the container was then collected and stored in a separate container as a stock solution. This stock solution was then analysed according to the methodology outlined by Kacar and İnal [43], (Table 2). The compost tea (stock solution) was then diluted with water to achieve an electrical conductivity (EC) of 2 dS m− 1 (11% of the total solution was added). The solution was applied once a week at a rate of 0.5 L per plant.

Cultural practices

Pruning was executed during the growing period by the removal of suckers emerging from the leaf axils and yellowed and diseased leaves in stake tomato cultivation. Furthermore, the leaves located beneath the harvested clusters were completely removed. In order to combat the presence of green caterpillars, whiteflies, and aphids, an organically certified insecticide (containing the active ingredient Azadirachtin) (LASER, Corteva, Turkey) (10 ml/100 L water) was applied on two occasions, at 45-day intervals.

Plant measurements and observations

Plant height (cm) was determined by measuring the distance from the soil surface to the top of the plant in the designated measurement plants from the time of planting. Stem diameter was measured in mm using a digital caliper from the root collar. Leaf number was determined by counting the leaves attached to the main stem of the plants. Leaf chlorophyll concentration (CCI) was determined using a chlorophyll meter (CCM-200, Opti-Sciences, USA). In the study, the number of inflorescences, the number of days until the first flowering, and the fruit set rate (percentage of flowers turning into fruit) were determined.

In the present study, the quantity of fruit harvested from the initial harvest to the final harvest date was determined as the total number of fruits, and their weights were measured using a scale with a sensitivity of 0.1 g. The average fruit weight was determined in grams by taking the mean of the obtained fruit weights. The yield per plant was calculated in kilograms by summing the weights of the harvested fruits (marketable product quantity).

Fruit firmness was measured by removing the fruit peel from two different sides of the equatorial part of the fruit, then using a hand penetrometer (4301, Instron, USA) with a 7.9 mm tip. The force required to pierce the fruit was expressed in Newtons (N). For the soluble solids content (SSC) assay, 10 fruits from each plant in the replicate plots were chopped in an electric mixer, and the resulting fruit juice was filtered through cheesecloth. A sufficient volume of fruit juice was measured using a digital refractometer (PAL-1, McCormick Fruit Tech, Yakima, USA), and the results were expressed as a percentage (%). For the titratable acidity (TA) assay, In order to undertake the titration of acidity, a 10-ml sample was subjected to dilution with 10 ml of distilled water, prior to titration with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide (NaOH) until the pH reached 8.1. The quantity of NaOH utilised in the titration was expressed in terms of citric acid equivalent (g citric acid 100 mL-1). The ascorbic acid concentration was determined through a series of chemical reactions and calculations. To this end, 25 g of tomato fruit was macerated with 25 mL of oxalic acid (0.4%), chopped in a blender, and filtered through filter paper. The resulting fruit extract was supported with 2.6-dichloroindophenol and the reading was taken on a spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 518 nm [44]. The results are expressed in milligrams of vitamin C per 100 g of fresh weight.

Statistical analyses

The experiment on greenhouse cultivation and fruit quality was conducted according to the Randomized Parcels Design with 3 replications, each comprising 9 plants. The study utilized 3 different fertilization treatments (Control, Compost solution and wood ash solution) and 2 different seedling types (grafted and un-grafted). Measurements and analyses were performed on 162 tomato plants within the scope of the experiment (3 fertilizers x 2 seedlings x 3 replicates x 9 plants). Under these conditions, the data were determined to be suitable for variance analysis, and statistical analyses were performed according to two-way ANOVA. Differences between groups were determined using the Duncan multiple comparison test (p < 0.05). The licensed SPSS 21 software package from OMU was used for data analysis.

Growth

Vegetative growth parameters, including plant height, stem diameter, and leaves number, have been shown to reflect the plant’s long-term growth potential. In the present study, WAS (246.6 cm) and CS (242.6 cm) applications yielded higher values than the control group (227.1 cm) in terms of plant height (Table 4). The findings of the present study are largely consistent with those of earlier research conducted on tomato cultivation, particularly with regard to the growth, development, yield, and quality parameters of the tomato plant [15, 25–28, 45–49].

A range of combinations of organic and inorganic fertilizers were found to have significant effects on the vegetative growth parameters of tomatoes. As demonstrated in the research, organic fertilizers have been shown to have a substantial impact on the parameters under investigation, with significant increases observed in stem diameter, plant height, leaf number and leaf chlorophyll content. However, it is notable that the highest values were obtained in the instance of combined use of chemical and organic fertilizers (bio-fertilizer) [45]. This finding indicates that nutrients derived from organic sources can be utilized by plants in a more balanced and sustainable manner. However, the observation that chemical fertilizer application elicits higher values in physiological parameters that respond promptly, such as chlorophyll content, suggests that the rapid uptake of mineral nutrients into the plant may be a contributing factor to this outcome. This perspective is corroborated by numerous researchers [8, 34, 48]. Therefore, the use of organic fertilizers in combination with inorganic fertilizers is recommended as an effective solution [34, 45]. Specifically, it has been stated that the combined use of 25% cow manure and 75% inorganic fertilizer provides the optimal conditions for tomato production [34]. However, Obaid et al. [49] posit that combinations of different organic fertilizers can be a significant alternative to chemical fertilizers. The findings of the study demonstrated that the combined utilization of mycorrhizal fungi (5 g plant− 1) and poultry manure resulted in enhanced performance in plant growth parameters.

It has also been reported that the types of chemical and organic fertilizers, the applied doses of fertilizers, the times at which they are applied, and the application methods have significant effects. The utilization efficiency of fertilizer is significantly impacted by the application of excess fertilizer, owing to the occurrence of antagonistic interactions between nutrients [50, 51]. In the present study, indications of nutrient deficiency resulting from antagonism were detected in the leaves of plants administered chemical fertilizer, yet not in those subjected to organic fertilizer.

Growth and productivity

The highest yield per plant was obtained with the wood ash solution (WAS; 3.56 kg/plant), followed by the control application (3.36 kg/plant), while the lowest yield was determined with the compost solution (CS; 3.15 kg/plant). With regard to the total fruit number, the wood ash solution (WAS; 28.3 fruits/plant) yielded the highest value, the control (24.8 fruits/plant) remained at a moderate level, and the lowest fruit count was observed in the compost solution (23.3 fruits/plant). The application of fertilizers was found to yield divergent results in terms of both yield and certain fruit characteristics. The mean fruit weight was found to be highest in the compost solution (135.6 g), similar in the control (134.6 g), and lowest in the wood ash solution (126.5 g). The results of the study indicate that the wood ash solution increased the yield of fruit, whilst the chemical fertilizer and compost solutions had similar effects on the average weight of the fruit.

Our study showed that chemical fertilizer use decreased compared to the WAS application. These results are thought to be related to fertilizer use efficiency. This is because it has been determined that increasing fertilizer doses in soilless tomato cultivation decrease the utilization rates of plant nutrients by tomato plants and fruits. Fertilizer use efficiency is also expressed as the nutrient uptake capacity of plants and may vary for different plant nutrients. This situation can often be attributed to antagonism caused by excessive fertilizer [50, 51].

The utilization of diverse types of farm manure and worm castings, in conjunction with recommended chemical fertilizers, has been documented to enhance yield by 134.07% in comparison to the control. The application of organic fertilizers has been demonstrated to enhance fruit quality to a considerable degree, whilst concomitantly effecting a substantial reduction in the use of chemical fertilizers (14.3%) [4]. In a study investigating the potential of green manure as an alternative to chemical fertilizers, conventionally grown tomato plants also achieved the highest stomatal conductance and yield [8]. In contrast to the findings of this study, it has been posited that green manure significantly improves tomato plant physiology and fruit quality characteristics. The study attributes the increase in yield to the soil’s enhanced water retention capacity, which is indirectly associated with elevated stomatal conductance and photosynthesis rate [24]. It is important to note that practices which increase microbial activity may not be sustainable in the long term. This phenomenon may be attributed to elevated nitrogen requirements, consequent to diminished nitrogen mineralization, particularly in scenarios where the C/N ratio within the soil matrix is elevated. This phenomenon may prove particularly salient in wood ash applications, which have been demonstrated to stimulate microbial activity [38, 52]. In studies where organic fertilizers are used as alternatives, nitrogen may be one of the main factors limiting productivity in long-term organic greenhouse tomato cultivation. The success of organic tomato cultivation may be contingent on the presence of soil with a high organic matter content. Alternatively, the application of organic material with a high nitrogen content has been demonstrated to enhance the efficacy of the process. It has been demonstrated that the incorporation of materials with a low C/N ratio into the soil is of significance, as they have been shown to possess considerable potential [15, 25, 26, 35, 38, 52]. Our study also shows that soil organic matter (5.8) is high. The results of the study show a decrease in favor of chemical fertilizers (Table 3). Furthermore, it is hypothesised that an elevated magnesium content is instrumental in enhancing yield. This is due to the fact that magnesium, being the central atom of chlorophyll, plays a crucial role in photosynthesis; consequently, a deficiency in magnesium immediately reduces chlorophyll levels and impairs photosynthesis. The natural consequence of this is stunted plant growth and yield loss [53–56].This situation is supported by the literature in terms of its reflection on yield values. As demonstrated in Tables 2 and 3, analysis of soil and fertilizer samples revealed elevated magnesium levels in both the WAS and control treatments. The WAS treatment was found to yield the highest results, with the control treatment ranking second (see Table 5). However, the hypothesis is put forward that the application of the WAS treatment in liquid form, in small amounts on a weekly basis, results in a reduction in the rate of nutrient leaching into the soil.

A similar study, which reviewed a substantial number (107) of research articles on this topic, demonstrated that the effect of organic fertilisers on tomato yield and quality is complex and depends on soil organic matter, total soil nitrogen, types of organic fertilisers, and other variables. In addition to the aforementioned points, it has been determined that the utilisation of organic fertilisers can result in a 42.2% increase in tomato yield [57]. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the positive effects of organic matter on soil physical properties and nutrient use efficiency also indirectly enhance yield and quality [57–59]. It is evident that an increase in soil organic matter is concomitant with an increase in tomato yield. Although this increase is gradual, it has been linked to the organic matter’s ability to enhance nutrient and water retention capacity [57, 60, 61]. Soil pH is another significant factor influencing tomato water and mineral nutrient uptake. It has been documented that an acidic soil pH (below 7) has been shown to increase yield by 1.82 times in comparison to alkaline soils (above 7). Furthermore, the application of organic matter has been demonstrated to reduce nitrogen volatilization by altering the structure of the soil microbial community involved in nitrification processes [57, 62, 63]. In the present study, no significant change in pH was observed; however, it was determined that the addition of high-WAS in soils with high organic matter content increases yield and quality. This phenomenon is believed to be primarily attributable to the augmented quantity and biodiversity of microorganisms present within the soil organic matter. This finding is consistent with the conclusions of other studies in the field [15, 27].

The utilization of organic fertilizers has been demonstrated to facilitate a balanced and gradual mineralization process within the soil. This can assist in achieving equilibrium between vegetative and generative development in tomato plants. This balance is pivotal to productivity and quality [25, 26, 35]. In the present study, the lowest yield values were determined from the compost solution. It is hypothesized that the primary material employed in the compost preparation process (namely, tomato waste) exerts a significant effect. As previously stated, tomatoes are a plant species that exhibit high levels of nutrient consumption. It is hypothesized that the comprehensive use of a variety of vegetables, wild legumes, nettles, food preparation waste, and other decomposable waste in the composting process will significantly enrich the content of the compost solution (compost tea).

In our study, tomato waste was chosen because it is the most widely produced vegetable in the world and generates significant waste [64]. However, enriching the content of compost tea depends on the variety of waste used [25]. Using a single waste material has limited our composting processes. However, the solid application of composted tomato plant residues increased pH and water-holding capacity, thereby increasing total N and C uptake. It is suggested that tomato plant residues can reduce chemical fertilization by 50% [65]. Similarly, it was determined that the application of organic fertilizers in solid form had a greater effect on the yield and nutrient uptake of tomato plants compared to liquid applications [66].

Fruit quality characteristics

The highest fruit firmness value was obtained in the wood ash solution (WAS; 14.91 N), followed by the compost solution (CS; 13.34 N), while the lowest hardness was determined in the control application (12.26 N). This indicates that the wood ash application, in particular, made the fruit tissue harder. Although not statistically significant, the highest soluble dry matter and titratable acidity values (SDA) were also measured in the wood ash solution (WAS).

This finding suggests that potassium-rich nutrition can enhance the accumulation of soluble dry matter in fruit. Potassium exerts a direct influence on numerous processes, including water balance, nutrient balance (optimizing nitrogen use), nutrient transport, and photosynthesis, thereby enhancing both yield and quality [67, 68]. A further study has indicated that a significant proportion of the potassium fertilization applied is transferred to the fruit, thereby significantly affecting fruit quality [51, 67]. The present study established that fruit quality increased with the WAS application, which has a high K content.

With regard to the measurement of titratable acidity, the wood ash solution (0.53 g citric acid/100 mL) exhibited the highest value, the control (0.49 g) maintained a medium level, and the lowest acidity was observed in the compost solution (0.44 g). With regard to vitamin C content, the highest value was determined in the compost solution (13.9 mg 100/g), followed by the wood ash solution (13.0 mg 100/g), while the lowest value was measured in the control application (12.4 mg 100/g). This finding indicates that the application of compost is a more effective strategy for increasing ascorbic acid accumulation in fruit.

In the context of tomato cultivation, the application of biological fertilization, comprising mycorrhizal fungi at a rate of 5 g per plant, has been demonstrated to exert a substantial influence on various physiological and biochemical parameters. The study observed a notable increase in the number of leaves per plant (19.9), the cluster weight, and the yield per plant (2.8 kg). Additionally, significant enhancements were recorded in the levels of total soluble solids (%9.31), acidity (3.93%), and vitamin C content (22.2 mg/100 g). Another biological fertilizer, Bacillus spp., resulted in the highest plant height (184.7 cm), cluster number (8.89), and fruit number (121.1). While organic fertilization (comprising palm leaf waste, vermicompost, and mushroom waste) applications yielded higher values than the control, they lagged behind biological fertilizers [49]. A similar set of results was obtained in a study that, like the present one, revealed the differing effects of different organic fertilizers. While the firmness values of conventionally grown tomatoes were found to be higher, acidity, soluble solids content, and vitamin C values were determined to be higher in organically grown tomatoes [69]. The utilization of organic materials, such as compost and wood ash, has emerged as a significant alternative for enhancing the organic and mineral matter content of soil, thereby improving its physical structure and augmenting its nutrient content [15, 25–28]. However, it has been hypothesised that nitrogen limits its efficiency in tomato cultivation. It is imperative that techniques aimed at improving mineralization in organic farming be developed. It has been demonstrated that the application of different organic fertilizers, or combinations of organic and inorganic fertilizers, will significantly increase growth, development, yield and fruit quality [31, 34, 35, 49, 70–74].

Studies recommending the combined use of organic and chemical fertilizers in tomato cultivation include Wu et al. [34] and Neofytou et al. [45], while studies examining the effects of organic and chemical fertilizers on different plant parameters include [8]. Furthermore, there are studies indicating that organic fertilizers used at lower rates with chemical fertilizers improve soil quality, thereby increasing yield and quality [34]. However, it is stated that the combined use of different organic fertilizers can provide significant advantages in tomato cultivation, which requires high amounts of plant nutrients. Specifically, the application of Bacillus spp. (10 g plant⁻¹) and Mycorrhizal fungi (5 g plant⁻¹) together with different plant residues significantly increases quality in tomato cultivation [49]. Similarly, in our study, quality improved with organic fertilizers. However, unlike our study, it was determined that the application of organic fertilizers in solid form had a greater effect on the yield and nutrient uptake of tomato plants compared to liquid applications [66].

Conclusions

The results obtained show that organic applications provide certain advantages over chemical fertilizer applications in terms of plant growth and some yield components, but that this effect is not the same for all parameters. The fruit set rate, number of fruits, and yield values have demonstrated that wood ash application has an effect on productivity. In contrast, it has been determined that the control and compost applications have a more pronounced effect on fruit size. When evaluated in terms of fruit quality characteristics, the application of wood ash was found to be particularly effective in enhancing fruit flesh firmness, soluble solids content, and titratable acidity. In contrast, compost application resulted in higher values with regard to vitamin C content.

It is hypothesised that for the utilization of waste materials containing various organic plant nutrients such as compost and wood ash to be efficacious, the soil in which they are applied must possess a high organic matter content. This phenomenon can be attributed to the fact that a high organic matter content fosters microbial diversity and contributes to pH regulation. Furthermore, by facilitating the uptake of plant nutrients, they promote sustainable growth and help prevent the occurrence of stress conditions. However, given the high nutrient requirements of tomato plants, it is evident that the exclusive utilisation of organic fertilisers can engender suboptimal conditions in soils with diminished organic matter content and reduced soil vitality. In conclusion, the present study has demonstrated that WAS and CS, which support increased crop yield, can be considered as alternatives or supplements to chemical fertilisers under appropriate conditions.

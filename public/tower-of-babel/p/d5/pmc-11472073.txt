How does regional cooperation affect green total factor productivity?—Evidence from the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay area in China
Authors: Peng Qiu, Chenghui Tang, Xiaofang Tu, Joep Crompvoets
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
As a key regional development strategy, regional cooperation affects the sustainable development of city-regions. This study uses social network analysis, spatial analysis, and negative binomial regression analysis to investigate the impacts of regional cooperation on green total factor productivity (GTFP) in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area (GBA). The main findings are as follows. First, regional cooperation is growing closer, unfolding a trend of networked and polycentric development patterns in the GBA. Second, the GTFP of most cities exhibits an upward trend, indicating a continuous improvement in green economic development in the GBA. Third, different types of regional cooperation have heterogeneous impacts on GTFP, with economic cooperation being essential for regional GTFP. Overall, this study provides a systematic and comprehensive analysis of the environmental effects of regional cooperation.

Introduction

Globalization and informatization have profoundly transformed the roles and interrelationships of states, regions and cities since 1970 [1]. International competitions have evolved into comprehensive competitions among urban agglomerations [2]. Urban agglomerations have achieved rapid economic booms, but have also faced unprecedented environmental problems, which constrain the sustainable development of regional economy [3]. These problems transcend the scope of individual cities and generate spatial correlations through spillover effects. The traditional management model faces fragmentation issues, posing serious challenges for local governments in coping with complex environmental problems [4]. In order to solve transboundary environmental pollution problems, many countries have adopted several cooperative strategies such as formulating cooperative agreements and forming strategic alliances. The USA has adopted unanimity rules to manage collective interests and common resources and has resolved water governance conflicts through river compacts [5]. The Baltic Sea Region in Europe has founded the Council of Baltic Sea States (CBSS), a bottom-up strategic alliance that helped to construct intergovernmental cooperation networks and formulate transnational cooperation initiatives. The rapid growth of these cooperation initiatives has provided the frameworks for environmental governance and sustainability in the Baltic Sea Region [6]. In China, upper-level governments have promoted the implementation of integrated regional cooperation agreements through their executive authority, and local governments have actively carried out common cooperation efforts under the unified policy framework [7], which is of great significance in reducing environmental pollution and promoting regional economic development. In this context, regional cooperation has become a focus of both academia and governments.

Scholars have conducted diversified explorations in regional cooperation and green economic development. Nonetheless, there are some gaps in the existing literature. First, scholars have tended to adopt case studies to analyze the characteristics [8], mechanisms [9], and models [10] of regional cooperation, but have not quantified regional cooperation. The research findings are often limited to specific institutional contexts and socio-economic environments and are difficult to generalize and replicate. Second, although scholars have analyzed the impacts of industrial agglomeration [11], environmental regulation [12], and technological innovation [13,14] on urban GTFP, they have seldom unveiled the impacts of government behavior on GTFP from the perspective of regional cooperation. Third, some scholars have focused on the socio-economic effects of regional cooperation and analyzed the effects of regional cooperation on the quality of public services [15], economic growth [16], and social equity [17], but less on the environmental effects of regional cooperation.

To address these gaps, this study utilizes spatial analysis, social network analysis, and negative binomial regression analysis to explore the impacts of regional cooperation on GTFP and its intrinsic mechanisms in the GBA based on unique cooperation news data. This study seeks to advance the research in the field of regional cooperation by elucidating three pivotal questions: (1) What are the spatial and temporal evolutionary characteristics of regional cooperation in the GBA? (2) What are the spatial and temporal evolutionary characteristics of GTFP in the GBA? (3) Does regional cooperation affect GTFP? If so, are there any heterogeneous impacts of different types of regional cooperation?

This study contributes to the literature in the following three ways: (1) In the research perspective, compared with the previous studies, this study analyzes the impacts of regional cooperation on urban GTFP from the perspective of government behavior, which deepens the understanding of the environmental performance of regional cooperation. (2) In the research methodology, this study employs big data technology to capture news on local intergovernmental cooperation from news reports, which is then quantified using social network analysis to offer a more accurate measurement of the strength of regional cooperation linkages. (3) In the research findings, the impacts of various forms of regional cooperation on GTFP are heterogeneous, with regional economic cooperation having the greatest impact and regional social cooperation having the least impact. These results enrich regional cooperation theory and point pathways for realizing regional sustainable development through cooperation.

Theoretical foundation and literature review

Regional cooperation under institutional collective action framework

The institutional collective action (ICA) framework has attracted widespread attention in recent years. It combines the theories of collective action, public economics, transaction costs, and social embeddedness and advocates understanding the collective action dilemmas under institutional arrangements from the perspective of transaction costs and cooperation risks [18], providing an explanatory theoretical framework for regional cooperation research [19]. Based on the ICA framework, regional cooperation is a collective action in which each region participates as a special “individual” or “group” and regards the governments as “rational people” [20]. Feiock (2013) points out that the behavioral motivation for local government cooperation originates from the net benefit of local governments, that is, the rational choice to obtain a higher cooperation benefit based on the consideration of transaction costs and risks. Transaction costs include information, bargaining, enforcement, and agency costs, and cooperation risks are affected by the nature of the cooperation problems, impacted jurisdiction compositions, and the institutional contexts [21].

From the perspective of ICA framework, regional cooperation alleviates the negative externalities caused by fierce competition among local governments, helping to provide the greatest benefits for the least transaction costs [22]. Previous research shows that in the governance of public affairs, inter-city governments form an incentive-compatible network structure through communication, negotiation, and consultation, which reduces transaction costs, increases cooperation benefits, and solves the collective action dilemmas caused by fragmented administrative authorities [23]. Under the ICA framework, regional cooperative agreements are tangible vehicles that portray network relationships. Local governments usually establish informal cooperative relationships or formal contractual partnerships through cooperative frameworks, opinions, and declarations [24]. Such cooperative agreements are usually documented on official government websites and reported by various news media reports [25]. Therefore, cooperation news can be viewed as an important tool for objectively recording regional cooperative behavior. Based on the ICA framework, regional cooperation refers to an institutional arrangement that coordinates actions among local governments through cooperation agreements, builds cooperation networks, and realizes the maximization of cooperation benefits.

Impacts of regional cooperation on GTFP

Regional cooperation weakens the barriers to the flow of production factors and reduces intercity transaction costs, contributing to the formation of a regional layout with sustainable and high-quality development [26]. Jonas and Ward (2007) believe that regional cooperation generates positive externalities, including the sharing of strategic resources and infrastructure and the agglomeration of production factors, which effectively promote the coordinated development of the regional economy and environment [27]. A large amount of theoretical and empirical research has demonstrated the importance of regional collaboration in achieving sustainable development [28]. Bavaria (Germany) has promoted green technology innovation and the utilization of renewable energy through regional cooperation and has also promoted the optimal allocation of resources and the transformation of industrial structures, thus enhancing GTFP [29]. Tan et al. (2022) conduct an empirical study on major urban agglomerations in China and point out that regional cooperation has spatial spillover effects, which significantly boosts the growth of the green economy in local and neighboring cities and considerably raises the level of regional green innovation [30]. Data sources and research methodology

Study area

To investigate the impacts of regional cooperation on GTFP, this study selects the GBA, one of the most open urban areas in the globe and the core engine of China's economy [42], as the study area. The GBA is comprised of 2 Special Administrative Region of Hong Kong and Macao and 9 cities from Guangdong Province, including Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Jiangmen, Foshan, Zhongshan, Dongguan, Huizhou, Guangzhou, and Zhaoqing (Fig. 2).Fig. 211 cities in GBA.Fig. 2

11 cities in GBA.

Due to the “one country, two systems” policy, the economic, political, and legal systems of Hong Kong and Macao have vast differences from the other nine cities. To promote the integration process of regional development and complementary advantages among different cities, central and local governments have implemented a series of institutional arrangements for regional cooperation, including joint meetings, economic and trade agreements, regional agreements, facility sharing, and regional co-construction, which provide an important guarantee of the promotion of regional cooperation [43]. In 2019, the promulgation of “the Outline Development Plan for the GBA” marked the transition of the GBA from the market and institutional integration to the stage of all-round integration in multiple fields, including the economy, institution, culture, society, and ecology. This plan elevated the integrated development model, originally confined to Guangdong Province, into a cross-institutional cooperation strategy among the three regions at the national level, which has become an important attempt to foster regional collaborative development that transcends institutional boundaries under globalization [44]. In this context, this study takes the GBA as the study area to clarify the development status and spatial evolution structure of regional cooperation. It can also serve as a reference for understanding the developmental processes of a green economy.

Data sources

This study investigates the impacts of regional cooperation on the green economy in the GBA using data from news media and statistical yearbooks. The regional cooperation data is mainly obtained from government websites, search engines, and news websites. The green economy data and social economy data are mainly from the statistical yearbooks and the official statistics bureau websites. These data are collected from January 2010 to December 2019.

Regional cooperation data

As tracking the behavior of regional cooperation is challenging, we utilize cooperation news to explore regional cooperation, as suggested by Zhang et al. (2021) [25]. These data objectively capture real regional cooperation behavior. Internet development has made regional cooperation behaviors important disclosures of government websites and online media reports, that is, cooperation news records regional cooperation behaviors, such as official exchanges, joint economic cooperation projects, joint policy formulation, and infrastructure co-construction, reflecting the frequency and results of local government interactions. The news recording these regional cooperation behaviors provides data source for this study. Cooperation news has limitations in reflecting all regional cooperation behaviors and revealing complex institutional linkages. However, owing to the advantages of the authenticity, richness, and reliability of these data, it provides a new tool for analyzing regional cooperation and is thus widely used by scholars [45].

Three approaches are employed in this study to collect data on regional cooperation using crawler software. These approaches complement each other, making an effort to improve the comprehensiveness of data collection. First, we entered the target cities’ names into the official government website. For example, we searched Hong Kong in the government website of Shenzhen and searched Shenzhen in the government website of Hong Kong to obtain cooperation data between Shenzhen and Hong Kong. Second, we employed keyword combinations of different cities, such as Shenzhen and Hong Kong, on Google to obtain the cooperation news. Third, we searched the same keywords on two influential news websites, China News and Southern News, to obtain cooperation data. We filtered 6787 cooperation news articles and eliminated duplicate and irrelevant news articles through manual screening to construct a regional cooperation news database.

Green economy data and social economy data

GTFP is the dependent variable and is calculated through the green economy data, which involves two major indicators of inputs and outputs. Social economy data contains other indicators that influence GTFP, such as indicators of scale, economy, technology, and industry. “China City Statistical Yearbook”, “China Energy Statistical Yearbook”, and “China Environmental Statistical Yearbook” provide us with data for 9 cities in Guangdong Province, while the data in Hong Kong and Macao come from the local Statistics Bureau, respectively.

Research methodology

Social Network analysis

Regional cooperation is based on relational data and therefore requires the selection of an appropriate measurement method. Social network analysis finds an explanatory path for regional cooperation based on relationships, taking the social network itself and the relationships among various actors as the starting point. It adds to the analysis of the relational interaction structure in addition to quantitative analysis, which is viewed as one of the mainstream analytical approaches for analyzing relational data and is highly compatible with regional cooperation research [46]. Therefore, this study draws on social network analysis to quantify cooperative networks. We follow Zhang et al. (2023) [7] and adopt the node degree centrality CD(Ni) to measure regional cooperation, which indicates the city node's importance within the network. This study uses UCINET software to calculate the node degree centrality, aiming to reveal the spatial and temporal evolution of regional cooperation in the GBA.(1)CD(Ni)=∑j=1gxij(x≠j)g‐1In Equation (1), the degree centrality of node i means the total number of direct connections between i and other g-1 nodes.

Measurement of GTFP

GTFP is a significant indicator of green economic development. This study follows Liu et al. (2021) [47] and uses a super-SBM model to calculate the urban GTFP within the GBA. This model does not require inputs to be scaled down or outputs to be scaled up in the same proportions and considers the slack in inputs and outputs, which is suitable for calculations in the case of undesired outputs [48]. To quantify the impacts of regional cooperation, we employ the Negative Binomial (NB) regression model to explore the impacts of regional cooperation on GTFP. The dependent variable in an ordinary least squares (OLS) regression model must satisfy the assumption of a normal distribution. The counting model, including the Poisson and NB regression models, is more appropriate than the OLS model [52]. The Poisson regression model requires equal means and variances, which is not suitable for over-dispersed data. The NB regression model considers the endogeneity problem caused by omitted variables and relaxes the Poisson regression requirement of equal means and variances, thus providing a better fit for dispersed data. The regression model is as follows:(2)GTFPit+1=β0+β1Networkit+β2Xit+ηi+λi+εitIn Equation (2), GTFPit+1 is the GTFP of city i in year t+1 from 2010 to 2019. Networkit is is the independent variable, Xit represents the series of control variables. ηi and λi are the fixed effects of city i and year t. β0 is a constant and εit is the error term. The fixed effects model, which properly accommodates the heterogeneity of the city and time variables, is suggested by the Hausman test to be the most appropriate model for this study.

The evolution of GBA's urban centrality.

With the implementation of a series of cooperation agreements, such as the “Guiding Opinions on Deepening Pan-Pearl River Delta Regional Cooperation”, regional cooperation in the GBA has become increasingly close from 2010 to 2019, gradually forming a new trend in networked development. First, the regional cooperation linkages in the GBA are hierarchical. Although there are fewer cities with high-level cooperation linkages, the strength of the linkages is stronger, whereas there are more cities with low-level cooperation linkages and the strength of the linkages is weaker. Second, regional cooperation linkages in the GBA are uneven, with high-level linkages mainly concentrated among economically developed core cities such as Hong Kong and Shenzhen, while economically backward peripheral cities such as Zhaoqing and Huizhou generally have lower linkage intensities and have not yet formed close linkages with other cities in the GBA. Finally, some intercity cooperation linkages have gradually broken through the limitations of geographic distance with characteristics of geographic jumping. In 2010, cooperation linkages mostly occurred in geographically neighboring cities, and since then, long-distance cooperation linkages, such as Foshan, Guangzhou, and Hong Kong, have gradually increased.

Node degree centrality indicates the significance of the nodes inside the network. First, Hong Kong and Shenzhen have a consistently higher degree centrality than the other nine cities, making them key actors in the regional cooperation network. As the core engines of economic development, Shenzhen and Hong Kong have been cooperating closely with other cities in political, economic, and ecological fields, and have dominated the flow of regional knowledge. Second, Guangzhou's degree of centrality increased continuously. With the integration of the twin cities in Guangdong Province and the implementation of a series of strategic plans in the GBA, Guangzhou's cooperation linkages with other cities have increased, gradually leaping from the periphery to the core of the regional cooperation network. Influenced by geographic location, economic development, human capital, and infrastructure, the degree centrality of peripheral cities, such as Zhaoqing and Huizhou, has been maintained at a low-level, and these cities have always been on the fringes of the regional cooperation network. Generally, the degree centrality of all cities in GBA increases to varying degrees; thus, the cooperation network shows a polycentric development pattern.

Evolution of regional GTFP

This study visually identifies the spatial and temporal evolution of the regional GTFP using ArcGIS software (Fig. 4). In general, the level of green economic development in the GBA is continuously increasing. First, the GTFP of most cities shows an upward trend, which means that the environmental quality of the GBA has been improving and the coordination between economic development and environmental protection has been increasing. Second, the levels of GTFP in Foshan, Zhongshan, Shenzhen, and Macao are high. These cities have sufficient innovation vitalities, which contribute to applying green production technologies, reducing environmental pollution, and improving the efficiency of resource utilization. Finally, Huizhou had the lowest GTFP. It is a heavily industrial city with the Daya Bay petrochemical industry base, due to the fact that petroleum refining requires higher energy consumption, resulting in heavy pollutant emissions and restricting the green economic transformation. Therefore, the GTFP of core cities in geopolitical proximity surpasses that of peripheral cities, as the former facilitates infrastructure sharing and construction activities to enhance the utilization of energy, thus promoting the sustainable development of the GBA.Fig. 4Spatial and temporal evolution of GTFP in GBA.Fig. 4

Factors influencing GTFP

According to the regression results, regional cooperation contributes significantly to urban GTFP, which is consistent with H1 (Table 4). This result verifies Klijn's (2010) view that regional cooperation networks contribute to improving governance performance [53]. Possible explanations are as follows. First, regional cooperation has spillover effects. A city with a high degree of green economic development has radiation and spillover effects on neighboring cities through technology transfer and knowledge spillovers; neighboring cities will also learn from the relevant green development experience, introduce upstream and downstream industries, and upgrade the industrial structure through imitation, learning, and communication, ultimately improving the overall degree of regional green economic development [54]. Zhongshan develops high-quality industrial projects and promotes industrial structure upgrading through the construction of Cuiheng industrial parks. Based on the manufacturing advantages, Zhongshan has aligned innovative resources with Shenzhen, helping to promote sustainable economic development. Second, regional cooperation has a scaling effect. The co-construction of intercity railways, logistics networks, ports, and other infrastructure for regional cooperation has optimized the regional spatial structure and reduced transaction costs, enabling the free flow of capital, labor, and other resources. For example, the Pearl River Delta's (PRD) intercity rail system has created a 1-h metropolitan loop, with Guangzhou serving as the hub. The convenience of rail transport also facilitates investment and industrial structure transformation in peripheral cities, thus promoting the regional GTFP. Third, regional cooperation has a regulatory effect. Regional cooperation typically involves the implementation of environmental regulations through regional agreements. Due to the unified standards, environmental regulation can prevent peripheral cities from becoming “pollution havens” [55]. In terms of control variables, city size, urban industrial structure, and transport accessibility significantly contribute to GTFP. City size is strongly correlated with GTFP. Larger cities tend to have higher levels of human capital, which leads to the upgrading of labor efficiency, production, and R&D, thus promoting regional GTFP. With regard to the urban industrial structure, the GBA has stepped up its efforts in energy restructuring and industrial transformation, particularly in the transformation and upgrading of highly polluting and energy-consuming enterprises, which has promoted reasonable control of pollutant emissions and further optimization of the regional ecological environment. In addition, the construction and operation of transport infrastructure in the GBA has improved regional transport accessibility, shortened intergovernmental commuting distances, and facilitated intercity cooperation. However, the regression results also show that the effect of urban investment openness on the GTFP is significantly negative. The pollution-haven hypothesis states that enterprises producing significant amounts of pollution typically relocate to areas with lax environmental regulations. Compared to Western countries, China is still dominated by labor-intensive industries, therefore, urban investment openness aggravates the migration of polluting firms rather than bringing about the transformation and upgrading of green technologies.

Economic cooperation aims to bridge regional economic gaps. Infrastructure interconnection, cooperation platform integration, and cluster development of emerging industries strengthen the support for GTFP, which proves H2. For example, the construction of major cooperation platform in the GBA, including Hengqin, Qianhai, Nansha, and Hetao, optimizes the industrial layout, reduces transaction costs, and realizes the transfer of assets, labor, and other factors from the core cities to peripheral cities, thus stimulating urban green innovation vitality. Economic cooperation is directly related to urban green economic development. It enhances green product and process innovation through firms’ knowledge transfer activities [13] and produces the effect of scale economy through the agglomeration of talent, capital, and other resources. Therefore, it is considered to have the strongest impact on GTFP.

Social cooperation, including the affairs of culture, education, and medical care, has a positive impact on GTFP, which proves H3. However, social cooperation has the weakest impact, probably because of the significant differences in language and culture among Guangdong Province, Macao, and Hong Kong. Guangdong Province has developed distinctive cultures such as Guangfu, Kejia, and Chaoshan, while Macao and Hong Kong have also integrated Western cultural traits and are more culturally diversified, partially hindering communication and exchanges in the GBA. In particular, cultural impact is a subtle process, therefore, the impact of social cooperation on GTFP often needs to be revealed over time.

Institutional cooperation is an intercity exchange process involving regional agreements, urban management experiences, spatial plans, and other affairs. For instance, the implementation of the “Outline Development Plan for the GBA” indicates the GBA's development strategic positioning, points out the direction of environmental governance actions, and contributes to GTFP, which proves H4. However, institutional cooperation has a minor impact on GTFP, mainly due to the “one country, two systems” policy and different legal frameworks, making the GBA's institutional coordination cost relatively high, which partially hampers effective cooperation promotion. Therefore, enhancing institutional and cultural integration is a crucial step toward achieving coordinated economic and social development in the GBA. The government should strengthen top-level design and policy leadership and reduce institutional barriers to regional cooperation through spatial plans and supporting policy systems, thus improving regional green economic development.

Environmental cooperation and GTFP also show a clear positive correlation. Through a series of environmental cooperation agreements, local governments in the GBA have reduced pollutant emissions, improved the level of regional environmental regulation, and realized a virtuous circle of economy and ecology, supporting H5. For example, local governments strengthen the regional joint prevention and control of ozone, PM2.5 pollution, and so on, by meeting the air quality standards of the PRD, forming a new sharing pattern of the regional ecological environment.

Conclusion

This study is dedicated to exploring the impacts of regional cooperation on GTFP. Four main findings are unveiled.(1)Regional cooperation in the GBA has become increasingly close and has gradually produced a networked and polycentric developmental pattern, with regional cooperation showing obvious hierarchies, unevenness, and geographical jumps.(2)The GTFP in the GBA intensified over time, indicating an increase in green economic development. However, there are still large differences in GTFP among cities.(3)Regional cooperation has a significant positive impact on GTFP. Regional cooperation has spillover, scaling, and regulatory effects, which help to promote the flow of production factors, optimize the industrial structure, and reduce environmental pollution emissions, thus enhancing the level of green economic development.(4)Heterogeneity analysis shows that there are differences in the impacts of different types of regional cooperation on GTFP. Economic cooperation has the strongest impact, followed by environmental cooperation, institutional cooperation, and social cooperation.

This study analyzes the impacts of regional cooperation on regional GTFP through quantitative approaches, which not only deepens the understanding of the factors influencing the regional green economy but also deepens the understanding of the government's environmental performance. From a theoretical viewpoint, this study provides a nuanced understanding of the impact of government behavior on GTFP and provides guidance for green economic development. In addition, distinguished from the bottom-up voluntary regional cooperation in Western countries [5], regional cooperation in China mainly relies on policy mobilization. Upper-level governments promote cooperative actions among local governments through top-down strategic agreements and policy frameworks. At a practice level, this study finds that regional cooperation has a significant impact on regional GTFP, with economic cooperation having the greatest impact and social cooperation having the least impact.

Accordingly, we propose some policy recommendations. On the one hand, according to the current circumstance of regional cooperation development, it is necessary not only to strengthen the regional radiation role of the core cities and realize the transboundary flow of production factors but also to expand the cooperation linkages of the peripheral cities and build an all-round regional governance structure, thus alleviating the regional inequality and enhancing the stability of cooperation networks. On the other hand, urban governments should adopt different cooperation strategies for complex public affairs to promote regional sustainable development. In particular, governments should pay attention to the differences in institutional backgrounds and cultures, and strengthen communication and cultural exchanges in order to promote the cooperation process successfully.

Constrained by the data availability, this study has some limitations. This study analyzes the impacts of regional cooperation at the city level, and in future research, a variety of methods and techniques can be used to obtain richer data and analyze the impacts of regional cooperation at the national level or county level, thus conducting a more detailed and nuanced discussion. Comparative studies of different countries or regions could also be conducted to deepen the understanding of the dynamics of regional cooperation and its impacts in different institutional contexts.

A report of herpetic necrotizing keratitis following coronavirus disease 2019 infection
Authors: Joshua M. Huang, Jordan J. Huang, Anna M. de Ribot, Francesc M. de Ribot
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Herpetic necrotizing keratitis is a severe ocular manifestation of herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection, often leading to significant visual impairment. We report a case of a 65-year-old female who presented with ocular pain and reduced acuity in the left eye, 2 weeks after a coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection, with a relevant history of a herpetic keratitis three decades prior. Examination revealed necrotizing keratitis with stromal necrosis, dendritic epithelial defect, corneal neovascularization, and diffuse conjunctival injection. This was managed concurrently with topical and systemic aciclovir, and topical prednisolone was introduced at the 2-week mark. Recovery to good visual function and resolution of symptoms was achieved. This is a rare case of herpetic necrotizing keratitis following COVID-19 infection and likely represents a reactivation of the latent HSV type 1 by Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2. This case emphasizes the importance of recognizing and managing inflammatory ocular complications in individuals with a history of herpetic infections and highlights another potential repercussion of COVID-19.

INTRODUCTION

Herpetic necrotizing keratitis is a severe ocular manifestation of herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection, characterized by corneal inflammation and stromal necrosis, often leading to significant visual impairment.[1] It is the leading infectious cause of blindness in the developed world, with a global incidence of 1.5 million new cases per year, 40,000 of which lead to severe visual impairment or blindness.[2] Predisposing factors include a history of herpetic infections, corneal trauma, and immunocompromised states.[1] We report a rare case of herpetic necrotizing keratitis following coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection.

CASE REPORT

The presented case involves a 65-year-old woman with a remote history of left eye herpetic infection in the 1990s, which was clinically diagnosed with no recurrences. The patient experienced irritation and decreased vision in the left eye 2 weeks after recovering from COVID-19, for which she tested positive following a limited episode of fever and cough. At presentation, best-corrected visual acuity was 6/18 in the left eye, with normal intraocular pressure. Slit-lamp examination of the right eye demonstrated a clear cornea with normal morphology and an otherwise unremarkable anterior segment. Examination of the left eye revealed conjunctival injection with stromal necrosis in the superonasal cornea and an epithelial defect of dendritic appearance measuring up to 1.5 mm, highlighted by fluorescein staining. Associated superonasal corneal neovascularization was present [Figure 1]. No keratic precipitates were observed, and corneal sensation was mildly reduced. Both eyes were pseudophakic, with no anterior or posterior chamber inflammation. Optic nerve excavation was 0.45 bilaterally, and fundoscopic examination showed normal retinal morphology with no evidence of vitritis or retinitis. The patient’s medical history was significant for well-controlled type two diabetes mellitus and hypertension. She had received four COVID-19 vaccinations, the most recent 15 months prior to presentation. Given the characteristic slit-lamp findings in the context of prior herpetic infection and recent COVID-19, a clinical diagnosis of herpetic necrotizing keratitis of the left eye following COVID-19 infection was made. Microbiologic confirmation was not performed as the patient’s clinical history provided sufficient diagnostic certainty to initiate prompt treatment. The initial management included topical antivirals (aciclovir ointment 3%, five times daily) and systemic antivirals (per oral aciclovir 400 mg, five times daily) to ensure both therapeutic levels in the cornea and prophylaxis against further viral replication.

Slit-lamp photography of the left eye showing (a) paralimbal dendritic epithelial defect with fluorescein uptake, central corneal scarring, and (b) conjunctival injection, superonasal stromal necrosis, and associated superior corneal neovascularization at presentation

At the 2-week follow-up, visual acuity in the left eye improved to 6/15. Clinical examination demonstrated reduced conjunctival injection, stromal necrosis, and neovascularization. On slit-lamp examination with fluorescein staining, there was minimal reduction in the size of the dendritiform epithelial defect but progressive healing [Figure 2]. Given this, topical prednisolone acetate 1% suspension was subsequently introduced at six drops daily and tapered by one drop per week to address the immune-mediated stromal inflammation. At 1-month follow-up, visual acuity further improved to 6/12 in the left eye. Examination revealed a small residual epithelial defect, a white and quiet conjunctiva, regression of corneal vascularization, and a smaller, less dense area of residual corneal haze at the superonasal limbal region [Figure 3].

DISCUSSION

This rare occurrence of herpetic necrotizing keratitis after COVID-19 warrants further investigation into potential associations. HSV-associated necrotizing keratitis is the leading infectious cause of blindness in the developed world, with vision loss resulting from corneal scarring, inflammation, neovascularization, and potential corneal thinning, which may progress to corneal melt or perforation.[23] Over the past 4 years, numerous ocular conditions have been reported in temporal association with COVID-19 infection, including recurrent conjunctivitis, keratitis, scleritis, acute anterior uveitis, optic neuritis, and retinovascular disease.[4] However, while several reports describe HSV keratitis following COVID-19 vaccination, fewer reports specifically document cases following acute COVID-19 infection.[5] As herpetic necrotizing keratitis following COVID-19 infection is a rare instance, this case may help elucidate the mechanisms underlying its pathogenesis.

Herpetic necrotizing keratitis is thought to be mediated by a complex interplay between viral pathogenicity and the host immune response, resulting in corneal tissue destruction.[6] Pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-1, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor-α, promote stromal inflammation, while IL-6 and IL-17 promote corneal neovascularization through upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor.[3] During severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection, these cytokines are also released systemically as part of the cytokine cascade, in proportion to disease severity.[7] In this patient, it could have been possible that the pro-inflammatory state of active COVID-19 infection potentiated the immune-mediated corneal inflammation observed in the patient’s necrotizing keratitis.

In addition, SARS-CoV-2 infection reduces CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell titers, which may have lowered the reactivation threshold for the presented patient’s latent HSV.[8] A reduction in CD8+ cells or exhaustion of the remaining population may have led to a reduced suppression of the patient’s latent HSV-1 within the trigeminal ganglion, allowing easier reactivation of HSV-1 during and after the acute COVID-19 infection. Even the patient’s fever and physiological stress associated with COVID-19 are known triggers, and it is possible that SARS-CoV-2 acted directly upon the patient’s neurons, stimulating HSV-1 reactivation through the corneal nerve.[9] This theory requires further research as a potential mechanism for herpetic necrotizing keratitis in conjunction with the above-described immune dysregulation during COVID-19 infection.

With this, there are several limitations to consider in this case. This patient has a history of both herpetic keratitis and type 2 diabetes. Although well controlled, both are established risk factors for HSV reactivation and therefore could have played a role in reactivation.[10]

Necrotizing keratitis can have serious ocular implications, and management includes antiviral therapy, such as aciclovir, to control viral replication, with the judicious introduction of topical corticosteroids to control inflammation.[1] It is thought that more aggressive antiviral therapy may be beneficial when treating necrotizing keratitis than as required for nonnecrotizing keratitis.[2] In the present case, the patient was treated with both systemic and topical antiviral therapy, along with a carefully tapered course of topical corticosteroids, resulting in a favorable clinical response. Notably, her early presentation likely contributed to the positive outcome. Prognosis depends upon the promptness of diagnosis and initiation of appropriate treatment.[1] Regular follow-up is crucial for monitoring response to therapy and preventing complications. Early control of inflammation is critical to achieving optimal visual outcomes, underscoring the importance of tailored management strategies.

Here, we hypothesize that COVID-19 may have acted as a precipitating risk factor for necrotizing keratitis in this patient through a multifactorial contribution of immune dysregulation, cytokine-mediated inflammation, and reduced viral suppression thresholds to the patient’s baseline susceptibility from her diabetes and age. This rare association warrants further investigation into necrotizing keratitis post-COVID-19 infection, as well as in a broader context of COVID-19-associated viral reactivation.

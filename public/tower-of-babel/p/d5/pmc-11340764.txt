Bibliometric analysis of dendritic cell-based vaccines over the past 15 years
Authors: Zhi Long, Yinghua Wu, Liangchen Zhong, Jiping Lu, Bo Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDendritic cells, which are crucial for inducing T-cell responses, are pivotal in current immunotherapy strategies aiming to replenish depleted T cells within the tumor microenvironment to combat tumors. Consequently, dendritic cell vaccine-based cancer therapies have garnered significant attention. Through bibliometric analysis, we examined research trends in this field. We searched the Web of Science core database and identified 16,476 articles on dendritic cell-based vaccines published from January 1, 2009, to December 30, 2023. The United States leads in this research domain, with Emory University being a prominent collaborator. The Journal of Immunology is the primary publication outlet, and Banchereau, J emerges as the most influential author. Recent hot keywords include nanoparticle, delivery, cancer vaccine, and clinical trial, indicating that cancer immunotherapy research, especially dendritic cell-based vaccines, is poised to become a future trend and hotspot.

Introduction

Dendritic cells (DCs), serve as pivotal antigen-presenting cells (APCs), bridge the innate and adaptive immune systems. Their specialized role lies in the processing and presentation of antigens to T cells, thereby orchestrating tailored T cell responses.1 Equipped with an array of pattern-recognition receptors (PRRs), dendritic cells distinguish between “danger signals” and “safety signals,” which is crucial for initiating appropriate T-cell responses. Moreover, they adeptly integrate cues from the local tissue microenvironment to fine-tune T-cell responses. Under normal physiological conditions, dendritic cells play a vital role in maintaining immune homeostasis by activating T cells to eliminate infected or malignant cells, promoting regulatory T cells to mitigate chronic inflammation. Dysregulated immunity contributes to cancer development and tumor-induced immunosuppression including T-cell exhaustion, posing significant hurdles to cancer immunotherapy. Reinvigorating depleted T cells to stimulate anti-tumor responses represents a primary objective of current immunotherapeutic strategies.2 The initiation and maintenance of robust T-cell responses hinge on effective antigen presentation by APCs, in which DCs are assumed to play a central role. Consequently, DC-based cancer vaccine strategies have gained increasing attention.3,4 The burgeoning volume of research output in this domain underscores growing interest. However, a comprehensive exploration and analysis of the current status and emerging trends in dendritic cell-based vaccines remains lacking.

Bibliometrics stands as a widely embraced scientific approach for scrutinizing the current landscape of a specific research field, identifying hotspots, discerning trends, and evaluating publication quality. Through bibliometric analyses, researchers undertake both qualitative and quantitative examinations of publication and citation data within their respective fields over defined timeframes.5,6 Among the databases employed for such analyses, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) emerges as the predominant choice.7 In recent years, bibliometric methodologies have seen widespread adoption across diverse research domains and a plethora of studies documented.8–10, To date, no bibliometric studies of dendritic cell-based vaccines have been reported. In this investigation, we conduct a bibliometric analysis to comprehensively summarize hotspots and trends in dendritic cell-based vaccine research between 2009 and 2023 by using VOSviewer and CiteSpace software.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The exploration of the relevant literature centered around the theme “dendritic cell-based vaccines” was meticulously executed using the WoSCC Expanded database (Thomson Reuters, New York, USA). Our search spanned from January 1, 2009, to December 30, 2023. The article types were filtered to include “articles” and “reviews,” while articles in non-English languages were excluded. Data analysis

CiteSpace (version 5.8.R1) and VOSviewer (Leiden University, Leiden, The Netherlands) were employed for visual analyses and to generate maps and clusters. CiteSpace was utilized specifically for keyword analysis and to identify co-citation bursts. The analytical settings included time slicing (from 2009 to 2023), year per slice (1), selection criteria based on the g-index (k = 25), and no pruning. Within the co-citation literature graph, cluster labels were assigned using index terms and the log-likelihood ratio (LLR).

VOSviewer, another bibliometric software tool, was utilized to create knowledge maps based on web data and to visualize and explore these maps. In the network visualization map produced by VOSviewer, consistent colors denote the same clusters, and the size of nodes reflects the frequency of co-occurrence. Total Link Strength (TLS) represents the cumulative weight of connections between nodes and is visually represented by the width of links, reflecting collaboration strength. This study leveraged VOSviewer for country, institution, journal, author, citation, and keyword clustering due to its intuitive and clear performance in clustering tasks.

Employing Bradford’s nucleus, we determine core journals within the field. Additionally, we apply Lotka’s Law to analyze prolific authors and utilize the Hirsch index (h-index) to assess highly cited papers.

Discussion

An in-depth bibliometric analysis was conducted to assess the current landscape and trends in dendritic cell-based vaccine research spanning from 2009 to 2023. The sustained interest observed in this domain underscores substantial research investment and the attainment of noteworthy scientific outcomes. Our findings serve as a valuable resource for researchers seeking to expedite topic selection, identify suitable collaborative partners and institutions, and stay abreast of global research frontiers.

There are mainly four types of DC vaccines with distinct characteristics and trends. Autologous DC vaccines are derived from patient’s monocytes of hematopoietic progenitor cells which is highly personalized for designing patient-specific neoantigen-based vaccines15 but is also time-consuming. While allogeneic DC vaccines are easier to standardize and produce because of its healthy donor derivation.16 There are also novel types like DCs loaded with tumor lysates and RNA or DNA-transfected DC vaccines. The former one provides a broader range of tumor antigens, especially in complex tumors with high mutational burdens and can be tailored to individual at the same time.17 While the latter one allows for utilization of gene-editing technologies to improve DC function but still needs further confirmation.18

The preeminence of the United States in dendritic cell-based vaccine research is apparent, reflected in its advanced research infrastructure, regulatory environment, numerous resources and clinical trials, and leadership in publication output. The U.S. possesses a well-established research infrastructure consisting numerous cancer institutions and universities. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration also offers a regulatory procedure for the development and approval of new regimens which ensured the quality of clinical translation. The mature and well-supported research ecosystem and substantial government funding like the National Institutes of Health have catalyzed robust extensive preclinical and clinical trials, boosting inter-country collaborations, particularly evident in the strong partnership between China and the United States, fostering transnational academic exchanges and driving progress in the field.

While the majority of the top 10 institutions by publication volume hail from the United States, one Chinese institution’s notable ranking underscores China’s increasing involvement. However, China still needs improvement in clinical translation due to its lack of consistency between research sites and flaws of trial design and execution. Although funding support from agencies like National Natural Science Foundation already exists, it is comprehensively less consistent and integrated compared to U.S. Considering that clinical trials existed, most of it conducted in China are in earlier stages, partly due to China’s large population that impact the progession of patient recruitment, data management and follow-up investigation. To enhance its research impact, China should bolster investment in infrastructure, standardize framework for drug and therapy approval, forge stronger partnerships with international institutes, and elevate its scientific influence. Notably, Emory University emerges as a frontrunner in total citations, reaffirming its authority in dendritic cell-based vaccine research.

Journal rankings shed light on publication patterns, with Frontiers in Immunology leading in paper quantity. Conversely, the Journal of Immunology claims the top spot in total citations. While Frontiers in Immunology boasts high publication volume, improvements in paper quality and research impact are warranted. Conversely, the Journal of Immunology stands as a paramount publication venue, serving as a cornerstone in the field. Awareness of these influential journals aids researchers in selecting appropriate outlets for manuscript submission, with top-ranked journals serving as authoritative representatives based on co-citation rankings.

Figdor, CG, De Vries IJM, and Ishii, KJ emerge as the foremost contributors in dendritic cell-based vaccine research, demonstrating exceptional scholarly impact. Figdor, CG’s focus on antigenic targeting across diverse dendritic cell subpopulations and their concurrent activation with adjuvants underscores innovative approaches to induce immunity.19,20 De Vries IJM’s research endeavors center on enhancing the immunogenicity and efficacy of dendritic cell-based vaccines, reflecting a commitment to advancing vaccine technology.21,22 Ishii, KJ’s notable contributions lie in the design and development of novel adjuvants for dendritic cell-based vaccines, indicative of pioneering efforts to bolster vaccine potency.23 Additionally, Banchereau, J stands out as the most co-cited author, underscoring the profound influence of his seminal research in this field.

The article by Banchereau, J et al. entitled “Dendritic cells and the control of immunity” was the most cited paper, and it led to the recognition of dendritic cells as a powerful tool for immunity.11 Post-2020, “mRNA vaccines – a new era in vaccinology” garners the highest citation rate, highlighting the transformative potential of mRNA vaccines in revolutionizing vaccine development.14 With their notable efficacy, rapid development capabilities, cost-effectiveness, and safe delivery mechanisms, mRNA vaccines have emerged as a focal point in contemporary vaccine research, offering a promising alternative to conventional vaccine modalities.

Current hot keywords in dendritic cell-based vaccine research include cancer vaccine, nanoparticle, delivery, and clinical trial. The tumor microenvironment’s complexity, immune evasion, and tumor heterogeneity hinder immunotherapy efficacy. Traditional immunotherapy heightened patient responsiveness but leads to increased side effects, cost, and treatment duration, while cancer vaccines characterized by individual-specific treatment seems as a solution for eliminating adverse aspects.

Nanotechnology, particularly nanoparticles, exploits nanocarriers’ physicochemical properties, enhancing targeted tissue delivery for improved immunotherapy efficacy. Nanoparticles offer a large surface area for enhanced immune interaction and controlled release capabilities, enhancing clinical applications. Targeting dendritic cells shows promise in activating immune responses. Effective antigen and adjuvant delivery to dendritic cells is essential for stimulating adequate T-cell responses against cancer.

Nanoparticles as vaccine delivery systems offer superior immune efficacy due to several factors: (1) Triggering in situ tumor antigen release, enhancing immune response while loading antigens and adjuvants to efficiently activate DCs, and avoiding immune tolerance caused by immature DC phagocytosis of antigens.24,25 (2) Improved immune response with simultaneous antigen and adjuvant loading, effectively activating DCs and avoiding immune tolerance.26 (3) Better spatial and temporal vaccine delivery by controlling nanoparticle size, charge, and physicochemical characteristics, significantly improving lymph node (LN) accumulation.27 (4) Different delivery modes including subcutaneous, intranasal, and oral administration, facing diverse immune environments.28 (5) Nanomedicine platforms enabling precise and stable adjuvant delivery.29

In addition to their role as delivery systems, nanoparticles possess the ability to elicit diverse immune responses and can serve as adjuvants for vaccines.30 Nanoparticles offer promising potential as adjuvants for nanovaccines, bolstering immune responses and enhancing anti-tumor effects. Notably, superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIO), which are renowned for its biocompatibility, find widespread application in medical imaging and drug delivery.31 SPIO represents a standard formulation utilized in vaccine visualization studies, such as for labeling dendritic cells (DCs) to monitor their migration to the lymph nodes (LN).32 There is speculation that SPIO may augment intracellular antigen delivery to antigen-presenting cells (APCs) through cross-presentation, thus harboring potential as a vaccine adjuvant.33 Furthermore, the adjuvant effect of nanovaccines for cancer therapy has been associated with targeting toll-like receptors (TLRs), a prevalent adjuvant mechanism. Poly(γ-glutamic acid) (γ-PGA), a biocompatible polymer derived from Bacillus licheniformis and Bacillus subtilis, serves as an antigen carrier for cancer vaccines.34 Studies by Yoshikawa et al. have illustrated the utility of γ-PGA nanoparticles as antigenic carriers for cancer vaccines.35 Immunization of mice with ovalbumin (OVA) encapsulated in γ-PGA nanoparticles has been shown to activate cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) responses and effectively retard tumor growth in mice without inducing toxicity. Moreover, in addition to their carrier role, γ-PGA nanoparticles activate DCs and elicit robust antigen-specific T-cell responses via Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and MyD88-dependent signaling pathways, thereby bolstering both innate and adaptive immune responses, particularly cellular immunity.36 Another notable advantage of γ-PGA nanoparticles is their potential for intranasal administration of antigen-encapsulated nanovaccines, hinting at the prospect of developing noninvasive cancer vaccines in the future.37

Numerous papers detail dendritic cell-based vaccine outcomes in clinical trials across various diseases, as increasing researches showed that solid tumors like melanoma for its high immunogenicity38 and hematological malignancies like leukemia39 are best suited for DC vaccines. For instance, the renowned vaccine named Sipuleucel-T(provenge) has been approved by the FDA in 2010 for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.12 Beatriz M Carreno et.al directed DC vaccines on melanoma-encoded amino acid, which broadened the breadth and diversity of antigen, namely antitumor immunity.40 A phase 2b trial of tumor lysate, particle loaded, dendritic cell (TLPLDC) vaccine conducted in stage III/IV melanoma patients showed improved OS and DFS.41 David J Chung et al. found that triple antigen-bearing mRNA-electroporated autologous Langerhans-type dendritic cells vaccine combined with standard maintenance therapy for multiple myeloma induces antigen-specific immune reaction and proved its safety.42 Sébastien Anguille and colleagues also showed that mRNA-electroporated DCs vaccinated patients with acute myeloid leukemia prevented or delayed relapse and improved OS.43 A recent phase II trial of dendritic cell-based adjuvant immunotherapy in post-surgery malignant peritoneal mesothelioma patients showed safety, feasibility, and significantly prolonged survival.44 Another phase 3 trial compared autologous dendritic cell-based immunotherapy with docetaxel and prednisone versus placebo in prostate cancer patients. Although well tolerated, it did not extend overall survival (OS) in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC).45 A clinical trial combining dendritic cell immunotherapy (DCVAC/LuCa) with carboplatin/pemetrexed in advanced non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer patients demonstrated tolerability and favorable treatment outcomes in stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.46 These trials suggest promising potential for dendritic cell-based vaccines in cancer immunotherapy. There are also insights into applying dendritic cell vaccines on ‘cold’ tumors like glioblastoma, but most of the clinical trials stuck in phase II and failed to meet the primary endpoint in phase III47 due to lack of rigorous inclusion criteria.48 The future direction lies in the bench-to-bed translation with regard to those clinical trials to benefit potential candidates.

Limitations

Our analysis of global dendritic cell-based vaccine trends covers literature from January 1, 2009, to December 30, 2023, excluding 2024 publications and possibly missing recent advancements. Limiting the study to English literature may introduce language bias. While WoSCC, a widely used scientometric database, was utilized, excluding articles not in WoSCC may introduce bias. Additionally, analyzing abstracts rather than full texts in VOSviewer may limit comprehensive information retrieval.

Conclusions

Overall, our study represents the inaugural comprehensive analysis of global trends in dendritic cell-based vaccine research. Due to its antigen selecting characteristics, DC vaccines are promising agents for personalization and combination therapies. The United States emerges as the primary driver of advancements in this field, demonstrating substantial leadership and contribution. Notably, the Journal of Immunology stands out as the most influential publication avenue within this domain. Authors Figdor, CG, De Vries IJM, and Ishii, KJ have emerged as pivotal contributors, showcasing significant contributions and serving as exemplary collaborators for researchers in this arena. Recent hot topics, including nanoparticle, delivery channel, cancer vaccine, and clinical trial, underscore the evolving landscape of research interests in this area. While research on dendritic cell-based vaccines displays strength, there remains ample room for further investment and exploration to advance our understanding and therapeutic potential in this crucial field.

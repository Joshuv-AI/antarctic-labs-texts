Population Pharmacokinetics of Dupilumab in Adults, Adolescents, and Children With Eosinophilic Esophagitis
Authors: Jenny‐Hoa Nguyen, Mirna Chehade, Evan S Dellon, Allen Radin, Jason Chittenden, Mohamed A Kamal, Margee Louisias, Christine Xu, Matthew P Kosloski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody that blocks key drivers of type 2 inflammation, is approved across 8 diseases, including eosinophilic esophagitis. Subcutaneous dupilumab 300 mg weekly improved outcomes vs. placebo in adults (aged ≥ 18 years) and adolescents (aged ≥ 12 to < 18 years) during the phase III LIBERTY EoE TREET study (NCT03633617). The phase III EoE KIDS study (NCT04394351) demonstrated efficacy of a weight‐tiered, higher exposure dupilumab dose approximating 300 mg weekly exposure in TREET vs. placebo in children aged ≥ 1 to < 12 years. To characterize dupilumab pharmacokinetics, inform clinical trial design, and optimize posology, a population pharmacokinetic model was developed using dupilumab concentration data from 6 single‐dose studies in healthy adults and 3 eosinophilic esophagitis clinical trials. Healthy volunteer data were used to construct a stable base model; incorporation of patient data allowed estimation covariate effects for the disease population. Observational data from up to 52 weeks of treatment in eosinophilic esophagitis trials validated model performance. Significant effects were identified for body weight on clearance and volume of distribution, including time‐varying weight in children and adolescents, and of albumin on clearance; age was not a significant covariate. Simulations identified alternative dupilumab regimens for patients weighing ≥ 5 to <15 kg (200 mg every 3 weeks) and ≥ 40 to <60 kg (300 mg weekly) with improved exposure matching to the 300 mg weekly dose in TREET. The final model was used to inform regulatory approval and potential future clinical trial design.

HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE?

HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE?

Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, progressive disease diagnosed by evidence of eosinophil infiltration of the esophagus. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 EoE has substantial negative impact on quality of life and is increasing in its incidence and prevalence. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 In patients with EoE, underlying type 2 inflammation in the esophagus is characterized by the actions of cellular mediators (e.g., mast cells, type 2 helper T cells, and eosinophils), type 2 interleukin (IL) cytokines, and chemokines, which drive pathophysiology and compromise the epithelial barrier. 6 , 7 If untreated, chronic inflammation in children can lead to progressive remodeling and fibrostenosis in adolescence and adulthood, associated with persistent symptoms (including chest pain and dysphagia), strictures and food impaction. 1 , 6 , 8

Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that blocks the IL‐4 and IL‐13 inflammatory pathways and has accumulated approvals in 8 type 2 inflammatory conditions internationally. 7 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 In EoE, dupilumab is approved for the treatment of patients aged ≥ 1 year, weighing ≥ 15 kg in the US and EU. 11 , 12 Subcutaneous (SC) dupilumab 300 mg administered weekly (qw) demonstrated improvements vs. placebo in histologic, symptomatic, and endoscopic outcomes after 24 weeks of treatment in adults/adolescents weighing ≥ 40 kg in the phase III LIBERTY EoE TREET study (NCT03633617). 14 During this study, dupilumab 300 mg every other week (q2w) reduced esophageal eosinophil counts vs. placebo at Week 24, but did not improve symptoms.

The multipart, phase III EoE KIDS study (NCT04394351) evaluated weight‐tiered, higher (HE) and lower exposure (LE) dupilumab regimens in children (aged 1 to < 12 years) with EoE during a 16‐week, placebo‐controlled period (Part A) and a 36‐week, active extension period (Part B). 15 The HE and LE regimens were based on those in the LIBERTY EoE TREET study, which was ongoing at the time, and were intended to approximate exposures of dupilumab 300 mg qw and q2w doses in adults/adolescents, respectively (Table 1 , Table S1 ). In EoE KIDS, the HE dupilumab regimen improved outcomes across histologic, endoscopic, and transcriptomic end points vs. placebo at Week 16. Although the study was insufficiently powered to assess symptomatic outcomes and treatment differences were not statistically significant, numeric improvements in EoE sign/symptom scores favored HE dupilumab vs. placebo.

Dose‐ and exposure‐dependent effects of dupilumab on clinical outcomes of patients with EoE emphasized the importance of achieving optimal exposures. 14 , 15 This manuscript describes a population pharmacokinetic (popPK) analysis conducted to describe the PK of dupilumab in patients aged ≥ 1 year with EoE. The model was used to simulate dupilumab regimens, including age and dose combinations not evaluated in clinical trials, to better inform the posology of dupilumab and the potential design of future pediatric clinical trials in patients with EoE.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients

Dupilumab concentration–time data were included from 6 single‐dose studies in healthy volunteers with dense PK sampling after single intravenous (IV) and SC doses (all phase I), 16 and 3 clinical studies in participants with EoE with sparse PK sampling after multiple SC doses: 1 phase II study in adults (aged ≥ 18 years), 1 phase III study in adults and adolescents (aged ≥ 12 years), and 1 phase III study in children (aged ≥ 1 to < 12 years) (Table S1 ). 14 , 15 , 17 The design and conduct of these studies, including institutional review board and ethical approvals, conduct in line with the Declaration of Helsinki, and acquisition of informed consent, have been previously published. 14 , 15 , 16 , 17

Bioanalytical methods

Concentrations of functional dupilumab (molecules with 1 or 2 free binding sites) in serum were measured using a validated enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), as previously described in the literature. 18 A validated electrochemiluminescence bridging immunoassay was used to measure antidrug antibodies (ADA) to dupilumab in serum samples. 19 The ADA assay involved up to 3 different evaluations, including an initial screening, confirmation step, and measurement of the titers.

Exploratory analysis

Before model development, observed concentration–time profiles of dupilumab from up to 52 weeks of treatment in LIBERTY EoE TREET, EoE KIDS, and healthy volunteers 16 were assessed in an exploratory manner. Plots were constructed to illustrate observed concentrations over time, stratified by relevant subject characteristics and/or study design features.

Time‐varying body weight

Time‐varying body weight was defined for pediatric patients aged < 18 years and used linear interpolation between observed weight measurements. If a weight measurement was unavailable and the interval between measurements exceeded 16 weeks, imputation was performed using the nearest measurement to determine the patient's weight percentile, noting patient age, then applying the US Center for Disease Control clinical growth table to extrapolate a weight at the missing/interpolated time point. For adults, negligible changes in body weight were anticipated over the course of the study and baseline body weight was used.

Base model construction

Model construction was split into 3 phases due to sampling differences between studies (healthy volunteers were densely sampled after a single dose; patients with EoE were sparsely sampled, primarily at troughs): (i) development of a structural model using data from healthy volunteer studies; (ii) extension of the healthy volunteer model to a base model in patients with EoE; and (iii) identification of a final model including additional covariates in patients with EoE.

A structural popPK model was constructed similar to those previously described for dupilumab in adults, adolescents, and children with atopic dermatitis. 18 , 20 , 21 Dense PK data from healthy volunteers who received single IV or SC doses of dupilumab in clinical studies were used to uniquely identify systemic distribution and elimination parameters, as well as the absorption parameters in the model. Subsequently, the structural model was extended to the EoE population through a stepwise covariate analysis. Healthy volunteer parameters were fixed, and potential relationships were first tested between EoE disease state and clearance (CL), volume of distribution at steady‐state (V ss, calculated as central volume of distribution [V c] + peripheral volume of distribution [V p], with covariate and random effects shared), maximum nonlinear elimination rate (V max), and bioavailability (F 1). Baseline albumin concentrations and maximum ADA titer category were also tested, as each typically strongly correlates with linear antibody CL (negatively with albumin concentration, and positively with ADA titer). Inclusion of inter‐individual variability (IIV) on V max was also considered. A complete list of covariates considered for evaluation is described in Table S2 .

For the final model, additional covariates were tested to assess the effects of time‐varying age on the absorption rate constant (K a) and CL, and time‐varying weight on CL, intercompartmental CL (Q), and V ss in the pediatric EoE population. This final stepwise procedure started with the inclusion of the effects identified in the base EoE model and tested the inclusion of additional effects before testing the removal of effects in backward elimination.

Stepwise covariate analyses, consisting of a forward addition procedure (with a significance threshold of P < 0.01) followed by a backward elimination procedure (P < 0.001), were applied to assess the effects of intrinsic and extrinsic factors. The final model was derived from the final step of the backward elimination.

Model covariate relationships are described mathematically in the Supplementary Materials.

Model evaluation

To allow for improved diagnosis of model misspecification, internal prediction‐corrected visual predictive checks (pcVPCs) were conducted to verify the predictive adequacy of the final model. 22 The parameter estimates were fixed to the values estimated in the final model and used to generate 1000 datasets, which replicated the design, subject population, dose regimens, sample sizes, and covariate distributions from the observed dataset. A measure of central tendency and appropriate percentile intervals calculated using simulated data were plotted as a function of time and other predictive variables. Observed data were overlaid with corresponding percentile intervals to provide a visual assessment of predictive performance.

Final model application and software

Covariate effects in the final model were illustrated by plotting the 95% prediction interval of PK parameters as a function of the covariate and overlaying the post hoc parameter values for each subject. This contrasted the expected range of parameter values in the model with the observed range of parameters against the distribution in covariate values.

Simulated concentration–time profiles for dupilumab regimens were generated for simulated populations with EoE, and descriptive statistics for steady‐state exposure metrics were determined; combinations of dose regimens and patient demographics (weight and age groups) not evaluated in clinical studies were also explored. These populations were obtained by drawing random subjects from the EoE dataset with replacement (N = 1000) for each of the strata in a simulation scenario. Subjects were sampled with random noise (± 10%) added to covariate values. For pediatric subjects (aged < 18 years at baseline), weight z‐score for age was first computed from baseline weight. The z‐score was then jittered by 10% and the sampled baseline weight percentile was used to generate a growth profile for weight using the CDC growth tables 23 over the duration of the simulation. Simulated concentrations and cumulative AUC values were sampled every week up to Week 54, when the last dose was given. Steady‐state exposure metrics were determined over 1 dosing interval following the final dose. Simulations included IIV and residual variability (RUV), but not parameter uncertainty.

Nonlinear mixed‐effects modeling methodology was implemented in this analysis using NONMEM® (version 7.5.0) software (ICON Development Solutions, Ellicott City, MD). The FOCEI method was utilized with an additional preconditioning step to ensure computational stability of the variance–covariance matrix. 24 Pre‐ and post‐processing of data from each modeling step and graphic analysis of the data was performed using R (version 4.2.2 or higher). Additional details of plot generation are described in the Supplementary Materials. Simulations were performed using the mrgsolve R package. 25

RESULTS

Population characteristics

The dupilumab dataset included a total of 632 unique subjects (202 healthy adults and 430 patients with EoE), providing 4459 post‐dose PK samples. Of the total PK samples, 3494 (78%) were quantifiable with the remaining samples below the level of quantification (965 [22%]).

Characteristics of the healthy adults who contributed data to the model have previously been described. 16 Baseline characteristics of the EoE cohort included in the popPK analysis are summarized here descriptively, overall and by age group (children [≥ 1 to < 12 years], n = 98; adolescents [≥ 12 to < 18 years], n = 97; and adults [≥ 18 years], n = 235) (Table S3 ). Overall, most patients were White (90%) and male (66%), in line with previous reports describing the EoE population. 3 , 26 All patients had evidence of active EoE at their study baseline (peak esophageal intraepithelial eosinophil count [EEOS]: 84.6 eos/hpf).

Nine patients were observed to have high post hoc random effect values during popPK model development. These anomalous data points were excluded from analysis due to observations of increased dupilumab concentrations between subsequent observations > 40 days after the last recorded dose in the study, with no intervening dose administered (Figure S1 ).

Exploratory analysis

Observed dupilumab trough concentrations were summarized from the LIBERTY EoE TREET and EoE KIDS studies (Table 1 ). In the overall HE and LE dupilumab groups in EoE KIDS, observed mean trough dupilumab concentrations in children ranged between 159–169 mg/L and 86.6–89.2 mg/L, respectively. The observed mean concentrations in adults/adolescents in LIBERTY EoE TREET were 159–196 mg/L and 65.7–73.6 mg/L for dupilumab 300 mg qw and q2w, respectively. As previously reported, development of anti‐dupilumab antibodies was rare and did not impact functional dupilumab concentrations in these studies. 14 , 15 , 27

Development of popPK model

The structural popPK model developed for healthy volunteers consisted of 3 transit compartments leading to an absorption compartment, 2 distribution compartments, and parallel linear and nonlinear (Michaelis–Menten) elimination from the central compartment (Figure S2 ); both first‐order and transit compartment absorption models were explored. 28 Allometric scaling of the compartmental volumes and clearances was assumed and provided significant improvements to the model, with a power of 1.0 for V ss and 0.75 for CL and Q. IIV was explored on all structural parameters and ultimately included on CL, V ss, K a, F 1, and mean transit time (MTT). The inclusion of the transit compartments in the structural model was critical to retaining the fit to the IV data in the presence of the SC data; otherwise, the peripheral compartment parameters would adjust to fit the nuances of the absorption profile, and the peripheral distribution phase and maximum concentration (C max) visible in the IV profiles would no longer fit.

Extending the model to the adult and pediatric EoE population, initial stepwise covariate analysis identified a chosen base model with EoE effects on CL, V max, and V ss, as well as an albumin effect on CL (Table S4 ). Due to the sparse nature of and lack of information for K a and MTT, IIV on these parameters was excluded from the model. An EoE effect on F 1 and IIV on V max, while possibly significant, did not provide stable minimization (Table S5 ). Goodness‐of‐fit and diagnostic plots of concordance indicated agreement between observed data and base model predictions, and PK parameters were well estimated when healthy volunteer parameters were fixed with patient data, demonstrating base model stability.

After developing the base model that differentiated patients with EoE from healthy volunteers, additional effects of age/weight, disease severity (as EEOS), dose, and study were evaluated. A forward stepwise covariate search found statistically significant relationships between age and K a and statistically significant improvements in the objective functional value by replacement of baseline weight with time‐varying weight in adolescents and children (using fixed allometry), and between time‐varying weight and CL, Q, and V ss (using estimated allometry). Backward elimination removed allometric estimation of Q (reverting to a fixed coefficient of 0.75) and the age effect on K a. At this point, η values showed a slight bias toward higher CL at lower baseline age (Figure S3 ). However, as this was deemed physiologically implausible, no age effect on CL was pursued. The final model included significant effects of EoE on CL, V ss, V max, and albumin on CL, tested and retained from the base model; and time‐varying weight on CL, V ss, and Q, with estimated allometric coefficients on CL and V ss (Figure S4 ).

Analysis of the final model PK parameters indicated these were well estimated for covariate effects (relative standard error [RSE] < 1% for both structural parameters and covariate effects), and the condition number (2.05) was < 1000, indicating a stable model (Table 2 ). IIV was modest for CL (31.9%) and lower for V ss (16.2%). η‐shrinkage was low for CL (8.62%) and high for V ss (64.9%).

Dupilumab exposure simulations

Data collected in EoE clinical studies were limited to sparse trough samples, while simulated concentration–time profiles facilitated a more comprehensive comparison of drug exposure between age groups and dose regimens. Overall, mean trough concentrations of dupilumab at steady‐state for children ≥ 1 to < 12 years of age with EoE receiving HE dupilumab exhibited overlapping distributions to adults and adolescents with EoE who received dupilumab 300 mg qw (Table S6 ); trough concentrations in children were closest to the 300 mg qw reference for 200 mg q2w (≥ 15 to < 30 kg) and 300 mg qw (≥ 60 kg), but skewed toward the lower end of the distribution for 100 mg q2w (≥ 5 to < 15 kg) and 300 mg q2w (≥ 30 to < 60 kg). In the latter group, that mismatch was primarily driven by the subset of children ≥ 40 to < 60 kg. Exposure in the LE dupilumab group was similar to or exceeded reference exposure in the adult and adolescent 300 mg q2w regimen; however, the 300 mg q2w dose regimen failed to demonstrate symptomatic improvement for adults and adolescents with EoE during the LIBERTY EoE TREET study.

Additional simulations were conducted to explore regimens that may more effectively match exposures across a range of body weights in children to the approved 300 mg qw dose in adults and adolescents ≥ 40 kg. In children ≥ 5 to < 15 kg, a 200 mg q3w regimen was expected to match the median C trough,ss of the 300 mg qw reference with a comparable upper distribution of C max (Table S7 ). In children ≥ 40 to < 60 kg, a 300 mg qw regimen was expected to match exposures of similarly sized adults and adolescents receiving the 300 mg qw regimen (Table S8 ). Likewise, adults and adolescents ≥ 30 to < 40 kg were predicted to exhibit similar exposures to children ≥ 30 to < 40 kg when both groups received 300 mg q2w (Table S9 ). Based on these simulations, an alternative weight‐tiered posology was identified to normalize exposures across all patients ≥ 1 year of age, irrespective of body weight (Table 3 ; Figure 3 ).

DISCUSSION

Building from structural modeling developed in healthy volunteers and applied to other diseases, 18 , 20 , 21 a popPK model was constructed that accurately predicts dupilumab PK in adults, adolescents, and children with EoE. Notably, data from patients with EoE as young as 1 year were used to construct the final model, and incorporation of time‐varying body weight in the growing pediatric population improved performance relative to baseline body weight alone. This model allows accurate prediction of drug exposure in patients with EoE and can be used to inform weight‐based dupilumab regimens for therapeutic use, particularly in groups not evaluated in previous studies.

Model development was initiated with IV and SC data from studies in healthy adults, which had the densest available PK sampling, including frequent sampling during SC absorption. These studies were used to obtain the initial set of parameters, while the addition of data from patients with EoE enabled comparison with healthy volunteers. Model parameters were fixed to the estimates obtained from healthy volunteers to avoid the tendency of sparsely sampled SC data to use distributional parameters (V p and Q) to account for misfit of absorption or other model misspecifications. The dense absorption profiles in the healthy volunteer SC data were described with transit compartments rather than first‐order absorption, providing more precise estimates of the distributional parameters across the healthy volunteer SC and IV data. Fixing the healthy volunteer parameters and applying IIV to V ss, instead of V c only or V c and V p separately, aimed to preserve the meaning of the peripheral compartment parameters and the contribution of the peripheral compartment to the distribution phase observed in IV data. An effect of body weight, including time‐varying body weight in children and adolescents, was included on CL, V ss, and Q as a power model. Standard errors for estimated parameters were quite small (RSE generally < 1%), possibly resulting from the fixing of several structural parameters to values from healthy volunteers, which may also have allowed for high precision of the remaining estimated parameters. High shrinkage was observed for V ss, implying that η values for V ss should not be relied on for detecting covariate relationships. However, as V ss η values were not used to make inferences on the model or to select models for testing, these factors were not considered problematic. It is possible that V max could change as a result of target expression and therefore differ between healthy volunteers and patients with EoE. As the healthy volunteers received a single dose of dupilumab with extensive sampling during the terminal elimination phase, the differences in parameters could also be a result of different dynamics that are not observable in patients with EoE, where most samples represent sparse collection of trough samples after multiple doses. Dupilumab demonstrated weight‐dependent CL and distribution volume in this population, consistent with prior analyses of dupilumab and monoclonal antibodies at large. An inverse relationship was also characterized between baseline albumin level and CL, where CL decreased as albumin levels increased. The typical V ss and linear CL at steady‐state in the EoE population were 5.01 L and 0.135 L/day, respectively, based on a reference body weight of 70 kg. The final model demonstrated improvements in the CL η distributions with respect to weight and age (continuous and categorical groupings) as compared with the base model. A positive trend in CL vs. weight observed using the base model was eliminated, and CL vs. age was left with only a slight negative slope that did not visibly deviate from the regression line. Additionally, although age is of particular interest as a covariate in pediatric populations, it was not supported as a covariate on K a in the final model. Differences in observed PK between pediatric and adult patients with EoE were primarily body weight driven and were accounted for in the model. In prior studies investigating monoclonal antibody clearance in pediatric subjects, a maturation effect has been identified in very young children, characterized by clearance increasing with age. 29 While this effect was not observed in this EoE popPK model, the data set did not include infants aged < 1 year in whom effects of maturation on CL are most pronounced. As such, sensitivity to detect this effect may have been diminished.

The attainment of symptomatic improvement with the 300 mg qw regimen in adults and adolescents in LIBERTY EoE TREET, but not the 300 mg q2w regimen, emphasized the clinical importance of achieving 300 mg qw‐like dupilumab exposures for pediatric patients with EoE. 14 Additional exposure–response analyses of data from LIBERTY EoE TREET further supported this higher effective dose of dupilumab in patients with EoE compared with indications in other type 2 inflammatory diseases. 27 While exploratory analysis indicated generally adequate exposure matching of the HE and LE regimens in Parts A and B of EoE KIDS with the 300 mg qw and q2w doses in LIBERTY EoE TREET, respectively, there were certain subgroups where exposures tended to be lower than the reference population. These subgroups of patients may benefit from more frequent administration and/or a higher dose of dupilumab, as demonstrated by the final model. The model was also utilized to determine an appropriate regimen for adults and adolescents weighing < 40 kg that were not evaluated in either LIBERTY EoE TREET or EoE KIDS.

A key finding of the model was that, after accounting for body weight, age did not impact the pharmacokinetics of dupilumab in patients with EoE. The dupilumab HE arm in EoE KIDS included a 300 mg q2w regimen for participants ≥ 30 to < 60 kg, resulting in pediatric patients weighing ≥ 40 to < 60 kg receiving dupilumab injections at half the frequency of the regimen approved in similar‐sized adults and adolescents. This popPK model, in concert with existing LIBERTY EoE TREET data that confirmed a favorable efficacy and safety profile in similarly sized adults and adolescents, supported extension of the 300 mg qw regimen to all patients with EoE weighing ≥ 40 kg, irrespective of age. Adults and adolescents < 40 kg were also expected to exhibit exposures comparable to similarly sized pediatric patients. Supported by the results of EoE KIDS in pediatric patients ≥ 30 to < 40 kg, the 300 mg q2w dose was extended to include the adult and adolescent population within that weight range who were previously excluded from the indicated population of patients ≥ 40 kg. Based on the combined clinical findings of the phase III dupilumab studies and the popPK modeling discussed herein, the US FDA approval of dupilumab in patients with EoE was extended to those aged ≥ 1 year and weighing ≥ 15 kg, consisting of dupilumab 200 mg q2w, 300 mg q2w, and 300 mg qw in patients weighing ≥ 15 to < 30 kg, ≥ 30 to < 40 kg, and ≥ 40 kg, respectively. 11

For pediatric patients weighing < 15 kg, empirical data for dupilumab are limited to a relatively small number of patients enrolled in EoE KIDS, and limit conclusions that can be drawn in this population. 15 The data that are available suggest that patients in EoE KIDS weighing ≥ 5 to < 15 kg who received dupilumab 100 mg q2w or 200 mg q4w achieved exposures that, while higher than the 300 mg q2w regimen in adults and adolescents, failed to reach those associated with the 300 mg qw reference regimen. Simulations suggested that a 200 mg q3w regimen would result in a C trough,ss for this group more closely matching the dupilumab exposure of the efficacious 300 mg qw dose in the ≥ 40 kg group in LIBERTY EoE TREET; however, those exposures also exceed the ranges evaluated in children < 15 kg to date. 15 While no exposure‐related safety events have been identified in any patients with EoE during clinical trials to date, it is important to verify the benefit:risk of this novel regimen in these young children in additional clinical studies. 14 , 15 , 17

The weight‐tiered regimen of dupilumab selected for the Part C 108‐week open‐label extension of the EoE KIDS study for participants < 12 years of age that completed EoE KIDS Part A and B was 200 mg q3w (≥ 5 to < 15 kg), 200 mg q2w (≥ 15 to < 30 kg), 300 mg q2w (≥ 30 to < 40 kg), and 300 mg qw (≥ 40 kg). 15 An additional clinical study to evaluate the safety, PK, and efficacy of dupilumab 200 mg q3w in children with EoE weighing ≥ 5 to < 15 kg is warranted.

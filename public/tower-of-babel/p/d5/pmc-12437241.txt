In this issue, 5 original articles and 1 brief communication have been published.

1. Analysis of Self-Sufficiency and Factors Affecting Migration Between Medical Service Regions in Korea Among CKD Patients.

Kim S, et al.

This study investigated the mobility of 37,748 CKD patients in South Korea using NHIS data (2018–2022). Results revealed high self-sufficiency rates in metropolitan areas, while transplant patients showed the lowest local service use. Factors such as age, income, hospital size, and community demographics influenced treatment location. Older and lower-income patients tended to remain local, while those visiting tertiary hospitals traveled more frequently. The findings suggest healthcare policy should provide clear transplant hospital selection criteria and address disparities in CKD patient self-sufficiency, ensuring equitable access and efficient resource management.

2. Is the Modified Allen's Test a Useful Tool for Evaluating the Vascular Dominance of the Forearm?

Ki SH, et al.

The modified Allen’s test (MAT) is often used before forearm flap harvesting to assess collateral circulation. This study measured radial and ulnar artery diameters by CTA and compared them with MAT results. Although the radial artery was significantly larger than the ulnar artery, no correlation existed between vessel size and MAT outcomes. The study concludes that MAT reflects collateral circulation but not arterial diameters. Therefore, surgical decisions involving radial or ulnar artery sacrifice should not rely solely on MAT. Preoperative CTA or ultrasonography is recommended for accurate vascular assessment.

3. Acute Total Hip Arthroplasty in Acetabular Fractures in Korea: National Claim Database Study.

Lee YK, et al.

Using Korean national claims data (2007–2018), this study analyzed outcomes of acute THA in 14,088 patients with acetabular fractures. Only 153 patients (1.1%) underwent acute THA, with an average follow-up of 5.8 years. Reoperation occurred in 14.4% of cases, mostly cup revisions. Ten-year survivorship was 92.1% for cup revision, but total revision rates were suboptimal. The declining use of acute THA in South Korea likely reflects these unsatisfactory outcomes. The findings highlight the need for careful patient selection and long-term evaluation of acute THA effectiveness in acetabular fractures.

4. Clinical Outcomes of Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation Use in Patients With Intracranial Hemorrhage.

Kim HS, et al.

This retrospective review assessed ECMO outcomes in 18 patients with intracranial hemorrhage and acute cardiopulmonary failure (2011–2020). Ten cases were traumatic, and the leading ECMO indication was ARDS. Survival outcomes were promising: 72% survived 30 days, and 61% were discharged. Two patients required neurosurgery for worsening hemorrhage, yet both recovered without neurological deterioration. These results suggest ECMO should not be categorically contraindicated in patients with intracranial hemorrhage. Instead, individualized evaluation may support its use as a life-saving option when conventional therapies fail.

5. Comparison Between the Mini-Mental State Examination and the Mini-Mental State Examination-2 in Patients With Mild Cognitive Impairment and Alzheimer’s Disease.

Kim S, et al.

This study compared MMSE-2 and K-MMSE in differentiating normal aging, MCI, and AD among 414 Korean participants. MMSE-2 achieved higher overall classification accuracy (71.1%) than K-MMSE (67.9%), particularly in distinguishing healthy controls from MCI patients. ROC analyses showed MMSE-2 versions had better sensitivity between normal and MCI, while K-MMSE outperformed in differentiating MCI from AD. Findings emphasize tailoring test selection to diagnostic goals: MMSE-2 is preferable for early cognitive impairment detection, while K-MMSE remains useful for distinguishing progressive decline toward Alzheimer’s disease.

6. Quantitative Changes in the Number of Basic Medicine Faculty at Medical Schools in Korea: A Cross-Sectional Study.

Kim S, et al.

This nationwide cross-sectional study surveyed 40 Korean medical schools (2023) to examine faculty and graduate student trends in basic medicine. Results showed MD professors comprised 53.7% of faculty, but 15% will retire within 5 years, with limited replacement by MD graduate students. Non-MD faculty are increasingly prevalent, especially at associate and assistant professor levels. The shortage is most severe in schools with increased student admissions. The study concludes that Korea faces a persistent decline in MD basic medicine faculty, urging strategic policy reforms, faculty recruitment, and ongoing assessments to sustain education quality.

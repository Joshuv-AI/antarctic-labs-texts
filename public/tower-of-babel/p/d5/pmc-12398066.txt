Quality of life among people with disabilities and its related factors in Iran: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Fatemeh Darabi, Zahra Najafi, Kamran Arvan, Nadia Mohammadi, Fatemeh Khosravi Shadmani, Shahin Soltani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDisability is a multifaceted global public health issue that adversely affects individuals’ quality of life (QoL), especially in low- and middle-income countries such as Iran. Despite the increasing prevalence of disabilities due to aging populations and chronic diseases, limited data exist on the QoL and its determinants among people with disabilities (PWD) in Iran. This study aims to assess the QoL status and identify related factors among adults with physical disabilities in Iran.MethodsA cross-sectional analytical study was conducted nationally from 2024 to 2025 involving 867 adults aged 16 years and older with physical disabilities who were members of the Iranian Society for People with Disabilities. Participants were selected via convenience sampling. Data were collected using demographic, socioeconomic, disability assessment (Washington Group Short Set), and WHOQOL-BREF questionnaires. Multiple linear regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with QoL domains and overall QoL score, adjusting for confounders. Socioeconomic status was derived using Principal Component Analysis.ResultsThe mean age of participants was 38.08 years; 63.44% were male. Higher education levels were positively associated with better QoL across all domains (physical, psychological, social, environmental) and total QoL scores (p < 0.001). Greater disability severity significantly decreased QoL scores in all domains (p < 0.001). Marital status negatively impacted physical health (β = -3.14, p = 0.04). Wealth index showed positive associations with environmental QoL (β = 1.06, p = 0.01) and a borderline effect on total QoL (p = 0.07). Age negatively influenced environmental QoL (β = -0.14, p = 0.02). Other demographic variables showed no significant effects.ConclusionThe study highlights that higher education and lower disability severity are key determinants of better QoL among Iranian adults with physical disabilities. Socioeconomic factors, particularly wealth and education, alongside disability level, significantly influence QoL outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of tailored policies and interventions focusing on education, rehabilitation, and socioeconomic support to enhance the well-being and social participation of PWD in Iran.

Background

Disability, defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as impairments, activity limitations, and participation restrictions caused by physical or mental conditions, is increasingly recognized as a critical global public health, human rights, and development issue [1, 2]. Globally, approximately 1.3 billion people—16% of the world’s population—live with significant disabilities, with 80% residing in low- and middle-income countries [3, 4]. In Iran, estimates range from 1 to 4% of the population [5]. The rising prevalence of disability is attributed to aging populations, chronic diseases, lifestyle changes, and advances in medical care [1, 6].

People with disabilities often face higher healthcare needs but encounter substantial barriers in accessing services, including high costs, limited transportation, negative attitudes, and systemic discrimination [7–9]. In developing countries, only a small proportion—2–3%—have access to rehabilitation services, while the vast majority remain underserved [9]. In Iran, similar barriers to healthcare access have been reported, along with insufficient policy implementation and cultural stigma [10–14].

Quality of life (QoL), encompassing physical, psychological, and social well-being, has emerged as a central concept for assessing and addressing the needs of individuals with disabilities [15, 16]. Numerous studies show that disability—particularly intellectual, physical, or chronic conditions—can significantly reduce QoL by limiting daily functioning, autonomy, and participation in social and family life [17–20]. Social isolation, self-esteem issues, and poor mental health are commonly reported among people with disabilities, especially in settings where social support is limited [21–26].

Evidence from Iran reflects similar patterns. For instance, individuals with physical and intellectual disabilities tend to report lower QoL scores, particularly in domains related to independence and social participation [18, 27, 28]. Factors such as employment opportunities, access to education, supportive environments, and inclusive urban design have been identified as key determinants of QoL [29–31]. Improving QoL for individuals with disabilities is not only a moral and developmental imperative but also essential for effective rehabilitation, social integration, and health policy planning [ 4, 27, 32].

While several studies in Iran have examined the quality of life among people with disabilities, most have been limited in scope—focusing on small samples, specific disability types, or single geographic regions. Few have assessed QoL at the national level while adjusting for socioeconomic status, disability severity, and demographic factors using validated instruments. This study fills that gap by using a large, diverse national sample and applying both the WHOQOL-BREF and the Washington Group Short Set to explore multidimensional QoL outcomes. It offers new insights into how educational attainment, economic conditions, and disability severity interact to shape QoL among adults with physical disabilities in Iran, thereby informing more inclusive and equitable health and social policies. This study, therefore, aims to explore the QoL and its associated factors among individuals with disabilities in Iran.

Method

This study was cross-sectional analytical research conducted to determine the quality-of-life status of individuals with disabilities and its associated factors in Iran. The study was conducted at the national level during 2024–2025.

Participants

The study population included adults (aged 16 years and older) with physical disabilities who were members of the Iranian Society for People with Disabilities at the time of the study. This organization had branches in all provinces of Iran and provided various educational, supportive, and cultural services to people with disabilities. Participants were selected using a convenience sampling method. Currently, around 50,000 individuals with physical disabilities were registered as members of the organization. Additionally, approximately 3,000 members were active in the organization’s Telegram, WhatsApp, and Eitaa channels.

Data collection

Data were collected at the national level through the organization’s 20 branches and 40 offices using a convenience sampling method. The questionnaire was distributed in both paper and electronic formats. Paper questionnaires were sent to each province’s association for individuals who lacked access to the internet or mobile phones and visited the branches in person. The electronic questionnaire link was shared in the organization’s Telegram, WhatsApp, and Eitaa channels. Eligible participants (based on the study’s inclusion criteria) were asked to complete the questionnaire. The study’s objective and inclusion criteria were explained alongside the questionnaire link to ensure that only eligible individuals participated.

Before completing the paper questionnaire, participants were asked whether they had already completed the electronic version. If they had not, they were allowed to complete the paper version with their consent. Participants who were unable to complete the questionnaire independently due to physical or literacy-related limitations were offered assistance from their caregivers, family members, or friends. These individuals only assisted with reading or writing responses as instructed by the participant, without interpreting or influencing the answers. The questionnaire guide included detailed instructions to ensure that the responses reflected the participant’s own views. This approach was intended to preserve the integrity of the data while ensuring inclusiveness and accessibility for participants with diverse needs. Instructions and necessary explanations for completing the questionnaire were provided in the questionnaires’ guides.

Sample size

Currently, the Iranian Society for People with Disabilities had approximately 50,000 registered members with physical disabilities. Given this population, the required sample size was calculated using Cochran’s formula for a finite population, with a 95% confidence level and a margin of error of 0.035. The estimated sample size was 772 participants. To account for potential attrition, the sample size was increased by 10%, resulting in a final sample size of 850 participants.

The questionnaire was distributed through both electronic (via SMS and social media channels) and paper formats (via organization branches).

Data analysis

Data analysis was conducted using Stata v14. Multiple linear regression was performed to identify factors influencing QoL, adjusting for potential confounders such as comorbid conditions. Socioeconomic status was assessed using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), classifying participants into five economic groups. All statistical tests were conducted at a significance level of 0.05, with p-values below this threshold considered statistically significant.

Results

The study included 867 participants, of whom 63.44% were male and 36.56% were female. The participants’ ages ranged from 16 to 69 years, with a mean age of 38.08 years (95% CI: 37.36–38.81). In terms of marital status, 43.82% were single, 50.11% were married, and 6.07% were widowed or divorced. Regarding household headship, 53.50% were identified as heads of their households. The majority of participants (79.70%) lived in urban areas, while 20.30% resided in rural regions. Family size varied, with most households having three (26.42%) or four (28.80%) members, while only 9.18% had six or more members. Educational attainment showed that 35.58% had completed secondary education, 20.43% held a diploma, and 8.31% had no formal education. Wealth distribution was relatively balanced, with each quintile comprising approximately 20% of participants. Disability severity, categorized into four quartiles, showed that 25.00% of participants fell into the least severe category, while 20.82% had the highest level of disability (Table 1).

Discussion

This study examined the quality of life among people with disabilities and its related factors in Iran. The study, found that disability severity had a consistently strong negative association with all domains of QoL, including physical health, psychological health, social relationships, environmental quality, and overall QoL scores. Higher education levels were positively and significantly associated with better outcomes across all domains, while marital status negatively impacted physical health, and age showed a slight negative effect on environmental quality. Wealth index had a modest positive association with environmental and overall QoL scores, though it was only borderline significant. Other demographic factors such as gender, household headship, family size, and urban/rural location did not show significant associations. Overall, the findings highlight that greater disability and lower educational and socioeconomic status are key predictors of poorer QoL among individuals with disabilities.

In this study, the disability score showed a strong negative association with physical health, a key dimension of QoL, indicating that higher levels of disability are associated with poorer physical health outcomes. A study conducted among students with disabilities in Kerman also demonstrated the differential impact of disability on QoL. Specifically, the type and cause of disability were found to have varying effects, with individuals with physical or multiple disabilities experiencing lower levels of QoL [42]. Severity and location of disability significantly influence QoL, with individuals who have severe or lower limb disabilities, especially those requiring third-party assistance, experiencing notably lower well-being [43].

Our study findings showed that higher education levels were consistently associated with improved QoL across all domains. In this regard, the study in Malaysia found that people with disabilities who have higher levels of education tend to experience a better QoL. Among disability types, individuals with physical disabilities had the lowest scores across most life domains, while those with visual impairments scored lowest in facility-related areas. Hearing and speech-impaired individuals reported higher levels of discrimination. Based on these findings, education policies for PWD should pay particular attention to environmental improvements for the visually impaired and physical accessibility for those with physical disabilities [44].

The findings of this study indicated that higher levels of disability were associated with lower psychological health scores. In another study, QoL was evaluated based on the type of disability. The findings revealed that scores in the psychological domain were consistently low, indicating challenges related to negative emotions, body image, appearance, spirituality, self-esteem, and cognitive well-being. Notably, the psychological domain showed reduced scores across all disability types [24].

Social support significantly influences the QoL in individuals with disabilities. Loss of support especially in workplaces or among peers lowers QoL, particularly in children with disability. Studies from Southeast Asia link increased social support and religiosity to better QoL, while research from Iran and Spain shows that stigma and discrimination negatively affect it [43].

The regression analysis in present study shows that older age and higher disability levels are linked to lower environmental QoL. Also, the results of a study showed that there is a significant relationship between QoL and environmental barriers so that as environmental barriers around children with cerebral palsy decrease, the QoL increases and vice versa [45].

The study also found that wealth status had a modest positive association with QoL, particularly in the environmental and total domains. Similar results have been reported in previous studies.The family’s economic status influenced students with disabilities’ QoL, with those from lower-income households experiencing poorer physical and mental health compared to their peers from more financially stable backgrounds [42].This suggests that individuals from higher-income households may have better access to healthcare, assistive technologies, and safer living environments factors that enhance QoL [46, 47].

Policy implications

These findings carry important policy implications. Addressing disparities in education and economic opportunities, while enhancing rehabilitation and support services for those with severe disabilities, is essential for promoting equity. Programs should aim to improve physical access, provide psychosocial support, and reduce discrimination, especially in underserved regions. Expanding disability-inclusive policies within the healthcare and education systems will be instrumental in reducing structural barriers.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design restricts causal inferences between predictors and quality of life outcomes. Longitudinal studies are required to better understand temporal relationships and potential bidirectionality of these associations.

Second, the use of convenience sampling from a specific organizational registry—namely, the Iranian Society for people with disabilities—introduces the possibility of selection bias. Individuals affiliated with this organization may differ systematically from the broader population of people with disabilities in Iran in terms of socioeconomic status, access to services, advocacy engagement, or digital literacy. As such, the study sample may not be fully representative of all individuals with disabilities, particularly those who are not connected to formal support networks or who face greater structural barriers. This limitation reduces the external validity and generalizability of the findings to the wider disabled population in Iran. Future studies using random or stratified sampling techniques across multiple service providers and geographic areas would provide a more representative assessment.

Third, the study did not include potentially influential variables such as the type of physical disability (e.g., upper vs. lower limb involvement), duration of disability, or access to assistive technologies and rehabilitation services. These variables are known to significantly impact quality of life. However, due to concerns about questionnaire length, accessibility, and response burden—particularly for individuals with physical or cognitive limitations—we prioritized the inclusion of core variables that could be reliably captured through self-report. Future studies should incorporate these detailed clinical and service-related variables to enable a more comprehensive understanding of quality of life determinants and inform targeted interventions.

Conclusion

This study highlights that the severity of disability, level of education, and economic status are key determinants of quality of life among people with physical disabilities in Iran. The findings underscore the profound impact of disability on all dimensions of QoL—physical, psychological, social, and environmental—and point to the protective role of education and financial well-being.

Improving the QoL of individuals with disabilities requires a comprehensive and inclusive approach that addresses structural and social barriers. Expanding access to education, creating equitable economic opportunities, and enhancing disability-inclusive healthcare and rehabilitation services are essential steps toward promoting well-being and reducing disparities.

Policymakers should prioritize multisectoral strategies that ensure equal access to resources and opportunities, reduce discrimination, and support the active participation of people with disabilities in society. By doing so, it is possible to narrow the QoL gap, advance social justice, and move toward greater health equity for this underserved population.

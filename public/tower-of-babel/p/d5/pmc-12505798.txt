Survival analysis of metachronous bilateral ectopic breast cancer utilizing the SEER database and the pioneering construction of a nomogram model
Authors: Lizhi Teng, Juntong Du, Yuhan Dong, Kang Li, Weiyang Tao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMetachronous bilateral ectopic breast cancer (MBEBC) is clinically rare, but the incidence has been increasing in recent years and no clear therapeutic guidance or prognostic assessment is available.MethodsData on MBEBC patients from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database were gathered and randomly split into a training set and a validation set at a 7:3 ratio. Independent prognostic risk factors were identified through both univariate and multivariate analyses, and a nomogram was constructed based on these factors to predict survival outcomes.ResultsFrom the SEER database, we collected data on a total of 8240 patients spanning the years 2005–2015. These patients were then randomly divided into a training set (5768) and a validation set (2472) for analysis. The clinicopathological features indicated that Grade 2 tumors were the most prevalent, with invasive ductal carcinoma comprising 71.2% of the cases. Additionally, the majority of MBEBC patients were classified as N0, and only a small fraction (4.2%) exhibited distant metastases. A multivariate COX regression model was developed to identify independent prognostic risk factors for patients whose first and second tumors were both invasive ductal carcinomas, as well as those with more extensive pathological types. Nomograms were also constructed for survival prediction of overall survival (OS) and breast cancer-special survival (BCSS) at 3, 5, and 10 years. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted and area under the curve (AUC) values were calculated. The AUC was greater than 0.7 in all models, with a 10-year OS of 78.0 (76.0–80.0) and a BCSS of 77.6 (76.0–79.3) in all patients. The calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrate that the nomogram possesses strong clinical predictive capability and high predictive accuracy.ConclusionThis study detailed the clinicopathological characteristics of patients with the clinically rare MBEBC and identified independent prognostic risk factors across various pathology types. Additionally, a nomogram was developed for individualized prediction of patients' BCSS and OS, offering a new adjunctive tool for the clinical management of MBEBC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03214-x.Keywords: Metachronous bilateral ectopic breast cancer, Infiltrating duct carcinoma, SEER database, Nomogram

Introduction

Breast cancer ranks as the most prevalent malignant tumor globally. Between 2015 and 2019, the incidence of breast cancer is projected to increase by 0.6–1% annually [1]. According to the World Health Organization, 2020 alone saw an estimated 2.3 million new cases among women, resulting in 685,000 deaths [2, 3]. Currently, breast cancer largely relies on American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) delineation for clinical staging (stages I–IV), which evaluates cancer progression through primary tumor size (T0–T4), regional lymph node metastasis (N0–N3), and distant metastasis (M0/M1) as a guide to treatment and prognostic judgment [4]. Consequently, it is a major challenge to break through the existing assessment framework and optimize the surveillance and clinical management of women diagnosed with two primary breast cancers, either concurrently or sequentially.

Metachronous bilateral ectopic breast cancer (MBEBC) is characterized by an interval of more than 6 months between diagnoses, with the two diagnoses occurring on different affected sides, distinguishing it from common tumor recurrence [5]. The clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of MBEBC are more severe and aggressive compared to solitary tumors and bilateral synchronous tumors [6]. Moreover, the prevalence of MBEBC has escalated progressively over the past four decades.

At present, clinical morbidity associated with MBEBC remains inadequately characterized, with an accompanying scarcity of comprehensive clinical descriptions of secondary tumors. And then, the more studies reported in clinical studies are limited to case reports. Case reports on MBEBC have documented instances where distinct histological variants are observed at different time intervals [7]. Additionally, an increased risk of distant metastasis has been identified in clinical settings where adjuvant chemotherapy was omitted after patients underwent bilateral mastectomy and axillary dissection [8]. A decade ago, Mikael Hartman et al. illustrated that MBEBC has a lower survival rate, while stating that age at first diagnosis and chemotherapy are indicative of prognosis. Unfortunately, the study was unable to quantify these risk factors and to establish the prognostic impact of other factors such as TNM stage. Therefore, it is particularly significant to quantify the different prognostic risk factors regarding MBEBC and to achieve individualized treatment with the construction of the nomogram.

Given that MBEBC is clinically rare yet its incidence is rising each year, there is a noticeable lack of effective strategies for survival prediction and treatment. Hence, this study should leverage the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to collect data on MBEBC patients from 2005 to 2015. By examining clinicopathological characteristics, identifying independent prognostic risk factors, and evaluating the impact of different factors on long-term survival, the research ultimately develop a nomogram to improve prognostic assessments.

Methods

The clinicopathological details of the patients were retrieved from the SEER database, which is considered the most authoritative source of information on cancer incidence and survival in the United States (https://seer.cancer.gov/). The SEER database compiles and publishes extensive statistics on cancer incidence and survival covering the years 1973–2018. It includes data on registered cancer patients from every state, encompassing demographic characteristics (such as age, gender, and race), treatment approaches, and survival outcomes [9, 10]. Access to the SEER database, along with a waiver of informed consent, was granted following a thorough review and application process, which concluded with the applicant logging in using their email address.

Access to demographic information

We collected clinicopathological data, treatment regimens, and survival status of patients from the SEER*Stat 8.4.4 software for the period between 2005 and 2015. Initially, there were 68,225 treatment records for patients with bilateral breast cancer. After excluding those with simultaneous bilateral concurrent disease, the dataset was reduced to 33,673 treatment records. The information of the enrolled patients was further refined based on specific inclusion criteria: (1) female gender; (2) age over 18 years; (3) bilateral breast disease with a heterochronous presentation; and (4) more than 12 months between diagnoses. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) male patients; (2) presence of a single tumor; (3) breast cancer identified as a secondary tumor; (4) patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy; and (5) tumors classified as originally recurrent. For patients with multiple consultations for bilateral breast cancer, data were meticulously curated using the records from their initial presentation and the final contralateral consultation. Ultimately, a total of 8240 patients meeting the criteria for MBEBC were identified and included in the analysis.

The baseline variables collected for the research included age, race (divided into white, black, and Asian or Pacific Islander), tumor grade (divided into G1, G2, G3, and unknown), pathology (comprising mainly infiltrating duct carcinoma of ICD-O-3:8500/3 and lobular carcinoma of ICD-O-3:8520/3), T-stage (divided into T1, T2, T3, and T4), N-stage (divided into N0, N1, N2, and N3), M-stage (divided into M0 and M1), surgical treatment (divided into conservative surgery and mastectomy), radiotherapy, chemotherapy, ER-status, PR-status, and the interval between clinical visits.

Development of the nomogram and validation of the model

A comprehensive analysis of 8240 patients was performed, the training (5768) and validation (2472) sets were randomly divided in a 7:3 ratio by simple randomization method through the sample() function in R 4.3.3, and the differences between the groups were tested by the chi-square test (p > 0.05). The training set was utilized to identify prognostically relevant independent risk factors, whereas the validation set served to assess both the predictive accuracy and clinical utility of the nomogram model (packages: rms). The independent prognostic risk factors, identified through univariate and multivariate analyses via SPSS 22.0, were ultimately incorporated into the construction of the nomogram. The sensitivity and specificity of the nomogram were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves (package: riskRegression and survival), and the overall efficacy of the model was assessed by calculating the area under the curve (AUC) values. To evaluate the accuracy of the probabilistic predictions made by the nomogram, calibration curves (package: rms and survival) were plotted for the consistency test. To explore the clinical utility of the nomograms, decision curve analysis (DCA) curves were constructed to assess the survival benefit (package: ggDCA, rms, and survival).

Statistical analysis

The chi-square test was utilized to compare differences between groups during the randomized grouping of the training and validation sets, ensuring no significant discrepancies (p ≧ 0.05). Clinicopathological characteristics were analyzed univariate using the log-rank test, and variables showing statistical significance (p < 0.05) were included in multivariate COX regression analysis. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used. To assess the accuracy, discrimination, and clinical predictive performance of the nomograms, ROC curves were constructed and quantified using AUC. Furthermore, the clinical outcomes and advantages were validated by employing the bootstrap method with 300 repetitions to generate calibration curves and utilizing DCA to evaluate the predictive models.

Results

The patients were randomly divided into a training set (5768; 70%) and a validation set (2472; 30%) in a ratio of 7–3. The training set was mainly used for the identification of independent risk factors and the construction of the nomogram, and the validation set was mainly used to verify the accuracy and practicability of the nomogram. Demographic information and baseline characteristics

In the training set, tumor grade G2 was the most common clinicopathological finding. There were significant differences in pathology types, with invasive ductal carcinoma accounting for 71.2%. Further analysis was conducted on patients who had consistent pathology across both assessments. The majority of MBEBC patients are staged as N0, with very few showing distant metastases (4.2%). Additionally, less than 30% of these patients receive adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy (Table 1).

For the baseline characterization of the two tumor incidences in MBEBC patients within the training set, 3169 patients were pathologically confirmed with invasive ductal carcinoma at both occurrences. The majority of initial breast cancers were detected before the age of 65, with a statistical significant difference in tumor grade or TNM stage between the consultations. While 66.6% of patients initially chose breast-conserving surgery, it was observed that for ectopic tumors arising at different times, 56.8% opted for mastectomy. Adjuvant radiotherapy was administered to over half of the patients after the initial tumor, whereas only 28.6% received this treatment for ectopic neoplastic tumors. Likewise, there was a significant reduction in the choice of adjuvant chemotherapy for those with ectopic neoplastic tumors (Table 2). For patients encompassing all pathology types, the clinicopathological data and baseline characteristics exhibited analogous trends, with only nuanced differences observed (Table 3). Based on recent access to the clinic, the clinicopathological stage of the patient is favorable, although options for adjuvant therapy remain limited. In addition, considering the initial baseline characteristics of the breast cancer, there remains a statistical significant disparity between tumor grade and TNM classification. However, variations are evident in the treatment strategies that follow.

Survival analysis and establishment of independent prognostic risk factors

In the training set, the BCSS rates at 3, 5, and 10 years for patients with invasive ductal carcinoma, observed at both visits in the MBEBC cohort, were 86.4, 79.9, and 73.7%, and the OS rates at 3, 5, and 10 years were 78.4, 66.9, and 49.3%. To determine the independent prognostic risk factors linked to MBEBC with invasive ductal carcinoma, both univariate log-rank analysis (Table 4) and multivariate analysis (Table 6) were employed. Among MBEBC patients with two pathological types of invasive carcinoma, BCSS linked to several factors: the grade, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, and chemotherapy of the initial tumor occurrence, as well as age, grade, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, surgery, HR-status, and the interval between contralateral occurrences (All variables p < 0.05) at the last visit. Simultaneously, OS was associated with the age at first tumor occurrence, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, and radiotherapy, as well as with age, grade, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, surgery, and HR-status (all variables p < 0.05) at the last visit.

Multivariate analysis of MBEBC patients with invasive carcinoma pathology

In the training set, the 3-, 5-, and 10-year BCSS rates were 89.4, 84.5, and 79.4%, respectively, whereas the OS rates for the same periods were 83.3, 74.2, and 60.0% for all MBEBC patients. To determine the independent prognostic risk factors for all patients, both univariate log-rank analysis (Table 5) and multivariate analysis (Table 7) were employed. In patients with MBEBC, BCSS was linked to several factors, including the grade, T-stage, N-stage, and M-stage of the initial breast cancer tumor. Additionally, age at the last visit, grade, pathology, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, surgery, radiation, HR-status, and the intervals between bilateral diagnoses (All variables p < 0.05) also influenced BCSS. Moreover, OS was associated with the age at the first breast cancer occurrence, pathology, T-stage, N-stage, and M-stage, as well as with age at the last visit, grade, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and HR-status (all variables p < 0.05).

Development and validation of predictive models using nomograms

Independent prognostic risk factors identified through multivariate analysis (Tables 6 and 7) were incorporated into the nomogram to predict 3-, 5-, and 10-year BCSS (Fig. 1) and OS (Fig. 2) for patients with MBEBC where the pathology indicated invasive carcinoma on both occasions, as well as for the broader MBEBC patient group (Figs. 3 and 4). Employing data from the validation set substantiates the accuracy and clinical feasibility of the developed nomogram. Weights were assigned to various prognostic risk factors within the nomogram. Vertical lines were drawn to represent the status of each variable, aligning with their respective scores. The individual scores were then totaled, and a vertical line corresponding to this total score was used to determine the patient's BCSS or OS at 3, 5, and 10 years.

The sensitivity and specificity of the nomogram were scrutinized using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the model's overall efficacy was appraised by calculating the area under the curve (AUC) values. An AUC value greater than 0.7 was deemed indicative of a good model performance [11]. Presented in Table 8 are the AUC values for various subgroups, complemented by ROC curves that depict the BCSS (Fig. 5a) and OS (Fig. 6a) at 3, 5, and 10 years for both MBEBC patients with invasive carcinomas and all patients (Figs. 7a and 8a) in the training set. Additionally, ROC curves for 3-, 5-, and 10-year BCSS and OS were plotted in the validation set for MBEBC patients with invasive carcinoma (Supplements 1 A and 2 A), as well as for all MBEBC patients (Supplements 3 A and 4 A). To assess the accuracy of the nomogram's probabilistic predictions, we plotted calibration curves for consistency testing. The points on these calibration curves aligned closely with the ideal diagonal in both the training (Figs. 5b, 6b, 7b and 8b) and validation (Supplement 1B, 2B, 3B and 4 A) sets. To explore the clinical utility of the nomogram, we constructed decision curve analysis (DCA) curves to assess the survival benefit in both the training (Figs. 5c, 6c, 7c and 8c) and validation (Supplements 1 C, 2 C, 3 C and 4 C) sets. By observing the model curves, we determined that the nomogram provided a superior net survival benefit.

Discussion

Contralateral breast cancer, recognized as a second primary tumor, has been acknowledged in the medical Literature since 1920 [12]. With advancements in imaging technology facilitating early breast cancer diagnosis, alongside improvements in medical treatments and extended patient survival rates, there has been a consistent year-on-year increase in the incidence of patients with MBEBC [13]. According to epidemiological analyses, the risk of developing contralateral breast cancer is 2 to 6 times higher for women with a breast cancer diagnosis than for those in the general population developing breast cancer for the first time [14]. This highlights the crucial need for vigilant monitoring of the contralateral breast's health in managing breast cancer patients. Regrettably, our understanding of MBEBC remains limited, and there is ongoing uncertainty about whether contralateral disease results from metastasis of an advanced primary tumor or if it is associated with prognostic factors like the patient's age [15].

In our study, we initially categorized patients based on the pathological type of their dual diagnoses, with particular emphasis on those diagnosed twice with invasive ductal carcinoma. We detailed the clinicopathological characteristics of MBEBC patients using comprehensive patient data sourced from the SEER database. Secondly, we evaluated the prognostic significance of diverse risk factors in both the primary and subsequent tumor diagnoses, elucidating the impact of the first-diagnosed tumors on survival outcomes in MBEBC patients. Finally, most importantly, we identified independent prognostic risk factors associated with MBEBC while constructing nomograms to quantify BCSS and OS at 3, 5, and 10 years for each risk factor.

A nomogram is an intricate graphical representation of mathematical formulas, akin to a calculating ruler, which illustrates the interplay and scale of variables within a specific equation [16]. In oncology, nomograms are extensively utilized to predict individual probabilities of various clinical events [17, 18]. We advanced our pursuit of personalized medicine by combining distinct MBEBC-associated risk factors to generate personalized survival probabilities. For example, in the clinical practice, for HR + patients in MBEBC, a nomogram is performed in conjunction with other risk factors of the patient to estimate the OS and BCSS, whereas for high-risk and low-survival patients, we can consider step-up and intensification of therapy, while for low-risk patients, we can consider step-down therapy to avoid side effects. This method enhances prediction accuracy and simplifies prognosis interpretation compared to traditional staging, thus aiding clinical decision-making.

Previous studies on survival prediction for MBEBC patients often appear to overlook factors associated with the initial tumor diagnosis. Assessing the prognosis of MBEBC is inherently challenging, as it hinges on factors such as age and the survival of women following their initial malignancy. It confirmed that MBEBC patients aged < 50 years were 3.9 times more likely to die from BC than women with unilateral cancer (95% CI, 3.5–4.5) [19]. An association between age and prognosis in MBEBC is equally supported by our findings, which showed that age at first diagnosis was highly correlated with long-term OS and was not significantly distinguished from BCSS. Simultaneously, the increased survival time of patients with onset of disease before 65 years of age following standardized treatment also leads to an increased risk of recurrence of the tumor. CI Li et al. showed that women diagnosed with their first breast cancer before the age of 45 have a risk of recurrence that is more than 5.0 times higher than those who develop their first breast cancer in the general population. Mikael Hartman et al. identified a susceptibility gene variant in 21.7% of 278 patients treated for bilateral breast cancer [20–22]. Combined with the results of this study, we recommend that MBEBC patients be tested for BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations, which can not only effectively guide clinical treatment but also pave the way for future research on the genetic susceptibility of MBEBC.

Invasive ductal carcinoma is the histological type with the highest incidence. However, it is noteworthy that lobular carcinoma seems to be associated with a poorer prognosis in patients with MBEBC. There is evidence that in situ lobular carcinoma and invasive lobular carcinoma typically exhibit more favorable clinical pathological characteristics and prognosis than invasive ductal carcinoma [23]. However, this contradicts the conclusions of this study, which found that invasive ductal carcinoma has a better prognosis than other pathological types. Additional research has revealed that the recurrence rate of breast cancer in patients with in situ lobular carcinoma is 2% [24]. The initial diagnosis of lobular carcinoma as a histological type was significantly linked to long-term survival (HR:1.087, 95% CI1.008–1.173, p = 0.030), with a first incidence rate of 8.3 percent in MBEBC patients. This rate increased to 11.3 percent in those with a second diagnosis, indicating a strong association with the BCSS (HR:1.162, 95% CI1.046–1.292, p = 0.005). The poor prognosis associated with lobular carcinoma was further substantiated by the quantification of various histological subtypes using a nomogram (Figs. 3 and 4). This finding suggests that patients in this group should receive close monitoring and regular evaluations during clinical follow-up.

Both tumor grade and TNM stage, assessed at the time of initial diagnosis and upon re-diagnosis, are intricately linked to patient prognosis and constitute a substantial portion of the nomogram. These elements are primary confounders that make it challenging to ascertain whether the cause of death was the first or second tumor, thus complicating prognostication and survival prediction efforts. A review of prognostic studies on MBEBC further supports the significant role of TNM stage and grade in prognosis in this study [12, 25–27]. The multivariate analysis results indicated that the risk associated with T-stage and N-stage was comparable, and the T-stage held similar weight for both the first and second tumors in the nomogram (Tables 6 and 7). In contrast, the risk significantly escalated for both tumors concerning M-stage, revealing that patients with distant metastases at the time of the second diagnosis faced a very poor prognosis (Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4). Notably, as reflected in Tables 2 and 3, T-stage, and N-stage exhibited a more favorable clinical phenotype upon the emergence of the second tumor. This improvement may be attributed to the critical role of early detection of the second tumor through regular monitoring by patients following the initial diagnosis.

In assessing surgical management, we observed that the percentage of patients choosing mastectomy initially surpassed 30% for both invasive ductal carcinoma and more extensive cancer types, and this figure rose to over 50% upon the occurrence of the second tumor. This elevated rate may be attributed to the widespread perception of mastectomy as a more aggressive treatment approach, or it may stem from patients'fear of the disease recurrence [28]. When it comes to surgery for patients with MBEBC, clinicians are increasingly concerned about the safety of breast-conserving surgery. A study by Qiuyan Huang et al. demonstrated that breast-conserving surgery is safe for specific MBEBC patients, but unfortunately, the study does not seem to define how to evaluate this in clinical practice [29]. However, in another investigation of OS after bilateral mastectomy in 94 MBEBC patients, the 5-year survival rate was 86.6%, a remarkably better result than the 5-year OS (74.2%) in the present study [30]. Several reports have shown that mastectomy generally has a better prognosis than breast-conserving surgery in patients with MBEBC and effectively reduces the risk of recurrence [21, 31, 32]. The aforementioned findings align with our results, indicating that total mastectomy offers greater benefits to patients in terms of BCSS and OS, irrespective of the pathology type. The study further elucidates that, in the surgical management of initially diagnosed tumors, long-term survival outcomes remain consistent regardless of the surgical approach utilized. This observation indirectly intimates that the manifestation of a second tumor is not contingent on the surgical method chosen for the initial intervention, thereby emphasizing the enduring significance of breast-conserving surgery.

Adjuvant therapy for patients with MEBEC is currently poorly defined and has not been mentioned in recent guidelines. Some studies propose that administering chemotherapy during the initial cancer episode reduces the risk of recurrence. Adjuvant chemotherapy treatment was illustrated in previous reports to have a dual effect on MBEBC patients while decreasing the risk, at the same time seems to worsen the prognosis [19]. The safety of the treatment was demonstrated in a single-center study of adjuvant radiotherapy in patients with MBEBC without metastasis at long-term follow-up [33]. As shown in Tables 6 and 7, the findings are similar: for invasive ductal carcinoma, adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy at the time of first diagnosis influence long-term survival differently. However, when it comes to recurrent tumors, adjuvant treatment does not appear to make a significant difference. Conversely, in more extensive pathological types, systemic adjuvant chemotherapy and local radiotherapy are pertinent to OS and do not show a correlation with the initial adjuvant treatment. Based on these findings, it is reasonable to infer that adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy exhibit varying degrees of sensitivity across different pathology types and tend to be less effective in cases of invasive ductal carcinoma [34]. Another possible explanation is that the contralateral tumor could be metastatic, influenced by prior systemic adjuvant chemotherapy, which may result in reduced sensitivity to subsequent treatments. Patients who undergo chemotherapy are often those with poorer clinical stage, potentially leading to outcomes such as a less favorable prognosis following chemotherapy.

The appearance of a second primary tumor at 5 years was associated with an improved survival prognosis in MBEBC patients in a study by Bo Pan et al. [35]. Similarly, in this article, we observed BCSS survival rates of 76.6% and OS survival rates of 54.0% for patients presenting with a neoplastic tumor within 5 years or less. For patients presenting with a neoplastic tumor after more than 5 years, the rates were 82.5% for BCSS and 64.1% for OS. Additionally, for those with a tumor onset at 10 years or more, the survival rates were 86.0% for BCSS and 68.8% for OS. The results indicate that the interval between the diagnosis of the first and second tumors seems to serve as a critical prognostic threshold at the 5-year mark. Beyond this point, patient survival progressively improves with each additional year past five years. This trend may be attributed to the fact that over 70% of MBEBC patients are HR positive, and endocrine therapy plays a significant role in their treatment and improved survival outcomes. Among breast cancer treatments, it has been evidenced that aromatase inhibitors in combination with tamoxifen have a better prognosis, while a 10-year regimen is superior to a 5-year course of treatment, with an absolute benefit of prolonged aromatase inhibitor therapy in women aged 50–79 years [36–38]. Both single-center and multicenter clinical studies on MBEBC have also illustrated the value of endocrine therapy for this group of patients, which can be reduced with endocrine therapy [24, 26]. Therefore, HR positivity in the initial tumor was not linked to survival in this study, likely due to the short interval between the patients'two cancers, allowing insufficient follow-up time to realize the effects of endocrine therapy. This was corroborated by the observed association between HR positivity and prognosis in the second tumor.

This paper has several limitations. Firstly, we unfortunately regret that HER2 status was only recorded in the SEER database in 2010, which led us to exclude HER2 + patients from the analysis in order to collect more comprehensive patient data. However, this does not disturb the overall results, as there is no record of targeted therapies in the SEER data, and even if we had access to this information, we would not have been able to explore it further. Simultaneous updates on targeted therapies in conjunction with clinical multicenter records will fill this gap in future studies. Secondly, some counter-intuitive variables emerged in certain nomograms due to an insufficient sample size and biases among different subgroups, but it does not affect our interpretation of the overall forecast results. Lastly, the selection of adjuvant chemotherapy and endocrine therapy remains controversial for MBEBC patients and, regrettably, these treatments were not included in the SEER database.

Hydrogen-Induced, Unexpectedly Strong 8c–2e ϕ Bond in An2H8 (An = Pa, Pu) Complexes
Authors: Xiaocheng Xu, Zongchang Han, Guang-Ze Zhang, Jia-Cheng Huang, Han-Shi Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the hierarchy of molecular orbital interactions, metal–metal ϕ bonding constitutes the zenith of angular momentum components, yet its realization in compounds remains perhaps the most striking manifestation of the stringent spatial demands required for effective orbital overlap. In this study, we report the theoretical identification of an unprecedented eight-center two-electron (8c–2e) ϕ bond, which is induced by six hydrogen atoms within the U–H6–U inverse sandwich subunit of the U2H8 diactinide hydride complex with a H–U–H6–U–H geometric configuration. This finding is supported by combined DFT and high-level ab initio calculations based on the CASPT2 method, which yield mutually consistent results. The U–H6–U inverse sandwich unit features strong 5fϕ–1sσ interactions between the two uranium atoms and the central bridging H6 moiety. Further bonding analysis reveals the delocalized multicenter σ, π, δ, and ϕ chemical bonds, with AIMD simulations confirming the thermal stability of U2H8. Extending the investigation across the actinide series (An = Th–Am), DFT calculations reveal that Pa2H8, Np2H8, and Pu2H8 adopt a similar H–An–H6–An–H geometric configuration featuring an 8c–2e ϕ bond. These findings enhance the fundamental understanding of covalency origins in f-block element hydride compounds, especially for uranium-based systems.

Introduction

Central to the discipline of chemistry, − the chemical bond is classified by the number of nodal planes along the internuclear axis into σ, π, δ, and ϕ bonds, associated with the angular momentum projections Λ = 0, ± 1, ± 2, and ± 3, in that order. , Beyond the ubiquitous σ and π interactions, the landscape of metal–metal bonding was transformed in 1965 by Cotton’s landmark discovery of [Re2Cl8]2–, which first demonstrated that two d orbitals could overlap directly to form a δ bond. Since then, direct M–M δ bonds in dimers or stabilized by ancillary ligands and M–L δ interactions have been characterized across the transition metal, lanthanide, and actinide compounds, leveraging the availability of d and f electrons. − In contrast, although the 5f orbitals of actinides have a unique shape exhibiting potential ability to form ϕ-type bonding, the reported ϕ bonds are rare, let alone the more contracted 4f orbitals.

Diatomic actinide species (An2) and compounds containing An-An units have been extensively investigated, yet no direct actinide-actinide ϕ bond has been reported to date. For instance, Roos and Gagliardi proposed in 2005 that the U2 dimer possesses a quintuple bond, based on state-of-the-art complete active space second-order perturbation theory (CASPT2) calculations. − In contrast, Stefan Knecht and co-workers suggested that U2 exhibits a quadruple bond, incorporating spin–orbit coupling (SOC) variationally in 2019. Notably, while 5fπ and 5fδ are involved in π- and δ-type bonding, the 5fϕ orbitals tend to remain fully localized due to the relatively long U–U bond distance in both of these two types of chemical bonds. Pa2 is predicted to exhibit the strongest bond among An2 species, with a formal quintuple bond, while the 5fϕ orbital remains nonbonding either. Several ligand-stabilized An2 complexes with shortened An–An bond lengths have also been theoretically reported, such as H3AsAnAnAsH3 (An = Ac, Th), , PhUUPh, An2Cp2* (Cp = C5(CH3)5, An = Th–Pu), An2(C8H8)2 (An = Th, Pa, U, Np), [An2X8]2– (An = U, Np, Pu; X = Cl, Br, I). Despite these advances, direct ϕ–ϕ bonding in diactinide compounds remains elusive.

Actinide-ligand interactions provide a viable route to exotic bonding modes involving ϕ orbitals. The first experimental evidence for side-to-head ϕ-orbital interactions was reported in the thorium sandwich complex (C8H8)2Th, as confirmed by K-edge XAS and theoretical calculations. − However, while the uranium analogue (C8H8)2U exhibits a similar ϕ-type interaction, the associated ϕ-orbital covalency was found to be negligible. Pioneering work by Yang and colleagues has revealed a rich landscape of ϕ-type interactions in actinide complexes. These discoveries encompass side-to-side ϕ back-bonding in Pa–Pu metallacycles, , the theoretical prediction that head-to-head U-diallyl ϕ back-bonding is inaccessible in U­(IV) species yet achievable at low-valent uranium centers, and most recently, additional computational validation of this insightful assumption and its extension to head-to-head ϕ back-bonding across other actinides, particularly Pa and U in the + II and + III oxidation states. More recently, Li and colleagues reported the theoretical discovery of a one-electron 8c-1e ϕ bond in the diuranium inverse sandwich complex U2B6, where this interaction accounts for approximately 1.7% of the total orbital coupling between the diuranium center and the B6 ligand.

The first experimental evidence for actinide-actinide bonding in uranium hydrides was obtained from U2H2 and U2H4 isolated in solid argon matrices. − The hydride ligand (H–) as the smallest known ligand facilitates these short U–U contacts (2.21 Å and 2.29 Å, respectively) through its characteristically compact M–H bonds, yielding distances markedly shorter than those in the bare U2 dimer (2.56 or 2.43 Å). , Motivated by these findings, we have carried out a comprehensive bonding analysis of the lowest-energy structures of U2H2n (n = 1–4) using both density functional theory (DFT) and wave function theory (WFT) methods. The most stable isomer of U2H8, H–U–H6–U–H, exhibits a distinct 8c–2e ϕ bonding interaction in its U–H6–U inverse sandwich core, representing the first theoretical example of this bonding mode. Remarkably, DFT bonding analysis reveals that this ϕ-type interaction accounts for 32.5% of the orbital coupling within the U2/H8 framework, representing a dramatic enhancement over the 1.7% contribution reported for the related U2B6 system. In addition, ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations further confirm the thermostability of U2H8. We subsequently extended our investigation to other actinide hydride complexes, An2H8 (An = Th–Am). DFT results indicate that An2H8 (An = Pa–Pu) exhibit 8c–2e ϕ bond with H–An–H6–An–H configuration, while Th2H8 and Am2H8 have other more stable configurations without ϕ bond. These findings establish an unconventional multicenter bonding motif in actinide chemistry and provide new insights into the origins of covalency in f-block hydrides, particularly those of uranium.

Methods

Density Functional Theory Calculations

Geometry optimizations of U2H2n (n = 1–4) and An2H8 (An = Th–Am), along with their isomers, were performed using DFT in various spin multiplicities with the ADF 2019.301 program. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and the PBE exchange-correlation functional were applied in all calculations, with the zero-order-regular approximation (ZORA) employed to account for scalar-relativistic effects. The TZ2P basis set, which includes a valence triple-ζ and two polarization functions, was used for all atoms. Small frozen core approximations were used as [1s2-4f14] for Th–Am. Additionally, the spin–orbit ZORA (SO-ZORA) calculations were performed to examine the impact of spin–orbit coupling on the bonding changes in actinide compounds. For the bonding analysis, the Nalewajski–Mrozek (N-M) and Gopinathan–Jug (G-J) bond order, , energy decomposition analysis with natural orbitals for chemical valence (EDA-NOCV), , localized electron density (ELF), quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM), and the enthalpy change of reactions were performed using the ADF 2019.301 program. The time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) approach was used to perform the spectroscopic simulations with the same computational methods reported in literature, which have been benchmarked against experimental spectra and shown to be reliable for actinide systems. Adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) analysis was performed using the Gaussian 16 program. The PBE functional and the Stuttgart energy-consistent relativistic pseudopotential ECP60MWB − and the corresponding valence basis set were used for U, while the Dunning polarized valence triple-ζ basis set (cc-pVTZ) , was used for H atoms. Several other functionals and basis sets were also tested and benchmarked against the CASPT2 results, which further supports the reliability of the PBE results (Tables S1 and S2) as discussed in the Supporting Information, Section S1. In addition, PBE showed the relatively good performance, with the corresponding U–U (2.30 Å) bond lengths deviating by less than 3% from the CASPT2 values (2.37 Å). The detailed comparison is provided in Table S3.

Global Minimum Search

The global minimum structural searches were carried out for the U2H8 cluster using the constrained basin-hopping algorithm implemented in the TGMin3 program, , supplemented by extensive manually constructed initial geometries to ensure broad configurational sampling. The calculations were performed at the GGA level with the PBE exchange-correlation functional and the triple-ζ plus two polarization (TZ2P) basis sets as implemented in the ADF 2019.301 package. Different initial arrangements of the hydride ligands, as well as multiple spin multiplicities (singlet, triplet, and quintet), were systematically explored. In total, more than 1500 distinct minima were evaluated, and the D6h-symmetric structure containing an H6 ring sandwiched by two U atoms was identified as the global minimum.

WFT Calculations

Geometry optimizations of U2H2n (n = 1–4) were also performed using ab initio electron correlation methods at the CASPT2 level, − implemented in the Molpro 2018 program. Based on the optimized geometry and energies of different states at the CASPT2 level, the SO coupling was treated using a state-interacting method on the basis of state average-CASSCF wave functions. , The Stuttgart energy-consistent relativistic pseudopotential ECP60MWB − and the corresponding valence basis set were used for U, while the Dunning polarized valence triple-ζ basis set (cc-pVTZ) , was used for H atoms. Additional different active space and ECP60MDF calculations (Supporting Information, Section S2) were performed to confirm the reliability of the computational results (Figures S1–S3 and Table S4).

AIMD Simulations

AIMD simulations of U2H8 were carried out using the DFT method with the PBE functional in the CP2K 7.1 program. , The polarizable double-ζ quality basis set and corresponding pseudopotential were used for U, which were developed recently. Long-range electrostatic interactions were treated with an additional plane-wave basis set with a 600 Ry cutoff, and the Grimme’s DFT-D3BJ dispersion correction was applied. , The simulations were performed at temperatures of 300, 400, and 500 K under the canonical (NVT) ensemble using the Nosé–Hoover thermostat with a step of 0.5 fs. Well-equilibrated trajectories of more than 15 ps were obtained for each simulation.

Results and Discussion

Ground State Electronic Structure and Molecular Orbitals of U2H2n (n = 1–4)

To explore potential ϕ bonding in actinide hydride molecules, a series of uranium hydride complexes U2H2n (n = 1–4) was first investigated using both DFT and WFT methods. This study builds upon prior experimental and theoretical work on uranium hydride molecules. , The most stable geometries of uranium hydride complexes U2H2n (n = 1–4), along with the representative isomers and corresponding DFT/CASPT2 energies of U2H2n complexes, are presented in Figure . For U2H2, both DFT and CASPT2 calculations predict a singlet state with a D2h point group, consistent with previously reported results. U2H2 exhibits the shortest U–U bond length (2.09 Å) at the CASPT2 level among the U2H2n (n = 1–4) complexes and features 5f-participating σ, π, and δ bonding (Figure S4). Notably, different from the reported U2 dimer with 2.43 Å U–U bond length which has three double occupied orbitals as (7sσg)2(6dπu)4 and four singly occupied orbitals as 6dσg, 6dδg, 5fδg and 5fπu orbitals with two fully localized 5fϕ orbitals, these electrons in U2H2 all double occupied bonding orbital with all 5f electrons participated in bonding because of the short U–U distance (Figure S5). However, no ϕ-type bond is observed in U2H2 due to the absence of appropriately symmetry-matched ligand orbitals. U2H4, previously reported to adopt a H–U–H2–U–H configuration, was found to favor an isomer with a U–H4–H structure, which is lower in energy by 8.47 kcal·mol–1. This isomer adopts a singlet state with a D4h point group, and the corresponding U–U bond length is 2.15 Å according to the CASPT2 results. An additional 5fπu bond is formed due to the two added hydrogen atoms compared to U2H2 (Figure S6).

Representative isomers of U n H2n (n = 1–4) and the corresponding relative DFT/CASPT2 energies (kcal·mol–1). The isomers with the relative DFT energies within 20 kcal·mol–1 were further calculated with CASPT2 methods.

As the number of hydrogen atoms increases, more potential configurations are considered for U2H6 and U2H8. U2H6 was calculated to adopt a H–U–H4–U–H configuration, which is consistent with previous reports, and its U–U bond length at the CASPT2 level is 2.23 Å while the two newly introduced terminal hydrogen atoms form σ bonds with the adjacent uranium atoms (Figure S7). Notably, although the configuration is not energetically favored, the U–H6–U structure exhibits an 8c–2e bond with a D6h point group and a singlet state (Figure S8). Given that the most stable configurations of both U2H2 and U2H6 feature hydrogen atoms at both ends, which may play a role in stabilizing such clusters, the U2H8 was further investigated. The most stable configuration of U2H8 was found to be H–U–H6–U–H with a 2.37 Å U–U bond length according to the CASPT2 (14e, 14o) results. Furthermore, after the introduction of the two additional terminal hydrogen atoms in the center of two uranium atoms, U2H8 forms an 8c–2e ϕ bond involving two U 5f orbitals and six H 1s orbitals (Figure S9). The influence of the SOC effect, which is known to significantly affect actinide compounds, was also considered. The effect of SOC on the scalar-relativistic Kohn–Sham molecular orbital (MO) levels is shown in Figure S10. The results indicate that energy level splitting is primarily observed in the 4e1u, 5e1u, and 2e2g orbitals under SOC, while the 1b2u (ϕ) orbitals show no noticeable change. Furthermore, the global minimum structural searches were performed across multiple spin states and different initial structural configurations. These calculations consistently identified the D6h-symmetric U2H8 structure as the global minimum, with all other low-lying 30 isomers in different spin multiplicities summarized in the Figures S11–S13.

The multireference wave function method CASPT2, accounting for both static and dynamic correlation, was employed to validate the DFT results. To balance the selection of the active space with the computational cost, a (14e, 14o) active space was chosen as shown in Figure . U2H8 contains a total of 20 valence electrons with six electrons from each uranium atom and eight electrons from the hydrogen atoms. Six of these electrons form three doubly occupied bonds, originating from hybrid atomic orbitals primarily composed of 7s and 6d character from the two U atoms. These three orbitals were excluded from the CASPT2 calculations. The remaining 14 electrons contribute to seven bonding MOs (two σg, two πu, two δg, and one ϕu) and five antibonding orbitals (two σu, two πg, and one ϕg). The remaining two orbitals are nonbonding orbitals (one 5fϕg* and one 5fπu). After excluding the bonding contributions from the eight hydrogen atoms (with a maximum contribution of 2 to EBO), the effective bond order (EBO) of U2H8 was found to be approximately 5, suggesting a quintuple bond between the two U atoms with six equatorial hydrogen ligands. Moreover, the EBO of the two-electron ϕ bond was calculated to be 0.76, which is stronger than the 0.47 reported for the single-electron ϕ bond in U2B6. Additionally, the configuration interaction (CI) weight of this electronic configuration was found to be 58.9%, while the second largest configuration contributes only 6.1%, which indicates that U2H8 exhibits appreciable multireference character but remains dominated by the leading configuration (Table S5).

Natural orbital contours and the corresponding occupation numbers of U2H8 based on the optimized CASPT2 (14e, 14o) calculations. The first three orbitals are bonding natural orbitals out of the active space (isosurface value = 0.03 au).

The SOC calculations were further performed to confirm the ground state of U2H8. In this process, two less relevant πu and ϕg* orbitals with small natural occupation numbers (0.05 and 0.03 |e|) were removed from the previous CASPT2 (14e, 14o). Consequently, the SOC calculations were primarily focused on an active space of CASPT2 (14e, 12o). A variety of electronic states with different spin multiplicities were considered, including 24 singlet states, 32 triplet states, and 11 quintet states. These states yield a total of 175 SOC states, with the first 22 of them presented in Figure , alongside the first 6 scalar-relativistic states. The results indicate that the 1Ag state lies 4.37 kcal·mol–1 (0.20 eV) lower than the first excited 1Au state, which confirms the ground state of U2H8.

Energy level diagram for the ground and low-lying excited states of U2H8 calculated at the CASPT2 (14e, 12o) level: SR (left), SOC (right). All energies are referenced to the SR ground state.

Following the confirmation of the ground state of static U2H8, its thermodynamic stability was further investigated. The trajectories of AIMD simulations for U2H8 at 300, 400, and 500 K in 15 ps are presented in Figure S14. U2H8 demonstrates good thermodynamic stability at 300 and 400 K. However, at 500 K, the complex tends to dissociate into U2H6 and H2 as the temperature increases. In addition, the following formation reactions were studied at the DFT level: U2(g)+H2(g)→U2H2(g);ΔH=−28.1kcal·mol−1 U2H2(g)+H2(g)→U2H4(g);ΔH=−37.5kcal·mol−1 U2H4(g)+H2(g)→U2H6(g);ΔH=−27.3kcal·mol−1 U2H6(g)+H2(g)→U2H8(g);ΔH=−10.5kcal·mol−1

All reactions leading to the formation of U2H n species are exothermic, indicating that their formation is energetically favorable and further confirms the intrinsic stability of these complexes. Meanwhile, these processes are characterized by a decrease in entropy, and an increase in temperature would facilitate the dissociation, in accordance with the AIMD results (Figure S14).

Bonding Analysis and Spectral Simulation of U2H8

Bonding analysis was further performed to explore the nature of chemical bonds, especially the ϕ bond in U2H8. Notably, the DFT ground state is consistent with the dominant configuration in the CASPT2 reference wave function. The frontier Kohn–Sham valence orbitals were also found to be consistent with the CASPT2 results, supporting the reliability of the DFT calculations (Figure S9). Based on this, a valence MO energy level diagram of U2H8 was constructed to investigate the correlations between the energy levels. As shown in Figure , the H–H6–H bond displays four types of delocalized chemical bonds as σ-type MOs (1A1g, 2A1g, 1A2u), π- type MOs (1e1u, 2e1u), δ-type MOs (1e2g, 2e2g), and ϕ-type MO (1b2u). These orbitals interact with various 6d/5f AOs of the U–U unit, leading to the formation of σ, π, δ, and ϕ bonding interactions such as 7A1g(σ), 5e1u(π), 2e2g(δ), and 1b2u(ϕ). Notably, the 1b2u(ϕ) MO is doubly occupied due to the orbital interaction between U–U 5fϕ and H6 1sσ orbitals. In this 1b2u(ϕ) MO, the U 5f AO contributes 68.9% while the rest of the H 1s AOs contribute 31.1%, indicating significant covalency in the ϕ bond.

Valence MO energy level diagrams of U2H8 and orbital interactions between fragment U–U and H8 at the PBE/TZ2P level (isosurface = 0.03 au).

The EDA-NOCV analysis was performed to gain further insight into the ϕ bond between the U–U and H–H6–H fragments, as shown in Table . The EDA results indicate that the covalent orbital interaction (ΔE orb) contributes −1065.8 kcal·mol–1 (56.1%) to the total attractive interaction, while the electrostatic attraction ΔE elect contributes the rest. This suggests that the covalent interaction is dominant between the U–U and H8 fragment. A breakdown of ΔE orb into its individual components reveals ten major contributions. The first term ΔE orb 1, representing the ϕ-type bonding interaction between the U 5fϕ and H 1sσ orbital, is the dominant contributor to the total orbital interaction ΔE orb, with electron density accumulating at the center of U–U and H8 fragments. The ELF and QTAIM analyses further confirm the electron accumulation in the central region of U2H8 with an electron density ρ of 0.1647 au at the U–U bond critical point (BCP) (Figures S15 and S16, Table S6). The term ΔE orb 2–ΔE orb 8 arises from σ-, π-, and δ-type interactions involving U 6d and 5f orbitals with H 1s orbitals. Among these contributions, the π-type interactions associated with the U 5fπ orbitals are notably stronger than those involving 6dπ orbitals. This is because the NOCV analysis focuses on the orbital interactions between the U–U and H8 fragments, and the intrinsic interactions between atoms within the fragment are partly neglected. Similarly, the inherent interatomic interactions within fragment H8 (ΔE orb 10) were partly disregarded. The term ΔE orb 9 is the orbital interaction between 5fσ and 1sσ. The above ten individual terms account for 99.2% of the total ΔE orb. The EDA-NOCV analysis confirms the unexpectedly strong ϕ bond in U2H8.

The AdNDP analysis was conducted to further elucidate the chemical bonding of U2H8, as shown in Figure . The molecule contains 20 valence electrons, with six electrons contributed by each uranium atom and eight electrons from the hydrogen atoms. The two hydrogen atoms at the ends of U2H8 form two two-center two-electron (2c–2e) σ bonds with the adjacent U atoms, while the six equatorial hydrogen atoms form an 8c–2e σ bond with the two U atoms, as depicted in the first row. The second row illustrates the two 8c–2e π bonds arising from the orbital interactions between the U–U dπ and H6 1sσ orbitals, with a little fπ and two 8c–2e π bonds between U–U fπ and H6 1sσ orbitals. Additionally, the U–U 5fδ orbitals participate in the formation of two 8c–2e δ bonds with the H sσ orbital. The remaining two electrons form an 8c–2e ϕ bond due to the orbital interaction between the U–U 5fϕ and H sσ orbital, and the occupation number of 2c–2e U–U was evaluated to be around 1.37 |e| (Supporting Information, Section S3). These delocalized multicenter bonds, each with an occupation number of about 2, contribute significantly to the overall chemical stability of U2H8, providing valuable insight into its high stability. A CASSCF-based AdNDP analysis and an explicit comparison between the AdNDP bonding pictures of U2H8 and U2B6 are also provided in the Supporting Information, which confirms the DFT-based AdNDP result and suggests that U2H8 exhibits more abundant 8c–2e bonding orbital due to its much shorter U–U distance (Figures S17 and S18).

Identifying experimentally accessible observables is crucial for validating the proposed ϕ bond. The IR spectra of the U2H2n (n = 1–4) series were simulated, and the corresponding characteristic vibrational modes were assigned (Figure S19) Although no isolated IR peak can be directly assigned to the ϕ bond (Supporting Information, Section S4). UV–vis–NIR spectroscopy is a useful tool for probing electronic transitions related to chemical bonding. Therefore, TD-DFT calculations were performed to simulate the absorption spectra of U2H8 and to examine the contribution of ϕ bonding. As shown in Figure S20, an absorption feature associated with the ϕ bond is predicted at 650–710 nm. Orbital analysis indicates that this feature mainly arises from a 5fϕ–5fδ transition. This low-energy 5f-based transition may serve as a potential experimental signature of the ϕ bond. The UV–vis spectra, including SOC, were also calculated (Figure S21). Although additional absorption peaks appear after including SOC, the ϕ-related transition remains close to that obtained at the scalar-relativistic level, which further supports the reliability of the assignment. Besides, the calculated absorption spectra of Pa2H8, Np2H8, and Pu2H8 exhibit similar 5fϕ–5fδ transitions (Figures S22–S24).

Bonding analysis of An2H8 (An = Th–Am)

Following the identification of a two-electron ϕ bond in U2H8, the potential existence of similar ϕ-type bonding in other actinide hydride molecules An2H8 (An = Th–Am) was investigated. Given that U2H6 with its U–H6–U configuration also exhibits a ϕ bond, albeit without a significant energy advantage, both An2H6 and An2H8 were studied to determine whether An–H6–An or H–An–H6–An–H isomers are the global minima. DFT calculations were performed on An2H6 and An2H8 (An = Th–Am) since previous studies suggest that the DFT calculations are relatively reliable.

The calculated isomers of An2H6 (An = Th–Am) are shown in Table S7, which reveals that the H–An–H4–An–H (An = Th–Am) isomers are energetically favored over the An–H6–An and H2–An–H2–An–H2 configurations, and there are no ϕ-type orbital interactions. The isomers of An2H8 (An = Th–Am) are shown in Table S8, suggesting that H–An–H6–An–H (An = Pa–Pu) are the most stable configuration in their isomers and exhibit an 8c–2e ϕ bond. Th2H8 prefers to adopt an H3–Th–H2–Th–H3 configuration with no occupations of the 5f orbitals (Figure S25), while the Am2H8 adopts an H2–Am–H4–Am–H2 structure and neither of them exhibits ϕ-type orbital interactions. The corresponding optimized coordinates of An2H8 (An = Th–Am) are collected in Tables S9–S13, and the bond length of An–An and An–H are shown in Table , where the H refers to the middle hydrogen atom in H6. For a clearer comparison, the H–An–H6–An–H configuration was adopted for all An2H8 (An = Th–Am) complexes.

The ground states of Th2H8 and U2H8 are singlets, while those of Pa2H8 and Np2H8 are triplets. As the nuclear charge increases across the actinide series, the 5f orbitals tend to become more contracted, despite the accumulation of additional 5f electrons in the center of An2H8. This leads to an increase in spin multiplicity, with Pu2H8 and Am2H8 exhibiting quintet and 13-fold ground states, respectively. This phenomenon is also reflected in the An-An bond lengths. The N–M bond order follows a similar trend, as it increases from Th to Np and then decreases from Np to Am. The corresponding G–J bond order and delocalization index (DI) are also provided in Table S14 for reference. Notably, the N–M bond order of U–U is approximately 5, suggesting the quintuple bond, while it is about 6 for Np–Np, indicating the sextuple bond. The stronger bond order may be associated with the enhanced orbital energy matching of Np 5f and H 1s orbitals (Figure S26). In addition, the bond lengths and N–M bond orders of An–H interactions exhibit trends similar to those observed for the An–An bonds.

The frontier Kohn–Sham valence orbitals and the corresponding energy levels of the neutral An2H8 molecules (An = Th–Am) with H–An–H6–An–H configuration are shown in Figures S27–S30, where the ϕ-type 1b2u bonds and the corresponding 5fϕ contribution are highlighted in red. It suggests that in addition to U2H8, both Th2H8 and Pa2H8 exhibit 8c–2e ϕ bond in which the 5fϕ orbital contributes 45.9% and 60.0%, respectively. However, Th2H8 prefers to adopt a H3–Th–H2–Th–H3 configuration with no ϕ-type interactions. The Np2H8 and Pu2H8 also possess ϕ bonds with increasing 5fϕ contributions of 74.7% and 78.9%, respectively. In contrast, Am2H8 shows a significantly higher 5fϕ contribution of 92%, and the orbital is singly occupied. Besides, similar to Th2H8, Am2H8 prefers to adopt a H2–Am–H4–Am–H2 structure. To further illustrate this trend, the Kohn–Sham orbitals corresponding to both of two and single-electron ϕ bond are also displayed in Figure S31. The 8c–2e ϕ orbitals are notably more diffuse and delocalized compared to the more compact single-electron variant, clearly demonstrating the difference in bonding character. Furthermore, the uranium stands out within the An2H8 series due to the optimal balance between its 5f orbital radial extension and 5f electron count. It possesses enough 5f electrons to form high-angular-momentum bonding orbitals, while avoiding substantial occupation of antibonding states. As shown in Figure S31, from Th–U, the gradually added 5f electrons mainly occupy the degenerate 5e1u (π) bonding orbitals, resulting in enhanced bonding. In contrast, from U to Am, the 5f orbital contraction and increased occupation of antibonding orbitals weaken the corresponding orbital interactions, leading to reduced orbital interactions. In summary, theoretical calculation combined with different bonding analyses confirms the presence of 8c–2e ϕ bond in An2H8 (An = Pa–Pu), and it provides valuable insights into the nature of ϕ-type interactions in actinide compounds.

Conclusions

In this work, we first systematically investigated the electronic structure and bonding characteristics of actinide hydride complexes U2H2n (n = 1–4) using both DFT and CASPT2 methods, with particular emphasis on identifying and characterizing the hydrogen-induced unexpected strong multicenter ϕ bond. Both DFT and CASPT2 calculations lead to consistent conclusions that among the various U2H2n (n = 1–4) complexes, U2H8 with a H–U–H6–U–H configuration exhibits a clear and robust two-electron ϕ bond, representing the first theoretical example of such bonding in a diactinide compound. This ϕ-type interaction involving significant overlap between uranium 5f orbitals and bridging hydrogen orbitals contributes DFT-based 32.5% to the total orbital interaction, indicating its important role in stabilizing the U–U interaction. AIMD simulations further demonstrate the thermal stability of U2H8 up to 400 K, while dissociation into U2H6 and H2 is observed at 500 K, in agreement with thermodynamic expectations. EDA-NOCV and AdNDP analysis were employed to gain deeper insight into the covalent character of the ϕ bond, revealing multicenter delocalized bonding patterns and further supporting the strong covalency of the U–U interaction.

The investigation was then extended to the full series of An2H8 (An = Th–Am) complexes. Our results show that Pa2H8, U2H8, Np2H8, and Pu2H8 all exhibit multicenter hydrogen-induced 8c–2e ϕ bond in their D6h configurations. In contrast, Th2H8 and Am2H8 adopt alternative geometries that preclude the formation of ϕ-type interactions. Across the series, we observe a trend in spin multiplicity, bond lengths, and N–M bond orders, highlighting the delicate interplay between 5f orbital occupation, spatial extent, and bonding capability. Overall, this study not only provides the first theoretical validation of a multicenter two-electron ϕ bond in diactinide hydrides but also offers fundamental insights into the bonding behavior of actinides. These findings may inspire future efforts in the design of actinide complexes with exotic bonding features, potentially contributing to the development of new actinide-based compounds.

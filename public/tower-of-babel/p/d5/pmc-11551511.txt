Successful resolution of duodenal stump leakage and bile duct disconnection after gastrectomy: endotherapy salvage
Authors: Federica Fimiano, Lorenzo Dioscoridi, Edoardo Forti, Francesco Pugliese, Marcello Cintolo, Giulia Bonato, Marianna Bravo, Massimiliano Mutignani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Video

Subtotal gastrectomy can lead to severe adverse events, including duodenal stump leakage (DSL), a life-threatening issue with 3% incidence and mortality rate ranging from 7% to 67%.1,2 Generally, endotherapy is not considered the standard treatment for DSL. This case report details a successful endoscopic approach to manage DSL associated with distal common bile duct (CBD) necrosis in a 46-year-old patient after subtotal gastrectomy for a gastric antral adenocarcinoma (pT3N2).

On the fifth postoperative day, the patient developed adverse events. Urgent CT scan of the abdomen identified gastroduodenal artery bleeding, which was promptly embolized. On the 15th postoperative day, the patient developed a fever, vomiting, and an elevated white blood cell count. A subsequent CT scan revealed a pelvic fluid collection likely attributable to DSL after gastroduodenal artery bleeding.

The use of a pediatric colonoscope endoscopy provided access to the duodenal stump, revealing complete DSL and distal CBD necrosis. Biliary cannulation of the residual CBD along the falciform ligament diverted bile into the duodenum using a 10-mm to 12-cm biliary fully covered self-expandable metal stent. Percutaneous drainage was achieved by replacing the 10-mm to 12-cm biliary FCSEMS with a 28F stent under fluoroscopy to wash in the cavity, and an 18F double-lumen nose-to-collection drainage was positioned to wash out it. After 18 days, at second-look endoscopy, we found no residual infected necrotic tissue into the periduodenal cavity. Main pancreatic duct cannulation was performed from the papilla, also dislocated in the peritoneum, and a 7F 20-cm plastic pancreatic stent (tailored by a nose-biliary drainage) was placed to redirect pancreatic juice into the duodenal lumen. Resolution of the DSL was observed at CT scan check after 2 months, and pancreatic and enteral stents also were removed. CBD recanalization was assessed after 3 months, and the biliary stent was finally removed (Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org). This case underscores the significance of endoscopy in tertiary centers for managing postgastrectomy adverse events in high-risk patients.

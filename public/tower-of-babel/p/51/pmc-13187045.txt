MFR-YOLO: advancing UAV object detection with multi-scale feature refinement via deformable convolution and global attention
Authors: Jiaxiang Ge, Huangming Lv, Yaoxuan Guo, Yao Chen, Yuchen Xu, Xuekun Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Unmanned aerial vehicle imagery exhibits extreme scale variance, high small-object density, and arbitrary geometric distortions, which continue to pose challenges to the state-of-the-art detectors like YOLO series. This paper proposes MFR-YOLO, an enhanced model built upon a multi-scale feature refinement network. First, a multi-scale feature extraction module is designed, integrating SPD-Conv to preserve fine-grained details and employing DCNv4 to dynamically adjust receptive fields for adapting to objects of varying scales and orientations. Standard convolutions in the backbone are systematically replaced with DCNv4 layers, enhancing feature propagation from low-level edges to high-level semantics. A lightweight Global Attention Module is incorporated into both the backbone and neck, leveraging dual channel-spatial self-attention to suppress background clutter and amplify critical object features. Additionally, the original C3K2 bottleneck is upgraded with a Pyramid-Pooling Attention mechanism, enriching multi-scale representations by fusing local and global contextual information. Comprehensive evaluations on the VisDrone2021 and UA-DETRAC benchmarks demonstrate that MFR-YOLO achieves superior overall detection accuracy, exhibits significant gains in small object detection, and maintains a balance between accuracy and real-time efficiency. Ablation studies validate the contribution of each proposed component. This research provides a novel technical framework for object detection algorithms in complex scenes.

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid advancement of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) technology, its applications have expanded significantly across various fields such as agricultural monitoring, traffic surveillance, emergency rescue, and urban security1. As a core technology for UAV environmental perception, object detection directly determines the accuracy and efficiency of task execution2. From the perspective of technological evolution, the improvement in the performance of UAV-borne sensors has made it possible to acquire massive visual data in real-time3. However, the challenge of rapidly and accurately extracting target information from this data remains a critical bottleneck constraining intelligent UAV applications. Particularly in complex and dynamic scenarios, such as traffic flow monitoring on highways, debris search and rescue after natural disasters, and crowd control at large-scale events, extremely demanding requirements are placed on the robustness and real-time performance of object detection4.

However, the object detection scenarios from the UAV perspective have significant particularities, posing multiple challenges to existing algorithms5. Firstly, constrained by the UAV’s flight altitude, ground targets often occupy only 10–30 pixels in images, with extremely sparse feature information and a high proportion of small-scale targets, leading to frequent missed detections in traditional detection models6. Secondly, tilted flight or attitude variations of UAVs can induce nonlinear geometric distortions of targets, yet networks with fixed convolution kernels struggle to adapt to such dynamic changes7. Thirdly, background elements such as the sky, vegetation, and buildings often overlap with target features, making the model prone to misclassifying background as the target8. Fourthly, in dynamic UAV flight scenarios, target positions change rapidly over time, and the detection frame rate must be maintained above 25 FPS to meet the requirements of real-time decision-making, which places higher demands on the lightweight design of the model9. Moreover, overly complex models may enhance accuracy but cause decision delays due to computational latency, while excessively lightweight models struggle with scene complexity10. This further exacerbates computational challenges for UAV platforms.

The YOLO series algorithms have been widely applied in the field of object detection by virtue of their core advantage of balancing efficiency and real-time performance11. Among them, YOLOv12 demonstrates outstanding performance in general object detection scenarios through its efficient single-stage detection architecture and lightweight design12. However, it still has obvious shortcomings in UAV scenarios: its backbone network employs traditional convolutions combined with stride convolutions and pooling operations for feature map compression, which can easily lead to the loss of fine-grained features of small objects. The feature fusion mechanism lacks targeted adaptation for perspective-distorted objects, making it difficult to effectively capture complete feature representations of distorted targets. Furthermore, the model lacks a dedicated background suppression module, resulting in significantly reduced detection robustness under the strong interference of complex backgrounds in drone scenarios13. Although in recent years, researchers have attempted to optimize the model by introducing attention mechanisms and improving feature fusion, most methods do not fully consider the multi-dimensional constraints of UAV scenarios, making it difficult to balance accuracy and efficiency14. For example, some models increase the computational load significantly by stacking deep networks to improve the small-target detection ability15. Some methods only optimize for a single problem and fail to form a systematic solution16. Recent transformer-based approaches17,18 have shown promise in addressing small object sparsity and feature ambiguity in high-resolution imagery, but they rely on complex transformer structures, which are not suitable for UAV scenarios with strict real-time requirements.

Therefore, in response to the unique requirements of UAV scenarios, customizing and improving YOLOv12 to enhance its performance in small-target detection, distortion adaptation, and background anti-interference has important theoretical value and application significance. In this study, an enhanced YOLOv12 model suitable for UAV scenarios is constructed by designing a multi-scale feature-extraction module, introducing deformable convolutions to enhance feature transfer, embedding a global attention mechanism to suppress background interference, and optimizing the feature-fusion module. The results of comparative experiments on datasets such as VisDrone and UA-DETRAC show that the proposed improved model significantly outperforms the YOLOv12 baseline method and other mainstream detection models in terms of detection accuracy and missed-detection rate. Related work

Applications of UAV visual object detection

With the maturation of UAV manufacturing technology, flight control systems, and sensor technology, UAV, as efficient and flexible low-altitude data-acquisition platforms, have expanded their application boundaries from traditional aerial photography and military reconnaissance to multiple important sectors of the national economy, giving rise to a large number of intelligent solutions based on visual object detection19.

In the field of agricultural monitoring, as an important support for precision agriculture, UAV equipped with high-resolution cameras can monitor the growth status of crops, pest and disease conditions, and irrigation needs in real-time20. By combining with object-detection algorithms, they can achieve automatic identification and counting of specific crops, locate weeds, and accurately identify disease-affected areas21. In the field of traffic surveillance, with their wide field of view and flexible mobility, UAV can fill the monitoring blind spots of fixed cameras in intelligent transportation systems. They can not only monitor road traffic flow in real-time, detect traffic violations, identify traffic accidents, but also conduct traffic flow statistics and analysis22. In the field of emergency rescue, for natural disasters such as earthquakes, floods, and fires, UAV can quickly enter dangerous areas to carry out search and rescue missions. By being equipped with thermal-imaging or visible-light cameras and combined with object-detection algorithms, they can rapidly locate trapped people, vehicles, or rescue supplies in ruins or complex terrains23. In the field of urban security, as an important complement to smart city construction, UAV inspections are widely used in scenarios such as crowd density monitoring and abnormal behavior recognition at large-scale events, inspection and fault detection of key infrastructure, and public-safety crime tracking. These applications place extremely high demands on the real-time performance, robustness, and multi-class recognition ability of object-detection algorithms24. Although significant progress has been made in applications across various fields, the unique aerial-photography perspective of UAV also brings common technical challenges such as drastic target-scale changes, dense small targets, complex backgrounds, variable shooting angles, and limited platform computing resources25.

YOLO series object detection models

Since the advent of the YOLO series algorithms, they have established a pivotal position in single-stage object detection through their exceptional balance between inference speed and detection accuracy. The development trajectory of this series clearly demonstrates the evolutionary path of object detection technology, which shifts from an early focus on accuracy improvement to a gradual emphasis on balancing efficiency and deployment practicality26. Looking back at its development, the pioneering works such as YOLOv1 to v3 proposed by Redmon et al.27 have laid a solid foundation for this research direction. Among these, YOLOv1 served as the starting point, pioneering the formulation of object detection as a unified regression problem. By using an end-to-end neural network to directly predict bounding boxes and class probabilities, it not only drastically simplified the detection process but also formally inaugurated a new paradigm for real-time object detection. Subsequently, YOLOv2 introduced the anchor box mechanism and multi-scale training strategy28, while YOLOv3 significantly improved the performance of small target detection through the Darknet-53 backbone network and a multi-scale prediction mechanism similar to FPN29. The performance optimization and engineering exploration stage YOLOv4-V8 further integrates advanced technologies, such as the “Bag of Freebies/Specials” strategy of YOLOv430, the fusion of CSPNet and PANet features, and the industrial-friendly PyTorch implementation of YOLOv531. And the continuous innovation of YOLOv6-v8 in areas such as backbone networks, label allocation, and anchor-free detection. In the recent stage of integrating cutting-edge technologies, the latest deep learning achievements are deeply integrated, such as the NMS-free training strategy of YOLOv10, the lightweight deployment of YOLOv11, especially YOLOv12, which serves as the baseline model of this study32. YOLOv12 maintains a lightweight architecture while optimizing the feature extraction module33, effectively balancing computational efficiency and the multi-scale feature extraction capability required for small target detection in drone scenarios34. Its adaptive anchor boxes and dynamic feature fusion mechanism can better adapt to the randomness of target distribution and posture in drone scenes. Compared with YOLOv1135, YOLO-NAS36, and RT-DETR37, YOLOv12 eliminates the shortcomings of insufficient small target detection accuracy, excessive computational overhead, and poor real-time performance. When it is released in 2025, it will integrate attention mechanisms on a large scale for the first time, optimizing spatial modeling capabilities and computational efficiency while maintaining real-time performance38. Despite its powerful performance, there is still room for optimization in its general design when dealing with the small targets, geometric distortions and complex backgrounds specific to UAV scenarios, which constitutes the starting point of this study.

Small target detection technology

Small object detection, as a recognized challenge in the field of object detection, is particularly prominent in scenarios such as UAV aerial photography. The challenge lies in their low pixel coverage, sparse features, and susceptibility to noise interference within images39. To address this, existing research improvement strategies primarily focus on three directions. The first one is multi-scale feature representation and fusion. The Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) proposed by Lin et al.3 represents foundational work in this area, achieving top-down path fusion to integrate high-level semantic features with low-level detail features. Subsequently, architectures such as PANet40, BiFPN41, and NAS-FPN42 have emerged successively. These further enhanced the efficiency of cross-scale feature interaction by employing more complex connection mechanisms or automated neural network search techniques. The second is to enhance the utilization of context information43. Spatial pyramid pooling captures rich context through multi-scale pooling, while dilated convolution aggregates broader background features by expanding the receptive field, effectively compensating for the insufficiency of the features of small targets themselves44. The third approach is data augmentation and generative methods45. To address the issue of insufficient small target samples, researchers have adopted techniques such as oversampling and copy-paste to expand the training data46. Further, they have utilized generative adversarial networks (GAN) to perform super-resolution reconstruction on small targets, converting low-resolution features into high-resolution forms, thereby transforming them into more manageable large target detection problems. These strategies jointly aim to enhance the model’s perception ability and detection accuracy for small targets47. Recently, transformer-based detectors have advanced small object detection through innovations such as density-guided query selection for crowded scenes18 and contrast-aware feature enhancement with uncertainty optimization17, but complex transformer structures lead to relatively high computational complexity, which limits their application in UAV scenarios with strict real-time and lightweight requirements.

The object-detection process of MFR-YOLO can be divided into five key stages: (a) First, the image is resized to the input size of the model, such as 640 × 640, and the pixel values are mapped to the range of 0–1 through normalization. (b) Subsequently, it enters the feature-extraction stage of the backbone network. The pre-processed image is first input into the multi-scale feature-extraction module. In this module, the SPD-Conv branch retains detailed features, and the DCNv4 branch captures spatial deformation features. After fusion, initial multi-scale features are formed. These features are transferred through the convolutional layers replaced by DCNv4, which avoids the feature compression of traditional convolutions, ensuring that detailed and deformation features are completely transferred to deeper layers. Moreover, at each feature level, the global attention module screens the features in both spatial and channel dimensions, suppressing background noise and focusing on target features. Finally, an optimized multi-scale feature map is output. (c) Next comes the feature aggregation and refinement in the neck network. The multi-scale features output from the backbone enter the neck and are cross-scale fused through the FPN (Feature Pyramid Network) structure. During the fusion process, the global attention module screens the features again to enhance key information. The fused features are then processed by modules such as C3K2-PPA, obtaining more representative multi-scale feature maps that provide high-quality input for the detection head. (d) The head network receives the feature maps output from the neck, generates bounding-box coordinates, classes, and object confidence scores, thus obtaining the initial prediction results. (e) After filtering out duplicate or low-confidence boxes through non-maximum suppression (NMS), the position, class, and confidence of the objects are finally output.

Spatial pyramid depthwise convolution

SPD–Conv is a novel convolutional module optimized for low-resolution image and small-target detection. Figure 2 shows its structure. It aims to replace the strided convolution and pooling layers in traditional CNNs to address the problem of easy loss of features in low-resolution images and small targets. Its core design concept is to enhance the adaptability to spatial deformations while avoiding convolutions with a stride greater than 1 and pooling operations in traditional convolutions, thus better retaining the detailed features of the image. This module improves the ability to capture small and deformed targets while reducing feature loss by separating the convolutional calculations in the spatial dimension and dynamically adjusting the position of the convolutional kernel. It is particularly suitable for object-detection scenarios from the UAV perspective.

Deformable convolution v4

DCNv4 represents the latest iteration of deformable convolutional networks, achieving significant performance breakthroughs through optimization of dynamic aggregation weights and enhancement of memory access efficiency. Global attention mechanism

Figure 4 shows GAM, a dual-branch feature enhancement mechanism that combines spatial attention and channel attention. It aims to focus on key target features and suppress background noise through dynamic weight allocation, making it suitable for object detection tasks in complex scenes from the perspective of unmanned aerial vehicles. The spatial attention branch calculates the correlation between pixels through a self-attention mechanism, focusing on the contour areas of targets such as vehicles and pedestrians and suppressing irrelevant backgrounds like the sky and ground. The channel attention branch, on the other hand, learns the weights of feature channels to select channels strongly related to the target, such as edges and textures, and weaken background noise channels like blurred shadows, further enhancing the discriminability of features.

C3K2-PPA module

The C3K2-PPA module is an enhanced feature processing unit formed by integrating the PPA module into the original C3K2 module of YOLOv12. Figure 5 illustrates the complete architecture and computational workflow. Its core function is to improve the capability of capturing features of targets at different scales through multi-branch collaborative learning, making it particularly suitable for drone-view scenarios where small targets, distorted targets, and complex backgrounds coexist. The design philosophy retains the advantages of cross-stage local feature fusion in the C3K2 module while expanding the range of feature receptive fields through the parallel branch structure of the PPA module, enabling multi-dimensional feature modeling from local details to global correlations.

The C3K2-PPA module achieves a stepwise expansion of feature receptive fields through its multi-branch structure, the local branch retains details of small targets, the global branch models scene context, and the serial branch balances semantic levels. Meanwhile, the adaptive weight fusion mechanism enables the model to dynamically adjust the contribution of each branch according to input content, demonstrating stronger feature adaptability in multi-scale target detection tasks under drone views.

Datasets

The mainstream public dataset VisDrone 2021 for unmanned aerial vehicle scenarios and the vehicle detection dataset UA-DETRAC were selected to cover different aerial photography scenarios and target characteristics.

VisDrone202148: the dataset consists of 400 video clips, 265,228 frames and 10,209 static aerial images, and we selected the 10,209 static aerial images for our experiments. The scenes cover urban main roads, rural roads, squares, Bridges, parking lots, etc., and span various weather and lighting conditions such as day and night, sunny/rainy/foggy. The image resolution ranges from 640 × 640 to 2000 × 1500, providing over 2.6 million fine horizontal bounding box annotations for 10 common types of targets: pedestrians, people, bicycles, cars, vans, trucks, tricycles, sunshade tricycles, buses, motorcycles, along with attributes such as occlusion, truncation, and visibility. It is applicable to research on small target detection, anti-interference against complex backgrounds, etc.

UA-DETRAC49: the dataset contains 82,000 key frames extracted from 10 h of continuous aerial video footage, covering over 60 typical traffic scenarios with an image resolution of 960 × 540. It provides 1.4 million annotated vehicle instances across three primary categories including automobiles, buses, and vans, along with 14 attribute labels describing factors such as vehicle size, occlusion, weather, and lighting conditions. Although originally designed for multi-object tracking, we adapt it for object detection by sampling one frame every five frames, which yields 16,400 frames for our experiments. These frames are randomly split into training and testing sets with a 70% and 30% ratio respectively, and the original category labels are mapped to our task-specific categories to ensure consistency with the detection requirements of our study.

Comparative experiments

To verify the overall superiority of MFR-YOLO, baseline models from the YOLO series and mainstream models from other frameworks, were selected for comparison. Performance evaluations were carried out on the VisDrone2021 and UA-DETRAC datasets. The introductions of each baseline model are as follows:

Faster R-CNN50: a two-stage detection architecture that achieves automatic candidate region generation by introducing a Region Proposal Network (RPN). It combines a ResNet backbone with ROI pooling operations, delivering strong detection accuracy in complex environments but facing limitations in inference speed.

YOLOv531: a lightweight single-stage detection model. This architecture utilizes pre-trained weight initialization combined with adaptive training strategies. By incorporating mosaic data augmentation and adaptive anchor calculation techniques, it achieves strong deployment capabilities even on edge computing devices.

YOLOv851: this detection framework employs an anchor-free design, integrating the CSPDarknet53 backbone with a task-aligned detection head. It simultaneously supports multiple tasks including object detection, classification, and instance segmentation, achieving an effective balance between accuracy and inference speed.

YOLOv1052: this model employs a dual-branch architecture that decouples classification and regression tasks. Combined with a global context awareness mechanism and dynamic label allocation strategy, it further enhances the model’s adaptability to complex scenes.

YOLOv1253: by introducing dynamic convolution operators and multi-level feature fusion mechanisms, this model significantly enhances small object detection performance while maintaining high inference efficiency. Its optimized feature transfer strategy also helps reduce recognition errors in complex scenarios such as image distortion.

RetinaNet54: a one-stage focal detection network. It solves the problem of positive-negative sample imbalance through Focal Loss. Combining with the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), it realizes multi-scale detection, improving the accuracy of small targets while maintaining a relatively fast speed.

SD-YOLO55: a small-object detection specialized model optimized for small target feature extraction, which enhances the detection ability of small objects by designing a dedicated feature enhancement module.

DINO-X56: a recent transformer-based detector with strong global feature modeling capabilities, which improves target detection accuracy by optimizing the query selection mechanism.

DDRN57: an AV-specific detection method, which is optimized for complex scene adaptation and has good performance in target detection under dynamic scenarios.

MFR-YOLO (ours): the improved YOLOv12 method proposed in this paper. It newly adds a multi-scale feature extraction module MSFEM, introduces a global attention module GAM, and improves the C3K2 module by introducing PPA.

This precision-efficiency balance benefits from the PPA improved C3K2 module, which efficiently aggregates features through local-global-serial branches, enhancing multi-scale context modeling capabilities while avoiding a significant increase in the number of parameters and computational load, ultimately achieving the optimal trade-off between performance and real-time performance.

The results show that each module has a clear division of labor and synergy in the improvement of accuracy, while achieving a balance between performance and real-time performance.

In terms of the core accuracy indicator mAP50, the model accuracy shows a step -by-step increase after gradually adding modules. When only the MSFEM is added, the mAP50 increases from the baseline of 38.3% to 38.7%, an increase of 0.4%, preliminarily verifying the fundamental role of MSFEM in capturing multi-scale features. After adding DCNv4, the mAP50 further increases to 38.9%, indicating that the deformable convolution’s ability to adapt to the geometric deformation of targets can supplement the distortion feature information not covered by MSFEM. After adding the GAM, the mAP50 breaks through to 39.3%, with a more significant increase than the previous two steps, reflecting the advantage of GAM in suppressing background interference and focusing on key target regions through spatial-channel dual attention. Separate ablations on C3K2-PPA components reveal that adding only C3K2 drops mAP50 to 37.4%, while PPA alone yields 38.1%. Only the complete C3K2-PPA restores performance to 38.9%, demonstrating that their integration is essential. Finally, the fully integrated MFR-YOLO boosted mAP50 to 40.1%, representing a 1.8% increase over the baseline and validating the effectiveness of synergistic optimization across all four modules.

Based on ablation results for small object detection performance mAP50-Small, the contributions of different modules exhibit high specificity. The initial MSFEM module only brings a modest gain of 0.4%. In sharp contrast, after the introduction of the DCNv4 module, this metric increases significantly to 29.4%, which confirms its strong ability to model the geometric distortions of small objects and effectively compensates for the limitations of MSFEM in this regard. Adding C3K2 alone drops small-object accuracy to 27.8%, while PPA alone achieves 28.6%. The complete C3K2-PPA jumps to 30.7%, which represents a 2.3% improvement over the baseline. This result confirms that integration is critical for small-target enhancement. Finally, under the complete model configuration, mAP50-Small reaches 31.1%, a 2.7% increase compared to the baseline. This result clarifies that the functionally complementary combination of MSFEM, DCNv4, and C3K2-PPA is the core for significantly improving small-object detection performance.

The optimization of the Miss Rate mainly depends on the collaboration between GAM and C3K2-PPA. The Miss Rate of the baseline model is 10.9%. After adding GAM, it drops to 10.3%, reflecting the enhancement effect of the attention mechanism on the recognition of sparse and occluded targets. C3K2 alone increases miss rate to 11.0%, which is worse than the baseline, while PPA alone reduces it to 10.5%. The complete C3K2-PPA achieves 9.9%, which is a 1.0% reduction from the baseline. This demonstrates that integrated multi-scale aggregation effectively reduces missed detections. After the full-module integration, the Miss Rate drops to 9.4%, a decrease of 1.5% compared to the baseline, verifying the improvement effect of ‘attention screening + multi-scale aggregation’ on the overall target recognition ability.

In terms of real time performance FPS, the impact of adding each module on efficiency is controllable. The FPS gradually decreases from the baseline of 140 FPS to 128 FPS for the full module model, with a decrease of only 8.6%, and it is still far higher than the real time detection requirement of UAV, which is no less than 30 FPS. Among them, MSFEM and DCNv4 have a relatively small impact on the FPS, with each reducing 2–3 FPS. Due to the introduction of attention calculation and multi-branch aggregation, GAM and C3K2-PPA reduce the FPS by 4 FPS and 1 FPS respectively, but the overall performance remains efficient, proving that the lightweight design of each module effectively balances the contradiction between accuracy improvement and increased computational cost.

In summary, the improved modules of MFR-YOLO are functionally complementary. MSFEM lays the foundation for multi-scale feature extraction, DCNv4 strengthens the deformation adaptation ability, GAM optimizes the target-background discriminability, and C3K2-PPA, through integration of multi-branch design, significantly improves small-target detection and reduces miss rate. The four modules work together to achieve a comprehensive optimization, providing a clear path for the contribution of each module to the improvement of detection performance in UAV scenarios.

Conclusion

This study investigates the technical bottlenecks of UAV scene target detection. To address the core challenges of high proportion of small targets, severe perspective distortion, complex background interference and strict real-time requirements in this scene, an enhanced MFR-YOLO model is proposed with YOLOv12 as the baseline model to achieve the collaborative optimization of accuracy and efficiency. This study proposes a solution through four key improvements: First, it designs a MSFEM module, integrating SPD-Conv and DCNv4, taking into account both high-resolution detail retention and spatial deformation adaptation. The second is to replace the traditional convolution of the backbone network with DCNv4 to enhance the integrity of feature transfer. The third is to embed the GAM, which suppresses background noise through dual screening of channels and space. Fourth, optimize the C3K2-PPA module and enhance the multi-scale target capture capability by relying on multi-branch aggregation.

Experimental results on the VisDrone2021 and UA-DETRAC benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed MFR-YOLO model significantly outperforms other comparison algorithms in overall detection accuracy. Notably, the model exhibits outstanding recognition capabilities for small-sized objects, with false negative rates effectively controlled. Although MFR-YOLO’s inference speed is slightly slower than YOLOv12, it still comfortably meets real-time requirements for practical applications with a substantial margin, achieving a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency. Systematic ablation experiments validate that each integrated improvement module positively contributes to enhancing detection performance from a drone perspective. The proposed technical framework not only offers novel solutions for UAV-based target detection but also provides valuable insights for practical applications such as intelligent traffic management and disaster emergency response. Furthermore, it establishes a methodological approach for optimizing detection models tailored to specific scenarios.

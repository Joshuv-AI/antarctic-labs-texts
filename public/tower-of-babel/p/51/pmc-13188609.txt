Evaluating function availability and user satisfaction with nutrition apps: user-generated content analysis and text mining study
Authors: Songping Li, Qinghua Yao, Feihe Shao, Kejun Yang, Xiao Yu, Lixue Zhou, Junyi Xin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNutrition apps are increasingly used to support dietary self-management and health promotion. However, how app functions align with nutrition-related tasks and user satisfaction with these apps remain unclear. Evidence from cross-country comparisons is limited.ObjectiveThis study aimed to compare function availability and user satisfaction of nutrition apps in China and the US and to examine key factors associated with nutrition app satisfaction in different market contexts.MethodsA large-scale review-based analysis was conducted using nutrition app data from major app stores in China and the US. Task–Technology Fit, Latent Dirichlet Allocation, the Two-Factor Theory, the KANO model, DeepSeek-R1, and the Analytic Hierarchy Process were combined to assess function coverage and satisfaction patterns. A total of 269,300 reviews from China and 455,245 reviews from the US were included.ResultsChinese nutrition apps showed broader coverage in target monitoring (n = 111, 84.73%) and nutrition education (n = 101, 77.10%), while US apps focused more on dietary behavior intervention (n = 364, 89.66%). Both countries showed clear gaps in personalized nutrition services. KANO analysis showed that all identified factors in China were classified as charm attributes, whereas in the US both charm and essential attributes were identified. AHP-based results indicated that overall user satisfaction was higher in China (0.8901/1) than in the US (0.8372/1). In the US market, dissatisfaction was primarily associated with issues related to the food database (0.7217/1) and fee (0.6795/1), whereas Chinese users more frequently reported concerns regarding reliability (0.7725/1).ConclusionsUser satisfaction with nutrition apps differs between China and the US in functional focus and expectation structures. Improving system reliability in China and optimizing core functions and pricing in the US, alongside strengthening personalized services in both contexts, are key to enhancing user satisfaction and intervention effectiveness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12937-026-01321-x.Keywords: Nutrition management, Mobile health, Apps, User satisfaction, Comparative study, Evaluation

Introduction

Nutrition management spans the entire process from sub-health intervention to chronic disease prevention and control, constituting a crucial component of public health strategies. Sub-health, an intermediate state between health and disease, is closely linked to poor dietary patterns [1]. Concurrently, the burden of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) is even more pronounced. As of 2023, NCDs accounted for 64.3% of the global disease burden measured in Disability Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) [2], with dietary risk factors consistently identified as major contributors [3–5]. Many countries, particularly low- and middle-income nations, face a dual burden of undernutrition coexisting with overweight and obesity [6, 7]. Unhealthy dietary patterns not only exacerbate this challenge but are also significantly associated with increased risks of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and cancer [8].

However, in the context of accelerating modern lifestyles, rising consumption of ultra-processed foods (UPFs) and irregular eating behaviors have become increasingly prevalent [9–11], further exacerbating dietary imbalances and posing persistent threats to public health. In response, countries and international organizations have taken active policy measures. For example, the US established the Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) commission in February 2025 [12], and Hangzhou, China, enacted the Regulations on National Health Promotion in the same year [13], both aiming to encourage healthy lifestyles. The World Health Organization (WHO) has consistently emphasized balanced nutrition as a fundamental pillar for health maintenance and disease prevention [14]. Translating these initiatives into sustainable individual-level behaviors requires effective, accessible, and personalized implementation pathways. In this context, proactive health, as an emerging service model for improving human health, offers significant advantages for empowering self-health management compared with traditional approaches [15].

Mobile health (mHealth), as a key branch of digital health, offers novel digital intervention tools for enabling proactive health management and addressing the nutritional challenges outlined above [16]. By 2024, global smartphone users had reached 4.88 billion, covering approximately 60.42% of the world’s population [17] and providing an extensive user base for mHealth services. With their portability, interactivity, and personalization, nutrition apps have rapidly become mainstream tools for public self-nutrition management [18, 19]. In the post-pandemic era, the widespread enhancement of public health awareness has further accelerated demand for such tools, as more individuals seek to improve immunity through optimized dietary patterns [20]. A growing body of evidence indicates that well-designed nutrition apps can effectively support users in improving dietary intake, managing weight, and enhancing nutritional knowledge, with their demonstrated utility fostering both widespread adoption and sustained engagement [21–24].

Existing research on nutrition app evaluation has yielded a body of evidence. Studies to date have largely focused on the apps themselves, emphasizing clinical effectiveness, function availability, and software usability. Methodological approaches have included randomized controlled trials [25, 26], user satisfaction questionnaires [27, 28], mobile application rating scales [29–32], the System Usability Scale (SUS) [33], and the App Quality Evaluation Tool (AQEL) [34, 35]. Geographically, research has spanned countries such as the US, China, France, and Spain [29, 31–33]. However, the current literature presents several limitations. First, most studies are confined to single-country or single-cultural contexts, lacking cross-national comparative perspectives. Second, predominant reliance on interviews and questionnaires introduces constraints including limited sample sizes, subjectivity, and potential Hawthorne effects, which impede comprehensive and objective reflection of real-world usage. By contrast, research based on app store user-generated content offers advantages such as large sample sizes, broad coverage, and objective, authentic data [36]. Third, although a few studies [37] have begun to explore user reviews of nutrition apps, they remain limited in sample scale (e.g., number of apps, volume of reviews, and countries covered) and analytical methods (currently still relying predominantly on topic modeling), with notably scant quantitative assessment of user satisfaction. Given that app value derives from user value, which is directly related to user engagement time and participation [38], there is a pressing need for more representative samples and comprehensive analytical methods to investigate nutrition apps. The research pathway based on user-generated content has already been widely applied and demonstrated significant value in fields such as hypertension, obesity, and pregnancy health management [39–42], providing important methodological references for nutrition app research.

This study selected China and the US as study settings based on their representativeness in population size, technological development, and digital health market maturity. China offers a large user base with diverse health needs, while the US leads in mHealth innovation and application ecosystems. The study aimed to systematically evaluate and compare function availability and user satisfaction of nutrition apps in the mainstream app stores of both countries. A comprehensive analytical framework was constructed. First, the Task-Technology Fit (TTF) theory, an information technology evaluation method based on aligning tasks with technology, was applied to quantify the compatibility between functions and requirements, assessing function coverage and adaptability. Second, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) combined with the large language model (LLM) DeepSeek-R1 was used to perform topic clustering on user reviews and identify influencing factors. Third, based on the Two-Factor Theory, Tobit regression and the Wald test were employed to analyze the asymmetric impact of each factor on satisfaction, followed by KANO model attribute classification. Finally, the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), a method critical for systematically setting optimization priorities and enhancing intervention efficiency in resource-limited contexts [43], was applied to determine relative weights of user satisfaction influence indicators for Chinese and US users, helping reveal pathways through which cultural differences shape user experience. Theoretically, this study proposes a systematic evaluation method applicable to other app domains. Practically, the findings identify cross-national differences in function implementation and user feedback, providing a scientific basis for developers to optimize product design and enhance cross-cultural applicability.

Data collection

Nutrition apps screening

Based on previous research [39, 44], this study identified 10 mainstream Chinese and US app stores as data sources. These include eight Chinese platforms (Apple China, Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, VIVO, Baidu, 360, and Tencent MyApp) and two US platforms (the US Apple App Store and Google Play Store). Data collection was completed in October 2025 using keyword searches for “nutrition”, “health”, “calories”, “diet”, and “recipe”. App screening was independently conducted by two uniformly trained researchers (SPL and KJY) following expert-reviewed inclusion and exclusion criteria (see Supplementary Table 1 in the Appendix). Inter-rater reliability was assessed using Cohen’s Kappa coefficient to quantify agreement. Disagreements were resolved through arbitration by a third domain expert (JYX). The screening process followed the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines.

User ratings and reviews collection

Following the screening of all included nutrition apps, user rating and review data from January 1, 2008, to October 20, 2025, were systematically collected. Data were obtained through the Qimai mobile app analytics platform (qimai.cn) supplemented by Python-based web scraping scripts. User ratings range from 1 (very poor) to 5 (very good), and all review content consists of original textual feedback voluntarily submitted by users during their usage.

Functional availability analysis

The TTF model, as an established framework for information technology evaluation, focuses on quantifying how well a technology supports task completion by assessing alignment between its functions and user task requirements [45]. This study applies the core concept of the TTF model. By systematically analyzing the alignment between the functions provided by nutrition apps and nutrition management task requirements specified in evidence-based clinical guidelines, an evaluation framework for function availability was constructed.

Summarization of health and nutrition management requirements and extraction of app functions

Two researchers (XY and LXZ) systematically retrieved and reviewed current clinical guidelines for nutrition management and diagnosis from nutrition professional bodies in China and the US, international organizations, and Chinese and English literature databases, using a cross-verification approach. All clinical guidance documents were reviewed and supplemented by a third domain expert (QHY). Based on this process, core task requirements for nutrition self-management among users in both countries were identified. Detailed procedures are provided in Supplementary Note 1 in the Appendix.

To extract functional features of the included nutrition apps, two researchers (SPL and KJY) independently identified features based on publicly available descriptions and interface screenshots from each app store. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient was used to assess inter-rater consistency. Disagreements were resolved through arbitration by a third domain expert (JYX).

Alignment between requirements and functions

Based on the task requirements and extracted functional features, two researchers (SPL and KJY) independently mapped each app’s functions to corresponding nutrition self-management tasks, following the principle that functions should align with relevant requirements. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient was used to assess inter-rater consistency, with disagreements resolved by a third expert (JYX). This formed the basis for quantifying the number and proportion of apps meeting each task requirement. Cross-country differences in functional configuration and task coverage were then compared to evaluate function availability in Chinese and US apps.

User satisfaction analysis

Review data preprocessing

Following established preprocessing methods from prior studies [39–41, 44], we performed a series of cleansing procedures on the collected user review. First, duplicate and blank reviews were removed. Second, machine learning-based methods were used to identify and filter reviews from bot accounts. Using the random forest classifier in the tweetbotornot package, we analyzed user behavior features (posting frequency, interaction patterns), content features (text repetitiveness), and metadata features, applying a 0.85 probability threshold for filtering. Validation with 200 samples yielded 92.3% accuracy. Third, entries containing non-Chinese/English text, garbled characters, or semantically invalid content were then removed. Fourth, a sentiment analysis algorithm based on SKEP (Sentiment Knowledge Enhanced Pre-training) [46] was applied to identify and delete reviews where sentiment polarity scores clearly contradicted the user’s star rating. As the analytical framework relies on rating deviation as the dependent variable, retaining reviews with positive content but inadvertently low ratings, often resulting from user misclicks, would introduce mathematical bias. Finally, Chinese reviews were segmented using the Jieba toolkit in Python, and English reviews were tokenized using the NLTK package, followed by stop-word removal based on standard Chinese and English stop-word lists.

Topic clustering and naming

This study employed LDA for topic modeling to identify latent thematic structures within user reviews, given its interpretability and established use in user-generated content analysis [47–49]. The optimal number of topics was determined using perplexity, selecting the point at which improvement plateaued to balance model fit and interpretability. The model was implemented using the Gensim package in Python, and topic coherence and semantic clarity were considered to retain meaningful topics.

To address synonym variability (e.g., “calorie tracking” vs. “diet logging”) and informal language, we adopted a two-step semantic normalization strategy. First, LDA generated keyword lists for each topic based on word co-occurrence. Second, DeepSeek-R1 synthesized these keywords into coherent thematic labels, ensuring that semantically related expressions were conceptually linked even if they were distributed across different topics. DeepSeek-R1 was chosen for its demonstrated capacity for semantic abstraction and structured instruction-following, making it suitable for transforming keyword sets into coherent thematic labels while maintaining contextual consistency [50]. The model version used was DeepSeek-R1-0528, with queries conducted in October 2025 at a temperature setting of 0.6 to balance response creativity and stability. Complete prompt templates, task instructions, input formats, and representative outputs are provided in Supplementary Tables 2 to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

While this LLM-assisted approach has proven effective in supporting qualitative interpretation [36, 41, 51–53], LLM outputs are probabilistic and may introduce interpretive bias or overgeneralization. Therefore, the generated topic labels were treated as analytical aids rather than authoritative categorizations. To mitigate this potential bias, all generated topic labels were independently reviewed and validated by two domain experts (JYX and FHS) to ensure accuracy, consistency, and alignment with human judgment. All generated topic interpretations were independently evaluated using a structured 1–5 rating instrument assessing naming accuracy, interpretive clarity, and classification rationality. Topics were retained based on agreement and overall acceptance, ensuring semantic validity prior to downstream modeling.

It should be noted that formal medical or nutritional ontologies (e.g., SNOMED CT, USDA Food Database) were not used in this process, as such static systems are designed for structured clinical data and may not be well-suited for the informal, diverse language of user-generated app reviews.

Satisfaction modeling based on the Two-Factor Theory and KANO model

Based on the Two-Factor Theory, we constructed two variables, positive deviation (PD) and negative deviation (ND), by calculating the absolute difference between a user’s individual rating and the app store’s average rating [40]. This theory has been extensively applied in digital health and human–computer interaction research to analyze asymmetric satisfaction patterns. This approach captures asymmetric evaluative responses by reflecting how user experiences deviate from collective expectations, thereby enabling rating behavior to serve as a measurable proxy for relative satisfaction and dissatisfaction. This interpretation aligns with expectation-disconfirmation theory, which posits that evaluations arise from discrepancies between experienced performance and reference expectations, providing theoretical grounding for mapping rating deviations to affective responses [54]. PD is defined as the positive difference when the individual rating exceeds the average rating, and ND as the negative difference when the individual rating falls below the average rating.

Given the highly skewed and often inflated nature of app store ratings, absolute scores can be misleading. The average rating was therefore used as the benchmark because it provides a stable, community-based reference that supports standardized comparison across reviews. Using a collective benchmark further allows deviations to capture relative evaluative positioning rather than absolute scoring levels, which is particularly important in digital platform environments where users interpret ratings in relation to community norms. Through this quantification, a larger PD indicates stronger positive disconfirmation (exceeding expectations), and a larger ND indicates a greater degree of unmet expectations (negative disconfirmation), effectively treated as a continuous spectrum rather than an absolute binary. Furthermore, given the cross-country context, computing deviations relative to platform-specific average ratings helps to mitigate systematic differences in rating styles across cultural settings.

We employed the Tobit model to evaluate key factors influencing user satisfaction. This model addresses left-censoring in the dependent variable and enables estimation of the structural relationship between latent variables and observed rating deviations [39]. The Tobit specification was chosen because PD and ND are bounded at zero, producing censored distributions for which ordinary least squares estimation may be biased. In the model, PD and ND served as dependent variables, and the independent variables were topic probabilities extracted via LDA. Parameters were estimated using maximum likelihood, with separate regression equations constructed for PD and ND (see Formulas 1 and 2). To assess asymmetry in positive and negative influences, the Wald test was applied to compare coefficient magnitudes across the two models, with significance evaluated at the 0.05 level.

To further elucidate the asymmetric patterns of the influencing factors and their implications for product optimization, the KANO model was applied. This model classifies into five attribute types: charm, essential, expectation, indifferent, and reverse factors [55, 56]. Based on the differential impact of each factor on satisfaction and dissatisfaction, attributes were categorized accordingly. Following previous studies [41], six indicators were established to support classification: Positive Significance (PS), Negative Significance (NS), Positive Direction (PD), Negative Direction (ND), Coefficient Difference (CD), and Number Tendency (NT) (see Supplementary Fig. 1, Supplementary Tables 3, and Supplementary Note 2–3).

Construction of the user satisfaction evaluation index system

The AHP is a decision-making method that structures subjective judgments through pairwise comparisons of factor importance within a hierarchical framework [57]. This method has been widely applied in satisfaction evaluation research [42, 58–60]. In this study, we adapt the mathematical structure of AHP, specifically its hierarchical decomposition and pairwise comparison logic, to serve a different purpose: quantifying the intensity of user attention across satisfaction dimensions. Based on key influencing factors derived from user reviews, we constructed a three-tier evaluation system consisting of target, guideline, and indicator levels (see Supplementary Fig. 2 for the structural diagram). By converting the frequency proportions of user reviews into judgment matrices, indicator weights at each level were calculated following consistency checks, thereby establishing a quantified user satisfaction evaluation index system. Detailed procedures are provided in Supplementary Figs. 3–6, Supplementary Tables 4–9, and Supplementary Note 4 in the Appendix.

Results

Number of nutrition apps and reviews

This study included 537 nutrition apps (US: n = 406 [75.61%]; China: n = 131 [24.39%]), with 724,545 user reviews (US: 455,245 [62.83%]; China: 269,300 [37.17%]). Inter-rater consistency during screening was high (Kappa = 0.81). The screening process is shown in Fig. 2. Fig. 3 presents annual trends in newly launched apps and user review volume for both countries. US nutrition apps emerged earlier, with launches beginning in 2008 and peaking in 2020 (n = 49). Chinese apps first appeared in 2011, peaking in 2016 (n = 24). Overall, the US had more apps than China in most years. Review volume peaked in the US in 2024 (59,702 reviews) and in China in 2020 (77,723 reviews). US review volume generally exceeded that of China, except during 2020–2021 when Chinese reviews surpassed US levels.

In China, the identified apps covered all task requirements, with particularly broad implementation in target monitoring (n = 111, 84.73%), while functional coverage for personalized services was relatively limited (n = 60, 45.80%). Notably, physical examination and body composition monitoring (n = 93, 70.99%), dietary plans and recipe development (n = 90, 68.70%), and intervention effect and compliance assessment (n = 86, 65.65%) were addressed by a larger number of apps. Distribution of user ratings and review topics

Among these reviews, 696,824 received ratings of 4 stars or above, representing an overall high-star rating rate of 96.17%. The proportions of high-star reviews were 92.58% (249,331/269,300) for Chinese apps and 98.30% (447,493/455,245) for US apps. Based on perplexity curves (Supplementary Figs. 7–8 in the Appendix) and LDA clustering performance, the optimal number of topics was 13 for Chinese reviews and 12 for US reviews. These topics were grouped into six categories: Nutrition intervention and monitoring function, Software usability, Software performance, Data management, Software cost, and Personalized services. Discussion

Principal findings

This study conducted a large-scale analysis of user perceptions of nutrition apps in China and the US by combining topic modeling with AHP-weighted satisfaction analysis of user reviews. From a nutrition and public health perspective, differences in app features and satisfaction structures may be associated with users’ dietary behavior change, adherence to nutritional guidance, and the scalability of digital nutrition interventions. Overall, users in both countries reported generally high satisfaction with nutrition apps, with Chinese users exhibiting slightly higher overall satisfaction than their US counterparts. Specifically, Chinese users expressed relatively greater dissatisfaction with app reliability, whereas US users’ dissatisfaction was primarily related to fee and food database issues. Furthermore, expectations regarding nutrition apps may differ between Chinese and US users in ways that are associated with differences in their respective digital health ecosystems, potentially reflecting disparities in market maturity, service models, and user evaluation criteria (see Supplementary Note 6 in the Appendix for exploratory analyses of several potential associations).

Functionally, nutrition apps in both China and the US prioritize health management, though with differing emphases. Chinese apps demonstrate broader coverage in target monitoring and educational support, a pattern associated with the country’s policy focus on prevention and health literacy [62]. By enabling self-tracking and knowledge reinforcement, these features are associated with sustained dietary behavior change. Some apps incorporate Traditional Chinese Medicine elements such as constitution assessments to offer personalized recommendations, yet such features remain fragmented and not fully integrated into comprehensive health services. US apps, in contrast, place greater emphasis on dietary behavior intervention, focusing on lifestyle optimization in ways associated with energy balance and weight management. Established systems including the Mini Nutritional Assessment, MyPlate guidelines, and the USDA Food Database provide a standardized evidence base for their development [63]. Nevertheless, apps in both countries fall short in delivering personalized services and facilitating multidisciplinary collaboration, with few offering seamless connections to dietitians or broader healthcare teams.

The PS and NS indicators further enable a KANO-style interpretation of functional attributes, revealing notable cross-country differences. In the Chinese market, all identified factors were classified as charm attributes, indicating that their presence is associated with increased satisfaction while their absence does not systematically trigger strong dissatisfaction. This pattern suggests that Chinese users may not hold clearly defined baseline expectations for specific app functionalities. Rather, a wide range of features, including reliability, personalization, and pricing, appear to be primarily perceived as charm elements that enhance user experience when present. In contrast, the US results exhibit a more differentiated structure, with certain functions such as convenience and health monitoring as essential attributes. These features appear to represent baseline expectations, where deficiencies, including incomplete food databases or inaccurate food identification resulting from barcode scanning failures, are more likely to elicit dissatisfaction. This divergence suggests that US users may maintain more clearly defined expectations regarding core app functionality, particularly in relation to efficient and reliable dietary tracking. These findings suggest that user expectations of nutrition apps differ not only in content but also in structure. US users tend to distinguish between baseline requirements and value-added features, whereas Chinese users appear to associate a broader range of functions with overall experience rather than treating them as strict prerequisites. This distinction highlights the importance of examining expectation structures, rather than individual features in isolation, when interpreting satisfaction patterns.

AHP-weighted satisfaction analysis further reveals several critical issues, where failures to meet basic functional expectations are associated with intensely negative reactions from users. In the US market, dissatisfaction most frequently pertains to issues related to the food database and pricing. Some user comments mention that barcode scanning fails to accurately identify products, or that common foods are absent from the database, necessitating manual entry of nutritional information. These issues reflect limitations in food identification and database coverage, which are associated with reduced efficiency of dietary tracking and lower perceived usefulness of the application. Additionally, some users express discontent regarding subscription fees or paid features, suggesting that perceived value relative to cost may be associated with overall satisfaction. In contrast, Chinese users more frequently report concerns related to system reliability and application stability. Negative reviews often cite app crashes, slow response times, or unstable system performance during use. When core functionalities fail to operate smoothly and consistently, users may perceive the application as unreliable for daily dietary tracking and health management purposes. These concerns carry significant implications for both clinical nutritionists and developers. For clinical professionals, inaccurate food identification or unstable system performance may be associated with limited potential of nutrition applications as supportive tools for dietary counseling and behavioral interventions. If core functionalities such as barcode-based calorie tracking cannot be relied upon to perform consistently, clinicians may hesitate to recommend these applications as components of patients’ self-management strategies. For developers, these findings suggest that enhancing the accuracy, completeness, and technical stability of core features may prove more critical.

It is also important to distinguish between user satisfaction and scientific efficacy when interpreting the findings of this study. High satisfaction scores primarily reflect the usability and perceived usefulness of application features, rather than the nutritional accuracy or clinical validity of the dietary guidance provided. In other words, functions that users prefer, such as convenient food logging or simplified calorie tracking, do not necessarily ensure that the nutritional advice provided by the application aligns with evidence-based dietary guidelines. Previous research [19, 64] has noted that many nutrition applications prioritize usability and engagement, while the quality and scientific rigor of nutritional information may vary substantially across applications. Therefore, although the present study identifies features associated with higher user satisfaction, these results should not be interpreted as evidence that such functions provide nutritionally optimal guidance.

Another important consideration is the potential influence of app store platforms as a source of unobserved heterogeneity. The present analysis treats nutrition apps as relatively independent artifacts, yet user reviews are inherently shaped by the platform environments in which they are generated. For instance, differences across platforms such as the Apple App Store and various Chinese Android stores in terms of recommendation algorithms, ranking mechanisms, and review display systems may be systematically associated with app download patterns, usage frequency, and the propensity of certain user groups to leave reviews. These platform-level factors may, in turn, be related to both the composition and sentiment distribution of the review data. Algorithmic promotion strategies, for example, may be associated with increased exposure of highly rated apps or with engagement from specific user segments, potentially leading to overrepresentation of certain types of user experiences. Similarly, variations in review moderation policies or visibility rules may be related to the balance between positive and negative feedback. Consequently, some observed cross-country differences in satisfaction patterns may partially reflect platform-driven selection effects, rather than reflecting differences solely in app functionality or user expectations. While the current study does not explicitly control for these platform-level influences, acknowledging their potential role is important for interpreting the findings.

Overall, nutrition apps function not only as digital tools but also as potential public health interventions. Their effectiveness depends on their ability to support accessibility, trust, sustained engagement, and actionable dietary behavior change across sociocultural contexts. The findings can also be interpreted through established nutrition behavior change theories. Core functions such as food tracking, weight management, and health monitoring enable self-monitoring, a central construct in Social Cognitive Theory that enhances self-efficacy and behavioral regulation. Differences in satisfaction with reliability and convenience may influence users’ confidence and sustained engagement, thereby affecting adherence to dietary guidance. From the perspective of the Transtheoretical Model and the COM-B framework, educational support and target monitoring may strengthen psychological capability and motivation, supporting progression toward action and maintenance stages. Features such as community interaction and personalized services also align with perceived behavioral control and social influence as described in the Theory of Planned Behavior. These connections suggest that nutrition apps influence dietary outcomes not only through functional design but also by shaping key behavioral determinants.

This study offers several recommendations for nutrition apps: First, for the US market, developers should ensure stable performance of essential attributes (Convenience and Health Monitoring Function) while strengthening charm attributes such as personalized services. Second, for the Chinese market, balanced improvement across all charm attributes is important to meet broad user expectations. Third, app development should also follow localization principles. In China, improving software reliability and expanding culturally relevant functions such as TCM-related services may enhance acceptance. In the US, clearer communication of pricing structures and value propositions may improve fee acceptance. Fourth, future development could expand coverage to sub-health populations, individuals with special dietary needs, and active lifestyle users [65]. Fifth, strengthening evidence-based foundations may enhance scientific credibility [66], and further highlight the potential role of professional oversight(e.g., clinical nutrition experts and nutrition guidelines) in improving the scientific quality of digital nutrition tools. Sixth, artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, including intelligent food recognition, precision nutrition, and personalized dietary guidance, offer further opportunities for innovation [67–69]. Additionally, given the importance of nutrition in older adults [70], age-friendly interface design and improved readability should also be prioritized.

Advantages of this study

This study offers several advantages in nutrition app evaluation. First, it addresses the limitations of single-country analyses by establishing the first comparative framework for examining nutrition apps in China and the US. Second, it introduces methodological innovation through the integration of TTF, LDA, the Two-Factor Theory, and AHP. Beyond method integration, the study constructs a multidimensional evaluation chain spanning from function coverage to user satisfaction. This framework validates the applicability of the Two-Factor Theory in digital health by revealing cross-cultural differences in user need attributes, while also extending the KANO model’s application to large-scale user-generated content analysis, thereby offering a novel theoretical lens for understanding satisfaction formation in digital health products. By synthesizing theories from information science, behavioral science, public health, and management decision-making, the framework establishes a complete evidence chain from software functionality to user experience. This analytical approach is transferable to evaluations of other health-related apps. Third, the study draws on over 720,000 authentic user reviews from major app stores in both countries, with findings grounded in real-world user experiences that enhance practical relevance. The identified motivators and hygiene factors offer an empirical foundation for prioritizing design improvements in nutrition apps.

Additionally, this study highlights the potential contribution of nutrition apps to nutrition science and public health. These tools increasingly support daily dietary decisions, long-term behavior change, and self-management across diverse populations. By generating large-scale real-world data, they capture users’ dietary concerns, barriers, and preferences in naturalistic settings. Systematically linking app functions with nutrition intervention tasks and user satisfaction helps clarify the relationships between digital tools and dietary behavior, adherence, and engagement. Unlike traditional studies relying on controlled settings or small samples, this research draws on extensive user-generated content that reflects dietary behavior in everyday life. This approach enables nutrition research to move closer to real-life behavior and population-level patterns. The cross-cultural comparison further identifies common gaps and context-specific needs, informing the design of more effective, evidence-based, and scalable digital nutrition interventions.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis is based on user reviews and ratings, which reflect perceived satisfaction rather than direct nutrition or health outcomes. The findings capture how users evaluate app functions and services, and therefore cannot be used to infer actual dietary behavior change or health improvement. The results should not be interpreted as evidence of causal effects.

Second, sentiment analysis and topic identification in this study relied on automated text mining approaches to process a large volume of user-generated reviews, with no dedicated validation subset incorporated. While such methods enable efficient analysis of extensive datasets, automated sentiment algorithms remain inherently limited in detecting sarcasm. Consequently, the filtering of extreme text-rating contradictions, though mathematically necessary, may have inadvertently removed a small fraction of valid ironically phrased feedback. Furthermore, this study did not include a manually coded validation subset to assess the precision and recall of the automated sentiment classification. Future research should incorporate manual annotation of a representative sample of reviews to further evaluate the robustness and accuracy of automated text analysis.

Third, the topic analysis is based solely on textual content and does not incorporate non-verbal feedback (e.g., emojis). Although emojis may convey additional emotional nuance, they typically appear alongside text and serve an ancillary rather than independent evaluative function. Their exclusion may somewhat attenuate emotional intensity but is unlikely to alter the main topics identified or the overall direction of user satisfaction.

Fourth, the analysis includes only users who submitted reviews, which may introduce selection bias, as reviewing users may differ from the broader user population in engagement level or motivation. In addition, user-generated reviews on app platforms often reflect more extreme experiences, as individuals who choose to leave feedback are more likely to report either highly positive or highly negative opinions. This tendency may amplify particularly favorable or unfavorable experiences, potentially influencing the overall assessment of app usability derived from review-based analyses. In this study, reviewers represent a very small proportion of total app downloads in both countries: 0.170% (455,245 reviews from 268,150,171 downloads) in the US and 0.015% (269,300 reviews from 1,801,303,607 downloads) in China. This low review rate is characteristic of app store data and reflects general user behavior rather than a study-specific limitation. Although the characteristics of reviewing users such as age and usage frequency cannot be directly compared with the full user population, the large volume of reviews enables the analysis to focus on recurrent and stable patterns rather than isolated or extreme opinions. The consistency of main findings across a wide range of apps and reviews supports the robustness of results at the pattern level, while precluding claims of full representativeness at the individual user level.

Fifth, the interpretation of PD and ND as proxies for user satisfaction necessitates methodological consideration. These metrics capture the direction and magnitude of deviation from a community norm, serving as an indicator of expectation disconfirmation rather than a direct measure of individual affective states. For instance, a rating of 4 stars, while objectively positive, may constitute a negative deviation if it falls below an application’s exceptionally high average rating. In such instances, ND reflects relative underperformance against expectations rather than absolute dissatisfaction. This relative approach, theoretically grounded in expectation disconfirmation theory, is essential for mitigating cultural and platform-specific response biases in cross-country comparisons. However, the findings, including KANO classifications, should be interpreted as reflecting systematic patterns of expectation confirmation and disconfirmation at the population level, rather than as direct classifications of individual user sentiment.

Sixth, the KANO classification in this study is based on patterns of statistical significance and coefficient comparisons derived from Tobit models. Although all classified factors demonstrated meaningful effect sizes, the binary distinction between significant and non-significant categories inevitably omits some continuous information regarding effect magnitudes.

Seventh, the cross-sectional design of this study precludes the assessment of long-term usage patterns or temporal dynamics in user satisfaction.

Future research should address these limitations through multiple complementary approaches. Behavioral indicators such as usage frequency, duration, and depth of feature access could help assess how users interact with specific functions beyond expressed opinions. Integrating functional use patterns with satisfaction signals would provide a more comprehensive understanding of user engagement and potential behavior change, while longitudinal data could track transitions from adoption to sustained use and clarify how functional exposure shapes satisfaction over time. In addition, this study applies a regression-based adaptation of the KANO model to analyze user-generated reviews using indicators such as PS, NS, and CD. Although effective in revealing user preferences from large-scale historical data, this approach differs from the traditional KANO questionnaire method that directly captures psychological expectations. Future studies could therefore employ conventional KANO surveys among targeted user groups and triangulate these findings with text-mining results to strengthen validation of the psychological mechanisms underlying user satisfaction with nutrition apps. Furthermore, future studies could further explore how these platform-level factors relate to review volume, sentiment distribution, and user engagement across different app store environments. Finally, advances in AI, particularly LLMs, such as personalized recommendation algorithms and intelligent nutrition advisors, present opportunities to further enhance user experience in nutrition apps.

Conclusions

This study indicates that user satisfaction with nutrition apps is associated less with the presence of specific features than with whether those features meet context-dependent baseline expectations. In the US, food database completeness and reasonable pricing are essential requirements, and deficiencies in these areas are associated with marked dissatisfaction. In China, system stability assumes greater importance, as performance issues may be associated with diminished overall user experience. These findings suggest that enhancing user experience depends on prioritizing the resolution of critical failure points rather than expanding functionality. For developers, this implies strengthening food database coverage and pricing transparency in the US, while ensuring technical stability and reliable performance in China prior to introducing advanced features. For clinical use, high user satisfaction should not be equated with nutritional validity, suggesting that careful integration of apps into dietary interventions remains necessary.

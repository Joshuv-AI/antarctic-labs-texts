The genetic spectrum of LRRK2 variants in Parkinson’s disease: findings from a large Chinese cohort
Authors: Juan Wan, Hongxu Pan, Dong Chang, Yuwen Zhao, Qian Xu, Lingyan Yao, Qiying Sun, Xuewei Zhang, Runcheng He, Chunyu Wang, Hainan Zhang, Lifang Lei, Yuzheng Wang, Mingqiang Li, Heng Wu, Jinghong Ma, Yiwen Wu, Yan Xu, Nian Xiong, Tao Wang, Xuejing Wang, Ling Chen, Yanming Xu, Hongmei Cao, Kezhong Zhang, Weiguo Liu, Yuhu Zhang, Chengjie Mao, Qing Wang, Zheng Xue, Zhentao Zhang, Oumei Cheng, Wei Huang, Guohua Zhao, Guiyun Cui, Tao Chen, Puqing Wang, Wei Di, Hong Liu, Houfeng Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pathogenicity of variants of uncertain significance in the LRRK2 gene remains underexplored. Investigating the LRRK2 variant spectrum in a large Chinese population cohort can provide deeper insights into its pathogenic mechanisms. This study examined the LRRK2 gene variants in 20,519 Chinese individuals, including 7,562 Parkinson’s disease (PD) patients, 3,077 Essential tremor (ET) patients, and 9880 healthy controls. We conducted a genetic analysis of low-frequency and common non-synonymous variants in the LRRK2 gene across the cohorts. A total of 287 low-frequency non-synonymous LRRK2 variants were identified in the PD and control cohorts. Among these, six reported pathogenic variants (p.R1325Q, p.R1441C, p.R1441H, p.V1447M, p.G2019S, p.I2020T) and three reported likely pathogenic variants (p.R1067Q, p.N1437D, p.R1728H) were enriched in PD cases, with a frequency of 0.71%. In contrast, only one pathogenic variant (p.R1325Q) and one likely pathogenic variant (p.R1067Q) were observed in healthy controls (0.11%), and the ET cohort exhibited similar variant distribution to controls (0.19%). Burden analysis and association analysis revealed novel likely pathogenic variants, including p.A312V, p.M968K, and p.R1320S as candidates. These novel variants were significantly more frequent in PD patients (0.79%) compared to healthy controls (0.20%) or ET patients (0.42%). Additionally, seven common missense variants of LRRK2 were identified, and significant associations with PD for p.A419V, p.R1628P, and p.G2385R were confirmed, but no common variants were linked to ET. This study provides the first comprehensive characterization of the LRRK2 variant spectrum in a large Chinese population, underscoring the pivotal role of LRRK2 in PD pathogenesis but not in ET. These findings advance the understanding of LRRK2 in neurodegenerative disorders and lay a foundation for personalized therapeutic strategies based on genetic profiling.Subject terms: Diseases, Genetics, Neurology, Neuroscience

Introduction

Leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 (LRRK2), a large multi-domain protein encompassing 2527 amino acids1, plays a critical role in cellular processes relevant to neurodegeneration and inflammation2–4. Its complex architecture incorporates several functional domains: an N-terminal armadillo (ARM) domain, followed by ankyrin (ANK) repeats, leucine-rich repeats (LRRs), a Ras-of-Complex (ROC) GTPase domain, a C-terminal-of-ROC (COR) dimerization domain, a kinase (KIN) domain, and a C-terminal WD40 domain5. Beyond its enzymatic GTPase and kinase activities, LRRK2 harbors multiple protein-protein interaction motifs (ANK, LRR, WD40), positioning it as a pivotal signaling hub capable of integrating diverse cellular signals and pathways6–8.

Pathogenic mutations within LRRK2 are firmly established as notable causes of autosomal dominant Parkinson’s disease (PD)9,10. Variants such as p.G2019S and p.R1441G/C/H within the kinase and ROC domains, respectively, demonstrate robust Mendelian inheritance and high penetrance in familial PD cases11–14. Other pathogenic mutations include p.N1437H, p.Y1699C, and p.I2020T15. Complementing these incomplete penetrant mutations, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and large-scale case-control analyses have identified specific LRRK2 variants as risk factors, conferring increased susceptibility to both PD and Crohn’s disease (CD). Notable examples include p.A419V, p.R1628P, p.N2081D, and p.G2385R16–18. The genetic pleiotropy of LRRK2 variants, contributing to both PD and CD, suggests that LRRK2 may play a role in a wider range of human diseases; however, systematic analyses of LRRK2 variants across large and diverse neurological disease cohorts remain scarce.

The landscape of LRRK2 variation extends far beyond these established mutations. Currently, nearly 200 LRRK2 variants are classified as variants of uncertain significance (VUS) (as cataloged in resources like MDSGene, https://www.mdsgene.org)19. While some VUS have been reported in individuals with PD, conclusive evidence regarding their pathogenicity remains elusive. Critically, there is a lack of data from multi-generational families needed to evaluate co-segregation with disease, as well as insufficient large-scale genetic association studies and functional validation using experimental models. Such evidence is essential to definitively establish the pathogenic nature of these VUS.

Remarkably, the geographic and ancestral distribution of established pathogenic LRRK2 variants exhibits pronounced heterogeneity20. For instance, the well-known p.G2019S pathogenic variant has a higher prevalence in PD patients from North African and Ashkenazi Jewish backgrounds compared to individuals of European ancestry21. The p.G2385R and p.R1628P pathogenic variants are more frequently observed in PD patients of East Asian descent16,22. This striking geographic disparity and the relative paucity of comprehensive data from other understudied groups like the Chinese population highlight gaps in our understanding of LRRK2-related PD genetics on a global scale. The vast majority of established pathogenic variants and risk alleles were identified primarily in populations of European or Middle Eastern descent11. Therefore, the role of LRRK2 coding variation in PD pathogenesis within the Chinese population, representing one of the world’s largest and genetically distinct groups, remains inadequately characterized.

To address this critical knowledge gap and contribute to a more comprehensive genetic map of PD, the present study conducted a systematic analysis of LRRK2 coding variants in large, well-characterized Chinese cohorts comprising both PD patients and ethnically matched healthy controls. Moreover, we included a large Chinese cohort of essential tremor as well to investigate the potential involvement of LRRK2 in ET.

Low-frequency and common non-synonymous variants of LRRK2 gene

A total of 287 low-frequency LRRK2 non-synonymous variants (minor allele frequency < 0.01, MAF) were identified in PD and control cohorts. These included 265 missense variants, five splice-site variants, ten frameshift deletion/insertion variants, three non-frameshift deletion variants, and 4 stopgain variants (Fig. 2). Among these, 95 low-frequency coding variants were unique to PD patients, 117 were unique to healthy controls, and 75 were shared by both PD patients and healthy controls. In total, 548 PD patients (7.25%) and 627 normal controls (6.35%) were identified as carriers of low frequency LRRK2 non-synonymous variants in this cohort (Supplementary Table 1).

In the ET cohort, a total of 98 non-synonymous variants were identified, consisting of 91 missense variants, two stop-gain variants, one non-frameshift deletion, and four splice-site variants (Supplementary Fig. 1). Among these variants, 47 were found exclusively in ET patients, three were shared between PD and ET, 11 were shared between ET and normal controls, and the remaining 37 were present in all three groups (PD, ET, and normal controls). In total, 213 ET patients (6.92%) carried low-frequency non-synonymous LRRK2 variants.

Additionally, we identified seven common LRRK2 non-synonymous variants (MAF ≥ 0.01) in our cohorts: p.A419V, p.N551K, p.R1398H, p.R1628P, p.S1647T, p.G2385R, and p.M2397T (Supplementary Fig. 2). The classification of variants as common or low-frequency was based on their MAF within our dataset. For instance, p.I723V, p.P1542S, and p.N2081D, despite being common (MAF > 0.01) in East Asian population databases, were low-frequency (MAF < 0.01) in our cohort and were thus classified as low-frequency variants. Conversely, p.A419V, which is rare in the general population (MAF < 0.01), was common in our cohorts (MAF ≥ 0.01) and was therefore analyzed as a common variant. Notably, a strong linkage was observed between the p.N551K and p.R1398H variants.

Burden analysis of low-frequency non-synonymous variants of LRRK2 gene

Burden analysis revealed a significant increase in low-frequency LRRK2 non-synonymous variants (MAF < 0.01) in the PD cohort compared to controls (Table 2). This increased burden was primarily driven by missense variants, particularly those predicted to be deleterious and rare variants (MAF < 0.001), indicating a critical role for LRRK2 in PD. In contrast, no significant difference was observed for LoF variants, suggesting that while LRRK2 gain-of-function contributes to PD pathogenesis, loss-of-function does not confer a significant protective effect.

To dissect the burden difference, we examined known pathogenic variants. In PD cohorts, 54 PD patients were identified as carriers of known pathogenic LRRK2 variants (Fig. 3). The pathogenic variants included: p.R1325Q, p.R1441C, p.R1441H, p.V1447M, p.G2019S, p.I2020T and the likely pathogenic variants included: p.R1067Q, p.N1437D, p.R1728H. The mutation rate of LRRK2 gene in our PD cohort was 0.71%. As for control subjects, only one pathogenic variant (p.R1325Q) and one likely pathogenic variant (p.R1067Q) were observed in 11 healthy controls, at a frequency of 0.11%, which was significantly lower than PD subjects. The ET cohort exhibited a variant distribution similar to controls, with only one pathogenic (p.R1325Q) and one likely pathogenic variant (p.R1067Q) detected in 6 subjects at a frequency of 0.19%, which was slightly higher than control subject but still significantly lower than PD subjects (Fig. 3).

To determine if this burden was driven solely by known pathogenic variants, we repeated the analysis after excluding their carriers. The burden of low-frequency non-synonymous variants remained borderline significant (P = 0.058), pointing to the contribution of additional, novel pathogenic variants in LRRK2 (Supplementary Table 2).

Potential novel P/LP variants of LRRK2 gene

We further identified three novel likely pathogenic LRRK2 variants in this study: p.A312V, p.M968K, and p.R1320S, which exhibiting significant differences in our case-control association analysis (Supplementary Table 3 and Supplementary Table 4). Collectively, these variants were carried by 60 PD patients, accounting for 0.79% of the cohort. Notably, with the exception of p.A312V, the other two variants were also present in 21 healthy controls (0.21%) and 13 ET patients (0.42%) (Fig. 3)

After excluding carriers of these three variants, a burden analysis of the remaining low-frequency non-synonymous LRRK2 variants revealed no significant difference between PD patients and controls, suggesting that these other variants are unlikely to be major contributors to PD (Supplementary Table 5).

Additionally, nine other low-frequency LRRK2 non-synonymous variants were identified in multiple PD cases but were absent in all controls (Supplementary Table 1). Specifically, eight of these variants were each carried by two PD patients (p.L117F, p.V296A, p.H442Y, p.H468L, p.I835V, p.R1628H, p.S1752C, and p.D1887V), while one variant, p.I207T, was carried by three PD patients.

Association analysis of common non-synonymous variants of LRRK2 gene

In this study, we performed association analysis on the seven identified common LRRK2 coding variants (Table 3). In PD cohorts, the p.A419V, p.R1628P and p.G2385R variants showed significant associations, but no significant association was observed for the p.N551K, p.R1398H, p.S1647T, and p.M2397T variants. Moreover, conditional analysis revealed p.A419V, p.R1628P and p.G2385R were independently associated with PD (Supplementary Table 6). As for ET cohort, no common variant showed significant association with ET (Supplementary Table 7).

Discussion

This study represents the most extensive analysis of LRRK2 coding variants in a Chinese cohort to date, encompassing 7562 PD patients, 9880 healthy controls, and 3077 ET patients. Our findings delineate a distinct genetic landscape of LRRK2-associated Parkinsonism in the Chinese population, characterized by a unique spectrum of pathogenic variants, specific risk alleles, and potentially novel pathogenic candidates.

A primary observation is the relatively low prevalence of established P/LP variants, which were identified in only 0.71% of our PD cohort. This suggests that while LRRK2 is a pivotal gene23, high-penetrance P/LP variants20 contribute to a minor fraction of hereditary PD in this population. In our cohort, 47% of PD patients carrying P/LP LRRK2 variants presented with early-onset disease (AAO < 50 years), with a mean AAO of 53.5 years for the entire group. This indicates that while LRRK2 P/LP variants, similar to other autosomal dominant genes, are associated with a later age of onset compared to some autosomal recessive forms, the onset is still notably earlier than that observed in idiopathic PD (iPD). This contrasts with PD associated with LRRK2 susceptibility loci, where the AAO typically does not differ from iPD22.

The spectrum of these P/LP variants in our Chinese cohort is markedly different from that in Caucasian populations, but resembles the spectrum found in other Asian regions, such as Japan and Malaysia19,24–26. The p.R1067Q variant emerged as the predominant mutation, accounting for 54% of all P/LP carriers and thus representing the most critical pathogenic LRRK2 variant in Chinese individuals. Conversely, the p.G2019S mutation, highly prevalent in European ancestry groups27, was detected in only three unrelated, native Chinese patients. This divergence, where only 9 of 26 known P/LP variants were observed19, highlights the population-specific genetic architecture of LRRK2-PD and cautions against extrapolating data from European populations to Chinese patients28.

The presence of certain P/LP variants, namely p.R1325Q and p.R1067Q, in both control and ET cohorts warrants careful interpretation. The comparable carrier frequency of p.R1325Q across PD, ET, and control groups suggests its pathogenicity requires rigorous re-evaluation. In contrast, the significantly higher frequency of p.R1067Q in PD patients points to its strong disease association. Its detection in ET and controls could indicate genetic pleiotropy, a variable age-dependent penetrance, or the presence of undiagnosed comorbid PD within the ET cohort.

The majority of LRRK2 gene variants were shared between PD cases and controls, making the identification of truly pathogenic variants critical for accurate PD diagnosis and treatment29,30. Therefore, we performed a gene-based burden analysis. This revealed a significant cumulative burden of LRRK2 coding variants in PD cases compared to controls; this signal was attenuated but remained borderline significant even after excluding known P/LP carriers. This finding strongly suggests the presence of additional, yet-to-be-identified pathogenic variants. Subsequent association analysis pinpointed three novel candidate variants—p.A312V, p.M968K, and p.R1320S—which were significantly enriched in PD patients (0.79%) versus controls (0.20%). The pathogenic potential of p.R1320S is bolstered by prior functional evidence of enhanced kinase activity31, while p.A312V and p.M968K represent novel candidates requiring functional validation. Notably, our burden analysis found no significant contribution from LoF variants, reinforcing a gain-of-function model as the primary pathogenic mechanism for LRRK2.

In parallel with our investigation of low-frequency variants, we confirmed the association of three common non-synonymous variants—p.A419V, p.R1628P, and p.G2385R—with PD risk in the Chinese population. Critically, these variants showed no association with ET, reinforcing their specificity to PD pathogenesis in this ethnic group.

This study has several limitations. On the one hand, integrating data from multiple independent cohorts poses inherent challenges due to technical heterogeneity. The participating sites used varying sequencing platforms, including both WES and WGS, which may lead to variability in coverage depth and uniformity, potentially affecting variant-detection sensitivity across cohorts. Furthermore, variations in bioinformatics filtering strategies and quality control pipelines across different sites could introduce batch effects. Even though a meta-analysis framework was employed, we cannot completely rule out the potential impact of residual technical biases on our findings, particularly for variants located in regions with inconsistent coverage across platforms. Moreover, the age mismatch between cases and controls could potentially affect the analysis of non-penetrant or risk-modifying variants associated with late-onset PD. Finally, our analysis was limited to coding regions and did not assess non-coding regulatory or structural variants32, it also did not address potential interactions with other PD high-risk genes such as GBA133,34.

In conclusion, our large-scale study provides the first comprehensive characterization of the LRRK2 variant spectrum in the Chinese population. We have established that Chinese LRRK2-PD is driven by a unique combination of a dominant P/LP variant, specific common risk alleles, and novel candidate pathogenic mutations. The profound heterogeneity in LRRK2 variant profiles across global populations underscores the critical need for regionally tailored approaches to genetic counseling, risk stratification, and the advancement of precision medicine for PD.

Methods

Participants

A total of 20,519 Chinese subjects were included in this study, comprising eight distinct cohorts: three PD cohorts, four healthy control cohorts and one ET cohort, including 7562 PD patients, 3077 ET patients, and 9880 healthy controls. All these participants were recruited from centers in the PD and Movement Disorders Multicentre Database and Collaborative Network in China (http://pd-mdcnc.com)35. All patients underwent diagnosis adhering to corresponding diagnostic criteria such as the Movement Disorder Society clinical diagnostic criteria for PD36, guidelines of the Tremor Task Force of the International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society and criteria from the Washington Heights-Inwood Genetic Study of Essential Tremor for ET37.

The study received approval from the ethics committees of Xiangya Hospital of Central South University and all participating centers (202005124). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians at the time of enrollment.

Genotyping

WES and WGS were performed according to previous descriptions38,39. Briefly, we stratified the samples into distinct cohorts based on enrollment time and clinical characteristics, employing diverse sequencing platforms and methodologies for genotyping. Specifically, the PD-1 and NC-1 cohorts underwent WES using the SureSelect Human All Exon Kit V6 (Agilent) for target enrichment and library preparation, followed by sequencing on the Illumina X10 platform. The PD-2 and NC-2 cohorts were subjected to WGS with moderate-depth on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform using 150 bp paired-end reads; genomic DNA was sheared to a size range of 100–1000 bp for Illumina DNA library preparation. And the PD-3 and NC-3/4 cohorts underwent WGS with high-depth on the BGI Genomics DNBSEQ-T7 platform with 150 bp paired-end reads, utilizing a PCR-free library preparation protocol on BGI’s DNBSEQ platform. Burrows-Wheeler Aligner-MEM algorithm40, Picard (http://broadinstitute.github.io/picard/), and Genome Analysis Toolkit41 were used to generate high-quality variants for all the samples. The gVCF file for each sample was obtained and combined into jointVCF files using Sentieon software following the suggested pipelines. ANNOVAR42 and VarCards43 were used to annotate the variants with gene regions (RefSeq, hg38), amino acid alterations, functional effects, allele frequencies in East Asian population (ChinaMap database, gnomAD database, ExAC database) and in silico pathogenicity prediction (SIFT, CADD, REVEL).

Quality control

Due to platform heterogeneity, joint variant calling was performed within cohorts sharing identical sequencing platforms and methodologies. Accordingly, distinct variant quality control (QC) parameters were applied to the different joint call datasets to minimize false positives. Specifically, the WES (100×) dataset employed thresholds of a Phred-scaled genotype quality (GQ) score of 30, an allele depth (AD) of 10, and a read depth (DP) of 30. The WGS (15×) dataset utilized a GQ score of 15, an AD of 2, and a DP of 5, whereas the WGS (30×) dataset required a GQ score of 30, an AD of 5, and a DP of 10.

For individual and variant quality control QC in WGS and WES, the data were recoded into binary PLINK input format using PLINK v1.944. Variant quality control procedures involved retaining autosomal variants with high call rate (missing rate < 5%) while excluding variants exhibiting deviations from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (P < 1×10−4). Individuals exhibiting discrepancies between inferred and self-reported sex, as well as those displaying heterozygosity/genotype calls deviating by more than ± 3 standard deviations, were excluded. Additionally, individuals demonstrating cryptic relatedness (identity by descent > 0.15) were omitted from subsequent analysis. Population outliers and stratification were assessed through principal component analysis using PLINK, with samples displaying divergent ancestry excluded from the analysis.

Variants stratification

Non-synonymous variants in LRRK2 transcript region (NM_198578 at chr12:40,224,997-40,369,285; hg38)45 were included in the study. For frequency stratification, variants were initially classified as common or low-frequency based on a minor allele frequency (MAF) cutoff of 0.01 in our dataset, with those below 0.001 further subcategorized as rare. For functional categorization, variants were annotated as missense, loss-of-function (LoF), or deleterious missense (Dmis). The LoF category comprised stop gain/loss, frameshift, and canonical splice-site mutations (located within two base pairs of exon-intron junctions). Deleterious missense variants were defined as those predicted to be damaging by CADD with a score of at least 20. In parallel, non-synonymous LRRK2 variants were classified according to MDSGene19 as pathogenic, likely pathogenic, variant of uncertain significance, likely benign, or benign. Additionally, low-frequency variants that were not previously defined as pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP) were designated as “novel likely pathogenic” if they demonstrated an increase in disease risk of suggestive significnace within our dataset.

Statistical analysis

For burden analysis, low-frequency coding variants were tested using the sequence kernel association test-optimal (SKAT-O), adding age, sex, and the first five principal components as covariates46,47. Variants of each frequency and functional group were analyzed. Meta-analyses of the SKAT-O resulting summary statistics in each variant class were performed using MetaSKAT method as implemented in metaSKAT R packages48.

For association analysis, PLINK was used to perform logistic regression with age, sex and first five principal components for population stratification as covariates. Conditional analyses were conducted by adding the most significant variant in our data to logistic regression as covariates. And we used meta-analysis to combine the association results from each dataset using METAL49 with a fixed-effects model.

For multiple testing correction, a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold was used. Meanwhile, we considered those results with a P-value < 0.05, but not surviving the Bonferroni correction as suggestive.

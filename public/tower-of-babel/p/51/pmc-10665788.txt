Elevated profiles of peripheral Th22, Th17, Th2 cells, and decreased percentage of Th1 cells in breast cancer patients
Authors: Zhiguo Peng, Xinyue Dong, Miao He, Yajing Zhao, Yujia Liu, Mo Li, Guosheng Li, Xiuwen Wang, Li Li, Yu Hu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTh22 subset is a particular type of CD4+ T helper cells subset. Our study aimed to explore the expression level of circulating Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells and the possible mechanism of these cells in breast cancer (BC) with different pathological features.MethodsOur study enrolled 43 newly diagnosed BC patients and 30 healthy controls. Frequencies of peripheral Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells were tested by flow cytometry. Concentrations of IL‐22 cytokine in plasma were examined by enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Real‐time PCR was done to test aromatic hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) and RAR‐associated orphan receptor C (RORC) gene expression.ResultsFrequencies of Th22, Th17, Th2 subsets, and the plasma IL‐22 level was obviously higher in the BC patients. A positive correlation between Th22 frequency and IL‐22 concentration in plasma was detected in BC patients. Furthermore, the percentage of Th22, Th2 subsets in peripheral blood of HER2 positive BC was higher than that in HER2 negative BC patients. A negative correlation between Th1 subset and Ki‐67% as well as a positive correlation between Th2 subset and Ki‐67% was found in BC patients. The proportion of Th1 cells in BC patients was significantly lower than that of the control group. Expression of AHR and RORC transcription factors were also observed to be upregulated in the BC patients. Furthermore, Th22 cells were positively correlated with BC tumor stage and clinical outcomes. The BC patients with a higher percentage of Th22, Th17, Th1 cells or a lower percentage of Th1 cells showed a decreased trend of survival rate.ConclusionTh22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 subsets may play an essential role in BC patients. Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells may have potential significance to be used as clinical markers in BC patients with different molecular classification. Th22 cells may have potential value in BC patients’ outcomes prediction, providing clinical value.Keywords: breast cancer, Th1 cells, Th17 cells, Th2 cells, Th22 cells

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer is by far the most common malignancy in women. Modern treatment for breast cancer is multimodal and includes surgery, radiotherapy, and medical treatment. 1 It has been reported that immune disorders occur in BC and are related to cellular immune cells. 2 , 3 , 4 Konjevic et al. also found that NK cell dysfunction is related to the progression of breast tumor. 5 CD4 positive T cells (T helper cells) play an important role in immune disorders of BC patients. 6 , 7 Naive CD4+ T cells differentiate into different T helper subtypes which mainly depends on the difference of cytokines in the local environment. Cytokine expression patterns characterize individual Th subsets and determine their function in immune defense of the host. 8 Further, cytokines produced by Th cells play a crucial role in regulating cellular responses between tumors and the immune system. 9

Th22 subset is an important subset of T cells. Th22 cells are identified as CD4+IL‐22+IL‐17− IFN‐γ− T cells. IL‐22 is mainly produced by Th22 subset in humans, and IL‐22 primarily mediates the function of Th22 cells. The target of IL‐22 is nonhematopoietic cells, although it is produced by immune lymphocytes. 10 Research indicates that IL‐22 is a cancer‐promoting cytokine. 11 Th22 cells are the key mediator in the pathogenesis of different kinds of malignant tumors. Many studies reported Th22 cells were obviously increased in some tumors, such as cervical, liver, colorectal, genital system, gastric, renal, and pancreatic cancers. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18

Th17 cells are dependent on cytokines, costimulatory molecules, chemokines, and cell‐cell interactions. The positive or negative role of Th17 cells in tumor is generally dependent on the tumor microenvironment. It is involved in the proliferation and expansion of CD8+ memory T cells and tumor‐infiltrating lymphocytes. The interaction of TGF‐β with IL‐21 and IL‐6 can favor the generation of regulatory Th17 cells. At the same time, IL‐1β and IL‐23 can induce the production of pathogenic Th17 cells. This indicates that the different cytokines in the tumor microenvironment will affect the differentiation of Th17 cells into different types of Th17 cell subtypes, producing their own unique biological functions. 19 Studies have found that Th17 cells promote tumor development in colorectal, lung, and liver cancers. 20 , 21 , 22

T helper 1 (Th1) lymphocytes, which release TNF‐α, IL‐2, and interferon‐γ (IFN‐γ), exert antitumor effects. The abnormal percentage of Th1 cells in the tumor microenvironment is associated with favorable outcomes in many malignant tumors including brain tumors and laryngeal cancer. 23 , 24

In addition, Th2 cells produce many cytokines as IL‐4, IL‐5, IL‐6, IL‐10, IL‐13 and promote tumor growth by suppressing the host immune system. 25 Some studies have shown that Th2 subset is increased in oral, pancreatic, esophageal, and gastric cancers. 26 , 27

The main cytokine secreted by Th22 cells is IL‐22. IL‐22 acts by binding to the complex of IL‐22R1‐IL10R2, using Janus kinase (JAK) 1 as well as tyrosine kinase (TYK) 2 to phosphorylate STAT3 and enhancing the expression of related genes. AHR is a major transcription factor carrying signals from the cell membrane to the nucleus and promotes the proliferation of tumors. 28 , 29 , 30 In addition to the JAK/STAT3 signaling, IL‐22 also activates MAPK (p38 and ERK) and Akt to induce tumorigenesis. 10 Th17 cells also transduce signals through RORC and are closely related to Th22 cells. 31 , 32 In addition to IL‐17, Th17 also induces the production of proinflammatory cytokines, such as TNF, GM‐CSF, and IL‐1β, 33 and IL‐1β promotes Th22 secretion and IL‐22 production. 34

Th1 can secrete TNF‐α, which promotes the differentiation of Th22. 35 A previous study has shown that the frequency of Th1 and Th22 cells in cervical cancer patients is approximately negatively correlated. 17 Major transcription factors mediate the ability of primary CD4+ T cells to differentiate into different subsets. Various transcriptional regulatory factors regulate Th1 and Th2 cells, and the regulatory factors between these two subsets inhibit each other. For example, T‐bet upregulates Th1 cells and downregulates Th2 cells. 31 , 36 Th1/Th2 imbalance affects allergic and autoimmune diseases 37 and plays a role in tumors. Some research has shown that increasing the Th1/Th2 ratio has an antitumor effect. 38

To the best of our knowledge, no studies have been published on the status of Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 subsets and their relationships in the peripheral blood of BC patients. Therefore, our study aimed to clarify the expression level of Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells in the peripheral blood of BC. In addition, we also attempted to further analyze their relationships in peripheral immune cells and offer further insight into the role of these Th subsets in the pathogenesis of different kinds of BC patients.

METHODS

Ethics statement

Our study was approved by the Medical Ethical Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University. An informed consent document was obtained from each participant. The informed consent stated that the unused portion or excess of the peripheral blood drawn from patients for complete blood cell counts was as the source of this research, or the peripheral blood drawn from healthy subjects for this research was voluntary.

Patients and controls

Forty‐three newly‐diagnosed primary BC patients (all females; age range, 33–67 years; median age, 52 years) were enrolled in this study. Enrollment took place between November 2017 and October 2021 in the Department of Medical Oncology of Qilu Hospital (Jinan, China). Patients with other neoplastic diseases, active or chronic infections, or connective tissue diseases were excluded. All patients met the pathological diagnosis of BC and had not received any kind of chemotherapeutic regimen or radiotherapy. Thirty normal women were included as healthy controls (age range, 35–60 years; median age, 46 years).

Flow cytometric analysis

Heparinized peripheral blood from BC patients and the healthy controls were collected into heparin‐anticoagulant vacutainer tubes. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated by red blood cell lysis buffer (Bio Gems International Inc.) according to the manufacturer's instructions. An appropriate amount of red blood cell lysis buffer was added to heparin‐anti coagulated peripheral whole blood and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. Th22, Th17, Th1 and Th2 cells were defined as CD3+CD8−IFN‐γ−IL‐17−IL‐22+, CD3+CD8−IFN‐γ−IL‐17+IL‐22−, CD3+CD8−IFN‐γ+ and CD4+IFN‐γ−IL‐4+cells, respectively.

Intracellular cytokines were detected by flow cytometry to reflect cytokine‐producing cells. In short, 400 μL of PBMC suspension was cultured with an equal volume of Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)‐1640 medium for 4 h at 37°C in 5% CO2 under the coexisting condition of 25 ng/mL of phorbol myristate acetate (PMA), 1.7 mg/mL of monensin, and 1mg/mL of ionomycin (all from Alexis Biochemicals).

Monensin was used to inhibit intracellular transport mechanisms and lead to the accumulation of cytokines in cells. PMA and ionomycin are pharmacological T cell activators that mimic the signals produced by the T cell receptor (TCR) complex, with the advantage of stimulating any antigen‐specific T cells. After incubation for 20 min at room temperature in the dark with APC‐Cy7‐conjugated CD3 and PE‐Cy7‐conjugated CD8 monoclonal antibodies (for Th2 subset detection, PE‐Cy7‐conjugated CD4 monoclonal antibodies were applied in this step), the cells were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with PE‐conjugated anti‐IL‐17 monoclonal antibodies, FITC‐conjugated anti‐IFN‐γ monoclonal antibodies, and APC‐conjugated anti‐IL‐22 monoclonal antibodies (for Th2 subset detection, FITC‐conjugated anti‐IFN‐γ monoclonal antibodies, and PE‐conjugated IL‐4 monoclonal antibodies were used in this step). Isotype controls were used to ensure correct compensation and to confirm the specificity of the antibody. All fluorophore‐conjugated antibodies were purchased from Thermofisher. Stained cells were analyzed by flow cytometry using a FACS Calibur cytometer equipped with CellQuest software (BD Bioscience PharMingen).

Quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction analysis

Quantitative real‐time PCR was done for testing AHR and RORC gene expression in BC patients and the control group. Total RNA was extracted using RNA‐Quick Purification Kits (Feijie Biotech). Reverse transcription was performed with 1 μg of total RNA using Fast All‐in‐One RT kits at 42°C for 15 min then 5 s at 85°C for one cycle. Real‐time PCR was conducted using Applied Biosystems 7500 Real‐Time PCR System according to the manufacturer's instructions. Each reaction (20 uL samples) was carried out under the following cycling conditions: Initialization for 5 min at 95°C and then 40 cycles of amplification, with 15 s at 95°C for denaturation and 1 min at 60°C for annealing and elongation. The relative quantity of target mRNA expression was calculated using the quantification cycle (Cq) method using the equation: Relative quantity=2−∆∆CT. β‐actin transcripts were used as an internal control. The primers for human RORC, AHR, and β‐actin were as follows: RORC: forward 5′‐TTT TCC GAG GAT GAG ATT GC‐3′ and reverse, 5′‐CTT TCC ACA TGC TGG CTA CA‐3′; AHR: forward 5′‐CAA ATC CTT CCA AGC GGC ATA‐3′ and reverse, 5′‐CGC TGA GCC TAA GAA CTG AAA G‐3′; β‐actin: forward 5′‐CCT TCC TGG GCA TGG AGT CCT G‐3′ and reverse, 5′‐GGA GCA ATG ATC TTG ATC TTC‐3′. The results of the targets were expressed relative to β‐actin transcripts as the internal control. All standards and samples were conducted in triplicate.

Enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay

Peripheral blood samples were placed into heparin‐anticoagulant vacutainer tubes. Plasma was obtained by centrifugation and stored at −80°C for determining the concentration of cytokine IL‐22. Human IL‐22 platinum ELISA kit (eBioscience) was used to test IL‐22 concentration. Plasma cytokine concentrations were determined using the enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay in line with the manufacturer's instructions (a minimum detection limit of 9 pg/mL, eBioscience).

Statistical analysis

All data were subjected to normality testing, and a t test was used for those that conformed to the normal distribution data, and the results are expressed as mean ± SEM. Nonparametric testing was used for data with non‐normal distribution, and these results are expressed as median interquartile range. Spearman's test was used for correlation analysis. The cumulative survival time was analyzed with the use of the Kaplan‐Meier method, and the log‐rank test was used to compare group differences. All statistical tests were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 6.0). Results with p‐values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Decreased percentage of circulating Th1 cells in BC patients

The data of Th1 did not conform to a normal distribution, and therefore a nonparametric test was used. The proportion of Th1 cells in the peripheral blood of BC patients (median 8.60%, interquartile range 5.715%, n = 36), was significantly lower than that of the healthy controls (median 10.83%, interquartile range 7.31%, n = 23; *p < 0.05; Figure 4a). A significant negative correlation was found between Th1 cells and Ki‐67 status in BC patients (r = −0.3851, *p = 0.0430; n = 28; Figure 4e). Also, no significant difference of Th1 was found between BC patients with different hormone receptor statuses (Figure 4b,c), with positive or negative HER2 status (Figure 4d). In addition, the status of P53 of BC patients did not have a significant relationship with Th1 cells percentage (Figure 4f). No significant difference in the Th1 subset was found between tumor‐bearing or nontumor bearing BC patients (Figure 4g).

Prognostic value of circulating Th22, Th17 and Th1 cells for the overall survival of BC patients

Based on follow‐up, we evaluated the relationship between percentage of circulating Th22, Th17, Th1, or Th2 cells and overall survival in BC patients. Compared to patients with low Th22 or Th17 or Th1 cell levels, the survival rate of patients with a higher percentage of Th22 (HR 1.683; 95% CI: 0.4878–5.845; p = 0.4115) or Th17 (HR 2.210; 95% CI: 0.3887–13.84; p = 0.3643) or Th1 (HR 3.893; 95% CI: 0.9514–13.03; p = 0.0633) cells showed a decreased trend (Figure 9a–c). BC patients with lower Th2 cell percentage levels showed a decreased trend (HR 0.6377; 95% CI: 0.1528–2.421; p = 0.3139) of survival rates compared to the high percentage Th2 cell group (Figure 9d). None of the survival data reached a statistically significant difference in BC patients.

DISCUSSION

T helper (Th) cells are characterized by different cytokine profiles used to define their subsets. Apart from a shift in the Th1/Th2 balance, T cell subsets (Th17 and Th22 cells) are often regarded as the principal instigator of immune disorder diseases. It was found that Th1, Th2, Th17, and Th22 have inhibitory or promoting effects in the occurrence and development of malignant tumors. 35 For example, an increased proportion of Th22 cells have been found in many malignant tumors except for BC. 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 Similarly, Th17 cells are increased in colorectal, liver, lung, and ovarian cancers. 20 , 21 , 33 , 39 In contrast, Th2 cells secrete IL‐4, IL‐5, IL‐6, IL‐10, and IL‐13. Among them, IL‐10 is a multipotent cytokine, which can inhibit T cell proliferation, macrophage ROS, inhibit the antitumor effect of Th1 cells, and so on. 27 Studies have shown that with the progression of the tumor, the differentiation change within T cell subsets takes place, the number of Th1 cells decreases, and the number of Th2 cells increases. 40 , 41 Breast cancer has many different molecular subtype states. Our study found an increased proportion of Th22, Th17, and Th2 cells in BC, a decreased Th1 frequency in the meanwhile by flow cytometry, which suggests that these cells may be involved in different molecular subtypes of BC. These discoveries all suggested Th22, Th17, Th1, Th2 cells may have a potential pathogenic role in BC patients.

We defined Th17 cells as CD4+IFNγ−IL‐17+IL‐22−cells. Th17 cells have both pro‐ and antitumor effects. It has previously been reported that mice with colon cancer MC38 cells subcutaneously inoculated with IL‐17 gene deficiency had faster tumor growth and more lung metastases. 33 However, most studies have shown that Th17 cells promote tumor development in colorectal, lung, and liver cancers. 20 , 21 , 33 Th22 cells are defined as CD4+ IFN‐γ−IL‐17−IL‐22+ cells. Many factors, such as IL‐1β, IL‐6, IL‐21, and IL‐23, can activate Th22 cells to produce IL‐22, and ROR‐γt and AHR are considered to be transcription factors for IL‐22 production by Th22 cells. 13 , 34 Studies have shown that IL‐22 itself does not appear to promote the occurrence of tumors. It promotes cell proliferation by regulating the expression of related genes through STAT3 mediated signal transduction. 28 In colorectal cancer, a similar phenomenon was found. 13 Our research showed that the percentage of circulating Th22 and Th17 cells in BC patients was significantly higher than that of the healthy controls. The results we observed suggested that Th22 and Th17 cells may be involved in T cell‐mediated immunity in BC patients. For all we know, there is little data on the effects of Th22 cells in BC patients. Our study indicated that Th22 cells might have potential pathogenic effects in BC patients.

IL‐22 is a member of the IL‐10 cytokine family and is secreted mainly by activated Th22 cells. The effect of IL‐22 in cancer progression has been observed in some kinds of epithelial cancers. When IL‐22 is generated by immune cells, it can act directly on tumor cells to stimulate tumor growth, aggressiveness, and treatment resistance. 42 Our research observed a significantly higher plasma IL‐22 level in BC patients than that of healthy controls. Furthermore, a significant positive correlation was observed between the plasma IL‐22 levels and Th22 cells in BC patients, but there was no significant correlation in the healthy controls. The positive correlation between the plasma level of IL‐22 and the percentage of Th22 cells in BC patients may be related to the fact that Th22 cells are an important subset of Th cells that produce IL‐22. It verified that the Th22 subset accounts for 37%–63% of the total amount of IL‐22 secreted by all IL‐22‐producing T cells. 43

HER2 status, status of hormone receptor, the positive rate of Ki‐67, and being P53 positive or not all have an important influence on BC patients' occurrence, development, and prognosis. Among those subtypes, estrogen receptor‐positive BC patients represent approximately two‐thirds of all BC patients. We further explored whether there was any correlation between the number of Th cells and different molecular subtypes of BC. HER2 gene, located on chromosome 17q12‐21, is the most frequently amplified gene in BC. HER2 can promote the proliferation of tumor cells and promote recurrence and metastasis. 44 The HER2 gene has been shown to induce CD4+ Th cell proliferation and cytokine production in patients. 45 Our research found Th22 and Th2 cells were more abundant in the peripheral blood of BC patients with positive HER2. Therefore, it is reasonable to speculate that the HER2 gene can promote Th22 and Th2 cells differentiation and produce tumor promotion immunity in breast cancer.

As one of the most widely detected immunohistochemistry (IHC) proliferation antigens, Ki‐67 has been proposed as a measure for the quantification of cell proliferation in BC samples. Generally, a high level of Ki‐67 expression in BC correlates strongly with more tenacious proliferation and poor prognosis of patients. Recent research shows that for TNBC patients, Ki‐67 > 30% was associated with a worse prognosis. 46 Ki67 is a popular and inexpensive biomarker widely used to measure and monitor tumor proliferation in BC. 47 Studies have shown that high expression of ki67 increases the risk of recurrence of BC patients by 64% and is an important independent prognostic predictor of early BC. 48 Our study showed that the proportion of Th1/Th2 was also found a decreased trend in BC patients than in the healthy controls. Further, a negative correlation between Th1 cells and Ki‐67 status with a positive correlation between Th2 cells and Ki‐67 status was found in BC patients. These results demonstrated that the immune‐suppressive effect is stronger in BC patients with positive HER2 expression and with a high Ki‐67 positive rate. Th1/Th2 cells imbalance in BC patients may play a particular role in the pathogenesis of BC and may be related to the malignancy of BC. The specific function of Th1 and Th2 cells in the tumor pathogenesis of BC patients needs further investigation.

P53 is the most frequently mutated gene in most types of human cancer, including BC. Overall, the gene has been reported to be mutated in 30%–35% of invasive primary BC. 49 Recent studies have shown that the loss of P53 in BC cells is strongly associated with cancer‐induced systemic neutrophilic inflammation. 50 In our study, no significant difference was found between Th cells and different P53 statuses. The results indicate that P53 does not play an important immune role in BC.

Tumor‐bearing refers to the number of cancer cells, the size of a tumor, or the amount of cancer in the body. Tumor‐bearing is also associated with cancer prognosis. We did not find a correlation between the number of Th cells and tumor‐bearing, so whether BC patients are tumor‐bearing or nontumor‐bearing, the cell immune status does not have a significant difference.

In our study, the correlation of Th22 percentage with breast cancer stages was found statistically significant, while no similar correlation was found in Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells with tumor stages. These data suggest that elevated circulating Th22 cells in breast cancer patients may be related to tumor development.

AHR is a crucial transcription factor in the differentiation of naive CD4+ T to Th22 subtypes. To a certain extent, its expression level can reflect the changes in the levels of Th22 cells. Therefore, we further analyzed the relative quantitative expression of AHR mRNA in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of BC patients. Our results showed that the expression of AHR mRNA in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of BC patients increased significantly compared with the control group. Combining the results of previous studies and our analysis, we speculate that in the peripheral blood of BC patients, AHR can induce naive CD4+ T cells to differentiate into Th22 cells and produce functional cytokine IL‐22, which plays a role in the pathogenesis of BC. The possible mechanisms are as follows: exogenous compounds, dietary and microbial‐derived AHR ligands are widely present in the body. After the corresponding ligands activate AHR, it can be transferred from the cytoplasm to the nucleus and combined with ARNT. Then, under the coordinated action of additional activators and transcription factors, the downstream corresponding target gene expression is initiated, and NF‐кB and ERK pathways are further activated to induce the secretion of IL‐8 and IL‐6, thereby enhancing the inflammatory response and inducing initial CD4+ T cells differentiate into Th22 cells and produce the functional cytokine IL‐22. 51 , 52 , 53

It is worth noting that AHR is a ligand‐dependent activation transcription factor. After binding to ligands widely present in the body, AHR can promote cell proliferation and inhibit cell apoptosis by initiating the expression of related downstream target genes. AHR activation and inflammatory cytokine signal transduction are standard features of tumors, which can synergistically induce various inflammatory factors, including IL‐6, to aggravate inflammation. At the same time, it can promote immunosuppressive regulatory T cells by increasing the expression of IL‐10 of differentiation. Thus, the production of systemic and tumor local AHR ligands and the enhancement of AHR expression may create an internal tumor environment that promotes inflammatory response and exhibits immunosuppressive effects, which is conducive to tumor cells evading immune surveillance and immune escape. 54 , 55

Based on the above analysis, we believe that Th22 cells may play a particular role in the pathogenesis of BC by secreting its functional cytokine, IL‐22, under the action of AHR. However, further studies are needed to confirm the specific mechanism and the relationship between Th22 and other CD4+ helper T cells in BC patients.

RORC is a major transcription factor that plays a vital role in guiding the Th17 lineage and regulating Th22 cell polarization. Our study also showed that the expression of RORC mRNA in the peripheral blood of BC patients was also significantly increased. Naive T cells and lymphoid cells can generate Th17 cells through a variety of pathways, including IL‐6 and transforming growth factor (TGF)‐β or IL‐23 stimulation. RORC is located in the nucleus of Th17 cells. To induce gene transcription, RORC binds to a coactivator complex, which then binds to a retinoic acid receptor‐associated orphan‐receptor response element (RORE) embedded in the DNA of Th17 cells. RORC‐induced gene transcription results in the expression of several proinflammatory cytokines, including IL‐17, IL‐22, and GM‐CSF. 56 Th22 and Th17 polarization require the transcription factors RORC and AHR. Similarly, RORC and AHR are also involved in the IL‐22 production process. 57 , 58 We observed a significant increase in both RORC and AHR expression in BC patients. Therefore, these associations may initiate Th22 and Th17 cells and promote an increased expression level of Th22 and Th17 cells in BC patients.

In summary, there is abnormally high expression of Th22, Th17 cells and cytokine IL‐22 in the peripheral blood of BC patients. Th22 cells may play a certain role in the pathogenesis of BC through its functional cytokine IL‐22 under the action of AHR. RORC plays a key role in the differentiation of Th17 cells in the peripheral blood of BC patients and regulates the differentiation of Th22 cells in BC. The number of Th22 cells in HER2(+) BC was significantly higher than that of HER2 (‐) BC patients so that the HER2 gene may promote Th22 cell differentiation and tumor growth. A shift of Th1 to Th2 cells in peripheral blood of BC patients may be related to immune dysfunction in BC patients. These results suggest that Th22 cells may play an essential role in the pathogenesis of BC through inflammatory reactions and other mechanisms and have a certain synergistic effect with Th17 cells. Th22 cells may have potential value in BC patients’ outcome prediction, providing clinical value. The in‐depth study of the specific mechanism of Th22, Th17, Th1, and Th2 cells in the pathogenesis of BC may hopefully provide a new idea for the treatment of BC patients in the future.

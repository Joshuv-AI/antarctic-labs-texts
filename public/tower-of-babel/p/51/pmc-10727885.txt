Symmetrical Longitudinal Erythronychia on Both Thumbs: A Case Report
Authors: Yu Xiao, Ping Tu, Yang Wang
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a clonal histiocytic disease characterized by the proliferation and accumulation of dendritic cells resembling epidermal Langerhans cells within various tissues. Although LCH develops in all ages, it is most common in 1- to 3-year-old children. Disease incidence is reported to be 2.6~8.9 per million children below the age of 15 years per year1.

An 11-year-old boy presented with symmetrical, asymptomatic nail abnormalities on both thumbs for one year. He noticed symmetrical longitudinal dark discoloration on both thumbnails one year ago. The discolored line expanded, and erythematous masses developed underneath the nail plates. He also noted a recurrent skin nodule on the penis. Upon physical examination, linear red mass of the nail plate traversing the lunula was observed on both thumbnails, with nail plate thickening and onycholysis (Fig. 1A). An ulcerative nodule with a 0.5 cm diameter scarring was observed on the penis (Fig. 1B). A skin biopsy of the ulcerative nodule revealed dense dermal infiltration of large histiocytes with reniform nuclei (Fig. 2A), which were positive for S100, CD1a, and Langerin staining in immunohistochemistry (Fig. 2B), consistent with LCH. No somatic BRAFV600E mutation was detected. As a further systemic review did not reveal multi-system involvement, a single-system cutaneous LCH was diagnosed. The patient received an intradermal injection of interferon alpha-2b (3 million IU) thrice per week. Both nail and skin lesions markedly regressed after 6 months without recurrence.

(B) Well-defined ulcerative nodule with a 0.5 cm diameter scarring on the root of the penis. We received the patient’s consent form about publishing all photographic materials.

LCH can infiltrate any organ, with a particular affinity for bone, skin, lungs, and pituitary. Accordingly, it presents as a spectrum of diseases ranging from indolent single-organ to fatal multi-system involvement. During diagnosis, comprehensive evaluations should be conducted to confirm disease extent and risk stratification. Patients in the multi-system group should be further evaluated on whether high-risk organs (bone marrow, liver, and spleen) are affected1. The pathogenesis of LCH has been associated with somatic activating mutations in the MAPK pathway since the discovery of the BRAFV600E mutation1, suggesting a neoplastic nature of LCH.

Nail involvement is exceedingly rare in LCH. Polydactylous longitudinal erythronychia is caused by the involvement of the distal matrix and hemorrhaging of vessels due to compression from the thinned plate present as red streaks2. The significance of nail involvement in patient prognosis is controversial. Although it is associated with multi-system involvement and poor prognosis, some studies have reported that patients with nail involvement show favorable prognoses, as in this case3,4. Therefore, whether nail change constitutes an independent indicator for prognosis remains to be elucidated.

Disease severity and risk level guide individualized treatment. For patients with unifocal single-organ lesions, local therapies are sufficient, whereas extensive and refractory conditions require systemic chemotherapy. Vinblastine-based regimens are recommended, and BRAF inhibitors should be considered in patients harboring the BRAFV600E mutation. Long-term follow-up is necessary, as half of the patients relapse within 5 years5. This case highlights the importance of recognizing uncommon clinical manifestations for systemic diseases and emphasizes nail involvement in LCH as it leads to early diagnosis and timely treatment.

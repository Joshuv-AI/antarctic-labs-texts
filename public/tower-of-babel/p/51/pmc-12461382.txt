Study on the Micromacro Cross-Scale Propagation Law of Hydraulic Fractures in Conglomerate Reservoirs
Authors: Jianxin Peng, Rui Huang, Ju Liu, Liao Wang, Dengfeng Ren, Zhanwei Yang, Jueyong Feng, Hao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The propagation of hydraulic fractures in conglomerate reservoirs is not only controlled by geological factors (geostress) and engineering factors (viscosity and volume of fracturing fluid) but also influenced by the strong heterogeneity of gravels. Based on the heterogeneity features of gravels in terms of “structure” and “material”, the sandy conglomerates of the Cretaceous Yagremu Formation in the Kuche Preclassic Kelasu Structure Belt were taken as the object of study, and a three-phase (gravel-sand-mud) composite rock skeleton model combining image analysis and modeling experiments was established to investigate the gravel-encountering behavior and mechanical response characteristics of hydraulic fractures under plane stress loading conditions and reveal the micromacro cross-scale propagation law of hydraulic fractures in conglomerate reservoirs. In addition, the heterogeneity indexes of “structure” and “material” were introduced to evaluate the main controlling factors affecting the propagation of hydraulic fractures in conglomerate reservoirs. The results show that (1) the difference between the gravels and matrix is the fundamental reason for the structural and material inhomogeneity of conglomerates; (2) higher gravel content and larger grain size significantly enhance the shielding effect of gravel, leading to a higher probability of the gravel-winding phenomenon of hydraulic fractures with increasing tortuosity; conversely, lower gravel content and smaller grain size effectively weaken the shielding effect of gravel, causing the gravel-penetrating phenomenon of hydraulic fractures with simple and flat shapes. The study helps predict the gravel-encountering extension behavior of hydraulic fractures during the fracturing of conglomerate reservoirs, thus providing theoretical guidance for the optimization of on-site fracturing design.

Introduction

Conglomerate reservoirs are usually deeply buried and are characterized by poor physical properties, low porosity and permeability, complex lithology, and strong inhomogeneity. Compared with the sedimentation process of homogeneous fine-grained reservoirs such as sandstone and shale, the hydrodynamic conditions in the sedimentation process of gravel rock are more complex, and the rock skeleton mainly consists of gravels and a heterogeneous matrix, and the matrix is usually mixed with a large number of gravel particles of different sizes (usually larger than 2 mm). − There is a significant difference in mechanical properties between gravels and the matrix, and the spatial distribution of gravel particles in the rock lacks regularity, resulting in strong heterogeneity of the conglomerates.

The strong heterogeneity characteristics of conglomerates are characterized by two main aspects: “structural” and “material”. − The “structural” inhomogeneity of conglomerates usually emphasizes the influence of the spatial distribution of gravels within the rock mass, which is also called “geometric” inhomogeneity. The rock skeletons of gravel rock mainly include four types from high to low gravel content: particles-supporting, multistage particles-supporting, particles-heterobase-supporting, and heterobase-supporting. The contact modes between gravels are correspondingly manifested as concave-to-convex contact, line-to-line contact, point-to-line contact, and point-to-point contact. The “material” inhomogeneity of gravel rock mainly highlights the differences in mechanical properties between gravel and the heterogeneous matrix, which is also called “mechanical” inhomogeneity. With the increase of the gravel content, the overall elastic modulus and compressive strength of the gravel rock increase significantly. In addition, the degree of cementation of the heterogeneous matrix also affects the overall compressive strength, plasticity, and brittleness characteristics of the conglomerate, and the tighter the matrix cementation, the greater the elastic modulus and compressive strength of the conglomerate. In addition, the calcium cementation is conducive to the enhancement of the compressive strength of the conglomerate, while the mud cementation weakens the compressive strength of the conglomerate. −

The strong heterogeneity of conglomerates not only affects the macroscopic mechanical response of conglomerates but also leads to the microscopic failure mechanism of conglomerates, which is different from other homogeneous fine-grained reservoirs. − Scholars at home and abroad have carried out a large number of studies on the failure modes of gravel reservoirs and the propagation laws of hydraulic fractures through the combination of physical modeling experiments and numerical simulations. −

The rupture of gravel rock is accompanied by the behaviors of penetrating gravels, winding gravels, and stopping cracking when encountering gravels, and multiple irregular fractures are formed. In terms of the fracture process, the plasticity and brittleness of the conglomerate are related to its gravel content. The higher the gravel content, the more significant the gravel-winding behavior, and the gravel-winding fractures indicate that the rock has undergone plastic failure. − The propagation law of hydraulic fractures in gravel reservoirs is complex, which is shown as follows: (1) hydraulic fractures mainly propagate along the cemented surface between gravels and heterogeneous matrix, and in a few cases, they undergo behaviors such as penetrating gravels, terminating, and being adsorbed by gravels; (2) the gravel-winding and gravel-penetrating behaviors are related to the mechanical properties of gravels and heterogeneous base, and the greater the difference between the two, the easier the fracture is to propagate around the gravels; (3) the higher the gravel content and the larger the size, the more complex the fracture morphology; (4) the direction of hydraulic fracture propagation in gravel reservoirs is related to the geostress, and the greater the difference in geostress, the stronger the control effect of geostress on hydraulic fractures. −

The current study still has the following shortcomings. First, it has not fully considered the geometrical and mechanical heterogeneity of conglomerates, i.e., the differences between gravels and matrix in terms of “structure” and “material”. Second, it has not sufficiently considered the microscopic rupture law of conglomerates, i.e., the influence of the spatial distribution of gravel particles within the rock mass. , Therefore, the macroscopic mechanical response and microscopic damage mechanism of gravel rocks need to be studied in depth. In this paper, based on the heterogeneity characteristics of conglomerates in terms of “structure” and “material”, the sandy conglomerates of the Cretaceous Yagremu Formation (hereinafter referred to as K1y ) in the Kuche Preclassic Kelasu Structure Belt were taken as the research object. Combining the image analysis and physical modeling experiments, a gravel–sand–mud three-phase composite rock skeleton model was established to explore the gravel-encountering extension behavior and mechanical response characteristics of hydraulic fractures under plane stress loading conditions, so as to reveal the micromacro cross-scale expansion law of hydraulic fractures in conglomerate reservoirs. At the same time, the main controlling factors affecting the expansion of hydraulic fractures in gravel reservoirs were evaluated, providing theoretical guidance for the optimization of the hydraulic fracturing design of conglomerate reservoirs.

Conglomerate rocks are mainly composed of gravels and matrix, so the difference in the properties between the gravels and the heterogeneous matrix is the fundamental cause of the inhomogeneity of conglomerates. Inhomogeneity mainly includes “structural” inhomogeneity and “material” inhomogeneity; in this case, the study of the spatial distribution characteristics of gravels and rock mechanical parameters is a prerequisite for evaluating the geometrical and mechanical heterogeneity of conglomerates and for analyzing the micromacro cross-scale damage mechanism of conglomerates.

The “Material” Inhomogeneity

The “material” inhomogeneity of conglomerates mainly highlights the differences in the mechanical properties between the gravels and the matrix. Theoretically, as a mixture of gravels and matrix, the mechanical properties of the conglomerate are affected by three aspects: gravels, matrix, and the cementation surfaces, of which the main components of the matrix are sand and mud. However, there is still a lack of accurate measurement methods for the cementation strength of the surfaces of conglomerates, which can be ignored. As shown in Figure , uniaxial compression experiments were conducted on the downhole cores of pure conglomerate, gravel-bearing sandstone, and pure sandstone of the K1y in order to obtain the different mechanical parameters.

Uniaxial compression experiment core pictures.

As shown in Table , the mechanical parameters (Young’s modulus, compressive strength) of the conglomerate-bearing sandstones are similar to those of the pure sandstones but differ significantly from those of pure gravels, which means there is a large difference in the mechanical properties of sand and gravel. Compared with pure gravels, the compressive strength of both the conglomerate-bearing sandstones and the pure sandstones is increased by about 30%, and thus, the hardness of the conglomerates is significantly lower than that of the matrix. The Young modulus of both gravel-bearing sandstones and pure sandstones decreases by about 20% compared with pure gravels, and the overall mechanical characteristics of conglomerate-bearing sandstones tend to lean toward pure sandstones.

Numerical Simulation of Hydraulic Fracture Propagation

Under the external loads, the stress concentration occurs inside the gravel rock, resulting in small deformation or dislocation between the gravels or between the gravel and the contact surface of the matrix, thus forming a local microcrack cluster. When the stress exceeds the compressive strength of the gravel rock, the microcracks continue to propagate and connect to form fractures, which are macroscopically manifested in the brittle failure of the gravel rock. However, due to the interference of gravel, the expansion shape of fractures deviates, thereby increasing the tortuosity of fractures. Therefore, by studying the stress distribution state of inhomogeneous gravel rock under external loading conditions, the microdeformation and failure laws of gravel rock can be revealed, thus characterizing the potential propagation path of hydraulic fractures. −

The Three-Phase Rock Skeleton Model

As shown in Figure , the pseudo-triaxial loading mode of the core is simplified to the plane stress loading mode. Assuming that the heterogeneous matrix mainly includes 2 types of sandy and muddy materials, the COMSOL finite element software is used to establish the rock skeleton model of the gravel–sand–mud three-phase composite material. The additional assumptions of the model are as follows: (1) the particles of the 3 types of materials are treated as microscopic units with the same shape and size but different mechanical properties; (2) units are homogeneous linear elastic materials and in close contact with each other, ignoring the influence of cementation; (3) micropores and microfractures formed by diagenesis and tectogenesis are ignored.

The Gravel-Encountering Extension Behavior of Hydraulic Fractures

As shown in Figure , under the condition that the mechanical properties of gravels and heterogeneous matrix differ greatly (more than 1.5 times), the propagation forms of hydraulic fractures in gravel outcrops after loading are mainly related to the contact relationship between gravels, which can be specifically divided into five categories: (1) if the gravels are dispersed and have no contact with each other, the hydraulic fractures mainly propagate around the gravels, forming “gravel-edge fractures”, and the larger the gravel size, the greater the tortuosity of the “gravel-edge fractures”; (2) if the gravels are in point-to-point contact, i.e., the gravel particles are very small, the hydraulic fractures propagate directly through the contact between the gravels and extend forward, so the fracture patterns are not deflected; (3) if the gravels are in point-to-line contact, i.e., there is a large difference in gravel size, the hydraulic fractures tend to be deflected around the edges of the large-sized gravels in the expansion process; (4) if the gravels are in line-to-line contact, i.e., the contact surfaces between the gravels are relatively flat, the hydraulic fractures can extend along the edges of the adjacent gravels to form secondary “branch fractures” in the process of expanding around the gravels; (5) if the gravels are in concave-to-convex contact, i.e., the gravels differ greatly in size, and the angle between the hydraulic fracture and the contact surface exceeds 60°, the hydraulic fracture will stop propagating or continue to expand along the outer edges of the gravels. In general, under the condition of large differences in mechanical properties between gravels and heterogeneous matrix, the hydraulic fractures tend to penetrate the contact points between gravels and continue to propagate while deviating around the gravels along the contact surface between gravels.

The Main Factors Affecting Hydraulic Fracture Propagation

The conglomerate reservoirs in the Cretaceous Yagremu Formation are typically buried at great depths (>5000 m), with high temperatures (130–150 °C) and high pressures (80–100 MPa), so the extension forms of hydraulic fractures are mainly controlled by the geostress, and the greater the difference in geostress, the stronger the control of the geostress on fractures. In addition to being controlled by geological factors (geostress) and engineering factors (fracturing fluid viscosity and displacement), the local extension patterns of hydraulic fractures are also affected by the structure (content and grain size) inhomogeneity and material (elastic modulus and strength) inhomogeneity of gravel rock. Therefore, COMSOL finite element software was used to analyze the stress distribution of three types of microelements in gravel rock under different factors after loading, in order to reveal the main controlling factors affecting the extension patterns of hydraulic fractures in gravel rock.

Impact of “Structural” Inhomogeneity

The Influence of Gravel Content

The distribution of stress concentration areas within the model after loading determines the potential propagation paths of the hydraulic fractures. As shown in Figures and , the potential extension paths of fractures after loading of the rock skeleton model are consistent with those of fractures after loading of the gravel outcrops. Hydraulic fractures can penetrate the gravels, bypass the gravels, and stop extending when encountering gravels during the propagation process, which is a verification of the reasonableness of the rock skeleton model.

Potential propagation paths of fractures with different gravel contents.

As shown in Figure , with the increase of gravel content, the number of stress concentration areas after loading of the model continues to increase, and the stress values continue to increase; i.e., the potential rupture points of the hydraulic fractures increase. The hydraulic fractures change from 4 short and straight fractures to 3 long and curved fractures, the propagation paths of hydraulic fractures increase obviously, and the degree of tortuosity also increases significantly.

As shown in Figure , with the increase of gravel content from 20% to 80%, the maximum internal stress slowly increases from 83 to 87.7 MPa, the structural inhomogeneity index of gravel decreases from 0.4 to 0.1, and the material inhomogeneity index remains at 0.22. It shows that the gravel content has a positive correlation with the internal stress, and a negative correlation with the structural inhomogeneity index of gravel rock.

Internal stress and inhomogeneity indexes with different gravel contents.

The Influence of Gravel Particle Size

As shown in Figure , with the increase of the gravel particle size, the number of stress concentration areas after loading of the rock skeleton model remains unchanged, and the stress values are not significantly different. However, the control range of a single stress concentration area expands obviously, the hydraulic fractures increase from two long curvilinear cracks to three long curvilinear cracks, and the tortuosity of the hydraulic fractures increases.

Potential propagation paths of fractures with different gravel particle sizes.

As shown in Figure , with the gravel particle size increasing to twice the sand particle size, the maximum internal stress remains at 84 MPa, while the structural inhomogeneity index decreases from 0.3 to 0.15, and the material inhomogeneity index remains at 0.22. It shows that the gravel particle size is negatively correlated with the structural inhomogeneity index, and there is no obvious relationship between gravel particle size and internal stress.

Internal stress and inhomogeneity indexes with different gravel particle sizes.

Impact of “Material” Inhomogeneity

As shown in Figure , the relationship between the extension pattern of hydraulic fractures and the gravel strength (elastic modulus and compressive strength) can be divided into 3 stages: (1) when the gravel strength is 0.3–0.9 times that of sand, with the increase of gravel strength, the number of stress concentration areas after loading of the model continues to increase. The hydraulic fractures gradually increase from a long and curved fracture to 4 long and curved fractures, and the degree of tortuosity of the hydraulic fractures shows a tendency of first increasing and then decreasing; (2) when the gravel strength is 0.9–1.2 times that of sand, i.e., when the strength difference between the two is not significant, the number of stress concentration areas after loading reaches the maximum, and propagation paths of hydraulic fractures also reaches the maximum, and the propagation form of hydraulic fractures is relatively simple; (3) when the gravel strength is 1.2–1.8 times that of sand, with the increase of the gravel strength, the number of stress concentration areas after loading decreases continuously, the hydraulic fractures gradually decrease from 4 long and curved fractures to 2 long and curved fractures, and the degree of tortuosity of hydraulic fractures shows a slow increasing trend.

Potential propagation paths of fractures with different gravel strengths.

With the increase of gravel strength, the propagation of hydraulic fractures in three stages indicates that the “material” inhomogeneity of gravel rock has a greater impact on the propagation paths of hydraulic fractures. (1) The increasing gravel strength not only enhances the ability to resist deformation but also exacerbates the concentration of shear stress at the contact surface between gravels or gravels and matrix. Hydraulic fractures tend to turn along the edge of the gravel, leading to local bending of the main fracture; (2) with the increase of gravel strength, the behavior of hydraulic fractures encountering gravels changes from surrounding gravels to penetrating gravels and then to surrounding gravels. When the strength difference between gravels and the matrix is significant, the stress field at the front end of hydraulic fractures is greatly shielded by gravels, resulting in local cancellation of the maximum horizontal principal stress. Hydraulic fractures tend to stop extending or propagating around gravels, and the greater the strength difference between gravels and matrix, the stronger the shielding effect of gravels on the stress field and the greater the tortuosity of hydraulic fractures. When the strength of the gravels and matrix is similar, the shielding effect of gravels on the stress field can be ignored, and hydraulic fractures are prone to penetrating gravels.

As shown in Figure , as the gravel strength increases from 0.3 to 1.8 times that of sand, the maximum internal stress increases from 84.2 to 120 MPa, the structural inhomogeneity index of gravel rock remains at 0.3, and the material inhomogeneity index decreases from 0.7 to 0.1 and then increases to 0.8, which indicates that the gravel strength is positively correlated with the internal stress. In addition, when the gravel strength is lower than the sand strength, the gravel strength is negatively correlated with the material inhomogeneity index. However, when the gravel strength is higher than the sand strength, the gravel strength is positively correlated with the material inhomogeneity index of the gravel rock.

Conclusions

(1)The fundamental reason for the macroscopic brittle failure of gravel rock is the stress concentration phenomenon inside the rock skeleton, which leads to microdeformation or displacement between gravels or between gravels and matrix contact surfaces, thus forming local microcracks. When the stress exceeds the compressive strength of gravel rock, microcracks continue to propagate and connect to form fractures.(2)Under the condition of significant differences in mechanical properties between gravels and matrix, hydraulic fractures are prone to penetrate the contact points between gravels and continue to extend or deviate around the gravels along the contact surface between gravels; the higher the gravel content, or the larger the grain size, the more obvious the hydraulic fracture behavior around the gravels, and the higher the degree of tortuosity.(3)With the increase of gravel strength, the gravel-encountering behavior of hydraulic fractures changes from gravel-winding to gravel-penetrating and then back to gravel-winding. When the strength of gravel is similar to that of the matrix, hydraulic fractures are prone to gravel-penetrating behavior. When the strength difference between the gravels and the matrix is significant, the shielding effect of the gravels on the stress field is also significant, and hydraulic fractures are prone to stopping or gravel-winding behavior.(4)Structural and material inhomogeneity indexes are introduced to quantitatively characterize the heterogeneity of gravel rock in terms of structure and material properties. Gravel content and particle size are negatively correlated with the structural inhomogeneity index. In addition, the gravel strength is negatively correlated with the material inhomogeneity index when the gravel strength is lower than the sand strength; conversely, the gravel strength is positively correlated with the material inhomogeneity index when the gravel strength is higher than the sand strength.(5)The shielding effect of gravel is closely related to gravel content and particle size. Based on the analysis of gravel images of core samples, parameters such as gravel content and particle size can be determined. This enables the prediction of gravel-encountering propagation behavior of hydraulic fractures during the fracturing process of gravel reservoirs, thereby providing theoretical guidance for optimizing parameters such as fluid pouring volume, sand supply volume, and injection rate in on-site fracturing design.

Location method for emergency rescue node on expressways based on spatio-temporal characteristics of vehicle operation
Authors: Xinghua Hu, Zhouzuo Wang, Jiahao Zhao, Ran Wang, Hao Lei, Wei Liu, Bing Long
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractExpressway networks are continuously developing and emergency rescue demand is increasing proportionately. The location of expressway emergency rescue nodes needs refinement to meet changing requirements. In this study, the expressway was modeled as an expressway network. The differences in the origin destination (OD) distribution matrices for working days and major holidays were used as the bases for determining the need for temporary emergency rescue nodes. Overlapping and non-overlapping community detection algorithms were used to extract the distribution characteristics of OD during both day categories. These distributions were used to determine permanent and temporary emergency rescue sites. In this study, we considered the differences in traffic volume, distance, and impact of four vehicle types on traffic accidents to select the location of emergency rescue nodes, and allocate emergency resources. An emergency rescue node selection model for an expressway network was established based on spatio-temporal characteristics. The results based on a regional example determined that 22 permanent and 25 temporary emergency rescue nodes were appropriate. The average rescue time for traffic accidents during working days and major holidays compared to the P-center location model, was reduced by approximately 27.08% and 6.70%, respectively. The coefficient of variation of emergency rescue time was reduced by approximately 28.22% and 21.41%, respectively. The results indicated that the model satisfied the expressway emergency rescue demand requirements, and improved the rationality of the rescue center node layout.Keywords: Expressway, Community detection, OD data, Emergency rescue, Node locationSubject terms: Engineering, Applied mathematics

Introduction

The demand for expressways has increased with an increase in the number of motor vehicles. The frequency and impact of expressway traffic events, including traffic accidents, vehicle breakdowns, and geological disasters, are increasing with the continuous expansion of expressway networks. The impacts of traffic incidents on expressways include personal injury and death, property damage, and traffic congestion. Therefore, the demand for effective emergency rescue services on expressways continues to increase. Heavy traffic flow and high speeds on expressways increase the probability of traffic accidents occurring and with more severe consequences. Owing to the characteristics of expressway traffic accidents, emergency rescue on expressways is challenging. Expressways are usually designed to have expressway emergency rescue nodes that meet expected emergency rescue requirements. The location of these rescue nodes is a key factor in improving the operational efficiency of expressways. The expressway emergency rescue node is where emergency services are located that are needed to minimize casualties, property losses, and to restore traffic flow following a traffic incident on an expressway. These nodes are usually located at the entrance to and exit from the expressway and are equipped with the necessary personnel, vehicles, facilities, and equipment. In this study, emergency rescue efforts for expressway traffic accidents are the primary consideration. The expressway emergency rescue node location is the most important aspect of high-speed emergency rescue. An effective emergency rescue node layout minimizes the response times of rescue teams, and therefore minimizes casualties and property losses. The expressway emergency response node location problem is a facility location–allocation problem (FLAP), which refers to the search for an optimal strategy for facility location and demand allocation and is widely used in the logistics, industrial, and transportation industries.

For the FLAP, early studies proposed classical location models such as the p-center, p-median, location set covering problem (LCSP), and maximum covering location problem (MCLP) location models1–4. Classical location models have emerged as special cases of generalized facility location and design problems. With the development of cities, classical location models no longer satisfy the requirements for facility location, and improved classical location models have been proposed to replace them. For example, the traditional MCLP model focuses on a single target to meet the multi-objective needs of location. The traditional MCLP model has been improved with a location model constructed by combining the location needs of different facilities, to improve the practicability and validity of the classical location model5–7. Wang and Zhou8 proposed a near-full-coverage model that balances facility investment and actual coverage, and successfully applied the model to the optimal location of railroad emergency rescue nodes in multiple scenarios.

The applicability of the same location model to different types of facilities varies due to the different facilities. Many corresponding location models have been constructed for different types of facilities. For example, the location of competitive facilities such as shopping malls, restaurants, and hotels needs to consider the location of competitors and competitive rival relationships. These studies typically focus on decentralized decision-making on the supply side while simplifying demand-side modeling9. For a competitive FLAP, there have been studies that have considered the influence of competitors around the location area. A generic framework was constructed with a new methodology to simultaneously optimize the location and design decisions for a set of facilities, thereby improving the relevance of traditional location models10. Improving the corporate supply chain is one of the main methods used to increase the competitiveness of companies in the marketplace and improve the efficiency of corporate material supply. The multi-objective and multi-factor facility location problem has been addressed in several studies by using different methods and algorithms to determine the number of facilities required for each period, their location, and the distribution of materials between them11,12.With the increased attention of people to environmental issues, green facility location has become a key object of research. For example, the charging demand distribution of urban new-energy vehicles have been analyzed and models built to determine optimal locations for new-energy vehicle charging nodes. The corresponding path planning reduced the cost of urban new-energy vehicle detours and reduced carbon dioxide emissions in the city13,14. Bike-sharing systems (BSSs) have also been emphasized in urban transportation systems. A BSS is an intelligent bike rental system that allows urban residents to pay to rent bikes for short periods and short-distance trips. BSSs play an important role in reducing carbon emissions in cities, decreasing traffic congestion, and improving the fitness of residents15. For the BSS location problem, many studies have considered the use of hybrid methods for location, using geographic information systems (GISs), analytical hierarchy processes (AHPs), Vise Kriterijumska Optimizacija I Kompromisno Resenje (VIKOR), and other methods to construct a hybrid location decision model16,17. Wang and Ma18 developed a dual-objective mixed-integer nonlinear programming model with the objectives of minimizing emergency resuscitation time and maximizing satisfaction with emergency material needs. They designed genetic algorithms to solve the emergency location and distribution model to obtain the Pareto-optimal solution set of the model. To solve the post-disaster emergency rescue problem, a facility node location-allocation model has been established in some studies to minimize the number of post-disaster casualties and property damage, and to improve the efficiency of the emergency rescue and capacity of the facility19–21. Yu22 proposed a two-phase pre-disaster location and storage model to protect against natural disasters such as floods and fires. The first stage of the model determines the location of the warehouse and the storage-level emergency resources. The second stage uses stochastic and robust optimizations to cope with the stochasticity of the affected area and the uncertainty of the disaster intensity, respectively. Linking the damage intensity of the disaster with partial capacity loss emergency facilities, the reliability of the facilities was considered in the framework of robust optimization.

In summary, the current research progress on the location of expressway emergency rescue facilities has the following characteristics: (1) there are relatively few previous studies that have investigated the location of expressway emergency rescue nodes, focusing on the depth of the need to be strengthened; (2) Existing research is primarily focused on rescue coverage and rescue efficiency, with limited applications available to assess facility point demand using a data-driven approach; (3) previous studies do not consider the organic combination of permanent and temporary facilities, which makes the construction of facilities costly and leads to a waste of resources.

In this paper, origin (O) and destination (D) are indicated as toll booths for entering and exiting the expressway, respectively. This study intends to adopt a data-driven approach based on expressway toll record data and expressway vehicle traffic origin and destination (OD) distribution data. It uses overlapping and non-overlapping community detection algorithms to deeply excavate the OD distribution characteristics of expressways for both working days and major holidays. Full consideration is given to the distribution characteristics of expressway emergency demand to construct the location model for expressway network emergency rescue nodes. It is driven by the operational spatio-temporal characteristics and aims to improve the efficiency of expressway emergency rescue and minimize casualties, property losses, and traffic congestion.

Solutions

Based on expressway toll data, this paper uses data mining to analyze the distribution characteristics of emergency rescue demand on the expressway from the perspectives of working days and major holidays. Based on this, we established a data-driven site-selection model for expressway emergency rescue nodes. The location model mainly focuses on expressway resident rescue site and temporary rescue site, when the traffic distribution characteristics of urban expressways on working days and major holidays differ greatly, permanent rescue nodes are unable to meet the demand for rescue on holidays, and temporary rescue nodes need to be located. Firstly, vehicle origin–destination (OD) data and expressway toll booth data are used to construct the expressway network. Graph theory is then applied to analyze the characteristics of vehicle OD distribution on both working days and major holidays. Based on knowledge of the network, the node weights of each partition were calculated, the toll station with the largest weight was selected as a permanent emergency rescue site of each partition, and the rescue node configurations matching the emergency rescue demand of each partition were established. Finally, an expressway emergency rescue demand node–rescue node matching system was constructed to improve the efficiency of expressway emergency rescue. Methodology

Expressway network community structure

The expressway network consists of nodes and edges. The objects in the network are nodes, and an edge defines the connection between nodes. Nodes are aggregated in a network, where nodes with similar characteristics are clustered together to form a community. The density of connecting edges between nodes within the same community is high, and the density of connecting edges between nodes in different communities is relatively low. Community detection is the process of revealing the structure of such communities in networks. Community detection offers a method similar to data dimensionality reduction. It clusters nodes into different clusters (communities) based on the topological relationships between network nodes. This approach divides the entire network, transforming the problem into a study and analysis of each cluster (community) while ensuring that the characteristics of the nodes within each cluster are similar. The partition results can be classified into non-overlapping community detection and overlapping community detection, as shown in Fig. 2. Community detection algorithms reveal the community structure of such expressway networks. Only one permanent rescue node in a non-overlapping community is responsible for handling all incidents in the subdistrict in which it is located, which is suitable for low-density incidents; temporary rescue nodes in an overlapping community belong to several partitions at the same time, and can serve the emergency rescue of different partitions, which is suitable for high-density situations. The non-overlapping community detection algorithm is suitable for rescue scenarios where the number of accidents or the need for emergency rescue is low, the time window required for emergency rescue is not very demanding (long time window), and the accidents are concentrated. The overlapping community detection algorithm is suitable for rescue scenarios where the time window required for emergency rescue is short and the accidents are disseminated. In some cities, the difference in expressway traffic between working days and major holidays is large. For these cities, temporary rescue nodes must be arranged during major holidays based on permanent rescue nodes. Therefore, non-overlapping and overlapping communities of expressways were classified as the basis for the locations of permanent and temporary rescue nodes, respectively. Permanent rescue partitioning

Non-overlapping community partitioning is the basis for location permanent rescue nodes. Non-overlapping community detection algorithms partition an expressway network into several disjoint sets of nodes, where each node can only belong to one set. Non-overlapping community detection mainly includes algorithms based on hierarchical clustering, objective function optimization, and random walk. In this study, we adopted an overlapping community detection algorithm based on label propagation. This method was first applied to the classification of data introduced by Raghavan to delineate the community structure using the label propagation algorithm (LPA)23. Temporary rescue node locating for multi-point response under small time window

Temporary rescue partitioning

Temporary rescue node locations are required if there is a large difference between the working days and major holiday OD matrices. Overlapping community partition is the basis for selecting temporary rescue node locations. In real-world networks, there may be many overlapping nodes, that is, a node belongs to multiple communities simultaneously, and there may be an overlap between communities, for example, a person can belong to different organizations such as family and work units simultaneously. Overlapping community detection algorithms include those based on regimen filtering, label propagation, and local extensions. Traditional label-propagation-based community detection algorithms can only be used for non-overlapping community detection. Non-overlapping community partition cannot satisfy the rescue scenarios of short time windows and accident emanation required for emergency rescue on major holidays. Therefore, this study adopted the Speaker–Listener Label propagation Algorithm (SLPA), which is an extension of the LPA and can be used for overlapping community detection25. Study case discussion

Overview of data

To construct an expressway network, this study needs to obtain three types of data: working day expressway toll station charging record data, major holiday expressway toll station charging record data, and expressway toll station-related spatial data. The toll data mainly included the names of the entry and exit toll stations and the corresponding toll dates; each data point represents a vehicle travel record. The spatial data of expressway toll stations can be obtained from open-source websites. The data collection date of toll collection records of expressways during working days was from December 1, 2020, to December 31, 2020, and the data collection time was 24 h a day, with a total of 866,943,431 original records and a total of 866,757,557 valid data points after deletion of abnormal data, completion of missing data, and other pre-processing. The data collection date of the toll collection records of expressway toll booths during heavy traffic hours was January 27, 2023 (Chinese New Year), May 3, 2023 (Labor Day), and October 6, 2023 (Chinese National Day), and the data collection time was 24 h a day, with a total of 2,589,227 original records, and a total of 2,532,925 valid data after deleting abnormal data, completing the pre-processing of missing data and other data. In this study, the traffic flow data were divided into four types of vehicles, minibuses, buses, minivans, and large trucks according to the model. Based on the entropy weighting method, we obtained the probability weights of traffic accidents for these four types of vehicles (0.575649588, 0.17348651, 0.1712603, and 0.079603602, respectively). The weights of the accident intensities were 0.495719263, 0.291609631, 0.164554816, and 0.048116291, respectively. The weights were used to merge the traffic flow data. The working day and major holiday OD distribution is shown in Fig. 5, and the OD distribution test yielded a Euclidean distance percentage PAB of approximately 0.48, and a Pearson correlation coefficient of approximately 0.42. This proves that compared with working days, the OD flow for major holidays was large, and the difference in the OD distribution was high. Therefore, the probability of accidents on major holidays is high and the distribution characteristics are different. If non-overlapping algorithms are used for major holiday data, it may lead to a reduction in rescue reliability. Therefore, it is necessary to use overlapping community algorithms to construct a temporary rescue node locating model.

Conclusion

The locations of emergency rescue nodes play an important role in improving the operational efficiency of expressways. In this study, a location model of the expressway emergency rescue node was established for the location of expressway emergency rescue nodes, using an expressway network. First, a network of expressway traffic was constructed. The difference in the OD matrix between working days and major holidays was used as the basis for determining whether to select permanent or temporary emergency rescue nodes, respectively. The non-overlapping community detection algorithm and overlapping community detection algorithm were used to analyze the OD distribution characteristics of expressway vehicles during working days and major holidays. This split also indicated non-overlapping and overlapping community detection results, respectively, which were used as the basis for selecting the location of permanent and temporary emergency rescue nodes, respectively. The node with the largest partition node weight was selected as the emergency rescue location. Considering the different impacts of zonal traffic volume, road length, and each vehicle type (minibuses, buses, minivans, and large trucks) on traffic accidents, this study used the probability weights of each vehicle type to obtain the partition-weighted traffic volume. Using this the resource allocation of rescue nodes was determined based on the ratio of the partition-weighted traffic volume to the length of the road. Finally, an emergency rescue demand node-rescue node matching mechanism was established based on the resource number and the KDTree matching method. We conducted an example study based on the toll data of working days and major holidays on a Chinese expressway in a city, which resulted in 22 permanent rescue nodes and 25 temporary rescue nodes. The application times and resource number configurations of the two types of rescue nodes were different, and they complemented each other to improve the efficiency of expressway emergency rescue.

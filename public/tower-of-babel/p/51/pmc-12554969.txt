Single-cell analysis of differential fat deposition in chicken pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues
Authors: Shuaipeng Zhu, Binbin Zhang, Wenjie Liang, Yuehua He, Tingqi Zhu, Ruili Han, Donghua Li, Yanbin Wang, Yadong Tian, Guoxi Li, Xiangtao Kang, Wenting Li, Zhunan Li, Guirong Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Excessive abdominal fat (AF) accumulation in poultry impairs laying performance and reduces slaughter rate. As an excellent local breed in China, the mechanisms underlying intramuscular fat (IMF) and abdominal fat deposition in Gushi chickens remain poorly understood. In this study, fat-related indices (including fat content, triglyceride, cholesterol, and phospholipids) in the pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues of Gushi chickens aged 2 to 22 weeks were analyzed. Concurrently, single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on the pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues of 2-week-old Gushi chickens to identify cell types and molecular characteristics specific to different tissues. The results showed that the AF rate of Gushi chickens presented a gradual increasing trend, while the IMF rate exhibited a "W"-shaped variation pattern throughout the entire process. Single-cell RNA sequencing of tissues from 2-week-old chickens identified cell-type-specific genes: genes such as G0S2 and NEBL were highly expressed in abdominal adipocytes, while genes including PDE3A and NOX4 were predominantly expressed in intramuscular adipocytes. Functional validation revealed that G0S2 upregulated adipogenic genes (e.g., CEBP/α, PPARγ) more significantly in abdominal adipocytes, and specifically increased FASN expression only in abdominal adipocytes (P < 0.01). These findings clarify the tissue-specific characteristics of fat deposition in Gushi chickens and the distinct role of G0S2, providing a reference for understanding the mechanisms of fat deposition in poultry.

Introduction

Poultry fat is classified into three categories: subcutaneous fat, Abdominal fat (AF), and Intramuscular fat (IMF)(Wang et al., 2022b). IMF is positively correlated with chicken meat tenderness, juiciness, and flavor (Hao, et al., 2022), Excessive deposition of AF and subcutaneous fat increases production costs (due to reduced feed conversion ratio), decreases economic benefits (owing to lower slaughter rate) (Chen, et al., 2023a), and elevates the risk of sudden death syndrome, ascites, and leg disorders in chicken flocks during production (Yuan, et al., 2023). IMF and AF exhibit distinct distribution patterns: IMF is primarily distributed within muscle tissues and between muscle fibers, while AF accumulates mainly in the AF pad, around the muscular stomach, and within the mesentery (Nematbakhsh, et al., 2021). Analysis of the two fat components revealed that IMF is primarily composed of triglyceride and phospholipids, while AF is predominantly composed of triglyceride. Notably, the triglyceride level in AF was significantly higher than that in pectoral muscle tissue (Liu, et al., 2018; Ma, et al., 2021). The content of n-3 fatty acids in IMF is higher than that in AF, which may represent a potential strategy for improving the quality of pectoral muscle (Mir, et al., 2017). Additionally, previous studies have shown that IMF deposition in the pectoral muscle is primarily associated with pyruvate and citrate metabolism, while AF deposition is mainly linked to acetyl-CoA and glycerol metabolism (Luo, et al., 2022). This indicates that the metabolic pathways and origins of adipocytes in these two tissues are distinct. Studies have demonstrated that the primary sources of intramyocellular adipocytes are PDGFRα⁺-expressing mesenchymal progenitor cells and fibroadipogenic cells (Lukjanenko, et al., 2019; Shayan and Huang, 2020). Therefore, this subset of cells can serve as adipocyte precursor cells. However, adipose stem cell and progenitor cell subsets (PDGFRα⁺, PPARγ⁺) also exhibit adipogenic characteristics and possess the ability to differentiate into adipocytes(Michelotti, et al., 2022). Vishvanath (Vishvanath, et al., 2016) used a pulse-chase lineage tracing mouse model to track the fate of PDGFRβ-expressing cells in adipose tissue; these cells can generate white adipocytes, indicating that at least a subset of abdominal adipocytes originates from PDGFRβ-expressing cells.

Unlike conventional transcriptomics, single-cell transcriptomics can identify nucleotide polymorphisms, gene copy number variations, genomic structural variations, and alternative splicing of the transcriptome in individual cells. Meanwhile, it enables the construction of developmental atlases for target cell subsets, better illustrates cell differentiation directions, and facilitates the understanding of developmental patterns in specific tissues. Cao et al. employed single-cell RNA sequencing technology to detect the pectoralis muscle tissue of Jingxinghuang chickens at two distinct developmental stages (5 days of age and 100 days of age). A total of 19 genes, including APOA1 and ENSGALG00000036073, were identified to be associated with IMF lipid deposition and metabolism, and these genes can serve as marker genes for intramyocellular adipocytes (Guo, et al., 2018). Li et al. identified (APOA1 and COL1A1) as marker genes for intramyocellular adipocytes via single-cell RNA sequencing (Buenrostro, et al., 2015) . Hahn Nahmgoon et al. conducted single-cell transcriptomic analysis on epididymal adipose tissue (EAT) and inguinal adipose tissue (IAT) in mice, and constructed a comprehensive atlas of adipose stem cell subsets in these two types of adipose tissue (Nahmgoong, et al., 2022). Meanwhile, intercellular communication between adipocytes and surrounding cells can also be deciphered using single-cell technologies. Inoue et al. isolated CD271⁺ progenitor cells from subcutaneous adipose tissue and confirmed their significant ability to promote angiogenesis in vivo (Inoue, et al., 2023). Qu et al. performed single-cell sequencing on human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hAMSCs) isolated from human adipose tissue; the results showed that under directed induction, the osteogenic clusters of hAMSCs underwent almost complete differentiation (Qu, et al., 2021). Both groups of cells are heterogeneous: some hAMSCs are primed for osteogenesis, while the variant OS clusters appear to be involved in the proper division of labor for general functions. Feregrino et al. employed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology at three key stages of chicken limb development (Feregrino, et al., 2019). This approach enabled the acquisition of dynamic changes in transcriptomic profiles at the whole-genome level, the identification of marker genes for different cell types, and the analysis of the diversity of chicken limb development from a cellular perspective. Wang et al. performed detailed transcriptomic analysis of cell populations present in the skin of black-feathered chickens and white-feathered chickens using single-cell sequencing technology (Wang, et al., 2024). They verified the specific expression of SGK1, WNT5A, CTSC, TYR, and LAPTM5 in black-feathered chickens, and these genes are potential key candidate genes that determine feather color differentiation in Lueyang black-bone chickens. In our previous study, we found that for Gushi chickens (a local breed), IMF deposition in the pectoralis muscle may occur after 2 weeks of age; therefore, we determined the fat content at different time points. We analyzed the variation trends of the main components in the pectoralis muscle and abdominal fat tissues of Gushi chickens at different weeks of age. Subsequently, we performed single-cell sequencing and constructed single-cell atlases of IMF and AF, and found that the pectoralis muscle tissue and abdominal fat tissues differ in cell composition, with a large number of differentially regulated genes present. Meanwhile, we demonstrated that the ability of G0S2 to promote lipid deposition in abdominal adipocytes is superior to that in intramyocellular adipocytes. These related studies provide a theoretical basis for the differential fat deposition between IMF and AF.

Materials and methods

Animals and sample collection

The animal experimental protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Henan Agricultural University (Zhengzhou, China) (Permit Number: HNND2020030118).

One hundred healthy 1-day-old female Gushi chicks were obtained from Henan Sangao Animal Husbandry Co., Ltd. (Henan Province, China) and randomly allocated into 5 groups with 20 birds per group. All chickens were reared under consistent environmental conditions with ad libitum access to water. Throughout the 22-week experimental period, husbandry and feeding protocols were implemented in accordance with the Chinese National Standard for Yellow-Feathered Broilers (Standard No.: NY/T 1871-2010). At 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 18, and 22 weeks of age, 5 birds were randomly selected from each group, fasted for 12 hours, euthanized by jugular venipuncture, and then slaughtered. Pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissues were collected under aseptic conditions, immediately immersed in liquid nitrogen, and thereafter stored at −80°C.

Slaughter trait analysis and fat content determination

Live weight: Weight of chickens after 12-hour fasting prior to slaughter; Unit: gram (g). Pectoral muscle weight: Weight of the left pectoral muscle; Unit: gram (g). Abdominal fat tissues weight: Total weight of abdominal fat tissues and fat surrounding the gizzard, unit: (g). AF percentage: Calculated as (abdominal fat tissues weight / slaughter weight) × 100 %. Fat content determination: Referring to the National Standard (GB/T 5009.6-2016 National Food Safety Standard: Determination Method for Fat in Foods), the Soxhlet extraction method was adopted. Each sample was measured in triplicate, and the average value was used for subsequent analysis.

Biochemical indexes (Triglyceride, Cholesterol, Phospholipid) detection methods

Pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissue samples: 0.1 g of fresh tissue was added to 900 μL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), followed by cryogenic grinding at 90 Hz for 3 min.Intramuscular and abdominal adipocyte samples (taking 12-well plates as an example): After washing twice with PBS, 100 μL of trypsin was added to each well and reacted for 1 min, followed by the addition of 200 μL of complete medium. The mixture was centrifuged at 5,000 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was washed twice with 300 μL of PBS, followed by resuspension in 100 μL of PBS and cryogenic grinding at 90 Hz for 3 min.

Triglyceride and cholesterol assay kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, while the phospholipid assay kit was obtained from Beijing Aikemei Biotechnology Co., Ltd. All experimental procedures were performed in strict accordance with the manufacturers' instructions. The absorbance for triglyceride and cholesterol was measured at a wavelength of 500 nm, and that for phospholipid was measured at 450 nm. For the detection of triglyceride and cholesterol, protein normalization was simultaneously conducted using a BCA protein assay kit.

Preparation of chicken breast muscle and abdominal fat cell (AFc) suspensions

Under sterile conditions, fresh pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues of chickens were carefully harvested and rinsed three times with cold PBS. The tissues were then minced into small pieces (approximately 1–2 mm³) using sterile scissors. These tissue fragments were enzymatically digested in DMEM/F-12 medium supplemented with 1 mg/mL collagenase IV (Sigma-Aldrich, Shanghai, China) at 37°C for 30 minutes. During digestion, gentle manual agitation was performed every 10 minutes to ensure uniform digestion. Subsequently, the enzymatic digestion was terminated by adding an equal volume of DMEM medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS). The resulting cell suspension was filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer and then centrifuged at 500 × g for 5 minutes. The cell pellet was resuspended in PBS for subsequent processing (Wang, et al., 2022a). An appropriate volume of the single-cell suspension was mixed with a Trypan Blue solution at a 9:1 ratio to assess cell viability. The number of viable cells was counted using a Countess® II Automated Cell Counter, with cell viability ensured to be ≥ 90 %. The cell concentration was then adjusted to an optimal level (not less than 1000 cells per microliter) for subsequent experiments.

10x genome library preparation and sequencing

The cell suspension was loaded onto the 10 × Genomics GemCode Single-Cell Instrument to generate Gel Bead-In-Emulsions (GEMs). Sequencing libraries were subsequently prepared using the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3′ Reagent Kits v3.1(Zhang, et al., 2022). These libraries were then subjected to high-throughput sequencing on the NovaSeq 6000 platform (provided by Guangzhou Genedenovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) using the paired-end 150 (PE150) sequencing mode.

Preprocessing of scRNA-seq data

Cell Ranger (10x Genomics, v4) was used to assess the quality of raw data and perform alignments to the reference genome GRCg7a (Zerbino, et al., 2018). Subsequent downstream analyses were conducted using the R package Seurat v2.3.2 (Butler, et al., 2018). For quality control, cells with abnormal UMI counts (≥8,000) or mitochondrial gene percentages (≥25 %) were excluded. Additionally, cells with gene counts < 500 or > 4,000 were filtered out to ensure data reliability.

Identification of cell types breast muscle and abdominal fat

A graph-based clustering method was implemented using Seurat with a resolution set to 0.75 (Dai, et al., 2020). T-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) (Linderman, et al., 2019) or Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) (Becht et al., 2019) was used for visualization of cell clusters. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with log2 (fold change) > 1 and p-value < 0.05 were considered significant. Cell annotation was performed based on the expression of known canonical marker genes for the corresponding cell types and subtypes, with relevant cell marker information is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Proposed chronological analysis to construct a cellular developmental atlas

Monocle 2 (version 2.10.1) was used for single-cell trajectory analysis(Trapnell et al., 2014). By examining the trajectories of gene expression changes in single-cell data, the temporal sequence of myogenic and AFc during development or differentiation was inferred, enabling the construction of a continuous developmental pathway and relational map of these cells.

Pathway analysis

DEGs with |log₂ fold change (logFC)| > 0.5 and adjusted P-value < 0.05 were used for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis. The R package ClusterProfiler was used for KEGG analysis to elucidate the biological functions or related pathways of these DEGs (Kanehisa, et al., 2025).

G0S2 overexpression and knockdown

The pcDNA3.1-G0S2 overexpression vector was constructed using homologous recombination. Based on the chicken G0S2 mRNA sequence (GenBank accession no.: NM_001190924.4), the coding sequence (CDS) was amplified with primers designed using Oligo 7 software. The purified PCR product and linearized pcDNA3.1 vector were recombined using a homologous recombination kit, and the ligation product was transformed into Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells for plasmid propagation. Chicken G0S2-specific small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) were designed using Invitrogen's online siRNA design tool. The sequences are provided in Supplementary Table 2 and named as G0S2-siRNA.

Chicken primary preadipocyte isolation and adipogenic differentiation assay

Primary intramuscular fat cell (IMFc) and AFc were isolated from the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissues of 2-week-old Gushi chickens, with detailed procedures described in our previous study(Zhu, et al., 2023). After cells reached 90 % confluence, preadipocytes were induced to differentiate using a sodium oleate medium (160 μM). Cells were collected at 0 h, 12 h, 2 days, 4 days, and 6 days post-induction. For each time point, cell samples from 5 biological replicates were collected and stored at −80°C until subsequent use.

Oil red O staining

At specific time points during adipogenic differentiation, lipid droplets were stained with Oil Red O following the procedures below: cells were first fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde solution for 30 minutes, then stained with Oil Red O working solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 20 minutes. The stained samples were photographed under a microscope (Nikon, Japan). After microscopic examination, isopropanol was used to dissolve lipid droplets, and the absorbance was measured at 490 nm. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA protein assay kit (EpiZyme, Shanghai, China) to normalize both triglyceride content and Oil Red O staining results (Zhang, et al., 2025).

Gene expression verification

Tissue and cell samples were used to verify gene expression in vivo and in vitro. Total RNA was extracted by TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). cDNA was synthesized using the HiScript Ⅲ RT SuperMix Kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd) following the manufacturer's instructions. The quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) primer sequences were designed by Oligo 7 (Table S2). The 2 × SYBR Premix Ex TaqTM II (TaKaRa) was used for qPCR to detect gene expression levels. All reactions were performed in triplicate. GAPDH was selected as the internal reference gene, and the relative gene expression level were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the data was performed using SPSS 26.0. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to analyze differences among groups. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and a P-value < 0.01 was considered extremely statistically significant. All graphs were generated using Graphpad Prism 8.0, and the data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Results

Deposition patterns of IMF and AF in Gushi chickens

To investigate the deposition patterns and differences between IMF and AF in Gushi chickens, we measured the live weight, pectoral muscle weight, abdominal fat tissues weight, and AF percentage at different weeks of age (0 to 22 weeks). The results showed that the body weight, pectoral muscle weight, and abdominal fat tissues weight of Gushi chickens all increased with age. However, the gain in abdominal fat tissues was not significant from 2 to 12 weeks of age (P > 0.05), abdominal fat tissues deposition initiated after 12 weeks, with a marked acceleration from 14 to 18 weeks, which was accompanied by a significant increase in AF percentage. We speculated that this trend might be associated with gonadal development. For pectoral muscle tissue, the pectoral muscle weight increased with age, but IMF exhibited an abnormal decrease at 12 weeks. To further analyze the differential lipid deposition between pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues of Gushi chickens, we determined the contents of major lipid components, including triglyceride, cholesterol and phospholipids. The results showed that triglyceride content in the pectoral muscle exhibited fluctuations, whereas that in abdominal fat tissue displayed an upward trend. Cholesterol and phospholipids contents showed consistent changing pattern in both tissues. After 2 weeks of age, the triglyceride content in pectoral muscle was significantly higher than that in abdominal fat tissues (P < 0.01). Cholesterol content in the pectoral muscle was significantly higher than in AF from 2 to 6 weeks, with no significant difference from 6 to 22 weeks. Phospholipid content differed between pectoral muscle and abdominal fat tissues only at 2 weeks, with no significant differences in other periods (P > 0.05) (Fig. 1B). Numerically, from 2 to 22 weeks, triglyceride content in pectoral muscle ranged from 0.33 to 1.27 μmol/g protein, while in abdominal fat tissues it ranged from 1.26 to 78.98 μmol/g protein. Cholesterol content in pectoral muscle was 0.3–1.23 μmol/g protein, and in abdominal fat tissues was 0.04–1.26 μmol/g protein. Phospholipid content in pectoral muscle was 6.95–13.05 ng/ml, and in abdominal fat tissues was 7.11–11.65 ng/ml. These findings indicate that triglyceride content and accumulation rate in abdominal fat tissues were substantially higher than those in pectoral muscle, while cholesterol and phospholipid levels were relatively stable in both tissues. To clarify the differences in gene expression between the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissues of Gushi chickens at different developmental weeks, q-PCR was used to detect the expression levels of adipocyte marker genes (CEBP/α, PPARγ), fatty acid synthesis-related genes (FAS, FABP4), and key lipid mobilization-related genes (HSL, ATGL) in the collected tissues at 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 18, and 22 weeks of age. The results showed that the expression levels of CEBP/α, PPARγ, FAS, FABP4, and ATGL in the pectoral muscle of Gushi chickens were significantly lower than those in abdominal adipose tissues, while the expression level of HSL was significantly higher than that in abdominal adipose tissues (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1C). IMFc and AFc were isolated from pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose of Gushi chickens, respectively. Under identical culture conditions, triglyceride accumulation in AFc was significantly higher than that in IMFc, with no significant differences observed in cholesterol and phospholipid levels between the two cell types (Fig. 1D and F). Additionally, IMFc and AFc were induced to differentiate using an adipogenic differentiation medium, and Oil Red O staining was performed at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 days post-induction. Microscopic observation revealed that the rate of lipid accumulation in AFc was significantly faster than that in IMFc (Fig. 1G). These results demonstrate that lipid accumulation in abdominal fat tissue of Gushi chickens is significantly higher than that in pectoral muscle both in vivo and in vitro, thereby providing a theoretical basis for investigating the mechanisms underlying heterogeneous fat deposition in different chicken tissues.

Single-cell sequencing data analysis

We performed single-cell transcriptomic sequencing on the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissues of 2-week-old Gushi chickens. The sequencing was conducted by Guangzhou OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). Data quality control was performed using Cell Ranger (Liao, et al., 2023), including detection of 10x barcodes, UMIs, and RNA sequences where bases with quality scores >30 accounted for >90 %. The proportion of Q30 bases in RNA reads exceeded 85 %, and the genome mapping rate was >80 % (Supplementary Table 3). Subsequent analyses were conducted using functions from the Seurat package. DoubletFinder was used to calculate the probability of GEMs being doublets (pANN value). High-quality cells were retained based on the number of identified genes (670.0–3500.0) and the proportion of mitochondrial gene expression (<10.0 %) in single cells (Supplementary Table 4). After removing apoptotic cells, the Pearson's correlation coefficient between UMI counts and the number of genes/mitochondrial percentage reached 0.96, indicating that the data met the requirements for subsequent analyses (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Differential gene expression analysis of adipocytes derived from pectoral muscle tissues and abdominal adipose tissues

We annotated cluster 16 in the pectoral muscle as an adipocyte cluster, which comprised 167 adipocytes and 3,681 genes. Cluster 3 in abdominal adipose tissue was annotated as an adipocyte cluster, containing 1,775 adipocytes and 3,683 genes. A total of 3,654 genes were co-expressed in these two adipocyte subpopulations (Fig. 4A). KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the upregulated genes in the adipocyte cluster of the pectoral muscle were mainly enriched in "Pathways in Cancer", "cGMP-PKG Signaling Pathway", and "PI3K-Akt Signaling Pathway", as well as "Oxytocin Signaling Pathway. A small number of upregulated genes were enriched in adipogenesis-related pathways, including "RAP1 Signaling Pathway", "Regulation of Lipolysis in Adipocytes", and "cAMP Signaling Pathway". Upregulated genes in abdominal adipose tissue adipocytes were significantly enriched in adipogenesis-related pathways, including the PPAR signaling pathway, Regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes, AMPK signaling pathway, insulin signaling pathway, and insulin resistance (Supplementary Fig. 2). Differences in lipid droplet accumulation and consumption between IMFc and AFc upon G0S2 overexpression/silencing

Overexpression of G0S2 significantly increased lipid droplet accumulation in both types of adipocytes (P < 0.01), In contrast, G0S2 silencing significantly reduced lipid droplet accumulation in these two cell types (P < 0.01) (Fig. 5C-F). Compared with the control group, IMFc with G0S2 overexpression showed a significant increase of 24.89 % in lipid droplet accumulation (P < 0.01) and an increase of 21.31 % in triglyceride content (P < 0.01). Similarly, AFc with G0S2 overexpression exhibited a significant increase of 34.91 % in lipid droplet accumulation (P < 0.01) and an increase of 24.48 % in triglyceride content (P < 0.01) (Fig. 5G and H). However, the degree of lipid droplet deposition in G0S2-overexpressed IMFc was lower than that in G0S2-overexpressed AFc. In IMFc, the G0S2-SI group showed a significant 7.96 % reduction in lipid droplet accumulation compared to the NC group (P < 0.01), with a 3.4 % decrease in triglyceride content (P < 0.01). In AFc, the G0S2-SI group exhibited a significant 19.23 % reduction in lipid droplet accumulation compared to the NC group (P < 0.01), along with a 19.43 % decrease in triglyceride content (P < 0.01) (Fig. 5J and K). These results indicate that G0S2 promotes lipid droplet accumulation in both adipocyte types, but with a more pronounced effect on AFc than on IMFc. Thus, we examined the differences in lipid droplet breakdown between IMFc and AFc upon G0S2 overexpression/silencing. The results showed that after G0S2 overexpression, glycerol content in IMFc significantly decreased by 23.16 %, and in AFc by 29.64 % (Fig. 5I). Conversely, after G0S2 silencing, glycerol content increased by 13.56 % in IMFc and by 14.96 % in AFc (Fig. 5L). These findings indicate that G0S2 exerts a more pronounced effect on AFc under the same treatment conditions, suggesting that the mechanisms of G0S2 action between AFc and IMFc. This confirms the existence of distinct fat deposition mechanisms in the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissue of Gushi chickens, thereby providing a reliable reference for poultry selective breeding.

Conclusion

Our study showed that the relative content of triglyceride in pectoral muscle tissue and IMFc was significantly lower than that in abdominal fat tissue and AFc (P < 0.05). The expression levels of fat deposition-related genes (PPARγ, CEBP/α, FABP4, FAS, ACC) in AFc were significantly higher than those in IMFc (P < 0.05). Through single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), we found that abdominal fat tissue was dominated by FAPs (accounting for 46.32 %), while pectoral muscle tissue was mainly composed of muscle cells (accounting for 47.84 %). We screened out genes such as G0S2, FTH1, NEBL, BMPER, PDE3A, and NOX4, which showed differential regulation in fat deposition between the two tissues. Functional studies on G0S2 revealed that G0S2 promotes lipid deposition; however, the degree to which G0S2 promotes lipid deposition in AFc was greater than that in IMFc. Meanwhile, G0S2 inhibits fat oxidative decomposition, and similarly, the degree of lipid decomposition inhibition by G0S2 in AFc was stronger than that in IMFc.

Discussion

Fat deposition in poultry has long been a research focus. Appropriate intramuscular fat content and low abdominal fat content are key targets in poultry breeding. However, the differences in fat deposition mechanisms between the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissue remain unclear. Prior to this, Xing et al. found that the rate of IMF deposition in chicken pectoral muscles accelerated significantly after 14 weeks of age (Xing, et al., 2020). Liu et al. found that the IMF content in the pectoral muscle of Beijing-You chickens began to increase slowly after 8 weeks of age (Liu, et al., 2016). Chartrin et al. also found that the deposition rate of intramuscular fat in duck pectoral muscles accelerated after 7 weeks of age (Chartrin, et al., 2007). This suggests that during the early stages, the development of poultry pectoral muscle tissue is dominated by muscle growth, while fat accumulation occurs continuously in abdominal adipose tissue. Our previous results also support these views. We found that intramuscular fat begins to appear in Gushi chickens at 2 weeks of age, and abdominal fat gradually increases after 2 weeks. Specifically, 12 weeks and 14 weeks of age are the turning points for intramuscular fat and abdominal fat, respectively.

Based on the maturity of single-cell transcriptome technology, we can investigate the differences in the mechanisms underlying intramuscular fat and abdominal fat deposition in Gushi chickens. Studies on mammals have shown that adipose tissue is composed of a variety of different cell types, such as adipose-derived stem cells (ASCs), preadipocytes, mature adipocytes, as well as fibroblasts, nerve cells, macrophages, endothelial cells, and muscle cells(Cho, et al., 2020; Hong, et al., 2021). In contrast, the cell types of muscle tissue mainly include FAPs, tenocytes, endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, immune cells (B cells, T cells, macrophages, neutrophils), neuroglial cells, and satellite cells(Cai, et al., 2023; Messmer, et al., 2023). In our sequencing results, we identified 8 cell subpopulations through cell annotation of chicken pectoral muscle tissue. Among these, muscle cells accounted for the largest proportion (47.84 %), while adipofibroblasts (13.67 %) and adipocytes (1.05 %) accounted for relatively small proportions. For abdominal adipose tissue, 9 cell subpopulations were annotated, with adipofibroblasts making up the largest proportion (46.32 %) and adipocytes accounting for 9.53 %. However, studies have shown that chicken fibroblasts can be converted into muscle cells and develop into mature muscle proteins (Ren, et al., 2022). Nevertheless, when treated with chicken serum (CS)-containing medium or fatty acids, chicken fibroblasts can also be effectively transformed into adipocytes and complete lipid deposition (Lee, et al., 2021). Similarly, studies have shown that the differentiation of PDGFRα⁺-expressing mesenchymal stem cells and fibroadipogenic cells into muscle cells and adipocytes occurs competitively in the same microenvironment(Sun, et al., 2017). Our results indicated that PDGFRα⁺-expressing mesenchymal progenitor cells and fibroadipogenic cells in Gushi chickens mainly differentiate into adipocytes. Therefore, we propose that intramuscular fat deposition in chicken pectoral muscles is a complex yet interesting process. Specifically, FAPs are differentially regulated at different stages and differentiate into either muscle cells or adipocytes.

From our sequencing results, we identified several candidate genes: G0S2, FTH1, NEBL, BMPER, NRP1, and MAN1A1 were highly expressed in abdominal adipocytes, while PDE3A, NOX4, TPI1, and other genes were highly expressed in intramuscular adipocytes. All these genes have been shown to be associated with fat deposition. All these genes have been shown to be associated with fat deposition. For example, FTH1 is highly expressed in human abdominal and muscle tissues(Moreno-Navarrete, et al., 2016) and is closely associated with lipid droplet content(Tirinato, et al., 2021). NEBL can be regulated by miR-20a-3p and interfere with adipogenic differentiation(Srinivasa, et al., 2021). BMPER is used as a marker gene for adipocytes and promotes fat deposition (Garritson, et al., 2023). TPI1 is a glycolytic enzyme that catalyzes the interconversion of dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP) and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (GAP) (Liu, et al., 2022); it has been shown to be associated with fatty acid synthesis in Luchuan pigs and Duroc pigs (Deng, et al., 2023)., and also demonstrated to potentially participate in the anabolic process of intramuscular fat in poultry by way of energy metabolism or amino acid metabolism (Yu, et al., 2023).

G0S2 is a transcriptional target of the adipogenesis regulator PPAR-gamma (Fernández-Garza, et al., 2025). Additionally, the G0S2 gene can selectively inhibit the triglyceride hydrolytic activity of adipose triglyceride lipase (Wang, et al., 2019). Park et al. found that G0S2 reduces abdominal fat deposition and alters fatty acid composition in chicken meat through the adipose tissue-specific peroxisomal oxidation pathway. Overexpression of G0S2 promotes adipocyte hypertrophy [69] and triglyceride accumulation in adipocytes of brown adipose tissue(Park, et al., 2019). Moreover, the homology between avian G0S2 and mammalian G0S2 is 50 % to 52 %, which indicates that G0S2 also plays an important role in the regulation of mammalian fat(Gershman, et al., 2023). In mammals, salt can promote the hydrolysis of intramuscular neutral lipids in pigs (biceps femoris) by inducing the phosphorylation of G0S2 (Heckmann, et al., 2014). Mouse embryonic fibroblasts from G0S2-deficient mice exhibit impaired adipogenesis and contain abnormally small intracellular lipid droplets. Overexpression of G0S2 in differentiated mouse 3T3-L1 adipocytes leads to increased protein levels of C/EBPα and PPARγ(Zhao, et al., 2017). The regulatory role of G0S2 in fat deposition is relatively obvious; however, there are few studies on the heterogeneity of fat deposition between the two sites (intramuscular and abdominal adipose tissues) in poultry.

Our results demonstrate that G0S2 regulates lipid deposition in Gushi chickens through lipid deposition-related genes including CEBP/α (Zang, et al., 2023), PPARγ (Zhang, et al., 2023), FASN (Chen, et al., 2023b), and FABP4 (Koung Ngeun, et al., 2024). However, we found that the effects induced by G0S2 expression differ between the pectoral muscle tissue and abdominal adipose tissue of Gushi chickens. This indicates that there may be differences in the downstream genes of G0S2 between the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissue of Gushi chickens, and also confirms the differences in fat deposition between the pectoral muscle and abdominal adipose tissue. Furthermore, this provides a theoretical basis for our research on the heterogeneous fat deposition in poultry.

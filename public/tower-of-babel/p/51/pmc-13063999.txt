Resolvin D1 alleviates liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways
Authors: Jiahuan Li, Lingfeng Zhao, Yuwen Xi, Xiaoling Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveResolvin D1 (RvD1) is lipid mediator converted from docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and has multiple pharmacological properties. Recently, it has been reported that RvD1 alleviates tissue fibrosis, including renal and pulmonary fibrosis in animal models. Here, we investigate the effects of RvD1 on liver fibrosis models in vitro and in vivo and the underlying mechanism.MethodsMale C57BL/6 mice treated with carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-activated LX-2 cells were used in the study. The serum levels of ALT and AST were analyzed to evaluate liver function. H&E and Sirius red staining were used to assess liver morphology and the degree of liver fibrosis. Quantitative RT-PCR and western blot were used to assay the gene expression and protein levels.ResultsIn vivo, the results showed that RvD1 significantly attenuated increased AST and ALT serum levels in CCl4-treated mice. Histological examination revealed that RvD1 ameliorated CCl4-induced fibrous proliferation and macrophage infiltration. Besides, RvD1 depressed the expressions of pro-fibrotic factors (α-SMA, Collagen I and TIMP-1) and pro-inflammatory factors (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α and MCP-1) in the liver tissues of model mice. In vitro, RvD1 also relieved LPS-induced inflammation and hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation in LX-2 cells. Furthermore, RvD1 was demonstrated to upregulate the expression of SIRT1, while inhibited the p-IκBα, p-NF-κB, TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 protein levels in these models.ConclusionsOur results suggest that RvD1 alleviates liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways and may be used as a novel therapeutic intervention for liver fibrosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12950-026-00492-y.Keywords: Resolvin D1, Liver fibrosis, Inflammation, HSC activation, SIRT1, NF-κB, TGF-β1/Smads

Introduction

Liver fibrosis is a clinically common chronic disease characterized by diffuse fibroplasia and pseudo-lobular formation in the liver, which results from long-term liver repair after recurring injury of various causes. Without effective interventions at the early stage of the disease, most patients may irreversibly progress to cirrhosis, liver failure, or hepatocellular carcinoma [1]. To date, the global incidence of live fibrosis and cirrhosis is dramatically increasing, with reported approximately 1 million deaths per year worldwide due to the lack of fully proven medications [2, 3].

It is believed that inflammation carries out a critical role in the evolution of liver fibrosis. When the liver is diseased or damaged, inflammatory cells, like Kupffer cells or macrophages, are activated and recruited to injured sites, resulting in a release of numerous cytokines and growth factors [4]. Upon the stimulation of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrogenic mediators, quiescent hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) are activated to myofibroblasts (MFB), which further express amounts of alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and promote the accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM), thus leading to liver fibrosis [5]. Based on the above, identifying antifibrotic agents that target blocking inflammation response and the activation of HSC might be paramount for liver fibrosis. Silencing information regulator 1 (SIRT1) is a relatively conserved nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) dependent deacetylase with high homology with yeast protein Sir2 [6]. In the liver injury, SIRT1 is involved in multiple metabolic and pathophysiological processes and can protect hepatocytes from apoptosis, aging, inflammation and oxidative stress [7-9]. Accumulated evidences have indicated that SIRT1 may be an important protective factor in the process of HSC activation [10, 11]. In vitro, the use of SIRT1 agonist blocked the phenotype transformation of HSC into MFB and promote the apoptosis of HSC [12]. In vivo experiments, knockout of SIRT1 significantly aggravated carbon tetrachloride (CCl4)-induced liver injury and fibrosis in mice [13, 14]. The alleviating effect of SIRT1 on liver fibrosis is closely related to inhibiting the activation of transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) signaling pathway [15, 16]. Previous studies have confirmed that several selective SIRT1 activators can significantly inhibit the activity of TGF-β1 and its downstream transcription factors Smad2 and Smad3 in HSC [17, 18]. Therefore, targeted blocking of SIRT1/TGF-β1/Smads pathway can be a key strategy to improve liver fibrosis.

Nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) is a nuclear transcription factor that mediates intracellular signal transduction and plays an important role in inflammatory response by enhancing the expression of pro-inflammatory factor genes, such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), and interleukin (IL)-6 [19]. Increasing evidences show that the activation of SIRTl can inhibit the transcriptional activity and activation of NF-κB, thus playing a protective role in inflammatory damage [20, 21]. In addition, SIRT1 can indirectly regulate the NF-κB pathway by regulating some antioxidant factors, such as Nrf2, and inhibiting the production of reactive oxygen species [21]. Inflammation and oxidative stress greatly contribute to the progression of liver fibrosis [5]. Thus, blockade of SIRT1/NF-κB pathway is particularly important for restoring the fibrotic liver.

Resolvin D1 (RvD1, chemical structure shown in Fig. 1A) belongs to one of the metabolites from docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and is generated through 15- and 5-lipoxygenases (LOX) epoxidation [22]. It has been shown that RvD1 exerts a significant improvement effect on sepsis-induced acute lung injury, which may be related to its promotion of SIRT1 expression and thereby inhibition of the activation of inflammatory signaling pathways, including STAT3, NF-κB, ERK and p38 [23]. In a mouse model of mechanical ventilator-induced pulmonary fibrosis, RvD1 has been demonstrated to dose-dependently reverse TGF-β1-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), reduce the content of TGF-β1 in alveolar cells, and block the activation of Smad2/3 pathway [24]. In addition, training of mice with β-glucan increased the production of RvD1 and soluble mediators in alveolar macrophages, which induced the expression of epithelial SIRT1 and reduced apoptosis of epithelial cells, thereby attenuating lung tissue damage [25]. However, it is unclear whether RvD1 can control inflammation and alleviate liver fibrosis by regulating SIRT1.

In the present study, we attempt to clarify whether RvD1 plays a protective role in liver fibrosis and the underlying mechanisms. We have verified the hypothesis that RvD1 affects the activation of HSCs and prevents the liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways in the progression of liver fibrosis.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

RvD1 (HPLC ≥ 95%, endotoxin-free) and CCl4 were purchased from Cayman (Ann Arbor, MI, USA) and Promoter (Wuhan, China), respectively. Primers used for quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis were synthesized by Tsingke (Beijing, China). Assays for serum transaminase and hepatic hydroxyproline content were acquired from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). Cell culture materials and reagents, such as Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum (FBS), and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) were obtained from Gibco, Thermo Fisher (Waltham, MA). Primary antibodies against TNF-α (cat. no. sc-12744, 1:100), IL-6 (cat. no. sc-130326,1:100), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1, cat. no. sc-52701, 1:200) were provided by Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Santa Cruz, CA, USA). Anti-NF-κB (cat. no. 8242, 1:1000), anti-phospho-NF-κB (p-NF-κB, cat. no. 3033, 1:500), anti-phospho-inhibitor of kappa B alpha (p-IκBα, cat. no. 9246, 1:1000), anti-cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2, cat. no. 12282, 1:1000), anti-SIRT1 (cat. no.8469, 1:1000), anti-Smad2/3 (cat. no.8685, 1:1000) and p-Smad2/3 (cat. no.8828, 1:1000) antibodies were from Cell Signaling Technology (Beverly, MA, USA). Anti-TGF-β1(cat. no. ab92486, 1:1000) was obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Biochemical detection kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, PR China). Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary anti-mouse (cat. no. SA00001-1, 1:3000) and anti-rabbit (cat. no. SA00001-2, 1:3000) antibodies were also from Proteintech. EX527 (selisistat), a selective SIRT1 inhibitor, was purchased from Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology Co., LTD.

Cell culture and experimental design

LX2 cells (human HSC line) with main characteristics of HSC, including vitamin A metabolism, fiber and ECM production, pro-fibrotic gene expression and signal transduction [26], were used for in vitro experiments. Cell culture was routinely performed in culture medium (DMEM + 10% FBS) at 37 °C with 5% carbon dioxide (CO2). According to the experimental needs, the LX2 cells were activated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS), or intervened with RvD1 or EX527 simultaneously during the activation. At least three independent experiments were carried out.

Animal experiment

The total of 24 adult male C57BL/6 mice (21 to 25 g, 8 weeks old) were maintained under specific-pathogen-free (SPF) condition of 12 h/12 h light/dark cycle with a constant temperature (24 °C ± 2 °C) and allowed to eat and drink ad libitum. After acclimation to the new environment for 1 week, all animals were randomly allocated in four groups (6 mice per group): (1) control group (Control); (2) CCl4 model group (CCl4); (3) CCl4 + low dose RvD1 intervention group (CCl4 + RvD1-L); (4) CCl4 + high dose RvD1 intervention group (CCl4 + RvD1-H). Mice in CCl4 group accepted CCl4 (20% CCl4/olive oil; 5 ml/kg) via intraperitoneal injection twice a week for 6 weeks to establish liver fibrosis model. In RvD1-intervention groups, as shown in Fig. 1B, mice received equivalent amount of CCl4 administration for 4 weeks and then were injected intraperitoneally with RvD1 at a low dose (100 ng/mice) or a high dose (300 ng/mice) daily, respectively, and co-treated with CCl4 for another 2 weeks. The dosage and administration time of RvD1 in mice during this project refers to previous studies [27, 28]. Equal volume of olive oil was given to mice by intraperitoneal injection as control. At experiment end, animals were sacrificed with overdose of pentobarbital followed by collection of serum and hepatic tissues for biochemical assays and histopathological evaluation. All animal operations in our study were ethically supervised by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Wuhan, China).

Biochemical analysis

The serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) in mice were detected to reflect hepatocyte injury by enzymatic method. The absorbance of transaminase was read at 510 nm according to the manufacturer’s protocols. As a unique amino acid present in collagen, hydroxyproline is measured to quantify liver collagen content. Briefly, approximately 30 mg of each liver sample was made into homogenate in 1 ml alkali hydrolyzed buffer at 95 °C for 20 min and centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 min to collect supernatants for this detection. The hydrolysates were measured at 550 nm using a spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, Inc., Winooski, VT, USA) and the hydroxyproline amount in liver was calculated as µg per mg of wet tissue.

Histological evaluation

For histological evaluation, fresh liver tissues from each mouse were excised and immersed 4% paraformaldehyde solution and made into paraffin blocks. Then, 4 μm-thick sections were processed by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Sirius red staining following standard operating procedures [29]. Sirius red, a class of strong acid anion dye that can react with alcaline collagen, is usually used to reflect the proportion of liver tissue with collagen fibers. Five high-magnification fields of each stained slice were randomly imaged by a phase contrast microscope (Olympus BX-51; Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) to evaluate liver injury and collagen deposition.

Immunohistochemistry

The 4 μm paraffin-embedded liver sections were used in immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis. According to the instructions provided in a SABC immunohistochemical staining kit (SA1020; Boster, Wuhan, China), the slices were dewaxed in xylene (100%) and gradient alcohol (100%, 95%, and 75%), followed by antigen retrieval in boiling citric acid salt buffer (pH 6.0). After eliminating endogenous peroxidase activity with 3% H2O2 for 10 min, the sections were then blocked with appropriate blocking solution for 30 min at room temperature and incubated with F4/80 (1:500 dilution) primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. Biotinylated secondary antibody was applied at a dilution of 1:200 the next day. Positive staining areas were visualized with a diaminobenzidine kit and imaged on Olympus BX-51 light microscopy. The dyeing positive rate of target proteins was quantified by Image J V1.8.0 software (National Institutes of Health).

Protein extraction and western blot assay

To obtain protein samples, liver tissues and cells were processed in the order of tissue lysis, centrifugation, protein concentration detection and heat denaturation. These prepared protein samples were then added to SDS-PAGE (5% concentrated gel + 10% separating gel) at 80 µg/lane (liver tissue) or 30 µg/lane (LX2 cells) in each gel. After a period of electrophoresis, the protein bands were separated and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (pore size 0.45 μm; Millipore; MA, USA), which were then incubated with blocking solution (Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 + 5% skim milk) for 1 h at room temperature. Following blocking, the blots were successively incubated with specific primary antibodies and the appropriate HRP labeled secondary antibodies with gentle shaking. Enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection reagents from Antgene (Wuhan, China) were used to make immunoreactive protein bands visible, and images were captured using chemiluminescence gel imaging system (FluorChem FC3; ProteinSimple, San Jose, CA, USA).

Real-time PCR

For real-time PCR analysis, total RNA was firstly isolated from frozen mouse liver samples (20–30 mg tissues) and LX-2 cells using TRIzol (Takara; Beijing, China), chloroform and acetone reagents. Then, RNA samples were subjected to a reverse transcription program for 15 min and converted to cDNA by Prime Script RT reagent kit (Takara; Beijing, China). Next, equal amount of reaction mixtures containing 5 µl TB Green, 1 µl primer, 1 µl cDNA sample and 3 µl sterile double-distilled water, was added to PCR array plate wells. Then, qRT-PCR was run on the Roche Light Cycler 480 System (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Mannheim, Germany). Sequences of PCR primers involved in this paper are outlined in Supplementary Table 1. The detailed PCR procedure was set as described previously: 1 cycle at 95 °C for 15 s, and 45 cycles at 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s [28]. Finally, the relative transcriptional expression levels of target genes were calculated according to the 2−ΔΔCt method by using GAPDH as internal controls.

Statistical analysis

Statistical differences between groups were calculated by analysis of variance and Tukey’s correction. Data were analyzed with GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1(La Jolla, CA, USA). And p < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

Results

RvD1 improves CCl4-induced liver injury and fibrosis in mice

To investigate the roles of RvD1 in liver fibrosis, we established a C57BL/6 mice model induced by 6 week-CCl4 injection, with or without 2 week-RvD1 intervention. As demonstrated by H&E staining, mice in CCl4 group exhibited severe pathological changes in liver, such as a disorderly tissue structure, widespread cell necrosis and massive fibrous proliferation with varying degrees of inflammatory cells infiltration. Nevertheless, these pathological damages in mice liver were partly restored following RvD1 supplement (Fig. 1C). Serum hepatic enzyme levels were detected to evaluate the effect of RvD1 administration on liver injury. As expected, a significant increase in the ALT and AST activities of mice serum was observed after CCl4 exposure compared to the control group (p < 0.01). However, RvD1 treatment obviously decreased ALT and AST levels in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1D). Liver fibrosis was assessed by Sirius red staining and hepatic hydroxyproline content detection. As depicted in Fig. 1C and E, Sirius red staining revealed that the area of collagen fibers was remarkably increased by 11.4 times after CCl4 exposure, but significantly decreased by 33.6%, 54.1% in mice administrated by low dose (100 ng) and high dose (300 ng) of RvD1, respectively. And the result of hepatic hydroxyproline content detection in mice further supports the anti-fibrotic effect of RvD1 in CCl4-induced liver fibrosis (Fig. 1F).

RvD1 repairs CCl4-induced liver inflammation in mice

Macrophages mediated inflammatory response is a major process involved in the pathogenesis of liver fibrosis. To characterize the functional involvement of RvD1 in chronic liver inflammation, macrophages infiltration and inflammatory cytokines expressions were detected in the liver of CCl4-induced mice. It was shown that the positive staining areas of F4/80 (macrophage cell specific marker) in the CCl4-treated mice were dramatically enlarged (p < 0.01). However, RvD1 intervention significantly attenuated the increase of F4/80 in the fibrotic liver tissues (Fig. 2A-C). Meanwhile, the elevations in expressions of hepatic pro-inflammatory genes, including COX-2, MCP-1, TNF-α and IL-6 in CCl4-induced mice, were also suppressed by RvD1, as determined by qRT-PCR and western blot (Fig. 2D-I). And the mentioned anti-inflammatory effects of RvD1 in CCl4-challenged mice were dose-dependent.

RvD1 relieves hepatic inflammation in mice induced by CCl4 through inhibition of the SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway

SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway plays a crucial role in driving liver fibrosis by mediating inflammatory response [30]. As shown in Fig. 3, compared with the control group, CCl4 significantly decreased the expression of SIRT1 protein in the liver of mice, while the administration of RvD1 restored the expression of SIRT1 protein in a dose-dependent manner. Correspondingly, RvD1 dose-dependently reduced the phosphorylation levels of IκBα and NF-κB p65 in mice with CCl4 intervention (Fig. 3A and B). These data implicit that SIRT1/NF-κB pathway may be involved in the hepatoprotection of RvD1 in CCl4-induced liver inflammation and fibrosis.

RvD1 protects against liver fibrosis in mice induced by CCl4 through inhibition of the TGF-β1/Smads signaling pathway

The TGF-β1/Smad2/3 is known as the main pro-fibrosis signaling pathway [31]. Compared with the control group, the expression levels of TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 were significantly increased under the stimulation of CCl4, while the protein expression levels of these genes were inhibited to a certain extent under the intervention of RvD1 (Fig. 4A and B). Accordingly, the mRNA expressions of marker genes of liver fibrosis (α-SMA, Collagen I, TIMP-1) in groups were consistent with the changes of the above signaling pathway (Fig. 4C-E). These results suggest that RvD1 alleviates liver fibrosis by regulating TGF-β1/Smads pathway.

RvD1 inhibits inflammatory response in vitro by regulating SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway

LX-2 cells were stimulated by LPS with increasing concentration (0-100 ng/ml), and the expression level of SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway was detected by western blot. As shown in Fig. 5A and B, with the increase of LPS concentration, the protein expression of SIRT1 was gradually decreased, and correspondingly, the protein expressions of p-IκBα and p-NF-κB were significantly increased. Subsequently, in order to investigate whether RvD1 could regulate SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway to control inflammatory response in vitro, LX-2 cells were treated by LPS (100 ng/ml) and interfered with different concentrations of RvD1 (2.5, 5, 10 nM). The results showed that under the intervention of RvD1, the expression of SIRT1 protein was increased in a dose-dependent manner, while the protein levels of p-IκBα and p-NF-κB were dose-dependently decreased (Fig. 5C and D), accompanied by reductions in the expression of inflammatory factors (Fig. 5E and F). The above results support that RvD1 inhibits inflammatory response in vitro by regulating SIRT1/NF-κB signaling pathway.

LX-2 cells were stimulated with increasing concentrations of LPS (0-100 ng/ml), and the expression level of TGF-β1/Smad2/3 signaling pathway was detected by western blot. As shown in Fig. 6A and B, with the increase of LPS concentration, the protein expression of SIRT1 was gradually decreased; and correspondingly, the protein expressions of TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 were significantly increased. These results indicate that TGF-β1/Smad2/3 signaling pathway is activated during LX-2 activation.

To further determine whether RvD1 could affect HSC activation by regulating TGF-β1/Smads in vitro, we induced the activation of LX-2 cells by LPS (100 ng/ml) while intervening with different concentrations of RvD1 (2.5, 5, 10 nM). The results showed that the protein levels of TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 were decreased (Fig. 6C and D). Correspondingly, the increased expressions of LPS-induced HSC activation markers α-SMA, Collagen I and TIMP-1 were also significantly decreased after the intervention of RvD1 (Fig. 6E-G). Above results support that the inhibition of LX-2 activation by RvD1 is closely related to the TGF-β1/Smads pathway.

Reverse validation of RvD1 improving liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways

Previous studies have shown that both NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways are regulated by SIRT1 [34, 42]. Therefore, we used EX527 (a selective SIRT1 inhibitor) to reverse verify that RvD1 improves liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways. After pretreatment with EX527 (1 µM) for 2 h, LX-2 cells were treated with LPS (100 ng/ml) and RvD1 (10 nM) for 24 h to reverse verify the regulatory effect of RvD1 on SIRT1 signaling pathway. The cells were divided into Control, LPS, LPS+R10, LPS+R10+EX527 groups. By detecting the protein expressions of SIRT1, p-IκBα, p-NF-κB, NF-κB, TGF-β1, p-Smad2, p-Smad3 and Smad2/3, we found that compared with LPS+R10 group, SIRT1 protein expression in LPS+R10+EX527 group was significantly decreased (Fig. 7A and B), while the protein expressions of p-IκBα, p-NF-κB, TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 were significantly increased (Fig. 7A-D). Besides, we found that mRNA levels of COX-2, TNF-α, MCP-1 and IL-6 inhibited by RvD1 were upregulated by EX527 (Fig. 7E). Similarly, the mRNA expressions of fibrosis markers inhibited by RvD1 were also increased after EX527 intervention (Fig. 7F). The above results reverse verify that RvD1 improves liver inflammation and fibrosis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways.

Discussion

With the increasing prevalence of chronic liver injury from various aetiologies, liver fibrosis and cirrhosis has been a fast-growing public health hazard [3]. However, there are limited clinical pharmacologic treatments approved to reverse liver fibrosis and a large number of end stage liver fibrosis patients have no treatment choice but liver transplantation. There is an urgent need to find an effective antifibrotic drug to address this dilemma. RvD1 is one of endogenous lipid metabolites converted from DHA and exerts multiple biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidation, anti-apoptosis [32, 33]. Since we demonstrated that RvD1 could alleviate liver damage and inflammation in methionine-choline-deficient diet–induced murine steatohepatitis previously [28], we further studied its effect and potential mechanism in liver fibrosis. Here, we find that RvD1 significantly attenuates liver fibrosis by modulating key events, including liver damage, inflammation, and HSC activation. Our results also show that the hepatoprotection effect of RvD1 is partly mediated by regulating the SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways both in CCl4-treated mice and LPS-induced LX2 cells.

CCl4 is widely used to establish animal model for studying hepatic fibrosis due to its strong hepatocyte toxicity and rapid poisoning time [34, 35]. After being absorbed into liver, CCl4 is rapidly converted to trichloromethyl radical (CCl3) and peroxyl radical, which in turn induces severe hepatocytes apoptosis and inflammatory cell infiltration, contributing to the activation of HSCs and subsequent fibrosis development. Furthermore, under the stimulation of CCl4 and its metabolites, a large number of inflammatory factors (such as TNF-α) and pro-fibrotic factors (such as TGF-β1) are generated, which in turn promotes the activation of HSCs and aggravates liver cirrhosis [36]. In our study, the livers of mice in the model group administered with CCl4 for 6 weeks showed relatively typical pseudolobule formation and significant fibrous strip hyperplasia in the portal area, indicating the successful establishment of a mouse liver fibrosis model. In addition, through pathohistological features and biochemical analysis, we primarily demonstrate that RvD1 improves CCl4-induced liver injury and fibrosis in mice.

SIRT1 is a relatively conserved NAD+-dependent deacetylase widely present in mammals and plays an important role in regulating pathophysiological processes, such as cell energy metabolism, aging, oxidative stress and gene transcription [6]. Our study found that the activity of SIRT1 was significantly decreased in fibrotic liver tissues of mice, while the expression of SIRT1 was significantly up-regulated with the inhibition of LX-2 activation, indicating that SIRT1 may be a core protective factor in the activation process of HSC, which is consistent with the results of multiple previous studies [13, 14]. Zhang et al., [37] found that administration of resveratrol (a SIRT1 agonist) to LPS-induced HSC-T6 could block the phenotypic transformation of HSC into MFB. However, Li et al., [13] confirmed that knockout of SIRT1 significantly aggravated CCl4-induced liver injury and fibrosis in mice. In this study, we observed that with the increase of RvD1 administration, the inhibited SIRT1 expression was up-regulated in mouse fibrotic liver tissue and activated LX-2 cells, and the expressions of pro-fibrotic factors and pro-inflammatory factors were decreased, suggesting that RvD1 exerts the anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects in liver injury by promoting SIRT1 expression.

Inflammation plays a catalytic role in the progression of fibrosis. However, in fact, acute liver injury does not lead to liver fibrosis, but due to the presence of long-term inflammation. In this process, inflammatory cytokines, such as MCP-1 and TNF-α, play a central role in the regulatory network. They can not only recruit macrophages and neutrophils, but also stimulate the activation of HSCs. Activated HSCs further release cytokines to amplify the pro-fibrotic process in a positive feedback manner [4]. NF-κB is a pleiotropic transcription factor that plays an important role in proinflammatory regulation, and its activation is regulated by IκB kinase. Under the action of various stimulating factors, IκBα is phosphorylated and then degraded, which makes the nuclear localization signaling region of NF-κB to be exposed, promoting the phosphorylation and nuclear transcription of NF-κB. Then, NF-κB enters the cell nucleus and mediates the transcriptional expression of specific genes and the release of cytokines [38]. SIRT1 inhibits the activation of phosphorylation of NF-κB p65 or its nuclear translocation by driving other inflammatory suppressors, such as AMPK, PPARα [39, 40]. Conversely, NF-κB signal transduction down-regulates SIRT1 activity through reactive oxygen species, and the inhibition of SIRT1 disrupts oxidative energy metabolism and stimulates the inflammatory response induced by NF-κB [41]. With LPS stimulation and SIRT1 inhibitor, we bi-directionally confirm that RvD1 controls the inflammatory cascade during fibrosis via the SIRT1/NF-κB pathway. Our results provide theoretical basis for the application of RvD1 in blocking fibrosis through promoting the regression of proinflammatory state.

TGF-β1 is one of the major pro-fibrosis factors, and its expression is significantly increased during the occurrence of liver fibrosis, which positively promotes the occurrence and development of liver fibrosis [31]. The pro-fibrotic effect of TGF-β1 is mainly achieved through Smad signal transduction. After binding to TGF-βR on the surface of HSC, TGF-β1 can form ligand receptor complex, activate Smad2 and Smad3, transfer signals into the nucleus, and then induce transcription of target genes, like Collagen I and α-SMA [42]. Thus, reducing the production or activation of TGF-β1 or blocking the TGF-β1/Smads signaling pathway is an important way to alleviate liver fibrosis. Our results showed that RvD1 dose-dependently inhibited the expression of TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3, while the level of HSC activation marker α-SMA was decreased and the expression of ECM components was decreased. These evidences suggest that RvD1 inhibits HSC activation by inhibiting TGF-β1/Smads signaling pathway, thereby alleviating liver fibrosis. The TGF-β1/Smads signaling pathway is regulated by SIRT1 to a certain extent. Xia et al. [16] have demonstrated that SIRT1 activation ameliorates rhesus monkey liver fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway. In this study, we observed that after the intervention of SIRT1 inhibitor EX527, the activity of SIRT1 activated by RvD1 was decreased in LX-2 cells, while the expressions of TGF-β1, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 inhibited by RvD1 were upregulated. Therefore, the regulation of SIRT1/TGF-β1/Smads signaling pathway may be the key for RvD1 to improve liver fibrosis.

There are some limitations in our study. First of all, since HSC activation is the core mechanism of liver fibrosis, this research focuses on the effect of RvD1 on HSCs. However, other cells, such as hepatocytes, may also be involved in the anti-fibrotic effect of RvD1, and it remains unclear whether the specific mechanism is related to the regulation of SIRT1. Secondly, although a histologically proven model of typical liver fibrosis was established in this study, advanced cirrhosis could not be achieved with a 6-week CCl4 intervention. Further work may be needed to fully elucidate the effects of RvD1 on liver fibrosis at advanced stage.

Conclusions

In summary, the results of this study suggest that RvD1 has a protective effect on CCl4-induced liver fibrosis in mice. RvD1 inhibits liver inflammation and HSC activation, thereby blocking fibrotic processes, which may be attributed to SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and TGF-β1/Smads pathways. Based on these findings, RvD1 could be considered as a potentially effective treatment for the prevention of liver fibrosis.

Evaluating the role of serum cystatin C as a circulating biomarker of diabetic retinopathy from a rural Indian population
Authors: Keerthana Raghu, R. Janani Surya, Chitaranjan Mishra, Karan Singh, Nabatika Mohanty, Jitendra Kumar Sahoo, Shiva Prasad Sahoo, Ashok Kumar Singh, Rajiv Raman
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PURPOSE:This study aimed to evaluate serum cystatin C as a potential biomarker for diabetic retinopathy (DR) in a rural Indian population, addressing the urgent need for effective screening tools amidst rising diabetes prevalence.MATERIALS AND METHODS:A cross-sectional study recruited 112 patients with diabetes mellitus from Sambalpur, Odisha, India, categorized into groups with and without DR. Serum cystatin C levels were measured alongside clinical and demographic parameters, using established diagnostic methods.RESULTS:Patients with DR exhibited significantly higher serum cystatin C levels compared to those without DR (mean: 0.99 ± 0.50 mg/L vs. 0.80 ± 0.36 mg/L, P = 0.027). Adjusted analysis showed a notable association between higher cystatin C tertiles and DR (adjusted odd’s ratio: 7.65, 95% confidence interval: 1.49–39.08, P = 0.01), highlighting its potential as an independent predictor.CONCLUSION:Elevated serum cystatin C levels were strongly associated with the presence of DR, suggesting its utility as a biomarker for early identification and risk stratification in diabetic patients. Integration of cystatin C measurement into routine clinical practice may enhance DR screening and management strategies, offering potential benefits in reducing vision loss and improving patient outcomes. This study underscores the need for further longitudinal research to validate these findings and elucidate the temporal relationship between cystatin C levels and DR progression.

Introduction

In 2021, the estimated number of diabetics worldwide was 463 million, projected to be 643 million by 2030.[1] Diabetes has become a public health epidemic in many developing countries with China and India leading in the numbers of diabetics. In India, the number was more than 74 million in 2021 and is projected to be more than 123 million by 2040.[2] In India, 13% of all diabetics have some stage of diabetic retinopathy (DR).[3] With the huge burden of diabetics and the limited vitreoretina (VR) trained manpower, the screening of all diabetics for the detection of DR remains a challenge.

The role of numerous circulating biomarkers has been verified to be associated with the severity of DR. The biomarkers studied in previous studies include complement factors (CFB and CFH), serpin A4 (kallistatin), α2 macroglobulin (A2m), cystatin C, thrombin (F2), lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2, lipids, leucine-rich α2 glycoprotein1, 8-hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine, afamin, hemoglobin A1C, and apolipoproteins A1 (ApoA1, ApoB, and ApoC3).[4] Out of all these biomarkers, cystatin C has been shown to be a triage test to potentially identify individuals with sight-threatening diabetic retinopathy (STDR). The known risk factors proportionately associated with the increasing stages of DR include age, duration of diabetes, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1C).[456] In the scenario of the challenge of DR screening globally, cystatin C as a potential triage test will help in the detection of diabetics who have a high possibility of developing STDR. DR affects mostly the working-age population and the management of DR poses a significant economic burden on the patient.[7] A study from India showed that the economic burden was significantly higher in patients with STDR compared to non-STDR (INR 31,820 vs. INR 14,356, P < 0.001 over 6 months).[8] Although the guarded visual prognosis and increased cost of management are significant for patients with STDR globally, this needs special mention for the patients from tier-2 cities and rural areas where vitreoretinal facilities are limited, patients have a delayed presentation and logistics related to travel and staying in bigger cities with available VR facilities are important concerns.[9] In this context, establishing the role of certain circulating biomarkers predicting the association with DR will help in the early detection and management of DR. In this study, we tried to evaluate the potential association of serum cystatin C as a biomarker in different stages of DR from a rural Indian population.

Materials and Methods

This was a cross-sectional study, and the data were collected from June 2023 to August 2023. A total of 112 patients were recruited in the study. This study was approved by the institutional review board and conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all participating adult subjects (approval number: VRF/674A-2018-P).

The patients were recruited from two cohorts. The first cohort was the patients with diabetes mellitus (DM) visiting one physician (diabetologist)’s clinic in Sambalpur, Odisha, India, for the management of DM. The patients did not have specific ophthalmic complaints. The second cohort was patients with specific complaints related to DR and visiting the retina clinic of Trilochan Netralaya (TN), Sambalpur, Odisha, India. The first Cohort had 57 patients, and the second cohort had 55 patients. The patients in the physician’s clinic were examined by the physician, for systemic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension (HTN), and hyperlipidemia. Fundus examination for the diagnosis and staging of DR was done with indirect ophthalmoscopy (IDO), by a fellowship-trained VR surgeon with 7 years of post-fellowship experience. The patients visiting TN were evaluated and managed by the same VR surgeon, including slit-lamp examination, IDO, fundus Photo (Canon CX-1 Hybrid Digital Mydriatic/Non-Mydriatic Retinal Camera [Canon Medical Systems USA, Inc.]), optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging by Cirrus HD-OCT 500 (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Dublin, CA) OCT machine, anti-VEGF injections, and VR surgeries with a vitrectomy machine (Stellaris PC [Bausch & Lomb]) as appropriate. The severity of DR was done by fundoscopy examination, and diabetic macular edema (DME) was documented with OCT. The serum cystatin C tests were done in a single machine (Mispa-i2 semi-automated specific protein analyzer, Agappe Diagnostics Ltd, x/588-CB, Block No. 32, KINFRA small industrial park, Nellad, Cochin - 686 669, Kerala, India). The laboratory staff were blinded from the clinical data. The systemic parameters and the ophthalmic parameters were documented in Excel Sheets. During the study period, out of all the patients with DM who visited the physician’s clinic and out of all the patients who visited the retina clinic of TN, only those patients who agreed to undergo the blood tests including serum cystatin C were included in the study. Needless to say, the patients who denied or did not get the blood test done, were not included in the study. None of the patients of the first cohort was included in the second cohort. All were new unique patients.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using statistical software (SPSS for Windows, version 20.0; SPSS Inc,Chicago, IL, USA). Results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and as percentages for categorical variables. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of the data. Except for systolic blood pressure (BP), triglyceride, very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), and blood urea, all other variables were normally distributed. The Student’s t-test was used to test the significance for variables with normal distribution, and the Mann–Whitney U-test was used for those not distributed normally. χ2-test was used to compare proportions among groups. Regression analysis was done to calculate the odd’s ratio (OR) for the cystatin C association with DR after adjusting for covariates such as age, gender, duration of diabetes, HbA1c, HTN, and eGFR. A P ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Out of the 57 patients of the first cohort (patients from the physician’s clinic), 9 patients had DR and out of 55 patients in cohort 2 (patients from TN), 42 had DR.

Discussion

This cross-sectional study aimed to explore the relationship between serum cystatin C levels and DR in patients with DM within the rural Indian population. The most notable finding of this study is the significantly higher serum cystatin C levels in patients with DR. This result is similar to the previous studies.[1011] Cystatin C, a cysteine protease inhibitor, is recognized as a marker of renal function and has recently been implicated in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular and retinal diseases.[11] Elevated cystatin C levels might reflect early renal dysfunction, which is common in patients with DR due to the shared microvascular pathology in both diabetic nephropathy and retinopathy.

The strong association between the highest tertile of cystatin C levels and the presence of DR (OR 8.83, 95% CI: 1.47–53.15, P = 0.01), even after adjusting for confounders, underscores the potential of cystatin C as a biomarker for DR. This finding is consistent with recent studies done in Indian population that have identified highest tertile of cystatin C as an independent predictor of DR severity. This study by PramodKumar et al. highlighted measuring serum cystatin C can serve as an effective biomarker for triaging patients at high risk for severe DR (STDR) and diabetic kidney disease (DKD). With a cystatin C cut-point of ≥1.11 mg/L accurately identifying 96.8% of individuals with STDR and DKD, this biomarker can help prioritize patients for urgent retinal screening and specialized care.[10]

A prior study involving patients from India and the UK concluded that serum cystatin C had good power of discrimination to differentiate between STDR and no DR.[4] This current study involving real-world patients from a rural Indian population further validates the findings of the prior study. Further, the prior study analyzed the stored and freeze-thawed serum samples, whereas the current study analyzed the serum samples on the spot without freeze-thawing. However, both studies agree with the fact that future studies involving patients from different grades of DR with and without DME will yield robust results.

The mean serum cystatin-C was significantly higher in the DR group than in the no-DR group. However, when examining mean cystatin C levels across the different stages of DR (mild NPDR, moderate NPDR, severe NPDR, and PDR), no significant differences were found (P = 0.07). These results suggest that while cystatin C levels are associated with the presence of DR, they do not show substantial variation with the progression of DR stages. This result contrasts with findings from other studies that reported significant variations in cystatin C levels across different DR stages.[410] These studies suggest that cystatin C might reflect the progression of DR more closely. Potential reasons for this discrepancy include differences in study design or sample size, as well as variations in measurement techniques. These contrasting results highlight the need for additional research to further explore the role of cystatin C in DR. Future studies should address these variables to better understand the biomarker’s relationship with DR severity and progression.

In the current study, the level of Hb was significantly lower in the DR group. This is consistent with the findings of previous studies. Bahar et al. found that diabetes patients with retinopathy had lower levels of Hb.[12] As per Wang et al., the presence and severity of DKD were associated with increased severity of DR. Presence of anemia with DKD further aggravated DR. Correction of anemia had a protective effect on DR.[13] Li and Chen showed that a high level of Hb has a protective role for DR.[14] These findings support the fact that measurement of Hb as a routine serological marker may help in the early diagnosis, monitoring of disease progression, and better management of DR.

The mean HDL was significantly higher in the DR group in this current study. The association of HDL and DR has been variable as per the literature. As per Xu et al., patients with high HDL have a higher risk of DR. There was a positive correlation between HDL and DR with an inverted U-shaped curve of the correlation.[15] Sasso et al. found a positive correlation between HDL and DR, with an increase in the severity of DR with the rise in HDL levels.[16] Morton et al. studied the association between HDL and DR and found that there was no association between them.[17] On the contrary, Popescu and Moța found that there was a negative correlation between HDL and DR.[18] Xu et al. have attributed this heterogeneity among various studies to the differences in the study design, study sample, and effect of confounding factors.[19]

The findings of this study supported the fact that patients with DR had higher cystatin C, low Hb, and high HDL levels than those without DR. Hb and HDL are regular blood tests those can be done in any peripheral setup and hence should be included in the routine blood testing of diabetics.

These findings have several important clinical implications. First, measuring serum cystatin C levels could help in the early identification of patients at higher risk for DR. Based on the study done by PramodKumar et al., patients with cystatin C ≥1.11 mg/L should undergo more frequent retinal screening, with early referrals to ophthalmologists for those at high risk.[10] Considering the financial burden on the patients with STDR and the risk of vision loss from STDR, the need for a triage serological test to prioritize the patients at risk of vision loss due to DR has been emphasized in previous studies.[19] General practitioners could use the cutoff value to triage patients, ensuring timely interventions for those at greatest risk of vision loss. Regular monitoring of related comorbidities such as anemia and dyslipidemia, alongside patient education on glycemic control, would further enhance DR management in resource-limited areas. This approach is particularly crucial in low- and middle-income countries like India, where comprehensive retinal screening for all diabetes patients is not always feasible. By integrating cystatin C measurement into routine blood tests, general physicians and diabetologists can more effectively manage DR, potentially reducing the financial burden and mitigating the risk of vision loss.

Second, the study highlights the importance of comprehensive evaluation and management of patients with DM, including regular monitoring of lipid levels, to mitigate the risk of DR. Finally, integrating cystatin C measurement into routine clinical practice could improve the screening, early detection, and management of DR, potentially leading to better patient outcomes.

However, the study is not without limitations. First, the study’s cross-sectional design limits the ability to infer causality between elevated serum cystatin C levels and DR. Longitudinal studies are needed to confirm these findings and to better understand the temporal relationship between cystatin C levels and the development of DR. Second, the relatively small sample size and the single-center design may limit the generalizability of the results. Future studies with larger, more diverse populations are required to validate these findings. Third, in our study, patients from the physician clinic were examined by a fellowship-trained VR surgeon with extensive experience using IDO without fundus photography and OCT. The expertise of VR surgeons significantly reduces the likelihood of underdiagnosis. IDO by trained professionals has been recognized as a promising and successful screening strategy for DR in resource-limited settings.[20] Furthermore, there are studies indicating that expert ophthalmologists using IDO have successfully detected peripheral DR lesions, which may be missed otherwise.[21] However, the addition of fundus photography and OCT imaging would have likely improved diagnostic accuracy and aided in further management.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates a significant association between elevated serum cystatin C levels and the presence of diabetic retinopathy in patients with DM. The findings suggest that serum cystatin C could serve as a valuable biomarker for DR, helping in the triage of diabetics at risk of development of DR and aiding in the early detection and management of this potentially blinding complication. Further research is necessary to establish the clinical utility of cystatin C in the routine monitoring of diabetic patients.

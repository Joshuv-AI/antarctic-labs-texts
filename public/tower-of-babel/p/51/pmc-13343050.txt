Translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation study of health instruments in Bangla: protocol for a systematic review of adherence to international standards
Authors: Irfan Nowroze Noor, Nawshin Islam, Sumaira Alam, Zerin Shabnam Rahman, M M Nahid Al Rakib, Sumaiya Samad Deepa, Deb Dulal Dey Parag, Mohammad Mostafizur Rahman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTo preserve the validity and reliability of health instruments like patient-reported outcome measures across linguistic and cultural contexts, translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation must be conducted using standardised, rigorous methodologies. Although international standards proposed by the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) and the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) are available, adherence to these guidelines in Bangla-language instrument studies has not been systematically evaluated. This systematic review aims to identify, synthesise and critically appraise Bangla-language translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation studies of health instruments for adherence to recommended methodological approaches, translation and cross-cultural adaptation guidelines and psychometric properties.Methods and analysisIn this study, we will search three databases, including Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed and EMBASE via Ovid, from 1 February 2026 to 1 March 2026 to retrieve published articles on translation, adaptation and psychometric validation of health-related instruments into Bangla. Two reviewers will independently conduct title and abstract screening, full-text eligibility assessment, data extraction, evaluation of translation and cross-cultural adaptation quality and assessment of psychometric properties. The certainty of evidence will be graded. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus, with the consultation of a third reviewer when necessary. Methodological quality and psychometric properties will be assessed based on the COSMIN Risk of Bias (RoB) checklist and COSMIN standards. Evaluation of translation and cross-cultural adaptation will be based on the extended ISPOR guideline. COSMIN-Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach will be used to rate the certainty of evidence.Ethics and disseminationThis study is a systematic review protocol, exclusively on published literature and publicly available data, and does not involve direct interaction with human participants or the collection of individual participant-level data. Therefore, ethical approval is not applicable. We expect to share our findings through national and/or international platforms. Moreover, the results will be published in a peer-reviewed journal. We anticipate that the result of this study will contribute to recommending a standardised roadmap for researchers, especially for conducting translation, validation and cross-cultural adaptation studies of health-related instruments in Bangla.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251250949.

Introduction

Measuring health outcomes is an essential component for both scientific research and clinical practice. These health measurement instruments are commonly known as patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). With the rising agreement and acceptance of shared clinical decision-making, the use of PROMs has grabbed greater attention than in the past.1 Clinicians and researchers design their plans for subsequent treatment, screening or diagnostic procedures based on those PROMs.2 As a result, these measurements must be as accurate as possible and have the innate ability to replicate the same outcome in a comparable population under the same circumstances.3 Utilisation of any measurement tool without the assurance of the measurement quality is not only a waste of resources but also unethical.4 The situation becomes more complex when researchers or clinicians attempt to use an instrument among a population speaking different languages and of different cultures, rather than the original language and culture in which the instrument was developed. Nevertheless, these attempts are essential for integrating contemporary methods or techniques into their practice. Recommendations on standardised procedures are available to ensure the integrity of measurement when used in another target language.

Authors advocated that careful methodological rigour is essential in translating and adapting instruments to ensure semantic and conceptual equivalence of the instrument in both the original and target languages. Yet, translation and cross-cultural validation are frequently under-prioritised in protocols, which may weaken the reliability and validity of the adapted tool.5 Globally, researchers recommend conducting rigorous translation, cultural adaptation, and psychometric testing aligned with internationally accepted standards before using PROMs in languages other than the original.6 7

In a resource-constrained setting like Bangladesh, PROMs pose a significant scope and, therefore, can be recommended especially for primary healthcare settings. Over the last two decades, several domain-specific instruments have been translated and validated in Bangla.811 Utilisation of those instruments demands a certain quality of the Bangla version in comparison to the original language. However, these efforts are fragmented and considerably vary in methodological quality.6 12 Therefore, there is a need for a comprehensive synthesis that has yet to be mapped, including which Bangla PROMs exist or how their measurement properties meet global benchmarks. COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) and International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) provide the most widely endorsed frameworks for evaluating these processes.13 14

The COSMIN Risk of Bias (RoB) checklist can appraise the methodological quality of those types of studies, and COSMIN standards are used to evaluate the quality of psychometric properties of an instrument.15 Parallelly, guidelines proposed by Cruchinho et al, which extend and operationalise ISPOR recommendations, can assess the quality of translation and the cross-cultural adaptation process.14 16 17 Furthermore, the certainty of evidence will be rated based on the COSMIN-Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (COSMIN-GRADE) approach.13 Together, these frameworks support a comprehensive appraisal of both the methodological robustness of included studies and the validity of the adapted instruments.

Therefore, this systematic review aims to critically appraise the Bangla-language translation, cross-cultural adaptation, and psychometric validation studies of health instruments for adherence to recommended methodological approaches, translation and cross-cultural adaptation guidelines, and psychometric properties. The methodological quality will be assessed by using the COSMIN RoB checklist. In addition, the extent of alignment between reported translation and cross-cultural adaptation procedures and internationally recommended ISPOR standards will be evaluated. Furthermore, the study will assess reported psychometric properties against COSMIN criteria for good measurement properties. Finally, the certainty of the evidence of the selected studies will be graded according to the COSMIN-GRADE approach. This comprehensive evaluation will inform a future roadmap for Bangla-language instrument translation, cross-cultural adaptation and validation studies.

Methods

Study design

This systematic review will retrieve published references aimed at extracting data on translation, cross-cultural adaptation, and psychometric validation of a health instrument in Bangla. We will solely concentrate on tools that have been validated in the Bangladeshi community to assess any physical, psychological or behavioural health outcomes reported by the patients. The study’s goal is to assess how well such research corresponds to international standards in terms of methodology. We will use the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 checklist to report our planned survey of the literature.18 Nevertheless, the authors will use the PRISMA-P 2015 checklist to report the protocol for the study (adherence to PRISMA-P guidelines is documented in online supplemental file S1).19 The protocol for this systematic review was registered in PROSPERO (ID No: CRD420251250949).

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

This systematic review will include (1) studies done among Bangladeshi Bangla-speaking individuals, (2) instruments that measure people’s health outcomes in clinical, academic, community or healthcare contexts, (3) published articles describing the process of translation, cross-cultural adaptation and/or psychometric validation (evaluation of psychometric properties) of health-related instruments into Bangla, (4) studies evaluating the Bangla version of health instruments, scales, (5) studies on instruments measuring any physical, psychological or behavioural outcomes, (6) studies measuring psychometric properties (content validity, structural validity, internal consistency, reliability, measurement error, criterion validity, hypothesis testing for construct validity, cross-cultural validity and responsiveness) and (7) methodological studies, validation studies, cross-sectional studies with translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation.

Exclusion criteria

The review will exclude papers if (1) the study does not include Bangladeshi Bangla-speaking individuals as respondents, (2) any study done among the respondents outside healthcare, community, academic or clinical settings, (3) the study does not report on the process of translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation (evaluation of psychometric properties), (4) studies using the Bangla version of the health instrument only to measure an outcome and (5) studies reporting either a qualitative or mixed-method design for reporting translation, cross-cultural adaptation and validation.

Publication criteria

The review will exclude an identified potential study if (1) the study is not an original article (ie, biographies, editorials, case reports, or conference abstract), (2) it is a secondary study, such as a review or systematic review. On the other hand, (1) articles published within the last 10 years (January 2015 to December 2025), (2) available in full text, (3) published in peer-reviewed journals and (4) published in English will be considered for the intended systematic review.

Search strategy

The search process will follow the University of Oxford recommendation for finding PROMs and the PubMed search filter guideline by Terwee et al, to yield search terms as keywords to initiate the process.20 21 Considering those terms, other potential search terms will be identified and applied. We will perform a systematic and comprehensive search in three English databases, as per the agreement of Cochrane methodology and its recommendations of including at least two databases for the systematic search.22 23 Depending on recommendations, population of interest, and content specificity, the research team will conduct the search activity from 1 February 2026 to 1 March 2026. We will search three databases: Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed and EMBASE. After the final selection of the papers from databases, their references will be screened for any more eligible literature. Our search in PubMed, Ovid MEDLINE and EMBASE databases will consist of all fields, TIAB and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms. Boolean operators will be used to combine these terms. The search concept of the review will consider core concept blocks in PICO format, based on our research question.

The blocks include (1) Population (P): Bangladeshi, Bangla-speaking individuals in clinical, academic, community or healthcare contexts on whom the health instrument was validated, (2) Intervention (measurement) (I): translation, cross-cultural adaptation, and psychometric validation in Bangla, (3) Comparison (C): internationally recommended guideline for translation, cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation (this concept block will not be searched as the standards are prefixed: recommendations by Cruchinho et al, and COSMIN guidelines, respectively) and15 17 24 (4) Outcome (O): measuring any of the psychometric properties, structural validity, internal consistency, reliability, measurement error, criterion validity, hypothesis testing for construct validity, cross-cultural validity and responsiveness. The planned initial search strategies for all the databases are provided in online supplemental file S2: section-A. We will select the search terms manually to avoid any potential bias from the automatic term mapping process of different databases. Results from the search will be incorporated into an EndNote library. During the search process, filters will be applied and adjusted based on the particular database. But in all cases, the common filters for the criteria of published articles will be applied.

Study screening

After searching through all the databases, the identified papers will be included in the EndNote software. Duplicate papers will be removed before screening. Initially, two researchers will independently screen titles and abstracts (SA and MMNAR). After the initial screening, the full text of the selected papers will be evaluated independently by two different researchers for the eligibility criteria (INN and NI). In both phases, any disagreement in selection will be resolved by consensus in the presence of a third researcher (ZSR and SSD, respectively). All the references for the selected papers will be checked for any other published articles as per the eligibility criteria of this review. Data extraction

Before data extraction, the data extraction table will be finalised based on a discussion and consensus within the research team. As per the recommended data extraction process for systematic review, two researchers (SA and ZSR) will extract data independently from the included papers, using a predefined data extraction form (online supplemental file S2: section-B), considering the data extraction table.23 Any difference in decision-making will be resolved in consultation with the third researcher (DDDP). The extracted data will be presented in tables: (1) General information (demographic characteristics) about the included studies (author’s name, year of publication, instrument name, construct measured, target population, original language, mode of administration, recall period, number of items/subscales, scoring systems and response options), (2) For assessing the study sample characteristics, we will find out the sample size, mean age, gender distribution and study setting, (3) Summary on methodological quality of the included studies based on the COSMIN RoB checklist, (4) Summary on quality of translation and cross-cultural adaptation based on Cruchinho et al recommendation, (5) Summary of the quality of psychometric property based on COSMIN standards and (6) Summary of certainty of evidence based on the COSMIN-GRADE approach (all tables are available as online supplemental file S5).

Methodological quality of the included studies

In this systematic review, we will use the COSMIN RoB Checklist to assess the methodological quality of the included studies. This review will evaluate (1) structural validity, (2) internal consistency, (3) cross-cultural validity/measurement invariance, (4) reliability, (5) measurement error, (6) criterion validity, (7) construct validity and (8) responsiveness.15 Each of the standards will be assessed through items listed in the COSMIN RoB checklist, through some items (varying from 3 to 35 items). Individual property will be assessed separately by assigning a five-level scale rating. The levels will range from ‘very good’, ‘adequate’, ‘doubtful’, ‘inadequate’ or ‘not applicable’ according to COSMIN standards. The checklist will be applied selectively to measurement properties evaluated in each study. Domains not assessed will be rated as ‘not applicable’. An overall rating of methodological property will be assigned to each of the categories and shown in figure 2. Two of the researchers (SA and ZSR) will rate each of the categories independently based on the worst score count method as proposed by the COSMIN RoB checklist.15 Any disagreement will be resolved with consensus between the two researchers or adjudication by a third researcher (INN). Finally, the result will be displayed as a risk of bias summary table with an explanation of the strengths and limitations of each study.

Quality of the translation and cross-cultural adaptation of included studies

The translation quality of the included studies will be critically appraised based on Cruchinho et al’s recommendations (2024 Reference Standard).17 In this paper, the authors proposed recommendations through their methodological review of 42 available documents on translation, cross-cultural adaptation and validation studies. Based on the guidelines, we will evaluate the quality of the translation and cross-cultural adaptation of included studies, considering the following domains: (1) preparation and permission, (2) forward translation, (3) forward translation synthesis, (4) back translation, (5) harmonisation, (6) decentring or dual focus strategy, (7) pretesting (cognitive debriefing and expert panel pretest) and (8) documentation and transparency. Based on the recommended criteria, the domains will be scored from 0 to 3. This is only a descriptive scoring and is based on prefixed criteria for each domain (online supplemental file S2: section-C). In each step, if the recommended criteria are fully completed with comprehensive documentation, the step will get ‘3’. If the step has been completed with a limitation but the rationale has been documented, the score will be ‘2’. If the step is mentioned but unclear with weak execution and missing key information, or the step has been completed with limitations but not documented the rationale, the score will be ‘1’. Finally, one step will get a ‘0’ score if the step is absent or not described at all. The total score will range from 0 to 24. According to the final scoring, the translation and cross-cultural adaptation process will be graded. The grading will be based on the total score of eight steps: high quality translation and adaptation (20–24), moderate quality (14–19), low quality (8–13) and insufficient quality (0–7). Two researchers (SSD and MMNAR) will independently score each step of translation and cross-cultural adaptation. In case of any disagreement, the final score and grading will be given after bilateral discussion and consensus. If needed, the third researcher (NI) will be consulted.

Psychometric measurement attributes appraisal and data synthesis

We will assess the quality of psychometric measurement attributes of all the included studies according to COSMIN standards. This tool can evaluate the strengths of the measurement property of health measurement instruments. In this review, we will assess the measurement attributes of an instrument in two principal domains: (1) internal structure (ie, structural validity and internal consistency) and (2) other measurement properties or remaining attributes (ie, test–retest reliability, measurement error, criterion validity and responsiveness).24 We will follow three steps to assess the quality of the psychometric properties of included studies in accordance with the COSMIN guidelines. Each of the studies will be evaluated and rated completely by two of the researchers (SA and ZSR) independently according to updated criteria for good measurement properties.15 24 25 The rating will be sufficient (+), insufficient (–), inconsistent (±) or indeterminate (?). In case of any disagreement between the researchers, the rating will be resolved either by discussion or in consensus with a third researcher (INN).

The result of the measured property will be synthesised qualitatively for each of the instruments and will be compared against the criteria for good measurement properties.24 The overall rating for each instrument property will be determined by aggregating the individual scores derived from the COSMIN criteria. A property will be rated sufficient (+) when all corresponding scores are sufficient, and insufficient (−) when all scores are insufficient. When the scores are mixed, the property will be rated as inconsistent (±), and the underlying reasons for this inconsistency will be reported. Consequently, indeterminate (?) will be the rating of the property if that is not measured or described by the study.

Certainty and grading of the evidence

The certainty of evidence for each measurement property will be evaluated using the COSMIN-GRADE approach, which adapts the GRADE principles for systematic reviews of measurement instruments. Each measuring property and instrument will have its own overall certainty rating, which will be categorised as high, moderate, low or very low. Certainty ratings will be determined by considering four factors: risk of bias, imprecision, indirectness and inconsistency. Publication bias will not be assessed, as the COSMIN-GRADE approach does not recommend assessing it.13 24 26

Risk of Bias

Risk of Bias will be determined through the COSMIN RoB checklist as proposed in earlier sections. Certainty will be downgraded for when methodological limitations are identified, including inadequate sample size, inappropriate statistical methods or incomplete reporting, following the COSMIN ‘worst score counts’ principle.

Imprecision

Imprecision will be measured qualitatively as recommended by COSMIN. It will be judged based on (1) adequacy of sample size for specific analysis (e.g., test–retest analysis or exploratory factor analysis (ECAs) or confirmatory factor analysis (CFAs)); (2) availability of reported confidence intervals (not only point estimates); (3) statistical robustness (e.g., reliance on Cronbach’s α only (without ω) or CFA model modifications without cross-validation). Downgrading for imprecision will be considered for studies that employed borderline or small sample sizes for factor analysis or reliability testing, failed to report confidence intervals for key estimates (e.g., Cronbach’s alpha, Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICCs), factor loadings) or relied on limited statistical evidence. One level of downgrading will be applied for serious imprecision, and two levels for very serious imprecision, defined as cases where statistical uncertainty is substantial.

Indirectness

Thereafter, indirectness will be evaluated according to the intended application of the instrument by examining (1) population indirectness, (2) construct indirectness and (3) setting or context indirectness. Certainty will be downgraded when evidence is found to be derived from populations that differed from the target users, when validation is conducted in settings not representative of the intended context, or when construct equivalence is uncertain. Indirectness will be considered particularly relevant when translated instruments lack qualitative assessment involving the target population.

Inconsistency

Finally, in line with COSMIN guidance, inconsistency will be assessed only when multiple studies evaluate the same measurement property for the same instrument. As each included Bangla instrument is expected to be evaluated in a single study, inconsistency will be considered not applicable and will not contribute to downgrading certainty in accordance with the COSMIN-GRADE approach.

Overall certainty rating

For each measurement property, downgrading decisions across applicable factors will be combined to derive an overall certainty rating. All grading decisions will be applied conservatively and transparently to reflect the strengths and limitations of the available evidence. The grading will be done by two independent researchers (SA and ZSR). Any dispute will be resolved through a discussion between the two researchers, with a third researcher (MMR) consulted if they fail to reach a consensus.

Patient and public involvement

There will be no involvement of patients or the public in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this study.

Ethics and dissemination

This research is a systematic review that is based only on publicly accessible data and published literature. It does not entail direct interaction with human subjects or the need for primary data collection. Additionally, no personally identifiable participant-level data will be used. Thus, the study will not require formal ethical approval. We expect to share our findings with national and/or international platforms. Moreover, we plan to publish the results in a peer-reviewed journal.

Strengths and limitations

The protocol will adhere to the PRISMA-P 2015 checklist, and the final review will adhere to PRISMA 2020 reporting standards. The study quality and the psychometric properties will be assessed using the COSMIN RoB checklist and the COSMIN standards, respectively. The translation and cross-cultural adaptation process of the incorporated studies will be analysed and synthesised based on the standards recommended by Cruchinho et al. The certainty of the evidence will be analysed through the COSMIN-GRADE approach. Limiting the search to three databases and excluding hand-searching and grey literature may result in missing relevant studies and introduce publication bias. Additionally, the authors intend to apply COSMIN and ISPOR principles to conduct a structured appraisal. Therefore, for studies that do not sufficiently offer methodological and reporting details for psychometric and translation-quality assessment, the appraisal might not be feasible. Moreover, we included only those studies published in English. Thus, the study may miss information about papers published in any other language. However, using the University of Oxford PROMs guidance and the PubMed search filter developed by Terwee et al to refine search terms will strengthen the study.

Discussion

This study aims to critically evaluate the methodological rigour of the selected studies and how well the Bangla version of health-related instruments is intended to measure a health outcome in comparison to internationally accepted standards. To the best of our knowledge, this study will be the first attempt to systematically evaluate the adherence of those published studies to international standards following the PRISMA-P 2015 checklist.18 The registration of the protocol under PROSPERO will, in itself, enhance the quality of the evidence.19 The COSMIN RoB checklist will be used to evaluate the methodological quality of the selected studies.15 24 25 The translation and cross-cultural adaptation process of the incorporated studies will be analysed and synthesised based on Cruchinho et al’s recommended standards, which were proposed through a methodological review in 2024, extending the ISPOR guidelines.14 16 17 Furthermore, we will also assess the quality of psychometric properties of the instruments under study based on COSMIN standards. Finally, the certainty of evidence will be documented using the COSMIN-GRADE approach.13 24 26 Where possible, tables will present the consistency of results across studies. Due to methodological heterogeneity, conducting a meta-analysis will be inappropriate and will not be attempted. To our knowledge, this systematic review will be one of the most comprehensive syntheses of Bangla-language health instrument translation, cross-cultural adaptation, and psychometric validation studies, using internationally recognised standards. By systematically evaluating methodological quality and alignment with ISPOR and COSMIN guidelines, the review will identify methodological strengths, gaps, and inconsistencies, and offer an evidence-informed roadmap to improve the quality, transparency and comparability of future Bangla-language instrument development and validation research.

Additional outcomes

During this study, we will create a comprehensive database regarding the Bangla version of the health-related instruments, along with their feasibility and acceptability for the intended measurement. In addition, this study may provide a roadmap to help Bangladeshi researchers improve their future work on translation, validation and cross-cultural adaptation studies of health-related instruments in Bangla, considering all accepted international standards.

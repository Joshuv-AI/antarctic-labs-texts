The American Association for Thoracic Surgery medical student opportunity scholarship in its fifth year: Are we making progress?
Authors: Marc R Moon
Published: 2026

AATS foundation medical school opportunity scholarship recipients at a recent Annual Meeting of the Association.

Central Message

The AATS Medical Student Scholarship began in 2021. The “return on investment” is 90% success in students pursuing thoracic surgery and a significant increase in Underrepresented in Medicine trainees.

In 2021, the American Association for Thoracic Surgery (AATS) Board of Directors promised a 5-year, $1.2-million commitment to develop and support the AATS Medical Student Diversity, now Opportunity Scholarship, with the backing of the AATS Foundation.1 The results have been nothing short of extraordinary. In 4 short years, the program has had a dramatic positive influence on the specialty of cardiothoracic (CT) surgery. Fifty-six scholarships have been awarded to 35 recipients totaling $980,000. Of the 24 individuals who graduated from medical school during that time, 14 are in integrated CT 6-year programs (CT i-6) or 4/3 early specialization programs (focused CT training after 3-4 years of general surgery), 6 chose general surgery residency programs to prepare for a career in general thoracic surgery, and 1 is pursuing a PhD in cardiovascular physiology. The positive return from this program has been nearly 90%. In addition, representation has increased in CT i-6 programs by 30% for Hispanic individuals (from 5.3% to 6.9%), 121% for Black individuals (from 2.2% to 4.8%), and 70% for those who up until recently had been categorized as underrepresented in medicine (UIM from 7.5% to 12.8%), numbers that had all remained stagnant during the previous 2 decades (Table 1).2,3 From 2003 to 2025, the Association of American Medical Colleges (AAMC) defined UIM as racial and ethnic populations that are underrepresented in the medical profession relative to their percentage of the general population.4 Powell and colleagues2 reported no change in the percentage of Black or Hispanic individuals in CT i-6 programs between 2006 and 2020. Current AAMC data demonstrate a significant increase in the percentage of UIMs in CT i-6 programs (P = .03).3 Obviously, the AATS Opportunity Scholarship cannot take all the credit for these changes, and there is still a long way to go, but these recent positive trends suggest we should all continue to do our part.

The AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship currently provides up to $11,500 for up to 2 years to support medical students who are planning to pursue a career in CT surgery and are from economically disadvantaged backgrounds or who have overcome significant adversity to pursue a career in medicine.5 The scholarship was originally focused solely on UIMs and through 2024 all the recipients were UIMs, but in 2025 the program was opened up to students of any race or ethnic group. The intent of the scholarship was to lessen financial burdens that can affect ultimate career decisions away from demanding, prolonged training programs such as CT surgery, which has historically impacted a substantially greater percentage of UIM students and those from economically disadvantaged backgrounds.1 Between 10 and 20 scholarships are awarded each year and are intended to help fund living expenses, which average $18,500 per year for medical students, so that students are less inclined to pursue noneducational jobs which can negatively affect class attendance, study efforts, board scores, research opportunities, and work-life integration for both students and their families. Applicants must demonstrate a very strong interest in CT surgery, with clear career goals expressed through a narrative that describes their initial revelation toward the field, previous and planned clinical exposure, and in most cases research contributions. Two letters of support from faculty are also required to confirm the applicant's robust interest in CT surgery. At the conclusion of each year of funding, the recipients submit a report highlighting all activities and the advances achieved and future plans for training.

In September 2025, the AAMC removed the definition of UIM from its website, but that does not mean that this population no longer exists. There are identity groups that have been, if not blatantly excluded from the specialty, have not historically been encouraged to enter the field. Diversity Through Inclusion has been interpreted as a process through which promotion based on identity more so than merit can occur. This is not the case with the AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship. The AATS Opportunity Scholarship promotes Diversity Without Exclusion, encouraging the most qualified of previously underexposed groups, because of social or economic limitations, to explore opportunities from which they may have previously been underrepresented.

Diversity, equity, and inclusion programs have fallen out of favor among some who argue that these programs prioritize identity over merit. That could not be further from reality regarding the AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship. AATS Opportunity Scholarship recipients have all demonstrated exemplary promise and academic aptitude, and the scholarship is intended to help support their initially strong desire to consider CT surgery as a specialty. The scholarship represents a mechanism through which we can encourage the “best and the brightest” to pursue CT surgery over other specialties that have historically been chosen by these particular subgroups of students. The goal for which we are already starting to see a return on our investment is to encourage highly qualified students who would have otherwise not been exposed to CT surgery to pursue CT surgery as their specialty of choice.

When I was honored with the AATS presidency, it was my goal to initiate programs I believed would conscientiously promote those who had historically faced great challenges to pursue a career in our specialty. During my 3 years in the presidential line of succession, it became obvious that these challenges were due in great part to a lack of exposure, a lack of mentorship, and in many cases, prohibitive financial constraints. The AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship was a mechanism through which we could address all three of these limitations to pursuing our field. Not all programs we initiated during my AATS tenure survived the test of time, but the AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship continues to flourish and grow.

I received this heartfelt message from a scholarship recipient last spring: “I do not exaggerate when I say that without this scholarship, I would not be going into cardiothoracic surgery, or even surgery at all.” That bright, accomplished student, who was at the top of his class, could have matched into any specialty he chose. He clearly states that without this scholarship program, he would have done almost anything else. I was thrilled that we were able to draw this phenomenal young man to our specialty by simply providing the modest economic support and mentorship he needed at a vulnerable time to continue his pursuit of CT surgery as a career. Truly remarkable. Through selection of only the most qualified applicants in this highly competitive application process, the quality of our workforce will remain of the highest standards.

As we enter the final year of our 5-year commitment, I will continue to lobby the AATS, AATS Foundation, AATS Members, and the entire CT surgical community to continue their support of the AATS Medical Student Opportunity Scholarship in perpetuity. It is truly remarkable how a modest contribution from the AATS Foundation and those who support the AATS Foundation can alter life's path. Let's not be ordinary. Let's commit to take action and lead the change we want to see in the world. Let's do something to make ourselves feel proud.

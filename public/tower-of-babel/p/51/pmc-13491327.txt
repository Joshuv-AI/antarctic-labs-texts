A Hemodynamic Scoring System for Optimizing Transcatheter Arterialization of the Deep Veins in No-Option Patients With Chronic Limb-Threatening Ischemia
Authors: Michael C Siah, Syed Muhammad Mashhood Ali Bokhari, Anahita Dua, Rick J Powell, Steven Kum, Steven D Abramowitz, Michiel Schreve, Daniel van den Heuvel, Dan G Clair, Mehdi Shishehbor, Ucci Alessandro, Peter A Schneider, Roberto Ferraresi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTranscatheter arterialization of the deep veins (TADV) with LimFlow (Stryker Peripheral Vascular) is an emerging revascularization strategy for patients with high-risk chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI). Variability in outcomes highlights the need for structured tools to guide arteriovenous crossing-site selection and optimize perfusion. We propose an angiographic TADV scoring system to quantify the hemodynamic impact of donor artery selection and evaluate its association with clinical outcomes.MethodsThis retrospective analysis included 43 randomly selected patients from the PROMISE II trial with Rutherford class 5 to 6 no-option CLTI. Pre- and postprocedural angiograms were reviewed, and a TADV score (−4 to +4; lower scores indicating worse residual foot perfusion) was calculated by integrating below-the-knee arterial contribution with crossing-site selection. The primary outcome was change in ischemia-related pain. Secondary outcomes included limb salvage, amputation-free survival, wound healing, and secondary patency. Intraclass correlation coefficient assessed score reliability. Linear regression evaluated associations between TADV score and pain, and Cox regression assessed the relationship between pain and major amputation.ResultsTechnical success was 100%. Mean pain decreased from 5.1 at baseline to 1.4 at 2 years, with median pain reaching 0 by 9 months. At 2 years, limb salvage was 60.7%, amputation-free survival 35.9%, and complete wound healing 62.5%. Intraclass correlation coefficient for the TADV score was 91.7%. Higher TADV scores were associated with lower pain at 1 and 3 months. Each one-point increase in pain increased major amputation risk (hazard ratio, 1.20; 95% CI, 1.02-1.41).ConclusionsThe TADV score correlates with early pain reduction and amputation risk, supporting its potential role in optimizing crossing-site selection and outcomes in no-option CLTI.Keywords: chronic limb-threatening ischemia, limb salvage, transcatheter arterialization of the deep veins

Introduction

Chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI) represents the most advanced stage of peripheral arterial disease and is characterized by ischemic rest pain, nonhealing ulcers, or gangrene.1,2 CLTI is associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, and healthcare utilization. Within this population, a growing subset of up to 20% of patients is classified as having no-option CLTI—in which conventional surgical or endovascular revascularization is not feasible due to diffuse distal disease, absence of suitable targets, or repeated failure of prior interventions.3 A recent contemporary multicenter observational study evaluating standard care in high-risk CLTI reported amputation-free survival (AFS) rates of approximately 37% and limb salvage (LS) rates near 55%, highlighting the unmet need in this population.4

Transcatheter arterialization of the deep veins (TADV) with LimFlow (Stryker Peripheral Vascular) has emerged as an alternative revascularization strategy for no-option patients. Performed percutaneously, the procedure establishes an arteriovenous connection between a proximal artery and a distal vein to enable retrograde delivery of oxygenated arterial blood to the ischemic foot via the venous system.5 Favorable outcomes have been reported from the PROMISE I, II, and UK trials, supporting broader clinical adoption of TADV.5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 Despite these advances, patients often have a frequent need for reintervention and early postoperative challenges such as ischemic pain and delayed wound healing.6

In this study, we introduce a novel TADV scoring system that integrates preprocedural arteriographic assessment with planned donor artery selection to model the hemodynamic impact and potential for ischemia of potential arteriovenous crossing sites. We hypothesize that this structured approach may predict early postoperative pain and correlate with subsequent clinical outcomes, thereby providing a mechanistically grounded tool to support procedural optimization in patients with high-risk CLTI.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study is a retrospective analysis of prospectively enrolled subjects from the PROMISE II trial (NCT03970538), which evaluated clinical outcomes following treatment with TADV and has been previously described.5 PROMISE II is a prospective, multicenter, single-arm study conducted between December 2019 and March 2022 across 20 centers in the United States. The trial enrolled 105 patients with no-option CLTI, defined as Rutherford classification 5 or 6 disease. All patients were screened and adjudicated by an independent committee of vascular specialists to confirm no-option status prior to enrollment.

In this study, 43 patients with complete baseline and post-TADV completion imaging were randomly selected from the PROMISE II study to form the analysis cohort. Pre- and postprocedural angiograms were independently reviewed by 2 experienced evaluators who were blinded to clinical outcomes. Angiographic assessments were performed using a novel scoring rubric developed to evaluate the hemodynamic impact of arteriovenous (AV) crossing sites and their potential association with post-TADV ischemia-related pain.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board at each participating center, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment in the parent trial.

The TADV score

The TADV score provides a structured framework for evaluating the hemodynamic consequences of AV crossing site selection and supporting patient selection and procedural planning. The objectives of the scoring system were 2-fold: (1) to identify patients who may be appropriate candidates for TADV and (2) to guide optimal artery selection for AV crossing to preserve foot perfusion and improve procedural outcomes.

The underlying principle of the scoring system is that creation of an AV fistula substantially decreases the selected artery’s contribution to distal foot perfusion. By integrating both the direction (negative or positive) and magnitude of perfusion impact, the TADV score simulates the hemodynamic consequences of different crossing-site strategies. The composite score ranges from –4 to +4, with lower scores indicating more severely impaired residual foot perfusion and higher scores indicating a crossing-site configuration with more favorable preservation of residual perfusion.

The TADV score workflow involves 3 steps (Central Illustration). In step 1, the contribution of each below-the-knee (BTK) artery’s perfusion is assessed on a semiquantitative scale from 0 to 2. Grade 0 indicates minimal or no risk of ischemia from exclusion of the vessel (proximal CTO), grade 1 reflects moderate perfusion dependence (mid-calf CTO), and grade 2 represents high perfusion dependence and the greatest potential risk of ischemia if that vessel is used as the donor artery utilized for AV crossing (vessel reaches the ankle/foot). In step 2, a negative sign is assigned to the below-the-knee artery selected for AV crossing and a positive sign to the remaining patent vessels. In step 3, the TADV score is calculated by summing across each donor artery option in each row from step 2.

Three-step TADV score workflow for selecting the optimal BTK crossing site. Step 1: each BTK artery is graded from 0 to 2 according to the anticipated risk of foot ischemia if that vessel or its substituting collaterals is occluded at the planned crossing point. The contribution grades of each artery are denoted as X, Y, and Z, respectively. Step 2: the contribution scores assigned to each BTK artery are entered into a scoring matrix that reflects the hemodynamic impact of using each artery as a donor vessel at each potential crossing site; the scoring matrix assigns a negative value to the BTK artery selected for AV crossing and a positive value to the remaining patent vessels. TPT has a negative value for both arteries arising from it (PTA and PER). Step 3: row-wise summation of matrix entries yields a TADV score for each donor artery option, with higher scores indicating more favorable preservation of residual foot perfusion. ATA, anterior tibial artery; BTK, below-the-knee; CTO, chronic total occlusion; PER, peroneal artery; PTA, posterior tibial artery; TPT, tibioperoneal trunk.

To estimate the contribution of each BTK artery to foot perfusion using this scheme, high-definition angiograms of the BTK and foot arteries were required. The assessment of arterial contribution was performed primarily by reviewing the angiographic runs in motion, rather than relying on single static frames, in order to better appreciate contrast filling, collateral pathways, and the temporal sequence of opacification in the distal vascular bed. Angiograms were independently reviewed and graded by 2 evaluators (MS and RF) using the predefined scoring rubric. In cases of scoring discordance, discrepancies were resolved through joint review and consensus discussion. The consistency of scoring among observers (interobserver agreement) was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) to evaluate the reliability and reproducibility of the score.

Examples of TADV score calculation in 3 different BTK anatomical patterns. For each case, preprocedural angiography (left) is used to assign a contribution grade from 0 to 2 for each BTK artery, according to the anticipated risk of foot ischemia if that vessel or its substituting collaterals is occluded at the planned crossing point. These grades are then entered into the scoring matrix (center), where each cell represents the hemodynamic impact of using a given donor artery at a specific crossing site (favorable interactions assigned a positive sign, unfavorable interactions a negative sign). Row-wise summation yields the TADV score for each donor artery option (right). In example 1, isolated ATA involvement results in high scores for PTA and PER (+2), indicating low risk of perfusion loss when crossing through these vessels. In example 2, isolated PTA involvement yields negative scores for TPT and PTA (−2) and positive scores for PER and ATA (+2), indicating that perfusion loss would be most detrimental when crossing through PTA. In example 3, a mixed pattern of disease produces differentiated scores (PTA +3, TPT and PER +1, ATA −1), identifying PTA as the most favorable crossing site and ATA as the least favorable option. BTK, below-the-knee; TPT, tibioperoneal trunk; PTA, posterior tibial artery; PER, peroneal artery; AT, anterior tibial artery.

Clinical outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was change in ischemia-related pain over time. Pain was evaluated on a numeric pain scale rating from 0 to 10, where 0 is no pain. Pain was assessed at baseline and at 1-, 3-, 6-, and 9-month follow-up visits, as well as at 1-year and 2-year intervals.

Secondary outcomes included LS, patient-reported pain score trajectories, overall survival, AFS, wound healing status, loss of secondary patency, incidence of transmetatarsal amputation, and procedural flow measurements. Hemodynamic flow assessments were obtained at 1, 3, and 6 months and at 1- and 2-year follow-up intervals.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline demographic and clinical characteristics. Continuous variables are presented as mean values with corresponding 95% CIs.

Linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate the association between TADV score and patient-reported pain outcomes at each follow-up interval. To assess the relationship between pain severity and major amputation, Cox proportional hazards regression modeling was used. Major amputation events were analyzed in relation to the most recent pain score measurement obtained prior to amputation, death, or censoring.

All statistical analyses were conducted using standard methodologies appropriate for longitudinal clinical data.

A history of endovascular intervention in the target limb was present in 67% of patients. Interobserver agreement

Interobserver agreement for the TADV score was excellent, with an ICC of 91.8%, indicating highly consistent scoring between the 2 independent evaluators.

Clinical outcomes

Primary outcome

Pain scores were recorded at baseline and at 1-, 3-, 6-, and 9-month follow-up visits, as well as at 1- and 2-year intervals (Figure 2). The mean baseline pain score was 5.1 (median, 5.5). Mean pain scores decreased to 3.7 at 1 month and 3.0 at 3 months. At 1 year and 2 years, mean pain scores were 1.5 and 1.4, respectively. Median pain score was 0 at 9 months and remained 0 at 1- and 2-year follow-up.

Box plot depicting mean and median pain scores over 2 years.

Secondary outcomes

The LS rate was 92.4% (95% CI, 84.41-100) at 1 month and 76.5% (95% CI, 64.21-91.21) at 3 months. LS was 70.8% at 6 months, 64.3% at 1 year, and 60.7% at 2 years. Overall survival was 88.1% (95% CI, 78.87-98.47) at 1 month, 85.1% at 3 months, 81.8% at 6 months, 67.5% at 1 year, and 59.1% at 2 years. The AFS rate was 81.4% at 1 month, 65.1% at 3 months, 57.9% at 6 months, 43.4% at 1 year, and 35.9% at 2 years.

Complete wound healing was observed in 7.1% of patients at 3 months, 22.7% at 6 months, 47.1% at 1 year, and 62.5% at 2 years. The proportion of wounds classified as worsening declined from 44.4% at 1 month to 0% at 2 years.

Kaplan–Meier estimates of loss of secondary patency were 7.4% at 1 month, 19.8% at 3 months, 49.3% at 6 months, 68.7% at 1 year, and 81.2% at 2 years. Clinical outcomes are summarized in Table 3.

Regression analysis

Linear regression analysis demonstrated an inverse association between TADV score and pain scores at early follow-up. Statistical significance was observed at 1 month (P = .03) and 3 months (P = .004) (Figure 3). Cox proportional hazard analysis demonstrated that each 1-point increase in pain score prior to major amputation, death, or censoring was associated with a hazard ratio of 1.20 for major amputation (95% CI, 1.02-1.41; P = .03).

Discussion

This study demonstrates that an angiography-based TADV score can capture the hemodynamic consequences of crossing-site selection and translate them into clinically meaningful differences in early symptom relief and limb outcomes. In this study, the TADV score demonstrated excellent interobserver agreement (ICC of 91.8%), supporting its reliability and suggesting that the scoring system can be applied consistently across different observers in routine clinical practice.

The TADV score provides a practical framework to quantify how different BTK crossing strategies are likely to redistribute flow and affect distal perfusion. By integrating both the contribution of each BTK artery to foot perfusion and the hemodynamic impact of using that vessel as a donor, the score can help to select a configuration in which tissue perfusion is preserved versus one that would precipitate additional ischemic burden. In our cohort, higher TADV scores were associated with lower pain scores at 1 and 3 months, indicating that more favorable crossing-site configurations translate into less early ischemic pain after TADV. Additionally, higher residual pain was independently associated with a higher risk of major amputation, with a 20% increase in hazard for each 1-point increase in pain score. These correlations suggest that the TADV score captures the balance between flow diverted into the venous circuit and flow preserved for the remaining arterial bed and that this balance is directly reflected in the patient’s early symptomatic response and subsequent limb prognosis. In practical terms, a crossing strategy with a higher TADV score appears to create an arterialization circuit that results in less incremental ischemia, leading to less pain, and a greater likelihood of achieving sustained limb salvage and wound healing.

The TADV score may have the potential to be optimized by additional interventions. The TADV score in this study was derived from baseline angiographic assessments in patients classified as no-option, meaning that conventional revascularization was not feasible. However, even in this setting, limited endovascular revascularization attempts may still be undertaken with the goal of achieving a partial, and often transient, increase in foot inflow, at least to the ankle level. Although these interventions are frequently prone to early restenosis or reocclusion, they can nonetheless provide temporarily incremental arterial supply during the vulnerable post-TADV period and thereby support tissue perfusion in the foot. It may be beneficial, whenever technically possible and clinically reasonable, to pursue conventional revascularization attempts before TADV, even when long-term patency is unlikely, with the primary objective to temporarily maximize the contribution of BTK vessels to foot perfusion. Such procedures may modify the TADV score in a favorable direction and enable a crossing-site strategy that minimizes additional compromise of distal perfusion while potentially improving the likelihood of overall procedural success.

Future research on the prospective use of the TADV score may help to identify patients in whom TADV may be contraindicated by helping to identify unfavorable anatomical scenarios. In patients with critically limited BTK perfusion, choosing a donor artery that contributes substantially to the last remaining inflow is expected to create an acute ischemic insult superimposed on chronic ischemia, with a high risk of severe postprocedural pain, early tissue loss, and clinical failure. In such cases, a low TADV score may prompt reconsideration of the revascularization strategy rather than proceeding with standard TADV.

Similarly, the TADV score may provide information into the timing of TADV within advancing disease and should be studied further. Our data suggest that TADV performed while at least part of the BTK arterial tree still provides meaningful perfusion—allowing for a crossing configuration with a favorable TADV score—results in early pain relief, durable limb salvage, and wound healing. By contrast, when BTK disease has advanced to the point that all major tibial vessels are critically diseased or provide only marginal residual inflow, any infrapopliteal arteriovenous fistula is more likely to precipitate acute ischemia on top of chronic hypoperfusion, potentially turning TADV into a high-risk intervention with limited chance of clinical success. In this late stage, a low or unfavorable TADV score may be interpreted as a signal that percutaneous TADV may no longer be appropriate and that alternative palliative or surgical strategies should be considered. Conceptually, this supports the potential for earlier consideration of TADV in the “poor-option” phase—before complete BTK failure—rather than reserving it exclusively as a last resort when the arterial tree has been entirely exhausted and should be studied further.

Limitations and future directions

This study is limited by its retrospective design, the relatively small sample size, and the fact that the TADV score was derived and tested in a single trial cohort without external validation. The angiographic assessment of perfusion contribution is semiquantitative and may be influenced by operator interpretation, although this was mitigated by blinded dual review and consensus adjudication. Diabetic neuropathy was not systematically evaluated in this cohort, which may have influenced patient-reported pain following TADV and could contribute to variability in postprocedural symptom reporting. Prospective studies are needed to validate the score in broader populations, refine the thresholds that define unfavorable configurations, and test whether score-guided strategy selection can improve patient-centered outcomes in high-risk CLTI.

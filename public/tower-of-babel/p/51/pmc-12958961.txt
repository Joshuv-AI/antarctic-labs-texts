Introduction

Gastric outlet obstruction syndrome (GOOS) is a clinically relevant complication of advanced malignancies, most frequently associated with gastric and pancreatic cancer, and is linked to impaired oral intake, malnutrition, reduced quality of life and limited tolerance to systemic therapy. Despite advances in oncological treatment, malignant GOOS remains a frequent and challenging clinical problem, particularly in patients with locally advanced gastric cancer 16 .

Current management strategies aim to restore gastric emptying, improve nutritional status and enable continuation of anticancer therapy. Contemporary guidelines recommend surgical gastrojejunostomy (GJ), preferably laparoscopic, for patients with reasonable functional status and an expected survival exceeding 2 months, while endoscopic stenting or endoscopic gastrojejunostomy is reserved for patients deemed unfit for surgery. 7 8 However, conventional GJ is associated with functional limitations, including delayed gastric emptying, bile reflux and recurrent obstructive symptoms, which may compromise postoperative recovery and downstream oncological management.7 8

Partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy (PSPG), also referred to as the modified Devine procedure, was originally introduced to address these functional shortcomings by diverting the alimentary stream away from the tumour-bearing distal stomach while preserving proximal gastric reservoir function. Although the technique was first described in the 1990s, it has continued to be applied sporadically over subsequent decades, particularly in East Asian surgical practice. More recent studies have suggested that PSPG may offer improved functional outcomes compared with conventional GJ or enteral stenting, including earlier resumption of oral intake and reduced delayed gastric emptying. 9

Importantly, the clinical context in which PSPG is applied has evolved substantially. Advances in systemic chemotherapy, the increasing use of neoadjuvant and conversion strategies, and improved perioperative care have expanded the role of palliative procedures as potential bridges to definitive oncological treatment. In this setting, timely restoration of oral intake may be critical to maintaining chemotherapy dose intensity and enabling tumour downstaging. Several contemporary observational studies and case series have reported the use of PSPG not only for palliation but also as a bridge to curative gastrectomy in selected patients with locally advanced gastric cancer and malignant GOOS. 10 11

Despite more than two decades of clinical use, the role of PSPG in malignant GOOS remains incompletely defined. Evidence is fragmented across case reports, small case series and heterogeneous observational studies, with substantial variability in surgical technique, patient selection, outcome definitions and reported endpoints. To date, no systematic review has comprehensively synthesised the available evidence focusing specifically on PSPG in malignant GOOS due to advanced gastric cancer, integrating surgical, functional and oncological outcomes within a unified analytical framework. By consolidating the fragmented literature on partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy, the aim of this systematic review is to evaluate the application, surgical outcomes, functional results and oncological implications of partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy in patients with malignant gastric outlet obstruction due to locally advanced gastric cancer, both as a palliative procedure and as a bridge to definitive surgical treatment.

Methods

The protocol and reporting of this systematic review were in accordance with the standards of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) Statement.12 This protocol has been registered within the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number: CRD42024593540).13

Eligibility Criteria

The PICO framework was used.14

Population (P): Adult patients with malignant gastric outlet obstruction undergoing partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy (PSPG) as palliative surgery or as a bridge to resection.

Intervention (I): Partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy (modified Devine technique).

Comparison (C): Conventional gastrojejunostomy, endoscopic gastroduodenal stenting or no comparator.

Outcomes (O): Surgical outcomes (morbidity, mortality), gastric outlet function, nutritional outcomes and oncological outcomes, including overall and progression-free survival when reported.

Eligible study designs: Randomised controlled trials, non-randomised comparative studies, observational studies (prospective or retrospective) and case series. Case reports will be considered only when providing unique technical or clinical insights.

Information Sources

A literature search was conducted in Medline, EMBASE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews and ClinicalTrials.gov databases. A grey literature search was conducted in Grey Literature Report (www.greylit.org) and Grey Literature Database (www.opengrey.eu).

In addition, backward and forward citation searching of included studies was performed.

Search strategy

The following search expression was used: (Devine* Surgery) OR (Devine* Procedure*) OR (Devine* Partitioning*) OR (Devine* Exclusion) OR (Gastric Partiti*) OR (Stomach partiti*) OR (gastric exclusion) OR (gastric partiti*). Searches were limited to human studies.

The search strategy was intentionally designed to maximise sensitivity and to identify all reports of partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy, regardless of underlying pathology. Cancer-related terms were not included, as pilot testing showed no added benefit and their inclusion risked excluding relevant studies, particularly mixed or surgically focused reports in which oncological status was not specified in the title or abstract. Oncological and clinical characteristics were subsequently captured during data extraction.

The full search strategies for all databases and registers are provided in online supplemental file 1. The literature search was conducted between August and October 2025 following protocol submission. The search strategies were not formally peer-reviewed by an independent information specialist or librarian. They were developed and internally reviewed by the authors, all of whom had prior experience in conducting systematic reviews. The full search strategies were prospectively deposited on SearchRxiv to ensure transparency and reproducibility (https://doi.org/10.1079/searchRxiv.2025.00838).15

Selection Process

Two independent researchers (MIR and APG) performed the literature review and the selection of articles. Titles and abstracts of the identified papers will initially be screened to determine their relevance based on the pre-defined eligibility criteria. Papers that cannot be excluded in this initial review stage will proceed to full-text screening. Any disagreements between the two reviewers at any stage will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, by consulting a third reviewer (VN). The number of studies identified, included and excluded at each stage will be described using the PRISMA flow diagram12 along with reasons for exclusion at the full-text review stage.

Quality and risk of bias assessment

Given the surgical nature of the intervention and the limited availability of randomised evidence, this systematic review will include randomised controlled trials, non-randomised comparative studies, observational studies and case series. The objective is not to perform a meta-analysis but to provide a comprehensive synthesis of the surgical, functional and oncological outcomes of partial stomach-partitioning gastrojejunostomy since its introduction. Restricting inclusion to randomised trials alone would fail to reflect real-world practice and would result in the exclusion of clinically relevant evidence. However, there is an inherent risk of bias associated with these study designs. When applicable, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS)16 will be used to assess the methodological quality of cohort studies. For studies with a high risk of bias, we will interpret the findings with caution and clearly highlight these limitations in the review.

To assess quality and risk of bias, we will evaluate study design, data completeness, sample size and the presence of potential confounders. For cohort studies where the NOS is applicable, it will be used to assess selection, comparability and outcome assessment. In cases where the NOS is not suitable, a narrative approach will be adopted to address potential biases and limitations in the design of case reports and case series.

Confidence in cumulative evidence

Given the expected heterogeneity in study designs, populations, interventions and reported outcomes, and the absence of planned quantitative synthesis, a formal grading of the strength of evidence (eg, GRADE) will not be applied. Instead, the overall confidence in the body of evidence will be assessed narratively, considering study design, methodological quality, consistency of findings and clinical relevance.

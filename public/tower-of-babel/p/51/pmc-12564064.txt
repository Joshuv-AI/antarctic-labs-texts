Effect of Graphene Incorporation on the Initial Setting Time of MTA and Biodentine: An In Vitro Pilot Study
Authors: Hrishikesh Saoji, Dinesh Rao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Mineral Trioxide Aggregate (MTA) and Biodentine are two biocompatible materials that are commonly utilized in endodontics because they can seal and are bioactive. However, the long setup time of MTA is a problem in the clinic.Materials and Methods:There were 40 samples used in an in vitro study. They were split into groups based on the type of material and the amount of graphene in it. There were four groups: MTA (control), Biodentine (control), MTA + 0.1% single-walled graphene, and MTA + 1% single-walled graphene. We used the Gilmore needle test to check the initial setting time and a very accurate balance (LWL Germany, Model LB-210S) to weigh the samples. There were 10 samples in each group.Results:The MTA control group had the longest setting time (12.1 minutes on average), followed by MTA + 0.1% SW graphene (9.8 minutes), MTA + 1% SW graphene (8.4 minutes), and so on. There was a very substantial difference (P < 0.001) between MTA and the other graphene-modified groups when the data were analyzed statistically. However, there was no big difference between the MTA and graphene-modified groups.Conclusion:In conclusion, adding 0.1% and 1% graphene to MTA and Biodentine sped up the initial setting time. However, it does not mean anything statistically. These results show that adding more graphene can improve the clinical management of MTA and Biodentine without changing how they build up in the first place.

INTRODUCTION

Mineral Trioxide Aggregate (MTA) and Biodentine are calcium silicate-based materials that are widely utilized in endodontic and restorative dentistry because they are biocompatible, can seal, and can help tissues heal.[12] However, MTA has not been used much in clinical settings because it takes a long time to set, sometimes more than two hours under normal conditions.[3] Biodentine was made as a replacement for dentin that is easier to work with, but its setting time may not always be optimal for clinical operations that need to be done quickly.[4]

Recent studies have looked into combining nanoparticles with regular dental materials to make them work better in terms of their physical and chemical properties. Graphene and its derivatives have gotten a lot of attention because they have a very large surface area, are very strong, and conduct electricity effectively. They may also speed up hydration events in cementitious materials.[56] Graphene’s two-dimensional structure and functional groups make it easier for the material matrix to interact with it. This changes how it sets and may make its mechanical properties and sealing efficiency better.[7]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and sample size determination

This in vitro experimental study was designed to evaluate the effect of graphene incorporation on the initial setting time of Mineral Trioxide Aggregate (MTA) and Biodentine. A total of 30 samples were prepared and divided into seven groups based on the presence and concentration of graphene. The required sample size per group was calculated using data from a previous study by Tanomaru-Filho et al.[8] (ISRN Dent. 2012), ensuring an 80% statistical power at a 5% significance level.

Sample size estimation was done using the following formula: n0 = k*nB (1 + 1/k) σ² (Z1−α/2 + Z1−β)² / (μA – μB)². From the above calculation, 10 samples were included per group.

We used a precision balance (LWL Germany, Model: LB-210S, accuracy: 0.0001 g) to measure single-walled and multi-walled graphene powders that are available for sale. These were added to the cement powders in certain amounts (0.1%, 1%, and 3% by weight) and mixed well to make sure they were evenly spread out before they were hydrated.

The Gilmore needle apparatus was used to find out how long it took for each sample to set up, following the ISO 6876 standards. We put each combination into a conventional cylindrical metal mold with a 2 mm depth and a 10 mm internal diameter. We then let it set in a chamber with controlled temperature and humidity. The Gilmore needle with a set weight was used at regular intervals until the material was strong enough to resist indentation, which showed that the initial set had been reached.

A P value of less than 0.05 was thought to be statistically significant. We used the right statistical software to look at the data.

RESULTS

A total of 70 samples were evaluated across seven experimental groups to determine the initial setting time of MTA and Biodentine with or without the incorporation of graphene. The measured setting times were analyzed and summarized in terms of group-wise means and standard deviations.

Initial setting time (minutes)

The highest mean initial setting time was observed in the MTA control group (no graphene), while the shortest was recorded for the MTA + 1% single-walled graphene. Incorporation of both single-walled graphene at concentrations of 0.1% and 1% demonstrated a reduction in setting time compared to control groups.

DISCUSSION

Graphene is thought to help create nucleation sites for hydration products because it has a large surface area and reactive functional groups. This speeds up the early setting of cementitious materials, like MTA and Biodentine.[2] Previous studies have shown that these kinds of nanostructures not only make materials stronger, but they also change the chemical and physical events that happen when materials set.[3] Graphene incorporation has helped both MTA types in our investigation; however, the reduction in setting time was not as strong in MTA, which is recognized for having slower setting properties.[4]

Adding 0.1% and 1% graphene to MTA did change its setting time, but not in a statistically significant way. Researchers have found similar effects when they added 0.05 wt% graphene to regular Portland cement. The tensile strength, compressive strength, and Young’s modulus all went raised by 79%, 8%, and 9%, respectively.[5]

The study’s results showed that increasing the amount of graphene shortens the setting time. This is especially important in clinical settings where materials need to set more quickly, such as when working with children or patients who are not cooperative, or when controlling moisture is difficult. It is possible to improve workflow and lower the danger of material washout during application by shortening the setting time of MTA while keeping its good qualities.[6]

Also, when handling the graphene-modified samples, there were no symptoms of inconsistency or excessive brittleness, which supports the idea that graphene can improve performance without weakening the structure. However, more research is needed to confirm these in vitro results, including tests of long-term strength, biocompatibility, and in vivo conditions.[7]

CONCLUSION

The incorporation of graphene, particularly at a 3% concentration, significantly reduced the initial setting time of MTA and Biodentine. This modification enhances the clinical handling of these materials, especially for procedures requiring faster setting without compromising structural integrity.

Introduction

Environmental Epigenetics is an Oxford University Press publication that is in its 10th year of operations. The journal publishes scientific and medical research related to the field of epigenetics with a focus on environmental impacts on epigenetics and associated biology and health. We obtained our first impact factor of 3.8 in 2023 and have recently achieved an impact of 4.8 in 2024. This puts us in the top ranking of epigenetics-related journals. Recently, we had our first manuscript submission in which artificial intelligence (AI) was used for the writing of the manuscript. This rapidly developing topic in publishing is discussed in this Editorial. The steps being taken by Environmental Epigenetics and Oxford are discussed. Additional Environmental Epigenetics updates involving the use of Graphical Abstracts and Cover Art are also discussed.

Environmental Epigenetics impact

The journal obtained its first impact factor of 3.8 in 2023, which is a very good initial impact factor for a relatively new journal with a rapidly developing scientific field. This year, in 2024, we obtained our annual modification of the impact factor index and now have an impact factor of 4.8. This puts Environmental Epigenetics in the top tier of journals in the epigenetics field (Table 1). Due to the clinically relevant increase of epigenetics involved in Clinical Epigenetics, (BMC Springer Nature Press), they have held the number one position with regard to impact for a number of years. This year, Environmental Epigenetics is tied with Clinical Epigenetics as one of the top journals in the field. The other main journals are Epigenetics & Chromatin (BMC Springer Nature Press), Epigenetics Insights (Sage Press), and Epigenetics (Taylor and Francis Press). Therefore, Environmental Epigenetics ranks well among its peers in the epigenetics field (Table 1) with its new 2024 impact ranking.

One limitation of reliance on impact factors and ranking is that the impact factor is generally directly correlated with the size of the scientific field in regard to the number of investigators. For example, the field of neuroscience has over a hundred thousand investigators worldwide, so the impact factors are very high for neuroscience journals (18–30 for the top 10). In contrast, the fields of evolution or reproductive biology are relatively small, with only a few thousand investigators, such that journals in those fields have lower impact factor rankings (3–10 for the top 10), in part due to the lower size of the fields. No normalization for field size is considered. Therefore, the best approach is to consider the journals within a specific field to put the impacts in perspective. With Open Access, the span of impact factors is also becoming somewhat normalized and scientific fields cluster together as shown in Table 1 for epigenetics.

Although we are delighted to see the increase in the 2024 Environmental Epigenetics impact factor, it is important to keep this in perspective. The major reason for our increased impact factor is the quality of our authors and submissions. In addition, the dedication and hard work of our Editorial Board members is a major factor. They play a critical role not only in the review process and recruiting manuscripts but also in the submission of manuscripts. The quality and effort of our editors are outstanding. Another factor is the very hard work and dedication of the Oxford Publication support staff of Ms Rebecca Lane and Ms Ruby Turok-Squire in the Oxford publishing office, Ms Elaine Halls as the Managing Editor for Environmental Epigenetics, and Mr Dom Notarangelo in Oxford production. Their dedication to supporting author submissions, reviewing, and producing manuscripts is also outstanding. A group effort is needed to have an efficient and author-friendly environment to boost our current impact. We look forward to the future and encourage submissions to Environmental Epigenetics.

AI and publishing

Recently, Environmental Epigenetics received its first manuscript that involved AI use in the writing of the manuscript. Although the use of AI tools in writing has increased to assist non-English speaking authors in correcting the language and grammar for submission, additional AI tools have also been developed to draft manuscript text and assist in the writing of the manuscripts. AI use for grammar and language would be considered acceptable, but the use of AI tools to draft the primary text raises significant concerns about the originality of the text, as well as the validity of the author’s discussion, data interpretations, and general quality of the manuscript. Therefore, most journals are developing policies for AI tool use. Oxford University Press’s recently developed policy, which Environmental Epigenetics has adopted, is below.

Environmental Epigenetics policy—use of AI tools

Natural language processing tools driven by AI do not qualify as authors, and the Journal will screen for them in author lists. The use of AI (e.g. to help generate content or images, to write code, to process data, or for translation) should be disclosed both in cover letters to editors and in the Methods or Acknowledgements section of manuscripts. Please see the “COPE position statement on Authorship and AI” (https://publicationethics.org/cope-position-statements/ai-author) for more details.

The key is open disclosure by the authors on the use of AI tools. It is imperative to clarify both in cover letters and in manuscripts any use of AI tools and types of use. In general, AI use beyond language correction needs to be explained by the authors upon submission.

Additional Environmental Epigenetics updates

Graphical Abstracts

The use of Graphical Abstracts in journal articles has become common and many journals now require them. We feel this is a useful tool to help convey the focus and observations of papers for the readers, as well as catch the reader’s attention. Graphical Abstracts can be used for a number of purposes by the author and readers. This year, Environmental Epigenetics will initiate the use of Graphical Abstracts as an optional feature for manuscripts the authors can use. Currently, Environmental Epigenetics will encourage the use of Graphical Abstracts. This does not replace the classic written Abstract but simply provides a useful tool to convey the focus and observations of the manuscript for readers. We look forward to incorporating Graphical Abstracts into Environmental Epigenetics manuscripts in the future.

Environmental Epigenetics Cover Art

Since the inception of Environmental Epigenetics, we have used a different cover for each season to represent key content in the published articles. We encourage the authors to submit appropriate and impactful Cover Art. An example of our Summer Cover Art (Figure 1) represents an AI-generated Cover Art to represent epigenetics and gene therapy in a recent publication [1]. Similar to our plan to use Graphical Abstracts for published manuscripts, the journal Cover Art helps to convey the content and focus of published articles in Environmental Epigenetics. We will continue to have Cover Art and encourage our authors to develop innovative and impactful covers for the journal.

Summary

We are delighted to report our increase in impact factor in 2024. We very much appreciate our authors’ support and submissions to the journal. The reviewers and editors are critical to help review papers and identify the high-impact studies and manuscripts. We look forward to an increase in Environmental Epigenetics submissions and a greater impact in the area of epigenetics.

AI tool use will undoubtedly increase with time and its impact on the quality and originality of publications needs to be assessed. We appreciate Oxford University Press and our Oxford staff in delivering the journal’s new policies on the “Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools.”

We encourage the authors to consider the use of Graphical Abstracts and, when appropriate, to submit potential Cover Art for the journal. We feel this is critical to easily convey the content and focus of Environmental Epigenetics articles. We look forward to your submissions.

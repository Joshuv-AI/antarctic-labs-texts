Lappaconitine protects SH-SY5Y cells from TNF-α-induced mitochondrial apoptosis by blocking the Bax-dependent Caspase-3 signaling pathway
Authors: Xu Yang, Jingjing Zhao, Yanxin Cheng, Chenshuo Jia, Jia Li, Xiaohui Li, Senming Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLappaconitine (LA) is a diterpene alkaloid isolated from Aconitum species and has been used in traditional Chinese medicine as an analgesic. Previous studies have also suggested that LA exerts anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects. Neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, both of which are associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis, contribute to the development and maintenance of neuropathic pain. We hypothesized that LA may protect SH-SY5Y cells against inflammation- and apoptosis-associated injury. To investigate whether LA attenuates TNF-α-induced inflammatory injury and apoptosis in SH-SY5Y cells and to explore the potential mechanisms involved. SH-SY5Y cells were pretreated with LA (0.1 or 1 μM) for 24 h and subsequently exposed to TNF-α (10 ng/mL) for 12 h. Apoptosis, mitochondrial function, inflammatory cytokine production, and the expression of mitophagy-related proteins were assessed in LA-pretreated and non-pretreated cells. LA pretreatment (0.1 or 1 μM) reduced apoptosis in TNF-α-exposed SH-SY5Y cells, increased Bcl-2 expression, and decreased the expression of Bax, Caspase-3, and Caspase-9. LA pretreatment also suppressed NF-κB activation and reduced IL-1β and IL-6 production. In addition, LA preserved mitochondrial integrity, as indicated by maintenance of mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced cytochrome c release, and decreased ROS accumulation. The expression of the mitophagy-related proteins PINK1 and Parkin was also increased following LA pretreatment. LA attenuated TNF-α-induced inflammatory injury and apoptosis in SH-SY5Y cells. These protective effects were associated with modulation of apoptosis-related signaling, suppression of inflammatory responses, preservation of mitochondrial function, and increased expression of PINK1/Parkin-related proteins. Introduction

Lappaconitine (LA), a diterpene alkaloid derived from Aconitum species, is predominantly utilized in traditional Chinese medicine for its analgesic properties1. Compared to other Aconitum alkaloids, LA exhibits superior antinociceptive efficacy and lower toxicity2. LA has been reported to exhibit stronger antinociceptive activity and lower toxicity. Clinically, it has been effectively used to manage pain associated with malignant tumors, trigeminal neuralgia, and herpes zoster3,4. The analgesic activities of LA are not related to the opioid system, as evidenced by the findings that these actions were not antagonized by naloxone5,6. In addition to its antinociceptive activity, accumulating evidence suggests that the anti-inflammatory properties of LA may contribute to its analgesic actions. Previous studies have shown that LA can inhibit neutrophil activation and reduce the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines4, and may alleviate inflammatory pain through modulation of lipid metabolism7.

Neuropathic pain is defined by the International Association for the Study of Pain as pain caused by a lesion or disease affecting the somatosensory nervous system8. It is commonly characterized by burning or electric shock-like sensations, hyperalgesia, allodynia, and spontaneous pain. It is commonly characterized by burning or electric shock-like sensations, hyperalgesia, allodynia, and spontaneous pain9–12. Recent studies have underscored the substantial role of neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, linked to mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis, in the initiation and persistence of neuropathic pain8,13. Increasing evidence indicates that neuroinflammation and oxidative stress play important roles in the initiation and maintenance of neuropathic pain13–15. Therefore, identifying therapeutic strategies that target inflammation- and oxidative stress-related pathways remains an important direction in neuropathic pain research15.

Although first-line treatments for neuropathic pain, including tricyclic antidepressants, serotonin- noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors, and pregabalin, are recommended in clinical practice, many patients still fail to achieve satisfactory pain relief. In addition, these agents are often associated with considerable adverse effects. Accordingly, the development of safer and more effective therapeutic strategies requires a better understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying neuropathic pain.

TNF-α is a pleiotropic immune cytokine belonging to the TNF super-family of receptor ligands. It is involved in different homeostatic and inflammatory processes and plays a major role in autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. TNF-α is a key regulator of macrophage activity in Wallerian degeneration. It produces a similar form of hyperalgesia when injected into the nerve16. At the site, TNF-α initiates the cascade of chemotropic and macrophage-mediated pathological events associated with Wallerian degeneration17–19. Meanwhile, inhibition of TNF-α results in temporal modifications of Wallerian degeneration and a reduction in pain18,19. For these reasons, the model of TNF-α-induced neuronal inflammation-related damage is widely used to mimic the effects of inflammation-induced neuronal pain.

Given the reported anti-inflammatory properties of LA, it is of interest to investigate whether LA can attenuate TNF-α-induced inflammatory injury in SH-SY5Y cells. In the present study, we examined the effects of LA on TNF-α-induced cellular injury in SH-SY5Y cells. Our findings suggest that the protective effects of LA are associated with modulation of Bcl-2/Bax/cleaved Caspase-3-related apoptotic signaling, suppression of NF-κB activation, and attenuation of mitochondrial injury.

Methods

Cell culture

Human SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells (ZQXZbio ZQ0050) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 4.5 g/L glucose, 3.7 g/L sodium bicarbonate, 4 mmol/L glutamine, 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin. The cells were maintained in a humidified cell culture incubator at 37 °C with a 5% CO2 atmosphere according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For all the experiments, the cells were trypsinized and seeded at a density of 0.5 to 1.0 × 104 cells per cm2 onto tissue culture-treated plasticware. The culture medium was replaced every two days. All experiments were conducted 24 h after the cells were seeded.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed via a CCK-8 kit (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. To elucidate the cytotoxic effects of TNF-α and assess the impact of LA on cell viability, SH-SY5Y cells were exposed to various concentrations of TNF-α (ranging from 5 to 100 ng/ml) for 12 h or different concentrations of LA (0.1 to 100 μM) for 24 h. To verify whether LA protects SH-SY5Y cells against TNF-α-induced cytotoxicity. The cells were pretreated with LA at concentrations of 0.1 and 1 μM for 24 h, followed by exposure to 10 ng/ml TNF-α for 12 h. Subsequently, 10 μL of the CCK-8 solution (10 mg/mL in phosphate buffer (PBS)) was added to each well of the plate, and the cells were incubated for an additional 4 h in an incubator at 37 °C. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm via a BIO-DL K3 Plus microplate reader (BIO-DL, China), which is known for its stable performance and user-friendly interface. The cell viability is expressed as a percentage of the value of the control group.

TdT-dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL)

To evaluate apoptosis, TUNEL assays were conducted via a one-step in situ cell death detection kit (ZS-C31011S; Zhongshi Gene Technology, Tianjin, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, after inducing apoptosis, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS (pH 7.4) for 1 h at room temperature, washed with PBS, and then incubated with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 2 min on ice. The cells were subsequently incubated in the TUNEL reaction mixture in a humidified atmosphere for 1 h at 37 °C in the dark. DAPI was used to label the nuclei. TUNEL-positive cells were imaged under a fluorescence microscope, with cells displaying red fluorescence identified as apoptotic cells.

Determination of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP)

The JC-1 probe, a widely used fluorescent indicator of the MMP, was used to detect changes in the MMP. This is due to its unique ability to shift from green to red fluorescence emission, indicating potential changes by the ratio shift, which reflects the status of the mitochondrial membrane potential. SH-SY5Y cells (1 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in triplicate in a 96-well plate in the presence of LA at concentrations of 1 and 10 μM for 24 h, followed by the addition of TNF-α at a concentration of 10 ng/ml for another 12 h. After treatment, the culture medium was discarded and replaced with fresh plain medium containing JC-1 staining dye at a concentration of 5 μg/ml. These cells were then cultured in a humidified incubator filled with 5% CO2 at 37 °C in the dark for 30 min, washed twice with PBS, and imaged via a fluorescence microscope. The ratio of the intensity of JC-1 monomer excitation at 510 nm, emission at 527 nm (green, indicative of a maintained MMP to the intensity of JC-1 aggregates at an excitation of 585 nm, emission at 590 nm (red, indicative of an increased MMP), is directly related to changes in the MMP.

Measurement of Intracellular ROS

The intracellular ROS levels were measured via the ROS probe DCFHDA (RP-RK1002, Report, Shijiazhuang, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. After the necessary treatment, the cells were washed with PBS and incubated with 10μmol/L of DC FHDA for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. The stained cells were then washed three times with PBS and visualized via a fluorescence microscope equipped with a digital camera. Measurement of proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1β and IL-6)

SH-SY5Y cells (1 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in triplicate in a 96-well plate and allowed to grow for 24 h. The cells were subsequently pretreated with LA (1 or 10 μM) for another 24 h. TNF-α (10 ng/ml) was then added, and the mixture was incubated for an additional 12 h. Following centrifugation at 14,000 ×g for 20 min at 4 °C, the contents of IL-6 (RK00004, ABclonal) and IL-1β (RK00001, ABclonal) in the supernatants from the culture media were analyzed via commercial kits according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Western blot analysis

Proteins were extracted from cell cultures following the treatments described above, and their concentrations were determined via a BCA assay (Pierce Biotechnology, USA). Equal quantities of proteins (30 μg) were loaded onto a 12% polyacrylamide gel for electrophoresis, followed by electrotransfer onto PVDF membranes. The blots were then blocked with 5% nonfat milk and incubated with the appropriate primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The primary antibodies used included anti-Bcl-2 (1:1000, ET1702-53, HA), anti-Bax (1:1000, ET1603-34, HA), anti-cleaved-Caspase 3 (1:1000, A27145, HA), anti-Caspase 9 (1:1000, ET1603-27, HA), anti-Cyt-c (1:1000, ET1610-16, HA), anti-NF-κB (1:1000, ET1603-12, HA), anti-P-NF-κB (1:1000, ET1604-27, HA), anti-IκB (1:1000, ET1603-6, HA), anti-P-IκB (1:1000, ET1609-78, HA), , anti-Parkin (1:1000, ET1702-60, HA), anti-PINK1 (1:1000, ER1706-27, HA), and anti-GAPDH (1:1000, RP-RA1005, Report) antibodies. After the blots were washed three times, the membranes were incubated with HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG for 1 h at room temperature. Protein signals were detected via an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) system (Pierce Biotechnology, USA). Densitometric analysis of the bands was performed via ImageJ software.

Measurement of Mitochondrial Cyt-c release

Isolation of Mitochondrial and Cytosolic Fractions. All steps were performed at 4 °C. Harvested SH-SY5Y cells were centrifuged at 600 × g for 5 min. The pellet was resuspended in mitochondrial isolation reagent (Beyotime,C3601), incubated on ice for 15 min, and ultrasonicated (3 s on/10 s off, 12 cycles). The homogenate was centrifuged at 600 × g for 10 min. The supernatant was then centrifuged at 11,000 × g for 10 min. The final supernatant (cytosolic fraction) was collected, and the pellet (mitochondria) was lysed in RIPA buffer with protease inhibitors on ice for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 10 min to obtain mitochondrial proteins. Protein concentrations were determined by Bradford assay. Samples were stored at − 80 °C for Western blotting.

Mitochondrial and cytosolic Cyt-c concentrations were analyzed separately via Western blotting using anti-Cyt-c kits (1:1000, ET1610-16, HA).

Results

LA attenuates TNF-α-induced cytotoxicity in SH-SY5Y cells

LA at the working concentration does not affect SH-SY5Y cell viability

To evaluate the cytotoxicity of LA, SH-SY5Y cell viability was measured using a CCK-8 assay after 24 h of exposure to different concentrations of LA. As shown in Fig. 1, high concentrations of LA (50 and 100 μM) significantly reduced cell viability, whereas lower concentrations (0.1, 1, 5, 10, and 30 μM) had no significant effect on cell viability after 24 h of incubation.

Effect of LA on SH - SY5Y cell viability. SH - SY5Y cells were cultured either in the presence or absence of different concentrations of lappaconitine (LA) for 24 h. Cell viability was evaluated using a CCK - 8 kit. The data are presented as the means ± SD from three independent experiments, with cells cultured in triplicate. ***p < 0.001 vs. cells cultured in the absence of LA.

LA protects SH-SY5Y cells from TNF-α-induced loss of viability

To assess the cytotoxicity of TNF-α, SH-SY5Y cell viability was measured using a CCK-8 assays after 12 h of exposure to 5, 10, 20, 50, or 100 ng/mL TNF-α. As shown in Fig. 2, even low concentrations of TNF-α (5 and 10 ng/mL) significantly reduced compared with that in the control group (p < 0.001 vs. control). Pretreatment with LA significantly improved cell viability following TNF-α exposure. Notably, even at low concentrations (0.1 and 1 μM), LA provided significant protection, as indicated by the markedly higher viability of SH-SY5Y cells than that of the TNF-α group (P < 0.001; Fig. 3).

LA reduces TNF-α-induced apoptosis in SH-SY5Y cells

SH-SY5Y apoptosis was evaluated via a Roche TUNEL assay kit. As shown in Fig. 4, pretreatment with LA markedly reduced TNF-α-induced apoptosis. Figure 4A presents representative fluorescence images of SH-SY5Y cells treated with 10 ng/mL TNF-α with or without LA pretreatment, while Fig. 4B presents the quantitative results from three independent experiments. TNF-α induced apoptosis in approximately 90% of SH-SY5Y cells (P < 0.001 vs. the untreated control), whereas LA pretreatment significantly decreased the apoptotic rate (P < 0.001 vs. TNF-α alone), demonstrating its protective effect against TNF-α-induced neuronal cell death.

Discussion

Lappaconitine (LA) has been reported to possess analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties in different experimental settings1–4. In the present study, LA pretreatment attenuated TNF-α-induced injury in SH-SY5Y cells, as reflected by improved cell viability, reduced apoptosis, suppression of inflammatory signaling and cytokine production, decreased ROS accumulation, preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced Cyt-c redistribution from mitochondria to cytosol, and increased expression of the mitophagy-related proteins PINK1 and Parkin. Taken together, these findings suggest that the cytoprotective effects of LA in this model were associated with modulation of inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis-related signaling, thereby supporting our original hypothesis while warranting a cautious interpretation of mechanistic causality.

The observation that LA at the working concentrations did not significantly affect SH-SY5Y cell viability is important because it suggests that the subsequent protective effects were unlikely to result from nonspecific proliferative stimulation or overt drug toxicity. By contrast, consistent with the previous report20,21, TNF-αexposure reduced cell viability and markedly increased TUNEL-positive cells, indicating that inflammatory challenge was sufficient to induce substantial cellular injury and apoptosis in this model. LA pretreatment improved cell viability and reduced the apoptotic rate under the same conditions, supporting the possibility that LA enhances cellular tolerance to TNF-α-induced stress. These data do not by themselves identify the primary upstream molecular target of LA, but they do provide a coherent phenotypic basis for further mechanistic interpretation.

The apoptosis-related protein changes observed in this study are also consistent with attenuation of intrinsic apoptotic signaling. Bcl-2 is a representative anti-apoptotic protein that helps maintain mitochondrial outer membrane integrity, whereas Bax is a pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family member that promotes mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization under cellular stress22. Caspase-9 functions as an initiator caspase in the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway, and cleaved Caspase-3 is a key executioner of apoptotic progression23. In our experiments, TNF-α decreased Bcl-2 expression while increasing Bax, Caspase-9, and cleaved Caspase-3, indicating a shift toward a pro-apoptotic state. LA pretreatment attenuated these changes, suggesting that its protective effects may involve regulation of Bcl-2/Bax balance and downstream Caspase activation. Importantly, these findings are consistent with modulation of apoptosis-related signaling involving Bax and Caspases, but they do not prove that a single Bax-dependent pathway is the exclusive or direct mechanism responsible for the actions of LA.

The mitochondrial data further support the interpretation that LA was associated with preservation of mitochondrial homeostasis during TNF-α exposure. MMP is a key indicator of mitochondrial functional integrity, and loss of MMP is commonly associated with mitochondrial depolarization, impaired bioenergetic status, and increased susceptibility to apoptosis24–27. In parallel, release of Cyt-c from mitochondria into the cytosol is a hallmark of mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and a critical step in intrinsic apoptotic signaling through apoptosome formation and Caspase-9 activation23. In the present study, TNF-α induced MMP dissipation and increased cytosolic Cyt-c while reducing mitochondrial Cyt-c, whereas LA pretreatment attenuated these changes. These findings are consistent with the possibility that LA limits TNF-α-associated mitochondrial injury and thereby reduces conditions that favor activation of the intrinsic apoptotic machinery.

Oxidative stress appears to represent another relevant component of the observed protective profile of LA. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are closely linked to inflammatory injury and mitochondrial dysfunction, and excessive ROS generation can further aggravate mitochondrial depolarization, protein damage, lipid peroxidation, and apoptosis25–27. TNF-α has been widely reported to promote ROS production through inflammatory and mitochondrial mechanisms24–26. In our study, TNF-α increased intracellular ROS accumulation, whereas LA pretreatment reduced this increase. This result suggests that LA may alleviate oxidative stress under inflammatory conditions. However, because the present experiments measured ROS accumulation rather than a full panel of redox-regulatory pathways, these findings should be interpreted as evidence of reduced oxidative burden rather than definitive proof of a direct antioxidant mechanism.

The anti-inflammatory findings are likewise relevant to the central hypothesis of this study. NF-κB is a major transcriptional regulator of inflammatory responses, and TNF-αis a well-established upstream activator of the IκBα/NF-κB signaling axis28–30. Phosphorylation of IκBα promotes its degradation and facilitates activation of NF-κB p65-dependent transcriptional programs, ultimately increasing the expression of inflammatory mediators such as IL-1β and IL-630,31. In the present study, TNF-α increased the p-IκBα/IκBα and p-p65/p65 ratios and was accompanied by elevated IL-1β and IL-6 levels, indicating activation of inflammatory signaling in SH-SY5Y cells. LA pretreatment attenuated phosphorylation of both IκBα and p65 and reduced IL-1β and IL-6 production, suggesting that the anti-inflammatory effects of LA in this model may involve suppression of TNF-α-induced NF-κB pathway activation. Because we did not directly assess nuclear translocation, transcriptional activity, or pathway-specific intervention, these results are best interpreted as being consistent with reduced NF-κB signaling rather than definitive proof of direct pathway blockade.

An additional aspect of the present study is the observation that TNF-α reduced the expression of PINK1 and Parkin, whereas LA pretreatment increased both proteins. Mitophagy is a selective lysosome-dependent quality-control pathway that removes damaged mitochondria and thereby contributes to mitochondrial homeostasis and cell survival under stress conditions. The PINK1/Parkin pathway is one of the best-characterized regulatory systems involved in stress-induced mitophagy. Under conditions of mitochondrial depolarization, PINK1 accumulates on the outer mitochondrial membrane and facilitates recruitment and activation of the E3 ubiquitin ligase Parkin, which helps label damaged mitochondria for downstream quality-control processing. In this context, the increase in PINK1 and Parkin expression after LA pretreatment suggests that mitophagy-related pathways may be involved in the mitochondrial protective effects of LA. However, this interpretation should remain cautious. Because the present study did not assess mitophagy flux, lysosomal delivery, autophagosome-mitochondria colocalization, or dynamic mitochondrial turnover, the data do not demonstrate functional activation of mitophagy. Rather, they indicate altered expression of mitophagy-related proteins that may be relevant to mitochondrial quality control. In addition, although Parkin has been reported in some contexts to interact with apoptosis-related signaling and to influence susceptibility to Bax-associated mitochondrial injury32,33, our data do not establish a direct causal link between PINK1/Parkin regulation and the anti-apoptotic effect of LA.

The overall pattern of results suggests that inflammatory signaling, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis were closely interconnected in this model. TNF-α-induced NF-κB activation and cytokine production may have contributed to oxidative and mitochondrial stress, while mitochondrial dysfunction, cytochrome c release, and ROS accumulation may in turn have amplified apoptotic and inflammatory injury34. The fact that LA pretreatment attenuated changes across all of these domains supports the possibility that its protective action involves coordinated regulation of multiple stress-responsive processes rather than a single isolated event. At the same time, the present data do not allow us to determine whether modulation of inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, or apoptosis is primary, secondary, or mutually reinforcing. Accordingly, a linear cause-and-effect sequence should not be inferred from the present experiments alone.

Limitation

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, SH-SY5Y cells are a transformed human neuroblastoma cell line rather than primary neurons, and therefore they cannot fully recapitulate the physiology, differentiation state, and microenvironmental interactions of mature neurons in vivo. Second, this was an in vitro study, and the findings require validation in primary neuronal cultures and in animal models of neuroinflammatory or neuropathic pain-related injury. Third, LA was administered as a pretreatment, which models a preventive exposure paradigm rather than a clinically realistic post-injury therapeutic intervention. Fourth, we did not perform pathway inhibition, knockdown, overexpression, rescue, or flux-based assays, neither Bax/Caspase-related signaling nor PINK1/Parkin-associated quality control can be considered functionally established as a direct mechanism.

Future studies should therefore extend these findings in several directions. Validation in primary neuronal models and in vivo neuropathic pain or neuroinflammatory models will be necessary to determine whether the present cellular observations translate into physiologically relevant neuroprotection. It will also be valuable to test whether LA retains protective efficacy when administered after TNF-α-induced injury rather than only as a pretreatment.

Conclusions

LA significantly attenuated TNF-α-induced apoptosis and inflammatory responses in SH-SY5Y cells. These protective effects were associated with modulation of Bax/Caspase-3-related apoptotic signaling, suppression of inflammatory signaling, and preservation of mitochondrial function. Together, these findings suggest that LA may protect SH-SY5Y cells against TNF-α-induced inflammatory and apoptotic injury. These effects may be relevant to the potential therapeutic value of LA in neuropathic pain.

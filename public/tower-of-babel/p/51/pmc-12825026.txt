Integrated GWAS and Fst analysis identify flowering‐time regulatory genes in maize
Authors: Dong Wang, Zhuoya Gao, Mengyue Dong, Tuan Li, Wenjing Zhou, Jialei Ou, Shiya Zhang, Shuaishuai Zhang, Xian Dong, Yalan Li, Caihui Zhang, Jiahao Tie, Yinuo Wang, Gaoyang Sun, Huiling Xie, Weihua Li, Jihua Tang, Pengshuai Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFlowering time in maize is a complex quantitative trait regulated by multiple genes, and its genetic variation mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated. In this study, we phenotypically evaluated flowering‐related traits (days to tasseling, days to pollen shedding, days to silking) across six different environments using an association population comprising 368 maize varieties. Genome‐wide association studies were employed to dissect the genetic basis of these traits. A total of 261 significant association single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs; logarithm of odds > 5.5) were detected, among which 23 SNPs were consistently identified across multiple environments or traits. By integrating population fixation index, we further screened 41 potential candidate genes, including Zm00001d006213, Zm00001d027394, and Zm00001d027395, which are likely key regulators of flowering time. These findings provide both theoretical insights into the genetic regulatory network of maize flowering time and valuable gene resources for molecular breeding.

INTRODUCTION

Maize, one of the world's most important crops, was domesticated from teosinte in the highlands of Mexico (Piperno & Flannery, 2001; N. Yang et al., 2023). Following long‐term domestication, maize cultivation has expanded from its original habitat to regions across the globe. A key factor in maize's successful adaptation during domestication has been the reduced sensitivity to photoperiod, enabling maize to flower and set seeds successfully under varying latitudes, day lengths, and temperature conditions (Shi et al., 2022; Su et al., 2024, 2021).

The summer maize‐growing area in the Huang‐Huai‐Hai region is one of China's major maize production areas, characterized by a warm temperate, semi‐humid climate (Han et al., 2025; Y. Wang et al., 2025). During the maize growing season, this region experiences high temperatures; uneven rainfall distribution and heavy precipitation; frequent localized severe convective weather; and recurring natural disasters such as droughts, floods, prolonged overcast conditions with insufficient sunlight, and wind damage. In recent years, frequent extreme heat events have often led to abnormal ear development, impaired anther extrusion and dehiscence, and reduced pollen viability, ultimately resulting in yield losses (Robinson et al., 2021; Yu & Zhai, 2020). Therefore, selecting early‐flowering varieties can help mitigate adverse effects of extreme heat on pollination. Additionally, given the constraints of the double‐cropping system (Yin et al., 2024), the flowering period has become a critical factor in ensuring maize yield and the timely rotation to subsequent crops. Thus, identifying key genes controlling maize flowering time and comprehensively elucidating their regulatory mechanisms are of paramount importance for breeding, selecting, and improving new maize varieties suitable for the Huang‐Huai‐Hai ecological zone and other regions.

Over the past few decades of research, numerous scientists have identified multiple genetic loci closely associated with maize flowering time by studying different populations and employing various methodologies. For instance, L. Wang et al. (2021) used a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population and linkage mapping techniques, including inclusive composite interval mapping, to discover 72 flowering time‐related quantitative trait loci (QTLs). Shi et al. (2022) leveraged both RIL and association populations, combining linkage mapping and genome‐wide association studies (GWAS) approaches, to locate 82 QTLs and predict 13 candidate genes. Wu et al. (2023) employed F2:3 and GWAS panel, integrating GWAS, linkage analysis, and transcriptome analysis to identify 25 candidate genes linked to flowering time. However, only a few genes, such as ZmCCT (Hung et al., 2012; Zhong et al., 2021) and ZCN8 (Guo et al., 2018), have been cloned and functionally validated to date. Moreover, flowering time is a typical quantitative trait that is co‐regulated by multiple genes, resulting in a complex regulatory network (Buckler et al., 2009). Therefore, to more comprehensively elucidate the genetic regulatory mechanisms underlying maize flowering time, it is both essential and urgent to further explore novel genetic loci and investigate their genetic basis in greater depth.

In this study, 368 maize varieties widely promoted in the Huang‐Huai‐Hai region were used as research materials and cultivated across six different ecological environments. Systematic investigations were conducted on flowering‐related traits, including days to tasseling (DTT), days to pollen shedding (DTP), and days to silking (DTS). Based on these observations, GWAS and fixation index (Fst) were employed to identify candidate genes associated with these traits. The findings aim to provide a crucial theoretical foundation and genetic resources for the genetic improvement of reproductive developmental traits in maize.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Plant materials

A total of 368 widely grown maize cultivars from the Huang‐Huai‐Hai region were used as research materials and further divided them into nine distinct subpopulations (Table S1).

Field experiment and flowering time‐related trait evaluation

In June 2024, these 368 maize cultivars were grown across six experimental sites with distinct geographical environments: Yuanyang, Henan (YY, 35.02°N, 113.71°E); Zhengzhou, Henan (ZZ, 34.91°N, 113.71°E); Suiping, Henan (SP, 33.16°N, 113.97°E); Shijiazhuang, Hebei (SJZ, 37.88°N, 114.61°E); Hebi, Henan (HB, 35.67°N, 114.28°E); and Fuyang Anhui (FY, 32.81°N, 115.70°E). Among these sites, the experiments at Yuanyang and Zhengzhou employed two planting densities (67,500 and 90,000 plants/ha), denoted as 6.75YY (the density treatment of 67,500 plants/ha planted in Yuanyang), 9.00YY (the density treatment of 90,000 plants/ha planted in Yuanyang) and 6.75ZZ (the density treatment of 67,500 plants/ha planted in Zhengzhou), 9.00ZZ (the density treatment of 90,000 plants/ha planted in Zhengzhou), respectively, while the other sites uniformly adopted a density of 67,500 plants/ha. At each location, the maize was planted in a four‐row layout, with each row measuring 4 m in length. For field management, fertilization, irrigation, pest and disease control, and weed management were conducted according to local conventional practices to ensure experimental conditions aligned with actual production environments.

Three flowering time‐related traits (DTT, DTS, and DTP) were recorded when 50% of the plants exhibited the corresponding traits. Descriptive statistical analysis was performed on all phenotypic data using the SPSS software. Frequency histogram plotting and analysis were conducted using the GraphPad Prism 7 software. The R 4.4.0 software package lme4 was employed to calculate broad‐sense heritability (H 2) and best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP).

Genotyping and quality control

Leaves from the 368 maize varieties were collected from the five‐leaf stage of maize seedlings. The tips of young and tender maize leaves of five plants were collected as a bulk to extract genomic DNA, according to the modified cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) procedure (Xie et al., 2023). Then, the DNA underwent quality assessment and was preserved for future use. This panel was genotyped by using Maize48K BeadChip. The sequencing was conducted on the DNBSEQ‐T7 platform, employing a paired‐end sequencing (PE150) strategy to achieve high‐throughput sequencing. A total of 435,917 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were called successfully among the 368 hybrids. SNPs with a missing rate of more than 20% and minor allele frequency of <0.01 were excluded from the genotyping dataset (DeRaad, 2022). Subsequent analyses were performed using a comprehensive set of 359,809 high‐quality SNPs (the SNPs were aligned to the maize B73_RefGen_v4; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16418559).

Genome‐wide association studies

A total of 359,809 SNPs were selected to perform a phenotype–genotype GWAS by using the GAPIT package in R 4.4.0 (Xiao et al., 2017). The FarmCPU model was applied for the GWAS in this study (Liu et al., 2016). We performed GWAS for DTT, DTP, and DTS under different conditions, along with their corresponding BLUP. After applying the Bonferroni‐adjusted correction for multiple testing, an SNP was considered to have a significant association when its logarithm of odds (LOD) value exceeded the threshold of 5.5 during the analysis (Duan et al., 2025; Li et al., 2019). Additionally, if the physical positions of two SNPs were within 200 bp of each other, they were defined as co‐localized.

Fst analysis

Fst was calculated using VCFtools 0.1.16 following Weir and Cockerham's (1984) method with a 2‐kb sliding window. Genetic differentiation between populations was assessed using standard thresholds for interpreting Fst values : (1) 0–0.05, negligible differentiation; (2) 0.05–0.15, moderate differentiation; (3) 0.15–0.25, significant differentiation; and (4) >0.25, very strong differentiation.

Candidate gene analyses

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) was calculated following the method of Hill and Weir (1994). At an r 2 threshold of 0.1, the LD decay distance for the 368 hybrids was approximately 150 kb (Figure S1). Therefore, using the maize B73 reference genome assembly V4, genes located within a ±100‐kb range of significant SNP physical positions were determined to be candidate genes for flowering time‐related traits. Gene function and annotation information were obtained by querying databases.

GO and KEGG analysis

Expression analysis of candidate genes was performed using the B73 gene expression data of Flower (B73_Female_Spikelet_Collected_on_day_as_silk, B73_Mature_Pollen, and B73_Silk), and expression data were obtained from the ZEAMAP (Gui et al., 2020). For further functional characterization, gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis were conducted using the OmicShare Tools (https://www.omicshare.com/tools).

RESULTS

Phenotypic evaluation in different environments

The phenotypic survey results revealed that the DTT, DTP, and DTS of the maize cultivars showed variation across different environments (Table S2). Specifically, the mean DTT values across different environments (9.00ZZ, 6.75ZZ, SJZ, SP, 6.75YY, 9.00YY, HB, and FY) were 49.45, 51.29, 52.72, 51.38, 51.79, 51.61, 49.64, and 52.27 days, respectively. The corresponding DTP values were 52.68, 53.46, 55.06, 52.85, 54.70, 54.70, 53.47, and 54.30 days, while DTS values were 51.98, 53.11, 54.77, 53.79, 55.22, 56.10, 51.83, and 54.55 days (Table 1). Heritability analysis demonstrated strong genetic control of these traits, with H 2 values of 0.79 for DTT, 0.83 for DTP, and 0.74 for DTS, suggesting these flowering‐related traits are predominantly governed by genetic factors.

GWAS of maize flowering time

To identify the significant SNPs (LOD > 5.5) associated with maize flowering time, the FarmCPU analysis was performed on the panel. A total of 261 SNPs significantly associated with maize flowering time were detected (Table S4, Figure 2). Among them, there were 78 SNPs related to DTT, with eight in the 6.75ZZ, nine in 6.75YY, nine in 9.00ZZ, nine in 9.00YY, nine in HB, 12 in SJZ, 13 in SP, and nine in BLUP. There were 91 SNPs related to DTP, with 12 in the 6.75ZZ, 11 in 6.75YY, 11 in 9.00ZZ, 11 in 9.00YY, 13 in HB, nine in SJZ, 10 in SP, 13 in BLUP, and one in FY. There were also 92 SNPs related to DTS, with 13 in 6.75ZZ, 11 in 6.75YY, 14 in 9.00ZZ, 11 in 9.00YY, 13 in HB, nine in SJZ, 8 in SP, 11 in BLUP, and two in FY. By comparing GWAS results from different environments, 23 stable SNPs associated with flowering time were identified (Table S5).

Genetically differentiated regions between Sub3 and Sub7

Multiple factors, including natural selection, restricted gene flow, and human activities (such as domestication and breeding), have collectively contributed to the divergence among different maize populations or subpopulations. These factors lead to differentiation at both the genomic level and agronomic traits. Fst is a widely used metric for detecting genomic regions under selection or isolation, effectively revealing genetic divergence caused by domestication, adaptation, or artificial selection. In this study, Sub3 and Sub7 populations exhibited significant differences in the key trait of flowering time, suggesting the potential existence of genetically differentiated regions containing flowering time regulatory genes. Accordingly, we performed Fst analysis on the Sub3 and Sub7 populations, which identified 31,908 genomic segments with Fst >0.25 (Figure 3). The discovery of these regions provide valuable candidate intervals for subsequent genetic and functional investigations.

Integrating GWAS and Fst to predict candidate genes

To predict candidate genes by integrating GWAS and Fst results, we identified seven consensus SNPs (rs_1_4417590, rs_1_274060286, rs_2_201994230, rs_3_137020638/rs_3_137020726, rs_7_171443502, rs_9_4695936, and rs_9_154979809) whose associated candidate genes overlapped with those predicted from seven genetic differentiation segments between Sub3 and Sub7 populations (Chr.1: 4,418,001–4,420,000 bp, Chr.1: 274,087,001–274,089,000 bp, Chr.2: 202,005,001–202,007,000 bp, Chr.3: 137,025,001–137,027,000 bp, Chr.7: 171,392,001–171,394,000 bp, Chr.9: 4,685,001–4,687,000 bp, and Chr.9: 154,978,001–154,981,000 bp). Through comprehensive analysis, we ultimately screened 41 candidate genes associated with flowering time (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

Application of flowering time genes in maize breeding

Flowering time in maize is a complex quantitative trait regulated by multiple genes, playing a significant role in genetic improvement (Buckler et al., 2009). Studies have shown that key genes such as VGT1 (Salvi et al., 2007), ZmMADS69 (Liang et al., 2019), ZCN8 (Guo et al., 2018), ZmCCT9/10 (Huang et al., 2018; Q. Yang et al., 2013), and ZmFKF1b (S. Chen et al., 2024) regulate flowering time. These genes promote early‐flowering phenotypes and reduce photoperiod sensitivity during maize domestication. This has facilitated the adaptive expansion of maize from tropical to temperate regions, greatly enriching the diversity of germplasm resources. However, current research on the molecular mechanisms and natural variations of these key flowering time genes remains limited. Therefore, a systematic analysis of these genetic regulatory networks will provide important theoretical insights into the mechanisms of maize regional adaptation and the improvement of tropical germplasm.

Related studies indicate that maize flowering time is significantly associated with thousands of SNPs and nearly a hundred QTLs distributed across all 10 chromosomes (J. Yang et al., 2024). Notably, genes such as ZmCCT10 and ZCN8 have been repeatedly validated in multiple independent genetic populations and studies (Guo et al., 2018; Hung et al., 2012; Q. Yang et al., 2013; Zhong et al., 2021), suggesting that functional variations in these genes may confer a selective advantage in natural populations. For example, Q. Yang et al. (2013) identified 42 flowering time‐associated loci in the promoter region of ZmCCT through GWAS of 368 maize inbred lines. Similarly, in this study, a GWAS of 368 maize varieties detected a DTT‐associated SNP linked to Zm00001d029885 (ZmCCT) under the HB, corroborating previous findings. This not only reinforces the critical role of this gene in regulating flowering time but also indirectly reflects the scientific rigor and reliability of the current study to some extent.

Combining genetic differentiation of populations (Fst) and GWAS to identify key genes regulating flowering time in maize

The combined application of genetic differentiation of populations (Fst) and GWAS enables the rapid identification of candidate genes. For instance, Yoshida et al. (2023) collected 202 rice varieties (including landrace rice, eating rice, and sake‐brewing rice) and conducted both Fst and GWAS. They identified several genomic regions with significant divergence between eating rice and sake‐brewing rice, and through GWAS and map‐based cloning, successfully discovered two novel genes regulating grain traits.

In this study, we integrated Fst analysis and GWAS to identify 41 candidate genes associated with maize flowering time. Notably, among the 41 candidate genes identified in this study, several encode transcription factors, such as Zm00001d044839 (bHLH‐transcription factor 182, bHLH182), Zm00001d044836 (TCP‐transcription factor 9, TCPTF9), and Zm00001d027395 (NAC‐transcription factor 78, NAC78; Table 2). Numerous studies have demonstrated that transcription factors play a pivotal role in plant growth and development, cell differentiation, and environmental adaptation (Ramachandran et al., 1994). For example, previous studies have clearly demonstrated that genes such as NAC2 (Zhang et al., 2018), ANAC075 (Fujiwara & Mitsuda, 2016), and NAC81 (Pimenta et al., 2016) are involved in regulating flowering time. In this study, we identified ZmNAC78, which our research group had previously found to modulate iron content in maize kernels (Yan et al., 2023). Earlier research has shown that iron deficiency delays flowering in Arabidopsis through the iron deficiency‐induced transcription factors bHLH38, bHLH100, and bHLH101 (bHLH38/100/101; W. Chen et al., 2021). These factors redundantly regulate flowering time and the expression of FLOWERING LOCUS T (FT) specifically under long‐day conditions. This evidence suggests that iron levels can influence flowering time in plants, implying that ZmNAC78 may serve as a potential candidate gene for regulating flowering time in maize.

Furthermore, among the 41 candidate genes examined in this study, Zm00001d006213 and Zm00001d027394 exhibited the highest expression levels in Flower (B73_Female_Spikelet_Collected_on_day_as_silk, B73_Mature_Pollen, and B73_Silk). Both genes encode histones, which have been previously demonstrated to regulate flowering time in plants. For example, studies in Arabidopsis have shown that H3 lysine 4 tri‐methylation (H3K4me3) and H2A.Z coordinately modulate flowering time (Ji et al., 2024). Additionally, in Arabidopsis, H2A.Z homologs have been found to influence flowering time, where loss‐of‐function mutations in HTA9 and HTA11 result in early flowering (Choi et al., 2007; Deal et al., 2007; March‐Díaz et al., 2008). These findings suggest that Zm00001d006213 and Zm00001d027394 may also serve as potential candidate genes regulating flowering time in maize. Those potential candidate genes identified via joint analysis could serve as valuable genetic resources for optimizing flowering time in maize breeding programs.

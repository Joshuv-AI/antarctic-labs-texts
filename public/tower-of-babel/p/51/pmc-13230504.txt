A haplotype-resolved genome of Mytella strigata, a globally invasive marine bivalve
Authors: Jiawei Zhang, Siyao Li, Yiwei Wang, Shijie Zhong, Chuangye Yang, Yongshan Liao, Deng Yuewen, Qingheng Wang, Zhe Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMytella strigata, a bivalve mollusk native to the Atlantic coast of South America, has recently become a globally significant marine invasive species, posing serious threats to native ecosystems and aquaculture operations. Here, we report a haplotype-resolved, chromosome-level genome assembly of M. strigata (2n = 30), generated using high-fidelity (HiFi) long-read sequencing and high-throughput chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C). Two haplotypes were independently assembled: haplotype 1 (Hap1) spans 692.37 Mb with a contig N50 of 6.93 Mb, and haplotype 2 (Hap2) spans 683.91 Mb with a contig N50 of 7.61 Mb. Both assemblies were anchored to 15 chromosomes, achieving anchoring rates of 93.84% (Hap1) and 97.08% (Hap2). Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) analysis revealed high completeness, identifying 92.33% and 93.22% of expected single-copy orthologs in Hap1 and Hap2, respectively. We annotated 27,887 protein-coding genes and conducted analyses of gene functions. This high-quality genomic resource provides a foundation for investigating the genetic mechanisms underlying invasiveness and environmental adaptability in M. strigata.Subject terms: Genome, Phylogenetics

Background & Summary

Maritime shipping facilitates over 90% of global trade, with ballast water discharge and hull fouling acting as major unintended vectors for the translocation of aquatic species across geographic regions1,2. Prominent invasive species include marine mussels, characterized by high fecundity, rapid growth, and broad environmental tolerance3,4. A representative example is Mytella strigata, a bivalve mollusk of the family Mytilidae (order Mytiloida, class Bivalvia). Native to the Atlantic coast of South America, M. strigata exhibits remarkable environmental adaptability, thriving across a wide range of salinities, pollution levels, and substrate types3. This adaptability enables M. strigata to rapidly colonize and spread in natural marine environments and in human-impacted areas such as ports and estuaries. Consequently, with the expansion of global shipping, this species has spread to multiple regions, including China, Singapore, the Philippines, Thailand, India, and the United States (Fig. 1)3–7. In the Indo-Southeast Asian region, M. strigata was first recorded in the Philippines between 2014 and 20158,9, followed by Singapore in 20164, Thailand in 20173, and India in 20197. The species was subsequently reported in Taiwan in 201910, in the intertidal zone of Donghai Island, Guangdong Province, China in 20205, and in Hong Kong in 202311.

Our recent investigations along the southern coast of China indicate that M. strigata extensively colonizes aquaculture facilities, such as mesh cages and rafts, resulting in the mortality in cultured organisms, increased cleaning and maintenance costs, and severe economic losses to the aquaculture industry (Fig. 2a–c). Furthermore, M. strigata competes with native bivalves, such as Perna viridis, for habitat space and food resources, leading to significant alterations in benthic community structure and biodiversity decline (Fig. 2d). Currently, no effective strategies currently exist to mitigate the spread and ecological impact of M. strigata, posing a serious threat to the sustainability of marine ecosystems and associated economic industries along the southern coast of China and beyond.

Given the widespread invasion of M. strigata and its ecological and economic impacts on marine ecosystems, the genomic resources obtained will facilitate the tracing of source populations and the detection of multiple introduction events, promote the development of molecular monitoring markers, and support the identification of adaptive alleles associated with invasiveness and environmental tolerance. These genomic insights will provide a valuable foundation for developing improved biosecurity measures and evidence-based management of M. strigata populations in affected regions. However, current genomic research on M. strigata remains limited. In this study, we present the first high-quality, chromosome-level genome assembly of M. strigata using PacBio HiFi long-read sequencing and Hi-C technologies. This genomic dataset provides a valuable foundation for investigating the molecular mechanisms driving the invasiveness and environmental adaptability of M. strigata.

Methods

Sample collection and DNA extraction

Healthy M. strigata specimens were collected from the intertidal zone of Xuwen, Zhanjiang Guangdong Province, China (20°41′N, 110°34′E). One adult individual without visible gonadal tissue selected for whole-genome sequencing. Specimens were rinsed with sterile seawater, immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until DNA extraction. Genomic DNA was extracted using the QIAGEN DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit following the manufacturer’s protocol. As M. strigata is an invasive species in China, no specific collection permit was required. Sampling was conducted in accordance with local environmental regulations.

Library construction and sequencing

Both the HiFi and Hi-C sequencing libraries were prepared from the same M. strigata individual to ensure consistency between the genome assembly and chromatin conformation data. SMRTbell libraries for circular consensus sequencing (CCS) were constructed for both haplotypes (Hap1 and Hap2) using the 15–20 kb template preparation kit (Pacific Biosciences, Menlo Park, CA, USA), following the standard PacBio protocol. Sequencing was performed on the PacBio Revio platform in CCS mode to generate high-fidelity (HiFi) reads. These HiFi reads were derived by producing multiple subreads through iterative passes of the polymerase around the circular DNA template, yielding consensus sequences with both long read lengths and high base accuracy.

Hi-C libraries were constructed following a modified high-throughput chromatin conformation capture protocol12. Briefly, tissue samples were fixed with 1% formaldehyde to preserve the three-dimensional chromatin architecture. Crosslinked DNA was then digested with MboI restriction enzyme. Sticky ends were repaired and labeled with biotinylated nucleotides, followed by proximity ligation with T4 DNA ligase to join spatially adjacent DNA fragments. Biotin-labeled ligation products were enriched using streptavidin-coated magnetic beads and used to prepare sequencing libraries. The libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ high-throughput platform.

Genome survey and assembly

The genome size, heterozygosity, and repeat rate of M. strigata were estimated using a k-mer frequency-based approach. K-mer (k = 21, 31 and 41) frequencies were calculated from HiFi reads using Jellyfish (v2.3.0)13 (parameters: -m 21/31/41 -s 1000000000) and analyzed with GenomeScope (v2.01)14 (parameters: -k 21/31/41 -p 2). Initial genome assembly was performed with Hifiasm (v0.19.6)15 using default parameters. Redundant haplotigs were subsequently removed using Purge_haplotigs (v1.0.4)16 with default parameters. To assess alignment and coverage metrics, HiFi reads were mapped to the genome assemblies using Minimap2 (v2.24)17 (parameters: -x map-hifi).

Hi-C data were used to anchor contigs to chromosomes. Juicer (v1.6)18 (default parameters) was employed to align the paired-end Hi-C reads to the assembled genomes and to assess the quality of the Hi-C libraries. Chromosome scaffolding was performed using the 3D-DNA pipeline (https://github.com/theaidenlab/3d-dna) with the parameter -r 0, which preserves input contigs without fragmentation. Manual checking and refinement of the draft assemblies were performed using Juicebox Assembly Tools (v1.11.08)19 (default parameters). Genomic features, including chromosome structure, GC content, and read depth distribution were visualized using CIRCOS (v0.69-6)20 (default parameters).

Repeat annotation

Prior to gene prediction, repetitive sequences in the M. strigata genome were systematically annotated using a de novo-based approach. Tandem repeats were identified using Tandem Repeats Finder (v4.09)21 (parameters: 2 7 7 80 10 50 2000 -d -h). Transposable elements were annotated using RepeatMasker (v4.0.9)22 (parameters: -nolow -no_is -norna) in combination with the RepBase database (http://www.girinst.org/repbase). A de novo repeat library was constructed using RepeatModeler (v1.0.1123)23 and LTR_FINDER (v1.0.7)24 (both with default parameters), and was subsequently used by RepeatMasker (v4.0.9) to classify different categories of repetitive elements. After result integration and redundancy removal, non-redundant repeat datasets were obtained for both haplotypes.

Gene prediction

Gene annotation was conducted by integrating evidence from homology-, de novo-, and transcriptome-based approaches. For homology-based prediction, annotation datasets from five closely related molluscan species (Bathymodiolus platifrons, Mytilisepta virgata, Mytilus chilensis, Mytilus coruscus, and Mytilus galloprovincialis) were retrieved from MolluscDB (http://mgbase.qnlm.ac) and used as reference genomes25. De novo models were trained using Augustus (v3.3.2)26 (parameters: autoAugTrain.pl --useexisting, augustus --uniqueGeneId = true --noInFrameStop = true --gff3 = on --strand = both), and gene prediction matrices were derived from the HumanIso.smat file of Genscan. RNA-seq data were generated from nine tissues (gill, foot, digestive gland, intestine, adductor muscle, heart, mantle, siphon, and hemocytes) of three adult M. strigata individuals from the same geographic population. Total RNA was pooled for short-read sequencing on the DNBSEQ platform (BGI, China) and full-length sequencing on the PacBio Sequel II platform. The combined short- and long-read data constituted the RNA-seq dataset. All lines of evidence were then integrated using MAKER2 (v2.31.10)27 (parameters: max_dna_len = 3000000, min_contig = 10000, pred_flank = 500, min_protein = 30).

In the first round, homology-based results were used to build preliminary gene structures. Predictions from de novo and transcriptome-based sources were then incorporated as supporting evidence, and MAKER2 re-predicted all potential gene models accordingly. During this step, MAKER2 refined 5′ and 3′ untranslated regions (UTRs) based on transcriptomic alignments and identified alternative splicing isoforms where supported by the RNA-seq data. During model integration, MAKER2 ranked candidate models using its built-in sensitivity/specificity distance scoring (annotation edit distance, AED) and retained the most evidence-consistent model for each locus.

Gene function annotation

Gene functional annotation was performed based on sequence similarity and domain conservation. First, protein-coding genes of M. strigata were aligned against multiple public databases, including NR, SwissProt, TrEMBL, KOG, TF, Interpro, GO, KEGG, and Pfam, using DIAMOND (v2.0.14)28 (parameters: --evalue 1e-05). The best hits from these alignments were used to assign putative gene functions. In addition, InterProScan (v5.61-93.0)29 (parameters: --seqtype p --formats TSV --goterms --pathways -dp) and HMMER/hmmscan (v3.3.1)30 (parameters: hmmsearch -E 1e-05 --domE 1e-05) were employed to identify transcription factors, protein domains, and conserved motifs.

Genome completeness assessment

The completeness of the haplotype assemblies was evaluated using BUSCO (v5.3.1)31 (parameters: -m genome–limit 10) with the mollusca_odb1031 lineage dataset, to estimate the proportion of conserved single-copy orthologs expected in Mollusca. The proportions of complete, duplicated, fragmented, and missing BUSCOs were then summarized to assess gene space completeness.

Data Records

The raw PacBio HiFi and Hi-C sequencing reads have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under accession number SRP631514 (https://identifiers.org/insdc.sra:SRP631514)32. The haplotype-resolved genome assemblies of M. strigata are available at European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) under accession number GCA_979236015.1 (https://identifiers.org/insdc.gca:GCA_979236015.1)33 and GCA_979236015.3 (https://identifiers.org/insdc.gca:GCA_979236015.3)34.

Genome survey and assembly evaluation

Based on k-mer analyses with k = 21, 31, and 41, the haploid genome size of M. strigata was estimated to be 639–636 Mb, with heterozygosity ranging from 3.07% to 2.43% and repeat content decreasing slightly from 38.6% to 27.8% (Fig. 3).

Distribution of k-mer frequencies in the M. strigata genome. The x-axis represents k-mer depth (i.e., sequencing coverage), and the y-axis indicates the frequency of each k-mer depth. (a) 21-mer; (b) 31-mer; (c) 41-mer.

HiFi reads were mapped back to the assemblies, achieving mapping rates of 99.73% (Hap1) and 99.92% (Hap2), indicating high mapping quality and overall assembly completeness. The Hi-C contact maps (Fig. 4) were consistent with accurate chromosomal organization. The final assemblies spanned 692.37 Mb (Hap1) and 683.91 Mb (Hap2), with contig N50 = 6.93 Mb / 7.61 Mb and scaffold N50 = 45.03 Mb / 47.36 Mb (Table 3). These assembly sizes are slightly larger than the haploid genome size estimated from k-mer analyses (639–636 Mb). Such a modest difference is within the expected range for highly heterozygous and repeat-rich molluscan genomes and is likely attributable to uncollapsed heterozygosity, retained repetitive elements, and inherent biases in k-mer–based modeling. A total of 93.84% (Hap1) and 97.08% (Hap2) of contig sequences were successfully anchored to 15 chromosomes (Table 4). The genomic landscapes of both haplotypes (Fig. 5) showed similar patterns of GC content, gene density, and transposable element distribution, suggesting no evidence of large-scale assembly bias or misjoins. The GC content versus sequencing depth plots (Fig. 6) exhibited unimodal distributions centered around 0.38 GC and 100–300 × sequencing depth, indicating even coverage and no GC bias. Outlier regions with extreme GC content or sequencing depth were further verified by BLAST searches against the NCBI NT database, confirming that they represent intrinsic genomic features rather than exogenous contamination. Together, these genome-wide patterns support the high continuity and completeness of the haplotype-resolved assemblies.

Repeat annotation

Repetitive sequences comprised nearly half of the M. strigata genome, representing 48.01% and 47.75% of the Hap1 and Hap2 assemblies, respectively (Table 5). The repeat proportions are comparable to those reported for other mussel genomes, such as Mytilus coruscus (52.83%)35, supporting the repeat-rich nature typical of Mytilidae genomes.

Assembly completeness and structural integrity

BUSCO analysis using the mollusca_odb10 dataset identified 92.33% and 93.22% complete single-copy orthologs in Hap1 and Hap2, respectively, while only 7.03% and 6.25% were classified as missing (Table 8). These results suggest that both haplotype assemblies of M. strigata are highly complete and structurally consistent. BUSCO assessment also revealed a high proportion of single-copy orthologs and very few duplicated BUSCOs (<1.62%), confirming that assembly redundancy was effectively controlled. The total estimated gap sizes introduced during Hi-C scaffolding were approximately 25.6 kb (Hap1) and 23.8 kb (Hap2), representing short linker regions inserted by the 3D-DNA pipeline between adjacent contigs. The small gap sizes indicate good assembly continuity with minimal effect on overall quality.

Discovery and characterization of canvuparatide, a once-weekly parathyroid hormone analog for the treatment of hypoparathyroidism
Authors: Fa Zhang, Diego Perez-Tilve, Matthew R. Allen, Corinne E. Metzger, Michael A. Dorato, Mary Jane Geiger, Richard D. DiMarchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hypoparathyroidism is a rare endocrine disorder characterized by hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, and low or undetectable levels of parathyroid hormone (PTH). Advanced treatments that precisely maintain blood calcium levels within the normal range to improve disease symptoms and outcomes are needed. Canvuparatide (formerly known as MBX 2109) is a once-weekly investigational PTH analog that undergoes a controlled-release conversion to a biologically active peptide through an intramolecular cyclization reaction controlled by temperature and pH. Here we demonstrate the biologically active PTH analog, derived from the canvuparatide prodrug, stimulated dose-dependent accumulation of cyclic AMP to a similar degree and selectivity as a synthetic form of the human PTH(1–34) peptide in human cells overexpressing the PTH type 1 receptor. In healthy rats treated with canvuparatide at 4–40 nmol/kg/day for 28 days and healthy cynomolgus monkeys treated with single doses of canvuparatide 3.75–7.5 nmol/kg, prodrug and active peptide concentrations increased dose proportionally and correlated with increases in serum calcium concentrations. In parathyroidectomized rats, canvuparatide treatment at 10–40 nmol/kg normalized serum calcium levels and increased bone formation in a dose-proportional manner. In a phase 1, randomized, placebo-controlled study (NCT05158335), single subcutaneously administered doses of canvuparatide (50–600 μg) were well tolerated in healthy volunteers. Pharmacokinetic clinical profiles displayed geometric mean t1/2 values of 81–101 (canvuparatide prodrug) and 133–186 h (canvuparatide active peptide) across dose groups, supporting once-weekly dosing. These collective findings support clinical advancement of once-weekly canvuparatide therapy for patients with hypoparathyroidism.

Introduction

Hypoparathyroidism (hypoPT) is a rare endocrine disease most commonly caused by injury or removal of the parathyroid gland during anterior neck surgery (in ∼75% of cases) [1,2]. The remainder of hypoPT cases are associated with an autoimmune or genetic etiology [1,2]. The primary biochemical indicator of hypoPT is low circulating parathyroid hormone (PTH); other signs include hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia, low vitamin D 1,25 levels, and elevated urinary calcium secretion [[1], [2], [3]]. Clinical signs and symptoms of hypoPT include neuromuscular complications (tetany, muscle stiffness/cramping, seizures, and basal ganglia calcification), renal abnormalities (kidney stones, chronic kidney disease, nephrocalcinosis), cardiac dysfunction (cardiac arrythmias, ischemic heart disease), and skeletal alterations (increased bone mass, reduced bone remodeling) [1]. These symptoms and burden of care greatly impair quality of life for patients with hypoPT [4].

The standard of care for hypoPT consists of calcium and active vitamin D supplementation; however, this approach often fails to adequately maintain serum calcium levels in the targeted range or fully alleviate symptoms of the disease [3]. Conventional therapy is also associated with substantial pill burden and contributes to renal complications [3]. PTH replacement therapy has been advanced as a treatment option to control the signs and symptoms of hypoPT [3]. Pump-administered PTH(1–34) has proven highly effective for the treatment of hypoPT and serves as a benchmark for clinical outcomes in chronic therapy [5]. However, the use of mechanical pump hormone infusion has proven unpopular in the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes and has not advanced as a preferred therapeutic option in insulin-dependent diabetes [6] or hypoPT. Recombinant human PTH(1–84) was approved in 2015 as an once-daily adjunct to calcium and active vitamin D to control hypocalcemia in patients otherwise not well controlled on conventional therapy [3]. However, this approach failed to provide a continuous PTH pharmacokinetic profile to support persistent basal hormone replacement; furthermore, recurrent manufacturing issues led to product recall in 2019 [3]. Most recently, palopegteriparatide, a transiently pegylated PTH(1–34) prodrug, normalized serum calcium levels, reduced supplemental calcium and active vitamin D, and demonstrated a suitable safety profile in phase 3 hypoPT studies [7,8]. Palopegteriparatide was approved in the US in 2024 as a once-daily PTH replacement therapy for adults with hypoPT [9]. However, palopegteriparatide and other investigational PTH replacement therapies (eg, eneboparatide) require daily subcutaneous (SC) injections, which presents a burden for patients and may reduce adherence (similar to the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes). This treatment burden may be alleviated with the development of basal hormone therapies with less frequent drug administration [10]. Teriparatide (synthetic human PTH 1–34), which is approved to treat osteoporosis [11], is not currently approved for hypoPT but has been used off-label to treat hypoPT in adults and children (as subcutaneous injection or via pump delivery). There remains a need for hypoPT treatments that provide sustained action, require less frequent dosing, and can safely and accurately normalize mineral metabolism and urinary calcium excretion, without the need for calcium and vitamin D supplements, to reduce the risk of both hypo- and hypercalcemia.

The development of long-acting macromolecular prodrugs has recently garnered attention as a means to reduce dosing frequency for the treatment of chronic conditions [12]. The progression from daily dosing to weekly administration provides more than an 85% reduction in the number of injections and represents a welcome convenience in chronic disease management. This therapeutic advance has been witnessed with multiple drugs and most recently with incretin-based peptides. The transition from twice-daily exendin-4 treatment to daily liraglutide administration was followed by weekly dulaglutide and semaglutide, which provide enhanced glycemic control [13,14]. Unexpectedly, semaglutide administered once weekly at a dose comparable to daily liraglutide (2 mg once weekly vs 3 mg once daily) is more than twice as effective in lowering body weight [15]. The superiority of weekly therapy for clinical outcomes was not possible to predict from preclinical and early clinical studies. Similar patterns have been observed with skeletal therapy, where bisphosphonates progressed from daily to monthly to yearly administration, also with improved efficacy in key properties that were only uncovered with prolonged post-approval studies [16]. Finally, improved convenience can lead to improved compliance and thus indirectly achieve improved patient outcomes.

Canvuparatide (formerly known as MBX 2109) is an investigational PTH analog designed as a PTH replacement therapy for hypoPT, to be administered once weekly. As a prodrug, canvuparatide is designed to be chemically stable and biologically inactive at the time of injection. Once administered, canvuparatide chemically converts through an intramolecular reaction at a controlled first-order rate to release a biologically active PTH agonist that offers a sustained duration, which provides a precise and less burdensome hormone replacement therapy. We report the chemical principles that make canvuparatide a suitable PTH analog for once-weekly administration, supported by pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) assessments through cellular experiments and studies in rodents, monkeys, and humans.

Methods

Design, synthesis, and chemical characterization

Canvuparatide was prepared by chemical synthesis at Indiana University for preclinical assessment and produced at a preparative scale for clinical study at PolyPeptide Laboratories Inc. Canvuparatide was formulated at Singota Laboratories at a concentration of 1 mg/mL in 20 mM phosphate buffer (pH 2.1) containing 14 mg/mL of propylene glycol, to enhance physical stability during storage.

Analytical reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) was performed on an Agilent 1260 Infinity with Phenomenex Kinetex C8 2.6μ 100A (75 × 4.6 mm) column. A flow rate of 1 mL/min and a gradient of 10%–80% acetonitrile in water with 0.1% of trifluoroacetic acid over 10 min (with additional 5-minute wash and pre-equilibration) were used for most analyses. Data were collected using UV-visible absorption at 214 nm, unless otherwise noted. Preparative HPLC purifications were performed on a Waters Controller model 600, Waters dual wavelength detector 2487, ProStar model 701 fraction collector (Waters Corporation) and Kipp & Zonen BD41 chart recorder (OTT HydroMet) using a Luna 10μ C8 100A AXIA (250 × 21.2 mm) column (Phenomenex) with flow rate of 12 mL/min. Data were collected using UV-visible absorption at 220 nm and further assessed by analytical HPLC for purity.

Research-scale synthesis of PTH peptides was performed using 0.1 mmol Fmoc-Lys(Mtt)-Wang Resin on an ABI-433A peptide synthesizer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using standard Fmoc/Oxyma/diisopropylcarbodiimide coupling protocols. Boc-Ser(tBu)-OH was used to introduce the N-terminal serine in the biologically active drug form of canvuparatide. Boc-dLys(Fmoc)-OH was used as the N-terminal d-lysine in canvuparatide, the prodrug form of the active PTH analog. The 4-methyltrityl group on the side chain of lysine was removed with 30% hexafluoroisopropanol/dichloromethane solution, and the assembly of the side chain that terminates with fatty acylation was done manually. Cleavage was conducted by treatment with 10 mL of trifluoroacetic acid solution containing 5% triisopropylsilane, 2.5% anisole, and 2.5% H2O at room temperature with gentle agitation for 1.5 h. The resin was filtered, and the peptide was precipitated by the addition of cold ether (50 mL). The peptide precipitate was collected by centrifugation and then washed with cold ether (3 × 50 mL) for subsequent purification by preparative HPLC.

In vitro characterization

The cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) Hunter™ Teriparatide bioassay (DiscoverX #95-0118Y2, lot #19J0305; Eurofins DiscoverX Products LLC) was used to measure cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) response to canvuparatide-released active peptide and other MBX PTH-related peptides. Synthetic PTH(1–34) (teriparatide) was used as a positive control. Serial dilutions (1:3) of the experimental and control PTH peptides were incubated with cAMP Hunter™ Teriparitide PTHR1 Bioassay cells (Eurofins) for 30 min in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 humidified incubator, and cAMP production was detected using the cAMP Hunter Detection kit (Eurofins). All samples were measured with triplicate wells for each dose. Raw luminescence signal from the bioassay plate was read using an Envision plate reader (PerkinElmer), and data were plotted using data visualization software (GraphPad Prism, version 10). A variable slope 4 PL curve fit was used to calculate half maximal effective concentration (EC50) values for the experimental and control samples.

In vivo characterization in rodents and cynomolgus monkeys

Model organisms and canvuparatide dosing

Canvuparatide was evaluated in multiple animal models (healthy Sprague Dawley rats, female Sprague Dawley rats that underwent surgical excision of the parathyroid glands [PTX rats]), and healthy cynomolgus monkeys). Canvuparatide was administered SC for all animals; single doses of canvuparatide ranged from 30 to 60 nmol/kg in healthy rats, 10–40 nmol/kg in PTX rats, and 2.5–7.5 nmol/kg in cynomolgus monkeys. Multiple doses of canvuparatide ranged from 4 to 40 nmol/kg in healthy rats and 7.5–40 nmol/kg in PTX rats. Dose ranges were selected based on previous dose-range finding studies.

PTX rats were used as a rodent model of hypoPT [17,18]. Rats that underwent sham surgery (all surgical procedures except removal of the parathyroid glands) were used as a negative control. PTX rats were maintained on a low-calcium diet (Teklad LM-485 irradiated pelleted chow, 1.0% calcium, Envigo) to sustain serum calcium at a reduced concentration without adverse acute consequences to the animals’ health [17].

All animal studies were conducted in accordance with the US National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The healthy and PTX rat studies were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the University of Cincinnati and were found to be in accordance with the US National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals; the facilities conducting the studies were accredited by the Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care International. The cynomolgus monkey study protocols were approved by the IACUC of Inotiv and were in accord with provisions of the US Department of Agriculture Animal Welfare Act, the Public Health Service Policy on Humane Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, and the US Interagency Research Animal Committee Principles for the Utilization and Care of Research Animals.

PK assessments

Plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active peptide were measured over time after single- or multiple-dose SC administration of canvuparatide. In PTX and healthy rats, whole blood was collected, processed to serum, and analyzed for calcium and phosphorus using colorimetric assays in accordance with the method provided by the manufacturer (Point Scientific or BioAssay Systems). Briefly, blood was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 8 min, followed by a spin at 8000 rpm for 2 min. The serum was removed, and samples were stored in plastic tubes to prevent any exchange of minerals with glass.

For in vivo PK analyses, concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active drug were measured using HPLC (ABSciex). In the human phase 1 study, concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active drug were quantified using a validated liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry method that employed stable isotopic-labeled PTH internal standards. Dipotassium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid was used as an anticoagulant for the human plasma samples.

For the healthy monkey studies, blood samples were collected from the femoral vein before canvuparatide dosing and at several timepoints after canvuparatide dosing. Within 1 h of collection, blood was centrifuged for 15 min at approximately 1500 rcf. The resultant plasma for each sample was transferred and stored in a tube. Plasma was analyzed for PK parameters (performed by Q2 Solutions). The PK analysis was conducted using Phoenix WinNonlin (Certara, version 8.1). PK parameters were determined from plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active drug using noncompartmental procedures.

PD assessments

Circulating levels of serum calcium and phosphorus were measured in healthy Sprague Dawley rats, PTX rats, and cynomolgus monkeys; blood samples were collected and processed as described above. The serum concentrations of calcium and phosphorus were measured using commercially available colorimetric assays (reagents: o-cresolphthalein complexone for measuring calcium and phosphomolybdate for measuring phosphorus).

Bone analyses in PTX rats were measured using imaging (micro-computed tomography), histology (static and dynamic histomorphometry), and mechanical testing (3-point bending). Micro-computed tomography was performed using methods previously published [19]. Proximal tibiae were scanned using a Skyscan 1172 (Bruker) at 12-μm resolution. A 1-mm region of interest starting roughly 0.5 mm from the distal end of the proximal growth plate was used for analysis of trabecular bone. The primary outcome was trabecular bone volume, reported as bone volume/total volume (BV/TV). Whole femurs were scanned using a Skyscan 1176 system at 18-μm resolution. The primary outcome was cortical bone area at the diaphysis midshaft. All computed tomography analyses were done in accordance with standard guidelines [20].

For histology analyses and dynamic histomorphometry, PTX rats received 10 mg/kg calcein (Sigma Aldrich) administered SC on study days 14 and 25; proximal tibiae were processed un-demineralized using standard methods as previously described [19]. After fixation in neutral buffered formalin at tissue collection, bones were subjected to serial dehydration and embedded in methyl methacrylate (Sigma Aldrich). Serial frontal sections were cut 4-μm thick and left unstained for analysis of fluorochrome calcein labels. A second 4-μm–thick section was stained with tartrate-resistant alkaline phosphatase for assessment of osteoclast surfaces. Histomorphometric analyses were performed using image analysis (BIOQUANT). A standard region of interest (ROI) of trabecular bone, excluding primary spongiosa and endocortical surfaces was analyzed. The primary outcome of these measures is based on surfaces that have incorporated the calcein label, administered in vivo, which represents areas of active bone formation. In the ROI, trabecular bone displaying a single calcein label surface, double calcein label surface, and an unlabeled surface were measured at 20X magnification. mineralizing surface/bone surface (MS/BS; [dLS + sLS/2]/BS∗100), mineral apposition rate (MAR; average distance between 2 calcein labels/10 days), and bone formation rate per unit of bone surface (BFR/BS; [MS/BS∗MAR]∗3.65) were calculated. Stained sections were analyzed for osteoclast-covered trabecular surfaces within a comparable ROI as described above. The primary outcome for this measure is osteoclast surface/bone surface (Oc.S/BS), %. All histomorphometry nomenclature adheres to standard conventions [21].

Bone mechanical properties were assessed using published methods [22]. Femoral diaphysis mechanical properties were assessed in 3-point bending on a mechanical testing system (Test Resources). After micro-computed tomography, bones were thawed to room temperature, hydrated in saline, and then placed posterior surface down on bottom supports (span = 18 mm). The upper support was brought down in contact with the specimen's anterior surface (9 mm from each of the bottom supports) at a displacement rate of 2 mm/min. Force versus displacement data were collected at 10 Hz, and structural parameters were determined from curves using a customized MATLAB program (MathWorks). Primary outcomes included ultimate load, stiffness, displacement to failure, and work to failure.

Statistical analysis

For rodent PD analyses, repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to determine the statistical significance of changes between groups for parameters measured over time (body weight, serum calcium, and phosphorus); additional 1-way ANOVAs (with post hoc test) were used to compare serum biochemistries at time 0 and 28. Student's t test, 1-way ANOVA, or 2-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparisons post hoc test was used to compare the bone measures (morphology, histology, and mechanics). For PTX rat studies, all statistical analyses were performed using data visualization software (GraphPad Prism, version 10). A P < 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance.

Phase 1 clinical studies

Participants, treatment, and study design

A phase 1, first-in-human, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study was conducted in healthy human volunteers (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05158335). The primary objective was to assess the safety and tolerability of canvuparatide administered as single and multiple SC doses to healthy adults. Eligible participants were adults (aged 21–60 years) in good health with a body mass index of 20.0–32.0 kg/m2. The study design and results from the multiple ascending-dose portion of the study have been reported separately [23]. Here, we report results from the single ascending-dose portion of the study, in which participants were randomized 3:1 to receive a single dose of either placebo or canvuparatide at doses of 50, 150, 300, 460, or 600 μg, administered SC in the abdomen.

The human study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its most recent update, Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, and the International Council for Harmonisation E6 GCP guideline. The study was approved by the appropriate institutional review boards and conducted in accordance with local legal and regulatory requirements of the country involved, and with standard operating procedures in place. All participants provided written informed consent before participating in the study.

PK assessments

Plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active peptide were measured over time among participants who received single-dose SC administration of canvuparatide. Blood samples from human volunteers were collected at specific times during the study to measure plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active peptide. Plasma concentrations of canvuparatide (prodrug) and the active peptide were determined using validated bioanalytical procedures (liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry with stable isotopic-labeled internal standards; dipotassium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid was used as an anticoagulant for the human plasma samples). The PK analysis was conducted by Fortrea using Phoenix WinNonlin software (Certara, version 8.1.1). PK parameters were determined from plasma concentrations of canvuparatide and the active peptide using noncompartmental procedures. Human volunteers were fasted for at least 8 h before receiving doses or having blood samples collected for clinical laboratory evaluations, and they also fasted for approximately 4 h after dosing.

Safety assessments

While residing at the study site and during each study visit, human volunteers were observed for signs or symptoms of hypercalcemia. Participants were also encouraged to report adverse events (AEs) occurring at any other time during the study. All AEs were recorded from the time study treatment was initiated through study completion on the electronic case report form. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were defined as AEs that started during or after administration of the first dose of study drug or started before administration of the first dose and increased in severity after administration of the first dose. Other safety assessments included routine clinical laboratory tests, electrocardiogram tracings, vital sign measures, and physical examinations.

Results

Design, synthesis, and in vitro characterization of canvuparatide

Canvuparatide is a PTH analog consisting of the first 32 amino acids of human PTH; the N-terminus is extended by 2 amino acids (d-lysine−1 and sarcosine0), and the C-terminus by a single lysine (Figure 1A). A C18 fatty diacid is covalently attached at both the N- and C-termini of the prodrug using a spacer similar to that used in semaglutide [14]. The low pH of the formulation (pH 2.1) prevents premature release of the active peptide from the prodrug and maintains the formulated peptide in a soluble, monomeric form. Under physiological conditions, based on controlled temperature and pH, the canvuparatide prodrug undergoes intramolecular cyclization to release the fatty-acylated, N-terminal dipeptide as a diketopiperazine derivative and the singly fatty-acylated active peptide. HPLC-mass spectrometry spectra demonstrated a high-quality, clinical-grade prodrug peptide that fragmented as expected during enzymatic digestion with Lys-C or Glu-C proteases (Fig. S1–S5). During an in vitro analysis of conversion from canvuparatide to the biologically active peptide under pseudo-physiological conditions (ie, 37 °C, pH 7.47; Fig. S6), a linear correlation of the negative natural logarithm of concentration versus time confirmed the first-order diketopiperazine cyclization (Fig. S7). In human cells overexpressing the PTH1 receptor, the canvuparatide prodrug stimulated cAMP production as a low-potency agonist, with a EC50 that was 1000-fold higher than that of synthetic PTH(1–34) control (Fig. 1B). A control prodrug MBX 2101, which is similar to canvuparatide but incapable of converting to active peptide, exhibited no bioactivity in the cAMP assay (Fig. 1C). In contrast, the singly fatty acylated PTH analog stimulated a potent, dose-dependent accumulation of cAMP, like what was produced by PTH(1–34), validating the full potency of the canvuparatide-derived active peptide (Fig. 1D).

In vivo pharmacokinetic studies of canvuparatide

Healthy Sprague Dawley rats

In healthy Sprague Dawley rats given a single 30-nmol/kg dose of canvuparatide, the plasma concentration of the active PTH analog released from the prodrug peaked 24 h after dosing. This concentration remained elevated beyond 72 h before returning to baseline levels 1 week after dosing (Fig. S8). The PK profile of the active drug released from the prodrug was prolonged in duration and much flatter (reduced peak/trough) in its extended profile relative to direct administration of the active PTH analog. On repeat daily dosing of canvuparatide (4, 8, and 12 nmol/kg), the plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and the active peptide increased dose proportionally and were sustained throughout the 28 days of treatment (Figure 2A). Healthy cynomolgus monkeys

Plasma concentration–time profiles for the SC injection of canvuparatide (2.5, 3.75, and 5.13 nmol/kg dose levels) and the associated active PTH analog in cynomolgus monkeys are presented in Figure 2C. The prodrug's apparent time to reach maximum plasma concentration (tmax) was 24 h and the majority was cleared after 1 week. The resultant active peptide from this same SC injection peaked at 48–72 h, and the concentration 1-week after dosing was approximately half of that at maximal concentration (Cmax). Mean t1/2 and tmax were relatively consistent across SC-administered dose levels, while the Cmax and AUC increased in a linear fashion with increasing doses (2.5, 3.75, and 5.13 nmol/kg) and plateaued between doses of 5.13 and 7.5 nmol/kg (Table S1).

A single IV bolus of 5 nmol/kg canvuparatide yielded a mean initial concentration of 963 ng/mL, mean t1/2 of 78 h, and clearance of 0.53 mL/h/kg body weight (Fig. 2D). The active PTH analog derived from this same single 5-nmol/kg bolus IV administration exhibited a longer mean t1/2 (149 h) and faster clearance than the prodrug (2.97 mL/h/kg body weight), consistent with the expectation of the prodrug design. The prodrug consists of 2 fatty acids and is biologically inactive but converts to a singly fatty acylated form that binds and activates the PTH1 receptor. When a single IV bolus of the active peptide (PTH[1–32]) was administered at a concentration of 5 nmol/kg, the mean initial concentration of active peptide was 937 ng/mL, mean t1/2 was 130 h, and mean clearance was 1.8 mL/h/kg body weight. In cynomolgus monkeys, the canvuparatide prodrug converted to the active peptide with a calculated bioconversion efficiency of 73% and an estimated bioavailability of 64% (Fig. S9).

In vivo pharmacodynamic studies of canvuparatide

Body weight and food intake

No appreciable changes in body weight or food intake were observed in healthy Sprague Dawley rats that received daily SC dosing of canvuparatide for 28 days (4, 8, and 12 nmol/kg; Fig. S10A–B). Similar results were observed in female Sprague Dawley rats (Envigo) that underwent surgical excision of the parathyroid glands (PTX rats). These rats received a daily dose of canvuparatide 7.5 or 15 nmol/kg for 28 days (Fig. S10C–D); cynomolgus monkeys were dosed with canvuparatide 1.5–2.5 nmol/kg once weekly for 5 weeks (data not shown).

Serum calcium

In healthy Sprague Dawley rats administered canvuparatide at 3 dose levels (4, 8, and 12 nmol/kg) or vehicle once daily for 28 days, serum calcium increased less than 0.5 nmol/μL, with no dose response over the course of the study (Figure 3A–B). PTX rats that received 4 daily SC doses of canvuparatide (10, 25, and 40 nmol/kg), exhibited a dose-proportional increase in serum calcium levels after 48 h; notably, the calcium level in the 40-nmol/kg group slightly exceeded that in the sham rats (Fig. S11A). This relationship was maintained 72 h after the last dose. In a separate experiment, similar dosing with canvuparatide for up to 10 days increased serum calcium levels in a dose-proportional manner but to levels that exceeded those observed in sham animals at the higher doses (Fig. 3C). Other pharmacodynamic endpoints

Phosphate

In PTX rats, daily SC dosing with canvuparatide (3 dose levels of 10, 25, and 40 nmol/kg) for up to 10 days resulted in decreased serum phosphate levels in a dose-proportional manner that was most evident on day 7 (last measurement in daily treatment) and day 10 (first measurement after stopping daily treatment; Fig. 3F). A post hoc analysis using Dunnett's test at each timepoint revealed significant differences between vehicle and sham controls and the 40-nmol/kg group on days 3 and 11 (P < 0.05). Daily treatment with canvuparatide in PTX rats over 28 days at doses of 7.5 and 15 nmol/kg/day (bracketing the lowest dose employed in the 10-day study) reduced circulating phosphate levels, measured as phosphorus, bringing them closer to the upper limit of normal, similar to that of sham controls but not statistically different from vehicle-treated rats (Fig. 3G).

Endogenous PTH

Endogenous PTH in cynomolgus monkeys was reduced through 168 h after dosing at each single-dose level of 2.5–5.13 nmol/kg (Fig. 3H).

Translational dosing in healthy human volunteers

Participants

A total of 36 healthy human volunteers were randomized and received a single SC dose of canvuparatide (50–600 μg). Participant demographics were similar across treatment groups (Table S2). The study included 23 male and 13 female participants, aged 22–59 years, with an overall mean body mass index (BMI) of 27.32 kg/m2. Most participants (23; 63.9%) were White; 11 (30.6%) were Black or African American and 2 reported as multiple race (1 American Indian or Alaska Native and White; 1 Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander and White). Thirty-three participants completed the study in accordance with the protocol and 3 discontinued the study: 1 in the canvuparatide 50-μg group (due to AE of positive SARS-CoV-2 test), 1 in the canvuparatide 150-μg group (lost to follow-up), and 1 in the canvuparatide 600-μg group (due to participant withdrawal).

Pharmacokinetics

In healthy human volunteers who received a single dose of canvuparatide, both the canvuparatide prodrug (Figure 5A) and active peptide (Fig. 5B) concentrations were determined to increase linearly and dose proportionally. The plasma concentration versus time profiles of canvuparatide were characterized by a slow absorption phase with similar median tmax values across dose levels. After reaching Cmax, plasma concentrations of the canvuparatide prodrug and active peptide appeared to decline in a generally monophasic manner. The geometric mean t1/2 across dose groups was 81–101 h (∼3.8–4.2 days) for the canvuparatide prodrug and 133–186 h (∼5.5–7.8 days) for the active peptide, supporting once-weekly dosing (Table S3). Increases in Cmax, AUC0-tlast, and AUC0-∞ for the canvuparatide prodrug were approximately dose proportional. For the active PTH peptide, increases in AUC0-tlast were approximately dose proportional while increases in Cmax were slightly less than dose proportional.Figure 5Arithmetic mean concentration time profiles by dose for (A) the canvuparatide prodrug and (B) active peptide after single doses of canvuparatide in healthy human volunteers.Figure 5

Safety

Overall, 22 (61.1%) of human volunteers who received placebo or canvuparatide reported 35 TEAEs (Table S4); 7 of these participants were in the placebo group. Most TEAEs were mild in severity; 2 TEAEs were moderate in severity (1 in the placebo group [headache] and 1 in the canvuparatide 150-μg group [injection site erythema]). The most commonly reported TEAEs were injection site erythema, injection site reaction, headache, positive SARS-CoV-2 test, and hypercalcemia. Most injection site reactions were mild in intensity (1 was moderate, none were serious or severe) and all resolved without intervention. Hypercalcemia was reported in 2 participants (both in the canvuparatide 600-μg group) and increased blood calcium level was reported in 1 participant (in the canvuparatide 460-μg group). The hypercalcemia events were all asymptomatic, mild in intensity, considered by the investigator as possibly treatment related, and resolved without intervention; the highest serum calcium level was 11.2 mg/dL (normal range, 8.4–10.6 mg/dL). There were no notable treatment-related findings in routine clinical laboratory measures (except for serum calcium), electrocardiogram tracings, vital sign assessments, or physical examinations. One participant in the canvuparatide 50-μg group was lost to follow-up after a TEAE of a positive SARS-CoV-2 test and the participant was recorded as being discontinued from the study due to this TEAE. There were no serious or severe TEAEs and no deaths during the study.

Discussion

Canvuparatide is an investigational PTH analog designed as a controlled-release hormone replacement therapy for patients with hypoPT. The biologically active peptide is produced by an intramolecular cyclization that is concentration independent and demonstrates selective bioactivity at the PTH1 receptor. The PK profile of canvuparatide and the resultant biologically active PTH fatty acylated analog were characterized to be dose proportional in rats, cynomolgus monkeys, and humans. The duration of action varies appreciably across these 3 species as the half-life of circulating albumin is much accelerated in rodents (∼2 days), compared with cynomolgus monkeys (∼7 days), and even more so in humans (∼21 days) [24,25]. Because of these differences in albumin clearance, the duration of action in humans can be upwards of 10 times longer than that in rodents. Therefore, once-daily dosing in rats is roughly equivalent to once-weekly dosing in humans. The t1/2 values for the prodrug and active peptide based on the precedent established in translation of fatty acylated insulin [10,26] and incretins [14] support the likelihood of time action consistent with once-weekly dosing. Administration of canvuparatide resulted in dose-dependent increases in circulating calcium levels in healthy rats, PTX rats, and cynomolgus monkeys. In PTX rats, canvuparatide effectively reduced low bone turnover, as measured by BFR/BS using histological analysis, after removal of the parathyroid gland. The highest dose of canvuparatide produced a response similar to that of the sham controls. In healthy human volunteers, single SC doses of canvuparatide (50–600 μg) were well tolerated; most TEAEs were mild in severity. Together, these results support the continued clinical development of canvuparatide for the treatment of hypoPT.

Canvuparatide is currently one of the only investigational PTH replacement therapies with a once-weekly dosing schedule, whereas most other PTH replacement therapies employ daily SC injection. The canvuparatide prodrug design offers several potential advantages relative to conventional and investigational therapies for hypoPT. By preventing oscillations in serum calcium levels and maintaining these levels within standard ranges without the need for calcitriol and calcium supplements, canvuparatide may reduce the risk of renal complications associated with conventional therapies [3]. This prodrug provides sustained release of the active PTH peptide, and thus canvuparatide has the potential to be a once-weekly, controlled-release therapy for replacing PTH in individuals with hypoPT, effectively providing pump-like PK without the need for a mechanical pump. This finding can be observed in Figure 5B, where the plasma concentration of the active drug one-week post-dosing is ∼80% of the Cmax. This peak-to-trough difference is further minimized with repeated weekly administration to a steady state concentration achieved after 3–4 injections [23]. While not identical to pump administration, the plasma concentration of active drug is remarkably persistent for a hormone that is cleared in its native form with a half-life of 1 h after subcutaneous administration. A more physiological-like PTH action profile can potentially improve basal control of disease symptoms, thereby enhancing the quality of life for patients with hypoPT. Additionally, the molecular structure of canvuparatide generally resembles that of fatty acylated glucagon-like peptide-1 agonists, such as semaglutide, which has been successfully formulated for daily oral administration [27]. Furthermore, the controlled-release, sustained-action design of this peptide prodrug has potential applications for multiple other hormones in other disease states requiring chronic therapy and precise control of drug concentration, such as obesity, diabetes, and hemophilia [12].

Canvuparatide produced a normalization of the parathyroidectomy-induced suppression of BFR, the key bone parameter of interest in this model. This PTX rat model shows a clinically relevant low bone remodeling state, which was normalized (BFR) or increased (Oc.S/BS) compared with sham levels with the higher dose of canvuparatide. As the rat bone continues to grow longitudinally, the structural changes to both trabecular and cortical bone are less noteworthy (and less clinically relevant). PTX treatment resulted in higher trabecular bone volume and no change in cortical structural or mechanical parameters. For cortical bone, similar effects occur in other rat disease models (eg, ovariectomized rats), which often show minimal effects in these animals compared with other species, including humans. This is likely because rodents lack intracortical remodeling, which is a key physiological determinant of cortical bone properties. In the current study, the lower mechanical properties, displacement, and work in the canvuparatide group are likely the result of the lower bone area. The reason for the effect on cortical bone area is unclear and deserves further study in species that have cortical bone that is more physiologically similar to humans.

In the human phase 1 study, injection site TEAEs were the most commonly reported treatment-related TEAE after single-dose administration of canvuparatide; most were mild in intensity and all events resolved without intervention. Based on the mechanism of action of canvuparatide, hypercalcemia was an on-target effect in this dose-ranging study. There were 2 participants with TEAEs of hypercalcemia and 1 participant with a TEAE of increased calcium; all 3 events were mild and resolved without intervention. A similar safety profile was observed among healthy volunteers who received 4 weekly doses of canvuparatide (200, 400, 600, or 900 μg) in the multiple ascending-dose portion of this phase 1 study [23]. The safety and tolerability profile of canvuparatide supports its advancement into later phase development for the treatment of hypoparathyroidism.

These studies had some limitations. Comparison of PK and PD results across species is challenging because of differences in the half-life of circulating albumin as previously noted [24,25]. To compensate for the much-accelerated clearance in rats, we administered the drug once daily, and we believe that the results obtained in PTX rats, as shown in Figure 2A, are comparable to weekly dosing in humans. Cynomolgus monkeys metabolically clear albumin approximately 3 times faster than humans [24,25], which is witnessed in the reduced peak to trough of the resultant bioactive peptide in the PK profiles shown when comparing Figure 2, Figure 5. It should also be noted that findings in healthy human volunteers may differ from patients with hypoPT due to differences in basal serum calcium levels and other disease-related characteristics. However, similar PK and PD patterns were observed in healthy rats and a rat model of hypoPT (PTX rats). Finally, human data reported herein are based on single doses and PD effects were not evaluated in the current study; effects of multiple ascending doses of canvuparatide in healthy human volunteers (including PK, PD, and safety outcomes) have been reported elsewhere [23].

In conclusion, canvuparatide demonstrated consistent PK/PD across multiple species and experimental models in line with the expected physiological activity of long-acting PTH replacement. These findings support further clinical evaluation of canvuparatide for the treatment of hypoPT.

Agreement between serum creatinine and fingerstick creatinine measurements in adult patients admitted to the emergency department
Authors: Serap Begüm Öztürk, Volkan Arslan, Nalan Metin Aksu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCritically ill patients are frequently encountered in the emergency department (ED) and requiring rapid diagnosis and treatment. It has been shown that fingerstick tests shorten examination and treatment durations in the ED. The aim of this study was to assess the agreement between fingerstick creatinine measurements and serum creatinine values obtained by laboratory enzymatic testing.MethodsThis study was conducted as a single center, prospective, observational study. 250 patients aged 18 years and over who applied to the adult emergency department between 12.12.2023 and 30.12.2023 and underwent the necessary serum biochemistry examination were included in the study. In addition to the blood biochemistry tests were requested by the patients’ doctors, blood samples taken from the patients’ fingertips were scanned using the i-STAT device and a creatinine cartridge. Intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to examine the consistency between serum creatinine values measured in the laboratory and fingerstick creatinine values, and the type 1 error level was set as 0.05. A graphical approach, the Bland-Altman plot was used to examine the agreement between measurements. A significance level of 0.05 was used for all comparisons.ResultsIn this study, after exclusion of 4 patients due to kit problems, 133 patients (54.16%) were female and 113 patients (45.94%) were male. The median age was found to be 55.5. A strong agreement was found between the measured values (ICC: 0.996). Patients when analyzed among themselves in terms of admission complaints, existing diseases, age, gender, admission vital signs and glomerular filtration rates, a strong agreement was observed between fingertip creatinine and serum creatinine in each group.ConclusionIn conclusion, the fingerstick creatinine test may be considered for early use in patients presenting to the ED, as it is easily applicable in examination and treatment management and has strong agreement rates with laboratory values.

Introduction

Emergency departments are places where critically ill patients are encountered, requiring rapid diagnosis and treatment. Increased patient admissions to the emergency department and overcrowding may lead to prolonged hospital stays, delays in treatment, and increased mortality [1]. The process of diagnosis, including the collection of blood samples, the time taken for tests to be completed and their interpretation by physicians determines the length of stay in emergency departments. Fingerstick tests, which are examples of point-of-care tests, are technologies that use low-volume samples, are cost-effective, easy to use, provide fast results, are portable, and have a high accuracy rate. Using such methods, patient management in emergency departments would be facilitated. It has been found that point-of-care tests accelerate triage in emergency departments, shorten the time to start treatment and increase the number of patients discharged on time [2].

In diseases that require urgent treatment, such as meningitis, it is useful to measure creatinine values ​​rapidly in order to adjust the dose of drugs (acyclovir, vancomycin etc.) that can damage the kidneys [3]. Similarly, in cases where contrast-enhanced computed tomography is required, it is useful to know the creatinine value early in order to start intravenous hydration therapy [4]. The aim of this study was to assess the agreement between fingerstick creatinine measurements and serum creatinine values obtained by laboratory enzymatic testing. In this way, kidney function values ​​can be measured early, speeding up diagnosis and treatment in the emergency department.

Also examined is whether there is a difference between creatinine measurements made with the i-STAT device at bedside and the laboratory creatinine measurement, depending on vital signs and chronic diseases.

Methods-materials

Study design and settings

Ethics committee approval was obtained from the Hacettepe University Clinical Research Ethics Committee (code number KA-22048). Financial support for the creatine kit was provided from Hacettepe University Scientific Research Unit (project code TTU-2023-20915). The study was designed as a single center, prospective, observational study.

Participants and measurements

A total of 250 patients aged 18 and over, who attended the Adult Emergency Department between 12.12.2023 and 30.12.2023 and underwent serum biochemistry examination, were eligible for the study; patients who did not give written consent were not included.

Patients or their relatives were informed about the study and written consent was obtained. Patients’ complaints, vital signs and accompanying diseases were recorded on the case report form. Vital signs were categorized as follows: heart rate, < 60 beats per minute was considered low, 60–99 beats per minute was considered normal and 100 or more beats per minute was considered high. Oxygen saturation was considered low if < 92 and normal if 92 or above. Systolic blood pressure was categorized as low if below 110 mmHg, normal if between 110 and 139 mmHg and high if 140 mmHg or above.

Creatinine levels were measured at the point-of-care using the ABBOTT i-STAT device and creatinine cartridge. This device is rechargeable, electric, portable and it operates using disposable cartridges for measurements. For sampling, a few drops of blood from the patients’ fingertips were dropped into the creatinine cartridge. The creatinine cartridge was inserted into the ABBOTT i-STAT device. After 120 s, the result appeared on the device screen and was noted on the case report form. The device measures creatinine values between 0.2 and 20.0 mg/dL [5].

At the same time, venous blood samples were taken from the antecubital fossae of the patients and creatinine measurements were performed in the laboratory using the automatic spectrophotometric method on a Beckman Coulter AU analyzer. In the laboratory, centrifugation takes 5 min and creatinine analysis takes 15 min. However, it takes about 1.5 h to get the results into the hospital automation system due to blood collection from the patient, transportation to the laboratory, operation of the device and other human factors.

Statistical analyses

The normality of continuous variables was assessed using histograms, and Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests. Frequency and percentage values were given for categorical variables. For continuous variables that did not follow normal distribution, median, minimum, maximum and quartile values were reported. Due to the lack of normal distribution assumption for the age variable between females and males, a Mann-Whitney U test was used to examine the difference. The data obtained were analyzed using SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Sciences) for Windows version 23.0 program and R software (version 4.3.1).

The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to examine the agreement between serum creatinine values measured in the laboratory and point-of-care values. A graphical approach, the Bland-Altman plot was used to examine the agreement between measurements, using the “BlandAltmanLeh” package. A significance level of 0.05 was used for all comparisons.

It was concluded that reliable findings could be obtained in the study completed with 246 cases with d = 0.25 effect size for nonparametric one sample Wilcoxon test with 95% power and 5% margin of error [6]. Power analysis was evaluated with the GPower 3.1 program [7].

Results

Two hundred and fifty patients gave written informed consent and were included in the study. Four patients were excluded due to kit errors during fingerstick creatinine measurement. Thus, 246 patients were included in the analysis. Of these, 54.16% (133 patients) were female and 45.94% (113 patients) were male. The median age of the patients was 55.5 (minimum: 18, maximum: 95, quartiles: Q1 = 39.0, Q3 = 68.0). When the patients were divided by gender, the median age for females was 56 (minimum: 18, maximum: 95, quartiles: Q1 = 41, Q3 = 68), and for males was 55 (minimum: 18, maximum: 91, quartiles: Q1 = 37, Q3 = 68). The median serum creatinine level of the patients was 0.71 (range: 0.3–4.96), while the median fingerstick creatinine level was 0.80 (range: 0.3–6.5).

Of the patients, 188 had normal serum creatinine levels, and their fingerstick creatinine levels were also normal. In 23, serum creatinine levels were normal, but fingerstick creatinine levels were high according to the reference range. In 35 patients, both serum and fingerstick creatinine levels were above the reference range. There were no patients with normal fingerstick creatinine levels in whom serum creatinine levels were high. These values ​​are shown in Table 1. The sensitivity of fingerstick test was 100% and specificity of fingerstick test was 89.1%. The positive predictive value of fingerstick test was 60.3% and negative predictive value was 100%.

Discussion

In our study, the median age of the patients was 55.5, with no difference between females and males – the median age for females was 56 and that for males was 55. Strong agreement were observed between the measured values in both the female and male groups. In a study conducted by Vilaine et al., involving patients who were to receive intravenous iodinated contrast medium before computed tomography imaging, serum creatinine was correlated with fingerprint creatinine in both men and women, but the agreement was lower in patients over 75 years of age and in patients with high serum creatinine levels [8].

In another study by Batte et al., conducted on 539 Ugandan children aged between 6 months and 4 years who were hospitalized, agreement was found between fingerstick creatinine measured using the i-STAT device and laboratory serum creatinine [9]. However, these authors observed a significantly lower agreement in children with creatinine levels < 1 mg/dL. Our study was conducted on patients aged 18 and over; we did not include pediatric patients, and so no conclusions can be made regarding this population.

In our study, laboratory serum creatinine and fingerstick creatinine measurements were found to be highly compatible when interpreted according to the Bland-Altman chart. The closest level of agreement to our study was demonstrated in a study conducted by Dashevsky et al. (0.946) [10]. In a study by Griffin et al., fingerstick creatinine value was higher than the serum creatinine value by an average of 22% and this discrepancy was found to be greater at higher serum creatinine values [11]. Since this study was conducted in Guatemala, on patients working outdoors and in a hot climate, it was considered that the reason for the high non-agreement was dehydration in the subjects. In fact, the authors suggested that these devices should have software adapted to climatic conditions.

In a community study conducted by Dally et al. in Nicaragua, i-STAT and StatSensor creatinine devices were compared for 3 groups of 213, 158 and 267 people; It was found that in terms of accuracy, the i-STAT device performed better [12]. In our study, we used the i-STAT device for fingerstick creatinine measurement.

When the patients were examined according to the vital signs measured at the time of admission, a strong agreement was observed between serum creatinine and fingertip creatinine results in the groups with low, normal and high vital values. In a study conducted by Shin et al. in 2018, carried out on 231 patients with systolic blood pressures below 90 mmHg, it was found that even in situations of capillary vasoconstriction and ischemia due to hypoperfusion, there was clinically acceptable similarity between fingerstick creatinine and serum creatinine measurements [13]. In our study, agreement was found in the low blood pressure group as in the other groups.

Limitations

One of the limitations of our study is that, due to it being conducted in an adult emergency department, no conclusions can be drawn concerning pediatric patients. The number of individuals included in subgroups such as hypertensive patients, diabetic patients, and cancer patients, could be increased to allow for more specific conclusions. Similarly, the number of patients with abnormal vital signs or high creatinine levels can be increased. Longer study duration, multi-center studies can be conducted to increase the robustness of the findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, it was observed that there was a strong agreement between the fingertip creatinine measurement performed with the i-STAT device at the bedside and the serum creatinine value measured in the biochemistry laboratory (ICC: 0.996). When the patients were compared in terms of their age, gender, complaints on admission, vital signs and diagnosed diseases to show which factors affected these two values, no significant difference was found.

In addition, i-STAT devices are simple and practical to use, making them a viable tool in the emergency department setting. Therefore, measuring creatinine at the point-of-care may assist emergency physicians expedite patient management.

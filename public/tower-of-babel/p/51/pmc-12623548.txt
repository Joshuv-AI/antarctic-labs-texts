Letter to the Editor Regarding ‘Profile of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease: Mapping Across Different Indian Populations (MAP Study)’
Authors: Rishabh Bose, Kalyani Sridharan, Rohit Gupta
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the article, ‘Prevalence of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease: Mapping Across Different Indian Populations (MAP Study)’, in Diabetes Therapy (2025) [1]. We commend the authors for this effort in highlighting the burden of MASLD across India. However, we would like to discuss several methodological and interpretative issues that warrant caution.

First, the study’s retrospective, multicentre design across 105 clinics lacked uniform data standardization, which may introduce variability. The baseline metabolic characteristics of the screened population were not clearly reported, leaving uncertainty about participants’ metabolic risk profiles. Additionally, a substantial portion of individuals initially screened were excluded from the final analysis, raising the possibility of selection bias in the results.

Second, the regional (‘zonal’) analysis, while novel, is limited by uneven state representation. In several zones a single state’s data dominate – for instance, Uttar Pradesh accounted for ~ 89% of the Central Zone cohort – undermining the generalizability of zone-wise prevalence estimates. The zonal classification scheme also appears arbitrary; for example, Uttarakhand was placed in the Central Zone (instead of the North), which may confound interpretation of the ‘North India’ MASLD burden.

Furthermore, as the study population comprised mainly clinic attendees rather than community participants, the findings may not represent the general population.

Third, the diagnostic approach in the MAP study warrants scrutiny. MASLD was defined solely by a controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) ≥ 238 dB/m (i.e. presence of hepatic steatosis), without confirming the metabolic dysfunction component required by the MASLD definition [2]. Relying on FibroScan steatosis alone may thus overestimate true MASLD prevalence. In addition, using CAP and Liver Stiffness Measurement (LSM) values as surrogates for steatosis severity and fibrosis stage is convenient but imperfect – these measures cannot sharply distinguish intermediate disease stages, potentially leading to misclassification of severity. Another pitfall on defining MASLD on the basis of just a CAP value is that, steatotic liver disease may be due to multiple causes such as alcohol or Hepatitis C. The data regarding whether there was underlying alcohol use, Hepatitis C or any other risk factor which could have led to a steatotic liver disease are not mentioned in the article.

Notably, the MAP study’s reported MASLD prevalence of 68.2% is nearly double the pooled NAFLD prevalence of 38.6% among Indian adults reported by a recent meta-analysis [3]. This discrepancy likely reflects the high-risk, clinic-based sample and differing methodologies, suggesting the MAP findings may not be directly comparable to general population estimates.

In conclusion, while the MAP study is a commendable beginning that provides valuable data and raises national awareness of MASLD, its limitations necessitate cautious interpretation of the results. We encourage further prospective, controlled studies with standardized protocols to validate and build upon these important findings.

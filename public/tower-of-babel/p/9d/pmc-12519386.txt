Comparative outcomes of culprit-only versus complete revascularisation in cardiogenic shock complicating acute myocardial infarction: insights from the Gulf-Cardiogenic Shock registry

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesTo compare in-hospital and long-term outcomes between culprit-only percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and multivessel PCI in patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock and multivessel coronary artery disease.DesignRetrospective subgroup analysis of the multicentre Gulf-Cardiogenic Shock registry.Setting13 tertiary care centres across six Gulf countries (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, UAE, Kuwait and Bahrain) between January 2020 and December 2022.Participants961 patients with angiographically confirmed multivessel coronary artery disease who underwent PCI were included from the Gulf-Cardiogenic Shock registry. Patients were divided into culprit-only PCI group (n=792, 82.4%) and multivessel PCI group (n=169, 17.6%). Patients with single-vessel disease were excluded.InterventionsPatients underwent either culprit-only PCI (intervention limited to the culprit artery) or multivessel PCI (immediate intervention to both culprit and non-culprit arteries during the same procedure).Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcome was in-hospital all-cause mortality. Secondary outcomes included reinfarction, cerebrovascular accident, major and minor bleeding events, target lesion revascularisation, target vessel revascularisation, hospital stay duration and freedom from major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCEs) at 6 and 12 months.ResultsHospital mortality was comparable between multivessel PCI and culprit-only PCI groups (43.2% vs 46.1%; p=0.493). Freedom from MACCE rates at 6 and 12 months were 62% and 46% for multivessel PCI versus 70% and 49% for culprit-only PCI, respectively (log-rank p=0.711). Subgroup analysis revealed that culprit-only PCI was associated with increased hospital mortality in patients older than 70 years (OR 1.55, 95% CI: 1.01 to 2.39). Multivariable analysis of the interaction between revascularisation strategy and the subgroups revealed that culprit vessel revascularisation was associated with increased mortality in patients with left main disease (OR: 1.99 (95% CI: 1.22 to 3.27), p=0.006) and left anterior descending lesions (OR: 1.54 (95% CI: 1.06 to 2.25), p=0.025).ConclusionsNo statistically significant differences in hospital mortality or long-term MACCE-free survival were observed between culprit-only PCI and multivessel PCI strategies in patients with cardiogenic shock complicating acute myocardial infarction. However, patients older than 70 years may benefit from a multivessel PCI approach. These findings support current guideline recommendations favouring culprit-only PCI due to reduced procedural complexity while highlighting the need for individualised treatment strategies based on patient age and clinical factors. Further prospective randomised studies are needed to validate these age-specific findings and identify optimal patient selection criteria for each revascularisation strategy.Keywords: Myocardial infarction, Observational Study, Coronary intervention

Introduction

Cardiogenic shock (CS) is defined as end-organ damage and tissue hypoxia due to cardiac dysfunction, with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) being the most common underlying cause. AMI leads to myocardial necrosis, loss of ventricular function and reduced cardiac output, making CS a leading cause of in-hospital mortality in patients with AMI.1 Within the USA, the incidence of CS in patients with AMI ranges from 5% to 10%, and despite advances in treatment, the incidence has been increasing since 2000.2 3 Mortality remains high, with 30-day mortality rates of approximately 40% and 1-year mortality rates approaching 50%. Survivors of CS often experience significant morbidity, including high rates of readmission and the need for ongoing supportive care.1

In patients presenting with AMI, urgent coronary angiography and revascularisation are critical for definitive therapy. However, the optimal revascularisation strategy, whether to perform percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) of the culprit lesion alone versus multivessel revascularisation, has been the subject of ongoing investigation. Current expert consensus recommends culprit vessel-only primary PCI in patients presenting with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and multivessel disease, particularly in the setting of CS.4 This recommendation is largely based on findings from the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial, which demonstrated that multivessel PCI in such patients does not offer additional survival benefits. Instead, it is associated with an increased risk of adverse outcomes, including mortality and the need for renal replacement therapy (RRT).5

Conversely, in low-risk patients, immediate multivessel PCI may be considered. This approach has been shown in certain studies to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), primarily by decreasing the need for repeat revascularisation. A meta-analysis by Bangalore et al demonstrated that single-procedure multivessel PCI significantly reduced the risk of death or myocardial infarction (MI) compared with culprit-only PCI, whereas staged multivessel PCI did not offer the same benefit.6 The COMPLETE trial further corroborated these findings, showing that complete revascularisation significantly reduced the composite outcomes of cardiovascular death or MI compared with culprit-lesion-only PCI.7 However, these studies largely excluded patients with CS, limiting their applicability to this high-risk population.

These findings emphasise the need for tailored revascularisation strategies in patients presenting with AMI complicated by CS (AMI-CS). Given the haemodynamic instability and metabolic derangements in this subgroup, the potential benefits of culprit versus multivessel PCI must be considered with respect to procedural complexity, contrast load and procedure time. To further analyse this, we conducted a retrospective analysis of patients with CS-AMI, comparing outcomes between those who underwent culprit-only PCI and those who underwent multivessel PCI.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This study is a retrospective subgroup analysis of the multicentre Gulf-Cardiogenic Shock (Gulf-CS) registry,8 a multicentre retrospective registry investigating CS secondary to angiographically confirmed MI.8 The registry included 1513 patients admitted to 13 tertiary care centres across six Gulf countries (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, UAE, Kuwait and Bahrain) between January 2020 and December 2022. For this analysis, patients with at least two-vessel coronary artery disease who underwent PCI were included, while those with single-vessel disease were excluded.

Subgroup classification

Patients were divided into two groups: (1) culprit-only PCI group (n=792): patients who underwent PCI limited to the culprit artery. (2) Multivessel PCI group (n=169): patients underwent immediate multivessel PCI to address both the culprit artery and non-culprit arteries.

Patient and public involvement

None.

Data collection

Baseline demographics, comorbidities, haemodynamic profiles, laboratory results and angiographic findings were retrieved from the Gulf-CS registry. Coronary angiographic procedural details, including stent characteristics and the number of vessels treated, were included. Data on in-hospital outcomes, complications and follow-up outcomes up to 2 years were also collected.

Study outcomes

Hospital outcomes were compared between the two groups. The primary outcome was in-hospital all-cause mortality. The secondary outcomes included reinfarction, cerebrovascular accident (CVA), major and minor bleeding events, target lesion revascularisation (TLR), target vessel revascularisation (TVR), hospital stay duration, discharge medications and freedom from major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCEs) at 6 and 12 months. The MACCE composite outcome included all-cause death, re-infarction, CVA, TLR and TVR.

Study definitions

CS was defined as a systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg for ≥30 min or the need for vasopressors, inotropes or mechanical circulatory support to maintain a systolic blood pressure ≥90 mm Hg with evidence of tissue hypoperfusion.9 The current study adopted the Society for Cardiovascular Angiography and Interventions (SCAI)–Cardiogenic Shock Working Group shock stage classification, and the maximum stage during hospitalisation was determined.10 11 The SCAI shock classification is a five-stage system (A–E). Stage A identifies at-risk patients not currently experiencing signs or symptoms of CS but at risk for its development, Stage B involves clinical evidence of haemodynamic instability without hypoperfusion and Stage C defines hypoperfusion requiring pharmacological or mechanical intervention beyond volume resuscitation. Stage D indicates failure of the initial support strategy to restore perfusion, while Stage E represents circulatory collapse with organ failure. The SCAI shock Stage A, a preshock stage, was excluded. The time of shock onset is the time when CS is first diagnosed. The fourth universal definition of MI was used.12 The culprit artery was defined as the coronary artery identified as the primary source of MI based on clinical, ECG, echocardiographic and angiographic findings.13 CVAs included ischaemic or haemorrhagic strokes and transient ischaemic attack. Bleeding events were categorised via the Bleeding Academic Research Consortium classification.14 TLR referred to any reintervention within 5 mm of a coronary stent, whereas TVR referred to any repeat intervention within the previously treated vessel, regardless of the segment.15 MACCE included the composite of the MI, CVA, TLR and TVR endpoints.

Statistical analysis

Stata V.18 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Data with normal distribution was presented as mean and SD and compared with the t-test. Skewed data were presented as median and (Q1–Q3) and compared with the Mann-Whitney test. Categorical data were presented as numbers and percentages and compared with the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate. Time-to-event data were plotted using a Kaplan-Meier curve and compared with the log-rank test. Univariable analysis was used to evaluate the interactions between subgroups of clinical importance and the culprit vessel PCI, and the ORs were plotted using the forest plot. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate factors associated with hospital MACCE. Stepwise logistic regression with forward selection was done, and non-collinear variables with a p value of <0.05 were included in the final model. Culprit PCI was introduced into the final model. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and comorbidities

Among the 1513 patients with CS-AMI in the Gulf-CS registry, 792 (52.4%) underwent culprit-only PCI, whereas 169 (11.2%) underwent multivessel PCI. Patients who underwent culprit-only PCI were older (61 vs 58 years, p=0.007), more likely to be women (22.2% vs 11.2%, p=0.001) and had a higher body mass index (BMI) (28 vs 26, p=0.003) than those in the multivessel PCI group. There were no significant differences in the prevalence of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, chronic kidney disease or other comorbidities between the two groups (table 1).

Clinical presentation and haemodynamics

At presentation, there were no significant differences between the culprit-only PCI and multivessel PCI groups in terms of Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) type, cardiac arrest status or arrhythmias (p>0.05). Haemodynamic parameters, including the mean arterial pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate and laboratory test results, were similar between the groups at presentation (p>0.05). However, the culprit-only PCI group had lower oxygen saturation (89% vs 90%, p=0.042). The prevalence of advanced SCAI stages was not significantly different between the groups (p=0.213), indicating a similar severity of shock at baseline (table 1).

Echocardiographic and angiographic findings

Echocardiographic findings revealed that the culprit-only PCI group had a greater left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (31% vs 27%, p=0.017). Other echocardiographic parameters, including right ventricular function and pulmonary artery systolic pressure, were comparable between the two groups (p>0.05). The culprit lesion location and SYNTAX score did not differ significantly between the two groups (p=0.618 and p=0.912, respectively), indicating similar coronary anatomy complexity (table 1).

Management strategies and procedural characteristics

There were no significant differences between the groups in the use of pressor support (91% vs 89.9%, p=0.654), mechanical circulatory support (85.8% vs 53.8%, p=0.058) or renal support (15% vs 18.9%, p=0.22). However, patients who underwent multivessel PCI had significantly longer stent lengths for the culprit lesions than did those who underwent culprit-only PCI (30 mm vs 28 mm, p=0.022). Additionally, multivessel PCI patients had a greater number of stents for culprit lesions (p<0.001) (table 2).

Hospital outcomes

There were no significant differences in hospital outcomes or discharge medications between the two groups (table 3). Subgroup analysis revealed that hospital mortality was not significantly affected by the type of PCI in male patients, patients with diabetes mellitus, those with LVEF>45%, those with STEMI or those with Left Anterior Descending Artery (LAD), Left Circumflex Artery (LCX) and Right Coronary Artery (RCA) as culprit lesions. However, culprit-only PCI was associated with an increased risk of hospital mortality in patients aged over 70 years (figure 1). Multivariable analysis of the interaction between revascularisation strategy and the subgroups (age >70, sex, diabetes, LVEF, STEMI, culprit vessel) revealed that culprit vessel revascularisation was associated with increased mortality in patients with Left Main (LM) (OR: 1.99 (95% CI: 1.22 to 3.27), p=0.006) and LAD (OR: 1.54 (95% CI: 1.06 to 2.25), p=0.025).

Follow-up survival rates

The median follow-up duration was 6 months (IQR 3–11 months). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis at 6 and 12 months revealed that the freedom from MACCE was 62% and 46%, respectively, for patients who underwent multivessel PCI and 70% and 49%, respectively, for those who underwent culprit-only PCI (log-rank p=0.711) (figure 2).

Discussion

There was no statistically significant difference in hospital mortality between multivessel PCI and culprit-only PCI. Freedom from MACCEs at 6 and 12 months did not significantly differ between culprit and multivessel PCI. The outcomes are largely comparable, aligning with current guidelines favouring culprit-only PCI owing to its reduced procedural complexity and reduced risk of RRT.4 5

Baseline characteristics

Patients in the culprit-only PCI group were older and more frequently female than those in the multivessel PCI group. Additionally, they had a greater BMI and LVEF. These differences potentially reflect physician selection for more aggressive revascularisation in younger patients and those with low ejection fraction. Other baseline characteristics, including rates of diabetes mellitus, hypertension and prior MI, were comparable between the groups, with no statistically significant differences.

Management and procedural characteristics

Both groups exhibited non-significant differences in the use of mechanical circulatory support (MCS) and RRT, with similar rates of pressor. Compared with the culprit-only PCI group, the multivessel PCI group had higher rates of MCS utilisation, reflecting the increased haemodynamic instability associated with treating multiple coronary arteries. The procedural characteristics further highlighted the complexity of multivessel PCI, with significantly longer stent lengths for all treated vessels and a greater number of stents per treated vessel. This finding highlights the procedural complexity of pursuing complete revascularisation and the trade-offs that may be associated with it.

Clinical outcomes

In terms of clinical outcomes, hospital mortality was slightly better for multivessel PCI. The rates of reinfarction, target lesion revascularisation and major bleeding were comparable. Notably, subgroup analysis indicated that culprit-only PCI was associated with increased mortality in patients older than 70 years.

Comparison with existing literature

Our results are consistent with those of the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial, which demonstrated that culprit-only PCI was associated with reduced risks of RRT and improved survival outcomes compared with multivessel PCI. The trial’s primary outcome—a composite of death or severe renal failure requiring RRT at 30 days—was lower in the culprit-only PCI group (45.9% vs 55.4%, relative risk 0.83; 95% CI: 0.71 to 0.96; p=0.01).5 In our study, hospital mortality rates were 46.1% for culprit-only PCI and rates of RRT were 15.2% versus 18.9% (p=0.221). Although our findings did not reach statistical significance and showed a slight advantage toward the multivessel PCI approach, they still highlight the elevated rates of renal support and the use of MCS, which has been independently associated with mortality in patients with CS-AMI.16 The major differences between our study and the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial are the younger age in our patients, high prevalence of SCAI Stage D shock and post-cardiac arrest PCI. The low lactate levels in our study may reflect early resuscitation efforts despite the advanced shock stage.

The advantages of culprit-only PCI in reducing renal complications are likely attributable to reduced procedural complexity, including shorter procedural times and lower contrast loads, which mitigate the risks of renal injury and haemodynamic instability.4 These factors are particularly important in the CS population, where patients are already predisposed to acute kidney injury and ischaemic complications.

In contrast, broader STEMI-focused studies, such as the COMPLETE trial, reported significant benefits of multivessel PCI, including reductions in cardiovascular death or MI (7.8% vs 10.5%, HR 0.74; 95% CI: 0.60 to 0.91; p=0.004).7 Similarly, meta-analyses by Bangalore et al and Levett et al showed that single-procedure complete revascularisation significantly reduced MACE, MI and repeat revascularisation compared with culprit-only PCI.6 17 However, these analyses were largely composed of stable patients and did not address the challenges of CS, such as the need for rapid stabilisation and the risks associated with prolonged procedures. In the subgroup analysis, STEMI was not associated with increased hospital in culprit versus multivessel PCI.

Our study also revealed a trend toward higher mortality in patients over 70 years who underwent culprit-only PCI, suggesting potentially worse outcomes with increasing age. This observation aligns with the literature, where advanced age has been identified as an independent predictor of mortality in patients with CS. More specifically, Vergara et al identified age >75 years as an independent predictor of 2-year cardiac mortality in patients with AMI complicated by CS treated with primary PCI, with an HR of 2.09.18

Freedom from MACCE at 6 and 12 months revealed no differences between the multivessel PCI and culprit-only PCI groups. Although these differences did not reach statistical significance, the slight early advantage in MACCE at 6 months for culprit-only PCI mirrors the trends observed in the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial, where culprit-only PCI reduced the composite outcome of death or severe renal failure requiring RRT.5 However, in broader STEMI populations without CS, studies such as the COMPLETE trial and meta-analyses by Bangalore et al and Levett et al have demonstrated significant benefits of multivessel PCI in reducing MACCE, driven by lower rates of repeat revascularisation and MI.6 7 17

Taken together, our results add to the growing body of evidence supporting a more conservative revascularisation strategy in patients with CS. By avoiding the additional procedural burden of multivessel PCI, culprit-only PCI minimises risks while maintaining comparable clinical outcomes. However, subgroup analyses in the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial did highlight specific patient populations—such as those with persistent ischaemia or anatomically simple lesions—where multivessel PCI might offer benefits.5 Further studies are warranted to refine specific patient subgroups that may benefit from multivessel PCI.

Limitations

The observational nature of this study limits its ability to establish causality. Additionally, subgroup analyses were limited by small sample sizes, reducing the statistical power to detect significant differences within specific patient populations. The study is also limited by the imbalance between the two groups, as the number of subjects who underwent culprit only was 4.7 times higher than those who received multivessel PCI, which further limited the statistical analysis.

Conclusion

In this study, we compared culprit-only PCI and multivessel PCI in patients with CS-AMI and found no statistically significant difference in in-hospital mortality between the two approaches. Our findings are consistent with current evidence recommending culprit-only PCI as the preferred strategy for patients with CS because of its reduced procedural complexity and lower risk of renal and haemodynamic complications. Individualised treatment strategies remain essential for optimising outcomes in this critically ill population.

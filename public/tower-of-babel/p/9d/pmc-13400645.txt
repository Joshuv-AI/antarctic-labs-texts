POTEF upregulation drives hepatocellular carcinoma progression via oncogenic signaling and immune modulation
Authors: Zan Li, Hao Li, Hao Ren, Shujie Cheng, Zhiyuan Luo, Jihong Yang, Jisen Zhao, Yuanlong Zhou, Feiyang Zhang, Peijun Ying, De Liu, Tingting Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMembers of the POTE gene family have been implicated in tumorigenesis across multiple malignancies. However, the biological and clinical significance of POTEF in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains unclear. Differential expression analyses of POTE family genes were performed using transcriptomic sequencing of 12 paired HCC and adjacent tissues and validated using TCGA and GEO datasets. POTEF expression was further confirmed by qRT-PCR, western blotting, and immunohistochemistry in clinical specimens. Functional assays, including CCK-8, wound-healing, apoptosis, and xenograft experiments, were conducted following POTEF overexpression or silencing in Huh7 cells. Transcriptomic analyses, pathway enrichment, and immune infiltration analyses were subsequently performed. POTEF was significantly upregulated in HCC tissues and was associated with unfavorable prognosis, advanced pathological stage, vascular invasion, and elevated serum AFP levels. Functional assays demonstrated that POTEF promoted hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation and migration while suppressing apoptosis in vitro, and xenograft experiments further showed that POTEF enhanced tumor growth in vivo.Transcriptomic profiling revealed enrichment of pathways related to extracellular matrix remodeling and carcinogenesis-associated signaling. Further experimental analyses suggested that POTEF may contribute to HCC progression partly through epithelial–mesenchymal transition-associated regulation. In addition, POTEF expression was positively associated with macrophage infiltration, particularly CD163-positive macrophage enrichment. Collectively, these findings indicate that POTEF is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma and is associated with EMT-related regulation and macrophage infiltration, highlighting its potential clinical relevance as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-56207-z.Keywords: POTEF, Hepatocellular carcinoma, EMT, Macrophage infiltration, Biomarker, PrognosisSubject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Oncology

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent and lethal malignancies worldwide, accounting for the majority of primary liver cancer cases and ranking as one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide1,2. Despite advances in surgical techniques, local ablation, and immunotherapeutic approaches, the overall survival rate of HCC patients remains poor due to late diagnosis3, high recurrence rates, and limited treatment options for advanced disease stages3–5. The complex molecular heterogeneity of HCC6 highlights the urgent need to identify novel oncogenic drivers and prognostic biomarkers to guide precision diagnosis and targeted therapy.

The POTE (Prostate, Ovary, Testis, and Embryo expression) gene family encodes a group of proteins characterized by ankyrin repeat and actin-binding domains7,8. These domains facilitate protein–protein interactions and cellular structural modulation9. The POTE family is primarily expressed in germline and embryonic tissues but aberrantly reactivated in various cancers10, classifying them as cancer-testis antigens (CTAs)11,12. This restricted expression in normal tissues, coupled with widespread overexpression in malignancies, suggests potential oncogenic and immunogenic functions10,13. Previous studies have linked POTE family members, including POTEE, POTEF, and POTEM, to tumorigenesis in breast, colorectal, prostate, ovarian, and lung cancers, where they participate in key biological processes such as proliferation, apoptosis inhibition, and metastasis9,13,14.

Among the POTE family members, POTEF has attracted increasing attention because of its potential involvement in tumor aggressiveness and cell survival regulation. In breast and prostate cancers, POTEF has been reported to promote invasive behavior, interact with survival pathways, and influence cell death mechanisms15. However, its functional roles and clinical significance in hepatocellular carcinoma remain largely unknown. Given the liver’s distinct metabolic and immunological environment, elucidating the potential role of POTEF in HCC may provide novel insights into tumor progression and immune regulation.

Accumulating transcriptomic datasets and cancer bioinformatics platforms, including The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), have facilitated systematic analyses of tumor-associated gene expression patterns and their clinical significance16. In parallel, increasing evidence highlights the critical role of the tumor microenvironment, particularly immune-cell infiltration17, in hepatocellular carcinoma progression and therapeutic responsiveness18,19. However, whether POTEF contributes to immune remodeling and macrophage-associated regulation in HCC remains unclear.

In this study, we systematically investigated the expression pattern, biological function, and clinical relevance of POTEF in hepatocellular carcinoma. Through integrated transcriptomic analyses, in vitro and in vivo functional experiments, and immune infiltration validation, we demonstrated that POTEF is markedly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma and is associated with unfavorable prognosis and aggressive clinicopathological characteristics. Mechanistically, POTEF was associated with transcriptional programs related to extracellular matrix remodeling and epithelial–mesenchymal transition. Furthermore, immune infiltration analyses combined with immunohistochemical validation revealed a close association between POTEF expression and macrophage infiltration, particularly CD163-positive macrophage enrichment. These results indicate that POTEF may function as a tumor-promoting factor and represent a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Materials and methods

Clinical samples and ethical approval

Hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous liver tissues were collected from patients undergoing surgical resection at the Affiliated Hospital of Hebei University. None of the patients received preoperative chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or immunotherapy. All specimens were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Hematoxylin–eosin (HE) staining was performed to confirm the tumor cell proportion (> 75%) in all cancer samples. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Hebei University (Approval No.: HDFYLL-KY-2024-198), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from tissues and cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Vazyme, China). The concentration and purity of RNA were measured using a Gen5 microplate reader (BioTek, USA). cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using the HiScript III RT SuperMix Kit (Vazyme). Quantitative PCR was conducted on an ABI 7300 system with ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme). β-actin served as the internal control. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences are listed in Table 1.

Western blotting

Protein lysates were prepared from tissues or cells using RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors. Equal amounts of total protein were separated via SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore). After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against POTEF (abcepta, China), E-cadherin (Proteintech, China), N-cadherin (Proteintech, China), SNAI1 (BYabscience, China), and β-actin (Abclonal, China), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Signals were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL, Epizyme, China) and quantified with the Vilber Fusion imaging system.

Cell culture and transfection

The human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line Huh7 was cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. For functional studies, cells were transfected with either POTEF-overexpression plasmids or siRNAs (20 nM) targeting POTEF using NB Transfection Reagent (Bioscien, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR and western blotting. siRNA sequences are provided in Table 2.

Cell proliferation assay (CCK-8)

Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Meilunbio, China). Transfected cells (5 × 10³/well) were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated for 24–72 h. At designated time points, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 1.5 h at 37 °C. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Wound-healing (scratch) assay

Cell migration was evaluated using wound-healing assays. Briefly, transfected cells were seeded into 6-well plates and grown to 90% confluence. A uniform scratch was made using a sterile pipette tip, and detached cells were removed by PBS washing. Serum-free medium was added, and wound closure was photographed at 0, 12, and 24 h under an inverted microscope. Migration areas were quantified using ImageJ software.

Flow cytometry for apoptosis analysis

Cell apoptosis was determined using an Annexin V-APC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Vazyme, China). After 48 h of transfection, cells were harvested, washed twice with cold PBS, and resuspended in 100 µL of binding buffer. Subsequently, 5 µL of Annexin V-APC and 5 µL of PI solution were added and incubated for 10 min in the dark at room temperature. Samples were analyzed by flow cytometry (BD FACSCalibur, USA) within one hour.

Establishment of stable POTEF-overexpression and POTEF-knockdown cell lines

Stable POTEF-overexpression and POTEF-knockdown Huh7 cell lines were established using lentiviral vectors constructed by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). Cells were infected according to the manufacturer’s instructions and selected using puromycin to establish stable cell lines. Stable overexpression and knockdown efficiencies were confirmed by western blotting.

Subcutaneous xenograft tumor model

BALB/c nude mice (male, 5 weeks old) were purchased from Huafukang Bioscience Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Huh7 cells stably transduced with POTEF-overexpression or POTEF-knockdown lentiviral vectors constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China), along with corresponding control cells, were suspended in PBS and subcutaneously injected into the right flank of mice (5 × 10^6 cells per mouse). Tumor volume was measured every 3 days using calipers and calculated using the following formula: volume = (length × width²)/2. At the experimental endpoint, mice were euthanized and tumor tissues were collected for subsequent analyses.

All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethical Committee of Hebei University (Approval No. IACUC-HBU2024M011) and performed in accordance with institutional guidelines for animal care and use.

Bioinformatic and statistical analyses

RNA-sequencing and clinical data for hepatocellular carcinoma were retrieved from the TCGA-LIHC database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and validated using the GEO datasets GSE214846 and GSE135631. Transcriptomic sequencing of 12 paired hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous tissues was performed by Hongxu Biotechnology (Shanghai, China) using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package in R (v3.6.3) with thresholds of |logFC| > 1.5 and adjusted p < 0.05. Survival analyses were conducted using the survival and survminer packages, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using the pROC package. Immune infiltration analyses were performed using the TIMER2.0 platform. Immunohistochemical staining images of POTEF in normal liver tissues and hepatocellular carcinoma tissues were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/). Kaplan–Meier survival analyses of selected genes were additionally validated using the Kaplan–Meier Plotter database (https://kmplot.com/analysis/).

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical staining was performed using a universal two-step detection kit (ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China). Briefly, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanol, and subjected to antigen retrieval using citrate buffer. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide, followed by incubation with normal goat serum for nonspecific blocking. Sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against POTEF, CD68 (Proteintech, China), CD163 (Servicebio, China), and CD66b (Epizyme, China). After washing, sections were sequentially incubated with reaction enhancer and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Immunoreactivity was visualized using DAB substrate, and nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin. Images were captured under a light microscope. Positive staining areas were quantified using ImageJ software. Correlations between POTEF mRNA expression and immune-cell-positive areas in matched hepatocellular carcinoma tissues were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted in triplicate. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and R software. Differences between two groups were assessed using Student’s t-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were evaluated by one-way ANOVA. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to evaluate correlations between variables.

Results

POTEF is upregulated and connected with unfavorable prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma

Transcriptomic sequencing was performed using 12 paired hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous tissues to identify dysregulated members of the POTE family. Differential expression analysis demonstrated that several POTE family genes were aberrantly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, including POTEC, POTEM, POTEI, and POTEF, whereas POTEJ showed relatively lower expression (Fig. 1A).

Among these candidates, POTEF demonstrated significant prognostic relevance and relatively favorable diagnostic performance and was therefore selected for subsequent analyses. Expression analyses of additional POTE family members in hepatocellular carcinoma are presented in Supplementary Figure S1-S5.

Analysis of the TCGA-LIHC cohort revealed that POTEF expression was significantly increased in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues compared with normal liver tissues (p < 0.0001; Fig. 1B). Consistent results were observed in the independent GEO validation cohorts GSE214846 and GSE135631, both of which confirmed elevated POTEF expression in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues (p < 0.001; Fig. 1C).

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with high POTEF expression had significantly poorer overall survival than those with low POTEF expression (HR = 1.58, 95% CI: 1.11–2.24, p = 0.010; Fig. 1D). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis further showed that POTEF possessed moderate diagnostic performance for distinguishing hepatocellular carcinoma tissues from normal liver tissues, with an AUC value of 0.679 (95% CI:0.612–0.746; Fig. 1E). Additional prognostic and diagnostic analyses of other POTE family members are provided in Supplementary Figure S1-S5. Pan-cancer analysis demonstrated that POTEF was aberrantly expressed across multiple tumor types, including hepatocellular carcinoma, suggesting that POTEF may exert a broader oncogenic role in human malignancies (Fig. 1F). Comprehensive pan-cancer expression profiles of additional POTE family members across TCGA datasets are shown in Supplementary Data. Collectively, these findings indicate that POTEF is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma and may serve as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker.

POTEF is overexpressed in clinical hepatocellular carcinoma samples and promotes malignant phenotypes

siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing and plasmid-mediated POTEF overexpression models were established. qRT-PCR analysis of 31 paired hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent tissues demonstrated that POTEF mRNA expression was significantly elevated in tumor tissues (Fig. 2A). Consistently, immunohistochemical staining and western blot analysis further confirmed increased POTEF protein expression in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues compared with adjacent tissues (Figs. 2B-C).To investigate the biological role of POTEF in hepatocellular carcinoma cells, siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing and plasmid-mediated POTEF overexpression models were established in Huh7 cells and validated by western blotting (Fig. 2D and H). Functional assays demonstrated that siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing was associated with reduced cell proliferation and migration, accompanied by increased apoptotic cell death (Figs. 2E-G). In contrast, plasmid-mediated POTEF overexpression enhanced proliferative and migratory capacities while reducing apoptosis (Figs. 2I-K).

POTEF expression is linked to aggressive clinicopathological features in hepatocellular carcinoma

Analysis of hepatocellular carcinoma samples from the TCGA-LIHC dataset demonstrated that POTEF expression was significantly associated with multiple aggressive clinicopathological characteristics. POTEF expression was significantly elevated in patients with advanced pathological stage, particularly in Stage III-IV tumors compared with Stage I tumors (Fig. 4A). In addition, patients with elevated serum alpha-fetoprotein levels (AFP > 400 ng/mL) exhibited significantly higher POTEF expression than those with AFP ≤ 400 ng/mL (Fig. 4B). Increased POTEF expression was also observed in patients with vascular invasion (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, POTEF expression gradually increased with advancing pathological T stage and was significantly higher in T3-T4 tumors compared with T1 tumors (Fig. 4D). In contrast, no significant associations were observed between POTEF expression and pathological N stage or patient age (Figs. 4E-F). These observations suggest that elevated POTEF expression is associated with aggressive tumor progression in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Transcriptomic profiling reveals POTEF-associated EMT- and tumor-related programs

Transcriptomic sequencing was conducted using 12 paired hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous tissues to explore the molecular programs associated with POTEF expression. Differential expression analysis revealed substantial transcriptional alterations between the POTEF-high and POTEF-low groups, as illustrated by the volcano plots (Fig. 5A).

(A) Volcano plots showing differentially expressed genes identified by transcriptomic sequencing of 12 paired hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous tissues. (B) KEGG enrichment analyses of POTEF-associated differentially expressed genes in POTEF-high and POTEF-low groups. (C) Western blot analysis of EMT-related proteins, including E-cadherin, N-cadherin, and Snail, following POTEF overexpression or knockdown in Huh7 cells. (D) Quantitative analysis of EMT-related protein expression normalized to β-actin.Data are presented as mean ± SD. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001.

KEGG enrichment analysis demonstrated that POTEF-associated differentially expressed genes were enriched in multiple cancer-related pathways. In the POTEF-high group, enriched pathways were primarily connected with extracellular matrix remodeling, xenobiotic metabolism, and carcinogenesis-related signaling, including ECM–receptor interaction, drug metabolism–cytochrome P450, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, and chemical carcinogenesis-related pathways. The POTEF-low group also showed enrichment in several tumor-related pathways, including cell cycle regulation, DNA replication, homologous recombination, p53 signaling, and ECM–receptor interaction (Fig. 5B). Given the close association between extracellular matrix remodeling, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and tumor migratory capacity, EMT-related proteins were subsequently examined by western blotting. Plasmid-mediated POTEF overexpression increased the expression of N-cadherin and Snail while decreasing E-cadherin expression. In contrast, siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing was correlated with reduced N-cadherin and Snail expression and increased E-cadherin levels (Figs. 5C-D).

Collectively, these results indicate that POTEF may promote hepatocellular carcinoma progression partly through EMT-related regulation and coordinated modulation of tumor-associated transcriptional programs.

POTEF-associated negatively correlated genes in hepatocellular carcinoma

Differentially expressed genes were screened using the criteria of |logFC| > 1.5 and adjusted p < 0.05. Venn analysis identified eight genes—CNTN1, PTCHD1, PHACTR3, MYO3A, OVOL1, NOTUM, IGF2, and RIMBP2, that were negatively associated with POTEF expression in hepatocellular carcinoma (Fig. 6A). Moreover, three positively correlated genes, including SERTM2, GRIK3, and PTPRQ, were identified; however, these genes did not exhibit significant prognostic associations in TCGA-based Kaplan–Meier survival analyses (p > 0.05; Supplementary Data). Therefore, subsequent analyses focused primarily on negatively correlated genes with potential prognostic relevance.

POTEF-associated negatively correlated genes in hepatocellular carcinoma. (A) Venn diagram showing genes inversely associated with POTEF expression. (B–I) Kaplan–Meier survival analyses of CNTN1, PTCHD1, PHACTR3, MYO3A, OVOL1, NOTUM, IGF2, and RIMBP2 in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma.

Kaplan-Meier survival analyses demonstrated that higher expression levels of CNTN1, PHACTR3, MYO3A, NOTUM, IGF2, and RIMBP2 were significantly correlated with prolonged overall survival in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (p < 0.05; Figs. 6B-I). PTCHD1 and OVOL1 exhibited similar survival tendencies; however, the differences did not reach statistical significance. These results indicate that several genes inversely linked to POTEF expression may be linked to favorable prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma.

POTEF expression is associated with macrophage infiltration in hepatocellular carcinoma

Immunohistochemical staining for CD68, CD163, and CD66b was performed on consecutive sections from clinical hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent tissues to validate the TIMER2-derived immune infiltration analysis. Compared with adjacent tissues, CD163-positive macrophage infiltration was significantly increased in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, whereas the overall CD68-positive macrophage area was reduced. In contrast, CD66b-positive neutrophils showed no significant difference between tumor and adjacent tissues (Figs. 7B-C).

Experimental validation of immune infiltration associated with POTEF expression in hepatocellular carcinoma. (A) TIMER2 analysis showing the correlation between POTEF expression and immune-cell infiltration in hepatocellular carcinoma. (B) Representative immunohistochemical staining of CD68, CD163, and CD66b in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent tissues. Scale bars = 50 μm. (C) Quantification of CD68-, CD163-, and CD66b-positive areas in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent tissues. Correlations were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation test.

Correlation analysis was subsequently conducted using matched hepatocellular carcinoma tissues from the same clinical cohort subjected to POTEF mRNA quantification. POTEF mRNA expression exhibited a moderate positive correlation with CD68-positive area (Spearman r = 0.485, p = 0.006) and a strong positive correlation with CD163-positive area (Spearman r = 0.785, p < 0.001) (Fig. 7D). However, no significant correlation was observed between POTEF expression and CD66b-positive area (Supplementary Figure S5). Although the overall CD68-positive macrophage area was decreased in tumor tissues compared with adjacent tissues, POTEF expression within tumor tissues remained positively associated with CD68-positive infiltration levels.

Taken together, these findings support the TIMER2 prediction and suggest that POTEF expression is more closely associated with macrophage infiltration, particularly CD163-positive M2-like macrophage enrichment, in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major global health challenge, with rising incidence and limited therapeutic success in advanced stages20. Identifying novel oncogenic drivers and clinically actionable biomarkers is critical to improving patient prognosis21. In this study, we identified POTEF as a potential tumor-promoting factor that may contribute to hepatocellular carcinoma progression through coordinated effects on cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis, and macrophage infiltration. Our integrative analyses combining clinical data, transcriptomic profiling, and functional assays demonstrated that POTEF may serve as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma.

The POTE gene family has recently attracted attention as a group of primate-specific cancer-testis antigens with emerging roles in tumor aggressiveness9,22. Previous studies have reported abnormal expression of POTE family members in multiple malignancies, including breast, prostate, and pancreatic cancers9,10,23. Consistent with these observations, our transcriptomic sequencing and multi-cohort analyses demonstrated that several POTE family members, including POTEF, POTEM, POTEC, POTEI, and POTEJ, were dysregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues. Among these candidates, POTEF was selected for further investigation because of its significant prognostic relevance, relatively favorable diagnostic performance, and consistent differential expression across multiple independent GEO cohorts (Fig. 1). Previous studies have suggested that members of the POTE ankyrin domain family may participate in tumor progression and immune-related signaling pathways9, including Toll-like receptor-related regulation24,25. These findings suggest that POTEF may be associated with malignant progression and macrophage-associated immune infiltration in hepatocellular carcinoma. Notably, elevated POTEF expression was significantly associated with advanced pathological stage, increased serum AFP levels, and vascular invasion (Fig. 4), suggesting a potential association between POTEF expression and hepatocellular carcinoma progression.

Functional experiments further demonstrated that plasmid-mediated POTEF overexpression enhanced proliferative and migratory capacities while suppressing apoptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma cells, whereas siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing exhibited the opposite effects (Fig. 2). In vivo subcutaneous xenograft experiments further supported the tumor-promoting role of POTEF in hepatocellular carcinoma (Fig. 3).

Transcriptomic profiling revealed that POTEF-associated differentially expressed genes were enriched in pathways related to extracellular matrix remodeling and carcinogenesis-associated signaling (Fig. 5). Because extracellular matrix remodeling is closely associated with epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)26,27, EMT-related proteins were further evaluated experimentally. POTEF overexpression increased the expression of N-cadherin and Snail while reducing E-cadherin levels, whereas siRNA-mediated POTEF silencing exhibited the opposite tendency. These findings suggest that POTEF-associated EMT regulation may contribute to enhanced migratory and invasive phenotypes in hepatocellular carcinoma. EMT is widely recognized as a critical process underlying hepatocellular carcinoma invasion and metastatic progression28,29, indicating that POTEF-associated EMT regulation may contribute to the aggressive biological behavior observed in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Furthermore, several genes inversely associated with POTEF expression, including CNTN1, PHACTR3, MYO3A, NOTUM, IGF2, and RIMBP2, were significantly associated with favorable prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (Fig. 6). These observations imply that POTEF-associated transcriptional alterations may involve multiple tumor-related regulatory networks. Although the precise biological interactions between POTEF and these negatively correlated genes remain unclear, several of these candidates have previously been implicated in epithelial differentiation, tumor suppression, and cellular regulatory processes30–33. Therefore, dysregulation of POTEF-associated gene networks may contribute to hepatocellular carcinoma progression through coordinated modulation of multiple biological pathways.

Another important finding of the present study is the close association between POTEF expression and macrophage infiltration in hepatocellular carcinoma. TIMER2 analysis combined with immunohistochemical validation demonstrated that POTEF expression was positively correlated with CD68-positive and CD163-positive macrophage infiltration, particularly CD163-positive macrophage enrichment (Fig. 7). Tumor-associated macrophages, especially M2-like macrophages, are widely recognized as important regulators of malignant progression34, immune suppression, angiogenesis35, and metastasis in hepatocellular carcinoma36. The observed association between POTEF expression and CD163-positive macrophage infiltration suggests a potential link between POTEF and macrophage-associated immune regulation in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, although transcriptomic and functional analyses suggested the involvement of EMT-associated regulation, the direct molecular mechanisms underlying POTEF-mediated signaling remain incompletely understood. Second, the current study primarily focused on macrophage-associated immune infiltration, whereas the broader immunological functions of POTEF within the hepatocellular carcinoma microenvironment require further investigation. Third, although multiple public datasets and clinical samples were included, larger independent cohorts, additional mechanistic studies, and validation in multiple hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines are still necessary to further confirm the biological and clinical significance of POTEF in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrated that POTEF is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma and is closely associated with unfavorable prognosis and aggressive clinicopathological characteristics. Functional experiments revealed that POTEF promotes hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation and migration while suppressing apoptosis both in vitro and in vivo. Transcriptomic and experimental analyses further suggested that POTEF may contribute to hepatocellular carcinoma progression partly through EMT-associated regulation. In addition, POTEF expression was positively associated with macrophage infiltration, particularly CD163-positive macrophage enrichment, indicating a potential role in tumor immune microenvironment regulation. Taken together, these findings suggest that POTEF may serve as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma and provide additional insight into the molecular and immunological characteristics of hepatocellular carcinoma. Further mechanistic and clinical studies are warranted to clarify its biological functions and therapeutic potential.

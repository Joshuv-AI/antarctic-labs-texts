Research progress on the mechanisms of EB virus reshaping the immune microenvironment in nasopharyngeal carcinoma
Authors: Liu Yudie, Xu Weihua, Chen Xinping
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is the most common EBV (Epstein-Barr virus) related epithelial malignant tumor. Over 90% of patients diagnosed with undifferentiated nasopharyngeal carcinoma are infected with EB virus. EBV infection of nasopharyngeal carcinoma leads to the remodeling of the immune microenvironment. There are now numerous research mechanisms regarding the remodeling of the immune microenvironment by EBV. This article systematically integrates single-cell transcriptomics, spatial metabolomics, and clinical research evidence, and from four dimensions: effector cell exhaustion, inhibitory cell expansion, metabolic reprogramming, and spatial organization of tertiary lymphoid structures, it analyzes the multi-level mechanisms by which EBV remodels the immune microenvironment. The core findings indicate that EBV constructs a multi-target and multi-level coordinated regulatory network through its encoded proteins (LMP1, LMP2A, EBNA1, and BRRF2) and non-coding RNAs (miR-BARTs and circRNAs). This regulatory mechanism dynamically evolves at different stages of the disease, with synergistic effects during the latent and lytic phases, forming an immunosuppressive microenvironment. However, the limitations in mechanism understanding have led to difficulties in clinical translation and drug use. The objective response rate of PD-1 inhibitors is only 20–30%. Single-target blockade is difficult to improve treatment efficacy. Therefore, based on previous research data, we propose that in the future, we need to shift from single-target blockade to the overall ecosystem regulation of the immune microenvironment, combined with targeted inhibition of immune checkpoints, chemokine axes, metabolic nodes, and viral own products, and based on EBV DNA load and TLS status for precise stratification. This is expected to break through the current treatment limitations and promote EBV-positive nasopharyngeal carcinoma from empirical treatment to mechanism-driven precise immunotherapy intervention.Keywords: NPC, EBV, Immune microenvironment, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma is the most common EB virus-related epithelial malignant tumor [1]. In terms of histopathology, NPC can be classified into keratinized type and non-keratinized type, and the latter can be further divided into differentiated type and undifferentiated type. Among them, the undifferentiated type of NPC has the closest relationship with EBV infection, and over 95% of cases can detect EBV infection [2, 3]. The association between NPC and EBV makes EBV an important clue and therapeutic target for understanding the pathogenesis of NPC.

EBV belongs to the γ-herpesvirus family. In NPC, EBV is in type II latent infection. Its common expressions include viral products such as nuclear antigen (EBNA1), latent membrane proteins (LMP1, LMP2), small polyadenylic acid RNA (EBERs), and BART miRNAs [4]. Recent studies have revealed that some cleavage zone proteins such as Z transactivator (ZTA) and BRRF2 are also expressed in NPC and participate in immune regulation [5, 6]. The detection of EBV DNA in tissues or plasma by qPCR serves as an objective indicator of the presence of the virus, but EBER in situ hybridization is the gold standard for detecting EBV infection, which can locate EBV-positive cells on tissue sections. EBV DNA seronegative is defined as the absence of detectable EBV DNA in peripheral blood, and EBV DNA seropositive is defined as detectable EBV DNA in plasma, with a titer greater than 1000 copies/mL.

Multiple studies have confirmed that the expression of LMP1 in NPC tissues shows significant heterogeneity, with a wide fluctuation range of positive rates (72.5% − 98%), and it shows a positive correlation trend with the clinical stage of NPC. The proportion of high LMP1 expression is higher in advanced patients, suggesting its involvement in tumor progression [7]. Even if EBV proteins are expressed at a low level, they can play an important role by activating signaling pathways and regulating downstream genes. For example, LMP1 regulates the MAPK/ERK and p38 signaling pathways, inhibits the transcription of matrix metalloproteinase tissue inhibitor 3 (TIMP-3), and leads to an increase in the invasive ability of tumor cells [8–10]. EBV not only regulates directly through proteins, but also indirectly through virus-encoded miRNAs. For instance, BART miRNAs promote tumor cell metastasis, and the EBV-encoded BART miRNAs can target tumor suppressor gene miR-34c, and EBV can downregulate miR-34c to activate the Erk1/2 pathway [11].

These viral products not only promote the survival and proliferation of tumor cells, but also reshape the immune microenvironment of NPC. Previous studies have shown the unique immune microenvironment of EBV-positive NPC, the enrichment of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and M2-type tumor-associated macrophages (TAM), the exhaustion of CD8 + T cells, the emergence of regulatory B cells (Bregs), as well as the complex ecosystem of chemokines, immune checkpoints, and metabolic pathways. The mechanism by which EBV reshapes the tumor microenvironment of NPC is a key to understanding the viral carcinogenic mechanism and can also serve as a theoretical basis for developing new immunotherapy strategies [12–14]. Additionally, we have also focused on tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), which are lymphoid aggregations formed ectopically in tumor tissues and have been proven to be closely related to the good prognosis of NPC patients and the response to immunotherapy [15]. At the same time, due to the difficulty in sample acquisition, the mechanism of EBV-negative NPC remains unclear, but understanding the differences between EBV-positive and negative NPC is crucial for accurately grasping the specific role of EBV [3, 21].

However, the prognosis for patients with recurrent/metastatic disease remains unsatisfactory [16], and the objective response rate still hovers around 20–30% [17], because patients with a higher baseline EBV DNA titer benefit less from anti-PD-1 treatment [18], while the TLS status (CTR score) can effectively predict treatment response [15]. Therefore, although the combined treatment based on radiotherapy and chemotherapy is the standard protocol for NPC, immune checkpoint inhibitors bring new hope, suggesting that combining immunotherapy might be a new clinical treatment approach, and suggesting that precise stratification based on EBV status and TME characteristics may be beneficial for immunotherapy. Thus, systematically analyzing the multi-level mechanisms of EBV remodeling of the TME and identifying multiple targets that can be intervened may become an urgent need to break through the current treatment bottleneck. The purpose of our review is to systematically summarize the research progress on the remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment in EBV-positive NPC, focusing on primary untreated EBV-positive NPC. We hope to provide theoretical references for exploring precise immunotherapy for EBV-related tumors.

Exhaustion and functional suppression of immune cells

Exhaustion and functional suppression of CD8 + T cells and NK cells

A single-cell RNA sequencing dataset from 19 untreated EBV-positive primary tumors of NPC and 7 non-malignant nasal cavity biopsy tissues systematically elucidated the composition of immune cells in EBV-positive NPC. They found that CD8 + tumor-infiltrating T cells continuously exhibited exhaustion signals during tumor infiltration, with the expression levels of their co-inhibitory receptors increasing, including PD-L1/PD-L2, TIM-3, TIGIT, LAG3, and CTLA4. Moreover, in vitro functional experiments confirmed that tumor cells were constantly inducing functional impairment in CD8 + T cells, manifested as a decrease in IFN-γ production by CD8 + T cells, accompanied by the expansion of Tregs [12]. This suggests that the emergence of T-cell immunosuppression may not be due to a lack of T cells, but rather in previous studies, it has been found that there are a large number of T cell infiltrations in EBV-positive NPC. Another study in 2025, through in situ hybridization and immunohistochemical analysis of 9 primary EBV-positive NPC tissues, confirmed that in both the tumor parenchyma and stroma of all cases, there was a large infiltration of CD8 + T cells, indicating the richness of immune cells in the immune microenvironment of NPC. Although these T cells are numerous, they are unable to effectively clear tumor cells [19]. Similar to CD8 + T cells, natural killer cells, as the core lymphocytes of the innate immune system, also showed a dysfunctional state in EBV-positive NPC. However, single-cell data showed that the infiltration of NK cells in EBV-positive NPC decreased, and the remaining NK cells highly expressed inhibitory receptors such as TIGIT and NKG2A, while the expression of cytotoxic molecules such as GZMB and PRF1 was downregulated [20] (Fig. 1).

The expression levels of co-inhibitory receptors on CD8 + tumor-infiltrating T cells are increasing, including PD-L1/PD-L2, TIM-3, TIGIT, LAG3, and CTLA4. The production of interferon-γ (IFN-γ) is decreasing, while Tregs are expanding. The infiltration of NK cells in EBV-positive NPC decreases, but inhibitory receptors such as TIGIT and NKG2A are highly expressed, and the expression of cytotoxic molecules such as GZMB and PRF1 is downregulated. Previous studies have identified the ZNF683 + resident NK cell exhaustion subpopulation in EBV-infected NPC. The level of PD-L1 in EBV-infected NPC cells is significantly increased, which further downregulates the function of CD8 + T cells and induces them to enter a lysosomal state. LMP1, through its intracellular special domain, further recruits ALIX protein, using the ALIX-dependent pathway, enhances the ability of PD-L1 to load into sEV, and the combination of these three forms a complex, promoting the transmembrane release of sEV to distant sites, long-distance regulation and multi-site inhibition of CD8 + T cell activity and IFN-γ expression. Additionally, LMP1 induces the expression of immune checkpoint molecule B7-H3 (CD276) through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, promoting the reduction of NK cell numbers. EBV-encoded miRNA-BART11 and miRNA-BART17-3p inhibit transcriptional repressor FOXP1 and PBRM1 expression by suppressing transcription, relieving the transcriptional inhibition of PD-L1. EBV-encoded circular RNA circRNA-BART2.2 further enhances the transcriptional activity of PD-L1 by promoting the expression of TFs such as NF-κB and IRF3. The upregulation of miR-155 is closely related to the loss of these two key immune molecules, ICOSL and MHC-I. EBV promotes the release of immunosuppressive factors and the upregulation of immune checkpoint B7-H3 (CD276) and induces the upregulation of tissue factor F3 through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, thereby promoting platelet aggregation and the release of TGF-β, and inhibiting the anti-tumor function of NK cells

Therefore, the research shows that T cells and NK cells exhibit different states in EBV-positive NPC. T cells are abundant but functionally exhausted. They retain cell numbers by inducing functional exhaustion. However, NK cells show both a decrease in quantity and functional inhibition simultaneously [12, 20]. This difference suggests that the regulatory mechanisms of EBV on adaptive immunity and innate immunity may have essential differences. The mechanism underlying this difference is still unclear and deserves further investigation.This may be related to the differences in the expression profiles of inhibitory receptors on the surface of T cells and NK cells, as well as the differences in sensitivity to inhibitory cytokines such as TGF-β, or the differences in the direct effects of EBV-encoded proteins such as LMP1 and LMP2A on these two types of cells [26, 29] (Fig. 1).

This difference may be related to the EBV DNA load. A single-cell study of 10 EBV-positive NPC tissues showed that, based on the pre-treatment peripheral blood plasma EBV DNA level for stratified analysis, in their study cohort, the proportions of T/NK cells and B cells in EBV-positive NPC were higher, while the proportions of epithelial cells and myeloid cells were more prominent in EBV-negative NPC. This finding suggests that even in patients with EBV-positive tissues, the level of viral load in peripheral blood corresponds to different tumor microenvironment characteristics, and it reveals that there are differences in the selection preferences of immune escape genes between EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC. In EBV-positive NPC, tumor cells preferentially express PDCD1, CTLA4, LAG3, and TIM-3, while in EBV-negative NPC, they selectively express TIGHT, ENTPD1, and TOX. This finding has important clinical significance. It explains why the same immune checkpoint inhibitors may have significant differences in efficacy among different patient groups. EBV-positive patients may benefit more from a combined treatment targeting PD-1/CTLA-4/LAG3/TIM-3, while EBV-negative patients may need to prioritize new-generation inhibitors targeting TIGHT or CD39 (encoded by ENTPD1) [21] (Fig. 1).

Therefore, different EBV DNA loads may correspond to different T-cell exhaustion states. Previous studies have confirmed that EBV DNA load affects checkpoint expression profiles, and checkpoint expression profiles are related to the exhaustion stage. Pre-emptive exhaustion (TCF-1 + PD-1+) and terminal exhaustion (TCF-1-PD-1hi) both highly express PD-1, but their functional states and responses to immunotherapy are different [22, 23]. Based on this, we propose the following testable hypothesis: EBV DNA-high-load patients’ T-cells may be more in the pre-emptive exhaustion state and have a better response to PD-1 blockade; while low-load patients may be more in the terminal exhaustion state and require combined other strategies. This hypothesis needs to be verified through single-cell sequencing combined with TCF-1/TIM-3 co-staining. The verification of this hypothesis will help achieve precise immunotherapy for EBV-positive NPC. However, to achieve this goal, we need to go beyond the limitations of overly focusing on the mechanism research of CD8 + T-cell exhaustion, and the roles of other effector cells such as NK cells, γδT cells, and NKT cells have been relatively neglected. However, as EBV is a virus that can simultaneously infect epithelial cells and B cells [1], its remodeling of the innate immune system may also be crucial. The depletion subpopulation of ZNF683 + tissue-resident NK cells discovered in previous studies [20], and the inhibition of NK cells revealed by single-cell data [29], suggest that NK cells are constantly damaged in the immune escape process of EBV-positive NPC. Future research should not only focus on the study of T-cells, but also systematically evaluate the relative role contributions of different effector cells in the immune surveillance of EBV-positive NPC. However, the mechanisms discovered independently are not yet clear about their interactions, hierarchical regulatory relationships, and relative contributions, and most of these mechanisms have been verified in vitro cell lines or small sample tissues collected from clinical cases, lacking confirmation in large clinical cohorts. Importantly, the physiological relevance of these mechanisms in the complex microenvironment in vivo still needs to be confirmed (Fig. 1).

The regulatory mechanism of EBV on the dysfunction of CD8 + T cells and NK cells

Regulation of immune checkpoints

EBV does not merely lie dormant passively; instead, it actively induces immune cell dysfunction and reshapes the microenvironment through various means. The infection of EBV itself can directly induce the upregulation of PD-L1 expression in tumor cells. Studies have found that the PD-L1 level is significantly elevated in NPC cells infected by EBV, which further reduces the function of effector T cells and induces the immune cells to enter a lysosomal state [24]. This constitutes the basis of EBV’s regulation of immune checkpoints. PD-L1 is not only expressed on the surface of tumor cells but can also be packaged into small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) and achieve remote immunosuppression through transmembrane movement. In vitro experiments have verified this process, and it was proposed that the high-level expression of LMP1 in NPC tissues is associated with restricted infiltration and dysfunction of CD8 + T cells, and LMP1 further recruits ALIX protein through its intracellular special domain, using the ALIX-dependent pathway to enhance the ability of PD-L1 loading into sEVs. The three combine into a complex, promoting the transmembrane release of sEVs to distant locations, achieving long-distance regulation and multi-site inhibition of CD8 + T cell activity and IFN-γ expression [25]. This means that even immune cells far from the primary lesion may be attacked by these inhibitory vesicles carrying PD-L1. LMP1 induces the upregulation of the immune checkpoint molecule B7-H3 (CD276) through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. In vitro functional experiments have confirmed that after co-culture with EBV-positive NPC cells, the number of NK cells significantly decreases, suggesting that the LMP1-B7-H3 axis may be one of the key mechanisms by which EBV inhibits the function of NK cells [26] (Fig. 1).

Transcriptional regulation mediated by non-coding RNAs

Based on this, EBV also amplifies its regulation of checkpoint molecules by targeting the host’s transcription factors (Transcription factors, TFs). On one hand, the EBV-encoded miRNAs BART11 and BART17-3p inhibit transcriptional repressors FOXP1 and PBRM1, thereby relieving the transcriptional repression of PD-L1 [27]; but on the other hand, the circular RNA circRNA-BART2.2 encoded by EBV promotes the expression of TFs such as NF-κB and IRF3, further enhancing the transcriptional activity of PD-L1 [28]. These findings reveal that EBV non-coding RNAs connect the regulatory axis of immune checkpoints through transcription factors. Additionally, in a study of primary untreated NPC tissues, the upregulation of miR-155 was found to be closely related to the loss of these two key immune molecules, ICOSL and MHC-I. In EBV-related tumors, EBV induces miR-155 simultaneously to inhibit the activated T cell function of ICOSL and the antigen recognition function of MHC-I, which explains why in EBV-positive NPC, although a large number of CD8 + T cells are produced, they are unable to effectively recognize and eliminate tumor cells and are in a significant functional disorder state [19]. However, whether the actual expression levels of miRNA-BARTs in tumor tissues are sufficient to produce significant regulatory effects, and whether there are synergistic or antagonistic effects between different EBV-encoded non-coding RNAs, these questions currently have no answers (Fig. 1).

Signal pathway

In addition to the aforementioned regulation through immune checkpoints and non-coding RNAs, EBV also induces immune cell dysfunction through host signaling pathways. The most crucial one is the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. EBV promotes the release of immunosuppressive factors and the upregulation of the immune checkpoint B7-H3 (CD276) [26], through this pathway, and inhibits the anti-tumor function of NK cells. EBV activates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway through LMP2A, mediates the upregulation of tissue factor F3, and thereby promotes platelet aggregation and the release of TGF-β [29]. However, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is involved in the functional regulation of various immune cells such as T cells, NK cells, and macrophages, and is a core node of immune metabolism. Whether EBV simultaneously regulates multiple immune cells through this pathway and whether there are differences in the sensitivity of different cell types to the blockade of this pathway have not been systematically studied (Fig. 1).

Recruitment and expansion of immunosuppressive cells

The above system elaborates on the exhaustion and functional inhibition of effector cells such as CD8 + T cells and NK cells in EBV-positive NPC. However, the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment not only results from the dysfunction of effector cells, but also depends on the recruitment and expansion of inhibitory immune cells. This chapter will focus on discussing the enrichment mechanisms and functions of regulatory T cells and M2-type macrophages, which are two core inhibitory cells, in EBV-positive NPC.

Recruitment and expansion of regulatory T cells

Regulatory T cells (Tregs) are crucial for maintaining immune homeostasis. However, their excessive enrichment in the immune microenvironment becomes a core driving factor for immunosuppression. Chemokines recruit more immunosuppressive cells to attack immune effector cells. In EBV-positive primary untreated NPC tissues, a large number of chemokines are involved in recruiting Tregs. Tumor-infiltrating macrophages (TAMs) will polarize into M2-TAMs, and simultaneously upregulate the expression of CXCL10 and CCL20. These ligands bind to the CXCR3 and CCR6 receptors on the surface of Tregs, forming a form of TAM recruitment of Tregs. Tregs maintain a positive feedback loop for the inhibitory phenotype of TAMs [30] (Fig. 2).

In the early stage of tumors, CXCL10 + macrophages recruit CXCR3 + effector T cells; however, as the disease progresses, the proportion of CXCR3 + Tregs gradually increases, while the number of effector T cells decreases. Tumor-infiltrating macrophages (TAM) will polarize into M2-TAM, and simultaneously upregulate the expression of CCL20, which binds to the CCR6 receptor on the surface of Tregs. Nuclear factor κB2 (NF-κB2) is overexpressed in EBV-positive NPC. It activates the infiltration of Tregs through the interaction signal of tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 7 and its receptor (CD70-CD27). Regulatory B cells (Bregs) actively secrete pro-inflammatory substances such as TGF-β1 and IL10, and promote the expansion of Tregs by increasing TNF-TNFR2 of tumor necrosis factor and interleukin 16-CD4 receptor (IL16-CD4). LMP1 can upregulate the expression of chemokines CCL5 and CXCL10 through activating the NF-κB pathway, promoting the recruitment of T cells and myeloid cells; LMP1 may also promote the production of CCL17 and CCL22 in the tumor microenvironment by recruiting dendritic cells, attracting CCR4 + Tregs to migrate towards the tumor tissue. EBNA1 upregulates the expression of TGF-β1, inducing the differentiation of initial CD4 + T cells towards Tregs, and by activating the TGF-β1-SMAD3/c-JUN signaling axis, downregulating the expression of miR-200a, thereby inducing the secretion of chemokine ligand CXCL12. CXCL12 binds to its receptor CXCR4, not only directly attracting CCR4 + Tregs to migrate towards the tumor, but also promoting the secretion of chemokine ligand CXCL12

However, chemokines exhibit dynamic temporal changes in their ability to recruit immune cells, and they do not initially participate in the immunosuppressive effect. In the early stages of tumors, CXCL10 + macrophages mainly recruit CXCR3 + effector T cells, exerting an anti-tumor effect; but as the disease progresses, the proportion of CXCR3 + Tregs gradually increases, while the number of effector T cells gradually decreases. As a result, the immune microenvironment undergoes a transformation from anti-tumor to pro-tumor [30]. This dynamic change reminds us that perhaps the chemokines themselves are more influenced by the composition of immune cells, and their effects depend on which cell is receiving the signal. Therefore, these theories provide potential approaches for clinical intervention. In previous in vitro co-culture experiments, inhibitors of CXCL10 were used, suggesting that reducing Tregs might also reduce the recruitment of effector T cells, and it is impossible to control whether the positive anti-tumor effect can outweigh the pro-tumor effect. Simply supplementing CXCL10 may also have limited effects due to insufficient concentration or short duration of action [35]. Future research needs to find a delicate balance between enhancing the recruitment of effector cells and blocking the infiltration of inhibitory cells, and deeply explore what mechanisms cause the downregulation of CXCR3 expression on the surface of effector T cells or the upregulation of CXCR3 expression on the surface of Tregs, the mechanism of cell source conversion, and what signaling pathways control the expression of chemokine receptor on the surface of different T cell subsets (Fig. 2).

The complexity of the chemokine network presents both opportunities and challenges for clinical intervention. In primary untreated hepatocellular carcinoma, inhibiting galectin-1 (Galectin-1) can block the recruitment of Tregs by the CCL20-CCR6 axis through TAM, and enhance the efficacy of anti-PD-1 therapy [31]. This strategy is also worthy of exploration in NPC. However, chemokine-targeted therapy requires caution (Fig. 2).

Tumor cell-mediated direct induction of Tregs expansion

In addition to chemokine-mediated recruitment, tumor cells can also induce Tregs expansion through direct contact. Gong L et al. conducted a multicenter single-cell cohort study on 36 primary untreated NPC tissue samples, observing over 350,000 cells. They found that nuclear factor κB2 (NF-κB2) was overexpressed in EBV-positive NPC. Through the interaction signal of tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 7 and its receptor (CD70-CD27), Tregs infiltration was activated, accelerating the immune escape of tumor cells [13]. This discovery links the viral-related intracellular signaling pathways with immunosuppression. Moreover, single-cell RNA sequencing of 10 pairs of primary untreated paired blood and tumor tissue samples revealed the imbalance of Tregs and CD8 + T cells in NPC tumor tissues. The proportion of Tregs increased in the tumor immune microenvironment, and was associated with higher levels of immune exhaustion expressed by CD8 + T cells [14](Fig. 2).

Bregs promote the enhancement of Tregs’ functions

Previous research data show that under hypoxic conditions, regulatory B cells (Bregs) actively secrete pro-inflammatory substances such as TGF-β1 and IL10. Further mechanism analysis reveals that Bregs promote the expansion of Tregs and enhance their inhibitory effect by increasing tumor necrosis factor - tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 1B (TNF-TNFR2) and interleukin 16-CD4 receptor (IL16-CD4) [14]. Therefore, by synergizing the effects of Bregs and Tregs, the immunosuppressive effect in the tumor microenvironment is further amplified, which is associated with poor prognosis. However, there are significant gaps in the research on Bregs-Tregs. How hypoxia induces the Bregs phenotype, EBV can infect B cells and drive their transformation, but whether EBV directly induces the differentiation of regulatory B cells, whether there are EBV-encoded miRNAs or proteins involved in the regulation of Bregs functions, these questions remain unanswered (Fig. 2).

Regulation of cytokines related to Tregs by EBV

Research data show that when using the nasopharyngeal carcinoma cell line for in vitro cultivation, it was found that LMP1 can upregulate the expression of chemokines CCL5 and CXCL10 by activating the NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting the recruitment of T cells and myeloid cells. LMP1 may also indirectly promote the production of CCL17 and CCL22 in the tumor microenvironment by recruiting infiltrating cells such as dendritic cells, thereby attracting Tregs expressing CCR4 to migrate towards the tumor tissue [32]. This recruitment mechanism reveals the distribution of T cells and macrophages in the immune microenvironment reshaped by LMP1 (Fig. 2).

EBV nuclear antigen 1 (EBNA1) creates conditions for the generation of immunosuppressive cells. Wang et al.‘s study of primary untreated NPC tissues revealed that the expression level of EBNA1 was positively correlated with the number of Foxp 3 + regulatory T cells (Tregs) in tumor tissues. In-depth mechanism research found that EBNA1 upregulated the expression of TGF-β1 and induced the differentiation of initial CD4 + T cells towards Tregs [27]. EBNA1 recruits Tregs through molecular pathways. Multiple studies have found that overexpressed EBNA1 in primary untreated NPC cells infected by EBV, by activating the TGF-β1-SMAD3/c-JUN signaling axis, downregulated the expression of miR-200a, and thereby induced the secretion of the chemokine ligand CXCL12. CXCL12 binds to its receptor CXCR4, not only directly attracting CCR4 + Tregs to migrate towards the tumor, but also through the c-Jun/STT3A pathway mediated by TGF-β1 of PD-L1 glycosylation, further enhancing the immune escape ability of the tumor [33, 34]. In addition, EBNA1 enhances the production of the chemokine CCL20, recruiting Tregs to migrate towards the tumor tissue [27]. The above-mentioned EBNA1 stimulates the chemotactic migration of Tregs through the TGF-β1-CXCL12-CXCR4 axis. This content in primary untreated EBV-positive NPC links EBV’s viral proteins, host miRNAs, chemokines, and immune checkpoint molecules into a complete regulatory network, suggesting that CXCR4 antagonists may have therapeutic value (Fig. 2).

Polarization and function of M2-type macrophages

EBV infection not only reshaped the composition and abundance of T cells in the immune microenvironment of primary untreated NPC tissues, but also restructured macrophages. In EBV-positive NPC, TAM exhibited significant M2-type polarization characteristics, becoming another core node of the immunosuppressive network.

The tumor cells of EBV-positive NPC secrete large amounts of the chemokine CCL5, which recruits a large number of peripheral blood mononuclear macrophages into the tumor [35].The recruited mononuclear macrophages enter the immune microenvironment and, under the influence of local factors, polarize into TAM. However, these myeloid cells cannot fight against tumors in the tumor immune microenvironment; instead, they induce polarization into tumor-promoting M2-TAM. Investigating the reasons for the polarization of macrophages, Chen et al. in their study on the mouse xenograft model of EBV-positive epithelial-related malignant tumors revealed that it is related to the synergistic effect of interleukin 10 (IL-10) and colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1), driving macrophages to polarize into tumor-promoting M2-TAM [35].

Studies have shown that in primary untreated EBV-positive NPC cells and in vitro animal models, the EBV-encoded cleavage gene ZTA significantly enhances the chemotactic ability of tumor cells by upregulating the expression of chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), and interleukin-8 (IL-8). This enhancement of the chemotactic signal directly leads to the attraction of more monocytes into the tumor microenvironment and their polarization into M2-type tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) under the influence of the local microenvironment [6]. This mechanism supplements our understanding of the reasons for the enrichment of M2-TAM in EBV-positive NPC, not only coming from the direct regulation of EBV on macrophage differentiation direction, but also from its strengthening of the recruitment signal of monocytes. Therefore, the regulation of the chemokine network by EBV is not limited to the latency period. Studies have shown that even during the abortive cleavage cycle of EBV, the virus still has an impact. However, there are new issues. Does the regulation of latency and cleavage periods exist in a synergistic or competitive relationship, and which mechanism dominates in different disease stages (Fig. 3).

The tumor cells of EBV-positive NPC secrete large amounts of chemokine CCL5, which recruits mononuclear macrophages. These cells then secrete interleukin 10 (IL-10) and colony stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) to induce the polarization of tumor-infiltrating macrophages into tumor-promoting M2-TAMs, thereby inhibiting CD8 + T cells. Additionally, the EBV-encoded cleavage gene ZTA can attract more monocytes into the tumor microenvironment by upregulating the expression of chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), and interleukin-8 (IL-8). EBV also remodels and polarizes tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) through its encoded proteins and non-coding RNAs. Regarding the regulation of EBV on M2-type TAMs, the EBV cleavage protein BRRF2 is packaged into extracellular vesicles (EVs), and BRRF2 activates the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-interferon gene stimulator signaling pathway (cGAS-STING), interfering with the interaction between cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) and dsDNA, and regulating the expression of P53-dependent apoptotic regulator (TP73) in in vitro co-culture experiments with EBV-infected NPC cells. It was observed that the proportion of M1-type TAMs decreased, while the proportion of M2-type TAMs significantly increased. EBV upregulates the expression of the secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1) gene, activating the cell adhesion molecule 44/Janus kinase 2/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (CD44/JAK2/STAT3) signaling axis, greatly promoting the development of M2 + TAMs, participating in angiogenesis and promoting tumor cells

Subsequently, Li W et al. in 2023’s single-cell study also confirmed that in EBV-positive primary untreated NPC, macrophages not only show an overall activated state but also the M2 phenotype dominates absolutely [21]. However, these mechanism studies mostly adopt in vitro co-culture or cell line overexpression models, lacking validation through in vivo lineage tracing and cell-specific knockout. The regulation of EBV on macrophages is either directly through infection of macrophages or indirectly through factors secreted by tumor cells. This issue remains unclear. Moreover, the binary classification of M1/M2 oversimplifies the true heterogeneity of macrophages. Single-cell sequencing has revealed multiple subgroups of tumor-associated macrophages with different functional characteristics and differentiation trajectories. Using the M1/M2 framework alone may mask the key subgroup heterogeneity and affect the accuracy of targeted strategies (Fig. 3).

Previous mechanism studies have demonstrated the functions of M2-TAM. It promotes tumor invasion to other sites by highly expressing matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9) to degrade the extracellular matrix. It also expresses chemokine (CCL13) to directly inhibit the killing function of CD8 + T cells, shaping an immunosuppressive microenvironment and weakening the inflammatory response. Moreover, compared with tumors without EBV infection, infected M2-TAM has more angiogenic properties [35]. Therefore, M2-TAM has multiple functions such as anti-inflammatory, promoting tumor metastasis and promoting angiogenesis. Its abundance is closely related to the disease progression risk and poor prognosis of NPC patients (Fig. 3).

The regulatory mechanism of EBV on M2-type macrophages

EBV also remodels and polarizes tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) through its encoded proteins and non-coding RNAs. The EBV lysis-stage protein BRRF2 is packaged into extracellular vesicles (EVs), and the combination of these two can undergo transmembrane movement and directly target the macrophages, thereby causing immunological dysfunction of the macrophages and preventing the initiation of type I interferon response. As a result, the anti-tumor function is reduced. The specific in vitro inhibitory mechanism experiments have clarified the relevant mechanisms. BRRF2 stimulates the cGAS-STING signaling pathway through cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-interferon gene stimulator factor, interfering with the interaction between cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) and dsDNA. Moreover, it has also been found from the clinical patient cohort that the level of BRRF2 + EVs in the serum of NPC patients is elevated, which is significantly associated with poor immune treatment response and drug resistance. The sensitivity for predicting the efficacy of anti-PD-1 treatment is 71.7% and the specificity is 82.7% [37] .

In addition, EBV regulates the expression of host genes to induce M2-type macrophage polarization. In an in vitro co-culture experiment where P53-dependent apoptotic regulators (TP73) expression was downregulated in NPC cells infected with EBV, it was observed that the proportion of M1-type TAM decreased, while the proportion of M2-type TAM significantly increased. Overexpression of TP73 reversed this effect, increasing the proportion of M1-type TAM to 8.85% and reducing the proportion of M2-type TAM to 1.06%. Animal experiments confirmed that the tumor volume in the EBV-infected group (456.19 ± 51.04 mm³) was significantly larger than that in the EBV-negative group (177.89 ± 17.14 mm³). After overexpression of TP73, it was observed that tumor growth was inhibited. Therefore, EBV may indirectly induce M2-type tumor-associated macrophage polarization by suppressing the expression of this host gene through regulation of gene expression [36]. Studies have shown that EBV upregulates the expression of the secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1) gene, activating the cell adhesion molecule 44/Janus kinase 2/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (CD44/JAK2/STAT3) signaling axis, which greatly promotes the development of M2 + TAM and participates in angiogenesis and promoting tumor cell metastasis [37, 38].

Metabolic reprogramming in EBV-positive NPC

Metabolic advantages of tumor cells

In addition to the exhaustion of immune cells and the expansion of immunosuppressive cells, single-cell and spatial metabolomics studies have revealed that EBV-positive tumor cells exhibit a high dependence on glucose. This metabolic characteristic promotes tumor self-proliferation and also releases harmful metabolites to immune cells through competition, thereby shaping a tumor immune microenvironment that is conducive to tumor survival and inhibits the function of immune cells. Like most solid tumors, EBV-positive primary untreated NPC cells show a significant tendency towards aerobic glycolysis, also known as the Warburg effect theory. Even in the presence of sufficient oxygen, tumor cells do not preferentially utilize mitochondria but instead prefer to convert glucose into lactate [39]. This metabolic strategy may seem inefficient, but it provides tumor cells with rapid intermediate products for the synthesis of biological macromolecules and creates an acidic microenvironment. This greedy uptake of glucose directly leads to competition for nutrients with immune cells. This mechanism has been verified in solid tumors such as melanoma [40, 41], although there are no direct studies on nutritional competition in EBV-positive NPC, but considering that NPC also presents a high glycolytic feature, similar metabolic competition is likely to exist, and it is worth in-depth exploration. Although the metabolic characteristics of tumors show the Warburg effect, recent studies have revealed significant metabolic heterogeneity within tumors. Some tumor cell subpopulations may rely on oxidative phosphorylation and form metabolic symbiosis with glycolytic subpopulations, which may have an important impact on treatment strategies. Targeting glycolysis alone may only selectively enrich the resistant subpopulation that is dependent on oxidative phosphorylation. When T cells are in a glucose-deficient environment, they are forced to enhance oxidative phosphorylation to meet energy requirements, but this metabolic transition is not beneficial to immune cells. This leads to mitochondrial damage and increased reactive oxygen levels, ultimately causing T cell dysfunction and exhaustion [40, 41]. Therefore, providing a deep exploration idea for the T cell dysfunction of NPC, this metabolic and immune imbalance driven by chronic EBV infection and continuous antigen stimulation may be part of the reason for tumor cell immune evasion (Fig. 4)

Lactic acid accumulation and immune suppression

The final product of glycolysis, lactic acid, plays a crucial role in the tumor microenvironment. Studies have suggested that lactic acid is an important indicator of poor prognosis for patients, and its concentration increase can promote tumor cell metastasis, invasion, and immune evasion [42]. Lactic acid itself is also a signaling molecule that can damage the functions of TAMs and dendritic cells (DCs) [43].

However, the combination of single-cell data from multiple solid tumors, in conjunction with mouse models and in vitro co-culture experiments, has confirmed that lactate promotes the function of regulatory T cells. Tregs actively take up lactate through the high expression of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), promoting the nuclear translocation of activated T cell nuclear factor 1 (NFAT1), thereby enhancing PD-1 expression [44]. This indicates that lactate may be a metabolic waste for CD8 + T cells, but it can maintain and promote the function of Tregs (Fig.4). Therefore, the reason for the differential response of lactate to the presence of CD8 + T cells and Tregs deserves in-depth exploration. We speculate that this may be related to the differences in the expression levels of MCT1 on the cell surface and intracellular signals of the two types of cells. It also suggests that we need to be cautious when targeting lactate metabolism, as simply reducing lactate concentration may weaken the function of Tregs, but it may also affect the metabolic adaptability of CD8 + T cells. Currently, most mechanisms have been verified in vitro cell lines, but the lactate concentration in the tumor microenvironment in vivo shows spatial heterogeneity. Simple high-concentration lactate treatment is difficult to simulate the real situation. Animal models and humans have differences in metabolic pathways and immune cell subpopulations, for example, the metabolic preference of mouse Tregs is different from that of human Tregs. Single-cell metabolomics technology is not yet mature, making it difficult to track changes in EBV-specific metabolic fluxes at the single-cell resolution and track the real-time impact of viral-encoded genes on metabolic fluxes. These limitations suggest that we need to be cautious when interpreting existing evidence and promoting clinical translation. Although these findings mainly come from solid tumors such as melanoma and lung cancer, the enrichment of Tregs in EBV-positive NPC and the presence of highly glycolytic characteristics at the same time suggest that the lactate-Treg axis may also play a role in NPC. Future studies need to conduct spatial metabolomics verification in NPC tissues to reveal the impact of the metabolic microenvironment on the localization and function of immune cells.

Hypoxia adaptation and angiogenesis

In addition to lactate production, studies have shown that hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) enhances glucose uptake and glycolysis by promoting the surface expression of glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1); at the same time, HIF can also increase the production of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), promoting tumor angiogenesis [35, 42]. The newly formed tumor vessels often have abnormal structures and abnormal permeability, which not only provides convenience for the hematogenous metastasis of tumor cells, but also further aggravates local hypoxia, forming a vicious cycle of hypoxia, glycolysis and angiogenesis. In addition, angiogenic mimicry (VM) has also been observed in EBV-positive primary untreated NPC, that is, the tumor cells themselves form vascular-like channels, not relying on endothelial cells. The formation of VM has been verified to be related to EBV in clinical tissue specimens and subcutaneous tumor models in mice（Fig.4).

Previous studies have shown that EBV-encoded miR-BART1-5p can promote tumor cells to acquire an endothelial-like phenotype by activating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and regulating the expression of Sprouty2 protein, thereby forming VM [27](Fig. 5). This mechanism reveals the ability of EBV non-coding RNA to directly regulate the plasticity of tumor cells. Additionally, the research by Xiang et al. revealed that EBV-positive tumor cells recruit and reprogram monocytes by secreting cytokines such as IL-6, IL-8, and GM-CSF, causing them to polarize into M2-type tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). These M2c-TAMs further secrete factors such as TGF-β, VEGF, and MMP9, which in turn promote the formation of VM [45]. This discovery directly links VM formation to the polarization of M2-type macrophages discussed earlier, and compared to endothelium-dependent vessels, VM channels lack an endothelial cell barrier, allowing tumor cells to directly interact with the bloodstream, making them more prone to hematogenous metastasis. Clinical studies have confirmed that VM formation is significantly associated with the risk of distant metastasis and poor prognosis in NPC patients. The formation of VM channels exacerbates hypoxia and accumulation of acidic metabolites within the tumor, and M2-TAMs not only promote VM formation but also inhibit the function of CD8 + T cells. The abnormal blood supply leads to uneven infiltration of immune cells. This discovery directly links VM formation to the M2-type macrophage polarization discussed earlier [45]. Therefore, mimetic vessels differ from endothelium-dependent vessels in terms of function, permeability, and the impact on immune cell infiltration. There is still a gap in comparative studies in EBV-positive NPC.

In terms of sugar metabolism, LMP1 activates the mTORC1/NF-κB signaling pathway to enhance the transcription of GLUT1. LMP1 can also induce the expression of key enzymes for glycolysis such as HIF-1α and hexokinase 2 (HK2). The EBV-encoded miRNA is also involved in this process. miR-BART1-5p promotes aerobic glycolysis and tumor cell proliferation by activating the AMPK/mTOR/HIF1 pathway. LMP1 promotes lactate production. LMP2A increases the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF through the PI3K signaling pathway, and the Annexin A3/PI3K/HIF-1α axis is also involved in EBV-induced tumor vascular malformation and angiogenesis. The EBV-encoded miR-BART1-5p can promote the acquisition of an endothelial-like phenotype by tumor cells through activating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and regulating the expression of Sprouty2 protein, thereby forming vascular pseudo-generation (VM)

In addition to inducing angiogenesis, the study of T-cell-specific HIF-1α knockout mouse models has revealed that HIF-1α-regulated glycolysis is a key driving factor for T cells to produce IFN-γ under hypoxic conditions. Specifically deleting HIF-1α from T cells significantly inhibits the induction of IFN-γ. HIF-1α-deficient T cells have a reduced ability to kill tumor cells in vitro, and tumor-bearing mice do not respond to immune checkpoint blockade therapy [46]. This suggests that although hypoxia in the immune microenvironment drives metabolic remodeling in tumor cells through HIF-1α, it is also necessary for the anti-tumor function of T cells. This indicates that when targeting HIF-1α for tumor treatment, we need to be cautious of the potential risk of simultaneously damaging T-cell function.

In vitro experiments have confirmed that LMP1 expression significantly increases glucose consumption and lactate production, upregulates HIF-1α and its target genes PDK1 and PKM2, while reducing mitochondrial oxygen consumption rate and membrane potential. This reveals the core mechanism of LMP1 in remodeling tumor metabolism by inhibiting oxidative phosphorylation and promoting aerobic glycolysis. Clinical specimen verification further supports the positive correlation between LMP1 and HIF-1α [39]. The Wobber effect of EBV-positive NPC is mainly driven by latent membrane protein 1.

The regulatory mechanism of EBV on metabolic pathways

Previous studies have observed that metabolic reprogramming and microenvironment remodeling do not occur spontaneously but are the result of the combined action of EBV-encoded proteins and non-coding RNAs. In terms of glucose metabolism, LMP1 activates the mTORC1/NF-κB signaling pathway, enhances the transcription of GLUT1, promotes glucose uptake and glycolysis, and this has been verified through in vitro cell line functional experiments [47]. Moreover, LMP1 can also induce the expression of key enzymes for glycolysis such as HIF-1α and hexokinase 2 (HK2) [48]. EBV-encoded miRNAs in EBV-positive primary untreated NPC also play a role in this process. miR-BART1-5p promotes aerobic glycolysis by activating the AMPK/mTOR/HIF1 pathway in animal models, and it has been confirmed to cause uncontrolled growth of nasopharyngeal carcinoma [49]. In EBV-positive primary untreated gastric cancer related to nasopharyngeal carcinoma, miR-BART6-5p promotes glycolysis and cell proliferation through the TGF-β/SMAD4 signaling pathway [50]. In terms of hypoxia and angiogenesis, LMP2A increases the expression of HIF-1α and VEGF through the PI3K signaling pathway, and the Annexin A3/PI3K/HIF-1α axis is also involved in EBV-induced tumor vascular malformation and angiogenesis [35] (Fig. 5).

What we consider more important is the bidirectional feedback between metabolic reprogramming and immune cell dysfunction. The Wadding effect of tumor cells leads to glucose deficiency, forcing T cells to switch to oxidative phosphorylation, causing mitochondrial damage and depletion; lactic acid inhibits the functions of CD8 + T cells and DCs, and simultaneously promotes the inhibitory function of Tregs through the MCT1-NFAT1 axis, which also promotes the aforementioned expansion of Tregs. Tregs further inhibit CD8 + T cells. Hypoxia induces angiogenic mimicry and recruits M2-type macrophages, which leads to a chain reaction that further exacerbates immunosuppression. The viral proteins and non-coding RNAs of EBV simultaneously drive metabolic reprogramming and the upregulation of immune checkpoints, forming a synergistic effect. This analysis reveals that the immunosuppressive microenvironment of EBV-positive NPC is the result of the combined effect and mutual reinforcement of abnormal cellular components and metabolic environment disorders. The glycolysis of tumor cells leads to the accumulation of lactic acid, which further enhances the function of Tregs and inhibits CD8 + T cells, resulting in a decrease in tumor clearance ability and continuous tumor proliferation. Eventually, the demand for glycolysis further intensifies, forming a metabolic and immune vicious cycle. This may be the core mechanism of immune therapy resistance in EBV-positive NPC (Fig. 5).

The role of tertiary lymphoid structures in EBV-related NPC

Composition and function of tertiary lymphoid structures

The first four chapters respectively expounded the immune microenvironment of EBV-positive NPC from the perspectives of effector cell exhaustion, inhibitory cell expansion, metabolic reprogramming, and the regulatory mechanism network of EBV. However, these cells and related cellular molecular events are not randomly distributed but are highly organized spatially. In the study of the immune microenvironment, one lymph-related structure that we cannot help but pay attention to is the tertiary lymphoid structure (tertiary lymphoid structures, TLS), which integrates the composition and functional state of cells in three-dimensional space.

TLS is an ectopic lymphoid tissue formed at sites of chronic inflammation (including tumors, autoimmune diseases, and infections), with a tissue structure similar to secondary lymphoid organs but lacking a capsule. Mature TLS typically consists of three core regions, including a T cell area dominated by CD4 + Tfh cells, a B cell area with a germinal center, and a network of follicular dendritic cells. The presence of TLS usually reflects an active adaptive immune response at the local site, and in most solid tumors, it is associated with a favorable prognosis [51]. NPC is no exception; studies have confirmed that the presence of TLS is significantly associated with improved survival in NPC patients [15, 52].

The construction of TLS is inseparable from high endothelial viens (HEVs). In NPC, there is a special type of interferon-responsive high endothelial viens (IFN-HEVs), and this substance has been proven to be closely related to the formation of TLS. These IFN-HEVs secrete CXCL9 and recruit CD4 + T cells expressing CXCR3 to gather in the TLS area, providing a cell source for the initial construction of TLS. The CTRscore scoring system based on the CXCL9-TLS axis can be used for the screening of PD-1 immunotherapy [15]. Therefore, TLS is a prognostic marker. Our future research needs to deeply explore the specific molecular mechanism of EBV regulating TLS, whether the continuous stimulation of viral antigens promotes or damages TLS. How immune cells affect the maturity of TLS and whether they synergistically exert anti-tumor effects, and previous studies have revealed that such as hypoxia can regulate the expression of chemokines through HIF-1α, thereby affecting lymphocyte recruitment. Therefore, lactate accumulation and hypoxic metabolic environment may also affect the formation and maintenance of TLS. In-depth research on these issues can provide more precise targets for immunotherapy strategies based on TLS. Moreover, IFN-HEVs will also promote the activation of the interferon signaling pathway. IFN-I and IFN-γ enhance the chemotactic effect of CXCL9, CXCL10, and CXCL11, promoting the recruitment of CXCR3 + CD8+ T cells, and exerting anti-tumor effects [30]; however, continuous IFN signaling and EBV infection induce PD-L1 expression, causing T cell exhaustion (Jin S, 2020). This coexistence of activation and exhaustion reveals the heterogeneity of the tumor immune microenvironment.

Mature TLS is a highly organized structure that consists of T cell regions dominated by CD4 + follicular helper T cells (Tfh), B cell regions, follicular dendritic cells, etc. Among them, Tfh cells are the main source of the chemokine CXCL13, and CXCL13 is a key chemokine for the formation of TLS. By recruiting CXCR5 + B cells and Tfh cells themselves, it drives the construction and maintenance of TLS and leads to better survival prognosis [53, 54].In addition, Gong et al. discovered through single-cell sequencing that a portion of exhausted CD8 + T cells highly express CXCL13, which is a key chemokine for TLS formation. These CXCL13 + exhausted T cells interact with B cells through the CXCL13-CXCR5 axis and may actively participate in the construction and maintenance of TLS [13]. This suggests that T cell exhaustion should not be regarded as a simple loss of function, but rather a redistribution of function, shifting from direct killing to assisting in the construction of the immune memory center. Different from our previous view of T cell exhaustion as a simple loss of function, this transformation challenges the simplistic notion that exhaustion is equivalent to a negative outcome, providing a new perspective for understanding the heterogeneity of exhausted T cells.

EBV infection and the formation of tertiary lymphoid structures

Studies have shown that latent EBV infection provides continuous viral antigens, such as the output of EBNA1, LMP1, and LMP2A, and this chronic antigen stimulation is a classic mechanism driving the formation of ectopic lymphoid tissues. In autoimmune diseases and chronic infections, continuous antigen exposure is a key driving force for the formation of TLS [52]. However, whether EBV infection directly affects the formation of TLS is currently in the preliminary stage, and we can only further speculate on the way of influence based on the existing data. Moreover, EBV has obvious characteristics of invading B cells. EBV may inhibit NK cell responses and apoptosis through EBNA1 and promote the survival of local B cells in TLS [54](Westhoff Smith D, 2021), which may affect the composition and function of the B cell area in TLS. We speculate that EBV may also reshape T cell and B cell differentiation through mechanisms such as LMP2A and BART-miRNAs while driving the formation of TLS, inhibiting the functional maturity of TLS. This might be the potential reason why TLS exists in EBV-positive NPC but the immunotherapy effect is still limited. Additionally, EBV regulates PD-L1 and metabolism through miRNAs, and whether these miRNAs also regulate the expression of TLS-related chemokines such as CXCL13 and CXCL9 has not been studied yet, but it is worth exploring. EBV may have a possible inhibitory effect on the maturity of TLS. The above speculations require further experimental verification.

Differences in the immune microenvironment between EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC

Differences in the composition of immune cells

Single-cell RNA data indicate that EBV-positive NPC is mainly characterized by T-cell infiltration, while EBV-negative NPC is dominated by B-cells [3].However, the sample size of this study was small, with only 2 cases of EBV-positive, and individual differences may be amplified(Fig. 6)༈Table 1༉༈Table 2༉. We need to further investigate the mechanism of B-cell dominance in EBV-negative NPC and whether B-cells play an anti-tumor or pro-tumor role. What is the functional state of B-cells in EBV-negative NPC and its relationship with prognosis? These issues remain to be clarified. Moreover, the cell composition may be affected by various factors such as tumor stage, anatomical location, and host genetic background, and is not fixed. It may also be influenced by factors such as regional distribution, age of onset, etc., which are not fully controlled in the current studies. These factors may independently affect the immune microenvironment. Although this article focuses on the remodeling mechanism of the immune microenvironment in EBV-positive NPC, understanding the differences between EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC is crucial for accurately grasping the specific role of EBV. Currently, EBV-negative is usually based on EBER in situ hybridization negativity, but there may be low-copy EBV infection or latent gene expression situations. More sensitive detection methods, such as EBV-DNA sequencing, may change the detection results. And due to the limited research content and analysis in the study of EBV-negative NPC, future research needs to verify this finding in a larger cohort and combine spatial transcriptomics to analyze the spatial distribution characteristics of T-cells and B-cells in tumor tissues.

Comparison between EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC cases

Differences in immune checkpoint molecule expression

Despite these differences, EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC also share certain common immune microenvironmental characteristics. Wan X et al. (2024) [55]previously proposed that regardless of the EBV status, the absence of TGFBR2 is an important driving event in the occurrence of NPC. The absence of TGFBR2 leads to a reduction in the number of effector cells and a weakened immune response, while promoting the secretion of fibroblast-related factors, jointly creating conditions for the immune escape of tumor cells. This finding suggests that the dysregulation of the TGF-β signaling pathway may be a common vulnerability of NPC, worthy of exploration as a treatment target that ignores the EBV status. At the gene expression level, both EBV-positive and EBV-negative tumors express a series of immune escape-related genes, including VEGFA, LAG3, PDCD1, LGALS3, GDF15, MCL1, CD163, and FOXP3 [56]. However, the expression levels of these genes in EBV-positive tumors are generally higher than those in EBV-negative tumors, suggesting that EBV infection may enhance the activity of these immune escape pathways, exacerbating the immunosuppressive microenvironment of the tumor. From the perspective of the IFN-γ-PD-L1 axis, IFN-γ functions in both EBV-negative and EBV-positive NPC, but EBV infection significantly enhances the induction effect of IFN-γ on PD-L1 expression, because EBV miRNAs such as miR-BART11/17-3p, by relieving enhancer inhibition, make the PD-L1 gene activated and more sensitive to IFN-γ signals [27]. This finding not only explains the phenomenon of high PD-L1 expression in EBV-positive tumors, but also suggests that immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 may have more significant efficacy in EBV-positive patients (Table 1)and Table 2.

Genetic background and copy number variation differences

We have understood the underlying reasons for the similarities and differences between EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC, and found that it is closely related to the genetic background. According to the analysis of copy number variations of 103 cases of nasopharyngeal carcinoma by Ooft et al. (2018) [57], it was pointed out that the chromosomal variations of EBV-positive and EBV-negative nasopharyngeal carcinomas have commonalities. Both subtypes have chromosomal copy number variations in classic regions such as 3p and 9p, suggesting that the occurrence of NPC occurs independently of the EBV status. However, EBV-negative NPC carries more copy number variations, and the involved driver gene spectra are significantly different from those of EBV-positive individuals. This suggests that the two subtypes may drive tumor occurrence through different molecular mechanisms. EBV-positive individuals rely more on viral genes (such as LMP1) for the regulation of host cells and viral reactivation-induced genomic instability, while EBV-negative individuals rely more on the accumulation of chromosomal copy number variations of the host genome [57]. This finding is related to the regulatory mechanism of EBV discussed earlier, suggesting that EBV directly regulates host cell functions through its encoded proteins and non-coding RNAs, possibly replacing the need for host genome variations to some extent(Table 1)and Table 2.

Treatment strategies for locally advanced and recurrent/metastatic EBV-positive NPC

Establishment and optimization of standard treatment for locally advanced NPC

A phase III clinical trial conducted has confirmed that compared with concurrent radiotherapy and chemotherapy (IMRT) alone, adding gemcitabine combined with cisplatin (GP) induction chemotherapy before concurrent radiotherapy and chemotherapy (CCRT) can significantly improve overall survival, without significantly increasing the risk of late toxicity [58]. The research data further confirm that post-induction chemotherapy alone radiotherapy can be used as an alternative to concurrent radiotherapy and chemotherapy, while retaining efficacy and reducing toxicity [59]. These studies jointly established the position of gemcitabine and cisplatin induction chemotherapy followed by radiotherapy as the standard treatment for locally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

In EBV-positive NPC, EBV DNA has begun to serve as a biomarker to guide individualized treatment decisions. In EBV-positive NPC, previous studies on 5-year follow-up analyses of low-risk locally advanced NPC (plasma EBV DNA < 4000 copies/mL before treatment) showed that using 2 cycles vs. 3 cycles of concurrent cisplatin (100 mg/m²) + IMRT, the 5-year PFS was 85.0% vs. 87.3% (non-inferiority P = 0.016). The late toxicities of the 2-cycle group, such as jaw clenching, dry mouth, and hearing impairment, were significantly lower. EBV DNA can guide the downgrading of chemotherapy intensity for low-risk patients [60]. This clinical finding is highly consistent with the association discussed earlier between EBV DNA load and immune microenvironment characteristics. Patients with low EBV DNA loads present a T-cell precursor exhaustion state and have a better response to treatment, thus allowing for a safe downgrading.

Additionally, a randomized phase II trial conducted showed that in locally advanced patients with newly diagnosed disease, the use of teprotumumab + GP as neoadjuvant therapy (3 cycles), followed by adjuvant teprotumumab (6 cycles), and finally CCRT (PD1 inhibitor Toripalimab, cisplatin plus gemcitabine neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy) significantly improved disease-free survival [61]. Therefore, the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors has led to an improvement in efficacy. The DIAMOND team further explored the feasibility of the platinum-free regimen, using teprotumumab + GP induction chemotherapy + radiotherapy (platinum-free group) vs. teprotumumab + GP induction chemotherapy + concurrent cisplatin radiotherapy (standard group). The results showed a 3-year failure-free survival rate of 88.3% vs. 87.6% (non-inferiority P = 0.002), a vomiting incidence rate of 26.2% vs. 59.8% (P < 0.001), and the results indicated that patient-reported quality of life was better, suggesting that immune therapy combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy can safely discontinue the use of platinum, while retaining efficacy and significantly reducing the severe toxicity of cisplatin [62]. Therefore, it was proven that moving immune therapy earlier to newly diagnosed patients confirmed that PD-1 inhibitors combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy can further enhance efficacy or achieve safe platinum discontinuation. Immunotherapy combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy is a potential treatment direction in the future. The reason why the platinum-free strategy mediated by immune therapy is feasible lies in the mechanism that PD-1 inhibitors reverse T-cell exhaustion and restore the tumor-clearing ability of the immune system, thereby partially replacing the direct cytotoxic effect of cisplatin. This is also related to the metabolic reprogramming discussed earlier, and immunotherapy may improve the metabolic competitive characteristics of the tumor microenvironment.

Therefore, the treatment for locally advanced EBV-positive NPC has evolved into a combination of GP-induced chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or a combination of both, along with or without immunotherapy. EBV DNA-guided down-regulation therapy and immunotherapy-mediated platinum-free strategies are reshaping clinical practice.

Breakthroughs in immunotherapy for the initial metastatic lesions of recurrent/metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma

Although patients with locally advanced disease can achieve a better prognosis through radical treatment based on radiotherapy, 10–20% of them will experience recurrence or distant metastasis. For patients with recurrent/metastatic disease, although distant metastasis has occurred, systemic treatment targeting the metastatic lesions still falls within the category of initial treatment. Moreover, the proportion of EBV-positive nasopharyngeal carcinoma in the metastatic cases is relatively high, suggesting that the EBV-driven tumor development mechanism continues to play a role during the metastasis process.

Before the use of immunotherapy, the standard treatment for recurrent/metastatic NPC was platinum-based dual-drug chemotherapy, but the median overall survival was less than 20 months. The 5-year follow-up results showed that the first PD-1 inhibitor provided high-level evidence for the long-term survival benefit of PD-1 inhibitors in recurrent/metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma. This study also revealed that using camrelizumab + GP vs. placebo + GP, the results showed that the median OS: 34.5 months vs. 26.6 months (HR 0.74, P = 0.047), and the 5-year OS rate: 37.8% vs. 24.2% (absolute benefit 13.6%). Moreover, this study confirmed that the OS of patients with rapid EBV DNA clearance was significantly longer (HR 0.32, P < 0.001), and this finding was highly consistent with the previous mechanism analysis. EBV DNA load reflects the tumor burden and immune microenvironment status, and it reflects the dynamic changes of the tumor and immunity. Rapid EBV DNA clearance indicates that the immune system successfully reestablishes control over the tumor, and is closely related to the reversal of T cell exhaustion and the activation of tertiary lymphoid structures.Therefore, this study not only established the position of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy as the standard first-line treatment for recurrent/metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma, but also provided a direct basis for individualized treatment guided by liquid biopsy. The dynamic monitoring of EBV DNA can be used to identify the benefit population at an early stage and promptly adjust the treatment strategy [63].

The link between immune microenvironment and treatment

Although the aforementioned clinical trials have achieved significant progress, the treatment of EBV-positive NPC still faces several challenges. Approximately 20–30% of EBV-positive NPC patients do not respond to PD-1 inhibitors. What are the characteristics of the immune microenvironment of these patients? Is it related to the terminal exhaustion state we previously discussed, or to the previously discussed TLS deficiency or immaturity? Some patients with initial response eventually experience disease progression. Does the mechanism of acquired resistance involve the upregulation of the LMP1-ALIX-sEV axis, discussed previously, or the adaptive changes in metabolic reprogramming? The Li et al. study only targeted patients with low EBV DNA load. Can high-load patients safely downgrade their EBV DNA? Is it necessary to combine antiviral treatment to reduce the viral load before conducting immunotherapy? This is directly related to the hypothesis proposed previously that EBV DNA load drives the remodeling of the immune microenvironment. The optimal timing of the best combination of immunotherapy with radiotherapy and chemotherapy is what we need to explore. This is closely related to the dynamic evolution of the interaction between metabolism and immunity discussed previously. Therefore, our future research directions can be based on adaptive treatment clinical trials with dynamic monitoring of EBV DNA, exploring new treatment strategies targeting EBV miRNAs (such as miR-BART11/17-3p), developing combined treatment regimens targeting metabolic-immune nodes such as MCT1 inhibitors + PD-1 inhibitors, and establishing an immune typing system integrating EBV status, TLS maturity, and exhausted T cell subtypes to guide precise treatment. However, there is a complex transformation form between the mechanism target and clinical efficacy. The dynamics of the tumor microenvironment, patient individual heterogeneity, and the optimal sequence and dose of combined treatment are all factors that we need to consider to improve.

Conclusion and outlook

Integration of the EBV regulatory network

After a systematic review, we have elaborated extensively in the previous part on the exhaustion of immune microenvironment effector cells, the expansion of inhibitory cells, metabolic reprogramming, EBV miRNAs regulation, the spatial organization of tertiary lymphoid structures, and the comparison between EBV positive and negative cases, delving into the transformation of clinical treatment strategies. Throughout this process, EBV has always been the core thread running through the entire text. EBV, through its two weapon arsenals - encoded proteins (LMP1, LMP2A, EBNA1, ZTA, BRRF2) and non-coding RNAs (miR-BART family, circRNA-BART2.2), has formed a multi-level regulatory network covering signaling pathways, transcriptional regulation, epigenetics, metabolic remodeling, and cell-to-cell communication. However, as we pointed out earlier, the various mechanisms discovered in current research have been independently identified, but the interactions and hierarchical relationships among them are still unclear. Here, we attempt to integrate the scattered regulatory mechanisms into a synergistic pattern to reveal how EBV systematically remodels the host immune microenvironment. The same viral product regulates multiple immune nodes. For example, LMP1 can simultaneously affect the function of effector cells, the recruitment of inhibitory cells, and metabolic reprogramming, efficiently remodeling the immune microenvironment (Table 3); the same immune node is often regulated by multiple EBV products, which is conducive to ensuring the survival of the virus and the immune escape of tumor cells, but targeting a single target for inhibition may be compensated by other viral products (Table 4); in addition, the regulatory mechanisms of EBV form synergistic effects at different levels (Table 5), collectively influencing the immune microenvironment of NPC.

Research limitations and future directions

A study published in Oral Oncology in 2019 conducted a systematic analysis of the EBV antibody profile in 272 NPC patients. The presence status of EBV in NPC is not a static type II latent state. Current evidence suggests that during the progression of the tumor from early to advanced stages, EBV may undergo intermittent intermittent reactivation, expressing immediate early and early cleavage antigen, thereby reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment. This implies that the regulatory role of EBV on immunity may differ between the initiation and progression stages of the disease, shifting from a static to a dynamic evolution [64]. This means that the regulatory role of EBV on immunity may vary between the initiation and progression stages of the disease. How to balance the latency and cleavage periods of EBV, which is mainly latent type II infection in NPC, but recent studies have revealed that cleavage period proteins (such as ZTA, BRRF2) are also expressed and participate in immune regulation [5, 6]. How the transition between latency and cleavage is controlled, and whether the expression of cleavage period proteins is random or induced by specific microenvironmental signals, the answers to these questions will determine whether antiviral strategies should target the latency period or cover the cleavage period simultaneously. Although we know that the EBV-related immune microenvironment is important, single-cell transcriptomics, although capable of resolving tumor heterogeneity at the single-cell resolution, is still an extreme reductionist study that cuts the complex pathological ecosystem into isolated cell subpopulations and molecular pathways, which may miss the regulatory laws at the system level. The dynamic evolution laws of diseases at different stages, including initiation/progression, localization/dissemination, sensitivity/resistance, and the clinical significance of EBV-negative patients, have indeed not been clarified.

Current data show that EBV-positive and EBV-negative NPC exhibit different recurrence patterns. A large-sample study (N = 2942) published in BMC Oral Health in 2025 found that EBER-negative (i.e., EBV-negative) patients had a higher risk of local regional recurrence (5-year local failure-free survival rate: 76.5% vs. 86.8%, P = 0.012) [65]. A multicenter study from Hubei Cancer Hospital (N = 120) revealed that EBV-positive patients were more prone to local recurrence (9.7% vs. 0%, P = 0.026), while all recurrence events in EBV-negative patients were distant metastases (14.6% had distant metastasis, with no local recurrence) (Xiong Y, 2023). The multicenter study confirmed that EBV-negative NPC is associated with poorer clinical outcomes [65–67]. The possible mechanisms includeEBV-negative cases are more likely to be keratinized, with more aggressive biological behavior; Diagnosis is often at an advanced stage; The recurrence pattern is mainly distant metastasis rather than local recurrence. These findings suggest that EBV-negative NPC may require different treatment strategies (such as more intensive systemic treatment). Moreover, whether the cases of EBV-negative NPC are truly EBV-independent, or the virus load is too low or in a latent silent state and cannot be detected, and the formation of the immune microenvironment in EBV-negative NPC is driven by genetic factors, environmental exposure, or inflammation, the current mechanism research is still unclear.

Based on our research analysis of primary untreated EBV-positive NPC, we have clearly recognized the cellular basis of EBV reshaping the immune microenvironment of NPC. In addition, we compared the NPC that relapsed after radiotherapy. Peng et al. conducted single-cell sequencing analysis on 4 primary and 2 relapsed NPC cases, and for the first time revealed the unique remodeling characteristics of the tumor immune microenvironment after radiotherapy. Compared with the primary untreated tumor, the infiltration of regulatory T cells in the relapsed tumor was significantly increased and was in a highly immunosuppressive state. CD8 + T cells simultaneously presented dual characteristics of high cytotoxicity and high dysfunction. The enrichment of M2-type macrophages and LAMP3 + dendritic cells further exacerbated immunosuppression. This discovery not only supplements our understanding of the dynamic evolution of the immune microenvironment of EBV-positive nasopharyngeal carcinoma, but also suggests that radiotherapy and other treatments themselves can profoundly reshape the tumor immune microenvironment. The immune suppression network of the relapsed tumor may be more stubborn [68]. A single-cell sequencing study published in 2024 recently analyzed the paired primary lesions and metastatic lymph nodes of EBV-positive NPC patients at high resolution, revealing the dynamic evolution of the tumor ecosystem from localized to disseminated. This study initially clarified the immune remodeling characteristics of EBV-positive tumors during the metastasis process, providing an important basis for understanding the precise role of EBV at different stages of the disease.

Although there are still few studies comparing the immune microenvironment of sensitive and resistant tumor tissues directly, the dynamic monitoring of plasma EBV DNA provides an important window for understanding the relationship between EBV and treatment response. Studies have shown that patients with EBV DNA remaining persistently positive or relapsing after neoadjuvant chemotherapy have significantly worse prognosis than those with cleared EBV DNA (5-year PFS of the persistent positive group is only 52.5%). Patients with residual EBV DNA after induction chemotherapy have significantly worse DMFS, DFS, and OS (P = 0.003–0.006); all endpoints are worse in patients with residual EBV DNA after radiotherapy (P < 0.001) [67]. Moreover, EBV-LMP1 can increase cisplatin resistance through Drp1-dependent mitochondrial morphology remodeling [69]. These findings suggest that the resistant state may be related to EBV reactivation or immune remodeling caused by the expression of specific viral proteins.

As Luo (2023) pointed out, cancer, including NPC, is essentially a pathological ecosystem with ecological and evolutionary characteristics [70]. Under this framework, EBV, tumor cells, immune cells, and metabolic products together constitute interacting species, and their evolution follows the laws of niche formation, population competition, and natural selection. Does the species interaction between Tregs and M2-TAM exist as mutualism? What is the mechanism by which effector T cells are displaced by niches? Analyzing EBV-related NPC within an ecosystem, including how EBV infection modifies the local microenvironment such as acidification, hypoxia, and nutrient deficiency, to create suitable living spaces and niches for itself and tumor cells; under the pressure of immunotherapy, how does the latent and lytic state of EBV transform and whether there is co-evolution between tumor cells and EBV.

Unlike viruses that infect hosts, we believe that NPCs should be regarded as a co-evolutionary ecosystem between viruses and hosts. In the future, the clinical treatment strategies for NPCs need to shift from single-target blockade to ecosystem regulation. Targeting multiple nodes of the immune suppression network for combined blockade, such as PD-1 inhibitors combined with chemokine receptor antagonists like CXCR4 inhibitors; moreover, due to the dynamic changes in the immune microenvironment, we can design treatment times and sequences that involve induction followed by blockade or reduction followed by clearance. For example, first use an EBNA1 inhibitor to reduce the viral load, then initiate immune checkpoint blockade; in addition, we can utilize biological markers such as EBV DNA titer, TLS status (CTR score), and immune escape gene preferences to divide patients into different ecosystem subtypes for matching treatment; furthermore, we can also regulate environmental factors such as hypoxia, acidification, and nutrient competition to shift the ecosystem from immunosuppression to immunosupport.

Future research should focus on the integrated application of multi-omics technologies, the combination of spatial transcriptomics and single-cell data, to achieve precise cellular localization of gene expression profiles and reveal the spatial neighborhood relationships between EBV-infected cells and immune cells. The introduction of proteomics and epigenetics can fill the gap between the transcriptional level and the functional level, and analyze how EBV regulates host gene expression through chromatin remodeling and protein modification. The establishment of lineage tracing and functional validation models such as EBV-infected organoids and humanized mouse models can elevate the correlation observations to causal mechanisms. The application of computational methods such as pseudo-batch analysis can compensate for the information loss caused by the sparsity and heterogeneity of single-cell data, and improve the sensitivity of detecting differentially expressed genes [71, 72].

IBRO mini-series spotlights Latin American young investigators in neuroscience
Authors: Angeles Salles
Published: 2023

In the realm of neuroscience, the path from budding enthusiasm to significant accomplishments is often only possible through a high level of dedication and with the help of external support. This is even more true for researchers faced with the hardships of doing science in countries with low resources and sometimes unstable political climates, such as those in Latin America. The International Brain Research Organization (IBRO) has consistently advocated for the growth of emerging neuroscientists. With the launch of its Mini-Series, the "IBRO Neuroscience Reports" journal has started a volume that sheds light on the outstanding contributions of Latin American researchers. This collection stands as a testament to the potential of early-career researchers, supported by diverse opportunities offered by IBRO. As I read this volume, I can see not only scientific progress but also the global spirit that fuels the advancement of neuroscience.

In a field that thrives on global collaboration, the IBRO Mini-Series transcends geographic boundaries, and promotes an environment of unity and shared knowledge. The featured Latin American researchers, coming from diverse backgrounds and experiences, work under the banner of IBRO's support to study the brain.

The Return Home fellowships, Travel grants, and Fellowships for Training provided by IBRO signify more than financial assistance; they mark a commitment to promoting intellectual curiosity and propelling careers. These initiatives serve as springboards for young researchers, offering them access to cutting-edge research environments around the world. The return to their home countries is marked not just by acquired knowledge and innovative techniques, but by a global perspective that fuels progress in neuroscience throughout Latin America.

Within this volume, a tapestry of research domains is presented, mirroring the diverse facets of modern neuroscience. From investigating the effects of amyloid-β on protein SUMOylation and uncovering the role of the vagus nerve in mediating ghrelin's actions, to exploring the mechanisms underlying synaptic pruning during brain development and unraveling the implications of Mycobacterium leprae infection on Schwann cells, the articles cover an array of topics. Additionally, the Mini-Series also includes examinations of bat auditory responses, rodent stereotaxic surgery techniques, and nociceptive alterations associated with gliosis after olfactory bulbectomy. This diversity underscores the multidimensional nature of neuroscience exploration.

Central to the success of this Mini-Series is its adherence to open-access principles. By showcasing these contributions in the "IBRO Neuroscience Reports" journal, IBRO continues to champion inclusivity and the democratization of knowledge. In a landscape where free access to groundbreaking insights is ever more important, open-access publications ensure that scientific discoveries are accessible globally and break down barriers to discovery.

I am proud to have been involved in this founding volume of IBRO Mini-Series as it is a resounding celebration of the remarkable achievements of Latin American early-career neuroscientists. By illuminating their contributions within the "IBRO Neuroscience Reports" journal, IBRO reaffirms its commitment to fostering a culture of collaboration and intellectual exploration. As we immerse ourselves in this collection, we are reminded of the multidisciplinary nature of our field and the significant advances made possible by the unwavering support of IBRO and the visionary researchers it champions.

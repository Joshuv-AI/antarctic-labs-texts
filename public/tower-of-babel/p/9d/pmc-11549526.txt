Complete Obstruction of Superior Mustard Baffle: Electrosurgical Transcatheter Recanalization—First-in-Human Case Report
Authors: Jessica Heibel, Asad Qadir, Odai Alhasanat, Paige Castaneda, Rolando Zamora-Salinas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSystemic venous baffle obstruction is a long-term complication that can arise from the Mustard operation. We outline a novel percutaneous approach to a long segment, complete occlusion of a Mustard baffle using electrosurgical recanalization and stenting.Keywords: congenital heart disease, recanalization, transcatheter electrosurgery

Introduction

The Mustard procedure was historically used for correction of d-transposition of the great arteries before introduction of the arterial switch procedure.1 One of the long-term complications of this procedure is obstruction of a limb of the systemic venous baffle. Baffle obstruction can lead to venous congestion and other difficulties with access to the heart if further intervention is warranted. There are surgical and percutaneous options for treatment of baffle obstruction. We describe a novel percutaneous approach to a long segment, complete occlusion of the superior Mustard baffle using electrosurgical recanalization and stenting.

Clinical case

A 43-year-old man with a previous diagnosis of d-transposition of the great arteries who had undergone a Mustard procedure at 2 years of age presented with chronic complete occlusion of the superior Mustard baffle. Although his condition was asymptomatic owing to the decompression of the superior vena cava (SVC) via the azygous vein, he required pacemaker implantation due to atrial arrhythmias and sick sinus syndrome. He was determined to be a high surgical risk, so he was recommended to undergo intravenous pacer placement, which necessitated recanalization of the superior Mustard baffle.

Initial angiograms confirmed complete luminal occlusion of the superior Mustard baffle, with no continuity between the SVC and the proximal superior limb (Supplemental Video 1). The length of the occluded segment was 9.8 mm (Figure 1A). Access was obtained through the right internal jugular vein and the right femoral vein and artery. Using both fluoroscopic and transesophageal echocardiogram for intraoperative guidance, the proximal superior limb was engaged with a coaxial system of a 4F Judkins right 4 catheter (Boston Scientific), Cantata microcatheter (Cook Medical), and 0.014-inch Asahi CTO wire (Asahi Intecc). A 0.035-inch Amplatz goose neck snare (Medtronic) was placed from the right internal jugular and positioned within the SVC against distal obstruction as an additional target. The wire failed to advance across the obstruction manually, so the procedure was converted to an electrosurgical approach using the procedure as described by Khan et al2(as demonstrated in Figure 2). The externalized end of the Asahi wire was denuded using a cautery scratch pad and clamped to an electrosurgical generator. Careful alignment of the wire in multiple planes toward the snare in the SVC was ensured. Electrical energy at a maximum of 10 W for 2 seconds was applied, allowing for partial advancement of the wire (Supplemental Video 2). Attempts to advance the microcatheter over the wire were unsuccessful. The femoral venous access was exchanged for an 8.5F Agilis steerable sheath (Abbott), which was advanced to the systemic venous baffle and directed toward the superior limb. A 4F straight tapered glide catheter (Terumo) and 0.014-inch Pilot wire (Abbott) were then used to successfully cross the remaining obstruction into the SVC. The wire was exchanged for a 0.035-inch Amplatz guide wire (Boston Scientific), which was then snared and externalized creating a venovenous loop (Figure 1B, C, Supplemental Video 3). This allowed for delivery of a P4010 Palmaz stent (Cordis) mounted on a 16.0-mm × 4.0-cm BiB balloon (B Braun Interventional Systems). Angiography demonstrated the stent was well opposed to the SVC and did not extend into the inferior limb of the baffle nor obstruct the atrioventricular valve (Figure 1D, Supplemental Video 4). The procedure was well-tolerated and anticoagulation with enoxaparin was started. He underwent successful intravenous pacemaker placement 9 months later with continued patency of the stented superior baffle.

Discussion

Baffle obstruction postatrial switch (Mustard/Senning) is a common long-term complication. Previous works demonstrate successful treatment with stenting vs surgical repair.3 Addressing complete obstruction is limited from an interventional standpoint and typical treatment methods include using a Brockenbrough needle. There are limited cases demonstrating successful interventional approach to a long segment complete occlusion of a Mustard baffle.4 A Brockenbrough needle was considered for this intervention as described for similar clinical scenarios (pacing lead insertion in atrial switch patients),5 but the distance and angle of occlusion was felt to be challenging with this tool. Radiofrequency was not available, so electrosurgery was used. This technique has been previously described for use in treating occluded vessels with excellent results.6,7 It offers use of a curved catheter and microcatheter, which allows for precise maneuvering, real-time imaging, resulting in improved procedural accuracy and reduced risk of perforation. The 3-dimentsional alignment affords a smaller radius, resulting in a reduction of required mechanical force, and reduces the risk, resulting in reduction of risk and complications.

The electrosurgical approach achieves controlled tissue ablation. The simplicity of the setup and the relatively reduced cost compared with that of radiofrequency makes this approach more available in clinical settings.

Conclusion

The novel use of electrosurgery allowed for successful percutaneous recanalization of a chronic long-segment occlusion of a Mustard baffle. The equipment required for this approach is generally accessible in most cardiac catheterization laboratories and does not necessitate specialized wires or catheters.

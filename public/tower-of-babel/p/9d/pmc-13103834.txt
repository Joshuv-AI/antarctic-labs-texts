A Metallic Tetrahexagonal NiN2 Monolayer Realized via Full Stone–Wales Reconstruction: A First-Principles Prediction
Authors: Thanasee Thanasarnsurapong, Piengaor Variyart, Suchanuch Sringamprom, Waraporn Namunmong, Natthamon Saisurin, Adisak Boonchun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We propose a novel two-dimensional (2D) tetrahexagonal NiN2 (tetrahex-NiN2) monolayer, derived through a full Stone–Wales transformation of penta-NiN2, which converts an all-pentagonal lattice into a network of four- and six-membered rings. First-principles calculations confirm that this new phase is dynamically and thermally stable. Tetrahex-NiN2 exhibits pronounced mechanical anisotropy and shortened N–N bonds, indicating significant structural reorganization. Hybrid functional calculations reveal metallic character, with strong hybridization between Ni-d and N-p orbitals near the Fermi level. Furthermore, the material demonstrates broadband and anisotropic optical absorption across the visible spectrum, suggesting promising potential for optoelectronic and photothermal applications. These findings position tetrahex-NiN2 as a stable and tunable 2D nitride and highlight the effectiveness of topological reconstruction as a strategy for engineering novel 2D quantum phases.

Introduction

The discovery of graphene in 2004 sparked widespread interest in two-dimensional (2D) materials due to their outstanding physical, electronic, and mechanical properties. − Graphene, the single-atom-thick hexagonal carbon network, has inspired extensive exploration of a broad range of 2D materials, including transition metal dichalcogenides (e.g., MoS2), hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), and MXenes. These materials have shown excellent potential in applications ranging from electronics and optoelectronics to energy storage and catalysis, owing to their tunable band gaps, high carrier mobility, and mechanical robustness. −

Beyond hexagon-based structures, the concept of all-pentagonal tiling led to the theoretical proposal of penta-graphene, a metastable 2D carbon allotrope composed entirely of pentagons. Penta-graphene has an indirect band gap, an intrinsic negative Poisson’s ratio, and remarkable mechanical strength, making it a candidate for flexible electronics, strain sensors, and thermal management. − This geometric motif has since been extended to other elements and compound systems, creating a broader family of pentagonal 2D materials, such as penta-PdSe2, penta-PdPSe, penta-NiN2, and more. − These systems combine pentagonal symmetry with diverse electronic behaviors, expanding the design space for next-generation functional materials. −

The introduction of Stone–Wales (SW) defects, which involves a 90° bond rotation to convert four adjacent polygons, typically hexagons, into a pair of pentagon–heptagon defects, has been proposed as an effective strategy for tuning the properties of 2D materials. SW defects have been reported to influence the electronic structure, chemical reactivity, and mechanical behavior of graphene, with potential implications for applications in nanoelectronics, sensing, and membrane technologies. − Additionally, Stone–Wales defect engineering has been extended to other two-dimensional materials. For instance, SW defects in h-BN have been shown to enhance gas sensing performance, while similar defects in phosphorene can modify adsorption behavior relevant for drug delivery applications.

More recently, the full application of Stone–Wales (SW) bond rotations has been proposed as an effective approach for reconstructing entire two-dimensional lattices, yielding tetrahexagonal (tetrahex) phases composed of four- and six-membered ring networks. A notable example is the transformation of penta-graphene into tetrahexcarbon, which exhibits modified electronic properties and enhanced chemical reactivity. Similar SW-induced reconstructions have subsequently been demonstrated in a growing family of pentagonal 2D materials. For instance, penta-SiC2 can be transformed into tetrahex-SiC2, penta-CN2 into tetrahex-CN2, penta-BN into tetrahex-BN, penta-BCN into tetrahex-BCN, and penta-BCP into tetrahex-BCP, each exhibiting distinct variations in structural symmetry, stability, and electronic behavior. These reconstructed tetrahex phases significantly expand the structural diversity of 2D materials while enabling tunable functional properties for nanoelectronic, optoelectronic, energy storage, sensing, and catalytic applications. − Additionally, in systems such as penta-PdSSe, full SW reconstruction can induce band gap modulation and generate new bonding configurations relevant to energy conversion technologies. ,

In this work, we extended this concept to a transition metal nitride, penta-NiN2, a recently proposed 2D monolayer with all-pentagonal tiling. By applying a full SW transformation, we constructed a previously unreported tetrahexagonal NiN2 (tetrahex-NiN2) monolayer, comprising alternating four- and six-membered rings. Using density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we systematically investigated its structural stability, phonon dynamics, thermal robustness, mechanical anisotropy, electronic structure, and optical response. Our findings confirm that the tetrahex-NiN2 phase is dynamically and thermally stable, mechanically robust, and metallic in character, offering a new direction in the design of 2D transition metal nitrides.

Computational Methods

First-principles calculations were performed within the DFT framework using the Vienna ab initio simulation package program. , The generalized gradient approximation in the form of the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional was adopted for most calculations. , The projector augmented wave method was applied, and a kinetic energy cutoff of 520 eV was used for the plane-wave basis set. Structural optimizations were conducted until the total energy converged to within 10–6 eV and the residual Hellmann–Feynman forces on each atom were below 10–2eV Å–1.

The Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE06) hybrid functional was used to provide more accurate results and address the well-known underestimation of electronic properties by the PBE functional. , Optical properties were computed using the random phase approximation based on the HSE06 hybrid functional. − For Brillouin zone integration, a Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack k-point mesh was employed. A k-point spacing of 0.03 (0.04) 2π Å–1 was employed for geometry optimizations using the PBE (HSE06) functional. For electronic property calculations, a finer k-point spacing of 0.01 2π Å–1 was employed for both functionals, while a spacing of 0.02 2π Å–1 was used for optical property calculations.

A vacuum spacing of 20 Å was applied along the out-of-plane direction to eliminate periodic image interactions. The finite displacement method was employed using the Phonopy package to obtain phonon dispersions. − This was applied to a 5 × 5 × 1 supercell with a k-spacing of 0.03 2π Å–1. Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were conducted in the canonical (NVT) ensemble using a Nosé–Hoover thermostat to assess thermal stability. , A 4 × 4 × 1 supercell and the same k-point mesh were used, as in prior calculations.

Atomic structures and charge densities were visualized using VESTA, while VASPKIT was used for postprocessing tasks, including the analysis of electronic and optical properties.

The application of a full SW transformation, involving 90° rotations of selected N–N bonds, converts this structure into a more compact arrangement of four- and six-membered rings, forming a new polymorph: tetrahex-NiN2. The results showed that the tetrahex-NiN2 has a rectangular unit cell containing three atoms, one nickel (Ni) and two nitrogen (N) atoms, unlike penta-NiN2, which has a square unit cell containing six atoms, two Ni and four N atoms. The Ni–N bond lengths remain nearly unchanged across both phases: 1.880 (1.868) Å in tetrahex versus 1.878 (1.867) Å in penta. Conversely, the N–N bonds become shorter in the tetrahex phase, decreasing to 1.219 (1.213) Å from 1.244 (1.231) Å in the penta phase. The electron localization function (ELF) plotted in Figure b reveals strong electron localization between the N–N bonds, indicating their covalent nature. Moreover, the ELF shows delocalization around Ni atoms and electron localization around N atoms, suggesting an ionic character for the Ni–N bonds. This interpretation is further supported by the large electronegativity difference between the Ni (1.91) and N (3.04) atoms.

Most tetrahex monolayers have a buckled trilayer structure comprising alternating tetragonal and hexagonal rings, yielding a finite thickness of typically 1–3 Å. For example, tetrahex-C is nearly flat at approximately 1.16 Å, while tetrahex-SiC x (x = 1, 2) and tetrahex-GeC2 show stronger buckling reaching 1.6 Å. , Main-group derivatives such as tetrahex-CX2 (X = N, P, As, Sb) and tetrahex-BX (X = P, As, Sb) exhibit larger distortions exceeding 2 Å, , whereas tetrahex-BN, tetrahex-BCN, and tetrahex-PdSSe remain moderately corrugated around 1–1.5 Å. ,, Conversely, tetrahex-NiN2 is completely planar, with all Ni and N atoms confined to one atomic plane. This flat atomic configuration reduces the primitive cell from the conventional 12-atom framework to only three atoms, establishing tetrahex-NiN2 as the thinnest and most compact member of the tetrahex family.

The dynamic stability of the tetrahex-NiN2 monolayer was evaluated by calculating phonon dispersion. As shown in Figure a, the absence of imaginary phonon frequencies throughout the Brillouin zone confirmed the dynamic stability of the structure. Typically, the presence of imaginary (i.e., negative) frequencies indicates structural instability, as it suggests the existence of nonrestorative atomic forces on perturbation. Notably, any minor negative values appearing near the Γ point are commonly attributed to numerical artifacts rather than genuine instabilities.

Mechanical Properties of Tetrahex-NiN2

The in-plane Young’s modulus (Y) of tetrahex-NiN2 was evaluated for the [100] and [010] crystallographic directions. The moduli were derived using the relations Y a = (C 11 C 22 – C 12 2)/C 22 and Y b = (C 11 C 22 – C 12 2)/C 11 for the [100] and [010] directions, respectively. The corresponding Poisson’s ratios (ν) were obtained as νa = C 12/C 22 and νb = C 12/C 11. These expressions provide insight into the anisotropic mechanical behavior of the monolayer. Table summarizes the computed Y and ν values for tetrahex-NiN2. Compared to the parent penta-NiN2 phase, the tetrahex structure exhibits reduced stiffness, indicating a softer mechanical response. When benchmarked against other tetrahexagonal materials, tetrahex-NiN2 displays a lower maximum Young’s modulus than tetrahex-C (288 N m–1), tetrahex-SiC2 (117.4 N m–1), tetrahex-CN2 (340 N m–1), and tetrahex-BCN (235.94 N m–1) but remains mechanically stiffer than tetrahex-PdSSe (42.533–45.105 N m–1).

Electronic and Optical Properties of Tetrahex-NiN2

Figure presents the electronic band structure and projected density of states (PDOS) computed using the HSE06 functional, while Figure S2 shows the corresponding results obtained from the PBE functional. The tetrahex-NiN2 monolayer exhibits metallic character, as evidenced by electronic bands crossing Fermi level near the Y point in the band structure. The PDOS reveals dominant contributions from Ni-d and N-p orbitals across the Fermi energy, indicating hybridization between Ni and N atoms. For comparison, the electronic band structure of the parent penta-NiN2 monolayer calculated using the HSE06 functional is shown in Figure S3, confirming its semiconducting nature with a band gap of approximately 1.05 eV, which agrees well with previous reports. , These results indicate that the Stone–Wales reconstruction induces a transition from a semiconducting penta-NiN2 phase to a metallic tetrahex-NiN2 monolayer. Notably, this metallic character distinguishes tetrahex-NiN2 from other reported tetrahex monolayers, which typically exhibit semiconducting behavior. − ,,−

The optical responses of tetrahex-NiN2 and its parent penta-NiN2 monolayer were examined through their complex dielectric functions, as shown in Figures and S4, respectively. For tetrahex-NiN2, the imaginary part of the dielectric function exhibits a strong and continuous low-energy enhancement, which rapidly increases toward the zero-photon-energy limit before stabilizing above approximately 2 eV. Concurrently, the real part becomes strongly negative at low energies, consistent with metallic screening arising from free carriers. In contrast, penta-NiN2 maintains the behavior typical of a semiconductor, with a clear onset around 1.9 eV corresponding to its direct band gap and well-defined interband transitions at higher photon energies. The real part of dielectric function for penta-NiN2 displays the characteristic dispersion near the absorption edge, further confirming its semiconducting nature.

The absorption spectra reveal distinct differences between the two phases. Tetrahex-NiN2 exhibits broadband absorption spanning from the infrared through the visible range and well into the ultraviolet, with peak intensities reaching the order of 105 cm–1. A pronounced polarization anisotropy is observed: the yy-polarization shows consistently higher absorption across the visible region and features a prominent peak near 5.5 eV, whereas the xx-polarization remains comparatively weaker. This anisotropy arises from the directional arrangement of Ni–N bonds in the rectangular tetrahex lattice, which enhances optical activity for electric-field orientations aligned with the stronger bonding direction. Conversely, penta-NiN2 displays a narrower absorption profile centered around 1.9 to 3 eV with nearly isotropic behavior, reflecting its higher in-plane symmetry and semiconducting band structure.

These findings reveal that the Stone–Wales reconstruction from the pentagonal to the tetrahexagonal framework fundamentally transforms the optical character of NiN2, converting a direct-gap semiconductor into a metallic monolayer with strong polarization dependence and broadband absorption. While previously reported tetrahex systems generally exhibit semiconducting behavior with moderate anisotropy, tetrahex-NiN2 uniquely combines metallic low-energy response, intense absorption over a wide spectral range, and marked in-plane optical anisotropy. This combination positions tetrahex-NiN2 as one of the most optically active members of the tetrahex family and a promising platform for broadband optoelectronic, photothermal, and polarization-sensitive applications.

Conclusions

In summary, we proposed and investigated a novel 2D tetrahexagonal NiN2 monolayer, obtained from the penta-NiN2 structure through a complete SW transformation. First-principles calculations reveal that this new phase, composed of alternating four- and six-membered rings, is both dynamically and thermally stable. The optimized geometry exhibits a reduced unit cell and modified bond lengths relative to the parent phase, while maintaining a negative formation energy, confirming its energetic feasibility.

The tetrahex-NiN2 monolayer displays pronounced mechanical anisotropy, as evidenced by its elastic constants and orientation-dependent Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio. Phonon dispersion and AIMD simulations further verify its structural robustness across a wide temperature range. Electronic structure calculations using the HSE06 functional demonstrate metallic behavior arising from hybridized Ni-d and N-p states. Additionally, the optical response exhibits a strong metallic low-energy contribution together with broad and highly anisotropic absorption extending from the infrared through the visible and into the ultraviolet regions, highlighting its potential for broadband photonic and polarization-sensitive optoelectronic applications.

Overall, our findings establish tetrahex-NiN2 as a viable and robust 2D transition-metal nitride with tunable mechanical, electronic, and optical properties, expanding the structural and functional landscape of tetrahexagonal materials.

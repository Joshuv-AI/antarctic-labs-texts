Effect of no reduction versus closed reduction on distal radius fractures in adults aged 65 years and older: a protocol for the DISCLOSE randomised equivalence trial
Authors: Laura Kärnä, Ville Ponkilainen, Aleksi Reito, Helle Østergaard, Stig Brorson, Kaspar Tootsi, Katriina Paasikallio, Tina Senholt, Yan Li, Juuso Heikkinen, Juha Kukkonen, Jorma Ryhänen, Robin Christensen, Ville M Mattila, Antti P Launonen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDistal radius fractures (DRFs) are common injuries, especially in older adults due to age-related frailty. Most DRFs in patients aged 60 and older are treated non-operatively since surgery offers no clinically important benefits. Although anatomical alignment has traditionally been the goal of the treatment, evidence suggests that in older populations, radiographic outcomes do not reliably correlate with functional outcomes. Current evidence, including one randomised trial, shows no functional benefit of closed reduction compared with casting alone, calling into question the routine use of the procedure in older patients. The primary objective is to evaluate whether no reduction is equivalent to closed reduction in patients aged 65 years or older with a displaced DRF, based on wrist-related pain and disability measured by the Patient-Rated Wrist Evaluation (PRWE) score at 12 months.Methods and analysisThis is a multi-centre, randomised controlled, equivalence trial conducted in hospitals in Finland, Denmark, Sweden and Estonia. We aim to enrol 532 patients aged ≥65 years with a displaced DRF (AO/OTA 23A/23C). Participants will be randomised (1:1) to receive either a dorsal cast without reduction (experimental intervention) or closed reduction followed by casting (control comparator). The primary endpoint is the difference between groups assessed using the PRWE outcome score at 12 months. The equivalence margin will be set at 6 PRWE points. Key secondary endpoints will include the Numeric Rating Scale for pain, patient satisfaction, quality of life (EQ-5D-5L Index) and serious adverse events at 3 months and 12 months and cosmesis at 3 months. Our main analyses will follow an intention-to-treat principle, analysed using repeated measures mixed model.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been granted by the Ethics Committee of Tampere University Hospital (R25001). Background

Distal radius fractures (DRFs) are one of the most common fractures, occurring at a rate of 212 per 100 000 person-years.1 These fractures typically result from low-energy trauma, such as falls from standing height, and are associated with age-related increases in frailty. As life expectancy in the general population continues to rise, so does the number of these fractures,2 3 presenting an ever-increasing challenge in terms of function and quality of life in both acute care and the long-term management of the fracture. The majority of patients aged 60 years or older with DRF are treated non-operatively because of the higher complication rate without significantly better functional outcomes associated with surgery.24,6

Although anatomical alignment has traditionally been the primary goal of the treatment, evidence suggests that radiographic improvement is not necessarily associated with better functional outcomes, especially in older individuals.7,9 Even when closed reduction is initially successful and radiographic alignment is achieved, several studies suggest that alignment frequently deteriorates during follow-up in older patients.9,11 For example, radial shortening tends to return to the pre-reduction state by the time the cast is removed at 5 to 6 weeks, whereas dorsal angulation seems to be maintained better.12,26 The reported total loss of reduction in adult patients with DRF ranges from 12% to 100%,12,1520 22 24 27 with patients aged 60 years or older having the highest rates (40% to 100%).10 28 29 Even though reduction is nearly always lost, a recent Finnish registry study revealed that in patients aged 65 years or older, the incidence of corrective osteotomies is very low (0.3 to 0.7 per 100 000 person-years).5

Patients often perceive closed reduction to be a painful and uncomfortable procedure.30 Indeed, studies show that closed reduction increases pain when compared with casting alone in patients awaiting surgery for DRF.31 There is also evidence that pain during closed reduction can decrease overall satisfaction and compliance with treatment.32

There is limited evidence regarding whether the closed reduction of DRF in older patients improves clinical outcomes since only one randomised controlled trial (RCT) that compares closed reduction and casting to casting alone has been published. In that RCT, Kelly et al reported no difference in functional outcomes between groups despite improved radiographic measures in the closed reduction group.26 Therefore, since radiographic measures do not correlate to functional outcomes, it is uncertain whether the routine use of closed reduction in DRF is effective in improving functional outcomes and patient satisfaction in patients aged 65 or older.7

Objective

To evaluate whether no reduction is equivalent to closed reduction in patients aged 65 years or older with a displaced DRF, based on wrist-related pain and disability measured by the Patient-Rated Wrist Evaluation (PRWE) score at 12 months.

Methods and analysis

Study setting

We will conduct a multicentre, parallel group, 1:1 randomised controlled equivalence trial. We will also include a prospective observational group for those eligible patients who decline to participate in the randomisation. The study will be conducted across multiple study sites, including university and central hospitals that provide emergency care for trauma patients living in Finland, Denmark, Sweden and Estonia.

Participants

The study population will include patients aged 65 years or older who have sustained displaced DRF. All eligible patients (box 1) will be informed about the aims of the study by the recruiting personnel, and those patients who agree to participate will be asked to provide written informed consent (online supplemental material 1).

Interventions

Experimental Intervention: no reduction

In the no reduction group, a dorsal cast will be applied to the patient’s wrist after the initial radiograph. Following casting, a control radiograph will be taken. Active range of motion exercises for the fingers and light use of the hand are recommended immediately.

Control comparator: closed reduction

The closed reduction will be performed in the emergency department by the treating physician using their usual technique, with lidocaine infiltrated into the fracture site. Re-attempts at reduction are allowed in accordance with local practice. Postreduction radiographs will then be taken to assess fracture alignment, although the findings do not influence the treatment of the patient.

In both groups, the cast will be removed after 5 weeks at either the health centre or the hospital’s outpatient clinic, in accordance with the local treatment protocol. Following cast removal, patients will be advised to resume everyday use of the injured wrist without restrictions. No radiographs will be taken during the casting period or before the 3-month follow-up.

Concomitant care

All groups will receive a similar home exercise protocol. Outpatient physiotherapy and occupational therapy will be allowed according to local practice.

Observational arm (not part of the randomised equivalence trial)

Eligible patients who decline participation in the randomisation will be offered the opportunity to join an observational group. The patients will receive standard care (closed reduction and casting) and will provide consent for follow-up. Closed reduction is performed in the emergency department and postreduction radiographs will be taken. The observational groups will be treated according to local treatment protocols. Patients in the observational cohort will be followed at the same time points using the same outcome measures as the randomised group. Study follow-up of the observational group will be organised remotely.

Outcomes

The outcomes of the DISCLOSE trial will include patient-reported outcome measures; Function - PRWE, pain - Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), patient satisfaction (PASS), quality of life (EQ-5D-5L Index), serious adverse events and cosmesis (box 2). Outcome selection was based on the Core Outcome Set for distal radius fractures, developed by the International Society for Fracture Repair (ISFR) and the International Osteoporosis Foundation.33

Baseline data

Baseline variables will be collected by study personnel via telephone within two working days of the randomisation. The study personnel will remain blinded to the randomisation results. Baseline variables include age, sex, hand dominance (right/left), height, weight, fracture type, smoking (yes/no), diabetes (yes/no), rheumatoid arthritis (yes/no), osteoporosis (yes/no), osteoporosis treatment (yes/no), previous fractures in same wrist and use of glucocorticoid treatment (yes/no).

Radiographic measurements (dorsal angulation, radial inclination, ulnar variance, step-off in the articular surface and intra-articular gap) will be measured from the initial radiographs at baseline (online supplemental material 2). PRWE, NRS for Pain and quality of life (EQ-5D-5L Index) will be collected at baseline via telephone, with patients asked to recall their status prior to sustaining the fracture. Study personnel will assess patient frailty at baseline using the Clinical Frailty Scale.34

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint will be the PRWE35 at 12 months. The PRWE is a 15-item questionnaire designed to measure wrist pain and disability in activities of daily living. The validity, reliability and responsiveness of the questionnaire have been reported to be high for assessing outcomes after DRF.36,38 The questionnaire consists of two subscales (pain and function), and the score ranges from 0 (no disability) to 100 (severe disability) (online supplemental material 3).

Equivalence margin for the primary endpoint

The minimal important difference (MID) for the PRWE has been reported as 11.5 points.39 Accordingly, the equivalence margin was conservatively set at 6 points, representing just over half the MID. This represents the maximum acceptable difference in means between the groups to still be able to consider the groups as equivalent.

Key secondary endpoints

PRWE

PRWE is additionally measured at 3 months.

Pain

The Numeric Rating Scale for Pain is a validated 11-point numerical rating scale for acute and chronic pain with terminal descriptors of 0 (no pain) and 10 (worst pain possible).40 41 Patients will be asked to evaluate their perceived pain during the previous 7 days. Pain will be assessed at 3 and 12 months.

Patient satisfaction

Patient satisfaction will be measured using the PASS.42 Considering all the different ways your injury is affecting you, if you would remain in this state, do you feel that your current state is satisfactory (yes/no)? PASS will be assessed at 3 and 12 months. Patient satisfaction will be reported as a percentage of patients with acceptable PASS.

Quality of life

EuroQol 5-dimension 5-level health-related quality of life questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) is a general measure of health-related quality of life that has been validated for assessing quality of life in patients with upper extremity disorders.43 44 It consists of five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression. We will use the EQ-5D-5L Index score, which is calculated based on each dimension using population-specific preference weights and will be reported on a scale from less than 0 to 1. Quality of life will be assessed at 3-month and 12-month timepoints.

Serious adverse events

Serious adverse events include but are not limited to the following: late surgical interventions such as corrective osteotomy or internal fixation due to symptomatic non-union; tendon repair; carpal tunnel release; acute nerve injury confirmed by electroneuromyography; and any other adverse event that results in hospitalisation, prolongation of existing hospitalisation, is life-threatening or leads to persistent or significant disability (for example, complex regional pain syndrome or incapacity). Serious adverse events will be assessed at 3-month and 12-month time points.

Asymptomatic malunion is not an adverse event, as most patients who undergo closed reduction after distal radius fracture develop malunion.10 26 29 Symptomatic non-union is defined as the presence of significant pain accompanied by the absence of a bony bridge on CT images. If a patient develops a symptomatic non-union, a CT scan will be taken at the 1-year time point.

Cosmesis

Participants will be asked a question about wrist cosmesis: Does the appearance of the wrist bother you? (yes/no). Cosmesis will be assessed at the 3-month time point.

Other endpoints

Control radiographs will be taken at the 3-month time point where we will measure the loss of reduction and compare the final measurements between the groups. Radiographic measurements will include dorsal angulation (degrees), ulnar variance (mm), radial inclination (degrees), step-off in the articular surface (mm) and intra-articular gap (mm) (online supplemental material 2).

Exploratory endpoints

All exploratory endpoints are published in separate publications.

PRWE, pain (NRS), quality of life, patient satisfaction (PASS), serious adverse events and mortality will be assessed at 5 years and are exploratory endpoints.

The upper-limb physical activity of the participants will be measured using a tri-axial accelerometer (Axivity Ltd, Newcastle upon Tyne, UK). Activity will be divided into four activity levels (inactivity, light, moderate and vigorous), and sleeping time will be counted and recorded. The participants will have an accelerometer sensor mounted on both wrists with a wristband. This will allow us to assess the asymmetry between the fractured and non-fractured arm. Patients in both groups will be advised to use the sensors for 7 days at 3-month and 12-month time points.

The costs of both treatment arms will be assessed at 12 months as the cost-effectiveness analysis. Included unit cost components include hospital stays and outpatient visits, including imaging, surgical and anaesthetic procedures, physiotherapy, complication management, re-operations and cast changes. Costs will be obtained directly from patients through questionnaires administered at the 3-month and 1-year follow-up and from medical records. Cost-effectiveness will be evaluated as the cost per quality-adjusted life-years gained.

Schedule of enrolment, interventions and assessment

Participants will meet a research personnel or study coordinator at the 3-month and 12-month time points. At the 3-month time point, radiographs will be taken. Thereafter, no additional radiographic follow-ups will be organised. Follow-up appointments will be carried out according to the preferences of the participants; all patients will be invited to attend an in-person follow-up visit; however, if this is unsuitable, the follow-up will be conducted remotely via telephone or email link by a study nurse. In the remote option, the accelerometer wristbands will be sent to the participants by regular mail. By offering a remote option, we aim to minimise the number of participants unavailable for follow-up.

All patients presenting with unsettling symptoms will undergo physiotherapy rehabilitation for up to 1 year, as in standard care. If the symptoms do not improve during this period, the patient will be referred to an orthopaedic surgeon or hand surgeon for further evaluation. Where appropriate, additional procedures, such as a CT scan or, in cases of symptomatic malunion or non-union, a corrective osteotomy, will be considered.

Sample size and power considerations

The sample size was determined based on an equivalence design with an equal allocation ratio (1:1). Based on the MID for PRWE of 11.5 points, the equivalence margin was conservatively set at 6 points. Based on the findings of previous studies, the SD of the outcome (PRWE) variable was estimated to be 21.9 22 45 46 Using these parameter estimates, a total sample size of 532 participants (266 per group) will provide 90% power to demonstrate equivalence between groups on the primary endpoint, assuming a true mean difference of <0.01, a common SD of 21, and equivalence margins of –6 to 6 on the PRWE score. The calculation was based on a 2-sided 90% confidence interval (α=0.05) and used the exact method for two-sample means under normal distribution assumptions.

Recruitment

Participants eligible for this trial will be recruited by orthopaedic residents/surgeons and emergency medicine doctors (recruiting personnel) in the emergency rooms of the participating hospitals. Eligible patients will be provided with a participant information sheet and given enough time to evaluate their willingness to participate and to ask any questions about the trial they may have. Patients who decline to be included in the randomised cohort of the study will be invited to participate in the observational cohort. Written consent will be obtained prior to inclusion from both study cohorts.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

The allocation sequence will be generated by an independent statistician who is not involved in conducting the trial. The randomisation sequence will be stratified by age group (65–74 years and >74 years) and by country, using variable block sizes ranging from 2 to 6 and a 1:1 allocation ratio. The sequence will be concealed from all the study personnel using an online randomisation system. Following written informed consent, participants will be randomised immediately in the emergency department using the REDCap online randomisation system.

Data collection

Data collection will be conducted by the study personnel who will enter the data into the REDCap system based on a phone interview or, if the patient prefers, through an email link. All patients who attend an in-person follow-up visit will enter their data during the visit. For those patients who participate in remote follow-ups, the data will be collected by study personnel via telephone or through an email link, depending on the preferences of the patients. These follow-up options are offered to minimise the amount of missing data and to enhance overall data quality.

Data management

Each patient will be assigned a unique trial identification number (TIN) matched with their personal identification number (ID). The TIN will be assigned when the patient has signed the informed consent form. The TINs are consecutive, and they are never reused. Throughout the trial, the research data will only be handled with a TIN. For patients recruited at Tampere University Hospital, the personal IDs of the patients will be stored in a secure electronic server. To ensure patient confidentiality, each research centre will have their own code keys for their patients, which can only be accessed by the study personnel of each centre. All the research data will be saved on a database with the online patient management programme REDCap and secured by two-factor authentication. Only study personnel will have access to the REDCap data on the secure study server at Tampere University Hospital. This approach will ensure patient de-identification and guarantees that individual identities will remain protected, even in the event of unauthorised access to server data. Furthermore, the research data on the server that are used by the study personnel will contain only de-identified TINs with a set of numbers acquired from the questionnaires, ie, each question will be answered with a number. To make a remote option (via email) available to patients, the email addresses of the patients will be matched to the patient’s unique trial identification numbers.

All the acquired data will be stored on the study server. Follow-up data will be entered either directly by patients using a tablet during visits to the participating hospital or remotely via an email link. When questionnaires are returned by regular mail or conducted through phone interviews, the follow-up data will be entered by a researcher or a study nurse. Patient-reported outcome data will be entered directly into the REDCap system by the patients using the ‘required fields’ option activated to ensure there are no missing items from the completed questionnaires. Researchers from each participating hospital will have access to the secure study server where the trial research data are stored. An information security committee has approved the server at Tampere University Hospital.

The copyright of the trial research data will be owned and created by the collaboration parties. The data will be shared freely among the collaboration parties. All participating researchers will have access to the data after the trial. Due to the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), public data sharing will be restricted until primary analysis and publication have been completed. Under certain circumstances, for example, when a new member joins the collaboration, we will grant access to the data.

Data analysis

The primary hypothesis is that patients aged 65 and older with displaced distal radius fractures treated non-operatively with casting alone (without closed reduction) will have clinically equivalent functional outcomes, measured by PRWE score at 12 months after injury, compared with those treated with closed reduction and casting.

The findings will be presented in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.47 48 All primary and secondary analyses will be based on the Intention-to-Treat (ITT) population; the ITT principle asserts that the effect of a treatment policy is best assessed by evaluating participants based on the treatment they were originally assigned to receive, rather than the treatment they actually received.

For the main comparison, PRWE total score between the study groups at 12 months will be analysed using a repeated measures mixed model. Fixed effect factors in the analysis will include the randomised study group (two levels), stratification factors (age group and country), assessment time (three levels (0, 3 and 12 months from baseline)) and the interaction between group and time, while patients will be treated as random factors. Since the main analysis model will include randomised treatment, stratification variables and the interaction between treatment group and time as fixed effects, with the baseline value of the endpoint included as a covariate, results will be presented as least squares mean differences between groups with corresponding 95% confidence intervals at 12 months postrandomisation. Missing data for continuous outcome measures will be handled indirectly using a mixed-effects linear model approach, assuming data are missing at random, consistent with the underlying assumption of these models.49 Categorical endpoints will be analysed for each individual time point by logistical regression using stratification factors as covariates. For categorical efficacy outcomes, missing data will be addressed using non-response imputation, in which patients who withdrew or did not provide a response will be considered non-responders.

We will perform a series of sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of the results.50 Because some patients are likely to experience important intercurrent events (eg, undergoing surgery within the first 12 months from baseline; ie, referred to as crossovers), sensitivity analyses will explore whether different assumptions about missing data could affect the conclusions51: We will apply an estimand that reflects a while-on-randomised-treatment strategy. This approach aims to estimate the effect of the interventions up to, but not beyond, the occurrence of an intercurrent event. Specifically, the outcome value(s) prior to the intercurrent event will be used for analysis.

Among the prespecified ancillary analyses is an exploration of whether age group and sex modify the effect of treatment on the primary endpoint at 12 months. Specifically, outcomes will be compared between patients aged <75 years and ≥75 years and between males and females.52 The approach outlined by Christensen et al promotes a transparent and practical method for assessing effect modification in randomised controlled trials by emphasising the use of formal statistical interaction tests. Rather than relying on potentially misleading subgroup analyses, the authors advocate for simple, accessible techniques to evaluate whether treatment effects differ across baseline patient characteristics. These secondary analyses are intended to be supportive, explanatory or hypothesis-generating only. The observational cohort will be analysed independently, employing statistical methods similar to those used for the RCT study groups.

Blinded data interpretation

To minimise the risk of biased interpretation, the results of this trial will be reported using a blinded data interpretation process.53 A blinded statistician will conduct the analyses, with the randomised study groups pseudonymised as treatment ‘A’ and treatment ‘B’. Before reviewing the primary endpoint results, the writing committee will prepare a ‘Background Assumptions’ document outlining their predefined MIDs for the outcome measures, along with a brief summary of the planned statistical analyses. This document will then be signed by all members of the writing committee and included as an appendix to the main publication.

Thereafter, the committee will conduct two separate interpretations of the primary endpoint results, first assuming that treatment A represents the no reduction group, and then assuming that treatment A represents the closed reduction group. All decisions regarding key analyses and the format for presenting the primary results will be made prior to unblinding. These decisions, along with the meeting minutes, will be formally recorded, signed by the writing committee and published as an appendix to the primary report.

Data monitoring

A Data Safety Monitoring Board will oversee the study to ensure the rights, safety and well-being of all participants throughout the trial. Monitoring will be conducted according to a pre-approved monitoring plan and in compliance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice (ICH GCP 5.18). The monitor will assess whether the trial is conducted, documented and reported in accordance with the protocol, regulatory requirements and applicable guidelines. Data monitoring will be carried out at only the primary institution, Tampere University Hospital.

Patient and public involvement

Patient and Public Involvement (PPI) was conducted in collaboration with the Pirkanmaa Bone Association. During a dedicated meeting with members of the association, both primary and secondary endpoints were thoroughly reviewed. Pain and functional ability in daily tasks were identified as top priorities. Based on our discussions with members of the association, maximum grip strength was excluded from the outcomes because it was not considered to have significant relevance to daily life. While the endpoints were considered appropriate, early recognition of pain emerged as a concern. In response, we improved the visibility of the contact information to ensure patients know where to seek assistance in cases of severe wrist pain. To maintain ongoing patient and public engagement, we will collaborate with a PPI representative throughout the entire study period. Furthermore, PPI will be fully reported in the final publication in accordance with the GRIPP2-SF reporting checklist.54

Ethics and dissemination

The DISCLOSE trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and all applicable local regulations in each participating country. Ethical approval for the study has been granted by the Ethics Committee of Tampere University Hospital (reference number R25001), and separate approvals will be obtained from the corresponding ethics committees in each participating country. Any significant protocol amendments will be submitted to the relevant ethics committees for approval prior to implementation. The approved amendments will then be communicated promptly to investigators, trial registries, regulatory authorities, trial participants and relevant journals.

Informed consent will be obtained by emergency department personnel or designated research personnel at each participating hospital before any procedures associated with the trial are undertaken. Potential participants will be provided with detailed verbal and written information about the study and will have sufficient time to ask questions prior to providing written informed consent.

All personal data collected during the trial will be managed in compliance with relevant data protection legislation. Access to identifiable participant information will be restricted to authorised study personnel only.

All principal investigators and study personnel involved in the DISCLOSE trial have declared no financial or other competing interests related to this study. Any potential conflicts of interest arising during the trial will be disclosed and managed appropriately.

The study protocol will be published in accordance with the SPIRIT Statement.55 Reporting of trial findings will follow the CONSORT statement.56 Study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, presentations at scientific conferences and updated in the clinical trial registry. Authorship eligibility will follow International Committee of Medical Journal Editors criteria, and all contributors will be appropriately acknowledged.

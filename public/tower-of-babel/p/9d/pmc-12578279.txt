Efficacy and Safety of Bictegravir Plus Lenacapavir: 48-Week Outcomes in People With HIV-1 Who Are Virologically Suppressed on Complex Antiretroviral Regimens at Baseline
Authors: Karam Mounzer, Jihad Slim, Moti Ramgopal, Malcolm Hedgcock, Mark Bloch, Jorge Santana, Keith Aizen, Xu Zhang, Peter Sklar, Jairo M Montezuma-Rusca, Sorana Segal-Maurer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractARTISTRY-1 is a phase 2/3 trial of bictegravir (BIC; 75 mg) plus lenacapavir (LEN; 25 mg or 50 mg) versus complex antiretroviral therapy regimens in 128 virologically suppressed people with HIV-1. At 48 weeks, BIC + LEN (at either LEN dose) was highly effective at maintaining virologic suppression and was well tolerated.Keywords: bictegravir, clinical trial, complex antiretroviral regimens, HIV-1, lenacapavir

Many people with human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1; hereafter, PWH) are unable to take currently available single tablet antiretroviral therapies (ARTs) due to drug resistance, intolerance, toxicity, drug-drug interactions (DDIs), or contraindications to existing single tablet regimens (STRs) [1–4]. PWH who are taking complex regimens face numerous challenges such as high pill burden, suboptimal adherence, reduced quality of life, and DDIs [1, 4, 5]. An STR of bictegravir (BIC) and lenacapavir (LEN) could optimize treatment in PWH who are virologically suppressed (VS) on complex regimens (CRs; thereafter, VS on CR).

ARTISTRY-1 (GS-US-621-6289; NCT05502341) is an ongoing phase 2/3 study investigating the efficacy and safety of BIC (75 mg) and LEN (25 mg or 50 mg) in PWH who were VS on CR [6]. BIC is a global guideline-recommended integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) with a high barrier to resistance [4, 7–9]. LEN is a first-in-class capsid inhibitor without documented de novo resistance in the absence of prior exposure [10]. Once-daily coadministered BIC + LEN (at either LEN dose) was highly effective in maintaining virologic suppression at week 24 (primary outcome of the phase 2 portion of ARTISTRY-1) and was well tolerated in participants switching from a complex ART regimen [6].

Here we report longer-term efficacy and safety data from the phase 2 portion of ARTISTRY-1 through 48 weeks of treatment.

METHODS

Detailed methods for ARTISTRY-1 have been described, including endpoints, assessments, and statistical methods [6]. Briefly, this was a phase 2/3 randomized, open-label, global, multicenter study (Supplementary Figure 1). Eligible participants were aged ≥18 years with virologic suppression (HIV RNA <50 copies/mL for ≥6 months) and on a stable complex ART regimen for ≥6 months prior to screening. A complex ART regimen was defined as: a regimen containing a boosted PI or non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) plus ≥1 other third agent from a class other than nucleos(t)ide reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI); a regimen of ≥2 pills/day, or a regimen requiring dosing more than once daily; or a regimen containing parenteral agent(s) (excluding a complete long-acting injectable regimen) as well as oral agents.

In the phase 2 portion of ARTISTRY-1, participants were randomized 2:2:1 to receive BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg, BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg, or to continue with their complex ART regimen. Single-agent BIC and LEN tablets were coadministered once daily as separate oral tablets, without regard to food intake.

Week 48 endpoints were the proportion of participants with HIV RNA <50 copies/mL (determined using the US Food and Drug Administration-defined snapshot algorithm [11]), change from baseline in cluster of differentiation 4 (CD4) cell count, and the proportion of participants with treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). Virologic rebound was defined as HIV RNA ≥50 copies/mL after day 1 of treatment and subsequently confirmed at the following visit, or HIV RNA ≥50 copies/mL at study drug discontinuation. HIV resistance testing was performed in the case of confirmed virologic rebound with HIV RNA ≥200 copies/mL.

P values are reported for descriptive purposes only.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the relevant institutional review boards. All participants provided written informed consent.

RESULTS

As previously described [6], 128 participants were randomized to switch to BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg (n = 51), BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg (n = 52), or to continue on a complex ART regimen (n = 25). At baseline, demographics and disease characteristics were generally similar between treatment groups (Supplementary Table 1) [6]. Median (range) age was 60 (26–79) years, 19% of participants were female at birth, 31% of participants were Black, and median duration of HIV treatment (quartile [Q]1, Q3) was 27.0 (19.9, 32.0) years. The most frequent reason for receiving a complex ART regimen was history of resistance (81%); no participants had a known history of resistance to INSTIs, while 52%, 64%, and 36% had a history of resistance to NNRTIs, NRTIs, and PIs, respectively. Participants were receiving a broad range of antiretrovirals (ARVs) at baseline, with 43%, 19%, 11%, and 27% of participants taking 2, 3, 4, or ≥5 pills/day, respectively, and 41% taking pills twice daily [6]. The majority (78%) of participants was taking a PI and 3% were taking an unboosted PI. Overall, 66% of participants were taking both PIs and INSTIs, with 19% in total taking a PI + INSTI alone, and 27% and 17% taking a PI + INSTI with an NRTI or NNRTI, respectively [6]. The most common comorbid condition was dyslipidemia (75%) and 24% had chronic kidney disease (CKD). Median (Q1, Q3) estimated glomerular filtration rate by Cockcroft-Gault equation was 81.4 (67.3, 99.6) mL/min.

At week 48, rates of virologic suppression were high across all treatment groups (Figure 1). Two participants in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg group and one in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg group had HIV RNA ≥50 copies/mL at week 48 (viral loads were 50, 69, and 305 copies/mL at week 48, respectively) (Figure 2). Both participants taking BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg did not meet the criteria for resistance testing and resuppressed without regimen change. The participant taking BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg met the criteria for resistance testing, showing emergence of N74T, a novel capsid polymorphism with minimal resistance to LEN. This participant switched to dolutegravir (twice daily) + maraviroc (twice daily) + subcutaneous LEN at the investigator's discretion, and achieved virologic resuppression by day 368. Two participants in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg group and four in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg group had missing virologic data during the week 48 window due to study drug discontinuation.

TEAEs are shown in Supplementary Table 2. All TEAEs with a frequency of ≥5% were grade 1 or 2, apart from one report of grade 3 diarrhea in a participant in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg group that was considered unrelated to the study drugs. No hepatitis B virus (HBV) infections or reactivations were detected. One participant in each of the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg and BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg groups discontinued due to a TEAE (BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg, grade 1 nausea; BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg, grade 3 worsening of vomiting in a participant who had a preexisting history of nausea and vomiting episodes), and one discontinuation in each group was due to participant decision. In the BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg group, one discontinuation was due to death on day 172 (caused by coronary artery disease, and unrelated to study drugs), one was due to lack of efficacy (detailed above), and one was due to investigator decision.

Change in CD4 count from baseline to week 48 was comparable among groups, with a median (Q1, Q3) change in CD4 count of +29 (−45, +96) cells/μL in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg group, +48 (−59, +150) cells/μL in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg group, and +31 (−46, +111) cells/μL in the complex ART group.

Weight remained stable through week 48, with participants in the BIC 75 mg + LEN 25 mg, BIC 75 mg + LEN 50 mg, and complex ART groups experiencing a median (Q1, Q3) change in weight of +0.6 (−0.6, +1.9) kg, +0.9 (−2.1, +3.3) kg, and −0.7 (−2.4, +0.2) kg, respectively.

DISCUSSION

These data add to week 24 outcomes [6] and demonstrate that BIC + LEN was highly effective in maintaining virologic suppression over 48 weeks in participants switching from a complex regimen, with good tolerability and similar safety profiles observed regardless of LEN dose.

Baseline demographic and disease characteristics of PWH who were VS on CR were similar across the groups. At baseline, all participants were taking multiple ARV tablets, and those taking ART regimens replaceable by existing STRs were excluded. Participants were often taking ARVs twice daily, and 78% of participants were on a PI-containing regimen (3% with unboosted PIs). Over 80% of participants in ARTISTRY-1 had a history of HIV resistance to previous ART regimens and 30% had intolerance to components of STRs, making optimization to existing STRs more difficult. Notably, the median age of participants was 60 years and most participants had ≥1 comorbid condition, including a quarter of participants with CKD at baseline. The combination of BIC + LEN may be beneficial for PWH who are VS and are taking concomitant medications, as well as those who are older, and/or with CKD.

At week 48, the combination of BIC + LEN maintained high rates of virologic suppression. BIC + LEN was well tolerated; discontinuations due to TEAEs were rare. Change in CD4 count from baseline to week 48 was comparable among groups. No HBV infections or reactivations were reported in this study; however, as new HBV infections could occur in participants receiving ART regimens without HBV activity, vaccination for HBV may be an important consideration in this population.

Limitations of the study have been previously reported and no new limitations became apparent during the 48-week follow-up [6].

Based on the previously reported phase 2 data [6], the phase 3 portion of ARTISTRY-1 will assess the efficacy and tolerability of an STR of BIC 75 mg/LEN 50 mg. Switching to a BIC 75 mg/LEN 50 mg STR could optimize the treatment regimens of PWH who are VS on CR due to resistance, toxicity, DDIs, or contraindications to currently available STRs. The safety and efficacy of a BIC/LEN STR is also being investigated in ARTISTRY-2, a phase 3 study investigating BIC/LEN versus bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide in PWH who are VS [12].

Overall, the findings from the week 48 follow-up of the phase 2 portion of ARTISTRY-1 support the continued evaluation of the combination of a BIC/LEN STR to optimize treatment in PWH who are VS on CR.

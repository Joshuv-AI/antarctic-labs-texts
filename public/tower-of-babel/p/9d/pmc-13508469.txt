Boron Amidate as a Modular Anionic Catalyst for Sequential Electron-Donor/Hydrogen-Atom-Transfer Catalysis
Authors: Bumpei Maeda, Nao Kojima, Raizo Inaba, Kohsuke Ohmatsu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Electron donor–acceptor (EDA) complex excitation has emerged as a powerful strategy for generating reactive radical species without external photocatalysts. However, the rational design of catalysts that integrate electron transfer with hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) remains underdeveloped. Herein, we report a modular anionic boron amidate that functions as a dual electron-donor/HAT catalyst within a single molecular framework. The π-conjugated anionic structure enables efficient donor–acceptor assembly, while structural modification at the nitrogen center allows systematic tuning of the donor strength, Brønsted acidity, and N–H bond dissociation free energy (BDFE). This catalyst enables EDA-driven radical generation followed by productive HAT, as demonstrated in Giese-type C­(sp3)–H functionalization. Furthermore, a Minisci-type reaction is achieved through modulation of the proton-donating ability of the boron amide, which proceeds under oxidant-free conditions and is presumably sustained by aerobic oxidation. These results establish a modular design strategy for anionic molecular catalysts that orchestrate electron transfer and HAT processes, thereby providing a framework for EDA-based catalytic transformations.

Introduction

Charge-transfer (CT) excitation of electron donor–acceptor (EDA) complexes has emerged as a molecularly designed photochemical platform for radical generation that eliminates the need for an external photocatalyst. − In recent years, chalcogen-based electron-rich donors have been employed as catalytic components in EDA-driven systems, where photoinduced radical formation is coupled with a hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) reaction. − Such EDA–HAT catalysis provides a conceptually attractive strategy for direct C–H functionalization, enabling catalyst-controlled radical generation under mild and photocatalyst-free conditions.

Efficient EDA formation and productive intermolecular CT require precise control of the electronic properties of the donor component. − In particular, the ionization propensity of the donor, which contributes to defining the CT excitation energy, as well as the extent of π-conjugation governing donor–acceptor assembly, are critical parameters in catalyst design. Leading studies have shown that structural modification of thiolate catalysts, including electronic tuning of substituents, significantly impacts the reaction efficiency, suggesting that donor properties critically influence productive EDA formation. Although structural modifications have been shown to influence the reactivity, a coherent design principle that enables systematic tuning of the donor strength and HAT reactivity remains underdeveloped.

Beyond the donor strength, an effective EDA–HAT catalyst must also generate a radical species capable of efficient hydrogen atom abstraction. N-Centered radicals are well-established hydrogen atom transfer agents and, importantly, offer tunable reactivity through the modification of substituents at the nitrogen center. − As such, they represent attractive candidates for variable EDA–HAT catalysis. Nevertheless, a deliberate molecular design that enables their catalytic generation and utilization within an EDA activation manifold remains largely unexplored. Herein, we report the development of an anionic boron amidate 1 as an electron-donor/HAT catalyst and demonstrate the reactivity for C–H functionalization (Figure ). The boron-activated ketoamide structure enhances the low acidity of the amide and enables the use of amidate as an electron donor. Upon deprotonation of the corresponding amide precursor, , the resulting anion delocalizes negative charge over an extended π-conjugated framework. This conjugation is expected not only to enhance donor strength but also to promote intermolecular assembly through π–π and dispersion interactions, thereby facilitating productive EDA formation. Furthermore, the variation of substituents at the nitrogen center enables the systematic tuning of donor ability, conjugate acid pK a, and N–H bond dissociation free energy (BDFE), providing a direct control over radical reactivity. The catalytic competence of this design was validated in Giese- and Minisci-type C­(sp3)–H functionalizations of hydrocarbon substrates.

Results and Discussion

Boron amidate 1a bearing a methyltribenzylammonium counterion (1a·NMeBn3) was prepared in three steps from a commercially available β-ketoester (Figure a). Square-wave voltammetry (SWV) measurement revealed an oxidation potential of +0.99 V vs SCE for 1a. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations estimated the pK a of the conjugate acid 1a·H in MeCN to be 24.6 (Figure S15), and the N–H BDFE was calculated to be 100 kcal/mol. , The calculated molecular orbital distribution indicates that the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of 1a is delocalized over the 1,3-dicarbonyl–boron core and extends across the entire molecular framework, including the phenyl substituent at the 3-position (Figure b). Although 1a itself exhibited no appreciable absorption beyond 400 nm, its mixing with electron-deficient olefins, such as benzylidenemalononitrile (2a), resulted in a pronounced bathochromic shift of the absorption edge, which was extended to approximately 500 nm (Figure c). The absorption in the visible region was further enhanced in less polar solvents, such as 1,2-dichloroethane (DCE). We considered that this solvent effect resulted from stabilization of the individual components by solvation in polar solvents, which weakened the donor–acceptor interaction. Using an analogous synthetic procedure, boron amidate 1b bearing a 1-naphthyl substituent at the 3-position and 1c featuring a phenyl group on the amide nitrogen were prepared (Figure d). Based on SWV measurements and DFT calculations, 1b·H was estimated to possess a BDFE comparable to that of 1a·H. By contrast, 1c displayed a lower oxidation potential, along with a lower pK a and a reduced N–H BDFE.

Having established the electrochemical and thermodynamic parameters of the boron amidates, we next evaluated their performance as electron-donor/HAT catalysts in Giese-type reaction. − As an initial benchmark, 2a, a readily reducible electron-deficient olefin, was paired with tetrahydrofuran (THF) as a model C–H nucleophile to probe the capacity of boron amidates to mediate intermolecular CT followed by hydrogen abstraction (Table ). Under irradiation at 456 nm in DCE, 2.5 mol % of 1a promoted the formation of the corresponding Giese adduct 3a in moderate yield after 16 h (entry 1). This initial result is consistent with our design hypothesis that the boron amidate has dual functions as both an electron donor and a hydrogen atom transfer catalyst within an EDA manifold. The replacement of 1a with 1b afforded a comparable yield, whereas the use of 1c resulted in slightly diminished reactivity (entries 2 and 3). Given the pronounced solvent dependence observed in the UV–vis absorption studies, we examined the influence of solvent polarity on the reaction outcome. Changing the solvent from DCE to MeCN led to a significant decrease in yield, and in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) the reaction was largely suppressed (entries 4 and 5). These results correlate with the attenuation of the long-wavelength absorption band in more polar media and are consistent with less effective intermolecular charge-transfer interactions under such conditions. To further delineate the factors governing reaction efficiency, we examined the parameters related to catalyst loading and reaction concentration. Increasing the catalyst loading from 2.5 to 5 mol % led to a modest improvement in the yield (entry 6). By contrast, investigating the reaction concentration from 0.2 to 0.5 M under otherwise identical conditions did not enhance the yield and instead resulted in a slight decrease (entry 7). This comparison suggests that the beneficial effect of higher catalyst loading cannot be ascribed solely to enhanced intermolecular CT interactions, and also likely reflects the efficiency of subsequent HAT and downstream events mediated by the boron amidate. Further systematic variation of the reaction parameters revealed that 425 nm irradiation at 2.5 mol % catalyst loading enhanced product formation (entry 8). This result aligns more closely with the CT absorption band of the EDA complex, suggesting that the improved reaction efficiency arises from a more effective excitation of the CT state rather than from changes in the concentration of the ground-state EDA complex. Independently, under the original irradiation conditions (456 nm) and catalyst loading (2.5 mol %), prolonging the reaction time to 24 h improved product formation (entry 9), consistent with the increased cumulative conversion under extended irradiation. Taken together, the combination of increased catalyst loading (5 mol %), irradiation at 425 nm, and an extended reaction time (24 h) provided the optimal conditions, affording 3a in a 65% yield (entry 10). In line with the predicted BDFE value, the EDA–HAT catalytic system of 1a was also applicable to the C–H functionalization of cycloalkanes. For example, under irradiation with a 390 nm LED, the Giese addition between cyclohexane and 2a afforded the corresponding product in a moderate yield (Figure S3).

We then investigated the catalytic function by taking into account the properties of the conjugate acid, boron amide 1·H. By acting as a proton donor, 1·H can protonate a heteroarene, thereby activating the heteroarene as an electron acceptor, while concomitantly generating the corresponding amidate 1 as an electron donor. To examine whether a catalytic reaction could proceed via excitation of an EDA complex formed through this acid–base equilibrium, we conducted the Minisci-type reaction of isoquinoline with THF (Table ). − As an initial attempt, a solution of isoquinoline and THF in DCM was treated with 10 mol % of 1a·H and irradiated with a 390 nm LED for 16 h; however, only trace amounts of the desired product were detected (entry 1). Taking advantage of the tunable proton-donating ability governed by the substituent on the nitrogen atom of 1·H, we next employed 1c·H, which was estimated to be more acidic than 1a·H as the catalyst. As anticipated, the reaction proceeded smoothly to afford the THF-coupled isoquinoline 4a (entry 2). These results indicated that the acid–base equilibrium plays a crucial role in the formation of the EDA complex between 1·H and isoquinoline. Although external oxidants are typically required for rearomatization in Minisci reactions, the present system exclusively furnished 4a without any added oxidant, presumably enabled by aerobic oxidation under these reaction conditions. Consistent with this hypothesis, performing the reaction under a N2 atmosphere resulted in only trace amounts of 4a. Subsequent experiments for reaction condition optimization revealed that the combined use of 1c·H and Brønsted acid additives led to the notable enhancement of the reaction efficiency. For example, the addition of 50 mol % of TFA improved the yield to 48%, and the use of TsOH as a cocatalyst further increased the NMR yield of 4a to 61% (entries 3 and 4). On the other hand, catalysts 1d·H–1h·H, bearing either electron-donating or electron-withdrawing substituents on the phenyl group attached to the nitrogen atom of 1c·H, were synthesized and evaluated under TFA cocatalyzed conditions; however, no further improvement in yield was observed (entries 5–9).

We next investigated the substrate scope of the Minisci-type reaction catalyzed by 1·H (Figure ). In addition to THF, substrates bearing hydridic C–H bonds, such as 1,4-dioxane and N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMA), reacted efficiently to afford the corresponding substituted isoquinolines 4b and 4c in moderate yields. Notably, in the reaction using DMA, the use of 1f·H as the catalyst afforded 4c in a slightly higher yield than 1c·H. Further, when 2-methyltetrahydrofuran was used as the substrate, isomer 4d resulting from C–H functionalization at the 5-position was obtained as the major product. This observation suggests that the nitrogen-centered radical corresponding to amidate 1 possesses relatively significant steric hindrance. Benzaldehyde could also be employed as an acyl donor, where radical addition followed by aerobic oxidation led to the formation of diaryl ketone 4e, which distinguishes this system from previously reported photoinduced HAT catalysts. , In the reaction between cyclohexane and isoquinoline, the use of 1c·H resulted in only trace formation of the desired product 4f, whereas employing 1f·H as the catalyst enabled product formation, albeit in a low yield. In terms of the heteroarene scope, a variety of quinolines and isoquinolines bearing different substituents were found to be compatible. For example, the reactions of 8-bromoquinoline, 4,6-dihaloquinoline, and 4-ester-substituted quinoline with DMA afforded the corresponding products 4g–4i. Additionally, isoquinolines bearing a halogen or a phthalimide group at the 4-position underwent smooth coupling with DMA to furnish 4j–4l in moderate to good yields.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we developed a modular anionic boron amidate as a dual-function catalyst that integrates electron-donor and HAT reactivity within a single molecular framework. The catalyst design enables the systematic tuning of the electronic properties and N–H bond dissociation free energy through structural modification, providing a coherent platform for controlling EDA–HAT catalysis.

The catalytic competence of this system was demonstrated in both Giese- and Minisci-type C­(sp3)–H functionalizations. In particular, modulation of the proton-donating ability of the boron amide was crucial for enabling the Minisci reaction, highlighting the modular nature of this catalyst system. Adjustment of the substituent on the nitrogen atom allows tuning of the acid–base properties, thereby facilitating productive EDA complex formation and subsequent photoinduced electron transfer.

These findings highlight the potential of anionic molecular catalysts as versatile platforms for orchestrating multiple elementary steps in photochemical transformations. We anticipate that this design strategy, which combines tunable electronic properties with proton-responsive behavior, will provide a general framework for the development of next-generation EDA-based catalytic systems.

Methods

Representative Procedure for Giese-type Reaction

An oven-dried test tube was charged with 2a (15.6 mg, 0.101 mmol) and boron amidate 1a·NMeBn3 (0.005 mmol, 5 mol %). The test tube was sealed with a rubber septum, and DCE (0.5 mL, 0.2 M) and THF (73.2 mg, 1.02 mmol, 10 equiv) were successively added via syringe. The resulting mixture was stirred under irradiation with either two EvoluChem 425PF (18 W, λmax = 425 nm) (fans are employed to maintain the temperature below 40 °C). After stirring for 24 h, the crude product was passed through a short pad of NH silica (hexane/EtOAc = 1:1) and further purified by column chromatography on silica gel (Merck, hexane/DCM/EtOAc = 9:1:0 to 0:1:1 as eluent) to afford 3a as a colorless oil.

Representative Procedure for Minisci-Type Reaction

An oven-dried test tube was charged with TsOH·H2O (0.05 mmol, 50 mol %), and 1c·H (0.01 mmol, 10 mol %). The test tube was sealed with a rubber septum, and DCM (0.5 mL, 0.2 M), isoquinoline (14.6 mg, 0.113 mmol), and THF (71.3 mg, 0.988 mmol, 9 equiv) were successively added via syringe. The resulting mixture was stirred under irradiation with two Kessil PR160L (40 W, λmax = 390 nm, 25% intensity) (fans are employed to maintain the temperature below 40 °C) under an air atmosphere using an air balloon. After stirring for 24 h, the reaction was quenched with saturated aqueous NaHCO3 and extracted with CHCl3 (2 mL × 3). The combined organic extracts were dried over Na2SO4, filtered, and concentrated in vacuo. The residue was purified by column chromatography on silica gel (Kanto, hexane/EtOAc = 1:0 to 2:3 as eluent) and NH silica gel (hexane/EtOAc = 1:0 to 2:3 as eluent) to afford 4a as a colorless oil.

Machine learning-based model for prediction of KCNJ5 mutation in unilateral primary aldosteronism based on [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT
Authors: Rui Zuo, Zhaoming Chen, Zhengjie Wang, Shenglong Li, Wenbo Li, Zhu Xia, Hua Pang, Lu Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundKCNJ5 mutations enhance aldosterone synthase expression and are closely associated with the prognosis of unilateral primary aldosteronism (UPA). This study developed machine learning-based models with [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT for predicting KCNJ5 mutations in patients with UPA.ResultsMost of the clinical characteristics and all of [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT parameters differed significantly between the KCNJ5-MT and KCNJ5-WT patients. Among these three models based on 40 min LCR, ΔLCR, size, body mass index, creatinine, duration of hypokalemia, serum renin, age, preoperative defined daily dose, the XGBoost model had the highest predictive efficacy in the training group and the AUC was 0.915; the AUC of the AdaBoost and RF was 0.914, 0.911, respectively. The AdaBoost model had the highest predictive efficacy in the test group and the AUC was 0.866; the AUC of the XGBoost and RF was 0.844, 0.859, respectively, however, there was no significant difference in diagnostic performance between the three models. Patients with KCNJ5-MT exhibit different general clinical characteristics to those with the KCNJ5-WT, but there is little difference in the initial surgical outcome assessment.ConclusionsThis machine learning models based on [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT may achieve promising diagnostic efficacy for predicting KCNJ5 mutations. [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT parameters are the main predictors for KCNJ5 mutations, suggests its potential as a noninvasive imaging biomarker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13550-026-01389-w.Keywords: Primary aldosteronism, KCNJ5, [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT, Mutation prediction

Introduction

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is a secondary hypertension characterized by high aldosterone and low renin. Unilateral PA (UPA) is a curable form of PA and classified mainly as aldosterone-producing adenoma (APA) or unilateral adrenal hyperplasia (UAH) [1, 2]. Excess aldosterone in PA patients is associated with endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and fibrosis [3], Moreover, there are direct effects on the heart and kidneys, increasing the risk of target organ damage, including coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, and microalbuminuria, among other conditions [4, 5]. High aldosterone secretion is thought to be related to ion channel disease caused by somatic mutations [6–8]. The KCNJ5 gene is the most common somatic mutation in Eastern Asia, affecting up to 70% of individuals, significantly higher than in Western countries [8]. KCNJ5 mutations are known to activate the G-protein-activated inward rectifier K (+) channel 4 (GIRK4), resulting in continuous cell depolarization and increased calcium concentrations, ultimately increasing the expression of aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2) and excessive secretion of aldosterone. In comparison with patients who possess the wild type of the KCNJ5 gene, patients who possess KCNJ5 mutations have a higher cure rate for hypertension after adrenalectomy [9]. They also exhibit better reshaped left ventricular structure and improved vascular endothelial function and diastolic function [10–12]. The results of preoperative KCNJ5 mutations can facilitate early surgical decision-making among UPA patients who are reluctant to treat their condition actively, thereby enhancing their prognoses.

Currently, the process of KCNJ5 gene sequencing involves the extraction of adrenal tumor tissue, which is a procedure that lacks non-invasive objective evaluation methods. [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor, a ligand imaging agent for chemokine receptor 4, has been demonstrated to reflect the expression of CXCR4 in tissues. Recent studies have shown the efficacy of [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT in facilitating the diagnosis of APA and the subtype of PA. Furthermore, the technology holds significant potential to assist in predicting postoperative outcomes. Aldosterone synthase expression is positively correlated with CXCR4 expression. The molecular mechanism underlying the association between KCNJ5 mutations and elevated aldosterone synthase expression suggests that [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT could serve as a potential imaging biomarker for evaluating the status of the KCNJ5 gene. The purpose of this study was to construct models based on [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT and clinical characteristics for predicting KCNJ5 mutations in patients with UPA, and subsequently to assess the correlation between KCNJ5 mutations and prognosis.

Methods and materials

Patients

Patients with confirmed UPA who underwent unilateral adrenalectomy and [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT were enrolled between December 2021 and December 2024 in the retrospective study. The diagnosis and treatment of PA were determined by clinicians following the 2016 Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline for PA management [2]. The identification and surgical decisions of UPA depends primarily on AVS showing unilateral predominant secretion and/or functional nodules with high [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor uptake. All patients underwent [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT before surgery and obtained KCNJ5 gene sequencing after surgery. The clinical data were obtained through a review of the medical records. The present study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of our hospital (No. K2023-039) and was exempt from informed consent.

[68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT imaging

The synthesis of [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor was conducted following a previous report [13]. The radiochemical purity of [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor exceeded 99%. No special preparation was required for the examines. The imaging agent [⁶⁸Ga]Ga-Pentixafor 111–185 MBq (3–5 mCi) was injected into the median cubital vein, and images of the adrenal region were captured at 10 min and 40 min after the injection with PET/CT scans (Philips Gemini TF64, Philips Medical Systems, USA). The CT images were obtained with parameters set at a voltage of 120 keV, a current of 100 mA, a matrix of 512 × 512, and a layer thickness of 2 mm. Subsequently, PET images were acquired with acquisition parameters of 120 keV, a current of 100 mA, and a layer thickness of 3–5 mm. CT data was used for PET attenuation correction and PET data were reconstructed using the ordered subset expectation maximization algorithm (two iterations and 20 subsets). Maximum intensity projection images and PET/CT fusion images were generated.

Imaging analysis

Adrenal lesions, including nodules or hyperplasia, were regarded as regions of interest (ROI), and the maximum diameter and SUVmax of the lesions were measured. The SUVmean of the contralateral normal adrenal tissue was measured in a circular region of 10 mm. The SUVmean of the posterior segment of the right posterior lobe of the liver was measured in a circular region of 20 mm, and this process needs to avoid abnormal areas, such as cysts or hemangiomas. Two experienced nuclear medicine physicians determined the delineation of all mapped regions, and any disagreements were resolved through consensus. To ensure the reliability of PET features, the same two physicians repeated the measurement after 1 week, and all features with good reproducibility (all ICCs ≥ 0.80). The ratio of lesion SUVmax to contralateral normal adrenal tissue SUVmean (LCR) and the ratio of lesion SUVmax to normal liver SUVmean (LLR) were calculated. We delineated the two time PET images of all patients and calculated the difference (Δ) by subtracting the former from the latter and dividing by the former. After this, the subjects were delineated again one week later, and the average of the two measurements was calculated.

KCNJ5 sequencing

Target tissue samples from the adrenal gland were frozen in liquid nitrogen at − 80 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using a TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (DP304), and the KCNJ5 gene target sequence was amplified using previously reported sequencing primers [14]. 5 µl of PCR amplification products were analyzed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and then subjected to Sanger sequencing. All KCNJ5 sequencing in this study was carried out centrally at the first affiliated hospital of chongqing medical university in accordance with standardized protocols.

Postoperative outcome assessment

Postoperative outcomes were assessed by PASO guidelines from two distinct perspectives: clinical and biochemical [15]. Each of the two outcomes is categorized into three criteria: complete success (remission), partial success (improvement), and absent success (persistence). Following the established guidelines, a preliminary assessment after adrenalectomy of patients with primary aldosteronism was conducted at 6–12 months. The final follow-up date recorded was June 2025. A total of 161 PA patients completed postoperative clinical outcome follow-up, and 105 PA patients completed postoperative biochemical outcome follow-up.

Statistical analysis

All data were processed by SPSS Statistics 26.0 (IBM), GraphPad Prism 9, and R 4.4.0 (www.Rproject.org) statistical software. The R packages used included ‘recipes’, ‘glmnet’, ‘caret’, ‘stats’, ‘ada’,‘randomForest’, ‘xgboost’, and ‘ggplot2’’. The data set was split into training and test groups before any preprocessing or analysis. Categorical variables were converted into dummy variables using one-hot encoding. Continuous variables were type-transformed numerically and processed using Z-score normalisation. Missing data were distributed as follows: Duration of hypertension (2.7%), Duration of hypokalemia (5.6%), and Preoperative defined daily dose (1.1%). No missing values were observed in the remaining variables. Missing values were replaced with medians.

To prevent data leakage and ensure the independence of evaluation, we adopted the following data processing procedure: stratified sampling was used to divide the training group (n = 127) and the test group (n = 53) at a ratio of 7:3. All preprocessing and feature selection steps were strictly and solely performed on the training group. Specifically, Z‑score standardization was first applied using the mean and standard deviation computed from the training group, with these same parameters then applied to the test group. Then, to address class imbalance in the training set, the synthetic minority over-sampling technique method was applied with an over-ratio of 1. This setting was chosen to oversample the minority class to achieve a perfect 1:1 balance with the majority class (mut: wt = 76:76). Based on the random forest (RF) model, we combine SHAP (Share Additive ExPlanations) method to quantify the importance of each feature in training group, that is, to get the SHAP value and rank it. We performed a correlation and significance analysis on the top 15 features in the SHAP analysis using the pearson correlation test. To remove redundant features, we eliminated those with a significantly strong correlation (setting the correlation threshold to 0.8 and the significance threshold to 0.05), finally determining the features used in the final predictive model.

Based on the selected features, three machine learning models are constructed: adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), RF, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). To compare their best performance, we trained the models using five-fold cross-validation and plotted receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. We calculated the area under the curve (AUC), as well as the sensitivity, specificity, negative predictive value, and positive predictive value. The prediction performance of the three models in the training and test groups were analysed using a confusion matrix. The DeLong test and radar plot were used to evaluate the differences in performance of the models in the test group. We used decision curve analysis (DCA) to evaluate the models’ actual clinical value.The workflow for developing and evaluating machine learning-based models in this study in Fig. 1. The completed TRIPOD-AI checklist and reproducible analysis code (with a fixed random seed) have been uploaded to Supplementary data to ensure full reproducibility.

The workflow of machine learning models development and evaluation in this study

Quantitative variables were expressed as Mean ± SD or Median (P25, P75), depending on the Shapiro-Wilk test. Student’s T test or Mann-Whitney test was used to compare the differences between groups. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentages), and the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare the differences between groups. Changes in parameters before and after adrenalectomy were compared using the Paired T test. p < 0.05 (two-tailed) was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics of included patients

The training group (n = 127) comprised 76 KCNJ5 mutants (KCNJ5-MT) and 51 KCNJ5 wild-type (KCNJ5-WT) patients, and the test group (n = 53) included 32 KCNJ5-MT and 21 KCNJ5-WT patients. There was no statistical difference between patients in the training and test groups except for lesion location (61.4% [78/127] vs. 43.4% [23/53], p = 0.046). In the training group, age, prevalence of refractory hypertension, duration of hypokalaemia, plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC), plasma aldosterone concentration (PRC), plasma aldosterone–renin ratio (ARR), pathology, and some [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET parameters has statistically differences between KCNJ5-MT and KCNJ5-WT patients (p < 0.05); and 10 min and 40 min [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET parameters were significantly different (p < 0.001). Age, body mass index (BMI), prevalence of refractory hypertension, prevalence of hypokalaemia, serum potassium, ARR, and some [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET parameters between KCNJ5-MT and KCNJ5-WT patients in the test group (p < 0.05); and the most significant differences were observed in [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET parameters (p < 0.001). Diagnostic performance of the developed models

We screened out the following nine features to develop models, including 40 min LCR, ΔLCR, size, body mass index, creatinine, duration of hypokalemia, serum renin, age, preoperative defined daily dose (Fig. 2). The XGBoost model had the highest predictive efficacy in the training group and the AUC was 0.915; the AUC of the AdaBoost and RF was 0.914, 0.911, respectively. The AdaBoost model had the highest predictive efficacy in the test group and the AUC was 0.866; the AUC of the XGBoost and RF was 0.844, 0.859, respectively (Fig. 3A-B). Then, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of the three models in the training and test group (Fig. 3C-D), the AdaBoost model yielded the largest accuracy of 0.829 in the training group and 0.811 in the test group.

Association between KCNJ5 gene status and surgical outcomes

Statistically significant differences were observed in terms of age, sex, BMI, prevalence of refractory hypertension and hypokalaemia, duration of hypokalaemia, serum potassium, PAC, PRC, ARR, pathology, and all imaging findings between the 108 KCNJ5-MT and 72 KCNJ5-WT patients (p < 0.05). Among these, significant differences were observed in terms of age, pathology, and all [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET parameters (p < 0.001). Supplementary Table 1 shows the clinical features and imaging results for the two groups.

There were statistically significant differences in PAC, ARR, and serum potassium levels before and after surgery in 108 KCNJ5-MT and 72 KCNJ5-WT patients (p < 0.001). DRC was observed to be significantly different between pre- and post-operative KCNJ5-MT patients (p = 0.011), but was not statistically significantly different in KCNJ5-WT patients (p = 0.810; Supplementary Fig. 1). 99 and 69 KCNJ5-MT patients achieved clinical and biochemical outcomes, respectively. Of these patients, only one had clinical persistence, while 98.9% had complete or partial clinical success. Furthermore, 58 patients (84.1%) achieved biochemical remission. 62 and 36 KCNJ5-WT patients achieved clinical and biochemical outcomes, respectively. All patients achieved clinical remission or improvement, and 28 (77.8%) patients achieved complete biochemical remission (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Discussion

In this study, we constructed three different machine learning classification models based on nine independent clinical and imaging parameters to determine KCNJ5 mutations. To our knowledge, this is the first study to predict KCNJ5 mutations based on [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT and to construct prediction models, where 40 min LCR in [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT was found to be the main factor in predicting KCNJ5 mutations.

[68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT is valuable in the diagnosis of aldosterone-producing adenomas and PA typing and treatment [16–18]. KCNJ5 mutation mediates upregulation of CYP11B2 expression, and CYP11B2 expression is significantly positively correlated with CXCR4 expression [16, 19], [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor uptake can reflect KCNJ5 gene status. Recently, our team’s research has found that patients with KCNJ5 mutations have higher [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor uptake. Based on 40 min LCR, we were able to distinguish KCNJ5-MT from KCNJ5-WT [20]. In this study, we further increased the sample size and, based on clinical and imaging parameters, built KCNJ5 prediction models. We also found that the 40 min LCR was an important predictor in determining the KCNJ5 mutations. Previous study have found that patients with KCNJ5 mutations have higher NP-59 uptake, and it was concluded that NP-59 SPECT could be used to predict KCNJ5 mutation [21]. NP-59 and [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor are two imaging agents that indirectly reflect KCNJ5 gene status by reacting with key ingredients in aldosterone synthesis and aldosterone synthase-related chemoattractant factors, respectively. This demonstrates the important value of molecular functional imaging in predicting KCNJ5 mutations, although it has not received enough attention in clinical practice.

KCNJ5 gene mutations are key to assessing the long-term prognosis of patients with PA, and studies have shown that the development of diabetes and endothelial dysfunction are also influenced by KCNJ5 regulation [10, 11, 22–24]. Understanding the KCNJ5 gene status of patients with PA is beneficial for clinical patient management and personalised treatment decisions. Currently, KCNJ5 mutations require sequencing of adrenal tissue, which is an invasive procedure that is not suitable for all patients. A previous study [25] has constructed a KCNJ5 mutation prediction model based on 87 clinical features and laboratory parameters of 328 UPA patients, achieving a maximum AUC of 0.905. The reduced model has a maximum AUC of 0.884 and still consists of 27 features. While the model composed of multiple features offers better predictive power, the inclusion of excessive features increases the risk of overfitting. In this study, we constructed the three models based on three [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT semi-quantitative parameters and six basic clinical features and obtained a maximum AUC of 0.915. Compared to previous study [25], this is considerably better and avoids the limitation that multi-feature models tend to be difficult to apply in clinical practice. Not only is [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT conducive to diagnosing and subtyping PA, it also indirectly enhances the prognostic value by predicting KCNJ5 gene.

Many published studies have shown that KCNJ5 mutations are more prevalent in women and that hypokalaemia is more severe [10, 22, 26]. Similar findings emerged in this study, but these parameters were not included in the final model construction. Our results suggest that prolonged hypokalaemia is a better predictor of KCNJ5 mutations than the severity of hypokalaemia, and that size, body mass index, creatinine, serum renin, age, preoperative defined daily dose are also predictors of KCNJ5 mutations. Those clinical characteristics associated with PET imaging parameters maximises the predictive value.

KCNJ5 mutation can regulate aldosterone synthase, causing high aldosterone secretion and directly or indirectly damaging cardiac structure and function, thereby leading to more cardiac adverse events [11, 12, 22]. In this study, we initially evaluated the surgical outcomes for patients with PA, finding that those with KCNJ5 mutations had a higher complete biochemical response rate and could also achieve greater clinical benefits, although significant differences are absent. Other studies [24, 27] have shown that long-term prognosis assessment is significantly associated with KCNJ5 gene status. KCNJ5 mutations reveal the important pathogenesis of PA, “ion channel abnormalities”, and provide a target for developing new therapies, enabling precise guidance of individualised treatment for PA. Our model provides an objective and practical means of non-invasively predicting KCNJ5.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size from a single centre is small. Secondly, as this is a retrospective study, it mainly includes patients with surgically treated UPA and typical adenomas, which may limit the application of this model to a wide range of patients with PA. This is because patients with bilateral disease who are ineligible for surgery may differ systematically from the surgical cohort in terms of aldosterone secretion levels, cardiac and renal target organ damage, and underlying molecular biological characteristics [5, 28]; moreover, the findings of this study have not yet been validated in patients with bilateral PA. Thirdly, this study only predicted KCNJ5 mutations and did not analyse different site mutations. In future research, we plan to collaborate with multiple institutions and collect prospective data to build independent and diverse patient cohorts for testing the model’s calibration and discrimination. Additionally, The initial follow-up was primarily conducted within 6–12 months postoperatively, with no data collected on longer-term outcomes. To obtain a more comprehensive assessment of therapeutic efficacy, the follow-up duration will be extended in future studies.

This study is the first to construct predictive models of KCNJ5 mutations using [68Ga]Ga-Pentixafor PET/CT functional imaging in combination with clinical features. This provides key information for clinical practice and could potentially become a promising non-invasive tool for assessing KCNJ5 mutations and guiding treatment decisions. Notably, the model has not yet undergone external validation, and future study will involve validating its generalizability using multi-center data.

Molecular PET imaging of integrin αvβ6 reveals biliary and fibrotic changes in preclinical MASH
Authors: Xiaoyi Wang, Mengqi Wang, Pengzhe Han, Huanhuan Song, Xuchen Zhao, Nannan Chen, Congzhong Hu, Xueqin Liu, Xiaolong Li, Xifeng Fu, Steven Liang, Zhifang Wu, Yanzhang Tian, Tuo Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe progression of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) involves complex crosstalk among hepatocellular injury, inflammation, and ductular reaction—a process characterized by biliary epithelial proliferation and fibrotic activation. Integrin αvβ6, an epithelial-specific receptor largely absent in normal liver but induced under chronic injury, plays a pivotal role in activating latent TGF-β and driving fibrogenesis. Here, we investigated αvβ6 as a molecular imaging biomarker in MASH using a PET-based strategy to non-invasively visualize epithelial fibrotic signaling in vivo.MethodsPET/CT imaging and autoradiography studies were performed in MASH mice at different disease stages using [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG, a radiotracer targeting integrin αvβ6. Tracer uptake was correlated with histopathological changes, immunofluorescent localization of αvβ6, and transcriptional markers of fibrosis, inflammation, and biliary expansion. Human liver biopsy specimens from MASH patients were analyzed as preliminary ex vivo evidence to explore the translational relevance of αvβ6 expression and tracer binding in human fibrotic liver tissue.ResultsIn both CDAHFD and MCDD models, hepatic [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG uptake increased progressively with MASH-associated fibrosis. αvβ6 signals were mainly localized to periportal and ductular regions, where they co-localized with expanding CK19+ biliary epithelial cells. Increased tracer uptake and αvβ6 expression were associated with collagen deposition and transcriptional activation of fibrogenic markers, including TGF-β1, α-SMA/Acta2, Col1a1, Vim, and Timp1. In human MASH liver tissue sections, αvβ6 expression and autoradiographic signals also increased with fibrosis stage, supporting the translational relevance of αvβ6-targeted imaging for fibrotic remodeling.ConclusionsThis study identifies integrin αvβ6-targeted PET as a potential molecular imaging strategy to map epithelial remodeling in MASH. Ex vivo analyses of human MASH specimens support the translational relevance of this target, although prospective clinical PET studies and longitudinal outcome validation are required before clinical diagnostic application.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40364-026-00965-5.Keywords: Integrin αvβ6, PET imaging, MASH, Liver fibrosis, Biliary epithelial cells, Diagnosis

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is a complex and progressive liver disorder characterized by hepatic steatosis, inflammation, hepatocellular ballooning, and varying degrees of fibrosis, which can further progress to irreversible cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma [1, 2]. The global prevalence of MASH has been increasing in parallel with the rise in metabolic diseases such as obesity, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia [3, 4]. Despite its clinical relevance, MASH remains challenging to diagnose and monitor due to the lack of effective non-invasive tools that can assess the dynamic changes occurring in the liver during disease progression.

The pathogenesis of MASH is driven by a combination of metabolic stress, inflammatory responses, and fibrogenic reactions that involve both hepatocytes and non-parenchymal liver cells [5]. One of the key features distinguishing MASH from simple steatosis is the activation of biliary epithelial cells (BECs), which undergo proliferation and contribute to the fibrogenic process through the release of extracellular matrix (ECM) components and fibrogenic cytokines such as transforming growth factor- β (TGF-β) [6–9]. This process, known as the ductular reaction, is critical for the progression of fibrosis and the loss of liver function in MASH.

Integrin αvβ6, a transmembrane receptor, plays a central role in the fibrotic ductular reaction [10]. It is normally expressed at low levels in the liver, but its expression is markedly upregulated in BECs during fibrosis. αvβ6 interacts with ECM ligands to activate TGF-β from its latent form [11, 12]. This activation promotes the recruitment and activation of hepatic stellate cells, which are the primary effectors of liver fibrosis [13, 14]. The upregulation of αvβ6 in the context of MASH fibrosis makes it a promising target for molecular imaging.

Current methods for assessing liver fibrosis, including serum biomarkers and non-invasive imaging techniques [15], are often insensitive to early stages of fibrosis and unable to distinguish ongoing fibrogenesis from established scarring [16–18]. Liver biopsy remains the gold standard, but its invasive nature and sampling variability preclude its use for routine clinical monitoring [19, 20]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for non-invasive imaging techniques that can accurately capture the molecular events driving MASH progression.

With advances in medical technology, imaging is beginning to play an integral role in the evaluation of the fibrotic response. Positron emission tomography (PET) has emerged as a powerful tool for in vivo imaging of disease-related molecular targets [21, 22]. Recent advances in integrin-targeted PET imaging have demonstrated its potential in a variety of fibrotic diseases, including pulmonary fibrosis, with promising results in both preclinical and clinical settings [23–26]. Cystine knot peptides, such as the R01-MG peptide, have shown particular promise for PET imaging due to their high stability, selectivity for αvβ6, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties [27, 28]. When labeled with the positron-emitting isotope gallium-68 ([68Ga]), these peptides can be used to non-invasively visualize αvβ6 expression in fibrotic tissues, offering a unique approach for monitoring the progression of MASH and assessing therapeutic interventions.

In this study, we investigated the use of the αvβ6-targeted PET tracer [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG in two preclinical models of MASH: the choline-deficient, L-amino acid-defined, high-fat diet (CDAHFD) and the methionine-choline-deficient diet (MCDD). We aimed to assess the feasibility of PET imaging for monitoring αvβ6 expression in biliary epithelial cells, evaluate its correlation with disease severity, and evaluate its potential as a non-invasive biomarker for tracking disease progression in MASH.

Materials and methods

Animal care and establishment of MASH mouse models

Male C57BL/6 mice aged 8–12 weeks (body weight, 22–25 g) were used. All animals were housed in a pathogen-free, temperature-controlled facility under a 12-h light/dark cycle. This study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Soochow University and was performed in accordance with its guidelines. Two diet-induced mouse models of MASH, CDAHFD and MCDD, were established. After a 1-week acclimatization period, mice in the CDAHFD groups were fed a choline-deficient, L-amino acid-defined, high-fat diet (60% kcal from fat, 0.1% methionine) and were euthanized after 3, 6, or 9 weeks of feeding. Mice in the MCDD groups were fed a methionine- and choline-deficient diet and were euthanized after 3, 5, or 8 weeks of feeding. Control mice were fed an isocaloric standard maintenance diet.

Liver perfusion and primary hepatocyte isolation

Primary hepatocytes, biliary epithelial cells, macrophages, and hepatic stellate cells were isolated from CDAHFD and MCDD mice and cultured. Briefly, a midline laparotomy was performed to expose the liver, and HBSS was perfused through the portal vein. The liver was enzymatically digested with Collagenase B (Roche), Pronase (Sigma), and DNase I (Roche). The digested cell suspension was centrifuged at 50 × g for 3 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected in a new tube. Live Cell Purification Solution (RWD) was added to the pellet, followed by centrifugation to collect hepatocytes. Subsequently, the collected supernatant was centrifuged at 500 × g for 7 min to isolate biliary epithelial cells, macrophages, and hepatic stellate cells. Primary cells were cultured in 6-well plates in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 0.5 mM penicillin-streptomycin. The purity of the isolated cell populations was estimated by calculating the proportion of lineage marker-positive cells relative to the total number of DAPI-positive cells.

Histological analysis and quantification of liver tissue

Liver tissues were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for 48 h. Paraffin-embedded tissues were cut into 5-μm sections for histological analysis. For hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin Y solution (Sigma) and mounted with Pertex. For Sirius red staining, sections were stained with Sirius red solution (Phygene), washed twice in acidified water, counterstained with hematoxylin to identify nuclei, and mounted with Pertex. Histological evaluations were performed under blinded conditions. Microscopic examination was performed using a light microscope to assess the severity of hepatic fibrosis and liver tissue architecture.

Serum measurements

Blood samples were collected from CDAHFD-, MCDD-, and chow diet-treated mice and allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 minutes. The samples were then centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4 °C and the supernatant was collected in a 1.5 ml EP tube. The measurements were performed using a Hitachi 7100 automated biochemistry analyzer with a 200 μl serum sample volume. The biochemical parameters included alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBIL), alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and albumin (ALB).

Immunofluorescent staining

Paraffin-embedded liver sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval. For cell samples, cells cultured for 3 days were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA). After washing with PBS, liver sections and cells were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 (Sigma) for 10 min and blocked with blocking buffer (Beyotime Biotech Inc.) for 45 min. The samples were then incubated with the indicated primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The primary antibodies used included anti-integrin αvβ6 (Thermo Fisher Scientific), anti-CK19 (Abcam), anti-α-SMA (Cell Signaling Technology), anti-F4/80 (Abcam), anti-albumin (Proteintech), and anti-TGF-β1 (Bioss). After rinsing with PBS, the samples were incubated with secondary antibodies (Proteintech) for 30–60 min at room temperature. Finally, nuclei were stained with antifade mounting medium containing 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). Images were acquired using a Leica high-resolution confocal microscope.

RNA analysis

Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues using the FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme). RNA concentration and purity were determined using a multimode reader. cDNA was synthesized from 0.9 μg of total RNA using the SuperScript First-Strand Synthesis System (Invitrogen). Real-time PCR was performed using Hieff UNICON Universal Blue qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA was extracted independently from each biological sample. qPCR analyses were performed using five independent liver samples per group for animal experiments and three independent human liver samples per fibrosis stage. All values were normalized to GAPDH and analyzed using the comparative ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences are provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from frozen liver tissue samples stored at − 80 °C. The proteins were incubated with loading buffer for 8 min at 95 °C and subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis using a 12.5% gel (EpiZyme). The proteins were then transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies diluted in blocking buffer. The primary antibodies used included anti-integrin αv (Abcam), anti-integrin β6 (Abcam), and anti-β-actin (Proteintech). The next day, blots were washed three times with TBST and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-rabbit antibody for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein bands were detected using ECL solution. Images were captured with an Amersham Imager 600, and the immunoreactive bands were analyzed using ImageJ software.

PET/CT imaging and data analysis

Mice were induced with 5% isoflurane and maintained under 1–2% isoflurane in oxygen throughout the experimental procedure. For image acquisition, 3.7 MBq of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG radiotracer was intravenously injected over 30 s, and a static emission scan was acquired 30 min after injection. All PET images were acquired with an Inviscan imaging system in three-dimensional acquisition mode. PET image reconstruction was performed using the row-action maximum-likelihood algorithm (RAMLA) based on lines of response (LORs). Low-dose CT scans were acquired for attenuation correction and anatomical localization. For quantitative analysis, hepatic uptake was determined by manually outlining regions of interest (ROIs). All image processing and quantitative analyses were performed using Inveon Research Workplace 3.0 (Siemens Medical Solutions).

[68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG autoradiography

Frozen sections of mouse and human liver tissues were cut at a thickness of 20 μm. The sections were pre-incubated in ice-cold Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) for 20 min and then incubated with [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at room temperature. After incubation for 1 hour, the sections were washed with Tris-HCl buffer to remove unbound radiotracer and then exposed to imaging film (GE Healthcare) for 6 hours. The imaging films were scanned using an image plate scanner (DÜRR MEDICAL CR 35 BIO). The scanned images were saved in an uncompressed format at full resolution and pixel depth. Data were analyzed using ImageJ and ImageQuant software.

Human tissue samples

Liver specimens were obtained from 12 patients diagnosed with MASH and 4 healthy individuals at Shanxi Bethune Hospital. The collection and use of human tissue samples were approved by the local Institutional Review Board. Detailed patient information is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 8. Results are presented as the mean ± SEM. Statistical significance between two groups was assessed using Student’s t-test, whereas comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001).

Results

Histological and biochemical evaluation confirms progressive liver injury and fibrosis in CDAHFD and MCDD MASH models

To validate the establishment of MASH-associated liver injury and fibrosis, histological and biochemical analyses were performed in both CDAHFD and MCDD dietary models at different disease stages. H&E staining revealed progressive hepatocellular ballooning, inflammatory infiltration, and disrupted hepatic architecture in both models, with increasing severity from mild to severe stages (Fig. 1A). Sirius Red staining demonstrated a marked, stage-dependent increase in collagen deposition in both models (Fig. 1B). Quantitative image analysis further confirmed these histological findings (Fig. 1C).

(B) Representative Sirius Red staining images showing collagen deposition in liver sections from CDAHFD and MCDD mice. (C) Quantitative analysis of Sirius Red-positive collagen area. (D) Changes in serum ALT and AST levels as indicators of liver injury. (E) Changes in the levels of the cholestatic markers TBIL and ALP and the hepatic synthesis marker ALB. Scale bars = 100 μm. n = 3. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001

Serum ALT and AST levels were markedly elevated in fibrotic mice and increased in parallel with histological severity in the CDAHFD model. In the MCDD model, ALT and AST levels peaked at the moderate stage and declined at the severe stage, possibly due to extensive necrosis and fibrotic replacement of the liver parenchyma (Fig. 1D). As the disease progressed, TBIL levels were also significantly elevated in both CDAHFD and MCDD mice (Fig. 1E). ALP and ALB levels did not vary significantly across disease stages, suggesting selective biochemical sensitivity to fibrotic injury. Collectively, these data confirm that both dietary models recapitulate key features of MASH pathology, including progressive hepatocellular injury, fibrosis, and altered liver function markers.

Integrin αvβ6 is upregulated with fibrosis progression and spatially associated with TGF-β1 in MASH

To determine the spatial and molecular association of integrin αvβ6 with fibrogenic signaling in MASH, we assessed its expression alongside TGF-β1 across different disease stages. Western blot analysis revealed a progressive increase in integrin β6 and αv subunits, consistent with fibrosis severity (Fig. 2A–D). These changes were corroborated at the mRNA level, where Itgb6, Itgav, Tgfb1, Tgfb2, and key fibrosis-associated genes (Acta2, Vim, Timp1, Col1a1) were markedly elevated in a stage-dependent manner (Fig. 2E).

(A, C) Western blot analysis of integrin β6 and αv expression at different stages of liver fibrosis in CDAHFD and MCDD mice. (B, D) Quantitative analysis of Western blot data for integrin β6 (left) and integrin αv (right). (E) mRNA expression levels of Itgb6, Itgav, and fibrosis markers, including Tgfb1, Tgfb2, Acta2, Vim, Timp1, and Col1a1. (F, G) Immunofluorescence co-staining analysis of integrin αvβ6 and TGF-β1 at different stages of liver fibrosis. n = 4–5. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001

To further define the anatomical localization of αvβ6 expression and its relationship with fibrogenic signaling, immunofluorescence staining was performed. In both models, αvβ6 was predominantly expressed in periportal regions and showed strong spatial overlap with TGF-β1, particularly in moderate and severe MASH stages (Fig. 2F–G). This co-localization suggests that αvβ6-expressing BECs may serve as key mediators of local TGF-β activation and fibrotic remodeling. Together, these findings demonstrate that αvβ6 upregulation not only reflects disease severity but also aligns with fibrogenic signaling domains, reinforcing its potential as a spatially resolved molecular imaging target in MASH.

Integrin αvβ6 is primarily localized to biliary epithelial cells and fibrotic niches in MASH models

To define the cellular source and spatial localization of integrin αvβ6 expression in MASH livers, we performed immunofluorescence staining in CDAHFD and MCDD models. Co-staining of integrin αvβ6 with the cholangiocyte marker CK19 revealed strong co-localization, indicating that αvβ6 was predominantly localized to biliary epithelial cells (BECs) in fibrotic livers (Fig. 3A). In contrast, αvβ6 showed little overlap with albumin-positive hepatocytes (Fig. 3B), suggesting minimal expression in parenchymal cells.

(A) Immunofluorescence staining showed strong co-localization of integrin αvβ6 (green) with the biliary epithelial cell marker CK19 (magenta) in the CDAHFD and MCDD models. (B–D) immunofluorescence staining showed little overlap of integrin αvβ6 (green) with the hepatocyte marker albumin (magenta), the activated hepatic stellate cell/myofibroblast marker α-SMA (magenta), or the kupffer cell/macrophage marker F4/80 (magenta). Staining for α-SMA revealed close spatial proximity between αvβ6-expressing ductular structures and activated myofibroblasts, highlighting their potential interaction within the fibrotic microenvironment (Fig. 3C). Additionally, integrin αvβ6 was found adjacent to F4/80-positive macrophages, particularly in fibrotic regions, suggesting a possible role in epithelial–immune crosstalk during MASH progression (Fig. 3D). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that αvβ6 is enriched in biliary and fibrotic niches and may contribute to epithelial–mesenchymal and epithelial–immune interactions during liver fibrosis.

Integrin αvβ6 is predominantly localized to biliary epithelial cells in primary cultures from MASH models

To investigate the cellular localization of integrin αvβ6 in primary liver cells isolated from CDAHFD and MCDD models, we performed immunofluorescence staining. The isolated cell populations were first evaluated using lineage markers to confirm their purity (Figure S1). Co-staining with the biliary epithelial marker CK19 revealed that integrin αvβ6 was predominantly expressed in biliary epithelial cells, with strong expression observed in both the CDAHFD and MCDD models (Fig. 4A). Little expression of αvβ6 was detected in albumin-positive hepatocytes, α-SMA-positive activated myofibroblasts, and macrophages identified by F4/80 staining (Fig. 4B–D). These findings indicate that integrin αvβ6 is specifically expressed in BECs in the context of MASH, and not in other hepatic cell types.

PET imaging demonstrates progressive upregulation of integrin αvβ6 with disease severity in MASH Models

To evaluate changes in integrin αvβ6 expression during MASH progression, we performed PET/CT imaging at different disease stages. PET images clearly showed a gradual increase in hepatic signal intensity from the mild to severe stages of MASH. In the CDAHFD model, the mild stage exhibited low signal intensity, whereas the signal markedly increased at the moderate and severe stages (Fig. 5A, Figure S2A). A similar trend was observed in the MCDD model (Fig. 5B, Figure S2B). These imaging findings were further supported by quantitative analysis of standardized uptake values (SUVs), which gradually increased with MASH severity (Fig. 5C, D). These data indicate that hepatic integrin αvβ6-associated PET signal increases with MASH progression, highlighting its potential as a molecular marker of fibrosis progression. These findings support the potential utility of PET imaging for quantitatively monitoring integrin αvβ6 expression in MASH.

Ex vivo autoradiography validates the association between [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG signal and integrin αvβ6 expression in MASH models

To validate the expression of integrin αvβ6 and assess the association between [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG binding and integrin αvβ6 expression, ex vivo autoradiography (ARG) was performed on liver sections from CDAHFD and MCDD models at various disease stages. ARG showed a marked increase in radioactivity in regions corresponding to fibrotic areas, with the strongest signal observed in the severe stages of MASH. In parallel, immunofluorescence staining for integrin αvβ6 showed increased αvβ6 expression in biliary epithelial cells (BECs) at the moderate and severe stages (Fig. 6A, B).

(A, B) In the CDAHFD and MCDD models, ARG images showed progressive accumulation of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG from mild to severe fibrosis. In parallel, the integrin αvβ6 fluorescence signal (green), which co-localized with CK19 (magenta), progressively increased. (C, D) Quantitative analysis of ARG radioactivity intensity, integrin αvβ6 fluorescence intensity, and the correlation between the two signals in liver sections from MASH mice. Scale bars = 50 μm. n = 3–4. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001

These findings were further supported by a significant correlation between ARG intensity and integrin αvβ6 fluorescence intensity (Fig. 6C, D). Quantitative analysis revealed that both ARG signal intensity and integrin αvβ6 fluorescence intensity increased progressively, with the highest levels observed in severely fibrotic regions. Statistical analysis confirmed a strong positive correlation between the αvβ6-positive area fraction and ARG signal intensity (R2 = 0.8452 for CDAHFD and R2 = 0.9585 for MCDD), reinforcing the association between integrin αvβ6 expression and fibrosis severity in MASH.

Fibrosis progression and integrin αvβ6 expression in human MASH liver samples

To examine the relationship between MASH progression and integrin αvβ6 expression in human liver samples, we evaluated histological and molecular changes in liver tissues stratified from F0 to F3 based on the METAVIR scoring system. H&E staining revealed progressive hepatocellular damage and architectural distortion from F0 to F3, while Sirius Red staining demonstrated an increasing extent of collagen deposition as fibrosis advanced (Fig. 7A). The percentage of Sirius Red-stained area significantly increased from F0 to F3, in parallel with fibrosis severity (Fig. 7B).

(C) Western blot analysis of integrin αv and integrin β6 expression at different fibrosis stages in patients with MASH. (D) Quantitative analysis of integrin β6 and αv protein levels. Western blot analysis of integrin β6 and αv expression showed progressive upregulation of both subunits, with significantly higher levels in F2 and F3 stages than in F0 (Fig. 7C, D). This upregulation was further confirmed at the mRNA level, where ITGB6 and ITGAV showed a similar trend, with significantly higher expression in F2 and F3 stages (Fig. 7E). In addition, key fibrogenic markers, including TGFB1, TGFB2, COL1A1, ACTA2, VIM, and TIMP1, were significantly upregulated in the later stages, particularly F2 and F3, further supporting the association between αvβ6 expression and fibrotic progression (Fig. 7E).

Ex vivo autoradiography and immunofluorescence validate integrin αvβ6 expression in human liver fibrosis stages

To further explore the clinical translational potential of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG for monitoring liver fibrosis, we performed ARG and immunofluorescence staining for integrin αvβ6 and CK19 in human liver tissue sections spanning fibrosis stages F0 to F3. ARG imaging showed a progressive increase in signal intensity, with the strongest signal detected in F3 samples, indicating severe fibrosis (Fig. 8A, B). Immunofluorescence staining confirmed that integrin αvβ6 was primarily expressed in biliary epithelial cells, as indicated by CK19 co-staining, and that its expression increased significantly with fibrosis progression from F1 to F3 (Fig. 8A, C). Quantitative analysis revealed a substantial rise in both ARG signal intensity and integrin αvβ6 fluorescence intensity with increasing fibrosis stage (Fig. 8B, C). Moreover, a strong positive correlation was observed between the αvβ6-positive area fraction and ARG signal intensity (R2 = 0.8862), supporting ARG signal intensity as a surrogate readout of αvβ6 expression in fibrotic liver tissue (Fig. 8D).

Discussion

This study systematically examined the role of integrin αvβ6 in MASH progression and evaluated the translational potential of the [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG PET tracer for non-invasive imaging of liver fibrosis. Fibrosis progression in MASH is closely linked to the activation of biliary epithelial cells (BECs) and the ductular reaction, which plays a central role in liver fibrogenesis [29]. In the current study, we observed significant upregulation of integrin αvβ6 in activated BECs in both CDAHFD and MCDD models, whereas little or no expression was detected in hepatocytes, macrophages, or hepatic stellate cells. This finding is consistent with previous studies showing that αvβ6 is an important epithelial marker induced during liver injury and fibrosis [30–32]. We also found a robust association between αvβ6 upregulation and fibrosis severity, accompanied by increased expression of TGF-β1/2, α-SMA, COL1A1, and TIMP1, as well as increased collagen deposition detected by Sirius Red staining. Importantly, similar changes were observed in human MASH liver tissues. These results suggest that integrin αvβ6 may contribute to the fibrotic response by promoting TGF-β signaling activation and extracellular matrix remodeling during liver fibrogenesis [33, 34].

Given the need for early and accurate diagnosis of liver fibrosis, we used [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG PET imaging to achieve αvβ6-targeted molecular imaging in vivo. [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG has been reported to show low background uptake in the liver and favorable αvβ6-targeting properties, which may help distinguish fibrotic lesions from non-specific inflammatory uptake [32, 35]. In our study, [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG demonstrated favorable imaging performance in both animal models and human liver tissue samples. ARG and PET/CT imaging revealed a progressive increase in signal intensity corresponding to fibrosis severity. In particular, human F3 fibrosis samples showed the most prominent ARG signal. Importantly, ARG signal intensity in liver sections from MASH models showed a strong positive correlation with the fluorescence intensity of integrin αvβ6, supporting [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG as an effective imaging probe for visualizing αvβ6 expression in fibrotic liver tissue [32]. These findings support the translational potential of αvβ6-targeted PET for future clinical monitoring of liver fibrosis and evaluation of therapeutic interventions. Clinically, [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG would be expected to serve as a diagnostic PET tracer, with limited biological effects at tracer-level doses. Potential risks mainly include radiation exposure, injection-related reactions, and rare hypersensitivity. Although related αvβ6-targeted cystine-knot tracers have shown acceptable safety in early clinical studies [25], dedicated safety and dosimetry studies of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG are still required.

This study also provides important insights into the stage-dependent changes in αvβ6 expression during MASH progression. The positive association between αvβ6 expression and fibrosis stage supports [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG as a potential non-invasive imaging tool for fibrosis staging [36, 37]. Compared with conventional imaging approaches, αvβ6-targeted PET may offer molecular information that helps differentiate early and advanced fibrosis. Our results showed that hepatic tracer uptake and integrin αvβ6 expression increased during mild fibrosis and continued to rise as fibrosis progressed, peaking in severe fibrosis. This stage-dependent pattern suggests that αvβ6-targeted PET imaging may be sensitive to early epithelial activation and ductular remodeling during fibrogenesis, although its early detection capability remains to be confirmed through functional validation and prospective clinical studies. In addition, the strong positive association between αvβ6 expression and traditional fibrosis markers, such as α-SMA and collagen, further supports the involvement of integrin αvβ6 in fibrogenic signaling and liver tissue remodeling [38, 39]. These findings suggest that αvβ6-targeted PET may provide a molecular imaging approach for visualizing ductular reaction-associated fibrotic remodeling in preclinical MASH. Further functional validation and prospective clinical studies are required to determine whether this strategy can improve early diagnosis or therapeutic monitoring in patients. Notably, compared with serum marker assays, [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG PET provided more direct spatial information for monitoring liver fibrosis. In our study, serum ALT and AST levels correlated with fibrosis severity in the CDAHFD model but not consistently in the MCDD model, and their levels declined at the severe stage despite marked fibrosis [19].

This study has several limitations. First, CDAHFD and MCDD models differ in metabolic phenotype, injury pattern, and fibrotic distribution. These differences may influence αvβ6 expression patterns and tracer uptake, thereby limiting direct extrapolation to the heterogeneous spectrum of human MASH. Second, this study did not include direct comparisons with established non-invasive fibrosis assessment tools, such as elastography, MRI-based methods, or alternative PET tracers. Therefore, future head-to-head studies are needed to define the added value of αvβ6-targeted PET in MASH fibrosis assessment. Third, several experimental analyses were performed with modest sample sizes, including animal imaging studies and human ex vivo tissue validation. Although the results showed consistent trends across models and assays, these findings should be considered exploratory and require validation in larger preclinical and clinical cohorts. In addition, the human component of this study was based on ex vivo liver specimens rather than in vivo PET imaging. Therefore, the clinical diagnostic accuracy, longitudinal value, and prognostic relevance of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG PET in patients with MASH remain to be established in prospective clinical studies with larger cohorts and outcome-based follow-up. The optimal clinical application stage for αvβ6-targeted PET also remains unclear, and future studies should determine whether it is most useful for early risk stratification, detection of significant fibrosis, or treatment monitoring.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study highlights the important role of integrin αvβ6 in MASH-related liver fibrosis and demonstrates the potential of [68Ga] DOTA-R01-MG PET imaging as a non-invasive tool for monitoring fibrosis progression. These findings lay the groundwork for the clinical translation of αvβ6-targeted PET imaging and provide a promising molecular imaging approach for future fibrosis staging, disease monitoring, and evaluation of therapeutic interventions aimed at fibrosis regression in MASH.

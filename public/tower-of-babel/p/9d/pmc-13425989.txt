Pooled survival and cost-effectiveness analysis of immune checkpoint inhibitors-based for metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer with PD-L1 lower than 1%
Authors: Kun Liu, Youwen Zhu, Hong Zhu, Manting Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to assess whether immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)-based treatments offer significant survival benefits and economic viability for patients presenting with PD-L1 lower than 1%.MethodsThis exploratory analysis was based on individual patient data from randomized controlled trials (CheckMate-227, CheckMate-9LA, KEYNOTE-189, and KEYNOTE-407). Survival outcomes evaluated for this study were overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Economic evaluations estimated total lifetime costs, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs), and incremental net-health benefit (INHB) using the Markov model. The willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds were $150,000/quality-adjusted life-year (QALY) for the USA and $36,942/QALY for China.ResultsAmong patients receiving immunotherapy-based treatments (N = 754) relative to those treated with chemotherapy-alone (N = 502), survival outcomes were significantly improved (median OS: 17.0 vs. 12.0 months; hazard ratio [HR]: 0.69; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.61–0.78; median PFS: 6.5 vs. 5.5 months; HR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.60–0.77). The cost-effectiveness analysis found that the ICERs with INHBs for immunotherapy-based versus chemotherapy-alone were $273,440.73/QALY with -1.00 QALYs in the USA and $32,783.93/QALY with 0.89 QALYs in China. Crucially, among ICI-based regimens, nivolumab plus ipilimumab-based (INHB: 0.09 QALYs) and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (INHB: 0.17 QALYs) were the most cost-effective options in the USA and China, respectively.ConclusionsFirst-line immunotherapies-based therapy was cost-effective in metastatic NSCLC with PD-1 lower than 1% in China but not in the USA. Nivolumab plus ipilimumab-based was identified as the most favorable first-line immunotherapy in the USA, while pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was preferred in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13561-026-00797-x.

Introduction

Lung cancer represents the most commonly diagnosed malignancy worldwide and is associated with high mortality. China and the USA collectively account for 43% and 9% of global incidence, respectively [1, 2]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), representing 80% of cases, is frequently diagnosed when the disease is advanced or metastatic. Real-world data indicate that over 48% of cases with advanced or metastatic NSCLC have PD-L1 expression of lower than 1% [3–6]. Unfortunately, these patients generally experience poor long-term survival outcomes and limited treatment efficacy, making them a population with significant unmet clinical needs [7]. Consequently, PD-L1 assessment has become a routine component of treatment decision-making, and patients with PD-L1 expression below 1% require particular attention in both experimental and clinical settings.

Previous studies indicate that chemotherapy is the predominant first-line option for treating over 60% of those with metastatic NSCLC and PD-L1 expression below 1%, whereas only approximately 25% receive ICI-based therapies [5]. Similar trends have been observed in other studies, with chemotherapy-alone treatment rates of 32% compared to 28% for ICI-based regimens [6]. Despite the increasing number of untreated NSCLC cases with advanced or metastatic disease and PD-L1 levels below 1% opting for chemotherapy, immunotherapy has emerged as a promising alternative based on current evidence and treatment guidelines. ICIs, in particular, have shown potential as transformative therapies for this patient population.

Recent work highlights the long-term survival benefits of first-line ICI-based interventions in NSCLC, including those with PD-L1 expression below 1%. Several phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs)—CheckMate-227 (NCT02477826), CheckMate-9LA (NCT03215706), and KEYNOTE-189 (NCT02578680)—have demonstrated that nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy significantly improved OS and PFS in both nonsquamous and overall metastatic NSCLC populations, without accounting for PD-L1 expression levels, when compared to chemotherapy alone over a five-year follow-up period [8–10]. Conversely, findings from phase III CheckMate-227 and KEYNOTE-407 (NCT02775435) trials suggest that while nivolumab plus chemotherapy and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy prolonged PFS, their impact on OS in squamous metastatic NSCLC with PD-L1 lower than 1% was less pronounced [8, 11]. Based on the optimistic and positive survival outcomes of these RCTs, two long-term pooled analyses included patients with tumors PD-L1 lower than 1%. A study of 442 patients found that pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy improved OS (hazard ratio [HR], 0.64; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.51–0.79) and PFS (HR, 0.66; 95% CI: 0.54–0.81) relative to chemotherapy alone [12]. Similarly, another study of 637 patients demonstrated that nivolumab plus ipilimumab, alone or combined with chemotherapy, markedly extended both OS (HR: 0.64; 95% CI: 0.54–0.76) and PFS (HR: 0.72; 95% CI: 0.60–0.87) [13].

The biological basis for integrating these different treatment options lies in the unique "cold" immune microenvironment characteristic of PD-L1-negative (less than 1%) NSCLC. Unlike tumors with high PD-L1 expression, these cases typically have lower intrinsic immunogenicity and a more suppressive T-cell environment, forming a distinct clinical entity that always requires enhanced treatment strategies such as chemotherapy-induced antigen release or dual immune checkpoint blockade to achieve clinical benefits [14]. The latest evidence from important trials (such as CheckMate-9LA and the KEYNOTE series) indicates that in the PD-L1-negative patient population, the survival advantages brought by these "immune checkpoint inhibitors + " strategies often exceed the traditional histological boundaries (squamous and non-squamous) [8–11]. Therefore, integrating individual patient data (IPD) from these treatment options provides a more powerful and statistically more effective framework for evaluating the overall clinical and economic value of first-line immunotherapy for this specific and difficult-to-treat population.

While single-agent ICI therapies have demonstrated efficacy, the absence of prospectively designed studies comparing different ICI-based treatment strategies has made it challenging to determine the optimal approach for metastatic NSCLC patients exhibiting PD-L1 expression below 1%. Additionally, the substantial financial burden associated with first-line ICI-based therapy, particularly with dual ICIs, raises concerns about cost-effectiveness. Therefore, aggregating survival and economic data is essential for guiding precision medicine strategies tailored to individual patients. In this context, evaluating both survival outcomes and cost-effectiveness is critical for determining whether immunotherapy should be expanded to other cancer types or modified through innovative treatment strategies. Further analysis is necessary to elucidate the implications of first-line immunotherapy in clinical practice. This study conducts a pooled analysis based on individual patient data (IPD) to comprehensively evaluate the survival benefits and economic impact of first-line ICI-based therapy for metastatic NSCLC in those exhibiting tumors PD-L1 lower than 1%.

Materials and methods

Patients and treatments

Data from four RCTs were used for the present study: CheckMate-227, CheckMate-9LA, KEYNOTE-407, and KEYNOTE-189. These trials included cases with histologically confirmed stage IV or recurrent NSCLC who had not previously received systemic anticancer therapy. Eligible patients had tumors with PD-L1 levels below 1% without any sensitizing ALK or EGFR mutations. In the CheckMate-227 trial, participants were randomly allocated to one of three regimens: nivolumab (3 mg/kg every two weeks for a maximum of two years) plus ipilimumab (1 mg/kg every six weeks for a maximum of two years), nivolumab (360 mg every three weeks) combined with chemotherapy (up to four cycles every three weeks), or chemotherapy alone (every three weeks for a maximum of four cycles) [8]. Similarly, the CheckMate-9LA trial randomized patients to undergo treatment using nivolumab (360 mg every two weeks for up to two years) plus ipilimumab (1 mg/kg every six weeks for a maximum of two years), either with or without chemotherapy (every three weeks for a maximum of two cycles), or chemotherapy alone (every three weeks for a maximum of four cycles) [9]. The KEYNOTE-407 and KEYNOTE-189 trials randomized patients to undergo treatment using pembrolizumab (240 mg every three weeks for up to two years) plus chemotherapy (every three weeks for a maximum of two cycles) or chemotherapy alone (every three weeks for a maximum of four cycles) [10, 11]. The chemotherapy regimens administered varied based on histology: paclitaxel (200 mg/m2) plus carboplatin (AUC 5–6 mg/mL/min) for squamous NSCLC and pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) together with cisplatin (75 mg/m2) or carboplatin, followed by pemetrexed maintenance therapy, for nonsquamous NSCLC (Supplementary Table S1) [8–11]. Patients exhibiting progressive disease (PD), as determined through radiographic assessments based on trial-specific criteria, were generally recommended docetaxel as the subsequent anticancer therapy [12, 13, 15, 16]. For economic evaluations, standardized patient characteristics were assumed: serum creatinine levels equalling 1 mg/dL, body weight of 70 kg (USA)/65 kg (China), and a 1.84 m2 (USA)/1.72 m2 (China) Data extraction

The main contents of the articles published in CheckMate-227, CheckMate-9LA, KEYNOTE-407, and KEYNOTE-189 are the patient characteristics and clinical data sources of this analysis. This study established a model and reconstructed IPD based on previous research for an indirect comparison. It does not include any new research conducted by any author on human participants or animals, so it does not require the approval of an independent ethics committee and is allowed access to published data.

IPD were obtained from Kaplan–Meier survival curves in the RCTs using the IPDfromKM method reported by Guyot P et al. [20]. The extracted data for PFS and OS were visually inspected to ensure consistency with the original Kaplan–Meier curves. Health utility values were determined according to different health states: 0.710 for PFS and 0.670 for PD, derived from published RCT data [21, 22]. Additionally, an average disutility of 0.084 was applied to account for grade 3 or higher adverse events (AEs) affecting more than 5% of patients, thereby adjusting the overall health utility estimates [17, 19, 23]. In addition, the duration of first-line immunotherapy was estimated using the PFS-linked method, where drug costs were incurred while patients remained in the progression-free state. Consistent with the primary clinical trial protocols, a maximum treatment duration of 2 years was enforced. Treatment was assumed to be discontinued upon disease progression, death, or reaching the 24-month limit, whichever occurred first.

Direct medical costs considered in the analysis included expenses related to drug acquisition, AE management, follow-up procedures (imaging and laboratory tests), drug administration, best supportive care (BSC), and terminal care (Table 2). The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Cost served as a source of information on cost data for the USA along with prior studies [18, 23–25], while Chinese cost estimates were obtained from local sources and previous research [19]. All costs were adjusted to 2025 values using the respective national medical care Consumer Price Indices (CPI) to account for inflation. End points and assessments

The primary survival endpoints in this post hoc analysis were OS, measured from randomization to all-cause mortality, and PFS, measured from randomization to PD or all-cause mortality. Economic evaluation metrics included total cost, life-years (LYs), quality-adjusted LYs (QALYs), incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICER) values, and incremental net health benefit (INHB). LYs were adjusted for quality-of-life considerations to align with the health status of a typical population. QALYs incorporated health utility adjustments to account for variations in quality of life. ICERs were determined as the incremental cost per unit of improvement in health benefits, while INHB quantified the net health impact of ICI-based treatments relative to chemotherapy in terms of QALYs. Cost-effectiveness was evaluated using willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds of $150,000/QALY for the USA and $36,942/QALY (three times GDP per capita) for China [17–19]. An annual 3% discount rate was applied to costs and effectiveness outcomes in the USA, whereas a 5% discount rate was used for China [17, 18]. The cost-effectiveness analysis followed the CHEERS 2022 reporting guidelines [26] (Supplementary Table S2).

Statistical analysis

Survival analyses for OS and PFS were conducted using the Kaplan–Meier product-limit method on the extracted IPD. Differences between treatment groups were assessed via log-rank tests, while hazard ratios (HRs) and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated with Cox regression models without stratification, including treatment grouping as the sole covariate. All statistical analyses were conducted in R (v 4.4.2, http://www.r-project.org).

A three-state Markov model (PFS, PD, and death) coupled with a decision tree, was designed to assess costs and health outcomes across treatment groups. Considering the differences in dosing intervals in clinical studies, we have chosen 6 weeks as a cycle period. The model was utilized with TreeAge Pro 2020 (Williamstown, MA, https://www.treeage.com) (Supplementary Figure S1). Transition probabilities were derived from pooled survival curves and fitted to various parametric survival models, including Log-logistic, Log-normal, Gompertz, Exponential, and Weibull distributions. Data extraction was performed with GetData Graph Digitizer (v 2.26, http://www.getdata-graph-digitizer.com/index.php). According to the best-fitting survival model, the Weibull distribution was selected, and its shape (γ) and scale (λ) parameters were approximated (Supplementary Figure S2 and Table S3). The reported and reconstructed median OS were showed in Supplementary Table S4.

Sensitivity analysis was undertaken to assess model robustness. One-way sensitivity analyses examined the effect of individual parameter variations (± 20% from baseline values) on ICER estimates [23]. Additionally, probabilistic sensitivity analyses were performed with 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations to assess the effects of simultaneous changes in multiple parameters [23].

Results

Patients and treatments

Pooled analyses included 754 patients with tumors exhibiting PD-L1 expression levels below 1% who received immunotherapy-based treatment. This cohort comprised 187 individuals who underwent nivolumab plus ipilimumab treatment from CheckMate-227, 177 who received nivolumab plus chemotherapy from CheckMate-227, 135 treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab and chemotherapy from CheckMate-9LA, 140 who were administered pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy from KEYNOTE-189, and 115 who underwent treatment using pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy from KEYNOTE-407. The chemotherapy alone consisted of 502 patients: 186 from CheckMate-227, 129 from CheckMate-9LA, 66 from KEYNOTE-189, and 121 from KEYNOTE-407. Baseline patient characteristics were generally comparable and well-balanced across the included studies [12, 13]. The median length of follow-up in the entire pooled population was 64.8 months (range: 58.8–73.2 months).

Survival outcomes

The pooled immunotherapy-based treatment demonstrated a significant OS benefit relative to chemotherapy alone, with the median OS of 17.01 months (95% CI, 15.50–20.00) versus 12.00 months (95% CI, 10.50–13.00), respectively (HR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.61–0.78; P < 0.0001). A 5-year OS rate of 15.50% was evident in the immunotherapy-based group versus 8.00% in the chemotherapy-alone group (Fig. 1A). Among different ICI-based regimens, median OS was 18.50 months (95% CI, 15.50–21.50) with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.58–0.80; P < 0.0001), 17.01 months (95% CI, 14.00–20.50) with nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy (HR, 0.66; 95% CI, 0.56–0.77; P < 0.0001), and 15.50 months (95% CI, 13.00–20.50) with nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR, 0.80; 95% CI, 0.66–0.96; P = 0.0154). Corresponding 5-year OS rates were 12.41%, 20.20%, and 10.31%, respectively (Fig. 1C). PFS was also improved with immunotherapy-based regimens compared to chemotherapy alone, with the median PFS of 6.50 (95% CI, 6.01–7.00) versus 5.49 months (95% CI, 4.51–6.00), respectively (HR, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.60–0.77; P < 0.0001). The 5-year PFS rates were 6.00% and 2.60% for the immunotherapy-based and chemotherapy-alone groups, respectively (Fig. 1B). Among specific ICI-based regimens, median PFS was 7.00 months (95% CI, 6.50–9.00) with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR, 0.64; 95% CI, 0.55–0.76; P < 0.0001), 6.01 months (95% CI, 4.51–7.50) with nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy (HR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.57–0.79; P < 0.0001), and 6.00 months (95% CI, 5.00–7.00) with nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR, 0.77; 95% CI, 0.63–0.93; P = 0.0062). Corresponding 5-year PFS rates were 4.71%, 7.62%, and 6.71%, respectively (Fig. 1D).

Cost-effectiveness analyses

Markov model predictions indicated that immunotherapy-based treatment resulted in 3.41 LYs in the USA and 3.17 LYs in China, representing increases of 1.20 LYs (14.40 months) and 1.05 LYs (12.60 months) compared to chemotherapy alone, respectively. QALYs also increased with immunotherapy, though at higher costs. The total costs for immunotherapy versus chemotherapy were $222,931.70 and $23,595.79, respectively, leading to ICERs (INHBs) of $273,440.73/QALY (−1.00 QALYs) in the USA and $32,783.93/QALY (0.89 QALYs) in China. Among the specific regimens, nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy ($182,545.33/QALY, 0.09 QALYs) and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy ($26,018.02/QALY, 0.17 QALYs) demonstrated the lowest ICERs and highest INHBs in the USA and China, respectively (Table 3). Discussion

PD-L1 expression levels are commonly used as a stratification factor in RCTs rather than as a primary grouping criterion. However, increasing evidence suggests that PD-L1 expression plays a critical role in stratifying NSCLC patients, particularly those with PD-L1 lower than 1%. Currently, immunotherapy-based regimens are recommended for these patients [15, 16]. Nevertheless, in real-world clinical practice, chemotherapy is often selected as the primary treatment due to factors such as survival outcomes and economic considerations [5, 27]. Given these circumstances, a pooled analysis of the efficacy and cost-effectiveness of first-line immunotherapy-based in metastatic NSCLC patients exhibiting PD-L1 levels below 1%, utilizing IPD, was warranted. This approach allows for a larger sample size than individual studies, offering more robust guidance on the selection of ICIs. The findings of this analysis demonstrate that immunotherapy-based regimens provide significant long-term benefits in both OS and PFS compared to chemotherapy alone. Additionally, OS benefits were observed across nearly all evaluated subgroups. From an economic standpoint, the cost-effectiveness of immunotherapy-based regimens varies based on the perspective of payers in different nations.

The results of this large-scale pooled analysis indicate a 31% drop in mortality risk among patients receiving immunotherapy-based treatment, nearly doubling the 5-year OS rate relative to chemotherapy alone in cases of metastatic NSCLC with PD-L1 lower than 1%. Similarly, the likelihood of disease progression or death dropped by 32%, with more than double the 5-year PFS rate compared to chemotherapy alone. These findings are consistent with previous pooled CheckMate-227 and CheckMate-9LA trial analyses, as well as the KEYNOTE-189 and KEYNOTE-407 RCTs. Specifically, in the pooled analysis of the CheckMate trials, the 5-year OS rate was 20% for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy compared to 7% for chemotherapy alone [13]. Similarly, in the pooled analysis of the two KEYNOTE trials, the 5-year OS rate was 12.5% for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus 9.3% for chemotherapy alone [12]. While immunotherapy-based regimens demonstrated significant survival benefits, comparisons among different ICIs, either against chemotherapy or each other, remain a key area of interest. The results of this pooled analysis align with prior studies, reporting HRs for OS relative to chemotherapy alone of 0.68 (previous study: 0.64) for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, 0.66 (previous study: 0.64) for nivolumab plus ipilimumab with chemotherapy, and 0.80 (previous study: 0.80) for nivolumab plus chemotherapy [8, 12, 13]. Similarly, the HRs for PFS were 0.64 (previous study: 0.72), 0.67 (previous study: 0.66), and 0.77 (previous study: 0.73), respectively. Moreover, this pooled analysis provides supplementary data on the long-term survival benefits of different ICIs, which were not comprehensively addressed in prior studies. Specifically, the 5-year OS (and PFS) rates for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy, and nivolumab plus chemotherapy were 12.41% (4.71%), 20.20% (7.62%), and 10.31% (6.71%), respectively. These results suggest that nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy may offer a more favorable long-term survival advantage compared to other ICI regimens. This benefit may be attributed to the distinct and complementary mechanisms of action, where ipilimumab enhances the expression of PD-L1 in individuals exhibiting PD-L1 below 1% and modulates T-cell responses, thereby augmenting the antitumor activity of nivolumab [28, 29].

To gauge the economic effects of these pooled survival outcomes, a cost-effectiveness model was constructed comparing immunotherapy-based regimens with chemotherapy alone for metastatic NSCLC with PD-L1 lower than 1% from the perspective of payers in the USA and China. The lifetime cost of treatment was substantially higher in the immunotherapy-based group relative to the chemotherapy-alone group, yielding ICERs of $273,440.73 per QALY (−1.00 QALYs) and $32,783.93 per QALY (0.89 QALYs) in the USA and China, respectively. These findings suggest that immunotherapy-based regimens exhibit greater cost-efficiency in China than in the USA. These economic results are in line with previous studies that did not specifically account for PD-L1 expression in metastatic NSCLC [25, 30, 31]. Sensitivity analyses indicated that the utilities of PD and the cost of nivolumab had the greatest influence. Despite these variables, the overall cost-effectiveness model remained robust and reliable. This study underscores the necessity of considering economic factors when selecting treatment strategies. The financial burden of anticancer drugs affects both high- and middle-income countries, with substantial variations in economic environments. In the USA, limited pricing transparency and the absence of federal pricing controls contribute to higher drug prices [32, 33]. In contrast, China has implemented multilateral healthcare negotiations, established cost benchmarks for drugs, and introduced policies aimed at reducing market prices through charitable programs [33]. In recent years, both the USA and China have begun referencing drug prices in other countries as a strategy to reduce costs [34]. If international price alignment efforts are successful, lower prices for immunotherapy-based regimens could lead to improved cost-effectiveness. Further "head-to-head" comparisons of different ICI regimens revealed that nivolumab plus ipilimumab alone or with chemotherapy and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy had the highest INHBs and the lowest ICERs in the USA and China, respectively. A detailed assessment based on WTP thresholds indicated that in the USA, nivolumab plus ipilimumab alone or with chemotherapy was preferred at WTP thresholds above $162,000/QALY, while chemotherapy alone was more favorable below this threshold. In China, treatment recommendations varied by economic region: chemotherapy was preferred in less developed areas with a WTP below $24,900/QALY, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was favored in developing regions with a WTP between $24,900 and $36,100/QALY, and nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy was the recommended option in relatively developed areas with the WTP above $36,100/QALY. These findings highlight the importance of tailoring treatment strategies to affordability and market-based pricing mechanisms across different countries and regions to ensure optimal access to effective therapies.

Prior studies have identified specific subgroups of NSCLC cases, including cases with brain metastases, squamous cell carcinoma, and metastases at other sites, who tend to have a poor prognosis and may not derive substantial benefits from standard treatments, particularly when their tumors exhibit PD-L1 expression levels below 1% [35, 36]. These findings underscore the urgent need for improved therapeutic strategies tailored to these underserved patient populations. The present analysis demonstrates that survival and cost-effectiveness outcomes in key subgroups largely align with those observed in the overall patient cohort. Immunotherapy-based regimens reduced the risk of death by 25% to 50% across all subgroups. However, the survival advantage was not statistically significant in non-smoking patients, potentially due to the role of cigarette smoke carcinogens in upregulating PD-L1 expression. This upregulation enables the evasion of T-cell killing, thereby diminishing the efficacy of immunotherapy [37]. Notably, immunotherapy afforded greater benefits to patients with baseline brain metastases compared to those without. This may be attributed to the poor effectiveness of chemotherapy alone in this subgroup. These findings align with previously reported OS outcomes in those with baseline brain metastases from prior pooled analyses. Furthermore, the economic evaluation revealed a similar trend, with immunotherapy-based regimens demonstrating superior cost-effectiveness in patients with baseline brain metastases compared to other subgroups in both the USA and China. Identifying patient populations most likely to benefit from immunotherapy can inform more cost-efficient treatment strategies. Therefore, clinicians and healthcare policymakers should emphasize patient stratification based on key clinical characteristics to optimize treatment allocation and maximize cost-effective outcomes.

Several limitations of this analysis must be acknowledged. First, the study utilized the IPDfromKM method to extract individual patient data from survival curves in RCTs for pooled survival and economic assessments. Consequently, any biases present in the original RCTs may be reflected in this analysis, introducing heterogeneity due to variations in study design, treatment protocols, and patient populations. This uncertainty is critical for cost-effectiveness models, as the tail-end height directly influences the estimation of long-term survivors and, consequently, the total QALYs gained. Specifically, if the reconstruction underestimated the tail-end plateau, our ICERs would be overly conservative (higher than reality). To ensure the robustness of our model, we performed a rigorous validation by comparing our reconstructed median survival outcomes with the original trial reports, which yielded high fidelity and minimal deviation. Second, many parameters in the economic assessment relied on tied values, which may not fully capture the precise cost-effectiveness for each included patient. Future prospective cost-effectiveness analyses are required to enhance the accuracy of economic evaluations. Third, this analysis did not account for potential differences in governmental and insurance policies regarding immunotherapy reimbursement in the USA and China, which could significantly impact real-world cost-effectiveness outcomes. Another limitation is that the health utility values were derived from general metastatic NSCLC populations rather than a specific PD-L1 < 1% cohort. Given the potentially worse prognosis of this subgroup, our base-case analysis might slightly overestimate absolute QALYs. However, our sensitivity analysis demonstrated that when lower utility values were applied to both treatment arms, the ICERs remained stable. This suggests that the relative cost-effectiveness of immunotherapy versus chemotherapy is not highly sensitive to baseline utility fluctuations, provided they affect both arms equally. Finally, as this was an exploratory post hoc analysis, significant statistical conclusions were drawn based on IPD and prior pooled studies. Therefore, caution is warranted in the interpretation of these findings.

Conclusion

The pooled analysis of data from the CheckMate-227, CheckMate-9LA, KEYNOTE-189, and KEYNOTE-407 trials demonstrated that first-line immunotherapy-based regimens provide durable survival benefits for metastatic NSCLC patients with tumor PD-L1 expression levels below 1%. Additionally, these regimens were found to be cost-effective in China but not in the USA. Further comparison of different ICI-based regimens indicated that nivolumab plus ipilimumab with or without chemotherapy was the preferred first-line option in the USA, whereas pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy was the more suitable choice in China. These findings reinforce the clinical utility of immunotherapy-based regimens for patient populations with high unmet medical needs and provide valuable insights for shaping reimbursement policies within the healthcare sector.

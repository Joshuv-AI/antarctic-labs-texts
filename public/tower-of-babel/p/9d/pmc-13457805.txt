Interpretable machine learning model based on non-contrast computed tomography within 24 hours of admission for early prediction of the risk of severe deterioration of acute pancreatitis
Authors: Yan Yan, Kangchen Gu, Hong Zhao, Chunlin Yin, Ruiyang Ni, Wenjun Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly prediction of progression to severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) remains challenging due to the lack of reliable tools within 24 hours of admission. Conventional contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) is often unavailable in emergency settings, and non-contrast CT alone provides limited information for severity assessment. Therefore, this study aimed to construct an early prediction model for SAP risk using intrapancreatic and peripancreatic radiomics features from non-contrast CT obtained within 24 hours of admission, combined with clinical characteristics, to provide a theoretical basis for early clinical intervention.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted involving 585 patients admitted within 24 hours after acute pancreatitis (AP) onset. Patients were classified according to the Revised Atlanta Classification [2012] at 48 hours after admission, resulting in 148 cases progressing to SAP and 437 cases classified as mild to moderate acute pancreatitis (MAP/MSAP). ITK-SNAP software was used to manually outline the pancreas contour on non-contrast CT images obtained within 24 hours of admission for AP. Regions were expanded outward from the pancreatic contour by 2 mm, 4 mm, and 6 mm, respectively. Radiomics features were extracted and selected via intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC >0.75), Spearman correlation (>0.9), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, and maximum relevance minimum redundancy (mRMR) algorithms. The optimal peripancreatic expansion distance was determined by comparing predictive performance. A combined radiomics-clinical model was built using the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm and evaluated by area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), DeLong test, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA). SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis was used for model interpretability.ResultsThe 4 mm peripancreatic region showed the best predictive performance. After feature selection, 35–47 robust features were retained across different regions of interest (ROIs). The combined model, incorporating intrapancreatic plus 4 mm peripancreatic radiomics features and clinical parameters [procalcitonin (PCT), white blood cell (WBC) count, calcium (Ca), lymphocyte count (LYM), albumin (ALB), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)], yielded an AUC of 0.852 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.782–0.921] in the validation cohort, which was significantly higher than the clinical model alone (AUC =0.807) and the radiomics model alone (AUC =0.829). The calibration curve showed good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities, and DCA demonstrated superior net benefit of the combined model across a threshold range of 0.1–0.6. SHAP analysis revealed that intrapancreatic heterogeneity and peripancreatic density were critical predictive factors. Both SHAP analysis and the developed nomogram demonstrated robust clinical interpretability.ConclusionsRadiomics features extracted from the 4 mm peripancreatic region on non-contrast CT scans obtained within 24 hours of admission, combined with clinical characteristics, effectively predict the risk of progression to SAP. This predictive model can support clinical evaluation and guide treatment decisions.Keywords: Acute pancreatitis (AP), peripancreatic, radiomics, tomography, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis

Introduction

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common acute gastrointestinal disorder characterized by a localized inflammatory response in the pancreas, which may lead to a systemic disease manifesting a distinctly biphasic clinical course and a complex, continuous disease spectrum (1). Clinically, AP may manifest as a mild form resolving spontaneously; however, approximately 20% of patients develop severe acute pancreatitis (SAP) accompanied by multiple organ dysfunction, with mortality rates ranging from 13% to 40% (2,3). Therefore, accurate and timely prediction of progression to SAP is critical for improving clinical outcomes.

Currently, individual laboratory parameters are typically sufficient for diagnosing AP, but no reliable indicator with high positive predictive value exists at admission to accurately predict progression to severe disease. According to the Revised Atlanta Classification [2012] and clinical scoring systems [e.g., Ranson score, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II), Bedside Index for Severity in Acute Pancreatitis (BISAP)], SAP diagnosis requires multidimensional assessments performed 48 hours after disease onset (4-9). However, systematic methods for early risk stratification within 24 hours of admission remain lacking. Conventional imaging modalities, primarily computed tomography (CT), can directly visualize pancreatic morphological changes and exudative alterations in surrounding tissues. When combined with laboratory results, CT within 24 hours of admission can support diagnosis (10,11). At this early stage, however, CT images often fail to demonstrate intrapancreatic necrosis and cannot characterize the composition of peripancreatic exudates, limiting their ability to predict disease progression. With advancements in radiomics, numerous studies have evaluated AP severity and prognosis. Nevertheless, most investigations have predominantly focused on intrapancreatic features (12-14), neglecting a comprehensive evaluation of peripancreatic involvement.

Against this background, the present study utilized non-contrast CT images obtained within 24 hours of admission from AP patients to jointly evaluate intrapancreatic and peripancreatic features. By integrating imaging features with clinical variables, we developed an interpretable machine learning model to facilitate early prediction of progression risk to SAP and guide timely clinical intervention. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0116/rc).

Methods

Study design and patient population

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (No. YX2024-127), and the requirement of patient approval or written informed consent for reviewing medical records or images was waived by the Ethics Committee in view of the retrospective nature based on anonymized radiology reports. This retrospective study analyzed 585 patients diagnosed with AP at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University from August 2020 to November 2022 (344 males, 241 females; mean age 48.76±17.92 years). Inclusion criteria were: (I) initial AP episode with CT performed within 24 hours of onset; (II) complete and high-quality non-contrast CT images covering the pancreas and its surrounding regions; and (III) comprehensive clinical data. Exclusion criteria were: (I) severe respiratory artifacts; (II) incomplete scans; (III) prior clinical interventions before imaging; and (IV) SAP diagnosis at admission. Patients were classified according to the Revised Atlanta Classification [2012] at 48 hours after admission, resulting in 148 cases progressing to SAP and 437 cases classified as mild to moderate acute pancreatitis (MAP/MSAP). Patients were randomly allocated into training (n=409, 70%) and validation cohorts (n=176, 30%) at a 7:3 ratio (Table 1).

CT image acquisition

CT examinations were performed using three scanners: Siemens SOMATOM Force CT, GE Revolution CT, and Philips Ingenuity CT. Scanning parameters included tube voltage of 120 kVp, tube current of 350 mA, slice thickness and interval of 5 mm, a field of view (FOV) of 38 cm × 38 cm, and a pitch of 0.984 patients were positioned supine and scanned from the subxiphoid region to the anterior superior iliac spine, fully encompassing the pancreas. Breath-holding training was provided to minimize motion artifacts.

ROI segmentation and processing

CT images were exported from the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) in DICOM format, standardized (window width: 350 HU; window level: 50 HU), and resampled (1 mm3 × 1 mm3 × 1 mm3). Two senior radiologists (Y.Y. and K.G.) independently delineated regions of interest (ROIs) covering the entire pancreas (head, body, and tail) using ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0), carefully excluding adjacent anatomical structures. In cases of discrepancies, a radiologist with 20 years of experience (W.Y.) resolved these differences to ensure accuracy and reliability of the segmentation.

Peripancreatic region expansion

The original pancreatic ROIs were expanded outward by 2, 4, and 6 mm, respectively. Areas extending beyond the pancreas after expansion were manually removed. Subsequently, ROIs were categorized into two major groups. The first category consisted of single ROIs: (I) ROI(intrapancreatic); (II) ROI(peripancreatic2mm); (III) ROI(peripancreatic4mm); and (IV) ROI(peripancreatic6mm). The second category comprised combined ROIs, further divided into two subgroups based on labeling methods: Category I, combined ROIs with a single unified label, including (I) ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic2mm); (II) ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic4mm); and (III) ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic6mm); and Category II, combined ROIs with two distinct labels, comprising (I) ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic2mm); (II) ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm); and (III) ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic6mm). Feature extraction

In this study, radiomics features were extracted using PyRadiomics software (version 3.7.12). For each single-label group, the extracted features comprised shape (n=14), first-order (n=360), and texture features (n=1,460). Consequently, 1,834 features were obtained from each single-label ROI [i.e., ROI(intrapancreatic), ROI(peripancreatic2/4/6mm), and ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic2/4/6mm)], whereas 3,668 features were derived from ROIs with dual labels [i.e., ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic 2/4/6mm)].

Feature selection

Inter-observer consistency in ROI delineation was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), retaining radiomics features with ICC >0.75. Features were normalized by Z-score standardization based solely on the training set to avoid scale-related bias. Redundant features with Spearman correlation coefficients >0.9 were removed. Feature dimensionality was further reduced through least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with tenfold cross-validation to identify features with non-zero coefficients. Finally, the maximum relevance minimum redundancy (mRMR) method was used to streamline the feature set.

Prediction model generation

In our research work, we employed logistic regression for linear modeling and integrated ensemble algorithms to handle more complex data structures. Using a curated set of features, multiple radiomics predictive models capable of accurately capturing intricate data patterns were constructed (Table S1-S7), among which the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model demonstrated the best performance. Five-fold cross-validation was performed for each model. To manage class imbalance, the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) was applied to the training cohort. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), and DeLong tests compared differences among models. Finally, the optimal radiomics model was combined with clinical variables to construct a radiomics-clinical model and corresponding nomogram.

Model explanation and visualization

Multiple image-filtering techniques were utilized to generate predictive features based on SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis. Each ROI underwent three-dimensional (3D) decomposition using Haar basis functions, yielding eight frequency sub-bands (LLL, LLH, LHL, LHH, HLL, HLH, HHL, HHH) for texture extraction. Gradient magnitudes capturing edge information were computed via a 3D Sobel filter (3×3×3 kernel). Additional features were derived using Gaussian, exponential, logarithmic, square root, Laplacian of Gaussian filtering, and 3D local binary patterns (3D-LBP). A small subset of features was directly extracted from raw images. Intensity normalization utilized the Nyúl linear stretching method (µ =100, σ =20), and color images were converted to grayscale following DICOM-standard monochrome mapping.

SHAP analysis provided interpretability for the combined model. SHAP summary plots intuitively showed global feature importance, with positive and negative SHAP values indicating feature effects on predictions. Vertical stacking reflected feature contribution distribution, and colors (blue to red) represented feature magnitude. Force plots visualized individual predictions, highlighting each feature’s specific contribution.

Statistical analysis

Clinical data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 25.0). The Shapiro-Wilk test assessed the normality of clinical features. Quantitative data satisfying both normality and homogeneity of variance assumptions were analyzed by t-tests; non-normally distributed data underwent analysis by the Mann-Whitney U test. Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), according to data distribution. Optimal decision thresholds were determined by maximizing the Youden index (J = sensitivity + specificity −1) in the training set, and these thresholds remained unchanged when applied to the validation set. Inter-observer reproducibility was evaluated by ICC, with ICC >0.75 indicating good stability. The ROC curves were generated for each predictive model to evaluate their discriminative performance, quantified by comparing AUC values. DeLong tests were subsequently performed to statistically compare model differences in AUC, while decision curve analysis (DCA) assessed the models’ potential clinical utility. Calibration curves were plotted to evaluate the calibration accuracy of the model. All of the above statistical analysis methods were verified for correctness by a statistical expert. Results

Clinical characteristics

This study included 585 patients diagnosed with AP, comprising 437 mild to moderate cases and 148 severe cases. Patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n=409) and a validation cohort (n=176) at a 7:3 ratio. The training cohort consisted of 307 MAP/MSAP and 102 SAP cases, while the validation cohort included 130 MAP/MSAP and 46 SAP cases (Table 1). As shown in Table 1, significant differences (P<0.05) were observed between the mild to moderate and severe groups in both training and validation cohorts regarding C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), interleukin-6 (IL-6), white blood cell (WBC) count, lymphocyte count (LYM), red blood cell distribution width-coefficient of variation (RDW-CV), albumin (ALB), calcium (Ca), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and glucose (GLU); other indicators showed no significant differences (P>0.05).

Table 2 presents the results of univariate and multivariate analyses of clinical indicators predicting AP severity. PCT, WBC, Ca, LYM, ALB, and LDH showed significant differences (P<0.05) between mild to moderate and severe groups in both analyses. PCT, WBC, and LDH were positively correlated with severity risk, while Ca, LYM, and ALB were negatively correlated.

Individual radiomics and IntraPeri radiomics fusion model and DeLong test

Redundant features identified in the training cohort were removed via Spearman correlation analysis. Subsequently, features were selected from the following ROI regions: ROI(intrapancreatic), ROI(peripancreatic2mm), ROI(peripancreatic4mm), ROI(peripancreatic6mm), ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic2mm), ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic4mm), ROI(intrapancreatic+peripancreatic6mm), ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic2mm), ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm), and ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic6mm). The number of remaining features was 282, 318, 306, 292, 301, 305, 298, 601, 589, and 587, respectively. After further selection with mRMR and LASSO, the final robust features numbered 35, 18, 36, 42, 46, 29, 29, 20, 29, and 47, respectively (Figure S1).

An XGBoost algorithm was utilized to construct predictive models based on the selected features. Table 3 presents the predictive performance metrics for each ROI category in both training and validation cohorts. Specifically, the ROI(intrapancreatic) group achieved an AUC of 0.808 in the validation cohort, while the ROI(peripancreaticXmm) groups demonstrated AUC values of 0.788 (X=2), 0.827 (X=4), and 0.800 (X=6), respectively. Among the intrapancreatic-peripancreatic combined groups, the ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm) yielded the highest performance, with an AUC of 0.829. Figure 3 depicts ROC curves along with DeLong test results comparing ROI groups, confirming that the ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm) group exhibited the highest AUC (0.829).

Discussion

Early prediction of the risk of AP progression is of great clinical significance for timely intervention. Although contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) is currently one of the gold standards for assessing pancreatic necrosis and severity in AP, its immediate implementation in the emergency setting is often limited by factors such as hemodynamic instability, contrast agent contraindications, and limited equipment availability in primary hospitals. Moreover, unnecessary CECT examinations expose patients to radiation and contrast-related risks. To address this issue, we developed an early prediction model for SAP based on radiomics features derived from non-contrast CT obtained within 24 hours of admission, combined with clinical indicators. The value of this model lies in providing an “early warning system” for emergency triage when CECT is not immediately available: it rapidly identifies high-risk patients to guide prioritized CECT examination or enhanced monitoring, while avoiding unnecessary examinations in low-risk patients, thereby reducing radiation exposure and contrast-related complications, filling the decision-making gap from “non-contrast CT to CECT”, and optimizing the allocation of medical resources.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses identified PCT, WBC, Ca, LYM, ALB, and LDH as key indicators of AP severity. Levels of Ca, LYM, and ALB negatively correlated with risk, indicating low values as potential severity markers. Each 1-unit increase in blood Ca reduced SAP risk by 68.9%. Hypocalcemia results from mechanisms such as fat necrosis-induced Ca saponification (15), systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) (16), and capillary leak syndrome (17); thus, reduced Ca is a crucial predictor of SAP. Similarly, each 1-unit increase in LYM decreased risk by 13%. Under stress, lymphocytes regulate inflammatory responses. Lu et al. (18) reported that elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio significantly correlated with persistent organ failure in AP patients, suggesting low LYMs indicate progressive inflammation or sepsis. For each 1-unit increase in ALB, SAP risk decreased by 4.7%. ALB reflects nutritional status and inflammatory response, showing better predictive value for organ failure than the Ranson score (19). Conversely, inflammatory markers (PCT and WBC) positively correlated with risk. Each 1-unit increase in WBC count elevated SAP risk by 11.3%, indicating intensified inflammation. Similarly, each 1-unit increase in PCT raised the risk by 1.6%. Elevated PCT, triggered by intestinal barrier disruption and bacterial translocation during SAP, strongly indicates necrotic infection and severe disease progression. Siriwardena et al. (20) demonstrated that PCT-guided antibiotic therapy reduced medication duration without increasing infection risk or mortality, highlighting its clinical utility. LDH was statistically significant (P<0.001), but each unit increase raised risk marginally (0.3%). Despite LDH’s limited predictive specificity due to widespread tissue distribution, it remains valuable when combined with other indicators because of its sensitivity to inflammatory injury.

In recent years, the peripancreatic region has increasingly attracted attention in AP research. Zhao et al. (21) reported that a radiomics model derived from pancreatic and peripancreatic CT images achieved excellent performance in predicting AP severity early, with AUC of approximately 0.894 in the test cohort. Gupta et al. (22) found that both the extent and location of extrapancreatic necrosis (EPN) correlated with AP severity and clinical outcomes. Chen et al. (23) indicated that a combined radiomics model integrating clinical characteristics with intrapancreatic and peripancreatic CT imaging exhibited enhanced predictive accuracy for AP prognosis. Zhou et al. (24) constructed an MRI-based radiomics model to predict early EPN, demonstrating superior performance compared to clinical models. Although previous studies have established the significance of peripancreatic radiomics features in predicting AP prognosis or severity, none have systematically evaluated how varying peripancreatic ranges influence predictive performance. In contrast, our study evaluated the predictive capabilities of radiomics features from intrapancreatic regions, peripancreatic X mm regions, and combined intrapancreatic and peripancreatic X mm regions (X=2, 4, 6) for AP severity. Results indicated that the AUC of the ROI (intrapancreatic) group (0.808) was lower than that of the ROI (peripancreatic4mm) group (0.827), suggesting that the 4 mm peripancreatic region provided superior predictive performance compared to intrapancreatic features alone. Intrapancreatic features mainly reflect pancreatic parenchymal damage (such as the extent of necrosis), which usually becomes evident 48 hours after onset. In contrast, peripancreatic features exhibit greater heterogeneity and reflect early signs of systemic inflammatory spread, risk of vascular invasion, and involvement of multiple organs (e. g., prerenal fascial thickening indicating kidney injury) within 6–12 hours (25,26). Therefore, extraction and analysis of peripancreatic features offer more comprehensive and timely information than intrapancreatic features alone. However, in the validation cohort, the AUC of the ROI (intrapancreatic) group (0.808) was higher than those of the ROI (peripancreatic2mm) and ROI (peripancreatic6mm) groups (0.788 and 0.800, respectively). This difference indicated that intrapancreatic features had better predictive performance than the 2 mm and 6 mm peripancreatic regions. We speculate that the 2 mm peripancreatic region often inadequately covers the extensive pancreatic edema, while the 6 mm peripancreatic region includes excessive non-relevant areas, encompassing more blood vessels and surrounding organs, thus introducing noise. Conversely, the 4 mm peripancreatic region effectively covers the core area of fat necrosis and avoids interference from distant structures. Thus, the 4 mm peripancreatic region provides an optimal observational window for inflammatory spread.

The ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm) model achieved the highest AUC values (0.988 training, 0.829 validation). Its superior performance stems from spatial complementarity (intrapancreatic features identifying localized necrosis, peripancreatic features indicating inflammatory diffusion), temporal complementarity (delayed intrapancreatic assessment, early peripancreatic signs), and biological complementarity (pancreatic enzyme activation and peripancreatic inflammatory release). These factors comprehensively enhance early prediction of the risk of progression to SAP.

Hult et al. (27) reported that including perilesional areas in rectal cancer radiomics analysis did not enhance predictive value but diluted tumor-derived information, suggesting excessive ROI expansion may obscure key features with nonspecific signals—consistent with our findings. The AUC of the ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm) group (0. 829) was the highest among comparable groups, whereas the ROI(intrapancreatic + peripancreatic4mm) (i.e., the combined ROI as a single region) had a lower AUC (0. 767). Extracting intrapancreatic and peripancreatic features together as a whole ROI blurred pathological distinctions, causing large-scale peripancreatic features to overshadow intrapancreatic details. In contrast, extracting intrapancreatic (focal necrosis) and peripancreatic (diffuse lesions) features separately preserved independent characteristics and avoided dilution due to differing CT values. Clinically, an integrated ROI can be used for rapid AP screening, whereas separate ROIs are more suitable for precision medicine.

Our study integrated significant clinical indicators with radiomics features [ROI(intrapancreatic) + ROI(peripancreatic4mm)] to construct a joint clinical-radiomics model, whose diagnostic efficacy was evaluated. ROC curve comparisons demonstrated that the combined model (AUC =0.852; 95% CI: 0.782–0.921) had superior predictive accuracy compared to either the radiomics or clinical models alone. Although ROC curves assess the discriminatory power of models, they cannot fully reflect clinical decision-making capacity. Therefore, DCA analysis was conducted, revealing that the joint model outperformed the single clinical or radiomics models when the threshold probability was between 0.1 and 0.6. This indicated that the combined model’s diagnostic strategy would likely yield higher clinical benefits than risks of non-intervention. To directly assess MAP/MSAP vs. SAP risk, an intuitive nomogram was established.

Consistent with previous studies (28-30), this study confirmed the excellent predictive performance of the XGBoost-based radiomics model. Despite its effectiveness (31,32), clinical application of XGBoost models requires interpretability. SHAP analyses, including summary and force plots, provided intuitive visualizations and explanations for clinical practitioners. Globally, SHAP summary plots concisely depict feature distributions and associations between feature values and predictive influence (33,34). In this study, intra_gradient_glcm_Imc2 and peri_log_sigma3_0_mm3D_firstorder_Kurtosis were the most critical features for predicting the risk of progression to SAP. Intra_gradient_glcm_Imc2 quantifies spatial heterogeneity and structural disorder within pancreatic lesions. High Imc2 values indicated uniform texture, associated with early edema or localized inflammation, while low values suggested structural disorganization and heterogeneity, predicting necrosis or infectious complications consistent with SAP pathophysiology (35-37). The feature peri_log_sigma_3_0_mm_3D_firstorder_Kurtosis quantifies the extremeness of density distributions within peripancreatic tissues. Low kurtosis reflected extreme density distributions, indicative of inflammatory exudation or fat necrosis linked to MSAP/SAP. Therefore, this study analyzed the radiomics features intra_gradient_glcm_Imc2 and peri_log_sigma_3_0_mm_3D_firstorder_Kurtosis. These features quantified, at the microscopic texture and macroscopic morphological levels, respectively, the structural deterioration of pancreatic parenchyma from homogeneous edema to heterogeneous necrosis, as well as the complexity of peripancreatic necrosis. These findings position radiomics as a vital link between imaging phenotypes and clinical severity, offering quantitative insights beyond traditional visual evaluation. Understanding feature mechanisms enables clinicians to utilize SHAP force plots for individualized prediction interpretation. Unlike traditional nomograms that require manual scoring (38), SHAP plots provide a more intuitive and efficient method for personalized risk assessment. For example, intra_gradient_glcm_Imc2 showed the strongest positive (red) contribution in a mild-moderate case (patient a) but a negative (blue) contribution in a severe case (patient b), indicating its negative correlation with severe predictions and stronger predictive utility in mild-moderate cases.

Beyond severity prediction, radiomics and artificial intelligence have also shown promise in predicting AP-related complications and quantifying pancreatic necrosis. Hu et al. (39) developed a contrast-enhanced CT-based radiomics model to predict post-AP diabetes mellitus, extending the application of radiomics to long-term outcomes. Similarly, Lu et al. (40) constructed an AI-assisted model to quantify pancreatic necrosis in AP, demonstrating the potential of deep learning to enhance the reproducibility and efficiency of lesion assessment. These studies further support the value of quantitative imaging approaches in the comprehensive management of AP.

The present study has several limitations. (I) Manual ROI segmentation is time-consuming and experience-dependent. Although we employed a double-blind method with a third evaluator to control for variability, this approach remains difficult to adapt to the emergency setting. Therefore, the current model is still at the “proof-of-concept stage”. Future work should integrate automated or semi-automated segmentation tools. We plan to develop an automated segmentation model using the manually delineated ROIs from this study as the reference standard to facilitate clinical translation. (II) The performance gap between the training set (AUC 0.988) and the validation sets (AUC 0.829/0.852) suggests a certain degree of overfitting, which may be attributable to the limited sample size (particularly the small number of severe cases) and the amplification of non-robust features by SMOTE. It should be noted that SMOTE was applied only to the training set; therefore, the validation set performance better reflects the model’s predictive ability under the real-world distribution. Nevertheless, external independent validation is still needed in the future to further evaluate the generalizability of the model. (III) This single-center retrospective study may contain inherent biases. Although comprehensive, the lack of external validation limits the generalizability of the findings. However, the use of CT scanners with different models and scanning parameters may partially mitigate this concern. Future multicenter prospective studies are warranted to enhance robustness and applicability. (IV) Potential inaccuracies in delineating pancreatic ROIs due to indistinct boundaries caused by peripancreatic edema might introduce errors. In future work, we will employ contrast-enhanced CT scans to improve contrast between the pancreas and surrounding tissues. (V) Etiology-specific differences (e. g., biliary vs. alcoholic pancreatitis) may affect the significance of pancreatic and peripancreatic radiomics features, potentially biasing pooled analyses. Future studies will classify patients by etiology to more accurately evaluate predictive performance across different ROIs.

Conclusions

Based on the critical role of peripancreatic exudation in the progression of AP, this study initially explored the most predictive lesion areas by expanding the peripancreatic region to different extents on non-contrast CT images obtained within 24 hours of admission to develop a radiomics model. This was further integrated with clinical features to establish an interpretable machine learning prediction model combining clinical and CT (intrapancreatic-peripancreatic) characteristics. The findings of this study may contribute to clinical treatment evaluation and improve patients’ quality of life.

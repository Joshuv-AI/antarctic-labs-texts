Development of a disulfidptosis-related prognostic model for endometrial cancer with potential therapeutic target
Authors: Chunmei Li, Xuefei Fan, Xue Wang, Yulan Yao, Bing Huang, Linlin Chen, Lu Cao, Tao Peng, Yingying Lin, Rong Cai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrognosis biomarkers for endometrial cancer (EC) are in need. Recent evidence demonstrated the critical role of disulfidptosis, a novel cell death modality, in cancer. However, limited studies have developed a disulfidptosis-related gene model for EC. Disulfidptosis prognosis score of EC (disulfidptosis-PSEC) were constructed using disulfidptosis-related differently expression genes with the RNA data of 544 EC patients from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset. Model was evaluated using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curve analysis for overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS), along with the hazard ratio (HR) between risk groups. Then, the cellular and molecular profile for different risk groups were performed, along with drug target inference. Disulfidptosis-PSEC demonstrated outstanding prognostic value for OS and DFS, with 5-year area under curve of 0.71 (95% CI, 0.58–0.75) and 0.69 (95% CI, 0.62–0.76), respectively. Low risk group demonstrated better survival with an HR of 0.38 (95% CI, 0.24–0.59) and 0.46 (95% CI, 0.32–0.66) for OS and DFS, respectively. The model was independent of TCGA subtype. Low risk group were featured with more immune cell infiltration and less gene mutation. Serval drug targets, and the therapeutic value of serotonin receptor among copy number (CN)-low subpopulation, were identified. Disulfidptosis-PSEC was a potential prognosis biomarker for EC with targetable biological process.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01384-4.Keywords: Endometrial cancer, Disulfidptosis, Drug target, Prognostic model

Introduction

In 2020, there were 417,000 newly diagnosed cases of endometrial cancer (EC) worldwide, making it the sixth most prevalent cancer among females. The estimated lifetime risk of developing EC approximately 3% and there continues to be a global increase in both incidence and mortality rates [1]. Traditional prognostic assessments in EC have primarily relied on clinicopathological characteristics, such as tumor grade, stage, and histological subtype. Recent advancements in molecular classification, integrating The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) classification, have significantly improved the stratification of EC patients and the personalization of treatment strategies [2, 3]. These molecular classifications divided EC into distinct subgroups based on genetic and molecular features, providing deeper insights into tumor biology and guiding clinical decisions more effectively. However, despite these advancements, there remains substantial challenges and limitations. Molecular classifications, while powerful, do not fully capture the complexity of EC, particularly in predicting outcomes for patients within the same molecular subgroup. The heterogeneity within these molecularly defined groups can lead to varied clinical outcomes, suggesting the need for additional biomarkers and models that can complement existing molecular classifications.

In this context, the concept of disulfidptosis, a novel cell death modality distinct from apoptosis and ferroptosis, has emerged as a promising area of research [4]. Disulfidptosis is induced by disulfide stress, often resulting from the accumulation of cystine within cells, which leads to cytotoxicity through the disruption of actin networks [5]. This cell death pathway is particularly intriguing in cancer biology, where metabolic vulnerabilities can be exploited for therapeutic gain [6]. Recent studies have begun to uncover the significance of disulfidptosis in various cancers, including bladder, breast, lung, and colon cancers, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target [7–10]. This novel pathway offers a unique perspective for cancer treatment, especially in metabolic therapies designed to selectively induce cell death in cancer cells. In endometrial cancer, the role of disulfidptosis remains not fully understood. Preliminary research has indicated that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) associated with disulfidptosis may have prognostic value [11, 12]. However, a comprehensive model integrating disulfidptosis-related genes with clinicopathological characteristics for survival outcomes in EC patients has not been established.

This study aims to develop the disulfidptosis prognosis score of EC (disulfidptosis-PSEC) model to predict overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in EC patients. Cellular and molecular profiles of high- and low-risk groups would be analyzed in the study, focusing on immune cell infiltration, gene mutation patterns, and potential therapeutic targets. This research may enhance our understanding of disulfidptosis in EC and contribute to cancer prognostication and personalized medicine.

Methods

Study design and patients

In this retrospective study, a total of 544 EC patients from TCGA were included to construct the disulfidptosis-PSEC. The characteristics of the participants were demonstrated in Table 1 [13]. Molecular subtypes were called using the “TCGAbiolinks” R package (version 2.32.0) for partial of the participants. Of whom, the median age was 64 years old (interquartile range [IQR] 57–71), 75% had endometrioid histology, 59.6% were classified as grade 3 or higher and 62.3% were at stage I. For RNA data, since the expression levels conform to a log-normal distribution, we applied a logarithmic transformation to the raw Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM) values of each transcript by taking the logarithm of (FPKM + 1). The study was exempt from ethical review due to the utilization of de-identified, publicly available data.

Establishment of the disulfidptosis-PSEC

A total of 22 disulfidptosis-related genes, as reported by previous literature [14, 15], were screened. First, these genes were compared between tumor and adjacent normal tissue. Those with a false discovery rate (FDR) less than 0.05 and a log fold change greater than 0.58 were identified as the differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The expression of these DEGs and their associations were analyzed.

We also use the String Interaction Network to visualize the biological protein network regarding these DEGs [16]. The prognostic value of these DEGs were analyzed separately using the optimistic cut-off value. The differences in protein expression of DEGs between normal and tumor tissue in endometrial cancer were analyzed using the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) database. Subsequently, the normalized expression of these DEGs was incorporated into a multi-variable Cox regression model with OS as the endpoint, to construct the disulfidptosis-PSEC. The risk score for each participant was estimated by adding the products of each DEGs expression value and the corresponding regression coefficients. Thereafter, a time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed, with the risk score as the predictor and the OS at 3 years as the endpoint. The optimal cut-off value of the risk score was determined by the corresponding Youden index [17]. Patients were classified as high risk or low risk according to the cutoff. We further fitted the risk score along with clinical variables (age, cancer stage and grade) to develop a composite prognostic model.

Evaluation of the disulfidptosis-PSEC

The prognostic value of disulfidptosis-PSEC was initially assessed using time-dependent ROC analyses for OS and DFS at 1-, 3-, and 5-years. Then a univariable Cox proportional hazard model was employed to estimate the hazard ratio (HR) and the corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI) for OS and DFS between high- and low-risk patients, with Kaplan–Meier curves surving as a demonstration. The evaluation was repeated for EC patients with copy number (CN)-low, CN-high, and microsatellite instability (MSI)-high molecular phenotype. Further, we incorpareated the risk groups along with clinical variables (age, cancer stage and grade) into both uni- and multi-variate Cox regression models to evaluate the independence of the prognostic value. The corresponding adjusted HR for OS and DFS was reported. Also, the prognostic value of the composite model was examined using time-dependent ROC analyses.

Annotation of the disulfidptosis-PSEC

The tumor microenvironment was profiled using ESTIMATE, single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) and Cell-type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT) algorithm [18–20]. ESTIMATE, a gene-signature-based method, profiled the tumor microenvironment by inferring scores of stromal and immune cells to estimate their respective presence. An overall ESTIMATE score was generated by combining these two scores. ssGSEA was a gene set enrichment method used to calculate cell infiltration scores. CIBERSORT employed a deconvolution algorithm to infer the proportions of 22 immune cells. The relationship between DEGs and the tumor microenvironment was examined using Spearman correlation analysis. Additionally, the tumor microenvironment was also compared between high- and low-risk population. We further profiled and compared the immune atlas in the CN-low group to explore the heterogeneity among them.

The functional enrichment analysis was conducted using 9 disulfidptosis DEGs and the DEGs with a log fold change > 1 and FDR < 0.05 based on the Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database [21, 22]. Gene mutation data was extracted from cBioPortal in “.maf” format. The frequencies of mutated genes were listed for both high and low risk group, and the mutation landscape was depicted by waterfall plots via “maftoll” R package (version 2.20.0) [23]. We also performed this analysis for the CN-low group to account for potential bias, as CN-high patients were more frequently classified as high-risk group.

We finally explored potential treatments for high-risk EC patients defined by disulfidptosis via CLUE, a connectivity map (CMap) based approach [24]. CLUE inferred the potential treatment targets by linking genes, drugs, and disease status [25]. An enrichment score is be generated for each drug, with a negative value indicating a higher probability of reversing biological processes. We reported the drugs with top five enrichment score the overall population and CN-low subpopulation.

Statistical analysis

The student’s t test and Mann–Whitney U test were employed to compare the difference of continuous variables as appropriate. Chi-square test was applied to compare the difference in portions. Correlation between continuous variables was estimated using Spearman correlation coefficient. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.0). Statistical significance was considered when a two-tailed P < 0.05 unless otherwise specified.

Result

Construction of disulfidptosis-PSEC

The basic information of EC patients and characteristics of EC in the TCGA database are shown in Table 1. According to previous literature, there are 165,554 DEGs that shown the difference between tumor and adjacent normal tissue, of which 22 genes are considered DRGs. An intersection of these two datasets of 9 DRGs (OXSM, PRN1, MYH9, MYH10, IQGAP1, DSTN, NDUFA11, GYS1, and CD2AP) that show the difference between tumors and adjacent normal tissues were selected. A Venn diagram to identify DRGs that were associated with difference between tumors and adjacent normal tissues is presented in Fig. 1A. Analysis of the 9 DRGs showed that the existence of tumor cells result in significant differential expression of all 9 DRGs, of which 5 (OXSM, PRN1, NDUFA11, GYS1, and CD2AP) are up- and 4 (DSTN, MYH9, MYH10, and IQGAP1) are down-regulated, as shown in Fig. 1B. The heatmap displaying the overall distribution and expression levels of the 9 DRGs with prognostic value between tumor and adjacent normal tissue was illustrated in Fig. 1C. The protein–protein interactions (PPI) network by Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interaction Gene/Proteins (STRING) database of 9 DRGs with prognostic score was demonstrated and displayed in Fig. 1D. Figure 1E showing the correlation heatmap representing the correlation between DRGs and immune cells. The correlation between these genes was shown in Fig. 1F. According to Fig. 1F, IQGAP1 has a positive correlation with CD2AP while NDUFA11 has a negative correlation with CD2AP. Additionally, a strong positive correlation between NDUFA11 and T regulatory cells (Tregs) and a strong negative correlation between MYH10 and Tregs as well as IQGAP1 and Tregs, are shown in the correlation heatmap in Supplementary Fig. S1.

Nine DRGs were applied for LASSO-Cox regression analysis to construct the disulfidptosis-PSEC index. The disulfidptosis-PSEC risk score was generated for each individual gene based on the normalized expression level of each gene and the accompanying Cox regression coefficients as follow:

Disulfidptosis-PSEC risk score = 0.059*expression level of RPN1 − 0.077*expression level of DSTN − 0.439*expression level of NDUFA11 − 0.157*expression level of IQGAP1 − 0.317*expression level of MYH9 + 0.049* expression level of MYH10 − 0.465*expression level of OXSM + 0.296*expression level of GYS1 − 0.049*expression level of CD2AP.

Validation of the prognostic value of disulfidptosis-PSEC

Disulfidptosis-PSEC demonstrated outstanding prognostic value. Time-dependent ROC curves for OS and DFS showed an excellent distinguishing capacity for disulfidptosis-PSEC at 5-year of follow-up, presented in Fig. 3A–B. For OS (Fig. 3A), AUC yielded values of 0.62 [95% CI, 0.52–0.72], 0.70 [95% CI, 0.64–0.77] and 0.71 [95% CI, 0.58–0.75] at 1-, 3- and 5-year follow-up, respectively. For DFS (Fig. 3B), the AUC yielded values of 0.53 [95% CI, 0.44–0.62], 0.68 [95% CI, 0.61–0.74] and 0.69 [95% CI, 0.62–0.76] at 1-, 3- and 5-year of follow-up, respectively. Regarding the primary outcome, patients in the high-risk group exhibited a notable reduction in OS and DFS (HR: 0.38 [95% CI, 0.24–0.59] and 0.46 [95% CI, 0.32–0.66] for OS and DFS, respectively) compared to patients in the low-risk group. Based on the observed median risk score, the patients were categorized into two groups: low-risk (risk score ≤ − 0.054) and high-risk (risk score > − 0.054) groups (Fig. 3C). A Sankey diagram was constructed to show the composition and correspondence of high and low-risk groups across different molecular subtypes (CN-high, CN-low, MSI, and POLE, respectively), presented in Fig. 3D. According to Fig. 3D, high-risk group is more closely associated with CN-high, while the low-risk group is more associated with CN-low and MSI. The associations between risk score and OS and DFS are presented in Fig. 4A, B, respectively. For OS (Fig. 4A), CN-low has a significantly lower hazard ratio for OS in overall population (HR: 0.25 [95% CI, 0.098–0.61]) while there is no significant correlation for OS of CN-low after correction (HR: 0.67 [95% CI, 0.24–1.85]). For DFS (Fig. 4B), both CN-high and CN-low show significant positive and negative associations with prognosis in overall population (HR: 1.61 [95% CI, 1.05–2.47], and 0.48 [95% CI, 0.26–0.87], respectively), while neither CN-high nor CN-low demonstrates significant correlation after correction (HR: 1.38 [95% CI, 0.89–2.13] and HR: 0.81 [95% CI, 0.40–1.64], respectively). Since there are no recorded deaths for the POLE subtype in TCGA database, POLE was not analyzed in Fig. 4.

Annotation of disulfidptosis-PSEC

Functional enrichment analysis

The impact of biosynthetic pathways on the molecular mechanism of disulfidptosis-PSEC was elucidated through functional enrichment analysis. This analysis was conducted on the biological functions and pathways related to disulfidptosis-PSEC utilizing GO and KEGG database. The analysis focused on DRGs between high- and low-risk groups. The GO enrichment analysis indicated that the DEGs between the high- and low-risk groups were associated with the actin cytoskeleton (Fig. 6A). Similarly, the KEGG analysis demonstrated that all 9 DRGs were associated with regulation of actin cytoskeleton between high- and low-risk groups. We assessed the FDR > 1 in DEGs, referring to the acceptable rate of false discoveries with an FDR > 1 as considered differentially expressed in Fig. 6B. According to Fig. 6B, the GO enrichment analysis indicated that the DEGs between the high- and low-risk groups were associated with steroid binding, while the KEGG analysis demonstrated that peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway were significantly associated with DEGs differential expression. The analysis of CN-low subgroup analysis utilizing GO and KEGG was presented in Supplementary Fig. S4.

The association between disulfidptosis-PSEC and the tumor microenvironment was demonstrated in Fig. 7 and Supplementary Fig. S5. Somatic mutation analysis

Given the significant influence of somatic mutations on cancer development, we have shown the somatic mutation spectrum in both high-risk and low-risk groups using disulfidptosis-PSEC (Fig. 8). Overall, the high-risk group exhibited a mutation frequency similar to low-risk group (95.96% vs. 94.86%). The 20 genes with the highest frequency of mutations were presented in two distinct risk groups, as shown in Fig. 8A, B. The high-risk group had a higher proportion of mutations associated with undesired biological activity, such as PTEN, compared to the low-risk group (34% vs. 58%). The overview of the mutations in Fig. 8C, D revealed that missense mutation, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP), and C > T for both high- and low-risk groups in overall population. The subgroup analysis of CN-low group was presented in Supplementary Fig. S6, which showed PTEN was equivalent for high-risk and low-risk group (53% vs. 53%), while FGFR2, KRAS, and CHD4 were comparable for high-risk and low-risk group [(27% vs. 15%), (27% vs. 12%), and (20% vs. 11%), respectively]. The missense mutation, SNP, and C > T mutation was also more common for CN-Low group.

Identification of potential drugs

CLUE was used to investigate potential treatments by comparing the DEGs across high- and low-risk groups. Based on the screening criteria, fluticasone-propionate, amiodarone, BRD-K47000838, refametinib, nicorandil, and PSB-1115 were considered as potential drugs for the high-risk group, as presented in Table 2. Interestingly, through subgroup analysis of the CN-low group, methantheline, BRD-K42205652, vortioxetine, cinanserin, and alaproclate were considered as potential drugs for this group. Serotonin receptor antagonist was the primary mode of action for the drugs above. Besides, acetylcholine receptor antagonists, Src inhibitors, and Syk inhibitors were also the potential targets for treating the CN-low group.

Discussion

In this study, we constructed and validated a 9-DRGs prognostic signature, disulfidptosis-PSEC, for EC patients. A predictive model for clinical prognostic stratification was constructed by integrating clinical parameters such as age, sex, and cancer stage with disulfidptosis-PSEC. Further research has been conducted to annotate disulfidptosis-PSEC, indicating a possible involvement of disulfidptosis in EC. Besides, a subgroup analysis was carried out in the CN-low group. Although the role of disulfidptosis in malignancies is sparse, evidence related to disulfide stress indicates the possible significance and clinical relevance of disulfidptosis in cancers. Our results demonstrated a potential utility of DRGs in predicting the prognosis of EC and highlighted positive significant role of 9 DRGs in EC prognosis.

Lately, the study revealed a strong association between lncRNAs and various types of cancer, including gastric cancer [26], colorectal cancer [27], and breast cancer [28]. Shetty et al. have reported that certain lncRNAs behave as "molecular sponges," capturing tumor suppressor miRNAs and thereby impeding their activities. This results in disruption of transcriptional control, which in turn promotes the growth and spread of uterine corpus endometrial cancer. Several investigations have documented the probable involvement of disulfidptosis in the development of endometrial cancer [29]. The research findings indicated that the high-risk group was primarily corresponded to pathways such as extracellular matrix interactions and cell adhesion molecules, implying a probable correlation with the potential for metastasis [30]. Nevertheless, there is still a need for further investigation into the underlying cause of cell death induced by disulfide bond formation-related proteins, as well as the potential therapeutic targeting of this process. Specifically, it is important to determine if glucose starvation leads to the rapid buildup of disulfide bond molecules in endothelial cells.

To evaluate the potential association between disulfidptosis and prognostic outcomes, we also further investigating the potential underlying mechanisms. Based on prior research, we have conducted disulfidptosis-PSEC which offers benefits in forecasting OS and DFS. We also provided a nomogram combining clinical variables and risk score for clinical application. Additionally, we have constructed a nomogram that integrates clinical factors and risk scores, which may be readily applied in clinical settings. One further benefit of our study involves the investigation of the possible efficacy of immunotherapy in the treatment of EC. In conclusion, a robust copper-related model has been established to accurately forecast prognosis, thereby holding considerable importance in the context of clinical decision-making.

The prognostic model developed in the study based on 22 DRGs that were differentially expressed in EC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues. These genes play a role in various cellular pathways and functions that can influence disulfidptosis. As highlighted by Liu et al. [15], the vulnerability of the actin cytoskeleton to disulfide stress is a key feature of disulfidptosis. DRGs such as IQGAP1, MYH9, and MYH10, which are directly involved in actin cytoskeleton dynamics, underscore the pathway through which disulfidptosis exerts its effects [31–33]. Disulfidptosis is linked to metabolic stress conditions such as glucose starvation, where intracellular disulfide accumulation becomes lethal for cells highly dependent on specific metabolic pathways [14]. DRGs such as GYS1 is involved in glucose metabolism and can influence the fibrotic response by altering cellular energy states. These genes not only participate in cellular structural integrity and metabolism but also in signaling pathways that modulate the behavior of stromal cells and the extracellular matrix, contributing to fibrosis and tumor progression. Then nine DRGs were identified from the 22 DRGs and included in the prognostic model, including OXSM, PRN1, MYH9, MYH10, IQGAP1, DSTN, NDUFA11, GYS1, and CD2AP. These genes may play distinct roles in EC development and progression. For example, MYH9, IQGAP1, and CD2AP have been previously implicated with tumor progression and prognosis in different cancer types, such as breast and gastric cancer [32–35]. Their involvement in EC found in our study suggests a similar role in regulating signaling pathways that influence EC aggressiveness. NDUFA11 gene was associated with mitochondrial function, overexpression of NDUFA11 may result abnormal cell metabolism [36]. Regulating dysfunctional metabolism via NDUFA11 gene may demonstrate anti-tumor effect. These DEGs are not only associated with disulfidptosis, a form of cell death, but also with various cellular and biological processes that impact tumor progression and prognosis. By integrating these functional networks, the prognostic model provides a broader view of the molecular landscape of EC beyond disulfidptosis, offering potential targets for therapeutic intervention and markers for predicting disease outcomes.

We also found that high-risk group were often characterized by lower infiltration of immune cells like cytotoxic T cells and higher levels of immunosuppressive cells such as Tregs and M2 macrophages, which could inhibit anti-tumor immune responses, allowing for tumor progression and leading to poorer prognosis. For example, the Spearman correlation analysis in Fig. 1F showed that NDUFA11 positively correlates with Tregs, which are more prevalent in high-risk groups and contribute to immune evasion by the tumor. While low-risk group typically showed higher infiltration of effector immune cells, such as CD8 + T cells and NK cells, which are associated with better anti-tumor activity and improved survival outcomes. There is almost no difference in tumor immune microenvironment in CN-low group. However, a higher enrichment of M2 macrophages was found for high-risk group among CN-low group, which may be associate with a worse prognosis. Functional enrichment analyses revealed that the PPAR signal pathway significantly differed between high- and low-risk group. It was found that PPAR agonists may inhibit EC proliferation and reduce the migratory capabilities [37, 38]. However, evidence is still scare for this pathway in EC. Gene mutation analyses for CN-low group suggested that FGFR2, KRAS, and CHD4 gene was more frequently mutated in high-risk group. These tumor-promoting genes may contribute to the undesirable disease outcome, however, drugs targeting FGFR2 and KRAS has progressed in other tumors and may improve the prognosis of this subset of endometrial cancer patients.

Potential drugs were identified for high-risk EC in our study, such as drugs targeting potassium channel. Potassium channel, efflux can lead to changes in cell membrane potential, which in turn affects intracellular redox state and iron metabolism, and these changes may be associated with a variety of cell death [39]. Interestingly, serotonin receptor antagonist and acetylcholine receptor antagonist were found to have a potential benefit for CN-low high-risk patients. This may reverse the tumor promoting effect of 5-HT and acetylcholine observed in tumor microenvironment [40, 41].

The disulfidptosis-PSEC model can be integrated into clinical practice to stratify EC patients based on their risk levels. Patients identified as high-risk may require intensified monitoring and tailored therapy aimed at specific molecular pathways associated with disulfidptosis. Furthermore, by identifying distinct molecular and cellular characteristics of high-risk groups, including immune cell infiltration and gene mutation profiles, the model facilitates the selection of personalized therapeutic regimens. For instance, our findings suggest that patients in the high-risk group, particularly those with specific gene mutations like FGFR2 or KRAS, may benefit from targeted therapies that are currently under clinical investigation for other cancers. The identification of drug targets, such as potassium channel modulators and serotonin receptor antagonists, highlights the potential for developing new therapeutic agents. Before clinical adoption, it is crucial to validate the disulfidptosis-PSEC model in larger, independent cohorts which would ensure the robustness and generalizability of the model across diverse patient populations. Additionally, prospective clinical trials should be designed to evaluate the efficacy of integrating the disulfidptosis-PSEC model into treatment planning. Further research is needed to elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms of disulfidptosis in EC since understanding these mechanisms will provide insights into how the identified therapeutic targets can be effectively manipulated to improve patient outcomes.

There are several limitations that should be noted in this study. Firstly, it is important to acknowledge the potential presence of selection bias in this retrospective analysis. This bias may arise from the exclusion of patients who either had insufficient data or were considered ineligible for sequencing. This holds significant relevance for patients who are unable to undergo surgical intervention due to comorbidities or the presence of tumor metastases. Secondly, there are currently limited studies on disulfidptosis resulting in only 22 disulfidptosis-related genes identified in the literature results. The number of DRGs are limited in this study. Thirdly, experimental validation for candidate gene expression in UCEC at both RNA and protein levels was not conducted externally due to the absence of related data, potentially impacting results’ reliability. Further validation of these genes will be pursued in larger cohorts in subsequent studies. Fourthly, the inability to include additional variables in our composite model was attributed to the absence of clinical data such as comorbidities, family history, treatment history, medical imaging data, etc. Fifthly, the present work used an exploratory approach to test possible medications, and it is recommended that future studies undertake additional efforts to validate these findings. Finally, the biological profile was conducted utilizing publicly available cohorts, and a concise analysis of the probable mechanisms of the genes in the model was presented. There is a need for additional prospective trials and greater investigation into the biochemical mechanism underlying disulfidptosis and EC.

In conclusion, we analyzed the differential genes associated with disulfidptosis, including molecular subtypes, and developed a signature for disulfidptosis, known as disulfidptosis-PSEC, using 9 DRGs. Nine DRGs were subsequently utilized to develop a prognostic risk model, and the accuracy of this model in predicting the prognosis of EC patients was confirmed. Subsequently, we proceed to investigate the potential role of tumor immunity in this characteristic, lighting the potential of specific small molecule medicines and immunotherapy for improved treatment of patients with EC. Furthermore, future investigations into the molecular mechanism of certain biomarkers will enhance our comprehension of the development of EC and assess its practicality in clinical settings.

PepLess: A computational tool developed to optimize SPOT synthesis and analyze related immunoassay data
Authors: Raíssa Medina-Santos, Leticia Lopes-de-Souza, Clara Guerra-Duarte, Daniel Freire Zampelin, Silvio Sanches Veiga, Vanete Thomaz-Soccol, Ricardo Andrez Machado-de-Ávila, Franck Molina, Carlos Chavez-Olortegui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSPOT synthesis is a simple and relatively inexpensive technique for synthesizing a large number of peptides on a cellulose membrane. However, this method becomes exhaustive and impractical when handling a large quantity of sequences with diverse specifications, often resulting in the generation of redundant peptides. Addressing this challenge, we present PepLess, an innovative computational tool designed to analyze multiple sequences simultaneously, thereby reducing the utilization of spot synthesis membranes. By avoiding the production of redundant peptides, PepLess offers a substantial improvement in efficiency and cost-effectiveness, minimizing both expenses and equipment usage. This work outlines the challenges associated with excessive peptide generation, introduces the functionality and benefits of PepLess, and concludes with validation through a case study employing Peruvian-origin Loxosceles laeta spider transcriptome sequences for SPOT membrane production. In this case study we observed approximately a 21 % reduction in required spots for membrane production. Another case study was also performed applying the spike glycoprotein sequences of SARS-CoV-2, and a reduction of 75 %–77 % was identified. The analytical technique within PepLess enables clearer and easier identification of the identified, providing an efficient and automated approach to peptide analysis.Keywords: Peptide, Spot synthesis, Bioinformatic, Automation, PepLess

Introduction

Peptides have emerged as invaluable tools in several fields, from biomedical research to drug discovery, due to their diverse applications and functional versatility. They can be useful from the elucidation of protein-protein interactions and epitope mapping to serve as therapeutic agents and diagnostic biomarkers [1,2]. Among the methodologies developed for peptide synthesis, SPOT synthesis has stood out since its first description in 1992, offering a rapid and efficient way to generate a multitude of peptides simultaneously [3,4].

Initially introduced as a solution for peptide synthesis, SPOT synthesis changed in 2002 with the introduction of cellulose membrane sheets. This technique enabled the dispensation of liquid samples onto membranes, forming circular spots upon absorption, which could then be subjected to further chemical or enzymatic transformations [4]. This approach facilitates the creation of numerous peptide arrays on a single membrane and expanded their applications, such as epitope mapping, analysis of post-translational modifications in proteins and protein-protein interactions [[4], [5], [6], [7]].

One of the most popular applications of SPOT synthesis, the mapping of interaction sites by overlapping peptides, normally uses a significant number of peptides with high identity. This application can be largely used in several areas, especially in the identification of linear epitopes from monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies [8,9]. While SPOT synthesis offers unparalleled convenience and versatility, its utility is often impeded by the challenges associated with synthesizing a large number of peptides with subtle yet crucial differences [10]. The exhaustive generation of peptide sequences requires significant equipment and reagent expenditures, often resulting in redundant responses and overwhelmed analyses [10]. Moreover, the manual analysis of vast datasets and results generated from employing SPOT synthesis becomes increasingly impractical as the number of sequences escalates. This limitation highlights the need for innovative approaches to streamline peptide synthesis and analysis, particularly in fields where rapid and efficient techniques are essential.

Our group has been using the SPOT technique over the years in the context of mapping neutralizing epitopes from animal toxins [[11], [12], [13]]. These mapped epitopes can then be produced synthetically produced and utilized as immunogens to replace native toxins in antivenom production. This approach has been especially successful in the case of Loxosceles spider venom. Also known as brown spiders, these animals are very small, and the amount of crude venom produced per individual is limited, typically arount 4 μL, containing approximately 65–100 μg of protein [14], making it challenging to obtain enough venom for research and antivenom production. Indeed, epitopes mapped by SPOT in the phospholipase D toxin LiD1, from Loxosceles intermedia, were represented as synthetic peptides and were able to induce neutralizing antibodies [11,12]. Lastly, the construction and characterization of a chimeric multiepitopic protein (rMEPLox), containing spot-mapped epitopes of the main proteins (phospholipases D, metalloproteases and hyaluronidases) of the venoms from Loxosceles genus spiders, conferred protection against the envenomation dermonecrotic effects of envenomation on an immunized rabbit [13].

As demonstrated for Loxosceles venom, integrating innovative peptide synthesis methodologies, such as SPOT synthesis, with the study of venom toxins holds promise for advancing our understanding of venom composition and the development of more effective antivenom therapies. Aligned with this, optimizing the number of peptides synthesized on cellulose membranes is essential to save time, reagents, and costs, thereby enhancing the overall feasibility of the analysis.

Following this premise, this study introduces the PepLess system, a pioneering computational tool designed to streamline SPOT synthesis and subsequent data analysis processes. By accommodating a multitude of sequences, conducting fragmentation analysis, and eliminating redundant peptides, PepLess advances the application of SPOT synthesis within a fully computational environment. Furthermore, it extends its functionality to include post-experimental membrane analysis, thus offering a comprehensive solution to the challenges posed by traditional SPOT synthesis methodologies.

Material and methods

PepLess

PepLess tool was developed using the Python programming language, which was chosen due to its widespread application in biological scientific data and the availability of numerous libraries supporting script automation. The name of PepLess was crafted by combining “peptide” and “less”, reflecting its primary function of reducing the number of peptides required for synthesis. Prof. Daniel Freire Zampelin collaborated in this work by assisting with the outline of functional requirements governing what the tool should achieve. A comprehensive list of needs was compiled through the analysis of activities commonly performed without the use of the PepLess tool (Table 1).

The PepLess workflow is organized into two main parts: one is dedicated to peptide membrane design and the other to the analysis of SPOT immunoassay results (Fig. 1).

PepLess tool workflow. Users provide upload a FASTA file along with length and offset files and (a) PepLess identifies non-identical sequences, (b) fragments them, and (c) generates graphical representation, (d) alignments, and (e) membranes. Post (f) membrane synthesis and (g) application in immunoassays, (h) users upload the scanned membrane file to the tool, which then (i) evaluates the intensity of each spot using Image J, (j) generates a digital membrane reflecting the results, (k) produces alignments featuring the selected positive values, and (l) generates a secondary artificial membrane containing exclusively positive results.

In the first part, PepLess receives protein sequences (a) and fragments them into overlapping peptides of user-defined size (length and offset) (b). From this set, the software selects unique peptides to minimize redundancy while preserving coverage. Each peptide is clearly annotated with information about its origin within the input sequences, and graphical representations are generated to facilitate data exploration (c). An alignment step (d) is included to improve user understanding regarding sequence overlap and redundancy. The tool then generates an output file compatible with automated peptide synthesizers (f) to produce peptide membranes, which will be subsequently used in SPOT immunoassays (g).

In the second part, after the immunoassay is conducted and the membrane is scanned (h), PepLess enables analysis of the membrane image using ImageJ software (version 1.53n) to identify reactive spots (i). It creates a mirrored virtual membrane aligned with the scanned image, annotated with signal intensities and peptide information (j). A filtering step is then applied, where peptides are re-aligned and scored based on a user-defined cutoff (k). Finally, a new artificial membrane is generated containing only the peptides that meet the selected threshold (l), facilitating focused downstream validation or refinement experiments.

As part of the peptide processing step, PepLess performs two internal alignments to map each generated peptide back to its original full-length protein sequence (Fig. 1-d and Fig. 1-k). This mapping enables the identification of the exact position of each peptide within the source sequence and helps verify the completeness and coverage redundancy of the specific region of interest in a peptide or protein. In addition, peptides can be aligned to known or predicted epitopes, when available, to assess how effectively they identify the immunologically relevant regions.

For the development of step (a), a script was required to utilize a file containing sequences and two additional files specifying peptide sizes and user-defined fragmentation coverage. From these three files, the script generates new files featuring the peptides after fragmentation. Subsequently, a second script, overseeing step (b), assesses the existence of each new peptide considering the previous ones from the same file, to avoid sequence redundancy. If the new peptide proves to be unique, it is incorporated into the final peptide file; otherwise, it is rejected. To differentiate peptides selected in step (c), a tag with the name of the source sequence is applied to each added peptide in the final file. In cases of rejection, the rejected peptide tag is then appended to its similar peptide incorporated to the final peptide file, accumulating tags as necessary, avoiding information loss.

In pursuit of visual results for the user, in step (d), scripts implementing the Matplotlib library (version 3.4.0) were employed to create a graphical representation based on data generated during fragmentations. On the other hand, for presenting data in alignment format, script integration with alignment programs and open-source analyses was performed, following the guidelines of each respective system's manual. Following all analyses, the script responsible for step (e) generates files with all user-performed fragmentations compatible with major systems for synthesizing peptides in cellulose membranes which use the SPOT technique.

Post-synthesis and use of these membranes in immunoassays, automated analyses of results become feasible. To accomplish this, a script connects a pixel analysis system with a macro. The macro will identify the markers indicating the beginning and end of each membrane for pixel analysis of the selected area. It is essential for the user to draw a 2 mm line manually or using any available software. This line needs to be drawn near the first and last spots on the membrane after conducting the immunoassays. This step is crucial to ensure the smooth analyses of the membranes, as the system relies on identifying these two marks to select the correct area of dots on the membrane. The line should be visible and in a dark color for optimal detection. Horizontal lines will be created across the area at all spots, indicating the location of each point. Then the system will provide the average of the pixels to calculate a single pixel intensity value for each point, enabling PepLess to analyze all spots and produce results, such as a virtual membrane showing the reactive SPOTs and a table with all statistical data from these analyses.

Although PepLess was developed to interface with automated peptide synthesizers, the output files can also be used for manual synthesis. Users performing manual SPOT synthesis can adapt these generated peptide lists according to their specific experimental setups. The tool provides clearly labeled peptide sequences and coordinates, which can be manually transferred to membrane grids or synthesis templates as deemed appropriate by the user.

Case-study

In the first case-study we chose to analyze protein-coding derived from the transcriptome data of the Peruvian Loxosceles laeta spider venom gland. A FASTA file containing transcripts of each toxins group that are more relevant to the venom's toxicity was created based on data from Medina-Santos et al., 2022, and incorporated into the PepLess tool. Representative sequences of proteins/toxins from the crude venom were added [15]. The following sizes were then informed to the tool for the fragmentation: Lengths 10, 12 and 15; Offsets 1, 2, 3 and 5. Both FASTA file and size files were used on PepLess, and the graphic and alignment results were analyzed. The best coverage was chosen from the output graphs of reduction and number of produced peptides. A one-sided paired t-test was conducted to evaluate whether the number of peptides generated by the PepLess tool was significantly lower than the original design, based on 12 paired comparisons across different length and offset parameters.

A specific file was generated to be used into the ResPep SL membrane synthesis automatic device (IntavisAG, Bioanalytical Instruments, Germany). Cellulose membrane synthesis was carried out using the ResPep SL synthesizer. The best coverage of the fragmentation for each spot (fifteen penta decapeptides superimposed by three residues) was selected and the membrane was synthesized covering the main toxins of Loxosceles laeta venom gland, following the protocol described by Laune in 2002 [16].

Once the membrane had been synthesized, the reactivity of antibodies against the peptides was evaluated. To verify the reactivity of the four metalloproteases present there, an anti-metalloprotease serum produced against LALP-1of L. intermedia was tested [17]. The membrane was washed with phosphate saline buffer (PBS pH 7.4) and blocked with 3 % TBS and 0.1 % Tween overnight. Following a new wash, the membrane was incubated with the serum for 120 min (1:100 dilution) and washed again with TBS 0.1 % Tween. Antibody binding was detected using alkaline phosphatase-linked anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibodies (Sigma, 1:2000 dilution). After 30 min of adding the BCIP-MTT substrate in 0.4 % MgCl2 in saline citrate buffer (CBS pH 7.0), the reactive regions of the membrane were identified by the formation of bluish precipitates on the spots that contain peptides recognized by the serum antibodies tested.

To perform the analysis of the SPOT immunoassay results, the membrane was scanned after the experiment, and was later regenerated, to be reused in other assays. The regeneration process consists of washing for 10 min with reagent A (1 % SDS; 8 M Urea; 0.1 % 2-Mercaptoethanol), reagent B (ethanol/water/acetic acid in proportions 50:40:10 vol/vol/vol) and at the end with methanol, to help dry the membrane.

To evaluate antibody reactivity with the other peptides in the membrane, the following sera were used: hyper-immune serum from horses immunized with Loxosceles laeta venom from Instituto Nacional de Salud (INS) – Lima, Peru (dilution 1:1000) and hyper-immune serum from horses immunized with Loxosceles spp. from the Production and Research Center for Immunobiological (CPPI) – Curitiba, Brazil (dilution 1:1000). As a control of these experiments, the same assays were performed with non-immune sera (dilution for rabbits 1:100 and, for horses, 1:1000) (data not shown).

After each SPOT immunoassay of the Peruvian L. laeta membrane using different antibody samples, the membranes were photo-documented and the images were uploaded into the PepLess tool and the Image J (version 1.53n) macros were used for automatic reading of pixels across the entire membrane. From the average obtained from each circumference, pixel intensity values were made available for analysis.

Values above 75 % of the mean of all values were considered positive. Aiming to look for potential epitopes, the positive sequences were then aligned with each other and with the chimeric multiepitopic protein rMEPLox (predicted and characterized by Lima and collaborators in 2018) [13].

In addition, to compare the results from this work with a standard membrane synthesis method, the same assay was performed using the sequence from the LALP-LP3 metalloprotease presented in Medina-Santos et al., 2019 [18]. Without using PepLess, the membrane occupied 61 spots (Length 15, Offset 3: L15O3). The same sequence was used in PepLess, and a second membrane was produced, with 53 spots (L15O3).

Additionally, to assess the applicability of PepLess in large-scale scenarios, we conducted a complementary case study analysis using 109 spike glycoprotein sequences of SARS-CoV-2 obtained from UniProt (retrieved using the keyword “COVID-19 spike”). These sequences, ranging from approximately 1200 to over 7000 amino acids in length, were used as input in FASTA format. The same fragmentation parameters applied to the first case study (Lengths 10, 12 and 15; Offsets 1, 2, 3 and 5) were used. The generated data were processed in PepLess, and the results regarding the number of peptides before and after redundancy filtering were recorded, followed by graphical analysis. A one-sided paired t-test was conducted to evaluate whether the number of peptides generated by the PepLess tool was significantly lower than the original design, based on 12 paired comparisons across different length and offset parameters.

Results and discussion

Tool development

Considering the need to optimize cost and space in SPOT membrane synthesis, but without losing efficiency and accuracy, PepLess tool was developed. The PepLess script requires three main data from the user: (i) a file in FASTA format with all the chosen sequences; (ii) a text file named “length” with the size of the peptides; and (iii) a second text file named “offset” with the amount of overlapping amino acids.

After the upload of those three files, PepLess selects and generates a new file with only the non-identical sequences, and then perform the fragmentations in this new file, according to the user specifications of lengths and offsets. From there, the system informs the number of generated peptides with and without the redundant fragments and produces a graphical result presenting the chosen lengths and offsets, with and without redundant peptides, as well as their percentage of reduction between analyses.

Following the graphical generation of the results, the tool provides an alignment option using MAFFT (Software for Multiple Alignment of Sequences) (version 7). Here, the user can observe and manipulate the alignment of all the peptides generated with the initial sequences or solely focus on the alignments between the peptides. This stage allows the mapping of interaction sites and facilitates the production of fewer membranes with the same peptide coverage as before.

From the alignment, PepLess presents different options for synthesizing these peptides on cellulose membranes. After selecting the file corresponding to the preferable alignment, the user will be able to examine a graphical interface (Graphical blueprint) illustrating the “map” of the SPOT membrane. The graphical blueprint viewer includes an interactive feature: when the user moves the cursor over a peptide spot, detailed information is displayed, including the protein of origin. This file will be then available to be uploaded to the peptide synthesizer system, which will produce the membrane. Following this step, the user can synthesize the peptides on the membrane and employ it in immunoassays.

Succeeding the immunoassay, each membrane has to be scanned, and their images processed, with the help of our developed macros, on Image J (version 1.53n). Then, the lines indicating the beginning and end of each membrane will be identified for pixel analysis of the selected area (Fig. 2A). Horizontal lines will be generated in the place of all points, indicating the location of them. Following this analysis, Image J will also measure the pixels average to calculate a single pixel intensity value for each point (Fig. 2B). From these data, the system provides: (i) a graphical result corresponding to the intensity value of each point; (ii) an Excel file with the peptide sequence corresponding to each intensity value of all spots; and (iii) a computed membrane “mirrored” with the corresponding data from the uploaded image (Fig. 2C).

Analysis screen from Image J and mirrored graphic membrane. (A) Delimitation and reading lines used for identifying membrane boundaries and correctly positioning the grid over the scanned image are marked in black. (B) Image J screen presentation where the analysis is automated from macros and the pixel average results are plotted in graphs. (C) Image J screen presentation with a second screen where a graphic membrane mirrored represents the membrane scanned image, produced from the pixel values identified in each spot.

After completing the Image J procedure, PepLess provides the intensity values for each spot, as well as a new alignment of the peptides, using a cutoff score selected from the previous results: default (excluding only background values), 25 %, 50 %, 75 %, or a personalized value chosen by the user. Only the spots considered positive by the user's analysis will be present on the alignment. The new alignment generates an artificial membrane containing only the peptides that meet the chosen cut-off value (with the possibility to change the cut-off multiple times), providing all relevant data from this work in a single location.

The SPOT technique is very useful for different types of biological studies and biotechnological products, but it needs adaptations and improvements. For this reason, this technique has been the focus of discussion in specialized research groups, when observing the difficulty in carrying out large-scale analyses. In addition, few projects aim to optimize the most critical points of this technique. Therefore, PepLess tool can be applied not only in the production of membranes but also in the analysis of their results, allowing to save time and synthesis reagents.

The features available in the PepLess system aim to support decision making, allowing the researcher to use reasoning and critical analysis of their results at each step for their subsequent choices. When creating a system with biological applications, the translation of ideas into the platform should be user-friendly, ensuring a more effective and widespread contribution to the scientific community [19,20]. In this way, the structure of the tool allows the users to always modify their decisions, evaluate the data in different specifications and choose which ones are the most relevant to them.

Case-study

For the case-study, we chose to analyze proteins/toxins retrieved from sequences from transcriptomic data sequences of the Peruvian Loxosceles laeta spider venom gland. A total of sixteen sequences from the most relevant toxins were uploaded (four metalloproteases, five phospholipases-D, one serine protease, one hyaluronidase, four insecticidal/ICK toxins and one TCTP) [15]. The desired peptides sizes of fragmentation (Lengths 10, 12 and 15/Offsets 1, 2, 3 and 5) were also provided and using this information, a graph was generated illustrating the quantities of peptides with (in red) and without (in blue) redundancy across all desired sizes and the percentage of reduction between the comparisons (Fig. 3A). Based on the data provided by the PepLess tool, the L15O3 (length 15 and offset 3) coverage, which comprised 599 peptides against 755 peptides compared to the original size (21 % reduction), was selected for further membrane synthesis. The reduction ranges from 21 % to 24 %, depending on the combination of length and offset used (Fig. 3B). A paired t-test confirmed that the reduction in peptide number was statistically significant when compared to the original design (df = 11, p = 0.0004386). Using the chosen size, the PepLess tool generated a graphical blueprint of synthesis (Fig. 3C) and specific file to be integrated into the ResPep SL membrane synthesis equipment. In addition, for comparison purposes, the complete peptide list generated by the PepLess tool for the selected design (L15O3) is provided as Supplementary File 1. This document follows the same format as that produced by the ResPep SL software and can therefore be used for membrane synthesis within the ResPep SL system.

To compare the results from this work with a standard membrane synthesis method, an immunoassay using anti-metalloprotease serum from L. intermedia was performed using a membrane containing the LALP-LP3 produced from Medina-Santos et al., 2019 [18]. In this membrane, the LALP-LP3 data was not processed by the PepLess tool, thus requiring the use of a larger number of spots (61 spots). The same LALP-LP3 sequence was incorporated into a membrane produced by PepLess (53 spots, a 13 % of reduction). This analysis was made to compare the effectiveness of using or not the PepLess tool. When the same anti-metalloprotease serum from L. intermedia was tested, it was possible to observe the same main reactive peptides (Fig. 7), highlighting that the reduction in the number of peptides did not impair the obtained results.

Expanding upon the analysis of Loxosceles laeta toxins, another case study was performed to explore the scalability of PepLess using longer protein sequences. A total of 109 spike glycoprotein sequences from SARS-CoV-2, retrieved from UniProt, were used and the same fragmentation settings previously used were applied (Lengths 10, 12 and 15; Offsets 1, 2, 3 and 5). The PepLess tool successfully processed the entire dataset, and a substantial reduction in the number of synthesized peptides was observed when comparing redundant versus non-redundant outputs. The results demonstrate the significant optimization that PepLess can achieve when handling large and complex protein datasets (Fig. 8A). A reduction of around 75 %–77 % was observed, depending on the combination of length and offset applied (Fig. 8B). A paired t-test confirmed that the reduction in peptide number was statistically significant when compared to the original design (df = 11, p = 9.28e-05). As in the first case study, the tool also generated synthesis-ready files and graphical blueprints compatible with SPOT membrane synthesizers, but further analyses were not carried out for this case study. The results reinforce the adaptability and robustness of the PepLess platform, particularly in large-scale immunoassay applications.

Importantly, unlike existing tools such as OptiTope, which ranks predicted immunogenic epitopes, PepLess adopts a conceptually distinct strategy focused on experimental design optimization [21]. To our knowledge, no currently available software provides this specific functionality, making PepLess a novel contribution to the field of peptide array design.

Conclusion

This work introduces PepLess, the first system designed to enhance and streamline the SPOT synthesis technique ensuring complete sequence coverage by removing only redundant overlapping peptides, while maintaining each region at least once. From both results of case-studies, we conclude that the system is fully operational, covering the entire workflow. The tool successfully handled both biological datasets, generating non-redundant peptide sets that maintained biological relevance. It was applied to a membrane containing peptides derived from Loxosceles laeta spider transcripts, where it enabled a 21 % reduction in the number of peptides without compromising immunoassay outcomes. In the second case-study (SARS-CoV-2), up to 75 % of reduction was made using this tool. This supports the tool's potential to optimize laboratory workflows by saving resources and reducing analysis time.

PepLess is not yet publicly available. Nevertheless, controlled access to the tool will be provided upon request after publication, allowing us also to incorporate early feedback into future versions to ensure smoother user experience. Improving the interface and usability for non-specialist users will remain a central focus for the upcoming development phases. In addition, future improvements, such as additional automated procedures for background correction, spot quality assessment, and direct integration with downstream statistical analysis would further distinguish PepLess and increase its scientific impact. With PepLess, the process of identifying peptides and distinguishing sequences becomes simpler and more practical. This approach not only reduces the consumption of laboratory resources but also speeds up the analysis, contributing to more cost-effective and time-efficient research.

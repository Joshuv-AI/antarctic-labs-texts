Design of immediate release combination preparation containing alkalizer and lansoprazole for mechanistic understanding of enhanced gastric stability, pharmacokinetics, and intragastric pH variability in healthy human subjects
Authors: Il-Ki Hong, Dinh Hoai Nam, Youngju Jeong, Jun-Bom Park, Chulhun Park, Beom-Jin Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We investigated the microenvironmental pH (pHM)-modulating mechanism and intermolecular interaction of alkalizers and their impact on the aqueous stability, release profiles of drugs and alkalizers, pharmacokinetics, and intragastric pH variability in 46 healthy human subjects of lansoprazole (LAN), an acid-labile model drug. Preliminary screening of five alkalizers (CaCO3, MgO, NaHCO3, meglumine, and L-arginine) in LAN-loaded wet granules under simulated gastric fluid demonstrated that calcium carbonate (CaCO3) appeared to be the best stabilizing agent for LAN in a dose-dependent manner according to solution pH changes and drug contents. Immediate-release fixed-dose combination tablets (IFT) containing LAN and CaCO3 were prepared via a dual granulation process using a fluidized bed dryer (FBD) and/or a high shear mixer (HSM). Unlike the commercial enteric-coated granule-loaded Lanston® capsule, IFT released LAN and CaCO3 simultaneously, achieving over 90% drug release within 30 min while maintaining its aqueous stability in pH 1.2 gastric fluid via the acid-neutralizing capacity of simultaneously released CaCO3. The increased modulation of pHM and the intermolecular complex of LAN with calcium ions are crucial for stabilizing LAN under low pH conditions. The optimal IFT formulation demonstrated long-term stability for 3 years, maintaining drug content and dissolution rates while liberating impurities (<0.2%) within regulatory guidelines. IFT demonstrated bioequivalence with the commercial enteric-coated Lanston® capsule with decreased “time to reach the maximum plasma concentration” and displayed superior intragastric pH control, maintaining pH above 4 for a longer duration over 24 h in a cross-over design in 46 healthy human subjects. The current novel IFT containing acid-labile LAN and CaCO3 can be used to substitute current enteric-coated LAN tablets for the immediate release and rapid onset of LAN without chemical degradation in the gastric fluid.

Introduction

Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are widely prescribed acid-suppressing medications that irreversibly inhibit H+/K+-ATPase in gastric parietal cells (Bruno et al., 2019). Although highly effective in reducing acid secretion by over 95% when adequately dosed, their therapeutic utility is often limited by formulation challenges owing to their pH-dependent solubility and stability (Srebro et al., 2022). Among PPIs, LAN has emerged as a particularly effective agent, demonstrating rapid symptom relief in gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and duodenal ulcers compared to other medications (Tolman et al., 2000). However, similar to other PPIs, LAN displays pH-dependent stability that is highly sensitive to acidic environments, leading to formulation challenges such as the use of alkalizers or enteric coating to enhance its gastric stability while maintaining its bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy (Kuroda et al., 2006).

Most commercial formulations utilize enteric coating or pelletization technologies to protect drugs from gastric acid degradation (Maderuelo et al., 2019). For example, Kang et al. demonstrated that the incorporation of alkalizers into hydroxypropyl methylcellulose-based matrix systems effectively modulated the microenvironmental pH (pHM) and enabled dual release while minimizing gastrointestinal irritation in vivo (Kang et al., 2017). Although effective, these approaches cause delayed absorption and onset of action, limiting immediate relief of acid-related symptoms (Tonini et al., 2003). Comparable findings have been reported for weakly basic drugs, in which the inclusion of acidifiers in solid dispersions effectively sustained an acidic microenvironment through controlled acidifier release, pHM stabilization, and maintenance of the amorphous structure, thereby causing markedly enhanced dissolution and minimal recrystallization (Tran et al., 2010b). The incorporation of alkalizers into amorphous solid dispersion–based orodispersible tablets markedly improved the dissolution and stability of acid-labile drugs under gastric conditions, demonstrating the current advancements in pH-modulated formulations beyond conventional enteric-coated systems (Alhamhoom et al., 2024). Although they are simple to manufacture, enteric-coated formulations fail to address the critical clinical need for rapid symptom control, particularly in patients with severe acid reflux or nocturnal acid breakthroughs (Hunt et al., 2007).

Among various alkalizers, CaCO3 was identified as the most effective pHM modulator on the basis of acid-neutralizing capacity, gastric stability of LAN, and an acceptable safety/regulatory profile, consistent with its long-standing use as a pharmaceutical excipient in oral solid dosage forms (Al Omari et al., 2016). CaCO3 also exhibits rapid acid-neutralizing properties through chemical reactions with gastric acid (Feng et al., 2024; Voropaiev and Nock, 2021). Upon ionization to Ca2+ and CO32-, it provides more efficient acid neutralization than sodium bicarbonate at lower doses based on ion stoichiometry (Feng et al., 2024). Conceptual evidences for employing alkalizers to regulate pHM in solid dosage systems was well established by Tran et al., who systematically explained the synergistic roles of pHM modulation, structural amorphization, and molecular interactions in enhancing the dissolution and stability of ionizable drugs (Tran et al., 2010a). This enhanced efficiency was attributed to the divalent nature of calcium ions and the dual neutralizing capacity of carbonate ions (Feng et al., 2024). For instance, 600 mg of CaCO3 can effectively modulate gastric pH to create a protective microenvironment (Voropaiev and Nock, 2021). The particle size, surface area, and crystalline structure of CaCO3 contribute to its rapid neutralizing action and sustained pH-modulating effects (Jimoh et al., 2017; Som et al., 2016). This unique combination of properties presents an opportunity to develop an innovative nonenteric formulation approach. Similarly, Nguyen et al. demonstrated that the incorporation of divalent metal alkalizers, particularly magnesium oxide, into solid dispersions established strong molecular interactions with benzimidazole derivatives and significantly enhanced the gastric stability via pHM modulation (Van Nguyen et al., 2017).

We investigated the effects of different alkalizers (CaCO3, MgO, NaHCO3, meglumine, and L-arginine) on gastric stability, solution pH, pHM, and intermolecular interactions using instrumental analysis. The pHM was determined potentiometrically using a pH meter equipped with a surface pH electrode. Detailed intermolecular interactions between LAN and the alkalizer in the granules were characterized using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR). Furthermore, surface morphologies were visualized using field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM).

In the present study, we hypothesized that a systematically selected low-dose divalent alkalizer, dispersed in a well-designed dual-granulation matrix, can modulate the pHM and form a transient intermolecular complex with LAN sufficient to protect the drug during gastric residence, thereby enabling immediate drug release without an enteric coating. To test this hypothesis, five alkalizers (CaCO3, MgO, NaHCO3, meglumine, L-arginine) were screened; CaCO3 was identified as the most effective and was formulated into an immediate-release fixed-dose combination tablet (IFT, T7) via a two-step granulation process using a fluidized bed dryer and a high-shear mixer. The resulting IFT achieved rapid and complete release of both CaCO3 and LAN, maintained long-term stability for 36 months, and demonstrated bioequivalence to the commercial enteric-coated Lanston® capsule in 46 healthy human subjects, with a shorter Tmax and superior 24-h intragastric pH control. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to (i) systematically compare five alkalizers for pHM modulation of LAN-loaded granules and tablets with depth-resolved pH measurement, (ii) mechanistically characterize the intermolecular complex between LAN and CaCO3 using DSC, PXRD, and FT-IR, and (iii) demonstrate bioequivalence and superior pharmacodynamic pH control of a non-enteric, CaCO3-based IFT versus the enteric-coated reference product in a large cross-over clinical study, thus providing a clinically meaningful alternative to conventional enteric coating for acid-labile PPIs.

Materials and methods

Materials

LAN was obtained from Hetero Drugs Ltd. (Hyderabad, India). The CaCO3 was supplied by Bioka Funka Kogyo Co. (Tokyo, Japan). MgO, sodium bicarbonate, and L-arginine were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Meglumine was purchased from Merck KGaA (Darmstadt, Germany). Povidone K30 and K90 were supplied by BASF SE (Ludwigshafen, Germany). Croscarmellose sodium (CCS; Vivasol®) and microcrystalline cellulose (MCC; Vivapur® 101) were obtained from JRS Pharma GmbH & Co. KG (Rosenberg, Germany). Sodium stearyl fumarate (SSF) was provided by JRS Pharma GmbH Co. KG (Rosenberg, Germany). Ethanol was purchased from Duksan Pure Chemicals Co., Ltd. (Ansan, Korea). Crospovidone (Kollidon® CL) was supplied by BASF SE (Ludwigshafen, Germany). All other chemicals and reagents were of analytical grade. Purified water was prepared using a Milli-Q water purification system (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA).

Preliminary screening of alkalizers for LAN stability

Preparation of wet granules containing LAN and alkalizer

Six different formulations (F1–F6) were prepared by wet granulation to evaluate the impact of different alkalizers on the stability of LAN. Each formulation contained 30 mg of LAN along with a selected alkalizer at a fixed concentration of 800 mg to modulate the pHM and protect the drug from acidic degradation. The alkalizers investigated were CaCO3, MgO, NaHCO3, meglumine, and L-arginine. Povidone K30 (3 mg) was incorporated as a polymeric binder to ensure the uniformity and proper granule formation. This addition was crucial for enhancing the mechanical strength of the granules and ensuring content uniformity. Moreover, CCS (3 mg) was employed as a super disintegrant to facilitate rapid disintegration and dissolution upon administration, promote water penetration, and accelerate the breakdown of the granules in aqueous environments. The wet granulation process involved mixing LAN with the selected alkalizer and Povidone K30, followed by controlled addition of a granulating solvent to form a cohesive mass. Next, this mass was passed through a sieve to obtain uniform granules, which were subsequently dried to optimize moisture content and stability. The dried granules were blended with CCS before being subjected to further physicochemical evaluation. Effect of alkalizers on gastric stability

The stability of LAN in six different LAN-alkalizer wet granule formulations (F1-F6) was evaluated in simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2). The stability testing was conducted in 900 mL of artificial gastric fluid (pH 1.2) at 37.0°C ± 0.5°C using USP dissolution apparatus II (paddle method) at 100 rpm for 2 h, simulating the average gastric residence time of oral dosage forms.

The granules were placed in a 900 mL dissolution vessel maintained at 37.0°C ± 0.5°C, with a paddle rotation speed of 100 rpm. The stability was monitored over 2 h to evaluate the extent of LAN degradation and the protective effect of each alkalizer. The optimal alkalizer, namely CaCO3, was selected based on its ability to effectively neutralize the acidic medium, prevent degradation, and maintain LAN integrity throughout dissolution. The results of these studies are crucial for determining the most suitable alkalizer for enhancing the stability of LAN and ensuring its efficacy in acid-labile drug matrix systems.

Effect of CaCO3 contents

The literature estimates the average fasting gastric fluid volume in humans to be between 50 and 70 mL (Mudie et al., 2014a; Nader et al., 2016). A standard 100 mL of simulated gastric fluid was selected as the reference medium to account for potential variations. Two different agitation speeds (250 and 350 rpm) were applied using a magnetic stirrer to simulate lower and higher gastric motility conditions, respectively. These agitation rates were selected on the basis of previous in vitro gastric-simulation studies, in which stirring speeds of 200–400 rpm applied to a small volume (50∼150 mL) of simulated gastric fluid have been shown to reproduce the hydrodynamic shear and Reynolds number encountered by oral dosage forms under fasted-state gastric motility (Kostewicz et al., 2014; Koziolek et al., 2013; Mudie et al., 2014b; Vertzoni et al., 2010).

Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM)

The surface morphology and microstructural characteristics of LAN, CaCO3, and their granules were evaluated using field-emission scanning electron microscopy. Selected samples, including pure LAN, CaCO3, and their granules, were examined to assess the impact of different granulation methods on particle characteristics. The samples were mounted on carbon adhesive tape, sputter-coated with gold to enhance conductivity, and analyzed using a field-emission scanning electron microscope operated at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV. Multiple fields were captured at different magnifications (10k× and 50k×) to comprehensively evaluate particle size, shape, surface texture, and aggregation patterns. These morphological observations provide insights into the physical transformations that occur during granulation processes and their potential influence on formulation performance.

pHM measurement of LAN-alkalizer tablet

Based on the preliminary formulation of wet granules as shown in Table 1, six LAN-alkalizer oblong tablets (836 mg total weight, containing 30 mg LAN, 800 mg alkalizer, and 6 mg magnesium stearate) with uniform dimensions and consistent hardness were prepared using the direct compression method. The pHM was measured based on the methodology established by Lee et al., with slight modifications (Tran et al., 2008). The pHM was determined potentiometrically using a Mettler Toledo SevenDirect SD20 pH meter equipped with a surface pH electrode (Greifensee, Switzerland) specifically designed for solid measurements.

Individual tablets were placed in Petri dishes (30 mm in diameter) and exposed to simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2). Only two to three drops of the pH 1.2 buffer solution were carefully applied to the tablet surface at predetermined time intervals (5, 10, 15, 30, and 60 min) to maintain the tablet's structural integrity during measurement. This minimal-volume approach prevented complete tablet disintegration while allowing sufficient moisture for accurate pH determination.

Following the exposure periods, the tablets were removed from the dissolution medium, and excess surface water was allowed to drain for 5 min at ambient temperature. The gradient pHM from the surface to the tablet center was determined as a function of time by sectioning the tablets into three distinct measurement zones. Based on the fractional dimensions relative to the tablet thickness, the measurement points were designated as d/d0 = 0 (surface), d/d0 = 1/2 (mid-depth), and d/d0 = 1 (core), where d0 represented the distance from the tablet edge to the center, assuming that the pH gradients from the center to both tablet margins exhibited similar profiles.

The pHM values were recorded immediately upon contact of the electrode with each designated tablet region. Measurements were performed in triplicate for each formulation and time point. The pHM was plotted as a function of time at different fractional distances (d/d0) to evaluate the diffusional behaviors and pHM gradient establishment of the alkalizer throughout the tablet matrix, correlating with the release profiles of alkalizers and LAN simultaneously.

Solid-state characterization of LAN–alkalizer formulations

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)

Thermal analysis of pure LAN, precipitated CaCO3, and the formulated granules (F1-F6) was performed using differential scanning calorimetry (Discovery DSC 25 System, TA Instruments). Samples (3–5 mg) were placed in aluminum pans and sealed with a lid. The thermal behavior was analyzed at a heating rate of 10°C/min from 20°C to 300°C under nitrogen purge (50 mL/min). The resulting thermograms were evaluated for the melting point, phase transitions, and potential molecular interactions between LAN and the alkalizers.

Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)

The crystalline properties of LAN, CaCO3, and the formulated granules (F1-F6) were characterized using a multi-purpose X-ray diffractometer (SmartLab 9 kW, Rigaku). Samples were loaded into a sample holder and scanned over a 2θ range of 5–40° at a step size of 0.02° and scanning rate of 2°/min using CuKα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) at 40 kV and 30 mA. Diffraction patterns were analyzed to identify characteristic peaks, assess crystallinity changes, and detect potential solid-state transformations or interactions between the components.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR)

Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy was conducted to examine the potential molecular interactions between LAN and the alkalizers in the formulations. Samples of pure LAN, CaCO3, and the formulated granules (F1-F6) were analyzed using an FT-IR spectrometer (Nicolet iN10 MX). Spectra were recorded in the wavenumber range of 400–4000 cm-1 at a resolution of 4 cm-1, averaging 32 scans per spectrum. Characteristic absorption bands were identified and compared across samples to evaluate the chemical interactions and structural integrity of LAN in the presence of different alkalizing agents.

Several formulation compositions (T2-T7) were prepared with different distributions of components between the two granulation methods to identify the optimal processing conditions. The first granulation step involved processing LAN using the FBD with Povidone K90 as the binding agent and CCS as the disintegrant. This method provides gentle processing conditions and uniform coating of LAN particles, protecting the acid-labile drug from moisture and potential degradation during manufacturing. This technique generated dense, uniform granules with optimized flow properties while facilitating rapid disintegration and acid neutralization upon administration. Microcrystalline cellulose and sodium stearyl fumarate were added to the final blend as filler and lubricant, respectively. The compressed tablets were designed to provide immediate release of CaCO3 for rapid acid neutralization while ensuring LAN stability in the gastric environment. In vitro dissolution studies

Dissolution profile of LAN from matrix formulations

Dissolution studies were conducted to evaluate the release profiles of LAN from different formulations (T2-T7) and compare them with the commercial reference Lanston® capsule. Tests were performed in quadruplicate using USP dissolution apparatus II (paddle method) with 900 mL of simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2) maintained at 37 ± 0.5°C and a paddle rotation speed of 100 rpm. Samples (5 mL) were withdrawn at predetermined time points (0, 5, 15, 30, 45, and 60 min) and replaced with an equal volume of fresh dissolution medium. Samples were filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane filter, and LAN concentrations were determined using a validated HPLC method. All results are expressed as mean percent drug released ± standard deviation.

Comparative release kinetics of LAN and CaCO3

The optimal formulation (T7) was further evaluated to monitor the simultaneous release of both LAN and CaCO3 into simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2) for 60 min or under sequential pH conditions (pH 1.2 for 2 h followed by pH 6.8 shift for 2 h) that mimic gastrointestinal transit. The dissolution test was conducted using the same apparatus and conditions described above. Samples were collected at predetermined time intervals (0, 5, 15, 30, 45, and 60 min) and analyzed for both LAN by HPLC and CaCO3 content by complexometric titration with EDTA, respectively. Commercial enteric-coated Lanston® capsules were tested under identical conditions for comparison. Tests were performed in sextuplicate (n = 6), and the mean percent release ± standard deviation was calculated for both components.

Quantitative analysis of LAN and calcium content

Quantitative analyses of LAN and CaCO3 were performed using HPLC and atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS), respectively, following the general testing methods of the Korean Pharmacopoeia. HPLC analysis was conducted using an Agilent LC system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), and atomic absorption measurements were performed using a Thermo Scientific atomic absorption spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

The HPLC system, for the analysis of LAN, was equipped with a UV detector set at 285 nm. Chromatographic separation was achieved using an Inno Column C18-SB (4.6 × 250 mm, particle size 5 μm) or equivalent column. The mobile phase consisted of acetonitrile, water, and triethylamine in a ratio of 400:600:10 (v/v/v), with the pH adjusted to 7.0 using phosphoric acid. The flow rate was maintained at 1.0 mL/min, and the injection volume was 20 μL. The column temperature was kept at 25°C ± 2°C.

The calcium content of CaCO3 was determined using the AAS. The measurements were performed at a wavelength of 422.7 nm using a deuterium (D2) quadline lamp for background correction. A nitrous oxide-acetylene flame was used at a gas flow rate of 3.9 L/min. Analytical measurements were conducted under optimized conditions, including proper dilution of the samples to fit within the linear range of the calibration curve. Standard solutions of calcium were prepared from certified reference materials for calibration.

Comparative pharmacokinetics and intragastric pH variability

Study design

A randomized, single/repeated-dose, 2 × 2 crossover study was conducted to compare the pharmacokinetic (PK) and intragastric pH variability of two 30 mg LAN-loaded formulations: the test, IFT (T7), containing 30 mg LAN and 600 mg CaCO3, and the reference, enteric-coated Lanston® capsule. The study was conducted at the Clinical Trial Center of Chungnam National University Hospital (Daejeon, Korea), and bioanalytical analysis was performed by PPC Korea, Inc. (Seoul, Korea).

Study protocol

The 46 healthy human subjects were enrolled and admitted to the clinical trial center 2 days before each period (Period 1: D-2, Period 2: D13). They received a standardized dinner between 16:00 and 18:00 and went to bed at 22:00, followed by an overnight fast for at least 10 h, except for water. On the day before drug administration (Period 1: D-1, Period 2: D14), after physical examination and vital sign measurements, a pH probe was inserted through the nasal cavity for 24-h gastric pH monitoring following standardized procedures.

Pharmacokinetic assessment

Blood samples for pharmacokinetic analysis were collected extensively throughout both study periods, with 62 samples per subject. During Period 1, samples were collected pre-dose (0h) and at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 24 h post-dose on day 1; pre-dose on day 6; and following the identical timepoints on day 7 for steady-state assessment. For Period 2, the same comprehensive sampling schedule was implemented on day 15 (single-dose), with a pre-dose sample on day 20 and the complete profile on day 21 (steady-state).

Plasma was separated by centrifugation and stored under the appropriate conditions until analysis. LAN plasma concentrations were determined using a validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) method. Pharmacokinetic parameters, including AUC0-t, AUC0-∞, Cmax, Tmax, and t1/2, were calculated using non-compartmental analysis. The bioequivalence between the test and reference formulations was assessed according to the regulatory guidelines.

Pharmacodynamic assessment

Gastric acid suppression was evaluated by continuous 24-h pH monitoring in 46 healthy human subjects. Monitoring was conducted during specific intervals: baseline measurements (D-1 to D1 in Period 1; D14 to D15 in Period 2) and after repeated dosing (D8 to D14 in Period 1; D21 to D22 in Period 2).

The primary pharmacodynamic parameter was integrated gastric acidity, which was calculated as the area under the hydrogen ion concentration-time curve. The percentage decrease in integrated gastric acidity from baseline was determined for both formulations. The comparative efficacy of the test and reference formulations was assessed using descriptive statistics.

Statistical analysis

Pharmacokinetic parameters were calculated using non-compartmental analysis with Phoenix WinNonlin 8.3 (CERTARA USA Inc.) and SAS® (Ver 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., NC, USA). The analysis employed a linear mixed-effects model, considering sequence, period, and treatment as fixed effects and between-subject variation as random effects. For repeated administration, AUCss, τ geometric mean ratios, and 90% confidence intervals were calculated.

The pharmacodynamic analysis focused on the percentage of time at pH > 4, comparing the baseline-adjusted values between the formulations. The primary endpoint analysis used geometric mean ratios with 90% confidence intervals, with equivalence demonstrated within the 0.8–1.25 range. Secondary endpoints were evaluated using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as appropriate, to compare both within-treatment (pre- vs. post-dose) and between-treatment (test vs. reference) changes from baseline. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS® (Ver 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results and discussion

Effect of alkalizers on gastric stability

The effect of alkalizers on the degradation rate of LAN-loaded granules in pH 1.2 gastric fluid is described in Fig. 2. F1 (non-alkalizer control) displayed minimal protective effect, lowering to content less than 30% for the first 30 minutes, confirming the critical role of alkalizer incorporation for pHM modulation. The organic alkalizers, F5 (meglumine) and F6 (L-arginine) exhibited limited neutralization effectiveness with the minimal buffer volume exposure maintain LAN stability below 30% of the initial content for 2h. The other alkalizers, F3 (MgO) exhibited moderate buffering capacity under the constrained acidic conditions, maintaining maximum about 85% of the initial content for 2h. Among the five alkalizers tested, two compounds, CaCO3 and sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) demonstrated superior protective effects, maintaining LAN stability above 90% of the initial content for 2 h. CaCO3 has emerged as an optimal alkalizer based on several advantageous characteristics. Its rapid ionization into Ca2+ and CO32-in the gastric environment enables efficient acid neutralization even at relatively low concentrations. This mechanism effectively protects against acid-labile LAN while maintaining physiological gastric pH regulation. Furthermore, the well-established safety profile of CaCO3, its extensive history in treating peptic ulcers, gastritis, and hyper acidic conditions, and its classification as an endogenous substance make it particularly suitable for long-term administration. The selection of CaCO3 is aligned with the development of a formulation that combines therapeutic efficacy and optimal safety for chronic use. The stability data demonstrated that the CaCO3-based formulation provided consistent protection against LAN under aggressive gastric conditions, suggesting its potential as a viable alternative to traditional enteric coating approaches for acid-labile drug delivery systems.

FE-SEM of LAN-alkalizer granules

Based on the alkalizer screening in Section 3.1, CaCO3 was the best alkalizer that simultaneously satisfied (i) sufficient acid-neutralizing capacity to maintain LAN stability above 90 % in simulated gastric fluid for 2 h and (ii) an acceptable safety and regulatory profile (i.e., without the cardiovascular sodium load of NaHCO3 or the limited neutralization of MgO, meglumine, and L-arginine) (Al Omari et al., 2016). The other four alkalizer–LAN combinations were therefore not advanced to the dual-granulation formulation stage and, for the same reason, were not further characterized by FE-SEM. The manufacturing process produced evident differences in the particle morphology. The 1st granulation (LAN granules) processed by FBD method (containing 30 mg LAN, 3 mg Povidone K90, 3 mg CCS, and 200 mg CaCO3) displayed transformed sheet-like structures with increased particle size, reflecting gentler processing conditions that helped preserve drug integrity while providing a protective coating. The complementary granulation approaches were expected to yield different disintegration patterns and modulation of the pHM under gastric conditions, with HSM-processed CaCO3 granules providing rapid alkalizer release to create a protective pH environment for acid-labile LAN from the FBD granules.

The other alkalizers, F3 (MgO) and F4 (NaHCO3), exhibited moderate buffering capacity under the constrained moisture conditions, reaching maximum pHM values of 2.34 ± 0.19 and 2.88 ± 0.02, respectively. As depicted in Fig. 4 (C), the tablet core measurements revealed that F2 provided the most effective pH control, with values ranging from 4.04 ± 0.04 to 6.46 ± 0.03. Depth-dependent pHM analysis confirmed that CaCO3 successfully established an optimal pHM above 4.0 even under minimal moisture conditions, which is essential for maintaining LAN stability and enhancing dissolution performance under acidic gastric conditions. This observation is consistent with previous findings that alkalizer-mediated pH modulation within matrix systems effectively stabilizes acid-sensitive drugs and controls their release kinetics (Kang et al., 2017). Analogous findings with Eudragit L100 pellet-in-ODT systems, which delay release under acidic conditions and switch to rapid release at pH 6.8, underscore the significance of maintaining pellet and matrix integrity to sustain a defined microenvironment for pH-triggered performance (Al-Hashimi et al., 2025). The ability to achieve effective pH modulation with only two to three drops of buffer solution demonstrates the practical applicability of this alkalizer approach for the in vivo gastric protection of LAN.

Thermal characterization of LAN–alkalizer formulations by DSC

The DSC thermograms of pure LAN, CaCO3, and their granules (F1-F6) are depicted in Fig. 5. Pure LAN exhibited a sharp endothermic peak at approximately 178°C, corresponding to its melting point, followed by decomposition. This distinct melting endotherm confirmed the crystalline nature of the drug. CaCO3 showed no significant thermal events in the studied temperature range (20–300°C), displaying a relatively flat baseline typical of inorganic compounds with melting points beyond the experimental range.Fig. 5DSC thermograms of pure LAN, CaCO3, and their granules (F1-F6).Fig. 5

DSC thermograms of pure LAN, CaCO3, and their granules (F1-F6).

An analysis of the formulations revealed varying degrees of interaction between LAN and alkalizing agents. Formulation F2 (containing CaCO3) substantially preserved the crystalline structure of LAN, as evidenced by the retention of the characteristic melting peak with minimal reduction in intensity. These results suggest limited solid-state interaction between LAN and CaCO3, indicating good physical compatibility in the formulation.

Formulations F3, F4, and F5 displayed significant modification of the LAN thermal profile, with F4 exhibiting a pronounced endothermic depression around 110°C, suggesting potential interaction between the drug and sodium bicarbonate. F5 displayed a distinct endothermic event near 120°C not present in pure LAN, indicating possible eutectic formation or molecular interaction with the alkalizing agent.

F6 demonstrated the most significant alteration in thermal behavior, with multiple thermal events including a sharp endothermic transition at approximately 250°C, suggesting extensive interaction between LAN and the organic alkalizer. Formulation F1 maintained the partial crystallinity of LAN, although with a reduced peak intensity compared to the pure drug.

These thermal analysis findings provide valuable insights into the compatibility profile between LAN and different alkalizing agents, supporting the selection of CaCO3 (F2) as the optimal alkalizer for formulation development because of its minimal interaction with the active pharmaceutical ingredient while maintaining adequate acid-neutralizing capacity.

An analysis of the formulations revealed varying degrees of crystallinity preservation, depending on the alkalizing agent used. Formulation F2 (containing CaCO3) maintained the characteristic LAN peaks, particularly the distinctive peak at 6.8°, although with reduced intensity compared to that of the pure drug. This suggests that LAN retained its crystalline structure in the presence of CaCO3 with minimal solid-state transformation.

Formulations F3 (MgO), F4 (NaHCO₃), and F6 (L-arginine) showed significant reduction or complete disappearance of the LAN crystalline peaks, particularly at 6.8°, indicating substantial amorphization or molecular dispersion of the drug. An analysis of the spectra of formulations F1-F6 revealed that all formulations maintained the principal characteristic peaks of LAN without significant shifts or disappearance of major bands. This observation suggests no strong chemical interactions between LAN and different alkalizing agents at the molecular level. The preservation of the key functional group bands, particularly the S=O stretching vibration of LAN, indicated that the chemical structure of the drug remained largely intact in all formulations.

The minor spectral differences observed among the formulations can be attributed primarily to the physical presence of different alkalizing agents rather than to chemical interactions. The relatively unchanged FT-IR profiles across all formulations suggested that the compatibility between LAN and the alkalizers observed in the dissolution studies was not due to chemical modification of the drug, but rather to the physical pH-modulating effect of the alkalizers in the microenvironment surrounding the LAN particles. This finding is consistent with the previous results for esomeprazole systems, in which the incorporation of alkalizers into polymeric matrices induced hydrogen bond formation with sulfonyl and benzimidazole groups, thereby mitigating acid-catalyzed degradation through localized pH elevation and molecular complexation. Similarly, alkalizer incorporation within solid matrices has been demonstrated to alter hydrogen bonding and van der Waals interactions, maintain an optimal pHM, and stabilize acid-labile drugs (Tran et al., 2010a; Van Nguyen et al., 2017). This spectroscopic evidence complements the thermal analysis data and supports the use of alkalizing agents to create a protective microenvironment for LAN without inducing chemical modifications that might affect its therapeutic efficacy.

The chemical stability of LAN is particularly noteworthy, considering its known susceptibility to acid-catalyzed degradation. The maintained stability could be attributed to the effective pHM modulating matrix system created by the optimized formulation process (T7). The minimal changes in both the assay and impurity profiles suggested successful protection of the acid-labile drug in the presence of CaCO3, even during long-term storage. These findings are consistent with those of previous stability studies on pHM-modulated solid dispersions, which demonstrated that the preservation of the pHM and strong drug–alkalizer molecular interactions are essential for preventing recrystallization and dissolution loss under high-temperature and high-humidity conditions (Tran et al., 2011b). These comprehensive stability data prove that the developed formulation maintains its physical and chemical integrity throughout the proposed shelf life of 36 months under normal storage conditions. These results support the commercial viability of this non-enteric-coated pH-modulating matrix system as a stable alternative to conventional enteric-coated LAN formulations.

From a manufacturing standpoint, the proposed dual-granulation process relies exclusively on standard unit operations (sieving, blending, fluid-bed drying [FBD] or high-shear mixing [HSM] granulation, drying, and direct compression) that are already established at commercial scales using widely available equipment platforms such as Glatt or Diosna granulators, with typical batch sizes in the 300–600 kg range. More importantly, the scientific contribution of the present work is to demonstrate that a pH-modulating matrix incorporating CaCO3 can confer PPI stability comparable to that achieved by multi-layer enteric-coated systems, without recourse to the sequential drug layering, sub-coating, enteric polymer coating, top-coating, and capsule encapsulation typically required for conventional enteric LAN products (Fang et al., 2014; Maderuelo et al., 2019). While a quantitative cost analysis lies beyond the scope of the present pharmaceutical investigation, the reduction in the number of independent unit operations and the elimination of solvent- or aqueous-polymer-based coating steps suggest that the matrix approach may offer practical advantages in terms of process simplification and shorter manufacturing cycle time, which would warrant a dedicated industrial evaluation in future work.

Safety rationale of CaCO3 as a formulation excipient in a PPI-combination immediate-release tablet

CaCO3 at high chronic doses have been associated with several well-characterized safety concerns. These include transient alterations in gastrointestinal motility (Maton and Burton, 1999), acid rebound following abrupt withdrawal (Hade and Spiro, 1992; Preclik et al., 1989), and intestinal phosphate binding in patients with impaired renal function (Rodriguez-Stanley et al., 2004; Shaman and Kowalski, 2016). Milk–alkali syndrome (Patel et al., 2013) and vascular calcification in long-term hyperphosphatemic or chronic-kidney-disease populations (Eknoyan, 2017) represent additional considerations at cumulative high exposures. These effects, however, are dose- and duration-dependent and are reported predominantly with chronic high-dose antacid or phosphate-binder therapy (> 1,500–3,000 mg/day elemental calcium) in patients with hyperphosphatemia, impaired renal function, or milk-alkali predisposition. The optimized IFT (T7) developed here contains 600 mg CaCO3 per tablet (≈ 240 mg elemental Ca2+), corresponding to < 10 % of the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) for calcium in adults (2,500 mg/day) (Ross et al., 2011). This single-dose strength is consistent with widely marketed OTC antacid tablets that carry a decades-long clinical safety record (Ching and Lam, 1994; Maton and Burton, 1999; Salisbury and Terrell, 2018).

In the present IFT, CaCO3 is co-administered with LAN, a proton-pump inhibitor that irreversibly blocks gastric H+/K+-ATPase and pharmacologically antagonizes the gastrin-driven acid-rebound mechanism classically associated with CaCO3 monotherapy (Hade and Spiro, 1992; Preclik et al., 1989). The combined use of a PPI with a carbonate/bicarbonate alkalizer is clinically precedented by FDA/EMA-approved products such as omeprazole/sodium bicarbonate (Zegerid®) and esomeprazole/sodium bicarbonate (Howden, 2005; Katz et al., 2007). Clinical and pharmacokinetic studies of these PPI–alkalizer combinations have confirmed safe and effective use across broad patient populations over extended follow-up medications (Castell et al., 2005; Howden et al., 2009). Furthermore, the rapid and essentially complete release of both CaCO3 (> 90 % within 15 min) and LAN (> 95 % within 30 min) in pH 1.2 gastric fluid confirms that the alkalizer is dissolved and fully ionized during its short gastric residence, minimizing the likelihood of intact CaCO3 particles reaching the small intestine to act as an intestinal phosphate binder or drug chelator at the therapeutic dose (Preclik et al., 1989; Rodriguez-Stanley et al., 2004; Shaman and Kowalski, 2016).

It should also be noted that CaCO3 has a well-established profile as a pharmaceutical excipient in oral solid dosage forms, where it is used as an alkalizer, buffer, and diluent in compressed tablets (Al Omari et al., 2016). In our IFT, CaCO3 therefore functions as a transient micro-environmental pH modulator that stabilizes acid-labile LAN during its few-minute gastric transit and enables immediate release without an enteric coating. This role is pharmacologically distinct from chronic antacid therapy or phosphate binding in hyperphosphatemic chronic kidney disease patients. Patients with advanced chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2), hyperphosphatemia, or documented milk-alkali syndrome will be excluded from the indicated population in line with the product label (Dağlı and Kalkan, 2017; Salisbury and Terrell, 2018), consistent with the established CaCO3 risk considerations in these vulnerable subpopulations (Eknoyan, 2017; Patel et al., 2013). Within this narrowly defined context and at the modest once-daily dose used (600 mg CaCO3), the risk/benefit balance of CaCO3 is favorable and consistent with documented PPI–carbonate combination products (Weberg et al., 1990).

Conclusion

An immediate release IFT containing LAN and CaCO3 was successfully developed. The modulation of pHM, the intermolecular complex of LAN, and the alkalizer are crucial for stabilizing LAN in low-pH gastric fluids. Among the alkalizers, CaCO3 was selected as the optimal solution. However, the efficient dispersion of CaCO3 through a two-step granulation process, with 200 mg in the first FBD granulation and 400 mg in the second HSM granulation, could form an optimal protective microenvironment for IFT against gastric fluid without requiring enteric coating protection. The optimal IFT (T7) provided a programmed sequential release of CaCO3 followed by LAN and demonstrated bioequivalence with the reference, Lanston® capsule, while exhibiting a slightly rapid onset of action. Furthermore, intragastric pH monitoring for 24 h confirmed superior acid suppression, maintaining a pH > 4 for an extended duration of action. The IFT formulation not only overcomes the limitation of the delayed onset of enteric coating but also maintains therapeutic efficacy. Consequently, the current findings provide a viable non-enteric and immediate release formulation strategy that maintains gastric stability of acid-labile LAN, which is preferable for the substitution of the current enteric-coated LAN preparations.

Multifaceted Effects of Starch Branching Enzyme and Soluble Starch Synthase Gene Editing in Rice with Different Wx Genotypes
Authors: Suting Yang, Yanxin Wang, Mengning Wang, Haiyan Zheng, Zhenqi Wang, Xinyan Xie, Jie Hu, Jiayuan Liu, Wentao Song, Lichang Zhang, Yunqiao Liu, Ming Luo, Guangyi Chen, Xiaoqiong Chen, Yongxiang Liao, Kangxi Du, Duo Xia, Hao Zhou, Xianjun Wu, Hongyu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRice endosperm, the major edible portion of the grain, plays an important role in regulating blood glucose and preventing intestinal diseases by increasing its resistant starch (RS) content. Previous studies have shown that suppressing amylopectin biosynthesis via genome editing can increase RS content. However, the influence of different Waxy (Wx) allelic backgrounds on RS accumulation in edited lines has not been systematically evaluated. In this study, we used glutinous rice Yunan Heixiangnuo (HXN) with a nonfunctional wx allele and indica rice Yixiang 1B (YX1B) with a weak Wxb allele as backgrounds. We simultaneously knocked out SSSIIIa, SBEI, SBEIIa, and SBEIIb using CRISPR/Cas9, and systematically analyzed changes in RS content, rice quality, and yield traits. The results showed that, in the HXN background, multigene knockout did not significantly alter amylose or RS content but largely maintained favorable eating quality. In contrast, in the YX1B background, quadruple-gene knockout lines exhibited an increase in amylose content from 17.7% to 53.7% and an increase in RS content to 2.48%, representing a 4.35-fold increase over the wild type, while gel consistency and seed-setting rate were significantly reduced. Scanning electron microscopy revealed that multigene knockout markedly remodeled starch granule structure, shifting from dense polygonal granules to loosely packed spherical particles and resulting in a floury endosperm. Collectively, enhancement of RS content through multigene editing was influenced by Wx gene function. Although the wx allele failed to increase RS content, it still participated in the regulation of grain quality and yield traits by modulating starch structure. Starch is the primary storage component of rice, accounting for approximately 80–90% of seed dry weight and serving as a major energy source for humans.(Le Corre et al. 2010; Miao and Hamaker et al. 2021). It consists mainly of amylose and amylopectin, with amylose playing a central role in rice consumption and grain quality. Amylose content strongly influences texture and end-use properties, making it a key determinant of eating quality. From a nutritional perspective, starch can be classified into rapidly digestible starch, slowly digestible starch, and resistant starch (RS). Resistant starch limits the accessibility of starch molecules to amylase and is therefore resistant to digestion. Acting in a manner similar to dietary fiber, resistant starch contributes to glycemic regulation, lipid metabolism, and intestinal health. (Shu et al. 2009; Deng et al. 2024; Shen et al. 2022). However, conventional rice contains low levels of RS, typically accounting for less than 3% of cooked rice (Ordonio et al. 2016; Patterson et al. 2020). Increasing RS content in rice grains is therefore considered a promising strategy to improve nutritional quality and support dietary-based approaches to chronic disease management.

Rice starch biosynthesis depends on the coordinated action of multiple enzymes, among which starch branching enzymes (SBEs) play a pivotal role in amylopectin formation, including OsSBEI, OsSBEIIa, and OsSBEIIb (Ke et al. 2025). OsSBEIIa and OsSBEIIb exhibit partial functional redundancy, whereas OsSBEIIb is specifically expressed in the endosperm and serves as a major determinant of amylopectin structure (Tanaka et al. 2004). By altering amylopectin chain-length distribution, SBEIIb influences starch gelatinization behavior and digestibility (Chen et al. 2022). In addition, loss of OsSSSIIIa has been shown to increase RS content in cooked rice under specific Wx genetic backgrounds and to affect starch granule morphology and physicochemical properties (Huang et al. 2024; Fujita et al. 2022).

The Waxy (Wx) gene, encoding granule-bound starch synthase I (GBSSI), is the primary genetic determinant of amylose content in rice. Its alleles include the high-expression Wxa (~ 20–30% amylose), the low-expression Wxb (~ 10–20%), and the loss-of-function wx (< 2%), which is typical of glutinous rice varieties. Previous studies aimed at increasing RS content have mainly focused on multigene editing in the Wxa background, often accompanied by substantial reductions in eating quality and yield performance (Wang et al. 2024).

To examine the effects of multigene editing on RS accumulation under different Wx allelic backgrounds, a CRISPR/Cas9-based multiplex genome editing system was employed to simultaneously target four starch biosynthesis-related genes, OsSBEI, OsSBEIIa, OsSBEIIb, and OsSSSIIIa (Ma et al. 2015). Homozygous mutants were generated in both the wx-type glutinous rice cultivar Yunan Heixiangnuo (HXN) and the Wxb-type indica rice cultivar Yixiang 1B (YX1B). The resulting lines were subsequently analyzed for starch composition, grain appearance, eating quality, and agronomic performance. These results provide a reference for future studies aiming to increase RS content in rice with different Wx genotypes through multigene editing.

To assess the effects of multigene editing on RS accumulation under different Wx allelic backgrounds, SBEI, SBEIIa, SBEIIb, and SSSIIIa were simultaneously edited in Wx-type glutinous rice Yunan Heixiangnuo (HXN) and Wxb-type indica rice Yixiang 1B (YX1B). In the HXN background, six homozygous mutant lines were obtained, including SSSIIIa-KO, SBEI/SSSIIIa-KO, SBEIIa/SSSIIIa-KO, SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO, SBEI/SBEIIa/SSSIIIa-KO, and SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO, all carrying SSSIIIa deletions (Fig.S1A–D). Apparent amylose content (AAC) in these single, double, and triple-gene knockout lines ranged from 2% to 4% and did not differ significantly from that of the wild type (Fig. 1A). Consistent with this pattern, RS content also showed no obvious change, which is likely attributable to the nonfunctional Wx allele (Fig. 1B).

By contrast, in the YX1B background, AAC increased with the accumulation of mutated genes. In the SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line, AAC was markedly increased compared with the wild type, accompanied by a substantial elevation in RS content. In contrast, total starch content was reduced (Fig. 1C-D). These results are consistent with previous reports indicating a positive correlation between AAC and RS content (Wang et al. 2025).

Notably, although the relative expression level of Wxb was significantly reduced in the SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line, amylose still accumulated to a high level (Fig.S2 A, B). This observation indicates that amylose accumulation is not solely dependent on a proportional increase in Wx transcript abundance. Instead, it is likely driven by altered carbon partitioning within the starch biosynthetic network. Previous studies have demonstrated that suppression of starch branching enzyme (SBE) activity can reduce amylopectin synthesis, thereby increasing the availability of ADP-glucose for granule-bound starch synthase (GBSSI), which promotes amylose biosynthesis (Han et al. 2019). In addition, reduced SBE activity can limit amylopectin formation and promote the accumulation of more linear glucan chains, further contributing to increased amylose content (Nishi et al. 2001). Moreover, compensatory regulation among starch biosynthetic enzymes, particularly in response to OsSSIIIa deficiency, may further reinforce these metabolic adjustments (Fujita et al. 2006).

Previous studies have shown that elevated levels of soluble starch are often associated with reduced grain quality and yield (Chen et al. 2022; Miura et al. 2021). Consistent with this trend, examination of grain appearance revealed that multigene knockout in the YX1B background resulted in a pronounced floury endosperm, while varying degrees of chalkiness were observed in both genetic backgrounds (Fig.S3 B). In the YX1B background, chalkiness increased approximately five- to six-fold in the SBEIIa/SBEIIb-KO and SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO lines compared with the wild type, resulting in a pronounced opaque floury phenotype. (Fig.S3C). Enzyme activity assays showed that the activities of starch branching enzymes and starch synthases were significantly reduced in grains of the SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line (Fig.S3D, E). In the HXN background, which inherently exhibits a floury endosperm, chalkiness could not be quantitatively assessed (Fig.S3A).

Scanning electron microscopy revealed marked differences in starch granule morphology between wild-type and mutant lines. In wild-type HXN, starch granules were predominantly polygonal, with smooth surfaces and dense packing. With increasing numbers of mutated genes, granules in the mutants became progressively smaller and more loosely arranged, eventually forming spherical particles. An exception was observed in the SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line, in which multiple small granules aggregated into larger, irregular polygonal structures with loose packing (Fig. 1E–K). Starch granules in wild-type YX1B exhibited a similar polygonal shape and were arranged more regularly and packed more tightly. As the number of edited genes increased, granules gradually shifted toward finer, spherical particles with reduced packing density (Fig. 1L–P).

Together, these observations indicate that multigene knockout substantially reshapes starch granule morphology and packing patterns, providing a structural basis for the development of floury endosperm and increased chalkiness.

Cooking and eating quality of rice are strongly influenced by amylose content, and gel consistency is commonly used as an indirect indicator of cooked rice texture and eating quality. In the HXN background, gel consistency of mutant grains remained comparable to that of the wild type and consistently exceeded 80 mm, consistent with the unchanged amylose content (Fig. 1Q). In addition, total protein content decreased significantly as the number of edited genes increased (Fig. 1R). These results suggest that knockout lines in the HXN background largely maintained favorable cooking and eating quality, although additional analyses such as RVA or sensory evaluation would be required for a more comprehensive assessment.

In contrast, in the YX1B background, gel consistency declined markedly with increasing amylose content (Fig. 1S). Specifically, gel consistency decreased to approximately 35 mm and 47 mm in the SBEIIa/SBEIIb-KO and SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO lines, both significantly lower than that of the wild type. Meanwhile, total protein content in mutant grains increased significantly (Fig. 1T). These changes suggest that, in the YX1B background, multigene knockout leading to elevated RS content is accompanied by a reduction in cooking and eating quality.

Previous studies have reported that increasing RS content through genome editing of the amylopectin biosynthesis pathway is frequently accompanied by reductions in yield-related traits (Wang et al. 2025). To evaluate whether similar effects occurred in the present study, major agronomic traits of the knockout lines were systematically examined.

In the HXN background, plant height, panicle length, and grain number per panicle showed no significant differences from the wild type in most knockout lines (Fig.S4 A–E). However, the seed-setting rate was reduced by approximately one-quarter in both triple-gene knockout lines. The thousand-grain weight decreased by 10.9% in the SBEI/SBEIIa/SSSIIIa-KO line and by 19.14% in the SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line (Fig. 1U, W).

In the YX1B background, panicle length and grain number per panicle also showed no significant changes in the knockout mutants. Plant height was significantly reduced in the SBEIIa/SBEIIb-KO and SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO lines (Fig.S4 F-J). In addition, the SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line exhibited an approximately one-quarter reduction in seed-setting rate relative to the wild type, accompanied by a substantial decrease in thousand-grain weight of about 40%.(Fig. 1V, X). Taken together, these results indicate that multigene knockout is associated with reductions in seed-setting rate and grain weight under both Wx genetic backgrounds, with more pronounced effects observed in the YX1B background.

The stickiness of glutinous rice results from natural mutations in the Waxy (Wx) gene that abolish granule-bound starch synthase activity and amylose synthesis. Consequently, most efforts to increase resistant starch (RS) content have focused on cultivars carrying the strong Wxa allele. In this study, we compared RS formation across different Wx allelic backgrounds. In Heixiangnuo (HXN) carrying a nonfunctional wx allele, multigene editing of amylopectin biosynthetic genes failed to increase RS content, indicating that amylose synthesis is essential for RS accumulation. In contrast, in Yixiang 1B (YX1B) with the weak Wxb allele, multigene knockout increased amylose content by more than threefold and RS content by more than fourfold, demonstrating the decisive role of Wx allelic type. Compared with previously reported quadruple-gene knockouts in the Wxa background, RS accumulation in the Wxb background was substantially lower, reflecting functional divergence of Wx alleles in starch biosynthesis and the stronger capacity of Wxa to support RS formation. Previous studies have shown that Wxa encodes a more active GBSSI enzyme than Wxb, resulting in higher amylose synthesis and a greater proportion of long-chain glucans, both of which are positively correlated with RS formation (Larkin and Park 2003). Although both studies involved disruption of amylopectin biosynthetic pathways, the targeted genes differed. In the present study, SSSIIIb disruption likely had a stronger effect on long-chain amylopectin distribution than SBEIIa disruption reported in Wxa backgrounds (Fujita et al. 2011). However, RS accumulation remained markedly lower in the Wxb background despite these potentially stronger effects on chain-length distribution. These results indicate that modifying amylopectin structure alone is insufficient to achieve high RS accumulation when GBSSI activity is limited. Instead, both amylose biosynthetic capacity and amylopectin chain-length architecture are key limiting factors for RS formation (Tetlow and Emes 2014). Therefore, the modest RS increase observed in the Wxb background is likely due to insufficient amylose synthesis and reduced long-chain glucan formation, which together constrain RS accumulation. Although multigene knockout consistently caused yield reductions, the affected traits varied among backgrounds and were not strictly correlated with RS levels. Notably, despite a substantial reduction in estimated grain yield per plant in the SBEI/SBEIIa/SBEIIb/SSSIIIa-KO line, RS yield per plant remained higher than that of wild-type YX1B, indicating a trade-off between grain yield and RS accumulation (Fig. S5). Chalkiness was commonly observed after multigene knockout and was associated with altered starch granule structure and loose packing in all backgrounds. Overall, these results highlight the critical role of Wx alleles in balancing RS content, yield, and grain quality, and provide useful guidance for RS-oriented rice breeding.

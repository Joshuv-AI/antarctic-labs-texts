Transforming hypercholesterolemia management: Spotlight on PCSK9 peptide vaccines
Authors: Amirhossein Sahebkar, Maciej Banach
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractProprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) is a therapeutic target for dyslipidemia and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). Two recent studies published by Fang et al.1 and Zhang et al.2 in Cell Reports Medicine and Cell Reports, respectively, show the efficacy of peptide vaccines in eliciting an antibody response against PCSK9 and reducing plasma cholesterol levels.

Main text

Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) peptide vaccines are emerging as a promising approach in the treatment of dyslipidemia and atherosclerosis, showing notable success in animal models. These vaccinations have demonstrated efficacy in eliciting a robust humoral immune response and lowering plasma PCSK9 levels.2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10 The vaccinations lower LDL-cholesterol (LDL-C) levels and atherosclerotic plaque, enhance cholesterol transport, and lessen inflammation and apoptosis by producing high-titer immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies against PCSK9.2,4,5,6,7,8,9,10

Novel approaches to delivering PCSK9 vaccinations have been investigated in recent studies. Vaccines based on virus-like particles (VLPs) have demonstrated an enhanced ability to enhance plasma PCSK9 clearance.3 In addition to producing long-lasting immune responses and anti-inflammatory effects, nanoliposomal anti-PCSK9 vaccinations have shown efficacy in reducing LDL-C levels in hypercholesterolemic mice.8,9 A recent approach reported by Fang et al.1 in Cell Reports Medicine involved fabrication of a PCSK9 nanoparticle (NP) vaccine through conjugation of PCSK9’s catalytic domain to self-assembled 24-mer ferritin NPs, which is another innovative strategy. This NP vaccination decreased plaque lesion areas and macrophage infiltration in atherosclerotic animals, efficiently produced interfering antibodies against PCSK9, and reduced plasma lipid levels in high-fat-diet-induced and adeno-associated virus-hPCSK9D374Y-induced hypercholesterolemic mouse models. It has been discovered in the study that T follicular helper cells and LDL receptors are necessary for the vaccine to be effective.1

Notwithstanding these developments, difficulties persist in the creation of PCSK9 peptide vaccines. A recent report by Zhang et al. published in Cell Reports posed for the first time the possibility of elevated plasma PCSK9 levels as a result of immune complex formation, which could modify PCSK9’s half-life and exacerbate the vaccine’s effects.2 In the referred study, the investigators showed that vaccination with a human PCSK9 mimic named HIT01 could elicit a strong antibody response in both mice and cynomolgus monkeys, paralleled with reductions in serum LDL-C. However, a 5- to 10-fold increase in serum PCSK9 concentrations was noted after vaccination, which could be attributed to the FcRn-mediated PCSK9 recycling by the generated antibodies, thereby extending PCSK9 half-life and confounding therapeutic effects of the vaccine.2 Whether or not this phenomenon is expected for other PCSK9 peptide vaccines deserves further exploration. Nevertheless, there is a great deal of therapeutic application potential for PCSK9 peptide vaccines. They may be used as a long-term dyslipidemia treatment, providing steady reductions in LDL-C and plasma PCSK9 levels while increasing the expression of LDL receptors.7,8,9 These vaccinations offer a potential therapeutic approach against atherosclerosis through additional mechanisms such as regulating reverse cholesterol transport and blocking apoptosis and inflammation.10 Furthermore, in nonhuman primates, VXX-401, a peptide-based vaccine against PCSK9, showed strong immunogenicity and long-lasting effects on serum LDL-C levels. High-affinity antibodies were produced by this vaccination, which prevented PCSK9’s inhibitory action on LDL-C uptake in hepatic cells and showed acceptable safety and tolerability profile in toxicity studies.11

While experimental studies in animal models have shown that PCSK9 peptide vaccines can induce long-lasting anti-PCSK9 antibody production and improve lipoprotein profiles,7,9 supporting evidence from clinical trials is still lacking. In a phase I, single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study involving 72 healthy subjects, 2 AFFITOPE peptide vaccines, AT04A and AT06A, were evaluated for safety, tolerability, antibody development, and reduction of LDL-C. Subjects received 4 subcutaneous immunizations over 60 weeks. The study found that both vaccines were safe and triggered robust, long-lasting antibody responses that cross-reacted with the physiological target epitope in naive PCSK9 protein. However, only AT04A showed significant LDL-C-lowering activity, with a mean peak reduction of 11.2% and 13.3% from baseline compared to placebo at weeks 20 and 70, respectively. The mean LDL-C reduction for AT04A over the study period was −7.2%. This reduction was associated with PCSK9 target epitope titers above 50, leading to clinically relevant LDL-C reductions with an individual maximal decrease of 39%.12 Both vaccines were safe and well tolerated, with injection site reactions being the most frequent adverse events (63% of all treatment-emergent adverse events). These reactions were mostly mild to moderate, while severe reactions occurred rarely and were transient.12 This further confirms that inducing a humoral response against PCSK9 could be a safe approach, which is consistent with the findings of preclinical studies in rodents and nonhuman primates. However, because different types of peptide vaccines have been introduced, safety aspects such as lack of eliciting autoimmune responses against self-antigens and chronic inflammation should be clinically confirmed for each individual platform. Furthermore, the efficacy of such vaccines needs to be verified in high-risk patients and those with existing atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) receiving maximally tolerated statin therapy. Expected high efficacy and safety of the anti-PCSK9 vaccines, assuming very good therapy adherence, also makes them an optimal tool for familial hypercholesterolemia patients—requiring effective lifelong therapies—as well in those with statin nonadherence or statin intolerance.13

Overall, as a safe and effective LDL-C-lowering therapy, PCSK9 peptide vaccines may represent a broadly accessible and long-acting option to treat hypercholesterolemia and prevent ASCVD, which is still the leading cause of deaths worldwide.

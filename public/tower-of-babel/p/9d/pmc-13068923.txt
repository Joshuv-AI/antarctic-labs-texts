Thermo-hydraulic performance optimization of different louvered strip inserts inside a heated tube employing Al2O3 and CuO water-based nanofluids
Authors: Bandar Awadh Almohammadi, H. A. Refaey, Abdullah M.A. Alsharif, Khairy Elsayed, Hosam M.Abdulhasieb, Mohamed A. Karali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Louvered strip insert (LSI) within heat exchangers is a widely used method for enhancing performance. However, optimization analysis on such inserts with many design parameters is limited in the literature. In accordance, the present study aims to conduct a thermo-hydraulic performance optimization. Using a surrogate-based optimization approach accompanied by 3D full numerical simulations. Four parameters are varied simultaneously namely: the slant angle (θ) between 5° and 39°, the pitch (P) between 85 mm and 106 mm, the Reynolds number (Re) range of 500 − 13,000, and the nanofluid concentrations (ϕ) between 0% and 2%. Two nanofluids are tested: Al2O3, and CuO using water as the base fluid. The LSI is installed in the forward direction to the fluid inlet inside a tube with a diameter (D) of 14.3 mm and a length (L) of 2219 mm.The optimization analysis is based on both boosting the heat transfer amount and the degradation of the friction factor through five performance indices. The results revealed that CuO nanofluid performs better than other tested fluids. A maximum overall performance factor of 2.61 is reached at optimum values of the design parameters Re, ϕ, θ, and P as: 500, 0.82%, 35°, and 105.37 mm, respectively. This work is useful for the optimum design of heat exchanger devices.

Introduction

Energy conservation is a critical concern for heat exchangers and other everyday uses. Since then, various approaches and strategies have been utilized to improve the thermal performance of these devices instead of lowering the necessary fluid pumping power1–3. These may be divided into two main categories: active and passive. In the active technique, turbulence is created, and the boundary layer is disturbed by external forces such as vibrating surfaces or magnetic or acoustic fields to increase heat transmission. But since they are expensive, passive approaches are often used. The following are examples of in-tube equipment insertions: fins, wire grids or brushes, twisted tapes, porous discs, turbulators, perforated plates, dimples, and so on4–32.

A louvered strip insert (LSI), which is fitted into the tube, is a particular geometric shape that serves as a turbulator fin. Mounted on the wire, it is composed of thin metals and has a flat, leaf-like form. It is thus anticipated that it may produce or enhance flow turbulence. Reduce the thickness of the thermal boundary layer while simultaneously increasing the flow surface’s heat transfer coefficient. Numerous studies have assessed the use of louvered strip inserts both experimentally and numerically, as well as their effect on improving heat transmission4–11. The insertion of louvered strip inserts has been shown to increase the average Nusselt number and friction factor assessed to the plain tube. Due to the thinning of the boundary layer thickness, the tube with louvered turbulators may produce a strong recirculation flow, which disrupts the formation of the boundary layer and increases heat transfer. An elliptic-shaped louvered strip inserts into a concentric tube heat exchanger’s smooth inner tube were the subject of several tests by Eiamsa-ard et al.4. Investigating how the heat transfer and pressure loss are affected by changing the inclination angles of 15°, 25°, and 30° for both forward and backward orientations of the inserts, concerning the fluid flow direction. The Reynolds number ranged between 6000 and 42,000. Their experimental results were contrasted with those from published data in simple tubes. According to experimental studies, using louvered strips increases the heat transmission rate compared to using a plain tube. The average friction loss and Nu increased by 284% and 413%, respectively, for the inclined forward louvered strip and 263% and 233%, respectively, for the backward louvered strip over the plain tube. Additionally, the overall improvement ratio is greater when the louvered strip is used in a backward configuration instead of a forward one, ranging from 9% to 24%. Yaningsih et al.5implemented an experiment on a concentric tube heat exchanger that merely included a forward inclined elliptic-shaped louvered strip insert within a smooth inner tube. To investigate how the slant angle affects the friction factor and heat transmission from single-phase forced convection. Their slant angles ranged from 15° to 25°, with Re ranging from 5300 to 17,500. Comparing the characteristics under study to the situation of a simple tube without inserts led to the development of an overall performance metric. According to their findings, the louvered strip insert increased heat transmission and friction factor over the plain tube by around 77.02% and 3.35, respectively. The findings showed that increasing the slant angle improves the heat transmission rates. Because of this, the greatest thermal performance factor was 1.12 at the maximum slant angle of 25°.The impact of differential louver pitch and inclination angle on the thermal performance of the frequent frosting/defrosting cycles was investigated by Park et al.6utilizing a louvered fin heat exchanger. Compared to the equal louver pitch scenario, the asymmetrical louver pitch caused a delay in the onset of frost blockage. Nevertheless, these studies showed that repeated cycling showed less variation in the pressure drop and heat transfer rate at larger angles. Sadeghianjahromi et al.7established correlations among heat transmission and flow friction based on the flow and heat transfer mechanism for tubular heat exchangers with louvered fins. They said the numerical simulation data was shown within 15% of their parameters’ extensive ranges. According to Wijayanta et al.8, the majority of experimental research studies that are accessible from the literature lack the data necessary to explain the mechanisms behind fluid flow and heat transfer. As a result, they conducted a numerical study of the thermal hydraulic performance within an upgraded tube that just included an oval-shaped backward louvered strip insert with different pitches for turbulent single-phase flow. As a computational solution, the RNG k-ε turbulence model was employed. Using water as the working fluid, various pitches of 40, 50, and 60 mm were examined for the Re from 10,000 to 17,500. They observed that the flow pattern on the rear of the inserts, which creates a vortex, defined the turbulent flow. Heat transport was improved by the vortices that developed. According to computational analysis’s findings, the improved tube’s louvered strip measuring 40, 50, and 60 mm in pitch distance raised the friction factor to 7.59, 6.51, and 5.77 times higher than that of the plain tube and the Nu to 1.81, 1.75, and 1.72 times higher, respectively.

Nanofluids are also assigned for heat transfer enhancement alongside the use of louvered strips in heat exchangers applications, as found through many investigations. Four different types of nanoparticles, Al2O3, CuO, SiO2, and ZnO, with diverse volume fractions, have been numerically tested by Mohammed et al.9. They examined different pitches and slant angles. Their findings showed that, in comparison to a plain tube, a high slant angle of 30° and a tiny pitch of 30 mm might improve heat transfer performance by around 367% to 411%. Faster flow mixing resulted from increased turbulence intensity caused by a bigger slant angle. Nakhchi and Esfahani10have conducted a numerical examination into the turbulent flow characteristics of a CuO-water nanofluid via a heat exchanger pipe augmented with single and double louvered strips. The volume percentage of the nanoparticles ranged from 0 to 2%, while the Reynolds number (Re) and slant angle were between 5000 and 14,000 and 15° to 25°, respectively. The finite volume approach was implemented using the RNG k − ε model. The findings demonstrated that a substantial flow disruption between the wall and the louvered strip mainly caused an increase in turbulent kinetic energy. Additionally, the nanoparticles enhance the working fluid’s thermo-physical characteristics, leading to improved heat transmission and at Re = 14,000, utilizing nanofluid rather than water results in a 15.6% rise in the Nu. Using a double perforated louvered strip with a slant angle of 25°, the maximum thermal enhancement value of 1.99 is achieved at Re = 14,000. The recirculation flow within the perforations may achieve significant improvement in thermal performance. Experimental research on the heat transfer and thermal performance factor of double-pipe heat exchangers with rotating inclined elliptical inserts was conducted by Nakhchi et al.11. The perforation sizes, slant angle, and rotation angle of the elliptical turbulators were between 0.5 and 1.5 mm, 15° and 25°, and 0° and 90°, respectively. In order to enhance thermal performance without significantly affecting pressure drop, the rotating inserts with holes may significantly increase flow unsteadiness and disrupt the thermal boundary layer. According to the experimental research, heat transmission has been enhanced by up to 30.7%. When the rotation angle is 90 degrees, the perforation width is 0.25 degrees, and the slant angle is 25 degrees, the rotating insert achieves the most significant thermal performance factor of 2.23. Compared to other research, this one yields a thermal performance factor that is much greater without raising production costs.

Optimization of heat tubes has been a major research focus aimed at improving thermal performance and reducing energy consumption in heat exchanger systems. Recent studies have explored various approaches, including topology optimization, surrogate modeling, and multi-objective algorithms. Aimed to design geometries that maximize heat transfer while minimizing flow resistance. Liu et al.33 executed a numerical study on fin-and-tube heat exchangers using topology optimization (TO) to enhance both thermal and hydraulic performance. The study demonstrated that optimized fin structures increased thermal performance by 22.64–28.04% and reduced hydraulic resistance by 33.37–47.72%, leading to an overall performance improvement of up to 12.7%. Feng et al.34performed numerical investigations on H-type finned tube heat exchangers incorporating longitudinal vortex generators (LVGs), dimples, and grooves. The application of the Taguchi method for parameter optimization revealed that the compound design markedly improved performance, resulting in a thermal-hydraulic efficiency enhancement of up to 57.1% relative to individual structures. Elboughdiri et al.35introduced arc-curved microchannels inscribed on segmented circular heat sinks. Artificial neural networks (ANN) were employed to enhance performance, resulting in a 25.9% increase in overall efficiency compared to traditional designs. Further, Xie et al.36optimized fin-tube heat exchangers with rectangular winglet vortex generators using Response Surface Methodology (RSM) and ANN. They found that ANN outperformed RSM in predicting performance, with R² values of 0.990 for the Nusselt number and 0.998 for the friction factor. The optimal design achieved a 25.5% improvement in relative efficiency compared to a plain heat exchanger. Hu et al.37introduced staggered concave curved vortex generators in wavy-finned-tube heat exchangers and optimized their arrangement using multi-objective genetic algorithms, resulting in a 30.4% increase in Nu and a 25.9% increase in the thermal performance factor. Li et al.38applied ANN and genetic algorithms to optimize microchannels with double delta-winglet vortex generators, achieving a remarkable 128.1% improvement in heat transfer and a performance evaluation criterion (PEC) of 1.89 for high heat flux applications. Complementing these studies, Gönül et al.39focused on delta winglet-type vortex generators in microchannels and employed multi-objective optimization using genetic algorithms and RSM. The results highlighted Nu enhancements between 3% and 87% and PEC values reaching up to 1.62, with vortex generator height and inlet velocity identified as key influencing parameters. Similarly, Sharma et al.40optimized curved trapezoidal winglets in fin-and-tube heat exchangers using RSM and ANN as surrogate models combined with genetic algorithms. The optimization yielded Pareto fronts that balanced Colburn factor improvements with minimized friction losses, offering designers flexibility for both design and off-design conditions. These collective advancements demonstrate the power of combining numerical simulations, surrogate modeling, and evolutionary algorithms in developing high-efficiency heat tube and heat exchanger systems. Numerical optimization research using graphene oxide nanofluid in water was recently presented by Raj et al.53on a heat exchanger tube with a hyperbolic cut twisted tape (HCTT) insert for a Reynolds number range of 10,000 to 22,000. An Artificial Intelligence (AI) approach was utilized in conjunction with an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) to assess the optimization process. Their findings demonstrated that the performance of heat exchangers could be accurately predicted and optimized through the AI model, facilitating enhanced design of heat exchangers. The Nusselt number, friction factor, and thermal efficiency for HCTT increased by 40.42%, 56.27%, and 25.90%, respectively, compared to a plain tube. The maximum thermal performance achieved was 1.26, corresponding to a cut ratio of 1.2 and a volume fraction of 4%. The findings indicated that nanoparticles enhanced heat transfer, with optimal performance observed at Re = 22,000.

Literature survey summary.

According to the preceding discussion and the summary presented in Table 1 on related literature and to the best of the author’s knowledge it could be deduced that: limited studies on the optimization of louvered strip inserts (LSI) are available. Consequently, there are still many geometrical aspects besides other fluid flow characteristics that are attractive for research to fill the gap in this field. Moreover, most of studies on LSI are exploring limited ranges of Re for laminar or turbulent flows. By other means, studying large Re range to cover both laminar and turbulent flows has scarcely existed. Consequently, the present study was planned and designed to conduct a novel study on the thermo-hydraulic performance optimization, including many parameters for the first time. Using a surrogate-based optimization approach accompanied by full 3D simulations. For a wide range of Re to cover both laminar and turbulent flows applications. Which also emphasis the necessity of adding such proposed turbulators for laminar flows. As a result, four parameters are varied and studied simultaneously, namely, the slant angle (θ); the pitch (P); the Reynolds number (Re); and the nanofluid concentrations (ϕ). Where two nanofluids: Al2O3, and CuO (using water as the base fluid) are tested. Therefore, this work aims to provide a novel contribution by focusing on the optimal design of heat exchanger devices. Specifically, it seeks to maximize heat transfer enhancement while minimizing the associated penalties of hydraulic resistance.

Methods

Geometrical parameters and domains

Figure 1 depicts the geometry of the studied problem using Ansys DesignModeler42. Figure 1 (a) represents the geometrical parameters and studied domain, and (b) shows a 3D view of the louvered strip inserts. The dimensions are engaged from the experimental work of Yaningsih et al.5, which is typically used with heat exchanger devices in practice. The studied geometry consists of a tube with a 14.3 mm diameter (D) and a length (L) of 2219 mm, while a distance of one-tenth the tube diameter is taken before and after the heat transfer region to ensure fully developed conditions. Therefore, the passing fluid identified the first computational domain, and the second domain was for the louvered strip inserts. The strips are like leaves of oval shape made of aluminum and have dimensions of 10 mm in length, 6 mm in width, and 1 mm in thickness, and stick on a core rod of 2 mm in diameter. The louvered strips are arranged in a forward orientation to the fluid inlet direction. They have the same pitch (P) on both upper and lower sides, and with a mutual distance (S) of half the pitch distance. This study includes the effect of changing the slant angle (θ) between 5° and 39°.The Pitch (P) is between 85 mm and106mm.

Boundary conditions and assumptions

The boundary conditions within the studied problem are as follows: the hot tube wall boundary condition was taken as constant surface temperature of 330 K. While the fluid inlet boundary conditions were adjusted to the fluid inlet temperature of 300 K. Along with characterized fluid velocity at the inlet that calculated based on both the corresponding Re range from 500 to 13,000 and the passing fluid properties (Eq. 1). It is worth noting that this range of Re is selected to start with the value of 500 to study the effectiveness of adding such turbulators of louvered strips for laminar flows. Other previous studies were also interested in studying the louvered strip inserts at low laminar range of Re43. Accordingly, it could be stated that one of the novelty keys from the present study can be addressed based on selecting such large range of Re from low laminar flows to highly turbulent flows which scarcely found from literature. The exit fluid surface is subjected to the atmospheric pressure outlet conditions. The following assumptions are considered for the present study: (i) the thermo-physical properties of the working fluids are constant; (ii) the fluid is incompressible and Newtonian; (iii) the effect of gravity is negligible; and (iv) conduction heat transfer in the louvered strips, central rod, and pipe walls is neglected.

Ansys solver42implements the standard k-epsilon model with scalable wall functions, allowing for accurate solutions even with fine near-wall meshes, improving robustness over older methods. It provides a balance of accuracy and computational cost, making it a standard choice. The present model encompasses the fundamental equations governing mass, momentum, and energy transmission. For the simulation of the nanofluid properties, there are two strategies in simulating the nano particles within fluids: the first is via the mixture model47,48; and the second is the single-phase model with modified properties based on the base fluid and the nanoparticles properties and volume fraction. Objective functions and model training

The present study aims to enhance the system’s overall thermal-hydraulic performance through an optimization analysis. This study formulates objective functions based on five key output parameters. As the optimization process aims to maximize the Nusselt number (Nu), Colburn factor (j), thermal-hydraulic performance index (JF), and the overall performance factor () all of which contribute to improved heat transfer characteristics. Simultaneously, it seeks to minimize the pressure drop (∆) and the friction factor (f) to reduce the flow resistance and the pumping power requirements. Thereby achieving a balanced and efficient thermal-fluid system. The optimization framework will be described as follows.

This study adopts a Surrogate-Based Optimization (SBO) approach to identify the optimal geometrical configurations and working fluid characteristics parameters that enhance the system’s overall performance. Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) was employed to efficiently sample the design space to generate 91 random design points. This ensured a representative and uniformly distributed Design of Experiments (DoE) while minimizing the number of required simulations. The geometrical models corresponding to these design points were constructed and evaluated through computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations. To comprehensively assess the overall thermal and fluid flow performance using the five key indicators mentioned above.

In order to reduce computational cost and accelerate the optimization process, surrogate modeling was integrated into the framework. A Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN) was trained and validated as a surrogate model to approximate the CFD results. The surrogate predictions at candidate optimal points were compared against corresponding CFD simulations. These points were added to the training dataset if notable discrepancies were found, and the model was retrained. This iterative refinement continued until the surrogate model’s predictions strongly agreed with CFD results, indicating training convergence.

Following the development and validation of the RBFNN surrogate model, the final stage involved executing the optimization process itself. A single-objective optimization strategy was implemented to determine each objective function’s most efficient design configuration. The optimization aimed to maximize all used performance indices except the friction factor. Figure 4 depicts the flow chart for the optimization framework. Figure 5 (a-e) shows the model predictions. It could be demonstrated from Fig. 5 that, the RBFNN exhibited high predictive accuracy across all tested scenarios, validating its effectiveness as a reliable and computationally efficient surrogate for CFD-based optimization.

CFD results with 3D plots

Figure 6 presents the 3D plot for the variation of Nu with the design variables. It is evidently shown from Fig. 6 (a) that the value of Nu increases with the decrease of P, while it decreases with the increase of θ. This makes sense because the reduction of P will increase the number of strips for the same tube length. Thus, increasing the number of strips and the slant angle (θ) helps increase the fluid flow turbulence and consequently promotes the convection heat transfer coefficient and Nu. However, it is worth noting that the effect of decreasing P is very influential on the Nu trend. However, a local jump increase of Nu could be observed at nearly P of 100 mm and θ of 20°, implying a local characterized interaction between both parameters. From Fig. 6 (b), it could be concluded that the Nu is increasing with the increase of Re, while it is wavy decreasing with the increase of ϕ. In principle, the increase of Re is the direct reason for the rapid transition of the fluid flow to turbulent and consequently promotes the convection heat transfer coefficient and Nu. While the decrease of Nu along with the increase of ϕ can be related to the enhancement of the heat conduction term that is represented by the fluid thermal conductivity (K) in Eq. (6), it is worth noting that the effect of the increase of Nu is much affected by the Re than ϕ. Also, a local characterized interaction between both parameters can be observed. Figure 6 (c) depicts the elementary distribution plot for the effect of the four test parameters on the value of Nu. Since Re has the highest standard deviation, it has a non-linear variation. Moreover, there is an interaction between θ and ϕ.

Figure 7 presents the 3D plot of the friction factor (f) with the design variables. It is obviously shown from Fig. 7 (a) that the f value is proportional to θ and inversely proportional to P. This can be explained as follows. The decrease of P will increase the number of strips for the same tube length. Therefore, increasing θ or decreasing P will increase the losses, consequently, the pressure drop (∆p) and friction factor will increase. The increase in the friction factor with the slant angle, is due to the boundary layer separation and increased recirculation zones which increases the energy dissipation. Figure 7 (b) shows that increasing Re reduces the value of f, which is a well-known behavior. This behavior can be explained due to three reasons. Firstly, as Re increases, inertial forces dominate the fluid flow and the viscous shear stress becomes smaller. Secondly, at high Re, the viscous sub-layer thickness becomes smaller and consequently its influence on the total shear stress reduces. Finally, as Re increases, turbulent eddies transfer momentum more efficiently across the flow. Therefore, the wall shear stress per unit velocity decreases. While the decrease of f along with the increase of ϕ can be related to the rise of the fluid density (ρ) in Eq. (7), it is worth noting that the Re has a much greater influence on the effect on f than ϕ. From Fig. 7 (c), it is clear that Re has the highest effect on the friction factor. There are interactions between θ, ϕ, and P.

It is clear from Table 8; Figs. 12, 13 and 14 for cases (1), and (2) that, at the given value of θ, the strips are close to the rod consequently the losses will be small as the separation region will be limited and the effect of the strips will be relatively small whereas this is not the case for the optimum case which presents better thermal performance but at a higher friction factor due to the increased separation regions.

It can be observed from the velocity contours plots presented in Fig. 12 the effect of changing the design parameters on the flow pattern. Where the optimum case shows a higher increase in the velocity magnitude with respect to the inlet velocity due to the conservation of mass. Moreover, the flow detachment occurs at the edge of the strips, followed by a separation region (vortex), attaches later to the rod. The recirculation flow generated near the walls increases flow disturbance which causes thermal boundary layer disruption near the walls. This type of flow (flow detachment, attachment and recirculation flow) significantly enhances the turbulent intensity and heat transfer process. Consequently, the optimum case exhibits the best thermal performance.

The turbulent kinetic energy contour with velocity vectors plots are displayed in Fig. 13. As explained before, the higher velocity magnitude due to conservation of mass is the main reason for higher turbulent kinetic energy exhibited by the optimum case. Strong turbulence intensity results in thermal boundary layer disruption which can increase fluid mixing and consequently enhances the heat transfer rate.

Figure 14 shows the temperature contours from case (1), case (2), and optimum case, respectively, while using a unified legend scale. It is obviously shown from Fig. 14 the higher temperature levels attained from the optimum case when compared to both cases (1), and (2). This is mainly attributed to the enhancement in the turbulence nature of the flow depending on the applied design values of the optimum case (Table 5). The formation of stronger vortex increases the fluid residence time (longer flow path), which results in a heat transfer enhancement. Thermal boundary layer disruption occurs due to such recirculating flow which is obvious in the optimum case, this helps temperature augmentation of fluid flow. The attained higher temperature levels of the optimum case can be clearly observed by comparing the colors gradients from all cases once together specifically at the tube downstream. As for the optimum case, the temperature gradient at the tube downstream is observed with the dim orange color, while for both cases (1), and (2) the colors are observed to change between light green to light yellow. Overall, it could be noticed that: the optimum case outperforms both cases (1) and (2) with an enhancement in the flow outlet temperature by about 2%. Which reflects its superior enhancement in the overall thermal performance as found from Table 8.

Conclusions

The present study was designed to introduce a big study on the thermo-hydraulic performance optimization for louvered strip insertions (LSI) inside a heated tube. Using surrogate based optimization approach accompanied with full 3D numerical simulations. Four parameters are varied and examined simultaneously as the first time in literature namely: the slant angle (θ) between 5° and 39°, the pitch (P) between 85 mm and 106 mm, the Reynolds number (Re) range of 500 − 13,000, and the nanofluids concentrations (ϕ) between 0% and 2%. Where two nanofluids: Al2O3, and CuO using water as the base fluid are tested. It is worth noting that the Re range is selected to cover both laminar and turbulent flows applications that scarcely exists in literature. Also starting the Re range from 500 is to emphasis the importance of adding proposed turbulators for laminar flows. The LSI is installed in forward direction to the fluid inlet inside a tube with diameter of 14.3 mm and length of 2219 mm. The optimization analysis is based on both boosting the heat transfer amount and degradation of the friction factor. Through five performance indices: Nusselt number (Nu), Colburn factor (j), thermal-hydraulic performance index (JF), and overall performance factor (). Based on the results the following concluding remarks are made:

The system’s thermal-hydraulic performance enhancement is mainly observed with the decrease of P and increase of θ. Due to that, the decrease of P will increase the number of strips or turbulators for the same tube length. As a result, increasing both the number of turbulators and slant angle (θ) helps to improve the fluid flow turbulence and consequently promotes the performance factors.CuO-water-based nanofluid is found the best to enhance performance over other studied fluids.Maximum overall performance factor of 2.61 is reached at optimum values of the design parameters, Re, ϕ, θ, and P as: 500, 0.82%, 35°, and 105.37 mm, respectively.With respect to literature, an enhancement in η by 133% has been obtained by using the optimum values form the present study.

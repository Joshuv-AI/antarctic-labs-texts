Impact of lymph node dissection on textbook outcomes in patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: a multi-center analysis
Authors: Cong Luo, Heqiang Xu, Tingfeng Huang, Ruilin Fan, Weiping Zhou, Jingdong Li, JiangTao Li, Shichuan Tang, Yongyi Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the association between lymph node dissection (LND) and Textbook Outcomes (TO) in patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC).MethodsThis retrospective multicenter study was registered in the Research Registry (registration number: researchregistry10392). We included patients treated at four major hospitals between 2011 and 2017. TO was defined as negative surgical margins, no perioperative blood transfusion, no postoperative complications, no prolonged length of stay (LOS ≤ the 50th percentile of the total cohort), no readmission within 30 days after discharge, and no postoperative death within 30 days. A random forest algorithm was used to determine the relative importance of factors associated with TO achievement. Subgroup analyses were conducted to identify patients who may benefit from LND. Adjusted TO was defined as achievement of both the conventional TO criteria and LND.ResultsA total of 376 patients were included in this study. Random forest analysis showed that LND was associated with the likelihood of achieving TO. Patients who underwent LND had higher rates of postoperative complications and longer hospital stays. Further analyses indicated that LND did not adversely affect overall prognosis and may contribute to prognostic stratification.ConclusionsLND may reduce the rate of achieving TO in the short term; however, its value for long-term prognosis and postoperative treatment planning should not be overlooked.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05090-z.Keywords: Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, Textbook outcome, Survival, Lymph node dissection

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinomais (ICC) an aggressive malignancy that is typically associated with a poor prognosis [1]. Its incidence has increased in recent years [2, 3]. Among available treatment options, surgical resection remains the only potentially curative approach, but it is suitable for only 20%-30% of diagnosed patients [4]. Although long-term prognosis remains a major concern, increasing attention has also been paid to the quality of surgical care and short-term postoperative outcomes [5, 6]. Contemporary clinical assessments predominantly rely on individual postoperative indicators, such as complication rates, mortality, and length of hospital stay. However, these isolated indicators may not fully reflect the overall quality of the surgical procedure [7, 8]. In this context, textbook outcome (TO) has emerged as a composite measure that provides a more comprehensive assessment of surgical quality [9].

For patients with ICC, the significance of lymph node dissection (LND) should not be underestimated [10]. Most contemporary guidelines recommend routine LND in the management of ICC [11, 12]. In addition to its potential impact on postoperative survival, standardized LND has important implications for accurate tumor staging and subsequent decisions regarding adjuvant therapy [13]. Therefore, LND is an important component of standard surgical management. TO is a comprehensive metric that evaluates healthcare quality by incorporating parameters such as negative surgical margins, avoidance of perioperative blood transfusion, absence of postoperative complications, optimized length of hospital stay, no 30-day postoperative mortality, and no readmission within 30 days after discharge. Because these criteria reflect essential surgical principles, achieving TO is generally considered to indicate better surgical quality and prognosis. Previous studies have shown that LND may increase the incidence of surgical complications, prolong operative time, and extend hospital stay [14, 15]. These factors may reduce the rate of TO achievement, even if they do not necessarily improve survival.

Although numerous studies have investigated the application of TO in ICC [8], the impact of LND on TO achievement and prognosis remains unclear. Some investigators have proposed incorporating LND into TO assessment criteria [16]. However, a comprehensive analysis validating this proposal is still lacking. In the present study, we used a random forest algorithm to explore the relationship between LND and TO. We further analyzed the impact of LND on TO achievement and prognosis, and finally assessed the rationale for including LND in the evaluation criteria for TO.

Materials and methods

Patients and data collection

The study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Mengchao Hepatobiliary Hospital of Fujian Medical University, and exempts the requirement of written informed consent (Approval No. 2024_041_01). All relevant procedures were performed in accordance with the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki.

Relevant data were from patients treated in 4 large-scale Grade A tertiary hospitals in China from 2011 to 2017, including Mengchao Hepatobiliary Hospital of Fujian Medical University, Eastern Hepatobiliary Surgery Hospital, Affiliated Second Hospital of Zhejiang University, Affiliated Hospital of Chuanbei Medical University.

Patients with ICC confirmed by postoperative pathology were included in this study. We evaluated patients with adequate liver reserve and recommended for hepatectomy according to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed ICC after surgery; and (2) primary ICC treated with initial surgical resection. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) receipt of other antitumor treatments before surgery; (2) missing clinical data; and (3) loss to follow-up immediately after discharge. Finally, a total of 376 patients were included.

Follow‑up

According to standard follow-up procedures, the patients were followed up every 3 months for the first 2 years, and then every 3–6 months. During follow-up, routine monitoring included medical record collection, blood test, and imaging. The time between the date of surgery and death for any reason or the last follow-up was defined as overall survival (OS).

Textbook outcome

Textbook outcome (TO) in this analysis was defined according to the following parameters: negative surgical margins, no perioperative blood transfusion, no postoperative complications, no prolonged length of stay (LOS ≤ the 50th percentile of the total cohort), no readmission within 30 days after discharge, and no postoperative death within 30 days. Tumor margins were classified as microscopically negative (R0) or microscopically positive (R1). A positive margin (R1) was defined as a margin width of less than 1 mm or tumor involvement of the margin. Perioperative blood transfusion was defined as blood transfusion during or after surgery. Readmission was defined as admission to any hospital within 30 days after discharge. Mortality was defined as death within 30 days after primary surgery. Patients who met all expected outcomes were considered to have achieved TO.

Adjusted textbook outcome

Patients who met the above criteria and underwent LND were considered to have achieved adjusted TO; otherwise, they were considered not to have achieved adjusted TO.

Surgical procedure and lymph node dissection

During the study period, the indications for laparoscopic surgery, the decision to perform lymph node dissection (LND), surgical techniques, and operative strategies varied across participating centers and were mainly determined by the experience and judgment of the operating surgeons. Both major and minor hepatectomies were included. Non-anatomical liver resection was defined as wedge resection, local resection, or tumor enucleation. Anatomical liver resection was defined as hepatectomy performed after prior control of the first- and/or second-order Glissonean pedicles at the hepatic hilum.

In this study, routine LND was defined as the intentional removal of regional lymphatic tissue from the hepatic hilum to the hepatoduodenal ligament. The mandatory dissection field included the perihilar lymph nodes and lymph nodes within the hepatoduodenal ligament, corresponding mainly to station 12 lymph nodes, including nodes along the proper hepatic artery, portal vein, and common bile duct. Lymph nodes beyond this field, such as common hepatic artery, retropancreatic, or para-aortic lymph nodes, were not routinely required and were removed only when clinically suspicious or technically indicated. Because of the retrospective and multicenter nature of this study, routine LND was defined according to the anatomical dissection field rather than a fixed minimum number of harvested lymph nodes. The actual number of retrieved lymph nodes was recorded based on the pathological report.

Statistical analysis

Propensity score matching was performed to reduce potential baseline imbalance between the LND and non-LND groups. The propensity score was estimated using a logistic regression model including age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, Child-Pugh grade, tumor size, tumor number, carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9, alpha-fetoprotein, carcinoembryonic antigen, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, tumor differentiation, microvascular invasion, perineural invasion, and postoperative adjuvant therapy. Patients in the LND group were matched with those in the non-LND group using 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with a caliper value of 0.2.

Variable importance is calculated by the random forest algorithm to determine the significance of each feature variable in classification, enabling the selection of highly correlated variables for modeling. The “importance” function in the randomForest package in R can be used to compute variable importance. The Kaplan Meier method was used to assess OS estimates for the entire study population from the date of surgery to the date of last follow-up or death; Differences in survival were assessed by logarithmic rank test. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables, and chi-square or Fisher’s exact test were used to compare categorical variables. All data analyses were performed by R software (version4.1.0, http://www.r-project.org).

Risk factors affecting the occurrence of TO

To elucidate the influence of LND on textbook outcomes, we identified 18 factors, LND included. The random forest algorithm was used to analyze the impact of various factors on TO, and the results revealed that LND has a significant influence on achieving TO (Fig. 1). In the entire cohort, 132 patients (35.1%) experienced an extended length of hospital stay. Perioperative blood transfusions were deemed necessary for 12.5%, while an overwhelming 95.21% attained R0 resection. Notably, 3.19% of patients faced either perioperative mortality or were readmitted within a span of 30 days post-surgery, as depicted in Table 2. Statistically significant differences were observed between the LND group and the non-LND group in terms of patient surgical margin status, incidence of complications, and length of hospital stay. Therefore, we further analyzed the impact of hospital stay and complications on survival. The results showed that patients without prolonged hospital stay had better overall survival (OS) and Disease-free survival (DFS) compared to those with prolonged hospital stay. Similarly, patients without postoperative complications showed a trend toward better OS, although the difference was not statistically significant, whereas DFS was significantly better in patients without complications (Fig. 2).

Factors influencing TO in patients undergoing LND

A total of 116 patients with LND were screened. Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that Carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9) > 200 ng/mL was the influencing factor of TO in patients with LND (Supplement Table 1). Therefore, we performed survival analysis again, dividing the patients into subgroups based on their CA19-9 levels. The results showed that there was no significant difference in OS between patients with CA19-9 levels above 200 ng/mL and those with CA19-9 levels below 200 ng/mL (Supplement Fig. 1). These results suggest that although CA19-9 may be related to TO achievement among patients undergoing LND, CA19-9 stratification alone does not identify a subgroup with a clear survival difference.

Construction of the adjusted textbook outcomes

We integrated lymph node dissection as a criterion within the textbook outcome assessment, termed as the adjusted textbook outcome. All patients has completed postoperative follow-up. The median follow-up time was 19 months, with a range of 1 to 74 months. The achievement rate of the TO was 52.9%, and the achievement rate of the adjusted-TO decreased to 20.5%. In the adjusted-TO group, except for the increase of patients with negative margin, the number of patients with other evaluation indicators decreased (Fig. 3). To minimize the effect of baseline errors on survival analyses, we performed Propensity Score Matching on the data set (Supplement Table 2). Lastly, a survival analysis based on the adjusted textbook outcome was carried out (Fig. 4A-B). The findings indicated differential survival rates among patients categorized under the adjusted textbook outcome without lymph node metastasis, the adjusted textbook outcome with lymph node metastasis, and those outside of the adjusted textbook outcome parameters. Incorporating LND into the TO framework may provide a complementary perspective by integrating perioperative safety with oncologic adequacy. Nevertheless, adjusted TO should be regarded as an exploratory concept rather than a definitive evaluation standard. Further prospective studies are required to validate its stability and clinical applicability across different tumor stages, surgical procedures, and lymph node dissection strategies.

Discussion

ICC represents a less frequent yet lethal condition [1]. Surgical resection remains a major therapeutic strategy for ICC [17]. In recent decades, the role of LND in ICC treatment has received increasing attention [18–20]. Previous studies have suggested that LND may increase the risk of complications, prolong operative time, and extend hospitalization [10, 21], thereby making TO achievement more challenging. Based on machine learning interpretability [22], we used a random forest algorithm to confirm that LND was an important factor associated with TO achievement. However, the potential influence of LND on prognosis and its relevance to tumor staging should also be considered [23, 24] .

In this study, patients in the LND group had a higher incidence of complications, a longer hospital stay, and more positive surgical margins. These findings indicate that LND was associated with a lower likelihood of achieving TO in the present cohort. However, this association should be interpreted with caution. Because this was a retrospective study, patients who underwent LND may have had more advanced disease, a higher suspected risk of lymph node metastasis, or more complex surgical conditions. Additionally, margin status and postoperative complications are closely associated with OS and DFS, which is consistent with previous studies [25, 26]. Miyata et al. [27] identified postoperative complications as a risk factor for postoperative survival in patients with ICC; however, these complications were not associated with disease-free survival. After propensity score matching, a multicenter study by Bagante et al. [28] suggested that patients with cirrhosis were more susceptible to complications after LND. Moreover, Littau et al. [29] reported that negative margins were associated with improved overall survival. Postoperative recovery may influence the selection of adjuvant therapy, which is closely related to lymph node status and tumor staging. In conclusion, complications and prolonged hospital stay may affect OS and DFS, but this does not necessarily mean that LND is directly responsible for these outcomes. LND remains important for accurate nodal staging and may guide postoperative treatment decisions [24]. Therefore, the importance of LND in ICC should not be denied.

Further subgroup analysis showed that, among patients undergoing LND, CA19-9 > 200 ng/mL was associated with a lower likelihood of achieving TO. However, survival analysis demonstrated that CA19-9 levels did not significantly affect long-term survival. Previous studieshave suggested that elevated CA19-9 levels may be associated with lymph node metastasis (LNM) [30, 31]. In our analysis, however, no statistically significant difference in prognosis was observed between patients undergoing LND when stratified by CA19-9 levels. Accordingly, CA19-9 should not be used alone to define the population that may benefit from LND. The clinical value of LND should be evaluated using more comprehensive information, including radiological lymph node status, pathological nodal status, tumor burden, extent of lymph node dissection, number of harvested lymph nodes, postoperative treatment, and long-term survival outcomes [32]. Future prospective studies are needed to determine whether specific subgroups of ICC patients may derive greater benefit from LND.

Given the distinctive characteristics of LNM in ICC, as well as the recommendations of the 8th edition of the AJCC guidelines and the NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines [33, 34], we support the inclusion of LND in TO assessment criteria. TO is an important benchmark of medical quality. However, the criteria used to define TO may vary slightly according to cancer type [41]. Previous studies have confirmed the ability of TO to evaluate short-term postoperative outcomes and its association with long-term prognosis [16, 35]. The main innovation of this study is the incorporation of lymph node dissection LND into the TO framework to establish an adjusted TO for ICC. While conventional TO primarily reflects short-term perioperative quality, adjusted TO integrates both perioperative safety and oncologic adequacy by considering nodal assessment, which is important for tumor staging, prognostic evaluation, and postoperative treatment planning. Therefore, adjusted TO may provide a more comprehensive evaluation of surgical quality, particularly for patients undergoing curative-intent resection in whom lymph node status has clinical relevance [36]. However, adjusted TO should be regarded as a complementary measure rather than a replacement for conventional TO and may be most applicable to resectable ICC patients suitable for curative surgery. Several limitations should be acknowledged, including the retrospective study design, lack of standardized LND practices across centers, incomplete consideration of LND extent and nodal yield, and the possibility that incorporating LND may lower TO achievement rates in patients for whom LND was not clinically indicated. Consequently, adjusted TO should be interpreted within the context of individualized surgical decision-making and requires further external validation.

This study has several limitations. First, because of its retrospective design, inherent selection bias may be present. Second, although the data were derived from multiple centers in China, the sample size should be expanded, and the incorporation of international multicenter data would enhance the credibility and generalizability of the findings. Third, although this study supports the inclusion of LND in TO evaluation, data limitations prevented further analysis of specific factors such as the number and anatomical stations of dissected lymph nodes. Future studies should address these issues. Furthermore, failure to achieve TO does not necessarily indicate poor surgical quality or worse survival. Advances in adjuvant treatments may improve survival outcomes for a broader patient population.

In summary, accumulating evidence underscores the importance of LND in surgery for ICC. TO is a useful metric for evaluating surgical quality and predicting survival. Incorporating LND into the evaluation criteria for TO may enhance its clinical relevance.

Conclusions

LND may reduce the rate of achieving TO. However, considering its potential value for long-term survival and postoperative treatment planning, the significance of LND should not be disregarded. Including LND in the evaluation criteria for TO may help stratify patient prognosis and inform subsequent treatment strategies.

Association of CAB39L gene methylation in peripheral blood leukocytes with the risk, chemotherapy efficacy and prognosis of gastric cancer and construction of risk prediction model
Authors: Yuqi Liu, Zhan Li, Yaping Feng, Yue Li, Yuxin Wang, Hongyu Sun, Ying Xu, Xingyu Gong, Yingying Yu, Yuqin Tang, Mingyi Zhang, Jing Ni, Xiujing Cao, Fang Wang, Yanfeng Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGastric cancer (GC) is a highly prevalent malignant tumor of the digestive tract worldwide, and there is an urgent need to explore non-invasive molecular markers for supporting precise clinical diagnosis and treatment. The study was to explore the correlation between CAB39L gene methylation in peripheral blood leukocytes (PBLs) and the risk, chemotherapy efficacy, and prognosis of GC, and correspondingly construct risk prediction model.MethodsOnline databases were used for bioinformatics analysis of CAB39L in GC tissues. A two-phased approach was subsequently employed: in the discovery stage (100 GC patients and 100 controls), we detected the methylation levels of all CpG sites in CAB39L to screen for potential CpG sites/regions associated with GC and response to platinum plus fluorouracil (PPF) treatment, and these findings were further validated in independent samples (250 GC patients and 250 controls). In addition, GC patients were followed up to investigate the correlation between CAB39L gene methylation and prognostic outcomes. Nomogram risk prediction models were constructed by integrating CpG sites, environmental and clinical parameters. The expression levels of CAB39L in the peripheral blood of 34 GC patients were detected by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Finally, the gene-environment interactions were investigated.ResultsUtilizing online databases, we discovered a negative association between the mRNA (messenger RNA) expression of CAB39L and the levels of DNA methylation, as well as a positive correlation with the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of 5-fluorouracil and cisplatin in GC tissues. In two stages study, it was found that the methylation status of three sites plus two CpG islands (CAB39La2, CAB39Lb3, CAB39Lb14, CAB39L-a, CAB39L-b) were associated with the risk of GC (PBH<0.05). The methylation status of three sites and one island shore (CAB39La13, CAB39Lc6, CAB39Lc7, CAB39L-c) were found to be related to the efficacy of PPF chemotherapy (PBH<0.05). The constructed nomogram had a good predictive value for the effect of PPF chemotherapy. The association between CAB39L gene methylation and prognosis of GC was not found in the follow-up study (P>0.05). Compared with the hypomethylation group of GC patients, the mRNA expression level of CAB39L in the hypermethylation group was decreased (P=0.041). Interactions between CAB39L methylation and environmental factors were observed.ConclusionsOur findings suggested that methylation of CAB39L in PBLs was associated with GC risk and chemotherapy response, and the prediction model based on CAB39L methylation site has potential value in predicting the efficacy of chemotherapy. Elevated CAB39L methylation levels of GC patients might influence gene expression. This study provides theoretical support for the diagnosis and treatment of GC patients.Keywords: Gastric cancer (GC), CAB39L, DNA methylation, peripheral blood leukocytes (PBLs), prediction model

Highlight box

Key findings

• The methylation of CAB39L in peripheral blood leukocytes (PBLs) is associated with the risk of gastric cancer (GC).

• CAB39L methylation in PBLs of GC patients correlated with platinum plus fluorouracil (PPF) chemotherapy efficacy. The nomogram showed good predictive value for PPF response [area under the curve (AUC) =0.877, sensitivity =91.3%, specificity =75.5%].

• CAB39L hypermethylation in PBLs of GC patients correlates with reduced mRNA expression.

What is known and what is new?

• The hypermethylation of CAB39L in GC tissues suppresses gene expression and drives tumorigenesis. However, there is currently a lack of relevant research on the methylation of this gene in the peripheral blood of GC patients.

• This is the first study to explore the relationship between the methylation level of CAB39L gene in PBLs and GC, and to evaluate the potential value of CAB39L methylation in diagnostic/predicting GC, prognosis, and chemotherapy response. The study also revealed the regulatory effect of CAB39L methylation on expression in peripheral blood.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The methylation of CAB39L in PBLs has the potential value as a non-invasive biomarker for GC risk assessment and prediction of chemotherapy response.

• Multi-center studies and functional experiments should be carried out to clarify the mechanism by which CAB39L methylation regulates the pathogenesis of GC.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) ranks among the most common malignant tumors globally, with more than one million new cases identified each year. Due to its substantial mortality rate and disease burden, GC currently ranks as the fifth leading factor in cancer-related mortality (1). GC patients are asymptomatic in the early stages of the disease, leading to advanced-stage diagnosis. Due to delayed diagnosis, many patients miss the opportunity for curative resection. Moreover, platinum plus fluorouracil (PPF) treatment as the first-line chemotherapy regimen for patients with advanced GC may lead to different treatment outcomes for various individuals (2). These clinical challenges highlight the urgent need to unravel the molecular mechanisms underlying GC development and progression, as well as to identify potential biomarkers or therapeutic targets that can deepen our understanding of disease pathogenesis.

As the core component of the LKB1 (liver kinase B1) tumor suppressor complex, CAB39L (calcium binding protein 39 like) can promote the activation of LKB1, thereby playing a positive role in maintaining cell polarity. Depletion of LKB1 results in the disruption of cell polarity and facilitates tumor progression (3,4). CAB39L expression correlated with the risk of bladder cancer, breast cancer, and renal clear cell carcinoma, among other malignancies (5,6). Choi et al. (7) found a frameshift mutation in CAB39L gene in GC, which may promote cancer development by inhibiting the activity of tumor suppressors. In addition, overexpression of CAB39L inhibited the tumor phenotype of GC cells (8). These findings collectively support the functional relevance of CAB39L in GC pathogenesis, laying the foundation for further exploration of its regulatory mechanisms in disease progression.

GC development involves the incremental buildup of a range of genetic and epigenetic aberrations. DNA methylation, the earliest and most comprehensive study mode of epigenetic modifications, plays a pivotal role in regulating gene activation (9). It was revealed that the silencing of CAB39L expression in GC tissues was mediated by promoter DNA methylation, and the hypermethylation of CAB39L promoter promoted its expression silencing (8). This observation underscores the potential of CAB39L methylation as a key epigenetic event involved in GC progression, warranting further investigation into its relevance beyond tumor tissue.

Inflammation-related immune responses during the development of GC can change the epigenetic characteristics of DNA in peripheral blood leukocytes (PBLs) (10). As PBL samples offer advantages of easy accessibility and minimal invasiveness to patients, DNA detection in PBLs has emerged as a valuable tool for exploring cancer-related epigenetic alterations. Indeed, methylation profiling of PBLs has identified several genes of which its epigenetic status correlates with GC development (11-13), suggesting that peripheral blood-derived epigenetic markers can serve as indirect reflections of systemic tumor-associated molecular changes.

Building on these insights, the present study was centered on PBLs as the primary investigative subject, with bioinformatics analyses employed as a complementary tool. First, bioinformatic approaches were utilized to delineate the regulatory roles of CAB39L expression and methylation in GC pathogenesis, thereby establishing a theoretical foundation for subsequent investigations into peripheral epigenetic alterations. On this basis, the associations of CAB39L methylation in PBLs with GC susceptibility, clinical prognosis, and chemotherapy responsiveness were probed—with the core aim of validating this methylation signature as a peripheral epigenetic correlate of GC-associated molecular perturbations. The potential utility of this methylation signature as a non-invasive diagnostic and prognostic biomarker was additionally assessed. Furthermore, the effect of CAB39L gene methylation on gene expression in peripheral blood of patients with GC was studied. The interaction between methylation and environmental risk factors was also explored. Collectively, tissue-derived bioinformatic evidence was integrated with peripheral blood epigenetic profiling data to comprehensively elucidate the multifaceted role of CAB39L in GC initiation and progression. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-2044/rc).

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis of CAB39L gene

UCSC database (14) was used to extract the expression data of CAB39L in 34 tumor samples, and R software was used to calculate the expression difference between normal and tumor samples in each tumor. UALCAN (15) and GEPIA (16) databases were used to study CAB39L expression in gastric carcinoma and the relationship between transcription. DNMIVD (17) and MEXPRESS (18) databases served to analyze the link between expression and methylation surrounding the CAB39L promoter region. ACLBI database was used to compare 5-fluorouracil and Cisplatin in the chemotherapy response of different degrees of gene expression. DNA methylation information extracted from the UCSC Xena database (14) was used for survival analysis in this study. The “gene” module within the TIMER database was utilized to assess the degree of immune cell infiltration in GC tissues (19). In addition, R software was used to compare the TIMER score of infiltrating immune cells with the expression level of CAB39L. The STRING database (20) was utilized to construct the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of CAB39L and its functionally related genes. The functions of CAB39L and its associated proteins were analyzed by GO-KEGG enrichment.

Study subjects

The study was conducted in two stages: discovery and validation. A total of 350 GC patients and 350 healthy controls (HCs) were enrolled from the first affiliated hospital of Anhui medical university to assess the DNA methylation level in PBLs. We tested 100 GC patients and 100 HCs in the discovery stage. An additional 250 GC patients and 250 HCs were enrolled for the validation stage. In the combined samples, 350 GC patients and 350 HCs in the discovery and validation stages were analyzed. All patients had GC confirmed by histopathological diagnosis. In addition, blood samples from 34 GC patients were included to measure the mRNA (messenger RNA) expression levels and methylation status of CAB39L gene. Inclusion and exclusion criteria have been detailed in our previous study (21,22). All participants signed written informed consent forms. This research was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. 20160234). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The estimation of sample size was provided in the supplementary materials.

Peripheral blood-based detection of DNA methylation

Professional healthcare personnel collected venous blood from the forearm of study subjects using EDTA anticoagulant tubes. PBL DNA was then isolated and extracted, and stored in a −80 °C freezer for subsequent use. Detailed DNA methylation detection techniques were calculated have been described in the previous study (23,24). In the discovery stage, 44 methylation sites on 2 CpG islands (CGIs) and 1 CpG island shore (CGI shore) from the CAB39L promoter were screened for sequencing. The locations of CGIs, CGI shore, and CpG sites were presented in Tables S1,S2. In the validation stage, the CpG sites identified in the discovery stage were subjected to sequencing. DNA Methylation detection personnel did not know the characteristics of study subjects.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis

Whole blood (5 mL) was collected via venipuncture, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated within 4 hours. TRIzol reagent was added to the lysed cells, followed by storage at −80 °C. Total RNA underwent extraction and purification with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). RNA quantification was performed using NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Shanghai, China). In accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, RNA samples of appropriate quantities were reverse-transcribed into cDNA using StarScript Pro All-in-one RT Mix with gDNA Remover (GenStar, Beijing, China). According to the guidelines, qRT-PCR analysis was conducted using SYBR qPCR Mix (GenStar, Beijing, China) mixed with cDNA and primers, performed on a PCR thermal cycler (Hema, Zhuhai, China). GAPDH expression was detected for normalization of CAB39L levels.

Evaluation of chemotherapy efficacy

Patients with recurrent or metastatic advanced GC treated with PPF chemotherapy were used to assess the effectiveness of chemotherapy. Patients included in the study had not undergone previous chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or interventional therapy before inclusion. A total of 92 eligible patients were included in the study of chemotherapy efficacy. Patients were assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST1.1) (25). The patients treated with PPF were categorized into two groups: the non-progressive disease (nPD) group and the progressive disease (PD) group. The nPD group consisted of patients who achieved complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), or stable disease (SD).

Prognostic evaluation of GC

The GC patients in the above case-control studies were followed up regularly to evaluate the prognosis since 2016. For GC patients who received radical surgery, follow-up assessments were conducted at 3-month intervals in the first 2 years postoperatively, 6-month intervals in the next 2 years, and on an annual basis thereafter. In cases of locally advanced or recurrent metastatic GC patients, efficacy and prognosis were evaluated every two chemotherapy cycles. The follow-up date ended on January 1, 2024. Disease-free survival (DFS), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) of GC patients were followed up.

Development of the nomograms in predicting

The risk prediction model established by nomogram was constructed to evaluate the potential value of CAB39L methylation in diagnostic/predicting GC, prognosis, and chemotherapy response by integrating CpG sites, environmental and clinical parameters. Logistic regression analysis was first performed on the CpG sites of the pooled sample to identify the corresponding positive CpG sites. On this basis, combinatorial optimization was achieved by integrating different positive CpG loci with clinical characteristics and environmental factors. Calibration curve, decision curve analysis (DCA) curve, and Hosmer-Lemeshow (H-L) test were used to validate the model. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to gauge the diagnostic and predictive value of CAB39L methylation model.

DNA methylation-environment interaction analysis

Logistic regression analysis was utilized to examine the additive and multiplicative interaction between methylation and environmental factors. To infer additive interactions between CpG sites and the environment, relative excess risk (RERI), attributable proportion (AP), and synergy index (SI) were utilized. Environmental factors included age, sex, smoking, drinking, tea, water intake, and salt intake.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R software (version 4.2.3) and SPSS software (version 26.0). GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) was used to generate images. Methylation level was expressed as a percentage. Continuous variables were described using the median or mean. Categorical variables were expressed as numerical values. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to evaluate the relationship between the methylation level of PBLs at each CpG site and GC risk as well as chemotherapy response. Cox regression was used to analyze the prognosis of GC. Results were expressed as odds ratio (OR), hazard ratio (HR), and 95% confidence interval (CI). In this study, the proportion of missing values in the methylation data was very low (approximately 1%). For missing data, the median was used for imputation. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant in the analyses. To control the type I error rate in multiple comparisons, we corrected the P values of CpG sites using the false discovery rate (FDR) method based on Benjamini and Hochberg’s theory.

Results

Bioinformatics analysis of CAB39L gene

Expression and methylation of CAB39L in GC tissues

First, Expression differences between normal and tumor samples in 34 tumors from the UCSC database were investigated. Results revealed that 8 tumors exhibited significant upregulation, while 24 tumors showed downregulation. CAB39L expression was considerably lower in gastrointestinal tumors than in normal tissue (Figure 1A). GEPIA and UALCAN databases were employed to investigate the expression of CAB39L in GC. The results revealed a significantly lower transcriptional level of CAB39L in tumor tissue compared to normal tissue (Figure 1B-1D). Furthermore, the relationship between CAB39L expression and various clinical parameters of GC was examined using UALCAN. Significant differences in CAB39L expression were observed among different age groups. Patients with grade I tumors exhibited lower mRNA expression of CAB39L than those with grade II and III tumors (Figure 1E,1F).

To explore the influence of CAB39L expression on chemotherapy effectiveness, we utilized ACLBI to assess the association between CAB39L expression and half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of 5-fluorouracil and cisplatin among CG patients (Figure 1G-1J). A positive correlation was observed between the expression levels of CAB39L and the IC50 values of 5-fluorouracil and cisplatin in GC patients. GC patients with low expression levels of CAB39L might exhibit increased sensitivity to this type of chemotherapy drug. These results indicated that CAB39L has potential value in predicting the chemotherapy efficacy of GC patients.

A significant correlation between the methylation level of CAB39L promoter and its transcript level was observed using the DNMIVD database (Pearson r=−0.130, P=0.016 (Figure 1K). In addition, the UCSC Xena database study found that patients with hypermethylation of CAB39L had poor OS (P=0.041) (Figure 1L). The methylation levels of CAB39L promoter and most CpG sites were found to be significantly correlated with transcript levels by MEXPRESS database (Figure 1M). We observed 17 CpG sites with negative correlations and 2 CpG sites with positive correlations between CAB39L methylation and expression. Based on the results mentioned above, the abnormal methylation level of CAB39L was related to the expression of CAB39L, chemotherapy response, and prognosis of GC patients.

Correlation between CAB39L expression and immune cell infiltration in GC

The correlation of immune cell infiltration between GC patients with high and low CAB39L expression in TIMER was evaluated. TIMER scores of CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells and macrophages were higher in CAB39L high expression group than in low expression group (P<0.05) (Figure 2A,2B). The expression level of CAB39L was positively correlated with the infiltration level of CD8+T cells, CD4+ T cells, macrophages and neutrophils in GC (Figure 2C-2H). In addition, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of the combined effect of CAB39L expression and immune cell infiltration on GC outcome was performed (Figure 2I-2K). The results showed that low infiltration of CD8+ T cells was associated with worse survival in GC in the low-expression group. However, in the high-expression group, highly infiltrating macrophages were associated with poorer survival in GC.

Construction of PPI network and GO-KEGG enrichment analysis

The PPI network of CAB39L and related genes constructed by STRING database revealed the top five genes significantly associated with CAB39L (STK11, STK24, CAB39, STRADA, STRADB) (Figure 2L). GO|KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the top 40 protein-coding genes positively and negatively correlated with CAB39L (Figure 2M,2N). The biological processes (BP) of these 40 protein-coding genes primarily included the processes of autophagy and the utilization of autophagy mechanisms. The cellular component (CC) was primarily associated with transferase complexes responsible for transferring phosphorus-containing groups. Molecular function (MF) was mainly enriched in protein serine/threonine kinase activity. KEGG pathway enrichment was mainly related to the AMPK signaling pathway.

Association between CAB39L methylation level in PBLs and GC risk

In the discovery stage, 7 CpG sites and 2 CGIs were screened for differences in methylation levels between GC and HCs (Table 2). Additional CpG sites screening information was provided in Table S3. The CpG sites and regions screened in the discovery stage that may affect the risk of GC were verified in the validation stage and the comprehensive analysis stage. After adjusting for age and sex, there are three CpG sites and two CGIs (CAB39La2, CAB39Lb3, and CAB39Lb14, CAB39L-a, CAB39L-b) of methylation confirmed the differences between the GC and HCs, and the GC group presented high methylation levels (OR >1, PBH<0.05). In the pooled samples, the differences were more significant, especially for CAB39La2 (OR =2.183, Pcombined=7.620×10−5, PBH=2.286×10−4). Among the CGIs, CAB39L-a and CAB39L-b showed hypermethylation levels in the GC group (CAB39L-a: OR =37.033, Pcombined=1.205×10−7, PBH=1.084×10−6; CAB39L-b: OR =51.304, Pcombined=1.586×10−6, PBH=7.138×10−6) (Table 3).

The CpG sites with statistically significant differences were selected and combined with sex and age to generate a diagnostic nomogram model. The calibration curve, DCA curve, and Hosmer-Lemeshow test results showed that the nomogram model had a certain diagnostic ability for GC (Table S4, Figure S1).

Association between CAB39L methylation level in PBLs and the response to chemotherapy

In the discovery stage, five CpG sites and one CGI shore were identified as being associated with chemotherapy response. Additional information on the other sites is provided in Table S5. No difference was shown in the validation stage. However, in the pooled sample analysis, the methylation levels of CAB39La13, CAB39Lc6 and CAB39Lc7 were different between PD and nPD groups (PBH<0.05). Patients with hypermethylation of CpG sites have better chemotherapy efficacy (OR <1). In addition, in the CpG region, CAB39L-c showed a high methylation level in the nPD group (OR =0.941, Pcombined=0.031, PBH=0.049) (Table 3).

Based on these findings, we combined the two identified CpG sites with environmental factors to establish a predictive nomogram model. The AUC of the nomogram was 0.877 (95% CI: 0.796–0.957), with a sensitivity of 91.3% and a specificity of 75.5% (Table S6, Figure S2). The calibration curve and Hosmer-Lemeshow test verified the model’s ability to accurately predict the response to chemotherapy of GC (P=0.574).

Association between CAB39L methylation level in PBLs and GC prognosis

According to the results of the two stages in the analysis, there were 6 CpG sites, 2 CGIs and 1 CGI shore that may be associated with the risk and chemotherapy efficacy of GC. Whereafter, we explored the relationship between these CpG sites/regions and prognosis. GC patients were grouped according to the median methylation level. The differences in DFS, PFS, and OS between the hypermethylated and hypomethylated groups were analyzed by multiple Cox regression. There was no association between CAB39L methylation in PBLs and the prognosis of GC patients (P>0.05, Table S7).

Association between CAB39L methylation and gene expression

Whether CAB39L methylation in peripheral blood was involved in the risk and chemotherapy efficacy of GC by regulating gene expression was further explored. This study included 34 GC patients to measure the mRNA expression and methylation levels of CAB39L. The methylation levels of CAB39L were divided into high and low groups based on the median value. The low-methylation group had a mean age of 60.18 years, with 13 males and 4 females, while the high-methylation group had a mean age of 62.53 years, with 12 males and 5 females. No significant differences were observed in age or gender between the two groups of GC patients (P>0.05). The results demonstrated that in GC patients, the mRNA expression level of the CAB39L gene was significantly lower in the high-methylation group compared to the low-methylation group, with a statistically significant difference between the two groups (P=0.041) (Figure S3).

Interaction analysis

The interaction between CpG site methylation and environmental factors in GC risk and chemotherapy response was analyzed by additive and multiplicative analysis using data from the combined samples. CAB39Lb13 was found to interact with age and gender in GC risk. (CAB39Lb13-age multiplicative interaction: OR =0.408, P=0.003; CAB39Lb13-sex multiplicative interaction: OR =0.508, P=0.042) (Tables S8,S9).

Discussion

Firstly, a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis was conducted on the gastric tumor tissues. The results revealed that CAB39L expression correlated with GC risk and the IC50 of first-line chemotherapeutic agents (5-fluorouracil, cisplatin). A prominent negative correlation was observed between CAB39L expression and methylation status, with hypermethylation associated with poor prognosis. Moreover, immune cell infiltration in the gastric tumor microenvironment was found to regulate CAB39L expression, thereby providing a theoretical rationale for investigating CAB39L methylation in PBLs. On the basis of these tissue-derived findings, during the two-stage study, PBLs of GC patients showed hypermethylation of CAB39L compared with HCs. There were different methylation patterns among different chemotherapy response groups. The prediction model based on the research results of chemotherapy efficacy has certain accuracy and practicality. However, longitudinal prognostic follow-up revealed no significant association between CAB39L methylation status and survival outcomes. Notably, an interaction between CAB39L methylation and environmental factors was identified, suggesting potential gene-environment interplay in gastric carcinogenesis.

In the bioinformatics analysis of GC tissues, it was found that the expression of CAB39L was significantly lower in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues, and a negative correlation was observed between CAB39L expression and methylation levels. In the subsequent two-phase study, it was discovered that the methylation level of CAB39L in PBLs of GC patients was higher than that in the HCs. Furthermore, mRNA level analysis revealed that the expression of CAB39L in PBLs of GC patients in high-methylation group was significantly lower than that in low-methylation cohort. These findings strongly supported the notion that promoter hypermethylation of CAB39L drives transcriptional silencing, thereby promoting the initiation and progression of GC. Cancer-associated inflammation drives tumor immune evasion and progression by remodeling the epigenetic characteristics of immune cell subsets. These alterations involve dynamic regulation of DNA methylation (26). DNA methylation is a covalent modification process catalyzed by DNMT, which mainly acts on CpG sites (27-29). Studies have shown that the DNA methylation pattern also accumulates and changes continuously in the early stage of tumor occurrence (30,31). We identified hypermethylated CpG sites and CpG islands in GC patients, consistent with the findings in GC cell lines and tissues of Li et al. (8) and reinforcing the importance of this epigenetic landscape in GC.

Platinum-based chemotherapy combined with fluorouracil is often used as a first-line treatment regimen for GC (32). Intriguingly, a significant positive linkage was found between CAB39L expression and the IC50 values of cisplatin as well as 5-fluorouracil in GC tissues, indicating that CAB39L suppression enhances chemosensitivity. This was further validated in our two-stage study, where advanced non-progressive patients exhibited CAB39L hypermethylation at specific CpG sites and CGI shores within PBLs. While the precise mechanism requires elucidation, bioinformatic analysis offers compelling insights: PPI networks and GO/KEGG enrichment analyses indicated that CAB39L, akin to its paralog CAB39, functions through the LKB1-AMPK pathway (33). Given that CAB39 promotes cisplatin resistance via AMPK-mediated autophagy (LKB1-AMPK-LC3 axis) and ROS regulation, we proposed that CAB39L hypermethylation silences expression, attenuating AMPK pathway activity (34,35). This suppression consequently enhances sensitivity to platinum and fluorouracil-based therapies by potentially inhibiting key drug-resistance mechanisms like autophagy. To facilitate the translation of research findings into clinical practice, this study developed a predictive model based on CpG sites of CAB39L gene, which was associated with GC risk and chemotherapy efficacy. By integrating the methylation status of CpG sites, environmental factors, and clinical data, the model significantly improved the accuracy of predicting the efficacy of chemotherapy.

Analysis of gastric tumor tissues indicated that lower CAB39L methylation correlated with improved survival rates. However, this association was not observed with CAB39L methylation status in PBLs during longitudinal follow-up. This discrepancy can be comprehensively explained from multiple perspectives. First, CAB39L methylation in tumor tissues directly regulates gene expression in tumor cells and further modulates core malignant phenotypes, including tumor proliferation and invasion, thus serving as a direct functional target for survival outcomes. In contrast, methylation in PBLs is mostly a secondary epigenetic alteration induced by tumor-associated inflammation, which only reflects a systemic correlation but does not directly participate in tumor malignant progression, thus representing an indirect systemic indicator. Therefore, the CAB39L promoter methylation pattern in GC tissues cannot be completely recapitulated in peripheral blood (36). In addition, this discrepancy likely reflects the dynamic and tissue-specific nature of DNA methylation, which fluctuates during development, disease progression, and across different cell types. Methylation levels in easily accessible PBLs may not fully capture the complex, compartment-specific epigenetic landscape of the tumor microenvironment influencing long-term outcomes (37). Moreover, the prognostic value of peripheral blood methylation tends to be masked by clinical factors, such as traditional clinical variables including TNM stage and differentiation grade, leading to the obscuration of its prognostic correlation by more dominant clinical factors (38). The above evidence indicates that the discrepancy in prognostic value of methylation between tumor tissues and peripheral blood cells is a prevalent phenomenon.

The interaction between immune cell infiltration and expression of multiple genes in GC has been widely recognized (39-41). Through the TIMER database, this study conducted an in-depth exploration of the correlation between the expression level of the CAB39L gene and the level of immune infiltration. The results of this study showed that CAB39L expression levels significantly correlate with the infiltration of multiple immune cell types, including CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, macrophages, and neutrophils, suggesting a pivotal role for CAB39L in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment. Notably, in patients with high CAB39L expression, elevated macrophage infiltration was strongly associated with poor GC prognosis. As key components of the tumor microenvironment, macrophages promote tumor progression through pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, angiogenesis stimulation, and suppression of antitumor immune responses (42). Thus, CAB39L might indirectly influence GC progression and clinical outcomes by regulating macrophage recruitment and function.

A significant interaction exists between DNA methylation and environmental factors, playing a crucial role in tumorigenesis and cancer progression (43). Our study further revealed that the methylation status of CpG sites in the CAB39L gene interacts with age and gender to influence GC risk. Previous studies have reported gender-specific differences in DNA methylation patterns, with females generally exhibiting lower genome-wide methylation levels than males (44). When hypermethylation is present, the additional contribution of gender may become less apparent. Additionally, age-related DNA hypermethylation predominantly occurs in promoter regions, which are common sites for methylation alterations in cancer-related genes (45). Methylation levels tend to increase with advancing age (46). When tumor suppressor gene methylation reaches elevated levels, their function may approach complete inactivation, potentially resulting in a plateau effect where further hypermethylation fails to confer additional cancer risk.

This represents the inaugural study exploring the relationship between the methylation level of CAB39L gene in PBLs and GC. Additionally, this study had a larger sample size and comprehensively covered all CpG sites within the promoter region of CAB39L. Nonetheless, our research was subject to certain constraints. Firstly, the sample was from Anhui province, China, and its representativeness was limited. Secondly, the follow-up period for the prognostic study was relatively short. The prognostic value of peripheral blood CAB39L gene methylation was not verified in this study and emphasized that future studies with larger peripheral blood cohorts and longer follow-up periods are needed to further explore the prognostic potential of these markers. Thirdly, the functional relevance of CAB39L methylation was primarily inferred from tumor tissue databases, and direct experimental evidence in PBLs is currently lacking; thus, the biological implication of PBL derived CAB39L methylation in GC merits further exploration to establish its precise functional role. Finally, the constructed prediction models were not validated. Moreover, the specific mechanism of CAB39L methylation affecting the risk and development of GC and the difference in chemotherapy efficacy still need to be further studied in vivo and in vitro.

Conclusions

The present study showed that the methylation level of CAB39L might be related to the risk of GC and the response to chemotherapy. The prediction model based on CAB39L methylation site has potential value in predicting the efficacy of chemotherapy. However, the impact of CAB39L gene methylation in PBLs on GC prognosis was not observed. Meanwhile, the methylation level of CAB39L of GC patients was associated with its gene expression level in the peripheral blood. Interactions existed between CAB39L gene methylation and environmental factors in GC risk. The findings provide a theoretical foundation and novel insights for further elucidating the pathogenesis of GC, improving early diagnosis rates, and developing more effective treatment strategies.

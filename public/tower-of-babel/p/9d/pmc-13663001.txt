The role of machine perfusion in preventing recurrence after liver transplant for hepatocellular carcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Ibrahim U. Garzali, Ez El Din Abou zeid, Ali Aloun, Amina I. Elyakub, Abdurrahman A. Sheshe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a leading indication for liver transplantation (LT), but post-transplant recurrence remains a major cause of treatment failure. Ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) has been implicated in tumor recurrence through modulation of the hepatic microenvironment. Machine perfusion (MP) techniques reduce IRI and may improve oncologic outcomes. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the impact of MP versus static cold storage (SCS) on HCC recurrence following LT.MethodsA systematic search of PubMed, CENTRAL, Scopus, Google Scholar and ClinicalTrials.gov was conducted from database inception to January 31, 2026. Studies comparing HCC recurrence after LT using MP or SCS graft preservation were included. The primary outcome was post-transplant HCC recurrence. Secondary outcomes included microvascular invasion (MVI), pre-transplant alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), Milan criteria status, and neoadjuvant therapies. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) and standardized mean differences (SMDs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using RevMan 5.4.1.ResultsTen studies involving 6,652 patients (4,944 SCS and 1,708 MP) were included. Nine studies evaluated hypothermic oxygenated perfusion and one assessed ischemia-free liver transplantation. Meta-analysis demonstrated a significantly higher risk of HCC recurrence in the SCS group compared with the MP group (OR 2.34, 95% CI 1.40–3.91; p = 0.001; I²=53%). Sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of the finding (OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.48–3.45; p = 0.0002; I²=22%). Subgroup analysis restricted to hypothermic oxygenated perfusion also favored MP (OR 1.97, 95% CI 1.25–3.10; p = 0.003). No significant differences were observed between groups regarding pre-transplant AFP levels (SMD 0.02, 95% CI − 0.09 to 0.13; p = 0.68), Milan criteria status (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.59–1.19; p = 0.32), use of neoadjuvant therapies, or MVI.ConclusionsMachine perfusion for liver graft preservation is associated with a lower risk of HCC recurrence after liver transplantation compared with static cold storage. This benefit appears independent of baseline tumor characteristics and pre-transplant treatments. Given the predominance of retrospective studies, further prospective multicenter studies and randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm these findings and define the oncologic role of machine perfusion in liver transplantation for HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05142-4.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is considered the third most common cause of cancer-related mortality [1–3]. One of the important risk factor for HCC is liver cirrhosis. Liver transplantation for HCC associated with cirrhosis is an ideal treatment option because it removes the cancer and cirrhotic liver thereby reducing the risks associated with the cirrhotic liver [4, 5]. This is the reason why HCC is among the main indications for liver transplantation over the past decade. To improve survival after liver transplant for HCC, multiple criteria have been developed for appropriate patient selection and by extension reducing recurrence and improving recurrence free survival and overall survival [6–8]. The criterion proposed by Mazzaferro et al. is among the main criteria developed and the survival rate attributed to patients within these criteria was 75%. However, one of the main limitations of these criteria is that it is very restrictive and so efforts were made to expand the criteria and develop other criteria that are not as restrictive [6–8].

Recurrence rate of 15% to 20% have been consistently reported after liver transplantation for HCC [9, 10]. Multiple factors have been associated with increased risk of recurrence. The factors include tumour related factors such as maximum tumour diameter of > 5 cm, multiple tumour nodules, poorly differentiated tumours and microvascular invasion. Other factors include increased platelet lymphocyte ratio, increased neutrophil lymphocyte ratio and elevated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP). Post-transplant factors associated with recurrence after liver transplant include the choice of immunosuppressive regimen, frequency of acute rejection episodes and adjuvant serofenib [11–13].

Recent studies have shown increased risk of cancer recurrence associated with ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) after liver transplantation [14–17]. The increased risk was said to be precipitated by the changes in hepatic microenvironment with release of growth factors and mobilization of progenitor cells caused all of which promote seeding of circulating tumour cells and the development recurrence and metastases [15, 16]. The use of dynamic preservation techniques such as machine perfusion of liver graft is associated with reduced IRI. In hypothermic oxygenated perfusion, there is reversal of the hypoxia of Static cold storage which results in increased mitochondrial function and reduced the generation and accumulation of reactive oxygen species which are mainly responsible for IRI [18–20]. In normothermic machine perfusion, IRI is dampened by reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines, neutrophil infiltration. It causes alteration of gene-expression profiles of liver tissue from pro-inflammation to pro-regeneration. It also reduces the number of interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and interleukin (IL)-17-producing T cells and increase the CD4+ CD25high CD127neg FOXP3pos regulatory T cell pool [21]. Tang et al. [22] described a modification of normothermic perfusion which consists of insitu normothermic machine perfusion starting before the harvesting of the graft and continues after harvesting with exsitu normothermic machine perfusion. They describe the technique as ischemia free liver transplantation. It has been hypothesized that the reduction in IRI associated with machine perfusion may translate to reduction in the risk of recurrence for patients that had liver transplantation for HCC. However, reports from studies comparing recurrence after machine perfusion versus static cold storage have been inconsistent [22–25]. Also most of the studies were retrospective studies and randomized controlled trials are lacking.

We performed this systematic review and meta-analysis of studies that compared the recurrence of HCC in patients that had liver graft preservation with static cold storage vs. machine perfusion during liver transplantation.

Materials and methods

Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) guideline was the guide in performing this review. The protocol for the review was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews, PROSPERO (CRD42023491647).

Search strategy

The following databases PubMed/MEDLINE, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), ClinicalTrials.gov, Scopus, and Google Scholar were systematically searched by two independent reviewers (IUG and AA). The search ranged from database inception until 31 January 2026. Additional relevant studies were identified through manual screening of reference lists of included articles and related reviews to ensure completeness of the search.

The search strategy incorporated both MeSH terms and free-text keywords related to liver transplantation, machine perfusion, and hepatocellular carcinoma. The following search terms and their combinations were used: “machine perfusion”, “dynamic perfusion”, “normothermic machine perfusion”, “hypothermic oxygenated perfusion”, “NMP”, “HOPE”, “static cold storage”, “extended criteria grafts”, “liver transplantation”, “hepatocellular carcinoma”, “HCC”, “liver cancer”, “recurrence”, and “ischemia-reperfusion injury”. Boolean operators (“AND” and “OR”) were used to combine search terms appropriately.

Studies were screened by two reviewers (IUG and AA) to assess for the fulfilment of the eligibility criteria. Disagreements among the reviewers regarding study selection or data extraction were resolved through discussion with another reviewer (AAS).

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria for a study to be included for the review are as follow: 1.Studies published from 1990 to date 2.Studies that compared recurrence of hepatocellular carcinoma after liver transplantation in patients whose graft was preserved using SCS and those whose grafts were preserved using machine perfusion. 3. Studies with full texts.

We excluded the following from our review: 1.Conference presentations 2. Lack of relevant data.

Quality assessment and risk of bias assessment

Quality assessment was performed independently by two reviewers using validated study design–specific tools. Retrospective cohort and case-control studies were assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS), which evaluates three domains: selection of study groups, comparability of groups, and ascertainment of exposure or outcome, with a maximum score of nine stars. Study quality was categorized according to conventional NOS thresholds as good (7–9 stars), fair (4–6 stars), and poor (0–3 stars). Randomized controlled trials were evaluated using the Jadad scale, which assesses randomization, blinding, and reporting of withdrawals/dropouts, with scores ranging from 0 to 5. Studies with a Jadad score of ≥ 3 were considered to be of good methodological quality. Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. Detailed scoring criteria and guidance for both the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale and the Jadad scale are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Publication bias

For an outcome that included 10 or more studies, publication bias was evaluated using the funnel plot.

Data extraction

Data extraction was be performed by 2 reviewers (AA and AIE). The data extracted included: first author, year of manuscript publication, study design, number of patients in each group, type of organ preservation technique, characteristics of HCC before transplant, transplant criteria, pre-transplant HCC therapy in the form of TACE, SBRT, RFA and resection and recurrence rate. If the study did not report the recurrence rate, then the data is extrapolated from the last reported recurrence free survival. Specifically, the recurrence rate was calculated as the complement of the reported recurrence-free survival estimate at the corresponding follow-up time point (recurrence rate = 1 − RFS). Studies requiring this conversion were Tang et al. [22] and Shu et al. [26]. A third reviewer (IUG) is involved to resolve the conflict between the reviewers.

Outcome

The primary outcome we analyzed was the recurrence rate of HCC per group. The secondary outcome we analyzed included MVI, pre transplant serum AFP, neoadjuvant treatment and transplant criteria.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were done using RevMan software (version 5.4.1). For dichotomous variable, the pooled odds ratio (OR) was calculated with 95 per cent confidence interval. However, for continuous variable, the weighted mean difference (WMD) or standardized mean difference (SMD) with 95 per cent CI was calculated from the mean and standard deviation reported from individual studies. In studies in which the mean and standard deviation were not reported, we utilized the Wan et al. [27] method to extract mean and standard deviation form median and interquartile range was. The pooled effect size was calculated using fixed-effects model if the data were not significantly heterogeneous. Otherwise, a random-effects model was used. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistics. I2 > 25% was considered as a statistically significant heterogeneity. Sensitivity analysis was done by sequential elimination of each of the included studies in the meta-analysis to identify the main source of heterogeneity.

Results

Results were reported in accord with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) checklist.

Study selection process and description of selected studies

We identified 242 references during the initial search. Out of these, 139 articles were excluded because of duplicate publications (Fig. 1). The 103 remaining references were further assessed based on titles and abstracts. Ninety one references were excluded for lack of relevant data. Twelve full text articles were retrieved but 2 articles were excluded for lack of relevant data.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome we compared was the overall recurrence after liver transplant for hepatocellular carcinoma. Nine of the included studies [22–26, 28, 30–32] compared the HCC recurrence rate between patients whose grafts were preserved with SCS and machine perfusion (DMP). Pooled sample size per group revealed that 1763 patients were in the SCS group and 115 patients belonged to the MP group. The meta-analysis revealed that recurrence is significantly higher in the SCS group when compared to MP group with an Odds ratio (OR) = 2.34 (95% CI 1.40–3.91) and p = 0.001. There was significant heterogeneity among the included studies with I2 = 53% so the random effects meta-analysis was used to estimate the effect size. Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of the pooled estimate. First, a leave-one-out analysis was conducted by sequentially removing each individual study to assess whether any single study disproportionately influenced the overall effect estimate. The pooled effect remained statistically significant after removal of each individual study, indicating that the findings were robust. In addition, studies identified as major contributors to heterogeneity (Tang et al. [22]. and Rigo et al. [25].) were excluded in a secondary sensitivity analysis. This reduced heterogeneity from I² = 53% to I² = 22%, while the direction and statistical significance of the effect remained unchanged (OR = 2.26, 95% CI 1.48–3.45, p = 0.0002). The details of this meta-analysis are displayed in Fig. 2A. Sub group analysis of only studies that used HOPE for liver graft preservation also showed that overall recurrence is higher among graft preserved with SCS compared to HOPE with OR of 1.97 (95% CI 1.25–3.10) and p value of 0.003. (Fig. 2B).

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes we compared in this meta-analysis were pre transplant tumour characteristics that may affect recurrence (AFP, microvascular invasion and Milan criteria) and pre transplant neoadjuvant treatments that may affect recurrence (TACE, RFA, SBRT and Resection).

Microvascular Invasion (MVI)

Five studies [22, 24, 25, 32], with a pooled sample size of 4010 patients in the SCS group and 833 patients in the machine perfusion group, compared the proportion of patients with microvascular invasion (MVI) before transplantation. Meta-analysis revealed that relative more patients in the MP group had MVI when compared to SCS. But the difference is not significant with an OR of 1.49 (95% CI 1.01–2.21) and a P value of 0.05. The details of the meta-analysis are displayed in Fig. 4A.

Milan criteria

Five studies [22, 24, 25, 29, 32], with a pooled sample size of 1000 patients in the SCS group and 504 patients in the machine perfusion group, compared the proportion of patients within the Milan criteria prior to transplantation. Pooled analysis revealed no significant difference between the patients in both groups with an OR of 0.83 (95% CI 0.59–1.19) and a P value of 0.32. (Fig. 4B).

Pre-transplant serum AFP

Four studies [22, 24, 25, 31, 32] with a pooled sample size of 749 patients in the SCS group and 492 patients in the machine perfusion group, compared pre-transplant serum AFP levels. Pooled analysis revealed no significant difference between the patients in both groups with SMD = 0.02 [95% CI: -0.09, 0.13] and a P value of 0.68. (Fig. 4C).

Neoadjuvant treatments

The use of transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) or radiofrequency ablation (RFA) prior to transplantation was compared in 6 studies [22, 24, 25, 28, 30, 32] with a pooled sample size of 1019 patients in the SCS group and 357 patients in the machine perfusion group. Meta-analysis revealed that there was no statistically significant difference between the 2 groups in terms of pre transplant treatment with TACE/RFA with an OR of 0.92 (95% CI 0.60–1.41) and a P value of 0.70. (Fig. 5A)

Discussion

With rising incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), HCC has become a common indication for liver transplantation (LT). Over the past decade, HCC has been reported to account for up to 24% of liver transplant registrations and 27% of liver transplants performed in the United States. It was reported as the most common reason for waitlist additions [33–35].

Post-transplant recurrence has remained an major challenge to transplant surgeons, hepatologist and oncologist alike. Multitude of factors has been implicated in recurrence of HCC after liver transplantation. Some of the factors are tumour related factors such as maximum tumour diameter of > 5 cm, multiple tumour nodules, poorly differentiated tumours and presence of microvascular invasion [1, 5, 6, 11]. Pre transplant neoadjuvant therapy has also been reported to affect recurrence after liver transplantation. Trans-arterial chemoembolization (TACE), TARE, RFA and SBRT have been reported to be associated with reduced risk of recurrence after liver transplant for HCC [36–39]. However, previous hepatic resection before eventual liver transplant for HCC is said to be associated with increased risk of recurrence [40]. Other factors are host factors and they include increased platelet lymphocyte ratio, increased neutrophil lymphocyte ratio and elevated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP). In addition to conventional locoregional downstaging therapies, including transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), radiofrequency ablation (RFA), stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT), and surgical resection, immunotherapy has recently emerged as a potential bridging strategy for selected patients with hepatocellular carcinoma awaiting liver transplantation [36, 41]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, including nivolumab and pembrolizumab, as well as combination regimens such as atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, have demonstrated promising antitumor activity and may facilitate tumor control or downstaging in patients who are otherwise at risk of progression beyond transplant eligibility criteria [36, 41]. However, the application of immunotherapy before transplantation remains controversial due to concerns regarding immune-mediated allograft rejection, as checkpoint inhibition may enhance recipient T-cell activity against the transplanted graft. The optimal interval between the last dose of immunotherapy and transplantation (washout period) remains uncertain, and current evidence is limited by small sample sizes and heterogeneous treatment protocols [36, 41]. Furthermore, the potential impact of pre-transplant immunotherapy on graft preservation strategies, including machine perfusion, remains largely unexplored. Future studies are required to determine the appropriate patient selection, timing, and safety profile of immunotherapy as a bridge to liver transplantation, particularly in combination with evolving graft preservation technologies.

A number of Post-transplant factors have also been associated with recurrence after liver transplant for HCC and they include the choice of immunosuppressive regimen, frequency of acute rejection episodes and the use adjuvant serofenib [11–14].

An association between severity ischemic reperfusion injury and recurrence of HCC after liver transplantation has been reported recently. Grat et al. [16] reported that IRI evidenced by post reperfusion AST and LDH is associated with increase recurrence for patients that are within Milan and up to 7 criteria. The severity of IRI is linked to duration of hypoxia, duration of vascular occlusion, age of the donor and degree of steatosis among others [14, 42–44]. Ischemic reperfusion injury results in cell death and mitochondrial dysfunction which subsequently lead to production of reactive oxygen species and oxidative stress. Oxidative stress leads to release of damage associated membrane pattern, inflammatory cytokines and microvascular dysfunction. Damage associated molecular pattern (DAMP) activates CXCL10 which indirectly promote tumour recurrence via production of pro inflammatory cytokines such as TNFα and IL-6. These cytokines increases the expression of adhesive molecules which encourage tumour growth and engraftment. CXCL10 also directly promote tumour recurrence by upregulating Rho-associated protein kinase-1 (ROCK1), VEGF and MMP all of which increases cell motility and invasiveness [14, 16, 42–44].

Machine perfusion techniques are associated with reduced IRI of liver graft which reduced the risk of early allograft dysfunction, primary non function and ischemic cholangiopathy [45–49]. This method of graft preservation has been utilized for preservation of marginal graft and extended criteria donors. It is postulated that machine perfusion techniques may reduce the risk of tumour recurrence after liver transplant for HCC. However, the reports from retrospective studies have been inconsistent. Mueller et al. [24] in a retrospective study reported that HOPE is associated with reduced tumour recurrence when compared to static cold storage despite extended tumour criteria. Similar results were also reported by Tang et al. [22] when they compared IFLT (insitu NMP and exsitu NMP) to SCS. However, in the studies of Schlegel et al. [23], there was no difference in term of HCC recurrence after liver transplantation when they compared HOPE to SCS. Recent studies by Rigo et al. [25] also compared HOPE to SCS and they found the preservation of liver graft by HOPE is no associated with reduced recurrence after liver transplant for HCC. Our meta-analysis revealed that recurrence is significantly higher in the SCS group when compared to DMP group and the difference was statistically significant. This reduction in recurrence is observed despite the fact that the patients in the machine perfusion group showed no statistically significant difference in term of pre-transplant serum AFP, pre transplant therapy (TACE, RFA, SBRT or resection) and microvascular invasion. The transplant criteria between the two groups was also found to be similar as the meta-analysis reveals similar proportion of patients were within Milan criteria.

Although subgroup analysis was performed according to the type of machine perfusion, specifically hypothermic oxygenated perfusion (HOPE), further stratified analyses based on other potentially relevant factors, including donor type, follow-up duration, and publication year, were not feasible. This was primarily due to the limited number of included studies and inconsistent reporting of baseline donor characteristics, graft parameters, and follow-up data across studies. In addition, several studies included mixed populations or did not provide outcome data stratified according to these variables, preventing reliable pooled estimates within specific subgroups. Therefore, further subgroup analyses may have resulted in underpowered comparisons and potentially misleading conclusions. Future prospective studies with standardized reporting of donor characteristics, preservation protocols, and long-term follow-up outcomes are required to enable more comprehensive subgroup analyses and better define which patient and graft characteristics derive the greatest benefit from machine perfusion.

Future studies should incorporate more refined tumor biology–based risk stratification approaches to better identify patients who may derive the greatest benefit from machine perfusion. Preoperative metabolic parameters obtained from 18 F-FDG PET-CT, such as maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), may provide additional information regarding tumor aggressiveness and recurrence risk beyond conventional clinicopathological criteria [50]. Stratifying patients according to these metabolic characteristics may allow future analyses to determine whether the potential protective effect of machine perfusion differs among patients with varying biological tumor profiles. Prospective studies integrating PET-CT parameters with graft preservation strategies, molecular tumor characteristics, and long-term oncological outcomes are warranted to develop personalized approaches for liver transplantation in patients with HCC [50, 51].

Limitations

Despite the important findings of this study, some limitations should be acknowledged. First, majority of the studies included in this meta-analysis were retrospective in design, which makes the results susceptible to selection bias, reporting bias, and unmeasured confounding. Second, the sample size of patients in the machine perfusion group was relatively small compared with the static cold storage group, which may limit the statistical power of the analysis and affect the precision of the pooled estimates. Third, moderate heterogeneity was observed among the included studies, likely due to differences in study populations, donor characteristics, tumor biology and follow-up duration. In addition, some included studies did not report recurrence rates directly so this was derived from the reported recurrence-free survival estimates. Although this approach allowed inclusion of relevant studies, it may introduce uncertainty because Kaplan–Meier-derived estimates are influenced by censoring, follow-up duration, and competing events. Therefore, these estimates should be interpreted cautiously. Finally, donor and graft-related variables, including donor age, degree of graft steatosis, and cold ischemia time, were inconsistently reported among the included studies. As a result, adjustment for these potential confounders was not feasible, and residual confounding may have influenced the observed associations. Therefore, while our findings suggest a potential association between machine perfusion and reduced recurrence of hepatocellular carcinoma following liver transplantation, these results should be interpreted with caution, and well-designed additional randomized trials with careful adjustment of key confounders such as tumor type, tumor size, tumor number, machine perfusion type, donor liver quality, and post-transplant immune suppressive treatment are needed explore further the role of machine perfusion strategies on tumor recurrence.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this meta-analysis suggests that the use of machine perfusion for liver graft preservation may be associated with a lower risk of hepatocellular carcinoma recurrence following liver transplantation compared with static cold storage. This effect appears to occur despite similar pre-transplant tumor characteristics, including microvascular invasion, serum alpha-fetoprotein levels, and the proportion of patients within the Milan Criteria.

However, given the retrospective nature of most of the included studies, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Future large-scale prospective studies and randomized controlled trials are needed to further clarify the impact of machine perfusion on oncologic outcomes following liver transplantation for hepatocellular carcinoma.

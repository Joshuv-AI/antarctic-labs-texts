Predictive value of systemic inflammation response index for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk in patients with hypercholesterolemia: a machine learning study with dual-cohort validation
Authors: Yu Chen, Weikang Huang, Shihan Zhao, Zhuoqi Ge, Yan Liu, Ruibing Huang, Dongmei Li, Qing Xu, Xingzhen Long, Kai Wei, Qi Chen, Changcheng Sheng, Cailin Tang, Xue Bai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundResidual cardiovascular risk persists in patients with hypercholesterolemia despite lipid-lowering therapy, underscoring the importance of inflammation in ASCVD development. This study evaluated the relationship between Systemic Inflammation Response Index (SIRI) (a composite biomarker derived from neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts) and ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. And to develop an interpretable machine learning (ML) model for predicting ASCVD risk in patients with hypercholesterolemia.MethodsThis study utilized data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (2001–2018), including a total of 6,645 patients with hypercholesterolemia. Additionally, a independent external cohort of 357 patients from Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital served as the validation cohort. Then, we used ML to analyze the effect of SIRI on ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia and established four risk prediction models. Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was used to evaluate model performance. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were applied for model interpretation, and a web-based application was developed for clinical use.ResultsOur findings indicated that SIRI is consistently associated with ASCVD in hypercholesterolemia patients in both cohorts. SIRI and other seven features were used to construct ML models. The XGBoost model achieved an AUC of 0.8001 in the internal validation cohort and 0.7030 in the external cohort. The model retained strong clinical relevance. SHAP analysis highlighted elevated SIRI levels as an important predictor of ASCVD risk in patients with hypercholesterolemia. The inclusion of novel inflammatory markers such as SIRI enhanced the model’s discriminative capability.ConclusionsThe study findings revealed that high SIRI levels were an independent risk factor for ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. In addition, this study constructed the first interpretable ML model combined with SIRI for ASCVD prediction in hypercholesterolemia patients. The model demonstrated acceptable performance and moderate generalizability. While its external specificity was limited, the model may still serve as a useful risk stratification aid to support early identification of high-risk individuals with hypercholesterolemia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02765-6.Keywords: Systemic inflammation response index, Hypercholesterolemia, Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, Machine learning, Risk prediction

Introduction

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) is a major cause of death and illness worldwide [1, 2]. Hypercholesterolemia, particularly characterized by elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), is a well-established factor in atherosclerosis formation and long-term cardiovascular risk [3–5]. While pharmacological interventions such as statin therapy significantly reduce LDL-C levels, a significant proportion of cardiovascular events, commonly referred to as residual cardiovascular risk, continue to occur despite optimal lipid control [6, 7]. Like lipids, inflammation is essential for the development and progression of atherosclerosis [8].

Recent studies have shown that Systemic Inflammation Response Index (SIRI) as a marker of inflammation may contribute to this residual risk [9, 10]. The SIRI integrates neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts to reflect systemic immune activation. Compared with traditional markers, SIRI may provide a broader measure of inflammatory burden and immune dysregulation relevant to ASCVD [11]. Although prior studies have linked SIRI to cardiovascular outcomes in various populations, its predictive value in ASCVD patients with hypercholesterolemia remains insufficiently characterized. Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment are essential to reduce the risk of ASCVD in hypercholesterolemia.

Recently, advances in Machine learning (ML) and big data have gained attention in medicine, especially for disease prediction and risk assessment [12–14]. The ML models can identify complex patterns that traditional models may miss, these intricate patterns hold significant potential for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of disease prediction. However, there are few studies that have evaluated the integration of SIRI with clinical and lipid parameters using ML approaches to predict ASCVD risk specifically in patients with hypercholesterolemia.

Therefore, in this study, we comprehensively evaluated the impact of SIRI on ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. In addition, we used SIRI to construct ML models in combination with other clinical features to predict ASCVD in such patients. The model integrates Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) to provide global and personalized insights and is deployed through a web-based application to enhance clinical utility.

Materials and methods

Data source and study population

This retrospective study utilized data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), a program conducted by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention to evaluate the health and nutritional status of adults and children in the United States. The NHANES dataset has been approved by the Research Ethics Review Board of the National Center for Health Statistics, and all participants or their legal representatives provided written informed consent. This study included NHANES data collected from 2001 to 2018, with disease status determined based on standardized medical condition questionnaires.

Exposure and outcome definitions

The primary exposure in this study was hypercholesterolemia, defined based on participants’ responses to the Medical Conditions Questionnaire. Participants were considered to have hypercholesterolemia if they answered “Yes” to the question: “Has a doctor or other health professional ever told you that you had high cholesterol?”

The primary outcome was ASCVD. Participants were classified as ASCVD-positive if they answered “Yes” to having ever been diagnosed with any of the following: coronary heart disease, angina, myocardial infarction, or stroke.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria were: (1) age < 20 years, (2) pregnancy (3) missing data on total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), or triglycerides (TG).

Data collection and processing

This study collected and analyzed a comprehensive set of demographic, lifestyle, lipid, glucose metabolism, liver and kidney function, and inflammation-related biomarkers to systematically assess key factors that influence ASCVD risk. Demographic variables included age, sex, and race. Lifestyle factors encompassed smoking status, defined as having smoked more than 100 cigarettes in a lifetime. Lipid and metabolic biomarkers included TG, TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c). Liver and kidney function markers comprised alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), serum creatinine (CREA), albumin, globulin, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, total bilirubin, total calcium, and uric acid. In addition, disease status variables included hypertension, kidney failure, and diabetes mellitus (DM). To further explore the role of metabolic and inflammatory status in ASCVD, several composite indices were calculated and analyzed, including the atherogenic coefficient (AC) [15], systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) [16], systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) [17].

Model development, validation

To identify potential predictive features, we used the “Boruta algorithm” along with “Spearman’s rank correlation” analysis. Boruta algorithm combines the Boruta feature selection method with SHAP, keeping the features that have a significant impact on the target variable. This helps improve both the understanding and performance of the model. The key parameters used in the “Boruta algorithm” are summarized in Supplementary Information Table S1. Four ML models were created to predict ASCVD in patients with high cholesterol: Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT), and Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM). The data was then randomly split, with 70% used for training and 30% for internal validation [18]. Due to class imbalance in the dataset, class weighting or the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (Supplementary Information Table S2) was applied to the training set within the machine learning models. We measured the performance of the models using standard metrics, such as area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), decision curve analysis (DCA) curves, sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and F1-score. To test the model further, we performed five and ten-fold cross-validation on the training data. Furthermore, we used SHAP method to visualize the contribution of each variable in the machine learning models and to interpret the model’s risk prediction mechanism. SHAP provides both global explanations (to assess overall feature importance across the dataset) and local explanations (to evaluate feature impact on individual predictions), which improves model transparency. Finally, the best model was tested on an external dataset to check how well it works in different settings and its clinical usefulness. The detailed process is illustrated in Fig. 1. External validation

To rigorously evaluate the performance of the ML model under diverse conditions and its clinical applicability, we performed external validation using a dataset of cardiovascular disease patients admitted to Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital from 2021 to 2024. The criteria for including and excluding patients in the external validation group were the same as those used in the original model group, making sure the data was comparable and the results reliable. By evaluating the model’s predictive performance in an independent population, we assessed its robustness across different data distributions, thereby confirming its effectiveness for real-world clinical application.

Web application deployment

To facilitate clinical use, a web application was developed using “Flask” as the Python-based backend web framework and “Bootstrap” as the frontend UI framework. The application allows clinicians to input patient-specific variables and obtain individualized ASCVD risk estimates.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were done using R software (version 4.2.2), following the survey design rules of the NHANES. During data processing, variables with more than 20% missing values were removed. For those with 20% (Supplementary Information Fig. S1) or fewer missing values were imputed using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) in R, with predictive mean matching (PMM) specified as the imputation method to ensure imputed values remained within a plausible range of the observed data. To evaluate the robustness of the imputation approach, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using an alternative imputation method (random forest). As shown in Supplementary Information Table S3, confirming the stability of the imputation results. To account for the multistage probability sampling design of NHANES, all analyses incorporated appropriate sample weights, adjusted for clustering effects, and stratified by primary sampling units. Continuous variables were shown as means ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using the t-test or Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages), and differences between groups were tested with chi-square tests. Moreover, we performed univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses to assess the association between each variable and ASCVD outcomes in patients with hypercholesterolemia. In the results, variables with P < 0.1 were included in the multivariable logistic regression model. The significance threshold was set at P < 0.05. Python (version 3.5) and R software were used to build the ML models, plotting and statistical analysis.

The results show that the average age of patients in the ASCVD group was 66 years, while in the No-ASCVD group, it was 55 years, suggesting that ASCVD patients are generally older. In addition, smoking, diabetes, hypertension, and kidney failure were more common in the ASCVD group than in the No-ASCVD group, indicating a possible link between these conditions and ASCVD. Furthermore, age-specific analysis revealed a clear trend: ASCVD incidence increased with age in internal validation cohorts. Across all age groups, the incidence of ASCVD was consistently higher in males than in females, showing an overall increasing trend with advancing age. Model development and performance comparison

Using the “Boruta algorithm” and “Spearman’s rank correlation” analysis, we identified eight key features for predicting ASCVD: Age, LDL-C, Hypertension, CREA, Smoke, SIRI, ALT and TG (Supplementary Information Fig. S2 and S3). Based on these features, we tested four ML models to predict ASCVD risk in patients with high cholesterol. As shown in Fig. 3A, the ROC curves indicate that the XGBoost model had the highest area under the curve (AUC = 0.8001) and XGBoost having a good net clinical benefit (Fig. 3C), making it the best-performing model. Further evaluation on the internal validation dataset (Fig. 4A) confirmed that XGBoost performed better across multiple metrics, with an accuracy of 72.57%, an F1-score of 49.77%, a sensitivity of 71.50%, and a specificity of 72.82%. In addition, the XGBoost also achieved good performance in the five and ten-fold cross-validation of the training set (Supplementary Information Fig. S4).

External validation of the final model

The external validation cohort included 357 patients with hypercholesterolemia, and their baseline characteristics are shown (Supplementary Information Table S4). When tested on this independent dataset, the XGBoost model achieved a relatively high AUC of 0.7030 (Fig. 3B) and had the greatest net clinical benefit (Fig. 3D), confirming its strong predictive ability. The XGBoost model also showed an accuracy of 71.99%, an F1-score of 80.31%, a sensitivity of 76.40%, and a specificity of 58.89% (Fig. 4B). These results further confirm the stability and reliability of the XGBoost model in predicting ASCVD risk, showing that it performs well across different datasets.

Feature importance

To gain deeper insights into the interpretability of the XGBoost model, we applied the SHAP algorithm to visualize the most predictive features of the model. The SHAP method enables both global interpretation, by identifying the most influential features across the entire dataset, and local interpretation, by explaining individual-level predictions, thereby enhancing model transparency. As illustrated in Fig. 5A and B, the SHAP summary plots highlighted the eight most influential features in the model. SHAP value analysis indicated that advanced age, hypertension, elevated CREA levels, smoke, elevated SIRI levels, and high TG levels were strongly associated with increased ASCVD risk in patients with hypercholesterolemia. And SHAP value analysis revealed a non-linear relationship between ALT and ASCVD risk. The SHAP value is near zero for a wide range of ALT values and only becomes negative (protective) at higher levels. Figure 5C presents SHAP waterfall plots for representative individuals with ASCVD and non-ASCVD, offering local interpretability by visualizing how each feature incrementally influences the final prediction. These plots revealed the cumulative effect of feature contributions toward the model’s risk estimation, allowing a clear understanding of how individual patient profiles drive the model’s decision-making process. Moreover, the Fig. 5D revealed the relationship between the actual values of these eight key features and their corresponding SHAP values. A SHAP value greater than zero indicated that the feature contributes to a higher likelihood of ASCVD prediction, whereas a SHAP value less than zero suggested a protective effect against ASCVD. For instance, both advanced age and elevated serum creatinine levels exhibited a positive correlation with SHAP values, suggesting that an increase in these variables significantly elevated the predicted ASCVD risk.

Furthermore, the majority of participants in our study cohort were undergoing statin therapy, which may reduce LDL-C levels and consequently attenuate its predictive power within the model (Fig. 6A). Pharmacological intervention could partially obscure the inherent risk effect of LDL-C by lowering its concentration. Therefore, to more comprehensively assess the impact of LDL-C, we conducted a stratified analysis based on LDL-C levels and compared baseline comorbidities across different LDL-C groups. The analysis revealed that ASCVD risk was not significantly reduced in the lower LDL-C group, and there were notable differences in the distribution of hypertension and Smoke across LDL-C categories (Fig. 6B). Additionally, further evaluation of biochemical parameters demonstrated significant variations in age, SIRI, TG, ALT, and CREA among the LDL-C subgroups (Fig. 6C). These findings suggest that the reduction in LDL-C levels may not be the sole determinant of ASCVD risk attenuation, and these comorbid conditions may play a critical role in ASCVD progression.

Discussion

In this study, we developed and externally validated an interpretable ML model to predict ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. By integrating nationally representative data from the NHANES with an independent hospital-based validation cohort, the model demonstrated a certain degree of generalizability and clinical applicability. Notably, one of the key findings of this study was the promising potential of the SIRI in ML-based ASCVD risk prediction. SIRI consistently emerged as one of the most important predictive features across in model, highlighting its potential clinical utility as an inflammation-related biomarker in patients with hypercholesterolemia.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to be combined with ML to predict the risk of ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. Among the four models tested, the XGBoost algorithm achieved the best performance, with an AUC of 0.8001 in internal validation and 0.7030 in external validation. Although predictive accuracy declined in the external cohort, the model maintained a relatively high sensitivity (76.40%) in the external cohort, supporting its potential utility for identifying high-risk individuals who may benefit from early preventive interventions. When compared to established risk prediction tools such as the Montreal FH-SCORE [19] and the SAFEHEART-RE model [20], which were specifically developed for familial hypercholesterolemia (FH), our model offers a broader hypercholesterolemic population. FH is a genetic and more severe form of hypercholesterolemia characterized by markedly elevated LDL-C levels and a substantially higher lifetime risk of ASCVD. While FH-based models are valuable for identifying very high-risk individuals, they are limited by relatively small sample sizes, ethnically homogeneous cohorts, and restricted external validation beyond FH populations. In contrast, our model was trained on a large, multi-ethnic, nationally representative dataset and externally validated in an independent hospital-based cohort. This design allows for broader applicability in real-world clinical settings.

SIRI integrates neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts into a composite index that reflects systemic immune and inflammatory activation [21, 22]. Unlike other inflammatory markers, SIRI captures a broader spectrum of inflammatory pathways and immune dysregulation [17, 23–25]. Previous studies have demonstrated associations between SIRI and adverse cardiovascular outcomes in general populations [26–28]. Our findings extend this evidence by demonstrating its predictive value for ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia. The positive correlation between elevated SIRI levels and ASCVD incidence suggests that systemic inflammation remains a key determinant of cardiovascular risk, even in the presence of lipid-lowering therapy. In recent years, increasing evidence has highlighted the role of inflammation in ASCVD pathogenesis, independent of cholesterol levels. Several landmark clinical trials provide evidence for inflammation. The CANTOS trial demonstrated that IL-1β inhibition with canakinumab significantly reduced ASCVD events without affecting lipid levels [29]. Similarly, the COLCOT and LoDoCo2 trials indicated that the anti-inflammatory drug colchicine has the potential to reduce the risk of cardiovascular events [30, 31]. This aligns with the concept of residual risk described in current guidelines [32, 33], whereby persistent inflammation contributes to ASCVD progression despite LDL-C control. Our results showed the predictive value of SIRI, that could help identify patients who may benefit from targeted anti-inflammatory interventions. Future prospective studies and randomized controlled trials are warranted to explore whether SIRI-guided therapy may reduce ASCVD events.

Furthermore, it is important to note that while LDL-C is a well-established risk factor for ASCVD in patients with hypercholesterolemia, its contribution to prediction was attenuated in our model, likely due to widespread statin use. Similar observations have been reported in previous studies [34, 35]. Our findings emphasize that LDL-C reduction alone does not eliminate ASCVD risk. The persistence of events despite lipid-lowering therapy highlights the importance of residual risk, mediated by other metabolic and inflammatory pathways. SIRI, as a readily available inflammatory biomarker, complements lipid markers and may help capture this residual risk more effectively.

The clinical implications of our findings are twofold. First, SIRI, derived from routine blood counts, represents a cost-effective and readily available biomarker that can be easily incorporated into ASCVD risk prediction. Second, by integrating SIRI into a transparent and interpretable machine learning framework, we provide a personalized risk estimation approach that may support more informed clinical decision-making. This strategy demonstrates the potential of leveraging systemic inflammatory indices in precision cardiovascular medicine. Furthermore, to enhance future translational potential, we have developed a web-based application prototype based on the final model, aiming to facilitate ease of use in clinical settings and bridge the gap between algorithmic development and bedside application.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, although the model demonstrated acceptable discriminative ability in the external validation cohort (AUC = 0.7030), the specificity was relatively low (58.89%), indicating a considerable false-positive rate. One potential reason for this may be the substantial differences between the training and external validation cohorts. The NHANES dataset represents a large, multi-ethnic, nationally representative U.S. population, whereas the external cohort was hospital-based and drawn from a single region in southwest China. These differences include racial differences, genetic background, health behaviors, and access to healthcare. Additionally, the relatively small sample size of the external validation cohort (n = 357) may have increased the variability in performance metrics, including specificity. These combined factors likely contributed to the differences between the development and validation datasets, which in turn affected the model’s generalizability. Notably, a drop in AUC between internal and external validation is not uncommon in clinical prediction modeling, particularly across heterogeneous populations. Similar findings were reported in a recent machine learning–based prognostic study by Chen et al. [35]. Therefore, while the model may provide useful risk stratification insights, its role as a stand-alone diagnostic tool should be interpreted with caution. Future studies involving larger, multi-center, and ethnically diverse cohorts are needed to further validate and improve the model’s clinical applicability. Another important limitation concerns the definition of hypercholesterolemia and ASCVD in the NHANES dataset. In this study, hypercholesterolemia and ASCVD were primarily defined based on self-reported responses to whether a healthcare professional had ever diagnosed the participant with high cholesterol and ASCVD. Although certain measures were taken during data collection to avoid systematic errors [36]. This self-reported approach may introduce recall bias, as participants may inaccurately remember or report past diagnoses. Moreover, this definition lacks objective laboratory-based criteria, which may lead to misclassification of lipid status and impact the robustness of model training.

Conclusion

This study developed and externally validated an interpretable ML model for ASCVD prediction in patients with hypercholesterolemia. By combining traditional risk factors and SIRI, the model achieved acceptable external generalizability and highlighted the importance of systemic inflammation in residual cardiovascular risk. The integration of SHAP enables transparent interpretation of the model’s predictions, addressing a key limitation of conventional ML approaches. Furthermore, the deployment of the model as a web-based clinical tool enhances its accessibility and supports practical implementation in real-world healthcare settings.

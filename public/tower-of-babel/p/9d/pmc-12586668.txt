Multi-stage iterative compressed sensing framework based on DIFF transformer and ISTA for remote sensing images
Authors: Rongrong Wang, Yong Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn remote sensing applications, image storage and compression require large amounts of memory and consume a lot of battery power. Compressed sensing (CS) can alleviate these problems while shifting the complexity to the decoding end. However, existing methods still face limitations, such as attention noise in standard Transformer architectures and gradient instability in deep-unfolded ISTA variants. To address these issues, we propose a multi-stage iterative compression sensing framework based on DIFF transformer and iterative shrinkage threshold algorithm (ISTA) for remote sensing images, which integrates local details and global structures and pays more attention to context-related information. Our framework includes sampling module and reconstruction module. The sensing matrix of the sampling module is obtained through data-driven training, and the initial reconstruction is performed based on the learned structural information. The reconstruction module uses DIFF Transformer and ISTA stacking and cross-talk to achieve multi-stage iterative reconstruction. Compared with other advanced methods, especially on the NWPU VHR-10 test set, our proposed method is improved by 2.98 dB (14.16 %), 3.15 dB (13.06 %), 2.84 dB (10.30 %) and 0.30 dB (0.01 %) over ISTA-Net+ method. In addition, the proposed method is almost unaffected by the added noise at low sampling rate .Keywords: DIFF transformer, Compressed sensing, Image reconstruction, Remote sensing, ISTASubject terms: Natural hazards, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Compressed Sensing (CS) has far-reaching background, influence and research significance in the field of remote sensing images1, 2. Due to its high resolution, multi-spectral and multi-temporal characteristics, remote sensing images generate a huge amount of data3. The traditional Nyquist sampling theorem requires a high sampling rate4, high data storage and transmission costs, and low processing efficiency. However, CS theory uses the sparsity of signals in certain transform domains to obtain sufficient information at a sampling rate far lower than that of traditional methods, thereby effectively reducing the cost of data acquisition, transmission and storage. This technology is not only particularly important in resource-constrained satellite and UAV remote sensing missions5, but also provides efficient solutions in applications such as change detection6, 7, environmental protection, and urban planning. Recently, new advances have been made in enhancing multi-scale feature representation and fine-grained fusion for aerial image analysis8, reflecting the growing trend of combining CS with high-level vision tasks. CS technology provides a new theoretical framework and method for remote sensing image processing. It combines knowledge from multiple disciplines such as signal processing, optimization theory, and machine learning, and promotes cross-disciplinary research and technological innovation in related fields. Researchers have continuously improved the application effect of CS in remote sensing images by improving CS algorithms, optimizing sampling matrices, and designing efficient reconstruction algorithms. At the same time, CS technology has also stimulated research interest in other types of data, such as video9, 10, radar signals11, 12, and medical imaging13, 14, expanding its application scope and influence. In short, CS in the field of remote sensing images not only solves the problem of collecting and processing large amounts of data, but also promotes the development of efficient data transmission and storage technology, improves the quality of image reconstruction, and promotes interdisciplinary research. It has broad and far-reaching influences and research significance.

The CS theoretical framework presents two crucial issues. The first issue involves designing a matrix to preserve the effective information of the signal during the sampling process. The second issue is how to reconstruct the target signal based on the measured signal . For a sparse signal , it contains only K non-zero values. To address this problem, scholars have been developing efficient, accurate, and stable reconstruction algorithms. There are four main categories of traditional reconstruction algorithms: convex optimization-based algorithms, greedy algorithms, combinatorial reconstruction algorithms, and Bayesian methods.

In the past few decades, there have been some image CS reconstruction methods based on deep learning (DL). The DL-based CS methods can be divided into two categories. The first is to combine traditional algorithms and networks, such as ISTA-Net15, ISTA-Net++16, ADMM-CSNet17, NeumNet18, AMP-Net19, etc. Compared with purely traditional algorithms, the reconstruction speed of these algorithms is significantly improved. The second is to use an end-to-end learning framework, such as DR2-Net20, ReconNet21, DPA-Net22 and CSNet+23. Although these algorithms do not require manual design, their interpretability is poor. Moreover, depending exclusively on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to capture local features may not be adequate to obtain global information.Transformer-based methods have been introduced to leverage global modeling for CS reconstruction. However, standard Transformers suffer from attention noise, reducing fine-detail capture and robustness. Meanwhile, deep-unfolded ISTA variants can face gradient instability, causing convergence and quality issues. These challenges call for a more effective, stable hybrid architecture. Recently, the DIFF Transformer24 can eliminate attention noise through the innovative differential attention mechanism, encourage the model to focus on key information, and successfully improve the performance of the model. We use the differential attention mechanism in both the encoder and decoder of the traditional Transformer, so that the model pays more attention to context-related information during encoding and decoding. In addition, the iterative shrinkage threshold algorithm (ISTA) is a popular gradient descent method that gradually reconstructs the image through a soft threshold operation at each iteration. This paper uses DIFF Transformer and ISTA algorithm to stack and cross to improve the compression CS stage to solve the problems and challenges in remote sensing images. We design a new multi-stage iterative framework based on DIFF Transformer and ISTA for image compression sensing, as shown in Fig. 1. In the sampling module, a data-driven sensing matrix is used to improve the image reconstruction performance. The initial reconstruction performs preliminary reconstruction of the image according to the trainable initial reconstruction matrix. The iterative reconstruction consists of a fixed number of stages, each of which is similar to an ISTA iteration, and gradient descent and soft threshold processing are performed between each stage. Each stage is based on the DIFF Transformer as the backbone, and the encoder and decoder are also similar to two ISTA iterations. Gradient descent and soft threshold processing are performed between them, and local convolution is introduced within the stage to assist in capturing local details of the image.

Illustration of our proposed the multi-stage iterative framework. Specifically, it is composed of three parts: sampling module, initial reconstruction, and iterative reconstruction. In sampling process, images are measured using a data-driven sensing matrix. The initial reconstruction process uses the transposition of the sensing matrix to complete the initial reconstruction of the image. The iterative reconstruction process is established by an iterative network of stacked and crossed DIFF Transformer and ISTA algorithm, which focuses on context-related information and iteratively generates reconstructed images using gradient descent and soft thresholding operations.

Therefore, the framework we proposed can effectively capture the global position dependency between image sub-blocks and pay more attention to context-related information for image reconstruction. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: 1) We introduced MHDA mechanism into the Transformer architecture to effectively suppress attention noise and enhance the collaborative modeling of local details and global structures, thereby significantly improving reconstruction performance in complex remote sensing scenarios. 2) We proposed a dual-threshold iterative strategy with stepwise shrinking both within and across stages, which accelerates the convergence of sparse optimization and enhances both reconstruction efficiency and accuracy.

Related work

This section briefly introduces related work, i.e., optimization-based CS methods, network-based CS methods, and Differential Transformer. These methods and frameworks are briefly reviewed, and the most relevant specific methods are introduced and studied.

Optimization-based CS methods

The convex optimization algorithm is an important branch to achieve sparse signal reconstruction. Common convex optimization algorithms in traditional CS reconstruction algorithms include basis pursuit algorithm (BP)25, interior-point method (IPM)26, group-based sparse representation (GPsR)27, and the iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithm (ISTA)28 and the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)29, etc. In the literature30, T. Saha et al. combined part of the application of Newton’s second-order iteration scheme for calculating the generalized inverse with Bregman iteration to improve BP algorithm. Literature31 uses gradient projection method to restore sparse signals in CS and establishes global convergence through backtracking line search. ADMM is a simple but powerful numerical optimization framework suitable for sparse signal recovery in CS32. In addition, Dinh et al. designed iterative weighted recovery (IWR)33 to improve the quality of restored images or videos, but the estimated magnitude-descending order has a large impact in IWR. Network-based CS methods

Recently, DL has led to significant research achievements in various fields. Scholars have applied deep learning techniques to CS, giving rise to two main categories of deep learning network algorithms: DL-based traditional algorithms and end-to-end DL networks. These models, compared to traditional algorithms, exhibit significantly faster reconstruction speeds. In addition, in some DL-based CS models, participating operators such as sensing matrices can be learned in the backpropagation process instead of being designed manually.

In the first category, researchers embed traditional CS algorithms into deep learning network structures to better adapt to large-scale data and enhance computational efficiency. These models typically fuse prior knowledge from traditional algorithms, such as sparse representation and optimization methods, to achieve more accurate image reconstruction. Zhang et al. proposed ISTA-Net and ISTA-Net+ models15, unfolding traditional CS iterative algorithms into multi-layer neural networks. Compared with ISTA-Net, ISTA-Net+ has more sparse images and introduces skip connections, which helps to train deeper networks. Subsequently, Di You et al. introduced the ISTA-Net++ model16, which flexibly handles multiple compression ratios for a single module. In addition, Sun Jian et al. based on the traditional ADMM algorithm proposed the ADMM-CSNet model17. Although these models adopt a data-driven strategy in reconstruction, traditional hand-crafted sensing matrices are still used in the sampling module, which potentially limiting reconstruction performance. Gilton et al. introduced NeumNet18, which employs Neumann sequences to address inverse imaging problems. NeumNet achieves high image reconstruction speed, but the reconstructed images are still noticeably affected by blocking artifacts. Similarly, AMP-Net19 introduces a deep unfolding strategy, mapping convolutional layers into the iterative recovery of the AMP algorithm, gradually improving image reconstruction capabilities. Chen et al. proposed a multi-layer residual coding scheme for image coding9. The prediction and quantization process of this framework are designed to be layer-independent, so each layer can be coded in parallel, thus saving a lot of coding time. However, these models monotonously focus on texture and detail extraction based on stacked convolutional layers, ignoring the importance of global correlation modeling in natural images.

In the second category, researchers adopt end-to-end DL frameworks to directly learn the mapping relationship from input to output without the need for cumbersome prior information. These end-to-end DL networks, through learning from extensive data, automatically extract features from images, resulting in higher-quality image reconstruction. For example, Yao et al. proposed DR2-Net20, which uses linear mapping networks and residual networks to reconstruct preliminary and final images. Additionally, Kulkarni et al. introduced ReconNet21, which directly reconstructs the original image through stacked convolutional layers. However, DR2-Net and ReconNet can only learn intra-block information and suffer from block artifacts. To address this issue, Sun et al. proposed DPA-Net22, attempting to improve image reconstruction quality by preserving image texture details using a dual-path attention network. Moreover, Shi et al. introduced CSNet+23, which utilizes a residual network to learn the mapping between measurement results and reconstructed images, achieving rapid end-to-end image reconstruction. Although DPA-Net and CSNet+ alleviate artifacts to some extent, these purely data-driven models have poor interpretability due to their black-box nature. Additionally, Zhuang et al. proposed UCSNet10, a priori-guided adaptive CS framework for underwater images. However, these models based solely on CNN face limitations in simulating global position relationships.

The above model must exploit inefficient stacking of convolutional layers to expand the receptive field of the convolutional neural network to learn global dependencies. To overcome these limitations, it is essential to investigate the establishment of a new DL-based image CS paradigm that can learn global relationships between sub-image blocks. Transformer35, a paradigm utilizing a classic encoder-decoder structure, has achieved state-of-the-art performance in natural language processing (NLP). Unlike using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or CNNs as sequence modeling mechanisms, Transformer combines position embedding (PE), multi-head self-attention (MHSA), and multi-layer perceptron (MLP). Although Transformer has become the de facto standard for NLP tasks, its professional application in visual tasks still requires further exploration. Recently, in the field of image CS, an exploratory work is CSformer36, which integrates Transformer and CNN in a dual-stream and black-box manner. We propose a network framework that integrates global structure and local details, giving full play to the respective advantages of Transformer and CNN to achieve high-quality image reconstruction from local to global.

Reconstruction module

Since the compressed measurements are obtained after the sampling module, we first introduce an initial reconstruction module to restore the size of the original image blocks. However, it is not enough to use this initial module because the initial reconstructed image has a low fit and a weak ability to preserve the image information. In addition, this module suffers from blocking artifacts after connecting the initial reconstructed image blocks. Therefore, in order to improve the accuracy of image reconstruction and reduce the blocking phenomenon, we also design iterative reconstruction. DIFF Transformer can eliminate attention noise and enhance context modeling capabilities. In addition, the ISTA algorithm can solve the reconstruction problem in CS through gradient descent and soft threshold processing. Inspired by ISTA-Net15, each stage of our method also corresponds to an ISTA iteration process. Unlike ISTA-Net, the encoder and decoder in our stage are also regarded as two ISTA iteration processes. Our iterative network not only uses DIFF Transformer to focus on key information modeling images, but also uses soft threshold iterations within and between stages to approximate the solution to the problem by continuously shrinking the iteration step size. In addition, the local features of the image cannot be ignored, so local convolution blocks are added at each stage for auxiliary reconstruction. Experimental results

This section conducts several experiments to study the restoration performance of our proposed framework on different datasets and compares it with other competing methods. Second, the running time of our method and other methods is analyzed. Finally, noise robustness experiments and ablation experiments are performed and discussed.

Experimental setting

We use the NWPU VHR-10 dataset38, which is a widely used remote sensing object detection benchmark released by Northwestern Polytechnical University. The dataset contains 800 images with image sizes ranging from to pixels. To construct the training set, we randomly select 70 % of the original images and extract 288,000 training sub-images through random cropping. We further apply standard data augmentation techniques, including flipping, rotation, and scaling. The remaining 20 % and 10 % of the NWPU VHR-10 images are used for testing and validation, respectively. To evaluate the cross-dataset generalization ability of our model, we additionally test on the McM1839 dataset, which differs significantly from NWPU VHR-10 in terms of imaging conditions, sensor types, and scene distributions.

We use peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) to evaluate the reconstruction effect of the image. The larger the values of these two indicators, the better the reconstruction effect and the better the performance of the model. After each epoch of training, the average PSNR and average SSIM will be calculated on the validation set, and the model with the highest current average PSNR value will be saved. Finally, our trained best model is tested on various sampling rates using several widely used datasets and compared it with several algorithms including ISTA-Net+15, CSNet+23, AMP-Net19, CSformer36, and TCS-Net40. ISTA-Net+ and AMP-Net are methods that combine traditional algorithms with DL networks. CSNet+, CSformer and TCS-Net are end-to-end deep learning network frameworks.

We set the stage n of the iterative reconstruction module to 4 and the number of heads h to 8. The local convolution block consists of 4 stacked convolutional layers with kernels size of ; stride ; zero bias; and the number of channels is determined by the relative position. Before training, the sensing matrix is initialized as a Gaussian matrix, and the initial reconstruction matrix is assigned the transpose of . In addition, the weight coefficient is initialized to 0.1, the iteration step size and the two shrinkage thresholds , are trainable , initialized to 1.0, 0.01 and 0.01 respectively. The proposed model was trained for 150 epochs with the batch size of 32. The learning rates for the first 100 epochs, 101 to 125 epochs, and the last 25 epochs are , and respectively. We use the Adam41 optimizer, which is a form of stochastic gradient descent. All the experiments are implemented on a platform with the PyTorch 1.7.1 framework and a AMAX TITAN V GPU.

Ablation studies and discussions

This section conduct ablation studies on the multi-stage iterative framework. We first introduce a baseline called Trans-ND, which removes differential attention from the encoder and decoder, to assess its impact on reconstruction performance and efficiency. Then, in order to study the effect of threshold on the reconstruction module, we analyze the pure DIFF Transformer framework (called PU-DTrans) and the single-threshold framework (called OS-DTrans and OE-DTrans, respectively). Among them, OS-DTrans only uses the threshold between stages, and OE-DTrans only uses the threshold between encoders and decoders. The number of iteration layers for Trans-ND, PU-DTrans, OS-DTrans, and OE-DTrans are also set to 4. In addition, in order to analyze the effect of the number of iterations on the performance of the framework, we set the number of iterations of the multi-stage iterative framework to be 2, 3, and 5, referred to as DTrans-2, DTrans-3, and DTrans-5. The Trans-ND, PU-DTrans, OS-DTrans, OE-DTrans, DTrans-2, DTrans-3, and DTrans-5 frameworks all use the same dataset, learning rate, optimizer, and number of epoch as the multi-stage framework during training.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a multi-stage iterative reconstruction framework for remote sensing images based on the DIFF Transformer and ISTA methods. The framework can simultaneously optimize the measurement matrix and the reconstruction operator during the training phase, and all parameters in the network are trained end-to-end. The multi-stage iterative framework performs the two stages of compressed sensing through a sampling module and a reconstruction module. The proposed method has well-defined interpretability, fully utilizes the advantages of the local to global position relationship network, and pays more attention to context-related information. Extensive experiments show that the multi-stage iterative compressed sensing framework has considerable improvements at different sampling rates under the cooperation of DIFF Transformer and ISTA, which is helpful for CS reconstruction of remote sensing images. In addition, the noise robustness is better at low sampling rates. Our future work is to develop a CS method with adaptive block size for remote sensing images. In addition, efforts to optimize the network structure are also a focus of our future work.

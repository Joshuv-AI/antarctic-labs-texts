Correlation Between Health Literacy and Tacrolimus Variability in Solid Organ Transplant Recipients
Authors: Astrid Bacle, Pauline Blanc‐Petitjean, Elouan Demay, Sarah Pelletier, Virginie Migeot, Marie‐Claude Langevin, Jean‐Philippe Adam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAdherence to immunosuppressive therapy is crucial for successful outcomes in solid organ transplantation. Tacrolimus intra‐patient variability (%CV) is a validated marker of adherence and has been associated with graft outcomes. Health literacy (HL), a modifiable factor, may influence this variability, but its role remains unexplored.MethodsWe conducted a prospective observational study including adult kidney, liver, and lung transplant recipients receiving tacrolimus. HL was assessed using the Short Test of Functional Health Literacy in Adults (S‐TOFHLA); a score <23 indicated insufficient HL. Tacrolimus %CV was calculated over 6 months post‐discharge. Linear regression models, adjusted for sociodemographic variables, evaluated the association between HL and %CV.ResultsNinety‐eight patients were included (kidney: n = 38, liver: n = 24, lung: n = 36). Patients with insufficient HL had higher %CV than those with sufficient HL (31.5% vs. 16.1%, p < 0.05). HL remained independently associated with %CV after adjusting for age and transplant type (β = −1.60, SE = 0.17, p < 0.05). The final model explained 53% of %CV variability.ConclusionsHL is independently associated with tacrolimus variability. HL screening and targeted educational interventions may help improve medication stability and long‐term transplant outcomes.Keywords: health literacy, immunosuppressive therapy, medication adherence, solid organ transplantation (sot), tacrolimus exposure

Introduction

Solid organ transplantation is a life‐saving intervention for patients with end‐stage organ failure. Patients’ adherence to immunosuppressive therapy is critical to ensure successful outcomes and to prevent graft loss over the long‐term [1]. However, non‐adherence remains a common issue, reported in 28%–52% of transplant recipients, and is associated with factors such as younger age, being unmarried, limited social support, emotional distress, and transplant‐related stress [2].

Health literacy (HL)—defined as the ability to obtain, process, and apply health information [3]—has emerged as an important factor in transplant care. Studies have shown that 9%–33% of transplant patients have low or inadequate HL, with variation depending on organ type [4, 5, 6, 7]. Low HL has been linked to poorer outcomes, including a 30% reduced likelihood of being listed for kidney transplantation and a 2.4‐fold increase in waitlist mortality [4]. Moreover, inadequate HL is associated with increased healthcare utilization in the first‐year post‐transplant [8]. Nonetheless, findings remain inconsistent, with some studies reporting no significant impact of HL on transplant outcomes [9, 10].

The association between HL and medication adherence has been explored in various chronic disease contexts and in transplant patients. Recent studies, such as a cross‐sectional analysis of HL and compliance to treatment in organ transplant recipients, further corroborate this link, highlighting the critical role HL plays in treatment adherence [11]. However, this study does not include direct adherence measures (e.g., pharmacy refill data or self‐reports), and therefore the causal pathway between HL and % CV through medication adherence remains to be definitively established.

Although the relationship between HL and medication adherence has been explored in various chronic disease contexts and in transplant patients [12, 13], no prior studies have specifically examined its impact on tacrolimus concentration variability (%CV). Tacrolimus variability, typically quantified as the coefficient of variation (%CV), has been associated with increased risks of graft rejection and long‐term complications [1].

This study addresses this gap by evaluating the relationship between HL and tacrolimus %CV in kidney, liver, and lung transplant recipients. Our hypothesis is that patients with insufficient HL may exhibit greater variability in tacrolimus levels, reflecting suboptimal adherence or medication management. Identifying this link could support the implementation of HL‐based interventions to improve therapeutic outcomes post‐transplant.

Methods

A prospective study, conducted at the Centre Hospitalier de l'Université de Montréal (CHUM) in Canada, include kidney, liver, and lung transplant recipients aged 18 years or older, capable of reading and understanding French, and receiving tacrolimus as part of their immunosuppressive therapy. The study period spanned from March 1, 2023, to December 1, 2023. The CHUM ethics committee approved this study protocol, and all participants provided written informed consent prior to inclusion. Demographic and clinical data were collected in the patient medical records. All tacrolimus levels included in the analysis were trough concentrations, measured just before the administration of the next dose in accordance with standard post‐transplant monitoring protocols. Six monthly trough concentrations per patient were used to calculate intra‐patient variability (%CV). The 6‐month post‐discharge period was selected because therapeutic targets for tacrolimus levels remained stable during this timeframe, minimizing potential confounding from shifting dosage goals. All measurements were conducted in a standardized outpatient setting within the same institution, ensuring consistent sampling procedures.

HL Assessment

Baseline HL was evaluated using the Short Test of Functional HL in Adults (S‐TOFHLA), a validated and standardized tool for assessing HL in adults [14]. The S‐TOFHLA is scored on a scale of 0–36, with higher scores indicating greater HL. A score below 23 was categorized as insufficient HL, while scores of 23 or higher were categorized as sufficient HL [10]. HL was assessed shortly after the transplant, prior to the start of structured educational sessions. This timing was chosen to evaluate patients’ baseline functional HL. Assessments were conducted only with patient consent, once the individual expressed that they felt comfortable, not in pain, and cognitively and emotionally ready to participate. This approach aimed to limit transient postoperative effects while capturing a literacy baseline.

The S‐TOFHLA was administered by clinical pharmacists who were not involved in subsequent patient education sessions. To avoid influencing the educational interventions or clinical management, the results of the S‐TOFHLA were not disclosed to patients or healthcare providers involved in their follow‐up. This procedure ensured that the assessment of HL did not affect tacrolimus variability or introduce bias into the study outcomes

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative variables were described by the mean and standard deviation, if normally distributed, or the median and interquartile range. Qualitative variables were presented by frequency and percentage. Missing values were reported. A linear regression model was used to evaluate the relationship between the coefficient of variation of tacrolimus (%CV), expressed as its square root to meet the assumptions of linear regression, and HL levels (insufficient if score <23, sufficient if score ≥23). The model was adjusted for sociodemographic factors potentially associated with tacrolimus variability. Sociodemographic covariate selection was guided first by an a priori identification of key determinants of %CV, followed by a stepwise elimination process. Organ transplant type (kidney, liver, lung) was included in the multivariable regression model as a categorical variable with dummy coding, using kidney transplantation as the reference category. During the univariate analysis, variables with a p value <0.2 were retained for multivariate analysis. Subsequently, the backward selection process was applied, iteratively removing the least significant variables until an optimized final model was achieved. All statistical analyses were conducted using Prism software (version 5.0, GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) and R (version 4.4.2).

Results

A total of 98 patients were included, consisting of kidney (n = 38), liver (n = 24), and lung (n = 36) transplant recipients. Eight patients declined to participate in the study, 10 were excluded because they received a second transplant, and one patient died during the study period. A significant negative association was observed between HL and %CV of tacrolimus for all three groups: kidney (𝑟 = −0.54, p < 0.01), liver (𝑟 = −0.45, p < 0.01), and lung (𝑟 = −0.51, p < 0.01) (Figure 1).

In univariate analysis (Table 1), level of HL was significantly associated with %CV (β (se) = −1.60 (0.18), p < 0.05). Patients with insufficient HL had an average %CV of 31.5%, while patients with sufficient HL exhibited an average %CV of 16.1%. Absence of professional activity, age higher than 65 years, and lung transplantation emerged as factors positively associated with higher %CV. After adjustment for age and transplant type, HL remained significantly associated with %CV. In the multivariate analysis (Table 1), insufficient HL was significantly associated with higher %CV compared to those with sufficient HL after adjusting for age and transplant type (β (se) = −1.60 (0.17), p < 0.05). Our model explains 53% of the variability in the coefficient of variation.

Discussion

This study provides the first evidence of a significant independent relationship between HL and tacrolimus concentration variability (%CV) in transplant recipients. Patients with higher HL levels demonstrated better stability in tacrolimus concentrations. This finding is clinically crucial, as higher %CV has been strongly associated with an increased risk of graft rejection and poorer long‐term outcomes [1]. However, it is important to note that clinical outcomes such as graft rejection or donor‐specific antibody development were not assessed in this study due to lack of available data. Including these outcomes in future research would enhance the clinical relevance and applicability of our findings. For instance, in kidney transplant recipients, high %CV predicts the accelerated progression of chronic histologic lesions [15]. Our findings emphasize HL as a key factor in managing immunosuppressive therapy, providing new insights into post‐transplant care.

Psychosocial factors such as age, low social support, and high emotional distress also play a crucial role in adherence. These factors likely interact with HL, and future studies should aim to include them in multivariate analyses to further refine intervention strategies. The present study's multivariate analysis supports this approach, confirming that HL significantly influences tacrolimus variability even after adjusting for other factors like age and transplant type. Transplant organ type was included in our model as a categorical variable, with kidney transplantation used as the reference group. While this adjustment accounts for organ‐related differences in pharmacokinetics and clinical follow‐up, future studies could further explore transplant‐specific dynamics by using stratified analyses or incorporating interaction terms between HL and organ type. This may help identify whether HL has differential impacts depending on the clinical context of each organ group. Evidence from previous studies indicates that interventions to improve HL can enhance treatment adherence, particularly in vulnerable populations with lower socioeconomic status or minority backgrounds [16].

However, it is important to acknowledge that this study did not directly measure medication adherence (e.g., via pharmacy refill records or self‐reports), limiting our ability to establish a causal relationship between HL and tacrolimus variability. This limitation is recognized and suggests that future research should incorporate direct adherence assessments to clarify these pathways.

One limitation of our study is that HL was assessed only once, shortly after transplantation. Although this time point was selected to capture a pre‐education baseline, the post‐transplant period can be associated with emotional overload, stress, or residual physical discomfort. To mitigate this, HL testing was conducted only with the patient's consent and once they reported feeling pain‐free and emotionally ready. Still, we acknowledge the possibility of transient influence on HL performance. Future studies should consider repeated assessments to capture the dynamic nature of HL and its potential to improve with education.

Improving HL is pivotal to the effective management of chronic diseases, and improving HL is strongly correlated with better adherence to complex regimens and improved clinical outcomes [17, 18, 19]. Targeted educational interventions focusing on HL may serve as a strategy to possibly enhance medication adherence [20]. Integrating HL assessments into clinical practice could help identify patients at risk for poor medication management and allow for personalized educational strategies that optimize clinical outcomes.

Our findings suggest that systematically assessing HL during the transplant process could help identify patients at risk of poor outcomes. Early evaluation, ideally during the psychosocial assessment phase, would allow for tailored interventions before transplantation. After transplantation, ongoing assessments and personalized educational strategies are critical to support patients as they adapt to complex treatment regimens. Best practices include simplified communication, visual aids, and regular follow‐ups to confirm understanding. For patients with low HL, interactive and one‐on‐one approaches may be particularly effective in reinforcing medication management skills.

Although this study uniquely demonstrates the relationship between HL and tacrolimus %CV, larger multicenter studies are needed to evaluate the long‐term impact of HL‐targeted interventions on clinical outcomes. Future research should also explore the interplay between HL, psychosocial factors, and adherence to better inform tailored interventions. Improving HL is a pivotal step toward optimizing post‐transplant care. Personalized education programs have shown promise in enhancing medication adherence, improving clinical outcomes, and reducing hospital readmissions [21, 22]. By systematically integrating HL‐focused interventions into transplant care, healthcare professionals can better address individual patient needs and possibly promote long‐term transplant success.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates a significant correlation between HL and tacrolimus variability in solid organ transplant recipients. These findings highlight HL's critical role in managing immunosuppressive therapy and suggest that targeted interventions for patients with lower HL could improve transplant outcomes. By addressing HL deficits through personalized educational programs, healthcare professionals can help reduce the risk of graft rejection and improve long‐term success. Future research should evaluate the effectiveness of these approaches in larger, multicenter studies to refine post‐transplant care strategies.

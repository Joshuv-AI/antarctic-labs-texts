A knowledge-informed neural network integrating fuzzy AHP and PCA for SME credit risk assessment
Authors: Tao Li, Majid Khan Majahar Ali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCredit risk assessment for agricultural small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is challenging due to information asymmetry, rendering traditional models ineffective. We propose a novel knowledge-informed neural network to assess credit risk in this data-scarce environment. Our two-stage model first uses Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (Fuzzy AHP) to quantify judgments from a survey of 202 stakeholders into risk scores, which serve as learning labels. Subsequently, after using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction, a neural network is trained to replicate the experts’ decision logic. The AHP analysis identified ‘Financial Status’ and ‘Development Planning’ as the most critical risk themes. On an independent test set, the trained network showed exceptional predictive accuracy, achieving a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of approximately 0.0100. This study validates that fusing expert knowledge with machine learning is a highly effective approach, providing an operational decision-support tool and a new paradigm for AI applications in information-asymmetric environments.Keywords: Neural network, Credit risk assessment, Fuzzy AHP, Principal component analysis (PCA), Knowledge-informed modelSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs) are the cornerstone of the global economy, making their healthy development crucial to the vitality and stability of national economies1. However, they consistently face significant challenges in accessing affordable financing, a phenomenon widely known as the “SME Financing Gap”2. This problem is particularly acute for agricultural SMEs, which not only exhibit common issues like non-standardized financial statements but also bear unique operational risks arising from natural conditions, market volatility, and long production cycles3. Traditional financial institutions, which rely heavily on standardized financial data and tangible collateral, often find their models inadequate for these enterprises. The true operational status of agricultural SMEs is deeply embedded in unquantifiable “soft information,” such as managerial experience and supply chain stability, rather than assets easily used as collateral. This study focuses on the vegetable industry in Shouguang, China—a representative microcosm of the financing difficulties encountered by specialized agricultural SMEs worldwide—to develop a more effective credit risk assessment model.

The core challenge in assessing agricultural SME credit risk is the mismatch between existing models and the reality of information asymmetry4. Traditional statistical models, such as Logistic Regression and Altman’s Z-score, are interpretable but require high-quality, standardized financial data that these enterprises often lack5. To overcome these linear limitations, modern machine learning models like Support Vector Machines and Random Forests have been applied, generally achieving higher accuracy when large-scale datasets are available6. However, their effectiveness is predicated on the availability of labeled historical data (i.e., clear records of default/non-default), which is a major barrier in specific, under-digitized industries like agriculture. To handle qualitative “soft information,” Fuzzy Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) methods, particularly Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (Fuzzy AHP), provide a rigorous framework for quantifying ambiguous expert judgments7. Nevertheless, these models are typically static and lack the data-driven adaptive learning capabilities of modern AI.

Therefore, a clear research gap exists: academia and industry lack a credit decision model that can systematically integrate high-dimensional, qualitative “soft information” and use it as a basis for intelligent and automated risk assessment8. To bridge this gap, this study proposes and validates a “Knowledge-Informed Neural Network”. The core idea is to first utilize Fuzzy AHP to systematically transform the domain knowledge of experts into high-quality, quantitative “proxy labels.” These labels are then used to supervise the training of a neural network, enabling it to learn and replicate complex, non-linear expert decision patterns without needing historical default data.

The primary objective of this study is to construct and validate this hybrid intelligent model for the precise and efficient credit risk assessment of agricultural SMEs. The main contributions of this research are twofold. Theoretically, it proposes a novel hybrid modeling paradigm that fuses MCDM with Artificial Intelligence, offering a feasible solution for complex evaluation problems in information-asymmetric and unlabeled data environments9. Practically, it delivers an operational and intelligent decision-support tool that can help financial institutions assess risk more accurately and assist SMEs in identifying their shortcomings to improve their “bankability”.

Results and analysis

This chapter aims to present the empirical results obtained by applying the research methodology established in Chap. 3 to the 202 valid survey questionnaires. The content of this chapter is divided into two core parts: first, a macro-diagnosis of the credit risk in the regional vegetable industry is conducted through Fuzzy AHP and Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA) to identify key risk themes; second, the predictive performance of the specially constructed “knowledge-informed neural network” model is evaluated to validate its effectiveness.

Performance evaluation of the knowledge-informed neural network model

After completing the regional diagnosis, the study proceeds to the second stage: validating whether a trained neural network can replace the complex evaluation process described above.

Model input and dimensionality reduction

The model takes the 120 raw indicator scores as input and the 202 AHP composite scores calculated in the first stage as the learning target. To avoid the curse of dimensionality, PCA was first applied to the 120 input indicators. The results show that the cumulative proportion of variance of the first 5 principal components reached 81.83%, retaining most of the information from the original data. Therefore, these 5 principal components were determined as the final input for the neural network, which is an effective and necessary preprocessing step when handling high-dimensional financial data12.

Predictive performance evaluation

We constructed and trained a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) neural network. On a test set containing 61 previously unseen samples, its performance evaluation is as follows:

Core Performance Metric: The RMSE between the model’s predicted scores and the “true” scores from the AHP expert model is approximately 0.0100.

Chapter summary

The empirical analysis in this chapter leads to two key conclusions. First, the AHP/IPA diagnosis reveals that the core strengths of the regional vegetable industry lie in its clear Development Planning (B2), whereas Financial Status (B1) represents a significant weakness, as also indicated in Table 2 and Sect. 2.1.2. Second, through comparison with benchmark models, we have successfully validated that the constructed “Knowledge-Informed Neural Network” model can automatically replicate the evaluation results of the expert system with high precision (RMSE ≈ 0.01), and its performance surpasses both classical statistical models and powerful ensemble learning models, proving the effectiveness and superiority of the method.

Discussion

This chapter aims to provide an in-depth interpretation and discussion of the empirical results from Chap. 4, elaborate on the theoretical contributions and practical implications of this research, candidly analyze the study’s limitations, and offer prospects for future research directions.

In-depth discussion of key findings

The empirical analysis of this study has produced several key findings with profound implications that merit in-depth discussion.

First, this study reveals a robust, “endogenously-driven” risk profile for the regional vegetable industry. As seen in the IPA diagnostic chart (Fig. 1), the two assessment dimensions most valued by experts—“Impact Level” (A1) and “Probability of Occurrence” (A2)—are precisely the areas where the region’s enterprises perform best. This indicates that through long-term market competition and development, the enterprises in this region have formed a set of effective internal risk control mechanisms. In contrast, the performance score for “Government Support” (A4) was the lowest, which implies that the robustness of these enterprises stems more from their own operational resilience rather than a reliance on external policies.

Second, this research precisely pinpoints the industry’s areas of strength and potential weaknesses. Through the analysis of the six risk themes (B1-B6), we found that the region’s core strengths lie in its sound “Financial Status” (B1) and clear “Development Planning” (B2), which corroborates the aforementioned conclusion about strong endogenous capabilities. Concurrently, the study also highlights relative shortcomings in “Technological Innovation” (B4) and “Management Level” (B3). This provides key insights for understanding the future development trajectory of the industry in this region.

Finally, this study validates the exceptional performance of the “knowledge-informed neural network” methodology. Our AI model achieved a RMSE of approximately 0.0100 on the test set, which means it has almost perfectly replicated the complex decision logic defined by the Fuzzy AHP expert system. This is more than just a successful model training; it profoundly illustrates that experts’ seemingly subjective decision-making knowledge contains highly consistent, non-linear internal patterns, and modern neural network technology is capable of accurately learning and capturing these patterns.

Comparative performance analysis with benchmark models

The Knowledge-Informed Neural Network (KI-NN) model proposed in this study (RMSE ≈ 0.0100) demonstrated significantly higher predictive accuracy than both the traditional Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) model (RMSE ≈ 0.2855) and the powerful Random Forest (RF) model (RMSE ≈ 0.0482).

The superiority of KI-NN over RF is particularly noteworthy. Random Forest is widely recognized as one of the best-performing machine learning algorithms on tabular data. The reason our KI-NN model performed even better may be that the “teacher labels” (the AHP scores) we provided to the neural network had an extremely high signal-to-noise ratio. The AHP process had already filtered out a significant amount of noise from the raw data and distilled the experts’ decision logic. Theoretical contributions

This research makes two main contributions at the theoretical level:

First, it enriches the theory of SME credit risk, particularly for the agricultural sector. Through a comprehensive system of 120 specific indicators, the study provides strong evidence that non-financial “soft information” is as important as traditional financial “hard information” in risk assessment. This offers new empirical support for the application of information asymmetry theory in the field of agricultural finance15.

Second, it contributes an innovative hybrid modeling paradigm to the field of decision sciences. The two-stage framework proposed in this study—“Fuzzy AHP weighting - PCA dimensionality reduction - neural network learning”—demonstrates how to organically combine knowledge-based MCDM methods with data-driven artificial intelligence methods to overcome their respective limitations. This “Knowledge-Informed Machine Learning” approach offers a new methodological reference for solving complex real-world decision problems9.

Limitations of the study

Although this study strived for rigor, it still has some limitations that await improvement in future research:

Geographical and Industrial Limitation: The sample for this study was drawn entirely from the vegetable industry in Shouguang, Shandong. Whether its conclusions and weighting system can be directly generalized to other regions or other agricultural sub-sectors (such as animal husbandry or aquaculture) requires further validation.

Static Nature of the Model: The weights calculated in this model are based on expert judgments at a single point in time, making it a static model. It fails to capture the dynamic changes in the importance of various risk factors under different economic cycles. In reality, building an adaptive dynamic risk model is a significant challenge16.

Methods

This chapter details the entire construction process of the “knowledge-informed neural network” model used for assessing the credit risk of agricultural SMEs. The methodological framework is an innovative two-stage hybrid model, designed to overcome the limitations of traditional models in handling high-dimensional, qualitative, and unlabeled data.

Overall research framework

The overall framework of this study is depicted in Fig. 3, which clearly illustrates the two core stages from expert knowledge extraction to intelligent model construction. The central idea of this framework is to utilize a Fuzzy AHP model in the first stage to systematically transform the domain knowledge of experts into high-quality quantitative labels (i.e., the “teacher’s” answers). These labels are then used to train a neural network (the “student”) in the second stage, enabling it to learn and replicate the experts’ decision logic, ultimately creating an efficient and intelligent evaluation tool9.

Data source and sample characteristics

The data foundation for this research originates from a specially designed survey questionnaire titled “Questionnaire on Enterprise Credit Risk Evaluation.” The main body of the questionnaire is divided into two parts: the first part collects basic information about the respondents, while the second part requires respondents to make pairwise comparisons for a pre-set risk indicator system.

To ensure the professionalism and validity of the judgments, this study employed Purposive Sampling to select the expert sample. The criteria for selecting experts were primarily based on their professional knowledge and practical experience in relevant fields. After a process of screening and collection, we ultimately obtained 202 valid questionnaires. The composition of the sample is as follows:

Financial Institution Experts: 65 individuals (32.2% of the sample), primarily senior client managers and risk department heads from state-owned commercial banks, joint-stock banks, and rural commercial banks, each with more than 5 years of credit approval experience.

Stage 2: Knowledge-Informed neural network model

Data preprocessing

Before being input into the neural network, all data requires standardization. This study uses Min-Max Normalization to scale all input features X (the 120 indicator scores) and the output label Y (the AHP expert score) to the interval [0, 1]. Subsequently, the dataset is randomly split into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%).

Dimensionality reduction based on PCA

Directly using 120 indicators as input for a neural network would lead to the curse of dimensionality and an overly complex model. Therefore, this study employs Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce the dimensionality of the input features. PCA is a classical statistical method capable of transforming a large number of correlated original variables into a few uncorrelated composite variables (principal components), while retaining most of the original information18.

In this study, PCA was fitted only on the training set to prevent data leakage and then applied to transform the test set. This ensures that no information from the test set leaked into the training process, thereby maintaining methodological rigor. The results showed that the cumulative proportion of variance explained by the first five principal components reached 81.83%, retaining most of the information from the original data. Consequently, these five principal components were used as the final input for the neural network.

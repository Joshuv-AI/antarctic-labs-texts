Prostate-specific Antigen at 3 Months as a Predictor of Radiologic Progression-free Survival in Metastatic Hormone-sensitive Prostate Cancer Treated with Apalutamide: Analysis of 633 Patients in a Real-world Database
Authors: Mario Hassi Roman, Kinga Mate, Pedro De Pablos-Rodriguez, Álvaro Zamora Horcajada, Ana Guijarro Cascales, Ángeles Sanchís Bonet, Antoni Vilaseca, Darío Vázquez-Martul Pazos, Estefanía Linares Espinós, Jesús Muñoz Rodríguez, José Manuel de la Morena Gallego, José Ramón Alemán, Juan Gómez Rivas, Luigi Formisano, Maria J Juan Fita, Marc Costa Planells, Mario Domínguez Esteban, Meritxell Pérez Márquez, Miguel García Sanz, Nagore García Expósito, Natalia Picola, Pol Servian Vives, Raquel Sopeña Sutil, Miguel A Climent Durán, Miguel Ramírez Backhaus

Those with a complete response have a low risk of progression at 2 yr, while nearly half of patients with an incomplete response will experience progression. Introduction

Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) represents a clinical challenge, with treatment objectives focused on delaying disease progression and maintaining good quality of life. Androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs), including enzalutamide, apalutamide, and darolutamide [1], [2], [3], [4], have revolutionized mHSPC management because of better survival and similar toxicity to conventional androgen deprivation therapy (ADT).

Identification of potential factors predictive of progression could play a crucial role in guiding treatment decisions, including intensification or de-escalation strategies, and clinical management, particularly regarding the frequency of patient visits and test procedures. Hussain et al [5] demonstrated that overall survival (OS) was correlated with the PSA reduction at 7 mo for patients with mHSPC receiving ADT monotherapy. Patients with PSA <0.2 ng/ml at 7 mo had better survival than patients with PSA >4 ng/ml (p < 0.001), with median OS of 75 versus 13 mo [5]. This association has also been suggested with doublet and triplet therapies involving ARPIs and/or chemotherapy [1], [2], [3].

The use of PSA response as an early biomarker for prediction of prognosis is being evaluated. Here we investigated the association between PSA response and radiologic progression in a large mHSPC cohort treated with apalutamide.

Patients and methods

This study included patients with mHSPC treated with apalutamide from May 2018 to September 2023 who were registered in the Real-World Evidence APA (RWE-APA) registry across one Italian and 19 Spanish centers. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Central ethics committee approval was obtained.

The inclusion criteria were patients aged >18 yr with adenocarcinoma of the prostate and metastatic disease with at least one metastatic lesion (extrapelvic lymph nodes, bone, or visceral lesions) who were treated with apalutamide plus ADT. Lesions were diagnosed via conventional imaging (CIM) or next-generation imaging (NGI; choline or prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography).

The primary objective was to investigate the relationship between PSA response at 3 mo and radiologic progression-free survival (rPFS) at 24 mo. Three groups were defined according to the PSA decline at 3 mo: complete response (CR), PSA ≤0.02 ng/ml; partial response (PR), PSA >0.02 and ≤0.2 ng/ml; and incomplete response (IR), PSA >0.2 ng/ml. rPFS was defined as the time from diagnosis to the first imaging-based documentation of progressive disease or death, whichever occurred first. A patient was considered to have metastasis progression if he had either progression of soft-tissue or bone lesions measured via CIM or NGI according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST). The secondary objective was to develop a model predicting a complete PSA response at 3 mo after treatment initiation.

Statistical analyses

Descriptive analyses of baseline characteristics and PSA kinetics were performed. Results are presented as the frequency and percentage for categorical variables, and the median and interquartile range for continuous variables. rPFS was compared between the PSA groups using log-rank tests and illustrated via Kaplan-Meier curves. Univariate analysis was performed for imaging technique (NGI vs CIM), tumor volume (low vs high), International Society of Urological Pathology grade group at diagnosis, metastasis location (M1a vs M1b vs M1c), and timing of metastasis presentation (synchronous vs metachronous) as covariates via Cox regression models. Multivariable logistic regression models were constructed to predict 3-mo PSA ≤0.02 ng/ml using covariates that were significant on univariate analysis. Multivariable proportional-hazards regression models by stratification factors at random assignment were developed for rPFS. All statistical tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. The statistical analyses were carried out using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

A total of 633 patients with mHSPC were included. Median follow-up was 16 mo (IQR 10–23). The median age at diagnosis was 69 yr (IQR 63–75) and median PSA was 16 ng/ml (IQR 7.5–64). A total of 323 patients (51%) had de novo mHSPC and 303 had recurrent disease, with data missing for seven patients (1.1%). In all, 400 patients (63%) had low-volume and 224 had high-volume mHSPC, with data missing for nine patients (1.4%).

Overall, 27% of the patients experienced a CR, while 42% had a PR and 31% had an IR at 3 mo after initiation of apalutamide treatment. As shown in Table 1, the CR, PR, and IR groups were not homogeneous. The CR group had lower median PSA than the IR group (8.7 vs 84 ng/ml; p < 0.001) and was younger (67 vs 72 yr; p < 0.001). Furthermore, lymph-node metastasis was more frequent (28.6% vs 17.2%) and visceral metastasis was less frequent (4.6% vs 8.6%) in the CR group than in the IR group. Most of the patients in the CR group were diagnosed via NGI. On the contrary, 68% of patients in the IR group were diagnosed via CIM. Lastly, the proportions of patients with low-volume disease diagnosed via NGI (75.7% vs 45.1%) and with metachronous metastasis (68.4% vs 27.4%) were higher in the CR group than in the IR group (both p < 0.001).

Secondary objective

Multivariable analysis revealed that in comparison to the IR group, the risk of progression was 82% lower for the CR group (hazard ratio [HR] 0.18, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.06–0.5; p < 0.001) and 49% lower for the PR group (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29–0.92; p = 0.02; Table 2). Among the other variables, only low-volume disease was significantly associated with lower risk of progression (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.18–0.66; p = 0.001).

Discussion

Our study includes the largest cohort of apalutamide-treated patients in a real-world data setting, with a focus on the prognostic significance of PSA response. The results show that patients with mHSPC treated with apalutamide who achieved PSA <0.02 ng/ml at 3 mo have significantly lower risk of radiologic progression within 2 yr. Conversely, nearly half of those not meeting this PSA threshold are likely to experience progression.

Oncological benefits in terms of a PSA response were documented nearly 20 yr ago in the SWOG 9346 trial. Patients who achieved a 7-mo PSA level <0.2 ng/ml experienced longer survival than those who did not, with median OS of 75 versus 13 mo [5]. More recently, a correlation between PSA response and OS was observed in studies involving doublet (ADT + ARPI) and triplet therapy (ADT + ARPI + docetaxel) [1], [2], [3]. Data from the CHAARTED trial [6] showed that at 7 mo of treatment with ADT + docetaxel, patients with PSA ≤0.2 ng/ml had longer median OS than those with PSA >4 ng/ml (60.4 vs 22.2 mo) [7]. In addition, a post hoc analysis for the LATITUDE trial showed that for patients with high-risk mHSPC, PSA ≤0.1 ng/ml versus >0.1 ng/ml after 6 mo of treatment with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone was strongly associated with better rPFS and OS, with risk reductions of 51% (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.37–0.64; p < 0.001) and 56% (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.33–0.58; p < 0.001), respectively [8].

Regarding treatment response to apalutamide, the TITAN trial revealed that patients who achieved PSA ≤0.2 ng/ml at 3 mo experienced longer rPFS (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.30–0.65) and OS (HR 0.35, 95% CI 0.25–0.48) [9] in comparison to patients without such a response. Similarly, the PR group in our cohort had a 49% reduction in the risk of radiologic progression (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.29–0.92). We did not calculate data for OS owing to the shorter follow-up period.

Considering data for triplet therapy, a retrospective analysis for the ARASENS trial showed better OS for individuals with PSA ≤0.2 ng/ml at both 6 mo and 9 mo in comparison to those with PSA >0.2 ng/ml, regardless of treatment arm (ADT + docetaxel + placebo vs ADT + docetaxel + darolutamide) [10]. Similarly, results from the phase 3 PEACE-1 trial, which randomized patients with mHSPC to ADT + six cycles of docetaxel +abiraterone acetate plus prednisone or ADT + six cycles of docetaxel + placebo, also indicated that achieving PSA ≤0.2 ng/ml at 8 mo was associated with better OS [11].

Various temporal cutoffs for treatment have been used when defining PSA responses. Prognostic determinations have been reported at 3, 6, 7, or 8 mo in the literature; however, the optimal threshold for prognostication remains ambiguous. Parikh et al [12] measured PSA responses at both 3 mo and 7 mo. They found that 87% of patients with PSA <0.2 ng/ml at 3 mo sustained this response at 7 mo. These results are consistent with our findings, as 95% of the patients with a CR at 3 mo still had a CR at 6 mo. We found that 13% of patients without an initial CR at 3 mo had achieved a CR at 6 mo, while the proportion of patients with this outcome in the study by Parikh et al [12] was 36%. This difference might be related to different PSA thresholds and treatment strategies. However, we believe that 3 mo might be a suitable time cutoff for the IR population as it offers an early predictive tool that could serve as an argument for treatment intensification.

NGI techniques were not significantly associated with rPFS on multivariable analysis, unlike low-volume disease and a complete PSA response. This could be because of the variation in diagnostic tests between the different groups. For example, in the IR group, the majority of low-volume disease diagnoses were made via CIM (55%, vs 36% in the PR group and 24% in the CR group).

Multivariable analysis revealed that a CR at 3 mo was more likely if patients were diagnosed via NGI and had baseline PSA <50 ng/ml. These findings are comparable to results reported by Harshman et al [7], who found that PSA <50 ng/ml and low-volume disease were predictive of PSA <0.2 ng/ml at 7 mo after starting ADT + docetaxel.

Future areas for prospective research might include strategies for identifying patients with a suboptimal PSA response to ARPIs for whom further intensification or alteration of conventional therapeutic approach might be considered. In addition, scaled-down trial frameworks and implementation of a treatment intermission could be warranted for patients who experience a complete PSA response.

Contemporary trials are progressively including prospective PSA response groups. The active A-DREAM phase 2 trial is recruiting patients with mHSPC who have a PSA level of <0.2 ng/ml following 18–24 mo of ADT and a minimum of 12 mo of ARPI therapy. The protocol entails cessation of treatment and subsequent reinitiation of both ADT and ARPI when PSA reaches ≥5 ng/ml, radiographic evidence of progression, or the onset of clinical symptoms related to prostate cancer (NCT05241860). The phase 3 PEACE-6 trial is recruiting patients diagnosed with mHSPC with a PSA level of 0.2 ng/ml following 6–12 mo of initial ADT with an ARPI, with or without docetaxel (NCT05974774). Patients are stratified to undergo either continuous maximum androgen blockade (MAB) comprising ADT and an ARPI, or intermittent MAB.

There are inherent limitations in any retrospective multicenter analysis. Our study population is diverse, and the groups analyzed lack homogeneity. Imaging follow-up represents a significant limitation because of the absence of a standardized protocol, which was left to the discretion of each center. However, this variability is supported by the lack of clear consensus in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, European Association of Urology, and Advanced Prostate Cancer Consensus Conference guidelines on follow-up protocols for these patients [13], [14], [15].

Our study supports use of the PSA response at 3 mo as a valuable biochemical marker for predicting rPFS. Its use in guiding treatment decisions and clinical management based on prognostic factors should be investigated to precisely identify which patients will benefit from specific therapies.

Conclusions

The depth of the PSA decline after apalutamide treatment can predict rPFS for patients with mHSPC. Patients who experience a CR to apalutamide have a very low risk of progression within 2 yr. Conversely, nearly 50% of patients with an IR to apalutamide will experience disease progression.

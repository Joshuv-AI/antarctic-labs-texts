Health-Related Quality of Life and Work-Related Outcomes in People with Obesity or Overweight Treated with Tirzepatide or Other Obesity Medications: United States Results from the Adelphi Real World Obesity Disease Specific Programme™
Authors: Theresa Hunter Gibble, Andrea Leith, Lewis Harrison, Claire Gerber, Sonya Kokil Raikar, Gayathri Ashok, Esther Artime
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis real-world exploratory study in the United States (US) aimed to describe the socio-demographics, clinical characteristics, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and work-related outcomes among people with obesity (PwO) undergoing a weight management program.MethodsSecondary data from the US cohort of Adelphi Real World Obesity III Disease Specific Programme™ (October 2023 and April 2024, and October 2024 and January 2025) were analyzed. This cross-sectional survey included physicians and their consulting PwO without T2D who had a physician-confirmed diagnosis of obesity [body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m2] or overweight (BMI of ≥ 27 kg/m2 and < 30 kg/m2) with ≥ 1 obesity-related complication. Outcomes were summarized for three study groups based on a weight management approach at data capture: (1) tirzepatide group (received tirzepatide), (2) obesity management medication (OMM) group (received OMM excluding tirzepatide), and (3) non-OMM group (did not receive an OMM). Analyses were descriptive.ResultsThe study included 1082 PwO (mean age: 47 years; female: 67.4%; tirzepatide: n = 199; OMM: n = 158; non-OMM: n = 725). The average time since OMM initiation was 7.4 months and 7.6 months for PwO in the tirzepatide and OMM groups, respectively. PwO in the tirzepatide group reported higher scores for most of the Short Form Health Survey version 2 (SF-36v2) domains than other groups; however, minimal differences were observed across all groups. A general trend towards greater work impairment among PwO in the non-OMM group than PwO in the tirzepatide and OMM groups was observed.ConclusionThis real-world exploratory study in the US suggests that PwO taking tirzepatide reported less impairment in some of the SF-36v2 domains than those not on tirzepatide. PwO on tirzepatide or alternative OMM treatment appeared to have numerically lower impairment in work-related outcomes than those not on treatment. These findings warrant evaluation for long-term impact of tirzepatide and OMMs on work-related and HRQoL outcomes among PwO.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12325-026-03580-w.

Introduction

Obesity is a chronic relapsing disease characterized by an abnormal and/or excessive accumulation of body fat, and is a growing public health concern [1]. According to the 2025 World Obesity Atlas, about half of the adult population will be living with obesity by 2030. Currently, about 44% of adults are living with obesity in the United States (US) [2]. Globally, people with obesity (PwO) are affected by reduced work productivity and impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [3–5], which may lead to unemployment [6]. Productivity loss in PwO due to missed workdays (absenteeism), early retirement, disability, or reduced caregiver productivity substantially impacts global economies and society [7]. In the US, the estimated annual excess cost associated with absenteeism among full-time working PwO was around US$12.8 billion in 2024, with an association between increasing body mass index (BMI) and work productivity impairment [8].

Obesity is associated with an increased risk of developing several obesity-related complications (ORCs), including cardiovascular, metabolic, and musculoskeletal diseases [9]. This further impacts HRQoL and work productivity, leading to a substantial physical and economic burden on individuals and society [10, 11]. The high disease burden and increase in healthcare costs due to obesity and its related complications underscore the need for early, effective, and sustained weight management approaches [12]. Sustained and consistent weight management strategies are likely to help maintain weight loss-related improvements in both body weight and HRQoL, which is essential for achieving long-term weight reduction and maintenance [13].

The American Association of Clinical Endocrinologists medical guidelines recommend a multidisciplinary approach involving lifestyle modifications and pharmacotherapy or obesity management medications (OMMs) for PwO [14], while bariatric surgery is restricted to PwO having a BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 [15]. Among the recently approved OMMs, tirzepatide, a once-weekly glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, has demonstrated substantial and sustained weight reduction as evidenced by the SURMOUNT clinical trials [16, 17].

As the treatment paradigm for obesity in the US is evolving at a prodigious pace [18], it is imperative to understand the current real-world disease burden, clinical characteristics, and patient-reported outcome (PRO) measures of PwO receiving these treatments. However, real-world data on the impact on HRQoL and obesity-related work impairment among PwO in the US are limited. This real-world study in the US aimed to describe the socio-demographics, clinical characteristics, HRQoL, and work-related outcomes among PwO without type 2 diabetes (T2D) who were prescribed OMMs (tirzepatide or alternative OMM) and those who were not prescribed an OMM using data from the Adelphi Real World Obesity III Disease Specific Programme™ (DSP).

Methods

Study Design

This study was a secondary data analysis of the US cohort from the multinational Adelphi Real World (ARW) Obesity III DSP collected between October 2023 and April 2024, and October 2024 and January 2025. The Obesity III DSP is a cross-sectional survey (including retrospective data) of physicians in primary care, internists, cardiologists, endocrinologists, obstetricians, and gynecologists, and their consulting PwO.

DSPs are independent and impartial data sources and are not designed to address pre-specified research questions or hypotheses. The DSP methodology has been previously described, validated, and demonstrated to be representative and consistent over time [19–22]. The DSP complied with the European Pharmaceutical Market Research Association code of conduct and ARW standard operating procedures. This study was conducted in accordance with ethical principles originating from the Declaration of Helsinki and is consistent with Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices and applicable laws and regulations. Ethics exemption was obtained from the Pearl Institutional Review Board in the US (approval no: #22-ADRW-136). Using a checkbox, all PwO provided their informed consent prior to the use of their anonymized and aggregated data for research and publication in scientific journals. Data were collected so that PwO and physicians could not be identified directly; all data were aggregated and de-identified before receipt.

Study Population

This analysis was based on the DSP sample and included physicians recruited through local fieldwork partners. Eligible physicians accepted all survey requirements and were responsible for the management and treatment decisions for at least 10 PwO per month.

Eligible PwO for this analysis in the DSP were adults (≥ 18 years) who were on a weight management program and had a physician-confirmed diagnosis of obesity (BMI of ≥ 30 kg/m2) or overweight (≥ 27 kg/m2 and < 30 kg/m2 with at least one ORC) without T2D. PwO involved in an obesity clinical trial at the time of data capture were excluded.

Data Collection and Study Outcomes

The present analysis was based on data collected as part of the DSP. Physicians in the DSP completed questionnaires for their next eight consulting PwO visiting for routine care; six PwO who did not receive an OMM (non-OMM group), two PwO who received OMM (OMM group excluding tirzepatide), and an additional sample of six PwO without T2D who received tirzepatide (tirzepatide group). Each PwO for whom the physician completed a questionnaire was then invited to voluntarily complete a self-reported questionnaire. Informed consent was collected from PwO who agreed to complete the self-reported questionnaire.

The physician-reported outcomes included PwO socio-demographics, clinical characteristics, treatment approaches, disease duration (time since obesity diagnosis), treatment history (time since initiation), and weight change (percent change in body weight between obesity diagnosis and data capture, percent change in body weight between treatment initiation and data capture, and proportion of PwO by weight change category between treatment initiation and data capture).

Exploratory analyses based on PwO-completed Short Form Health Survey version 2 (SF-36v2) and Work Productivity and Activity Impairment (WPAI) questionnaires at data capture were assessed. WPAI outcomes were also described by weight change categories since obesity diagnosis [weight gain, no change, and weight reduction (0 to < 5%, ≥ 5 to < 10%, and ≥ 10%)].

SF-36v2 questionnaire: The SF-36v2 is a 36-item validated generic PRO measuring the impact of disease on HRQoL. It consists of eight domains: Physical Functioning; Role-Physical; Bodily Pain; General Health; Vitality; Social Functioning; Role-Emotional; and Mental Health, and two component summary scores: Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS). The domain and component summary scores are transformed to a scale (range: 0–100) based on the 2009 US general population, with a mean of 50 and a standard deviation (SD) of 10. For the US population, a mean t-score ranging from 47 to 53 is considered within the “normal” range, while scores < 47 indicate impairment; higher scores indicate better HRQoL [23–25].

WPAI questionnaire: The WPAI is a 6-item validated instrument that evaluates the disease’s impact on work productivity and daily activities during the past 7 days. It includes: percent activity impairment, percent overall work impairment (calculated by combining presenteeism and absenteeism), percent presenteeism (work impairment when at work), and percent absenteeism (absence from work). The WPAI scores range from 0 to 100%; higher scores indicate greater impairment and less productivity [26, 27].

Statistical Analyses

All analyses were descriptive. Comparative statistical analyses and adjustments were not conducted, and any comparisons made between study groups should be considered purely descriptive. This approach was chosen to reflect the real-world nature of the data and to avoid overinterpretation of findings that may be influenced by multiple uncontrolled variables. Categorical variables were represented as numbers and percentages, while continuous variables were expressed as median, mean, and SD or standard error of mean (SEM). Additional subgroup analyses were performed based on percent weight change at data capture since obesity diagnosis (weight gain, no change, and weight reduction [0 to < 5%, ≥ 5 to < 10%, and ≥ 10%)] to describe the impairment in activity and work productivity based on the percent weight change. Missing values were excluded from the analysis and not imputed; therefore, the base number of PwO differs by variable and is reported separately for each analysis. All analyses were conducted using Stata 18 software, Version 18.5 (StataCorp. 2025. Stat Statistical Software: Release18. College Station, TX, US: StataCorp LLC) and/or UNICOM Intelligence Reporter version 7.5 (UNICOM Systems, Mission Hills, CA, US).

Results

Overview of Study Participants

This analysis was based on data from 151 physicians who provided data for 1,082 PwO without T2D, with 199 PwO in the tirzepatide group, 158 in the OMM group, and 725 in the non-OMM group at data capture (Supplementary Fig. 1). Of these, 221 (20.4%) PwO completed the voluntary PRO questionnaires. An overview of PwO who provided PRO data and those who did not is provided in Supplementary Table 1. The demographics and clinical characteristics across the two groups were broadly similar.

Socio-Demographics and Clinical Characteristics of PwO at Data Capture

The socio-demographics and clinical characteristics among PwO are summarized in Table 1. Overall, the mean (SD) age was 47.0 (13.7) years; majority were female (67.4%) while 32.6% were male white (68.8%), and working full-time (69.4%). At data capture, the majority of the PwO had a BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2 (tirzepatide group: 73.7%; OMM group: 82.7%; and non-OMM group: 80.5%). The overall mean (SD) BMI was 36.9 (6.0) at treatment initiation [tirzepatide group: 36.4 (6.0) and OMM group: 37.5 (5.9)]. The overall mean (SD) number of ORCs was 2.3 (1.9), with 83.4% PwO having at least 1 ORC. The mean (SD) number of ORCs was similar across the three groups [tirzepatide: 2.5 (2.2); OMM: 2.5 (2.1); non-OMM: 2.1 (1.8)]. Overall, hypertension (43.0%), dyslipidemia (35.2%), depression (14.0%), anxiety (14.0%), prediabetes (13.7%), and hypothyroidism (10.3%) were the commonly reported ORCs among PwO. Discussion

This real-world study provided insights into the socio-demographic and clinical characteristics of PwO without T2D and their perspectives on the impact of obesity on HRQoL, work productivity, and activity impairment. Our study highlighted that PwO on tirzepatide treatment showed numeric differences, suggesting less impairment in some of the SF-36v2 domains (role limitations-physical, bodily pain, vitality, social functioning, role limitations-emotional, and mental health) and MCS; however, minimal differences were noted across the study groups for PCS. PwO in the tirzepatide and OMM groups may be associated with lesser activity and overall work impairment than PwO in the non-OMM group. Moreover, PwO who reported weight reduction appeared to have lower activity and work impairment than those who reported no change or weight gain. No formal statistical comparisons were conducted to validate these differences.

For most of the PwO, the time since first diagnosis of obesity was approximately 3 years; however, the time since treatment initiation was reportedly about 7–8 months. Most of the PwO reported at least one ORC. The most commonly reported ORCs were hypertension, dyslipidemia, depression, and anxiety, which were consistent with previous literature [28]. The current study findings report the prevalence of ORCs to be numerically similar across all three groups. Previously, it has been demonstrated that the prevalence of ORCs increases over time in PwO and was noted particularly in PwO with higher obesity classes, which, in turn, corresponds to increased healthcare costs [29]. Thus, early weight management interventions may help prevent or manage the progression of ORCs, potentially reducing the associated healthcare costs and burden [30].

The study findings suggest that PwO may present with impaired activity and reduced work productivity, consistent with the previously reported Adelphi DSP study [3]. In this current real-world study, PwO on tirzepatide and OMM treatments reported numerically lower levels of activity and overall work impairment than those not on an OMM. Additionally, the results suggest that PwO with weight reduction tended to report lower impairment in work-related outcomes than PwO who reported no change or weight gain. Clinical findings have reported that newer OMMs, including tirzepatide, have clinically meaningful weight reduction among PwO [16, 31, 32]. PwO in the non-OMM group reported numerically higher levels of productivity loss than other groups, suggesting that when PwO do not take sick leave, they may report impairment at work. Hence, pharmacotherapy, adjunct to lifestyle modifications, can be associated with improved weight management and disease burden [33].

Consistent with previous findings, results of the SF-36v2 questionnaire in our study suggest that PwO across all groups appeared to have impairment in general health [3] and physical functioning, while PwO on tirzepatide treatment tended to report numerically higher scores in some of the SF-36v2 domains and MCS than those not receiving tirzepatide treatment. Improvement in physical functioning is an important aspect among PwO aiming to achieve weight reduction [34]. A previous study of 72-week tirzepatide treatment among PwO reported improvement in physical function and weight reduction, with greater weight reduction being associated with greater improvement [35]. Conversely, this real-world study showed that PwO reported impairment for physical function and minimal differences were reported across study groups for PCS. Psychological and social outcomes are important measures in effective obesity management. In this study, PwO on tirzepatide treatment appeared to have less impairment for domains attributed to social and mental health than those not receiving tirzepatide.

Recent real-world studies reported that persistent tirzepatide users observed a mean percent weight reduction of 11.9–12.9%, with 61.5–69.0% achieving ≥ 10% weight reduction at 6 months [36, 37]. The present study suggests that among PwO initiating OMM (tirzepatide or alternative OMM), those treated with tirzepatide may be associated with a greater mean percent weight reduction (8.4%) than those who received an alternative OMM (6.7%). Previous reports have observed a general trend of greater weight reduction and a higher proportion of individuals achieving weight reduction targets with tirzepatide treatment than with other treatments [38, 39]. In concordance with the current guidelines, diet and exercise were the most commonly used weight reduction approaches among PwO before the current regimen [40]. More than half of the PwO in the tirzepatide and OMM groups were on diet and exercise at data capture, as the use of OMMs is recommended along with reduced calorie intake and physical activity [31, 41].

Strengths and Limitations

An important strength of this study is that it provides insights into real-world socio-demographic and clinical characteristics using the well-established and validated DSP methodology. The study design included voluntary surveys, which translated the perspectives of PwO without T2D on work productivity and HRQoL in real-world clinical practice settings, addressing an important research gap. Notably, the study has limitations. The cross-sectional design of this DSP study does not allow for the demonstration of cause-and-effect relationships. Individuals may have received tirzepatide and OMMs for varying times, so comparisons cannot be made. The study did not conduct any adjustments to account for the underlying differences in demographic characteristics and clinical status across the 3 groups. The association between HRQoL or work-related outcomes and important social determinants of health (such as gender, socioeconomic status, and race/ethnicity) among PwO on tirzepatide or alternative medication was not assessed in the current analysis and was outside the scope of the current study. Minimal inclusion criteria governed the selection of the participants. Physicians provide data for a consecutive series of PwO to avoid selection bias and to establish a geographically diverse and pragmatic PwO sample reflective of a real-world consulting population of PwO. However, participation was influenced by willingness to complete the survey, and completion of self-reported questionnaires was voluntary. Further, the study’s findings may not be generalizable due to the small sample size and imbalance in study groups; however, the study’s findings align with previous literature. The physicians and consulting PwO surveyed may not accurately reflect the broader US physician population in obesity management. Recall bias, a common limitation of surveys, may have affected physician responses to questions. However, physicians had access to medical records, reducing the risk of recall bias. The PwO in the tirzepatide and OMM groups included a persistent population of individuals who remained on the treatment at data capture. Hence, PwO who discontinued the treatment were not included in the present analysis. Information on dosing patterns was not included in this analysis, which may have provided additional context for interpreting the findings. All analyses were descriptive, and no formal comparative statistical testing was conducted; therefore, any comparison mentioned in the study is purely descriptive.

Conclusions

The results from this real-world cross-sectional secondary analysis of data from the DSP study suggest that PwO on tirzepatide treatment may be associated with less impairment in some of the SF-36v2 domains and MCS than those not receiving tirzepatide. Additionally, PwO on tirzepatide or alternative OMM treatment may be associated with lower work impairment than those who were not receiving treatment. The study findings also suggest that PwO who appeared to have weight reduction reported lesser impairment in work productivity than those who had no weight change or weight gain. Comparable to existing research, a numerically greater mean percent weight reduction was reported by PwO on tirzepatide than those who received alternative OMM. These real-world results support future research to assess the long-term impact of tirzepatide treatment on work productivity and HRQoL, which can aid physicians in strategizing treatment regimens for optimizing treatment outcomes.

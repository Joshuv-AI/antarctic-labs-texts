Epidemiological characteristics of infectious mononucleosis among hospitalized children in Henan Province, China: a retrospective study
Authors: Xin Yuan, Shouhang Chen, Haiyan Yang, Guangcai Duan, Yaodong Zhang, Yuefei Jin, Fang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInfectious mononucleosis (IM), is mainly caused by the primary infection with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV). Generally, most patients with IM are deemed to have a favorable prognosis, yet a small proportion of children will need hospitalization. This study aimed to explore the epidemiological features of IM among hospitalized children in Henan Province from 2014 to 2023 and forecast monthly IM hospitalizations in 2024.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of all inpatients with IM in Henan Children’s Hospital and the Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University from January 2014 to December 2023. Demographic information, clinical diagnosis, and admission time were meticulously analyzed. Wavelet analysis and SARIMA model were employed to identify disease periodicity and forecast hospitalization with IM, respectively.ResultsThere were 7,269 IM inpatients, which accounted for 0.70% (7,269/1,036,764) of all hospitalized cases during this period. The number of male patients exceeded that of females in different age groups, admission years, and regions. The overall male-to-female ratio was 1.44:1. The median age of hospitalization was 3 years (IQR 1–5 years), the 1–3 years old children had the highest hospitalization among all age groups. The hospitalization peaked from June to September but decreased to the minimum between January and February each year. In addition, the best-fitted SARIMA model was identified as (1,0,0) × (1,1,0)12.ConclusionsThis study presents primary data to enhance the understanding of the epidemiological characteristics of IM in Henan Province, China. The utilization of the SARIMA model enables precise forecasting of the trend of IM hospitalization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11466-5.Keywords: Infectious mononucleosis, Epstein-Barr virus, Hospitalized children, Epidemiological characteristics

Introduction

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a human lymphotropic herpesvirus belonging to the Herpesviridae, Lymphadenovirus genus, gamma-subfamily. It has an envelope and double-stranded DNA [1]. Like other herpesviruses, the EBV enters a latent phase following primary infection, persisting in epithelial cells and B lymphocytes, where it remains for life [2]. This primary infection occurs only in humans through saliva droplets or close oral contact. Once infected with EBV, individuals become lifelong carriers of the virus [3, 4]. Additionally, primary acute infection with EBV can result in infectious mononucleosis (IM), as well as some serious diseases that can significantly impact the health and quality of life of children, such as Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal cancer, aplastic anemia, and EBV-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (EBV-HLH) [5, 6].

IM is a clinical syndrome caused by EBV, characterized by atypical lymphocytosis [7, 8]. Significantly, pediatric patients afflicted with IM manifest a classical triad of symptoms including pyrexia, pharyngitis, and lymphadenopathy. Additionally, they may also exhibit periorbital edema, hepatosplenomegaly, and hepatic impairment [9]. In adults, IM may manifest as a sore throat, lymphadenopathy, and fatigue [10]. In developed countries, IM primarily occurs during adolescence and late adolescence [11, 12]. However, as a developing country, a high incidence of IM in China was found in preschoolers, among which children aged 4–6 years reached the peak [13, 14].

Despite being a generally benign and self-limiting disease with a favorable prognosis, IM can exhibit atypical and life-threatening manifestations, particularly in pediatric EBV infections, whose clinical spectra span multiple systems and may result in severe illness [15, 16]. Additionally, although some studies have examined the epidemiological characteristics of IM among the global pediatric population [11, 17, 18], there remain few studies on the epidemiological characteristics and prediction of IM in hospitalized children in China. Therefore, data from two children’s hospitals between 2014 and 2023 were extracted and retrospectively analyzed. The primary objective was to investigate the epidemiological characteristics and hospitalization patterns of IM in this particular region, with the ultimate goal of providing fundamental baseline data for both researchers and clinicians.

Materials and methods

Data collection

This study retrospectively collected general demographic and clinical data on children hospitalized for IM from 2014 to 2023 from Henan Children’s Hospital and the Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University. Henan Children’s Hospital (Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Zhengzhou University) is equipped with 2,000 beds (https://www.zzsetyy.cn/), is the National Children’s Regional Medical Center and the Children’s Medical Center of Henan Province. The Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (Henan Provincial Maternal and Child Health Hospital) boasts 1,982 available beds (https://www.zdsfy.net/) and is recognized as one of the seven training bases on the Chinese mainland designated by United Nations Children’s Fund. The survey included a total of 8626 cases of all pediatric patients treated for IM at the two hospitals between January 2014 and December 2023. We used a self-made questionnaire to extract and analyze relevant medical information such as the gender, age, clinical diagnosis and admission time of the patients from the electronic system of inpatient medical records. The self-made questionnaire is shown in Table S1. The hospitalized children were divided into five age groups: infants (0–1 years), toddlers (1–3 years), preschoolers (4–6 years), school children (7–12 years), and adolescents (13–18 years).

Diagnostic criteria for IM

According to the Hoagland criteria [19], which are most commonly referenced for diagnosing IM, as well as the Clinical Pathway for Infectious Mononucleosis (2019 Edition), all hospitalized children diagnosed with IM fulfilled the following diagnostic criteria: (1) at least three of the following clinical symptoms were present: fever, tonsilliopharyngitis, cervical lymph node enlargement, liver enlargement, or splenomegaly; (2) there are more than 50% lymphocytes and more than 10% atypical lymphocytes in peripheral blood white blood cells; (3) IgM to EBV viral capsid antigen (VCA-IgM) and IgG to EBV capsid antigen (VCA-IgG) were positive, with absence of an antibody to Epstein-Barr nuclear antigen (EBNA).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

According to the diagnostic criteria as well as inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 1,357 cases were excluded from the study, leaving 7,269 cases included in the final analysis. Patients who were diagnosed with IM and hospitalized were included. Children ranged in age from 0 to 18 years. However, data of children who had unclear or lacked age, gender, current address, and time of admission were excluded. Patients whose current address was not in Henan Province at the time of onset were also excluded. Infections such as cytomegalovirus (CMV) and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) were excluded.

Statistical analysis

The data was filtered and organized using Excel 2016 software, then categorized into distinct groups with corresponding quantities and percentages calculated for each group. Major and discrete trends in the data were analyzed using the median and interquartile range (IQR). As shown in Supplemental Methods, Wavelet analysis and Fourier series were respectively performed using MATLAB 8.4.0 and R version 4.4.1, while SPSS 26.0 was utilized for constructing the ARIMA model. The regional distribution of IM inpatients in Henan Province was mapped using ArcGIS 10.7. Graphs were created with GraphPad Prism 9.0.0, while tables were produced using Adobe Illustrator 2020. A significance level of P < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

Results

Demographic information of children hospitalized with IM between January 2014 and December 2023 in Henan Province, China

From January 2014 to December 2023, a total of 7,269 children met the inclusion criteria of this study, accounting for 0.70% (7,269/1,036,764) of all hospitalized cases. Among these children, males and females accounted for 59.03% (4291 cases) and 40.97% (2978 cases), respectively. The male-to-female ratio was 1.44:1 (Table 1). Males were predominant in different age groups, admission years, and residence, except for the 13–18 y age group where the female proportion was higher (Fig. 1). The children aged 1–3 y accounted for 51.28% (3,728 cases), followed by 4–6 y, 7–12 y, 0–1 y, and 13–18 y, with proportions of 26.41% (1,920 cases), 11.49% (835 cases), 10.06% (731 cases), and 0.76% (55 cases), respectively. In terms of hospitalizations, the number of admissions in 2020 was the largest with a proportion of 13.48% (980 cases), followed by 2021, 2014, 2019, 2023, 2015, 2018, 2022, 2016 and 2017, accounted for 13.37% (972 cases), 13.18% (958 cases), 11.34% (824 cases), 9.97% (725 cases), 9.85% (716 cases), 8.50% (618 cases), 8.03% (584 cases), 6.74% (490 cases), 5.53% (402 cases). Hospitalized children with IM living in urban and rural areas accounted for 53.56% (3,893 cases) and 46.44% (3,376 cases), respectively. The median LOS for hospitalized IM patients was 8 days (IQR 6–10 days) from 2014 to 2023 (Table 1).

Age distribution of hospitalized children with IM in Henan, China

Of the 7,269 cases with IM, their median age was 3 years (IQR 1–5 years), ranging from 11 days to 17 years, with a peak age of 1 to 3 years. Children less than 6 years old accounted for 87.76% (6,379/7,269) (Fig. 2A). The distribution of age groups remained consistent between 2014 and 2023, with the highest proportion of the age group of 1–3 y, followed by 4–6 y. The age groups of 0–1 y and 7–12 y exhibited a similar proportion of cases, and the proportion of children aged 13–18 y was the lowest (Fig. 2B).

Time trend and seasonal pattern of hospitalization with IM in Henan, China

As shown in Fig. 3 and Figure S1, we analyzed the ratios of IM hospitalization to total hospitalization within the corresponding groups in different age and gender groups. June and September accounted for the majority of top hospitalizations, with the exception of February and March in 2020 and November in 2022 (Fig. 3A). Between 2014 and 2018, the annual hospitalization for IM decreased and stabilized at about 0.5% (2,226/415,597). From 2019 to 2021, the percentage increased to approximately 0.8% (2,776/364,036). However, the percentage decreased back to 0.6% after 2022. The highest monthly and yearly ratios of IM hospitalization were observed in September 2014 (2.3%, 89/3,800) and 2014 (1.7%, 958/55,532), respectively (Fig. 3A and Figure S1A). In different age groups, there were discernible disparities in hospitalization among the five age groups. The highest ratio of hospitalization due to IM was observed in children aged 1–3 y group. However, it was difficult to distinguish between children aged 1–3 y and 4–6 y after August 2021 (Fig. 3B). The annual hospitalization for IM remained relatively low (approximately 0.2%) among children 0–1 y, 4–6 y, and 7–12 y (Figure S1B). Meanwhile, the IM hospitalization ratio was almost higher in males than in females (Fig. 3C and Figure S1C).

Periodicity and prediction of hospitalization with IM in Henan, China

The wavelet analysis revealed the presence of two short cycles lasting 10 ~ 13 months between 2017 ~ 2020 and 2022 ~ 2023, as well as a longer cycle spanning 29 ~ 33 months during 2020 ~ 2022. The highest powers were observed within 10 ~ 13 months. All findings passed the test of significance, but it was worth noting that the short cycle of 10 ~ 13 months during 2022 ~ 2023 and the long cycle of 29 ~ 33 months during 2020 ~ 2022 were significantly impacted by boundary effects (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, the Fourier series results for the monthly data from 2014 to 2023 indicated that using a 12-month period (K = 4 harmonics) enabled the fitting curve to effectively capture annual periodic fluctuations, revealing a consistent cyclical pattern over time, with a model R² of 0.8865 (Figure S2). When combined with wavelet analysis, the 12-month main cycle identified through Fourier fitting aligns with the short-cycle oscillation of 10 ~ 13 months observed in the wavelet analysis. We also identified the optimal SARIMA (1,0,0) × (1,1,0)12 model with the highest R2 of 0.547 and a BIC of 6.023. The residual order was presented as a whitening sequence. This model was utilized to forecast monthly hospitalizations for IM in 2024 (Fig. 4B). Additionally, the number of IM cases reported in 2024 was utilized for model validation through back-calculation. The results demonstrated that all observed data points fall within the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the fitted values, suggesting that the model exhibits satisfactory performance (Table S2).

Regional distribution of hospitalized children with IM in Henan, China

From 2014 to 2023, hospitalized children with IM were distributed in 18 prefecture-level cities in Henan Province. The top five cities were Zhengzhou, Zhoukou, Zhumadian, Xuchang, and Shangqiu, with 4270, 478, 456, 388, and 268 cases, accounting for 80.62% (5,860/7,269) of the total. Interestingly, except for Xuchang, the regional population of the other four cities all rank among the top six in Henan Province. The ratios of the proportion of IM cases to the population proportion are 4.4, 0.8, 0.9, and 0.5, respectively (Table S3). The hospitalized IM cases in Henan Province were primarily concentrated in the central and eastern regions, with relatively few cases reported in the western region (Fig. 5).

Discussion

EBV infection is widely prevalent among the global population, with significant rates of infection. Over 90% of adults have tested positive for anti-EBV antibodies, indicating a high level of exposure to the virus [20–22]. Nevertheless, IM is a clinical syndrome that arises from the initial infection with EBV, typically characterized by elevated body temperature, pharyngeal inflammation, and lymph node swelling in the neck [23]. Hence, this retrospective study analyzed the clinical data of 7,269 pediatric patients admitted for infectious mononucleosis (IM) at Henan Children’s Hospital and the Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University between 2014 and 2023. The aim was to gain insights into the hospitalization patterns and epidemiological characteristics of pediatric IM cases in Henan Province.

Our findings unveiled a male-to-female ratio of 1.44:1, devoid of any discernible regional, age, or year of hospitalization predilection. This aligns with the conclusions delineated in certain antecedent literature. Kiran Devkota et al. indicated that the male-to-female ratio of EBV-infected children in Hubei, China was 1.5:1 [24]. Shi et al. stated that the male-to-female ratio of outpatients and inpatients with EBV infection among children in Hangzhou, China was 1.56:1 [25]. Shi et al. reported that the male-to-female ratio of children with EBV infection in Suzhou, China was 1.36:1 [26]. Additionally, Liu et al. analyzed the epidemiological characteristics and disease burden of IM in hospitalized children in China and discovered that the male-to-female ratio was 1.48:1 [13]. It has been believed that boys tend to suffer more frequently than girls due to poorer innate immunity [27]. Females generally demonstrate a more robust basal immune response, as their X chromosomes harbor a greater number of immune-related genes, including TLR7 and various pattern recognition receptor genes. Additionally, estrogen enhances humoral immunity and modulates the Th1/Th2 balance. In contrast, male tend to exhibit weaker cellular immunity, particularly in terms of CD4⁺ T cell and NK cell activity, as well as a reduced antiviral response. Following pathogen infection, males are more susceptible to disease progression due to lower immune clearance efficiency [28–30]. In summary, our study, in conjunction with existing literature, clearly demonstrates a higher prevalence of hospitalization due to IM among male individuals compared to their female counterparts.

In this study, the prevalence of IM demonstrated two distinct peaks among young children, specifically toddlers aged 1–3 years and preschoolers aged 4–6 years. This finding is in line with previous studies conducted in China, thus indicating a consistent pattern across different investigations [31, 32]. The primary mode of transmission among young children is exposure to saliva that tests positive for EBV, either through direct contact or via contact with contaminated food [33]. In China and other developing nations, the majority of initial EBV infections occur in children, particularly among preschool-aged individuals, who constitute a significant portion of patients diagnosed with IM [34]. In developed nations, the onset of infection often occurs post-adolescence [35]. This discrepancy may be attributed to lifestyle choices, population density, and hygiene standards [36]. Additionally, children aged 1 to 3 are in a critical developmental period during which the immune system transitions from reliance on innate immunity to the gradual establishment of adaptive immunity. During the neonatal period, maternally derived antibodies progressively diminish, while the antigen recognition and immunological memory capabilities of the child’s own T and B lymphocytes remain immature. Following viral infection, there is an increased risk of excessive inflammatory responses or immune evasion [37]. Although adaptive immunity becomes more established in children aged 4 to 6, with improvements in T-cell receptor (TCR) diversity and enhanced affinity of B-cell produced antibodies compared to earlier stages of childhood, the immune response remains suboptimal when encountering novel or virulent viral strains [38]. Furthermore, during this developmental stage, children typically begin attending daycare or preschool settings, leading to increased exposure to external pathogens and environmental antigens [39].

Our data revealed that the proportion of IM hospitalizations relative to total hospitalizations is 0.70% (7,269/1,036,764), comparable to the data analyzed in the national retrospective study on hospitalizations [13]. From 2014 to 2018, there was a noticeable decrease in the annual hospitalization of IM cases, which might be associated with improving economic conditions, and EBV infections were postponed further [40]. Nevertheless, there was an upsurge in hospitalization between 2019 and 2021, which might have been related to COVID-19 pandemic interventions that reduced pathogen exposure and immunization training, giving rise to an “immunity debt” that led to lower herd immunity compared to the pre-pandemic levels. This aggravates the susceptibility of children, indicating an unanticipated effect of mitigation strategies on population immunity [41, 42]. However, post-2022 saw a documented decrease of 584 inpatients, marking a significant 40% reduction compared to 2019. This decline can be attributed to the normalization of the COVID-19 pandemic, indicating a positive trend in healthcare management and containment efforts [43].

In addition, it was noted that except in February and March 2020 and November 2022, significant hospitalizations were predominantly seen in June and September, but decreased from January to February each year. A previous study on the epidemiology and clinical characteristics of IM associated with EBV infection in children in Beijing, China revealed that EBV-IM occurs throughout the year and peaks in autumn [44]. Furthermore, a national retrospective study in China showed that the incidence of IM was high from July to September, followed by a decline reaching its lowest level in February of the following year before rising again [13]. These findings suggest that there may be a seasonal pattern in the prevalence of IM among Chinese children.

The study further revealed that the hospitalized children with IM were distributed across 18 prefecture-level cities in Henan Province, predominantly in Zhengzhou, Zhoukou, Zhumadian, Xuchang, and Shangqiu. Moreover, the population of the remaining four cities except Xuchang was also among the top in the province. This may be attributed to the higher concentration of the population in urban areas. Superior medical conditions in metropolitan areas facilitate more proactive medical consultations, while visits and hospitalizations for rural patients are limited [45, 46]. This is also consistent with research findings indicating that the hospitalized children in urban areas (53.56%) outnumbered those in rural areas (46.44%). Furthermore, these hospitals are located in Zhengzhou, the capital city of Henan Province, which is abundant in medical resources and more accessible for local people and patients from neighboring cities seeking medical care.

It is evident that the 29 ~ 33 months cycle observed from 2020 to 2022 serves as a distinct demarcation between the two shorter cycles. This period corresponded with the global outbreak of COVID-19. During this time, non-pharmaceutical interventions, such as school closures and mask mandates, substantially curtailed viral transmission, potentially delaying or accumulating the pool of susceptible individuals [47]. As public health restrictions were gradually lifted, the resurgence of cases may have prolonged the inter-epidemic interval, thereby extending the overall cycle duration [48]. This observation aligns with the assertion by Lloyd et al. that intervention measures can alter disease transmission patterns and reshape the dynamics of susceptible populations, ultimately influencing epidemic cycling [49]. The combined application of wavelet analysis and Fourier series offers deeper insights into the temporal dynamics of monthly data spanning from 2014 to 2023. The Fourier series, characterized by its 12-month periodicity (K = 4 harmonics) and high goodness-of-fit (R²=0.8865), confirms the dominance of a stable annual cycle throughout the dataset. This consistent periodic pattern is clearly illustrated through a smoothed fitting curve, reflecting persistent seasonal or annual rhythms, potentially driven by recurring environmental conditions or systemic annual processes. In addition, the SARIMA model was effectively applied to forecast the number of IM cases in 2024, demonstrating a high degree of consistency between the observed and predicted values. Therefore, the SARIMA model and wavelet analysis results can be utilized to predict peak seasons and facilitating the allocation of additional pediatric resources, such as hospital beds and antiviral medications, to alleviate the burden on the healthcare system.

Although the main data of this study were collected up to 2023, long-term monitoring from 2014 to 2023 revealed a high degree of stability in the age distribution, gender differences, and seasonal patterns of IM hospitalizations, with no significant shifts or anomalies observed during this period. This consistent epidemiological profile aligns closely with the reported characteristics of childhood IM both domestically and internationally over the same timeframe [17, 31, 50]. Moreover, the number of IM cases in 2024, following the relatively unique period of the COVID-19 pandemic, was selected for back-substitution in the SARIMA model. The results indicated that all actual values fell within the 95% CI of the fitted values, suggesting that the model accurately captured the incidence trend and was consistent with the real-world situation. Furthermore, key factors potentially influencing the incidence of IM—such as children’s immune development profiles, social interaction patterns, and the biological transmission characteristics of the EBV—have not undergone substantial changes in the short term (1–2 years) [38, 39, 45]. In addition, we are continuously monitoring IM hospitalization data from 2024 onwards and plan to conduct a prospective cohort study to analyze the dynamic changes in age distribution, gender differences, and epidemic cycles. This initiative will provide a scientific basis for refining long-term prevention and control strategies.

There were some limitations in our study. Firstly, the post-pandemic data are limited to 2023 and may not comprehensively reflect the post-pandemic epidemic trends. Consequently, continuous surveillance of IM prevalence will be necessary in the future to achieve a more comprehensive understanding. Secondly, while SARIMA is a mature and applicable technique for predicting infectious diseases, the selection of the most appropriate SARIMA model is subjective and challenging. Despite these shortcomings, our results offer valuable insight into the epidemiological characteristics of hospitalized patients with IM in Henan, China.

Conclusions

In summary, the hospitalization of male children exceeded that of females. Among the different age groups, hospitalization for IM was highest among children aged 1–3 years. It was also observed that the admission time of children with IM may be seasonal. The SARIMA model successfully predicted the monthly number of IM hospitalizations in 2024, demonstrating its efficacy in forecasting healthcare trends. The data are beneficial for developing targeted strategies related to monitoring and optimizing the allocation of medical resources. In particular, it is necessary to strengthen health education in childcare facilities for children aged 1–6 and male children and implement EBV antibody screening to protect susceptible children at an early stage.

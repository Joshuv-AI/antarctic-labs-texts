Efficacy of dupilumab on chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps and concomitant asthma in biologic-naive and biologic-pretreated patients
Authors: María Sandra Domínguez-Sosa, María Soledad Cabrera-Ramírez, Miriam del Carmen Marrero-Ramos, Delia Dávila-Quintana, Carlos Cabrera-López, Heleia González Cuervo, Jesús Javier Benítez del Rosario, Teresa Carrillo-Díaz
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesDupilumab, an anti-IL-4 receptor monoclonal antibody (mAb), was recently approved for the treatment of severe chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP). The main objective of this study was to assess whether previous exposure to biological treatment affected the clinical outcomes in CRSwNP and asthma patients, treated with dupilumab over time. A collateral secondary objective was to analyse the effects over time of dupilumab in patients with and without aeroallergen sensitization.MethodsSingle-centre retrospective observational study on severe CRSwNP patients treated with dupilumab. Nasal polyp score (NPS), visual analogue scale (VAS) symptom score, sinonasal outcome test (SNOT-22), aeroallergen sensitization, total serum IgE levels, and blood eosinophil counts were assessed at baseline and after 4, 6 and 12 months.Results42 patients were included, 40 (95.2%) had asthma. Twenty-one (50%) patients received dupilumab without prior biological treatment (Group A: naive) and 50% switched to dupilumab from previous biological treatment (Group B: pre-treated). NPS, VAS symptoms, SNOT-22 improved significantly after 12 months treatment in both groups of patients (p < 0.001). After 12 months, VAS overall symptom score showed a significant reduction from 6 (IQR, 4.6–8.6) and 6 (IQR, 3.8–7.1) for Group A and Group B patients respectively, to 1.2 (IQR, 0.8–2.7) and 1.2 (IQR, 0.2–2.5); NPS from 6 (IQR, 4.0–7.0) and 5 (IQR, 3.5–6.0), respectively, to 1 (IQR, 0.0–2.0) and 0 (IQR, 0.0–3.0) and SNOT-22 from 64 (IQR, 56–78) and 71 (IQR, 47.5–76.0) respectively, to 5.5 (IQR, 4–21) and 6 (IQR, 4–15). IgE reduced from 57 to 22.1 and from 46.9 to 30.2 in Group A and Group B respectively (p < 0.001).ConclusionsDupilumab improves symptom severity, polyp size, and health-related quality of life, regardless of the presence or absence of comorbid aeroallergen sensitization and previous administration of biologic therapy.Keywords: Chronic rhinosinusitis, nasal polyps, dupilumab, monoclonal antibody, perennial allergen sensitization

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) is a phenotypic subtype of chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) that has a substantial impact on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [1] and a significant annual economic burden [2]. Cytokines play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of CRSwNP; T2 cytokines are of particular importance given their role in eosinophil recruitment and maintenance of inflammatory response [3]. Epidemiological, clinical and pathophysiological studies suggest that CRSwNP patients often have multiple comorbidities, such as asthma, allergic rhinitis (AR), and aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) [4–6]. Approximately 8% to 26% of patients with CRSwNP have comorbid AERD [7, 8], and 51% to 86% of CRSwNP patients are sensitized to at least 1 aeroallergen [9]. Patients with CRSwNP and comorbid asthma have more severe disease and asthma in the presence of nasal polyposis is also more difficult to control [6, 7]. In recent years, a new term, severe uncontrolled CRSwNP, has been proposed for CRS that cannot be controlled with optimal medical and surgical treatment [4, 10]. The underlying inflammatory pathology associated with uncontrolled CRSwNP presents a therapeutic challenge to otolaryngologists [11]. Despite surgery and ongoing topical corticosteroid medical therapy, many CRSwNP patients remain symptomatic and have incomplete disease control [12]. Biologic therapies are a good alternative to these patients and some products have been approved by US and European regulatory authorities [13]. In recent years, several monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have been approved to treat not only severe asthma, but also other disorders linked to type 2 immune responses including CRSwNP. Biological therapy with mAbs can be used in type 2 CRSwNP to target specific immune mediators that are the basis of the underlying inflammatory process, including anti-IL-4/IL-13 signalling (dupilumab), anti-IL-5 pathways (mepolizumab, benralizumab), and anti-IgE antibodies (omalizumab) [14].

Among these biologics, dupilumab is a fully human mAb that binds with the alpha subunit of IL-4 receptors (IL-4Rα type 1 and type 2) to inhibit IL-4 and IL-13 signalling; it was the first biological drug approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of CRSwNP (June 26th, 2019) in adults as add-on therapy with intranasal corticosteroids (INCS) [15]. Dupilumab was approved in Spain for CRSwNP in January 2022, but dupilumab for CRSwNP was not funded by the Spanish Public Health System during the period of our study. The choice of dupilumab as biologic therapy was made in consensus with pneumologists and allergists from our health area.

The primary objective of the study was to investigate whether dupilumab could improve CRSwNP symptoms, showing a linear trend in outcomes over time, and whether prior exposure to biologics (Group A: naive vs. Group B: previously treated patients) affected clinical outcomes in CRSwNP and asthma patients treated with dupilumab. Our aim, then, was to estimate whether both groups improved over time and if they did so differently.

As a collateral secondary objective, this study also sought to determine whether the effect over time differed between patients sensitized and non-sensitized to aeroallergens.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a single-center retrospective, observational study in a real-life setting that included CRSwNP patients attending the multidisciplinary Airway Diseases Unit of a tertiary care hospital in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (Canary Islands, Spain). Patients were treated with dupilumab 300 mg, administered subcutaneously once every 2 weeks, in addition to standard of care.

From January 2022 to January 2023, the private database of electronic medical records from the Airway Diseases Unit was reviewed to select the study population.

The study was approved by the Hospital Ethics Committee (CEIm code: 2023-053-1), and all patients provided written informed consent before the collection of data.

Diagnosis of CRSwNP was strictly determined according to the European Position Paper on Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps (EPOS) 2020. Dupilumab was prescribed in patients with recurrent refractory severe bilateral CRwNP according to biologic treatment indications established by EPOS/EUFOREA 2023 [16].

We included patients older than 18 years with symptoms of diffuse and severe CRSwNP, defined as follows: endoscopic nasal polyp score (NPS) ≥ 4, with a minimum score of 2 in each nasal cavity; Sinonasal Outcome test (SNOT-22) ≥50; ≥1 polyp surgeries in the last 10 years; inadequate symptom control with standard of care and/or failure of previous endoscopic functional or reboot sinus surgery (ESS), and, exceptionally, patients with a medical contraindication to sinonasal surgery and concomitant asthma. The following were considered as exclusion criteria for treatment: asthma exacerbation requiring hospitalization within 4 weeks prior to inclusion; immunosuppressive therapy before inclusion; concomitant long-term corticosteroid therapy for chronic autoimmune disorders.

The medical records of 42 patients were reviewed and analysed for preoperative demographic characteristics and medical history, including smoking history, comorbid asthma, AERD, AR, and atopy defined by prick test and clinical symptoms.

Asthma was diagnosed using the 2022 Global Initiative for Asthma Strategy 2021 [17], and AR was diagnosed according to the 2019 Allergic Rhinitis and its Impact on Asthma guidelines [18]. All patients with severe uncontrolled asthma met the criteria according to the ATS/ERS guidelines [19].

Study methods

Patients were evaluated in standard clinical visits at baseline, before starting biological therapy and during treatment after 4, 6 and 12 months, after the first administration. At baseline and follow-up visits, patients underwent nasal endoscopic polyp assessment (NPS), evaluation of clinical sinonasal symptoms using a visual analogue scale (VAS) symptom score, the validated SNOT-22 scale, total serum IgE levels, blood eosinophil counts (BEC), and prednisone intake.

In our study, patients were stratified into two distinct groups according to their prior exposure to biological therapy. Patients in Group A received dupilumab as their first biologic treatment. In contrast, Group B included patients who switched to dupilumab from a previous biological therapy. This group B had already included 15 CRSwNP patients who were treated with mepolizumab in a previous paper by the same authors [20].

Aeroallergen sensitization was performed at baseline in all study patients. To achieve the collateral objective of assessing dupilumab’s efficacy based on aeroallergen sensitization, patients were further classified into sensitized and non-sensitized.

Endonasal endoscopic evaluation

NPS was evaluated endoscopically at each study visit. NPS was assessed for each nostril using the sum of left and right nostril scores ranging from 0 (no polyps) to 4 (large polyps causing complete obstruction of the inferior meatus), giving a total score of up to 8 [21].

Sinonasal symptoms evaluation

Patients evaluated their current symptoms (nasal obstruction, nasal discharge, postnasal discharge, olfactory disorders, facial pain, and overall symptom scores) on a VAS scale (0–10); scores were divided by 10 and reported across a range from 0 (none) to 10 (as bad as you can imagine). We reported the VAS score for each symptom and an overall mean composite severity VAS symptom score (combining scores for nasal obstruction, nasal discharge, throat mucus, facial pain, and loss of smell).

Sinonasal quality of life assessment

The 22-item SNOT-22, a sinonasal disease-specific measure of HRQoL was used. This is a questionnaire consisting of 22 CRS-related items scored from 0 to 5 (total score ranges from 0 to 110 with higher scores indicating worsening of symptoms) [22].

Atopic profile

The skin prick test, as described by the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology [23] was performed in all patients. We evaluated all patients attending our institution with a history of AR using a standard skin prick panel that included commonly inhaled aeroallergens (mites, pollens, animal dander, moulds, and insects), a positive histamine control, and a negative saline control. Positive tests were defined as a wheal diameter of 3 mm with flare at 15 min in the absence of dermographism.

Laboratory tests

At baseline and at follow-up, eosinophils, and total serum IgE were measured in blood. Total serum IgE levels expressed in IU/L were measured using an immunofluorometric assay according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The normal value of total IgE was < 100 IU/L.

Prednisone intake

During the study, prednisone intake and exacerbations (defined as episodes requiring oral corticosteroids [OCS] therapy for at least 3 days or an intramuscular injection) were recorded at all time points.

Safety

Adverse events and serious adverse events were recorded at each visit.

Radiological tests

CT scans were performed at baseline, using the Lund-Mackay score (LMK-CT). The LMK-CT is a composite score, with a range of 0–12 on each side and a maximum of 24 for both sides, with high scores indicating more severe disease [24]. To avoid unnecessary ionizing radiation, we did not perform CT scans after treatment.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (n) and percentages (%), while continuous variables are expressed as mean and standard deviation (SD) when the data adhere to a normal distribution. In cases where the distribution deviates from normality, as determined by a Shapiro-Wilk test, variables are represented as median and interquartile range (IQR, 25th–75th percentile). These metrics are provided for the entire sample and for subsets of biologic-naive (Group A) and biologic-experienced (Group B) patients, and for patients with and without aeroallergen sensitization.

Friedman’s non-parametric tests for repeated measures were used to assess the intra-group comparisons for VAS overall symptom score, SNOT-22, NPS, total serum IgE, and BEC at baseline, 4, 6 and 12 months. This test does not make any assumptions about the distribution of the outcome variables under study, assuming there is no change over time or within-subject effect.

The Mann-Whitney test, a non-parametric method, was used separately to compare the distribution between 2 distinct groups: Group A versus Group B, and those with aeroallergen sensitization versus those without. Statistical significance was determined at p < 0.05.

To compare patients’ performance on the 2 versions of the previous therapy, a repeated measures design using generalized linear models (GLM) was used. GLMs for repeated measures were designed to evaluate variations in VAS, SNOT-22, NPS, total serum IgE and BEC between subjects (Group A: naive and Group B: previously treated with biologics) during the observation period. The same analysis was used for patients who were sensitized and not sensitized to aeroallergens.

Data were analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics, v.27.0 software, while graphs were generated using Python (version 3.8). Violin and line plots were carried out using matplotlib and plotly libraries in a Jupyter Notebook environment.

Changes in VAS overall symptom score, NPS, SNOT-22, total serum IgE levels and BEC at 4, 6, and 12 months compared with their baseline values were statistically evaluated using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and Hodges-Lehmann estimator.

Results

Analysis of patient characteristics

We analysed the data from 42 CRSwNP patients who received 300 mg of dupilumab, administered subcutaneously once every 2 weeks between January 2022 and June 2023 at our centre. A total of 42 patients completed the 4-month treatment, 34 completed the 6-month treatment, and 19 completed the 12-month treatment. No patients withdrew from the study.

Twenty-one patients (50%) received dupilumab without having received prior biological treatment (Group A). In the pre-treated group (Group B), 15 patients were switched from mepolizumab [20], 2 from benralizumab, and 1 from omalizumab due to uncontrolled CRSwNP, and 2 patients were switched from mepolizumab and 1 from benralizumab due to uncontrolled asthma.

Our results show that male sex was predominant (57.1% men and 42.9% women) at a ratio of 1.3:1. There were no apparent differences in mean age and gender between naive and pre-treated patients – mean age, 50.20 years; SD, 12.1 years. At baseline, 40 patients (95.2%) had asthma; AERD was described in 20 patients (47.6%), and 18 patients (42.9%) had perennial aeroallergen sensitization. Patients who exhibited positive skin test reactions to dust mites and negative reactions for other inhaled aeroallergens were defined as having atopy to perennial aeroallergens.

All patients continued to receive INCS and nasal saline irrigations during the study period. Safety profile

Dupilumab was well tolerated, and no severe treatment-associated adverse effects were reported.

After 12 months, VAS overall symptom score, NPS, SNOT-22 and IgE decreased significantly in both groups. The Chi-squared statistic for Friedman’s test of the ‘overall change over time’ in our parameters of interest, VAS, NPS, SNOT-22 and IgE, and whatever group analysed, is highly significant (p < 0.001 in most cases). These results indicate that at least 1 of the mean values of the outcome variables at a given time point is significantly different from the mean value at 1 of the other time points. However, this test cannot determine between which time points the statistically significant differences occur. These differences were further analysed using a GLM.

No statistically significant differences were found between Group A and Group B at baseline and 4-, 6-, and 12-month follow-ups, except for SNOT-22 at 4 months (p < 0.05). The same results were obtained for the groups with and without aeroallergen sensitization using respective Mann-Whitney U tests.

At baseline, the median VAS overall symptom score was 6 (IQR, 4.6–8.6) and 6 (IQR, 3.8–7.1) for Group A and Group B respectively; this notably fell to 1.2 (IQR, 0.8–2.7) and 1.2 (IQR, 0.2–2.5) for Group A and Group B respectively after 12 months. It is worth noting that statistically significant differences were found in the mean VAS values over time in both patient groups (p < 0.001).

At baseline, the median NPS for Group A and Group B patients was 6 (IQR, 4.0–7.0) and 5 (IQR, 3.5–6.0), respectively; this fell to 1 (IQR, 0.0–2.0) and 0 (IQR, 0.0–3.0) for Group A and Group B patients after 12 months, respectively. The percentage of patients with severe nasal polyps (NPS ≥ 4) was 85.7% (36/42) at baseline, and 10% (2/19) at the 12-month follow-up.

After 1 year, SNOT-22 substantially fell from a median value of 64 (IQR, 56–78) to 5.5 (IQR, 4–21) in patients Group A and from 71 (IQR, 47.5–76.0) to 6 (IQR, 4–15) in Group B. A large jump was observed in all variables and in both groups between baseline and the first 4 months of treatment which continued until the sixth month, before stabilizing after 12 months.

Total serum IgE shows the greatest decrease when median values after 12 months are compared with baseline, falling from 57 IU/L to 22.1 IU/L and from 46.9 IU/L to 30.2 IU/L in the Group A and Group B, respectively.

Changes in eosinophils

A differentiated analysis is warranted for the changes in BEC over time in both groups, as statistically significant differences were found in mean BEC values for both groups at baseline, with median eosinophil values of 540 cells/mcL (IQR, 275–855) and 130 cells/mcL (IQR, 60–440) in Group A and Group B, respectively (see Table 2). However, in subsequent time periods (at 4, 6, and 12 months), the differences between groups cease to be statistically significant. If we separately analyse changes in BEC over time within each group using Friedman’s test, the mean values for the pre-treated group (Group B) differ significantly over time, whereas those for the naive group (Group A) do not.

Similar trends were observed in the parameters (VAS overall symptom score, NPS, SNOT-22, and total serum IgE) measured in patients with and without aeroallergen sensitization, characterized by a sensitive and persistent reduction in levels, except for eosinophils in the sensitization group. After 12 months of treatment, patients with AR showed a significant reduction in VAS from 6 (IQR, 4.1–7.2) to 1.2 (IQR, 0.5–2.4); NPS from 6 (IQR, 4.0–7.0) to 0 (IQR, 0.0–2.0); and SNOT-22 from 65.5 (IQR, 56.5–72.2) to 6 (IQR, 4.0–12.0) (Table 3). Patients without aeroallergen sensitization also experienced significant reductions.

Changes over time and interaction effects of switching biologicals on outcomes

To evaluate the existence of an intra-group time effect and to determine the time periods between which statistically significant differences occurred, we used a GLM for repeated measures estimation and the Bonferroni test. Table 4 shows the results of GLM repeated measures estimations considering the interactive effects of ‘Time’, ‘Time x Previous treatment group’ and ‘Time x Aeroallergen sensitization group’. The main effect of time on all outcomes is statistically significant, indicating that the values changed significantly during the observation time in both groups (Group A: naive/Group B: pre-treated). In both groups, outcomes decrease steadily at 4 and 6 months, but the parameter remains stable or increases at the 12-month follow-up. In contrast, there is no evidence of a significant interaction effect between time and group (Group A/Group B), (Figure 2(a–d)) except for eosinophilia. A distinct effect is observed between both groups at baseline. This is due to pre-treated patients starting dupilumab treatment with significantly lower eosinophil levels. We observed a statistically significant increase in the mean BEC starting from the baseline period, with a peak at 4 months. The difference remained significant at 6 months; however, at 12 months, there was no statistical significance compared to baseline values (Figure 2(e)).

Significant changes over time in outcomes

To determine the time periods between which statistically significant differences occurred, we used GLM repeated measures pairwise comparisons. As shown in Table 5, there was a statistically significant reduction in VAS overall symptom score, SNOT-22, NPS and total serum IgE at 4, 6 and 12 months compared to baseline: VAS: −3.3, −4.2 and −3.5 (p < 0.001 for all comparisons); SNOT-22, −45, −57 and −52.2 (p < 0.001); NPS: −3, −4 and −4 (p < 0.001); and the greatest reduction in IgE total serum: −4.5, −28 and −88.1. No statistically significant differences were detected in the median values of VAS, NPS, SNOT-22 or IgE between months 6 and 12. In contrast, eosinophils significantly increased between baseline and months 4 and 6 but returned to initial levels with no statistically significant differences between baseline and 12 months.

Discussion

In this study, the efficacy and safety of dupilumab were evaluated in the treatment of patients with recurrent refractory severe bilateral CRwNP, regardless of the presence of other comorbid conditions, such as AR and atopic profile.

CRSwNP is an inflammatory condition with a high symptom burden that has a significant impact on patient HRQoL and significant cost implications for the healthcare system [10].

CRSwNP recurrence is very common, despite medical treatment and surgical management, particularly in cases driven by a type 2 inflammatory pathway [25, 26]. Furthermore, sinus surgery does not always provide long-lasting results, and recurrence rates can range from 40% to 80% at 3 to 12 years after surgery [12, 27]. Given the wider availability of biologics such as mAbs that inhibit proinflammatory mediators, the therapeutic scenario for CRSwNP management has changed [28].

The development of this new therapeutic approach based on underlying pathophysiological mechanisms offers a higher chance of response to a specific treatment and a tailored clinical approach to the disease and forms the basis for precision medicine [13]. In recent years, several mAbs have been approved for the treatment of severe uncontrolled CRSwNP.

Dupilumab is a fully human mAb that binds specifically with the IL-4Rα receptor subunit, thus blocking both IL-4 and IL-13 signalling; as a result, it inhibits cytokine/chemokine-induced responses and the formation of IgE [15, 29].

The US FDA approved dupilumab in 2017 for treating moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis and moderate-to-severe asthma, and in June 2019 dupilumab was the first mAb to be approved by the FDA for the treatment of CRSwNP. The European Medicines Agency (EMA) later approved it for the treatment of severe CRSwNP.

Dupilumab has radically changed the treatment of CRSwNP when systemic therapy with corticosteroids and/or surgery does not achieve adequate disease control [30]. Both randomized clinical trials (RCTs) [15] and real-life observational studies [10, 31–35] have demonstrated that dupilumab is effective in treating patients with CRSwNP.

According to DUPIREAL [35], dupilumab improved NPS, sinonasal symptoms score, and quality of life rapidly and significantly within one month of treatment. Reduced nasal symptoms have also been reported in other real-life studies [10, 31–34]. In our study, dupilumab was highly effective in the treatment of severe and uncontrolled CRSwNP. Between baseline and month 4, all objective and subjective parameters (NPS, VAS overall symptom score, and SNOT-22) had decreased significantly in both groups, a reduction which continued until month 6 before stabilizing after 12 months.

As in other real-life studies [10, 31–35] and RCTs [15], our study demonstrated a significant decrease in NPS after the first 4 months of treatment compared to baseline, both for Group A and Group B patients. NPS at baseline (median: 6; IQR, 4–7) and at 4 months for Group A: naive (median: 1; IQR, 0–4) and NPS at baseline (median: 5; IQR, 3–6) and at 4 months for Group B: pre-treated (median: 2; IQR, 1–4) both showed a p value of less than 0.001. NPS fell from median values of 6 and 5 at baseline for Group A and Group B patients to 1 and 0 at 12 months, respectively.

Few studies have evaluated the efficacy of dupilumab in patients with CRSwNP and an atopic profile. The Canary Islands have a higher prevalence of persistent rhinitis and asthma. Furthermore, weather conditions and the impact of Sahara dust favour the development of dust mites, so exposure to perennial allergens is likely to be significant [36]. Accordingly, in our study, we observed a high frequency of positive dust mite allergy tests: 18 out of 42 patients (42.9%) had perennial aeroallergen sensitization.

As a result of the particular composition of our cohort, we were able to compare patients with CRSwNP who were sensitized to aeroallergens and those who were not. With regard to the behaviour of NPS over time and across groups, the same effects were found, but there was a slight non-statistically significant interaction in the initial period when aeroallergen-sensitized and non-sensitized cohorts were considered.

As part of the post-hoc analysis of the pooled SINUS-24 and SINUS-52 studies, Peters AT, et al. [37] evaluated the effects of dupilumab on objective and patient-reported outcomes in patients with severe CRSwNP and associated AR and found significant improvements in both groups. The same trends are observed in our study, among both patients with and without aeroallergen sensitization. After 12 months of treatment, patients with allergies showed a significant reduction in the VAS symptom score from 6 (IQR, 4.1–7.2) to 1.2 (IQR, 0.5–2.4); NPS from 6 (IQR, 4.0–7.0) to 0 (IQR, 0.0–2.0); and SNOT-22 from 65.5 (IQR, 56.5–72.2) to 6 (IQR, 4.0–12.0). Patients without aeroallergen sensitization also experienced significant reductions in these parameters, and in magnitudes similar to allergic patients.

In the LIBERTY NP SINUS-24 and LIBERTY NP SINUS-52 real-life studies [15] and others, such as that of Böscke et al. [38], total IgE levels in serum decreased consistently. In our study, we observed the greatest decrease when comparing median values after 12 months with baseline, reducing from 57.0 IU/mL (29.6–203.5) to 22.1 (16.3–85.3) and from 46.9 IU/mL (28.3–159.3) to 30.2 (15.7–88.0) in Group A and Group B populations, respectively. In aeroallergen-sensitized patients, median total serum IgE levels decreased from 49.4 IU/mL (29.9–277.7) at baseline to 30.8 (22.1–88.0) at 12 months, p = 0.011.

Dupilumab reduced the use of OCS and improved nasal symptoms, including loss of smell, one of the most debilitating symptoms of CRSwNP. HRQoL also improved, as evidenced by an improvement in SNOT-22 scores that exceeded the threshold for clinically relevant improvement [14]. After 1 year, SNOT-22 underwent a significant reduction from a median value of 64 (IQR, 56–78) to 5.5 (IQR, 4–21) in patients not treated with other biologics previously (Group A), and from 71 (IQR, 47.5–76.0) to 6 (IQR, 4–15) in pre-treated patients (Group B). In persons without CRS, a SNOT-22 median score of 7 is considered normal [39], a value that is slightly higher than the post-dupilumab median value in our study. This discrepancy may be because our series presented several comorbidities, including patients with severe nasal polyposis and asthma (95.2%); AERD was described in 20 patients (47.6%) and 18 patients (42.9%) had perennial aeroallergen sensitization. These patients have experienced a great improvement in quality of life since starting treatment with dupilumab.

In our study, none of the 42 patients required rescue surgery, as also reported in other real-life studies, e.g. Jansen et al. [40].

The main objective of this study was to assess whether previous exposure to biological treatment affected the clinical outcome of patients with CRSwNP treated with dupilumab over time, i.e. the ‘overall change over time’. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the first studies to compare the outcome of variables over time between CRSwNP and concomitant asthma patients who have previously received other biological treatments and those who initially received only dupilumab.

No differences were observed in terms of general characteristics at baseline between patients with CRSwNP who had received previous biologic treatment and those who had not. At all follow-ups, no significant differences in subjective and objective measures emerged between the groups, indicating that both groups improved significantly on dupilumab regardless of whether they had previously received biologic therapy or not. In both groups, the VAS overall symptom score, NPS and SNOT-22 outcome levels decreased steadily at 4 and 6 months, but these parameters remained stable or increased after 12 months.

There is a high rate of comorbidities among patients with CRSwNP, including asthma, allergies, and AERD [5]. Patients with CRSwNP and comorbid asthma have significantly greater sinus inflammation and are more likely to undergo sinonasal surgery [4, 41]. A switch to dupilumab may be beneficial for asthmatic patients who continue to present a high CRSwNP symptom burden when receiving mAb [42]. Switching between biologics has only been reported to be effective in severe asthma patients to date. The symptoms of CRSwNP are exacerbated in these patients, since both diseases increase the clinical burden by causing more severe nasal obstruction and olfactory loss, poorer lung function, reduced asthma control, and worse quality of life [43–45]. Concomitant asthma was observed in 95.2% of patients in our study. Twenty-one (50%) were switched from other biologics for uncontrolled asthma and severe CRSwNP after they refused revision surgery or were deemed not suitable for it. The available data, although limited, suggest that switching can be performed without a washout period [43], and in our study, no washout period was conducted. The decision to continue a biologic or switch to an alternative product should be made in consultation with the patient, in a multidisciplinary committee with the participation of pneumology, allergy, and otorhinolaryngology experts.

Dupilumab treatment is associated with hypereosinophilia, defined as BEC ≥ 1.5 × 109 cells/l, in approximately 4%–25% of patients [46]. Taking OCS or discontinuing dupilumab is rarely needed for the treatment of hypereosinophilia caused by dupilumab [47]. De Corso, et al. [48] in their real-life single-center study found that nearly half of the patients treated with dupilumab for severe uncontrolled CRSwNP experienced transient increases in BEC and then returned to normal or slightly reduced values after 1 year. In our study, BEC significantly increased between the initial period and the subsequent 4 and 6 months. A statistically significant reduction occurred between the 6th month and the 12th month, but no statistically significant differences were found between the initial period and after 12 months of dupilumab treatment. No patient had hypereosinophilia BEC ≥ 1.5 × 109cells/l during the observation period.

In previous studies, nasopharyngitis, nasal polyps (worsening nasal polyps, need for surgery or OCS, or both), headache, asthma (worsening asthma), epistaxis, and injection site erythema were the most reported adverse effects [15]. We found dupilumab to have a similar safety profile. There was a mild case of facial xeroderma in one patient, but it improved with local treatment after a few weeks, and dupilumab therapy was not discontinued. Long-term follow-up is essential to determine the impact and frequency of these adverse effects.

Comparing dupilumab to other biologics currently approved by the FDA for CRSwNP, such as omalizumab and mepolizumab, we find improved endoscopic, clinical, and HRQoL outcomes in patients with CRSwNP with or without comorbidities. In the end, the choice depends on the preferences of the patient and the provider, and on the health system coverage.

Several potential limitations must be considered when interpreting the results of this study. We report the results of our first single-center patient series, which included patients with the most severe and difficult-to-treat CRSwNP. Although dupilumab is approved for the treatment of CRSwNP in Spain, this indication was not funded during the period of the study, so except for 2 patients, our patients received this biologic therapy when asthma comorbidity was present. There was no placebo control group, and all our patients were treated with dupilumab. Another aspect to consider is the relatively small sample size in the comparison groups. Furthermore, 23 patients have not completed 12 months of treatment follow-up, which may introduce a potential attrition bias. Our results should be interpreted with these considerations in mind.

Conclusions

Our preliminary data confirmed the effectiveness of dupilumab 300 mg administered subcutaneously every 2 weeks as add-on therapy to INCS in patients with severe uncontrolled CRSwNP in a real-life study. Improvements were observed in symptom severity, polyp size reduction, improvement of HRQoL, and reduction in the need for systemic corticoids and revision surgery as rescue treatment, regardless of the presence or absence of a comorbid atopic profile. Both biologic-naïve (Group A) and biologic-pretreated (Group B) patients with CRSwNP significantly improved on dupilumab regardless of their previous treatment. According to our results, dupilumab therapy may be a well-tolerated therapeutic option for uncontrolled CRSwNP.

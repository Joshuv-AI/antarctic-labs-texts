Non-canonical ceRNA-independent mechanism of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis targeting KLF4 in Hcy-triggered VSMCs dysfunction and atherogenesis
Authors: Xinpeng Ma, Xiang Zhang, Yi Yan, Tingrun Mo, Xing Ma, Xiuyu Wang, Minghao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) act as critical post-transcriptional regulators in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis (As). However, the non-canonical regulatory mechanisms of lncRNAs and miRNAs, independent of the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) pathway, especially in homocysteine (Hcy)-induced vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) dysfunction—a key event in As progression—remain largely unclear. In the present study, high-throughput small RNA sequencing, combined with bioinformatics analysis, identified miR-212-5p as a pivotal mediator of Hcy-induced VSMCs hyperproliferation, increased migration, and decreased apoptosis. Both in vitro and in vivo experiments consistently demonstrated that Hcy significantly upregulated the expression of lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p in VSMCs and aortic media of hyperhomocysteinemic (HHcy) ApoE⁻/⁻ mice. Mechanistically, combined with gain- and loss-of-function assays, dual-luciferase reporter assays and molecular interaction experiments, our results suggest that lncRNA-H19 can directly interact with miR-212-5p to form a regulatory-associated molecular cascade, which jointly downregulates Krüppel-like factor 4 (KLF4) expression. Notably, this interaction mode may differ from the canonical ceRNA sponge regulatory mechanism and may represent a potential non-canonical interaction pattern. Further functional experiments indicate that homocysteine modulates the expression of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis to reduce KLF4 levels, and is involved in mediating homocysteine-triggered vascular smooth muscle cell dysfunction. In summary, the present study preliminarily suggests that a potential regulatory association that may not depend on the classical ceRNA mechanism may exist between the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis and KLF4 in homocysteine-related atherosclerosis. We speculate that these two molecules may serve as potential candidate biomarkers and research-oriented intervention targets for hyperhomocysteinemia-associated atherosclerosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-60161-1.

Introduction

Atherosclerosis (As) is a chronic inflammatory vascular disorder that serves as the primary pathological basis of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases worldwide1,2, with hyperhomocysteinemia (HHcy) recognized as an independent and modifiable risk factor for its onset and progression3,4, Vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) dysfunction, characterized by abnormal proliferation, enhanced migratory capacity and impaired apoptotic response, is a central cellular event driving plaque formation and progression in homocysteine (Hcy)-induced As5,6. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that microRNAs (miRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) mediate Hcy-induced VSMCs phenotypic remodeling7,8. However, the non-canonical regulatory mechanisms between lncRNAs and miRNAs —independent of the classical competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) pathway—in this process remain largely unexplored, which limits the identification of specific molecular targets for HHcy-associated As. This knowledge gap prompted us to explore novel lncRNA-miRNA regulatory modes in Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction and As progression.

MiRNAs are evolutionarily conserved small non-coding RNAs that modulate gene expression post-transcriptionally by targeting the 3′-untranslated region (3′UTR) of messenger RNAs, and their dysregulation is closely linked to VSMCs dysfunction in As9,10. Our previous study confirmed that miR-145 alleviates Hcy-induced VSMCs proliferation and migration by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway7, establishing miRNAs as core mediators of Hcy-induced vascular injury. In the present study, high-throughput sequencing identified miR-212-5p as a significantly upregulated miRNA in Hcy-treated VSMCs, and preliminary functional assays suggested its pro-atherogenic role in promoting VSMCs hyperproliferation and migration. Bioinformatics analysis further predicted Krüppel-like factor 4 (KLF4) as a potential target of miR-212-5p, a well-characterized protective factor in VSMCs phenotypic modulation and atherosclerosis pathogenesis11,12. However, the regulatory mechanism of miR-212-5p on KLF4 expression, as well as its upstream lncRNA regulators, in Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction, remains unclear, which is a key focus of the present study.

LncRNAs regulate cardiovascular disease progression primarily through the classical competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) sponge mechanism, in which lncRNAs sequester miRNAs to de-repress their target mRNAs8,13. LncRNA-H19, an imprinted lncRNA, has been implicated in atherosclerosis by regulating VSMCs proliferation and apoptosis14,15, and our preliminary data revealed its upregulation in Hcy-treated VSMCs. By prediction analysis using the Encyclopedia of RNA Interactomes (ENCORI) database, we suggest there exists a physical interaction between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p. This interaction fails to fit the canonical ceRNA sponge model, in which lncRNAs bind and sequester miRNAs to relieve the inhibitory effect on downstream target mRNAs. This finding suggests that a potentially non-canonical interaction pattern may exist between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p during homocysteine-induced VSMCs dysfunction.

In this study, we aimed to elucidate the regulatory relationship between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p, and their combined effect on KLF4 expression in Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction. Using an in vivo HHcy model in ApoE-/- mice and an in vitro Hcy-treated VSMCs model, combined with gain- and loss-of-function experiments and dual-luciferase reporter assays, we preliminarily explored the potential non-canonical synergistic interaction between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p, and preliminarily analyzed the role of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p/KLF4 axis in regulating homocysteine-induced VSMCs dysfunction. This study provides preliminary clues for the existence of a potential, possibly ceRNA-independent regulatory mode in HHcy-related atherosclerosis, and offers candidate biomarkers and research-oriented therapeutic clues for this disease.

Materials and methods

Animal treatment

Six-week-old male ApoE-/- mice (25–28 g) were purchased from Beijing Weishang Rituo Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and randomly divided into two groups (n = 6 per group): a normal diet group (ApoE-/-+ NC) and a 1.7% methionine diet group (ApoE-/- + HMD) for hyperhomocysteinemia modeling. After 15 weeks of feeding, all mice were fasted overnight, anesthetized with intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg body weight), and orbital sinus blood was collected for serum Hcy detection. Mouse aortas (ascending and thoracic segments) were rapidly isolated, frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately, and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent molecular experiments. Mice were euthanized with 5% isoflurane at the end of the experiment16,17. All animal experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Ningxia Medical University (Ethical Approval No.2024-N184) and strictly complied with the ARRIVE guidelines and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Serum Hcy detection

Orbital sinus blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to separate serum. Serum Hcy concentrations were quantitatively detected using an automatic biochemical analyzer (SIEMENS, Munich, Germany) following the manufacturer’s instructions16.

Cell culture and treatment

Human Vascular smooth muscle cells (HVSMCs) were purchased from BeNa Culture Collection (BNCC, Suzhou, China) and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) supplemented with 7% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂16. VSMCs at passages 3–7 were used for all experiments and divided into different groups for 48 h of treatment: Control (Hcy-free), Hcy (100 µmol/L), Hcy + pcDNA-H19 (lncRNA-H19 overexpression), Hcy + GFP (empty vector control), Hcy + si-H19 (lncRNA-H19 knockdown), Hcy + si-NC (siRNA negative control), Hcy + miR-212-5p mimic (miR-212-5p overexpression), Hcy + mimic NC (miRNA mimic negative control), Hcy + miR-212-5p inhibitor (miR-212-5p knockdown), Hcy + inhibitor NC (miRNA inhibitor negative control), Hcy + APTO-253 (AP)(5 µmol/L, KLF4 agonist, MCE, HY-16291), and Hcy + Kenpaullone (Ken) (2µmol/L, KLF4 inhibitor, MCE, HY-12302)16–18.

High-throughput sequencing analysis

Total RNA was extracted from Control and Hcy-treated VSMCs using a miRNA rapid extraction kit combined with TRIzol reagent (Aidelie Biotechnology, Beijing, China). RNA concentration and integrity were verified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer and agarose gel electrophoresis, respectively. Small RNA-seq libraries were constructed and high-throughput sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) with 3 biological replicates for each group19. Raw sequencing data were converted to FASTQ format, which contained sequence information and corresponding quality scores.

Bioinformatics analysis

Differentially expressed miRNAs between Control and Hcy-treated VSMCs were screened using the edgeR package with the criteria of |log₂(fold change, FC)| ≥ 0.58 and P ≤ 0.05. Target genes of upregulated miRNAs were predicted via TargetScanHuman (https://www.targetscan.org), and the miRNA-target gene regulatory network was constructed using Cytoscape software (v3.9.0). For miR-212-5p, protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks of its target genes were built on the STRING database (confidence score ≥ 0.4) and visualized with Cytoscape, with node centrality analysis performed using the CytoNCA plugin and subnetwork mining using the MCODE plugin. Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of miR-212-5p target genes were performed using the R package clusterProfiler (v4.18.1) (www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html)20–22. The potential interaction between miR-212-5p and lncRNAs was predicted in the ENCORI database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php)23.

Cell transfection

LncRNA-H19 overexpression plasmid (pcDNA-H19), empty vector (GFP), lncRNA-H19 small interfering RNA (si-H19), and siRNA negative control (si-NC) were synthesized by Genepharma (Shanghai, China). MiR-212-5p mimic, miR-212-5p inhibitor, and their corresponding negative controls were synthesized by GENE (Shanghai, China). VSMCs were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well and transfected at 90–95% confluence using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. The transfection mixture (100 µL per well) was added to the cells, and the culture medium was replaced with fresh complete DMEM after 6 h of incubation. Cells were cultured for an additional 24 h, and transfection efficiency was verified by reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) and Western blot analysis before subsequent functional experiments24,25.

Cell proliferation assays

CCK-8 assay

VSMCs were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 10³–1 × 10⁴ cells per 100 µL and treated as aforementioned for 48 h. After treatment, 10 µL of Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was added to each well, and cells were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The optical density (OD) value at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). The cell proliferation rate was calculated using the following formula: [(OD₄₅₀ of treated group − OD₄₅₀ of blank group)/(OD₄₅₀ of 0 h group − OD₄₅₀ of blank group)] × 100%26.

EdU assay

VSMCs in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded in laser confocal dishes at a density of 5 × 10³ cells per well and treated for 48 h. After treatment, cells were incubated with 50 µM 5-Ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EdU) reagent (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) for 2 h at 37 °C. Cells were then fixed with 4% cold paraformaldehyde for 30 min, neutralized with glycine for 5 min, and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min. Apollo staining solution (1×) was added for 30 min to stain EdU-positive cells, and cell nuclei were stained with Hoechst 33,342 in the dark for 30 min. The number of EdU-positive (proliferating) cells was counted under a fluorescence microscope (Leica Microsystems GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). The cell proliferation rate was calculated as the ratio of EdU-positive cells to the total number of Hoechst-positive cells27.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell wound scratch assay

VSMCs were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured to 90–95% confluence. A 200 µL pipette tip was used to make uniform linear scratches on the cell monolayer along pre-drawn horizontal lines. The culture medium was aspirated to remove floating cells, and fresh medium containing 0 or 100 µmol/L Hcy was added. Cell migration was observed under a light microscope at 0 h and 48 h after scratching, and representative images were captured. The scratch area was measured using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA), and the cell migration rate was calculated using the formula: [(scratch area at 0 h − remaining scratch area at 48 h)/scratch area at 0 h] × 100%28.

Transwell assay

Matrigel gel (Corning, NY, USA) was diluted 1:8 with serum-free DMEM on ice, and 60 µL of the diluted gel was added to the upper chamber of Transwell inserts (8 μm pore size) and polymerized at 37 °C for 3 h. The upper chamber was hydrated with 100 µL of serum-free DMEM for 30 min. VSMCs were trypsinized, resuspended to a density of 3 × 10⁵ cells/mL in serum-free DMEM, and 100 µL of the cell suspension was added to the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with 600 µL of complete DMEM containing 5% FBS and corresponding treatments. After 48 h of incubation at 37 °C, cells on the upper surface of the membrane were gently wiped off with a cotton swab. Cells that migrated to the lower surface of the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 25 min and stained with 0.2% crystal violet for 20 min. Stained cells were counted under a light microscope, and representative images were captured25.

Reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from mouse aortic media and VSMCs using the RNA simple Total RNA Kit (Tiangen Biotech, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and purity of extracted RNA were determined using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. A total of 1 µg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). RT-qPCR was performed on a LightCycler System (Roche Applied Science, Basel, Switzerland) using a SYBR Green PCR Kit (DBI Bioscience, Shanghai, China). The specific primer sequences for target genes (miR-206, miR-39, miR-212-5p, KLF4, lncRNA-H19, PCNA, p27, Bax, Bcl-2) are listed in Table 1. The PCR amplification program was as follows: 37 °C for 30 s; 95 °C for 15 min; 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 55 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 30 s. GAPDH was used as the internal reference gene for mRNA and lncRNA normalization. For mature miRNA detection, the stem-loop reverse transcription method was adopted, the specific primers for miR-212-5p RT and qPCR were purchased from Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd., and U6 snRNA was used as the internal reference gene, The relative expression level of target genes was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method29. All RT-qPCR reactions were performed in triplicate30.

Western blot analysis

Total proteins were extracted from mouse aortic media and VSMCs using a Whole Protein Extraction Kit (KeyGen Biotech, Nanjing, China) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail. Protein concentration was determined using a SimpliNano™ Biochrom Spectrophotometer (Biochrom, Cambridge, UK). Equal amounts of protein (20 µg per sample) were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (MilliporeSigma, Burlington, MA, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 (TBST, 0.1% Tween-20) for 1 h at room temperature, then incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight: anti-KLF4 (1:1000, ABclonal, A13673), anti-PCNA (1:1000, ABclonal, A12427), anti-p27 (1:1000, ABclonal, A19095), anti-Bax (1:800, ABclonal, A19683), anti-Bcl-2 (1:1000, ABclonal, A19693), and anti-β-actin (1:1000, Affinity, af7018). After washing three times with TBST, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (anti-rabbit ZB2301/anti-mouse ZB2305, 1:5000, ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) for 4 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (KeyGen Biotech, Nanjing, China), and band density was quantified using ImageJ software. β-actin was used as the loading control for normalization31.

Flow cytometry for cell apoptosis detection

VSMCs were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per well and treated for 48 h. After treatment, cells were harvested by trypsinization, washed twice with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and resuspended in 100 µL of 1× binding buffer (prepared by mixing sterile water and concentrated binding buffer at a ratio of 9:1). Then 5 µL of PerCP and 5 µL of PE Annexin V (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) were added to the cell suspension and gently mixed, followed by incubation for 15 min in the dark at room temperature. After incubation, 400 µL of 1× binding buffer was added to the suspension, which was then filtered through a 900-mesh filter membrane. The cell apoptosis rate was detected using a flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA), and data were analyzed with NovoExpress software (ACEA Biosciences, San Diego, CA, USA)32.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type (WT) sequence of KLF4 3′UTR and the partial fragment of lncRNA-H19 containing the predicted miR-212-5p binding site, and their mutant (MUT) versions with the binding site deleted were chemically synthesized and cloned into the pmirGLO firefly luciferase reporter vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to construct KLF4-WT/MUT and H19-WT/MUT plasmids. VSMCs and HEK293T cells (FDCC, Shanghai, China) were co-transfected with miR-212-5p mimic/miR-NC and KLF4-WT/MUT or H19-WT/MUT plasmids using Lipofectamine 2000. After 48 h of transfection, cell lysates were prepared using passive lysis buffer (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) on a microplate reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). Renilla luciferase activity was used to normalize firefly luciferase activity33.

Statistical analyses

All experimental data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from three independent biological replicates. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The Shapiro-Wilk test was first applied to evaluate the normality of data distribution. For normally distributed datasets, unpaired Student’s t-test was used for two-group comparisons, and one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for multiple-group comparisons. For non-normally distributed datasets, the Mann-Whitney U test was used for two-group comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post-hoc test was used for multiple-group comparisons. A significance threshold of P ≤ 0.05 was adopted for all statistical tests.

Results

Homocysteine induces VSMCs dysfunction via promoting proliferation, migration, and suppressing apoptosis

To explore the potentially pro-atherogenic influence of Hcy on VSMCs, we first assessed VSMCs’ proliferation, migration, and apoptosis after 48 h of treatment with 100 µmol/L Hcy. CCK-8 and EdU assays showed that Hcy treatment significantly enhanced the proliferative capacity of VSMCs compared with the Control group (Fig. 1A, B, C). Consistently, the mRNA and protein expression levels of the proliferation marker proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) were significantly upregulated (Fig. 1D, E), while the expression of the anti-proliferation marker p27 was markedly downregulated in Hcy-treated VSMCs (Fig. 1H, I). For in vivo validation, we established a HHcy model in ApoE-/- mice by feeding a 1.7% methionine diet. Serum Hcy concentration was significantly elevated in the ApoE-/- + HMD group (≥ 20 µmol/L), confirming the successful construction of the HHcy model (Fig. 1F)34. In line with in vitro results, PCNA expression was upregulated, and p27 expression was downregulated in the aortic media of ApoE-/- + HMD mice (Fig. 1G, J).

Wound scratch and Transwell assays demonstrated that Hcy treatment markedly increased the migratory capacity of VSMCs compared with the Control group (Fig. 1K, L, M, N). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that Hcy treatment significantly reduced the apoptosis rate of VSMCs (Fig. 2A, B), which was accompanied by the upregulation of anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 (Fig. 2C, D) and downregulation of pro-apoptotic Bax at both the mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 2F, G). In vivo experiments showed that aortic media of ApoE-/- + HMD mice also exhibited elevated Bcl-2 protein expression and reduced Bax protein expression (Fig. 2E, H). Collectively, these in vitro and in vivo data suggest that Hcy induces VSMCs dysfunction, characterized by abnormal proliferation, enhanced migration and suppressed apoptosis—key cellular events associated with atherosclerotic progression.

MiR-212-5p is identified as a key differentially expressed miRNA mediating Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction

To explore the post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism underlying Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction, we performed high-throughput small RNA sequencing on Control and Hcy-treated VSMCs. A total of 88 differentially expressed miRNAs were screened out with the criteria of |log₂FC| ≥ 0.58 and P ≤ 0.05, including 20 upregulated and 68 downregulated miRNAs (Fig. 3A, Table S1). Cluster analysis was performed on expression patterns of differentially expressed miRNAs in each group to generate the cluster heat map, which intuitively reveals the differences in miRNA expression characteristics and trends among different groups(Fig. 3B), among which novel-miR-39, hsa-miR-206, and miR-212-5p were significantly upregulated (Fig. 3C). RT-qPCR validation confirmed that only miR-212-5p was consistently and significantly upregulated in both Hcy-treated VSMCs (Fig. 3D) and the aortic media of ApoE-/- + HMD mice (Fig. 3E), while the expression levels of miR-39 and miR-206 showed no significant changes. These results identify miR-212-5p as a pivotal miRNA mediating Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction.

MiR-212-5p directly targets KLF4 and may interact with lncRNA-H19 in a manner that may not depend on the ceRNA mechanism

To elucidate the molecular mechanism of miR-212-5p in Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction, we performed bioinformatics analysis of its target genes and potential regulatory partners. Combining PPI network construction, node centrality analysis, and literature mining, KLF4 was identified as a key miR-212-5p target gene involved in VSMCs proliferation and apoptosis35. (Fig. 4A, B, Table S2). GO and KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the target genes of miR-212-5p were significantly enriched in cellular biological processes regulating proliferation, apoptosis, and migration (Fig. 4C, D), further supporting the critical role of miR-212-5p in VSMCs dysfunction. ENCORI database prediction revealed a potential physical interaction between miR-212-5p and lncRNA-H19 (Fig. 4E, Table S3).

Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed the direct binding of miR-212-5p to the 3′UTR of KLF4 and the predicted miR-212-5p binding region within lncRNA-H19 (Fig. 4F, I): miR-212-5p mimic significantly inhibited the luciferase activity of H19-WT and KLF4-WT plasmids in both VSMCs and HEK293T cells, while no significant change in luciferase activity was observed in cells transfected with MUT plasmids (Fig. 4G, H, J, K). In vitro and in vivo expression validation showed that lncRNA-H19 expression was significantly upregulated in Hcy-treated VSMCs and the aortic media of ApoE-/- + HMD mice (Fig. 4L, M), while KLF4 mRNA and protein expression levels were markedly downregulated (Fig. 4N, O, P). Notably, the concurrent upregulation of lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p accompanied by the downregulation of KLF4 may suggest a non-canonical ceRNA regulatory mode (lncRNA-H19 does not relieve the inhibition of KLF4), which is a key novel finding of this study.

LncRNA-H19 may upregulate miR-212-5p and repress KLF4 to promote Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction

To verify the regulatory relationship between lncRNA-H19, miR-212-5p, and KLF4, we performed gain- and loss-of-function experiments on lncRNA-H19 in Hcy-treated VSMCs. RT-qPCR results confirmed the successful overexpression of lncRNA-H19 in the Hcy + pcDNA-H19 group and efficient knockdown in the Hcy + si-H19 group (Fig. 5A). LncRNA-H19 overexpression significantly upregulated miR-212-5p expression and downregulated KLF4 mRNA and protein expression in Hcy-treated VSMCs; conversely, lncRNA-H19 knockdown significantly downregulated miR-212-5p expression and upregulated KLF4 expression (Fig. 5B, C, D).

We next validated the role of miR-212-5p in the lncRNA-H19/KLF4 regulatory axis in Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction. These results suggest that changes in the expression of lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p are associated with each other in Hcy-treated VSMCs, and that miR-212-5p directly represses KLF4 expression.

KLF4 is a key downstream effector of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis in regulating VSMCs’ function

To confirm that KLF4 is the functional downstream effector of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis, we treated Hcy-induced VSMCs with the KLF4 agonist AP and KLF4 inhibitor Ken. RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis confirmed that AP treatment significantly upregulated KLF4 mRNA and protein expression, while Ken treatment downregulated KLF4 expression in Hcy-treated VSMCs (Fig. 9A, B). Functional assays showed that KLF4 activation by AP significantly inhibited Hcy-induced VSMCs proliferation (Fig. 9C, D, E) and migration (Fig. 9J, K, L, M), which was accompanied by the downregulation of PCNA and upregulation of p27 (Fig. 9F, G, H, I). Conversely, KLF4 inhibition by Ken further enhanced Hcy-induced VSMCs proliferation and migration, with the opposite expression patterns of PCNA and p27 (Fig. 9).

Integrated analysis of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p/KLF4 regulatory axis

In summary, our in vitro and in vivo experimental data preliminarily suggest that non-canonical regulation may exist in homocysteine-related atherosclerosis: Hcy upregulates the expression of lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p. A correlation may exist between their expression changes, jointly repressing KLF4 expression. Downregulation of KLF4 further induces VSMCs hyperproliferation, enhanced migration, and suppressed apoptosis, further driving potential pro-atherogenic cellular changes associated with VSMCs dysfunction (Fig. 11). Based on current data, the present study suggests that this regulatory axis may differ from the classical ceRNA sponge mechanism, offering a potential new perspective on the current understanding of lncRNA-miRNA crosstalk in vascular biology.

Discussion

Atherosclerosis (As) is a chronic inflammatory vascular disorder that constitutes the pathological foundation of most cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases36,37, and vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) dysfunction is a central cellular event driving plaque formation and progression1,38–40. Homocysteine (Hcy), a well-recognized independent risk factor for atherosclerosis3,34,41, induces VSMCs hyperproliferation, enhanced migration and suppressed apoptosis, but the underlying post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms remain incompletely elucidated—especially the non-canonical interactions between lncRNAs and miRNAs that go beyond the classical competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) sponge model. In the present study, we preliminarily found the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p/KLF4 molecular cascade that may be involved in regulating homocysteine-induced VSMCs dysfunction. We preliminarily observed that a potential synergistic interaction, which may differ from the classical ceRNA mechanism, may exist between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p during the pathological process of HHcy-related atherosclerosis.

MiRNAs are key post-transcriptional regulators that modulate gene expression by targeting the 3′UTR of mRNAs, and their dysregulation is closely linked to VSMCs’ phenotypic remodeling in atherosclerosis6,7,42,43. Our previous study confirmed that miR-145 alleviates Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway7, which established miRNAs as core mediators of Hcy-induced vascular injury. In the present study, high-throughput sequencing and functional validation identified miR-212-5p as a key miRNA in Hcy-treated VSMCs associated with potential pro-atherogenic cellular changes. The role of KLF4 in VSMCs phenotypic regulation may depend on the cellular state, microenvironment, and the specific disease context. On one hand, KLF4 represses contractile marker gene expression, promoting VSMCs switching to a synthetic phenotype44,45. On the other hand, KLF4 acts as a protective factor in VSMCs function and atherosclerosis pathogenesis, inhibiting VSMCs proliferation and migration11,12,46. However, under pathological stimulation such as Hcy, KLF4 expression is downregulated, and this protective effect is diminished, thereby promoting VSMCs dysfunction18,47. Several studies have shown that miRNAs can inhibit VSMCs proliferation and migration by targeting KLF4, such as miR-148b and miR-92a48,49, while other miRNAs, such as miR-506-3p, can promote VSMCs proliferation and migration by targeting KLF450. Notably, the disease models used in these studies differ from that of the present study (HHcy-induced), suggesting that KLF4 may exert different or even opposite effects depending on the pathological microenvironment. Our findings extend this knowledge by demonstrating that Hcy-induced miR-212-5p upregulation is a key molecular mechanism underlying KLF4 repression in VSMCs.

LncRNAs regulate the progression of cardiovascular diseases primarily through the classical ceRNA sponge mechanism, in which lncRNAs sequester miRNAs to de-repress their target mRNAs8,51. LncRNA-H19, an imprinted lncRNA, has been implicated in atherosclerosis by regulating VSMCs proliferation and apoptosis52,53, but its regulatory mode in Hcy-induced vascular injury has not been reported previously. Our study found that Hcy significantly upregulates lncRNA-H19 expression in VSMCs and aortic media of ApoE-/- mice, and gain- and loss-of-function experiments revealed that lncRNA-H19 promotes Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction by upregulating miR-212-5p and repressing KLF4 expression. Notably, dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed a physical interaction between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p, but this interaction did not follow the classical ceRNA model—i.e., lncRNA-H19 did not sequester miR-212-5p to increase KLF4 expression; instead, lncRNA-H19 synergized with miR-212-5p to further repress KLF4 expression. This interaction pattern, which may differ from the classical ceRNA mechanism, reflects the exploratory nature of the present study. It preliminarily enriches existing knowledge of lncRNA-miRNA regulatory patterns in vascular biology, and preliminarily suggests a potential functional link between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p, which differs from previously reported ceRNA networks in atherosclerosis research.

The synergistic repression of KLF4 by lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p is the core molecular mechanism underlying Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction in our study. KLF4 mediates the transcriptional activation of multiple anti-atherosclerotic genes, including those that regulate VSMCs quiescence and apoptotic response54,55. Our functional validation suggested that KLF4 activation reverses Hcy-induced VSMCs hyperproliferation and migration, while KLF4 inhibition exacerbates these potential pro-atherogenic cellular changes associated with VSMCs dysfunction. These findings suggest that KLF4 may be a critical downstream effector of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p axis, and may link this regulatory axis to the well-characterized protective role of KLF4 in vascular biology. Based on current data, we propose a molecular regulatory model: Hcy upregulates lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p expression; lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p may synergistically repress KLF4 expression; KLF4 downregulation further induces VSMCs hyperproliferation, enhanced migration, and suppressed apoptosis—cellular changes that are potential pro-atherogenic cellular changes associated with VSMCs dysfunction.

This study has several notable strengths that enhance its scientific validity: we used both in vitro (Hcy-treated VSMCs) and in vivo (ApoE-/- mice with hyperhomocysteinemia) models to validate our key findings, combined gain- and loss-of-function experiments with gold-standard dual-luciferase reporter assays to confirm direct molecular interactions, and preliminarily suggest a molecular regulatory pattern that may differ from the classical ceRNA mechanism, which offers certain referential clues for further research on Hcy-related atherosclerosis. However, our study also has certain limitations that warrant further investigation in future research. First, the exact molecular basis of the synergistic interaction between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p (e.g., whether lncRNA-H19 modulates miR-212-5p biogenesis or stability, or forms a ribonucleoprotein complex with miR-212-5p to enhance its targeting of KLF4) remains to be elucidated. Second, the present study focused on VSMCs, and the role of the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p/KLF4 axis in other cell types involved in atherosclerosis (e.g., endothelial cells, macrophages) requires further study. Third, the therapeutic potential of targeting this regulatory axis (e.g., via lncRNA-H19 silencing or miR-212-5p inhibition) needs to be validated in more complex animal models (e.g., mice with advanced atherosclerosis or comorbidities such as hypertension or diabetes).

Atherosclerosis is the primary pathological basis of cardiovascular diseases worldwide, and vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) dysfunction is a central cellular event driving plaque initiation and progression. Homocysteine, an independent and modifiable risk factor for atherosclerosis, induces VSMCs hyperproliferation, enhanced migration and suppressed apoptosis. Still, the non-canonical regulatory mechanisms between long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) in this process remain largely unexplored. This critical gap in vascular biology is the key scientific question our study addresses, and our findings provide novel molecular insights for targeted therapy of hyperhomocysteinemia-associated atherosclerosis. The present study preliminarily proposes that the lncRNA-H19/miR-212-5p/KLF4 axis may be involved in regulating Hcy-induced VSMCs dysfunction, and we also preliminarily observed a potential synergistic interaction between lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p that may differ from the classical ceRNA mechanism in vascular biology. These findings preliminarily enrich existing knowledge of post-transcriptional regulation in Hcy-related atherosclerosis, and regard lncRNA-H19 and miR-212-5p as potential diagnostic biomarkers and research-oriented therapeutic clues for HHcy-related cardiovascular diseases.

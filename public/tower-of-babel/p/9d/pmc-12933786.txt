Introduction

Healthcare systems are currently experiencing heightened pressures due to increasing multimorbidity, the complexity of therapies and persistent workforce shortages, all of which are diminishing inpatient capacity.1 2 Clinically stable patients admitted primarily for monitoring, diagnostics or parenteral therapy continue to occupy hospital beds and use staff resources that could be reallocated if equivalent care could be safely administered at home.

In response, the Dutch healthcare system introduced the 2023 Dutch Integrated Care Agreement (Integraal Zorgakkoord), which emphasises hybrid care. By the end of 2026, the national ambition is that, across the healthcare system as a whole, an average of 70% of care is delivered in a hybrid format, with 50% of patients receiving hybrid care.3 4 This strategy aims to redesign the patient journey by reserving hospital admission for patients who require direct medical supervision, while delivering other aspects of care at home to optimise inpatient ward capacity.

This transition can be operationalised through a Virtual Ward (VW), a model that provides hospital-level care in the home setting while patients remain under the responsibility of their hospital team. Ward-based observation is substituted with remote, patient-performed vital sign monitoring and structured symptom reviews. Diagnostics and treatments typically administered at the bedside are provided through coordinated home services delivered to hospital standards.

Evidence indicates that VWs may reduce the length of stay without increasing readmissions or mortality and may enhance patient experience in selected populations.5 This effect is especially pronounced in specific patient populations, including individuals with heart failure and those with COVID-19.69 However, the feasibility and key operational determinants within the Dutch healthcare system remain unclear. Decision-makers require information on which inpatients are eligible and willing to transfer to a VW, the speed at which transfers can be accomplished, and the workload generated by the model. This includes expected alert frequency, verification workload for tele-nurses, the volume and type of contacts, and the experiences of patients and professionals with these hybrid pathways. These factors will determine whether a VW can deliver care more efficiently while maintaining safety and quality.

This protocol outlines the VW for Early Discharge in Patients Receiving Inpatient Care (VIP Care) study, a prospective feasibility cohort evaluating a multispecialty VW supported by a digital healthcare platform. The platform includes an electronic health record (EHR)-integrated clinician cockpit and a patient application for telemonitoring supported by questionnaires and informational content related to the VW. The study focuses on seven predefined subcohorts that commonly require inpatient admission but may be suitable for early discharge with hospital-at-home support. The primary objective is to assess feasibility as adherence to transfer, defined as the proportion of eligible inpatients who provide written informed consent and are successfully transferred to the VW, with monitoring activated and first data received. Secondary objectives are to quantify reach during screening weeks by reporting: (1) the proportion of all admitted patients who meet eligibility criteria, (2) who are invited to participate and (3) who provide written informed consent. During the VW treatment, we will characterise operational performance, length of stay on the ward and in the VW and readmissions. Additionally, determinants of implementation are collected through interviews for further development and scale-up.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

VIP Care is a prospective, single-centre feasibility cohort study conducted at Erasmus University Medical Center (Erasmus MC), Rotterdam, the Netherlands. The recruitment process commenced in May 2025 and is scheduled to span 36 months, with a potential 12-month extension should recruitment progress slower than anticipated. The study encompasses several departments, including Internal Medicine, Cardiology, Haematology, Pulmonology, Gastroenterology, and Dermatology.

Participants and eligibility

The study targets adult participants (18 years and older) admitted to Erasmus MC for one of seven predefined clinical subcohorts: (1) bacterial, fungal or parasitic infections; (2) viral respiratory infections; (3) dehydration; (4) decompensated heart failure; (5) high-dose corticosteroid treatment for transplant rejection or an autoimmune or inflammatory flare; (6) post-transsphenoidal pituitary surgery follow-up and (7) severe inflammatory skin disease with or without bacterial or viral superinfection. Participants must necessitate hospital-level monitoring and/or therapy and demonstrate proficiency in using the telemonitoring application. They are required to reside with an adult caregiver and live within a 45-minute travel distance to the hospital. Exclusion criteria include a Clinical Frailty Scale score greater than 6, end-of-life care, unstable vital signs within 24 hours prior to planned discharge and an anticipated VW stay of less than 24 hours. The Virtual Ward intervention

Monitoring and digital platform

The VW provides hospital-at-home care through remote physiological monitoring and structured symptom assessment. Patients use CE-marked devices to measure vital signs, including heart rate and blood pressure (iHealth Track KN-550BT), oxygen saturation (iHealth Smart Pulse Oximeter), temperature (Braun ThermoScan) and weight (Omron HBF-222T). Respiratory rate is self-assessed through a 1 min count. Data on vital signs and symptom questionnaires are submitted via the Digizorg patient application (figure 1).10 Secure, encrypted data transfer is facilitated through authentication with the Dutch government DigiD.11 Measurements are scheduled at 08:00, 15:00 and 22:00, unless a lower frequency is specified in the care pathway. At each scheduled submission, patients complete a structured digital symptom review consistent with routine ward nursing assessments. Data are accessible in the Digizorg clinician cockpit (figure 2), integrated within the EHR and available to the responsible clinical team and VW tele-nurses.

Diagnostics and treatment at home

Diagnostics and treatment are administered at home through established home-care services. Regional phlebotomy teams conduct home blood sampling, with samples transported to Erasmus MC for analysis and results documented in the EHR. Home treatments may include intravenous antibiotics or antifungals, corticosteroids, diuretics, crystalloids and oxygen supplementation. Technical home-care nursing teams manage infusions, provide peripheral venous access care and conduct medication reconciliation according to protocols. Additional support for activities of daily living may be arranged as necessary.

Medical contact, alerts and escalation

Ward physicians receive training and pathway-specific instructions before enrolling patients VW. Medical review is conducted by telephone daily, including weekends. During these contacts, the physician reviews the submitted vital signs and symptom responses, interprets laboratory results when available and performs a structured clinical assessment by telephone. When no thresholds are exceeded, one scheduled physician contact per day is expected. Additional contacts occur when monitoring thresholds are exceeded.

Each subcohort has predefined thresholds for vital signs and questionnaire responses (online supplemental file 1). VW tele-nurses review all scheduled submissions within 1 hour. Deviations are classified as low-exceeding or high-exceeding, triggering a prespecified verification and escalation process.

Alert logic

The intended alert logic is hierarchical and mutually exclusive: high-exceeding thresholds override low-exceeding thresholds, ensuring that only the highest-severity alert is triggered for any given parameter at any time. These thresholds serve as escalation triggers aligned with the local operationalisation of the Early Warning Score (EWS) used in routine in-hospital care since validated triggers for hybrid care models are currently unavailable.12 Therefore, the low-exceeding thresholds are generally mapped to vital parameters that would yield 1 EWS point, while ≥2 EWS points trigger high-exceeding (immediate escalation). Importantly, these thresholds are not intended as long-term treatment targets for patients at home. Instead, they act as predefined safety triggers to prompt verification, repeat measurement and timely clinical review, as patients would otherwise still be monitored on the ward.

Low-exceeding deviation: the tele-nurse contacts the patient to verify data quality, including rest before measurement, correct device use and completion of symptom items. A repeat measurement is requested after 60 min. If the value normalises, routine monitoring resumes. If the deviation persists, the responsible physician is notified.

If the responsible physician is unavailable, escalation follows a predefined call chain, including a second responsible physician and the supervising medical specialist on duty. Patients and home-care nurses can contact the VW service at any time and may submit ad hoc measurements. These are reviewed and escalated when indicated.

Following assessment, the physician selects one of three actions: (1) continue monitoring without treatment change, (2) adjust treatment at home or (3) arrange urgent hospital reassessment. In life-threatening emergencies, patients are instructed to contact emergency services for immediate assessment. Discharge from the VW is determined by the responsible physician when pathway-specific discharge criteria are met. Follow-up is arranged through routine outpatient care. Screening, recruitment and consent

Treating physicians and senior ward nurses identify potential candidates for transfer to the VW and notify the research team. The study team provides oral and written study information and obtains written informed consent before any study-specific procedures are performed. After eligibility is confirmed and consent is obtained, the clinical team initiates discharge planning and practical arrangements for VW transfer, followed by activation of home monitoring and delivery of treatment as indicated.

To estimate reach, the research team will conduct structured screening during prespecified screening weeks. In the first week of each 2-month period, all new admissions to participating wards will be assessed over a continuous 7-day period using a standardised checklist aligned with the seven subcohorts and core inclusion and exclusion criteria. For each admission during screening weeks, we will record eligibility, whether the patient was invited to participate, and whether written informed consent was provided. For eligible patients during screening weeks, we will also record whether transfer to the VW was achieved, enabling estimation of adherence to transfer during screening weeks.

Outside screening weeks, recruitment proceeds through routine case-finding by the clinical teams and reach denominators are not collected. To assess representativeness, baseline characteristics of admitted patients during screening weeks will be compared with those during non-screening weeks.

Data collection

After consent, baseline demographics and clinical data will be extracted from the EHR. Patients will complete the Clinical Frailty Scale,13 Program of Research to Integrate the Services for the Maintenance of Autonomy (PRISMA)-714 and EuroQol 5-Dimension 3-Level (EQ-5D-3L) questionnaires.15 Devices will be issued and the Digizorg application will be installed. Patients will practise submitting vital signs and symptom questionnaires before discharge to support reliable data capture.

During the VW episode, the dataset will capture time-stamped vital sign submissions and questionnaire completions. It will also capture alerts, including classification, verification steps and repeat measurements. All contacts will be recorded, including patient-initiated, tele-nurse-initiated and physician-initiated contacts, alongside reasons for contact and resulting actions. Length of stay on the ward and in the VW will be recorded. Follow-up at 30 days after VW discharge will capture emergency department visits, hospital readmissions and deaths. Patient experience will be assessed using a satisfaction survey.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

Feasibility will be assessed as adherence to transfer, defined as the proportion of patients who provide written informed consent and are successfully transferred to the VW, with monitoring activated and first data received.

Secondary outcomes

Reach will be quantified during screening weeks and reported as: (1) the proportion of all admitted patients who meet core eligibility criteria (eligibility rate), (2) the proportion of eligible inpatients who are invited to participate (invitation rate) and (3) the proportion of eligible inpatients who provide written informed consent (consent rate), regardless of whether transfer to the VW is ultimately achieved.

Operational outcomes during the VW episode will include alert frequency and handling, the verification workload for tele-nurses, contact volumes and resulting actions. Additional outcomes include length of stay on the ward and in the VW, emergency department reassessments and hospital readmissions during the VW episode and within 30 days after VW discharge, including reasons for readmission. Implementation determinants will be assessed through qualitative interviews with patients and healthcare professionals.

Sample size and feasibility threshold

The feasibility threshold for adherence to transfer is prespecified at 30% (consented and successfully transferred). Given the Dutch policy target of 50% hybrid care across the healthcare system by 2026, and considering that this represents a high-complexity new hybrid care model, a lower target was chosen for this feasibility phase. This target allows for evaluation of the model’s initial viability and optimising towards the 50% target by the conclusion of the implementation. A one-sample, one-sided proportion test indicates that n=51 eligible patients per subcohort provides 90% power at α=0.05 to exclude a true adherence proportion of <30% (H₀: p≤0.30; H₁: p>0.30). Feasibility estimates will be presented with 95% CIs. No multiplicity adjustment is planned in this feasibility phase.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables will be summarised as counts and percentages. Continuous variables will be summarised as mean (SD) or median (IQR), depending on distribution assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Feasibility outcomes and event rates will be reported with 95% CIs. Comparisons between included patients during screening and non-screening periods will use χ² tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Time-to-transfer and length of stay will be summarised as mean (SD) if approximately normally distributed and as median (IQR) if skewed. Analyses will use available cases.

Qualitative evaluation

Semistructured interviews will be conducted with patients and professionals to identify barriers and facilitators for implementation. Patient interviews will be conducted by telephone after completion of the VW episode and will last approximately 30–60 min. A topic guide mapped to the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) will be used.16 Initially, two patients per subcohort will be interviewed, with the option to extend to three per subcohort to increase diversity or depth. Interviews will start after approximately 30%–50% of planned enrolment is achieved within each subcohort.

Professional interviews will include physicians, ward nurses, VW staff, hospital discharge coordination staff and home-care nurses. Interviews will use a CFIR-mapped guide and will include the Normalisation Measure Development questionnaire to assess integration into routine work.17 Interviews will be audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim and analysed deductively by two researchers. Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion.

Data management and statement

Data will be captured prospectively in a secure electronic case report form (eCRF Castor). Each participant will receive a unique study identifier. Personal identifiers will be stored separately from analysis datasets. Data integrity will be supported by range checks, logic checks, validation procedures and audit trails. We will minimise missing data through real-time completeness checks in the eCRF, automated range and logic checks, and verification of key endpoints against the EHR. We will describe the amount and pattern of missingness for each variable and outcome. For the primary outcome, eligibility, invitation, consent and transfer status are recorded prospectively. If transfer status cannot be confirmed, the participant will be classified as not transferred. For secondary outcomes, we will use available-case analyses.

Access to data will be role-based and hosted on institutional infrastructure compliant with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Erasmus MC information-security policy. The final de-identified dataset will be archived for 15 years. Data and code sharing will be considered on reasonable request, subject to appropriate approvals.

Patient and Public Involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting or dissemination plans of our research

Ethics and dissemination

The study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice. Ethical approval was granted by the Erasmus MC Medical Ethics Committee (MEC-2024–0060, March 2025) and the amendment MEC-2024–0060 A0001 (September 2025). The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06936891) https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06936891 and the Dutch CCMO registry (NL85516.078.24) https://onderzoekmetmensen.nl/nl/node/57355/pdf. Participation is voluntary and requires written informed consent.

No data monitoring committee is planned because incremental risk is considered minimal. Potential risks include telemonitoring failure and delayed escalation. These risks are mitigated through CE-marked devices, DigiD authentication, prespecified thresholds and workflows, tele-nurse review within 1 hour, a predefined escalation call chain and the option for urgent hospital reassessment or emergency services. Serious adverse events considered related to study procedures will be reported to the MEC in line with local requirements. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and an accessible lay summary.

Data protection

Personal data will be handled confidentially and in compliance with GDPR. Only authorised team members will have access to identifiable data. Analyses will use de-identified datasets.

Trial status

Protocol V.3.1 (21 September 2025). Recruitment began in May 2025 and is ongoing. As of 1 December 2025, 96 patients had been screened for eligibility. Written informed consent was obtained from all eligible participants. Seven participants were enrolled, of whom six were transferred to the VW. One consented participant could not be transferred due to limited home-care capacity.

During the first 6 months, two cohorts were opened (heart failure and bacterial infections). Recruitment was initially limited to these care pathways. From December 2025 onwards, the remaining care pathways were opened for recruitment following the initial enrolment phase and optimisation of the infrastructure.

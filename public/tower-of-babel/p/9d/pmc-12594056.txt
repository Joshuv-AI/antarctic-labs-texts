“Gaming” inherent in gambling: A narrative review of the reference to “gaming” in the acts regulating gambling across different states and Union Territories of India
Authors: Yatan Pal Singh Balhara, Swarndeep Singh, Anshika Chaudhry, New F. Seth
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Given the overlap between gaming and gambling, and the need to demarcate between the two; it becomes important to understand the use of the term “gaming” in the Acts regulating gambling.Aim:The current narrative review aimed to ascertain various contexts of the use of the term “gaming” across the different Acts regulating gambling in India.Methods:We based our literature search on the online portal of India Code, including all online available publications. We conducted an electronic search in India Code for “gambling,” “gaming,” and “betting” to obtain all published Acts.Results:We were able to identify a total of 26 relevant Acts. The term “gaming” or “game” was used in the title of eight Acts. The term “gaming” was used in the description of all but eight Acts. Also, six of the Acts, while targeted at gambling, did not include the term gambling and rather used the term gaming instead. In addition to the title and the definition/description, the term “gaming” has been used in various other sections of the Acts on gambling including the use of the phrase “common gaming house” and “instruments of gaming.” Most of the Acts use the terms wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming.” Most of the Acts make some reference to the games of skill and/or game of chance.Conclusion:This review not only presents the specific themes of concern, but also highlight the heterogeneity in the existing regulatory framework for gambling across different regions of India. Such heterogeneity is likely to pose additional and unique challenges in context of the online gaming and gambling.

INTRODUCTION

With the introduction of gaming disorder as a diagnosable mental disorder in the most recent version of the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems (ICD-11), interest in addictive disorders reached a crescendo.[1] The emerging findings from the research on addictive behaviors continue to capture the attention and interest of mental health professionals.

Currently, two specific addictive behaviors have been included in the ICD-11. These are the gaming disorder and the gambling disorder. Despite the clear distinction between gaming and gambling in the diagnostic systems, there is a considerable overlap between the two in real world settings. The boundaries between the two can at best be described as blurred. While this overlap is evident in various contexts, one of the key areas of overlap between the two is in context of the regulatory framework.[2]

The regulatory framework for gambling in India dates to 1867, when the Public Gambling Act was introduced. Since gambling is a state subject in the country, multiple Acts aimed at regulating gambling have been enacted across the different states and Union Territories (UTs). Interestingly, the term ‘gaming’ has been widely used widely in the acts, even though they are primarily aimed at regulating gambling.

Given the overlap between gaming and gambling, and the need to demarcate between the two; it becomes important to understand the use of the term “gaming” in the Acts regulating gambling. This exercise will not only help understand the current usage of the term in these Acts, but will also help ascertain if there is a need to review and revise the usage of the term “gaming” in the various Acts regulating gambling in the country. This issue has not yet been adequately addressed in the published literature.

Narrative reviews have been identified as useful educational articles since they condense the available information into a readable format.[3] They offer a broader view of a given theme.[4] The narrative reviews are also of great benefit to both the practitioners and policymakers alike as they help them stay updated.[5]

The present review

There is no published review on the use of the term “gaming” in the context of “gambling” within the existing regulatory framework in India. The current narrative review aimed to ascertain various contexts in which the term “gaming” was used across the different Acts regulating gambling in India. Since there were no previous reviews to guide the formation of hypothesis, this review was designed to be exploratory but expected to identify salient and significant aspects of the use of the term “gaming” within the regulatory framework governing “gambling.”

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Data bases for search

We based our literature search on the online portal of India Code, including all online publications. The online portal of India Code (indiacode.nic.in) is designed and developed by National Informatics Centre and the content is provided by the Ministries and Departments of the Government of India. In addition, we used the Google search engine to find other relevant Acts.

The review of legislation was restricted to the India Code online portal, which is the official government repository of all Central and State Acts across India. Unlike secondary legal databases or private compilations, India Code provides authenticated, updated copies of legislation as published by the Government of India. Limiting the search to this portal ensured that only legally valid and officially notified statutes were considered. This approach also eliminated discrepancies that may arise from outdated or unofficial sources, thereby improving the reliability and transparency of the analysis. In addition, since the academic research databases (such as PubMed, SCOPUS, Web of Science) do not index the legislations these were not suitable for searching the relevant publications.

Electronic search strategy

We conducted an electronic search in India Code using the terms “gambling,” “gaming,” and “betting” to obtain all published Acts with no limits imposed on the start or end dates for the search period.

Publication selection

Screening phase: Titles were scanned for publications in English and Acts related to gambling, gaming, or betting. Two of the authors (YPSB, NFS) independently screened the titles of the relevant result findings for further scrutiny. The full texts of the Acts were accessed and underwent full-text screening. The Acts were included if these were about prevention or regulation of gambling activities.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The Acts that pertained to prevention or regulation of gambling were included. Since the term “gaming” is often used in the context of gambling, the Acts related to gaming were also included if they dealt substantially with the regulation of gambling. The Acts available in English language were included.

The Acts for which full text was not available were excluded. The Acts that were not about gambling, the activities listed were beyond the purview of gambling Acts, excluded activities involving wagering and betting, were later struck down by the judiciary, and related only to horse racing, lotteries, or casinos were excluded.

Data extraction

The following information was extracted: short title of the Acts, status of use of the term “game”/“gaming” in title, use of term “game”/“gaming” in description; use of term “gambling” in title, use of term “gambling” in description, use of wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming,” use of term “gaming” in the text of the Acts, reference being made to “game of skill” or “game of chance,” distinction made between game of skill and game of chance, and the status regarding inclusion of online gaming.

The extracted information was entered into a Microsoft Excel sheet for further descriptive analysis and narrative synthesis of the salient findings.

None of these records were removed and all were sought for retrieval. We were able to retrieve all the records and screened them for eligibility. A total of 236 records were excluded. Use of the term “gaming” in title and description of the act

The term “gaming” or “game” was used in the title of eight Acts.[1521232426273335]

The term “gaming” was used in the description of all but five Acts.[1022323334] The Arunachal Pradesh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2012 did not use the term “gaming” in its description.[32] The Chhattisgarh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2022 used the term “game,” while describing games of skill and games of chance. It did not use the term “gaming” to represent gambling.[34] While The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 used the term “game” in its title, the term “game” or “gaming” has been used in the description of the act only to refer to the object of w ager, wagering contract, totalizator, and pool transaction.[24] It uses the terms “game or sport” in this context. This act aimed to extend the Public Gambling Act of 1867 for the whole of the state of Assam. However, it did not use the term gambling or gaming in any of the sections that were specifically added as part of the act. Instead, it used the term “betting” on multiple instances. The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games act, 2022 describes “online game” as any game, whether or not there are other participants in the game, that is played on an electronic or a digital device and is managed and operated as a software through the internet or any other kind of electronic or other technology for facilitating communication.[35]

Interestingly, four of the Acts did not use the term gambling in their title or the description.[15232627] The Puducherry Gaming Act, 1965 does not refer to term gambling anywhere in the Act.[23] In addition, only twelve Acts include gambling in the description of the Act.[192021242528293233343536] The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games act, 2022 describes online gambling.[35] The Meghalaya Prevention of Gambling Act, 1970 uses the terms gaming and gambling as synonyms.[25]

Also, six of the Acts, while targeted at gambling, did not include the term gambling and rather used the term gaming instead.[152123242627] The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 uses the term betting in the title of the Act.[24]

Instances where the term “gaming” has been used

In addition to the title and the definition or description, the term “gaming” has been used in various other sections of the Acts on gambling.

These include the use of the phrase “common gaming house.” The term has been used in all but four Acts.[24333435] The “common gaming house” typically refers to a place where gaming takes place and/or the instruments of gaming are kept. The common gaming house has been described as “any house, walled enclosure, room or place in which cards, dice, tables or other instruments of gaming are kept or used for the profit or gain of the person owning, occupying, using or keeping such house, enclosure, room or place, whether by way of charge for the use of the instruments of gaming, or of the house, enclosure, room or place, or otherwise howsoever” in the Public Gambling Act, 1867.[10] A ‘common gaming house’ can be a house, room, tent, enclosure, vehicle, vessel or any other place gaming or gambling takes place or where instruments of gaming are kept or used for gaming or gambling therein. Only The Chhattisgarh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2022 uses the term common gambling house.[34] The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games act, 2022 uses the term online gaming service and online gaming server.[35] The Andaman and Nicobar Islands Public Gambling Regulation, 1951 and The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 use the term betting house.[1724]

The term gaming has also been used in the context of “instruments of gaming.” The term has been used in all but six Acts.[102224333435] The “instruments of gaming” typically, includes any article, prepared, procured and used for the purpose of carrying on or facilitating gaming. These may include dice, cards, coins, gaming tables/boards, any boxes, tickets, gaming cloths, tables used for gaming. These may also include the register used to keep the records- of gaming/wining/prize/its distribution/intended to be distributed. Only the Chhattisgarh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2022 uses the term instrument of gambling.[34] The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 uses the term instrument of betting.[24] The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955 mentions instruments of gambling or gaming.[19]

The term gaming has also been used in context of online gaming server. The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games act, 2022 uses the term online gaming server.[35]

Most of the acts also mention the penalty for record of gaming, owning, or keeping, or having charge of, a gaming-house and penalty for being found in gaming-house, the penalty for being found gaming in a gaming house, and power to make arrest. The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 uses the terms records of betting in lieu of records of gaming.[24]

The Chhattisgarh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2022 uses the term “game” to describe what is meant by “game of skill” and “game of chance.” The other reference to the term “gaming” in the Act is to specify that the provisions of the Act are not applicable to “any game played for mere skill.”[34]

3.3 Use of terms wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming”

Some definition or description of the term “gaming” was included in 17 Acts. Most of the Acts used the terms wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming.” Only nine Acts did not use wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming.”[101314172232333435]

The Tripura Gambling Act, 1926 mentions the term “stake.”[14] The Andaman and Nicobar Islands Public Gambling Regulation, 1951 uses wagering to describe betting.[17] The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955 uses wagering in context of both gaming and gambling.[19] The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970 uses the term wager and betting but not to describe gaming.[24] The Arunachal Pradesh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2012, The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games act, 2022 and The Nagaland Prohibition of Gambling and Promotion and Regulation of Online Games of Skill Act, 2015 use the term wagering specifically in context of gambling.[323335]

Distinction between the games of skill and games of chance

Most of the Acts make some reference to the games of skill and game of chance. Only two Acts do not mention the games of skill or game of chance.[1924] The Kerala Gaming Act, 1960 mentions the provision of the Act shall not be applicable in case in any game the element of skill is more predominant than the element of chance.[21]

The Nagaland Prohibition of Gambling and Promotion and Regulation of Online Games of Skill Act, 2015 specifies that “Games of chance” mean all such games where there is a preponderance of chance over skill. Also, it describes that “Games of skill” shall include all such games where there is preponderance of skill over chance, including where the skill relates to strategizing the manner of placing wagers or placing bets or where the skill lies in team selection or selection of virtual stocks based on analyses or where the skill relates to the manner in that the moves are made, whether through deployment of physical or mental skill and acumen. The Games of skill may be card based and action/virtual sports/adventure/mystery and calculation/strategy/quiz based.[33]

The Chhattisgarh Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2022 specifies that the “Game of Skill” means that outcome of the game is predominantly determined by the knowledge, training, expertise, and experience of the participant. The “Game of chance” is described as to mean that outcome of the game is not predominantly determined by the knowledge, training, expertise and experience of the participant but is determined by chance or luck.[34]

The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games Act, 2022 describes the “online game of chance” as any online game that involves both an element of chance and an element of skill and the element of chance dominates over the element of skill; or involves an element of chance that can be eliminated only by superlative skill; or is a game that is presented as involving an element of chance; or involves cards, dice, wheel or such other device, which works on random outcome or event generator.[35]

While most of the Acts specify that the provisions of the respective Act shall not be applicable to the games of skill, The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955, The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970, The Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974 and The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 do not make such exception.[19242627]

The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955 mentions that the State Government may by a general or special order exempt any social club or party playing games for the bona fide purposes of recreation only.[19]

The Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974 and The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 mention that the provisions of the Act are applicable to any act of risking money or playing stakes or otherwise on the result of a game or an event including on a game of skill.

Inclusion of online gaming

Four of the Acts made the mention of “online gaming” in the title and/or description of the Act.[262735] The Tamil Nadu Prohibition of Online Gambling and Regulation of Online Games Act, 2022 defined online game as “any game, whether or not there are other participants in the game, that is played on an electronic or digital device and is managed and operated as a software through the internet or any other kind of electronic or other technology for facilitating communication.”[35] As mentioned above, the term gaming had also been used in context of online gaming server, online games provider, and establishment of Tamil Nadu Online Gaming Authority. However, the acts states that this authority shall regulate online games. Given the description of “online game” in the act, all online games (games of skill and chance) seemingly fall under the purview of the Act. This further leads to limited clarity as the Act mentions that the online gambling (and online games of chance) is prohibited.

The Nagaland Prohibition of Gambling and Promotion and Regulation of Online Games of Skill Act, 2021 uses the term ‘virtual’ and describes it as games played online by means of computer automation and exercise of skill.[33]

The Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974 and The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 refers to online gaming cyberspace while describing common gaming house and mention “online electronic transfer of funds distributed or intended to be distributed in respect of any gaming,” while defining “instruments of gaming.” Additionally, Telangana Gaming Act, 1974, mentions “online gaming” for money or any other stakes, while defining “gaming.”[2627] The Kerala Gaming Act, 1960 makes reference to central computer server.[21]

DISCUSSION

Gaming and gambling disorders have been recognized as two distinct addictive behavioral disorders in the most widely recognized international classificatory system (ICD-11) for diagnosing mental health disorders. Although, the criteria for diagnosing these two disorders have used the terms “gaming” and “gambling” in a mutually exclusive manner in the description of their respective diagnostic criteria; they have not described what exactly constitutes gaming behavior.[2] This issue becomes even more complex if we also consider a new category of people involved in online or digital gaming indirectly as watchers or “observers” of the gameplay involving other gamers through YouTube or other social media platforms.[37] Further, the diagnostic criteria fail to clarify whether engaging in betting or monetary transactions as part of newer forms of digital gameplay is to be included as part of gambling behavior, gaming behavior, or both. For instance, fantasy sports have been classified differently as either gaming or gambling behavior across different jurisdictions depending on the law of the land.[38] This grey area assumes even greater importance when we examine the overlap between the use of terms related to gaming and gambling from the lens of existing laws governing gambling across different states and UTs in India. The issue is further complicated by the lack of consensus on the definition of gaming.

The term “gaming” has been used either in title or the main body in most of the Acts identified in this review that were dealing with regulation of gambling across different states and UTs in India. Moreover, the term “gaming” was used in the description of all but eight Acts. Although these Acts are targeted at regulation of gambling, some have still used the term “gaming” instead of “gambling” in them. Interestingly, four of the Acts did not use the term gambling in either their title or the description. The Puducherry Gaming Act, 1965 does not refer to the term gambling anywhere in the Act.[23] In addition, only some Acts included gambling in the description of the Act. The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955 used gaming and gambling as synonyms.[19] Most of the Acts used the terms wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming.” Only eight Acts did not use wagering or betting to describe “gaming.”

Most of the Acts were drafted in the pre-internet or digital boom period in India. Thus, they are likely to have been framed keeping in mind the prevalent socio-cultural scenario of that time. The use of terms or phrases such as “common gaming house” or “instruments of gaming” or “dice, cards, coins, gaming tables/boards, any boxes, tickets, gaming cloths, tables used for gaming” in many Acts included in this review seems to support this assertion. Further, many of the Acts attempted to distinguish games of chance from games of skill (or games of mere skill), with the latter being mentioned as not to be including as a gambling activity in some of them. However, there have been instances where the same activity has been classified differently across various jurisdictions. For instance, the online Rummy is considered as game of chance and banned under the Tamil Nadu Act akin to gambling. However, the Supreme Court of India considered it as “…not a game entirely of chance like the ‘three-card’ game mentioned in the Madras case… We cannot, therefore, say that the game of Rummy is a game of entire chance. It is mainly and preponderantly a game of skill.”[39] Thus, in other states and UTs where online Rummy has not been exclusively banned under the respective Acts, it shall be considered as a legally permissible gaming activity.

In addition, While most of the Acts specified that the provisions of the respective Act shall not be applicable to the games of skill, The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955, The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970, The Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974 and The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 do not make any such exception.[1719242627] The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955 mentioned that the State Government may by a general or special order exempt any social club or party playing games for the bona fide purposes of recreation only.[19] The Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974 and The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 mentioned that the provisions of the Act shall be applicable to any act of risking money or playing stakes or otherwise on the result of a game or an event including on a game of skill.

Most of the acts in India have used the term wagering in context of “gaming.” Although the description of the term is not uniform across the different acts. Only nine Acts did not use wagering and/or betting to describe “gaming.” The term “wagering” is often typically used in the context of gambling.[40] However, the judiciary, in the past, have observed that playing skill-based games for money in the virtual space renders them illegal.[40] The Odisha Prevention of Gambling Act, 1955, The Assam Game and Betting Act, 1970, Andhra Pradesh Gaming Act, 1974, The Telangana Gaming Act, 1974 do not contain the games of skill exception and accordingly do not allow any game to be played for monetary stakes.[19242627]

Lastly, with the rapidly rising market of online gaming in India and gamblification of games through introduction of several newer forms of digital gameplay related features such as loot boxes, monetary transactions; it is critical to have a relook at existing laws governing both online gaming and gambling activities.[41] Thus, there is need for conducting future research delineating which game characteristics and gaming contexts should be used for classifying an activity as gambling behavior vis-a-vis gaming behavior. Further, there is need for having a wider consultative process involving various stakeholders including researchers, lawmakers, and the end users for arriving at a consensus to formulate a uniform definition of gaming and gambling across different Acts in the country.

This conflation of gaming and gambling in most Acts is cause of concern. The usage of the term “gaming” across the Acts intended at regulation of gambling is confusing. While it might not have caused problems in the earlier times, the growing popularity of gaming (specifically online) has completely changed the paradigm. There is a need to have clear demarcation between gaming and gambling in the current context. Lack of a clear distinction between the two in the diagnostic systems does not help in resolving this conundrum either.[2]

The current review is aimed at sensitizing various stakeholders on the complexities involved in the context of gambling and gaming in the regulatory framework across the country. This article not only presents the specific themes of concern, but also highlights the heterogeneity in the existing regulatory framework for gambling across different states and UTs in India. Such heterogeneity is likely to pose additional and unique challenges in context of the online gambling and gaming.

Since, both gambling and gaming disorders pose a significant public health challenge, having a clear definition and a clear distinction between the two is essential to improve the quality of data collected, preventive and therapeutic interventions, and the formulation and implementation of policies and regulatory frameworks targeted at safeguarding the public interest in context of gambling and excessive or problematic gaming.

Cloud assisted blockchain-enabled split federated learning framework for security and privacy-preserving of IoMT in healthcare 5.0
Authors: Abdullah Baihan, Natalia Kryvinska, Mohammed Amoon, Weiwei Jiang, Zabeeh Ullah, Muhammad Shafiq
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid adoption of Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) in Federated Learning (FL)-enabled Smart Healthcare 5.0 raises pressing concerns regarding privacy, security, and real-time threat detection. FL remains vulnerable to training-phase attacks, parameter breaches, and aggregation threats, with the central server posing risks of inference, poisoning, and single-point failure. To address the challenge of single point failure and lack of efficient decentralized trust mechanism, we propose a novel framework that integrates Split Federated Learning (SFL) with Blockchain. SFL uses hybrid Deep Learning (DL) model which is divided between the IoMT and Edge layers by the architecture: Edge-based BiLSTM networks identify threats and temporal patterns, while lightweight CNN at the IoT layer extract spatial features from patient data. While SFL facilitates safe decentralized training, Blockchain uses the Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus mechanism to guarantee tamper-proof integrity, trust, and authentication. This method improves decentralized trust, protects privacy, and lessens data leakage. The framework is a robust and real-time intrusion detection solution for next-generation smart healthcare, as demonstrated by experiments on ToN-IoT and IoT healthcare datasets, which show improved privacy, robustness against attacks, block commit rate (450 b/s), and reduced consensus time (250 ms) in addition to superior accuracy (99.95%).Keywords: Cloud computing, Blockchain, Split federated learning, Edge based devices, Internet of medical things, Healthcare 5.0, Security, Decentralized trustSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Healthcare 5.0 has been transformed by the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) industry through the integration of intelligent, autonomous, cost-effective, and user-friendly technologies1,2. Inspired by Industry 5.0, Healthcare 5.0 offers human-centric, intelligent, and secure healthcare services by integrating IoT, AI, blockchain, and Federated Learning (FL) into IoMT-based medical ecosystems3. Due to the IoMT industry rapid adoption of advanced technologies, consumer-driven medical devices are producing large amounts of data. Cloud computing faces difficulties in processing this data, especially in terms of security, latency, and scalability4,5. By moving processing and security tasks to decentralized edge nodes, Edge Computing (EC), in contrast to cloud computing, improves performance and security. Low latency, effective bandwidth use, and decreased computational overhead—essential components of CE in healthcare—are supported by this method.

FL and EI together improve intrusion detection and fortify IoMT security in healthcare systems while protecting patient privacy. However, the diversity of IoMT devices’ resource capacities and persistent privacy issues make FL implementation difficult. Clients are exposed to constantly updated model versions during training, which may present extra privacy risks from the standpoint of machine learning6. Split Neural Networks (SplitNN) or Split Learning7 is a Deep Learning (DL) technique that was recently introduced by the Camera Culture group at MIT Media Lab to address FL challenges. As the name suggests, this technique splits a global neural network into several parts, each of which is trained using local data on a different local device (learner). Collaborative model training is made possible by passing the output of the last layer from one segment—known as smashed data—to the subsequent segment on a different device. Compared to FL, SplitNN improves model privacy and lessens the computational load on individual learners because no learner has access to the complete global model.

Split Federated Learning (SplitFed or SFL) integrates two distributed DL techniques, FL and SplitNN8, to create a more efficient hybrid collaborative learning algorithm. Similar to SplitNN, SplitFed divides the neural network between the clients (learners) and the server in order to maximize the use of computational resources and improve the privacy of data and models. Blockchain technology, a decentralized and impenetrable ledger system, has demonstrated promise in enhancing security, trust, and transparency in distributed environments such as IoMT-enabled Healthcare 5.0. With SFL, which combines the benefits of FL and SplitNN, decentralized trust management becomes a major challenge. By dividing the neural network between edge devices and a central server, SFL usually entails several IoMT devices working together to train a global model. The server that handles model aggregation is a key point of vulnerability introduced by this architecture, though, and it could be compromised or act maliciously, eroding the system integrity and confidence. Furthermore, in extremely dynamic environments, secure coordination and trust enforcement are challenging due to the resource limitations and vulnerability of IoMT devices to adversarial attacks. By removing the need for a centralized authority and offering a decentralized framework for trust, blockchain technology solves these issues. Blockchain allows several edge validators or fog nodes to safely and cooperatively check model updates (such as smashed data) before combining them into the global model by utilizing the consensus protocol Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT). Related work

This section covers the research work related to the proposed methodology. It is divided into two subsection, Federated Learning and Blockchain-based Federated Learning.

Federated learning

The authors of9 discussed the present state of privacy-preservation solutions in edge healthcare and IoT applications. It lists the typical techniques frequently employed by intelligent edges and technologies in healthcare systems to incorporate privacy. The study also discusses the limitations of these approaches in terms of sustainability, efficacy, and technological complexity. Insightful future applications are encouraged by the study’s emphasis on privacy concerns and the present research avenues that have propelled IoT and edge healthcare solutions. A new cooperative task scheduling and resource management paradigm for digital healthcare applications in edge intelligence systems was presented by Liu et al.10. This method uses a two-step optimization strategy that combines the Sequential Markov Decision Process (SMDP) with alternative reward estimation for computation offloading and the Multi-armed Combinatorial Selection Problem (MCSP) for task scheduling. The authors of11 examined the most recent advancements in FL and SL and provide an overview of the cutting-edge technologies for integrating these two learning approaches in an edge computing-based Internet of Things setting. In an effort to pique the attention of the academic community in this developing topic, they also discussed potential future research directions and outlined a few unresolved issues. The authors of12 propose distributed learning methods, such as FL and SL, to train the ML models in a distributed way by merely sharing model parameters. While SL has a higher communication overhead when the local dataset is large, FL demands more computing power on the UE side. to efficiently train an ML model while taking into account the variety of UEs with varying processing capacities. Using the concurrent model training mechanism of FL and the model splitting structure of SL, they first present a unique distributed learning architecture, a hybrid split and federated learning (HSFL) algorithm. Next, using a random UE selection technique, they provide its convergence analysis under non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data. The authors of13 proposes an SDN-enabled DL model enhanced with GAN-based synthetic data generation to address class imbalance in IoMT security. The approach outperforms existing methods, improving accuracy and F1-score by over 4%. SFL is examined in this paper14 as a potential collaborative learning strategy for 6G networks, with a focus on its benefits in terms of data privacy, lower overhead, and adaptation to 6G use cases and technology. Using knowledge distillation-based federated learning, the authors of15 investigate edge intelligence for protecting consumer electronics in healthcare and suggest a small, privacy-preserving detection model. In order to minimize communication overhead when training deep models on resource-constrained IoT devices, the authors of16 suggest a FedSL architecture. The method increases training efficiency by optimizing power consumption, device scheduling, and split layer selection. The authors of17 suggested an SDN orchestrated anomaly detection system based on Split SL to solve the cybersecurity vulnerabilities related to IoT in smart cities. The pertinent traffic features are chosen after the traffic produced by the CE has been pre-processed. In order to separate model training from the requirement for direct access to raw data, the model is then divided into at least two sub-models using SL. While server-side sub-models are trained on servers, client-side sub-models are learned on local training data on client devices. Experiments demonstrate that by only computing a sub-model rather than the entire computationally expensive model, the suggested SL-based CE network anomaly detection framework reduces computation requirements on the client side. For better personalization, the authors of18 suggested a gradient-fusion technique that incorporates both local and shared gradients into the locally modified model. Whereas the local gradients, which come from the data of each device, represent distinct data distributions, the shared gradients, which are combined from all IoMT devices via a learnable matrix, represent collective intelligence. Data heterogeneity is successfully reduced by this dynamic combination of device-specific and collective knowledge. An SDN-driven DL framework for H-IoT is proposed by the authors of19. It guarantees safe and effective data handling and surpasses current techniques in terms of accuracy, latency, and energy efficiency.

Cloud assisted blockchain-based federated learning

By tackling issues with scalability, security, and privacy, the authors of20 characterize blockchain-based federated learning (FL) as a safe option for IoT networks. Using blockchain and federated learning, the authors of21 suggest a cooperative IDS for automotive networks. The method improves defense efficiency against cyberattacks. The authors of22 proposes an AI-driven GAN-DRL framework for H-CIoT enhances anomaly detection and routing, outperforming OSPF in throughput, latency, and security. The authors of23 presents DA-FL framework that overcomes centralized FL limitations in trust, supervision, and privacy. The authors of24 proposed a Two-Stage FTL framework for IoMT in CE/CT, integrating differential privacy, adaptive learning rates, and cloud–edge secure aggregation to enhance privacy, efficiency, and energy use. A novel data-driven dynamic graph-embedded Transformer network (DGETN), which has surfaced in various graph data mining tasks for emotion detection of EEG signals in the context of IoMT, was proposed by the authors of25. To reduce redundancy and noise information, they first extract the frequency features differential entropy (DE) and then employ the linear dynamic system (LDS) approach. Second, a unique feature extraction module is built by embedding the dynamic graph convolution operations in the Transformer encoder for mining the discriminant features of data in order to efficiently explore the long-range information and local modeling abilities. This work26 proposes HADA-FL, a federated learning framework with SHAP- and DP-based adaptive aggregation that enhances privacy, convergence, and robustness. Whie12 and23 explore blockchain-assisted learning frameworks, they do not incorporate SFL for optimizing training across client–server partitions, nor do they integrate PBFT-based trust management for ensuring consensus and mitigating malicious model updates. Our proposed model uniquely leverages PBFT to provide decentralized trust, prevent single-point failures of the aggregation server, and improve reliability in heterogeneous Healthcare 5.0 environments. These distinctions have now been clearly highlighted to demonstrate the novelty and advantages of our framework over existing approaches. The authors of27 suggested a novel cloud-based user authentication system for safe medical data authentication. Both the wearable sensor node and the user create a secret session key that is used for upcoming secure connections upon successful mutual authentication. The authors of28 proposed the lightweight blockchain-enabled RFID-based authentication protocol (LBRAPS), a novel effective lightweight blockchain-enabled RFID-based authentication protocol for supply chains in a 5G mobile edge computing environment. LBRAPS’s security is guaranteed by the widely recognized Automated Validation of Internet Security Protocols and Applications (AVISPA) tool, which is used in simulation-based formal security verification. A thorough overview of the most recent developments in this topic is given by the authors of29 . First, they described the evolution from conventional ML to domain adaptation (DA) and DG, which culminate in FDG, and we offer the formal definition that goes along with it. We then categorize current approaches into four groups: LSs, DM, FDAL, and AO, and describe suitable algorithms for each. Next, we provide an overview of frequently used datasets, applications, assessments, and benchmarks. This survey concludes by outlining possible avenues for future research. In order to assure rigor and repeatability, the authors of30 undertook a systematic literature review (SLR) of peer-reviewed papers published between 2017 and 2025 using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) standards. 55 papers satisfied the inclusion requirements out of 2,407 publications that were examined. These studies included datasets, applications, assessments, and benchmarks. This survey concludes by outlining possible avenues for future research.

Proposed methodology

This section elaborates the layered approach combining SFL, blockchain, clint side model CNN, and server side model BiLSTM. It consists of three layers, Healthcare device layer, Edge layer, and Cloud layer. The architecture of the proposed model is shown in Fig. 1.

The proposed framework architecture.

Healthcare IoMT device layer

This layer consists of smart IoMT devices which collect their own data. To maintain the privacy of collected data, the client side model is trained on collected data and extract useful features. In this layer client models are trained locally on these devices using their private data, ensuring data privacy. The smashed data is forwarded to server model for further processing. The server model is trained on the server by aggregating and decrypting these logits, allowing it to learn from the collective knowledge of all edge devices without accessing their private data.

Batch normalization

Avoiding covariance shifts that could occur when a DNN switches between layers is the primary goal of batch normalization (BN). These changes could lead to instability and lessen the learning process’ efficacy. BN is crucial for reducing generalization errors and accelerating optimization32.

Edge layer

The edge layer controls the main server, which maintains the distributed ledger, aggregates local sub-models, and carries out backward gradient propagation. While contribution-based rewards encourage participation and recorded metrics identify malicious or inactive nodes, smart contracts authenticate IoT devices, verify model updates, and control blockchain access. Devices can train locally and send updates for verification thanks to the genesis block, which also stores device metadata, model architecture, and hyperparameters. Edge servers iteratively improve the global model until the accuracy threshold or maximum rounds are reached by exchanging updates via a peer-to-peer network, carrying out consensus, and appending validated blocks to the blockchain. The following elements make up the edge layer.

BiLSTM Based Server Side Model

Two parallel LSTM layers that process the input sequence in both forward () and backward () directions make up a bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) network. While the backward LSTM processes the sequence from future to past to capture contextual information, the forward LSTM computes hidden states by propagating information from past to future time steps. The forward and backward hidden states are concatenated to produce the final BiLSTM output at each time step. This allows the model to take advantage of both preceding and succeeding contextual dependencies for better temporal feature representation. Blockchain-based trust-aware FedAvg with PBFT in SFL

The edge layer Fed Servers are responsible of maintaining the distributed ledger and employing smart contracts to verify the local updates that are obtained from the IoMT. Smart contracts also aid in controlling blockchain access and authenticating IoMT devices. The Fed servers provide a system of rewards for smart devices that produce greater degrees of contribution in order to encourage the IoMT devices to take part in SFL. It is important to remember that the blockchain also records the performance and level of contribution from each device. This information helps the edge server identify devices that are stragglers, lethargic, or malicious. First, the genesis block includes information on the IoMT devices, the structure of the DL model and the DL model hyperparameters, including local epochs, global rounds, and SFL accuracy threshold. The IoMT devices begin local model training as soon as they receive the initial model. Each device then transmits the updates to Fed server, which verifies the model changes and authenticates the device using smart contracts. The Fed server then engages in a P2P manner for sharing of model updates exchange of model updates. The cloud functions as a supportive layer for the edge layer global model. It serves as a backup for the edge layer node in cases of a single point of failure takes place. First, FL is a natural benchmark for assessing new distributed learning approaches since it is the most popular collaborative learning paradigm in consumer electronics and healthcare IoT. Implications for practical deployment

The suggested framework has a great potential being used in real-world Healthcare 5.0 environments. Because it divides computation between CE devices and edge servers, it reduces latency and resource consumption, making it suitable for wearable and implantable medical devices. The use of blockchain-based trust management strengthens defenses against hostile attacks by enhancing accountability, transparency, and device authentication. Moreover, We offer a more thorough explanation of the main trade-offs related to the suggested framework. Local model training, frequent gradient exchanges, and cryptographic operations necessary for blockchain participation are the main causes of energy overhead, which can be difficult for IoMT devices with limited resources. Asynchronous updates in the split federated learning process and data heterogeneity among clients can cause convergence instability, which could result in slower or erratic convergence in highly non-IID settings. Consensus mechanisms (like PBFT), which add communication and validation delays, are another source of blockchain latency trade-offs. Although these mechanisms improve auditability, security, and trust, they may lengthen response times. Future research on energy-aware training, adaptive aggregation, and lightweight consensus protocols is encouraged by these trade-offs, which emphasize the harmony between security, privacy, and system efficiency.

Limitations of the current approach

The suggested blockchain-enabled SFL framework improves decentralized trust and data privacy, but it is not impervious to sophisticated adversarial attacks. Backdoor attacks could be introduced by malicious client updates prior to blockchain validation, and potential vulnerabilities like inference attacks could still take advantage of intermediate activations. Furthermore, the existing framework does not specifically include specialized defense mechanisms like anomaly detection or robust aggregation against complex poisoning strategies; instead, it mainly concentrates on secure aggregation and trust management.

Conclusion and future work

This paper introduced a novel framework that integrates SFL, Blockchain, and Edge Intelligence to enhance security, privacy, and real-time threat detection in healthcare systems. FL, which is incorporated into Smart Healthcare 5.0, provides significant opportunities for collaborative learning while maintaining data privacy. Traditional FL architectures are still vulnerable to inference attacks, model poisoning, and single-point failures caused by centralized aggregation. In order to overcome Split Federated Learning’s (SFL) limitations in adapting to devices with heterogeneous and non-IID data distributions, future research will investigate the integration of meta-learning techniques. Faster personalization and more stable convergence across various IoT and IoMT devices are the goals of this strategy. Moreover, to improve resilience against adversarial attacks, future research will examine the integration of robust aggregation techniques and backdoor detection. To increase overall system robustness, privacy-enhancing defenses against inference attacks, like secure activation masking and differential privacy, will also be investigated.

Discovery of compound candidates for the treatment of allergic diseases: integration of DFT analyses, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and ADMET profiling
Authors: Velid Unsal, Erkan Oner, Reşit Yıldız, Supriyo Saha, Başak Doğru Mert, Mehmet Emrah Aksan, Abdulkerim Hatipoğlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, a combination of computational analyses (drug similarity, physicochemical properties, pharmacokinetic properties, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulation, principal component analysis (PCA), free energy landscape (FEL), density functional theory (DFT), and absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADMET) analysis) was used to evaluate the efficacy of natural compounds (aloe emodin, fisetin, vitamin D3, β-carotene, berberine, and cinnamic acid) against allergic diseases and compare them with pharmaceuticals such as cetirizine and mizolastine. Elementary molecular structures and electronic properties were determined through DFT calculations accounting for reactivity by natural bond orbital (NBO) and nonlinear optical properties (NLO) analyses. Molecular docking studies were performed on critical proteins interleukin-10 (IL-10), filaggrin, thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), and high-affinity Fc receptor for immunoglobulin E (FcεRI) to elucidate the energetics of binding and to reveal interaction mechanisms facilitated by hydrophobic and hydrogen bonds. Highlighted in molecular docking, molecular dynamics (MD) simulation analysis of beta carotene and mizolastine was performed for IL-10, TSLP, FcεRI, and filaggrin, respectively. MD simulation revealed that the beta carotene-IL-10 complex exhibited higher structural stability. MD simulation showed that both filaggrin apoprotein and the mizolastine-filaggrin complex maintained their structural stability with consistent root mean square standard deviation, root mean square fluctuation, radius of gyration, and solvent accessible surface area (SASA) values. MD simulation revealed that both TSLP apoprotein and the beta carotene-TSLP complex maintained their stable conformations. MD simulation showed that both the FcεRI apoprotein and the beta carotene-FcεRI complex achieved stable structures with consistent radius of gyration and SASA values. PCA and FEL analyses revealed ligand-induced conformational variability. Although some physicochemical properties require further development, β-carotene, exhibiting very strong protein binding, and vitamin D3, particularly displaying good integration, superior drug-like properties, low toxicity, and good binding affinity, have been identified as promising candidates for the treatment of allergic diseases. This comprehensive approach combines structural chemistry, energetics, and drug discovery to create a powerful model for the rational design of allergy medications.

Introduction

Allergic diseases such as allergic rhinitis (AR), allergic asthma (AAS), atopic dermatitis (AD), food allergy (FA), and eczema are systemic disorders that occur as a result of the weakening of the immune system and are caused by the complex interaction of genetic and environmental factors. These diseases affect more than twenty-five percent of individuals in industrialized countries and constitute a serious and ongoing public health problem [1–3]. FcεRI, a high-affinity IgE receptor, regulates antigen-IgE interaction and triggers allergic reactions. Due to the extremely high binding affinity between IgE and FcεRI, inhibition of IgE-FcεRI interactions is seen as a promising therapeutic strategy [4]. Aloe-emodin, a natural anthraquinone, has been shown to have numerous pharmacological activities, such as anti-angiogenesis, anti-metastasis, anti-inflammation, antioxidant and mast cell stabilizer effects [5]. Filagrin is a major epidermal protein that plays an important role in the pathogenesis of atopic dermatitis and allergic diseases. Although filaggrin is an important genetic risk factor for AD, it is also an important disease regulator in AD. Patients with filaggrin-related AD and asthma experience more frequent hospitalizations and long-term drug costs. Atopic dermatitis patients with FLG mutations are more often affected by health-related reductions in quality of life compared to AD patients with wild-type FLG. Filaggrin is associated with both the monogenic disease ichthyosis vulgaris (IV) and AD, asthma and FA [6–8]. Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) is a type of cytokine that plays a role in immune responses in the body, particularly those causing allergies, and regulates many cells. A monoclonal antibody targeting TSLP has been approved for the treatment of severe asthma. Furthermore, recent studies indicate that therapies targeting the TSLP pathway are emerging as a promising treatment option not only for asthma but for many other diseases as well [9]. IL-10 are versatile molecules that play a key role in the regulation of the immune system, with a strong immunosuppressive capacity, especially in achieving peripheral tolerance to allergens and protecting the host from excessive inflammatory responses or autoimmune diseases associated with pathogens [10, 11]. Fisetin suppresses allergic responses [12]. In a mouse model of atopic dermatitis, it increases the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 while decreasing serum IgE levels. It also exhibits immunosuppressive effects by inhibiting the production of Th1 and Th2 cells and T cell proliferation [13]. Fisetin also significantly reduces FcɛRI α-subunit mRNA expression [14]. Therefore, it is thought that fisetin may have potential as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of allergic diseases by modulating various immunological and inflammatory processes [15]. Vitamin D, which is a fat-soluble vitamin, affects the onset and progression of allergic diseases such as atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis and asthma due to its important role in regulating the immune system, and thanks to this feature, it has a significant effect on the formation and development of these diseases [16]. β-carotene plays an important role in alleviating allergic reactions. β-carotene exerts its effect by suppressing the production of antigen-specific IgE antibodies, inhibiting degranulation from mast cells and basophils, and reducing the release of chemical mediators [17]. Berberine is noted for its various pharmacological benefits, including anti-allergic antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, hypoglycemic, hypolipidemic, and antioxidant properties [18, 19]. Cinnamic acid, a natural aromatic carboxylic acid found in plants, fruits, whole grains, vegetables, and honey such as Cinnamomum cassia (Chinese cinnamon) and Panax ginseng, has antioxidant, antimicrobial, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and antidiabetic properties [20–23]. In the development of antihistamine drugs, addressing histamine-mediated allergic reactions is crucial. Cetirizine and mizolastin, which stand out as critical therapeutic agents in the management of histamine-mediated allergic reactions and were used as control group drugs in our study, are effective in various allergic conditions due to their strong and selective antagonist properties against H1 receptors [24–26]. Drug discovery is a critical area situated at the intersection of structural biology, biochemistry, and medicine, presenting significant challenges such as high costs (often exceeding billions of dollars), low success rates (typically less than 10%), and extremely lengthy processes (often more than a decade). In the twenty-first century, computational approaches known as artificial intelligence and computer-aided drug discovery have demonstrated significant advantages in overcoming these challenges and accelerating the process, which has made them vital to the pharmaceutical industry and scientific research. These in silico approaches have revolutionized the current understanding of experimental drug discovery methods [27–29]. Proteins such as IL-10, filaggrin, FcεRI, and TSLP are critically important in the pathogenesis of allergic diseases. Natural compounds such as aloe-emodin, fisetin, vitamin D3, β-carotene, berberine, and cinnamic acid are also involved in this process. Molecular docking is a fundamental method for predicting how small molecules will interact with target proteins. By helping to identify binding affinities, orientations, and potential inhibitors or activators, it guides the optimization of the leading compound and reduces experimental replications. These selected compounds were chosen not ‘simply because they are anti-allergic’, but ‘because, although known to be anti-allergic, how they interact with the aforementioned proteins, which are crucial in allergy, is not yet known, and to fill this knowledge gap’. One of the main rationales for the selection of these compounds is to make a mechanism-based discovery. Docking provides the first step in rational drug design by narrowing down promising candidates. MD (Molecular Dynamics) simulations complement docking by modeling the dynamic behavior of protein-ligand complexes under physiological conditions. By revealing the stability, conformational changes, and flexibility of biomolecules, they offer insights into long-term interactions that static docking cannot capture. [30–32]. Density Functional Theory (DFT) provides a quantum-level understanding of molecular electronic structures, reactivity, and energy distributions. It is crucial for evaluating chemical stability, charge transfer, and interaction energies that affect drug efficacy and safety. NBO analysis further enhances this by examining electron density, orbital interactions, and binding properties. It helps to elucidate noncovalent interactions such as hydrogen bonds and van der Waals forces, which are critical in drug-target recognition. Together, these methods provide a comprehensive framework: they identify docking candidates, MD verifies stability, DFT and NBO explain molecular interactions, and ADMET ensures safety [31, 33, 34]. Their integration accelerates drug discovery, minimizes the experimental burden, and increases the possibility of developing new, low-toxicity therapeutic agents. The main objective of this study is to learn the roles of these compounds in allergic diseases at the molecular level, to determine the extent to which they compounds are suitable for drug discovery and development processes, to contribute to the identification of new drug candidates with low toxicity and pharmacokinetic properties, and to develop new therapeutic candidates. In addition, the data obtained in this study provide a roadmap for the treatment of allergic diseases and pave the way for the discovery of new agents.

DFT

The Gaussian 09W and GausView 5.0 programs were used in this study to implement the Density Functional Theory (DFT) approach [38, 39]. The NLO properties of aloe emodin, fisetin, vitamin D3, β-carotene, berberine, cinnamic acid, cetirizine, and mizolastine compounds, focusing on their polarizability (α) and first hyperpolarizability (β), were determined using DFT with the B3LYP and 6-31 G (d, p) basis set. Beck’s (B) three-parameter hybrid model and Lee, Yang, and Parr’s (LYP) correlation functional basis set were the diffuse formation used for density functional theory calculation. Sometimes, atomic orbitals are distorted from their original shape and polarized by the effect of surrounding functional groups; in such a situation, a polarization factor is added, such as (d,p), where polarization is added to both the d and p orbitals. In the case of large molecules, the central nucleus loses control of certain functional groups, and then those functional groups form a new orbital. To counter this factor, diffuse formation factor was added. A structure with a lone pair of electrons or an anion group must have this diffusion factor (+) to calculate the orbital energy effectively. As our preferred chemical structures do not contain any lone pairs of electrons or anions, in this manuscript, we considered the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) basis set to calculate the electronic and optical characteristics of the molecules through DFT analysis. The B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level of theory was selected because it can accurately represent electrical characteristics and features at a reasonable computing cost, and it has been shown to be reliable for organic compounds with heteroatoms [37, 39–44]. The energy of the highest occupied molecular orbital (EHOMO), the energy of the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (ELUMO), energy gap (∆E) between LUMO and HOMO, dipole moment, softness (s), electrophilicity (ω), nucleophilicity (Nu), hardness (η) and electronegativity (χ) and NBO charges on the backbone atoms were determined and presented in Tables 1 and 2 [40–44]. Molecular docking

To understand protein-ligand interaction, molecular modelling methods, especially molecular docking, have been proposed as an effective tool to help unravel the molecular basis of disease and drug discovery [45–47]. 2D structures of aloe emodin, fisetin, vitamin D3, β-carotene, berberine, cinnamic acid, cetirizine, and mizolastine were obtained from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/, access date: February 13, 2025) Ligands were sketched in the ChemDraw and subsequently energy-minimized using Chem3D with the MMFF94 force field, and saved in PDB format. Energy minimisation of ligands was performed in ChemDraw programme and ligands were recorded in pdb format. Interleukin-10 (IL-10) (PDB ID: 1ILK), Filaggrin PDB ID: 4PCW, Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP): Cytokine receptor complex-Crystal form 1A: PDB ID: 4NN5 and Structure of the high affinity receptor fc(epsilon)ri TM PDB ID: 8Y84 protein structures were obtained from the Protein Data Bank in pdb format (https://www.rcsb.org/), access date: February 15, 2025) Grid box center coordinates for IL-10 (X: 17.86, Y: 46.47, Z: 38.42), filaggrin (X: −15.10, Y: 1.88, Z: 11.19), TSLP (X: 12.81, Y: −12.39, Z: 24.29), and FcεRI (X: 184.5, Y: 138.68, Z: 125.75). The grid box dimensions were specified as X_Y_Z 40 × 40*40. Then put the target receptor in Autodock Tools 1.5.7, polar hydrogens, Kollman and Gasteiger charges were applied. Protein_ligand complex files were saved in pdbqt format. Then finally performed the molecular docking procedure using AutoDock Vina software. Discovery Studio Visualizer 2021 was used for visualization of docking interaction [48, 49].

Molecular dynamic simulation (MDS) of the selected compounds

In the initial step, molecular docking analysis of different phytoconstituents with IL-10, filaggrin, TSLP, and FcεRI was performed. Beta carotene emerged as the best docked molecule for IL-10, TSLP, and FcεRI receptors, and mizolastine showed the maximum docking interaction score with filaggrin. To establish the docking analysis and analyze atomic-level interactions, a 100 ns MDS was performed for the best-docked molecules and their corresponding receptors using the GROMACS 2022.4 software module. In the first step, the protein-ligand complex was transferred into the GROMACS environment, and the complexed ligand was removed. The structures of the ligand molecules were saved in mol2 format, followed by conversion into simulation ready coordinate file formats using Swissparam web tool (https://swissparam.ch/ Access date: February 26, 2025). Then the Chemistry at HARvard Macromolecular Mechanics (CHARMM) force field and Transferable Intermolecular Potential 3 Point (TIP3P) solvents were applied to both the receptor and the ligand, and the resulting systems were saved in GROMACS-accessible gro format, along with the generation of topology files [50]. During simulation 24,691, 28,187 water molecules and 2 and 4 chloride ions were added to the topology files for IL-10 apoprotein and beta carotene-IL-10, respectively. During MDS 10,352, 7859 water molecules and 4 and 5 sodium ions were added to the topology files for the filaggrin apoprotein and mizolastine-Filaggrin, respectively. During MDS 23,680, 16,439 water molecules and 6 sodium ions were added to the topology files for the TSLP apoprotein and beta carotene-TSLP, respectively. During MDS 142,531, 100,346 water molecules and 13.6 sodium ions were added in topology files for FcεRI apoprotein, beta carotene- FcεRI, respectively. Then 100,000 energy minimization steps were applied to the receptor-ligand complex, followed by equilibrating the system in terms of temperature, volume, and pressure. In NVT (Isothermal-isochoric ensemble) and NPT (Isothermal-isobaric ensemble) equilibrium systems 50,000 steps were applied at 300° K temperature with leap-frog algorithm for integrating Newton’s equations of motion as the choice of integrator, bonds with H-atoms as bond constraints, and Fast Smooth Particle-Mesh Ewald (SPME) as electrostatics [51]. After completion of MDS, root mean square standard deviation (RMSD) and root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) were determined for every receptor-ligand complex to identify the stability of ligand-receptor interaction and amino acid residues with observable fluctuations during interaction. Also, receptor-ligand complex stability was assessed using the radius of gyration and solvent-accessible surface area. MDS data were reported in comparison with the apoenzyme. Hydrogen bond analysis was also performed to identify hydrogen bonds between the ligand and the receptor. QtGrace software was used to analyze the simulation trajectories [52].

MM/PBSA analysis

Molecular Mechanics-Poisson-Boltzmann surface area (MM-PBSA) calculations were used to calculate the binding energy for beta carotene-IL-10, beta carotene-TSLP, beta carotene-FcεRI, and mizolastine-filaggrin complexes using g_mmpbsa program. In MM-PBSA calculations van der Waals (VDW) energy, electrostatic (ES) energy, polar solvation (PS) energy, solvent-accessible surface area energy, and binding energy were determined [53].

PCA and FEL Analysis

PCA is a statistical technique that transforms a set of correlated variables into linearly uncorrelated variables, called principal components, using a covariance matrix. Here we consider the backbone as variable, where analysis incorporates finer details of secondary structure rearrangements, making FEL valleys correspond to subtle conformational substates. This approach projects data into an eigenspace to determine the directions of maximum variance. PCA reduces the complexity of multidimensional variable sets by lowering their dimensionality, facilitating the identification of diffusive properties throughout the protein folding process [54–56].

PC1 usually represents the largest conformational change (e.g., folding/unfolding, domain motion). PC2 captures the second most significant orthogonal motion. Together, they provide a reduced 2D representation of the conformational space, where FEL minima correspond to stable states. The FEL of a protein, represented as a high-dimensional hypersurface, characterizes changes in a protein’s energy as its structure evolves. A comprehensive understanding of the FEL is essential for understanding protein stability and dynamics. PCA and FEL analysis were performed for beta carotene-IL-10, beta carotene-TSLP, and beta carotene-FcεRI receptors and mizolastine-filaggrin, along with apoproteins [57–60].

ADMET

These compounds were subjected to in silico calculations of their physicochemical, molecular, and pharmacokinetic properties using ADMETlab 3.0 software (https://admetlab3.scbdd.com, Access date: February 18, 2025) [61].

Results and discussion

This study presents evidence suggesting that compounds such as aloe emodin, fisetin, vitamin D3, β-carotene, berberine, and cinnamic acid, considered to have potential in the treatment of allergic diseases, may have effects on allergic diseases. Information on the efficacy, safety, drug similarity, physicochemical properties, and pharmacokinetic profiles of these compounds is presented. For this purpose, ADMET, DFT, NBO, and NLO analyses were performed on these compounds in this study. Molecular coupling analyses of these compounds (with IL-10, Filaggrin, TSLP, and FcεRI proteins) were examined in detail in terms of protein-ligand binding properties. The data obtained in this study suggest a hypothesis of molecular interactions between selected compounds and proteins that play a role in allergic diseases, which have not been directly linked before. On the other hand, the findings obtained in this study may lead to the development of future treatment options in the treatment of allergic diseases.

During the drug development phase, various factors are taken into consideration to determine which chemical compounds will proceed to the trial process. The desired compounds are to show high efficacy and low toxicity if possible. On the other hand, it is also critical that the compounds reach the treatment target and reach sufficient concentration in the target area. While the factors affecting target access are evaluated separately, pharmacokinetic properties are examined. For this examination, the SwissADME Web tool simultaneously calculates the physical-chemical, pharmacokinetic, drug-like, and other relevant properties of one or more molecules. These calculations contribute to drug development studies for researchers [35].

In recent years, the importance of MRP1 in human health and disease has become clear and this transporter has great clinical importance due to its function of excluding drugs and physiological molecules. MRP1 plays an important role in tumor multidrug resistance, especially in cancer treatment, and may also be effective in the efficacy of drugs such as antibiotics, opiates, antiviral agents and statins. In addition, MRP1 is involved in the etiology of a number of pathologies such as inflammatory diseases, cardiovascular diseases and neurological disorders [67, 68]. Breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP, ABCG2) is a clinically important ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter and functions as an outflow pump that transports its substrates from inside to outside the cell using ATP. BCRP, which is commonly expressed in different tissues and physiological barriers, carries drugs such as sulfasalazine, antineoplastics, statins, as well as endogenous compounds such as natural products, carcinogens, steroid metabolites, urates and porphyrins. With this feature, BCRP is of great importance in terms of drug distribution and transport of various substances in the body [69]. PCA is a statistical technique that transforms a set of correlated variables into linearly uncorrelated variables, referred to as principal components, by utilizing a covariance matrix. This approach projects data into an eigenspace to determine the directions of maximum variance. PCA reduces the complexity of multidimensional variable sets by lowering their dimensionality, which facilitates the identification of diffusive properties throughout the protein folding process. The free energy landscape (FEL) of a protein, represented as a high-dimensional hypersurface, characterizes changes in a protein’s energy as its structure evolves. NLO analysis

The hyperpolarizability (β) and polarizability (α) components offer important information on their optical and nonlinear optical (NLO) characteristics, which are important to possible applications. The polarizability tensor components (αₓₓ, αᵧᵧ, and αzz) describe the linear optical response, while the first hyperpolarizability components (βₓₓₓ, βₓₓᵧ, βₓᵧᵧ, etc.) reflect the nonlinear response of these molecules to an external electric field (Table 14). Because of its strong linear optical response and considerable electronic delocalization, β-carotene has the highest polarizability among the substances under analysis across all tensor components, especially αₓₓ (2498.1403 a.u.), αᵧᵧ (1102.1322 a.u.), and αzz (914.8753 a.u.). Cetirizine and mizolastine, on the other hand, exhibit significantly lower polarizability values, indicating less pronounced electronic polarization effects when an electric field is applied. These values are anisotropic, especially in compounds like berberine and cinnamic acid, where the differences between αₓₓ, αᵧᵧ, and αzz indicate a directional dependency of their electronic response [38–42]. Aloe emodin and berberine exhibit significant βₓₓₓ values (1545.3717 a.u. and 2174.5530 a.u., respectively) for the initial hyperpolarizability components, demonstrating their NLO response. Because of its broad π-conjugation system, β-carotene, on the other hand, has a very negative βₓₓₓ (−53158.8480 a.u.), suggesting a severe reaction to external fields. Mizolastine and cetirizine exhibit comparatively modest hyperpolarizability values, indicating that their NLO behavior is restricted. Furthermore, notable differences between various β tensor components, including βₓₓᵧ and βᵧzz, emphasize the nonlinear response’s directional dependency, which is important in modifying molecular characteristics for certain technological uses [41–43]. The polarizability and hyperpolarizability parameters of the compounds

The NLO characteristics offer important insights into the electrical flexibility and charge redistribution capacity of biologically active compounds, even though they are mainly studied in relation to optical applications. The capacity of a molecule to experience electronic deformation in response to external disturbances, such as electrostatic fields found in biological contexts or at receptor binding sites, is specifically linked to molecular polarizability and initial hyperpolarizability. Stronger hydrogen bonds, electrostatic interactions, and charge-transfer effects are made possible by molecules with greater polarizability and a strong anisotropic response. These electronic characteristics complement the outcomes of molecular docking by directly affecting molecular recognition and binding stability. As a result, rather than suggesting direct pharmacological activity based solely on NLO performance, the DFT-derived NLO descriptors function as electronic-level indications of reactivity and interaction propensity, offering a mechanistic explanation for the observed binding behavior.

NBO analysis

The stabilization energy E(2) was determined through Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) calculations using the second-order Fock matrix. These calculations were performed for each donor (i) and acceptor (j) to elucidate intramolecular bonding interactions. The stability resulting from delocalization effects is reflected in the reported E(2) values, which provide insight into the charge transfer between bonding and antibonding orbitals. The C–C bonding orbitals and the C–C* or C–O* antibonding orbitals in particular showed strong donor–acceptor interactions for aloe emodin. Particularly, interactions like BD (C3–C4) → BD*(C7–O30) (33.76 kcal/mol) and BD(C3–C4) → BD*(C8–O29) (33.85 kcal/mol) had the greatest stabilization energy values, indicating significant electronic delocalization (Table 14). Additionally, BD(C9–C10) showed similar interactions with BD*(C7–O30) (33.64 kcal/mol) and BD*(C8–O29) (33.94 kcal/mol), confirming the existence of important conjugative effects in the molecular structure. BD(C9–C10) → BD*(C11–C13) (13.90 kcal/mol) and BD(C9–C10) → BD*(C14–C15) (17.53 kcal/mol) are examples of lower stabilization energy values that indicate comparatively weaker but still significant interactions that contribute to the overall electronic structure [44]. The presence of multiple principal delocalizations, including vicinal (v), geminal (g), and remote (r) interactions, suggests significant electronic coupling between oxygen and carbon atoms, contributing to the stabilization of the molecular framework. Significantly, the energy values of the lone pairs on oxygen atoms O29 and O30, which were determined to be −482.46 kcal/mol and −486.40 kcal/mol, respectively, show a stronger capacity to donate electrons. Conclusion

Differences in perturbation energies were found among the analyzed compounds, indicating several key electronic interactions, significantly contributed by bond interactions and lone pair effects. This has improved our understanding of the compounds’ mechanical properties by demonstrating the complex interplay between molecular structure, reactivity, and stability. For example, in some compounds, such as cetirizine and mizolastine, interactions involving lone pairs (LP) exhibited high decay energies (e.g., 49.37 kcal/mol for the LP interactions in mizolastine), indicating more pronounced electronic effects. However, compounds such as beta-carotene and cinnamic acid exhibited lower perturbation energies, suggesting less pronounced electron donor-acceptor interactions. These differences significantly impact drug similarities and therapeutic applications. According to the resulting diagrams, vitamin D3, β-carotene, and berberine exhibited optimum ranges for all criteria. Vitamin D3 falls within optimal ranges for parameters such as Mw, TPSA, Csp3 fraction, and rotatable bonds, making it the most suitable candidate in terms of physicochemical properties. While vitamin D3 appears promising due to its balanced physicochemical profile, its high lipophilicity may limit solubility, a problem that can be overcome through structural optimization. β-carotene, although outside the optimal ranges for molecular weight, TPSA, and molar refractive index, has been favorably linked in molecular docking studies, making it a noteworthy compound. Berberine had favorable pharmacokinetic properties and high QED values, indicating a higher likelihood of success in clinical studies. However, its genotoxic and hematotoxic risks, similar to aloe emodin and fisetin, necessitated controlled dose and frequency of use. Cinnamic acid and β-carotene demonstrated better permeability in the Caco-2 and MDCK models, suggesting improved absorption potential. Molecular docking calculations showed that β-carotene, mizolastine, and vitamin D3 have the strongest binding affinity to proteins such as IL-10, filaggrin, TSLP, and FcεRI, driven by hydrophobic and hydrogen bonding effects. MD simulations showed that β-carotene and mizolastine formed stable complexes with IL-10, TSLP, FcεRI, and filaggrin. Furthermore, DFT analysis confirmed that β-carotene enhances molecular interaction capabilities. Although cinnamic acid has the lowest binding affinity for IL-10, filaggrin, TSLP, and FcεRI, its low mutagenic and toxic potential suggests that it could be a target for various proteins. In conclusion, β-carotene and vitamin D3 emerged as prominent candidates due to their superior drug-like properties and strong communication links with the relevant proteins. However, additional preclinical and clinical studies are needed to definitively establish the efficacy and safety of these drugs. The multifaceted evaluations of this study will provide significant advances in the field of drug development targeting proteins and potential compounds in allergic diseases. This highlights the need for meticulous optimization of additional drugs to reduce the risk of toxicity. This includes using nanoparticles or liposomes to alter the absorption and dissolution of drugs, perhaps reducing their permeability and limiting their bioavailability.

Limitations of the study

Molecular docking and dynamic simulations did not confirm the biological consequences of the proposed interactions, and therefore the findings need to be supported by in vitro and in vivo studies. Furthermore, this computational study proposes a hypothesis of molecular interactions between select compounds and proteins involved in allergic diseases that have not previously been directly linked.

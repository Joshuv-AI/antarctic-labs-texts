Rap1b: A cytoskeletal regulator Advantageous to viral infection
Authors: Beibei Zhang, Shuli Li, Juntao Ding, Jingxia Guo, Zhenghai Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryRAS-like small GTP-binding protein 1b (Rap1b) is a small GTPase involved in numerous deformation-mediated physiological processes, including viral infection, through cytoskeleton regulation on the premise of dynamic changes in the loading state of GTP or GDP. Previous studies have mainly focused on Rap1b’s roles in tumor metastasis and platelet hemostasis due to their significant cytoskeleton rearrangement. The complexity of Rap1b and cytoskeleton functions has limited research on their role in viral infections, and the molecular mechanisms for drug and vaccine development targeting Rap1b remain unclear. Here, we reviewed the mechanisms used by Rap1b to sustain cancer growth through the cytoskeleton and analyzed the role of Rap1b protein in the regulation of the cytoskeleton, as well as its use to promote replication in response to viral invasion, highlighting that it may be a potential target for development of antiviral strategies.Subject areas: Virology, Cell biology

Introduction

The RAS-like small GTP-binding protein 1 (Rap1) is a small G protein belonging to the GTP-binding protein family that can load guanosine triphosphate (GTP) or guanosine diphosphate (GDP) in order to switch between active and inactive states in the physiological process.1,2 The guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) and GTP enzyme activating protein (GAP) are responsible for GTP and GTP loading, respectively, due to their catalytic effect on nucleotide-binding site.2 Notably, the competition between prenylation and phosphorylation at the C-terminal CAAX box determine the membrane affinity of Rap1b after its activation about the transfer between the nuclear, cytoplasmic locations and cell membrane locations, and the activation of downstream signaling pathways.3,4 Certainly, a GTP loaded-Rap1b with polyisoprene (terpene) groups is the most important form for membrane-binding triggered cascade signals.5 Currently, the GEF family include Crk SH3 domain-binding Guanine nucleotide exchange factor (C3G),6 RAS guanyl nucleotide-releasing protein 2 (RasGRP2)7 and PDZ domain GEFs (PDZ-GEFs),8 however, only GAP-specific for Rap1 (Rap1GAP) and signal-induced proliferation-associated protein 1 (SPA-1)9 were found to play a role in this regard.2 The regulation of Rap1 biological activity is reported in Figure 1.

The Rap1 protein is typically located free in the cytoplasm and nucleus, with a prevalence of phosphorylated form rather than prenylated form under regular cell conditions, to avoid an excessive activation to save the energy and for intercellular adhesion stability. Once external or internal stress factors occur, a transition is driven by GEFs and GAPs from Rap1b-GTP to the formation of Rap1b-GDP and an excessive prenylated Rap1b. This is followed by a membrane distribution of prenylated Rap1b-GTP, which results in a recruitment of downstream effectors from cytoplasm or reply of membrane proteins. Afterward, the cascade signaling pathway induces the transcription and translation of downstream functional proteins or effector molecules, in order to develop stimulus-induced physiological processes, ultimately leading to beneficial or harmful outcomes.

Rap1 consists of two isomers: Rap1a and Rap1b, with an amino acid (aa) sequence homology of up to 95%, with only 9 divergent aa, 6 of which located at their carboxyl end.1,5,10 Although several evidences confirmed that Rap1a and Rap1b are involved in the development of tumors11 and exertion of platelet functions,12 only a few reports report about functional differences of the two isomers during the different tumorigenesis and hemostatic processes,13,14 suggesting a highly overlapping functional potential. Nevertheless, increasing data showed that Rap1, especially the subunit Rap1b, is more relevant in the process of metastasis and rapid growth of malignancy,15,16 as well as in the thrombotic plug formation of platelet-led hemostasis,14 which strictly depends on cytoskeleton regulation.12,17,18 Interestingly, cross-talk between virus and cytoskeleton is involved in the entire period of viral entry, replication and release of viral particles by providing orbital or spatial structures, but also for the internal or external morphological changes of the cell structure.19,20,21 Starting from the internalization, surfing, intracellular transport, and replication toward the complete release into intracellular space, the cytoskeletal regulation, microfilaments, microtubules, and intermediate fibers, all respond to upstream host factors of virus hijacking, providing the progression of the steps above.22,23 In particular, Rap1b is a crucial factor for these cytoskeleton-based processes, as evidenced by in vitro and in vivo investigations.14,23,24,25 Therefore, the causal relationship among Rap1b, cytoskeleton and virus release were obtained, however, little information and research are available about the molecular mechanisms.

The classical model of treadmilling, is characterized by the two forms of G-actin (depolymerization form) and F-actin (polymerization form) converting to each other by phosphorylated Cofilin and dephosphorylated Cofilin, respectively,26 and mainly regulated by the upstream signals from Rho GTPase family.27 Among them, the cell division cycle protein 42 (CDC42), Ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 (RAC1) and Ras homolog gene family member A (RhoA) are known to mediate several effector proteins in downstream pathways, such as Wiskott Aldrich Syndrome protein (WASP), Rho associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase (ROCK), p21-activated kinases (PAKs), and LIM domain kinase 1 (LIMKs), to form a complex signal pathway network for the regulation of actin cytoskeleton rearrangement through the “response-executive partner” of Cofilin and F-actin.27,28,29 Given the key potential of Rap1b in the regulation of the cytoskeleton during virus infection, attempt by Gao et al.30 demonstrated that the respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) disrupts the airway epithelial barrier by decreasing cortatin expression and disrupting the stability of the F-actin network, and is associated with decreased activity of Rap1b, the downstream target of cortical actin, and Rap1b activation can mitigate pathogen invasion caused by barrier disruption. Similarly, our works on avian hepatitis E virus (aHEV),23 further emphasized the detailed mechanism of Rap1b-Cofilin-F-actin signaling for cytoskeleton regulation during the early stage of viral infection, strongly supported by the noteworthy activation of Rho and cytoskeleton-dependent effector integrin.31 Unfortunately, these efforts did not clarify the underlying mechanisms. Hence, we summarized the knowledge about Rap1b and its characteristic with the potential to regulate cytoskeleton, and about the Rap1b and cytoskeleton-triggered viral infection as a target for antiviral therapy.

Rap1b is associated with cytoskeleton-triggered biological processes

Cytoskeletal rearrangement contributes to the tumor metastasis

Malignant tumors have the essential attributes of high metastasis rate, once escaping from the primary site, they can metastasize to the whole body though multiple manners (direct invasion, lymphatic metastasis and/or blood metastasis, etc.) and lesions occur randomly, leading to recurrence and treatment failure, or even death.32,33 The metastasis analysis showed a consistency with this phenomenon when migration and invasion were all monitored using malignant tumor cell lines and mouse models (Table 1).34,35,36,37,38 Mechanically, the imbalance of cytoskeletal homeostasis is the trigger for tumorigenesis and reduced adhesion, ultimately promoting intertissue migration or invasion.17 Moreover, cytoskeletal remodeling for tumor progression and metastasis was also demonstrated as a strongly causal, driven strictly by Rap1b,15 making it a potential biomarker of clinical cancer prognosis.39 Notably, numerous chemicals targeting Rap1b such as baicalein,40 mevastatin,41 glucocorticoids42,43 and statins44 etc.,45 they all play a role on tumor progression and distant metastasis decline as demonstrated by in vitro and in vivo assays through the inhibition of its expression or activation, and the key step is to inhibit Rap1b-mediated cytoskeleton to limit the invasive proliferation of tumor cells.

The relevance of Rap1b in different tumors

Cytoskeletal remodeling triggers platelet activation

Normal platelets usually circulate in the vessel, but are promptly activated following a wound, leading to local aggregation,56,57,58,59 adhesion,60,61 clot retraction,14,62,63 and coagulation,25,56,64 to stopping bleeding. In this process, integrin is a transmembrane heterodimer consisting of α and β subunits, and alterations in its affinity are accountable for the aforementioned changes in cytoskeletal shape (Table 2).65 Significantly, The lack of Rap1b in the platelets causes a reduction in coagulation and thrombosis because of the cytoskeleton remodeling,66,67,68 as well as a decrease in the spreading and adhesion of platelets within the vessel, causing a prolonged hemostatic period in the mouse model.14 In theory, an increase in platelets is indeed beneficial for a rapid hemostasis.69 However, thrombocytosis may be beneficial for cancer metastasis, as not only is combined with tumor cells to form tumor-thrombus, and to build a bridge for its endothelial metastasis and invasion, but also it may promote the formation of local blood vessels to create a microenvironment for tumorigenesis.70,71 Also, the speeding of the tumor cells or the platelets depend on the changes in Rap1b-induced integrin activity and on the following cytoskeleton-triggered membrane liquidity,17 as shown in Figure 2. To prevent the overactivation of Rap1b, it is crucial to maintain an antagonistic balance between the Rap1 regulator RAS guanyl releasing protein 1 (RASGRP1, agonist) and Ras p21 protein activator 3 (RASA3, inhibitor), which is essential for regulating platelet adhesion at sites of circulatory and vascular injury.12 Theoretically, Rap1b, which is significantly activated in tumor cells in primary tumor, lays down the remodeling of cytoskeleton-based tumorigenesis and reduction of intercellular adhesion and vice versa.51,52 In the absence of intercellular forces, the solid tumors cells can easily migrate throughout the body by adjacent monolayer endothelial vessels.72 To reduce the hemorrhage at the spot of tumor detachment, there is a cytoskeleton-triggered rapid activation of platelet which can aggregate and form clumps to change the speed and viscosity of local blood flow in order to prevent blood outflow.72,73 In the meantime, platelets with a strong affinity for tumor cells and vascular endothelium guide the tumor cells to colonize other tissues,74 and this was also proved to be related to Rap1b.75 Therefore, the therapeutic target of Rap1b was investigated for the inhibition of tumor metastasis, but further studies about the contradictory role of platelet activation in hemostasis and tumor colonization with cytoskeleton remodeling at different stages of tumorigenesis, may be beneficial in the future.

The role of Rap1b in platelet activation

Hypothetic diagram of Rap1b mechanisms underlying tumor metastasis and hemostasis by triggering the integrin-cytoskeleton-mediated cell spreading and thrombosis

During the phase before metastasis, the increasing cell number and tumor volume with abundant blood vessels reduce the adjacent tissues to obtain the nutritional uptake and space to proliferate. Meanwhile, the persistent activation of accumulates as the activated form of prenylation (Pre-Rap1b-GTP) on the intima of blood vessels play a role in the recruitment of effector proteins. These bind the cytoplasmic tail of transmembrane integrins that have different α and β subunits combined, followed by an integrin-driven cascade signaling activation. Later, the activated signals are responsible for a frequent disaggregation and aggregation between G-actin and F-actin, leading to an active rearrangement of cytoskeleton, along with a relevant reduction in the adhesion and connection between tumor cells. Once the tissue barrier is damaged, tumor cells can migrate through the blood vessels through the activated cytoskeleton. Simultaneously, a rapid cytoskeleton remodeling of platelets in the blood vessel is induced by the Rap1b-integrin signal pathway in response to the tumor rupture and metastasis, to induce more spreading and aggregation of platelets, and the formation of thrombi for an effective hemostasis. Additionally, the spreading and clot retraction of platelet occurs also at the site of invasion after tumor metastasis to allow the invasion.

Manifestations of the diverse functions of Rap1b in other physiological processes

Rap1b exerts significant influence on the precise regulation of the cytoskeleton,23,66 and this regulatory mechanism is extensively involved in various physiological processes beyond tumorigenesis and platelet activation. During cellular migration and differentiation, Rap1b facilitates cytoskeletal remodeling by activating a signaling cascade that drives protrusion at the leading edge of the cell while retracting its tail, thereby propelling forward cell movement.77 Furthermore, Rap1b plays a vital role in regulating the formation and stability of intercellular junctions such as tight junctions and adherens junctions,78 with their maintenance being contingent upon cytoskeletal homeostasis.79 In the nervous system, Rap1b activity is pivotal for establishing neuronal polarity, axon outgrowth, and synaptic plasticity.80 It enhances both stability and directional extension of microtubule networks within neurons, providing pathways for axonal growth while influencing presynaptic and postsynaptic membrane structure and function—thereby modulating neural signal transmission and processing.81 Additionally, Rap1b contributes to regulating cellular immune responses; in immune cells it impacts migration, phagocytosis, and antigen presentation through dynamic alterations in the cytoskeleton,82 thus participating in various aspects of immune surveillance and defense mechanisms. Clearly, Rap1b not only engages in cell migration, differentiation as well as nervous system development but also profoundly influences immune response dynamics—underscoring its critical role across diverse physiological processes.

Rap1b is a regulator of cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton dynamic relies on the three well-defined cytoskeletal polymers (microfilaments, microtubules and intermediate fibers) for the stability of morphological structure and the progress of life activities,83,84 and the integrins were considered as a strong sensor of these cellular behaviors.85 Since the discovery of the first integrin, fibronectin,86,87 a total of 18 α subunits and 8 β subunits were identified so far, generating at least 24 heterodimers through noncovalent binding, and β1 was found to participate in the majority of them.88 Mechanically, the extracellular domain and intracellular tail of integrin itself are connected with specific proteins of extracellular matrix (ECM) and different intracellular cytoskeleton regulatory proteins.89 Generally, an integrin can conduct bidirectional signal transduction with cytoskeleton from cytoskeleton to integrin.90 Integrin is usually dispersed on the cell surface when it is not combined with actin, and not connected to cytoskeleton.91 Once combined with the ligands in ECM, it will relate to cytoskeleton through its intracellular domain and cluster.92 There is feedback between integrin and cytoskeleton regulating the assembly of cytoskeleton, along with the integrin that mediates the adhesion itself activating small G proteins belonging to the Rho family.93 Consequently, through the combination of extracellular and intracellular domains with cytoskeleton, signal transduction molecules and other proteins, the integrin acts as a bridge in order to mediate the two-way information transmission between the microenvironment and the cell.94

The activation of Rap1b was confirmed to be associated with platelet function,18,57,67 malignant tumor metastasis,39,95,96 embryonic development,97,98 and other physiological processes involving cytoskeleton regulation.54,99 It is noteworthy noting that these normal or pathological cell activities are the results of the use of integrin to respond to external signals.31,65,85 Nevertheless, Rap1b is responsible for the switching of the signal to the downstream response effectors and of the effective prenylation of its C-terminal region and effective loading of GTP on the conserved domain (Figure 1).41,55,100 Arguably the mode of action of Rap1b-integrin is not direct, but rather it occurs through a cascade of adaptive molecules.18,63 This post-translational modification not only increases the binding ability of the C-terminal of the positive charge rich Rap1b to the negative charge intima, but also induces the assembly and stability of the integrin activation complex consisting of Rap1b, RIAM (Rap-GTP-interacting adapter molecule).101 integrin, Talin102 or Kindlin.103 Mechanically, Talin-1 is usually randomly distributed within the cytoplasm in an inactive form through intramolecular self-inhibition,104 but after being stimulated by signals and recruited to the cell membrane, it can bind to the cytoplasmic tail of integrin, causing its conformational change and conversion into an active state.105 The interaction between membrane anchored Talin-1 and the cytoplasmic tail of integrin is considered a key step in endowing integrin with internal to external signal transductions.106,107,108 RIAM interacts with Talin-1 through its N-terminal domain as an important adaptor protein that responds to Rap1b activation, inducing Talin-1 to remain on the cell membrane for continuous activation of integrin.104,108 Meanwhile, other adaptor proteins of Kindlin (Kindlin-1, 2 and 3),109 were identified in the Talin-1-mediated integrin activation process with a non-dominant role by providing mechanical force for the spatial conformational changes of integrin.110,111 Moreover, Kindlin was found to be capable of binding the NxxY motif at the distal end of the integrin membrane and recruit paxillin, integrin-linked kinase (ILK) and pseudokinase, in order to activate the integrin cytoplasmic tail. Further, these multi-subunit complexes rely on paxillin-β-Pix signal axis to activate focal adhesion kinase (FAK), CDC42/RAC1, or actin-related protein 2/3 (Arp2/3) complexes, with the effects of regulation of cell migration and adhesion.103,112,113,114 Notably, the protein kinase A (PKA) serves as the upstream node of Rap1b activation,100 undergoing a transition between activation (phosphorylation) and deactivation (dephosphorylation) forms, which in turn promotes the transition of Rap1b to the phosphorylated and prenylated forms, in order to keep Rap1b activation within an overall controllable range.4,100,115 Despite several progress that has been performed in the research of integrin activation to regulate cytoskeleton-mediated morphological changes and participation in physiological processes, the molecular mechanisms underlying Rap1b’s involvement are still not fully understood, in particular the one related to the ability of Rap1b to mobilize the adaptor protein within the integrin-cytoskeleton feedback.

The evolutionary homology of Rap1b and its potentiality in virus infection

Rap1b aa sequences among different species exhibit high homology

To better understand the evolutionary characteristics of Rap1b sequence, a preliminary sequence analysis was conducted. Firstly, the aa sequence from chicken (GenBank number: NP_001007853.1), duck (GenBank number: XP_032040931.1), goose (GenBank number: XP_013053721.1), rabbit (GenBank number: XP_002711365.1), pig (GenBank number: XP_003476149.1), human (GenBank number: NP_001010942.1), mouse (GenBank number: XP_021030062.1), sheep (GenBank number: XP_014950249.1), cattle (GenBank number: NP_787018.1), horse (GenBank number: NP_001240767.1), elephant (GenBank number: XP_003405241.1), rhino (GenBank number: XP_010137005.1), crocodile (GenBank number: XP_019398797.1), python (GenBank number: XP_007423466.1) and turtle (GenBank number: XP_037742927.1) were downloaded from the NCBI database, followed by the Clustera W Method -based analysis using MegAlign Pro 17.1 software (DNASTAR, Madison, WI, USA). The results obtained showed that majority of Rap1b aa sequences in poultry, mammals, and reptiles consist of 184 aa highly conserved among different species and almost identical in most species, suggesting the functional conservation (Figure 3A). However, differences in Rap1b aa sequences were found in individual species, such as horses where the 182 aa changes to glutamine, but its phosphorylation (aa 179 and aa 180) and prenylation (aa 181) did not, consistently with the Rap1b sequence of other species. Subsequently, NCBI-based BLAST alignment analysis was performed among the sequences of varying spinal species (Figure 3B). The results showed that even Rap1b with significant differences showed a homology of over 94.7%.

Although there is no constant pattern of differential aa in these species, it is noteworthy considering that almost all differential sequences changed from serine to threonine, or asparagine at positions 180. However, the hydroxyl groups of these two aa was dehydrate with the phosphate group to form phosphate esters.3,4,57 Therefore, the mutation of tyrosine did not affect the phosphorylation of Rap1b at this site, but this still requires further investigation. In addition, differences in multiple aa were only found in some species and not constantly (Figure 3B), suggesting that they may be adaptive mutations occurring in individual species.116,117 Surprisingly, even for these species with significant aa differences, the aa in position 179 (serine) and 181 (cysteine) remained unchanged. Although the aa 180, which plays a crucial role for serine’s phosphorylation, undergoes evolution, this does not affect its phosphorylation, with subsequent regular activation of Rap1b.100,115

Rap1b in membrane distribution is associated with virus infection

The online website “The Human Protein Atlas” (https://www.proteinatlas.org/search/Rap1b) predicted that Rap1b is distributed throughout the human organism, in a moderate amount in the liver but highly abundant in the spleen. In addition, the signal peptide prediction demonstrated that no signal peptide sequence is present, indicating that its function is more likely regulated by post-translational modification rather than the signal peptide sequence, consistently with the research results about the Rap1b regulating activation and cell localization through phosphorylation (serine 179 and 180) and prenylation (cysteine 181) sites.3 More importantly, the subcellular localization analysis suggested that Rap1b is a potential transmembrane protein, and that it undergoes prenylation and activation by altering its aa composition to increase the affinity with the cell membrane (phosphorylation is responsible for its repulsion with cell membrane proteins). Therefore, it was speculated that Rap1b may be converted into a transmembrane form and function after activation, however, the specific mechanism is still unknown. Considering the preliminary analysis of Rap1b and model of action in platelets,14,105 cancers52,102 and viruses,23,30 we speculated that Rap1b, as a cell membrane protein with a conserved function, is located on the cell membrane following the activation and it may react to the external environment by regulating the cytoskeleton-mediated changes at a cell membrane level.

It is well known that the incorporation of the viral particle into the host cell is a prerequisite for the establishment of an effective infection,118 and the cell membrane, as the primary barrier, is widely involved during the virus entry, replication, and release by various viruses, such as severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SAR-CoV-2),119 human immunodeficiency virus 1 (HIV-1),120 herpes simplex virus type I (HSV-1)121 and rubella virus (RV),122 with the morphological changes of the membrane, including protrusion, invagination, and polarization driven by the cytoskeleton.123 Additionally, the regulatory relationship between Rap1b and cytoskeleton has been extensively confirmed by several studies.14,23,24,76,124 Our previous research confirmed that Rap1b regulates the cytoskeleton by recruiting integrin-activated linking molecules after the cascade signal and subsequently activating Rho proteins, and contributes to early infection of aHEV. At the same time, inhibitors that block Rap1b activation significantly inhibit viral infection while also slowing cytoskeletal rearrangement,23 indicating a strong link between viruses and the Rap1b-cytoskeleton feedback. However, there is little research on the participation of Rap1b in virus infection by regulating cytoskeleton (Table 3).23,30,38,125,126 Accordingly, it was hypothesized that Rap1b is involved in the steps related to cell membrane for several virus infections by regulating cytoskeleton, and further validations with different types of cells should be conducted in permissive cells.

Summary of the mechanism of Rap1b involvement in virus infection

Rap1b plays a crucial role during the whole virus infection period

Actin, as the main network structure regulating the cytoskeleton, in a polymerizate and depolymerized form is involved in the expansion, invagination, migration and other cellular morphological changes of cells.127 Several viruses, including murine leukemia virus (MLV),128 coronavirus (CoV),129 influenza A virus (IAV),130 respiratory syncytial virus (RSV),131 and HSV-1132 were found to use the cytoskeleton to invade host cells. It is generally recognized that virus infection initiates from the gliding of virion in host cells, followed by a receptor mediated entry, intracellular replication, assembly, and release, all steps completed through the participation of the cytoskeleton (Figure 4).133 Consistently with the causal relationship between Rap1b and cytoskeleton,24,66 and with the association between viruses and cytoskeleton, the molecular mechanisms of Rap1b-cytoskeleton response may be used as target to prevent or treat virus infection.

Hypothetical model of Rap1b contribution to viral infection through cytoskeleton in permissive cells

Once bound with the receptors in a permissive cell, the virus-receptor binding complexes can activate multiple effectors on the cell membrane or within the cell, followed by the activation of the downstream cascade signaling pathways including the one mediated by Rap1b, to allow a smoother progression of the viral life cycle and cell-to-cell infection. During these processes, the outer membrane is prepared for the virus attachment into cells, prerequisite for an efficient virus infection. Once the virus is internalized, it takes advantage of the mechanical forces or motion trajectories generated by the cytoskeleton of the cell to efficiently replicate and produce progeny virus. Subsequently, viral particles are released from the host cell using the cytoskeleton-mediated membrane release mechanism and infect the following cells through two main modalities: cell-to-cell infection and random infection during vascular wandering.

Entry

Virions attach on the extracellular surface initially, followed by binding to specific or non-specific membrane receptors, and in signal transduction and following entry, which is primarily characterized by obvious membrane invagination in the sites of virus aggregation.134 The time continuous confocal microscopy analysis of porcine hemagglutinating encephalomyelitis virus (PHEV) infection in N2a cells showed that F-actin-triggered membrane deformation, which was induced by integrin α5/β1, and its internalization was significantly observed about 20 min after viral attachment.135 On the contrary, the treatment of cells with CytoD, an inhibitor of actin depolymerization, significantly inhibited the morphological changes induced by the cytoskeleton, and the early infection phases (attachment and entry) were reduced as well.23,135 Li et al., also demonstrated that rabies virus (RABV) also utilizes actin and actin-based cytoskeleton to enter the Vero cells during clathrin-mediated endocytosis.136 Likewise, rotavirus was found to indirectly influence the actin microfilaments dynamics to achieve a smoother progress of virus internalization by regulation of membrane remodeling though correlated effectors, such as actin-binding proteins and small GTPase proteins following the onset of virus infection.137 Interestingly, similar mechanisms were also observed in our previous studies on early HEV infection.23,138 Additionally, the flaviviruses structure proteins can be in contact with actin filament and microtubules directly to facilitate virus entry through conformational changes of cell cytoskeleton.139 Overall, viruses that cross the cell membrane barrier should be classified as transmembrane movement of cargos, while virus-cytoskeleton interactions are all associated with the progression of the infections.140,141

Trafficking

To enter the host cell, viruses can penetrate the plasma membrane or enter through endocytosis. Once in the cell, viruses should reach the nucleus through the cytoplasm, where they can hijack the host-cell machinery and replicate.142 The composition of cytoskeleton (microfilaments, intermediate filaments, and microtubules) plays a crucial role in the intracellular movement of viruses.133,143 In detail, microtubules are the largest component of the cytoskeleton and are involved in the transport of organelles and vesicles throughout the cell. Motor proteins, such as dynein and kinesin, are located along microtubules and carry the cargo to their respective destinations.144 Some viruses, such as HIV-1, utilizes the dynein motor protein to shuttle along microtubules toward the nucleus, allowing the virus to reach its site of replication.145 Other viruses, such as adenovirus (AdV), use microtubules less extensively relying more on actin filaments.146 Actin filaments are thinner than microtubules, but more in abundance and form a dense network of fibers throughout the cytoplasm. Actin networks are involved in many cellular processes, including the formation of pseudopodia involved in the trapping of extracellular particles.147,148 Viruses, such as vaccinia virus (VACV), can exploit the actin-based network in order to move within the cytoplasm and reach their destination.149 For instance, VACV was demonstrated to hijack the actin polymerization to promote its own intracellular movement.150 Microtubules and actin filaments both play important roles in intracellular virus transport,19 as by manipulating the host cell’s cytoskeleton, viruses can efficiently reach the sites of replication evading the host’s immune system.137,151,152

Propagation

The cytoskeleton is also involved in intracellular replication processes, such as viral replication and bacterial intracellular survival through organizing and transporting viral and bacterial components to a specific site within the host cell, facilitating the formation of replication complexes, packaging new viral or bacterial components, and aiding in the budding process of them from the host cell.21,153,154 The microtubule network and actin cytoskeleton play a crucial role in locating the anchor viral or bacterial proteins, as well as in the organization and transport of components to the assemble sites.155,156 The cytoskeleton is involved in intracellular replication processes by facilitating the formation of replication complexes,157,158 that are necessary for viral genome replication and assembly of new virus particles. This requires the coordination of multiple proteins and cellular components, and is involved in the movement of virus within the host cell, such as IAV,159 HIV-1145 and vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV).160 Furthermore, cytoskeleton is also involved in the regulation of cellular signaling pathways for intracellular replication processes,134 transporting the signaling molecules to the appropriate subcellular locations.161 In this regard, the microtubule network transports the signaling molecules to the nucleus, where they can regulate gene expression and control cellular processes.162 Furthermore, the regulation of cell division is another function of the cytoskeleton during the virus infection.20 In this regard, the cytoskeleton helps to organize and separate the chromosomes, ensuring that each new cell receives a complete set,163 and it is involved also in the maintenance of cellular homeostasis, which is critical for the survival of cells and viral infection.164,165 In summary, the cytoskeleton is a complex network of protein fibers that plays critical roles in intracellular replication processes, including transport of viral components to the site of replication,159 packaging and release of new virus,131 and regulation of cellular signaling and cell division.134 The understanding of the mechanisms of the cytoskeleton’s role in intracellular replication processes may provide insights into the pathogenesis of infectious diseases leading to the development of new therapeutic approaches.

Egress

Viral egress is the process by which newly formed viruses are released from the host cell and spread to other cells.166,167 The cytoskeleton is involved in various steps of viral egress, such as the transport of viral components to the site of assembly, assembly and budding of new virions, and the release of new virions from the host cell.168 For the first step, the cytoskeleton provides the structural support necessary for the transport of viral components,169 through the microtubule network as viral components are bounded to molecular motors, such as dynein, which walk along microtubules carrying the viral components toward the site of assembly.170 As for the second step, that is to say the assembly and budding of new virions,131 a viral protein shell is formed around the genetic material of the virus, which is then packaged into new virus particles. In this regard, the cytoskeleton is responsible for a proper packaging and assembly, since the actin is involved in the formation of the viral envelope, which is essential for the budding of new virus particles,171,172 and in the formation of the contractile ring, which is necessary for the separation of the virus particle from the host cell membrane.173,174 As for the last step, the budding, the microtubule network guides the budding of new virus particles toward areas of the host cell membrane that are rich in lipids, which is important for the successful release of new virus particles.121,175 Understanding the role of the cytoskeleton in viral egress is important for developing new therapies to prevent the spread of viral infections.

Conclusion

The cytoskeleton is a dynamic network of protein fibers that provides a structural support to cells being involved in many cellular processes, including viral entry, intracellular transport, and egress.176 Recent studies demonstrated that Rap1b may be involved in the regulation of several human viruses, including HIV, HCV (hepatitis C virus), and HEV.15,23,177 The regulation of the cytoskeleton dynamics by Rap1b has potentially broad research applications for the development of new preventive and therapeutic strategies for viral infections. For example, Rap1b could be a potential target for inhibiting viral replication and blocking viral entry into host cells, as well as to disrupt viral transport and egress, which are critical for viral spread within the host.23 Therefore, the understanding of the role of Rap1b in virus infection could lead to the development of new antiviral drugs targeting the cytoskeleton in order to inhibit the viral replication and spread. Notably, Rap1b is likely a protein with a conserved function in different species, and with a strong regulatory relationship with the physiological process of cytoskeleton and cell membrane, a barrier for the virus entry or release from cells. Currently, only a limited number of studies are available on Rap1b-mediated regulation of cytoskeleton during virus infection,23,30 and most of the research is only reported on the regulation of cell deformation by virus hijacking and on the role of Rap1b in the development of a favorable physical environment for early viral entry.23 However, there is still a lack of systematic and comprehensive research on the activation response of Rap1b after virus triggering, as well as on the mechanisms underlying the initiation of signal cascade reactions to change the cellular environment and promote the completion of the virus’s life cycle, as in the case of HSV-1 due to the inefficiency of the treatment methods in epithelial cells and nervous system.178,179

In summary, Rap1b plays a significant role in the process of virus infection by regulating the cytoskeleton, and it is considered a preferred candidate for developing antiviral drugs that disrupt cytoskeleton dynamics while inhibiting the viral replication. Additionally, the interaction between Rap1b and viral proteins could be a potential target to experiment further therapeutic approaches. More importantly, the knowledge gained about the role of Rap1b in virus infection could also have broader implications for understanding the regulation of cytoskeleton dynamics in other cellular processes, such as cell division, migration, and differentiation, which can be object of future investigations on antiviral strategies.

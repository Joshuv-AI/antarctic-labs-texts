Prognostic value of systemic inflammation indices in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer treated with TAS-102: a single-center retrospective real-world study
Authors: Ming Liu, Yue Cui, Lili Chen, Feifei Zhang, Fangqi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile the efficacy of trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102) in late-line metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) is established in clinical trials, real-world predictive and prognostic factors remain poorly characterized. This study aimed to identify these factors in mCRC patients receiving TAS-102 at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University.MethodsData were extracted from The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University to evaluate the clinical outcomes of microsatellite stable mCRC patients treated with TAS-102 in late-line between 2019 and 2025. Pan-immune-inflammation value (PIV), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) values, neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) before treatment and other potential risk factors were retrospectively collected. The receiver operating characteristic curves were applied to determine the cut-off values of PIV, SII, NLR, and PLR in patients. The risk factors for progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were compared with Kaplan-Meier curves and determined by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. Factors with P<0.05 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate analysis.ResultsA total of 61 mCRC patients were enrolled. In the multivariate analysis of PFS, two favorable indices were significant: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS) <2 (P=0.03) and SII <650 (P=0.003). Two independent prognostic factors identified in the multivariate analysis of OS were ECOG-PS <2 (P=0.002) and NLR <2.35 (P=0.03).ConclusionsOur findings demonstrate that pretreatment inflammatory indices (NLR and SII) combined with baseline ECOG-PS independently identify mCRC patients likely to derive clinical benefit from TAS-102 therapy.Keywords: Metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), systemic inflammatory markers, trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102), prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• In metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) patients receiving late-line trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102) therapy, a baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS) of 0–1 independently predicted better progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).

• A low pretreatment systemic immune-inflammation index (SII, defined as platelets × neutrophils/lymphocytes) (<650) was an independent predictor for longer PFS.

• A low pretreatment neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR, defined as neutrophil count/lymphocyte count) (<2.35) was an independent predictor for longer OS.

What is known and what is new?

• Systemic inflammatory indices such as NLR and SII are recognized prognostic markers in colorectal cancer. ECOG-PS is a general prognostic factor in oncology.

• This study specifically identifies and validates pretreatment SII and NLR as novel, independent prognostic biomarkers for PFS and OS, respectively, in the context of late-line TAS-102 therapy for mCRC. The optimal cut-off values (SII <650, NLR <2.35) are defined for this treatment setting.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The findings imply that a simple, low-cost panel combining ECOG-PS, SII, and NLR can help stratify mCRC patients likely to benefit from TAS-102. In clinical practice, these readily available parameters should be integrated into pretreatment assessment to guide more personalized therapeutic decisions, potentially optimizing patient selection for TAS-102 treatment.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the second leading cause of cancer-related fatality and the third most commonly diagnosed cancer globally, accounting for nearly 10% of all cancer cases (1). In China, CRC ranks as the second most common cancer, as well as the most commonly diagnosed cancer with an estimated 517,100 new cases resulting in approximately 240,000 deaths annually, making it the fourth leading cause of cancer-related death (2). Over 20% of CRC patients are diagnosed with metastatic disease, with the rate plummeting to around 15% due to the high metastatic potential and chemoresistance (3,4).

There has been little new progression in systemic treatments recently, as the standard of care in first- and second-line regimens for mCRC remains chemotherapy including oxaliplatin, irinotecan, and fluoropyrimidine-based agents (5). For right-sided or RAS-mutant tumors, the addition of antiangiogenic antibodies to standard therapy has been shown to improve clinical outcomes. Similarly, in patients with left-sided or RAS wild-type tumors, combining epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-targeted antibodies with chemotherapy also demonstrates enhanced efficacy (6-8). However, the option of systemic treatment, particularly chemotherapy regimens, remains limited for unselected mCRC patients in third or later-line treatment (9-11).

Trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102) is a novel oral chemotherapeutic drug, which combines trifluridine, a thymidine analog, and tipiracil, a thymidine phosphorylase inhibitor. The synergistic mechanism demonstrates prolonged antitumor activity beyond that of monotherapy (12). These findings led to Food and Drug Administration (FDA)/European Medicines Agency (EMA) approvals for third-line mCRC treatment (13). While clinical trials demonstrate that TAS-102 confers modest but significant overall survival (OS) improvements in late-line (>2 lines) mCRC patients, the limited durability of these benefits highlights an urgent need for clinically applicable prognostic biomarkers to optimize the patient selection.

Systemic inflammation has long been linked to CRC progression and chemoresistance, with inflammatory indices as practical prognostic tools. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) reflect the imbalance between pro-tumor inflammation and anti-tumor immunity, while the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) enhances assessment by integrating multiple indices. The pan-immune inflammation value (PIV) further provides insights into monocyte-related immune crosstalk (14,15). Despite the established role of these inflammatory indices in CRC biology, their performance under the unique myelosuppressive and immune-modulating effects of TAS-102 remains unknown. Given the increasing use of TAS-102 in late-line mCRC patients, inexpensive and dynamic biomarkers are urgently needed. This study evaluated the predictive value of these inflammatory indices in order to identify potential prognostic factors, thereby optimizing personalized treatment strategies for mCRC patients receiving TAS-102-based regimens. We present this article in accordance with the REMARK reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-aw-2406/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

We retrospectively evaluated data from patients with histopathologically confirmed metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma who received TAS-102 treatment at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University from September 2019 to March 2025.

All the enrolled patients had advanced disease prior to TAS-102, were at least 18 years old, and had previously undergone ≥2-line standard treatments (including fluorouracil, irinotecan, oxaliplatin, bevacizumab, or cetuximab) until disease progression or treatment intolerance. Patients were in generally good condition, with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS) scores of 0–2. Patients who could not be followed up further due to referral, loss to follow-up, incomplete, or missing key records were excluded (Figure 1).

Flow chart of the study. mCRC, metastatic colorectal cancer; TAS-102, trifluridine/tipiracil.

Treatment

All patients were treated with TAS-102 at the starting dose of 35 mg/m2 administered orally twice daily on days 1–5 of each 7-day cycle (2 weeks of treatment followed by 14 days of rest). Treatment continued until disease progression according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1, or unacceptable toxicity based on Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Treatment efficacy was evaluated every two cycles using contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) of the chest/abdomen/pelvis, serum tumor markers [carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9)], and clinical symptom assessment.

Study population and data collection

Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, data from 61 patients with metastatic CRC who were treated with TAS-102 at least 1 cycle and received more than one line therapy prior between September 2019 to October 2024 were retrospectively evaluated. Clinical basic and pathological characteristics, as well as treatment data, were collected from the selected patients. These data included sex, age, survival status, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS), tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, and disease characteristics such as primary tumor location and resection status, history of therapy (number of different lines of therapy), RAS and BRAF mutational status, microsatellite status, the interval from disease diagnosis or first-line therapy to the initiation of TAS-102, the number of cycles of TAS-102 therapy, and baseline parameters. The baseline parameters consisted of hematological parameters (neutrophils, lymphocytes, platelets, monocytes, serum albumin, hemoglobin, and tumor markers such as CA19-9 and CEA) measured within one week before TAS-102 therapy. Moreover, inflammatory indices, including NLR defined as neutrophil/lymphocyte, SII calculated as platelet × neutrophil/lymphocyte, PLR defined as platelet/lymphocyte, and PIV calculated as neutrophil count × platelet count × monocyte count/lymphocyte count, were retrospectively collected.

Statistical analysis

For the statistical evaluation of the study data, the SPSS 25.0 software package was utilized, with a two-tailed significance criterion set at α=0.05. Results with a P value less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The construction of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, along with the generation of survival curves, was accomplished through the use of R (version 3.6.3) and GraphPad Prism (version 9.0, USA) software platforms.

Statistical processing was conducted with two-tailed α=0.05 significance threshold (P<0.05). ROC curves for inflammatory indices (NLR, SII, PLR, and PIV) were generated using Youden’s index to determine optimal cutoff values based on OS classification. Survival endpoints were defined as follows: OS as the interval from TAS-102 initiation to death from any cause, and PFS as the time from TAS-102 initiation to first radiographically or clinically confirmed disease progression or death. Kaplan-Meier curves were constructed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and compared via log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied for both univariate and multivariate analyses: initial univariate screening identified variables with P<0.05, which were subsequently entered into a backward stepwise multivariate model (retention threshold P<0.05) to identify independent prognostic factors. Results were expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The diagnostic performance of inflammatory indices was quantified by area under the ROC curve (AUC). All analyses adhered to a 5% significance threshold, and no multiplicity adjustments were applied.

Ethical statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (approval No. YX2025-110). All patient information was collected and processed in strict confidentiality, and all data were anonymized to protect patient privacy. The requirement for individual patient informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics

This study enrolled 61 consecutive patients with mCRC treated with TAS-102. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age of the patients was 59.5 years (range, 40–83 years). Forty-one patients (68%) were male, and 84% had left-sided or rectal tumors at baseline. The majority of patients had an ECOG-PS of 0 or 1 (79%) and exhibited microsatellite stability (97%). Prior treatment history revealed extensive systemic exposure, with 54 patients (88.5%) having received ≥3 lines of therapy for advanced disease. In 88% of cases, at least three prior systemic treatment regimens for advanced disease had been administered. RAS and BRAF mutations were detected in 29 (48%) and 1 (2%) cases, respectively. Based on the RAS/RAF mutation status, 40 (66%) patients had received an anti-vascularendothelial growth factor (VEGF) monoclonal antibody, while 13 (21%) had received an anti-EGFR monoclonal antibody as part of their previous therapies. All patients had complete datasets for inflammatory indices of NLR, PLR, PIV, and SII.

Prognostic of survival in the univariate and multivariate analyses

In the patient cohort, the median PFS was 2.37 months (95% CI: 1.5–3.23), and the median OS was 9.5 months (95% CI: 7.2–12). The results of the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis are presented in Figure 3. Variables that demonstrated an association with PFS or OS in the univariate analysis were incorporated into the Cox proportional hazards multivariate models. The outcomes of the multivariate analysis examining the associations between each variable (ECOG, NLR, SII, PIV, and PLR) and PFS or OS are detailed in Table 2. In our research, elevated levels of SII, PIV, and PLR, along with an ECOG-PS of 2, were found to be linked to a heightened risk of disease progression. Moreover, these factors also indicated a poorer OS rate. The findings suggest that SII and ECOG-PS are independent predictors of PFS, while NLR and ECOG-PS serve as independent predictors of OS. Patients with an ECOG-PS of 0–1 exhibited significantly better median PFS [hazard ratio (HR) =0.39, 95% CI: 0.171–0.887, P=0.03; Figure 4A] compared to those with an ECOG-PS of ≥2. A high level of SII was correlated with unfavorable PFS (HR =0.302, 95% CI: 0.135–0.674, P=0.003; Figure 4B). Regarding OS, better OS was observed in patients with an ECOG-PS of 0–1 (HR =0.253, 95% CI: 0.106–0.604, P=0.002; Figure 4C), while elevated NLR was associated with poor OS (HR =0.250, 95% CI: 0.070–0.895, P=0.03; Figure 4D). Discussion

CRC patients often exhibit extended survival durations, making the strategic sequencing of late-line treatment strategies particularly crucial. While TAS-102 has emerged as the sole newly recommended chemotherapeutic agent gradually implemented in clinical practice (16-18), a key challenge persists in identifying biomarkers that can reliably predict treatment response and guide patient selection.

Emerging evidence highlights that chronic inflammation and immune evasion, as hallmark features of cancer, synergistically drive disease progression and impair survival across multiple solid tumors, including CRC by remodeling the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and disrupting cytokine homeostasis (19,20). Preclinical studies indicate that elevated levels of neutrophils, platelets, and monocytes drive tumor progression through cytokine/chemokine secretion, while lymphocytes exhibit antitumor effects by stimulating cytotoxic immune responses. Specifically, platelets have been implicated in triggering epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in circulating tumor cells, aiding their spread to distant organ sites (21,22). Neutrophils release pro-angiogenic factors like VEGF and matrix-remodeling proteases to facilitate adhesion and metastatic site formation (23,24). Conversely, lymphocytes serve a pivotal function in antitumor immunity by mediating cytotoxic elimination of malignant cells and suppressing neoplastic cell growth and metastatic dissemination, thereby dictating the host’s adaptive defense mechanisms against cancer progression (25). Subsequent studies have revealed that inflammatory-related biomarkers, such as NLR, PLR, PIV, and SII reflect the equilibrium between protumor inflammation and antitumor immunity, serving as prognostic predictors in CRC patients (26-32).

Eraslan et al. systematically evaluated inflammatory indices [NLR, PLR, LMR, SII, Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI)] in neoadjuvant-treated locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC), identifying SII <748.34 as the sole independent predictor of pathological complete response (pCR) (OR: 0.471, 95% CI: 0.224–0.991; P=0.047) (33). A parallel analysis by Passardi et al. A study in 289 mCRC patients further validated the clinical relevance of pretreatment inflammatory scores. Survival outcomes significantly favored cohorts with baseline SII <730, NLR <3, and PLR <169 (34). Furthermore, the prognostic power of systemic inflammation indices may be linked to their reflection of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which is increasingly recognized as a key determinant of therapeutic efficacy. Notably, recent evidence suggests that certain chemotherapeutic agents, including fluoropyrimidine-based regimens, can themselves exert immunomodulatory effects that may interact with this baseline inflammatory state (35). This interplay underscores the complexity of using inflammatory markers to guide therapy with agents like TAS-102.

Synthesizing cumulative evidence, we investigated the prognostic utility of systemic inflammatory indices (NLR, PLR, SII, and PIV) in CRC. The prognostic capacity of systemic inflammatory indices, particularly SII and NLR, demonstrates critical clinical relevance in mCRC patients receiving TAS-102 therapy (AUC >0.80), corroborates its dual role in reflecting both inflammatory burden and immune dysfunction, which are critical determinants of resistance to chemotherapy. Patients were stratified into low-SII and high-SII groups based on the optimal cutoff value of SII [650]. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed significantly prolonged PFS in the low-SII group versus the high-SII cohort. Using the predefined NLR cutoff, patients were categorized into low and high NLR groups. Cox regression analysis revealed that the low NLR group had significantly longer OS than the high NLR group. These results align with findings from Chen et al. and Xie et al. The prognostic cut-off values identified in our study (SII <650, NLR <2.35) are generally lower than some commonly cited thresholds in the broader mCRC literature (36,37). This discrepancy may be attributable to the distinct characteristics of our cohort—specifically, patients receiving third-line or later therapy who may exhibit a greater systemic inflammatory burden—as well as differences in the methodological approach for determining optimal cut-offs. While SII and NLR demonstrate clinical utility in CRC prognosis, integration of their evaluation with conventional clinicopathological indices may enhance risk stratification and therapeutic decision-making in CRC management.

Apart from the aforementioned findings, our multivariate analysis revealed ECOG-PS ≥2 as the sole independent negative prognosticator in TAS-102-treated mCRC patients, diverging from previous reports by demonstrating non-significant associations for tumor location, CEA levels, or metastatic organs. Notably, this study establishes NLR and SII as novel independent prognosticators in TAS-102 therapy, potentially reflecting the drug’s mechanism in targeting inflammation-associated chemoresistance.

The differential association of SII with PFS and NLR with OS may reflect a distinction between predictive and prognostic utility. A predictive factor is specifically linked to response to a given therapy (here, PFS on TAS-102), while a prognostic factor informs the overall disease course irrespective of treatment (reflected in OS). Thus, SII could be interpreted as a potential predictive marker for TAS-102 efficacy, whereas NLR served as a robust prognostic marker for OS in this cohort. These observations, generated from a retrospective study, warrant prospective validation.

All in all, these findings collectively propose that baseline ECOG-PS, NLR, and SII collectively demonstrate robust prognostic utility for clinical outcome prediction in TAS-102-treated mCRC populations. While demonstrating potential status for routine prognostication in clinical practice, inherent limitations warrant consideration: (I) the retrospective design introduces selection bias due to its single-center nature and limited sample size (n=61) retrospective observational methodology with inherent selection bias (n=61); (II) the absence of prospective data collection and long-term follow-up may limit the generalizability of our findings. Future studies should prioritize multi-center, prospective, large-scale cohorts with extended follow-up periods to validate these prognostic biomarkers and establish their clinical utility.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that ECOG-PS is an independent prognostic factor for both PFS and OS in mCRC patients receiving TAS-102 therapy. Among pre-treatment inflammatory indices, SII independently predicts PFS, while NLR independently predicts OS. These low-cost, accessible biomarkers (derived from routine clinical assessment and peripheral blood tests) offer a convenient approach to predict TAS-102 efficacy, supporting the integration of ECOG-PS, SII, and NLR into clinical decision-making for late-line mCRC. Prioritizing TAS-102 for patients with “ECOG-PS 0–1 + SII <650 + NLR <2.35” can maximize survival benefits and reduce futile treatment, optimizing personalized therapeutic strategies. Future prospective validation with larger cohorts is warranted to consolidate these findings.

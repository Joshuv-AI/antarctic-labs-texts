Antibacterial activity and molecular docking studies of 1-(benzothiazolylamino)aryl methyl-2-naphthol catalyzed by steel waste-sourced iron oxide nanoparticles as a heterogeneous catalyst
Authors: Parisa Gholami-HasanAbadi, Javad Safaei-Ghomi, Elahe Mashhadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtracting and reusing valuable components from iron-rich waste materials pose significant challenges but are crucial for economic benefits and environmental sustainability. In a recent study, Particles of γ-Fe2O3 were produced from different iron-rich waste materials. Additionally, a simple, efficient, and eco-friendly procedure for synthesizing 1-(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthol derivatives was studied using iron oxide nanoparticles obtained from steel waste recycling as a catalyst. The final products were thoroughly characterized by FT-IR, 1H NMR, and 13C NMR spectroscopy, while the structure of γ-Fe2O3 was comprehensively analyzed using XRD, FT-IR, SEM, VSM, and TGA techniques. Molecular docking analysis was conducted to position the compounds within the active site of E. coli 2EG7, aiming to understand their potential mechanism of action, binding affinity, and how the molecules are oriented at the receptor's active site. The results revealed that all the synthesized compounds interact with the agonist within the active site of the 2EG7 protein. These findings suggest promising potential for these compounds as effective antibacterial agents. Introduction

A key objective of sustainable waste management is to enhance recycling and reutilization [1]. Steel, being a major contributor to metal waste, is a fully recyclable material that retains its original properties after recycling. Therefore, its reuse helps reduce the demand for raw material extraction, lowers energy consumption, and minimizes water and soil pollution [2]. Therefore, recovering valuable products from iron-rich steel waste not only reduces environmental hazards but also helps preserve critical metal resources [2, 3]. Large quantities of iron-bearing waste materials, produced globally contain approximately 72% iron, amounting to nearly five million tons annually. These wastes can be utilized to extract different forms of iron oxides [4]. Among these, maghemite (γ-Fe₂O₃) stands out as the second most stable iron oxide, known for its wide range of applications.[5]. In this regard, steel, which significantly contributes to metal waste, is a recyclable material that preserves its initial characteristics after recycling. The reuse of steel reduces the demand for raw material extraction, lowers energy consumption, and mitigates water and soil pollution [8]. Steel waste can be converted into new iron-based compounds through various physical and chemical processes, notably acid digestion [9], With the aim of integrating circular economy principles and sustainable catalyst development [6, 7], this study employed a facile and scalable acid digestion strategy for the valorization of steel waste, directly transforming it into an efficient magnetic nanocatalyst. As will be detailed in the Experimental Section, this approach successfully yielded maghemite (γ-Fe₂O₃) nanoparticles characterized by their strong magnetic response, facilitating easy recovery [8, 9]. This method allows the synthesis of iron oxides with tailored structural and morphological properties suitable for multiple applications This method allows the synthesis of iron oxides with tailored structural and morphological suitable for multiple applications [10]. The adoption of heterogeneous catalysis is a fundamental pillar of modern sustainable chemical processes, primarily due to its ability to overcome the significant practical limitations associated with homogeneous catalytic systems [10, 11] While homogeneous catalysts often exhibit high activity, their industrial application is severely constrained by intrinsic difficulties in separation from the final product mixture and poor recyclability. These challenges inevitably lead to substantial chemical waste generation, increased operational costs, and environmental concerns [10, 12]. In contrast, heterogeneous catalysts exist in a distinct solid phase, enabling straightforward separation from liquid reaction media through simple filtration or centrifugation. This inherent characteristic facilitates multiple recovery and reuse cycles, significantly enhancing process economics and reducing environmental impact by minimizing waste [11, 13]. These operational advantages are further enhanced when the solid catalyst is magnetic, allowing for its rapid and selective retrieval from the reaction mixture using an external magnet without requiring energy-intensive separation techniques [14, 15]. The overarching drive toward sustainability has also inspired innovative paradigms in catalyst synthesis. A prominent strategy involves the valorization of industrial waste streams as alternative raw materials, a concept that transforms environmental liabilities into valuable functional nanomaterials. Within this context, advancements in nanotechnology have consistently highlighted the efficacy of magnetic iron oxide nanoarchitectures, particularly maghemite (γ-Fe₂O₃) and magnetite (Fe₃O₄), as robust, stable, and highly active heterogeneous catalysts for diverse organic transformations, including multicomponent reactions (MCRs) [8, 16]. Recent reviews have comprehensively documented the pivotal role of heterogeneous nanocatalysts in green synthesis methodologies, emphasizing their exceptional recyclability and minimal environmental footprint [17]. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that the design of magnetic heterogeneous catalysts enables facile recovery and multiple reusability, leading to significantly improved sustainability of chemical processes [18]. Their inherent Lewis acidity, combined with high surface-to-volume ratio and convenient magnetic responsivity, establishes them as ideal candidates for designing efficient and environmentally benign synthetic protocols [17, 18]. If Moreover, synthesizing iron oxides via acid digestion of ferrous metal compounds contributes to creating a cycle of pollutant control from waste materials [19]. The design, synthesis, and application of supported nanoparticles derived from recycled steel waste as nanostructured catalysts have recently attracted considerable attention, especially in facilitating important organic transformations [20]. This study presents the sustainable utilization of recycled steel waste as a valuable source for synthesis of iron oxide nanoparticles, which serve as efficient heterogeneous acidic catalysts in the one-pot synthesis of 1-(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthol derivatives under environmentally benign conditions. Iron oxide nanoparticles, due to their magnetic properties and high chemical and thermal stability, are recognized as highly efficient heterogeneous Lewis acid catalysts, They can act directly as active catalysts in various synthetic reactions, particularly multicomponent syntheses, under environmentally benign conditions without requiring additional coatings or functional groups. Their easy magnetic separation and the possibility of repeated reuse without significant loss of catalytic activity make these nanocatalysts an economical and eco-friendly choice. Consequently, the use of iron oxide nanoparticles as heterogeneous Lewis acid catalysts enables high-yield chemical reactions under simple operational conditions and plays a vital role in advancing sustainable and efficient organic synthesis processes [21–23]. Unlike previous studies relying on highpurity raw materials, our approach combines waste valorization with catalyst development, offering a cost-effective and environmentally friendly method for nanocatalyst production. The produced nanocatalyst demonstrates notable catalytic activity, facile magnetic recovery, and reusability in multicomponent reactions, collectively enhancing process sustainability and operational practicality. Moreover, the antimicrobial efficacy of synthesized compounds has been thoroughly validated through in vitro biological assays and molecular docking simulations [24–30]. Multicomponent reactions (MCRs), involving the simultaneous reaction of three or more components in a single step, have gained increased attention recently for providing efficient and versatile synthetic route [31–34]. These compounds are of therapeutic importance and are valuable in various other applications [35]. The benzothiazole structure is of great importance due to its pharmacological, biological, agricultural, and electrochemical significance, which enhances the value of derivatives such as 1-(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthols [36, 37]. Furthermore, to gain deeper mechanistic insight and understand the molecular-level interactions, molecular docking studies were employed as an essential and complementary tool to the in vitro biological evaluations. These computational studies are crucial not only for predicting the binding mode and orientation of the synthesized ligands within the active sites of putative protein targets (essential bacterial enzymes) but also for establishing a structure–activity relationship (SAR) model to guide the rational design and optimization of pharmacologic agents with higher potency and selectivity [36, 38]. This integrated approach (synthesis, biological evaluation, and computational studies) is widely adopted in rational drug and bioactive agent design, including the development of new antifungal, antiviral, and particularly antimicrobial and herbicidal agents [39–42]. For instance, similar studies on dihydropyridine (DHP) and β-amino carbonyl derivatives have effectively demonstrated the power of this strategy in identifying lead compounds with promising bioactivity and predicting their pharmacokinetic and toxicity (ADMET) profiles [43, 44]. The antimicrobial efficacy of the synthesized compounds has been thoroughly evaluated via in vitro biological assays and molecular docking simulations. Molecular docking, a key computational technique in drug development, identifies optimal ligandbinding orientations and affinities within target receptors, aiding in understanding antibacterial mechanisms [45, 46]. The ultimate goal is to develop a molecular template based on the structural features that contribute to antibacterial activity. In this study, through molecular docking and biological evaluation, the synthesized 1(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthol derivatives showed promising antibacterial properties. This combined approach of waste valorization, catalytic application, and bioactive compound synthesis promotes sustainable processes and contributes new insights into eco-friendly catalysis and drugdiscovery (Fig. 1).

Investigation of catalyst recovery and reusability

Easy separation and recovery are among the most important features of magnetic catalysts. In this section, the separation process and reusability of the γ-Fe₂O₃ nanocatalyst are investigated. In the synthesis of 1-((benzothiazolylamino)methyl)-2-naphthol derivatives, after the reaction completion, the catalyst is magnetically separated from the reaction mixture and washed several times with ethanol to remove any remaining organic impurities. The catalyst is then dried in an oven at 60 °C for 6 h and prepared for the next cycle. The results indicate that this recovered catalyst can be effectively reused up to 5 times without a significant loss in its catalytic activity. This observation demonstrates the high chemical stability and excellent recoverability of this magnetic nanocatalyst.

Molecular docking to predict and analyze the interactions between these compounds

The primary aim of this study is to investigate the antibacterial properties of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)methyl-2-naphthol compounds against E. coli bacteria through virtual simulation and identify the best ligands for targeting this bacterium. The molecular docking analysis results of the synthesized compounds are presented in Table 3.

In the context of molecular docking, docking energy measures of the binding strength between a ligand and a receptor. More negative values signify a stronger binding interaction. The molecular docking calculations were assessed based on Lipinski's guidelines, which dictate that a drug compound's molecular mass should be below 500 g/mol. The synthesized compounds in this study adhered to this rule. This molecular mass threshold is important for ensuring drug likeness and favorable pharmacokinetic properties. Another critical aspect is the ligand dissociation factor, which aims to balance a drug molecule's hydrophilicity and lipophilicity. Ideally, the octanol/water partition coefficient should not surpass 5. However, some compounds in the study exceeded this recommended value. Additionally, the number of hydrogen-donating groups (NH and OH) and hydrogen acceptor groups (N and O) should not exceed 5, a guideline that all combinations adhere to Cell permeability is also essential for bioavailability and absorption, with an ideal rate exceeding 500 nm s−1. All combinations in the study met this criterion as well. Overall, these findings underscore the significance of Lipinski-based guidelines in drug development and optimization, as they help identify drug candidates with favorable properties for therapeutic interventions [53].

The compound 1-((benzo[d]thiazol-2-ylamino)(4-(methylthio)phenyl)methyl)naphthalene-2-ol, recognized as the best mutual compound of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)aryl methyl 2-naphthol, interacts within the active site of the E. coli bacterial protein 2EG7. Within this site, it establishes an essential hydrogen bond with tryptophan 79 via the hydroxyl (OH) group of the naphthalene ring. These specific interactions are vital role in stabilizing the ligand bound to the target protein. The energy level of the complex structure is -7.735, indicating that this compound is the most effectively bound among the derivatives of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)methyl-2-naphthol (Fig. 7) [54].

A plausible mechanism for the preparation of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)methyl-2-naphthols using γ-Fe2O3 as a catalyst

The mechanism, illustrated in Fig. 9, demonstrates the efficient synthesis of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)methyl-2-naphthols using γ-Fe2O3 as a catalyst in a one-pot, three-component reaction. The process involves ortho-aldolization between 2-naphthol and an aldehyde to form the key intermediate (a). The unique electronic properties and high surface area of the γ-Fe2O3 catalyst play a vital role in promoting the nucleophilic attack of the 2-amino benzothiazole amino group on the intermediate (a), which is essential for the formation of the desired carbon–nitrogen bond connecting the benzothiazole and naphthol moieties. Additionally, the γ-Fe2O3 catalyst enhances the electrophilicity of intermediate (a) and stabilizes the transition states, resulting in increased reaction rates and yields. Notably, γ-Fe2O3is an environmentally friendly and affordable catalyst, making it an ideal choice for sustainable organic synthesis. This method is especially important for creating efficient and eco-friendly chemical processes to synthesize biologically active compounds [55].

Conclusions

In summary, the use of γ-Fe₂O₃ as a magnetically recoverable catalyst has demonstrated great effectiveness in enabling the one-pot, three-component reaction of 2-aminobenzothiazole, aldehydes, and 2-naphthol, resulting in the efficient formation of 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)arylmethyl-2-naphthol. This method provides several advantages, including its environmentally friendly nature, simple operation, minimal chemical waste production, short reaction times, and consistently high yields. Furthermore, its mild reaction conditions, a wide range of applicable substrates, ease of use, and reusability make it a versatile and practical approach. The exceptional catalytic performance, thermal stability, and ease of separation and reuse of the catalyst further establish this green method as a superior alternative to existing protocols. The molecular binding analysis indicated that the 1-(benzothiazol-2-ylamino)arylmethyl-2-naphthol compounds exhibited strong antibacterial properties, suggesting their potential as effective antibacterial agents that warrant further optimization and development. These findings highlight the promising potential of repurposing iron-bearing wastes to produce valuable materials with applications in water treatment and pharmaceuticals.

Experimental section

General

All chemicals and solvents used in this study were purchased from commercial sources and used without any further purification. Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were recorded on a Nicolet Magna-400 spectrometer using KBr pellets. Proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) spectra were obtained in DMSO-d6 with a Bruker DRX 400 spectrometer, employing tetramethylsilane as an internal standard. Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted on a Philips X’pert diffractometer with monochromatized Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). The magnetic properties of the magnetite nanoparticles were examined using a vibrating sample magnetometer (PPMS-9 T) at 300 K at Kashan University. The morphology of the nanoparticles was analyzed via field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) using a MIRA3 instrument. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed with a DuPont 2000 V5.1 A analyzer.

Synthesis of γ-Fe₂O₃

Iron filings waste was dissolved in a 0.1 M hydrochloric acid solution at a ratio of 10 g of steel per liter of solution. After the dissolution reaction was complete (pH ~ 0), the pH was increased to 9 through the gradual addition of a 5 M ammonium hydroxide solution, while stirring continuously. The resultant precipitates were then filtered, and γ -Fe2O3 was washed with distilled water until neutral, then dried at 60 °C in a kiln [46].

General procedure for the preparation of 1-(benzothiazolylamino)aryl methyl-2naphtol

A one-pot synthesis of 1-(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthol derivatives was performed using a mixture of 2-amino benzothiazole (1 mmol), 2-naphthol (1 mmol), aromatic aldehyde (1 mmol), and an γ -Fe2O3 catalyst (0.05 g). The reaction mixture was heated in an oil bath at 80°C and its progress was monitored using TLC with a solvent mixture of n-hexane and ethyl acetate (1:1). Upon completion, the catalyst was separated using an external magnet. The final product was purified through recrystallization from ethanol. This method demonstrates an efficient and environmentally friendly approach to synthesizing 1-(benzothiazolylamino)methyl-2-naphthol derivatives at lower temperatures, without using harmful solvents. The optimal reaction conditions were determined to be a temperature of 80°C, with shorter reaction times and higher yields, as shown in Table 1. The obtained products were initially evaluated using molecular docking, followed by antibacterial testing (Fig. 10). Increasing the catalyst amount or temperature did not improve the results.

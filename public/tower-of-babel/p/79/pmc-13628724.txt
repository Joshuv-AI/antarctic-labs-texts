Pleomorphic adenoma: does the immunohistochemical profile direct the clinical profile: retrospective study and review of literature
Authors: Amirah Alnour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPleomorphic adenoma is a benign salivary gland tumor that has a potentiality to recurrence and malignant transformation as well. The genetic alteration of this tumor is not completely studied yet. Investigating the growth factor receptors and tumor microenvironment may yield answers to the nature of this tumor.MethodsThirty-six formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded pleomorphic adenoma specimens were collected from Al-Mouassat University Hospital. EGFR, HER-2 and BCL-2 antibodies have been used in immunohistochemistry stains to investigate the most probable affected pathway in this progress of this tumor.ResultsImmunohistochemical analysis revealed total negativity for HER2 expression. While, EGFR and BCL-2 expression was positive in diffuse patchy pattern. The positivity was membranous and/or cytoplasmic predominantly within the tumor cell population.Statistical analysis revealed no significant association between EGFR/BCL-2 expression and patient gender (p = 0.848) or tumor site (p = 1.000). The absence of HER2 expression was consistent across all cases, regardless of clinicopathological parameters. The expression of EGFR and BCL2 did not show a statistically significant correlation with demographic variables, suggesting a tumor-intrinsic expression pattern rather than dependence on clinical factors.ConclusionOn this study, pleomorphic adenoma demonstrated a distinct immune-profile with positivity of EGFR and BCL-2 and negative expression of HER-2. These findings suggest HER-2 unlikely contribute to tumor behavior, meanwhile the positivity of EGFR and BCL-2 may be related to the tumor survival rather than malignant transformation. Moreover, these markers do not same to consider as reliable predictors of malignant transformation in pleomorphic adenoma.Keywords: Pleomorphic adenoma, Tumor behavior, EGFR, HER-2, BCL-2

Introduction

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is the most common benign salivary gland tumor of major and minor salivary glands worldwide. PA represents approximately 60% of all salivary gland tumors with predilection for the parotid gland and slight female predominance. Although it is considered as a benign entity, it demonstrates a distinct clinical behavior, characterized by risk of recurrence and potential malignant transformation mainly into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma (CXPA), a malignant entity with worse prognosis [1, 2].

The molecular basis that is involved in the progression form PA to malignancy remains an area of continuous investigation. Most studies have shown genetic alterations mainly PLGA1 rearrangements which constitutes the principle molecular changes of PA. Although the rate of malignant transformation is low, the follow-up of malignant entities is a clinically important issue cause of the association with invasive nature and poorer prognosis. Furthermore, subtypes and stromal variation may represent potential precursors to carcinoma [3].

Numerous studies assured the positive expression of HMGA2 protein in the etiology of this tumor. Meanwhile, recent researches highlighted the role of HMGA2 alteration in the progression to CXPA and that was associated. As well as, the studies emphasized on the alteration in MDM2 and that occurrence with PA cases expressed atypia on routine examination [4].

Lngman et al., tried to explain the diversity of pleomorphic adenomas and suggested that it could be explained by the expression of Wilms’ tumor-suppressor gene (WT1) in the neoplastic myoepithelium. They concluded that WT1 led a mean marker in neoplastic myoepithium cells in PA, emphasizing the need to further researches to approve its role in transformation into malignancy [5].

Taking into consideration the increasing interest in the role of human papilloma virus in head and neck tumors by detecting p16, recent researches have investigated the role of p16 in the etiology of PA. They noticed alteration in p16 as well p53 mutations mainly in epithelial neoplastic cells and in the stromal component of the tumor, suggesting the prone of these cells to malignant trasfromation in PA [6].

In the continuous endeavor of understanding the biological behavior of PA and its malignant progression, studies have been conducted to examine potential roles of many molecular biomarkers in this tumor proliferation, survival, and biological behavior. The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), a transmembrane tyrosine kinase, has been known for its role in cell proliferation and survival in several epithelial tumors. Furthermore, dysregulated EGFR pathway signals has been noticed in the pathogenesis of various malignancies, and its expression levels have been correlated with histologic grade and as a result in clinical outcomes in several tumors. In salivary gland tumors, EGFR expression has been reported in PA, however, with unclear prognostic significance [7, 8].

In the same receptor family, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/ERBB2), is a well-established oncogenic driver mainly breast carcinoma. HER2 overexpression and the gene amplification are well documented in specific salivary gland carcinoma types such as salivary duct carcinoma. Its expression in benign PA is absent or minimal, making it relevant that HER2 may be more campaign with malignant progression rather than to benign growth. Theories suggest that HER2 could be a biomarker of malignant transformation [9, 10].

On the other hand, apoptotic regulation has essential role in tumor biology, and the anti-apoptotic protein BCL2 has been the candidate marker of cell survival. Studies demonstrated BCL2 expression in PA, that enhanced cell survival mechanisms may contribute to tumor persistence and possibly to aggressive behavior in a subset of lesions. Mounted studies pointed that BCL2 expression is variable in salivary gland tumors and may be related to the tumor growth and differentiation, meanwhiles, it may be predictive factor for malignant transformation in PA specifically remains to be fully elucidated [11, 12].

Collectively, EGFR, HER2, and BCL2 play a key role in molecular pathways implicated in proliferation, survival, and apoptosis of neoplastic cells. We conducted this research to study their expression patterns in pleomorphic adenoma by IHCs, that could suggest insights into the biological behavior of PA and explain of the mechanisms of malignant transformation. We aimed in in this study to characterize the immunohistochemical profile of EGFR, HER2, and BCL2 in pleomorphic adenoma and to detect their potential role in tumor behavior and its malignant potential.

Pleomorphic adenoma displays a unique immunohistochemistry profile marked by varied expression of EGFR, HER2, and BCL2. We propose that EGFR and BCL2 expression indicate tumor cell survival and biological persistence rather than malignant transformation, while the lack of HER2 expression underscores its minimal role in benign pleomorphic adenoma and its possible link to malignant progression rather than tumor maintenance.

So we conducted this study to assess the immunohistochemistry expression profiles of EGFR, HER2, and BCL2 in pleomorphic adenoma and examine the correlation between the expression of these markers and clinicopathological factors, including patient gender and tumor location.

The results may explain the probable involvement of EGFR, HER2, and BCL2 in the biological characteristics of pleomorphic adenoma and their significance in malignant transformation.

By directly evaluating the relationship between immunohistochemical expression and clinicopathological features, this study moves beyond descriptive analysis and contributes to understanding whether these biomarkers may have practical clinical relevance in predicting pleomorphic adenoma behavior.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the expression of HER2, EGFR, and BCL2 in pleomorphic adenoma and to investigate their possible association with clinicopathological parameters.

Methods

This retrospective observational study comprised 36 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue specimens histopathologically identified as pleomorphic adenoma (PA). All specimens were derived from complete surgical excision procedures. All cases were obtained from the historical records of the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Pathology. The initial diagnosis was examined and validated in accordance with the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of head and neck neoplasms.

Clinicopathological data, encompassing patient gender and tumor location, were extracted from pathology records. Cases characterized by insufficient tissue, significant necrosis, or suboptimal antigen preservation were removed.

Immunohistochemical staining

Immunohistochemical (IHC) examination was conducted on 4-µm-thick slices obtained from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks and affixed to positively charged slides. Sections were deparaffinized using xylene and subsequently rehydrated with a series of graded alcohols.

Immunohistochemical staining was carried out using primary antibodies against HER2, EGFR, and BCL2. The clone type, manufacturer, and dilution of each antibody were as follows: anti-HER2: clone: RMab, manufacturer: Bio SB, dilution: ready to use, anti-EGFR: clone: RMab, manufacturer: Bio SB, dilution: ready to use, and anti-BCL2: clone: RMab, manufacturer: Bio SB, dilution: ready to use. All staining procedures were performed in accordance with the manufacturers’ recommended protocols.

Antigen retrieval was performed with heat-induced epitope retrieval in citrate buffer at pH 6.0. Endogenous peroxidase activity was inhibited with 3% hydrogen peroxide. Slides were subsequently treated with primary antibodies targeting EGFR, HER2, and BCL2 in accordance with the manufacturers’ protocols.

Appropriate positive control tissues were included in each IHC staining run according to standard laboratory practice to validate staining performance.

Detection was accomplished via a conventional streptavidin–biotin or polymer-based detection technique, thereafter visualized with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) as the chromogen. Sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, dried, and mounted with coverslips.

Assessment of immunohistochemical expression

Immunoreactivity was evaluated independently by two seasoned pathologists who were unaware of the clinicopathological information.

EGFR and HER2 expression were assessed via membranous staining in tumor epithelial cells.

BCL2 expression was evaluated by cytoplasmic staining.

Cases were deemed positive when at least 10% of tumor cells exhibited particular immunoreactivity. Discrepant situations were collaboratively examined to achieve consensus.

Statistical examination

Statistical analysis was conducted with SPSS software. Descriptive statistics were employed to summaries the distribution of biomarker expression, sex, and tumor location.

The Chi-square test was utilized to assess the correlation between biomarker expression and clinicopathological factors, such as gender and tumor location. A p-value less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Ethical considerations

The research was performed in compliance with institutional guidelines. Due to the retroactive nature of this examination of archive material, informed consent was waived, and all patient data were anonymized.

All immunohistochemical slides were independently evaluated by a single experienced pathologist. As no second observer was involved, interobserver agreement analysis was not applicable.

Discussion

Although pleomorphic adenoma has been previously studied, most researches have focused on describing immunohistochemical marker expression without clearly relating these results to the clinical data such as the gender and the anatomic location. In contrast, our study aimed to explore whether the immunohistochemical profile of the tumor reflects meaningful clinical variables such as patient gender, anatomical location, or recurrence.

In addition, much of the available literature is based on European or East Asian populations. Our work provides data from an underrepresented population, which may help account for geographic or healthcare-related differences that could influence the tumor presentation and prognosis.

In spite of this study was conducted at a single centre with a relatively limited sample size, Al-Mouwasat University Hospital serves as the largest referral institution in the country, receiving tumor cases from various provinces nationwide. As such, the study cohort reflects a diverse patient population drawn from a wide geographic catchment area.

The immunohistochemical evaluation in this study was based on a single cutoff value to ensure methodological consistency and reproducibility.

EGFR membranous expression in pleomorphic adenoma exhibited no substantial connection with demographic or anatomical factors. HER2 expression was consistently negative in all instances, but BCL2 exhibited frequent cytoplasmic positivity, indicating its involvement in tumor cell survival rather than in clinicopathological classification.

The results of this study align closely with earlier publications indicating prevalent EGFR and BCL2 expression in pleomorphic adenoma, while also affirming the lack of HER2 expression in benign tumors. Numerous investigations have indicated EGFR positivity in roughly 50% of pleomorphic adenomas, with no definitive correlation to aggressive behavior or malignant transformation. BCL2 expression has consistently been detected in pleomorphic adenoma, especially in the epithelium and myoepithelial components, indicating a function in tumor cell survival rather than oncogenic advancement. Conversely, HER2 overexpression is confined to malignant salivary gland tumors, namely carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, underscoring its minimal significance in benign conditions.

The immunohistochemistry profile of pleomorphic adenoma (PA) in this study, marked by prevalent EGFR membranous and BCL2 cytoplasmic expression and consistently lacking HER2 expression, corresponds with extensive literature data and highlights the tumor’s intricate biological behavior.

HER2 overexpression is acknowledged in malignant salivary gland tumors, especially in carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma (CXPA) and salivary duct carcinoma (SDC), where it constitutes a component of aggressive molecular profiles and may inform targeted therapy in specific instances. A thorough systematic review and meta-analysis indicated that HER2 positive in CXPA is significant (pooled estimate of 39%), underscoring HER2’s importance as a marker linked to malignant development rather than benign PA. Conversely, HER2 expression in benign salivary gland tumors, including classic pleomorphic adenoma, was remarkably low to insignificant across many cohorts, reinforcing the notion that HER2 is not characteristic of the benign disease profile [13].

In signaling pathways, EGFR and HER2 are associated with salivary gland carcinomas via the activation of downstream cascades involving Akt and mTOR, with both gene amplification and overexpression recorded in certain malignant tumors. The data substantiate the perspective that receptor tyrosine kinase pathways have a role in the malignancy of salivary gland neoplasms, especially in high-grade forms as SDC and CXPA [14].

Although HER2 is not present in PA, previous immunohistochemistry studies have indicated that EGFR expression occurs in benign PA, though with varied frequency. Historical analyses of salivary gland neoplasms revealed that around one-third of pleomorphic adenomas have EGFR positive, characterized by membrane and cytoplasmic staining patterns in luminal tumor cells, suggesting the presence of EGFR in benign tumor epithelium. This localization implies that EGFR’s activity in PA may pertain to epithelial proliferation and maintenance rather than malignant transformation [15].

The prevalent presence of anti-apoptotic proteins such BCL2 in PA highlights the significance of survival pathways in the biology of these tumors. Case–control studies comparing benign pleomorphic adenoma (PA) with malignant variants such as mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) have demonstrated that BCL2 expression is more pronounced in PA, indicating that apoptotic resistance may be a defining property of benign neoplastic proliferation that facilitates tumor persistence [16].

The immunoprofile identified in this study EGFR membranous and BCL2 cytoplasmic positivity alongside HER2 negativity indicates that pleomorphic adenoma represents a biological condition characterized by increased survival and controlled proliferation, rather than explicit oncogenic signalling linked to transformation. The lack of HER2 expression in all instances supports the idea that HER2 activation is not a contributing factor in benign PA, and its appearance in malignant variants probably signifies a subsequent occurrence in the neoplastic continuum. This explanation aligns with models in other tumor systems, where initial lesions exhibit survival and proliferation indicators without activating the more potent oncogenic pathways associated with malignancy.

Other clincopathological correlation such as the age categories and daily habits (smoking, alcholo cosumption, previous tumors incidence, HPV related salivary glands lesions) are all impotant variables, however they could not be evaluated in this study, because there are limitations in the documents at the healthcare system records in the main hospital treatning these tumors (Almowasat hospital). Although the HPV-related salivary gland tumors has not been clarified or approved in literature, further studies focus on these varibale and how they contribute to the incidence and etiology of salivary gmland tumors and maily pleomprphic adenoma, would present a great vlaue to the current evidence in this field.

The data collectively indicate that pleomorphic adenoma is a tumor in which cell survival pathways, such as those mediated by BCL2, and growth signals, such as EGFR, play roles in tumorigenesis and persistence, whereas HER2 activation signifies a molecular transition linked to malignant development. The current findings underscore the value of a multi-marker immunoprofile in enhancing the biological comprehension of PA and emphasize the necessity for molecular studies to uncover more events that could forecast malignant change.

Limitations and future perspectives

This study has certain limitations that must be recognized. The limited sample size restricts the statistical power to identify modest correlations between immunohistochemistry markers and clinicopathological factors. This is especially pertinent for subgroup analysis based on tumor location, where the incidence of small salivary gland cases was restricted.

The study solely utilized immunohistochemistry analysis, which indicates protein expression but fails to reveal underlying genetic or molecular modifications, such as gene amplification or activating mutations. The incorporation of molecular approaches may provide enhanced understanding of the biological relevance of the identified expression patterns.

Noticably, no cases of carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma were identified in the present study. Therefore, the current study does not provide direct evidence regarding the role of the investigated immunohistochemical markers in malignant transformation.

EGFR and BCL2 expression may also be observed in normal salivary gland tissue; therefore, their positivity in pleomorphic adenoma should not be considered a specific diagnostic feature. Comparative analysis with other salivary gland tumors was beyond the scope of this study.

We acknowledge no correlation with recurrence, atypia, or duration of disease is provided, absence of follow-up data; that is maily because of the shortages of interruptions in care due to regional instability and imited access to diagnostic and monitoring technologies, all of this exacerbate the difficulty in maintaing comprehensive patient datastes.

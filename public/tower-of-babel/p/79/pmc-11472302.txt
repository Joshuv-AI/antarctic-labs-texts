Reevaluation of ADHD diagnoses in referral patients: A medical chart review in an advanced psychiatric hospital in Japan
Authors: Kosuke Suzuki, Tomihisa Niitsu, Maria Kato, Masaomi Iyo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Our study examined the diagnostic accuracy of ADHD among referral patients, revealing that 66% had confirmed ADHD while 24% had their diagnosis changed, primarily to bipolar disorder. These findings emphasize the need for comprehensive evaluation and multidisciplinary collaboration to ensure accurate diagnosis and effective treatment.

Attention‐deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) often coexists with various comorbidities, particularly bipolar disorder (BD). 1 , 2 A misdiagnosis of ADHD may lead to misuse of medication. Therefore, careful diagnosis considering psychiatric comorbidities is essential. Advanced treatment hospitals in Japan accept referred patients with treatment‐resistant and complicated mental disorders for comprehensive assessment and treatment. However, few reports have investigated the reconsideration of diagnoses and prescriptions for referred patients with suspected ADHD in such hospitals. This study aimed to elucidate diagnostic reconsiderations and prescription patterns for patients with suspected ADHD referred to an advanced treatment hospital in Japan, revealing current challenges in diagnosing and treating ADHD and its comorbidities.

We retrospectively reviewed the medical charts of patients in Chiba University Hospital with suspected ADHD, defined as those with a provisional ADHD diagnosis by the referring psychiatrist. These patients were referred from local psychiatric clinics and facilities between January 2019 and October 2021. The review was conducted in October 2021 with data on diagnosis changes up to that point. Therefore, the durations between the initial consultation and diagnosis changes were varied. Anonymous data collected at the final visit included demographic data, age, sex, changes in diagnoses, presence of comorbidities, and medications. Clinical statistics were reported in compliance with Chiba University Hospital's code of ethics.

Of the 284 patients with suspected ADHD, 181 (64%) were male and 103 (36%) were female. Regarding age distribution, 33 (12%) patients were 6 years or younger, 94 (33%) were 7–12 years, 45 (16%) were 13–17 years, and 112 (39%) were 18 years or older. The initial ADHD diagnosis was reconfirmed for 189 (66%) patients, while 67 (24%) experienced a diagnostic change, 15 (5%) had additional comorbidities, and 13 (5%) were pending diagnosis or below the ADHD diagnostic threshold (Figure 1). Among the diagnostic changes, 29 (44%) were diagnosed with BD, and of those with reconfirmed ADHD, 15 had additional psychiatric comorbidities, with 11 (73%) having BD. Regarding medications, 113 (40%) received one or more ADHD medications: 46 (41%) guanfacine, 45 (40%) atomoxetine, and 21 (19%) methylphenidate. Antipsychotics and mood stabilizers were prescribed for 113 (40%) and 44 (15%) patients, respectively.

ADHD diagnosis was confirmed in 66% of the patients. It was revised in 24%, with BD accounting for 44% of these changes. This underscores the importance of comprehensive evaluation and consideration of comorbid conditions in patients with ADHD. While not asserting evidence of diagnostic superiority, we observed that the university hospital setting facilitates more comprehensive evaluations, with multi‐professional assessments, use of assessment tools in measurement‐based care, and discussions among specialized psychiatrists. Previous studies highlight a diagnostic overlap between ADHD and BD, with overlapping symptoms like mood lability and impulsivity. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 This is reflected in the final prescriptions, with antipsychotics and mood stabilizers prescribed to 40% and 15% of patients, respectively. This complicates diagnoses and treatment decisions. Clinicians must exercise caution when considering central stimulant medications for patients with ADHD and comorbid BD, as psychostimulants, including methylphenidate and amphetamines, have been shown to exacerbate manic symptoms in patients with BD. 7 , 8 This presents a significant challenge in treating ADHD with comorbid BD, as stimulant medications are widely used in ADHD treatment. Further, 32% of diagnostic changes occurred within developmental disorders, including autism spectrum disorder and mental retardation, highlighting the need for a broad differential diagnosis and comprehensive assessments to accurately identify underlying neurodevelopmental conditions that may coexist with or mimic ADHD symptoms.

Our study has some limitations. First, as a retrospective chart review, it is subject to incomplete or biased data recording. Second, being a single‐center study, the findings may not be generalizable to other settings or populations. Finally, the lack of a control group limits our ability to attribute changes in diagnosis or treatment outcomes solely to the studied factors.

Clinicians must meticulously assess patients with suspected ADHD for potential comorbidities, particularly BD and developmental disorders. Our study highlights the importance of collaboration among multiple medical institutions to address diagnostic and therapeutic challenges, ensuring optimal patient care and outcomes. Future studies should investigate longitudinal outcomes of patients with ADHD and comorbidities and evaluate the effectiveness of multidisciplinary approaches in enhancing diagnostic accuracy and treatment efficacy.

The complete chloroplast genome of Rhamnoneuron balansae, the only species of Rhamnoneuron (Thymelaeaceae)
Authors: Kang Wang, Meifei Li, Jinxuan Zheng, Yonghong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRhamnoneuron balansae (Drake) Gilg is the only species of the genus Rhamnoneuron in Thymelaeaceae. In this study, we sequenced, assembled, and annotated the complete chloroplast genome of R. balansae. The genome is 172,971 bp in length with a typical quadripartite structure, comprising an LSC region of 85,819 bp, an SSC region of 2,900 bp, and two IR regions of 42,126 bp each. A total of 139 genes were annotated, including 93 protein-coding genes, 38 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis strongly supported R. balansae as sister to Edgeworthia species. Our results provide valuable genetic resources for further studies of Thymelaeaceae.

Introduction

The genus Rhamnoneuron Gilg of Thymelaeaceae is monotypic, comprising the only species Rhamnoneuron balansae (Drake) Gilg 1894 (Gilg 1894). This perennial shrub or small tree is native to tropical and subtropical mixed forests in China, Vietnam, and Laos (Halda 2001; Herber 2003; Wang and Gilbert 2007). For centuries, R. balansae has been used as a raw material for traditional Vietnamese Dó paper, which is resistant to moisture and acid (Cho et al. 2022). Additionally, extracts from its stems and roots exhibit anti-aging effects on humans (Cho et al. 2020, 2022).

The taxonomic position of R. balansae in Thymelaeaceae has long been controversial. It was initially placed in the genus Wikstroemia since its first description in 1889 (Del Castillo 1889), then recognized as an independent genus of Rhamnoneuron. Halda (2001) subsumed it within a broad circumscription of Daphne, but this treatment was not accepted in the Flora of China (Wang and Gilbert 2007). Clarifying the phylogenetic position of R. balansae through integrated morphological and genetic approaches is therefore essential to facilitate its utilization as a biological resource.

In this study, we sequenced and analyzed the complete chloroplast genome of R. balansae for the first time, providing fundamental genomic resources for future molecular studies and phylogenetic inference.

Materials and methods

Material collection

Fresh leaf samples of R. balansae were collected from a healthy individual growing in Pingbian County, Yunnan Province, China (N22°57’20′’, E103°42’33′’) (Figure 1) and identified by Dr. Yong-Hong Zhang. Voucher specimens (RXK28) were deposited in the Herbarium of Yunnan Normal University (YNUB, website: https://life.ynnu.edu.cn/, contact: Jian-Lin Hang, Email: hjlynub@163.com).

(C) a close-up of the flowers. Key morphological features: R. balansae is a perennial shrub, 2–4 meters in height, with ovate, oblong, or lanceolate leaves 10–19 cm long and 2.5–5.5 cm wide. The inflorescence is a terminal or axillary panicle consisting of capitula. Each capitulum typically bears four flowers. The red calyx tube, approximately 1.2 cm in length, bears eight stamens arranged in two distinct whorls.

DNA extraction and genome sequencing

The total genomic DNA was extracted using a modified CTAB protocol (Allen et al. 2006). The short-insert paired-end (2 × 150 bp) library was constructed and sequencing was performed using the Illumina HiSeq 2500-PE150 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Genome assembly and annotation

The raw data (3.84 GB) were filtered using NGS QC Toolkit v2.3.3 with default parameters (Patel and Jain 2012) to remove low-quality sequences, resulting in 3.72 GB clean data, which comprised 12,385,479 clean reads for de novo assembly with NOVOPlasty v4.2 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017). The assembled chloroplast genome was annotated using Geneious V2022.02 (Kearse et al. 2012) with Daphne tangutica (GenBank accession no. MK455800) as a reference (Yan et al. 2019a). To assess the sequencing depth, the assembled genome was aligned with the quality-filtered clean reads using the same software. The circular gene map of the chloroplast genome was constructed using CPGView software (http://www.1kmpg.cn/cpgview/; Liu et al. 2023). After manual check and adjustment, the chloroplast DNA sequence with complete annotation information was submitted to GenBank under accession number ON183350.1.

Phylogenetic tree construction

To analyze the phylogenetic position of R. balansae, the complete chloroplast genomes of 31 species of Thymelaeaceae and Malva verticillata (NC_059767), Gossypium sturtianum (NC_023218), Ceiba speciosa (NC_057077) from Malvaceae as outgroups were downloaded from the NCBI GenBank database (National Center for Biotechnology Information). All 34 chloroplast genomes were aligned by the MAFFT v7.450 software (Katoh and Standley 2013). The maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was then constructed using IQ-TREE v1.6.10 software (Nguyen et al. 2015) under the TVM + F + R2 best-fit model (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). Branch support was tested with 10,000 replicates using ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot) (Hoang et al. 2018) and SH-like approximate likelihood ratio test (SH-aLRT) (Guindon et al. 2010). The resulting phylogenetic tree was visualized and edited using FigTree v1.4.4 (https://github.com/rambaut/figtree).

Results

Characteristics of chloroplast genome

The complete chloroplast genome of R. balansae exhibits a quadripartite circular structure with 172,971 bp in length and an average sequencing depth of 1157.7× (Figure S1), including a large single-copy (LSC) region of 85,819 bp, and a small single-copy (SSC) region of 2,900 bp, separated by a pair of inverted repeats (IRA and IRB) region of 42,126 bp. The GC content in whole cp genome, LSC region, SSC region, and IR regions was 36.8%, 34.9%, 29.4% and 38.9%, respectively (Figure 2). The chloroplast genome contains 139 genes, including 93 protein-coding genes, 38 transfer RNA genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA genes. Among these genes, 16 genes (rps16, rpl2, rpl16, atpF, rpoC1, petB, petD, ndhA, ndhB, trnK-UUU, trnG-UCC, trnL-UAA, trnV-UAC, trnL-GAU, trnA-UGC, trnL-GAC) contain one intron, and 2 genes (ycf3, rps12) contain two introns. In total, 10 cis-splicing genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhA, ndhB) (Figure S2A) and one trans-splicing gene (rps12) (Figure S2B) were identified.

Phylogenetic relationships

Phylogenetic analysis revealed that all sampled species of Thymelaeaceae formed a monophyletic clade with 100% support. Within Thymelaeaceae, R. balansae and the clade comprising three Edgeworthia species (E. gardneri, E. albiflora, E. chrysantha) were resolved as sister groups with 100% support (Figure 3). This study supports Gilg’s classification that Rhamnoneuron is an independent genus. The complete chloroplast genome of R. balansae will provide a useful resource for the in-depth molecular studies of this species as well as for phylogenetic studies of Thymelaeaceae.

Discussion and conclusions

The chloroplast genome of R. balansae exhibits typical structure and gene content, resembling those of its closely related species, E. chrysantha (Lee et al. 2020), E. albiflora and E. gardneri (Qian et al. 2021), reflecting the conservation of chloroplast genomes within Thymelaeaceae. Notably, the SSC region of R. balansae is remarkably short (2,900 bp), a consistent feature across Thymelaeaceae (Zhang et al. 2019; He et al. 2021; Qian et al. 2021). Such size variation in seed plant chloroplast genomes is often attributed to IR expansion/contraction (Jansen and Ruhlman 2012). Similar IR boundary variations occur in the closely related species of Edgeworthia (Qian et al. 2021). In Aquilaria, IR expansion has led to a pronounced shortening of the SSC to 3,234–3,351 bp (Zhang et al. 2019, Hishamuddin et al. 2020). Comparative analysis of six Wikstroemia species similarly revealed IR expansion events, accompanied by SSC contraction to 2,779–2,871 bp (He et al. 2021) (Figure S3). A recent comprehensive study demonstrated that variation in total gene number across Thymelaeaceae chloroplast genomes is primarily driven by IR contraction and expansion, as well as pseudogenization of certain genes (Abdullah et al. 2026). These studies collectively indicate that IR boundary dynamics represent a widespread and evolutionarily significant feature of chloroplast genomes in Thymelaeaceae.

Phylogenetic analysis confirms R. balansae as sister to Edgeworthia species, supporting molecular evidence for a close genetic relationship between the genera Rhamnoneuron and Edgeworthia. Furthermore, the close phylogenetic relationship between R. balansae and species from Eurasian genera such as Edgeworthia, Wikstroemia, and Daphne is consistent with the morphology-based treatment proposed by Herber (2003). This provides a key foundation for revising the taxonomy of Thymelaeaceae and resolving taxonomic ambiguities within this group. However, this study focuses exclusively on the chloroplast genome and lacks nuclear or transcriptomic data, which may limit a comprehensive understanding of the species and its phylogenetic relationships (Leebens-Mack et al. 2019, Witharana et al. 2025).

In conclusion, this study reports the first complete chloroplast genome assembly and annotation of R. balansae, the sole and previously uninvestigated species of Rhamnoneuron. This organellar genetic resource provides a valuable foundation for future research on the taxonomy, phylogeny, conservation, and medicinal applications of this monotypic genus.

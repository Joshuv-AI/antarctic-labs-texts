Laparoscopic abdominal cerclage after radical trachelectomy: a surgical video
Authors: Joel Laufer, Santiago Scasso, Martin Sarragua, Mariana Boutmy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Step-by-step educational demonstration of laparoscopic abdominal cerclage after radical trachelectomy.•Identification of key anatomic landmarks and safe needle placement during the procedure.•Surgical technique highlighting its role as a fertility-preserving strategy in selected patients.

Objective

To provide an educational, step-by-step demonstration of laparoscopic abdominal cerclage placement after radical trachelectomy for early-stage cervical adenocarcinoma, illustrating the surgicaepartment of Medicine , Catholic University of Uruguay , Montevideo , Ul technique and its role as a fertility-preserving strategy (Clark and Einarsson, 2020).

Setting

The procedure was performed at the Gynecologic Oncology Unit of a tertiary referral center with expertise in minimally invasive surgery.

Participant

The patient was a 34-year-old nulliparous woman with a previous diagnosis of FIGO stage IB2 endocervical adenocarcinoma, Silva pattern A. She had undergone radical trachelectomy with sentinel lymph node mapping and concomitant cerclage placement using 1–0 Prolene® as definitive treatment (Morice et al., 2024). Following recovery, she pursued in vitro fertilization, with two unsuccessful attempts: one early miscarriage and one pregnancy loss at 23 weeks’ gestation. After multidisciplinary evaluation with the oncofertility team, she underwent laparoscopic transabdominal cerclage as a fertility-preserving strategy (Kini et al., 2009, Meglic et al., 2017). Preoperative MRI demonstrated a minimal cervical remnant and a normal-sized uterus, with no findings suggestive of tumor recurrence.

A subsequent embryo transfer resulted in a successful full-term pregnancy.

Interventions

The procedure began with diagnostic hysteroscopy to confirm patency of the endocervical canal. A Hegar dilator was used intraoperatively as a guide for cerclage placement and to ensure canal patency. Laparoscopic access was then established, and the vesicovaginal and rectovaginal spaces were carefully dissected to expose the residual cervical stump. A transabdominal cerclage was performed using a Mersilene® band, which was passed at the level of the dissected vesicouterine reflection, medial to the uterine vessels andapproximately 10 mm lateral to the Hegar dilator placed within the cervical canal. This trajectory ensured proper orientation of the suture while avoiding vascular injury. The band was secured anteriorly with intracorporeal knots (Kini et al., 2009, Meglic et al., 2017). The operation was completed without complications, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day one.

Conclusion

Laparoscopic abdominal cerclage was successfully performed after radical trachelectomy and failed initial cerclage, with no complications. In selected cases, this approach may represent a safe and feasible fertility-preserving option (Kini et al., 2009, Meglic et al., 2017).

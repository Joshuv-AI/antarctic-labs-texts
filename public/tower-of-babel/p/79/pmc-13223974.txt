Big Endothelin-1 Predicts the long-term survival in patients undergoing septal myectomy
Authors: Changpeng Song, Xiaohong Huang, Jingang Cui, Xinxin Zheng, Qiulan Yang, Shuiyun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prognostic role of endothelin-1 (ET-1) in patients with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM) who underwent septal myectomy remains unclear. We aimed to assess its prognostic importance in patients undergoing septal myectomy.MethodsWe recruited 1207 oHCM patients for this analysis. The participants were divided into three groups according to the tertile of big ET-1. Cox regression analyses were conducted to assess the prognostic value of big ET-1 on all-cause death and cardiovascular death.ResultsThe mean age of the participants was 49.9 ± 12.0 years, 713 (59%) study patients were male. female (adjusted coefficient 0.106, 95% CI: 0.053–0.158) and left atrial dimension (adjusted coefficient 0.005, 95%CI: 0.001–0.009) were significantly associated with big ET-1 levels. During mean 3.8 years follow-up, 35 patients died including 27 cardiovascular deaths. Multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that patients with highest tertile had the highest incidence of all-cause mortalities (adjusted HR = 3.483, 95% CI: 1.365–8.887, P = 0.009) and cardiovascular mortalities (adjusted HR = 8.328, 95%CI: 1.883–36.828, P = 0.005), compared to the referenced patients with lowest tertile. When Big ET-1 was evaluated as a continuous variable, data showed that the big ET-1 was correlated with higher risks of all-cause deaths (adjusted HR = 1.588, 95%CI: 1.148–2.196, P = 0.005) and cardiovascular deaths (adjusted HR = 1.757, 95%CI: 1.250–2.470, P = 0.001). Furthermore, interactions were detected in the subgroups of gender, and maximum wall thickness.ConclusionOur study revealed that high level of plasma big ET-1 could predict worse clinical outcomes for oHCM patients undergoing myectomy.Keywords: Endothelin-1, Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, Outcomes, Septal myectomy

Introduction

Endothelin-1 (ET-1), a well-known potent vasoconstrictor peptide, is primarily produced by vascular endothelial cells. ET-1 plays a critical role in vascular tone modulation, endothelial function, and cardiovascular remodeling [1], [2]. Elevated levels of ET-1 have been documented in heart failure, pulmonary, and cardiomyopathy, where it is associated with disease severity and adverse cardiovascular outcomes [3], [4], [5], [6]. In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), big ET-1 was also positively associated with poor outcomes [7]. However, to our knowledge, the long-term prognostic value of ET-1 and whether big ET-1 can serve as a biomarker of poor outcomes in patients with obstructive HCM (oHCM) who receive septal myectomy have not been well studied.

This study was designed to examine the association of plasma big ET-1 levels with long-term survival in those undergoing septal myectomy for refractory symptoms and prominent left ventricular outflow tract gradients. We hypothesized that elevated big ET-1 levels are associated with high risk of mortality and could serve as a favorable biomarker for risk stratification for long-term outcomes in oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy.

Material and methods

Study population

Patients with oHCM, undergoing surgical septal myectomy, at Fuwai Hospital between 2012 and 2019 were screened. The diagnosis of HCM included: (1) asymmetric myocardial hypertrophy (maximum thickness of the left ventricular wall ≥ 15 mm, or ≥ 13 mm with a family history of HCM); (2) without other cardiac or non-cardiac diseases which can result in the magnitude of hypertrophy. Septal myectomy was indicated for patients with severe left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction (LVOT gradient ≥ 50 mmHg at rest or with provocation) and symptoms which persisted even after receiving guideline determined medical therapy [8]. Patients without big ET-1 levels or with a diagnosis of infective endocarditis were excluded from the analysis. The study was approved by the Ethics Committees of Fuwai Hospital.

Measurement of big ET-1

After admission, a venous blood sample was drawn preoperatively from patients within 24 h. Plasma was collected after centrifugation. Then the Big ET-1 level was measured at the laboratory of Fuwai Hospital in accordance to standard protocol. The measurements of plasma big ET-1 concentrations were performed using Big Endothelin-1 ELISA Kit (BI-2008 2H, Biomedica, Vienna, Austria).

Follow-up

We collected the outcome data by telephone interview or clinic visit. The primary endpoint was all-cause death including cardiovascular deaths and non-cardiovascular deaths. The second endpoint was cardiovascular death which included heart failure-related death, sudden cardiac death, fatal myocardial infarction, and fatal stroke. Sudden and unexpected deaths within 1 h from the onset of symptoms were defined as sudden cardiac death.

Statistical analysis

The participants were divided into three groups according to the value of big ET-1 (T1: <0.25 pmol/L; T2: 0.25–0.39 pmol/L; T3: > 0.39 pmol/L). Continuous data with normal distribution were shown as mean ± standard deviation and tested using ANOVA test, while data with non-normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range) and tested using non-parametric test. Categorical variables were presented as number (percentage) and assessed using the Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test. Linear regression analyses were used to identify factors related to big ET-1.

For the survival analysis, Kaplan–Meier survival curves to demonstrate the difference of survival among three group. The hazard ratio (HR) of Big ET-1, as a continuous variable and a categorical variable, for all-cause deaths and cardiovascular deaths was evaluated using univariate Cox analysis. Model 2. adjusted for age, gender, while model 3. adjusted for age, gender, body mass index (BMI), left atrial dimension (LAD), left ventricular dimension, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), maximum wall thickness, moderate to severe mitral regurgitation (MR), maximum LVTO gradients, New York Heart Association (NYHA) III/IV, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and atrial fibrillation (AF). In addition, subgroup analyses of different subgroups (age, sex, hypertension, BMI, AF, LAD, maximum wall thickness, and LVEF) were performed using stratified Cox proportional hazards regression models. A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 was recognized statistically significant. SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp) and GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software) were used for the statistical analyses and figure generation.

Study outcomes

During mean 3.8 years follow-up, 35 patients died including 27 cardiovascular deaths and 8 non-cardiovascular deaths. The K-M curve showed the incidences of both all-cause mortality (log-rank P = 0.001) and cardiovascular mortality (log-rank P < 0.001) were significantly higher in T3 group (Fig. 1). The association between big ET-1 and adverse outcomes was examined by Cox regression analysis. After adjusting for age, gender, BMI, LAD, left ventricular dimension, LVEF, maximum wall thickness, moderate to severe MR, maximum LVTO gradients; NYHA class III/IV, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and AF (Model 3), patients in group T3 had the highest incidence of all-cause mortalities [adjusted HR = 3.483, 95% confidence interval CI 1.365–8.887, P = 0.009] and cardiovascular mortalities (adjusted HR = 8.328, 95%CI 1.883–36.828, P = 0.005), compared to the referenced patients in group T1. Big ET-1 level, as continuous variable, remained correlated with higher risks of all-cause deaths (adjusted HR = 1.588, 95%CI: 1.148–2.196, P = 0.005) and cardiovascular deaths (adjusted HR = 1.757, 95%CI: 1.250–2.470, P = 0.001) after adjustment for the possible confounding variables in Model 3. Subgroup analyses

Subgroup analyses were performed to assess the association between big ET-1 and prognosis according to age, sex, BMI, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, AF, LAD, maximum wall thickness, and LVEF. Big ET-1 was significantly associated with all-cause mortality in most subgroups, with a significant interaction in subgroups of sex and maximum wall thickness (P for interaction < 0.05). Similarly, a significant association between big ET-1 and cardiovascular mortality was also observed in most strata, and interactions were detected in the subgroups of age, gender, and maximum wall thickness (all P value for interaction < 0.05). Discussion

In this large-scale cohort, we explored the prognostic significance of big ET-1 in the patients undergoing septal myectomy. The study showed that high plasma big ET-1 level was an independent risk for poor outcomes including all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality. This prognostic ability persisted across most patient subgroups, with notable effect modifications by sex and maximum wall thickness. These results potentially position big ET-1 as a potent, independent biomarker for risk stratification in oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy.

Our findings are in line with previous studies that identified the endothelin system as a key mediator of cardiovascular remodeling and disease progression. ET-1 is a potent vasoconstrictor and its effects involved in vascular smooth muscle contraction, hypertrophy, and fibrosis [1], [2], [9]. Elevated ET-1 concentrations have been reported in multiple cardiovascular disorders, such as heart failure, hypertension, and HCM [5], [7], [9].

Big ET-1 is a precursor of ET-1 and can reflect the systemic activation of the endothelin system [7], [9] Many factors were associated with the big ET-l levels in previous studies on cardiovascular diseases, including age, female, LAD, hypertension, AF, and severity of heart dysfunction [3], [7]. In the setting of oHCM, our data demonstrated the correlations of big ET-1 with AF, MR, and LAD. Increased atrial pressure could result in the release of ET-1[10], which contributes to left atrial remodeling, especially in disease states like left ventricular diastolic pressure elevation [11]. As the elevated left ventricular diastolic pressure and MR are common in oHCM, there comes to a vicious cycle: elevated atrial pressures increase ET-1 production, which in turn promotes atrial fibrosis, electrical instability, and further atrial dilatation, perpetuating AF [12], [13]. Our finding that patients with higher big ET-1 more frequently underwent concomitant maze ablation and mitral valve procedures provided direct clinical evidence which could support this theoretical assumption.

Previous studies have shown its prognostic value in various cardiovascular conditions. In a large-scale community-based cohort, plasma ET levels were associated with pulmonary hypertension, future mortality, and HF admissions [3]. The role of big ET-1 in cardiomyopathy and heart failure has also been well investigated, and elevated ET-1 can predict the severity of disease and clinical adverse outcomes [4], [5], [14]. In HCM, a relatively small study reported that high level of plasma big ET-1 predicted all-cause death and progression to NYHA class III/IV [7], while we previously documented an association between big ET-1 and the incidence of postoperative AF in oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy [15]. However, to our best knowledge, the impact of preoperative big ET-1 level on long-term postoperative clinical outcomes in oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy has not been studied. Based on this, our current study conducted and demonstrated an independent relationship between elevated big ET-1 and worse survival in the oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy.

The association between elevated big ET-1 and adverse outcomes may be explained by several potential mechanisms. First, ET-1 promotes vasoconstriction, increases afterload, and stimulates myocardial hypertrophy and adverse remodeling [14], [15]. Previous study showed that big ET-1 was positively associated with late gadolinium enhancement, which is the symbol of cardiac fibrosis [7]. Cardiac fibrosis and adverse remodeling are widely recognized as the risk factors of poor outcomes [16]. Secondly, big ET-1 is a key player in neurohormonal cross-talk. It potently stimulates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and the sympathetic nervous system [17]. This creates a deleterious feed-forward cycle, as angiotensin II itself can upregulate ET-1 expression, amplifying vasoconstriction and fibrotic signaling [18]. Thirdly, as noted above, endothelin may directly contribute to atrial structural remodeling and arrhythmogenesis. In line with previous study [19], our study had revealed the association of big ET-1 with LAD and AF, and AF subsequently elevates the risk of stroke, heart failure hospitalization, and death.

Although the exact mechanisms are not well studied, our findings remain have several potential clinical implications. Big ET-1 measurement could provide valuable prognostic information in oHCM patients undergoing septal myectomy, complementing traditional parameters. In female patients and those with maximum wall thickness ≥ 30 mm, the prognostic value of big ET-1 was more prominent. The assessment of Big ET-1 could be integrated into risk stratification algorithms to identify a distinct high-risk population that may benefit from more vigilant monitoring. Furthermore, the endothelin system represents a potential therapeutic target. Selective endothelin receptor antagonists have demonstrated efficacy in pulmonary arterial hypertension [20]. In the future, large-scale and well-designed studies are required to confirm our findings and evaluate the efficacy of endothelin receptor antagonists on the long-term clinical outcomes after septal myectomy.

Several limitations of this study should be reported. First, as an observational study with its inherent limitations, our results should be viewed with caution and as hypothesis generating. Second, genetic data were not available, and the interactive effect between genetic background and big ET-1 levels on the outcomes remains unknown. Third, only preoperative measurement of ET-1 may not be sufficient because of its potential fluctuations, and ambulatory monitoring during the follow-up period may provide more information.

Conclusions

Our study showed that high level of plasma big ET-1 predicted worse clinical outcomes for oHCM patients with undergoing septal myectomy. This finding may help optimize risk assessment and promote the development of endothelin-targeted therapies to improve patient outcomes.

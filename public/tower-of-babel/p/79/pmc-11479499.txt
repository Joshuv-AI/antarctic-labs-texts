Physical activity modified the association of blood cadmium and lead with Helicobacter pylori infection: A cross-sectional analysis with NHANES data
Authors: Wei-Feng Wang, Yu-Xiang Liu, Chao-Qun Li, Xian-Yong Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cadmium (Cd) and lead (Pb) exposure have been identified as risk factors for Helicobacter pylori seropositivity, possibly due to the immune suppression by Cd and Pb. Physical activity (PA) can induce an immune response. However, whether PA can reduce the effect of Cd and Pb on H pylori infection remains elusive. This study aims to investigate the association of blood Cd and Pb levels with H pylori infection and explore the intermediary effects of PA. This cross-sectional survey was conducted using the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) of the 1999 to 2000 cycle (n = 9965). Participants without clear serological testing data, or absent in PA, blood Cd, and Pb information were excluded. Collinearity analysis was performed to remove the variables with high collinearity. Restricted cubic spline curve analysis was adopted to assess the nonlinear association of Cd and Pb with H pylori infection. The logistic regression analysis, generalized linear models, sensitivity analysis, and P for trend test were used to further analyze their relationship. Then, we analyzed the association of Cd and Pb with H pylori infection in 2 PA groups. Totally 3638 participants were divided into H pylori-negative (n = 2545) and H pylori-positive group (n = 1093). Pb exhibited a linear relationship but Cd had a nonlinear relationship with H pylori infection. Besides, the elevation of Cd and Pb both independently predicted H pylori infection after adjusting various variables (P < .05). The robust relationship was confirmed by the P for trend test (P for trend < .05). Under Cd exposure, the risk of H pylori infection was lower in the active PA group than in the inactive group (P < .05). A reverse result was found under the Pb exposure (P < .05). Exposure to Cd and Pb are positively linked to H pylori infection. PA may alleviate the effect of Cd on H pylori infection but may enhance H pylori infection under Pb exposure. Therefore, PA should be recommended in the appropriate season or region.

1. Introduction

Helicobacter pylori is a spiral-shaped, microoxic, and Gram-negative bacterium, and about 50% of people are suffering from its infection worldwide.[1] Its prevalence varies geographically with 30% to 50% and 85% to 95% in developed and developing countries, respectively.[2,3] There is little difference in the incidence between genders with 46.3% in men and 42.7% in women.[4] It is mainly acquired in childhood and may persist in a host throughout their lifetime.[5] Spontaneous clearance is relatively common in children, but it is usually related to diseases treated with antibiotics.[6] Fecal-to-oral and oral-to-oral routes are considered the most likely routes of transmission.[7,8] Besides, environmental factors including contaminated diet, cigarette smoking, alcohol abuse, and hygiene conditions are crucial in the pathogenesis of H pylori infection; while virulence factors, bacterial survival, and the immune system are essential in the disease development.[5,9]

Cadmium (Cd) and lead (Pb) are 2 primary types of heavy metal exposure derived from contaminated food, tobacco use, and ambient air.[10] Heavy metal exposure levels have declined in recent decades due to governments implementing a range of public health policies, such as air pollution reduction, hazardous waste remediation, and tobacco control.[11] Nonetheless, long-term exposure to environmental Cd and Pb, even below the World Health Organization’s maximum permissible limits, can cause damage to health.[12] Heavy metal exposure is estimated by examining the number of heavy metals ingested through the types and quantity of food in daily diet; however, after absorption and distribution, heavy metals may change to different forms.[13] Wang et al proposed the concentration of heavy metals in the blood is more reliable to reflect exposure levels in the body.[14] Notably, blood Cd and Pb elevation was positively related to H pylori seropositivity, suggesting that the increased susceptibility to H pylori infection may be attributed to the suppression effects of Cd and Pb toxicity on the immune response.[15] Thus, it is necessary to develop preventive strategies to minimize the adverse effects of heavy metal exposure.

Physical activity (PA) is based on the needs of the body through various sports to promote health, enhance physical fitness, and enrich life.[16] PA is a stressor that produces inflammation, induces different immune responses associated with interleukin production, and thus induces anti-inflammatory responses.[17] Therefore, PA may exert immune protection and regulation by maintaining a good balance between pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines.[18] Madhawa et al found that there was an inverse relationship between PA and gastric cancer risk in the H pylori-positive subgroup.[19] However, little is known to whether PA can modify the association of Cd and Pb with H pylori infection. Herein, we collected data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) database to investigate the association of blood Cd and Pb levels with H pylori infection and explored the intermediary effects of PA.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design and participants

NHANES (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/index.htm) is a cross-sectional population survey with a multistage stratified probability sampling design to assess the health and nutrition status of the representative noninstitutionalized civilian population in the United States. The survey was administered by the National Centre for Health Statistics (NCHS) in 2-year cycles since 1999, which mainly included laboratory data, physical examinations, and interviews. This study has been approved by the NCHS Ethics Review Board (Protocol #98-12), and informed consents were obtained from all the participants. This present study was limited to subjects with H pylori data in the NHANES 1999 to 2000 cycle (n = 9965). Exclusion criteria: participants with missing data or ambiguous values for serological testing for H pylori (n = 2642); absence in PA information (n = 3682); no blood Cd and Pb data (n = 3). Therefore, 3638 samples were enrolled for final analyses (Fig. 1).

The flowchart of participant selection.

2.2. Helicobacter pylori seropositivity

The H pylori immunoglobulin G antibodies were detected using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit (Wampole Laboratories, Waltham, MA). According to the optical density (OD) value, participants were divided into H pylori seropositive group (OD value ≥ 1.1) and seronegative (OD value < 0.9). The values between 0.9 and 1.1 were considered ambiguous values that were excluded from this study to avoid misleading statistical results.[20]

2.3. Blood heavy metals

Cadmium and lead are simultaneously measured in whole blood using adaptations of the methods of Miller et al, Parsons et al, and Stoeppler et al Cd and Pb quantification is based on the measurement of light absorbed at 228.8 and 283.3 nm, respectively, by ground-state atoms of Cd and Pb from either an electrodeless discharge lamp or a hollow cathode lamp source. Human blood samples, bovine blood quality control pools, and aqueous standards are diluted with a matrix modifier (nitric acid, Triton X-100, and ammonium phosphate). The Cd and Pb contents are determined on a PerkinElmer Model SIMAA 6000 simultaneous multi-element atomic absorption spectrometer with Zeeman background correction.

2.4. Physical activity assessment

Metabolic equivalent (MET) refers to the oxygen consumption necessary for maintaining resting metabolism. Based on energy expenditure in a quiet sitting position, it is a common indicator to express the relative energy metabolism level in various activities.[21] Information on PA in NHANES has been self-reported by participants using the Global PA Questionnaire. The recommended MET values for vigorous work-related activity, moderate work-related activity, walking or bicycling, vigorous leisure-time activity, and moderate leisure-time activity were 8.0, 4.0, 4.0, 8.0, and 4.0, respectively. PA was based on the MET values, weekly frequency, and duration, which was calculated using the following formula: PA (MET-min/week) = MET × weekly frequency × duration of each PA.[22] PA was classified into 2 levels: inactive (<600 MET-min/week), and active (≥ 600 MET-min/week). PA levels ≥ 600 MET-min/week are associated with substantial health benefits.[23]

2.5. Covariates

Sociodemographic characteristics and lifestyle behaviors included: age, gender, race, education, family poverty income ratio (PIR), alcohol use, and smoking. Alcohol use was based on the “Yes” or “No” answer to the question “Have you ever had at least 5 drinks of alcoholic beverage almost every day?” Smoking status was categorized as never smokers (defined as not smoked at least 100 cigarettes in life), former smokers (defined as smoked at least 100 cigarettes in life, but not current smoking), and current smokers (defined as smoked at least 100 cigarettes in life and current smoking). Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as the ratio of weight (kg) to the square of height (meter). Diabetes was diagnosed as glycohemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥ 6.5 mmol/L, or taking diabetes drugs. Hypertension was defined as a systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mm Hg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mm Hg, or taking a prescription for hypertension. The C-reaction protein (CRP) was also collected.

2.6. Statistical analysis

Data analyses were performed by R studio (version 4.2.2). Categorical variables were expressed as count (percent) and the differences between the groups were compared by the Chi-square test. The continuous variables were presented as median (IQR) and group differences were evaluated by the Mann–Whitney U test. Multicollinearity means that the model estimation is distorted or difficult to estimate accurately because of the exact correlation or high correlation between the explanatory variables in the linear regression model. As a measurement of multicollinearity, the variance inflation factor (VIF) represents the ratio of the variance of the estimated regression coefficients to the variance when no linear correlation is assumed between the independent variables. The possibility of collinearity among independent variables increased with the VIF value. VIFs larger than 5 were rejected. P < .05 indicates a significance level.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve analysis was performed to fit the nonlinear association of the continuous predictor variable (blood Pb level and Cd level) with the outcome variable (H pylori infection) with 3 knots.[24] Next, logistic regression models were performed to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) for associations between Pb level as a continuous variable and H pylori infection. Then, the concentration of Pb was categorized into 3 groups based on tertiles by setting the tertile 1 group as the referent. The relationship between Cd level and H pylori infection was evaluated by the generalized linear models (GLM) with the tertile 1 group as the reference, shown as β and corresponding 95% CI. To further explore the effect of covariates on this association, we employed Crude Model 1 (unadjusted), Model 2 (BMI, age, PIR, gender, race, and education were adjusted), and Model 3 (CRP, HbA1c, hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, and alcohol use were adjusted).[25,26] Subsequently, the association of Pb and Cd groups with H pylori infection was investigated using the trend test for sensitivity analysis. To explore the influence of PA on the association of Pb and Cd with H pylori infection, we used the same 3 models to analyze their associations in 2 PA groups. Finally, we assessed the association of Pb and Cd with H pylori infection in different subgroups to identify high-risk groups (hypertension, diabetes, alcohol use, smoking status, gender, and race).

3.2. Lead exhibited a linear relationship but cadmium had a nonlinear relationship with H pylori infection

To evaluate the nonlinear association of Pb and Cd with H pylori infection and select a suitable model for further investigating their association, we conducted an RCS analysis by adjusting age, gender, race, BMI, education, PIR, alcohol use, smoking status, CRP, HbA1c, diabetes, and hypertension. Pb level was linearly correlated with H pylori infection (P for nonlinear > .05) (Fig. 2A), while Cd was nonlinearly linked to H pylori infection (P for nonlinear < .05) (Fig. 2B).

Restricted cubic spline analysis. (A) A linear relationship between lead level and H pylori infection. (B) nonlinear relationship between cadmium and H pylori infection. Adjusted factors: age, gender, race, BMI, education, PIR, alcohol use, smoking status, CRP, HbA1c, diabetes, and hypertension.

3.3. Heavy metals elevation correlated to H pylori infection

Since the linear association of Pb level with H pylori infection. Three logistic regression models were used for further evaluation. As a continuous variable, Pb was significantly linked to H pylori infection with an OR of 1.268, 1.121, and 1.146 in model 1, model 2, and model 3, respectively (P < .05). As a categorical variable, the OR values were 1.567 and 2.663 in the tertile 2 group and tertile 3 group, respectively in model 1 (P < .05). After adjusting BMI, age, PIR, gender, race, and education in model 2, the relationship between Pb and H pylori infection was still robust with OR = 1.330 and 1.833 in the tertile 2 group and tertile 3 group, respectively. When CRP, HbA1c, hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, and alcohol use were adjusted in model 3, the OR value was 1.678 in the tertile 3 group with statistical significance (P < .05) (Table 3). P for the trend test confirmed the positive and robust connection between Pb and H pylori infection.

4. Discussion

Herein, we used data from the NHANES database to reveal the positive association of blood Cd and Pb with H pylori infection. Besides, we found that PA might alleviate H pylori infection with Cd exposure, but increase the infection with Pb exposure. Our study may provide a novel insight into reducing the harmful effects of heavy metals and taking corresponding approaches to prevent H pylori infection.

H. pylori infection is a gastric pathogen that has infected over 50% of the population globally. It has been classified as a Group I carcinogen by the World Health Organization since 1994 and is capable of causing serious chronic diseases, such as gastric cancer, peptic ulcers, and chronic gastritis.[27] H pylori infection has additional effects on extra gastric disease including ophthalmic disorders, neurological disorders, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome.[28] The colonization and the establishment of H pylori infection consist of 4 main stages: first, H pylori adapted to the hostile acidic environment using their urase activity upon entering the stomach; second, the cells move towards and penetrate the gastric epithelial cell barrier with the aid of their flagella-mediated motility; third, H pylori adhesins attach to specific receptors, and hence resulting in successful colonization and persistent infection; Finally, the effector proteins or toxins are produced by H pylori to damage the host tissues. During infection, the release of chemokines and cytokines triggers innate immunity and activates the neutrophils, thereby increasing the levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and leading to inflammation.[29]

Heavy metal exposure is a major public health problem Because of its prevalence, persistence in the environment, and high toxicity.[30] Heavy metals can enter the circulatory system through the digestive tract, respiratory tract, or skin and accumulate in various organs.[31] Its exposure is related to reproductive, neurological, and cardiovascular disease.[32,33] Psychiatric disorders including depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and cognitive impairment were related to heavy metal exposure.[34,35] Heavy metals play a crucial role in inducing oxidative stress that is caused by an imbalance between the production and elimination of ROS.[36] Multiple enzyme systems including heme oxygen, thioredoxin, paraoxonase, and glutathione peroxidase (GPX) were protective mechanisms against ROS.[37] Notably, GPX was the key to protecting the body against ROS, while the heavy metals can bind to the GPX to reduce its levels, causing a decrease in its concentration and an increase in ROS levels.[38,39] Blood Cd and Pb levels were positively related to atherosclerosis and dyslipidemia.[40] Buser et al reported that elevation of Cd and Pb was linked to depression incidence.[41] We found that blood Cd and Pb levels were significantly higher in the H pylori-positive group and they could independently predict the H pylori infection. Based on the above research, it is reasonable to speculate that Cd and Pb might affect H pylori infection by disrupting GPX levels and elevating ROS levels to enhance inflammation.

Subsequently, we found an interesting result that PA could reduce the association of Cd with H pylori infection while strengthening the relationship between Pb and H pylori infection. On the 1 hand, PA positively correlated with GPX levels to decrease ROS levels.[42] Besides, endurance training can induce changes in antioxidant enzyme activity in the spleen, lymph nodes, and thymus, thereby affecting aerobic metabolism in these immune-related organs.[43] The authors inferred that PA might have a beneficiary impact on H pylori infection with Cd exposure via upregulating GPX levels to downregulating ROS levels. Another explanation for the mitigation of PA on the association of Cd with H pylori infection is related to the p38MAPK pathway, which was essential in regulating inflammation caused by Cd exposure.[44] Notably, p38MAPK is named an “Exercise and Myotrophic Factor,” which has an intimate relation with muscle activity and exercise. These findings trigger us to speculate that Cd exposure increases oxidative stress and inflammation to be vulnerable to H pylori infection, which can be alleviated by PA by regulating the p38MAPK pathway.[45] Although PA exhibits a favorable effect on improving health both physically and mentally,[46] our results showed that PA may increase the relationship between Pb and H pylori infection. There is limited research on the regulation of PA on H pylori infection induced by Pb exposure. Whereas, it has been demonstrated that PA might increase the influx of pollutants into the body under certain conditions and Rhie showed a positive association of PA with Pb.[47,48] Higher levels of PA exposed to extreme pollutants might lead to severe immune responses and impaired peripheral vascular diastolic function.[49] Humans are exposed to Cd by eating contaminated food and cigarette use; while being exposed to Pb by inhaling contaminated air, and dust, and ingesting contaminated soil.[15] Therefore, we should take action to minimize Pb exposure and consider various conditions such as seasonal variations and regional variations in atmospheric Pb concentration. PA should be recommended away from seasons and areas with high air pollution levels.

For strengths, this is the first study to explore the joint effect of PA and blood heavy metals on H pylori infection using a highly representative population in the US. Moreover, we performed RCS analysis to evaluate the nonlinear relationship for determining the following appropriate regression method to further analyze the association of blood heavy metals with H pylori infection. For limitations, the NHANES database only includes H pylori information from 1999 to 2000 and does not include data about the PA location, and thus we failed to distinguish the differences in blood heavy metal concentration between indoors and outdoors. Besides, an inherent limitation in the cross-sectional design is the inability to infer causality. We also acknowledge the potential recall bias in PA reporting. However, our study provides a future direction for investigating the underlying mechanism of PA modifying heavy metal exposure to H pylori infection.

In conclusion, blood Cd and Pb had a positive relation with H pylori infection in the US representatives. Moreover, PA could reduce the association of Cd with H pylori infection, but strengthen the relationship between Pb and H pylori infection. Cd and Pb levels might be potential biomarkers for H pylori infection and we should do PA appropriately to prevent the infection. Longitudinal studies will be conducted to explore causality and investigations into the mechanisms by which PA influences the relationship between heavy metal exposure and H pylori infection in the future.

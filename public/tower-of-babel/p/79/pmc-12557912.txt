Immunoglobulin G N-glycosylation predicts long-term risk of ischemic stroke: a nested case-control study
Authors: Shuyu Sun, Jiheng Hao, Chengxu Yu, Hongzhi Liu, Qihua Guan, Yinghao Wang, Jie Wang, Xinyue Wang, Mingzhu Zhang, Xu Zan, Siqi Ge, Hualei Dong, Liyong Zhang, Shaocan Tang, Haifeng Hou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIschemic stroke (IS) ranks as the leading cause of disability and the second cause of mortality in adults worldwide. This investigation aimed to describe the fingerprint of immunoglobulin G (IgG) N-glycans and feature of circulating inflammatory cytokines that were associated with the progression of IS, as well as to screen potential biomarkers for screening individuals at higher IS risk.MethodsA prospective cohort, Liaocheng City Stroke Risk Cohort (LCSRC), was launched in December 2014, in which 1,599 healthy participants were enrolled from Liaocheng People’s Hospital (LPH). During nine years of follow-up, 67 participants who were diagnosed with IS were enrolled in the case group. Meanwhile, 201 individuals who did not develop IS were randomly selected as the controls. The hydrophilic interaction chromatography based on ultra-performance liquid chromatography (HILIC-UPLC) was utilized to detect the IgG N-glycans profiles in serum. A prediction model for the occurrence of IS was constructed using a logistic regression.ResultsThe levels of GP1, GP2, GP3, GP4, GP6, GP7, GP11, GP13, GP19 and GP24 were considerably higher in the IS group compared with the controls, whereas GP5, GP8, GP14 and GP18 were lower among the IS cases. In addition, compared to the controls, the IS patients had substantially higher levels of matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP). In terms of four major features of IgG N-glycosylation, a high level of fucosylation, monogalactosylation, and digalactosylation was significantly associated with a decreased risk for IS occurrence. Moreover, the glycosylation-based prediction model was of promising capability to predict IS risk with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.756 (95%CI: 0.696 to 0.815).ConclusionsOur findings, on the basis of nine-year follow-up, demonstrated that the decreases in fucosylation and galactosylation of IgG are significantly associated with the development of IS, potentially mediated by upregulation of chronic inflammatory processes. Background

Stroke, including ischemic stroke (IS) and hemorrhagic stroke (HS), has been the second leading cause of mortality, constituting 11.6% of all deaths in 2019 [1]. IS, also known as cerebral infarction, represents 87% of all strokes [2]. IS is typified by a pro-inflammatory condition and increased concentrations of inflammatory markers, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and C-reactive protein (CRP) [3–5]. In 2019, a total of 77.19 million individuals were afflicted with an IS, which resulted in 3.29 million deaths [6]. IS places an immense economic and social burden on public health; therefore, emphasizes the necessity to identify individuals at high risk of this disease and to implement targeted preventive measures [1].

Glycosylation is a post-translational modification orchestrated by enzymatic activities, in which oligosaccharides are attached to specified locations on biological macromolecules to create glycoconjugates, which include glycoproteins and glycolipids [7, 8]. Glycosylation participates in a wide array of physiological and pathological processes due to its substantial influence on the structure and function of proteins. In the context of humoral immunity, immunoglobulin G (IgG) constitutes a crucial component that mediates the inflammatory response [9]. For every IgG molecule, the fragment crystallizable (Fc) section in the heavy chains has two N-linked glycosylation sites at asparagine 297 (Asn-297) [10]. Through affecting the binding affinity of Fcγ receptors, N-glycans in the Fc section modulate the activity of IgG effectors [11, 12]. Aberrant N-glycosylation at the Fc can seriously interfere the functionality of IgG, which can result in metabolic disorders, inflammatory diseases, or cancers [13–16].

Considering the inflammatory characteristics of IgG N-glycans and its association with IS risk factors, including aging, obesity, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia [17–21], it is probable that by modulating the inflammatory response, IgG N-glycans play a role in the development of IS. This study aimed to describe the fingerprint of IgG N-glycosylation and the feature of circulating inflammatory cytokines that were associated with the long-term risk of IS, as well as to screen potential biomarkers for screening people at high risk of IS.

Methods

Study participants

This study was a nested case-control study design. A prospective cohort, Liaocheng City Stroke Risk Cohort (LCSRC), was established at Liaocheng People’s Hospital (LPH) in Shandong Province, China. Between December 15, 2014, and February 2, 2015, a total of 1,599 participants were recruited into this community-based cohort study. The inclusion criteria of the cohort were presented as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) no other severe somatic or psychiatric disorders; (3) blood sample available and (4) complete demographic data were available. The exclusion criteria were: (1) participants with any types of stroke history; (2) participants who declined to participate in this study; (3) patients with cancers, or other cerebrovascular diseases; and (4) those participating in other studies.

During nine years follow-up, 67 participants who were newly diagnosed with IS were enrolled in the case group. Meanwhile, 600 participants were identified without any type of stroke. A matched case-control design was implemented to mitigate the potential confounders, by which the 1:3 case-control matching ratio was employed. Matching criteria required exact matching for sex, while age was subjected to caliper matching with a tolerance of ± 2 years. For cases failing initial matching, the age caliper was systematically expanded to ± 5 years to ensure adequate control allocation. Finally, 201 age- and sex-matched healthy participants were enrolled in the control group (Supplementary Box S1). IS was diagnosed in accordance with the International Classification of Diseases (10th revision, codes 163). The flowchart of the participants inclusion was shown in Fig. 1.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The study protocol was examined and authorized by the ethics committee of Liaocheng People’s Hospital (No. LPH085). All procedures performed in the study involving human participants were in accordance with the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before the study.

Collection of blood samples

The blood samples were taken during baseline survey. Following an overnight fast, 2 mL of blood samples were acquired from each participant by venipuncture. Serum was obtained by collecting the sample in a vacuum tube devoid of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). After undergoing processing within eight hours, all the obtained blood samples were maintained at − 80 °C until additional measurements were carried out.

Covariates

Participants’ demographic traits, including gender, age, marital status, education level, height, weight, waist circumference, hip circumference, body mass index (BMI), waist-to-hip ratio (WHR), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), smoking status, and alcohol use, were gathered by trained physicians. Participants in this study were classified into two groups: those with hypertension (SBP ≥ 140 mmHg or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg) and those having normal blood pressure. Among the biochemical and hematologic parameters, fasting blood glucose (FBG), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were assayed using an automatic analyzer (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). The participants were categorized into either the hyperglycemia group (with FBG ≥ 6.1mmol/L) or the normal blood glucose group (with FBG < 6.1mmol/L) [22, 23]. Participants were categorized with dyslipidemia according to Chinese guidelines, defined by TC ≥ 6.2 mmol/L, TG ≥ 2.3 mmol/L, HDL-C < 1.0 mmol/L, or LDL-C ≥ 4.1 mmol/L [24].

Analysis of IgG N-glycans

The IgG N-glycans were detected in the baseline serum samples in accordance with the method previously reported [25]. In brief, IgG was initially isolated from serum by means of a protein G monolithic plate (BIA Separations, Slovenia). After eluting the separated IgG using formic acid, ammonium bicarbonate was promptly added for neutralization purposes. Second, after adding the PNGase F enzyme, the IgG samples were incubated for the release of N-glycans. And then, a mixed solution reagent containing 2-aminobenzamide (2-AB) was used to label the released N-glycans. The labeled glycans were then analyzed by the ultra-performance liquid chromatography with hydrophilic interaction chromatography (HILIC-UPLC) (Walters Corporation, Milford, MA) [26]. Subsequently, manual adjustments were implemented on individual chromatograms to ensure analytical consistency. Through standardized separation protocols, all chromatograms underwent identical segmentation into 24 distinct peaks. Quantitative analysis of glycans was achieved by calculating the proportional representation of each peak relative to the total glycan peak area (GPs) [27–29], thereby enabling comparative assessment of specific glycan concentrations (Supplementary Box S2). Furthermore, 54 derived glycans traits, as well as four major features of IgG N-glycosylation (i.e., fucosylation, sialylation, bisecting N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc), and galactosylation) were calculated on the basis of the initial glycans (Table S1) [10, 26].

Measurement of inflammatory cytokines

To precisely assess the levels of inflammatory cytokines, an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, Jiangsu Meimian industrial Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) was used to test a representative subpopulation of 60 IS patients and 60 healthy controls for six inflammatory cytokines—that is, IL-1, IL-10, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α).

Statistical analysis

Data were excluded from statistical analysis if they displayed inconsistent logic, incomplete or missing data. In univariate analyses, cases with missing data on the variable being analyzed were excluded. For the construction and fitting of all multivariable models (e.g., logistic regression model), we employed complete-case analysis. This required that an observation have no missing data on any variable included in the final multivariable model. All data were analyzed in SPSS 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, United States) and R 4.2.1 (R Core Team) using a two-sided test, with P < 0.05 being considered statistically significant. Moreover, multiple comparison correction was performed with the Benjamini-Hochberg method of false discovery rate (FDR) correction. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was utilized to employed if the glycan measurements conformed to a normal distribution. The median (M) and interquartile range (IQR) were employed for descriptive statistics because the continuous variables were not normally distributed. The Mann-Whitney U test was utilized to assess the differences between IS patients and controls. Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test were used to assess categorical variables, which were represented as n (%). Then, the variables assessed significant in univariate regression analysis, along with clinically relevant confounders identified through systematic literature review, were incorporated into the multivariate logistic regression model for in-depth analysis. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to employe the relationship between IS and initial glycans, derived glycans and inflammatory cytokines after adjusting for covariates (including gender, age, hypertension, hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, and BMI).

Participants were randomly divided into two sets at a ratio (7:3) [30, 31]: 187 participants in the training set and 81 in the validation set. The procedures of developing prediction model of IS were as follows: First, variables with P ≤ 0.1 were selected as preliminary predictors. A multicollinearity test was performed to test potential multicollinearity among initial glycans. Generally, variance inflation factor (VIF) ≥ 5.00 and tolerance < 0.20 were considered that there were multicollinearity issues between variables [32]. Thus, the initial glycans were screened for the purpose of dimension reduction by employing least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression [33]. Secondly, glycan biomarkers for the prediction of IS were identified through the integration of glycans selected with LASSO into a stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis. Third, a predictive model for IS incidence was established using the significant glycan biomarkers. Ultimately, the performance of the prediction model was assessed by means of a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, from which the area under the curve (AUC), 95% confidence interval (CI), sensitivity (Se), and specificity (Sp) were computed [34]. A nomogram was developed based on the predictive model. The calibration plots and Hosmer-Lemeshow test were used to evaluate the validity of the nomogram.

To evaluate the stability of our findings, sensitivity analyses were systematically implemented. In light of potential pathophysiological interactions between cardiovascular comorbidities and IS pathogenesis, we performed an analysis after excluding the participants with cardiovascular conditions.

Association of IgG N-glycans with IS

A total of 24 initial glycans were identified by the HILIC-UPLC assay (Table 2), by which 54 derived glycan traits were then developed (Table S2). It is worth noting that all biospecimens used for glycosylation profiling were collected at baseline. The nested case-control study provides critical temporal resolution: by design, all glycomic profiles were ascertained during the preclinical phase of IS development. In the univariate analysis, significant differences in 14 initial glycans and 31 derived glycans were observed between IS and the controls (P < 0.05, q < 0.05). For the 14 initial glycans, the levels of ten glycans (i.e., GP1, GP2, GP3, GP4, GP6, GP7, GP11, GP13, GP19, and GP24) were conspicuously higher in the IS group as compared to the controls. In contrast, the levels of GP5, GP8, GP14, and GP18 were lower in the IS group (P < 0.05, q < 0.05). Among the derived glycan traits, 18 derived traits, i.e., FBGS/(FBG + FBGS), FG2S2/(FG2 + FG2S1 + FG2S2), FBStotal/FStotal, FBS1/FS1, FBS1/(FS1 + FBS1), GP1n, GP2n, GP4n, GP6n, GP7n, GP11n, GP13n, G0n, FBG2n/G2n, Bn/(Fn + FBn), FBG2n/FG2n, FBG2n/(FG2n + FBG2n), and BG2n/(FG2n + FBG2n) were higher in the IS group (P < 0.05, q < 0.05).

Additionally, in order to elucidate the modulatory function of IgG N-glycans in the development of IS, we also assessed the levels of four major glycosylation features [35]. As shown in Fig. 3 and Table S5, the percentage of fucosylation was substantially lower in the IS group (94.40%) than the controls (94.80%). Regarding to galactosylation, the level of agalactosylation (G0) was 27.15% in IS group, which was significantly higher than the controls (24.93%). A decrease in monogalactosylation (G1) was identified among the IS patients compared to the controls (32.74% vs. 33.35%). Meanwhile, the abundance of digalactosylation (G2) was also lower in the IS patients (16.13% vs. 17.90%). However, there no difference in the bisecting GlcNAc and sialylation. The logistic regression analysis showed that fucosylation (OR = 0.753, 95% CI: 0.609 to 0.933), G1 (OR = 0.782, 95% CI: 0.660 to 0.927), and G2 (OR = 0.856, 95% CI: 0.770 to 0.950) were significantly associated with a decreased risk for IS occurrence (Table 3).

Correlation between IgG N-glycans and inflammation cytokines

The spearman correlation coefficients between initial glycans and circulating inflammatory cytokines were listed in Table S7. The level of hs-CRP showed a positive correlation with GP19 and GP24 (P < 0.05). MMP-9 exhibited a positive correlation with GP14, GP18 and GP19, while it was negatively correlated with GP2 and GP6 (P < 0.05).

Development of predictive model of IS using IgG N-glycans

Details of the multicollinearity test were provided in Table S8, the results of tolerance and VIF values indicated that multicollinearity issues existed. Therefore, a LASSO regression was carried out to screen potential initial glycans associated with IS for dimension reduction. In detail, 5 glycans (GP1, GP5, GP7, GP8 and GP14) were selected, with an optimal λ of 0.0563963 (Fig. 4). Then, the selected initial glycans by LASSO regression were incorporated into the stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify the glycan-based determinants of IS. Finally, GP1, GP5, and GP7 were employed to establish logistic regression models for the prediction of IS. We constructed three logistic regression models to predict the occurrence of IS: (1) Model 1 included GP1, GP5, and GP7; (2) Model 2 included age, gender, and hypertension; (3) Model 3 included age, gender, hypertension, GP1, GP5, and GP7.

Validation and performance of the predictive model

As shown in Fig. 5(A) and Table S9, the ROC curve analysis was used to assess the performance of the predictive models. The AUC was equal to 0.653 (95%CI = 0.574 to 0.731) for the Model 2. We discovered that the Model 1 (AUC = 0.756, 95%CI = 0.696 to 0.815, Se = 85.1%, and Sp = 65.2%) had a similar predictive value (P > 0.05) with the Model 3 (AUC = 0.773, 95%CI = 0.714 to 0.832, Se = 82.1%, and Sp = 67.2%). Given the simplicity of the model in the clinical application context, Model 1 might be a robust predictive model. As a result, we chose the Model 1 as the effective prediction model in the research. Regarding internal validation, the glycan-based models had an AUC of 0.779 (95% CI = 0.662 to 0.897) with a Se of 76.5% and a Sp of 70.3% (Fig. 5(B)).

Discussion

This nested case-control study systematically analyzed the association between IgG N-glycosylation and the onset of IS among a community-based cohort during nine years follow-up. Unlike conventional cross-sectional or case-control studies, this study was prospectively designed. Through rigorous exclusion of pre-existing stroke cases at baseline, this cohort included the participants who were not with stroke in the recruitment phase. Hence, our measurements of glycosylation represented the patterns of IgG N-glycans prior to the onset of IS. This design mitigated reverse causality concerns inherent in retrospective studies, and provided evidence that IgG N-glycosylation alterations act as upstream events in IS pathogenesis. We observed that lower levels of fucosylation and galactosylation were associated with an elevated long-term risk of IS after adjustment for covariates. Specifically, higher levels of fucosylation, and galactosylation functioned as protective factors against IS incidence, whereas agalactosylation constituted a significant risk factor. Meanwhile, the initial glycans detected by HILIC-UPLC were correlated to circulating inflammatory cytokines (e.g., hs-CRP, TNF-α, and MMP-9). In addition, we established a glycan-based prediction model that can effectively predict the occurrence of IS in a long-term follow-up.

Abnormal IgG N-glycans bias the role of IgG in inflammation regulation, switching from anti-inflammatory to pro-inflammatory functions [36]. In details, the decreases in fucosylation, sialylation and galactosylation, as well as the increase in bisecting GlcNAc substantially contribute to the development of many inflammatory and non-communicable diseases (NCDs) [37–40]. The divergent roles of IgG N-glycosylation arise from Fc domain conformational changes dictating receptor binding specificity. Fucosylation is recognized as one of the most predominant and common modifications of IgG. Fucose inhibits antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) by reducing Fc binding to natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages, and neutrophils, down-regulating the pro-inflammatory activity of IgG [41, 42]. Studies demonstrated that the lack of fucose enhances the ability of IgG to trigger ADCC via binding to FcγRIIIA (CD16) on NK cells, leading to an increase in inflammatory cytokines produced by monocytes, including IL-1β, IL-6, CRP, and TNF-a [43, 44]. Moreover, studies have proved that the ADCC activity is enhanced by up to 100-fold when IgG-Fc glycan were in absence of core fucose [41]. In the current study, we observed significantly reduced fucosylation levels in IS patients compared to healthy controls. This inverse association substantiates fucosylation as a protective factor against IS development.

Regarding galactosylation, this study observed an increase in agalactosylation and decrease in galactosylation of IgG, which is in line with our findings in a cross-sectional investigation [45]. A higher level of galactosylation can enhance anti-inflammatory activities of IgG by facilitating the affinity for the inhibitory FcγRIIB receptor [46]. Conversely, decreased terminal galactosylation of IgG can lead to the upregulation of complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) activity and an increase in its pro-inflammatory activities. In detail, mannose-binding lectins interact with GlcNAc, glucose, and fucose; however, they do not interact with galactose, thereby exposing terminal GlcNAc residues in agalactosylated glycans for binding [47, 48]. This mannose-binding lectin activation occurs in a pro-inflammatory manner triggering the lectin complement pathway [49]. Therefore, this evidence emphasizes the potential role of the pro-inflammatory activity induced by agalactosylation in the pathophysiology of IS. So far, all these discoveries show that IgG N-glycosylation is important for modulating the antibody mediated inflammatory response.

IS is a multifactorial and complicated disease driven by a variety of pathologies, including genetic, demographic, and key modifiable risk factors (i.e., hypertension, diabetes, central obesity, and dyslipidemia) [50, 51]. The inflammatory response modulated by inflammatory factors is regarded as a critical factor in the progression of cerebrovascular diseases [52]. Increasing evidences have shown that CRP levels predict myocardial infarction, peripheral arterial disease, stroke, and sudden death [53]. A chronic level of inflammation has been understood of its importance in the progression of IS [54]. The current study identified higher levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., hs-CRP, TNF-α, and MMP-9) in the case group. This finding supports an association between inflammation and IS progression.

Our study also observed a correlation between glycans and inflammatory cytokines. This finding has been emphasized in a series of diseases, which elucidated the function of IgG N-glycosylation in modulating the inflammatory response [39, 48, 55]. Glycosylation of IgG-Fc determines IgG effector function by modulating its binding to Fc receptors, and then modulates the balance between pro- and anti-inflammatory responses [11, 56].

Several guidelines suggest using the Framingham Stroke Risk Profile (FSP) as a robust instrument for predicting 10-year stroke risk [57]. However, the FSP tends to overestimate risk for stroke among Chinese populations [58]. While circulating biomarkers, i.e., CRP, IL-6, and fibrinogen, are widely used in prediction of stroke risk, much of the evidence pertains to shorter-term evaluation, potentially reflecting processes in the imminent stroke pathway or concurrent subclinical disease burden [59, 60]. Currently, the non-invasive IgG N-glycan assay exhibits a remarkable degree of accuracy in the identification of immunological and inflammatory disorders [61]. This study constructed a clinical nomogram integrating three glycomic biomarkers to stratify individuals at elevated IS risk, facilitating early preventive intervention. Diverging from conventional risk algorithms dependent on downstream clinical manifestations (e.g., hypertension diagnosis), this instrument detects upstream pathophysiological processes through IgG N-glycosylation signatures that reflect incipient immune-metabolic dysregulation preceding cerebrovascular injury. The prediction model including GP1, GP5, and GP7 was of promising capability to predict long-term occurrence of IS (AUC = 0.756, 95%CI = 0.696 to 0.815), which demonstrated the potential of IgG N-glycosylation as a predictor of IS.

Notably, GP5 was employed to develop a discrimination model in a separate cross-sectional investigation. This glycan serves as both an agalactosylated structure and a high-mannose N-linked glycan. Research has demonstrated that elevated mannose concentrations suppress macrophage-mediated inflammatory responses and modulate immune functions to achieve anti-inflammatory effects, whereas reduced mannose levels conversely enhance inflammatory processes [45]. In our current investigation, GP5 emerges as a biomarker candidate for developing preventive strategies or therapeutic interventions against IS. Our prediction model derived from multivariable logistic regression analysis not only has robust predictive performance but also can be visualized by a nomogram, which is convenient for its clinical application. Future research exploring multi-marker panels incorporating glycosylation alongside markers of innate inflammation, coagulation, and endothelial function might yield superior risk stratification tools for primary prevention.

Beyond the biological insights, the role IgG N-glycosylation in early predictor of IS raise important considerations regarding potential clinical translation and public health impact. Screening individuals at high risk of IS holds significant promise for cost-effective prevention strategies. Lifestyle is one of the key determinants of stroke. Lifestyle intervention has been considered as one of modulators of IgG N-glycosylation during IS prevention [62, 63]. Specifically, targeted lifestyle modifications including Mediterranean diet, high-intensity interval training, and stress optimization can beneficially reshape IgG glycosylation profiles toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, resulting in a decrease in stroke risk. Early interventions targeting modifiable risk factors in high-risk subgroups could avert expensive acute stroke care, long-term rehabilitation, and the substantial societal burden of disability [64]. The clinical translation of IgG N-glycosylation profiling, however, confronts impediments. Wider implementation would necessitate the development of standardized, high-throughput, and cost-effective analytical platforms and rigorous inter-laboratory standardization of protocols and reporting to ensure result comparability. Affordable, fast, and sensitive N-glycan quantitative analysis can be robustly performed using two well-established platforms: (1) HILIC-UPLC, and (2) capillary gel electrophoresis with laser-induced fluorescence (CGE-LIF) [65, 66]. The HILIC-UPLC method has been extensively implemented for decades and validated in multiple large-scale studies due to its robustness, reproducibility and high throughput performance [66]. Furthermore, the blood collection was minimally invasive and can be clinically detected using serum, enabling simple and cost-effective acquisition of samples without requiring specialized techniques or expensive equipment [67].

As is known, atrial fibrillation, carotid artery stenosis, and prior transient ischemic attacks, are involved in the development of stroke [68–71]. Atrial fibrillation promotes cerebral embolism through intracardiac thrombus formation resulting from blood stasis, while carotid artery stenosis contributes to ischemic stroke via plaque rupture or distal embolization of atherosclerotic material [72, 73]. A history of transient ischemic attacks indicates active vascular instability such as vulnerable plaques or prothrombotic states that may progress to complete occlusion [74]. Pharmacologically, anticoagulants mitigate embolic risk by inhibiting coagulation cascade factors, whereas statins stabilize plaques and attenuate inflammation through both lipid-lowering and pleiotropic mechanisms [75]. This study focused on IgG N-glycosylation to explore early predictors of ischemic stroke in a prospective cohort. To minimize potential confounders that may independently bias the association between glycosylation and stroke risk, we excluded the participants with any of the aforementioned conditions during recruitment stage.

Several limitations and insufficiencies in this study also must be recognized. First, although the nested case-control studies are widely used, certain bias might be introduced to the result, due to the nature of the study design. While acknowledging that complete elimination of bias is unattainable, we have implemented a series of rigorous statistical approaches to minimize its impact. Second, the participants of our cohort were recruited from the Liaocheng City in Northern China, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, the relatively small sample size of this study constrains the robustness of our conclusions. Hence, it is essential to validate this hypothesis through multicenter studies. While we fully acknowledge that the single-center design constrains generalizability, rigorous protocol standardization ensured high external validity. Third, while the relative percentage quantification of glycans employed in this study enables comparability and mitigates batch effects, it inherently does not reflect absolute abundance. Future investigations should incorporate absolute quantification methodologies to resolve differences in concentration. Finally, our study did not include formal cost-effectiveness modeling studies due to the unavailability of real-world billing data, a limitation frequently encountered in early-stage biomarker validation studies, though we acknowledge the pivotal role of economic evaluations in clinical translation. To address these gaps, future work will integrate multi-omics datasets, expand recruitment through international consortia, and implement prospective cost-utility models using hospital-partnered billing databases to assess translational feasibility.

Conclusions

Our findings, on the basis of nine-year follow-up, demonstrated that the decreases in fucosylation and galactosylation of IgG are significantly associated with the development of IS, potentially mediated by upregulation of chronic inflammatory processes. IgG N-glycosylation fingerprint might serve as a potential biomarker for predicting long-term risk of IS.

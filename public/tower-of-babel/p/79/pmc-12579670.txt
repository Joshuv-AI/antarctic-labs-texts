Emerging prospects of Bacillus species in attaining the sustainable development goals
Authors: Harsh Kumar, Shivani Guleria, Kamil Kuča, Lukas Peter, Arifullah Mohammed, Tabarak Malik, Nidhi Sethi, Daljeet Singh Dhanjal, Dinesh Kumar, Hasnita Binti Che Harun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractThe world is witnessing a serious challenge of poverty which is reflected as hidden hunger and simultaneously overpopulation puts a grave burden on our ecosystem. In order to overcome these serious issues, the sustainable development goals (SDGs) serve a very significant role. Concurrently, the contribution of microorganisms in achieving the SDGs cannot be overlooked. Bacillus species as a probiotic has led to the development of various commercial fermented products that are available in the market. Additionally, the formulated products have been clinically validated for their health benefits. Bacillus species have also been reported to serve as bio-preservatives and biocontrol agents because they aid in preserving and maintaining the final food product as well as post-harvest goods. The utilization of Bacillus spp. as probiotics has significantly contributed to the overall well-being of animals. Moreover, many different species of Bacillus have been reported to be utilized as bio-cementing agents. All the aforementioned roles of Bacillus species contribute to addressing zero hunger (SDG 2), good health and well-being (SDG 3), and industry, infrastructure, and innovation (SDG 9). Key points• Role of Bacillus spp. as probiotic have been successfully proven by clinical studies.• Prescence of antimicrobial genes in the Bacillus spp. genome play important role in bio-preservation and biocontrol.• In animal feeds Bacillus spp. are a good alternate of antibiotics and use of Bacillus spp. in cement industry is innovative approach.Keywords: Bacillus spp., Human health, Probiotic, Functional feed and food, Sustainable development goals

Introduction

Bacillus species of bacteria own a global repute of falling into the category of Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) microbes (Bahaddad et al. 2023). However, certain members of this species including Bacillus cereus, Bacillus brevis, Bacillus alvei, Bacillus circulans, Bacillus infantis, Bacillus coagulans, Bacillus idriensis, Bacillus licheniformis, Bacillus laterosporus, Bacillus macerans, Bacillus sphaericus, Bacillus pumilus, Bacillus thuringiensis, and Bacillus subtilis can be pathogenic in nature and thus have been associated with discomforts and diseases (Bahaddad et al. 2023). The Bacillus spp. possesses the capacity of forming spores that can withstand harsh conditions, and these have recently displayed tremendous promise for use as probiotics (Kim et al. 2019a, b; Soares et al. 2019). In addition, they are capable of producing antagonistic substances acting against a wide array of bacterial pathogens, providing extracellular digestive enzymes (β-glucanase, phytase, xylanase, protease, amylase, β-mannanase), which enhance nutrient absorption and feed digestibility, and regulate animal host immunity (Table 1) (Kumar and Bhalla 2004a, b); Dutta and Ghosh 2015; Liu et al. 2015; Thakur et al. 2018). Bacillus direct-fed microbials (DFM) also proved their ability in enhancing both humoral and cellular immune responses mediated through raising solitary lymphoid follicle numbers in the mucosa of the intestine, controlling gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) development, enhancing macrophage function, and stimulating antibody responses post vaccination (Bahaddad et al. 2023). Various strains of a few Bacillus spp. are currently being employed as probiotic supplements in feeds of the animal. Other than that, their potential to develop into spores is advantageous as it facilitates long-term preservation without a decline in viability in contrast to those non-spore-forming bacteria. Moreover, spores can withstand harsh conditions like low pH of the gastric barrier and can successfully reach the small intestine to express their probiotic properties (Mingmongkolchai and Panbangred 2018). A noteworthy innovation in concrete technology constitutes developing self-healing cementitious materials by incorporating bacterial agents (Milović et al. 2024). This bio-inspired material uses microbial calcite precipitation for healing cracks developing autonomously, thus reducing maintenance costs and enhancing the durability of the material (Milović et al. 2024). Bacillus spp., particularly Bacillus subtilis and Bacillus pasteurii, are commonly used owing to their ability in thriving the high pH concrete environment and produce calcite, thereby effectively sealing micro-cracks (Wang et al. 2014; Vijay et al. 2017; Yamasamit et al. 2023). The microbial technology serves the critical function of driving sustainable development through diverse applications and industries (Choi et al. 2020). Green microbiology aims at harnessing the microorganism power in providing eco-friendly solutions and contributes towards environmental sustainability. Additionally, it aims to diminish the environmental footprint of industries by incorporating the microbial technologies. The various applications of green microbiology include energy generation, food production, infra, and waste management (Barzee et al. 2021; Nayak et al. 2025a, b). The objective is the promotion of sustainable practices through leveraging microorganisms in industry as well as providing eco-friendly solutions for diverse sectors (Qumsani 2024). Microbes can play a critical role in the advancement of the United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), encompassing social, environmental, and economic growth (Jiang et al. 2023). Integration of microbial technology for achieving SDGs makes it possible for addressing significant global challenges associated with resource depletion as well as environmental sustainability including food security, well-being improvement, green energy development, and healthcare provision (Le Vo et al. 2012) . Educating individuals about the positive attributes of microorganisms in society can result in intelligent utilization of microbes for sustainable development (Park et al. 2020).

This review explores the emergent role of Bacillus spp. in accomplishing the SDGs: SDG 2 (zero hunger), SDG 3 (well-being and good health), and SDG 9 (industry, infrastructure, and innovation). Our first objective explains the role of various Bacillus spp. in probiotic preparations forming different varieties of fermented food. The second objective emphasizes the clinical aspects of Bacillus spp. The third objective underlines the role of different Bacillus spp. in the preservation of food. The fourth objective explains the function of Bacillus spp. in the protection of agricultural crops and improvement of the overall well-being amongst farm animals. These four objectives, on the whole, successfully provide details on the significance of Bacillus spp. in attaining SDG 2 and SDG 3. Finally, the objectives also signify the importance of Bacillus spp. in the concrete industry along with aiding in accomplishing the SDG 9.

Application of Bacillus spp. as probiotics

The global hunger problem cannot be handled by simply increasing the productivity of food but also through making improvements in food accessibility for the general public (Akinsemolu 2018). As per the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), the hunger problem of the world majorly results from undemocratic and inequitable distribution and inadequate food access (Akinsemolu 2018). A lot of fruits, vegetables, and raw materials get wasted due to improper storage conditions or poor food processing, attributed to unfavourable climate triggering their spoilage. There are diverse methods of preserving food, including canning, drying, and freezing. Most of the processed foods are expensive or have food safety implications such as botulism (caused by the bacterium Clostridium botulinum) disease in canned foods. With respect to this, fermentation technology can be considered a valuable tool employed in different approaches for the preservation of food, thus eliminating hunger (SDG 2) across the globe (Akinsemolu 2018). Fermentation is a green and economically viable process by which vegetables and fruits can be kept under storage for long-term consumption (Ross et al. 2002). Employment of microbes such as Lactobacillus, Acetobacter, and Saccharomyces results in the production of some primary as well as secondary metabolites with a wide range of applications in the preservation of food (Caplice and Fitzgerald 1999; Ross et al. 2002). Recently, different Bacillus spp. have been found to play their role in developing various kinds of fermented food, depending on the non-dairy and dairy food components used as raw materials (Table 2). Several commercial products have been marketed that contain Bacillus spp. (Table 3).

Raj and co-workers (2023) have isolated Bacillus velezensis FCW2 probiotic from coconut water fermented naturally and claimed to contain four thousand fifty-nine coding sequence (CDS) genes encoding proteins involved in adhesion, micronutrient production, and stress resistance. The genes responsible for the production of exopolysaccharides, secondary metabolites, and other beneficial nutrients were identified. Both intracellular cell and intact cell-free extracts of PUFSTP38 (B. amyloliquefaciens KT921420), PUFSTP39 (B. subtilis KT921421), and PUFSTP35 (B. licheniformis KT921419) strains were isolated in vitro from brine mango pickle fermented traditionally, possessing significant antioxidative, antityrosinase, and antidiabetic properties (Ragul et al. 2020). Dong and teammates (2020) have documented the development of natto chestnut food through fermentation using Bacillus natto. The study reported that, on comparison between unfermented and natto-chestnut, significant functional activities including fibrinolytic activity, hypoglycemic effect, and antioxidant levels were displayed. In addition, the functional components such as total flavonoids, volatile compounds (VC), and total phenol increased significantly. The study concluded that the fermented chestnut possessed good functional and physicochemical properties, and it could be designed as a new kind of functional food with health benefits in the food product industry. Immunomodulatory properties of exopolysaccharides (EPS) obtained from B. licheniformis BioE-BL11, extracted from fermented Korean kimchi, were assessed by Kook and co-workers (2019). The study results showed that EPS isolated from BioE-BL11 caused significant inhibition of IL-6, the pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion in lipopolysaccharide-stimulated mouse macrophage RAW264.7 cells by 40.7%, and enhancement in the secretion of IL-10, the anti-inflammatory cytokine, in a dose-dependent fashion, suggesting that EPS generated by the Bacillus strains might be a useful functional ingredient for the food, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics industries. Chen and team (2015) have documented that Bacillus isolates producing β-D-glucosidase recovered from beans of vanilla were indirectly or directly involved in the hydrolysis of glucovanillin and influenced the formation of flavor during vanilla curing. Chettri and Tamang (2015) have reported that fermented food from soybeans named Bekang and Tungrymbai, commonly consumed in the North-East region of India, has mainly been fermented using Bacillus spp. The authors have identified Bacillus subtilis (55%), Bacillus licheniformis (25.5%), and Bacillus pumilus (19.5%) from Tungrymbai, and several Bacillus species from Bekang including Bacillus brevis (2%), Bacillus coagulans (6.5%), Bacillus circulans (7.5%), B. licheniformis (16.5%), Bacillus sphaericus (4.6%), B. subtilis (51.8%), and B. pumilus (9.1%).

Bacillus sp. as probiotics: the clinical aspects

Good food ensures good health (SDG 3). A human being impoverished of the daily requirements of nutrition cannot stay healthy and hence has more proneness towards diseases (Akinsemolu 2018). In this regard, the aim must not be mere production of higher food quantities but to ascertain its quality and safety for maintenance of the well-being of those consuming it (Akinsemolu 2018). As discussed previously, food preservation through fermentation technology has proven to be an important tool to ensure food quality and minimise the waste (Ross et al. 2002). Furthermore, probiotics possess immunomodulatory properties, which further improve gut microbiome as well as increase nutrient bioavailability. They alter the composition and functioning of the gut microbiome and boost both neurobiology and immunity (Hemarajata and Versalovic 2013). A combination of lactulose and B. coagulans Unique IS2 was evaluated for its efficacy in treating functional constipation amongst adults using a double-blind randomised trial (Venkataraman et al. 2023). The results displayed a significant reduction in the sensation of defecation, incomplete evacuation, and abdominal pain related to the ability of strains in producing short-chain fatty acids. The authors observed no adverse events in all the groups, as well as normal vital parameters during the complete course of this study. On the whole, the results indicated that Bacillus coagulans Unique IS2 inclusion to lactulose decreased the time required in relieving constipation in comparison to lactulose alone. A study by Kang et al. (2021) revealed that B. coagulans SNZ 1969 (BC) supplementation in healthy adults suffering from mild intermittent constipation showed improvement owing to BC-induced enrichment in Lactobacillales and reduction of Synergistales. Madempudi and co-workers (2019) have reported Bacillus coagulans Unique IS2 administration in adults with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), displaying improvement in comparison to placebo. The researchers noticed no significant changes in levels of proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, INF-γ, IL-6, IL-12) as well as anti-inflammatory cytokine (IL-10) upon completion of 8-week Bacillus coagulans treatment in comparison to placebo. Majeed and teammates (2018) have conducted the efficacy and safety of the Bacillus coagulans MTCC 5856 strain as a probiotic for the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) amongst IBS patients. The findings showed a significant alteration (p = 0.01) in favour of the probiotic strain as observed for the primary efficacy measures Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS), Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression (HAM-D), irritable bowel syndrome quality of life questionnaire (IBS-QOL), and Center for Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CES-D). An inflammatory biomarker, serum myeloperoxidase, was also significantly decreased (p < 0.01) when compared to the end and baseline of the study.

Application of Bacillus spp. in food preservation

Bacillus species not only play a significant role as probiotics for the development of different kinds of fermented foods but also have a unique feature of having antifungal or antimicrobial genes in the genome that aid as biocontrol agents and in food preservation in the agriculture sector for the management of various pests in plant growth as well as during postharvest time, thus making them versatile amongst other probiotic genera. Kim and researchers (2024) have partially purified a Bacillus spp.-isolated bacteriocin-like inhibitory substance (BLIS) from fermented Korean foods. The obtained antibacterial substance caused inhibition of fungi and aerobic bacteria growth on the ripening room and Gouda cheese walls. Bacicyclicin XIN-1, a circular bacteriocin, was purified from Bacillus species Xin1 and effectively eliminated or inhibited Staphylococcus aureus ATCC6538, Listeria monocytogenes LM201, and B. cereus ATTCC14579 in skim milk (Xin et al. 2021). In another study, Chhetri and co-workers (2019) have isolated the bacteriocin (subtilin and subtilosin) produced by B. subtilis (P5-6) from soy sauce and salted fish. The isolated bacterial culture was further applied to a cottage cheese model, and the protective culture was capable of reducing S. aureus along with significant assistance in prolonging cheese freshness over 16 days with no effect on its physicochemical properties, in particular pH.

The yield of a cyclo-lipopeptide, iturin A, was enhanced through gene shuffling methodology in B. amyloliquefaciens LZ-5 (Shi et al. 2018). Additionally, the iturin A efficacy for controlling food spoilage by yeast, the anti-yeast action of the cyclo-lipopeptide was assessed in orange juice that was incubated alongside Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The orange juice treated with iturin A at 0.76 mg/mL displayed a significant (p < 0.05) control over the yeast population during storage, similar to the control by natamycin at 0.30 mg/mL. Also, iturin A displayed a minute effect on the chemical-physical properties of the orange juice. Plipastatin, an antimicrobial lipopeptide, has been identified using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS) from B. atrophaeus OSY-7LA, extracted from the Chinese delicacies by Guo et al. (2016). Supplementation of OSY-7LA crude extract supernatant in Vienna sausages, inoculated using Listeria innocua, exhibited a 2-log reduction after 24 and 12 h. It was found that the new strain, as well as related antimicrobials, was potentially beneficial in food preservation.

Application of Bacillus spp. as agricultural biocontrol

B. velezensis CMML21–47 displayed strong antagonistic activity towards two main fungal diseases in sweet potato caused by Ceratocystis fimbriata and Fusarium oxysporum in field assays (Yoon et al. 2024). The experimentation used ultra–high performance liquid chromatography with quadrupole time–of–flight mass spectrometry (UPLC–Q–TOF–MS) selectively and detected lipopeptides, such as fengycin, bacillomycin, and surfactin (Table 5). Choub et al. (2024) have reported that flowering cherry seedlings inoculated with bacterial culture broth containing B. subtilis CV21 inhibited Alternaria alternata CH10 and A. alternata CH3 phytopathogens’ growth, and in turn reduced the severity index (DSI) in cherry leaf spot (CLS), a common disease in comparison to the control and chemical treatment group. A hydroponics assay was conducted on three strains of B. velezensis, i.e., F18, L73, HC-5, wherein substantial lowering of disease (Ralstonia solanacearum causing wilt) incidence was observed in ginger seedlings (Cui et al. 2024). The three strains of B. velezensis, particularly HC-5 and F18, showed remarkable efficacy in controlling bacterial wilt disease in greenhouse experiments. The tubers soaked in the above biocontrol agents demonstrated reduced pathogen populations and disease severity. B. amyloliquefaciens YZU-SG146 displayed 84.21% control efficiency towards Verticillium dahliae (causing wilt of cotton) in the pot experiment (Liu et al. 2023). It was found that the strain produced siderophores and some lytic enzymes.

The Bacillus vallismortis BS07 (BS07) strain, having plant growth-promoting rhizobacterium (PGPR) ability, was evaluated for its potential as biocontrol agents towards multiple pathogens attacking chili pepper (Park et al. 2013a, b). In greenhouse settings, the chili pepper seedlings under the chemical control, benzothiadiazole (BTH) or BS07 treatment by soil drench caused significant suppression of soft rot incidence by decreasing the percentage of disease lesion area for each leaf disk in comparison to the negative control. Treatment with BTH or BS07 induced plant resistance towards phytophthora blight disease resulting from Phytophthora capsici upon pathogenic challenge. It was found that there was a larger reduction in anthracnose infection resulting from Colletotrichum acutatum on the matured fruits in BTH or BS07 treated plants in comparison to the untreated control. In field settings, the percentage incidence of soft rot and phytophthora blight disease severity was reduced significantly in BTH and BS07 treated plants in comparison to the untreated control.

Application of Bacillus spp. in chicken feed

Bacillus spp. serves as functional feed for improving the overall health as well as milk and meat quality amongst the livestock. Feeding Bacillus subtilis exhibited desirable effects on increased egg production, eggshell quality, and modulation of ceacal microbial profile despite organic acid co-supplementation (Park et al. 2024). Dietary supplementation of B. subtilis with viable spores of 1.0 × 107 to 768 single day-old broiler chicks (Ross 308) displayed positive improvement in ileal energy as well as crude protein digestibility, optimized intestinal morphology, increased Lactobacillus counts, increased intestinal digestive enzymes activity, and upregulated sodium/glucose cotransporter protein-1 (SGLT-1), peptide transporter 1 (PEPT-1), and glucose transporter-2 (GLUT-2) expression in jejunum (Wang et al. 2023). Water-supplemented Bacillus clausii (0.03 ml/L) showed enhanced feed conversion ratio (FCR) and higher (p < 0.05) weight gain in broiler chicks aging 120 days (Mushtaq et al. 2023). Histo-morphological results demonstrated increased villus height, villus surface area, and villus height: crypt depth. Moreover, supplementation with 12 × 106 spores at 0.03 ml/L enhanced (p < 0.05) the net profit owing to increased production. A study by Memon et al. (2022) has investigated the entire chicken (Chinese native chicken breed with yellow feathers) gut microbiota responses towards probiotic feeding of B. subtilis in the absence as well as presence of Eimeria tenella pathogen infection. The study findings showed that probiotic supplementation in the presence of Eimeria tenella infection enhanced the abundance of several commensal genera, including Clostridium sensu stricto 1, Enterococcus, Romboutsia, Corynebacterium, Subdoligranulum, Bacillus, Weissella, and Turicibacter with roles in anti-inflammation, metabolic reactions, protective pathways modulation against pathogens, and butyrate production.

Dietary supplementation with B. subtilis ATCC19659 fed at colony-forming unit (cfu)/g of 5 × 108, significantly increased bodyweight, enhanced immune response, improved antioxidant capacity, and nutrition absorption in 600 Arbor 1-day-old Acres broilers (Mohamed et al. 2022). The probiotic B. subtilis 29784 (Bs29784) strain specifically brought enrichment of butyrate-producing families Lachnospiraceae and Ruminococcaceae, as well as Clostridiales vadin BB60 family members in 1-day-old broiler chicks (Ross 308) (Choi et al. 2021).

Application of Bacillus spp. in fish feed

Recently, Dighiesh and co-workers (2024) have evaluated the symbiotic effects of multi-strain Bacillus (MSB) (Bacillus pumilus, B. subtilis, and B. licheniformis) probiotics in the diet of Oreochromis niloticus, Nile tilapia, on exposure to Aspergillus flavus fungus infection. The findings revealed that the fish growth, blood parameters, rearing water quality, and total proteins, globulins, albumin, as well as amylase activity were increased significantly (p < 0.05) in all of the MSB-treated groups. Also, the expression of mRNA for growth hormone (GH), insulin-like growth factor receptor-1 (IGF-1R), interleukin-8 (IL-8), and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) was enhanced in a dose-dependent fashion owing to dietary inclusion of MSB (p < 0.05). Tachibana et al. (2021) have included 0.08% and 0.04% B. licheniformis and B. subtilis, respectively, in the Nile tilapia feed. The results showed that the enrichment modified the intestinal microbiota and improved growth performance, reducing the potential pathogenic agent species class (Table 6). Feeding Carassius auratus fish with 109 and 107 cfu/g Bacillus velezensis JW enriched diets showed significant improvement in the survival rate on Aeromonas hydrophila challenging (Yi et al. 2018). The study revealed that the antibacterial action was attributed to the presence of gene clusters of 3 Polyketide Synthetase (PKS), 4 bacteriocins, and 5 Nonribosomal Peptide-Synthetase (NRPS). Selim and Reda (2015) have found that Nile tilapia fed with B. amyloliquefaciens spores at 1 × 104 and 1 × 106 cfu/g concentrations led to a significant increase in tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 (IL-1) mRNA levels as noticed in the regions of anterior kidney tissues on the commencement of the feeding trial.

Application of Bacillus spp. in pig feed

In a recent study, Júnior et al. (2023) have found that supplementation of B. subtilis DSM 34520 improved the performance of piglets feeding on diets without or with pure fiber sources. Additionally, irrespective of the source of fiber, Bacillus subtilis reduced diarrhoea incidence and improved intestinal integrity and morphology in nursery piglets with no effect on the inflammatory markers’ gene expression. Dietary supplementation of complexes of B. subtilis and B. licheniformis in growing pigs could significantly improve the digestibility of crude protein (CP) and reduce fecal counts of Escherichia coli, hydrogen sulfide (H2S), and ammonia (NH3) emissions (Kim et al. 2022). Deng and researchers (2020) have reported that the diet of piglets rich in B. subtilis tends to modulate the microbiota by an increment in the Firmicutes (Lactobacillus reuteri, Lactobacillus johnsonii) abundance and diminishing the E. coli abundance when compared to the control group (p < 0.05). Additionally, an increase in the serum glucose as well as villus height of the ileum was observed. Feed supplementation of B. subtilis (DSM 25841) improved the F18 E. coli growth rate in infected pigs by improving gut integrity and reducing gut permeability (Kim et al. 2019a, b). However, there was no clear decrease in fecal β-hemolytic coliforms and diarrhoea in pigs with B. subtilis supplementation. Feeding B. subtilis could also impact inflammation of the intestine in E. coli infected animals.

Application of Bacillus spp. in cattle feed

Dairy Holstein calves fed on conventional milk replacer combined with a dose of 7.5 mL B. subtilis probiotic daily experienced improved growth performance as well as a favourable metabolic profile for the initial 90-day period of their lives (Antanaitis et al. 2024). It was found that the treatment group (TG) displayed consistently high body weights in comparison to the control group (CG) at ages 30 days, 60 days, and 90 days, with increases recorded as 4.11% for 30 days, 3.75% for 60 days, and 2.91% for 90 days, indicating enhanced growth. In addition, the TG demonstrated enhanced liver health, as revealed by a 41.12% decrease in aspartate transaminase (AST) activity after 30 days, and a better protein metabolism, evidenced by a 64.68% increment in the activity of gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) and a 9.36% enhancement in phosphorus concentration. Recently, Cappellozza et al. (2024) have reported that the Holstein–Friesian dairy cows fed with Bacillus-derived direct-fed microbial (DFM) showed greater milk yield, total solids and lactose yield, milk production efficiency, and also a better energy-corrected efficiency of milk production and yield of milk protein. In addition, cows feeding on DFM displayed greater mean plasma levels of insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I), with no differences observed for plasma insulin and glucose. Urakawa et al. (2022) have shown that B. subtilis (BS) C-3102 feeding dairy cows were associated with lower thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) and cortisol levels and enhanced the proportion of CD11c+ and CD4+ T cells, high CD172a dendritic cells presence in the mastitis blood of dairy cows. The study concluded that Bacillus subtilis C-3102 could be used as a potential probiotic and also has preventative capacity towards mastitis in the cows.

SDG 9 (industry, innovation, and infrastructure)

The persistent requisition of adopting green technologies and microbes being a sustainable choice could help us achieve SDG 9 only if used intelligently, effectively implemented, and widely promoted (Akinsemolu 2018). Microorganisms may also exert positive impacts (e.g., substance healing) as their action could be examined by creating a bio-oriented shielding system which is intended for use as construction materials (Makul et al. 2022). Under all situations, the comprehending interaction between cement-based materials and microbes is a vital step towards developing safer, better-quality, and more sustainable buildings in different conditions (Makul et al. 2022). Bacillus species can either be exploited as an environmentally dependable course of action, concrete/mortar surface crack healing, improvement of durability properties, or lowering the ions ingress into the cement matrix in the field or at large scale (Table 7) (Mutitu et al. 2019).

Milović and co-workers (2024) have studied that the inclusion of Bacillus muralis and Bacillus licheniformis in co-culture or as individual bacteria, at 8 logs cfu concentration, induced initiation of calcium carbonate precipitate formation, which effectively sealed the formed cracks, thereby restoring and yet enhancing the strength of the material. Besides short-term improvements, the study also estimated long-term improvements, along with measurement of compressive strength over periods extending up to 180 days. Their findings demonstrated strength improvements and sustained self-healing capabilities under different environmental conditions, thus emphasizing the possibility for long-term applications in real-world infrastructure. The addition of B. subtilis to the concrete showed an increment in strength as compared to the control mix (Jena et al. 2020). A maximum increase of 32% compressive strength, 14% split tensile strength, as well as 29% flexural strength was noticed in the specimen containing bacterial concentration at 105 cells/mL in water. Roadmap for future research

Bacillus spp. holds significant promise amongst various sectors, thus necessitating a comprehensive roadmap for research to fully harness their potential. Key areas of future investigation include exploring metabolic and genetic engineering for improvement in desired traits, developing sustainable processing and harvesting techniques to decrease costs, and optimizing cultivation methods for enhancement of bioactive compound production and biomass yield. In addition, rigorous clinical studies become essential in validating Bacillus spp. therapeutic applications, especially its anti-ageing, anticancer, and anti-inflammatory properties. Understanding the market dynamics as well as consumer acceptance can facilitate in developing Bacillus species-based products, meanwhile exploring its potential in space biotechnology may possibly support life in the extra-terrestrial environments. Addressing these areas will enable future research to fully harness the potential of Bacillus spp. in health, technology, and sustainability.

Conclusion

Bacillus species as a probiotic could be used to develop both non-dairy and dairy fermented foods. These probiotic strains could effectively treat various chronic human diseases with no side effects. The role of Bacillus spp. in food preservation as well as a biocontrol agent has been possible due to the presence of antimicrobial genes; hence, the products are capable of destroying plant pathogenic bacteria and fungi. In the animal farming sector, a variety of Bacillus spp. have been used as probiotics (also considering them as a substitute for antibiotics) for enriching functional feed that tends to improve the overall quality of meat and milk and ameliorate various disease problems. Putting all these Bacillus spp. together, their activities make them strong candidates for fulfilling the SDG 2 (End hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition and promote sustainable agriculture) and SDG 3 (Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being of individuals at all ages) objectives. While in the cement industry, Bacillus spp. not only increase the mortar strength but also help in self-healing ability, which further helps in developing durable/sustainable infrastructure as well as fulfilling the SDG 9 (Build resilient infrastructure, foster sustainable and inclusive industrialization and promote innovation) objective.

Subarachnoid haemorrhage – managing the workflow – secondary/acute care
Authors: Haris Memon, Eze Ifeanyi, Lynsey Crowley, Amna Razzaq
Published: 2024

Introduction

Subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH) is a type of haemorrhagic stroke caused by bleeding into the subarachnoid space around the brain. The incidence of SAH in the UK is approximately 8 per 100,000 population.1 SAH is commonly caused by rupture of a cerebral aneurysm.2 Other causes include head trauma, vascular malformations, hypertension or coagulation disorders. Treatment options are mostly surgical, by clipping the base of the aneurysm or by endovascular coiling, placing a platinum coil in the aneurysm via an intra-arterial catheter to initiate thrombosis. Overall, the principal aim is to prevent further bleeding.3 Outcomes are usually poor if not managed in a timely and adequate manner.

Conclusion

All patients had a neurological examination performed within the first 24 hours of admission; however, only 60% of these were complete or adequately documented. The Glasgow coma score (GCS) was only recorded in 70% of patients, despite it being an important prognostic indicator.

Only 40% of patients audited had a CT scan within 1 hour of arrival to hospital. This is an important area for the trust to improve upon; however, these data may also have been influenced by the means of data collection, as many patients did not present with classical symptoms of SAH and thus were not initially assessed to require an urgent CT scan of their head.

There is limited data on the trust's compliance with the audit standard of securing ruptured aneurysms, probably because of small sample size, not being a neurosurgical centre and most patients being managed conservatively.

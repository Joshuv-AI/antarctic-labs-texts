USP18 mediates high glucose‐induced cardiomyocyte injury by regulating the JAK/STAT signaling pathway through stabilizing FOXC2 expression
Authors: Zhengrong Xu, Weidong Ren, Jun Gu, Wenjuan Deng, Lijuan Zuo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCardiomyopathy is one of the complications of diabetes, among which myocardial fibrosis is the most typical feature. Ubiquitin‐specific peptidase 18 (USP18) is a deubiquitinating enzyme. Multiple studies suggest that it may have the potential to protect against myocardial injury under diabetes, but the specific mechanism remains unclear.MethodsThe high glucose (HG) was used to treat neonatal mouse ventricular myocytes (NMVMs) to induce diabetic myocardial injury models, and gene expression was detected by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR) and Western blot. The phenotypes of NMVMs were measured using cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8), the corresponding kits, flow cytometry, enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and DCFH‐DA probe. Besides, cell fibrosis was reflected by immunofluorescence (IF) and Western blot assay. Ubiquitination analysis and Cycloheximide (CHX) experiment were applied to assess protein ubiquitination and degradation, respectively.ResultsUSP18 was downregulated in HG‐induced NMVMs, and USP18 overexpression promoted viability and inhibited apoptosis of NMVMs exposed to HG. Meanwhile, the inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis of NMVMs impelled by HG were reversed after USP18 upregulation. Mechanically, USP18 stabilized forkhead box C2 (FOXC2) expression by reducing its ubiquitination to participate in regulating NMVM viability, apoptosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis via the Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathway.ConclusionsUSP18 inhibits the ubiquitination of FOXC2 to stabilize its expression, thereby mediating HG‐induced myocardial injury through the JAK/STAT pathway.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a typical complication of diabetic patients. The common symptoms are abnormalities in cardiac function and structure, and it has a relatively high incidence and mortality rate 1 . Myocardial fibrosis is a prominent feature of DCM, caused by the accumulation of extracellular matrix derived from cardiac fibroblasts 2 . It is of great significance to explore the mechanism of diabetes‐induced myocardial injury and seek effective means for the prevention and treatment of myocardial injury.

Ubiquitin‐specific peptidase 18 (USP18), a deubiquitinating enzyme belonging to the ubiquitin‐specific protease family, regulates the stability and function of target proteins by removing their ubiquitinating modification, thereby regulating multiple signaling pathways 3 . USP18 functions in a variety of diseases, such as cancer 4 , cardiovascular diseases 5 , and immune metabolic diseases 6 . It has been reported that the ubiquitin–proteasome system had potential therapeutic significance in DCM7. Furthermore, the expression of USP18 was downregulated in the cardiac tissues of diabetic mice 7 . Feng et al. 8 found that the downregulation of USP18 would interfere with the protective effect of cardamom on myocardial remodeling, while its overexpression would enhance this effect. The above studies suggest that USP18 may potentially protect against diabetic myocardial injury.

Forkhead box C2 (FOXC2) is a transcription factor that is involved in various physiological processes, including epithelial–mesenchymal transition 9 , lipid metabolism 10 , and embryonic development 11 . It has been reported that FOXC2 is frequently dysregulated in various cancers and functions as an oncogene 12 . Also, FOXC2 was associated with insulin resistance in gestational diabetes mellitus 13 . Furthermore, FOXC2 was a protective factor for myocardial injury 14 and was also essential for ventricular development 15 . However, whether FOXC2 is involved in the regulation of diabetic myocardial injury by USP18 remains unknown.

This study aims to extract cardiomyocytes derived from neonatal mice to elucidate the mechanism by which USP18 regulates myocardial injury under HG stimulation in vitro, providing a new target for the treatment of DCM.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Cell separation and culture

According to the previous studies 16 , 17 , the neonatal mouse ventricular myocytes (NMVMs) were extracted from the C57BL/6J mice (1–3 days) (SPF Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) using a well‐established enzymatic digestion protocol. Briefly, ventricular tissues were dissected, minced, and subjected to enzymatic digestion to dissociate the cardiomyocytes. Then, the cells were cultured in the DMEM medium (Sunncell, Wuhan, China) added with 10% fetal bovine serum (Sunncell) and 100 IU/mL penicillin/streptomycin (Sunncell). The NMVMs in the high glucose (HG) groups were cultured in the DMEM medium containing 35 mmol/L d‐glucose (Targetmol, Boston, MA, USA). For the protein degradation analysis, the NMVMs were treated with Cycloheximide (CHX, Maokang, Shanghai, China) for different times (0, 2, 4, and 6 h). The NMVMs should be passaged and treated for experiments within 2 weeks. The process of cell extraction from mice complied with the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Hebei North University.

Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR)

The RNA was isolated using Trizol (Sangon, Shanghai, China). After measuring the concentration of the RNA, reverse transcription was conducted by applying the Hifair® AdvanceFast 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China). Then the cDNA was mixed with primers and SYBR Green I (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) to prepare qPCR reaction solution, and the qPCR was performed based on the following procedures: Predegeneration, 95°C 2 min; Degeneration, 95°C 15 s; Anneal/extension, 60°C 20 s; 40 cycles. The GAPDH was used as the reference gene, and the expression of genes was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt manner. The primer sequences were shown in Table 1.

Western blot

The protein was isolated using the RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) added with PMSF (Beyotime). Then the protein was mixed with 5 × SDS‐PAGE protein loading buffer (Yeasen) and boiled at 100°C for 10 min. After the protein bands were separated, they were transferred onto the PVDF membrane (Beyotime), which was closed using the Fast Blocking Western (Yeasen). The incubation with primary antibodies (anti‐USP18, 1:1,000, 53229, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA; anti‐collagen I (Col I), 1:5,000, ab316222, Abcam, Cambridge, UK; anti‐Col III, 1:1,000, ab184993, Abcam; anti‐FOXC2, 1:1,000, ab314051, Abcam; anti‐p‐Janus kinase (pJAK2), 1:5,000, ab32101, Abcam; anti‐JAK2, 1:5,000, ab108596, Abcam; anti‐p‐signal transducer and activator of transcription (pSTAT3), 1:10,000, ab76315, Abcam; anti‐STAT3, 1:2,000, ab68153, Abcam; anti‐GAPDH, 1:2,500, ab9485, Abcam) at 4°C for 12 h and with secondary antibody (1:50,000, ab288151, Abcam) at 4°C for 2 h was conducted. The images were obtained under the ChemiDoc™ imaging system (Bio‐rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

Overexpression vector and small interfering (si)RNA establishment and transfection

The overexpression vectors targeting USP18 (oe‐USP18), the siRNA of FOXC2 (si‐FOXC2), and the corresponding negative controls (oe‐NC and si‐NC) were synthesized by Genscript (Nanjing, China). According to different experiment requirements, the transfection of the above vectors or siRNAs into cells was carried out using the Hieff Trans® Universal Transfection Reagent (Yeasen).

Cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8)

The cells were spread into the 96‐well plate and treated with different conditions. After adding the CCK‐8 solution, the cells were incubated in the 37°C incubator with 5% CO2, and the absorbance at 450 nm was then tested.

Adenosine 5′‐triphosphate (ATP) assay

The cells were inoculated into a six‐well plate and treated with different conditions. Based on the ATP Assay Kit (Beyotime), the treated cells were lysed, and the cell supernatant was collected after centrifugation. After the preparation of the standard curve, the ATP detection solution was added to the detection tubes and reacted for 4 min to consume the ATP in the background. Then the tubes were added with samples or standard substances, and the ATP RLU values were detected using the luminometer at least 2 s later.

Flow cytometry

The apoptosis was tested by applying the Annexin V‐FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime) under the flow cytometry manner. The treated cells were collected and re‐suspended in phosphate buffer saline (PBS) for cell counting. Following centrifugation, the cell pellet was mixed and reacted with Annexin V‐FITC binding solution and Annexin V‐FITC as well as propidium iodide, successively. The apoptosis was examined using the flow cytometer after incubation for 15 min away from light.

Enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The levels of tumor necrosis factor‐α (TNF‐α), interleukin‐6 (IL‐6), and IL‐1β were determined using the corresponding ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China). Briefly, the cell culture supernatant was collected and centrifuged at 4°C, 1000 g for 20 min to collect the supernatant samples. The Micro ELISA Plate was divided into the standard well, blank well, and sample well, in which standard substances, sample diluents, and supernatant samples were added, respectively. The Biotinylated Detection Ab Diluent was added to each well to react for 1 h after incubation at 37°C for 90 min. The concentrated HRP conjugate and substrate reagent were used for reaction at 37°C for 30 min and at 37°C for 15 min successively. After adding Stop Solution to terminate the reaction, the optical density of each well was detected at 450 nm.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) evaluation

The ROS levels were analyzed using the ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime). The cell culture fluid was removed from the cell culture plate and the diluted DCFH‐DA was then added and reacted at 37°C for 20 min. The fluorescence intensity was measured using the microscope (Wumo, Shanghai, China).

Superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) assessment

The activity of SOD and CAT was detected by applying the Total Superoxide Dismutase Assay Kit with NBT (Beyotime) and the Catalase Assay Kit (Beyotime), respectively. For SOD examination, the cells were collected and homogenized in precooled PBS. Followed by centrifugation at 4°C, the supernatant was used as the samples. The sample, black1, and black2 wells were set up in the 96‐well plate. The sample wells were added with 20 μL samples, 160 μL NBT/Enzyme working solution, and 20 μL Reaction start‐up working solution; the black1 wells were added with 20 μL SOD detection buffer, 160 μL NBT/Enzyme working solution, and 20 μL Reaction start‐up working solution; the black2 wells were added with 40 μL SOD detection buffer and 160 μL NBT/enzyme working solution. After incubation at 37°C for 30 min, the absorbance was examined at 560 nm. The SOD activity was calculated according to the prepared standard curve. For CAT determination, the cells were cracked using the RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) and attenuated with the catalase detection buffer. The samples were mixed with catalase detection buffer and hydrogen peroxide solution to react at 25°C for 5 min. The reaction was terminated using the catalase reaction termination solution and the addition of catalase detection buffer and chromogenic working solution was conducted within 15 min. Following the incubation at 25°C for 20 min, the absorbance at 520 nm was detected and the CAT activity was quantified using the prepared standard curve.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

The treated cells were fixed with 4% neutral formalin (Yeasen) and then permeabilized by Triton X‐100 (Beyotime). After being closed with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA; Oumarsi, Shanghai, China) for 30 min, the incubation with anti‐Col I (1:100, ab316222; Abcam) or anti‐Col III (1:100, ab184993; Abcam) at 4°C overnight and with secondary antibody (1:1000, ab150077; Abcam) at 37°C for 1 h was performed successively. Followed by nuclear staining with 4′,6‐diamidino‐2′‐phenylindole (DAPI; Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 37°C for 10 min away from light, the cells were observed under the microscope (Wumo).

Online database analysis

The ubibrowser website (http://ubibrowser.bio‐it.cn/ubibrowser_v3/home/index) was applied to predict the potential target of USP18.

Ubiquitination analysis

The cells were lysed using the radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Beyotime) with protease inhibitor (Beyotime) and treated with ultrasonic. The anti‐FOXC2 (1:500, ab314051; Abcam) was bound with the Protein A/G agarose beads to form the antibody–bead complex, which was added to the samples to allow the antibody to bind to the ubiquitinated substrate specifically. The antibody‐bead complex was washed using the washing buffer solution to remove proteins that were not specifically bound. The precipitated proteins were separated under the SDS‐PAGE. The anti‐ubiquitin antibody (1:5,000, 10201‐2‐AP; Proteintech, Wuhan, China) and anti‐FOXC2 (1:1,000, ab314051; Abcam) were used for Western blot analysis.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed applying the GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), and the differences between two or more groups were evaluated by utilizing the Student's t‐test and analysis of variance. The significance was represented by P < 0.05.

RESULTS

USP18 mitigates the effects of HG on NMVM viability and apoptosis

Compared with the Control group, USP18 was lowly expressed in the HG‐treated NMVMs (Figure 1a,b). After transfection of oe‐USP18, USP18 expression was upregulated in NMVMs compared with the oe‐NC group (Figure 1c,d). Under the HG context, the viability of NMVMs was decreased, which was reversed by USP18 overexpression (Figure 1e). Besides, HG‐inhibited ATP levels in NMVMs were reversed after transfection of oe‐USP18 (Figure 1f). Meanwhile, the increase in apoptosis caused by HG was impaired by oe‐USP18 (Figure 1g). Therefore, USP18 weakens HG‐induced viability inhibition and apoptosis promotion in NMVMs.

HG‐induced viability decrease and apoptosis increase of NMVMs are reversed by USP18. (a) USP18 mRNA expression in NMVMs of Control and HG groups was detected by RT‐qPCR. (b) USP18 protein expression in NMVMs of Control and HG groups was measured by Western blot. (c) USP18 mRNA expression in NMVMs transfected with oe‐NC and oe‐USP18 was detected by RT‐qPCR. (d) USP18 protein expression in NMVMs transfected with oe‐NC and oe‐USP18 was measured by Western blot. (e) CCK‐8 was used to detect the viability of NMVMs in the Control, HG, HG + oe‐NC, and HG + oe‐USP18 groups. (f) The ATP levels of NMVMs in the Control, HG, HG + oe‐NC, and HG + oe‐USP18 groups were detected by the specific kit. (g) Flow cytometry was applied to examine apoptosis of NMVMs in the Control, HG, HG + oe‐NC, and HG + oe‐USP18 groups. **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001.

USP18 alleviates HG‐induced inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis in NMVMs

After exposure to HG, the levels of pro‐inflammatory factors (TNF‐α, IL‐6, and IL‐1β) were increased in the supernatant of NMVMs, and the levels were undermined after overexpression of USP18 (Figure 2a–c). Furthermore, USP18 suppressed HG‐resulted ROS level elevation (Figure 2d,e) and SOD and CAT level reduction (Figure 2f,g). Consequently, HG‐induced inflammation and oxidative stress are attenuated by USP18 overexpression. The effects of USP18 on HG‐induced fibrosis of NMVMs were further explored. HG exposure‐induced Col I and Col III expression enhancement was impeded in NMVMs with USP18 overexpression, which was reflected by IF and Western blot results (Figure 3a,b). Collectively, USP18 ameliorates HG‐triggered NMVM fibrosis.

USP18 represses FOXC2 ubiquitination to enhance its expression

In this study, FOXC2 was predicted as the target of USP18 using the ubibrowser website (Figure S1), and Western blot results showed that the expression of FOXC2 was blocked by HG exposure, but it was overturned with USP18 upregulation (Figure 4a). Moreover, as shown in Figure 4b, the FOXC2 ubiquitination level was reduced after USP18 overexpression. Additionally, CHX was used to detect the degradation of FOXC2 protein in NMVMs. The results illustrated that the expression of FOXC2 in NMVMs was negatively correlated with the treatment time of CHX; besides, compared with the HG + oe‐NC group, the FOXC2 expression in the HG + oe‐USP18 group was higher (Figure 4c). Accordingly, USP18 expedites FOXC2 expression via ubiquitination.

The ubiquitination of FOXC2 is retarded by USP18. (a) FOXC2 protein expression in NMVMs of the Control, HG, HG + oe‐NC, and HG + oe‐USP18 groups was detected by Western blot. (b) The ubiquitination of FOXC2 in NMVMs of the oe‐NC and oe‐USP18 groups was measured using ubiquitination analysis. (c) Western blot was applied to determine FOXC2 protein expression in NMVMs co‐treated with HG + oe‐NC or HG + oe‐USP18 and CHX for 0, 2, 4, and 6 h. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001.

FOXC2 diminishes USP18‐mediated effects on NMVMs exposed to HG via the JAK/STAT pathway

To investigate the mechanism of USP18 and FOXC2 in HG‐induced NMVMs, the recovery experiments were conducted. After transfection of si‐FOXC2, FOXC2 expression was decreased in HG‐induced NMVMs (Figure 5a). USP18‐triggered viability and ATP increase in HG‐exposed NMVMs were inhibited by FOXC2 knockdown (Figure 5b,c). Beyond that, the reduction of apoptosis (Figure 5d) and pro‐inflammatory factor levels (Figure 5e–g) mediated by USP18 in HG‐induced NMVMs was relieved after silencing FOXC2. Meanwhile, FOXC2 silence abolished the decline of ROS levels and the ascent of SOD and CAT levels resulting from USP18 upregulation in HG‐exposed NMVMs (Figure 5h–j). The depressed fibrosis of NMVMs caused by USP18 overexpression, displayed by the reduced Col I and Col III expression, was also improved with FOXC2 deficiency (Figure 5k). Taken together, FOXC2 knockdown reverses the effects of USP18 on HG‐induced NMVMs. The JAK/STAT signaling pathway plays an important role in inflammatory diseases, myocardial fibrosis, and other diseases 18 , so the effects of USP18/FOXC2 on the JAK/STAT signaling pathway were measured. As described in Figure 6, the HG‐induced increase of pJAK2/JAK2 and pSTAT3/STAT3 ratios in NMVMs was weakened by USP18 overexpression, which was further reversed after FOXC2 knockdown. The above results demonstrate that the USP18/FOXC2 axis may regulate the phenotypes of HG‐induced NMVMs through the JAK/STAT signaling pathway.

DISCUSSION

Cardiomyocyte apoptosis is the phenotype during the development of DCM, especially in the initial stage, and chronic inflammation is an important inducer 19 . Additionally, a previous study has shown that the HG environment could induce the release of ROS and stimulate the activity of NF‐κB, which mediated the transcriptional activity of inflammatory factors 20 . Consistent with this, this study believed that HG exposure could inhibit the viability of NMVMs, promote apoptosis and the release of inflammatory factors, as well as the occurrence of oxidative stress. Under the stimulation of HG, oxidative stress and inflammation stimulate the production of myocardial Col, inducing the occurrence of fibrosis. Fibrosis further induces the release of more inflammatory factors, thereby aggravating the fibrosis process 20 . Similarly, this study found that HG induced the production of Col, indicating that HG stimulated myocardial fibrosis.

As a deubiquitinating enzyme, USP18 was found to remit LPS‐induced oxidative stress and inflammation in human pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells 21 . Additionally, USP18 might protect PC12 cells from oxygen–glucose deprivation and reoxygenation‐induced damage by regulating the inflammatory response and cell apoptosis 22 . The basal inflammation, senescence, and insulin resistance in coronary endothelial cells were reduced by USP18 23 . Beyond that, a comprehensive analysis revealed that USP18 could serve as a key target related to stress‐induced cardiac damage in mouse models 24 . The upregulation of USP18 enhanced the protective effect of Xinbao Pill on cardiac function and alleviated cardiac fibrosis 25 . Interestingly, in the present research, overexpression of USP18 slowed down HG‐induced apoptosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis of NMVMs, indicating that USP18 alleviated myocardial injury caused by HG exposure and supporting the previous finding regarding the protective role of USP18 in pathological cardiac remodeling 26 .

To further explore the mechanism of USP18 in DCM, the potential target FOXC2 of USP18 was predicted. FOXC2 plays a significant role in heart diseases. For example, the conditional inactivation of FOXC2 in neural crest cells contributed to cardiac abnormalities 27 . What more, it was found that the FOXC2 variants with impaired function existed in the patients with conotruncal heart defects, a subtype of congenital heart disease induced by abnormal cardiac outflow tract development 28 . FOXC2 was also related to the hepatic glucose and lipid metabolism disorder in type 2 diabetes mellitus 29 . More importantly, FOXC2 alleviated myocardial ischemia–reperfusion injury through the nuclear factor erythroid 2‐related factor/heme oxygenase 1 (Nrf2/HO‐1) pathway 14 . Therefore, it was hypothesized that USP18 could stabilize the expression of FOXC2 by promoting the deubiquitination of FOXC2, thereby exerting a protective effect on cardiomyocytes induced by HG. The ubiquitination regulation between the two was verified through ubiquitination analysis and CHX experiments. Besides, further research proposed that the downregulation of FOXC2 could weaken the alleviating effects of USP18 on myocardial injury and fibrosis under the background of HG.

The JAK/STAT signaling pathway is a promising target for cardiovascular diseases 30 and plays a crucial part in inflammatory diseases, myocardial fibrosis, and other diseases 31 , 32 . Moreover, it was known that inhibiting the JAK/STAT signaling pathway could alleviate myocardial injury in diabetes 33 , 34 , 35 . This study further examined the effect of USP18/FOXC2 on the JAK/STAT signaling pathway. This study found that USP18 inhibited the HG‐activated JAK/STAT signaling pathway, and this effect was recovered after FOXC2 knockdown. Hence, USP18 regulates the ubiquitination of FOXC2 by blocking the activation of the JAK/STAT pathway, thereby alleviating the occurrence of myocardial injury under HG stimulation.

Hereon, this research explores the protective effect of USP18 on myocardial injury by exposing NMVMs to the HG environment to induce the DCM models in vitro. These findings suggest that USP18 stabilizes FOXC2 through deubiquitination, thereby exerting a myocardial protective effect via the JAK/STAT pathway. It provides a reference for further in vivo research on the progression of DCM and a new target for the treatment of DCM. However, whether this mechanism can be truly applied in vivo and clinical treatment remains to be explored.

Interventional treatment of iliac intraosseous arteriovenous malformation: a case description
Authors: Zihao Wu, Zhenyin Liu
Published: 2026

Introduction

Arteriovenous malformation (AVM) is defined as a high-flow vascular lesion caused by abnormal vascular development in early embryogenesis. AVMs exhibit aggressive characteristics and are often associated with severe complications (1). The prevalence of AVMs is low, with an annual incidence rate of 1 case per 100,000 people (2). AVMs are characterized by a direct arteriovenous connection without an intervening capillary bed, leading to the formation of aberrant vascular masses. Intraosseous AVMs are exceedingly rare, with existing literature primarily limited to case reports involving the jaws. When involving bone, AVMs may lead to pathological fractures in addition to typical clinical manifestations. AVMs represent one of the most clinically significant vascular malformations, and timely diagnosis and intervention are critical to preventing life-threatening complications. Herein, we report a rare case of iliac intraosseous AVM, detailing its diagnostic process, therapeutic intervention, and clinical outcomes.

Case presentation

A 16-year-old female patient presented in February 2023 with persistent swelling and pain in her right lower extremity and buttocks (3 years of illness). Physical examination revealed the following: the skin of the right buttock and thigh was swollen, the right buttock was obviously tender, and she had a limp. The patient had been diagnosed with right iliac, hip, and femoral AVMs in other medical institutions and had undergone interventional arterial embolization treatments twice, the specific course of which is unknown. However, there was no significant improvement in the hip pain after surgery. The patient was usually in good health and denied a family history of genetic or congenital diseases. Contrast-enhanced pelvic computed tomography (CT) performed at Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, Guangzhou Medical University revealed a right iliac intraosseous AVM. The AVM lesion within the right iliac bone demonstrated marked enhancement, accompanied by significant thinning of the surrounding cortical bone and an expansion-like change (Figure 1). On 4 April 2023, the patient underwent endovascular embolization via retrograde catheterization through the left femoral vein using a Furusawa sheath. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) confirmed accurate catheter placement within the lesion. Multiple controllable coils were deployed, followed by embolization using polyethylene glycol-based sclerosing foam. The procedure was uneventful, with no intraoperative complications observed. Postoperative right internal iliac artery angiography demonstrated a marked reduction in the right iliac intraosseous AVM lesion and surrounding tortuous vessels (Figure 2). At a follow-up on 19 January 2024, imaging revealed no evidence of recurrence. The patient reported substantial symptom improvement, including complete resolution of pain and gait abnormality. Follow-up imaging (Figure 3) confirmed stable coil positioning within the lesion without displacement or extrusion.

Discussion

AVMs are congenital vascular anomalies arising from aberrant vascular development, which disrupts normal blood flow regulation and can lead to significant clinical sequelae. These lesions are characterized by the absence of a capillary bed, resulting in direct arteriovenous connections (3,4). AVMs are relatively uncommon, with a reported prevalence of 1.5–4.5% and an annual incidence of approximately 1 per 100,000 (2). Clinical manifestations of skeletal AVMs are diverse, including ischemia, pain, swelling, pathologic fractures, and functional impairment (5). Previous studies have demonstrated that 82.6% of early-diagnosed AVMs worsen over time, highlighting the importance of early diagnosis and intervention (6-8). Currently, DSA remains the gold standard for AVM diagnosis, particularly for detecting nidus architecture (9). Existing literature on intraosseous AVMs has predominantly focused on the maxillofacial skeleton due to its surgical relevance and the risk of life-threatening hemorrhage (10). In contrast, intraosseous AVMs in the iliac bone are exceedingly rare and poorly recognized. Surgical resection and endovascular embolization are the primary therapeutic modalities (5,11). Endovascular embolization and sclerotherapy are established primary treatment modalities for high-flow AVMs, effectively reducing nidus flow and symptoms, and are widely used either as stand-alone therapy or as part of multimodal management to improve clinical outcomes and facilitate adjunctive surgery (12). Polidocanol foam is a widely used sclerosant agent in the treatment of peripheral vascular anomalies, including venous and slow-flow malformations, where it induces endothelial damage and vessel occlusion; its application has been documented as effective and safe in sclerotherapy for extracranial vascular malformations (13). Combining coil embolization and liquid embolic agent is a highly effective minimally invasive strategy for extremity intraosseous AVMs, achieving 70–85% obliteration rates with excellent functional preservation and low morbidity in clinical studies (14,15). To the best of our knowledge, this is a rare reported case of an intraosseous AVM within the iliac bone. The lesion occupied the medullary cavity, induced cortical thinning, and posed a potential risk of life-threatening hemorrhage (16). Compared with soft tissue AVMs, skeletal lesions are more localized, allowing for better treatment outcomes.

Conclusions

This report describes a rare case of intraosseous AVM in the iliac bone, successfully treated with coil-assisted sclerotherapy and embolization. The treatment strategy was based on a thorough clinical, radiographic, and angiographic evaluation. Post-treatment assessments revealed marked symptomatic relief and resolution of functional impairment, with no procedural complications or evidence of recurrence on follow-up imaging. However, since the damage to the endothelium caused by glue and coil embolization is not as invasive as that of alcohol sclerosing agents, we still need to conduct long-term follow-up on the therapeutic effect on the patients. This case underscores the clinical significance of prompt diagnosis and intervention for rare skeletal AVMs and provides a reference for its safe and effective management.

Comparison of the Risk of Diabetic Retinopathy Between Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors and Dipeptidyl Peptidase-4 Inhibitors in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in Japan: A Retrospective Analysis of Real-World Data
Authors: Masaya Koshizaka, Tomoaki Tatsumi, Fumiko Kiyonaga, Yoshinori Kosakai, Yoko Yoshinaga, Mami Shintani-Tachi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDiabetic retinopathy (DR), a microvascular complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), is a leading cause of blindness and has detrimental effects on patients’ quality of life. We compared the risk of DR diagnosis with sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) versus dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors (DPP-4i) in patients with T2DM in Japan.MethodsThis Japanese retrospective cohort study used the JMDC Claims Database (data collected from January 2015 to September 2022). Patients with T2DM and no record of microvascular or macrovascular diseases who were newly treated with an SGLT2i (23,061 patients) or a DPP-4i (53,986 patients) were matched 1:1 using propensity score (10,166 per matched group). Incidence rates (IRs) and cumulative IRs of DR diagnosis were calculated for each treatment group; hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated using Cox proportional hazard models to compare the risk between the groups.ResultsThe IR of DR diagnosis was 46.23 and 57.12 per 1000 person-years in the SGLT2i and DPP-4i groups, respectively, with a significantly lower risk in the SGLT2i group than in the DPP-4i group (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.75–0.92, P = 0.0003).ConclusionsIn this study, the risk of DR diagnosis was lower with SGLT2i compared with DPP-4i in patients with T2DM without microvascular and macrovascular diseases in Japan. Findings suggest that early SGLT2i treatment may be beneficial in preventing DR development in early-stage T2DM.Graphical abstract available for this article.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13300-024-01649-9.

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a microvascular complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and is a leading cause of visual impairment and blindness [1]. Globally, approximately 30‒40% of patients with T2DM develop DR [2]. In Japan, the incidence and progression rates of DR were 38.3 per 1000 person-years (PY) and 21.1 per 1000 PY, respectively, during the 8-year follow-up (1996–2003) of a large Japanese randomized trial [3]. With the growing aging population, increasing prevalence of diabetes, and lifestyle changes, the prevalence and disease burden of DR are expected to rise [2, 4]. In patients with T2DM with early stages of nonproliferative DR, chronic hyperglycemia damages the microvasculature of the retina and causes ischemia, vascular leakage, and central vision loss due to diabetic macular edema (DME) [5]. In later stages, secondary neovascularization throughout the retina and subsequent hemorrhage and/or retinal detachment cause vision loss [5]. DR has detrimental effects on the patients’ quality of life [6, 7] and is associated with reduced functioning and impaired mental health [8].

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have been shown to improve the risk factors for DR, including reduction in hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), systolic blood pressure (SBP), and body mass index (BMI) [9, 10]. Additionally, in a double-blind randomized study, 6 weeks of dapagliflozin treatment not only improved diabetes control and blood pressure but also reduced retinal capillary hyperperfusion and arteriole remodeling, which are contributing factors for DR progression [10]. Recent studies have also reported the benefits of SGLT2i in preventing DR. A large cohort study of patients with T2DM in Taiwan reported that the risk of sight-threatening DR was significantly lower in patients receiving SGLT2i than in those receiving a dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitor (DPP-4i). In another Taiwanese retrospective cohort study, the risk of proliferative DR and composite surgical outcomes were significantly lower in the SGLT2i group than in the glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist group, although the rate of any DR was comparable between the groups [11]. Moreover, a Korean real-world study reported that the incidence of DR was reduced by 11% with SGLT2i versus DPP-4i [12]. However, data among patients with T2DM in Japan are still limited. Furthermore, there is limited evidence reporting the reduced DR risk with SGLT2i in patients with low-risk, early-stage T2DM; to our knowledge, no previous studies have focused on the preventive effects of SGLT2i on DR in the early stages of T2DM.

Using a large administrative database in Japan, we aimed to compare the risk of DR and DME development in patients with T2DM newly treated with an SGLT2i (either alone or in combination with antidiabetic drugs other than a DPP-4i) versus those newly treated with a DPP-4i (either alone or in combination with antidiabetic drugs other than an SGLT2i). The risk of DR development with SGLT2i and with DPP-4i was also compared among subgroups of patients with different baseline characteristics.

Methods

Study Design

This was a retrospective cohort study using data from the JMDC Claims Database (JMDC Inc., Tokyo, Japan; Fig. S1) between January 1, 2015, and September 30, 2022. The JMDC Claims Database is a large administrative database containing inpatient, outpatient, and pharmacy claims and health checkup data provided by health insurance associations in Japan. As of February 2024, cumulative data of approximately 17 million individuals are included. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Affairs Japan-Protocol Review Committee of Astellas Pharma Inc. (protocol no. 1941-MA-3477). The study was conducted in accordance with the protocol, the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and all applicable regulations and guidelines governing clinical study conduct. Patient informed consent was not required because data used in this study were retrospectively collected and deidentified.

Study Population

Patients included in this study had at least one prescription for either an SGLT2i or a DPP-4i on or after January 1, 2015 (see Table S1 for medication codes) and had a confirmed diagnosis of T2DM (International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision [ICD-10]: E11.x) or unspecified diabetes mellitus (ICD-10: E14.x) on or before the index date, where the index date was defined as the date of the first receipt of an SGLT2i or a DPP-4i, preceded by a minimum of 1 year of database record, on or after January 1, 2015. Patients were excluded from this study if they met any of the following criteria: aged < 18 years at the index date; had used an SGLT2i or a DPP-4i, or a fixed-dose combination drug containing both an SGLT2i and a DPP-4i, during the pre-index period, which was defined as the period between the index date and 1 year before the index date; had used both an SGLT2i and a DPP-4i, including a fixed-dose combination drug containing both, on the index date; had a diagnosis of type 1 diabetes mellitus (ICD-10: E10.x) on or before the index date, or gestational diabetes (ICD-10: O24) during the pre-index period or on the index date; < 1 year of database record before the index date; or had a diagnosis of microvascular or macrovascular disease (see Table S2 for microvascular and macrovascular disease codes), DR, or DME (see Table S3 for DR, DME, and ophthalmoscopy codes) during the pre-index period or on the index date.

Outcome Measures

The primary endpoint was the first record of DR diagnosis (including DME) preceded by a record of ophthalmic examination ≤ 1 month prior to the month of the DR diagnosis (see Table S3 for DR, DME, and ophthalmoscopy codes). The secondary endpoint was the first record of ophthalmologic procedures related to DR (including DME) following a DR diagnosis preceded by an ophthalmic examination record as defined for the primary endpoint above (see Table S4 for ophthalmologic procedure codes). Focusing solely on DME, the exploratory endpoint was the first record of DME diagnosis preceded by ophthalmic examination ≤ 1 month prior to the month of the DME diagnosis (see Table S5 for DME and relevant ophthalmic examination codes). For DR and DME to be considered as events, both ophthalmoscopy and diagnosis had to occur on or after the index date.

Statistical Analysis

All patients meeting the eligibility criteria were included in the analysis and were matched 1:1 (SGLT2i:DPP-4i) using propensity score matching, which was calculated using a multivariate logistic regression model. Baseline variables used as covariates for propensity score matching were sex, age, index year, hospital size (i.e., number of beds), smoking status, BMI, dyslipidemia, hypoglycemia, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), adapted Diabetes Complication Severity Index (aDCSI), coronary revascularization, SBP, HbA1c, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), medications (antidiabetic drugs, antihypertensive drugs, antiplatelet drugs, renin-angiotensin system [RAS] inhibitors, antihyperlipidemic drugs), treatment status of antidiabetic drug (treatment-naïve and treatment non-naïve), and cataract surgery. Code lists for CCI and aDCSI are shown in Tables S6 and S7. Missing data were not imputed; if data for any of the variables used for matching were not recorded during the baseline period, defined as the pre-index period plus the index date, patients were not included for matching or the subsequent analyses. Variables that had ≥ 25% missing baseline data were excluded completely from the propensity score calculation. The degree of balance was assessed using standardized mean difference (SMD), where an SMD > 0.1 indicated a residual imbalance between the treatment groups.

Baseline characteristics, laboratory measurements, chronic conditions, and relevant medical history were summarized descriptively for the crude population and the matched cohort. These baseline data were extracted during the baseline period, except for BMI, SBP, HbA1c, and laboratory measurements, including LDL-C and HDL-C, which were measured within 180 days before the index date. Patients were followed from the index date to the earliest of the following events: clinical event of interest (DR diagnosis, ophthalmologic procedures related to DR, and DME diagnosis), treatment discontinuation, end of patient data, and death. Treatment discontinuation was defined using a grace period of 60 days; patients were considered to have discontinued the treatment if the difference between the prescription date plus the days of medication administration and the current prescription date was ≥ 61 days or if they switched or added on the other index drug (SGLT2i or DPP-4i). The incidence of DR was compared between the treatment groups as primary analysis by evaluating the event-free rate. In the primary analysis, DR was defined as having any of the Japan-specific standard disease or procedure codes for DR or ophthalmoscopy, listed in Table S3, excluding retinopathy (standard disease code: 3,621,043), maculopathy (standard disease code: 3,625,036), chronic retinopathy (standard disease code: 8,840,403), circinate retinitis (standard disease code: 8,841,100), and neovascular maculopathy (standard disease code: 8,849,638). The incidence rates (IRs) per 1000 PY were calculated for each group, and cumulative IRs were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method. Hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated using Cox proportional hazard models to compare DR risk between the groups; P-values were calculated using log-rank tests, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. The same analyses were performed to compare the incidence of ophthalmologic procedures related to DR and incidence of DME. Sensitivity analyses were performed for the primary endpoint by calculating the IRs of DR diagnosis and adjusted HRs using a shorter grace period of 30 days and a broader disease definition (i.e., having any of the DR or ophthalmoscopy codes listed in Table S3). Multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed using the crude population to estimate adjusted HRs. Furthermore, subgroup analyses were performed for the primary endpoint. Subgroup analyses were based on BMI (< 25 kg/m2, ≥ 25 kg/m2), SBP (< 130 mmHg, ≥ 130 mmHg), HbA1c (< 7%, 7–8%, > 8%), age (< 65 years, ≥ 65 years), concomitant use of lipid-lowering drugs (yes, no), concomitant use of RAS inhibitors (yes, no), and treatment status of antidiabetic drugs (treatment-naïve, treatment non-naïve). All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Study Population

A total of 345,337 patients with T2DM who were newly treated with either an SGLT2i or a DPP-4i were identified from the database (Fig. 1). Of those, 77,047 patients (SGLT2i: n = 23,061, DPP-4i: n = 53,986) met the eligibility criteria and were included in the crude population. Ophthalmologic Procedures Related to DR

Among the matched cohorts, the IR of ophthalmologic procedures related to DR after a DR diagnosis was 1.69 per 1000 PY in the SGLT2i group and 2.34 per 1000 PY in the DPP-4i group (Table S9). The risk of ophthalmologic procedures related to DR after a DR diagnosis was not significantly different between the treatment groups (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.44–1.20, P = 0.2140; Fig. S11a).

DME Diagnosis Preceded by Ophthalmic Examination

Among the matched cohorts, the IR of DME diagnosis was 2.00 per 1000 PY in the SGLT2i group and 2.74 per 1000 PY in the DPP-4i group (Table S9). The risk of DME diagnosis was not significantly different between the treatment groups (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.47–1.18, P = 0.2070; Fig. S11b).

Discussion

This is the first large retrospective cohort study using an administrative database to evaluate the risk of developing DR in patients with T2DM without microvascular and macrovascular diseases who are newly treated with an SGLT2i or a DPP-4i in Japan. This study showed that the risk of DR diagnosis was significantly lower in patients newly treated with an SGLT2i than in those newly treated with a DPP-4i, with a significant risk reduction observed in patients with baseline characteristics indicative of early-stage T2DM or fewer predisposing DR risk factors. Findings suggest a class effect of SGLT2i in preventing the development of DR in patients with T2DM who have no microvascular and macrovascular diseases in Japan.

The significantly lower risk of DR diagnosis with SGLT2i than with DPP-4i observed in this Japanese study was consistent with previous non-Japanese studies [12, 13]. In a retrospective cohort study conducted in Korea [12], SGLT2i demonstrated a lower risk of DR occurrence compared with DPP-4i (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.89–0.97, P = 0.006) in patients with T2DM who had no history of DR. A retrospective cohort study in Taiwan reported that the risk of both early- and late-stage DR was also lower in SGLT2i users than in DPP-4i users (adjusted HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.6–0.78) [13]. However, while the current study excluded patients with microvascular and macrovascular diseases, focusing on patients with early-stage T2DM only, these previous studies included patients with and without microvascular and macrovascular diseases, indicating that the results are for a mixture of patients with early-stage and more advanced T2DM. Together with existing evidence of potential cardiorenal-protective effects of SGLT2i in the early stages of T2DM [14], the current findings further suggest the benefit of early treatment with SGLT2i, indicating that SGLT2i may prevent not only cardiorenal complications but also retinal complications, including DR.

The retinoprotective mechanisms of SGLT2i have been thought to be multifactorial and complex. Inhibition of SGLT2 expressed on retinal pericytes may directly prevent the crucial steps of early-stage DR pathogenesis, including retinal pericyte swelling and loss and hyperperfusion in the retina [15–18]. A previous Japanese study reported that SGLT2i led to simultaneous reductions in HbA1c of ≥ 0.5%, BMI of ≥ 3%, and SBP of ≥ 2 mmHg compared with DPP-4i [9], and the authors concluded that SGLT2i may enable comprehensive and simultaneous management of cardiometabolic risks in Japanese patients with T2DM. These pleiotropic effects of SGLT2i on cardiometabolic outcomes are other potential mechanisms for the prevention of cell-toxic consequences in retinal cells that lead to chronic inflammation, retinal hemodynamic changes, oxidative stress, gene polymorphism, and neurodegenerative changes [16, 19, 20], enabling the prevention of DR-related complications. SGLT2i have also been reported to reduce retinal capillary blood flow, prevent the structural changes in retinal arterioles [16], and prevent the prolongation of oscillatory potential peaks [16, 21]. Moreover, SGLT2i-induced low-grade hyperketonemia is thought to reduce oxidative stress and inflammation, improving retinal hypoxia and neuronal damage [22]. Therefore, the findings observed in this study may be explained by these various mechanisms of SGLT2i acting on the complex pathophysiology of DR.

In this study, the results from the subgroup analysis generally showed that the risk of DR diagnosis was lower with SGLT2i than with DPP-4i in patients who had baseline characteristics indicative of early-stage T2DM, including younger age, lower HbA1c and SBP, no concomitant use of lipid-lowering drugs and RAS inhibitors, and no prior antidiabetic drugs, although the background factors were not matched between subgroups, and the number of patients was small in some subgroups. Given that hyperglycemia, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia are risk factors of DR development and progression [23], these results suggest that SGLT2i could reduce the risk of DR in patients with relatively mild or well-controlled T2DM or those with fewer predisposing risk factors for DR. Again, the findings suggest that the effects of SGLT2i in reducing DR may be enhanced by early initiation of SGLT2i treatment in patients with early stages of T2DM.

DME is a critical complication of DR that can develop at any time during the progression of DR [24–26]. In addition to DME, DR can progress into other complications, including vitreous hemorrhage, retinal detachment, and severe fibrovascular proliferation [27], and is one of the leading causes of neovascular glaucoma [28]. When focusing only on DME occurrence, this study showed that the risk of DME was not significantly different between patients newly treated with an SGLT2i and those treated with a DPP-4i. Furthermore, DR-related ophthalmologic procedures are generally required in patients with more severe disease, and their presence can be an indicator of disease progression. In this study, the risk of ophthalmologic procedures related to DR, which included retinal photocoagulation, vitrectomy, and glaucoma surgery, preceded by DR diagnosis was also not significantly different between SGLT2i and DPP-4i groups. However, it should be noted that the IRs of DME and ophthalmologic procedures related to DR were very low. This is possibly because this study only included patients who had no pre-existing microvascular and macrovascular diseases, indicating that these were patients with early-stage T2DM who were at lower risk of developing DR-related complications during the observation period of this study. In addition, although the JMDC database includes records of when individual ophthalmic examinations were performed, it does not include the results of those examinations. Further studies with longer observation periods and more detailed individual data, including ophthalmic examination results, are required to confirm the effects of SGLT2i versus DPP-4i on the risk of DME and disease progression requiring DR-related ophthalmologic procedures.

This was the first large real-world study to evaluate the risk of DR diagnosis in patients with T2DM in Japan and included approximately 10,000 matched patients in each treatment group. Patients had no record of microvascular and macrovascular diseases; although the absence of vascular diseases was based on disease codes in the database and may not be accurate, patients may generally more closely reflect a large proportion of patients with T2DM treated in real clinical settings in Japan [29, 30]. Additionally, patients were followed even if they changed clinics or hospitals. Limitations of this study included potential bias due to the presence of unobservable confounding factors that may guide therapy selection, and outcomes were not accounted for in the analyses. The study compared the class effects of SGLT2i and DPP-4i and not the individual drugs. The JMDC Claims Database contains claims and health checkup data of all employed individuals and their dependents provided by health insurance associations. Therefore, the proportion of elderly patients (aged ≥ 65 years) in this study was likely low compared with the general Japanese population. There were differences in baseline characteristics between the treatment groups before matching. As a result of propensity score matching, the characteristics of patients treated with a DPP-4i more closely resembled those of patients treated with an SGLT2i; therefore, the results may not apply to all patients who were prescribed these drugs. Laboratory test results are limited to tests performed as part of regular annual health checkups only. Patients with no record of laboratory tests or diagnostic data for any variables selected for propensity score matching would have been excluded; however, as this would apply to both treatment groups, it would be unlikely to affect the results. Information on all drug use was obtained using prescription claims from the database, and the actual usage of the prescribed drugs is unknown. Furthermore, information on concomitant drugs used for subgroup analyses was obtained during the baseline period; therefore, the use of these drugs during the follow-up period that may affect treatment effectiveness is unknown.

In conclusion, in this study, SGLT2i was associated with a lower risk of DR diagnosis compared with DPP-4i in patients with T2DM without microvascular and macrovascular diseases. Findings from this study suggest the benefits of early SGLT2i treatment in preventing the development of DR in patients with early-stage T2DM.

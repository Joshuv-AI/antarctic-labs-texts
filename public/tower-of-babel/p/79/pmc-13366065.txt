IFPHOR0026 Bridging the Nutrition Gap: Adolescents’ Diet and Health Challenges in Shireen Jinnah Colony, Karachi
Authors: Misbah Ajaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUrban low-income neighbourhoods’ face hidden nutritional crises among adolescents vulnerable to socio-economic pressures. Shireen Jinnah Colony exemplifies challenges of food insecurity, limited dietary diversity, and low health awareness.MethodsA cross-sectional mixed-methods study was conducted in Shireen Jinnah Colony, Karachi, among 150 adolescents aged 12–18 years selected through systematic household sampling. Quantitative data included anthropometric assessment (height, weight; stunting and underweight based on WHO standards, and BMI-for-age) and a standardised household food insecurity questionnaire. Health-seeking behaviours were assessed through structured interviews. For the qualitative component, a purposive sample of 20 caregivers was recruited to capture variation in socioeconomic background; semi-structured interviews explored nutrition knowledge, dietary practices, and barriers to accessing health services. Quantitative and qualitative data were analysed separately and integrated at the interpretation stage using triangulation to contextualize statistical findings with participant perspectives.ResultsNearly one-third of adolescents were underweight, and 22% were stunted. Household food insecurity affected 45% of households, with limited access to fruits, vegetables, and protein-rich foods. Only 60% reported regular health check-ups, and 18% had incomplete vaccination status. Caregivers identified financial constraints, limited awareness, and poor availability of healthy foods as key barriers, reinforcing the quantitative patterns.DiscussionThese findings highlight important nutrition and health challenges, but they are context-specific and limited by the cross-sectional design and single-site sampling, which restrict causal inference and generalisability. The qualitative component strengthens interpretation, but the combined evidence remains exploratory.ConclusionThis study offers localised evidence supporting context-sensitive responses to adolescent nutrition challenges in underserved urban settings.

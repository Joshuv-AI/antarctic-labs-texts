The complete chloroplast genome of the Korean endemic plant Sorbaria sorbifolia var. glandulifolia J.-H. Song & S.-P. Hong (Rosaceae) and phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Young-Kee Kim, Yeonchan Kang, Heewon Ji, Jun-Ho Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSorbaria sorbifolia var. glandulifolia, endemic to Korea, is distinguished by glandular trichomes on the abaxial leaflet surface. In this study, we present its first complete chloroplast genome (159,476 bp) with a typical quadripartite structure comprising 112 unique genes, including 78 protein-coding genes, 30 transfer RNAs, and four ribosomal RNAs. Phylogenetic analysis of 18 complete chloroplast genomes revealed that S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia forms a monophyletic group with other Sorbaria species and clusters with Adenostoma. Furthermore, two well-supported clades within Sorbaria corresponded to the sections Sorbaria and Kirilowiana. This study provides the first chloroplast genome report for S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia, offering valuable genomic insights for future phylogenetic and taxonomic research on the genus Sorbaria.Keywords: Sorbaria sorbifolia var. glandulifolia, endemic, section, chloroplast genome, phylogeny

Introduction

Sorbaria (Seringe) A. Braun 1860 (Rosaceae Jussieu) is a small genus comprising nine taxa, including infraspecific taxonomic ranks, as reported in a recent study (Song and Hong 2021). The genus is classified within the tribe Sorbarieae Rydberg and the subfamily Amygdaloideae Arnott (Potter et al. 2007). Members of Sorbaria are morphologically characterized by odd-pinnate, stipulate leaves, and flowers possessing five distinct carpels (Brouillet 2015). S. sorbifolia (Linnaeus) A. Braun var. glandulifolia J.-H. Song & S.-P. Hong 2019 is an endemic infraspecific taxon (Song and Hong 2019), distinguished by the high density of glandular trichomes on the abaxial surface of the leaflets (Figure 1). This variety is restricted to the Republic of Korea.

Leaves are oblong to oval in outline, pinnately compound with 9–25 narrowly lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate leaflets. The panicle is erect, with narrowly spreading branches. Flowers are bisexual and white. (B) Infructescence in a natural habitat. Each flower bears five follicles, which are aggregated and partially connate. The follicles are cylindrical, somewhat leathery, and dehisce along the distal adaxial suture. (D) The adaxial surface is moderately pubescent with glandular hairs (black arrow) on the veins. Photographs of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia were taken by Jun-Ho Song at the type locality, Mt. Jeombong-san, Inje-gun, Gangwon-do, South Korea (38°01′25.9″N, 128°28′18.9″E).

The chloroplast is a vital organelle in angiosperms due to its role in photosynthesis. Chloroplast genomes exhibit a highly conserved circular quadripartite structure, comprising a large single-copy (LSC) region, a small single-copy (SSC) region, and a pair of inverted repeats (IRs) (Palmer 1991). Specific regions of the chloroplast genome have been widely employed as DNA barcodes for species identification and phylogenetic inference (CBOL Plant Working Group 2009). With advancements in sequencing technologies, an increasing number of chloroplast genomes have been published in recent years. A total of 756 chloroplast genomes from the family Rosaceae were available in GenBank, NCBI (accessed in 9 July). However, the chloroplast genome of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia had not yet been investigated. In this study, we assembled the complete chloroplast genome of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia and investigated molecular phylogenetic relationships within Sorbarieae species. The chloroplast genome data generated in this study provide a valuable resource for advancing species identification, conservation strategies, and evolutionary research.

Materials and methods

In this study, fresh leaf material of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia was collected from Inje-gun, Gangwon-do, Republic of Korea (38.0238665°N, 128.4719271°E). A voucher specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of Chungbuk National University (Herbarium Code: CBU) under the accession numbers CBU-C-510 and CBU-V-448 (J.-H. Song, jhsong@chungbuk.ac.kr). The dried leaves were ground into a fine powder in liquid nitrogen using a mortar and pestle. Total genomic DNA was extracted from the powdered tissue using the DNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. DNA quality and integrity were evaluated by UV–VIS spectrophotometry and agarose gel electrophoresis.

Total genomic DNA was subjected to short-read sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA), yielding 41,711,998 raw reads. Adapter removal and quality filtering were performed using Trimmomatic v0.89 (Bolger et al. 2014). The resulting reads were used for de novo assembly of the chloroplast genome using NOVOPlasty v.4.3.1 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017). To estimate sequencing coverage depth, the assembled chloroplast genome and the trimmed reads were aligned using BWA (Li 2013). Genome annotation was conducted in Geneious R10.2.6 using Sorbaria arborea (NC057434) as the reference. The circular chloroplast genome map was visualized using OGDraw (Greiner et al. 2019) and the schematic representation of cis- and trans-splicing genes was generated using CPGView (Liu et al. 2023). The chloroplast genome of S. sorbifolia (OK375440) was used to compare sequence variety using by MAFFT v7.475 (Katoh et al. 2002) without IR-A region.

The phylogenetic position of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia was inferred using 18 complete chloroplast genomes, comprising 16 representative taxa from the family Rosaceae (three subfamilies and eight tribes of the subfamily Amygdaloideae) and two outgroup taxa. All chloroplast genome sequences were retrieved from GenBank (NCBI). A total of 79 protein-coding genes and four rRNA genes were extracted from each of the chloroplast genomes. Sequence alignments were performed using MAFFT v7.475 (Katoh et al. 2002) with default settings. Poorly aligned regions were removed using trimAl v1.2 (Capella-Gutiérrez et al. 2009). The resulting trimmed alignments were concatenated using AMAS (Borowiec 2016). For each alignment, the best-fit nucleotide substitution model was determined using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) and the best-fit substitution models are listed in Table S1. A maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was reconstructed based on the concatenated dataset with a partitioned nucleotide substitution model scheme using IQ-TREE2 (Minh et al. 2020), with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (Hoang et al. 2018) and obtained best-fit models (Table S1). The final phylogenetic tree was visualized using TreeGraph2 (Stöver and Müller 2010).

Results

The complete chloroplast genome of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia was 159,476 bp in length, with an overall GC content of 36.1%. The average sequencing coverage-depth was 1035.65× (Figure S1). The chloroplast genome exhibited the typical quadripartite structure characteristic of most flowering plants, consisting of a LSC region and a SSC region, separated by two IR regions. The length of the LSC, SSC, and each IR region were 88,692 bp, 18,062 bp, and 26,361 bp, respectively (Figure 2).

Chloroplast genome map of Sorbaria sorbifolia var. glandulifolia. Genes located on the inner side of the circular map are transcribed in the clockwise direction, while those on the outer side are transcribed counterclockwise. The innermost dark gray part represents the GC content, with the light gray indicating overall nucleotide content. Genes are color-coded according to their functional categories. The thick line on the outer circle indicates the boundaries of the inverted repeat regions (IRa and IRb), which partition the genome into the large single-copy (LSC) and small single-copy (SSC) regions. Gene names marked with an asterisk indicate the presence of one or two introns.

A total of 112 unique genes were annotated, including 78 protein coding genes, 30 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, and four ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes. Among the protein coding genes, 12 genes contained one or two introns. Eleven genes (atpF, clpP, ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, rpl2, rpl16, rpoC1, rps16, ycf3) were identified as cis-splicing genes (Figure S2), while one gene (rps12) was identified as trans-splicing gene (Figure S3). Four intergenic spacer (IGS) regions, trnR-UCU–atpA, trnT-GGU–psbD, ndhC–trnV-UAC, and accD–psaI – were identified as highly variable. These regions were characterized by AT-rich or repetitive sequences. No significant inversions, large-scale rearrangements of the chloroplast genome, expansions or contractions of the IR regions, or gene losses were identified between the two chloroplast genomes (Figure S4). The phylogenetic reconstruction including 16 Rosaceae species and two outgroup taxa provided strong support for the monophyly of the tribe Sorbarieae (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusions

In this study, the complete chloroplast genome of Sorbaria sorbifolia var. glandulifolia was successfully assembled and annotated. The genome size, gene contents, and gene order were highly conserved and nearly identical to those of other previously reported Sorbaria chloroplast genomes. A ML phylogenetic tree was reconstructed to infer the phylogenetic position of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia within the family Rosaceae (Figure 3). The tribe Sorbarieae was recovered as monophyletic with strong bootstrap support (100). Within the genus Sorbaria, four taxa were resolved into two distinct clades, corresponding to the previously proposed infrageneric classification of section Sorbaria and section Kirilowiana J.-H. Song & S.-P. Hong (Song and Hong 2021). Both sections were robustly supported with bootstrap values of 100. The genus Sorbaria formed a sister group with the genus Adenostoma Hook. & Arn., consistent with previous phylogenetic analysis based on morphological traits and nuclear internal transcribed spacer (nrITS) sequences (Song and Hong 2020, 2021). In a previous study, Sorbaria was recovered as the sister group to the genus Chamaebatiaria (Porter ex W.H. Brewer & S. Watson) Maxim. (Song and Hong 2020). However, the chloroplast genome based phylogenetic tree inferred in this study suggested that Sorbaria is paraphyletic, with Chamaebatiaria millefolium nested within the Sorbaria. Such phylogenetic discordance has also been documented in Rosaceae (Hodel et al. 2021). Possible biological explanations for this incongruence include incomplete lineage sorting, differences in evolutionary rates between nuclear and chloroplast genome, introgressive hybridization, and chloroplast capture. In addition, the paucity of sequence data for Chamaebatiaria, a monotypic genus, may further contribute to the observed conflict. To resolve the phylogenetic relationships within the tribe Sorbarieae more robustly, broader taxon sampling will be necessary.

In conclusion, this study reports the first complete chloroplast genome of S. sorbifolia var. glandulifolia. The chloroplast genome structure, gene content, and gene order were found to be highly conserved across Sorbaria species. The inferred phylogenetic relationships confirmed the monophyly of Sorbarieae and supported the division of Sorbaria into two well defined morphological sections. These findings provide valuable genomic resources for future investigations into the phylogenetic relationships, evolutionary history, and taxonomy of the genus Sorbaria.

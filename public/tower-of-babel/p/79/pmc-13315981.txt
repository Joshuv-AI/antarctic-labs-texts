Comment on “Laparoscopic and robotic extravascular stenting of the left renal vein for anterior nutcracker syndrome in a single-center series”
Authors: Sangameshwar Manikya, Varshini Vadhithala, Rajnish Kumar, Pankaj Nainwal
Published: 2026

Tang et al1 present a structured single-center experience with laparoscopic and robot-assisted extravascular stenting for anterior nutcracker syndrome, integrating a standardized five-step intraoperative checklist with a postoperative duplex ultrasound target. The effort to align technical execution with quantifiable hemodynamic attainment addresses a long-standing gap in procedural reproducibility for this anatomically complex disorder.

The proposal of a postoperative aortomesenteric peak systolic velocity (PSV) of ≤72 cm/s as a primary end point is clinically appealing yet methodologically sensitive. Although the reported area under the curve approaches 0.92, threshold derivation and performance assessment were conducted within a small cohort, increasing the likelihood of optimism in discrimination estimates.2 In settings with limited outcome events, receiver operating characteristic optimization may favor cutoffs that perform well internally, but prove unstable externally. From a translational perspective, discrimination alone is insufficient; calibration and predictive stability across different duplex protocols and operators are equally critical before universal adoption of a fixed PSV target.3

The five-in-a-row checklist represents a pragmatic framework linking fibrotic ring resection, superior mesenteric artery mobilization, tributary division, tailored cuff placement, and secure fixation to postoperative velocity normalization. However, the mechanistic weight of each step remains bundled. For example, systematic tributary ligation may influence renal venous hemodynamics beyond decompression of the aortomesenteric segment, yet its independent association with PSV reduction is not quantified. Deconstructing individual procedural elements and correlating them with duplex changes and symptom resolution would clarify which components are indispensable and which function as adjunctive refinements.4

Finally, combining symptomatic and imaging success into a composite end point may mask temporal divergence between structural decompression and microvascular recovery. Nutcracker syndrome involves venous hypertension-mediated parenchymal changes; thus, symptom resolution may not perfectly parallel early PSV shifts.5 Serial patient-reported outcomes integrated with longitudinal duplex assessment would help to determine whether 3-month hemodynamic attainment predicts durable clinical benefit.

Overall, this study advances a process-outcome paradigm for extravascular stenting. External validation with calibrated hemodynamic thresholds and mechanistic dissection of operative steps may consolidate its role in standardized management of anterior nutcracker syndrome.

Comparative transcriptome profiling of leg muscle development from the embryonic to growth stages in Huanglang chickens
Authors: Xu Liu, Zihan Wang, Ting Chen, Hongni Liang, Can Yang, Hai Yang, Yuying Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe leg muscles of poultry are an important source of high-quality protein and key trace elements. Most of the research on muscle development are focused on the embryonic or growth stage, there is relatively little research on the muscle development of chicken from embryonic stage to growth stages. Therefore, in this study, transcriptome sequencing was performed on leg muscle samples from female Huanglang chickens across embryonic (E13, E17) to growth stages (D1, D14, D35). A total of 7,691 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified by comparison in pairs of the ten groups, and the highest number of DEGs was observed between E13 and D35, with 4,645 DEGs. Time-series expression analysis using STEM revealed three distinct expression clusters: cluster I (2,582 genes, downregulated genes), cluster II (1,666 genes, upregulated genes), and cluster III (614 genes, transient upregulation peaking at D1). GO and KEGG enrichment analyses showed that the DEGs were enriched in pathways such as cell cycle and DNA replication (cluster I), metabolic and muscle structure-related pathways (cluster II), oxidative phosphorylation and mitochondrial function (cluster III). Protein-protein interaction network analysis identified hub genes, including CDC45/MCM complex members in cluster I, mitochondrial genes such as CYTB and ND1 in cluster II, and oxidative phosphorylation genes including NDUFS3 and COX5A in cluster III. Transcription factor prediction highlighted KDM5A, KDM5B, PHF8, and SAP30 as key regulators. The RT-qPCR results of seven genes (ACTN2, PDK4, MYOG, MYOD1, MYH1F, MYH1B, and MYF6) were consistent with the RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis. This study is the first to systematically analyzed the leg muscle development mechanisms of local chicken breeds from embryonic stage to early growth stage. It provides a comprehensive transcriptomic resource for understanding leg muscle development in chickens.Keywords: Huanglang chickens, Muscle development, Embryonic, Growth stage, Transcriptome

Introduction

Muscle development in poultry critically determines meat quality, production performance, and economic profitability (Ren et al., 2024). Importantly, the development of skeletal muscles is a dynamic and multi-stage process, starting from embryonic development and continuing until hatching. During the embryonic stage, the main characteristic is proliferative growth, during which muscle progenitor cells proliferate, differentiate, and fuse to form primary and secondary muscle fibers (Zhao et al., 2026). After hatching, muscle growth transitions to the stage of hypertrophy, characterized by an increase in muscle fiber diameter and length, accompanied by sustained maturation of metabolic and contractile characteristics. This temporal transition from hyperplasia to hypertrophy is orchestrated by a highly coordinated network of regulatory factors, including myogenic regulatory factors, transcription factors, signaling pathways, and a growing array of non-coding RNAs (Jin et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025). The interruption or alteration of these fine tuned processes can have a lasting impact on muscle structure, fiber type composition, and ultimately meat quality characteristics. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the developmental pattern of skeletal muscles from the embryonic to growth stages.

In recent years, a growing number of studies have demonstrated that RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) technology is a powerful tool for studying muscle development in poultry. Han et al. (2025) compared the transcriptomic profiles of breast muscle at 20-d-old (D20) and D28 of age in broiler chickens using RNA-seq, a total of 1,536 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, and 5 significantly enriched pathways were found: MAPK signaling pathway, ErbB signaling pathway, insulin signaling pathway, and regulation of actin cytoskeleton, ribosome and cell cycle pathways. Transcriptome analysis of breast muscle at 4 weeks (M4F), M8F and M12F of age in male Jinghai yellow chickens showed that 83, 3,445 and 3,903 DEGs were separately obtained from M4FvsM8F, M4FvsM12F and M8FvsM12F. Pathway analysis showed that these genes were mainly related to steroid biosynthesis, carbon metabolism, focal adhesion, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, biosynthesis of amino acids and salmonella infection signaling pathway (Zhang et al., 2021a). In addition, there are also some studies on other poultry. Hu et al. (2026) analyzed the gene expression profiles of Pekin duck and Yulin black duck during three embryonic developmental stages (E15, E21, and E27), and the results showed that the significant metabolic pathways included the Wnt signaling pathway, regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, biosynthesis of amino acids, cell adhesion molecules, and the insulin signaling pathway. Transcriptome analysis of leg muscle at E15, E23, and D1 in Shitou goose (STE) and Wuzong goose (WZE) showed that the expression profiles of 89 myogenic genes specific to STE, 27 myogenic genes specific to WZE, and 127 myogenic genes shared between the two breeds (Shao et al., 2025). To our knowledge, few transcriptomic analyses specifically investigate new genes or metabolic pathways in local chickens from embryonic stages to early growth stages.

Huanglang chicken, also known as Xianghuang chicken, has a breeding history of more than 2,000 years and is an important dual-purpose indigenous breed from Hunan Province, China, valued for both egg and meat production (Ouyang et al., 2026). Xianghuang chicken is mainly characterized by yellow hair, mouth, and feet (three yellows), with a small body size, short body, strong adaptability, resistance to rough feeding, tender meat, and delicious taste. However, its growth rate is slow, and it is slaughtered at around 140 days old with a weight of about 1,200 g (CNCAGR, 2011). In this study, five stages of embryonic stages (E13, E17) to growth stages (D1, D14, and D35) of Huanglang chickens were selected for the study of muscle development patterns. Consequently, key genes and signal pathways related to leg muscle development were identified, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding the myofiber development.

Materials and methods

Animals, experimental design, and sample collection

The Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Hengyang Normal University (Hunan, China) approved the animal use in the current study (Permit No. HNU-12).

A total of 100 fertilized eggs from Huanglang chickens were used in this study. All fertilized eggs were incubated in controlled gradient conditions: 37.9 °C with 50% relative humidity from E1 to E7, 37.8 °C with 55% relative humidity from E8 to E14, 37.5 °C with 65% relative humidity from E15 to E17, and 37.2 °C with 70% relative humidity from E18 to E21. Eggs were turned automatically every 2 h during incubation. After hatching, the chickens were reared in a three-layer stacked net cage, with 70 cm in length × 68 cm in depth × 33 cm in height, and a feeding density of 12 birds per cage. The composition of diets fed to Huanglang chickens was presented in Table S1. Both diets and water were supplied ad libitum. Birds were exposed to 24 h of continuous light, with temperature and relative humidity held at 30°C ± 0.5 and 67.7% ± 2.62 for the first 3 days, then gradually decreased to 21°C at the end of the experiment. At each of the five developmental stages (E13, E17, D1, D14, and D35), 8 female embryos or chickens with similar body weight were selected. Leg muscle samples (gastrocnemius muscle) from the same side were collected and frozen at −80°C for RNA extraction.

Total RNA extraction, library construction, and sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from the leg muscle using the TRIzol method (Hammad, 2023). The contamination and degradation were examined by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. The purity and concentration of the extracted RNA were measured using a Nanodrop One micro UV visible spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and the integrity and total amount of RNA were accurately determined using a 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Only RNA specimens with RNA integrity number (RIN) greater than 8.7 and A260/A280 ratio of 1.8-2.0 were used for library construction. Total RNA was extracted from each sample, and 5 μg of RNA was used for library construction. Steps are as follows: mRNA was enriched from total RNA using Oligo(dT)-conjugated magnetic beads by virtue of its poly(A) tail; the enriched mRNA was then reverse-transcribed into double-stranded cDNA using dNTPs as substrates; the purified double-stranded cDNA was subjected to end-repair and ligation of poly(A) adapters; fragments within the size range of approximately 370–420 bp were selected and subsequently amplified by PCR to generate the final cDNA library for sequencing. After quality inspection of the library, RNA samples were sequenced on the IIlumina HiSeq 6000 platform. All RNA samples were processed and sequenced by Novogene Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to evaluate potential batch effects. The transcriptomic data have been deposited in NCBI under accession number PRJNA1449229.

Sequencing data quality control and read mapping

Using CASAVA base recognition to convert sequencing data into sequence data (reads), filter the raw reads to obtain clean reads, with the following filtering content: (1) trim adapter sequences from reads; (2) remove uncertain base information; (3) remove reads containing more than 10% of unknown nucleotides; and (4) remove reads with Q-value ≤ 20 that account for more than 50% of the entire read length (Bolger et al., 2014). Finally, calculate the Q20, Q30, GC content, and sequence-duplication level of the clean reads. All the downstream analyses were based on clean data with high quality. The filtered reads were mapped to the reference genome of the Gallus gallus 5.0 (GCA_000002315.3), and all transcripts were quantified using HISAT2 (v 2.1.1) software (Kim et al., 2019). The reads were assembled the gene expression levels were estimated using StringTie (v.2.1.4) (Pertea et al., 2015). Pairwise comparisons were conducted between all five time points (E13, E17, D1, D14, D35), resulting in 10 contrasts (Supplementary Table S3). For each contrast, sample expression was determined by FPKM (Fragments per Kilobase of exon model per million mapped reads), and the genes with FRD < 0.01 and |log2(foldchange)| > 1 were taken as the threshold for significantly differential expression (Hu et al., 2026).

Time series expression profile clustering

Short Time-series Expression Miner (STEM) is a Java software program specifically designed for clustering, comparing, and visualizing short time series gene expression data (typically ≤ 8 time points). We performed STEM analysis to cluster genes based on their temporal expression trajectories. Prior to analysis, genes were pre-filtered using a minimum absolute expression change threshold of 2. The maximum number of model profiles was set to 20, limiting the number of representative temporal expression patterns considered for gene assignment and downstream analysis. The statistical significance of the number of DEGs per contour compared to the expected number was calculated according to the algorithm proposed by Ernst and Bar Joseph (2006). After STEM identified all statistically significant temporal profiles, these profiles were summarized into three broader categories representing the core dynamic patterns (upregulated, downregulated, and transient/peak patterns), following the established interpretative framework in time-series transcriptome studies (Liu et al., 2020).

Functional enrichment analysis of differential expression analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of the all DEGs was implemented by GOatools. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of the all DEGs was used the KOBAS 3.0 web-server (Bu et al., 2021). To control the false positive rate of calculations, multiple tests were performed using FDR and BH (FDR) methods. All analyses were conducted with a significance threshold of P-value < 0.01 and a minimum gene count ≥ 2 per term.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis and transcription factors (TFs) prediction

The protein-protein interactions encoded in DEGs were analyzed using PPI networks, and the information on protein-protein interactions was sourced from the STRING database (Szklarczyk et al., 2023). The PPI network of the DEGs was visualized using Cytoscape software (version 5.3.1) (Shannon et al., 2003), where each node represents a protein encoded by a gene and the connections between nodes are displayed. Hub genes were identified using the Degree score, the top 6 genes with the highest scores were the key genes. By using NetworkAnalyst website (http://www.networkanalyst.ca) predicts potential transcription factors for key genes. The TFs prediction of key genes is based on ENCODE ChIP seq data, with screening criteria of peak intensity signal value < 500 and predicted regulatory potential score < 1. The results are visualized using Cytoscape software.

Quantitative real-time PCR validation

To verify the reliability of RNA-seq data from leg muscle samples of Huanglang chicken, this study randomly selected seven candidate genes of myosin heavy chain 1F (MYH1F), myogenic factor 6 (MYF6), myosin heavy chain 1B (MYH1B), myogenic differentiation 1 (MYOD1), pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 4 (PDK4), myogenin (MYOG), actinin Alpha 2 (ACTN2) for quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis. Firstly, RNA samples extracted using the previous method were selected for sample selection. Primer design for genes was completed using Primer BLAST (v.2.5.0), and synthesized by Shanghai Biotech Co., Ltd. (Table S2). Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (GAPDH) gene was used as the dual internal standard for normalizing transcript abundance of mRNA expression. Each PCR reaction mixture contained 2 µL of diluted cDNA template (1:9), 12.5 µL of SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ II, 1 µL each of forward and reverse primers (10 µmol/L), and 8.5 µL of sterilized distilled water. The thermal cycling conditions were set as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 30 seconds, followed by 35 cycles of 95°C for 5 seconds and 60°C for 30 seconds. At the end of the amplification, dissociation curve analysis was performed by gradually raising the temperature from 55°C to 95°C. The relative expression levels of target genes were calculated by the 2-△△Ct method as described by Livak and Schmittgen (2001).

Statistical analysis

The results were expressed as mean ± SD of at least three independent biological replicates. Significant differences in leg muscle gene expression in different periods were determined by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test and Duncan’s test. Statistical significance was set at a P < 0.05.

Results

Overview of RNA-sequencing quality assessment

After the transcriptomic analysis of leg samples from the embryonic to growth stages in Huanglang chickens, a total of 617.49 million clean reads with an average of 41.17 million reads per sample (range: 37.17 to 43.89 million) were obtained, and the Q20 and Q30 percentage were more than 96.63% and 91.51%, respectively. Meanwhile, the GC content of the libraries ranged from 46.39% to 52.99% (Table S3).

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) profiling

The DEG profiles were investigated at five time points, a total of 7,691 genes were differentially expressed (Fig. 1A and Table S4). Comparing successive ages within each stage, 2,933, 1,102, 785, and 476 DEGs were found in E13 vs E17, E17 vs D1, D1 vs D14, and D14 vs D35, respectively. Moreover, the numbers of DEGs were the smallest in D14 vs D35, including 248 up-regulated genes and 228 down-regulated genes. The numbers of DEGs were highest in E13 vs D35, including 2,436 up-regulated genes and 2,209 down-regulated genes. The PCA results demonstrated clear separation among the five stages, with strong intra group correlation, indicating good repeatability within sample groups and reliable sample quality (Fig. 1B).

Basic analysis of DEGs. (A) Numbers of upregulated and downregulated DEGs of leg muscle of Huanglang chickens through pairwise comparisons. (B) Plot of PCA of samples.

Abbreviations: DEGs, differentially expressed genes; PCA, principal component analysis

Sample time series analysis of DEGs

Since the data were collected across multiple time points, STEM was used to cluster and visualize the expression dynamics of 7,691 DEGs during leg muscle development (Fig. 2A). Among the 20 model profiles, six exhibited statistically significant enrichment (P < 0.05), encompassing 4,862 genes. Of these, profiles 0, 1, and 4 with downregulated patterns contained 634, 1,148, and 800 DEGs, respectively (Fig. 2B-2D and Table S5), while profiles 17 and 18 with upregulated patterns contained 1,073 and 593 DEGs, respectively (Fig. 2E-2F and Table S5). Profile 16 with 614 genes as the third pattern showed an increase from E13 to D1 and reached a peak at D1, then decreased from D1 to D35 (Fig. 2G and Table S5). Thus, the expression pattern of DEGs could be divided into three clusters: cluster I (profiles 0, 1, and 4, total of 2,582 DEGs) with downregulated pattern; cluster II (profiles 17 and 18, total of 1,666 DEGs) with upregulated pattern; and cluster III (profile 16, total of 614 DEGs). These findings provide novel information for further characterizing molecules associated with skeletal muscle development in local chickens.

The x-axis for each panel represents the developmental stages (from left to right: E13, E17, D1, D14, D35). The y-axis represents the normalized expression level.

Gene ontology (GO) annotation and enrichment analysis

To classify and characterize DEGs functions and pathways, a Gene Ontology (GO) classification was performed, and the GO terms of the three clusters were shown in Fig. 3 and Table S6. In cluster I (Fig. 3A), a total of 543 significantly enriched biological process terms were identified (P < 0.01), including mitotic cell cycle process, DNA replication, and DNA-dependent DNA replication. For cellular component, 39 terms were significantly enriched (P < 0.01), such as polymeric cytoskeletal fiber, cytosolic ribosome, and chromosomal region. For molecular function, 48 significant terms were obtained (P < 0.01), with structural constituent of cytoskeleton, structural constituent of ribosome, and single-stranded DNA-dependent ATP-dependent DNA helicase activity ranking as the top three. For cluster II (Fig. 3B), 636 significantly enriched biological process terms were identified (P < 0.01), including drug metabolic process, generation of precursor metabolites and energy, and purine nucleoside triphosphate metabolic process. A total of 46 cellular component terms were significantly enriched (P < 0.01), such as myofibril, contractile fiber, and I band. For molecular function, 59 significant terms were obtained (P < 0.01), with phosphoric ester hydrolase activity, phosphatase activity, and NADH dehydrogenase (ubiquinone) activity. For cluster III (Fig. 3C), 132 significantly enriched biological process terms were identified (P < 0.01), including generation of precursor metabolites and energy, cellular respiration, and oxidative phosphorylation. A total of 50 cellular component terms were significantly enriched (P < 0.01), such as mitochondrial part, mitochondrial protein complex, and mitochondrial inner membrane. For molecular function, 46 significant terms were obtained (P < 0.01), with proton transmembrane transporter activity, proton-transporting ATP synthase activity, rotational mechanism, and cofactor binding ranking as the top three. In summary, the three clusters represent a progressive transition from proliferation (Cluster I) to differentiation and early function (Cluster II), and finally to oxidative metabolism and mature tissue performance (Cluster III), closely mirroring the temporal hierarchy of normal developmental maturation.

Abbreviations: GO, gene ontology; DEGs, differentially expressed genes.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

Enrichment of the KEGG pathways was performed to further reveal the biological function of DEGs (Fig. 4 and Table S7). In cluster I (Fig. 4A), 14 KEGG pathways were significantly enriched, including ribosome, cell cycle, and DNA replication. For cluster II (Fig. 4B), the DEGs were significantly enriched in 19 pathways, such as calcium signaling pathway, adipocytokine signaling pathway, and carbon metabolism. For cluster III (Fig. 4C), 15 pathways were significantly enriched, comprising oxidative phosphorylation, carbon metabolism, and citrate cycle (TCA cycle). Thus it could be seen that the KEGG pathway progression from biosynthesis and cell division (Cluster I) through signaling and metabolic adaptation (Cluster II) to oxidative energy metabolism (Cluster III) recapitulates the temporal hierarchy of normal development, transitioning from proliferation to differentiation and finally to mature tissue function.

Identification of hub genes and pathways through protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis of DEGs

To further investigate the interactions of the differentially expressed genes associated with the muscle growth and development, protein-protein interactions were predicted using the STRING database, followed by the identification of hub genes based on degree. In cluster I (Fig. 5A), the PPI subnetwork contained 27 nodes and 172 edges, the top 6 hub genes were CDC45, MCM4, MCM6, POLE, MCM2, and CDC6 (degree value≥17). For cluster II (Fig. 5B), the PPI subnetwork contained 27 nodes and 151 edges, the top 6 hub genes were CYTB, ND1, MT-ND5, ND4, COX3, and ND3 (degree value≥15). For cluster III (Fig. 5C), the PPI subnetwork contained 73 nodes and 1355 edges, the top 6 hub genes were NDUFS3, NDUFS8, COX5A, UQCRC2, NDUFB5, and NDUFA9 (degree value≥ 53).

Abbreviations: PPI, protein-protein interaction; DEGs, differentially expressed genes.

Prediction of transcription factors (TFs) for key genes

The results showed that except for the hub gene in cluster II, which did not predict TFs, the other two clusters predicted TFs. In cluster I (Fig. 6A), the CDC45, CDC6, MCM4, MCM2, and MCM6 genes predicted 65, 46, 29, 20, and 3 TFs, respectively. Among these TFs, KDM5A was shared by CDC45, CDC6, MCM2, MCM4 genes. PHF8, SAP30, KDM5B were shared by CDC6, MCM2, and MCM4 genes. For cluster III (Fig. 6B), the NDUFS8, UQCRC2, NDUFS3, COX5A, NDUFB5, and NDUFA9 predicted 52, 31, 22, 21, 10, and 7 TFs, respectively. Among these TFs, KDM5A was shared by UQCRC2, NDUFS3, COX5A, and NDUFA9 genes. KDM5B, PHF8, SAP30 were shared by NDUFA9, NDUFS8 genes. Therefore, KDM5A, KDM5B, PHF8, and SAP30 were predicted as important TFs for key candidate genes.

Validation of DEGs by RT-qPCR

The expression of 7 randomly selected DEGs, including ACTN2, PDK4, MYOG, MYOD1, MYH1F, MYH1B, and MYF6 were verified through quantitative real-time PCR. As shown in the Fig. 7, the expression of these 7 genes was consistent with the results of the RNA-seq, which proved the reliability of the RNA-seq results.

Discussion

Muscle development is a complex process involving proliferation, differentiation, and maturation (Zhao et al., 2024). Although numerous studies have identified key genes and pathways involved in myogenesis, the dynamic transcriptional landscape during the transition from embryonic to post‑hatch growth stages in leg muscle of indigenous breeds have not been systematically characterized. Therefore, the study performed a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis of leg muscle development in Huanglang chickens across five time points from the embryonic to growth stages (E13, E17, D1, D14, and D35). By integrating RNA-seq quality assessment, DEG profiling, STEM clustering, GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, PPI network analysis, transcription factor prediction, and RT-qPCR validation, we systematically revealed the gene expression changes of Huanglang chicken leg muscles from the embryonic to growth stages, which elucidated the molecular mechanisms underlying muscle development and provided valuable resources for genetic improvement of leg muscle characteristics.

The transcriptome results of consecutive time periods showed that the number of DEGs gradually decreases from 2,933 DEGs (E13 vs E17) to 476 DEGs (D14 vs D35), indicating gene expression patterns tend to mature and stabilize with the passage of development or differentiation time. Besides, the highest number of DEGs was observed in E13 vs D35 (4,645 DEGs) among the 10 comparison groups, suggesting that development is a continuous and cumulative process in which larger time span result in more significantly expression differences. This pattern was consistent with the known transition from hyperplastic to hypertrophic muscle growth in poultry (Hu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025).

STEM analysis further resolved this temporal complexity by clustering 7691 DEGs into three expression clusters. Cluster I (downregulated pattern, 2582 genes) contained genes that were highly expressed in embryos and significantly downregulated after hatching, with consistently lower expression at D1, D14 and D35 compared to the embryonic stage. GO enrichment for this cluster included mitotic cell cycle, DNA replication, and cytosolic ribosome, indicating active myoblast proliferation during embryogenesis. KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that DEGs were significantly enriched in ribosome, cell cycle, and DNA replication signaling pathway. These were well-established core pathways regulating cell proliferation and metabolism. In muscle development, the cell cycle signaling pathway plays a “switch” role, receiving external signals from myogenic and growth factors to precisely control the proliferation and differentiation of myoblasts (Contriciani et al., 2024). The DNA replication signaling pathway is responsible for precise replication of DNA during myoblast division, providing the genetic material basis for multinucleation of muscle fibers (Pan et al., 2025). The ribosomal signaling pathway significantly influences protein translation and the regulation of skeletal muscle size, thereby contributing to protein synthesis in skeletal muscle (Chaillou, 2019). The enrichment of mitotic cell cycle, DNA replication, and ribosome pathways in Cluster I indicated that leg muscles are dominated by myoblast proliferation, with robust DNA synthesis and protein translation during embryonic stages. This was consistent with the result that the number of muscle fibers in birds was determined by the embryonic stage (Liu et al., 2023; Shao et al., 2025). PPI network analysis identified a core regulatory architecture centered on 6 master regulators: CDC45, MCM4, MCM6, POLE, MCM2, and CDC6, all of which were critical components of the DNA replication. Their high expression in embryos reflects the intense proliferative activity required to establish the myofiber number. MCM2 was core members of the MCM protein family, which play a key role in DNA prereplication complex assembly, replication initiation, and replication fork extension (Liu et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2025). MCM2 genes played significant roles in regulating breast muscle development of Beijing-You chicken (Li et al., 2020). Cell Division Cycle 6 (CDC6) is a key regulatory factor in the initiation of DNA replication in eukaryotes, while CDC45 is the core component of the CMG complex. Together with MCM2-7 hexamer and GINS complex, it forms the replication helicase responsible for DNA unwinding in eukaryotes. Research found that CDC6 and CDC45 are key genes in the pectoral muscle development of chicken and duck (Li et al., 2019; Pan et al., 2025), and similar research results have also been observed in other animals (Rong et al., 2024). CDC6 is responsible for loading MCM2-7 onto DNA and establishing replication permission status. Subsequently, CDC45 was recruited onto MCM2-7 to activate the CMG helicase complex, initiating DNA unwinding and replication. The research on the interaction between CDC6/CDC45 and MCM2-7 mainly focuses on human diseases (Cao et al., 2025; Kyei Barffour and Acheampong, 2021; Xu et al., 2016), and there was very limited research on this interaction in economic animals, particularly poultry.

Cluster II (upregulated pattern, 1,666 genes) contained DEGs showing a progressively increasing expression trend from E13 to D35, which the average expression at D35 was significantly higher than at E13. GO terms such as myofibril, contractile fiber, and I band, along with KEGG pathways like calcium signaling pathway and carbon metabolism. These pathways are critical for muscle contraction, energy supply, and post-hatch muscle functional maturation. In muscle development, the calcium signaling pathway acts as a key trigger for excitation-contraction coupling, directly regulating myofiber contraction and relaxation (Cao et al., 2023). The carbon metabolism pathway provides ATP and metabolic intermediates to support the high energy demand of sustained muscle activity and hypertrophic growth after hatching (Zhu et al., 2024). Several studys have reported the function in muscle development of these signaling pathway in poultry. This aligns with the observation that post-hatch muscle growth in birds occurs primarily through myofiber enlargement and enhanced contractile apparatus assembly, rather than an increase in fiber number (Bhasin et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a). The hub genes were all mitochondrial-encoded: CYTB, ND1, MT-ND5, ND4, COX3, and ND3. Transcriptome analysis of bovine skeletal muscle satellite cells (SMSCs) revealed that ND1, ND3, and CYTB were significantly DEGs closely related to the differentiation and energy metabolism of SMSCs (Wang et al., 2025). Studies on broiler chickens have also found that ND1, CYTB, and COX3 genes could increase mitochondrial energy, thereby fostering body protein deposition and the performance of broilers in the starter phase (Brito et al., 2017). In our paper, their progressive upregulation from D1 to D35 indicated a sustained increase in oxidative capacity, which may be related to the high endurance demands of leg muscles in walking and standing (Chen et al., 2023). The prominence of mitochondrial hub genes in our study suggested that leg muscle of Huanglang chickens is metabolically poised for sustained activity, a trait that may have been preserved in slow-growing indigenous breeds.

Cluster III (transient upregulation, peaking at D1, 614 genes) is particularly noteworthy because the DEGs in this cluster were enriched in pathways such as oxidative phosphorylation, TCA cycle, and mitochondrial inner membrane components. In muscle development, the TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, both localized to the mitochondrial inner membrane, generate the vast majority of ATP required for sustained muscle contraction and calcium handling (Zhang et al., 2021b). Recent studies have shown that this signaling pathways were significantly enriched in muscle transcriptome analysis, indicating their association with growth performance (Wu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2026b). The sharp increase at D1 (day of hatching) likely reflects an adaptive response to the dramatic physiological changes associated with hatching: the transition from chorioallantoic to pulmonary respiration, the onset of thermoregulation, and the initiation of weight-bearing activity. The hub genes included NDUFS3, NDUFS8, COX5A, UQCRC2, NDUFB5, and NDUFA9. NDUFS3 and NDUFS8 are involved in citric acid (TCA) cycling respiratory electron transport and respiratory electron transport biological processes. Transcriptome analysis showed that NDUFS8 regulated loin muscle area of beef cattle by participating in actin synthesis and muscle development (Xue et al., 2025). COX5A, recognized as a crucial regulator, is involved in mitochondrial energy metabolism, which could affect the muscle metabolism and indirectly affect the shear force of the muscles (Guan et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). A comparative transcriptomic analysis was conducted on leg muscles in AA+ broilers and Dagu chicken, the results showed that the expression of COX5A was significantly upregulated and had a promoting effect in muscle development (Zhu et al., 2024).

Among the predicted transcription factors, KDM5A and KDM5B showed significantly enriched binding motifs in the promoters of genes belonging to both Cluster I and Cluster III. The SAP30 gene is an important component of histone deacetylase complex in eukaryotic organisms, and it has already been identified as a factor of transcriptional regulation in the gene expression in mouse skeletal muscle (Magnusson et al., 2005). However, It is worth noting that the causal relationships between the predicted TFs and their target hub genes are not functionally validated. Our findings are correlative and hypothesis-generating. Future research should include in vitro and in vivo experiments to verify the regulatory roles of these candidate genes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study identifies three temporal gene clusters in developing chicken leg muscle, each enriched with functionally distinct genes—from DNA replication to mitochondrial energy production (e.g., MCM2, CYTB, ND1, COX5A, and UQCRC2). The predicted targeting of multiple gene clusters by common TFs (e.g., KDM5A) points to potential gene regulatory hubs. The novel insight is that D1 as a critical window for mitochondrial energy adaptation. These findings provide a molecular foundation for breeding strategies aimed at optimizing leg muscle growth and metabolic efficiency in indigenous chicken breeds.

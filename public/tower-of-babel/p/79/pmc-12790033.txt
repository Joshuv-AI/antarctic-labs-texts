High-Sensitivity Troponin I
Authors: Pavel Martinez-Dominguez, Nilda Espinola-Zavaleta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

In the evolving field of cardio-oncology, anthracycline-based regimens have transformed the prognosis of breast cancer and lymphoid malignancies, improving long-term survival and rendering many aggressive lymphomas largely curable.1 However, this carries the well-recognized liability of dose-dependent cardiotoxicity, ranging from asymptomatic heart failure and subclinical declines in global longitudinal strain (GLS) to a notorious decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).2 Survivors ultimately face increased cardiovascular mortality driven by cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction, which may manifest years after chemotherapy completion.3

High-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) has emerged as a leading candidate for identifying subclinical myocardial injury.4 Guidelines from the European Society for Medical Oncology and the Heart Failure Association and International Cardio-Oncology Society recommend serial troponin monitoring during high-dose chemotherapy as an opportunity for early intervention.5 However, the optimal timing, threshold, and clinical actionability of hs-cTnI remain unsettled.6

In a secondary analysis of the multicenter Cardiac CARE trial published in this issue of JACC: CardioOncology, Loganath et al7 evaluated 175 adult patients (mean age 52 ± 11 years, 86.5% women) with breast cancer or non-Hodgkin lymphoma who were scheduled to receive at least 3 cycles of anthracyclines (epirubicin) (median cumulative dose 600 mg/m2; Q1-Q3: 513-660 mg/m2). Participants underwent standardized hs-cTnI measurements before each chemotherapy cycle and at 2, 4, and 6 months after completion, as well as blinded core laboratory cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR)–quantified LVEF and GLS at baseline and 6 months. This design captured both acute and subacute biomarker dynamics alongside gold-standard imaging of cardiac function.

The study’s principal findings are as follows:•Cycle-dependent troponin release: Peak hs-cTnI occurred at 2 months after therapy and was statistically significantly correlated with the number of anthracycline cycles (P < 0.001) but not with cumulative dose (P = 0.32).•Modest prediction of LVEF decline: Although no patient’s LVEF decreased to <50%, about 14% experienced absolute declines ≥10%. Changes in hs-cTnI correlated weakly with LVEF decrease (r = −0.19) and offered modest discrimination (area under the curve, 0.67-0.74 at 2-4 months).•Discordance with strain imaging: hs-cTnI elevations did not predict a >15% reduction in CMR-derived GLS, a validated indicator of future systolic dysfunction.

This study’s strengths include its prospective, multicenter design; prespecified, uniform biomarker sampling; harmonized hs-cTnI assays; and blinded, core laboratory CMR analysis, which together minimize measurement bias. By focusing on a well-characterized, predominantly low-risk cohort, the trial provides high-fidelity insights into subclinical myocardial injury in modern oncology practice. However, the 6-month follow-up window may miss late onset cardiotoxicity documented in longer term series associating persistent troponin elevations with delayed cardiomyopathy.8 The predominance of low-risk patients (79% per Heart Failure Association/International Cardio-Oncology Society criteria) may limit the power to detect smaller functional changes and the generalizability to higher risk groups. The inclusion of both breast cancer and lymphoma populations introduces heterogeneity, as adjunctive therapies may variably affect myocardial injury. Finally, reliance on CMR-derived GLS, though precise, may not translate directly to centers using echocardiographic strain.

Future Directions

•Longer follow-up: Prospective registries with ≥2 years of follow-up to link early biomarker and imaging changes with clinical heart failure and mortality•High-risk and pediatric cohorts: Focused trials in patients with baseline cardiac comorbidities, genetic risk variants, or pediatric survivors to unmask stronger biomarker-outcome relationships•Multiple-marker algorithms: Development and validation of machine learning models combining hs-cTnI, natriuretic peptides, GLS, extracellular volume, and novel fibrosis markers (galectin-3, soluble ST2)9•Biomarker-guided interventions: Randomized studies to test whether troponin-triggered initiation of cardioprotective therapies (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, beta-blockers, dexrazoxane, neprilysin inhibitors) preserves myocardial health and improves patient-centered outcomes.

In summary, the investigators deliver a rigorous multicenter characterization of hs-cTnI kinetics and their relationship to early CMR-assessed left ventricular dysfunction, revealing that anthracycline cycle number, not cumulative dose, is associated with biomarker signals, which only modestly predict subclinical LVEF decline. Their findings challenge a troponin-only surveillance paradigm in low-risk cohorts and underscore the imperative for integrative strategies that combine biochemical, mechanical, and tissue assessments to safeguard cardiac health in patients treated with anthracyclines.

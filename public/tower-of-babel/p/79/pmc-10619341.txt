Different Exercises Generate Similar Musculoskeletal Adaptations—The Difference Is in the Total Work
Authors: André Pontes-Silva, André Luiz Lopes, Bruno Costa Teixeira, Randhall Bruce Carteri, Gustavo dos Santos Ribeiro
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

The results of the study by Oliveira et al 4 are very interesting and raise an important discussion in the area of musculoskeletal rehabilitation. The authors concluded that although postoperative, bilateral, isotonic resistance exercise provided better strength gains to the reconstructed limb, unilateral exercise was more effective in obtaining between-limb symmetry in functional and muscle strength for patients who underwent anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction with contralateral bone–patellar tendon–bone (BPTB) graft. However, when we reviewed the intervention protocol, we did not observe the accounting of the total work performed (i.e., it is not clear which group performed more total physical effort). Exercise science demonstrates that expending more energy (total work) 2 promotes better adaptations to musculoskeletal rehabilitation and overall health. In addition, recording the total work allows reproducibility of the clinical trial.

According to Figueiredo et al, 1 the volume of resistance exercise (unilateral or bilateral) 4 is a major contributor to muscle adaptations, with dose-dependent effects. Thus, the volume appears to be one of the most important, if not the most important, factors influencing musculoskeletal rehabilitation, as long as training is of sufficient intensity. Increasing the volume of resistance training seems to be the most easily modifiable variable when musculoskeletal rehabilitation and health outcomes are the main goals. 1 Ignoring the calculation of this variable leads the exercise prescription in the wrong direction with regard to adaptive phenomena. 3 Namely, the gap in the literature (is unilateral isotonic resistance exercise more effective than bilateral exercise for obtaining postoperative functional and muscular strength symmetry between the donor limb and reconstructed limb for patients who received BPTB graft?) remains open.

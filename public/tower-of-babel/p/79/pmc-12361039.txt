The endoglucanase gene LOC_Os09g23084 is involved in rice development and susceptibility to sheath rot disease
Authors: Ping Wan, Chi-Kuan Tu, Kai-Jie Jang, Su-May Yu, Shuen-Fang Lo, Meng-Yi Lin, Zun-Jie Syu, Yu-Hsuan Chiu, Tuan-Hua David Ho, Miin-Huey Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSheath rot disease, caused by Sarocladium oryzae, is a severe problem in rice cultivation and can result in significant yield loss worldwide. In this study, we analyzed the function of LOC_Os09G23084, encoding an endoglucanase-1 precursor, through gene overexpression. Two single T-DNA insertion homozygous overexpression lines, 1–16 S and 4–10 S, derived from Oryza sativa cv. TNG67, were used for functional characterization. In field conditions, overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 resulted in a decrease in rice development and an increase in susceptibility to sheath rot disease at the harvest stage. The overexpression lines showed delayed maturation, reduced internode and panicle neck length, deformed and less protruded vascular bundles, lower lignin content, and decreased yield. To verify the susceptibility of the overexpression lines to sheath rot disease, we set up a leaf-cutting inoculation method on seedlings. Lesion length was used to assess disease severity and was confirmed by fungal colonization using a GFP-tagged S. oryzae transgenic strain. The data confirmed that the overexpression lines were more susceptible to S. oryzae than wild-type lines. The reduced internode length and panicle neck length, less protruded peripheral vascular bundles, and low lignin content might contribute to the susceptibility to sheath rot. In this study, we provide insights into the potential function and mechanism of the endoglucanase gene LOC_Os09g23084 in rice susceptibility to sheath rot disease. Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is the staple food for half of the world’s population. However, rice cultivation is threatened by various pathogen attacks, from the nursery to the paddy field. Recently, rice sheath rot, caused by Sarocladium species, has become a severe problem during flowering and grain-filling stages, resulting in significant yield loss (Peeters et al. 2021; Sakthivel 2001). Sarocladium is a seed-borne pathogen transmitted by wind and insects such as the rice tarsonemid mite and the brown planthopper (Peeters et al. 2021). Wounds and stomata are necessary for Sarocladium conidia to germinate and invade the hosts (Bigirimana Vde et al. 2015). Subsequently, mycelia colonize in vascular, mesophyll, and leaf sheath tissues (Hittalmani et al. 2016). Infection on the flag leaf sheath can cause emerging lesions that spread to the young panicle. The necrotic lesions may extend to the entire flag leaf sheath, inner stem, and panicle, resulting in sheath rotting and discoloration, empty and sterile grains, and fungal toxin contamination in rice endosperm (Bigirimana Vde et al. 2015; Hittalmani et al. 2016; Ou et al. 2020; Peeters et al. 2020). Sarocladium attenuatum and S. oryzae are two common pathogens of sheath rot in rice fields that can cause 3–85% yield loss per harvest in Taiwan, especially in the second cropping season with high growth temperature (Lin et al. 2018; Ou et al. 2020).

There is not much information available on rice resistance or susceptibility to Sarocladium, which may be partially related to pathogen inoculation. The common inoculation method for Sarocladium involves a standard grain inoculum technique or injecting conidial suspension on rice tissues, and disease severity is evaluated on the flag leaf sheath or injected rice tissues 7 days to 1 month after the inoculation (Ngala 1983; Peeters et al. 2020; Sakthivel and Gnanamanickam 1987; Saravanakumar et al. 2008). Additionally, a large rice plant, approximately 11-week-old plants at flag leaf stage, is usually required for inoculation. These inoculation methods can lead to some difficulties, such as taking more than 2 months to screen new breeding-resistant varieties. Therefore, developing a technique for Sarocladium inoculation and disease evaluation on seedlings might facilitate a large-scale screening for disease resistance.

Disease resistance breeding is one of the important strategies in disease management in rice. In Taiwan, TNG67 is an imperative variety because it has tolerance to high fertilizer and lodging, resistance to rice blast, and slight resistance to bacterial blight and brown planthopper (Huang 1979; Syu et al. 2023). These traits make TNG67 frequently used in genome analysis experiments to identify useful functional genes, such as establishing the Taiwan Rice Insertional Mutant (TRIM) library. In the TRIM library, gene functions can be investigated through library screening, followed by the identification of affected gene(s) and then the functional demonstrations of the target genes. In our previous study, a TRIM line (M0050164) was found to be slightly resistant to Pythium arrhenomanes. Two genes, LOC_Os09g23084 and LOC_Os09g23070, encoding endoglucanase-1 precursor and malonyl-CoA decarboxylase, were up-regulated in this TRIM line and might be involved in rice resistance to Pythium infection (Syu et al. 2023). The functions of these two genes have not been characterized yet.

LOC_Os09g23084, also named as Os09g0394300 in RiceXPro, encodes an endo-(1,4)-β-glucanase (EC3.2.1.4) predicted in Rice Genome Annotation Project (http://rice.uga.edu/index.shtml). It belongs to the glycosyl hydrolase 9 family (GH9) with cellulose hydrolase activity. Cellulose is a polymer of disaccharide units of D-glucopyranose in a β(1→4) glycosidic linkage and is a key component of the cell wall of plants and oomycetes, such as Pythium species. LOC_Os09g23084 is located on chromosome 9 of rice. The expression profile of LOC_Os09g23084 shows a relatively broad expression pattern, with high expression in the stem, internode pith parenchyma, whole internode, stigma, and embryo sac (Kundu and Sharma 2016). During development, the transcripts of LOC_Os09g23084 are mainly detected in the leaf, panicle, and developing seed (Kundu and Sharma 2016). In studies related to plant-pathogen interactions, LOC_Os09g23084 expression is associated with rice resistance to the rice blast pathogen Magnaporthe oryzae and the plant parasitic nematode Hirschmanniella mucronate through transcriptomic analysis. In the rice blast-resistant Pi21-RNAi transgenic rice line, LOC_Os09g23084 expression is upregulated (Zhang et al. 2016). In H. mucronate-resistant and susceptible rice varieties, LOC_Os09g23084 (Os09g0394300, OsGH9B11) is highly expressed in the resistant variety (Sun et al. 2019). However, the true function of LOC_Os09g23084 in rice development and resistance to biotic and abiotic factors has not been characterized yet.

In this study, we aimed to characterize the function of LOC_Os09g23084 by overexpressing it in the rice variety TNG67 and analyzing the effects of its overexpression on rice development, yield, and disease resistance. After multiple field trials, we found that the overexpression (OE) lines were more susceptible to rice sheath rot. Furthermore, we set up an artificial inoculation system to verify the susceptibility of LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression lines to Sarocladium infection at the seedling stage. Additionally, overexpressing LOC_Os09g23084 affected multiple phenotypes related to plant development, yield, and disease susceptibility to root rot caused by Pythium. Furthermore, this study provides insights into the potential function and mechanism of LOC_Os09g23084 in rice susceptibility to sheath rot disease.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growth conditions

The rice variety Oryza sativa Japonica cv Tainung 67 (TNG67) was used in this study to generate gene overexpression lines. TNG67 and transgenic seeds were harvested from the same season for each assay. TRIM lines derived from M0050164 were obtained as indicated by Syu et al. (2023). Seeds were soaked in water for 3–4 days, a process referred to as seed soaking, after surface sterilization as described previously (Syu et al. 2023; Tu et al. 2023). The soaked seeds were planted in a hydroponic solution for assays as indicated or sowed in a nursery tray for transplanting in a paddy field. Rice lines grown under field conditions were planted with one seedling per planting and 24 plants in a block (3 × 8) per line at the Beigou agricultural experimental farm of the National Chung Hsing University in Taichung City from 2019 to 2022 with two crops per year. The central plants in the block, such as 15 plants (3 × 5) and 18 plants (3 × 6), were used for phenotyping.

Overexpression vector construction, rice transformation, and Southern blotting

The coding region of LOC_Os09g23084 was PCR amplified using primers (Supplementary Table S1) from the cDNA of the homozygous TRIM line 164 H (164-homo) (Syu et al. 2023) and cloned into the UMP vector to be driven by the maize ubiquitin (Ubi) promoter (Lee et al. 2009) after digestion with SpeI and EcoRV. The construct was transferred into TNG67 via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation as described previously (Lee et al. 2009). To screen the transgenic lines, hygromycin selection was conducted first. The hygromycin selection medium was used to culture the seeds using 1/2 MS selection medium [2.2 g Murashige & Skoog medium (Duchefa, Netherlands), 10 g agar in 1 L H2O] containing 50 ppm Hygromycin. Potential lines were selected for Southern blotting.

Total rice genomic DNA was extracted from the seedling shoots using the CTAB method as described previously (Syu et al. 2023). DNA was digested with restriction enzyme SacI or HindIII, and the hygromycin resistance gene was used as the probe. After SacI or HindIII digestion, a single DNA fragment larger than 2412–2457 bp, respectively, would be detected in the single insertion event in LOC_Os09g23084-overexpression lines. The restriction enzyme-digested genomic DNA (20 µg) was mixed with a dye solution containing xylene cyanol and bromophenol blue, and separated by 200 ml 8% agarose gel electrophoresis. Southern bolting was conducted as described previously (Kuo et al. 2021).

RNA extraction and gene expression assay

Total RNA was extracted from the rice seedlings using Trizol solution. RNA was converted to cDNA with M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen) after treatment with RNase-free DNase I (Promega). Gene expression was analyzed using semi-quantitative RT-PCR, and the actin gene (LOC_Os03g61970) was used as an internal control. The PCR cocktail contained 2 µl of 10X PCR buffer (Bee Taq buffer), 0.4 µl dNTP (each 10 mM), 0.4 µl forward primer and reverse primer (each 10 µM; Supplementary Table S1), and 0.4 µl Bee Taq DNA polymerase (Fanyuan Co., Taiwan), and sterilized Milli-Q (MQ) water was added to a final volume of 20 µl. PCR conditions were as follows: 2 min at 94 ℃, 28 to 32 cycles of amplification reaction (94 ℃ for 30 s, annealing temperature for 30 s, 72 ℃ for 30 s), and additional extension at 72 ℃ for 7 min. PCR products, 2 µl per sample, were detected by 1.2% agarose gel electrophoresis. The PCR products amplified from TNG67 cDNA under different cycle numbers were separated by agarose gel electrophoresis to determine the optimal number of cycles for unsaturated amplification, as described by Lee et al. (2010). As shown in Figure S1, 32 cycles were selected for semi-quantitative RT-PCR across all tested lines. This method effectively demonstrated significant gene overexpression but did not allow for precise quantification of fold changes in gene expression among the different lines. Therefore, quantitative RT-PCR (qRT-PCR) using the comparative CT method was employed to determine the fold change in expression levels, as described previously (Yu et al. 2017).

Assays for seed germination and shoot and root length

A total of 2.5 g seeds were surface sterilized and placed in a 5.5-cm-diameter petri dish with ten ml reverse osmosis (RO) water for seed-soaking, with the RO water changed every day, for four days (Tu et al. 2023). Approximately 100 seeds per replicate with three replicates for each line were investigated in each experiment. Seed germination was categorized into four levels based on shoot and root formation: no shoot or root formation, shoot or root length < 0.7 cm, shoot or root length between 0.7 and 1.4 cm, and shoot or root length > 1.4 cm.

To measure seedling growth, water-soaked seeds were grown in a hydroponic system with 5 seedlings per well and 3 replicates per line (Syu et al. 2023). The shoot and root lengths of 10-day-old seedlings were measured using ImageJ.

Investigation of diseases occurring in paddy fields

Several diseases were commonly observed in the field, such as sheath rot, sheath blight disease, rice blast, brown spot, and white panicle damaged by pink stem borer or rice stem borer. Therefore, these naturally occurring diseases in the fields were investigated during harvest for five cropping seasons from 2019 to 2021. A total of 15 plants per line were investigated in a cropping season for each disease.

Disease incidence was investigated for sheath rot, sheath blight, and white head (panicle damages caused by stem borers) and determined by estimating the percentage of the diseased panicles in each plant. The disease severity of rice panicle blast was recorded by using the 0–9 disease rating scale (0, 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9) listed in IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank of International Rice Research Institute. Measurements of rice height, internode length, and grain yield in field trials

Rice plant height was measured at the tillering stage from 2019 to 2021. The tallest rice tiller per plant and 18 plants per line were measured. The length of the internode, panicle, and panicle neck was measured during harvest. The thickest and central position stalk of each plant was selected to calculate the length, with 15 plants per rice line. Tiller numbers, panicle numbers, and grain weight per plant were counted. The panicle length was measured from the base of the panicle to the first internode. The panicle neck length was measured from the panicle base to the top of the flag leaf sheath. At least 15 plants per line were detected in each cropping season. Grains were collected and dried at 37 ℃ for 1 week in an oven. The mean of grain weight per plant from four cropping seasons (2019-I, 2020-I, 2020-II, and 2021-I) in each line was then calculated and presented.

Anatomic analysis of rice tissues

Rice tissues were embedded in 3–4% agarose and sliced into 40 μm sections using a Microslicer (DTK-1000 N, Dosaka, Kyoto, Japan) with three to five plants per line. At least two sections from each line were used for vascular size measurement or staining assay. The size of the vascular bundles was measured using ImageJ software. For lignin analysis, a phloroglucinol-HCl staining method was employed, as previously described by Weng et al. (2010). The sections were stained with a solution containing 1% phloroglucinol (w/v) in 12% HCl for 5 min and subsequently examined under a microscope. For cellulose staining, the Calcofluor White staining was utilized, following the procedure described by Ursache et al. (2018). The sections were treated with 0.1% Calcofluor White for 15 min, followed by a gentle wash with MQ water. Staining observations were conducted using an excitation wavelength of 405–445 nm and emission detection at 430–490 nm. Lignin content was quantified based on color intensity, measured using the Histogram function in ImageJ. The brightness levels obtained from the histogram were used as an indicator of lignin abundance. Darker red coloration, corresponding to higher lignin content, produced lower intensity values, while brighter regions with lower lignin content yielded higher intensity values. The average histogram intensity was used as a proxy for relative lignin levels.

Enzyme activity assay

The endo-1,4-β-glucanase activity was determined using the Cellulase Assay Kit (Megazyme, Ireland) with the CellG5 method (Mangan et al. 2016). Leaves from 3- or 21-day-old rice seedlings were used for enzyme activity detection following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, leaves collected from ten seedlings per line were grounded into powder in liquid nitrogen and a 100-mg powder was used for the assay. A standard curve for endo-1,4-β-glucanase was created using a known enzymatic unit of Trichoderma cellulase provided in the kit. Total protein concentrations were determined using the Bio-Rad Protein Assay (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA).

The glucanase activity of germinated seeds was assessed following the method described by Horvath et al. (2000) with modifications. Seeds were germinated for 72 h, heated for 1 h at 45℃, cut in half, and placed endosperm-side down on Murashige-Skoog medium containing 2 g/L carboxylmethyl cellulose, then incubated at 37℃ for 24 h. The zymogram was developed after staining with 0.2% Congo red for 30 min and de-staining in 1 M NaCl solution for 5 min. Five seeds per line were used in the assay and the sizes of clear zones were measured using ImageJ.

Pythium arrhenomanes inoculation assay

P. arrhenomanes inoculation and disease severity assay were conducted as described previously (Syu et al. 2023). Seeds harvested from 2019 to 2021 were used. In each experiment, five seedlings per inoculation and three inoculations per line were conducted.

Sarocladium oryzae inoculation assay

Sarocladium oryzae strain SA3-8, provided by Dr. Chi-yu Chen, Dept. of Plant Pathology, National Chung Hsing University, was isolated from a diseased rice plant (Ou et al. 2020). Conidia collected from the SA3-8 colony grown on PDA for 2 weeks were resuspended in water and filtered through a layer of miracloth (MERCK). The suspension was adjusted to 2 × 106 and 2 × 107 spores/ml for leaf-cutting inoculation on rice leaves of seedlings that were 3 weeks old. Rice leaf tips were cut off with sterile scissors to remain 2 to 3 cm long, and the cut surface was soaked in the spore suspension or sterilized water for 10 min. The seedlings were kept in a moist environment for 1 week. The lesion length was measured using ImageJ. Seeds harvested from 2020 to 2021 were used in this experiment, and 15 seedlings per line were inoculated. The lesion length was measured from the cut side (the inoculation site) to the lesion margin, which was confirmed with S. oryzae colonization assay as mentioned below.

Generation of GFP-tagged Sarocladium oryzae

The GFP-tagged S. oryzae strains were generated using the Agrobacterium-mediated T-DNA transformation method to transfer plasmid p1300-CT74(F) into S. oryzae strain SA3-8, as described previously (Kuo et al. 2021). The generated SA3-8 GFP1 and SA3-8 GFP2 strains were selected to verify S. oryzae colonization on the inoculated leaf using the leaf-cutting inoculation method.

S. oryzae colonization assay

The colonization of S. oryzae on the inoculated leaves was assayed. After inoculation for 7 days, the leaves were collected and fungal colonization was detected by microscopic examination and by cultivating leaf fragments on water agar (WA). For microscopic examination, the inoculated leaves were sliced, and examined for fungal hyphal colonization under a fluorescence microscope. For cultivation on WA, the inoculated leaves were sterilized in 75% ethanol for 1 min, then in 2.5% sodium hypochlorite for 1 min, and washed three times with sterilized RO water. The sterilized leaf was cut into small pieces with 2 mm in length starting from the inoculation site. These pieces were then placed sequentially on WA containing 10 ppm chloramphenicol. Hyphae grown out from the leaf pieces were examined under a dissecting microscope. Rice leaf pieces colonized with S. oryzae hyphae were used to confirm the colonization distance (lesion length) from the inoculation site to the lesion margin in each leaf. All inoculated leaves were assayed for S. oryzae colonization.

Data analysis

Statistical Package for Social Science software, version 20 (IBM SPSS Statistics 20) was used for data analysis. The significance of the difference between the OE line and its segregated wild-type line was determined by the Independent Student’s t-test between the two groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan test (P < 0.05) was used to identify differences between more than two groups. All experiments were performed at least twice. Data were presented with sinaplot and boxplot using ggplot2 and ggforce packages or presented with means and their associated standard deviations.

Results

Bioinformatic analysis of LOC_Os09g23084 and the selection of overexpression lines

LOC_Os09g23084 and Os09g0394300 are the same genes reported in two different databases, the Rice Genome Annotation Project and RiceXPro, respectively. However, the two genes are indicated with different sizes of the coding region and generate protein sizes of 441 amino acids and 556 amino acids, respectively. A 115-amino acid region was missed in the N-terminus of Os09g0394300 (Gene symbol LOC4346968 in NCBI). Therefore, we amplified the 5’ region of the cDNA generated from this gene for sequencing to verify whether the 115 amino acids are present in the LOC_Os09g23084 of TNG67. Our data showed that LOC_Os09g23084 of TNG67 carries the additional 115 amino acids at the N-terminus. The CDS length of LOC_Os09g23084 is 1671-bp made up of three exons in TNG67 (Fig. 1A). Analysis of the protein sequence using Interpro indicates that LOC_Os09g23084 encodes an endo-(1,4)-β-glucanase, containing a secreted signal peptide at the N-terminus and a GH9 domain at the C-terminus.

The cDNA of LOC_Os09g23084 amplified from TNG67 was cloned under the control of the Ubi promoter to generate overexpression transgenic lines. A total of 32 transgenic lines were generated. Hygromycin selection medium and Southern blotting were used to identify the LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression homozygous (OE) and segregated wild-type (sWT) lines. Southern blotting identified single T-DNA insertion homozygous OE lines 1–16 S and 4–10 S and their associated sWT lines 1–11 sWT and 4–6 sWT, respectively (Fig. 1B). The single band size of 1–16 S was different from 4–10 S, indicating that they are two independent homozygous lines with T-DNA insertion in different positions. Therefore, they were selected for further analysis.

The expression of LOC_Os09g23084 was detected using semi-q-RT-PCR, and the data from two independent experiments were showed in Supplementary Figure S1. The results revealed that 1–16 S and 4–10 S exhibited significantly higher LOC_Os09g23084 expression levels than TNG67 and their sWT lines. In addition, gene expression levels were further quantified using qRT-PCR (Fig. 1C). The data verified the overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 in the OE lines 1–16 S and 4–10 S. In addition, the mRNA level of LOC_Os09g23084 was significantly higher in the OE lines than in the LOC_Os09g23084-activated homozygous TRIM line 164-homo (164 H) (Fig. 1C and Supplementary Figure S1).

LOC_Os09g23084-oe lines exhibit delayed growth and reduced grain yield

Seed germination ability was assayed by measuring the length of the embryonic shoot and root after seed soaking. All rice lines had no significant differences in the percentage of seeds without root formation, indicating that their germination abilities are similar. However, the OE lines showed significant differences in root development. Compared to the sWT lines, the OE lines have a higher percentage of seeds with root lengths shorter than 0.7 cm and a lower percentage of seeds with root lengths longer than 1.4 cm (Fig. 2A), indicating that root growth was inhibited in the OE lines. There was no significant difference in shoot development between the OE lines and their sWT lines (Fig. 2B). In 10-day-old seedlings, the two OE lines had shorter shoot and root lengths than TNG67 and their sWT lines, but the difference was not found in the LOC_Os09g23084-activated homozygous TRIM line 164 H (Supplementary Figure S2A).

The OE lines also showed a significant delay in growth in field conditions. In the nursery tray, the OE lines showed shorter plant height than TNG67 and their sWT lines (Supplementary Figure S2B). In the paddy fields, the growth and panicle development of the OE lines were noticeably delayed compared to those of the wild-type lines (Fig. 3A). The plant height of the two OE lines was shorter than their sWT lines and TNG67 at the tillering stage (Fig. 3B), indicating that the OE lines had delayed growth compared to the sWT lines.

To understand if LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression affects rice production, grain weight per plant was calculated from four harvest seasons from 2019 to 2021 (Fig. 3C). OE lines had significantly less grain weight per rice plant than their sWT line and TNG67, but no difference in tiller numbers per plant was found. OE line 1–16 S decreased by 30.8% in grain weight per plant compared to TNG67 and by 33.4% compared to 1–11 sWT; OE line 4–10 S decreased by 21.7% compared to TNG67 and decreased by 26.3% compared to 4–6 sWT.

LOC_Os09g23084-oe lines exhibit shorter internode and panicle neck length

LOC_Os09g23084 exhibits high expression in the internode pith parenchyma, whole internode, and stigma of rice plants (Kundu and Sharma 2016). To investigate the impact of LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression on rice internode development, the lengths of different internodes at the mature stage were measured. The length of the first internode (the uppermost internode; IN1) in the OE lines was found to be significantly shorter compared to their sWT lines (Fig. 4A). No consistent differences were found between OE lines and their corresponding sWT lines in the lengths of the second internode (IN2), the third internode (IN3), and the panicle. In addition, the panicle neck of the OE lines was frequently covered with the flag leaf sheath. The panicle neck length, from the panicle base to the top of the flag leaf sheath, of the OE lines was significantly shorter than that of the TNG67 and sWT lines (Fig. 4B).

LOC_Os09g23084-oe lines exhibit less-protruded vascular bundles and reduced lignin content

The cross-section analysis of IN1 revealed a significantly lower level of protrusion of peripheral vascular bundles in OE lines compared to the wild-type lines (Fig. 5A). Additionally, a distinct difference was observed in the shape of the vascular bundle between the OE lines and wild-type lines. The OE lines exhibited longer inner vascular bundles and shorter peripheral vascular bundles compared to wild-type lines (Fig. 5B). The length-to-width ratio of the vascular bundle was calculated, and the data demonstrated that LOC_Os09g23084 OE lines had a significantly lower length-to-width ratio of peripheral vascular bundles and a higher length-to-width ratio of inner vascular bundles compared to the WT lines (Fig. 5C).

A higher disease incidence of naturally occurring sheath rot was found in LOC_Os09g23084-oe lines compared to the wild-type lines

In field conditions, five rice diseases were commonly observed. Throughout five cropping seasons from 2019 to 2021, severe sheath rot disease was observed in the OE lines (Fig. 7 and Supplementary Figure S3). This disease often occurred on the flag leaf sheath (Fig. 7A). It could reduce grain quality and cause grains to become discolored especially when the infected flag leaf sheath covered the panicle (indicated by red arrows in Fig. 7A). Representative data on disease incidence from the 2021-II crop are shown in Fig. 7B. The incidence of sheath rot disease in the OE lines was significantly higher than that in TNG67 and their corresponding sWT lines in each season (Supplementary Figure S3). The average disease incidence from the five harvests was 17.66% and 1.63% for 1–16 S and 1–11 sWT lines, respectively, 12.32% and 1.11% for 4–10 S and 4–6 sWT lines, respectively, and 1.50% for TNG67 (Fig. 7C).

Additionally, four other diseases, namely sheath blight, rice blast, brown spot, and white head caused by stem borers, were investigated over two seasons. However, no associations were observed between LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression and disease severity or incidence for these four diseases (Supplementary Table S2).

LOC_Os09g23084 oe lines are more susceptible to S. oryzae infection, as demonstrated by seedling leaf-cutting inoculation

To confirm the observations that OE lines were more susceptible to sheath rot disease in field conditions, an in-vitro inoculation assay was set up. The inoculation with a spore suspension of 2 × 107 spores/ml caused necrosis on the cutting edge of TNG67 at 3 days post-inoculation (dpi). The early symptom was browning that developed along the leaf veins at the cutting edge at 3 dpi (central panel in Fig. 8A). On the cutting surface, mycelia grew out from the infected veins (indicated with an arrow in the bottom panel of Fig. 8A). Almost all vascular bundles of the pathogen-treated leaf had turned brown color, which was not observed in the control leaf (Fig. 8B). The lesion length was used as a measurement to detect the susceptibility of rice lines to S. oryzae. By 7 dpi, the necrotic lesion expended and became measurable (Fig. 8C). However, some small leaves after cutting could wilt (marked with a white arrow in Fig. 8C), which was not highly distinguishable from the long necrotic lesion examined with the naked eye. Therefore, the lesion length was verified with mycelium growth on water agar (marked with yellow lines on the agar plates in Fig. 8D). The lesion length of OE line 1–16 S was significantly longer than that of 1–11 sWT and TNG67. The OE line 4–10 S also showed larger lesions than 4–6 sWT (Fig. 8E).

LOC_Os09g23084 oe lines are not resistant to Pythium arrhenomanes

The LOC_Os09g23084 activated TRIM line M0050164 of TNG67 was shown to slightly increase resistance to the rice seedling damping-off pathogen Pythium arrhenomanes (Syu et al. 2023). Therefore, the LOC_Os09g23084 OE lines were assayed for their resistance to P. arrhenomanes inoculation. The reduction percentage of primary root length in the Pythium treatment compared to that in the water treatment was used to evaluate the disease severity of Pythium infection. No consistently significant difference was found between the OE and the wild-type lines in multiple independent inoculation assays. The data from two independent inoculation assays using seeds collected from 2020-I and 2021-I were presented (Supplementary Figure S4).

The LOC_Os09g23084-activated homozygous TRIM line 164 H expressed LOC_Os09g23084 mRNA at a lower level than the OE lines and a higher level than the wild-type lines (Fig. 1C and Supplementary Figure S1). Compared to the wild-type lines, LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM line 164 H did not show a significant difference in seedling growth, plant height, and grain yield (Supplementary Figure S2; Syu et al. 2023) as well as the length of internode, panicle, and panicle neck (Supplementary Figures S5 and S6). In addition, no significant difference in sheath rot incidence was detected in LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM line 164 H compared to the wild-type lines (Supplementary Figure S7).

The seeds of LOC_Os09g23084 oe lines have higher cellulase activity

Total endoglucanase activity was detected using the CellG5 method. OE lines showed slightly higher enzyme activity than their sWT in 3-day-old seedlings, but the difference was not detected in 21-day-old seedlings. By Congo red staining, the embryos of OE lines showed slightly higher cellulase activity compared to their sWT lines and TNG67 (Supplementary Figure S8).

Discussion

Various rice diseases occurred in the field causing severe yield loss, and potentially leading to a food crisis worldwide. Rice breeding for disease resistance is a crucial approach to reduce this loss. By screening the TRIM library for disease resistance to Pythium, one line with two up-regulated rice genes showed slight resistance to Pythium arrhenomanes (Syu et al. 2023). However, the function of these two genes has not been characterized. In this study, we overexpressed one of these genes, LOC_Os09g23084, in rice to analyze its function. We found that overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 resulted in increased susceptibility to sheath rot disease and decreased development, including delayed maturation, reduced internode length, deformed vascular bundles, reduced lignin content, and decreased yield. Artificial inoculation demonstrated the increased susceptibility of the OE lines to the sheath rot pathogen. The deformed vascular bundles and low lignin content might contribute to the susceptibility to sheath rot.

Plant cellulases, primarily endoglucanases, are classified in the GH9 family. This family is further divided into three structure subclasses (A, B, C) based on the presence of transmembrane domains, the signal peptide, and the carbohydrate-binding module CBM49, in which only subclass A contains transmembrane domains (Urbanowicz et al. 2007). Twenty-five rice GH9 genes (OsGH9) have been classified according to this domain presence classification, with LOC_Os09g23084 annotated as GH9B11 based on the nomenclature of the GH9 family in Arabidopsis (Xie et al. 2013). In the same year, Kundu and Sharma annotated LOC_Os09g23084 as a member of the GH9C group using ANN predictor, which is based on machine learning with sequence features and 3D structures. Endoglucanases play roles in cellulose biosynthesis and degradation, as well as in modifying the association of cellulose microfibrils with other wall polysaccharides, or in wall loosening during cell elongation (Perrot et al. 2022). Therefore, the OsGH9 gene family is likely to play roles in rice growth and development. Two membrane-bound endoglucanase genes, OSGLU1 and OSGLU3, are known to be involved in plant growth. OSGLU1 affects plant internode elongation (Zhou et al. 2006), while OSGLU3 is required for root cell elongation and division in rice (Zhang et al. 2012). Functional studies of OsGH9 genes are primarily related to biofuel production (Huang et al. 2017; Huang et al. 2019; Nigorikawa et al. 2012; Sharma et al. 2013; Xie et al. 2013). For example, the overexpression of rice exoglucanase EXG1 leads to various abnormalities in leaves and enhanced saccharification of rice straws (Nigorikawa et al. 2012). Additionally, overexpressing two OSGH9 genes (OsGH9B1 and OsGH9B3) enhances saccharification and increases bioethanol production, with a slight impact on plant growth (Huang et al. 2019). In this study, overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 influenced root and shoot elongation during plant growth. The slow growth appeared to delay the maturation of rice and affect grain yields. LOC_Os09g23084 was found to be expressed at a moderate level in roots and old stems, and at a relatively high level in young stems, mature leaves, internodes, radicles, embryosacs, and endosperm (Kundu and Sharma 2016; Xie et al. 2013), which correlates with the phenotypes observed in the overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084. Overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 inhibited root growth during seed germination and seedling growth, as well as shoot and internode growth at the seedling and vegetative stages, respectively. However, compared to the wild-type lines, the LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM mutant 164 H did not show differences in root and shoot growth at the seedling stage or in plant growth in the field, indicating that the growth retardation of OE lines is likely caused by the constitutive overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084.

The internodes of rice plants do not elongate significantly during vegetative growth. Elongation of internodes begins during the transition from vegetative to reproductive growth, allowing the panicle formed at the stem apex to be pushed upward (Hirao et al. 2001; Nagai and Ashikari 2023). In this study, we observed that the OE lines showed retardation in the growth of the first internode (IN1), resulting in the panicle neck being almost fully covered by the flag leaf sheath. This phenomenon might affect grain production and the occurrence of sheath rot (see below). LOC_Os09g23084 is highly expressed in the internode pith parenchyma, whole internode, and stem (Kundu and Sharma 2016; Xie et al. 2013). Consistent with these expression patterns, our data demonstrate that LOC_Os09g23084 overexpression plays an important role in internode elongation. Genes such as OsGLU1, phosphatidylserine synthase, and gibberellins homeostasis are involved in culm development and internode length in rice (Ma et al. 2016; Wang et al. 2017; Zhao et al. 2021; Zhou et al. 2006). In addition to OsGLU1, LOC_Os09g23084 might be the second OsGH9 gene found to be involved in internode elongation in rice.

Overexpressing LOC_Os09g23084 affects vascular bundle development and causes deformation of peripheral and inner vascular bundles. Additionally, it results in a decrease in lignin content but no significant change in cellulose content. These changes may be related to long-term alterations in cell wall construction in the OE lines. Lignin content can impact the enzymatic hydrolysis of lignocellulosic materials, thereby affecting cell wall construction and reconstruction (Phakeenuya et al. 2020). The protrusion level of the peripheral vascular bundle of IN1 is less pronounced in the OE lines, which may lead to changes in the shapes of both the peripheral and inner vascular bundles in the OE lines. The loss-of-function of OSH15, a member of the knotted1-type homeobox gene family, and the overexpression of OsOFP, a transcription factor of Ovate Family Protein, have been shown to alter the arrangement of rice vascular bundles (Sato et al. 1999; Schmitz et al. 2015). The positioning of vascular bundles might be influenced by the presence of capped sclerenchyma fibers and the composition of lignin in the cell wall (Marcelo et al. 2017).

Rice cell wall composition plays a potential role in determining the degree of horizontal resistance to the rice sheath rot pathogen Sarocladium (Joe and Manibhushanrao 1995; Mvuyekure et al. 2017; Zhang et al. 2021). Sarocladium secretes cell wall-degrading enzymes and toxins to colonize hosts (Manibhushanrao et al. 1986; Pearce et al. 2001; Peeters et al. 2020; Sakthivel et al. 2002). In this study, we observed that the cell wall composition of the OE lines differs from the wild-type, with lower lignin in the OE lines. The lower lignin content might contribute to the higher susceptibility of the OE lines to Sarocladium. Lignin is crucial for the enzymatic hydrolysis of lignocellulosic materials and then may influence the infection of Sarocladium. In addition to lignin contents, the lower protrusion level of the internode vascular bundle and the coverage of the panicle neck with the flag leaf sheath also contribute to the susceptibility of the OE lines to sheath rot. Sheath rot lesions are commonly found on sheaths covering IN1, and infection of IN1 can lead to the abortion of emerging panicles and the development of unfilled grains (Bigirimana Vde et al. 2015; Ou et al. 2020; Sakthivel et al. 2002). In the field, rice sheath rot disease is mainly transmitted by the tarsonemid mite (Steneotarsonemus spinki Smiley) (Ayyadurai et al. 2005). The female mite is 222–350 μm in length and 84–108 μm in width, while the male mite is 217–244 μm in length and 109–122 μm in width (Hummel et al. 2009; Kayal et al. 2021). Their bodies are larger but their cheliceral stylets are much shorter than the protruded vascular bundles, which might affect their efficacy in injecting the epidermis of rice tissues. Overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 results in less protruding vascular bundles that might increase the injection efficacy of mites into the rice tissues and subsequently enhance the transmission of the pathogen, thereby increasing disease incidence.

Sarocladium requires wounds and stomata to enter rice, and all growth stages of rice can be infected by the sheath rot pathogen (Peeters et al. 2021). The typical inoculation method for Sarocladium species involves injecting a spore suspension into the flag leaf sheath or the stem, with a limitation on plant age of 11 weeks (Ngala 1983; Ou et al. 2020; Saravanakumar et al. 2008). Lesions caused by this method appear as oblong or irregular spots (Bigirimana Vde et al. 2015; Peeters et al. 2020). Besides, this method takes over 7 days for a lesion to appear and 14 days to develop a large lesion for evaluation, making it challenging to rapidly estimate disease severity. In this study, a leaf-cutting method using seedlings as inoculation materials was developed. This method significantly reduces rice cultivation time and avoids irregular lesion formation. However, a drawback of this method was identified that some leaf slices with yellowing and necrosis symptoms were not colonized by the pathogen. This could be due to the young seedling leaf being more susceptible to the toxins produced by Sarocladium oryzae or to the wounding caused by cutting damage. S. oryzae can produce toxins during rice infection (Bigirimana Vde et al. 2015; Peeters et al. 2020, 2021). Using this inoculation method, the growth time of rice for inoculation can be largely reduced from 11 weeks to 3 weeks, which is important for rice breeding for resistance to sheath rot disease.

In a previous study, the LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM line was shown to be slightly resistant to P. arrhenomanes (Syu et al. 2023). However, the LOC_Os09g23084 OE lines in this study were not resistant to Pythium. One reason for this discrepancy may be the poor root growth ability in the OE lines. Our data indicate that cellulase activity is elevated in the germinated seeds of the OE lines, which may facilitate shoot emergency and elongation but adversely affect root growth. P. arrhenomanes primarily attacks young roots and germinated seeds with longer embryonic roots exhibit greater resistance to Pythium infection (Chun and Schneider 1998; Syu et al. 2023; Toda et al. 2015). Faster-growing roots might have a shorter susceptibility period to the infection of Pythium infection (Chun and Schneider 1998; Syu et al. 2023; Tu et al. 2023). Shorter roots were observed in the seedlings of OE lines, which could contribute to their susceptibility to Pythium infection. It is possible that the constitutive overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 inhibits embryotic root development and then influences the susceptibility of the OE lines to P. arrhenomanes. The overexpression lines exhibit significantly higher levels of LOC_Os09g23084 expression compared to the LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM line (164-Homo in Fig. 1C), which may differentially affect root development processes, such as primary root elongation and cell wall modification, and consequently lead to varying levels of susceptibility to Pythium infection. The function of LOC_Os09g23084 in the slight resistance of the LOC_Os09g23084-activated TRIM line to P. arrhenomanes remains unclear.

Gene silencing or knockout approaches, such as RNA interference (RNAi) or CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing, are widely used for functional characterization of genes. However, several limitations must be considered when applying these techniques. LOC_Os09g23084 is a member of a large gene family, raising the possibility of off-target effects and functional redundancy. In RNAi-based silencing, incomplete knockdown may result in residual gene expression, complicating phenotypic interpretation. Similarly, CRISPR/Cas9 may generate off-target mutations or fail to fully eliminate gene function due to compensatory expression from homologs. In this study, we employed a gene overexpression strategy to investigate the potential function of LOC_Os09g23084. While this approach revealed phenotypic consequences associated with elevated expression, it does not allow us to determine whether LOC_Os09g23084 is strictly required for these phenotypes. Further reverse-genetics analyses, such as precise knockout and complementation studies, are needed to establish its necessity in these biological processes.

Plant susceptibility to pathogen attack can result from multiple biochemical and structural factors. These include impaired pattern-triggered immunity (PTI) due to ineffective recognition of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), weakened effector-triggered immunity (ETI) caused by the absence or dysfunction of resistance (R) genes, disrupted hormonal signaling and inappropriate hormone crosstalk, activation of susceptibility (S) genes, insufficient reinforcement of the cell wall, limited capacity to detoxify pathogen-derived toxins, accumulation of metabolites that favor pathogen proliferation, and suppression of host defense compound biosynthesis and pathogenesis-related (PR) gene expression (Dong and Ronald 2019; Gorshkov and Tsers 2022; Lapin and Van den Ackerveken 2013; Miedes et al. 2014; Van Esse et al. 2020). Additionally, modifications to cell wall structure can affect plant immunity triggered by damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), which include molecules released from the plant cell wall (e.g. wall-derived glycans) and peptides (Bacete et al. 2018; Souza et al. 2017). In the present study, we show that constitutive overexpression of LOC_Os09g23084 significantly increases susceptibility to sheath rot disease in rice. This phenotype is accompanied by developmental alterations, including reduced internode and panicle neck length, diminished protrusion of peripheral vascular bundles, and decreased lignin accumulation, all of which may compromise physical barriers against fungal invasion. Regarding the biochemical analysis, the OE lines exhibited higher cellulase activity in germinated seeds but did not show significantly elevated activity in larger seedling. Variability in the observed total cellulase activity may be attributed to the enzymatic assay method, the developmental stage of the sampled plants, and the complexity of the cellulase gene family in rice. To further elucidate the underlying biochemical mechanisms, additional targeted analyses are warranted. These includes comprehensive analysis of cell wall composition in the flag leaf sheath and first internode, quantification of fungal toxin accumulation at infection sites, assessment of toxin detoxification responses in infected plants, evaluation of antimicrobial compound production, expression profiling of genes involved in cellulose and lignin metabolism as well as defense-related genes, and characterization of immune signaling pathways. Collectively, these investigations may provide mechanistic insight into how LOC_Os09g23084 modulates the rice immune landscape and facilitates fungal pathogenesis.

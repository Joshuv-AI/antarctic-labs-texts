Durable MRD‐Negative Remission With Blinatumomab and Venetoclax in Heavily Pretreated Relapsed/Refractory Philadelphia Chromosome‐Positive Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia
Authors: Mohammad Shahzaib Qadir, Shereen Farooq, Omer Jamy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionTyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) are standard therapy for Philadelphia chromosome‐positive (Ph+) acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL); however, relapsed or refractory disease remains challenging due to resistance driven by ABL kinase domain mutations.MethodsWe report the case of a heavily pretreated patient with relapsed/refractory Ph+ ALL treated with blinatumomab and venetoclax without concurrent TKI therapy.ResultsThe patient achieved complete remission and remains in a deep, durable remission exceeding 1 year.ConclusionThis case highlights a potential kinase‐independent, TKI‐free strategy for relapsed/refractory Ph+ ALL, warranting further investigation.Trial RegistrationThe authors have confirmed clinical trial registration is not needed for this submission

Introduction

Ph+ALL, characterized by the t(9;22) translocation and resulting BCR::ABL1 fusion protein, accounts for approximately 25% of cases of B‐cell precursor ALL. Although historically associated with poor prognosis, incorporation of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), like imatinib and ponatinib, into regimens has yielded complete remission (CR) rates exceeding 90%. Acquired ABL kinase domain mutations, including T315I or F317L, make managing relapsed or refractory (R/R) Ph+ ALL a challenge, highlighting the need for kinase‐independent therapeutic pathways [1, 2, 3].

Blinatumomab, a CD19/CD3 bispecific T‐cell engager (BiTE), has demonstrated antileukemic activity in R/R and minimal residual disease (MRD)‐positive B‐ALL [4]. In Ph+ patients, cohort studies have demonstrated that blinatumomab is an effective salvage option following the failure of second‐generation TKIs [5]. However, its efficacy may be limited by high leukemia burden, an immunosuppressive microenvironment, and T‐cell exhaustion, supporting combination strategies that enhance blinatumomab efficacy [6].

BCL‐2 has emerged as a therapeutic target in B‐ALL. Venetoclax, a selective BCL‐2 inhibitor, restores the intrinsic apoptotic cascade in B‐ALL blasts and has demonstrated promise in combination with blinatumomab in small Phase I/II and retrospective studies for Ph‐negative B‐ALL [7]. However, data regarding this combination in Ph+ ALL remain scarce. To our knowledge, the administration of this combination without a concurrent TKI in heavily pretreated Ph+ patients has not been well described. We present the case of a patient with heavily pretreated R/R Ph+ ALL, harboring an F317L mutation that progressed after multiple therapies, including TKIs, STAMP inhibitors (asciminib), inotuzumab ozogamicin, and two CAR‐T cell treatments, who achieved an MRD‐negative CR with blinatumomab and venetoclax.

Case Presentation

A 25‐year‐old woman was diagnosed with Ph+ ALL in October 2020 during the third trimester of pregnancy. Her white blood cell count was 42,000/cmm with 80% blasts. Cytogenetic evaluation revealed a karyotype of 46, XX, t(9;22)(q34;q11.2) and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) demonstrated a BCR::ABL1 p190 transcript level of 100% on the international scale (IS).

She received induction chemotherapy per the CALGB 10403 protocol without peg‐asparaginase due to pregnancy‐associated thrombotic risk. TKI therapy was initially deferred for fetal safety concerns, but imatinib with prednisone was introduced during induction after risk/benefit discussion due to persistent leukocytosis and circulating blasts. This resulted in a rapid hematologic response and subsequent CR with MRD detected by PCR.

Following delivery, the patient received consolidation therapy of dasatinib with hyperfractionated cyclophosphamide, vincristine, doxorubicin, dexamethasone; methotrexate, cytarabine (HyperCVAD/MA), achieving an MRD‐negative remission. The patient subsequently experienced multiple molecular and hematologic relapses despite sequential TKI therapy, including bosutinib, ponatinib, and asciminib in combination with chemotherapy‐based and inotuzumab‐containing regimens. Mutation testing identified an ABL kinase domain substitution (F317L).

In January 2023, the patient enrolled in a clinical trial of allogeneic CD19‐directed CAR‐T cell therapy, after fludarabine/cyclophosphamide lymphodepletion, achieving morphologic remission with low‐level residual disease detectable by PCR (0.0069% IS), followed by asciminib maintenance. The patient did not experience cytokine release syndrome (CRS), immune effector cell‐associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), or severe infectious complications. There were no prolonged Grade III‐IV cytopenias observed during this treatment. No graft‐versus‐host disease was observed. Subsequently, after an additional relapse in November 2023, the patient underwent a second CD19‐directed CAR‐T therapy with brexucabtagene autoleucel (brexucel), achieving CR with incomplete hematologic recovery (CRi) and PCR MRD negativity by January 2024. This treatment was complicated by Grade 2 CRS, which resolved with standard management and without any dose reduction or interruption; no ICANS or severe infectious complications were observed, and there were no prolonged Grade III‐IV cytopenias. This was followed by asciminib and ponatinib maintenance.

Molecular relapse recurred with rising BCR::ABL1 transcript levels despite asciminib and ponatinib therapy. During ponatinib therapy, the patient developed a stroke, which influenced further treatment decisions. Bone marrow biopsy in November 2024 confirmed R/R Ph+ ALL. Mini‐CVD plus inotuzumab was initially administered, but circulating blasts indicated refractory disease. In December 2024, treatment was changed to venetoclax plus blinatumomab without concurrent TKI therapy. Venetoclax (400 mg) was initiated at the full target dose on Day 1, without any dose escalation, and administered for 14 days per cycle. Blinatumomab was administered as a continuous 28‐day infusion with a standard stepwise dose escalation. Bone marrow evaluation after the first cycle demonstrated persistent disease. Following the second cycle, a bone marrow biopsy in February 2025 demonstrated CRi (MRD‐negative by PCR). The patient experienced Grade 2 CRS with the first cycle, with no subsequent events. Tumor lysis syndrome was not observed with venetoclax. Socioeconomic barriers precluded allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. The patient completed two induction cycles and two consolidation cycles of blinatumomab and venetoclax, achieving and maintaining molecular CR throughout. Following these four total cycles, she transitioned to maintenance therapy, with the combination, every 12 weeks, with undetectable BCR::ABL1 PCR results to date. The blinatumomab treatment regimen was modeled after the TOWER trial schematic [4]. At the last follow‐up in April 2026, she remained in durable molecular remission for over 1 year after starting blinatumomab and venetoclax. During maintenance, the patient did experience Grade 2 neutropenia, one episode of herpes reactivation, and one episode of viral pneumonia. Discussion

We report the case of a young woman with heavily pretreated R/R Ph+ ALL achieving durable MRD‐negative remission using a novel, TKI‐free salvage regimen of blinatumomab and venetoclax. Her disease course was exceptionally refractory, progressing through multiple lines of advanced therapy. To our knowledge, this represents one of the first reported cases demonstrating the use of combined BCL‐2 inhibition and bispecific T‐cell engagement without concurrent tyrosine kinase inhibition in a pretreated Ph+ ALL patient.

The integration of BCR::ABL1‐directed TKI treatment regimens has transformed the therapeutic landscape of Ph+ ALL, with CR rates now exceeding 90% when TKIs are combined with induction therapy [1, 2]. This profound antileukemic effect initially established allo‐HSCT as consolidation in first remission with 5‐year overall survival (OS) rates approaching 50%. In the contemporary era, the role of transplant in first remission is less uniform. In a retrospective analysis of 230 adults achieving complete molecular remission within 90 days, allo‐HSCT in CR1 did not significantly improve overall, although it remained associated with lower relapse risk [8]. Similarly, patients achieving early deep molecular remission with second‐ or third‐generation TKIs may defer transplant, supporting an individualized MRD‐directed approach [2]. Long‐term outcomes have improved, with 5‐year OS rates ranging from 43% to 50% and 5‐year disease‐free survival (DFS) rates between 32% and 52%. However, relapse remains common, largely driven by resistance‐conferring substitutions in the ABL kinase domain [9].

To overcome kinase‐dependent resistance, immunotherapeutic approaches are increasingly utilized in the R/R setting. Blinatumomab bypasses BCR::ABL1 signaling by engaging CD19 on leukemic blasts and redirecting CD3‐positive cytotoxic T cells to induce targeted cell lysis. In a Phase II study of heavily pretreated adults with R/R Ph+ ALL, 92% of patients received more than 3 prior TKIs and 51% had prior exposure to ponatinib; however, none had prior CD19‐directed CAR‐T therapy, and newer agents, such as asciminib or inotuzumab, were not included [3]. In contrast, our patient had received these therapies, highlighting the particularly refractory nature of her disease. Single agent blinatumomab demonstrated a 36% CR rate within the first two cycles, with 88% achieving a complete MRD response. Notably, these responses were achieved independent of BCR::ABL1 mutational status, including the presence of the highly resistant T315I mutation [3, 5]. However, relapse‐free survival with monotherapy can be limited, and blinatumomab is typically combined with TKIs, with studies reporting CR rates up to 80%–90% [1, 2, 5].

In our patient, relapse occurred despite temporary MRD‐negative remissions. The failure of these sequential therapies may suggest significant T‐cell exhaustion within an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. BCL‐2 is frequently overexpressed in leukemic blasts, sequestering proapoptotic factors and promoting resistance to cytotoxic and immune‐mediated cell death [7]. Venetoclax, a selective BCL‐2 inhibitor, restores apoptosis and demonstrates activity in R/R B‐ALL [10]. By lowering the apoptotic threshold of leukemic blasts, venetoclax enhances the cytotoxicity of T‐cell‐based therapies, offering a rationale for combined administration.

The concurrent administration of venetoclax and blinatumomab has emerged as an active, chemotherapy‐free strategy in Ph‐negative B‐ALL. In a retrospective cohort study, this regimen achieved a 92.3% CR rate with MRD negativity after one induction cycle. Residual leukemia cells cleared within 14–21 days, comparing favorably with the 66% CR rate reported with blinatumomab monotherapy in the SWOG 1318 trial [11, 12].

A synergistic mechanism beyond direct apoptosis could be responsible for this efficacy. Translational studies suggest venetoclax alters the tumor microenvironment, reducing regulatory T cells and naive CD8+ T cells, while increasing memory CD8+ T cells. These changes may enhance the cytotoxic activity of blinatumomab by counteracting T‐cell depletion and limiting BiTE activity [13]. Early results from the Phase I GMALL‐BLIVEN trial support this regimen, with median OS of 6.6 months in R/R and 16.9 months in MRD‐positive Ph‐negative B‐ALL [7]. Currently, there are no published data evaluating the use of blinatumomab and venetoclax in Ph‐positive B‐ALL. Our case provides early clinical evidence that this dual‐targeted, immune‐enhancing approach can successfully eradicate multidrug‐resistant, heavily pretreated Ph+ ALL. By bypassing mutated BCR::ABL1 kinase, this novel TKI‐free regimen offers a promising and tolerable salvage strategy, warranting prospective investigation for patients who have exhausted standard targeted and cellular therapies.

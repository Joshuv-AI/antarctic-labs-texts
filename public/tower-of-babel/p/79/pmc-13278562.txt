Mindfulness-based counseling for sexual relational concern in infertile women: A clinical trial protocol
Authors: Romina Fili, Fereshteh Behmanesh, Maryam Nikpour, Angela Hamidia, Sedigheh Esmaeilzadeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Infertility affects the sexual and psychological behavior of women and causes anxiety. Physical infertility treatment without psychological intervention is not successful enough. Mindfulness cognitive behavioral therapy, as the simplest yet most effective type of meditation in the world, can probably improve the level of psychological-sexual components of infertile women, including sexual relational concern. This study will investigate the effectiveness of mindfulness-based counseling in reducing sexual relational concern among infertile women.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This randomized clinical trial will involve 80 infertile women selected through simple sampling from March 2024 to July 2025 in Babol, Iran. If participants meet the inclusion criteria, they will complete demographic and sexual relational concern questionnaire. Then will be randomly assigned to intervention or control groups using randomization with blocks of 4. After a face-to-face counseling session, the intervention group will receive seven sessions of mindfulness-based counseling by telephone at one-week intervals according to Kabat-Zinn’s standard protocol. Participants in the control group will not receive any training. All participants will complete the sexual relational concern questionnaire immediately and one month after the study. Data will be analyzed with SPSS version 22 software using Chi-square, T-test, and repeated-measures ANOVA tests. A significance level of 5% will be used for the tests.CONCLUSION:This study will demonstrate the impact of mindfulness-based counseling on sexual relational concern in infertile women. The positive results of this counseling could be a beneficial solution to reduce the psychological complications of infertility in women, and so, increase the effectiveness and speed of infertility treatment.ETHICS AND DISSEMINATION:This trial protocol has been approved in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT) at 2024-01-20. Registration reference is IRCT20180218038783N8.

Introduction

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), infertility is defined as the absence of pregnancy after 12 months of regular and unprotected sexual intercourse.[1] In a study of National Health Survey, primary and secondary infertility in women worldwide was reported to be approximately between 0.6-3.4% and 8.7-32.6%, respectively (during 1990-2010).[2] As a developing country, Iran has a higher rate of primary infertility than the global average.[3] In a meta-analysis, the prevalence of primary infertility (no children yet) and secondary infertility (infertility after the birth of at least one child) in Iran was 18.3% and 22.5%, respectively.[4]

Infertility is not just a medical problem,[5] but affects every aspect of a couple’s life.[6] It is associated with physical, psychological, and social stress.[7] These people are at high risk of depression[8] anxiety,[9] sexual dysfunction,[3] and sexual dissatisfaction.[10] However, women’s psychosexual behaviors are more likely to be affected by this problem than men’s.[811] Even if the problem of infertility is caused by men, women inflict great physical, mental and financial damage on themselves in the treatment of infertility.[12] Frequent visits to the doctor, planned sexual relationships, and social problems affect their anxiety levels. For example, previous studies have reported the prevalence of anxiety in infertile women in Iran, Saudi Arabia, and Sweden to be 58.1%, 21.8%, and 14.8%, respectively, suggesting that the prevalence of this disorder is higher in infertile women than in general and healthy women.[13]

Increased anxiety during sexual intercourse can lead women to avoid sex, decrease sexual satisfaction, and experience anorgasmia.[14] Also, anxiety is a significant factor in nearly all female sexual disorders. Women’s sexual issues should be addressed beyond just the medical realm, with specialists considering all aspects.[15] Thus, treatment of infertility alone is not enough.[16] Despite the high prevalence of anxiety symptoms in infertile women, psychological treatments are offered to them less frequently, which is particularly concerning.[17] Psychological interventions and counseling should be utilized to successfully treat infertility.[16] In other words, this treatment method increases fertility chances and reduces depression and anxiety.[18] The addition of psychological treatments to infertility treatments increases women’s resistance to the stress and the effectiveness of infertility treatments. It also allows women to undergo infertility treatment with optimal mental health.[1019] Various studies have utilized methods like psychotherapy, interpersonal psychotherapy, mindfulness meditation, drug therapy, and internet therapy to address anxiety.[20]

Mindfulness is a cognitive-behavioral therapy,[21] is a simple yet powerful meditation technique rooted in traditional cultures, defined as non-judgmental moment-to-moment awareness.[22] In other words, this training helps people to be present in the moment without judgment[23] and not to burden themselves with pain or stress due to past or future events.[23] A study confirmed that accepting the present as the main component of mindfulness reduces critical introspection in sexual functioning and the tendency to have harmful thoughts.[24]

Greater mindfulness capacity may reduce distraction, self-criticism, judgment, and anxiety during sex.[25] For example, new evidence suggests that lower levels of mindfulness are associated with higher levels of sexual anxiety.[26] Also, the results of a mindfulness group counseling have been shown to enhance infertile women’s psychological well-being, including acceptance, positive relationships, autonomy, environmental mastery, life meaning, and personal growth.[6] However, there are few counseling interventions for infertile women’s sexual health with a mindfulness approach.[10] In this case, in addition to infertility treatment, the other aspects of infertile women’s lives should be considered, and psychological-sexual health care should be provided for them.[27]

While mindfulness relates to various psychological and sexual aspects in women,[28] no study has specifically examined its effect on sexual relational concern in infertile women. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of individual mindfulness-based telephone counseling, differing from prior face-to-face group interventions.[29] Telephone counseling is a suitable alternative for people who find it difficult to leave the house or take time off work.[30] The telephone is accessible to the general public, which solves time, location, and economic problems.[31] This study’s results can contribute to developing accessible, effective interventions for improving psychological and sexual health in infertile women and help fill gaps in mindfulness-based intervention research.

Materials and Methods

Study status

The sampling started from March 2024 and it is expected to end in August 2025.

Study design

This randomized clinical trial will be conducted in parallel with infertile women referred to the Fatima Al-Zahra Infertility Center in Babol, Iran. The results of this study will be published in databases to inform participants, healthcare professionals, interested individuals, people, and researchers.

Study population

Participants will be eligible infertile women attending the infertility center who meet the inclusion criteria: primary infertility diagnosis, literacy, personal cellphone ownership, age under 50, and a sexual relational concern score above 8. Exclusion criteria include participation in other psychological interventions, husband’s sexual dysfunction, missing two or more counseling sessions, and diagnosed psychiatric illness.

Accounting for a 15% dropout rate, the target sample size was increased to 40 per group.

Participants will be selected through convenience sampling based on eligibility criteria. They will then be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to intervention (Group A) or control (Group B) using block randomization with blocks of four. To achieve a sample size of 80, 20 blocks will be generated. The randomization list will be managed by a researcher independent of the sampling process to prevent prediction of group allocation. After eligibility is confirmed, the sampling researcher will receive the group assignment code from this independent researcher.

Allocation concealment will be ensured using sealed, opaque envelopes containing the random sequence. A total of 60 numbered envelopes will be prepared, each containing a card indicating group assignment. Envelopes will be sealed and stored in a box, and one envelope will be opened sequentially at the time of participant enrollment to determine group allocation. Due to the study design, it will be conducted as an open-label trial.

Intervention

The researcher (midwifery student) will recruit eligible women at Fatima Al-Zahra Infertility Clinic in Babol. The study’s purpose and intervention details will be explained, emphasizing voluntary participation, the right to withdraw at any time, and confidentiality of personal data. Participants will complete the Sexual Relational Concern Questionnaire; those scoring above 8 will be reported to the responsible researcher for group assignment disclosure. Demographic data will then be collected. The intervention group will receive eight weekly mindfulness-based counseling sessions, developed by researchers and a psychiatrist, following the standard Kabat-Zinn protocol.[32] The researcher will be trained under the supervision of a psychiatrist to have the necessary skills to provide counseling based on mindfulness.

The initial counseling session at the fertility clinic will be conducted face-to-face to explain the study objectives, procedures, and methods, including body scan training and the raisin-eating technique. The subsequent seven sessions will be delivered weekly via 30-minute phone calls, each focusing on specific mindfulness exercises. Participants will be encouraged to practice mindfulness in daily life. To enhance data accuracy and support, the researcher will maintain regular communication between sessions through phone calls and social media, providing daily exercises, meditations, and responding to participant inquiries. Measurement and scale

Two demographic and sexual relational concern questionnaires will be used:

Demographic Questionnaire: The research team developed this questionnaire to meet the study’s objectives, which encompass the age, education levels of both spouses, occupations, body mass index, duration of infertility and marriage, and place of residence.Sexual Relational Concern Questionnaire: This questionnaire is one of the five domains of the 30-question Sexual Satisfaction Scale for Women (SSS-W) developed and validated by Meston and Trapnell (2005). Responses are rated on a 5-point Likert scale from 1 to 5 (ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree). Items are summed to produce a total score, ranging from 30 to 150, with higher scores reflecting greater sexual satisfaction. Each subscale scores between 6 and 30. Relational concern comprises 6 items. Both the total scale and subscales demonstrate strong reliability, temporal stability, and valid concurrent, convergent, and divergent measures. Cronbach’s alpha for the SSS-W total scales was 0.94, while it was 0.88 for the Relational Concern domain. The highest test-retest reliability was seen among sexually dysfunctional women for the Relational Concern domain (r = 0.80).[33]

Confidentiality and data protection

Telephone counseling sessions will not be recorded, and data will be coded without personal identifiers and stored in a password-protected database accessible only to the research team. Confidentiality measures will be explained in the informed consent form prior to participation.

Data analysis

The analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle to maintain randomization benefits. To reduce dropout, reminder calls/messages will be sent before each counseling session. If a participant misses a call, contact will be reattempted within 48 hours to reschedule. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation under the MAR assumption, with sensitivity analyses to assess result robustness. Analyses will be conducted in SPSS v22 with significance set at P < 0.05. Descriptive statistics will include means ± SD or frequencies and percentages. Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests will compare qualitative variables, while independent t-tests will assess quantitative variables between groups. repeated-measures ANOVA, controlling for baseline measures, will compare post-intervention sexual relational concern means between and within groups; time effects will be analyzed via ANOVA. Effect size will be reported using mean differences and 95% confidence intervals.

Ethics and dissemination

The ethics code of this study was issued by the research ethics committee of Babol University of Medical Sciences (Code: IR.MUBABOL.REC.1402.141). All study procedures will be performed according to the guidelines, and participants will receive written informed consent before the study.

Discussion

This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of mindfulness-based telephone counseling on sexual relational concern in infertile women. Anxiety is a key emotional consequence of infertility,[6] and Women are more affected in terms of psychological and social aspects. And following this injury, they experience more violence, stress, depression, anxiety, and low self-esteem.[9] Psychological interventions alongside infertility treatments enhance mental health and treatment adherence in infertile women.[8] Considering that sexual relational concern is one of the most common psychological problems in Iran[35] and infertile patients have a significant risk of anxiety, the present study will answer this question: “Does mindfulness counseling based on telephone effective in reducing sexual relational concern in infertile women?”. Mindfulness helps people learn to be in the moment without judgment.[36] A study of mindfulness-based sex therapy stated that methods that can focus people’s minds on sex can be effective in improving and treating sexual disorders.[37] A researcher reported that mindfulness-based interventions reduce symptoms of stress, anxiety, and depression in infertile women.[9] However, few studies have examined the effect of mindfulness-based counseling interventions on the sexual health of infertile women.[10] Therefore, we decided to investigate the effectiveness of counseling intervention based on mindfulness in infertile women over the phone. To provide the possibility of individual counseling, there are no problems of time and place,[31] and people who find it difficult to leave the house for any reason and take time off from work can also participate in the study.[30]

One of the main strengths of this study is the telephone-based delivery of counseling, which allows for flexible, individualized sessions without the constraints of time or location. The personalized nature of the intervention is expected to enhance participant engagement and the quality of service. Several limitations exist. First, establishing trust for disclosing sensitive sexual concerns may be difficult in Iran’s cultural context, where stigma may cause underreporting or biased responses. Counselors will use a non-judgmental approach and ensure confidentiality to mitigate this. Second, while mindfulness interventions generally improve psychological well-being, evidence on their effect on sexual and relational issues is mixed, possibly due to individual, cultural, and fidelity differences. Thus, results should be interpreted cautiously within the study’s cultural and methodological context.

This study suggests mindfulness-based counseling may reduce sexual relational concerns in infertile women, potentially alleviating psychological complications and enhancing infertility treatment outcomes. Future research should compare telephone versus face-to-face counseling and examine effects on other health aspects.

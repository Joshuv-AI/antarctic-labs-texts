Prediction of Coal Ash Flow Temperature Based on Gray Relational Analysis, Support Vector Regression and Genetic Algorithm
Authors: Kaidi Sun, Zhen Liu, Haiquan An, Baozi Peng, Lingmei Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoal ash flow temperature significantly influences the operating conditions of entrained flow bed gasification. The relationship between the flow temperature of coal ash and its chemical composition remains uncertain despite being determined by it. To construct a reliable and accurate predictive method, machine learning models were used, and different support vector regression models were built to predict the flow temperature. The prediction results of the proposed gray relational analysis–genetic algorithm–support vector regression model can achieve high accuracy, with a root-mean-square error of 28.37, mean absolute error of 19.48K, and average deviation of 1.58%. Moreover, the prediction results of the proposed model are more accurate and efficient than those calculated by FactSage software, with a mean absolute error of 93.73K. This demonstrates the viability of applying the proposed machine learning model to predict the flow temperature of coal ash and its promising potential application in the area of coal chemical engineering.

Introduction

Entrained flow gasification is the mainstream coal gasification technology at present. It has a large single furnace capacity, high carbon conversion rate, high effective gas content, and good coal adaptability. Liquid slagging is adopted in entrained-flow gasifiers and discharging slag smoothly from the gasifier is crucial to ensure long-term stable operation of the gasification unit.1,2 In this case, The operation temperature of the furnace should be controlled 50–100 K higher than the flow temperature (FT) of coal ash. Also, the operation temperature cannot be too high, which will lead to an increase of oxygen and coal consumption, a reduction of active components of syngas, and an acceleration of the erosion of refractory bricks. In this case, coal with FT in a certain range should be utilized as feed coal in the gasification unit to ensure the stable operation of the gasifier.

The experimental method to measure FT3 is time-consuming and normally difficult to test based on a daily routine. If the quality of coal is unstable, the fluctuation of FT can only be noticed after raw coal is put into the gasifier, which may lead to unstable operation. Therefore, it is important to develop a more rapid detection or prediction method for FT. Since the chemical composition of coal ash affects ash fusion temperature significantly, and the measurement method for the main composition and content of coal ash3 is quick and mature, developing a prediction model between FT and coal ash components is promising.

Some research has been carried out on the prediction of ash fusion temperature, both thermodynamic models and data-driven models were developed recently. Sasi et al.4 collected data from approximately 300 coal samples worldwide. A thermodynamic model for FT based on FactSage calculations and an artificial neural network (ANN) model were developed with root-mean-square error (RMSE) values of 86.4 and 54.4, respectively. Modeling software FactSage is widely applied in ash fusion behavior analysis.5 Li et al.6 proposed an FT prediction formula based on the temperature of 90% liquid phase (T90), which is calculated by FactSage. Ten coal ash samples were used to verify the accuracy of the prediction model, and the standard deviation is 31.15 K. Shi et al.7 presented a thermodynamic model to predict FT for ash samples with chemical composition in the range of 70% ≤ SiO2 + Al2O3 ≤ 77% and 1.5 ≤ SiO2/Al2O3 ≤ 2.5, and this model can be used when FactSage is unavailable. Liu et al.8 developed an FT prediction model using an ant colony optimization–back-propagation (ACO-BP) neural network, and 80 coal ash samples were used for training and testing, achieving an average prediction error of 5.16%. Liang et al.3 applied a BP neural network to predict coal ash deformation temperature (DT) based on 28 coal samples, and the maximum error was 75 K. To predict the FT of biomass ashes, Sakiewicz et al.9 proposed a neural network model, the mean absolute error (MAE) of which was in the range of 46.388 to 102.821 K. Xiao et al.10 developed a support-vector regression (SVR) model optimized with the gray-wolf algorithm to predict DT. The model was trained and tested using 58 samples, which were prepared by mixing. For the testing set, the model achieved an average relative error of 2.94% and a maximum relative error of 10.25%.

In summary, most thermodynamic models rely on the FactSage software. For data-driven models, neural network models lack interpretability, and it is necessary to have a large enough training set to prevent overfitting. SVR models can avoid these problems. Therefore, developing an SVR model for FT that can accommodate diverse coal origins is necessary. Furthermore, for a typical machine learning model, major chemical compositions (Fe2O3, SiO2, Al2O3, CaO, Na2O, K2O, MgO, SO3) were applied as inputs.4 It is also important to consider combination parameters based on these chemical compositions, such as the silica–alumina ratio and acid–base ratio, as additional inputs for the model.

In this research, a robust SVR model for predicting FT was developed using almost 200 coal samples originating from 32 coal mines, distributed in two provinces of China, Shaanxi and Inner Mongolia. The gray relationship analysis (GRA) method was adopted to determine the inputs of the SVR model, and Genetic Algorithm (GA) was used for parameter tuning. By combining GRA, GA, and SVR, a new prediction model, termed the GRA-GA-SVR model, was proposed for the prediction of ash flow temperatures. The developed model was analyzed and discussed. Furthermore, both the experimental approach and thermodynamic approach based on FactSage calculation were applied to validate the proposed GRA-GA-SVR model.

Experimental Section

Ash Sample Preparation and Composition Analysis

185 coal samples obtained from 32 coal mines distributed in Shaanxi (102 coal samples) and Inner Mongolia (83 coal samples) were used in this research. All coal samples were crushed and ground to fine powders with sizes smaller than 75 μm. The prepared coal samples were then treated at 815 °C in a muffle furnace according to the Chinese national standard GB/T 1574–2007 to obtain ash samples.

Chemical compositions of ash samples were then examined by X-ray fluorescence (XRF, Bruker S8 Tiger, Germany) spectrometry. The total mass fractions of the components (Fe2O3, SiO2, Al2O3, CaO, Na2O, K2O, MgO, SO3, TiO2, and P2O5) for all ash samples exceeded 95%.

Ash Fusion Temperature Tests

Ash fusion temperatures (AFTs) of ash samples were determined by the Carbolite CAF 1600 ash fusion point meter (Carbolite Gero, Britain) according to the American standard ASTM D1857–04. For each sample, the pyramid ash cone was heated to 1560 °C with a heating rate of 8 K/min under a mild reducing atmosphere (VCO/VCO2 = 3/2). Characteristic temperatures including deformation temperature (DT), softening temperature (ST), hemisphere temperature (HT), and flow temperature (FT) were obtained based on the shape of the ash cone.11

Support Vector Regression algorithm

In this research, the SVR algorithm15,16 was used to solve the regression problem. SVR algorithm is based on Vapnik Chervonenkis dimensional theory and structural risk minimization principle,17 it is suitable to solve regression problems for small sample sets.18 In the proposed prediction model, the radial basis function was applied as the kernel function. The input factors for the model were determined through GRA, while the output factor was FT.

There are three tunable parameters applied in the SVR model that need to be determined and optimized. The first parameter is penalty factor C, which is the tolerance for error. The value of C balances the empirical risk and structural risk. If the value of C is too high, the structural risk is greater, and it is easier to cause model overfitting. If the value of C is too small, low model complexity may lead to underfitting. The second parameter is gamma, which is the parameter of the radial basis function. The value of gamma influences the speed of training and prediction. The third parameter is epsilon (ϵ), and its value determines the width of the tube around the estimated function or hyperplane.19 These tunable parameters were optimized by a genetic algorithm, which will be introduced in the next section. Additionally, the performance of the developed model was evaluated using mean squared error (MSE), root-mean-square error, mean absolute error, and coefficient of determination (R2). Genetic Algorithm (GA)

GA is based on the concept of natural evolution, which is expressed in the form of a computer simulation method and applied to solve optimization problems in global scope.13,20,21 In this research, genetic algorithm was used to optimize tunable parameters in the SVR model. Each parameter was considered as gene, and the value of parameter was known as allele. Any combination of parameters was regarded as a chromosome. The chromosome was composed of three parameters, including C, gamma, and ϵ. The population consists of several chromosomes. The genetic algorithm method was conducted by following steps.

For the genetic algorithm optimization process, the first step was population initialization, and the initial population size was set as 10. The second step was fitness evaluation, and fitness values were calculated by eq 8, which are the values of MSE of developed model. A lower MSE value indicated better fitness. The third step was the selection operation. When a chromosome led to a better fitness value, that is, a set of SVR model parameter led to a lower MSE value, this parameter set had greater probability to be selected and employed in the next generation. In this research, the expected value selection method was applied to select good individuals. The fourth step was the crossover operation. Two parent chromosomes were randomly selected, and a new chromosome was generated by recombination or exchanging genes of parent chromosomes. In this step, crossover rate was set as 0.9, with utilization of the single-point crossover method. The fifth step was the mutation operation. This step can increase the diversity of the chromosome and avoid optimization process trapping in a local optimum solution. Here, the single-point mutation method was used and the mutation rate was set as 0.1. The sixth step was algorithm termination. The algorithm could be terminated when the threshold value was met. In this research, the generation number was set as 20, and the chromosome with the highest fitness value was determined as the globally optimal solution.

Thermodynamic Calculations

FactSage thermodynamic software was used to simulate phase equilibrium, calculate propotions of solid phase and liquid phase, and predict FT. Major ash compositions obtained from XRF analysis were applied as inputs, including contents of SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3, CaO, MgO, Na2O, K2O, SO3 and TiO2. Thermodynamic phase equilibrium was conducted by using databases FToxid and FactPS. Calculations were performed under reducing conditions, 60% CO and 40% CO2, which is consistent with the atmosphere of ash fusion tests. The pressure for the calculation was 1 atm. The temperature range for calculation was 800 to 1600 °C, with interval of 20 °C.

Results Analysis and Discussion

In this work, the raw experimental data, including ash compositions and FT values, were collected as introduced in Section 2. A new set of factors, including ash compositions and their combinations, was generated, and GRA was then utilized for factor evaluation. The new set of factors with higher GRG was considered as a better representation of the input data. Standard normalization was exploited to scale the input data into a specific data range. The processed data was later divided into training and testing data sets. During the training process, the GA method was first initialized with a start condition and then optimized for several iterations to generate the best parameters for the SVR model. Finally, the testing data set was used to validate the effectiveness of the entire process. The detailed steps of the proposed method are listed in Figure 2.

Experiment Setting

All experiments were conducted on a Windows 10 system PC equipped with an Intel Core i5–8250U CPU and 8G RAM. Python 3.10 was selected as the programming environment. For data segmentation, an 80/20 split was exploited for training and testing. To ensure reproducibility, the random seed was set as 42.

As shown in Figure 5, the FTprediction obtained by GRA8 and ASH8 models exhibit similar trend with FTtest. For the GRA8 model and ASH8 model, the maximum difference between FTprediction and FTtest is −106.88 and −107.03 K, and the corresponding maximum deviation rates are 8.15 and 8.16%, respectively. For the remaining 36 samples in the testing set, the maximum differences between FTprediction and FTtest are 51.49 (GRA8) and 63.44 K (ASH8), and the corresponding deviation rates are 4.32 (GRA8) and 5.49% (ASH8). Meanwhile, the MSE, RMSE, and MAE for the GRA8 model are 804.87, 28.37, and 19.48, which are obviously lower than that of the ASH8 model. Moreover, the R2 for the GRA8 model is 0.68, which is higher than the R2 value of 0.6 for the ASH8 model. These results indicate that both the GRA8 model and ASH8 models can accurately predict the FT of coal ash. However, the GRA8 model demonstrates superior performance, thereby validating the effectiveness of the proposed GRA-GA-SVR method shown in Figure 1.

For the GRA8 model, the input factors consist of R250, dolomite ratio, S/A, TiO2 %, acid %, SiO2 % + Al2O3 %, SiO2 %, and Al2O3 %. Also, for the ASH8 model, the input factors are SiO2 %, Al2O3 %, Fe2O3 %, CaO %, MgO %, SO3 %, TiO2 %, and Na2O % + K2O %. When comparing the input factors of the two models, in the GRA8 model, the influences of Na2O %, K2O %, and SO3 % were excluded. Meanwhile, the effect of SiO2 % and Al2O3 % and their combined parameters were emphasized during modeling. The performance of the GRA8 model is better, which indicates its input factors exhibit a stronger impact on FT values. This conclusion is consistent with previous research that FT decreases with increasing S/A;24 a high amount of SiO2 contributes to higher FT.25

Conclusions

In this research, two SVR models and the FactSage calculation method were applied to predict the FT of coal ash. The relationship between the ash compositions and FT values was analyzed. The FactSage calculation method resulted in a maximum deviation of 18.64% in the FT prediction. Both SVR models were capable of accurately predicting FT values, with the GRA8-GA-SVR model exhibiting superior performance and more precise prediction results, including an RMSE of 28.37, MAE of 19.48, and average deviation of 1.58%. The proposed GRA8-GA-SVR model, which combines gray relational analysis, genetic algorithm, and support vector regression, is a promising method to achieve rapidly predicting the FT of coal ash. This prediction approach has wide applications, such as in the coal blending industry, where it can guide the blending and production of commercial coal for gasification. Moreover, the models can also be adopted in the coal chemical industry to analyze the compatibility between the coal feed and gasifier to ensure stable operation. Furthermore, these data-driven models can be integrated into software platforms, such as coal quality databases and coal quality management platforms. The prediction model will be updated as the data in the database grows, thereby enabling the intelligent operation of coal chemical systems. However, this work has its limitations, including a restricted data set and sole reliance on SVR models. Future research can address these limitations by expending the data set to improve the prediction accuracy and generalization ability of the model. Additionally, exploring other machine learning models and ensemble learning methods could further enhance the prediction capabilities of the model.

Efficacy of the SUPERvised PREhabilitation with Personalised, Aerobic training, Respiratory muscle training and EDucation (SUPER-PREPARED) multi-model in patients with abdominal surgery: protocol for a single-blind, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Jiao Wang, Yuekong Hu, Xiaojie Ju, Yonghong Yang, Hongyu Chen, Yonghua Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGlobally, millions of abdominal surgeries are performed annually, placing a substantial burden on public health systems worldwide. Postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) are common negative consequences after major abdominal surgery that can worsen recovery outcomes. Although the effect of prehabilitation has been frequently investigated in this population, the feasibility and efficacy of delivering a supervised, intensive exercise programme within a limited preoperative period remain underexplored.This randomised controlled trial (RCT) aims to evaluate the effectiveness of the SUPERvised PREhabilitation with Personalised, Aerobic training, Respiratory muscle training and patient EDucation (SUPER-PREPARED) multi-model to reduce the incidence of PPCs among the population undergoing abdominal surgery.Methods and analysisThe SUPER-PREPARED trial will be a single-centre, prospective, two-arm RCT. A total of 120 participants will be recruited and randomly allocated to either the SUPER-PREPARED group or the usual care group. The primary outcome is the incidence of PPCs, assessed at specific time points (1 day before discharge and 1 month, 6 months and 1 year after surgery). The secondary outcomes include patient-centred measures (functional capacity, quality of life), clinical measures (respiratory muscle function, pulmonary function) and postoperative recovery metrics (postoperative intestinal ventilation timing, length of intensive care unit (ICU) stay, length of postoperative hospital stay, postoperative hospitalisation expenses and readmission rate). The detailed criteria defining PPCs and the schedule of the study are displayed in tables. Data analysis will be independently conducted by two experienced clinicians via the per-protocol analysis.Ethics and disseminationThis study will be conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2024). The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee on Biomedical Research, West China Hospital of Sichuan University (2024 nianshen No. 2049). The final results will be disseminated by peer-reviewed journals and presentations at academic conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500102615.Keywords: REHABILITATION MEDICINE, Pancreatic surgery, Hepatobiliary surgery, Clinical Protocols

Introduction

Abdominal surgeries are the most commonly performed procedures in North American hospitals, placing a significant burden on healthcare resources.1 2 The number of these surgeries is reflected in the increasing incidence of related conditions. For example, the global annual number of pancreatic cancer diagnoses has multiplied over the past two decades and was projected to exceed 490 000 in 2020.3 4 In China alone, in 2017, 83 600 new cases were reported, with an incidence rate of 5.92 per 100 000.5 Pancreatic cancer is a malignancy with a particularly poor prognosis, featuring a 5-year survival rate of less than 10%.6

Surgical resection is an effective treatment for many abdominal tumours, including pancreatic tumours, hepatic carcinomas and duodenal neoplasms.7 8 Although advances in surgical techniques and preoperative care have significantly reduced postoperative mortality,9 the rate of postoperative complications remains high, ranging from 30% to 73%.10 11 PPCs are notably common after abdominal surgery12 and can lead to respiratory failure or death, severely impacting patients’ survival and quality of life.13 Advanced age (>75 years) is known as an independent risk factor for complications following pancreatic resection.14 Other predictors of poor prognosis include pre-existing cardiopulmonary diseases, diminished cardiopulmonary function and respiratory muscle weakness.15 Furthermore, postoperative factors such as residual effects after anaesthesia, respiratory muscle disuse from mechanical ventilation and incision pain, which inhibit phrenic nerve function, can impair respiratory muscle contraction. This impairment increases the risk of adverse outcomes such as pneumonia and impaired airway clearance.

Prehabilitation, a key component of perioperative management, offers a non-invasive intervention to enhance functional capacity and reduce postoperative complications. Core strategies include aerobic training, resistance exercises and respiratory muscle training, which have been shown to significantly reduce pulmonary complications. Systematic reviews report associated benefits, including higher peak oxygen uptake (+2.0 mL/kg/min, 99% CI 0.3 to 3.7), greater maximum inspiratory pressure (WMD +12.2 cmH₂O, 99% CI 6.3 to 18.2) and a shorter hospital stay (WMD −2.3 days, 99% CI −3.82 to −0.75).16 Other studies have confirmed that prehabilitation improves 6 min walking distance (6MWD), health-related quality of life and anxiety17 while reducing the incidence of PPCs,18 19 overall complications20 and morbidity.19

However, existing prehabilitation programmes for abdominal surgery often prescribe lengthy protocols (eg, 4–6 weeks). Within the specific context of the Chinese healthcare system, such extended durations are often impractical and hinder wider implementation. Therefore, our study aims to implement and evaluate the effectiveness of a personalised, intensive prehabilitation multi-model for patients awaiting abdominal surgery. We sought to determine its feasibility and efficacy in reducing PPCs and improving relevant postoperative outcomes.

Methods and analysis

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee on Biomedical Research, West China Hospital of Sichuan University. This study protocol describes the SUPERvised PREhabilitation with Personalised, Aerobic training, Respiratory muscle training and patient EDucation (SUPER-PREPARED) RCT following the instructions of the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) Checklist (online supplemental file 1).

Study design and setting

This study, which is estimated to be implemented between June 2025 and December 2026, is a single-blinded randomised controlled trial and will enrol 120 patients awaiting abdominal surgery. Eligible participants will be allocated randomly to two arms: (i) the SUPER-PREPARED group, which will conduct an intensive personalised prehabilitation programme (aerobic training, respiratory muscle training and patient education) preoperatively and (ii) the usual care group, which will receive the standard patient care. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients aged between 18 and 85 years old and scheduled for abdominal surgery who agree to the study are eligible for inclusion. Participants with any contraindications for exercise training will be excluded, including severe arrhythmia, active haemorrhage, unstable angina, acute myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, acute endocarditis, acute pulmonary embolism, uncontrolled hypertension, asthma, arterial aneurysm or dissection, unstable fracture, fever (>38℃) and untreated pneumothorax. Individuals with cognitive impairments, disturbance of consciousness and inability to cooperate will not be eligible for the study. Patients will be excluded if they experience severe surgical complications, including massive haemorrhage (>800 mL), sharp fluctuations in blood pressure during the operation or prolonged surgical duration (>5 hours).

Sample size calculation and statistical determination

According to previous studies, the incidence of PPCs following abdominal surgery ranges from 10% to 50%. This rate decreases to 12%–20% for patients who participate in prehabilitation programmes.12 16 18 We therefore assume an incidence rate of 20% for the SUPER-PREPARED group and 50% for the usual care group. Using PASS software (version 15.0), a sample size of 60 participants per group was calculated to detect this difference with 90% power and a 95% one-sided CI. Allowing for a 20% dropout rate, we will enrol a total of 120 participants.

We will analyse all the data via SPSS (version 25.0). Initially, the normal distribution and homogeneity test of variance will be performed, and repeated-measures analysis (ANOVA) will be used for consistent data with normal distribution. The data will be analysed by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test between each group if a normal distribution is not conformed to. Mann-Whitney test analysis will be performed for each outcome between two different groups. The non-parametric test will be performed by the Friedman test. A p-value of less than 0.05 will be considered as a statistical significance.

Recruitment

The SUPER-PREPARED RCT will recruit 120 participants from both groups in total. Eligibility identification will be conducted at the outpatient clinic or the inpatient room at the Department of General Surgery, West China Tianfu Hospital, Sichuan University, Chengdu, China. A professional surgical doctor from the multidisciplinary team will explain the study to eligible participants, and a written informed consent for participation will be signed after their agreement.

Randomisation and blinding

Patients will be randomised at a 1:1 ratio to either the SUPER-PREPARED group or the usual care group via a computer-generated randomisation list. Patient allocation and assignment will be concealed from investigators using sealed envelopes generated according to the recruitment order by an experienced surgeon. Owing to the nature of the intervention, it is impossible to blind participants, nurses and other care providers. However, radiologists, nutritionists and sonographers who perform necessary examinations will not be informed of the group allocation. Additionally, allocation will continue to be concealed for investigators throughout the data analysis procedure. Revealing will be permitted if emergent treatments are required following adverse events.

The SUPER-PREPARED group

The exercise intervention (table 1) consists of two to three intensive preoperative training sessions combined with personalised aerobic training and respiratory muscle training under the supervision of physiotherapists. Before training parameters are prescribed, functional capacity (6MWD), pulmonary function (FEV1, FEV1%, FEV1/FVC, FVC, PEF, MVV), maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) and maximal expiratory pressure (MEP) will be assessed by an experienced physiotherapist who is specialised in exercise prescription and respiratory physiotherapy.

Aerobic training will be performed via a bicycle ergometer in the inpatient room at the Department of General Surgery in West China Tianfu Hospital, Sichuan University. The workload for training will be calculated based on the 6MWD and patient height using the formula (predicted maximal workload=0.122×6MWD+72.683×height(m)−117.109).21 Initially, 40% of the predicted maximal workload will be performed, and it will progress to 60% by 10% increment of workload in each session. Every 5 min, the training will pause for 1 min considering weak physical fitness among specific participants, who are commonly in poor nutritional status. Apart from the pause for resting, the duration of aerobic training will be conducted for 20 min.

A tapered flow resistance device (Blue Whale, Xeek, Xiamen, China) will be implemented as the tool for respiratory muscle assessment and training. Before training, the individual MIP will be initially assessed, and the training load will start intensively from 60% to 80% of the MIP with an increment of 10% MIP per session in the sitting position. In total, 30 inhalations will be asked to complete with effort followed by a 1-min interval for recovery after every 10 inhalations.

In addition to the standard patient education (pain management, breathing exercises, early mobilisation), the physiotherapist-dominated education will be initiated after group allocation. A physiotherapist will contact participants and educate them by offering a quick response code or delivering a leaflet, including the information about specific abdominal diseases, the importance of prehabilitation, the precautions about the surgery, the step-by-step schedule of early mobilisation after surgery and airway clearance techniques (active cycle of breathing techniques).

The usual care group

Apart from any exercise training, the usual care group will receive only the same assessments as the SUPER-PREPARED group. However, both groups will be given the same standard patient education and perioperative management after admission. Data collection

Patient demographics will be collected after admission, and the surgical approach will be supplemented from medical records before discharge for further analysis (table 2). Assessment will be assessed at three specific time points, namely after admission, 1 day before surgery and 1 day before discharge. Follow-ups will be continued by performing clinical assessments and collecting questionnaires at 1 month, 6 months and 1 year after surgery by a physiotherapist. If necessary, additional assistance or consultation will be provided from the doctor after the study is completed. All the data will be recorded and kept by an independent practitioner who is blinded to the group allocation. A surgeon and a physiotherapist, who are both independent from any competing interests, will monitor and analyse the collected data to ensure the accuracy at relevant timepoints (table 2).

The primary outcome is the incidence of PPCs at specific timepoints (1 day before discharge, 1 month, 6 months and 1 year after surgery). Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes, assessed at the timepoints detailed in table 2, include clinical measures, patient-centred outcomes and postoperative recovery metrics, with further details as follows:

Respiratory muscle function

The function of respiratory muscles will be measured through assessing the MIP and the MEP.

Functional capacity

The 6 min walk test (6MWT) will be performed to detect the functional capacity of the participants.

Pulmonary function

The following parameters will be assessed: forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1), FEV1% predicted (FEV1%pred), forced vital capacity (FVC), the ratio between FEV1 and FVC (FEV1/FVC), peak expiratory flow (PEF) and maximal voluntary ventilation (MVV).

Quality of life

The health-related quality of life will be assessed by the short form-36 (SF-36) health survey and the European organisation for research and treatment of cancer quality of life questionnaire-core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30).

Length of ICU stay

The length of stay (in days) in the ICU will be recorded.

Length of postoperative hospital stay

The length of hospital stay (in days) after surgery will be recorded.

Postoperative intestinal ventilation timing

The time to flatulence (in hours) will be recorded as hours after surgery.

Postoperative hospitalisation expenses

The overall medical cost after surgery will be acquired through the medical system manually.

Readmission rate

The readmission rate will be collected throughout the follow-up period by recording the main reason.

Adverse events

Adverse events will include severe training-induced discomforts that necessitate additional treatment or cessation. These include chest tightness, severe dizziness and shortness of breath, rapid heart rate (>160 bpm) and leg discomfort (inability to continue training after an interval for recovery).

In this study, if patients’ condition deteriorated or if any unexpected condition occurred, the doctor and the physiotherapist will re-evaluate their ability whether to continue the trial according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and if necessary, patients will be referred to the corresponding specialist for further treatments.

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee on Biomedical Research, West China Hospital of Sichuan University. This trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study design and implementation adhere to the extension of the SPIRIT 2013 statement for clinical trial protocols,22 and the results will be reported following the CONSORT statement.23 Any significant protocol modifications will be communicated to all relevant parties, including the supervisory committees and trial registries. All the information will be used only for conducting the trial and reporting the results and will not be shared with any third parties.

Discussion

Herein, we introduce the SUPER-PREPARED trial, a single-centre, multimodal clinical trial conducted by a multidisciplinary team of abdominal surgeons and physiotherapists in China.

PPCs following major abdominal surgery occur in approximately 30% of patients.24,27 These complications increase morbidity, mortality, length of postoperative hospital stay and healthcare costs. Prophylactic prehabilitation is a vital strategy for reducing PPC risk. A recent meta-analysis confirmed that preoperative physiotherapy reduced the odds of developing PPCs by 47%, particularly among high-risk patients (eg, those who smoke, are aged≤ 45 years, have an abnormal body weight, have multiple comorbidities or are undergoing bariatric or upper gastrointestinal surgery).28 This meta-analysis, which included 800 patients from two randomised trials (the LIPPSMAck POP trial and the Swedish trial), provided preoperative breathing exercises and patient education to an intervention group. A 33% lower risk of PPCs was reported in patients receiving prehabilitation.28 However, implementing a longer duration of prehabilitation programme before abdominal surgery is challenging within the Chinese healthcare context because of factors such as limited medical resources and health insurance coverage. Furthermore, few studies have explored the effectiveness of intensive prehabilitation delivered within a short preoperative period (ie, less than 1 week).16 29 30

Therefore, we innovated the SUPER-PREPARED pathway to minimise PPC risk, enhance recovery and improve postoperative quality of life via an approach suited to the current medical situation in China. We hypothesise that the incidence of PPCs will be significantly lower in the SUPER-PREPARED group than in the usual care group because of the comprehensive bundle of interventions. We also expect the secondary outcomes to be improved through enhanced physical fitness and preoperative education.

If our hypotheses are supported, this study will be novel in China as it is an effective, multimodal bundle combining supervised, intensive aerobic training, respiratory muscle training and physiotherapist-dominated patient education. These findings will offer valuable evidence for implementing this strategy in the clinical practice, even with limited preoperative preparation.

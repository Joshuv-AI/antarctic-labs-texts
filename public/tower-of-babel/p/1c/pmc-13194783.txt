A broadband Terahertz metamaterial absorber enabled by a water-assisted square-pyramid architecture with optical transmittance
Authors: Yuxin Zhao, Peng Hu, Xiexuan Zhang, Qiang Li, Tao Xie, Yuquan Yuan, Xiaoxiao Wu, Fang Ling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTerahertz (THz) absorbers with broad bandwidth and high absorption are significant interest for applications in electromagnetic shielding and photonic devices. In this work, we propose a broadband THz metamaterial absorber consists of square-pyramid structure with optical transmittance, composed of liquid water, polyethylene terephthalate, and indium tin oxide. The continuously varying square-pyramid configuration promotes multiple reflections and electromagnetic field superposition, in combination with the strong intrinsic THz absorption of water, which leads to efficient THz absorption. As a result, the proposed absorber achieves absorption exceeding 90% from 0.5 to 10 THz, over 95% from 0.65 THz to 10 THz, and over 99% from 1.2 THz to 10 THz. In addition, the absorber exhibits polarization-insensitive behavior and maintains a broadband absorption bandwidth of 7 THz with absorptance above 90% for incident angles up to 70°. These features highlight the proposed design as a promising platform for broadband THz absorption in practical applications where visible-band optical transmittance is desirable.Keywords: Terahertz metamaterial absorber, Broadband, Square-pyramid structure, WaterSubject terms: Materials science, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Terahertz (THz) waves, covering 0.1–10 THz, possess unique electromagnetic characteristics that have attracted increasing interest in recent years, driving the rapid development of THz technologies. In particular, the advancement of THz radar and communication systems has created a growing demand for high-performance THz absorbers. To suppress undesirable THz radiation, artificially structured electromagnetic metamaterials (MMs) have been extensively investigated since the proposal of the perfect metamaterial absorber by Landy et al.in 20081. Although numerous absorber designs have demonstrated high absorptance, their practical implementation is often limited by narrow operational bandwidths2–4 or complex structural configurations5–7. Notably, most reported THz absorbers rely on electronically conducting materials8–12, as electronic conduction loss is generally considered the dominant dissipation mechanism in the THz regime. However, such materials typically exhibit strong absorption or reflection in the visible spectral range, limiting their use in applications requiring optical transparency, such as aircraft windows or optical display systems.

To address this limitation, researchers have developed THz metamaterial absorbers that incorporate electronically conducting films while maintaining visible-band optical transmittance13–16. Among these materials, indium tin oxide (ITO) has been widely used due to its favorable optoelectronic properties and mature fabrication technology. Nevertheless, many ITO-based THz absorbers still suffer from narrow absorption bandwidths17–19, degraded performance under oblique incident20,21, or complex fabrication processes22,23. In 2023, an optically transmissive absorber composed of ITO, water, and polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) was reported, achieving absorption over 90% over the frequency range of 0.26–30.26 THz24. In the same year, an all-dielectric, three-dimensional printed THz absorber exhibiting optical transmittance was experimentally demonstrated, showing absorptance above 80% from 0.31 THz to 1.5 THz25. In addition, pyramid-based THz absorbers have also been explored, however the previous studies have primarily focused on flat-topped pyramid geometries rather than pointed, continuously scaled structures26–28. Our previous work indicated that continuously scaled geometries can effectively enhance broadband absorption, although its incident angle was not investigated. Despite these advances, the realization of THz broadband absorbers that simultaneously achieve ultra-broadband absorption, optically transmittance, and wide-incident angle robustness remains challenging.

In this work, we propose a THz metamaterial absorber based on a water-filled square-pyramid structure. The absorber is composed of liquid water, polyethylene terephthalate (PET), and ITO, thus the absorber is optical transmittance. The pointed, continuous scaled pyramid geometry enhances electromagnetic field trapping through multiple reflection, while the strong intrinsic THz absorption of water provides efficient energy dissipation. Meanwhile, the use of constituent material with intrinsic visible-band transmittance ensures visual compatibility of structure. Simulation results show that the proposed absorber achieves absorptance exceeding 90% over the frequency range from 0.5 THz to 10 THz and exhibits polarization-insensitive behavior, and maintains broadband performance for incident angles up to 70°. These features suggest its potential for THz shielding and other applications where optical transmittance is desirable.

Structure design and simulation

To achieve broadband and highly absorption in the THz regime while maintaining visible-band transmittance, liquid water (hereafter referred to as water for simplicity) is a convenient and promising natural material. Owing to its strong interaction with THz waves, water exhibits pronounced absorption characteristics in the THz range, making it particularly suitable for THz absorber applications. It can be observed from Fig. 1 that both the real Re(ε) and imaginary Im(ε) parts of the permittivity of water vary with increasing temperature from 270 K to 345 K, indicating that the dielectric response of water is dependence on both frequency and temperature. In this work, the primary performance of the proposed absorber is evaluated at room temperature (300 K). Nevertheless, in practical applications, temperature variations may arise from environmental conditions or operational heating. Therefore, examining the influence of temperature on the absorption characteristics is essential for evaluating the thermal stability and reliability of the proposed absorber.

To maintain visible-band optical transmittance while achieving efficient THz absorption, water, PET, and ITO are incorporated into the design. PET serves as an encapsulating layer for water, ensuring structural integrity and suppressing evaporation, while maintaining optical transmittance in the visible spectral range. Meanwhile, the ITO layer acts as a reflective layer in THz regime, which enhances absorption by confining electromagnetic energy within the absorber and promoting multiple internal interactions. The proposed structure consists of materials with optical transparency in the visible range, including ITO17, liquid water, and PET31. Therefore, the absorber is expected to exhibit a certain degree of visible-light transmittance due to the intrinsic transparency of these materials31. Owing to the selected constituent materials and pointed square-pyramid assembly, the proposed absorber simultaneously exhibits visible-band optical transmittance and robust THz absorption, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Notably, the unit cell adopts a square-pyramid geometry, which enhances multiple internal reflection and refraction effects, thereby improving absorption efficiency and broadening the effective range of incident angles. The structural parameters are optimized by systematically tuning the geometric dimensions and incorporating dielectric materials with a high THz loss tangent, allowing the absorber to maintain absorptance above 90% over a broad THz frequency range with good wide-angle robustness. Moreover, the structure geometry and constituent materials are compatible with existing fabrication techniques such as microfluidic molding and thin-film deposition.

Results and discussions

The electromagnetic performance of the proposed THz absorber is analyzed using CST Microwave Studio, a commercial full-wave electromagnetic simulation software. Periodic boundary conditions are applied along the x and y axes, while an open boundary condition is imposed along the z axis. The THz wave propagates along the negative z axis and is x-polarized. The frequency-domain solver is employed to simulate the reflection coefficient (S₁₁) and transmission coefficient (S₂₁), from which the absorption (A) is obtained using A = 1- (S₁₁)2 – (S₂₁)2. Accordingly, achieving high absorption requires simultaneously suppressing both reflection and transmission. In the simulations, the conductivity of ITO is set to 2857 S/m32, the relative permittivity of PET is defined as 3.213, and the temperature-dependent permittivity of water at 300 K is adopted. It is noteworthy that the electrical conductivity of ITO is significantly lower than that of conventional metals such as gold, copper, and silver, which requires a relatively thicker ITO layer than the metal film typically used in THz absorbers. Figure. 3(a) demonstrates that the proposed absorber with a square-pyramid structure exhibits ultra-broadband absorption, maintaining an absorption above 90% over the frequency range from 0.5 to 10 THz. In contrast, when the square-pyramid structure is replaced by a cubic structure, the absorption efficiency decreases significantly. This comparison indicates that the square-pyramid geometry plays a crucial role in achieving broadband THz absorption. As shown in Fig. 3b, the normalized impedance approaches that of free space over a broad frequency range, indicating good impedance matching between the absorber and free space. Such impedance matching enables efficient coupling of incident THz waves into the absorber, thereby suppressing reflection and enhancing electromagnetic energy dissipation. Notably, the absorber exhibits particularly strong absorption in the frequency range of 0.65–10.65 THz, where absorption exceeds 95%, as highlighted in the magnified inset. Unlike resonant metamaterial absorbers based on metallic resonators, the proposed water square-pyramid absorber operates through a non-resonant broadband absorption mechanism. The broadband absorption mainly originates from gradual impedance matching provided by the pyramid geometry together with the strong dielectric loss of water in the THz band, which promotes efficient dissipation of the incident electromagnetic energy. This performance demonstrates competitive broadband absorption capability compared with previously reported THz absorbers with optical transmittance.

To further investigate the influences of water and ITO on the absorber, the absorption spectra of the structures without ITO and without water are shown Fig. 3(a), respectively. When the absorber consists only of water and PET, i.e., without the ITO layer, the absorption decreases markedly in the low-frequency region, while relatively strong absorption is still observed at higher frequencies. In contrast, removing water from the structure leads to a significant degradation of absorption below 5 THz. These results indicate that water plays a crucial role in providing dielectric loss and enhancing electromagnetic dissipation within the proposed absorber. To further analyze the absorption mechanism, the distributions of the electric field and power loss density are presented in Fig. 4.

Distributions of (a) the electric field and (b) the power loss density at different frequencies: 0.5 THz, 1 THz, 3 THz, 5 THz, 7 THz, and 10 THz.

Figure 4a illustrates spatial distributions of the electric field under normal incidence of THz waves at several representative frequencies. At 0.5 THz, the electric field is mainly confined to the lower region of the absorber, indicating that the electromagnetic response is dominated by electric resonance associated with the bottom region and the ITO layer. As the frequency increases to 1 THz, the field concentration gradually shifts toward the central region of the structure. At 3 THz, pronounced field oscillations appear within the square-pyramid geometry, with strong localization in both the lower and middle regions. When the frequency further increases to 5 THz, the electric field remains distributed along the pyramidal profile, with stronger intensity appearing in the upper region. At 7 THz, the electric field further localized at more acute apex. At 10 THz, the field is predominately concentrated near the apex of the square pyramid. These results indicate a frequency-dependent field localization behavior, where low-frequency absorption mainly originates from resonant responses in the lower region, while higher-frequency absorption is enhanced by electromagnetic confinement provided by the tapered pyramid geometry.

Figure 4b presents the distributions of the power loss density at different frequencies, showing a similar evolution trend. As the frequency increases from 1 THz to 10 THz, the dominant power dissipation gradually shifts from the lower region toward the upper part of the square pyramid, and the loss is mainly concentrated in the water region. This indicates that the THz energy is primarily dissipated in water due to its strong intrinsic dielectric loss. Moreover, the continuously varying pyramid geometry supports Fabry-Perot-like resonances and multiple reflections inside the structure, which effectively trap electromagnetic energy over a broad frequency range. Notably, at 0.5 THz, significant power loss is also observed in the ITO layer, suggesting that electric resonance plays a major role in the absorption at low frequencies. Therefore, the ultra-broadband absorption of the proposed absorber results from the combined effects of low-frequency electric resonance, multiple-reflection-induced interference enabled by the pyramid geometry, and the strong intrinsic THz absorption of water.

As illustrated in Fig. 5a, the height of the water layer h2 primarily influences the absorption behavior in the low-frequency region, where electric resonance dominates the absorption mechanism. In contrast, variations in the pyramid water height h₃ lead to noticeable changes in absorption across both low- and high-frequency ranges, as shown in Fig. 5b. Nevertheless, within the investigated parameter range, the absorber consistently maintains high absorptance, indicating that its absorption performance is robust against variations in water height-an advantageous feature for fabrication tolerance and practical implementation.

The influence of temperature on the absorption performance, the complex permittivity of water at different temperatures was introduced into the simulations. The absorption spectra were calculated for temperatures ranging from 270 K to 345 K, covering typical environmental conditions. as shown in Fig. 5c. The results show that the absorber maintains an absorption higher than 90% over most of the operating frequency band, while temperature mainly affects the absorption behavior at the lower-frequency region. This phenomenon can be attributed to the relatively small variation in the dielectric properties of water within this temperature range in the THz band, while the broadband absorption is primarily governed by the structural design and impedance-matching mechanism. In other words, this behavior can be attributed to the temperature-dependent permittivity of water, which has a stronger impact on electric resonance than on the Fabry-Perot resonance. Therefore, the continuously varying height geometry supports broadband Fabry-Perot-like resonant behavior, which mitigates the influence of temperature variations and keeps stable absorption at higher frequencies.

In addition, the absorption responses for right-handed circularly polarized (RCP) and left-handed circularly polarized (LCP) THz waves are simulated, as presented in Fig. 6b. Owing to the structural symmetry, the absorption responses for RCP and LCP are identical. It is evident that under circular polarization, the absorber maintains broadband and high absorption over the frequency range of interest. This polarization-insensitive behavior originates from the in-plane structural symmetry of the absorber, which is highly desirable for practical THz applications.

In practical applications, tolerance to a wide range of incident angles is a crucial factor that directly affects the feasibility of electromagnetic absorbers. For example, in THz electromagnetic shielding scenarios, absorbers are required to effectively attenuate incident THz waves over a broad angular range to mitigate undesired signals. However, conventional absorbing materials often suffer from pronounced angular sensitivity due to Fresnel reflections, which leads to a significant degradation of absorption performance at oblique incidence. To investigate the impact of incident angle on the proposed absorber, absorption characteristics under both x- and y-polarized THz wave incidence are analyzed. The corresponding absorption bandwidth, defined as the frequency range with absorption exceeding 90%, is extracted in Fig. 7(b), indicating that a bandwidth larger than 7 THz is preserved for incident angles up to 70°. Similarly, Fig. 7(c) presents the absorption spectra under y-polarized THz wave incidence, revealing a comparable broadband and angle-insensitive response. The bandwidth analysis shown in Fig. 7(d) further confirms that the absorption bandwidth remains above 7 THz when the incident angle is below 70°. Collectively, these results demonstrate that the proposed absorber exhibits robust broadband absorption over a wide angular range, highlighting its suitability for applications requiring wide-angle THz attenuation. The good angular stability mainly originates from the non-resonant broadband absorption mechanism and the graded square-pyramid geometry. The continuously varying pyramid structure provides a gradual impedance transition from free space to the lossy water medium, which suppresses reflection over a wide range of incident angles. In addition, since the absorption mechanism relies primarily on dielectric loss rather than resonant effects, the structure exhibits relatively weak angular dependence.

Overall, the proposed THz absorber exhibits effective broadband absorption performance. Conclusion

In conclusion, a water-assisted THz metamaterial absorber based on a square-pyramid structure is proposed to achieve ultra-broadband absorption while maintaining optical transmittance in the visible regime. The absorber exhibits absorptance exceeding 90% from 0.5 to 10 THz and above 95% from 0.65 to 10 THz. Benefiting from the use of visible-transparent materials, the structure maintains partial optical transmittance, enabling potential compatibility with optically transmittance systems. The broadband absorption arises from the combined effect of the gradually varying pyramid geometry and the strong intrinsic THz loss of water, where electric resonances and Fabry-Perot resonances contribute to the enhanced bandwidth and efficiency. In addition, the symmetric unit-cell design ensures polarization-insensitive absorption and stable performance for incident angles up to 70°, indicating promising potential for practical THz applications.

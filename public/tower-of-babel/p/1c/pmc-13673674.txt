Root-zone waterproof cultivation advanced coloring by accumulation anthocyanins and modulated epiphytic bacteria rebuilding in Marselan grape
Authors: Xiaoshu Jing, Yuxia Sun, Ling Su, Yang Yang, Dongyue Yang, Xueqiang Guan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn Shandong Peninsula, the hygrothermal and rainy climate leads to unstable wine color and insufficient aroma of wine grapes. Plant cultivation patterns play a crucial role with various advantages. Root-zone waterproof cultivation is a cultivation technique by restricting the external water entering the root-zone during the rainy season. This study aims to investigate the berry quality, rhizospheric and epiphytic bacterial communities, the anthocyanins contents and the correlation analysis between anthocyanins and bacterial community under the root-zone waterproof treatment.ResultsCompared to normal conditions, the root zone waterproof cultivation advanced coloring at early veraison and accumulated more total soluble solids at maturity stage. LC-MS data showed that the root zone waterproof cultivation reduced the procyanidin contents and accumulated more anthocyanin 3-O-glucosides/galactosides at early veraison. There were significant differences in the diversity and relative abundance of epiphytic microorganisms between the normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment at maturity stage. Moreover, correlation network between anthocyanin profiles and microorganisms showed that petunidin-3-O-(6’’-O-caffeoyl)-galactoside, petunidin-3-O-(6’’-O-malonyl)galactoside, malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl)galactoside and delphinidin at early veraison were more closely related to the the bacterial community at maturity stage. Thus the changed specific anthocyanins modulated the structure of grape epiphytic bacterial communities. At the same time, the berries at S3 stage exhibated higher total antioxidant capacity to alleviate plant stress during the rainy season.ConclusionsThe root zone waterproof cultivation pattern emerges as a potential alternative choice for the sustainable management of wine grapes in Shandong Peninsula.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12866-026-05418-5.Keywords: Cultivation, Wine grapes, Anthocyanin, Epiphytic microorganisms

Background

The regional distinctiveness in wine traits, generally known as “terroir”, is an important aspect of recognition and economic value in wine production [1]. Geography, soil types, climate, and cultivation conditions significantly influence the structure of microbial communities [2–5]. Diverse microbes that successfully colonize the carposphere and become an important aspect of terroir [6]. Shandong Peninsula is an important wine region in China, characterized by temperate monsoon-type climate, with hygrothermal and rainy summers. In the temperate monsoon-type climate, the high temperature and heavy rainfall not only causing serious diseases but also have an adverse effect on the formation of flavor substances of wine grapes [7]. Excessive precipitation reduces the synthesis of anthocyanins in berries by allocating a large amount of anabolic products to vegetative growth and decreasing the allocation of anabolic products to berries. Days of rainfall, especially during the berry ripening period, not only resulted in the insufficient aroma substances synthesis, but also with increased biotic stress. Therefore, the hygrothermal and rainy climate leads to unstable color, insufficient aroma and more fungus of the berries. In such hygrothermal and rainy regions, proper viticultural practices are necessary to promote grape ripening and the accumulation of flavonoid compounds [8]. Many appropriate cultivation techniques were employed to benefit berry quality. Root restriction (RR), as a type of stress, is a novel cultivation technique by restricting the root growth in a certain volume, was applied widely and practiced in fruit tree cultivation with various advantages [9–11]. Root-zone waterproof cultivation is another cultivation technique by restricting the external water entering the root-zone during the rainy season. In this study, we found the root-zone waterproof advanced coloring of the berries at the early veraison stage. The outcome requires more experimental evidence to understand how microbial communities and metabolites are impacted under the root-zone waterproof treatment.

The micobiome, which is considered the second plant gene pool, can help alleviate plant stress and contribute the quality of fruits [6, 12]. During the grapevine cultivation, microbes colonized carposphere play a significant role in multiple physiological and biochemical processes, and subsequently contributes to the distinctive aroma and flavor profiles of wine [6, 13, 14]. High-throughput sequencing (HTS) and multivariate data analysis have been used to study microorganisms in grapes [2, 14–21]. The microbial community structure was differed by the grape genotype, developmental stage, vineyard management (biodynamic or conventional), and cultivation technique (such as pruning, root restriction, irrigation, and leaf removal) [2, 6, 14–23]. Anthocyanin, mainly in the skin of grape, is a class of flavonoid compound and is synthesized during maturation [24]. The compositions and contents of the anthocyanins during the grape berry development and ripening stages were easily changed with biotic and abiotic stress [8, 9, 11]. Because of high detection sensitivity and accurate quantification, targeted metabolomics is performed to reveal the changing metabolites and the corresponding metabolites upstream and downstream of the candidate metabolites. However, at present, the consequences of Marselan grape under the root-zone waterproof treatment on the associate of bacterial and the anthocyanin composition are unexplored.

This study used HTS and targeted metabolomics to explore the effects of the root-zone waterproof treatment on Marselan wine grape, the targeted metabolomics and microbiomics analyses were preformed to identify the anthocyanins compositions and the biodiversity of microbiome. To investigate the interactions between the microbial diversity and anthocyanins accumulation patterns under the root zone waterproof cultivation during the early veraison and harvest-ripe stage of grape berry, the network analysis between anthocyanins and bacterial community was further conducted. Our study comprehensively characterized the anthocyanins changes and bacterial diversity across the fruit’s developmental stages under the root-zone waterproof treatment and provided theoretical basis for production practice.

Materials and methods

Location description and sampling

From June to October in 2023 and 2024, the seven-year-old own-rooted Vitis vinifera L. cv. Marselan grapevines, procured from COFCO (China National Cereals, Oils and Foodstuffs Corporation) & Arrive Vine Plant (Yantai) R & D CO., Ltd., were used for sampling from COFCO GreatWall Wine Co., Ltd. (37°74′N, 120°86′E) located in the Shandong Peninsula (Penglai District, Yantai City, Shandong Province, China). The grapevine rows were arranged in a nearly south–north orientation and spaced at 1 m × 2.5 m (vine × row). All the vines selected as biological replicates in this study originated from completely independent experimental plots. The experimental vineyard with a total of 450 vines, 210–240 vines for the waterproof treatment and 210 − 40 vines under the normal conditions. The grapevine with the root-zone waterproof cultivation were planted in isolation with the plastic film from the next rows and with the plastic-film mulching. The ditch between the two grape planting rows is 80 cm deep and 40 cm away from the grape planting row (Fig. 1A). An unsampled buffer vine rows were reserved between the two waterproof treated rows. The normal group was planted in the same region. To eliminate the difference caused by plot position, the same watering and fertilizer strategies were applied to the two groups. And the vines at both extremities of the vine rows are excluded from sampling to eliminate positional bias induced by vineyard edge effects. Besides, 2 rows of buffer vines plots were reserved to avoid the marginal effect and the water cross-interference between normal and the waterproof treatment areas.

To evaluate the changes in the microbial community during grape development, samples (2023) from the following four stages were collected aseptically: Berries pea size stage (S1), early veraison stage (S2), harvest-ripe stage (S3), and on-tree-storage stage (S4) corresponding to stages 31, 35, 38, and 39 in a modified Eichhorn–Lorenz (EL) system [25] to investigate the composition and dynamics of the grape microbiome. For each soil biological replicate, the mixed soil sample (2023) from 5 sample points were collected at a distance of 20 cm away from the root and at a depth of 15–20 cm and combined together. For each grape biological replicate, 5 clusters (2023) was collected from random positions on the grapevines in the same row and the berries were randomly selected from different parts of the cluster. All samples were immediately stored in 50 mL sterile tubes, transported to the laboratory in low-temperature boxes. Four independent biological replicates were used for each microbial analysis.

Microbial diversity analysis

Total genomic DNA samples were extracted and 16 S rRNA genes of 16 S V4 regions were amplified used specific primer (515 F − 806R). All PCR reactions were carried out with 15 µL of Phusion® High - Fidelity PCR Master Mix (New England Biolabs); 0.2 µM of forward and reverse primers, and about 10 ng template DNA. Thermal cycling consisted of initial denaturation at 98 °C for 1 min, followed by 30 cycles of denaturation at 98 °C for 10 s, annealing at 50 °C for 30 s, and elongation at 72 °C for 30 s and 72℃ for 5 min. The obtained PCR-amplified products were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform at Novogene Bioinformatics Technology Co., Ltd.

Determination of anthocyanin contents

The skins of the berries at S2 and S3 stages were freeze-dried, ground into powder and dissolved with 0.5 mL methanol/water/hydrochloric acid (500:500:1, V/V/V). The extract were vortexed for 5 min, sonicated for 5 min, and centrifuged at 12, 000 g under 4 °C for 3 min [26]. The supernatant was collected and the procedure was repeated once. The combined supernatant was filtered with a microporous membrane (0.22 μm pore size) and were analysed using ExionLC™ AD coupled to Applied Biosystems 6500 Triple Quadrupole mass spectrometer with Waters ACQUITY BEH C18 (1.7 μm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm) at 40 °C. The mobile phase contained of 0.5% formic acid in water (solvent A) and methanol (solvent B). The gradient program was from 5% to 50% for 0–6 min and 50% to 95% at 6–12 min for solvent B, followed by 2 min at 95% solvent B. The flow rate was set to 0.35 mL/min and the injection volume was 2 µL. Three independent biological replicates were used for anthocyanin determination.

Determination of physiological indices and physicochemical properties

Physiological indices, including vertical diameter, transverse diameter, single fruit weight, total soluble solids (TSS), titratable acid (TA), of the grape samples at the two consecutive years (2023; 2024) were measured [24]. The vertical diameter and transverse diameter of ten berries were measured by a vernier calliper. One hundred grapes berries were measured by an electronic balance. Then the vertical diameter, transverse diameter and mass of a single berry was calculated. The juice of grape berries was squeezed and the soluble solids content value was recorded by the sugar metre (PAL-1; ATAGO Co., Japan). The TA was determined by NaOH titration. For physiological index measurements, 5–6 biological replicates were set for each indicator. The quantification of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and total anthocyanin contents of the grape skin was performed using commercial kits (Solarbio, Beijing, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The analysis principle of the T-AOC is that in acidic environment, Fe3+-tripyridyltriazine (Fe3+-TPTZ) can be reduced to produce blue Fe2+-TPTZ. Then, the blue Fe2+-TPTZ was quantified by absorbance at 593 nm. Anthocyanin content is calculated by measuring absorbance values at 530 nm and 700 nm at different pH values. Four T-AOC and total anthocyanin contents, the measurements were made in four biological replicates (n = 4).

Statistical analyses and correlation analysis

Statistical analyses was used by SPSS 27.0. One-way ANOVA was used to evaluate the differences in microbial community structure under different cultivation patterns. Duncan’ s tests were used to analyse the differences in soil physicochemical properties, bacterial community composition, and diversity between different vegetation types (P < 0.05). Bacterial communities analysed by nonmetric multidimensional scaling (NMDS) and a Principal coordinate analysis (PCA) using the NovoMagic online tool. A linear discriminant analysis effect size (LEfSe) to identify the significantly different microbial communities across different cultivation patterns were preformed using the NovoMagic online tool (https://magic.novogene.com). Correlation analysis of between the bacterial communications and the anthocyanin contents was performed using the Metware Cloud, a free online platform for data analysis (https://cloud.metware.cn). The raw sequence data reported in this paper have been deposited in the Genome Sequence Archive in National Genomics Data Center [27], China National Center for Bioinformation/Beijing Institute of Genomics, Chinese Academy of Sciences (GSA: CRA042109) that are publicly accessible at https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/gsa.

Results

Berry quality under root zone waterproof treatment

The main fruit quality physiological indices, such as vertical diameter, transverse diameter, TSS, TA at maturity stage (S3 stage) were analysed between the root zone waterproof treatment and the normal plants. The TSS of juice increased remarkably under the root zone waterproof treatment at maturity stage of the two consecutive years (Fig. 1DE). The single fruit weight, phenotype of berry development and the total anthocyanins contents at different stages are also determined (Figs. 1BC, S1). Significant differences in grape skin color were observed at the S2 stage, accompanied by enhanced anthocyanin accumulation over the two consecutive years. (Fig. 1BC). At the S3 stage, the difference in anthocyanin content in berry skins between the treatment and normal groups was no longer statistically significant across both experimental years (Fig. 1BC). At the S4 stage, the difference remained non-significant in 2023, while the treatment group showed a significantly higher anthocyanin than the normal group in 2024 (Fig. 1BC). This inter-annual difference is mainly caused by environmental conditions such as accumulated light hours, precipitation and larger diurnal temperature difference.

Bacterial community structure and diversity patterns

Community structure and diversity patterns of rhizospheric bacteria

The relative abundance of bacterial communities of Marselan at different growth stages at phylum and genus levels is exhibited in Fig. 2. For rhizosphere bacterial communities, totals of 3872, 3010, 3096 and 2898 ASVs were identified at different growth stages under normal conditions, while totals of 3200, 4053, 2254 and 2599 ASVs were identified under the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 2A).

At the phylum level, Acidobacteriota, Proteobacteria, Actinobacteriota, Chloroflexi, Crenarchaeota, Gemmatimonadota, Verrucomicrobiota were the most abundant phyla in the rhizosphere under the normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 2B). The root zone waterproof treatment resulted in an significant increase of Actinobacteriota and Chloroflexi at stage S2 (Fig. 2B). The root zone waterproof treatment resulted in a decrease in the presence of certain phyla, including Gemmatimonadota (S1), Bacteroidota (S2) and Myxococcota (S2) (Fig. 2B).

At the genus level, RB41 predominated in the bacterial population under normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment at the different stages (Fig. 2C). RB41 were followed by Candidatus_Nitrososphaera, Candidatus_Udaeobacter, MND1 and Nitrospira, of which the relative abundances were the more than 1% at the different stages (Fig. 2C). The root zone waterproof treatment resulted in an significant increase of Streptomyces, Gaiella and Ellin6067 but a decrease of Sphingomonas at S2 stage. The abundances of Dongia at stage S1 and Solirubrobacter at S4 stage (1.20% and 0.58%) were higher under root zone waterproof treatment than those in the normal samples (0.71% and 0.32%) (Fig. 2C).

According to the alpha diversity indices (Chao1, Simpson and Shannon indices), the diversity bacterial communities in this study tended to decline over time (Fig. 3A). S1 stage showed the highest richness indicated by the the α-diversity indices (Fig. 3A). Significantly lower values of the Chao1 indices were obtained at S1 and S3 stages under the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 3A). Under the root zone waterproof treatment, the Chao1 and Shannon indices at S1 stage were significantly higher at S4 stage. In addition, the Chao1 index also revealed the statistical difference between S1 stage and S3 stage under root zone waterproof treatment. Under normal conditions, the α-diversity indices were significantly higher at S1 stage compared to those at S2 and S4 stages (Fig. 3A). There is no significant differences between the bacterial communities at S1 and S3 stages under normal conditions (Fig. 3A).

Beta diversity was calculated to evaluate the degree of variation in species composition among the samples. The PCA analysis of the rhizospheric bacterial communities revealed that the first (PCA1) and second (PCA2) principal components respectively accounted for 10.45 and 8.95% of the variations between samples (Fig. 3C). In the NMDS analysis, all the samples were interspersed with no significant separation (Fig. 3C).

Community structure and diversity patterns of grape berry epiphytic bacteria

Under the normal conditions, totals of 113, 119, 23, and 24 ASVs for bacterial communities were identified (Fig. 2D). Under the root zone waterproof treatment, the identified ASVs were less than those under the normal conditions except for the ASVs at S3 stage. The ASVs were 81, 117, 35 and 18 at the different stages, respectively (Fig. 2D).

At the phylum level, the most massive colonization of the grape berries was Proteobacteria (Fig. 2E). Proteobacteria were followed by Actinobacteriota, Firmicutes and Bacteroidota, with higher relative abundances at the different stages (Fig. 2E). Under the normal conditions, the Firmicutes existed in high abundance at S1 and S2 stages (S1 stage: 4.77%, S2 stage: 5.60%) and subsequently decreased at S3 and S4 stages (S3 stage: 2.70%, S4 stage: 1.56%). Under the root zone waterproof treatment, the highest abundance of Firmicutes enriched at S3 stage (9.02%), which increased as the development stage proceeded (S1 stage: 4.67%, S2 stage: 7.57%), but decreased at the S4 stage (1.66%, ; Fig. 2E).

At the genus level, the dominant taxa Ralstonia was found to be massively enriched in the grape epidermis, which decreased as the development stage proceeded, but increased at the S4 stage (Fig. 2F). The massive colonization of the grapes by Methylobacterium-Methylorubrum showed significant difference between the normal conditions and the treatment at stage S3 (Fig. 2F). It existed in high abundance at stage S3, especially under normal conditions (S3 stage: 45.85%) compared to that under the root zone waterproof treatment (S3 stage: 3.63%; Fig. 2F). The third highest abundance was shown by Vibrio, especially at the S2 stage under the normal conditions (25.31%) and under the root zone waterproof treatment (26.24%). Aeromonas, Bacillus, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella, Streptococcus and Vibrio had significantly higher relative abundance in the TS3 samples than those in the CKS3 samples (Fig. 2F). However, Sphingomonas and Pseudomonas, except for Methylobacterium-Methylorubrum, had significantly higher relative abundance in the CKS3 samples than those in the TS3 samples (Fig. 2F). At the S4 stage, the relative abundance of Aeromonas, Delftia and Vibrio in the TS4 samples was significantly higher than that in the CKS4 samples.

The α-diversity of epiphytic bacteria were strongly affected by the phenological stages but with no significant differences (Tukey HSD test, p > 0.05) between the treatment and the normal groups. Alpha diversity indices tended to increase at S2 stage with a subsequently decline under normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 3B). Under the root zone waterproof treatment, Chao1 index of grape berries epiphytic bacteria were significantly higher at S2 stage, whereas Shannon and Simpson indices were shown significant differences among S1 and the other stages (Fig. 3B). Under normal conditions, significant difference was presented among S2 stage and the other stages in Shannon index diversity, while significant difference was recorded between S2 stage and S4 stage in Chao1 and Simpson indices (Fig. 3B).

The first two principal axes of the PCA plot explained 64.09% of the total variation (Fig. 3D). In detail, the PCA plot showed clear sample separation along Axis 1 (explaining 41.75% of the total variation), while a less pronounced separation is observed along Axis 2 (explaining 22.34% of the total variation, Fig. 3D). The berry epidermis bacteria at S1, S2 and S4 stages were distinguished by the different phenological stages but unseparated by the cultivation effect (Fig. 3D). Notably, PCA and NMDS at S3 stage exhibited similar results, both showing that samples were roughly divided into two classes. Statistical results revealed that the community structure of grape epiphytic bacteria was significantly affected by the development stage (Table S1). Meanwhile, at S3 stage, the community structure of grape epiphytic bacteria was affected by the waterproof treatment (p = 0.016, Table S1). These results showed that the grape epiphytic bacteria segregated mainly according to the berry phenological stages and slightly according to the cultivation, except for the diversity of microbial communities at S3 stage (Fig. 3D, Table S1).

Prediction of the functional potential of the microbial community

Prediction of rhizospheric bacterial function

PICRUSt2 was used to predict the functions of the bacterial community. For rhizospheric bacteria, the first two principal axes explained 51.51% of the total variation. On the PC1 axis there were not significant difference effected by the phenological stages or the cultivation (Fig. 4A). Samples at different stages separated along PC2 axis (explaining 21.13% of variation). On the PC2 axis, S1 and S3 were mainly distributed towards the negative direction, while S4 were mainly clustered in the positive portion of PC2 (Fig. 4A). Despite having the majority potential functions as unclassifed at S2 stage and the other stages, the main potential functions of the rhizospheric bacteria at S4 stage were more different from those at S1 and S3 stage on the PC2 axis. The results of KEGG functions showed no significant differences effected by the cultivation at all stages (data does not shown).

Prediction of epiphytic bacterial function

For the PICRUSt2 function prediction of the epiphytic bacteria, the PCA analysis revealed that the contribution rates of PC1 and PC2 were 50.53% and 26.57% respectively, with a cumulative contribution rate of 65.18% (Fig. 4B). On the PC1 axis, CKS1 and TS1 were well-represented on the negative part of PC1, while CKS2, TS2, CKS3 and TS3 were mainly clustered in the positive portion of PC1 (Fig. 4B). Similarity with the β-diversity analysis, the PCA analysis revealed that the function of epiphytic bacteria at the S1 stage as well as the S4 stage were mainly clustered in the negative portion of PC1, while those at S2 and S3 stages were mainly clustered in the positive portion of PC1 (Figs. 3D and 4B). On the PC2 axis, the maximum distance was recorded between the CKS3 and TS3 samples (Fig. 4B). CKS3 were mainly distributed towards the positive direction of PC2, while TS3 were mainly distributed towards the negative direction (Fig. 4B).

The results of KEGG functions showed that the global and overview maps of metabolism (such as ubiquinone and other terpenoid-quinone biosynthesis, prenyltransferases, isoflavonoid biosynthesis), cellular processes (cytoskeleton proteins), and genetic information processing (folding, sorting and degradation) were higher under the root zone waterproof treatment at S3 stage while the global and overview maps of metabolism (such as amino acid metabolism, carbon metabolism, xenobiotics biodegradation and metabolism), cellular processes (cell motility) and cellular processes and signaling were higher under the normal conditions at S3 stage (Table S2). Vibrio cholerae infection pathway was specifically enriched in TS3 samples, which is consistent with the finding that the relative abundance of Vibrio was also enriched in TS3 samples (Table S2). Heatmap cluster analysis indicated that the root zone waterproof treatment at S3 stage decreased epiphytic bacterial functions related to bacterial survival, such as amino acid biosynthesis, carbohydrate biosynthesis, fatty acid and lipid biosynthesis and generation of precursor metabolites and energy (Fig. 4C). In addition, “superpathway of sulfate assimilation and cysteine biosynthesis” and “assimilatory sulfate reduction” exhibited patterns of significant enrichment in CKS3 sample compared to TS3 (Fig. 4C). Conversely, “fatty acid degradation” and “peptidoglycan maturation” were found to be significantly increased in TS3 sample. In addition, the enrichment of these metabolic pathways in CKS3 were siginificantly negtively associated in the abundances of the Aeromonas, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella, Streptococcus and Vibrio (Fig. S2), which had significantly higher relative abundance in the TS3 samples. Moreover, the total antioxidant capacity of grape skins were significantly increased in TS3 sample (Fig. 4DE).

Physicochemical characteristics

The root-zone waterproof advanced coloring at the early veraison stage (Fig. 1BC). Anthocyanins and their derivatives are the crucial pigments responsible for the red wine color. The S2 and S3 stages corresponded to the initiation and rapid accumulation phases of anthocyanin biosynthesis, respectively. This interval also precisely corresponded to the phase in which the root-zone waterproofing treatment exerted the pronounced modulatory effect on pigment accumulation. The difference in anthocyanin contents between treatment and normal groups diminished to a non-significant level at S4 stage. So the S2 and S3 stages were selected as the targeted metabolism analytical stages. And the S4 stage was excluded from targeted metabolism detection. To compare the composition of metabolites related to the Marselan berry skins anthocyanin accumulation between the treatment and normal groups, we used LC/MS to determine the anthocyanin contents at S2 and S3 stages (Fig. 5A). In the present study, a total of 69 metabolites were detected. Among the samples, more metabolites were shared at S2T samples, S3CK samples and S3T samples (Fig. 5A). Notably, detected flavonoid metabolites at S3CK samples shared more metabolites at S3T samples than those at the others (Fig. 5A).

The detected target metabolites were divided into 8 categories, including 6 anthocyanin monomers (malvidin, petunidin, peonidin, pelargonidin, delphinidin, cyanidin), procyanidin and flavonol derivatives. At the S2 stage, root zone waterproofing treatment resulted in a significant increase in the contents of cyanidin, delphinidin, malvidin, petunidin and peonidin, but a decline in the contents of pelargonidin and procyanidin (Fig. 5B). Both the PCA analysis and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) model revealed robust separation between normal conditions and treatment groups at S2 stage (Fig. 5C), in accordance with the berry skin color at S2 stage (Fig. 1BC). In addition, the target metabolites were analysed by a cluster heatmap. In details, there were 39 differential metabolites in CKS2 vs. TS2, including 35 up-regulated metabolites and 4 down-regulated metabolites (Fig. 5D). Most of the anthocyanin glucosides/galactosides in berry skins were advanced accumulated at TS2 samples (Fig. 5E). Under the root zone waterproof treatment at S2 stage, the major anthocyanins in Marselan berry skins was malvidin-3-O-galactoside, malvidin-3-O-glucoside, malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O- acetyl)glucoside (Fig. 5E). Under normal conditions at S2 stage, the top three anthocyanins were quercetin-3-O-glucoside, malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl)glucoside and procyanidin B3. Effected by the phenological factor, most up-regulated metabolites continued to accumulate at S3 stage. Malvidin-3-O-glucoside and malvidin-3-O-galactoside were lower under the root zone waterproof treatment at S2 stage then those at S3 stage under the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 5E). Only delphinidin, malvidin-3-O-glucoside-5-O-galactoside and petunidin-3-O-(6’’-O- caffeoyl)-rhamnoside were increase at S2 stage under the root zone waterproof treatment but reduced at S3 stage under treatment or not (Fig. 4D). Some anthocyanins only accumulated significantly at S3 stage, such as cyanidin-3,5-O-diglucoside, cyanidin-3-sinapoyl-rhamnoside, delphinidin-5-O- rhamnoside, delphinidin-3,5,7- triglucoside, delphinidin-3-O-sophoroside (Fig. 5D). Only 1 differential metabolites in CKS3 vs. TS3: peonidin-3-O-(caffeoyl)rhamnoside was slighted increased under the root zone waterproof treatment at S3 stage (Fig. 5D). In accordance with anthocyanins contents at TS2 samples, the top three of all the anthocyanins were malvidin-3-O-galactoside, malvidin-3-O-glucoside and malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl) glucoside in CKS3 and TS3 samples. In details, the content of malvidin-3-O- glucoside was the highest of all the anthocyanins under treatment conditions, while malvidin-3-O-galactoside was the highest under normal conditions (Fig. 5E). The contents of malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl)glucoside were the third top under the normal conditions and the treatment at S3 stage (Fig. 5E).

Correlation analysis between anthocyanins and bacterial community

Pearson correlations between the relative abundance of the top 30 bacteria at the genus level and the anthocyanin contents at S2 stage and S3 stage further illustrated. In the correlation network graph, the genera were depicted as triangles and metabolites as circles, with red and blue lines representing positive and negative correlations, respectively (Fig. 6). At S2 stage, the genera Acidobacteria displayed associations with the 18 significantly up-regulated metabolites and 4 down-regulated metabolite of the 39 differential regulated metabolites (Fig. 6A). At S2 stage, the Methylobacterium-Methylorubrum showed a significantly negative correlation with 11 significantly up-regulated metabolites of the 39 differential regulated metabolites. At S3 stage, peonidin-3-O-(caffeoyl)rhamnoside, the only metabolites with differential regulated contents, displayed associations with 7 bacterial genera (Fig. 6B). Of the 6 positive correlated genera, 4 genera (Aeromonas, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella, Vibrio) had significantly higher relative abundance in the samples under the root zone waterproof treatment.

The Mantel tests were also performed to analyze the correlations between the bacterial community and the anthocyanin contents and elucidate their interactions with the bacterial at S2 and S3 stages (Figs. 6C, S4). The results showed that these bacteria at S3 stage were significantly correlated with the anthocyanins at S2 stage. The correlation between the bacteria at S3 stage and the differential regulated anthocyanins at S2 stage was greater than that between the bacteria at S2 stage and the differential regulated anthocyanins at S2 stage (Fig. 6C). In addition, the bacteria at S3 stage showed a higher correlation with the anthocyanins at S2 stage than the anthocyanins at S3 stage (Fig. S3). Two down-regulated anthocyanins (delphinidin-3-O-(coumaroyl)glucoside-5-O-galactoside and delphinidin-3-O-(6-O- malonyl-beta-D-glucoside)) and four up-regulated anthocyanins (delphinidin, petunidin-3-O-(6’’-O-caffeoyl)-galactoside, malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl)galactoside and petunidin-3-O-(6’’-O-malonyl)galactoside) at TS2 samples showed higher correlation with the bacteria at S3 stage (mantel correlation > = 0.4, P value 0.01–0.05, Fig. 6C). These data suggested that the root-zone waterproof cultivation advanced accumulated more anthocyanins at S2 stage, and with a subsequent microbiome rebuilding at S3 stage.

Discussion

During the growth progress, a large number of flavor compounds and volatile aromas are synthesized. Thus, the Marselan berries accumulate color and gradually deepen. However, the composition of metabolites, which related to fruit quality and yield, were influenced by terroir [6, 9, 11, 24]. Terroir is now considered to encompass all the ecological elements surrounding the crop plant, such as climate, soil, topography, agricultural management, and microbiota [1–6, 9–11, 17–19]. In a wide variety of plants, bacterial community structures were effected by plant developmental stages, the natural environments, external stresses and the cultivation technique [2, 6, 12, 14–24]. For grape berries, the rainfall and vine water status were the key factors shaping the carposphere microbial community [6, 8, 24]. Root-zone waterproof cultivation is a type of root-zone soil moisture estimation cultivation technique by restricting the external water entering the root-zone during the rainy season (Fig. S4). In our study, the root zone waterproof cultivation significantly advanced coloring of the berries at the early veraison stage compared to normal conditions (Fig. 1B). Veraison marks the beginning of grape ripening, which is a critical time point for grape metabolism and growth. Anthocyanin is one of the most important traits that influences the quality of grape berry colour. In order to further reveal the mechanism of the root-zone waterproof cultivation, the changes of bacterial communities and anthocyanin contents based on HTS and targeted metabolomics at different developmental stages were qualitatively and quantitatively analyzed to explore the effects of root-zone waterproof treatment on Marselan wine grape.

The variations in metabolic compositions related to fruit quality and yield were prone to be influenced by stresses and cultivation [7, 9–11]. The root zone waterproof treatment induced significant changes in metabolite profile, particularly at S2 stage (Fig. 5). There were 39 anthocyanins with significant differences at S2 stage between the treatment and normal groups (Fig. 5D). Among the 39 differenced anthocyanins, 35 metabolites were up-regulated (Fig. 5D). The root zone waterproof treatment significantly increased the contents of anthocyanin 3-O-glucosides/galactosides in berry skins compared to normal groups (Fig. 5E). Under the root zone waterproof treatment, the Marselan berry skins accumulated more cyanidin-based anthocyanins, delphinidin-based anthocyanins, malvidin-based anthocyanins, petunidin-based anthocyanins and peonidin-based anthocyanins at S2 stage (Fig. 5B). The root zone waterproof treatment significantly reduced the contents of both pelargonidin-based anthocyanin and procyanidin in berry skins compared with normal samples at S2 stage (Fig. 5B). In this study, the top three highest content of the up-regulated anthocyanins at S2 stage under the root-zone waterproof treatment were malvidin-based anthocyanins (malvidin-3-O-galactoside, malvidin-3-O-glucoside and malvidin-3-O-(6’’-O-acetyl)glucoside) (Fig. 5E), the main component of anthocyanins in the peel of most grapes [28]. Accordingly, the accumulation of these anthocyanins at early veraison stage under the root-zone waterproof cultivation resulted in advanced coloring of the berries (Fig. 1BC). The changes of secondary metabolites between the normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment appeared less significant at S3 stage compared with those at S2 stage (Fig. 5DE).

The diversity and composition of the bacterial communities were effected by the water status and cultivation [6, 23, 29–33]. The root zone waterproof treatment exerted stronger influence on the grape berries microbiota than the grapevine rhizosphere microbiota (Figs. 2). Similar with the previous studies [20], the main bacteria of grape skins at phylum levels (Firmicutes) and the genus level (Aeromonas, Bacillus, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella, Streptococcus and Vibrio) had undergone significant changes between the normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment at S3 stages (Fig. 2). LEfSe analysis showed the core members had undergone significant changes at S3 stage between the normal conditions and the root zone waterproof treatment at S3 stage (Fig. S5). The diversity and main potential functions of epiphytic bacteria were strongly affected by the phenological stages at S1, S2 and S4 stages, whereas the effect of root zone waterproof treatment was shown substantially greater at S3 stage than other stages (Figs. 2, 3 and 4).

In this study, the anthocyanin-microbe correlation network based on the metabolic profiling and the microbial community derived from S2 and S3 stages was generated. At S2 stage, the Acinetobacter exhibited positive correlation with most of the differential metabolisms at S2 stage (Fig. 6A). The only differential metabolites at S3 stage, peonidin-3-O-(caffeoyl)rhamnoside, displayed associations with 5 of the 7 the differential bacterial genera at S3 stage (Fig. 6B). It has been reported that Acinetobacter can fix nitrogen, solubilize minerals, produce siderophores, and even be used as plant epiphytes or endophytes, allowing it to assist hosts in detaching pollutants and tolerating environmental stresses [34–36]. Studies have shown that plants can recruit microbes to participate in the key physiological processes and modulated microbial rebuilding to respond to biotic or abiotic stresses and improve environmental fitness [37–40]. The correlation network between anthocyanin profiles and microorganisms showed the bacterial community at S3 stage were more closely related to the metabolisms at S2 stage (Figs. 6C, S3). This highlights the metabolisms changed by the root zone waterproof treatment at S2 stage resulted in the bacterial communities’ rebuilding. According to previous studies, the most well-known and reported bacterial antagonists are species of Pseudomonas and Bacillus [41–43]. In our study, the root zone waterproof treatment altered core bacterium at S3 stage (Fig. S5). Bacillus (form phylum to genus) and Pseudoalteromonas (form family to genus) with higher relative abundance were the core bacterium at S3 stage and only existed under the root zone waterproof treatment (Figs. 2C, S5). Thus, the microbiota function notably altered with the changed bacterial construction (Fig. 4). PICRUSt2-based predictions showed that pathway enrichment analysis at CKS3 sample revealed that the significantly influenced metabolic pathways related to (pentose phosphate pathway) sucrose metabolism and amino acid biosynthesis, which play critical roles in stress conditions (Figs. 4C, Table S2). In addition, the enrichment of these metabolic pathways in CKS3 were significantly negatively associated in Aeromonas, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella, Streptococcus and Vibrio, 5 of 6 significantly increased genus under the root zone waterproof treatment (Fig. 2F, S2). Notably, Aeromonas, Pseudoalteromonas, Shewanella and Vibrio among these species were recorded as marine bacteria with stress adaption [44–48] and some were known as plant growth promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) strains for growth promotion and non-biotic tolerance potential [49–51]. Based on existing microbial ecology evidence, several closely related species within these genera are known to participate in biofilm architecture, interaction with other dominant epiphytic microbes, secreting extracellular enzymes and bioactive metabolites, promote plant growth under salty or drought stress an so on [44–51]. Given that four genera identified from grape berry skins are commonly found as marine bacteria, it is more reasonably interpreted as a signal of microbial immigration from external environmental sources, rather than evidence of long-term, stable colonization on the grape berry surface. Marine bacteria experience fluctuations in osmolarity that the marine bacteria respond to the accumulating metabolites under the root zone waterproof treatment. Moreover, PICRUSt2-based predictions showed that peptidoglycan maturation at TS3 samples was increased (Fig. 4C). Peptidoglycan plays a vital role in protecting bacteria from external stress [52]. These results indicated that the microbiome changes at S3 stage were associated with the altered pathways closely related to stress response based on PICRUSt2 functional predictions. Under waterproof treatment, total antioxidant capacity in grape berry skins at S3 stage were significantly elevated in the two consecutive years (Fig. 4D, E). This observed correlation supports that the specific metabolites showed a strong association with the microbial rebuilding, as well as the changed bacteria exhibited a parallel dynamic trend with higher total antioxidant capacity in alleviating plant stress to the adverse effects of climate change, with increasing temperatures exacerbating, rainy-related stress and further limiting crop performance.

Notably, the specific metabolites that modulated epiphytic bacteria rebuilding remains to be functionally validated. While the anthocyanin accumulation at S3 stage coincided with the significant differentiation of bacterial community structure, the current dataset does not provide direct evidence confirming that anthocyanins are the exclusive driving factor for the bacteria rebuilding. Further experiments, such as in vitro recruitment assays or metabolite supplementation validation, are required to verify whether these pigment compounds actively recruit microbes. Similarly, the specific bacterial taxa that potentially contribute to the elevation in plant total antioxidant capacity have not been functionally confirmed. The correlational results presented here suggest that the restructured microbiome at S3 may be linked to improved plant performance under stress, but direct evidence from isolate inoculation or microbiome manipulation experiments is still needed to establish a validated relationship between specific microbial members and enhanced antioxidant capacity.

Conclusion

In summary, our results showed some metabolomic and microbiotic differences and their interactions between the root zone waterproof treatment and the normal groups. In this study, we found that (1) the root zone waterproof cultivation significantly advandced coloring with more anthocyanins at early veraison and accumulated more TSS at maturity stage; (2) the root zone waterproof cultivation modulated the epiphytic bacterial rebuilding; (3) the root zone waterproof cultivation elevated the total antioxidant capacity the grape berry skins during the rainy season. It is worth noting that the root zone waterproof cultivation could alleviate the increasing temperatures and rainy stress at Shandong Peninsula. All these results also suggested that the root zone waterproof cultivation is a potential alternative cultivation for manufacturing practices.

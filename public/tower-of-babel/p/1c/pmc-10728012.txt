Subcutaneous epcoritamab monotherapy in Japanese adults with relapsed/refractory diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma
Authors: Koji Izutsu, Takahiro Kumode, Junichiro Yuda, Hirokazu Nagai, Yuko Mishima, Youko Suehiro, Kazuhito Yamamoto, Tomoaki Fujisaki, Kenji Ishitsuka, Kenichi Ishizawa, Takayuki Ikezoe, Momoko Nishikori, Daigo Akahane, Jiro Fujita, Minh Dinh, David Soong, Hidehisa Noguchi, Jeppe Klint Buchbjerg, Elena Favaro, Noriko Fukuhara
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractEpcoritamab is a subcutaneously administered CD3xCD20 bispecific Ab that showed deep, durable responses with a manageable safety profile in patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (DLBCL) in the global multicenter pivotal phase II trial EPCORE NHL‐1. Here, we present results from the similar EPCORE NHL‐3 phase I/II trial evaluating epcoritamab monotherapy in Japanese patients with R/R CD20+ B‐cell non‐Hodgkin's lymphoma previously treated with two or more lines of therapy. Epcoritamab was dosed subcutaneously in 28‐day cycles; once weekly during cycles 1–3, every 2 weeks during cycles 4–9, and every 4 weeks from cycle 10 until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Step‐up dosing and cytokine release syndrome (CRS) prophylaxis were used during treatment cycle 1. As of January 31, 2022, 36 patients received treatment with 48 mg epcoritamab monotherapy. At a median follow‐up of 8.4 months, overall response and complete response rates by independent review committee were 55.6% and 44.4%, respectively. The median duration of response, duration of complete response, and overall survival were not reached at the time of data cut‐off. The most common treatment‐emergent adverse events of any grade were CRS (83.3%), injection‐site reactions (69.4%), infections (44.4%), neutropenia (38.9%), hypokalemia (27.8%), and decreased lymphocyte count (25.0%). Cytokine release syndrome occurrence was predictable; events were primarily low grade (grade 1–2), all resolved, and none led to treatment discontinuation. These encouraging results are consistent with previous findings and support the ongoing clinical evaluation of epcoritamab for the treatment of R/R DLBCL, including in earlier treatment lines.

INTRODUCTION

Diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common B‐cell non‐Hodgkin's lymphoma (B‐NHL) and accounts for approximately 36% of all malignant lymphomas in Japan and approximately half of all B‐cell neoplasms in Japan. 1 , 2 It is estimated that there is an incidence of approximately 10,000 DLBCL diagnoses per year in Japanese adults. 3 Overall, outcomes for patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) DLBCL are poor, especially for patients with primary refractory disease. Additionally, prognosis worsens as the number of prior lines of treatment increases. 4 Despite recent improvements in the treatment of R/R DLBCL and the approval of novel agents in Japan, such as chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T‐cell therapy and polatuzumab vedotin, safety and efficacy remain suboptimal. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 Chimeric antigen receptor T‐cell therapy, in particular, remains difficult to access because few centers can offer this therapy, and its manufacturing process, production time, and logistics further complicate accessibility. 12 , 13 Chemotherapeutic agents can have cumulative toxicity and limited clinical effectiveness in later lines of treatment. 14 Thus, there remains an unmet need for accessible, chemotherapy‐free, efficacious, and well‐tolerated off‐the‐shelf therapies for patients with R/R DLBCL.

Epcoritamab is a subcutaneously administered CD3xCD20 bispecific Ab created using the DuoBody technology platform (Genmab, Copenhagen, Denmark). It is a T‐cell engager that redirects T cells and induces T‐cell–mediated killing of CD20+ malignant B cells. 15 In the previously reported pivotal EPCORE NHL‐1 phase II trial, epcoritamab showed deep and durable responses and a manageable safety profile in patients with R/R large B‐cell lymphoma, with an overall response rate (ORR) of 63.1% and 38.9% of patients achieving complete response (CR). 16 Complete responses were durable, with the majority of complete responders remaining in response; median duration of response (DoR) among complete responders was not reached. 16 Epcoritamab is approved by the US FDA for the treatment of adult patients with R/R DLBCL, not otherwise specified, including DLBCL arising from indolent lymphoma, and high‐grade B‐cell lymphoma after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. 17 In Japan, epcoritamab is approved for the treatment of adults with the following R/R large B‐cell lymphomas: DLBCL, high‐grade B‐cell lymphoma, primary mediastinal large B‐cell lymphoma, and follicular lymphoma grade 3B after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. 18 Here, we report dose escalation and expansion results of epcoritamab monotherapy in Japanese patients with R/R B‐NHL.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and patients

EPCORE NHL‐3 is a pivotal Japanese phase I/II open‐label trial in patients with R/R mature B‐cell lymphoma (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04542824; JapicCTI‐205408; jRCT2080225312). The study consisted of two parts: dose escalation and expansion.

Eligible patients were at least 20 years of age with documented R/R CD20+ mature B‐cell neoplasm according to the WHO 2008/2016 19 , 20 classifications, measurable disease, and prior treatment with at least two lines of systemic therapy, including at least one anti‐CD20 mAb‐containing regimen. Patients had to have a life expectancy of more than 3 months with standard of care treatment, and patients with DLBCL had to be ineligible for or have failure of autologous stem cell transplantation. The DLBCL monotherapy expansion cohort, described herein, enrolled patients with R/R DLBCL, not otherwise specified, including DLBCL transformed from indolent lymphoma, and double‐hit or triple‐hit lymphoma (high‐grade B‐cell lymphoma), with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 translocations with DLBCL morphology. 20 Full inclusion criteria are available in Appendix S1.

Patients in dose‐escalation and dose‐expansion cohorts were treated with single‐agent subcutaneous epcoritamab in 28‐day cycles as follows: once weekly during cycles 1–3, once every 2 weeks during cycles 4–9, and once every 4 weeks during cycles 10 and beyond, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Step‐up dosing in cycle 1 included 0.16 mg on day 1 and 0.8 mg on day 8, followed by full doses thereafter. In dose escalation, full doses started at 24 mg and were escalated to 48 mg. In dose expansion, patients were treated with the recommended phase II dose (RP2D) of 48 mg.

Prophylaxis for cytokine release syndrome (CRS) was given in cycle 1 and included prednisolone 100 mg i.v., diphenhydramine 50 mg i.v., and acetaminophen 650–1000 mg orally (or equivalent) given prior to each epcoritamab dose on day 1, day 8, day 15, and day 22. Additionally, prednisolone 100 mg was given once daily on days 2–4, 9–11, 16–18, and 23–25. In dose escalation, hospitalization was required during cycle 1 to monitor for dose‐limiting toxicities (DLTs). Patients in the dose‐expansion cohort were required to be hospitalized for the first full dose of epcoritamab (on cycle 1 day 15) and for 24 h following administration to better characterize CRS.

End‐points and assessments

In the dose‐escalation part of the trial, the primary end‐points were DLTs and adverse events (AEs), with the objective of determining the maximum tolerated dose and/or the RP2D. Maximum tolerated dose was defined as the full‐dose level that induces DLT in, at most, one‐third of the patients. Secondary end‐points included pharmacokinetic parameters and preliminary efficacy (ORR, CR rate, and DoR). Results were based on investigator assessments.

In dose expansion, the primary end‐point was ORR. Key secondary end‐points included CR rate, DoR, duration of CR (DoCR), time to response (TTR), time to CR, progression‐free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Safety end‐points included AEs and laboratory abnormalities. Minimal residual disease (MRD) was analyzed as an exploratory end‐point. Results were based on independent review committee (IRC) assessments.

Efficacy assessments included fluorodeoxyglucose PET and computed tomography (CT) imaging, physical examination, ECOG performance status assessment, and MRD assessment. The PET‐CT scans were carried out at screening, at 6, 12, 18, 24, 36, and 48 weeks, and every 6 months thereafter. Minimal residual disease samples were collected on day 1 of cycles 3, 5, 7, 10, and 13, and every 6 months thereafter; an additional assessment was completed in the event of CR. Pharmacokinetics were assessed based on venous blood samples.

Minimal residual disease was assessed by circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) 21 , 22 using the clonoSEQ next‐generation sequencing assay (Adaptive Biotechnologies). The clonoSEQ assay is an in vitro diagnostic that uses a next‐generation sequencing‐based assay that identifies rearranged IgH (VDJ), IgH (DJ), IgK, and IgL receptor gene sequences, as well as translocated BCL1/IgH (J) and BCL2/IgH (J) sequences to track malignant tumor cells of B cell origin. Trackable tumor clones were identified with clonoSEQ using screening tumor biopsies. Blood samples for MRD analysis were collected in 10 mL K2‐EDTA blood collection tubes. For each MRD sample and timepoint, the absence of any detectable tumor clone sequence was determined based on the quantified ctDNA level and limit of detection. Patients were considered MRD negative if ctDNA was undetectable in at least one on‐treatment sample. All remaining patients in the MRD‐evaluable set were considered MRD positive.

Adverse events were classified using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities version 24.1 and graded using the NCI Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell‐associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) were graded according to the American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (ASTCT) criteria. 23 Based on ASTCT guidelines, tocilizumab treatment was considered for certain grade 1 CRS events (eg, advanced age, high tumor burden, circulating tumor cells, and fever refractory to antipyretics) and recommended for grade 2 or higher CRS events. Scheduled monitoring for ICANS was required, including assessment of immune effector cell‐associated encephalopathy (ICE) score and evaluation of four other neurological domains. Clinical tumor lysis syndrome was graded according to criteria by Cairo–Bishop. 24

Statistical analysis

In this single‐arm trial, there was no formal statistical hypothesis testing. The efficacy criterion was that epcoritamab would yield an ORR of >35%. A sample size of 35 patients was chosen for the DLBCL dose‐expansion cohort to determine whether this was achieved.

For each cohort, the full analysis set (used for efficacy analyses) and the safety analysis set were each defined as all patients who received at least one dose of epcoritamab. The MRD analysis was carried out on the MRD‐evaluable set, defined as patients in the full analysis set who had at least one on‐treatment MRD sample.

Where applicable, efficacy end‐points were determined according to PET‐based criteria of the Lugano classification. 25 , 26 Continuous data were summarized using descriptive statistics, and categorical data were summarized using frequency count and 95% confidence interval (CI), if applicable. Time‐to‐event data were summarized using the Kaplan–Meier method.

Overall response rate was defined as the percentage of patients who achieved either CR or partial response as best overall response; the 95% CI of ORR was calculated by using the Clopper–Pearson method.

Ethics

The protocol was approved by an independent ethics committee before the start of the trial. The study was carried out in compliance with the International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice (GCP) E6 (R2) guidelines, local guidelines (Japan GCP), Principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and relevant regulatory requirements. All patients reviewed and signed an informed consent form before enrollment.

Dose‐escalation cohort

Study population and exposure

Beginning August 20, 2020, nine patients were enrolled and treated with epcoritamab in the dose‐escalation part of the trial. Three patients were treated with full doses of 24 mg, and six patients were treated with 48 mg full doses. As of the data cut‐off date of January 31, 2022, at a median follow‐up of 14.9 months, four patients remained on treatment; five patients had discontinued due to progressive disease.

Safety

All dose‐escalation patients experienced treatment‐emergent AEs. No treatment‐emergent AE led to treatment discontinuation, and there were no fatal treatment‐emergent AEs. The most common treatment‐emergent AEs are summarized in Table S1. No patients experienced DLTs.

At least one CRS event was reported in eight of nine patients (88.9%). The vast majority of events were low grade (grade 1, n = 5 [55.6%]; grade 2, n = 2 [22.2%]), with one patient (11.1%) experiencing a grade 3 CRS event. There were no grade 4 or 5 events. Cytokine release syndrome events were most commonly observed following the first full dose on cycle 1 day 15 (66.7% of patients). All CRS events were transient and resolved, and none led to treatment delay, modification, or discontinuation. No ICANS was observed. Grade 3 clinical tumor lysis syndrome was reported in one patient (11.1%).

Pharmacokinetics

The pharmacokinetic profile of epcoritamab was characterized by slow absorption; following the second full dose on cycle 1 day 22, the geometric mean time to reach maximum plasma concentration was 95.6 h for the 24 mg dose and 94.3 h for the 48 mg dose. For patients receiving the 48 mg dose, the mean maximum plasma concentration was 5464.7 μg/L.

Efficacy

Based on investigator assessments and Lugano criteria, the ORR for dose‐escalation patients was 55.6% (n/N = 5/9; 95% CI, 21.2%–86.3%), and the CR rate was 44.4% (n/N = 4/9; 95% CI, 13.7%–78.8%). The median DoR was not reached (NR; 95% CI, 1.4–NR).

Recommended phase II dose

A 48 mg full dose of epcoritamab was selected as the RP2D based on pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic modeling, safety, and efficacy results from this trial, as well as results from the EPCORE NHL‐1 pivotal trial. Based on the safety and preliminary efficacy findings in dose escalation, the trial was expanded.

Dose‐expansion cohort of DLBCL patients

Study population and exposure

Beginning January 6, 2021, 36 patients were enrolled across 15 sites in Japan and treated in the DLBCL cohort of the expansion part of the trial. As of the data cut‐off date of January 31, 2022, at a median follow‐up of 8.4 months (95% CI, 6.5–11.2), 23 patients remained in trial follow‐up; of these, 14 patients remained on epcoritamab. Of the 22 patients who discontinued treatment, 20 (55.6%) discontinued due to progressive disease and only two (5.6%) discontinued due to AEs (not considered related to treatment).

Demographics and baseline clinical characteristics are shown in Table 1. Patients were heavily pretreated and had a short time from diagnosis to treatment (median of 2.2 years), with 20 patients (55.6%) having primary refractory disease, 21 (58.3%) refractory to at least two consecutive lines of prior therapy, and 20 (55.6%) with three or more prior lines of treatment. Although allowed, no patients had previous CAR T‐cell therapy. The population was high risk: 26 patients (72.2%) were 65 years of age or older, and the majority (63.9%) had an International Prognostic Index (IPI) ≥3. Seven patients (19.4%) had previously received an autologous stem cell transplant, and eight (22.2%) had the germinal center B‐cell DLBCL subtype.

Patients were treated with a median of 5 cycles or 16 doses of epcoritamab; median duration of treatment was 5.0 months (range, 0–12).

Efficacy

Efficacy outcomes for the expansion cohort are summarized in Table 2. The ORR by IRC per Lugano criteria was 55.6% (n/N = 20/36; 95% CI, 38.1%–72.1%), and the 95% CI lower bound exceeded the prespecified response rate of 35%. The CR rate was 44.4% (n/N = 16/36; 95% CI, 27.9%–61.9%). Overall, responses were achieved early; at the first assessment (week 6), 52.8% of patients had responded and 13.9% had achieved CR. The median TTR was 1.4 months (range, 1.1–2.6), and the median time to CR was 2.6 months (range, 1.3–5.5; Figure 1). Only one patient progressed prior to receiving the first full dose. Efficacy subgroup analyses are shown in Figure S1; results were generally consistent across predefined and exploratory subpopulations. The IRC and investigator assessments were generally concordant (Table S2); ORR by investigator was 52.8%, with a CR rate of 47.2%.

Safety

All 36 patients in the DLBCL expansion cohort experienced treatment‐emergent AEs; 31 patients (86.1%) experienced grade 3 or higher treatment‐emergent AEs, and 27 (75.0%) had grade 3 or higher events considered related to treatment. Serious treatment‐emergent AEs were observed in 15 patients (41.7%). Two patients experienced treatment‐emergent AEs that led to treatment discontinuation (chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, grade 2, and pancreatic carcinoma, grade 3; neither were deemed as treatment‐related and both patients had achieved a lymphoma CR). There were no fatal treatment‐emergent AEs.

The most common treatment‐emergent AEs (in ≥10% of patients) are summarized in Table 3 and include CRS, injection‐site reaction, infections, neutropenia, hypokalemia, and decreased lymphocyte count.

Overall, 16 patients (44.4%) experienced infections (grade 1, 5.6%; grade 2, 19.4%; grade 3, 19.4%). No grade 4 or 5 infections were observed. Two patients experienced infections after week 36. The most common infection events were urinary tract infection in four (11.1%) patients, and bronchitis, herpes simplex, and pneumonia in two patients (5.6%) each. Ten patients (27.8%) had an infection event considered related to epcoritamab. No COVID‐19 cases were reported.

There were 14 patients (38.9%) who experienced neutropenia (22.2% grade 3; 16.7% grade 4); 10 patients (27.8%) required granulocyte‐colony stimulating factor treatment, and 37 of 40 total neutropenic events resolved. There was one case of febrile neutropenia (grade 3), which was considered treatment‐related and resolved.

DISCUSSION

There remains an unmet need for effective therapies for R/R DLBCL, especially for challenging‐to‐treat patients who have received two or more prior lines of treatment. Epcoritamab is approved by the FDA for the treatment of R/R DLBCL, not otherwise specified, including DLBCL arising from indolent lymphoma, and high‐grade B‐cell lymphoma after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy based on a global trial, EPCORE NHL‐1. 16 , 17 In Japan, the approval is for the treatment of adults with the following R/R large B‐cell lymphomas: DLBCL, high‐grade B‐cell lymphoma, primary mediastinal large B‐cell lymphoma, and follicular lymphoma grade 3B after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. 18 This Japan‐specific study, EPCORE NHL‐3, reaffirmed the global findings. Both studies were designed to treat until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The rationale for treating until progression in this setting is that repeated activation of T cells and continuous suppression of malignant cells may allow for the best possible tumor control, lower risk of relapse, and the best chance for long‐term remission in this highly refractory patient population. Of note, epcoritamab is being explored as a fixed‐duration therapy in earlier lines of treatment and in combination with various standards of care and novel therapies.

The EPCORE NHL‐3 trial showed that Japanese patients with highly refractory DLBCL treated with subcutaneous epcoritamab had rapid, deep, and durable responses, including high rates of CR and MRD negativity. Overall, patients in the DLBCL expansion cohort were high‐risk and heavily pretreated, with an IPI ≥3 reported at baseline in 63.9% of patients and primary refractory disease in 55.6% of patients. Responses were observed in 20 of 36 patients, for an ORR of 55.6% with 16 patients (44.4%) achieving CR; 17 of 29 MRD‐evaluable patients (58.6%) achieved MRD negativity. Patients responded early during epcoritamab treatment, with the majority of responses seen by the first assessment at 6 weeks. Median duration of response, median duration of CR, and median OS were not reached at the time of analysis. As of the data cut‐off date, 38.9% of patients remained on epcoritamab treatment.

Results show a manageable safety profile of epcoritamab, with CRS, injection‐site reaction, infections, neutropenia, hypokalemia, and decreased lymphocyte count as the most common treatment‐emergent AEs. No DLTs were reported in the dose‐escalation portion of this trial. Cytokine release syndrome was primarily low‐grade and manageable, with only three patients (8.3%) in dose expansion experiencing grade 3 events. Furthermore, timing of CRS onset was predictable and consistent with previous reports, 16 , 27 with most events occurring following the administration of the first full dose. All CRS events resolved, and none led to treatment discontinuation. One patient in dose expansion experienced ICANS (grade 1), which resolved. Per protocol, hospitalization was not required in the expansion part other than for 24 h after the first full dose of epcoritamab (similar to EPCORE NHL‐1). Despite the trial being undertaken during the COVID‐19 pandemic, no COVID‐19 infections were reported. One limitation to this study design is that there is no control or active comparator group. With a median follow‐up of 8.4 months in the expansion cohort, the trial follow‐up is ongoing.

Results from this trial are consistent with those from the multicenter EPCORE NHL‐1 trial and support continued research on epcoritamab treatment in patients with B‐NHL. 16 Epcoritamab efficacy outcomes are comparable to those reported for treatment with the chemotherapy option of polatuzumab vedotin (a CD79b‐targeted Ab–drug conjugate) + bendamustine and rituximab (pola + BR), which was approved in Japan in 2021 for the treatment of R/R DLBCL. 11 A study was undertaken in Japan to evaluate pola + BR; sample size and population were similar to EPCORE NHL‐3, although patients overall were in earlier lines of treatment. 10 In Japan and globally, CAR T‐cell therapy remains difficult to access and has certain disadvantages, such as manufacturing dependencies, strict eligibility criteria, and the risk of progressive disease while awaiting manufacturing. 13 , 28 In contrast, epcoritamab is an off‐the‐shelf treatment with no need for initial bridging or debulking therapy and less stringent eligibility criteria. It can be administered regardless of whether the treatment facility offers cellular therapy, allowing physicians more flexibility and patients more treatment opportunities. Furthermore, the better safety profile of epcoritamab with lower incidence of CRS or ICANS provides further advantages compared with CAR T‐cell therapy, although there has been no comparative study. Chemotherapy use remains widespread in later lines of treatment, although its efficacy is suboptimal; a retrospective analysis of US patients with R/R DLBCL in the third or later line of treatment found that the majority of patients treated with chemotherapy had poor outcomes, with less than 10% of patients achieving CR and median OS of approximately 4–8 months. 14 Compared to other immunotherapies, the subcutaneous, once‐monthly epcoritamab injection enables ease of administration and seamless patient experience.

Novel, efficacious, well‐tolerated, and off‐the‐shelf treatment options are needed to improve outcomes for patients. Dual targeting of CD3 and CD20 with epcoritamab represents an emerging, accessible option. Epcoritamab elicits clinically meaningful responses and MRD‐negativity rates, with a manageable safety profile in hard‐to‐treat R/R DLBCL. These encouraging results of epcoritamab monotherapy in Japanese patients with R/R DLBCL reaffirm the previously reported pivotal findings of the EPCORE NHL‐1 global trial 16 and support the continued clinical evaluation of epcoritamab treatment for B‐NHL. Additional trials evaluating epcoritamab across multiple indications and in various settings and combinations are ongoing in Japan, including the phase III EPCORE DLBCL‐1 trial for patients with R/R DLBCL.

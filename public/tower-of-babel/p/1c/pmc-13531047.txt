Background

Severe mitral annular calcification (MAC) remains one of the most challenging settings for transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR), largely because of the risk of left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction, valve embolization, and paravalvular leak (PVL).1,2 Balloon-assisted translocation of the anterior mitral leaflet (BATMAN) has emerged as a leaflet-modification strategy to mitigate LVOT obstruction, particularly in anatomically complex patients with elongated or heavily calcified anterior mitral leaflets.3, 4, 5 A recent multicenter, international registry demonstrated the feasibility, efficacy, and safety of the BATMAN technique during transseptal TMVR6; however, outcomes specifically focused on BATMAN for valve-in–mitral annular calcification (ViMAC) remain limited.Take-Home Messages•BATMAN-facilitated native valve-in-MAC TMVR was technically feasible in this high-risk case series.•Despite favorable immediate hemodynamics, early morbidity and mortality were substantial.•This approach should be reserved for carefully selected patients after multidisciplinary review and shared decision-making.

Methods

We retrospectively evaluated all consecutive patients undergoing BATMAN-facilitated TMVR for native ViMAC at Henry Ford Hospital between July 24, 2024, and March 12, 2026. Baseline clinical, echocardiographic, computed tomographic, procedural, in-hospital, and 30-day follow-up data were collected from the final BATMAN registry. Technical success was defined as successful leaflet modification with the BATMAN technique followed by transcatheter heart valve deployment without significant LVOT obstruction, specifically a final LVOT mean gradient <10 mm Hg.

History of Present Illness

The cohort included 11 patients (mean age 76.5 ± 9.4 years), 72.7% of whom were women (Table 1). Most patients were highly symptomatic, with 81.8% in NYHA functional class III/IV. Past Medical History

Common comorbidities included hypertension (81.8%), chronic kidney disease (72.7%), diabetes mellitus (50.0%), coronary artery disease (45.5%), and chronic lung disease (18.2%).

Differential Diagnoses

The differential diagnosis for the patients’ dyspnea and functional limitation included severe MAC-related degenerative mitral stenosis, mixed mitral valve disease, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, pulmonary hypertension, coronary ischemia, atrial arrhythmias, chronic lung disease, anemia/deconditioning, and chronic kidney disease–related volume overload.

Investigations

Given the severe MAC, elevated transmitral gradients, preserved left ventricular systolic function, and advanced NYHA functional class, symptoms were primarily attributed to severe MAC-related mitral valve obstruction with secondary pulmonary venous hypertension.

Anatomic complexity was notable. Mean mitral annular area on computed tomography was 688.5 ± 201.2 mm2, mean MAC score was 8.2 ± 1.5 and mean predicted neo-LVOT area was 196.3 ± 88.8 mm2, reflecting a cohort at high risk for LVOT obstruction. Baseline LVOT gradients, whether assessed invasively or by echocardiography, were not available.

Management

Alcohol septal ablation before TMVR was performed in 27.3% of patients, cerebral protection was used in 63.6%, and mechanical circulatory support in 9.1%. BATMAN translocation used a Shockwave balloon in 36.4% of cases.

BATMAN balloon selection was individualized rather than protocolized and was guided by preprocedural computed tomography anatomy, anticipated transcatheter heart valve size, and leaflet length/calcification. Operators escalated balloon diameter only as needed to achieve effective leaflet modification while avoiding excessive oversizing, annular injury, or uncontrolled extension of leaflet disruption. Shockwave intravascular lithotripsy was used as a bailout for heavily calcified or resistant leaflet/MAC anatomy and was used as the final BATMAN balloon in 4 of 11 cases (36.4%). For Shockwave cases, intravascular lithotripsy was delivered during low-pressure balloon inflation to facilitate calcium fracture and leaflet compliance, followed by repeat expansion to confirm improved balloon profile and leaflet displacement.

BATMAN-facilitated TMVR achieved 100% technical success without valve embolization. One patient (9.1%) required conversion to surgery, and 1 (9.1%) required a second valve. Moderate or greater paravalvular leak occurred in 27.3% of patients, all of whom underwent adjunctive PVL closure during the index procedure for hemodynamically significant residual PVL, with subsequent normalization of left atrial pressures. Major bleeding, cardiac perforation, and stroke each occurred in 1 patient (9.1%); 2 patients (18.2%) required pericardiocentesis. In-hospital mortality was 18.2% (2 of 11). Importantly, annular rupture, valve embolization, or LVOT obstruction did not occur in any patient in the present cohort. Among patients with predischarge echocardiographic data, the mean transmitral gradient improved to 5.4 ± 1.7 mm Hg. No adverse events were directly linked to the BATMAN technique itself.

Follow-Up

At 30 days, 4 of 11 patients (36.4%) had died: 2 from noncardiac causes and 2 from cardiac causes, specifically congestive heart failure and left ventricular perforation. Among patients with available follow-up, rehospitalization occurred in 6 of 9 (66.7%). However, among those with paired Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire data, the mean score improved by 16.9 ± 14.0 points.

Discussion

In this single-center experience, BATMAN-facilitated TMVR in severe native ViMAC was technically feasible and yielded favorable immediate hemodynamic results in a cohort with advanced anatomic complexity and substantial comorbidity burden. These findings are directionally consistent with prior reports suggesting that BATMAN can serve as an alternative leaflet-modification strategy in patients at high risk for LVOT obstruction, particularly when leaflet length or calcification may limit other approaches.4,6

Nonetheless, 30-day all-cause mortality was 36.4% (4 of 11), an unacceptably high rate that substantially tempers the favorable technical success and immediate hemodynamic findings. Although 2 deaths were attributed to noncardiac causes and the cohort represented a highly comorbid, anatomically complex native ViMAC population with limited therapeutic alternatives, this mortality signal underscores that procedural feasibility alone is insufficient to justify broad adoption. However, it is important to note that no adverse events were directly linked to the BATMAN leaflet modification; therefore, the high mortality may be related to the ViMAC TMVR procedure itself.

BATMAN-facilitated TMVR in this setting should be reserved for carefully selected patients after rigorous multidisciplinary review, explicit shared decision-making regarding early mortality risk, and consideration of whether the anticipated symptomatic or survival benefit outweighs the substantial peri-procedural hazard.

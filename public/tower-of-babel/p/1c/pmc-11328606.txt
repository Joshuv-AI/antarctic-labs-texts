Surface functionalized nanomaterial systems for targeted therapy of endocrine related tumors: a review of recent advancements
Authors: Limei Liu, Miao Yang, Ziyang Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe application of multidisciplinary techniques in the management of endocrine-related cancers is crucial for harnessing the advantages of multiple disciplines and their coordinated efforts in eliminating tumors. Due to the malignant characteristics of cancer cells, they possess the capacity to develop resistance to traditional treatments such as chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Nevertheless, despite diligent endeavors to enhance the prediction of outcomes, the overall survival rate for individuals afflicted with endocrine-related malignancy remains quite miserable. Hence, it is imperative to investigate innovative therapy strategies. The latest advancements in therapeutic tactics have offered novel approaches for the therapy of various endocrine tumors. This paper examines the advancements in nano-drug delivery techniques and the utilization of nanomaterials for precise cancer cures through targeted therapy. This review provides a thorough analysis of the potential of combined drug delivery strategies in the treatment of thyroid cancer, adrenal gland tumors, and pancreatic cancer. The objective of this study is to gain a deeper understanding of current therapeutic approaches, stimulate the development of new drug DDS, and improve the effectiveness of treatment for patients with these diseases. The intracellular uptake of pharmaceuticals into cancer cells can be significantly improved through the implantation of synthetic or natural substances into nanoparticles, resulting in a substantial reduction in the development of endocrine malignancies.Keywords: Functionalized nanomaterials, endocrine related tumors, thyroid cancer, adrenal gland cancer, pancreatic cancer, nanocarriers, targeted therapy

Introduction

Endocrine tumors often result in disruptions in hormone production, occasionally leading to distinct hormonal disorders. Malignant tumors can be found in several endocrine glands, such as the thyroid, adrenal, pancreatic, parathyroid, and pituitary glands. While certain cancers are uncommon, they can exhibit significant death rates. Generally, any sign of metastasis suggests a poor prognosis, having a high likelihood of recurrence and a limited response to therapies (Schulte et al., 2012; Wong et al., 2014; Scollo et al., 2016). Thyroid carcinoma is the predominant form of endocrine cancer, primarily impacting 77% women. Based on the SEER (Surveillance, Epidemiology and End Results Program), around 56,000 new instances of tumors in the thyroid were projected in 2016, constituting 3.8% of all newly diagnosed cancer cases (Ferlay et al., 2015; Lima et al., 2017). The incidence of thyroid cancer has increased in recent decades, primarily attributed to the increased prevalence of papillary tumors (Verkooijen et al., 2003). Epidemiological evidence substantiates the assumption that obesity is autonomously correlated with an increased prevalence of diverse solid tumors, including thyroid cancer (Renehan et al., 2008). Various theories have been proposed to explain the potential mechanisms underlying this relationship, involving factors such as inflammation, hyperinsulinemia, oxidative stress, and adipokines (Pappa and Alevizaki, 2014). Nonetheless, the relationship between obesity and thyroid carcinogenesis appears to be complex; numerous metabolic factors must be taken into account in the obesity–thyroid cancer connection. Head and neck ionizing radiation exposure during childhood is a well-established risk factor for thyroid cancer among the various factors that are assessed (Ron et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2017). Further potential risk factors for thyroid tumors include environmental contaminants, iodine consumption, and chromosomal and genetic alterations (Crnčić et al., 2020). Currently, the findings remain inconclusive and occasionally controversial. Furthermore, it is possible that unidentified carcinogens, particularly those present during intrauterine development or early childhood, could account for the higher incidence of thyroid cancer.

Parathyroid carcinoma is an exceedingly uncommon neoplasm, comprising a mere 0.005% of the total cancer cases (Ferraro et al., 2019). These tumors typically release parathyroid hormone, resulting in the development of acute hyperparathyroidism (DeLellis, 2011). A recent epidemiological study revealed that 25% of patients diagnosed with parathyroid cancer experience distant metastases, primarily affecting the lungs, liver, and bones (Givi and Shah, 2010). Despite patients typically undergoing several surgical operations for repeated recurrences, there is still no universally endorsed staging classification, consistent treatment, or care regimen for parathyroid cancer (Tsai et al., 2023). The occurrence of parathyroid cancer is infrequent and may be attributed to a lack of adequate evidence for further investigation, confirmation by postsurgical histological evaluation, and the presence of metabolic problems (Radu et al., 2023). The likelihood of survival is typically associated with the size of the tumors, association to the lymph nodes, and the existence of distant metastases. However, the rates of morbidity and death are frequently attributed to these factors (Goldner and Fingeret, 2023).

Adrenal tumors, also known as adrenocortical carcinoma, is an uncommon and typically very aggressive cancerous tumor, characterized by frequent reoccurrence and limited effectiveness of chemotherapy (Mizdrak et al., 2021; Sahlander et al., 2023). Given the progress in diagnostic techniques, additional study in this field is necessary. The prevalence is highest among individuals aged 40 to 50, with a greater impact on females (Sahlander et al., 2023). Adrenal gland tumors, such as adrenocortical carcinoma, require a unique approach in terms of treatment and diagnosis because they are rare and the adrenal glands have multiple functions (Sedlack et al., 2023). Pancreatic cancer is an extremely lethal illness, with a five-year survival rate of around 10% in the US (Caban and Małecka-Wojciesko, 2023). Furthermore, it is progressively becoming a more prevalent contributor to cancer-related deaths. Key risk factors linked to the onset of pancreatic cancer include a hereditary inclination, weight increase, diabetes, and tobacco use (Luo et al., 2023). Pancreatic cancer often presents at an advanced stage and has commonly metastasized to other anatomical sites by the time of diagnosis. Pancreatic cancer, clinically known as adenocarcinoma, refers to a malignant tumor that develops in the glandular tissues of the pancreatic ductal cells. PDAC is the predominant form, representing about 90% of pancreatic cancers (Aier et al., 2019; Ushio et al., 2021).

Although radiotherapy and surgery are the most valuable and effective remedies for local and non-metastatic cancers, they are ineffective when the disease has metastasized. The main objective of these treatments is to eliminate tumors while extending patients’ lives. However, tumor recurrence and treatment resistance pose challenges to these conventional techniques in cases of advanced and metastatic tumors. Surgical intervention proves to be ineffective when the tumor appears or metastasizes to distant organs, such as the lung, bone, or liver. Cancer drugs, including hormones, biological therapies, and chemotherapy, are presently the preferred approach to dealing with metastatic cancers due to their ability to reach all parts of the body via the circulatory system (Chabner and Roberts, 2005; Liyanage et al., 2019). The objective of radiotherapy is to decrease or eliminate tumor size by administering high-energy waves that interfere with tumor cell division. While chemotherapy and radiotherapy are effective cancer treatment methods that can improve survival rates, they can also cause rapid and long-lasting negative effects on the healthy organs of patients (Liyanage et al., 2019). Moreover, the overexpression of specific proteins within tumor cells contributes to multidrug resistance, which poses a significant challenge. This frequently results in a significant reduction in the efficacy of chemotherapy. Radiation therapy is a localized treatment that specifically targets the region where the tumor is located. However, incidental harm to adjacent healthy tissues may result in adverse effects. Considering the detrimental effects associated with conventional cancer treatment methods, there is an urgent need to explore innovative and efficacious alternatives.

Alternatively, there has been an increase in interest regarding the application of nanomaterials as a viable drug delivery system in the context of cancer treatment. Continual research endeavors strive to enhance this approach in order to ultimately mitigate the detrimental consequences associated with conventional approaches. Recently, nanomedicine has been increasingly utilized in the medical treatment of many ailments, encompassing the utilization of chemotherapeutic, and cancer immunotherapy agents (Sahu et al., 2021). The nanomaterials commonly employed in cancer research encompass organic particles such as liposomes, polymeric particles, and dendrimers, as well as inorganic particles including Au, C-based particles, silica, and quantum dots (Yao et al., 2020). Nanomaterials utilized in systems for drug delivery are often selected or engineered depending on their size and properties, taking into consideration the specific pathophysiology of the malignancies (Yetisgin et al., 2020; Raj et al., 2021). In cancer treatment, these systems in general can be used to provide targeted (cellular or tissue) delivery of drugs toward cancerous cells by utilizing the carrier function of nanomaterials and the positioning impact of the targeted chemical once it is ingested (Pourmadadi et al., 2022). The nano-carriers contain drugs such as standard chemotherapeutic treatments and nucleic acids, suggesting that they can be used for both chemotherapeutic and genetic therapy purposes (Chen et al., 2015; Gaspar et al., 2016). NPs possess small size and specific surface features that enable them to extend the lifespan of medications and facilitate their accumulation in cancerous cells. Additionally, NPs have the ability to boost penetration and retention, further promoting their effectiveness (Kalyane et al., 2019).

The goal of targeted treatments is to inhibit the function of particular cancer proteins or biologic transduction pathways that contribute to tumor growth and progression. These targets are molecules (i.e. growth factors, receptors, kinase cascades, or molecules related with apoptosis and angiogenesis) that are normally present in healthy tissues but are either overexpressed or altered in cancer. The concept behind these revolutionary therapies is to either inhibit the signals that promote uncontrolled growth and division of malignant cells, induce apoptosis to induce the death of cancer cells, increase the immune system, or deliver chemotherapy agents selectively to cancer cells while decreasing the destruction of healthy cells and preventing undesirable side effects (Chabner and Roberts, 2005; Hanahan and Weinberg, 2011). There are two ways to achieve targeted therapies: one is through direct approaches that use small molecule inhibitors or monoclonal antibodies to alter specific cell signaling events (Wu et al., 2006). The other is through indirect approaches that use molecular targets that are either overexpressed or exclusively expressed on tumor cells to deliver cytotoxic molecules like chemotherapeutic agents, toxins, cytokines, or radionuclides. These molecules can then be attached to peptide ligands or monoclonal antibodies through a chemical linker, or included in nanocarriers to avoid the lack of specificity in conventional chemotherapy. This allows for higher concentrations of cytotoxic molecules in tumors while decreasing collateral damage (Allen, 2002; Okarvi, 2008; Danhier et al., 2010; Hughes, 2010). It is possible to design monoclonal antibodies (Scott et al., 2012) to bind to particular proteins on cancer cells, thereby enabling the immune system to identify and eliminate these cells (Weiner et al., 2009). They can also be selected based on their capacity to attach to the growth factor receptors that are excessively expressed in specific cancer cells, thereby obstructing the binding sites of the growth factors and preventing the signals that promote cell division (Jungbluth et al., 2003). The selection of small molecule inhibitors is based on their capacity to obstruct signaling pathways that are engaged in cancer cell-generated abnormal proliferation, anti-apoptotic, and angiogenic processes (Wu et al., 2006). Tumors need new blood vessels to feed the tumor mass. A process by which they are generated from preexisting vessels is referred to as angiogenesis. Anti-angiogenic therapies have also employed monoclonal antibodies and small molecule inhibitors to target the angiogenic proteins responsible for tumor vascularization. For instance, bevacizumab, the first anti-angiogenic drug approved by the FDA, is an anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody; SU-11246 is a small molecule inhibitor of VEGF (Zogakis and Libutti, 2001).

Nanomaterials and targeted cancer therapy

Nanocarriers are small colloidal systems at the nano-scale that can transport anticancer agents, including small molecular weight drugs or macromolecules like genes or proteins. This allows for targeted therapy by enabling these anticancer agents to escape normal tissues and accumulate in tumors. Therefore, the concentration of these agents in tumors is significantly higher, leading to a more effective treatment, while reducing toxicity in the rest of the body (Danhier et al., 2010; Pérez-Herrero and Fernández-Medarde, 2015). Furthermore, nanocarriers serve to protect the drug against degradation, prolong its half-life in the circulation and decrease renal clearance, enhance the payload capacity of cytotoxic drugs, enable regulation of the release kinetics, and improve the solubility anticancer agents (Davis et al., 2008; Danhier et al., 2010). Tumors commonly exhibit characteristics such as vascular permeability and inadequate lymphatic drainage. Various approaches have been suggested to specifically target tumors, such as utilizing the enhanced permeation and retention effect (Shinde et al., 2022). This phenomenon exploits the permeability of blood arteries in tumors, which enables the infiltration of minuscule molecules (Ejigah et al., 2022). In particular, tumors passively accumulate nanocarriers in tumoral tissues, releasing chemotherapeutic agents in the vicinity of the tumor, due to angiogenesis, which produces new blood vessels to the tumor. However, these new vessels have increased permeability, also known as the enhanced permeability and retention effect (EPR effect) (Figure 2) (Maeda, 2001; Fang et al., 2011). In order to effectively take benefit of the unique characteristics of the tumor blood vessels, nanocarriers must have circulation half-life to passively target the tumor environment. This entails evading the reticulo-endothelial system (RES) and the mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS) while passing through the bloodstream and releasing anticancer drugs within the tumor (Arias, 2010; Maeda, 2010). To evade the MPS and achieve extravasation into tumors via the EPR effect, which is significantly more effective with diameters below 200 nm, the size of the nanocarrier must not exceed 400 nm (Maruyama, 2011). Unlike free medications that can easily spread, a nanocarrier has the ability to enter tumor tissue by exploiting the leaky blood vessels, thanks to the improved penetration and retention effect (Izci et al., 2021). Various ligands can be used to efficiently target tumor cells that express or overexpress specific antigens or markers. Nanocarriers holding chemotherapy drugs can have various moieties conjugated to their surfaces, allowing for active targeting. These moieties may include monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, peptides, and growth factors (Figure 2). Nanocarriers enable the incorporation of various targeted ligands because of their large surface-area-to-volume ratio, which leads to multiple binding possibilities (Hong et al., 2007; Danhier et al., 2010). Enhancing cellular targeting with active methods enhances the effectiveness of therapy by reducing nonspecificity and improving cellular uptake (Talelli et al., 2013). Active targeting nanocarriers have the capability to significantly enhance the efficacy against tumors when compared to untargeted carriers (Kirpotin et al., 2006). Smaller molecules exhibit a higher rate of accumulation in tumors compared to bigger molecules, but bigger ones have the ability to be kept within the tumor’s cells for longer durations. Hence, the size of the drug plays a crucial role in regulating its accumulation at the location of the tumor.

The TME is distinguished by its acidic environment, elevated levels of GSH and reactive oxygen species (ROS), and increased expression of particular enzymes (MMP-2/cathepsin B). Therefore, the incorporation of TME-responsive components into nanoparticles offers potential for facilitating targeted drug delivery and tumor therapy. These internal stimuli can cause changes in molecular function, dispersion behavior, morphology, and degradation kinetics. This process facilitates eitherintracellular internalization or their evasion from disintegration in endosomes/lysosomes, leading to the release of active therapeutic components (Banstola et al., 2021). Aside from endogenous responsive nanosystems, several exogenous stimuli-sensitive nanoparticles also exhibit advantageous targeting behavior through their application of controllable external elements, including lasers, temperature, ultrasound, and magnetism. Employing a targeted drug delivery system (DDS) can effectively decrease the required dosage of drugs by achieving pharmacologically efficacious concentrations at lower levels compared to non-targeted drug administration. When compared to the conventional chemotherapy method, a targeted delivery system has the potential to drastically decrease side effects caused by toxicity and harm to healthy cells and tissues. Thus, novel treatment approaches, such as combination therapy and NPs-based targeted DDS, may help reduce side effects.

Nano-carriers in targeted therapy

NPs possess the capacity to enhance the attributes of drug intake, distribution, metabolism, and elimination (pharmacokinetics), as well as the impacts of pharmaceuticals on the body (pharmacodynamics) (Choi and Han, 2018). Furthermore, nanomaterials have the ability to improve the intravenous delivery of medications and optimize drug stability in order to reduce deterioration (Kumar et al., 2017). Various nano-carriers are used for the purpose of delivering drugs. Lipid-based nano-carriers are typically spherical structures consisting of a lipid bilayer that encloses an aqueous phase. These nanocarriers include liposomes, lipid nanoparticles, and emulsions. Examples of polymeric nano-carriers are dendrimers and polymer micelles. Inorganic nanoparticles containing metals can serve as nanocarriers for medication DDS owing to their specific use and compatibility with imaging in medicine (Mitchell et al., 2021). For the aim of developing intracellular drug DDS, nano-carriers can be manufactured in a modular form utilizing a wide variety of materials, each of which can be produced in a variety of shapes and sizes.

Recently, there have been a growing number of nanotherapeutic medications that have been made available for sale or have reached the clinical testing phase. The inaugural phase I clinical trial employing a nanomaterials-based technology with targeted delivery of siRNA to individuals with solid tumors was executed in 2010 (Davis et al., 2010). A clinical research found that an actively directed polymeric nanoparticle, that contained chemotherapy drug docetaxel (DTXL), demonstrated a more effective treatment of tumors when compared to a solvent based DTXL formulation (Song et al., 2016). The introduction of hybrid NPs has further enhanced the advancement in the field of nanoparticle-based medication DDS. Hybrid nanoparticles amalgamate the characteristics of diverse nanoparticles, hence augmenting the efficacy and durability of drug-DDS (Mottaghitalab et al., 2019). She et al. developed a prospective nano-scale drug DDS for breast tumor therapy that displayed strong anticancer effectiveness with minimal adverse effects (She et al., 2013). Similarly, pan et al., produced tiny carriers of DOX-Mesoporous silica NPs-TAT peptide conjugates that demonstrated potential in conquering multi-drug resistance in malignant cells by boosting treatment efficacy and lowering adverse effects (Pan et al., 2013). This study provides an overview of the fundamental ideas behind the use of nano-carrier systems in endocrine-related cancer therapy. The article explores the existing challenges and elucidates the prospective avenues for further research in this domain.

Polymeric NPs

Polymeric NPs can be generated from materials that are synthetic or natural, including monomers or premade polymers, providing a diverse array of possible forms and characteristics. These carriers can be engineered to exert precise control on multiple NP properties and are typically efficient in delivering substances due to their biocompatibility and easy manufacturing parameters. Polymeric NPs are manufactured using a variety of processes, including emulsification, nanoprecipitation, ionic gelation, and microfluidics, all of which produce diverse end products (Le et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2019). Pharmaceuticals can be encapsulated inside the core of NPs, confined within the polymer matrix, chemically linked to the polymers, or affixed to the surface of the NPs. Polymeric NPs are advantageous for co-delivery applications due to their capacity to transport a wide range of substances, including both hydrophobic and hydrophilic compounds, along with cargos with varying molecular weights, like small molecules, macromolecules, proteins, and antibodies (Afsharzadeh et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2020). PLGA NPs have been widely employed to encapsulate a variety of tiny anti-cancer medicines, including paclitaxel, cisplatin, doxorubicin and haloperidol. The clinically utilized anticancer DOX has toxicity, cardiomyopathies, and chronic heart failure issues. However, encapsulating DOX in PEGylation-PLGA nanomaterials has increased its antitumor activities (Chen et al., 2013; Shah et al., 2021).

Lipid NPs

Lipid-based NPs have a variety of specific shapes, but they usually resemble spherical platforms with at least one bilayer of lipids encircling minimum one internal watery compartment. Lipid-based nanoparticles provide multiple benefits as a delivery vehicle, namely formulations ease, self-assembling, biocompatibility, excellent bioavailability, the potential to transport large payloads, and a spectrum of physical and chemical characteristics that can be manipulated to control their biological features (Fonseca-Santos et al., 2015; Sercombe et al., 2015). Liposomes, which are a prominent category of lipid-based nanoparticles, are mostly made up of phospholipids. These phospholipids have the ability to form both unilamellar and multilamellar vesicular structures. The liposome has the ability to transport and administer medications that are hydrophilic, hydrophobic, and lipophilic. Additionally, it can encapsulate both hydrophilic and lipophilic substances inside the same system, hence broadening its applications (De Leo et al., 2022). Liposomes are frequently modified on their surface to prolong their circulation and improve distribution, as they can be quickly absorbed by the reticuloendothelial system. This modification has facilitated their therapeutic application (Fonseca-Santos et al., 2015).

Inorganic NPs

Nanostructured materials for pharmaceutical delivery and imaging purposes are being synthesized using inorganic substances for instance Au, Fe, and silica. The inorganic nanoparticles are meticulously designed and can be manipulated to possess a diverse range of sizes, shapes, and geometries. AuNPs, have been extensively researched, are utilized in several configurations including spheres, rods, stars, and nanocages (Yang et al., 2019). Moreover, inorganic nanoparticles possess distinctive physical, electrical, magnetic, and optical characteristics, which arise from the inherent features of the underlying material. For example, AuNPs possess unbound electrons on their surface that continuously oscillate at a frequency specified by their size and shape, leading to photothermal properties. They can be readily modified, so conferring them with supplementary characteristics and delivery capabilities (Wang et al., 2019). FeO is commonly researched for the synthesis of inorganic NPs, and iron oxide nanoparticles make up a substantial proportion of inorganic nanomedicines that have received FDA approval (Bobo et al., 2016). Magnetic iron oxide nanoparticles, specifically magnetite or maghemite, exhibit superparamagnetic characteristics at specific sizes. These nanoparticles have demonstrated efficacy as contrasting agents, pharmaceutical carriers, and thermal-based therapies (Arias et al., 2018). Additional commonly found inorganic NPs are calcium phosphate and mesoporous silica NPs, which have demonstrated efficacy in the transportation of genes and medicines. In summary, inorganic nanoparticles offer a significant advantage as drug carriers. This is because they can be easily modified with targeting molecules, allow for controlled drug release in response to various stimuli, and efficiently deliver drugs to specific locations. Therefore, they enhance the effectiveness of therapy and minimize adverse effects.

Exosomes

Exosomes are a class of nanoparticles ranging in diameter from 30 to 120 nm which are secreted by a vast majority of body cells (Record et al., 2011; Vlassov et al., 2012). Extracellular fluids such as blood, urine, amniotic fluid, saliva, and cerebrospinal fluid are all viable sources from which they can be extracted (Vlassov et al., 2012). Targeted therapies necessitate the presence of a targeting peptide or protein on the surface of exosomes in order to function as effective drug delivery vehicles, particularly for cancer treatment applications involving the delivery of chemotherapeutics with severe adverse effects. Ohno et al. produced a complex fusion protein that includes hemagglutinin, myc-tag, and a targeting peptide (GE11 or EGF) attached to the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR). Exosomes produced could express these components on their surface because of PDGFR serving as a pedestal. Since GE11 bound the EGFR selectively without causing the receptor to become activated, it was demonstrated to be a suitable targeting peptide for drug delivery to tumor tissue (Ohno et al., 2013). This kind of targeting might be appropriate in general cancer treatment because EGFR is increased in cancer cells of several types of solid tumors (Nedergaard et al., 2012). Two recent studies have presented convincing proof regarding the potential transfer of chemotherapeutics, specifically doxorubicin, into exosomes. These findings are of particular significance to the domain of exosome-based cancer therapies. Exosome-loaded doxorubicin or exosome-mimetic nanovesicles inhibited the in vivo growth of xenograft tumors derived from breast and colon adenocarcinomas (Jang et al., 2013; Tian et al., 2014). Moreover, the effectiveness of doxorubicin was significantly improved through the direct delivery of exosomes from immature dendritic cells to the tumor tissue. The increased effectiveness was accompanied by considerably fewer negative effects on major organ systems, particularly the heart, which are typically affected by doxorubicin therapy. This suggests that exosome-based delivery may mitigate the most significant drawback of this chemotherapeutic agent (Tacar et al., 2013; Tian et al., 2014).

To summarize, the targeted molecules on the surface determine the spatial targeting of modified exosomes, allowing for a higher concentration of exosomes inside tumors and a lower concentration in other healthy organs. Despite the increasing number of research on targeted exosomes, there is still a shortage of suitable targeted molecules that can effectively guide exosomes to tumor places and prevent their absorption by normal tissues.

Cytomembrane-based nanomaterrials

Natural biological cell membrane-coated nanoparticles have received increased interest in the field of nanomedicine due to the fact that they possess not only the physical and chemical properties of nanomaterials but also the benefits of homologous cells (Hu et al., 2011). For intratumoral immunotherapy, one biotechnology company has considered the clinical application of cytomembrane-cloaked NPs (i.e. platelet membrane-coated, resiquimod-loaded polylactic acid NPs) (Bahmani et al., 2021). The surface of nanoparticles can be coated with virtually any type of cell membrane, from erythrocytes to cancer cells, resulting in nanoformulations with enhanced functionality that can be tailored to specific applications (Hu et al., 2011; Fang et al., 2014). Cancer cells possess the capacity to evade the immune system, according to studies, because of immune adjuvants and cancer-associated antigens that are present on the membrane surface (Li et al., 2017; Rao et al., 2019), and homologous targeting ability (Rao et al., 2019; Xie et al., 2019). Previous research has established that biomimetic nanoparticles that are coated with cancer cell membranes are capable of acquiring the homologous targeting properties (Rao et al., 2019). This is attributed to the presence of the homologous targeting-related protein galectin-3 on the membrane of the cancer cell (Rao et al., 2016). For the purpose of targeted therapy against colon cancer, Zhang et al. designed a CP/miRNA365 delivery nanoplatform enveloped in the MC38 colon cancer cell membrane. Transporting the target gene miR365 mimic to the site of tumorization, this “Pseudotumor cell” resembles a “Trojan horse” and executes targeted delivery by exploiting the immune system’s evasion mechanism and the homologous targeting of tumor cells (Zhang et al., 2022). This strategy allowed the cancer cell membranes coated nanoparticles to maximize their anti-tumor effects in vivo.

Other drug delivery systems

Micelles exhibit significant promise as a DDS for the treatment of cancer. The process of capturing drugs into micelles is achieved by various chemical, physical, or electrostatic relationships (Kedar et al., 2010). To enhance the internalization process, the hydrophilic outer layer of micelles is altered by attaching various targeting ligands that selectively recognize the excessively expressed receptors identified in cancer cells. A star-shaped micelle with several functions had been created by self-assembling a copolymer of four-arm, where the hydrophobic and hydrophilic segments were linked by disulfide bonds. The conjugation of hydrophilic terminal groups with folate units for active targeting demonstrated excellent stability and demonstrated controlled release over time (Shi et al., 2014). Despite these favorable characteristics, further efforts are required to enhance the in vivo stability of polymeric micelles. Due to the fact that polymeric micelles are predominantly retained by noncovalent interactions, they have a tendency to dissociate in the circulation and are subsequently eliminated from the body. To address this limitation, one possible approach is to augment the stability of polymeric micelles through chemical cross-linking in either the inner core domain or the outer shell layer. Hydrogels have been extensively researched due to their potential for regulated drug delivery. The in situ gelation of hydrogels is the primary determinant of their effectiveness as a medium for drug delivery. Injectable hydrogels made from chitosan-based brush hydrogels grafted with polyurethane have been utilized in tissue engineering. Chitosan nano-hybrid scaffolds with improved mechanical properties have been documented as drug carriers for biological purposes. The nanohybrid scaffolds/hydrogels exhibited a high degree of porosity and a 3D linked structure, allowing for regulated drug release. Additionally, these scaffolds/hydrogels demonstrated biocompatibility (Mahanta et al., 2018). The inclusion of DOX in microgels exhibited anticancer properties against HeLa cells, whereas supramolecular hydrogels composed of α CD- and polyethylene-modified Au nano-crystals demonstrated extended drug release via host-guest interactions. Chen et al. employed HMDI within a Pluronic F127 polymer for their connectivity. Thermogelation took place at a temperature of 37 °C and remained stable for a duration of 30 days, demonstrating sustained release over an extended period. The thermosensitive HDI-PF127/HA hydrogel exhibited suitable physiochemical and biological characteristics, making it a suitable vehicle for the controlled release of anticancer medicines in cancer treatment (Chen et al., 2013).

Nanoparticles probe for cancer therapy

The optical imaging technique demonstrates considerable promise in the field of sensitive cancer diagnosis, specifically during the initial phases of cancer progression. This method is inexpensive, noninvasive, non-ionizing (and thus clinically safe), and sensitive. The effectiveness of cancer imaging using optical techniques is heavily dependent on the use of highly sensitive and stable optical probes. Early-stage cancer diagnosis is a challenging endeavor that has received significant attention in current research. Conventional noninvasive diagnostic imaging methods are adequate for the detection of macroscopic abnormalities. These methods include computed tomography (CT), positron emission tomography (PET), magnetic resonance imaging (MR), single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), and ultrasound (Joshi and Wang, 2018). However, sensitive imaging of microscopic abnormalities has proven to be extremely difficult. he advancement of nanotechnology drastically simplifies the process for developing diagnostic and therapeutic agents. Therefore, various optical imaging techniques employing nanoprobe technology have been devised for implementation in photoacoustic (PA), fluorescence, and phosphorescence imaging. Common nanoparticle probe employed in cancer diagnosis include quantum dots (QDs), gold nanoparticles (AuNPs), and polymer dots (PDs) (Harun et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2017). Wu et al. described the application of AuNCs (approximately 2.7 nm in diameter) for in vivo fluorescent tumor imaging of breast cancer (MDA-MB-45) and cervical cancer (HeLa) mouse xenograft models (Wu et al., 2010). Theranostic nanomedicine, which integrates therapeutic and diagnostic capabilities, detects and treats disease concurrently by employing imaging and therapeutic functions. Therefore, it facilitates the consecutive assessment of agent distribution, release, and efficacy, thereby potentially enabling the development of personalized medicine (McCarthy, 2010; Xie et al., 2010). Therefore, imaging modalities based on nanotechnology have made significant advancements in cancer research due to their capacity to generate highly sensitive probes for the detection of cancer.

Cysteine (Cys) is an essential component of lysosomal homeostasis and biological systems. For the rapid, specific, reversible, and sensitive detection of Cys, Liu et al. developed a probe based on coumarin. The probe ECMA was effectively employed to detect the real-time alterations to Cys levels in living cells. Moreover, cellular imaging clearly demonstrated that the exogenous Cys resulted in an increase in lysosomal ROS levels. This finding is valuable for studying the connection between oxidative stress and lysosomal Cys (Liu et al., 2024). In addition to the aforementioned, Lu et al. developed a diagnostic system designated as core-satellite semiconducting polymer nanoparticles @ cobalt hydroxide oxide (SPNs@CoOOH), which is specifically designed for tumor microenvironments. This approach minimizes the possibility of “off-targeted” toxicity and nonspecific activation within normal tissue, allowing for rapid and accurate tumor treatment by adjusting to the ideal pH (6.6). The precise chemical reaction between 1O2 and thiophene-based SPNs enables the chemiluminescence from SPNs@CoOOH to be closely associated with the production of 1O2 under different conditions (such as varying pH, concentration, or time). This correlation demonstrates a strong connection with the rates of cancer inhibition in vivo (Lu et al., 2023). Dong et al successfully developed a new type of nanoparticles called perfluorocarbon (PFOB)@manganese oxide (MnOx) core-shell nanoparticles (PM-CS NPs). PM-CS NPs demonstrate an activatable T1/T2-MRI contrast and a “turn on” 19F-MRI signal in response to acidity or GSH stimulation, which correlates with the release of manganese (Dong et al., 2022). Furthermore, a ternary alloy PtWMn nanocube was prepared to serve as a reservoir for manganese. A microenvironment-triggered nanoplatform was designed to precisely release manganese ions within the tumor, thereby increasing the production of ROS, and using an excess glutathione to enhance nonferrous ferroptosis synergistically. Furthermore, by employing the signal variations of T1-/T2-MRI, this nanoplatform elicits a responsive signal in high-field magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), allowing for real-time reporting of manganese release and monitoring of ferroptosis initiation (Guan et al., 2022). The resulting nanoplatform offers an innovative approach to the storage, delivery, and precise release of manganese ions, which can be employed in MRI-guided high-specificity ferroptosis therapy.

Nanomaterials in thyroid cancer

The incidence of thyroid cancer is growing rapidly worldwide and is expected to rank as the fourth most often diagnosed cancer in the US by 2030. The rise in thyroid tumor occurrence among children is linked to a concurrent rise in bigger tumors with advanced forms of the disease, suggesting that the increase is not solely due to the presence of slow-growing tumors. While vigorous surgical removal and iodine radioactive treatment are effective in treating most thyroid tumors, there is a subgroup of cases that develop into medically refractory illness (Davies et al., 2015). Moreover, the assertive therapeutic approach involving surgical removal and radioactive iodine treatment necessitates lifelong administration of thyroid gland hormone replacements for patients, resulting in a substantial financial strain on the health care sector. Given the increasing rates of occurrence and detection, it is crucial to distinguish and categorize individuals who require intensive treatment from those who would be better off with a less aggressive approach. There is a pressing need to identify tumor biomarkers that have the potential to forecast the course of the disease (MacDonald et al., 2020).

Metallic nanomaterials are highly effective tools that enhance the criteria for diagnosing and delivering drugs in cancer treatment because to their selectivity, convenience, and favorable properties. Au nanoparticles are particularly important in the treatment of thyroid cancer among various types of metallic nanoparticles. Several studies have examined the function of AuNPs in thyroid tumors. Furthermore, Liu et al examined the effects facilitated by AuNPs on BCPAP and TPC-1 papillary thyroid cancer cell lines (Liu et al., 2019). Mesoporous silica NPs are regularly employed as nano-carriers due to their exceptional physicochemical stability, large surface area, lack of toxicity, favorable surface permeability, and chemically alterable surface. Li et al synthesized silicon dioxide nanostructures using a ligand that specifically targets the thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor, enabling them to selectively target thyroid cancer cells. These nanoparticles exhibited a preference for the controlled release of doxorubicin (a very effective anti-cancer medication) in response to acidic conditions, making them suitable for cancer treatment. In vitro, silicon dioxide NPs containing doxorubicin and modified with thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) were able to induce apoptosis in human FTC-133 cells. Furthermore, these NPs were capable of suppressing the proliferation of xenografts in immunodeficient mice and reduced size of the tumor within the body (Li et al., 2017). Zhang et al utilized methoxy-incorporated kaolin nanoclay to encapsulate doxorubicin particles and control their diffusion rate. This approach demonstrated in-vitro treatment efficacy, which was reliant on the dosage. Furthermore, it showed minimal cytotoxicity toward papillary thyroid tumor cells (Zhang et al., 2016). Cisplatin-loaded lipid-polymer nanoparticles were synthesized Gao et al and tumor targeting was achieved by including TSH. An accumulation of TSH-conjugated NPs that was four times greater than that of non-targeted NPs was found in FTC 133 xenografts. The NPs conjugated with TSH exhibited a markedly augmented antitumor efficacy in comparison to the other experimental groups (Gao et al., 2015). In the same way, Paolino et al synthesized liposomes loaded with gemcitabine that were targeted toward TSH accumulated 3.5 times more in xenografts of CHO cells that were transfected with TSHR (TSH receptor). Additionally, the liposomes addressed toward TSH showed considerably stronger anti-tumor activity in FTC-133 xenografts compared to non-targeted liposomes (Paolino et al., 2014).

Zhu et al developed a new nanoparticle by using PAMAM as the main material, encapsulating Dox inside, and loading IR780 and PIP on the outer shell. This nanoparticle is referred to as PAMAM-Dox@Lip-PIP-IR (PD@L-P-IR) (Figurre 4 A), and it was designed to take advantage of the superior properties and specific functions of these molecules. PD@L-P-IR has a preference for accumulating in tumor tissues because to the tumor-targeting ability of IR780 in the shell and the inherent EPR effect of the NPs. This preference is observed in tumor tissues with a greater blood supply and increased cellular metabolism. The study demonstrated the nanoparticles’ effective capacity to target tumors and release drug in a regulated manner using LIFU. Additionally, the nanoparticles were found to successfully penetrate the cell membrane. In addition, the controlled release capability of the core PAMAM-Dox, together with the help of LIFU, resulted in a steady acceleration of the amounts of Dox in the tumor tissue. PIP acted as a competitive substrate that bound to the ABC transit system, leading to the suppression of MDR and the synergistic augmentation of the curative properties (Figure 4B) (Zhu et al., 2023).

Traditional agents used for differentiating thyroid cancer, such as retinoic acids (RA), aromatic fatty acids, histone deacetylase inhibitors, and PPAR-γ, stimulate the expression of the NIS gene. However, these drugs encounter difficulties due to their limited ability to be absorbed by the body, the need for high doses, and their lack of specificity in targeting the desired cells (Terada et al., 2015). The liposomes, consisting of DPPC/Chol/DSPE-mPEG2000 in a molar ratio of 6:3:1, were specifically formulated to encapsulate ATRA. This is because liposomes have the capacity to alter the biopharmaceutical characteristics of the contained molecules and enhance the stability of the drug being used. The liposomes coated with PEG were characterized by an average diameter of around 200 nm, PI of 0.1, and a negatively charged surface. The liposomes containing ATRA had the most potent antitumor effect on FRO cells, resulting in a reduction of FRO cell viability by up to 80%. In addition, the PTC-1 cells exhibited little susceptibility to the effects of ATRA, suggesting that ATRA has a distinct differentiating impact on anaplastic thyroid cancer (ATC) cells. Nanostructures loaded with ATRA demonstrated promise for the treatment of ATC while mitigating adverse effects (Cristiano et al., 2017). Promising results have been shown in both laboratory and live animal studies, demonstrating the potential of using nanoparticle medication delivery methods for treating thyroid cancers. Nanoengineered drug DDS offer a promising alternative for cancer patients, particularly advanced patients who are not eligible for surgery.

Nanomaterials in adrenal cancer

The incidence of adrenal tumors in the population varies from 3% to 10%, with most of them being small, benign, nonfunctioning adenomas. Adrenocortical carcinomas (ACCs), on the other hand, are uncommon and deadly malignant tumors that have a low overall survival rate. The majority of ACC instances result from the gradual accumulation of mutations occurring sporadically (Bilimoria et al., 2008). Patients diagnosed with localized and regionalized adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) have the potential to be cured with total surgical removal. The importance of surgery among individuals with recurring or spreading disease is still a subject of discussion. Recent studies have shown that individuals who undergone surgery for a recurring disease had a little improvement in survival if the period of time without the disease was more than 12 months (Datrice et al., 2012).

Nanotechnology offers a viable and efficient means to improve the effectiveness and safety of drugs. This is due to the ability to create NPs systems with customized characteristics that are specific to the desired location. A recent study advocated the use of NPs systems including CUR- and LA-HSA conjugates with improved redox responsivity as effective means to increase the in vitro antitumor activity of Doxorubicin against ACC, a highly aggressive and uncommon solid tumor. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analyses and release experiments were conducted to illustrate the redox responsiveness of the NPS. The results showed a 10-fold rise in the size of the NPs and a significant enhancement in the release profile after incubation in a 10 mM concentration of GSH. This can be attributed to the greater disruption of the cross-linking points within the NPs. The increased effectiveness of the Doxorubicin-loaded NPs in fighting cancer was confirmed through cytotoxicity assays on the H295R tumor adrenocortical cell lines. The experiments showed a considerable increase in the toxicity of the drug after just 24 hours of incubation. In contrast, cells treated with the unbound medication exhibited no significant harmful effects. Cell uptake studies demonstrated the nanoparticles were quickly and effectively taken up by the cytoplasmic fraction of tumor cells, indicating that the proposed carrier is suitable for reducing the side effects associated with DOX (Curcio et al., 2020). Langer et al examined the comparative assessment of two new nanotechnological approaches for formulating mitotane, which involve the utilization of liposomes and albumin-stabilized drug NPs. Storing stability at 4–8 °C was established for the two compositions and additional at 20–24 °C for BSA-MT. Researchers managed to demonstrate that BSA-MT was much more efficient at treating NCI-H295R 3D spheroids than DOPC-MT. This effectiveness was complemented by the expected tolerance in human dermal fibroblasts, which served as a cell model for normal tissue. Owing to these encouraging findings, evaluation in a real-world setting would be the next phase toward assessing the possibility of BSA-MT in ACC therapy (Langer et al., 2022). Synthetic nanoparticles (sHDL) that are devoid of cholesterol can work together with chemotherapeutic drugs used in ACC, resulting in significantly lower dosages needed to achieve in vitro effectiveness. The combination of these effects may be due in part to the particular focus of the steroidogenic pathway, like M, which enhances apoptosis. The combined strategy is a potentially innovative and less harmful technique to enhance treatment efficacy by combining different methods and avoiding the negative effects of excessive dosage, while still retaining the therapeutic advantages of M and the Italian protocol (Subramanian et al., 2016). The formulation of sHDL was enhanced to facilitate the transportation of a new lipophilic compound - WGA-TA. When WGA-TA was introduced into sHDL, it was demonstrated that this combination was more effective in killing SRB1-expressing H295R tumor cells in a laboratory setting compared to the medication being used alone. Furthermore, the sHDL cargo molecules gathered in the tumor areas of ACC in live organisms, and the WGA-TA-sHDL composition exhibited greater efficacy in impeding development of ACC tumors compared to the standard-of-care procedure used in Italy. Collectively, the administration of small high-density lipoproteins (sHDL) could serve as a more effective method for delivering hydrophobic chemotherapeutic drugs in tumors like ACC that have an increased expression of SRB1. This approach needs to be further investigated to determine its suitability for clinical use (Kuai et al., 2017).

Nanomaterials in pancreatic cancer

Pancreatic tumors is a gastrointestinal illness defined by the presence of malignant cells, which greatly affects the prognosis of patients (Ashrafizadeh et al., 2023). Despite recent advancements in understanding the molecular nature of pancreatic cancer, its management and therapy remain challenging, resulting in a low five-year survival rate of 5% for patients (Liu et al., 2023). Various histological kinds of pancreatic cancer exist, with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma being the most common. PC ranks as the 8th or 9th highest cause of mortality globally and is the 4th most prevalent illness (Elsayed and Abdelrahim, 2021). In the year 2020, a total of 481,000 new instances of PC were detected. It is worth noting that even in industrialized countries, the occurrence and mortality rates of this particular cancer are significantly elevated. The progressing nature of pancreatic cancer, which initially presents no symptoms, is a significant challenge for patients (Kleeff et al., 2016; Saadh et al., 2023). Pancreatic cancer frequently affects the elderly population and, in addition to being diagnosed at a later stage, it exhibits rapid progression and can develop resistance to treatment (Wang et al., 2020). It is sometimes referred to as the silent killer due to its initial manifestation as discomfort and subsequent spread to other tissues in advanced stages (Giansante et al., 2023).

Various therapeutic approaches are employed in the treatment of PC, such as radiation, chemotherapy, surgical removal, and immune therapy. Recently, researchers have used plant-based natural chemicals to inhibit the advancement of PC (Goldsmith et al., 2015). Furthermore, gene therapy has developed as innovative tactic in the management of PC, where it can modulate gene expression to hinder the development of tumors in PC. Nevertheless, there remains ample room for perfection in the medical treatment of cancer patients. In addition, the aforementioned therapies have notable drawbacks, prompting scientists to pursue the development of novel cancer treatments (Meng et al., 2020; Di et al., 2021). Furthermore, phytochemicals have instilled significant optimism for the cure of human cancers. However, their limited ability to be absorbed and utilized by the body is a challenging issue that necessitates the use of nano-scale delivery devices. Gene therapy methods encounter challenges such as enzyme breakdown and insufficient internalization in cancer cells, necessitating the usage of NPs for precise and targeted delivery. The limitations of current methods have prompted researchers to explore the use of NPs for the therapy of human tumors, particularly PC (Ashrafizadeh et al., 2020; Mirzaei et al., 2021) (Table 1).

Nanotechnology plays a significant role in the medical field, particularly in cancer treatment, by facilitating the targeted delivery of medications. However, an issue associated with nanoparticles while using in the therapy of PC is their identification by the reticuloendothelial system (RES) as foreign entities, leading to their elimination by the body’s immune system. Biomimetic nanoparticles, which have been enhanced with biological cell membranes, possess increased potential for precise medication delivery to specific targets (Ji et al., 2020). The NPs coated with macrophage membranes possess the ability to enhance circulation within the bloodstream, evade the reticuloendothelial system (RES), evade the immune system’s defenses, and enhance therapeutic outcomes in vivo for the treatment of diseases (Gao et al., 2020).

PLGA nanoparticles were synthesized by Cai et al to facilitate the targeted distribution of gemcitabine in the treatment of pancreatic cancer. Additionally, the nanoparticles’ surface was altered with macrophages membrane. This effect boosts the biological compatibility of NPs and increases their accumulation within tumor cells. The concurrent utilization of NPs and erlotinib resulted in the inhibition of growth and synthesis of DNA, hence limiting carcinogenesis both in laboratory experiments and in living organisms (Cai et al., 2021). The observed results may be ascribed to the fact that the macrophage membrane not only act as a targeted delivery vehicle due to the inflammatory homing effects, but also could sequester the inflammatory cytokines and chemokines to further reduce the inflammation level. NPs can be produced to closely resemble cell membranes, in addition to undergoing surface modification. Phospholipids and cholesterol can be utilized to create liposomes that transport gemcitabine to cancer cells and imitate the characteristics of cell membranes in PC treatment (Bulanadi et al., 2020). Oridonin is a naturally grown chemical from Rabdosia rubescens that possesses significant pharmacological properties, which includes as antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer. The GPC1-targeted AuNPs have been utilized for the administration of oridonin in PC treatment, resulting in the induction of apoptosis in PC cells. The efficacy of these NPs is superior to that of non-targeted AuNps. The AuNPs exhibited a high level of biocompatibility, making them suitable for the purpose of imaging of PC due to their significant functionalities (Qiu et al., 2018). Furthermore, extracts of leaves can be encapsulated in NPs to enhance their effectiveness in cancer treatment. AgNPs have been synthesized to facilitate the administration of berberis thunbergii leaf extract, and they have demonstrated significant inhibition of PC cell viability (Guo et al., 2022). Wang et al developed a novel photosensitizer by synthesizing a triple-anthracene derivative that exhibits sustained ROS production even after light exposure has ceased. The researchers demonstrated an innovative photodynamic effect of these compounds, which enhances the efficiency of fractionated photodynamic therapy (PDT) by directly and effectively disrupting biomolecules. It is worth noting that they have developed a fractionated PDT protocol that successfully eradicates orthotopic pancreatic tumors (Wang et al., 2024).

PLGA NPs have been created to simultaneously administer erlotinib and alantolactone in PC treatment. Once ingested by tumor cells by endocytosis, the PLGA NPs release the medicines. Erlotinib hinders the EGF/EGFR axis, whereas alanolactone decreases the STAT3 pathway, both of which impede carcinogenesis. Furthermore, alantolactone has the capability to enhance the production of ROS via upregulating p38, leading to the initiation of apoptosis (Bao et al., 2021). Berberine, a natural compound, enhances the production of ROS to induce programmed cell death (apoptosis) in prostate cancer (PC) cells. Furthermore, berberine induces DNA damage and halts the cell cycle in PC cells. The liposomal nanostructures loaded with berberine and irinotecan have proven to be advantageous in the treatment of PC. These nanostructures have a synergistic effect, effectively inhibiting carcinogenesis (Cai et al., 2021). Moreover, lipid nanoparticles coated with berbamine have the capability to hinder the survival rate, development, and invasion of PC cells. These nanostructures induce programmed cell death and have a stronger harmful effect on tumor growth in living organisms in an animal model. Recently, Uz et al developed pentablock copolymers that are responsive to changes in temperature and pH. These copolymers consist of a central block made of a temperature-responsive material called Pluronic® F127, and end blocks made of a pH-responsive material called poly(diethylaminoethylmethacrylate) (PDEAEM). The purpose of these copolymers is to deliver both miR-345 and GEM simultaneously. Recent investigations have also explored the utilization of nanogels as precise nanomedicines to enhance the efficacy of PC therapy and enhance treatment outcomes (Uz et al., 2019).

A recent study conducted by Kamerkar et al has shown that exosomes can be modified to avoid being cleared by phagocytosis and to hinder the activity of KRAS by specifically transporting short interfering RNA (siRNA) or short hairpin RNA (shRNA) to PC cells. The researchers noted that the presence of CD47 on the exosome plays a role in controlling their removal by circulating monocytes. Exosomes derived from CD47-deficient mouse fibroblasts and loaded with siRNAs or shRNA specifically targeting mutant KrasG12D effectively transported the therapeutic cargo to both orthotopically implanted and autochthonous pancreatic cancers. This led to a significant reduction in tumor growth and metastasis, ultimately resulting in better survival rates (Kamerkar et al., 2017). Macrophage-derived exosomes have been investigated for the encapsulation and transportation of chemotherapeutic drugs to prostate cancer cells. Exosomes derived from THP-1, a human macrophage cell line, and containing GEM and Deferasirox, an oral iron chelator, successfully suppressed the growth of GEM-resistant PC cells in both 2D and 3D in vitro conditions (Zhao et al., 2021). Gao et al. synthesized hollow, biodegradable mesoporous organosilica NPs that exhibit pH-sensitivity toward the acidic microenvironment of pancreatic cancers. These NPs efficiently released the medicine specifically into the tumor (Gao et al., 2019). This nano-system demonstrated precise administration of both GEM and pirfenidone in both laboratory and live organism experiments. Hence, the system would be beneficial while treating drug-resistant pancreatic tumors. Furthermore, ultrasound-induced microbubble disruption was employed to enhance infiltration into the cancerous tissue. Li et al. developed lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticles (NPs) for the targeted delivery of FOLFIRINOX to pancreatic cancers. The lipid-polymer hybrid NPs were consistently produced by combining an enhanced double emulsion technique for polymer nanoparticle synthesis with the use of ultrasound to facilitate the self-assembly of a lipid layer. The internal polymer components were fabricated using mPEG and PLA, which are established and biodegradable polymers extensively employed for drug delivery in laboratory and living organisms. These materials have been authorized for clinical usage by the US Food and Drug Administration. The effective synthesis of mPEG-PLGA copolymer was confirmed by conducting NMR and FTIR measurements, as shown in Figure 6 (Li et al., 2015). It can be inferred that, the developed system shows great potential as a carrier to deliver nanomedicines with controlled release for effective cancer treatment.

Safety of nanomaterials in DDS

Priory said, drug delivery methods, regardless of their appeal, lack significance unless they possess sufficient biocompatibility (Jain and Thareja, 2019). Similarly, this is true in the absence of authorization from a regulatory body such as the FDA or the European Medicines Agency (EMEA) (Iglesias-López et al., 2019). Utmost caution must be exercised to preserve the potency and efficacy of a medicine intended for encapsulation within the nanocarriers. After targeting an anti-cancer treatment using nanoparticles (NPs), several approaches utilizing tetrazolium salts such as MTT, MTX, and XTT tests can be employed to assess the metabolic activity or cell integrity and determine cell viability (Porosk and Langel, 2022). For example, colorimetric tests such as MTT, MTS, and XTT assays assess the capacity of mitochondria in living cells to convert tetrazolium salts into highly pigmented formazan dyes. The increasing number of novel applications of nanotechnology in medicine and the growing number of requests for permission from research institutes worldwide highlight the clear need for regulatory rules specifically tailored to nanotechnology. Nevertheless, there is still a lack of set uniform guidelines. Overall, further in vitro and in vivo research is necessary to establish a comprehensive database of information regarding the biocompatibility of nanoparticles. Regulatory authorities will only undertake the hard task of drafting new guidelines once there is ample scientific data available (Saifi et al., 2018).

Future prospects

Despite significant advancements in our comprehension, endocrine-associated cancers continue to be considered one of the most fatal and formidable forms of cancer to manage. Further investigation and experimentation are required to establish effective treatments for endocrine tumors. However, the emergence of nanocarrier-based therapy shows great potential in improving patient survival rates. Nanomaterials-based approaches can enhance tumor responsiveness to chemotherapy by integrating the administration of chemotherapy substances, small molecule medicines, and gene therapy. Nevertheless, despite the wide range of approaches, all techniques depend on stimulating the patient’s inherent anti-tumor immunity. The creation of versatile nanoparticles (NPs) with a range of different and complimentary functions can be utilized to improve the effectiveness of treatments for endocrine tumors. Multifunctional nanoparticles have the ability to combine and distribute several types of payloads, including as medicines, immune-stimulatory molecules, and tumor-associated antigens (TAAs). They also include targeting capabilities and theranostic functionality, allowing for multiple therapeutic and diagnostic functions. If the endeavor is successful, these versatile nanoparticles have the potential to minimize the required dosage, decrease expenses, and mitigate the toxicity associated with cancer treatments. These adaptable methods can also be employed to treat cancers that have spread to other parts of the body, resulting in a “systems approach” that predicts negative outcomes and expands treatment choices.

The development of therapeutic approaches utilizing extracellular vesicles (EVs) and exosomes has advanced significantly in recent years. Exosomes are being considered as a potential natural means of administration for targeted medicines. Furthermore, the implementation of exosome delivery, together with advancements in molecular and nanotechnological aspects of precision medicine, is empowering researchers to create enhanced therapy alternatives (Tran et al., 2020). Nanocarrier-based techniques are mostly used to transport miRNA, siRNA, and plasmids for gene silencing and expression. These methods have inherent constraints. Although the gene-silencing consequences of siRNA are temporary when occurring inside a living organism, miRNAs have the ability to control many targets, which might result in unintended effects on non-target genes. CRISPR-Cas9 techniques have become prominent methods for precisely and efficiently manipulating gene expression. Recently, researchers have utilized nanocarrier-based transport of the CRISPR/Cas9 system to manipulate the tumor microenvironment. This manipulation aims to modify how well patients respond to immunotherapy, in combination with chemotherapeutic and photodynamic therapy, specifically in cases of melanoma (Yang et al., 2020).

The domain of therapeutics utilizing nanocarriers has experienced significant growth in recent years. Nevertheless, only a limited number of nanomedicines designed to combat cancer, such as drug-antibody conjugates, have successfully progressed to clinical use. There are over 250 clinical trials now being conducted in the United States to assess the possible clinical advantages of NP-based formulations. The majority of these clinical studies include the use of combination medicines, with just a limited number of research focusing on the use of NP-based formulations as standalone treatments. To progress NP-based methods of administration from the lab bench to the bedside, it is necessary to tackle the obstacles that arise. One challenge is that the results obtained from studies on the effectiveness and safety of nanomedicines or nano-vaccines conducted in animal models may not accurately predict results in humans. This is because the behavior and interaction of the tested nanomedicine or nano-vaccine with blood components can vary significantly in vivo (Hua et al., 2018). A further constraint arises from the challenge of expanding the manufacturing of intricate nanocarrier systems, a prerequisite for their implementation in clinical settings. An additional obstacle that could hinder the advancement of nanomedicines to the market is the scarcity of contract facilities capable of consistently producing intricate nanomedicines under cGMP settings for utilization in clinical trials.

Based on the researches discussed in this article, anticancer targeted therapy and diagnosis via multifunctional nanocarriers containing therapeutic and imaging agents may become promising cancer treatments in the future due to their ability to detect cancerous cells selectively, eradicate them with minimal side effects, visualize them in vivo using real-time imaging techniques, and monitor the treatment’s efficacy in real-time.

Conclusion

Endocrine malignancies are extremely aggressive tumors that present substantial difficulties in treatment. Although surgical excision continues to be the principal method, its effectiveness has not shown any improvement over time. Chemotherapy is the primary treatment for the disease. Nevertheless, therapy outcomes are frequently constrained by drug resistance, thick tumor interstitium, and heterogeneity. Nanomaterials in medicine have promise for surmounting biological obstacles in drug administration. The nanomaterials drug delivery method has the ability to selectively target tumor cells and hypoxic microenvironments inside tumors, enabling precise and controllable release of medications. Pharmaceutical delivery utilizing nanotechnology holds great potential as a viable approach for achieving effective treatment. More precisely, the effective synthesis of targeted nanoparticles that accumulate in target tissue and facilitate increased cell uptake can be achieved through the attachment of specific binding entities to the nanoparticle surface. Hence, it is anticipated that active targeting, in conjunction with other targeting-based methodologies, will constitute an efficacious strategy. A number of ligand-targeted nanotherapeutics have been granted approval or are undergoing clinical evaluation, thereby giving rise to second-generation nanomedicines. Moreover, this comprehensive and concise description is a great reference for beginners in this field. While previous studies have shown impressive results in targeted cancer therapy, there is still a significant amount of progress needed before these treatments may be used in preclinical or clinical settings. It is our firm conviction that further efforts comprising multiple fields (e.g. chemistry, material science, pharmaceutics, biology, and clinical medicine) may substantially contribute to the progression of cancer therapy in the future by facilitating the targeted elimination of cancer cells.

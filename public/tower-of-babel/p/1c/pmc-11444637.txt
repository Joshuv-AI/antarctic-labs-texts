Comparing watch-and-wait vs. resection in radiologic complete response after conversion therapy for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: correspondence
Authors: Long Luo, Yao Liu, Ling Zhu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The study by Li et al.1 addresses a crucial question in the management of initially unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC): whether patients who achieve a radiologic complete response (rCR) following conversion therapy should undergo surgical resection (SR) or adopt a watch-and-wait (W-W) strategy. This pioneering research fills a significant gap in the current literature.

The authors found that the W-W strategy yielded overall survival (OS) rates comparable to SR, though progression-free survival (PFS) was lower. For patients achieving clinical complete response (cCR), the W-W group showed OS and PFS rates similar to the SR group. Moreover, when compared to patients with pathologic complete response (pCR) or non-pathologic complete response (non-pCR) following surgery, the W-W group demonstrated comparable OS outcomes. These findings suggest that the W-W strategy might offer a viable alternative to SR in managing uHCC patients who respond well to conversion therapy, potentially impacting clinical practice paradigms.

However, the study has several limitations. First, the sample size—74 patients in the W-W group and 75 in the SR group—may be insufficient to draw reliable and generalizable conclusions. The limited sample size also restricts the study’s capacity for detailed subgroup analyses. Future research with larger cohorts is necessary to validate these findings.

Second, the data were derived from a single tertiary cancer center, potentially excluding patients from lower socioeconomic backgrounds who might not have access to such high-level care. This selective inclusion could underestimate the practical utility and feasibility of the W-W strategy or SR in diverse socioeconomic settings. Therefore, multicenter studies involving a broader range of socioeconomic backgrounds are required to ensure the results’ generalizability and representativeness.

Third, the authors performed subgroup analyses to identify patients who might benefit more from either strategy, based on Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer staging, different conversion therapies, and achievement of cCR or pCR. Nonetheless, various confounding factors, such as genetic predispositions, comorbid conditions, and lifestyle factors (e.g. smoking and alcohol consumption), were not thoroughly addressed. Including these variables in future studies will enhance both the internal and external validity of the findings.

In conclusion, Li et al.’s1 study provides evidence that the W-W strategy may be an effective, nonsurgical, and cost-efficient alternative to SR for initial uHCC patients achieving rCR after conversion therapy. Despite the promising results, the study’s limitations emphasize the necessity for further research. Addressing these limitations will improve the reliability and applicability of the findings, ultimately enhancing clinical decision-making for uHCC patients.

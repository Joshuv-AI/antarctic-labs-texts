The hidden costs of aneuploidy: New insights from yeast
Authors: Yuerong Wang, Xian Fu, Yue Shen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The molecular mechanisms underlying the paradoxical effects1 of aneuploidy are still not completely understood. In this issue, Rojas et al.2 systematically analyzed the associated costs of aneuploidy and the molecular drivers involved, which revealed that aneuploidy stress is primarily driven by the cumulative effects of genes per chromosome. Notably, gene length was predicted as the most significant indicator of aneuploidy toxicity by machine learning.

Main text

Aneuploidy, characterized by an abnormal number of chromosomes, is commonly linked to cellular stress, decreased fitness, and various genetic disorders.3 Despite its generally harmful effects, recent studies suggest that aneuploidy can confer adaptive advantages by rapidly adjusting gene dosage in response to environmental stress. For instance, approximately 20% of wild budding yeast isolates exhibit aneuploidy and may have evolved mechanisms that mitigate its fitness effects.4 Therefore, investigation of these paradoxical effects of aneuploidy will deepen our understanding of the broader impacts of aneuploidy on cell fitness.

Two alternative mechanisms have been proposed to account for the aneuploidy costs: the genic load model5 and the driver gene model.6 The genic load model highlights the burden of excess gene products as the main cause of aneuploidy stress, while the driver gene model focuses on the impact of few specific dosage-sensitive genes. Recent findings suggest a comprehensive model, which indicates that both mechanisms may contribute to the overall impact of aneuploidy.7 However, the complete understanding of molecular mechanisms underlying the paradoxical effects of aneuploidy is hindered due to many challenges including (1) variable effects across karyotypes and cell types,8 (2) difficulties in generating stable aneuploid strains, and (3) a lack of systematic studies in controlled environments.

In this issue of Cell Genomics, Rojas et al. present a systematic investigation into aneuploidy’s deleterious effects under optimal growth conditions. This work introduced a multi-factorial model that integrates both the cumulative costs of single-gene duplications and the impact of noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) to elucidate the effects of aneuploidy. A natural oak-soil isolate of Saccharomyces cerevisiae (YPS1009) was used in this study to generate aneuploid strains, offering valuable insights that enhance understanding of evolutionary processes. Additionally, a key question remains: what factors cause the varying degrees of toxicity observed in different gene duplications? Understanding why some duplications are more harmful than others is essential to refine our understanding of deleterious gene duplications. The authors employed machine learning techniques to identify predictive toxicity of these genes. By comparing 1,177 deleterious genes with 3,028 neutral or beneficial ones, they found that gene length was the most significant predictor of deleterious gene duplications. The authors speculated that longer genes and transcripts might be more deleterious when duplicated due to increased chances of error during protein synthesis.10 These errors might lead to proteotoxic stress, a key driver of the harmful effects associated with gene duplication.

In summary, Rojas et al.’s work presents a comprehensive and sophisticated analysis of the fitness costs linked to chromosome duplication, uncovering the molecular mechanisms behind aneuploidy toxicity. Whether the findings in this study are also true under different genetic backgrounds and environmental conditions would require further efforts. Still, this work opens new avenues for studying the broader implications of aneuploidy, which will potentially lead to the development of novel therapeutic avenues in cancer biology.

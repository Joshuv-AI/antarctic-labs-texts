Effect of nano-NPK fertilizer and BAP on growth and flowering of Viola tricolor
Authors: Fanar Hashum Al-Hashemi, Zohoor Fuaad Abdaljabar, Khawlah Mahmood Al Nooh, Mohammed Sarheed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe interaction between nutrients and growth regulators may provide an effective means for enhancing the productivity of crops. The effects of nano NPK fertilizer (0, 0.5, 0.75, and 1 g pot−1) in combination with 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP, 0, 50, and 75 ppm) on Viola tricolor L. growth and flowering were evaluated in a 4 × 3 factorial experiment in a completely randomized design with three replications, conducted in a shaded hot-house in Mosul, Iraq, during spring 2022. Results showed that applying NPK alone had a greater effect than that of applying BAP alone; however, the use of both NPK and BAP together resulted in a considerably stronger synergy when both products were added to the nutrient solution. The treatment with NPK1BAP75 had the most pronounced effects on plant height (68% increase), leaf number (94% increase), and branch number (16% increase) relative to control. Biomass accumulation was also maximum for this treatment with fresh and dry weight returns of 168 g and 56 g, respectively. Additionally, the photosynthesis rate and chlorophyll concentration increased by 72% and 107%, respectively, compared to the control. Combined application of nano NPK and BAP resulted in increased flowering response, as indicated by earlier bud opening and higher number of flowers compared to the control. Moreover to enhanced flowering capabilities, the accumulation of nutrients by plants increased dramatically, as evidenced by the increased potassium content in treated plants increasing over twelve times that of control group plants. Therefore, the combination of nano NPK 1 g pot−1 combined with 75 ppm BAP was the most effective treatment, as it shows that an integrated approach to enhance plant growth and flowering is feasible for developing a sustainable and efficient agricultural production system.Keywords: Nano NPK fertilizer, 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP), Synergistic effect, Nutrient uptakeSubject terms: Physiology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Viola tricolor L. known as pansy or common violet, is an annual herbaceous wildflower and an ornamental and medicinally important plant1. The herb grows to be between 10 and 20 cm tall and produces mixed and pure-coloured blooms. Due to its ornamental nature, V. tricolor is a popular choice for garden and as well as in homes. There are numerous ways to produce V. tricolor including seed, stem cuttings and division of roots. Moderate watering, sufficient light and well draining media are required, but V. tricolor is very sensitive to changes in temperature which could lead quickly to loss in vigor1. V. tricolor has traditionally been used for its decorative properties but has also been employed for medicinal purposes including into today for the treatment of inflammation, respiratory and skin ailments as well as rheumatic diseases2. The most effectively produced V. tricolor requires proper nutrient management. Primary macronutrients (N, P and K) play crucial roles in metabolic activity, vegetative growth and reproduction, while deficiency of these macronutrients may result in severe physiological disorders3,4. The use of nano-fertilizers, especially nano-fertilizers produced from NPK, has been shown to be an effective way to provide nutrients to plants, increase growth and flowering, and reduce environmental damage and production costs, compared to conventional fertilizers5. Nitrogen promotes leaf expansion and stimulates the synthesis of proteins. Phosphorus is important for energy transfer as well as for root development. By maintaining the balance of water in the plant, potassium maintains osmotic pressure and regulates all cellular functions6,7. Nano‑fertilizers enhance nutrient uptake efficiency by increasing specific surface area and facilitating deeper penetration into plant tissues, thereby improving nutrient transport and availability compared to conventional fertilizers8. Studies have shown that nano‑formulations of fertilizers exhibit controlled or slow nutrient release and increased surface area, which enhances nutrient penetration and uptake by plants, leading to improved nutrient use efficiency and growth performance9.

Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs) are important agents that regulate plant growth patterns and productivity by improving the ability of the plant to take up nutrients and create good growth forms. A commonly used synthetic cytokinin is a type called Benzyl aminopurine (BAP), which is known to promote cell division and axillary shoot development as well as indicating increased branching and compacted plant structures with the greatest number of flowers per plant10–12.

Although previous studies have reported the individual benefits of nano-NPK fertilizers in improving nutrient uptake and BAP in promoting growth and flowering, there is limited information on their combined effects on V. tricolor. Understanding this potential synergy is crucial for optimizing growth, flowering, and nutrient use efficiency in ornamental plants. Therefore, this study was designed to investigate the individual and combined impacts of nano-NPK and BAP on the growth, flowering traits, and nutrient absorption of V. tricolor, addressing this knowledge gap and providing a scientific basis for sustainable horticultural practices.

Materials and methods

Experimental site

The Department of Horticulture and Landscape Engineering, College of Agriculture and Forestry, University of Mosul, Nineveh, Iraq (36°20′43″ N, 43°07′52″ E; 223 m altitude) conducted this experiment during the spring season of 2022 within a wooden shaded structure situated on campus The shaded hot-house was about 50% shady area during this experiment. The average temperature per day during the experimental period was 20–30 °C with a relative humidity of 50–70%. Plants were regularly watered to prevent over-drying of soil. Pest management and uniform nutrient applications were used for growing the plants, the same as with all standard cultural practices.

Plant material and propagation

The propagation of V. tricolor through softwood cuttings first utilized a perlite/peat moss (1:1) mixture for propagation prior to moving into a soil/peat moss (1:1) mixture in 20 cm pots after rooting. Vegetative cuttings were grown under the conditions specified in the shaded house. Each treatment had three replications, each consisting of three pots. The experimental design was a completely randomised design (CRD) 4 × 3 factorial. V. tricolor is widely cultivated by farmers on a large commercial scale and was obtained exclusively from cultivated sources. The plant material was not collected from wild or natural populations; therefore, no specific permits were required. The study complies with relevant institutional, national, and international guidelines, and no endangered or protected species were involved.

Preparation of NPK nanofertilizer

The NPK (20:20:20) Nano Fertilizer used in this research came from Khezrā Nano Fertilizer Company (Iran) which is a balanced NPK formulation providing equal proportions of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium delivered through nanotechnology that significantly increases nutrient availability and uptake efficiency compared to normal NPK Fertilizers. The particles were specified to have a size between about 50–80 nm, spherical shape and a zeta potential of -25mV; this indicates that the particles have a high degree of colloidal stability. These properties help explain how the nano formulation improves the efficiency of nutrient delivery and absorption when compared to conventional formulations.

Treatments and experimental design

A completely randomized design (CRD) factorial experiment (4 × 3) was performed with three replicates of each treatment. Each replicate was made up of 3 pots (20 cm diameter × 5 L).

Nano-fertilizer application

The NPK nano-fertilizer (20:20:20) produced by Nano Kud Khazra contains nanoparticles (50–80 nm) that are spherical in shape with a zeta potential of -25 mV. The fertilizer was applied to the pots using a drip irrigation method at various rates (0.0 g pot-1 (control), 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 g pot− 1) which correspond to 2.5, 3.8, and 5.0 g per l− 1 of culture medium for the 0.5, 0.75 and 1 g treatments respectively. Each plant was treated with 150 ml of the proposed solution for each and every fertilising time period. As part of the solution, electrical conductivity (EC) was kept within the limits of 1.2 to 2 dS/m− 1 to avoid stress induced by excess salts from the fertilisers. Each of the three fertilisation events took place at an interval of one month, beginning on April 22, 2022.

Growth regulator (BAP) application

The foliar sprays of benzylaminopurine (BAP, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) were formed in solvent concentrations of 50 and 75 ppm (mg L−1) in distilled water13. The volumes of spray solution applied per pot were around 150 mL sprayed until there was such a volume that it began to rain down or runoff. The sprays were applied at the same time in the early morning hours under very similar weather conditions between all pots. The applications were done over a period of one week. The concentrations used are within the previously reported ranges that had been found effective for growth regulation in ornamental species, so both BAP concentrations were used as part of each treatment replicated three times.

Data collection and measurements

Vegetative traits

To measure plant height (cm), the distance was taken from the ground to the meristem at the tip of the main stem. The number of branches and leaves per plant was measured by hand during the full developmental stage. Each treatment had three replications, with three randomly chosen plants from which data were collected for each replication.

Biomass

Post-harvest biomass for each plant was quantified based on above-ground production and root biomass. Roots were removed from the soil without disturbance, then washed carefully using tap water to remove soil and debris before weighing them. Roots and shoots of plants were weighed separately as FW before subsequently drying them in an oven until they reached a constant DW (dry weight). The drying process was conducted at a temperature of 70 °C.

Photosynthetic rate

To measure the net photosynthetic rate of each plant, we used fully expanded leaves from the upper third of the plant with an LI-COR 6400XT (LI-COR, USA) portable infrared gas analyser. Net photosynthesis measurements were performed between 09:00–11:00 h, using ambient light. The leaf chamber was held at a temperature of 25 °C, 50–60% relative humidity (RH), and 400 µmol/mol carbon dioxide concentration.

Physiological traits

The determination of chlorophyll content involved analysing fully developed leaves located at the upper third of each plant. Samples consisted of approximately 0.5 g of fresh leaf material from each replicate, with three replicates for each treatment. Chlorophyll extraction was conducted in an 80% acetone solution, followed by spectrophotometric determination of chlorophyll content according to Lichtenthaler (1987). Results are presented as mg/g fresh weight14.

Flowering traits

Reproductive development was tracked by the presence of flower buds (flowering) rather than the interval between sowing and recording data due to variability in environmental conditions. Flowering traits were recorded on four specific dates (10 April, 18 April, 24 April, and 17 May) corresponding to key stages of the flowering period, including early bud emergence, peak flowering, and late flowering. On each date, the number of flowers per plant was counted, and flower diameter was measured using a digital caliper (mm) to comprehensively capture the dynamics of flowering under different nano-NPK and BAP treatments.

Nutrient content

The above-ground parts of the plants were collected before they bloomed, dried in an oven at 70 °C until they weighed the same each day for a period of 24 h, and blended into a powder. Nitrogen (N) content was obtained based on procedures laid out by Brenner (1996) through the Kjeldahl method, involving concentration of the dissolved sample with concentrated sulfuric acid and Cu and Se catalysts, followed by distillation and titration (Bremner, 1996). The Concentration of phosphorus (P%) was determined by colorimetry with a spectrophotometer after wet digestion using a solution containing both nitric acid and perchloric acid15. The concentration of potassium (K%) was determined using a flame photometer16. All analyses were performed in triplicate for each treatment based on dry weight.

Statistical analysis

The normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and all variables were assumed to be normal. All data were subjected to analysis by a factorial experiment analysis of variance (ANOVA) measured in a completely randomized design (CRD) for means comparisons. Analysis of means comparisons were calculated where appropriate using LSD Test with a significance of (p < 0.05) to assess differences for the mean comparisons. All statistical analysis was conducted in SAS software (version 9.4), while most correlation analysis will include hierarchical clustering and principal components analysis (PCA) using R software (version 4.2.3) and SRplot with appropriate packages.

Results and discussion

Synergistic effects of nano NPK and BAP on vegetative growth

The use of NPK1BAP75 resulted in the greatest increase in plant height, leaf number, and branch number by 68.07%, 94.40%, and 16.06%, respectively, compared to the control (Fig. 1a–c). Providing NPK provides continuous access to growth, protein synthesis, energy metabolism, and enzyme function6. In contrast, BAP functions as a growth regulator by facilitating cellular division, shoot initiation, and leaf senescence17. These results are compatible with previous experiments performed on ornamental plants like Petunia hybrida and Lilium18,19, where the influence of cytokinin was more evident when nutrients were not limiting. Application of zeo-char loaded nano-NPK resulted in substantial increases in plant height (up to 35.9%), leaf number (up to 73.3%), leaf area (up to 40.2%), stem diameter (up to 28.7%), root number and length (up to 105.7%)20. In Kalanchoe blossfeldiana, nano NPK treatments led to marked increases in number of leaves, plant diameter, and flower number outperforming basic NPK and control treatments21. In Impatiens walleriana, the highest shoot proliferation was achieved with a medium containing 1 mg/l BAP and 0.5 mg/l NAA, while root length and rooting percentage were maximized with 0.5 mg/l BAP and 0.5 mg/l IBA22.

Boosting plant growth and biomass through NPK-BAP application

The combined treatment exerted a combined and synergistic (positive) effect, with the greatest increments observed with NPK1BAP75 in each parameter tested: 168.33 g (FW), 56.11 g (DW), 11.33 g (root FW), and 5.83 g (root DW), followed by NPK1BAP50 and NPK0.75BAP75, indicating that high rates of nano NPK and BAP applied together may result in important increases in shoot and root biomass (p < 0.05) (Fig. 2a, b,c, d). NPK provides three major macronutrients that are responsible for protein building, energy transfer, and root growth6, but BAP as a synthetic cytokinin promotes cell division, shoot growth and leaf senescence through hormone pathways23. In a recent study, Ali et al. (2023) demonstrated increases in both fresh and dry weights for24, Chrysophyllum oliviforme with NPK 24:20:10 fertilizer treatment. Similarly, the application of higher doses (5–10 g L−1) of NPK increased biomass in Cupressus spp.25. The application of NPK 20:20:20 with biological extracts (seaweed or Moringa) significantly enhanced root development and plant growth in Tagetes erecta26 Particularly under drought and heat-stress conditions, additional treatment with NPK 20:20:20 resulted in a robust growth response with increasing root dry weight measurements and improved tolerance for the heat stress. In greenhouse trials, the addition of NPK 15:15:15 increased the root biomass of Cucumis sativus significantly (Ngwu and Edeh, 2018). Similarly, the use of 0.005% BAP (50 mg L−1) was beneficial in reducing heat stress in Phaseolus vulgaris, increasing fresh and dry weights for both roots and shoots27. According to Santoro et al. (2013), Mentha piperita had 2.5 mg L−1 BAP application resulted in increased lateral root and shoot formation and increased dry root weight, although there was a slight reduction in shoot fresh weight. Foliar application of BAP at 0.005% resulted in increases in root and shoot dry biomass of drought-stressed Pisum sativum28. In vitro cultures of Astragalus adscendens as a medicinal plant were evaluated with a treatment of 4 mg L−1 BAP + 0.5 mg L−1 NAA which improved shoot and root biomass, also, 100% of inoculated plants produced roots29.

Nano NPK and BAP synergistically improve plant photosynthesis

The combined treatment exhibited a synergistic effect on both photosynthesis and chlorophyll accumulation. The highest maximum photosynthetic rate of NPK1BAP75 was 72.6% higher than the control (p < 0.05). For photosynthesis, NPK1BAP50 and NPK0.75BAP75, also had higher rates compared to the control by 65.5% and 62.5%, respectively (p < 0.05). NPK1BAP50 exhibited the highest levels of chlorophyll (18.5 mg/g) which reflects an increase of 107.9% from baseline), followed by NPK1BAP75 (17.17 mg/g (92.9% from baseline), and the NPK0.5BAP75 (14.2 mg/g) treatment resulted in 59.6% of the baseline (Fig. 3a, b). These increases in photosynthetic rate and chlorophyll content may be attributed to the enhanced nutrient availability provided by nano-NPK30 and the stimulatory effect of BAP on chloroplast development and leaf expansion31. The combination of silica and NPK led to increased chlorophyll content and grain yield in rice32. For instance, Solidago virgaurea demonstrated that the application of nano-NPK on the leaves at the time of 1, 1.5 and 2 mL/L caused significantly higher levels of chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll and carotenoids compared to conventional NPK33. Similarly, in Kalanchoe blossfeldiana, nano-NPK fertilizers improved growth, and flowering while improving leaf mineral and chlorophyll contents21. As for Phaseolus vulgaris, foliar application of 0.005% BAP (50 mg L-1) increased chlorophyll levels and photosynthesis rates under heat stress27. Similarly, the application of BAP (1–2 mg L−1) improved chlorophyll content and leaf quality in Tagetes erecta, subjected to water deficit in their study34.

Acceleration and enhancement of flowering via nano NPK + BAP

The interactive effects of NPK and BAP were found to be significant for flowering traits including bud appearance, flower diameter, and number of buds. The quickest bud emergence was recorded at NPK1BAP75 (29.33 days) and the latest bud emergence was recorded at NPK1BAP50 (36 days) (p < 0.05). Nano NPK interventions typically elevated flower diameter, with peak observations noted at NPK0.75 (49.4 mm; 25.6% more than the control). Significantly, NPK0.5BAP50 and NPK0.75BAP50 produced the highest flower numbers on April 10 (6 flowers; 80.2% increase compared to control), April 18 (11.5 flowers; 115.6% increase), April 24 (12 flowers; 125% increase), and May 17 (38 flowers; 123.5% increase). Some combinations, such as NPK0.75BAP75, only produced slight increases (Fig. 4a–f). The increase in flower number may be due to the synergistic effects of nanoNPK35, which enhances the uptake and transport of nutrients, especially phosphorus and potassium, which are critical for reproductive growth, together with BAP36, which stimulates cell division and lateral branch growth, thereby promoting earlier bud formation and more flower production. Previous studies have established that NPK fertilization plays an important role in regulating flower characteristics by redistributing nutrients to the reproductive sinks37–39. For example, in the species Tagetes erecta, NPK application increased flower diameter, flower count, and flower longevity by enhancing carbohydrate translocation and nitrogen availability for the production of proteins40. In a similar vein, research indicating that chrysanthemum (Chrysanthemum morifolium) would respond positively to NPK (15:15:15) in order to stimulate early bud initiation and flower size suggests there was increased partitioning of photosynthates41. Exogenous BAP application had an effect on Gerbera jamesonii increasing flower stalk number and quality, whereas application of BAP impacted flower phenology in Rosa hybrida, resulting in earlier bud emergence and a larger flower diameter42.

Nano NPK and BAP promote plant nutrient uptake

The synergistic application of NPK and BAP significantly improved the accumulation of nutrients in the plant (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5a–c). NPK1BAP75 yielded the most nitrogen (2.033 g/plant; 247.3% gain from control), though NPK1BAP50 (1.819 g/plant; 210.5% gain from control) and NPK1 (1.803 g/plant; 207.5% gain from control) also produced comparable nitrogen values to the control (Fig. 5a). The maximum phosphorus accumulation occurred in NPK1BAP75 (0.807 g/plant; 324.2% more than control). Also, NPK1BAP50 (0.773 g/plant; 307.9% more than control) and nano NPK1 (0.612 g/plant; 221.1% more than control) showed a significant increase in phosphorus accumulation (Fig. 5b). Similar increases were also observed for potassium concentrations, with NPK1BAP75 eliciting the highest concentrations (2.167 g/plant; 1228.8% control), followed by NPK1BAP50 (1.833 g /plant; 1025.2% control), and nano NPK1 only (1.286 g/plant; 688.3% over control; Fig. 5c). The increase in potassium concentration may be attributed to the increased uptake and transport efficiency provided by the nano-NPK formulation43, while BAP likely facilitates nutrient mobilization and allocation in the plant, and overall contributes to greater potassium accumulation in aerial tissues44. In Solanum lycopersicum, the application of nano NPK fertilizer resulted in an increase in nitrogen and phosphorus contents leading to a subsequent increase in growth and fruit quality45. Similarly, in Capsicum annuum, NPK fertilizer (20:20:20) increased potassium uptake and nutrient-use efficiency46. Additionally, it has been observed that BAP contributes to increased nutrient utilization when modifying source/sink relationships and delaying leaf senescence47. For instance, BAP enhanced N and P accumulation under heat stress in Phaseolus vulgaris48, nd cytokinin treatments improved K uptake and redistribution in Oryza sativa with an increase in biomass formation49.

Heatmap and PCA of NPK–BAP effects on plant traits

The correlation heatmap revealed notable positive relationships among treatments and plant characteristics. Nano NPK 0.5 was a significant induction factor of early flowering (24 April), and so was nano NPK 0.5 with BAP 50 (p < 0.05). Chlorophyll content has also increased in the Control, NPK 0.5 + BAP 75, and NPK 0.75 + BAP 75 treatments, and potassium was higher in NPK 1 with BAP. Nitrogen was enhanced by both treatments of NPK (0.5 and 1) as well as BAP (50 and 75). NPK 0.75, BAP 75 and treatments combining the two treatments all showed greater overall plant biomass (FWP), which would suggest potential improvements to nutrient uptake, photosynthesis and growth under regular application of NPK and BAP (Fig. 6a).

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was conducted to examine the multivariate relationships among the measured traits. Variables were selected based on their relevance to flowering dates and related parameters. PCA1 and PCA2 explained 86% and 9.3% of the total variance, respectively. The similarity among treatments, considering PCA1 and PCA2, accounted for 63.3% and 14.5% of the variance, respectively, with a total explained variance of 77.8%. The PCA results are represented as shown in Fig. 6b, in which positive relationships were observed between growth and yield parameters (flower diameter, bud appearance date, number of flowers [17 May and 10 April], number of branches, height, rate of photosynthesis, shoot and root biomass, number of leaves, phosphorus content) and the following treatments: BAP 50, BAP 75, NPK 0.5 + BAP 50, NPK 0.5 + BAP 75, NPK 0.5. BAP alone or at low NPK levels, by itself or in combination, tends to positively influence plant growth, flowering, branching and nutrient accumulation (Fig. 6b).

Conclusion

Nano-NPK + BAP increased plant growth, amount of biomass produced, rate of photosynthesis, uptake of nutrients, and flowering traits as demonstrated in this pot experiment. The treatment which showed the most favourable results across all parameters was NPK1BAP75 when they were used together as growth enhancers in V. tricolor. The results suggest that the combination of both nutrient sources could be advantageous for increasing growth and reproductive performance through improved nutrient use efficiency and horticultural production; thus, future field studies will be required to establish the combined effectiveness of these components across the spectrum of growing environments.

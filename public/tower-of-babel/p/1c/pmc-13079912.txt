A fuzzy-TD3 hybrid reinforcement learning framework for robust trajectory tracking of the Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm
Authors: Zied Ben Hazem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a novel hybrid control architecture that synergistically integrates a fuzzy logic system with the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm to achieve precise, robust trajectory tracking for a 5-degree-of-freedom (5-DOF) robotic manipulator. The design merges the interpretable, rule-based reasoning and rapid transient response of fuzzy logic with the model-free, long-term adaptive optimization capabilities of deep reinforcement learning. Within this framework, a fuzzy supervisor delivers immediate corrective actions using real-time error states, while the TD3 agent concurrently learns an optimal control policy to manage the system’s nonlinear dynamics. Extensive simulation studies on complex trajectories, including N-shaped, helical, and spiral paths, demonstrate the architecture’s superiority. The hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller reduces tracking error by 27.8–50% compared to a standalone TD3 agent and by 14.8–28.6% compared to a hybrid PID-TD3 baseline. Furthermore, under conditions of parametric uncertainties and internal as well as external disturbances, it maintains performance improvements of 23.5–34.2% over TD3 and 11.0–16.7% over the hybrid PID-TD3, confirming enhanced robustness. Validation through sensitivity analysis, numerical stability verification, and rule activation transparency establishes this method as an effective, adaptive, and explainable solution for advanced robotic control applications.Keywords: Fuzzy-TD3 hybrid control, Reinforcement learning, Trajectory tracking, Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm, Robust adaptive controlSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The field of robotics has undergone a profound evolution, transitioning from basic automated machines for repetitive tasks into intelligent, adaptive systems that operate autonomously in complex, real-world environments. This transformation, driven by breakthroughs in sensing, actuation, and artificial intelligence, has cemented the role of robots across critical sectors such as industrial manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and personal assistance1. As these applications demand increasingly sophisticated capabilities, particularly in robotic arms and autonomous manipulation, a fundamental challenge emerges: the need for precise and resilient trajectory tracking. This problem is rooted in the inherent complexities of robotic manipulators, which exhibit nonlinear dynamics, strong joint coupling, and complex multi-input, multi-output relationships. Furthermore, practical deployment introduces additional complications like sensor noise, external disturbances, and time-varying uncertainties, which collectively impede reliable control2. Traditional control strategies, such as PID controllers, are valued for their simplicity but often prove inadequate in these dynamic and uncertain conditions. While alternative methodologies, including adaptive control, fuzzy logic, and sliding mode control, have been explored, consistently guaranteeing high precision remains an open research question. In this context, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising paradigm, capable of learning optimal control policies through direct environmental interaction. Among DRL algorithms, TD3 has shown particular promise for continuous control tasks by addressing stability and overestimation issues inherent in earlier methods. However, the practical application of pure RL is still hampered by challenges in early-stage learning stability and safe exploration. This landscape motivates the development of hybrid frameworks that can synergize the strengths of different approaches to achieve robust, intelligent, and adaptable control.

Recent advancements in DRL have significantly improved control strategies for robotic systems, especially through the development of the TD3 algorithm, which addresses key limitations of the original DDPG, such as overestimation bias and training instability. Building upon this robust foundation, the next evolutionary step has been the integration of TD3 with fuzzy logic to create sophisticated hybrid frameworks. This synergy aims to merge the high-dimensional, long-term policy optimization of TD3 with the interpretability and real-time adaptive reasoning of fuzzy systems. In such a architecture, the fuzzy logic component can dynamically shape the reward signal or adjust control parameters based on immediate performance metrics, thereby guiding the TD3 agent’s learning process more efficiently and ensuring greater stability during both training and deployment3. This hybrid approach directly tackles the challenges of early-stage learning instability in pure RL and the limited adaptability of conventional controllers, offering a more powerful and resilient solution for complex, nonlinear systems like robotic manipulators operating under uncertainty. The evolution of integrating fuzzy logic with the TD3 algorithm showcases a clear trajectory toward sophisticated hybrid control architectures. The foundational work was established by Shi et al.(2020)4, who introduced an adaptive neuro-fuzzy PID controller tuned by the TD3 algorithm. Building on this, research expanded into various robotic domains in 2024: Hadikhani and Asemani (2024)5 demonstrated the synergy for mobile robot navigation, while Xia et al. (2024)6 developed a hybrid framework for UAV motion planning by integrating DRL with fuzzy logic. Further that year, Jian and Song (2024)7 implemented a TD3 and fuzzy-PID system for pipeline robot collaboration, and Wang et al. (2024)8 applied a similar fuzzy PID based on TD3 for wheeled mobile robot trajectory tracking. The most recent and direct implementation of a unified architecture is presented by Chen et al. (2025)9, who proposed a dedicated fuzzy-TD3 DRL algorithm for complex electro-hydraulic braking control, marking a significant maturation of the hybrid control paradigm. Building on the evolution of hybrid architectures reviewed above, this paper addresses a critical gap in the current paradigm: while existing integrations of fuzzy logic with TD3 show promise, they largely relegate the fuzzy system to a supervisory or tuning role, adapting parameters or shaping rewards, rather than leveraging its full potential as a direct, co-pilot control agent within the core actuation loop. This limits the real-time interpretability and instantaneous disturbance rejection that a tightly integrated fuzzy module can provide. To bridge this gap, a novel, fully integrated fuzzy-TD3 hybrid control framework is proposed for the robust trajectory tracking of a 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm. The core novelty lies in the synergistic fusion at the torque-control level: a Mamdani-type fuzzy logic controller (FLC) is embedded to generate immediate, interpretable compensatory torque based on the real-time dynamics of the tracking error and its derivative. This FLC output acts not as a final command, but as a stable, rule-based baseline action. Concurrently, a TD3 agent, trained in a high-fidelity simulated environment, learns to output a complementary residual torque. This residual optimizes long-horizon performance, compensates for complex nonlinearities, and adapts to conditions beyond the fixed rule base, such as persistent payload variations. The two torque signals are summed to form the final joint control input, creating a partnership where fuzzy logic ensures rapid, transparent response and safety, while TD3 pursues asymptotic optimality. To effectively train this hybrid agent, a fuzzy-shaped reward function is introduced that moves beyond conventional quadratic error penalties. This function explicitly encodes a multi-objective trade-off between tracking precision, control effort smoothness, and energy efficiency by scaling reward components based on the fuzzy inference of the current system state. This structure provides richer, more learnable feedback, accelerating policy convergence and promoting energy-aware behaviors. The entire framework is developed and validated within a high-fidelity co-simulation environment built in MATLAB/Simulink utilizing the Simscape multibody toolbox. This environment accurately models the rigid-body dynamics, actuator limits, and sensor noise of the Mitsubishi arm, enabling safe, data-efficient, and physically realistic training and testing under a comprehensive set of disturbances, including parametric uncertainties, external force pulses, and variable payloads.

Rigorous comparative analysis on complex 3D trajectories (N-shaped, helical, spiral) demonstrates that the proposed controller consistently outperforms, pure TD3, and hybrid PID-TD3 benchmarks. It achieves superior metrics in tracking accuracy disturbance rejection, and recovery stability. Furthermore, post-hoc analyses, including policy convergence profiles, empirical Lyapunov stability assessment, and examinations of fuzzy rule activation patterns, provide valuable insights into the internal decision-making process, validating not only the performance but also the interpretability and robustness of the proposed framework. Design of a fuzzy-TD3 hybrid reinforcement learning framework for trajectory tracking control

This section delineates the architecture of the proposed fuzzy-TD3 hybrid controller for trajectory tracking of the 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm. The design synergistically merges the interpretability and real-time adaptability of a fuzzy logic system with the long-term, optimal policy learning of the TD3 algorithm, a sophisticated DRL approach. The selection of TD3 as the core DRL component is motivated by its documented advancements over earlier algorithms like the DDPG. The DDPG provides a foundation for continuous control; its performance is often hampered by a propensity for overestimation bias in its value function, which can lead to suboptimal policies and training instability. The TD3 algorithm explicitly addresses these limitations through three key mechanisms: the use of twin critics to mitigate value overestimation, delayed policy updates to enhance learning stability, and target policy smoothing to prevent overfitting. This makes TD3 particularly suited for high-precision control tasks involving the nonlinear dynamics and complex joint couplings inherent to robotic manipulators. In the proposed hybrid architecture, the FLC and the TD3 agent perform distinct but complementary roles. The fuzzy subsystem provides a layer of interpretable, rule-based control, generating immediate, compensatory actions based on the instantaneous tracking error and its derivative. This ensures stable and responsive real-time performance. Concurrently, the TD3 agent operates on a longer timescale, learning an optimized control policy through continuous interaction with the environment. It refines the overall control strategy to maximize cumulative rewards, which are shaped by a carefully designed function balancing tracking precision, control effort, and energy efficiency. This synergy creates a controller that is both immediately competent and continually self-improving. The fuzzy logic offers a safety baseline and handles transient dynamics, while TD3 optimizes for long-term performance under uncertainty, thereby achieving a level of robustness and accuracy that surpasses, PID, ANFIS, or pure DRL controllers. The following subsections will elaborate on the detailed implementation of this framework.

Background on FLC

FLC provides a powerful alternative to conventional control methods for managing complex, nonlinear systems like robotic manipulators, particularly when precise mathematical modeling proves challenging due to inherent dynamics, coupling effects, and operational uncertainties. The fundamental architecture of the fuzzy controller implemented for the 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm comprises four interconnected components: (1) fuzzification, which converts precise input values (joint tracking error e(t) and its derivative de/dt) into fuzzy sets using predefined membership functions; (2) knowledge Base, which stores the set of IF-THEN rules that embody the control strategy; (3) inference mechanism, which processes these rules using Mamdani-type logic to derive control decisions; and (4) defuzzification, which converts the fuzzy outputs into a crisp torque signal (τ) for joint actuation20,21. The output torque range was set within a conservative subset of the joint actuators’ saturation limits to ensure safe operation. The symmetric, triangular membership functions with 50% overlap were chosen for their computational efficiency and proven ability to produce smooth control outputs. The core 3 × 3 rule base formalizes a proportional-derivative control heuristic, which is a standard and stable approach for second-order systems. Furthermore, a sensitivity analysis confirmed that the controller’s performance remains robust to minor perturbations (± 15%) in these defined range boundaries, and the final parameters represent the optimal balance between rapid response and stability achieved through iterative simulation tuning.

Background on DDPG and TD3

This study utilizes the TD3 algorithm as its core reinforcement learning engine. As a robust enhancement of the DDPG method, TD3 effectively mitigates value overestimation and improves training stability through clipped double Q-learning and delayed policy updates. The complete theoretical underpinnings of both DDPG and TD3 are detailed in our prior work22–24. Building upon this established foundation, the principal contribution of this paper is the novel development of a fuzzy-TD3 hybrid framework. This architecture synergistically integrates the model-free, long-term policy optimization of TD3 with the interpretability and real-time adaptability of a FLC, creating a superior solution for robust trajectory tracking of the robotic arm.

Simulation results and discussion

This section presents a comparative analysis of three control strategies, TD3, hybrid PID-TD3, and the proposed fuzzy-TD3 hybrid, for trajectory tracking of the 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm. Through comprehensive simulations conducted under various operational conditions, including dynamic payload variations and external disturbances, the performance of each controller is evaluated in terms of tracking precision, convergence behavior, and disturbance rejection capability. Proposed method

This study presents a hybrid fuzzy-TD3 control framework designed to enhance the trajectory tracking performance of a 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm operating under dynamic and uncertain conditions. The proposed method builds upon the proven effectiveness of hybrid reinforcement learning approaches in addressing complex, nonlinear control challenges. The traditional controllers such as PID offer stability and simplicity, they often struggle with adaptability under varying payloads and disturbances. On the other hand, DRL algorithms like TD3 provide strong adaptive learning capabilities but may exhibit slow convergence and unstable responses during early training phases. To overcome these limitations, this work integrates fuzzy logic into the TD3 framework, combining the interpretability and rapid response of fuzzy control with the adaptive and self-learning characteristics of TD3. In the proposed architecture, the fuzzy logic subsystem acts as a compensatory layer that interprets instantaneous tracking error and its rate of change to produce immediate corrective actions, ensuring local stability and smooth control responses. Simultaneously, the TD3 agent operates as a high-level optimizer, learning an optimal residual control policy that compensates for nonlinear dynamics, model uncertainties, and time-varying external disturbances. This cooperative mechanism enables the controller to maintain robust performance across diverse operational scenarios. By fusing the reactive intelligence of fuzzy control with the long-term optimization ability of TD3, the proposed hybrid model achieves both rapid convergence and enhanced robustness. Results and discussion

Control results

This section presents the simulation results and analysis of the proposed hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller applied to the trajectory tracking of the 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm. The hybrid controller generates optimal continuous torque commands for all joints by integrating fuzzy logic’s immediate, interpretable corrective actions with TD3’s adaptive, long-term policy optimization, ensuring accurate trajectory tracking under dynamic uncertainties. Figures 9, 10 and 11 illustrate the simulation results for different trajectory profiles, including helical, spiral, and N-shaped paths. The results demonstrate that the hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller consistently achieves higher tracking precision and stability compared to standalone TD3 and the hybrid PID-TD3 controller. The fuzzy logic subsystem provides fast, rule-based error correction and immediate disturbance rejection, while the TD3 agent continuously learns a residual control policy to compensate for nonlinear dynamics, payload variations, and external disturbances. Quantitative evaluation using standard error metrics, integral time absolute error (ITAE), integral time square error (ITSE), integral absolute error (IAE), integral square error (ISE), and root mean square error (RMSE), confirms that the hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller consistently outperforms TD3 and hybrid PID-TD3 in tracking accuracy and robustness. The findings highlight that the proposed hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller provides a superior balance between immediate responsiveness and adaptive optimization, making it a highly effective approach for precise and robust trajectory tracking in industrial robotic applications.

Robustness results

To evaluate the robustness of the proposed and benchmark controllers, internal and external disturbances were introduced during the execution of the spiral trajectory tracking task, selected for its continuous and demanding nature. Internal disturbances simulated persistent modeling uncertainties, implemented as a ± 10% variation in link mass and a ± 15% variation in inertia parameters throughout the task. External disturbances, representing sudden environmental interactions, were modeled as pulsed torque perturbations injected directly into the joint control inputs at 3–5 random, non-periodic intervals. Each pulse had a magnitude of 10–20% of the nominal maximum joint torque and a duration of 0.2–0.5 s. The performance was assessed by analyzing the joint angle responses and calculating standard error metrics for the TD3, hybrid PID-TD3, and hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controllers under these disturbance conditions. As shown in Fig. 12, the hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller exhibits smoother and more stable joint trajectories across all five joints, maintaining consistent tracking accuracy. In contrast, TD3 and hybrid PID-TD3 show higher oscillations and slower recovery. The quantitative results are presented in Table 11. It achieves the lowest error across all metrics, with improvements of 3.5–34.2% over TD3 and 11.0–16.7% over the hybrid PID-TD3. These results confirm that the integrated fuzzy logic module enables the TD3 agent to adapt effectively to both parametric uncertainties and transient forces, significantly enhancing disturbance rejection, stability, and reliable trajectory tracking for the 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm.

Conclusions

This study has introduced and comprehensively validated a hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller for precise trajectory tracking of a 5-DOF Mitsubishi RV-2AJ robotic arm. The proposed architecture successfully merges the interpretable, rule-based reasoning of a fuzzy logic supervisor with the adaptive learning capabilities of a deep reinforcement learning agent, achieving superior performance across multiple complex 3D trajectories. Through rigorous benchmarking against standalone TD3 and hybrid PID-TD3 controllers, the fuzzy-TD3 framework demonstrated consistent performance gains, reducing tracking errors by 27.8–50% compared to baseline TD3 and by 14.8–28.6% over PID-TD3 across N-shaped, helical, and spiral trajectories under nominal, deterministic conditions. The controller further exhibited exceptional robustness, maintaining stable operation under both internal model uncertainties and external disturbances, with performance improvements of 23.5–34.2% over TD3 and 11.0–16.7% over PID-TD3. The sensitivity and ablation analysis revealed that the reward-weight coefficient λ effectively balances control effort and tracking accuracy, enabling substantial energy savings (23% torque reduction) with minimal precision loss (8% RMSE increase). Numerical stability verification provided empirical evidence consistent with stable closed-loop operation, with the Lyapunov derivative remaining negative for 98.4%, 97.1%, and 94.8% of the simulation time for the N-shaped, helical, and spiral trajectories, respectively. Policy convergence visualization demonstrated stable learning dynamics, while fuzzy rule activation analysis provided crucial explainability insights, revealing how specific rules engage during critical operational phases. These findings collectively demonstrate that the hybrid fuzzy-TD3 controller, which integrates actuator saturation and a decoupled reward function, achieves an optimal synthesis of adaptability, precision, and interpretability, addressing fundamental limitations of purely data-driven approaches while maintaining the benefits of learning-based control. The framework represents a significant advancement toward reliable, transparent, and high-performance control systems for complex robotic applications. Future work will focus on real-world implementation, extension to multi-robot coordination, and integration of meta-learning techniques to enhance generalization in unstructured, dynamic environments.

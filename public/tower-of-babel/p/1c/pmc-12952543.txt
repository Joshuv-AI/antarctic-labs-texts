Redesigning Computer-aided Design and Manufacturing Guides for Mandibular Reconstruction: Dental Reference Cap and Synchronized Screw-Hole Guides
Authors: Chen-Hsuan Yen, Jian-Jr Lee, Shyun-Jing Wee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Computer-aided design/manufacturing with a free fibula flap has improved mandibular reconstruction, yet the adoption of patient-specific mandibular plates remains limited by cost and regulatory barriers. Premade mini-plates (MPs) are inexpensive and widely available but lack contouring capacity, making positional accuracy essential. We developed an innovative computer-aided design/manufacturing guide system incorporating a dental reference cap and synchronized screw-hole guides to bridge the gap between virtual planning and surgical execution. The dental reference cap anchors the guides to residual teeth, enhancing stability without requiring extensive bone contact and reducing tumor spillage risk. Synchronized screw holes repurpose temporary fixation points as reference sites for positioning guides on both the mandible and fibula. This enables the fibular segments to be assembled into a single reconstruction block before transfer, minimizing drilling, preserving periosteal vascularity, and achieving precise alignment with the mandibular defect. A 42-year-old man with right lower alveolar squamous cell carcinoma underwent mandibular reconstruction using this system. Postoperatively, he tolerated a full diet with stable occlusion, and functional outcomes remained favorable at 15 months. Quantitative analysis demonstrated a mean deviation of 1.94 mm, a root mean square of 3.27 mm, and 73.9% of vertices within ±2 mm. Key mandibular landmarks were reproduced with submillimeter accuracy. By integrating a dental reference cap and synchronized screw-hole guides, this system achieves accuracy comparable to patient-specific mandibular plates while retaining the cost-effectiveness and accessibility of MPs, offering a practical and reproducible solution for mandibular reconstruction.

Computer-aided design and manufacturing (CAD/CAM) with 3-dimensional (3D) printing has significantly advanced mandibular reconstruction using a free fibula flap (FFF).1 CAD/CAM-assisted FFF reconstruction includes fibular and mandibular cutting guides, positioning guide, and fixation plates.

Although patient-specific mandibular reconstruction plates (PSMP) provide excellent precision, their high cost and regulatory restrictions limit widespread use.2,3 Premade reconstruction plates are more accessible but remain bulky, may interfere with osteointegrated dental implants, and carry a risk of localized bone atrophy around screw holes.4,5 In contrast, premade mini-plates (MPs) are thin, cost-effective, and provide sufficient stability.6

Nevertheless, discrepancies persist between preoperative virtual planning and surgical outcomes. Even minor deviations in reconstruction can accumulate and compromise function, particularly when altering the positional relationship between fibular segments and residual mandible.7 To address this challenge, we introduced an innovative CAD/CAM guide system incorporating a dental reference cap and synchronized screw-hole guides, designed to achieve precise fibula–mandible alignment while maintaining affordability and practicality, thereby bridging the gap between virtual planning and surgical execution.

CASE PRESENTATION

A 42-year-old man with right lower alveolar squamous cell carcinoma underwent mandibular reconstruction using an FFF.

Preoperative Virtual Planning

Preoperative computed tomography scans of the mandible and fibula (0.625-mm slice) enabled 3D modeling. Virtual surgical planning with Geomagic Design X simulated mandibular defect reconstruction and determined precise osteotomy planes. Three patient-specific guides—mandibular cutting, fibular cutting, and positioning—were subsequently designed and fabricated with J3 DentaJet 3D printer.

Design of Dental Reference Cap and Synchronized Screw-Hole Guides

A dental reference cap (Fig. 1, orange circle) was incorporated into the mandibular cutting guide, using the patient’s remaining teeth as stable reference points to enhance stability and accuracy during osteotomy.

Synchronized screw-hole guide design. The mandibular holes predrilled by the cutting guide (M1, M2) for temporary fixation later served as reference holes for the position guide (P1, P2). The fibular holes predrilled by the cutting guide (F3, F4) for temporary fixation later served as reference holes for the position guide (P3, P4).

Synchronized screw holes (Fig. 1) in the mandibular cutting guide initially functioned as temporary fixation holes (M1, M2) during osteotomy and subsequently as precise reference holes (P1, P2) for attaching the positioning guide. The fibular cutting guide used the same principle, with holes (F3, F4) providing initial temporary fixation and later serving as reference holes (P3, P4) for securing fibular segments onto the positioning guide, ensuring accurate alignment with the mandibular defect.

Surgical Technique

During surgery, the mandible cutting guide was positioned using the dental reference cap and secured with fixation holes before osteotomy. For fibular harvest, the cutting guide was stabilized with both fixation holes and a malleolus cap, the latter reported to reduce sliding and rotational errors before osteotomy.8 The fibular segments were aligned and fixed onto the positioning guide through predrilled synchronized screw holes, forming a single-unit reconstruction block. This unified block was then transferred and aligned with the mandibular defect via corresponding predrilled holes on the mandible.

Reconstruction stability was achieved using 0.8-mm-thick titanium MPs (MatrixMIDFACE Plating System, DePuy Synthes). Each MP was secured with three 1.5-mm self-tapping titanium screws at both the distal and posterior ends. After confirming stable fixation, the positioning guide was removed, and microvascular anastomoses were performed using 9-0 nylon (Ethilon, Ethicon). The operative time was 300 minutes, with an ischemia time of 30 minutes.

RESULTS

At 8 weeks postoperatively, the patient tolerated a full diet without dysphagia or malocclusion. Both the head-and-neck region and the donor site healed uneventfully, with no wound-related problems or gait disturbance. At 13 months, occlusion remained stable, and a computed tomography scan confirmed solid union between fibular segments and the mandible (Fig. 2). Accuracy assessment by 3D superimposition demonstrated close concordance between the postoperative model and preoperative plan, using MeshLab software (Visual Computing Lab, Pisa, Italy). At 15 months, the patient achieved excellent mandibular function, unrestricted mastication, intact speech, and favorable UW-QOL (University of Washington Quality of Life Questionnaire) scores, supporting the clinical relevance of achieving precise reconstruction through our CAD/CAM guide system (Table 1) (Fig. 3).

DISCUSSION

Recently, CAD/CAM-assisted FFF reconstruction has significantly improved the precision of mandibular reconstruction. However, the fixation plate choice remains controversial. PSMPs offer precise fixation and may shorten ischemia time, but are limited by cost, global availability, and regulations. In contrast, premade MPs are cost-effective, thin, and broadly accessible, yet their lack of contouring capacity demands high positional accuracy. Studies report that even with meticulous preoperative planning, positional deviations of mandibular cutting guides can range from 0.2 to 8.7 mm.9 Therefore, achieving high positional accuracy with a widely accessible and affordable fixation system remains a critical unmet need.

The described CAD/CAM guide system integrates a dental reference cap and synchronized screw-hole guides to address current limitations. The dental reference cap, anchored to the patient’s residual teeth, enhances guide stability and precision without extensive bone contact. This reduces bone exposure and minimizes the risk of tumor spillage compared with conventional guides. The synchronized screw holes repurpose temporary fixation points as reference sites for positioning guides on both the mandible and fibula, reducing unnecessary drilling, preserving periosteal vascular supply, and enabling fibular segments to be assembled into a single-unit reconstruction block before transfer. This approach achieves positioning accuracy comparable to PSMPs while maintaining the accessibility of standard MPs, conceptually echoing the drill-hole sharing technique described by Wang et al10 but specifically adapted to the requirements of MP fixation.

In the automated Hausdorff distance analysis, the mean deviation between the surgical plan and postoperative reconstruction was 1.94 mm, with a root mean square (RMS) deviation of 3.27 mm, and 73.94% of vertices within ±2 mm. The higher RMS reflected sensitivity to a few localized extremes rather than generalized inaccuracy. Per-vertex and color mapping confirmed that these deviations were expected—stemming from the concavity after removal of 3 incisors and convexities from surgical clips—which disproportionately inflated RMS values. Despite this, the low mean deviation, high proportion of vertices within ±2 mm, and submillimeter replication of key mandibular landmarks (condyles, gnathion) demonstrated excellent geometric fidelity, comparable to or exceeding reported ranges.

For MP fixation, where no contouring capacity exists to offset positional errors, achieving maximal reconstruction accuracy is critical. The novel CAD/CAM guide system achieves this precision while preserving periosteal integrity and circumventing the financial and regulatory challenges of PSMPs. By securing fibular segments to the positioning guide via synchronized screw-hole guides before transfer, the system creates a unified reconstruction block, providing intraoperative handling and stability comparable to PSMPs. Its simplicity, compatibility with widely available hardware, and cost-effectiveness indicate strong potential for adoption across diverse surgical settings.

A potential limitation of this approach lies in its applicability to edentulous or dentition-compromised patients. However, further innovations to enhance stability and accuracy in such cases are warranted in future studies.

CONCLUSIONS

The use of MPs in mandibular reconstruction requires high positional accuracy. Incorporating our novel dental reference cap and synchronized screw-hole guide design into CAD/CAM surgical guides ensures precise segment alignment while minimizing periosteal disruption. This approach combines the accessibility and cost-effectiveness of MPs with positional stability comparable to patient-specific plates, providing a practical and high-precision solution for mandibular reconstruction.

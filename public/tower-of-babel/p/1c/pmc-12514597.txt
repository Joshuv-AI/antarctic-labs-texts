Radiomics models predict early neurodevelopment in infants with congenital cardiac septal defects
Authors: Shuting Cheng, Meijiao Zhu, Feng Yang, Jingjing Zhong, Mingcui Fu, Mingwen Yang, Ying Wang, Xuming Mo, Ming Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with congenital cardiac septal defect (CCSD) may still have neurodevelopmental abnormalities. This study aims to establish a combined model of brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and clinical features for early prediction of neurodevelopment in infants with CCSDs.MethodsForty-eight infants diagnosed with CCSD by cardiac ultrasonography were prospectively enrolled. Neurodevelopmental outcome was assessed at 1 year of age using the Gesell Developmental Scale. Radiomics features of neonatal brain MRI and clinical characteristics at birth and follow-up were obtained. After the relationship between neurodevelopmental outcomes and clinical or radiomics features was assessed using Pearson and Spearman correlation analysis, we performed multiple stepwise linear regression models to determine the most significant predictors of developmental status.ResultsAt 1 year of age, 25% (12/48) of infants with CCSD exhibited developmental delays, with the highest percentage of delays observed in language development (10.4%). Multiple stepwise linear regression analysis showed that shape features were independent predictors of gross motor (standardized coefficients β=−0.34; P=0.02). Guardians’ education was an independent predictor of fine motor (standardized coefficients β=0.36; P=0.01). Wavelet-HLH-gray-level co-occurrence matrix-maximum correlation coefficient were independent predictors of adaptation (standardized coefficients β=−0.32; P=0.03). Wavelet-LHH-gray-level size zone matrix-small area low gray level emphasis (standardized coefficients β=0.31; P=0.03) and LoG-neighbourhood gray-tone difference matrix-strength (3D, σ=10 mm) (standardized coefficients β=0.29; P=0.03) were independent predictors of language development. Birth weight (standardized coefficients β=0.36; P<0.001) and wavelet-HLH-GLCM-cluster shade (standardized coefficients β=−0.27; P=0.04) were independent predictors of personal-social behavior.ConclusionsSome infants with CCSD may have neurodevelopmental delays at 1 year of age. A combined model based on radiomics features of neonatal brain MRI and clinical factors may be useful in predicting early neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD.Keywords: Neurodevelopment, radiomics, congenital cardiac septal defect (CCSD), prediction model

Introduction

Atrial septal defect (ASD) and ventricular septal defect (VSD) are the most common congenital heart diseases (CHD) (1). Clinical studies demonstrate that patients with congenital cardiac septal defect (CCSD) may still have neurodevelopmental abnormalities, such as poorer neuropsychological scores, social cognition, and executive function (2-4), even when peripheral oxygen saturation is normal. Congenital heart defects and neurodevelopmental disorders may share a common genetic basis (5,6). A long-term study suggests that these deficits will persist until adolescence and young adulthood (7). Therefore, early detection of brain development changes in CCSD is of great importance for clinicians to give appropriate protective treatment in time, and ultimately improve neurological function.

The prenatal environment may have a crucial influence on brain development in children with CHD (8-10). Neonatal brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides a better picture of the overall impact of the prenatal environment on brain development. Radiomics, which converts images into useful high-resolution spatial data through high-throughput quantitative analysis (11,12), has been shown to have strong application in non-neoplastic lesions in children, such as attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (13), autism (14), cognitive disorders (15), and brain development of preterm infants (16), which can provide more information and references to the clinic (12).

Brain development of CHD has attracted more and more attention in recent years. However, previous studies mostly focused on the brain alteration of complex CHD (17-21). And the studies were relatively concentrated in the school age and preschool age. Few studies have been conducted on uncomplicated congenital cardiac septal abnormalities even if it has a higher incidence. This study intends to obtain CCSD neonatal brain MRI and clinical features, as well as the follow up outcomes of the neurodevelopmental at one year of age. A combination model based on brain MRI radiomics and clinical features was constructed to predict early neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD. We hypothesized that the radiomics-clinical model could provide predictive information for early neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD, which could put forward for a new tool for early prediction and timely intervention for neurodevelopment in these infants. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2025-565/rc).

Methods

Study participants

The study included 48 CCSD (22) neonates confirmed by echocardiography at the Children’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University from March 2019 to February 2021. The brain MRI examination was also performed in 48 neonates. Enrollment criteria: (I) ASD, VSD, and ASD + VSD confirmed by routine echocardiography. None of the cases were accompanied by congestive heart failure requiring treatment and did not progress to pulmonary hypertension with right-to-left shunt and cyanosis; (II) the age of brain MRI examination was less than 28 days; (III) image quality met diagnostic requirements; (IV) the infants were followed up at 1 year of age according to the requirements of the study. Exclusion criteria: (I) newborns with other congenital malformations except CHD, nervous system infections, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, or metabolic diseases that will affect brain development in the later stage; (II) premature infants. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Children’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (No. 202412051-2). Informed consent was signed by all the patients’ legal guardians. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Clinical features

Basic information, including sex, gestational age, birth weight, maternal gestational age, heart defect size, and brain MRI imaging findings, was collected from the electronic medical records. The infants were divided into a normal group and an injured group according to the presence and absence of injuries, such as subarachnoid hemorrhage, subdural hemorrhage, and white matter injury. The enrolled infants were followed up at the age of 1 year, and their parents filled in a questionnaire to evaluate the basic condition of the infants, the family’s education, and socioeconomic level (18). Follow-up information includes height, weight, body mass index (BMI), upper arm circumference, head circumference, age of follow-up, family annual income, guardian education background, and cardiac defects of the infants at follow-up.

Neurodevelopmental outcomes

Forty-eight infants with CCSD were followed up at 1 year of age and their neurodevelopment was assessed using the Gesell Developmental Scale (GDS), which includes five areas, namely gross motor, fine motor, language, adaptive, and personal-social behavior. The results were determined by a development quotient (DQ), which was defined as normal by a score of 86 or greater, 76–85 as a boundary value, and 75 or lower as developmental delay (23).

MRI data collection

Siemens Avanto 1.5T (Munich, Germany) superconducting magnetic resonance scanner was used for MRI scanning with standard 8-channel orthogonal coils. Before scanning, all infants were given 5% chloral hydrate orally at a dose of 1 mL/kg, and their brains were scanned in the supine position after they fell asleep. During scanning, the newborn’s head and peripherals were restrained and protected with soft pads, and special earphones were worn to protect hearing (24). Axial diffusion weighted imaging sequence, repetition time: 3,200 ms, echo time: 99 ms, turning angle: 150°, field of view (FOV): 180 mm × 180 mm, layer thickness: 4.5 mm, B value: 0 and 800 s/mm2. Apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) images were generated automatically using the standard single exponential fitting on the magnetic resonance scanner.

Radiomics feature extraction

Manual mapping was performed to delineate brain parenchyma as region of interest (ROI) on ADC images using MRIcron software (Figure 1). The three-dimensional ROI of all cases was jointly completed by two pediatric neuroradiologists with experience in reading neonatal brain MRI. All of them were double-blind to the basic information and neurodevelopmental results of infants. The ROI maps for each patient were imported into 3D-Slicer software, and the radiomics module was used to extract radiomics features. Before feature extraction, all images were preprocessed. A total of 1,130 features were extracted from each patient. Spearman correlation analysis was used to perform feature dimensionality reduction on 1,130 image histological features. Highly correlated features with correlation coefficients greater than 0.7 were removed, resulting in 88 radiomics features, including 3 shape features, 2 first-order histogram features, 6 texture features, 15 LoG features and 62 Wavelet features.

Statistical analysis

After the normal distribution test in 88 radiomics features and 16 clinical features, Pearson correlation analysis was used for continuous variables with normal distribution, Spearman correlation analysis was used for skewness variables, and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for categorical variables to analyze their correlation with neurodevelopmental results. Multivariate stepwise linear regression analysis was used to evaluate the predictors of five neurodevelopment results. In correlation and multivariate analyses, P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. The final result was corrected by false discovery rate (FDR). All statistical analyses were performed in SPSS 25.0.

Results

General condition

A total of 48 children with CCSD were included (24 males and 24 females), including 43 cases of ASD, 2 cases of VSD, and 3 cases of ASD together with VSD. Brain MRI during the neonatal period showed abnormal imaging findings in 12 cases. The MR Images were graded according to the scoring system devised by Andropoulos et al. (19). The cases in this study were all mild brain injuries. At 1 year of age, echocardiography showed that 21 of them (43.75%) still had ASD or VSD. The general situation is shown in Table 1. Neurodevelopmental findings at 1 year of age showed (Table 1, Figure 2) the average gross motor quotient was 99.37±16.72, the average fine motor quotient was 89.93±8.74, the average adaptive behavioral quotient was 92.76±9.38, and the median score of language quotient was 94.05 (81.25, 101.26). The average personal-social DQ was 107.33±19.43. At least one developmental skill was delayed in 12/48 infants (25%), and language was delayed in 5/48 infants (10.42%); 4/48 (8.33%) infants had personal and social behavior delay; 4/48 (8.33%) of the infants had gross motor behavior delay; 3/48 cases (6.25%) had developmental delay of adaptive behavior;1/48 (2.08%) had fine motor delay. The histogram of GDS development results in infants with CCSD at 1 year of age is shown in Figure 1. There was no statistical difference in neurodevelopment at 1 year of age between CCSD newborns with brain injury and normal brain MRI findings (Table 2).

Correlation analysis of neurodevelopmental outcomes of 1-year-old infants

Correlation analysis showed that 1 clinical feature and 7 radiomics features were associated with gross motor scores, 3 clinical features and 1 radiomic feature were associated with fine motor, 2 radiomic features were associated with adaptation, 4 radiomic features were associated with language, and 1 clinical feature and 2 radiomic features were associated with personal-social behavior. After adjusting for multiple comparisons using FDR (P<0.05), the observed differences were not statistically significant (Table 3). However, in the unadjusted analysis, the variables showed nominal significance (original P<0.05), indicating the existence of potential biological correlations.

Multiple linear regression analysis of neurodevelopmental outcomes at 1 year of age

Gross motor, fine motor, personal-social, language and adaptive DQs were used as dependent variables. Factors with P<0.05 on correlation analysis were included in multiple linear regression. The multiple linear stepwise regression analyses showed (Table 4, Figure 3) that original-shape-surface volume ratio was an independent predictor of gross motor (standardized coefficients β=−0.34; P=0.02). Guardians’ education was an independent predictor of fine motor (standardized coefficients β=0.36; P=0.01). Wavelet-HLH-gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM)-maximum correlation coefficient (MCC) was an independent predictor of adaptive behavior (standardized coefficients β=−0.32; P=0.03). Wavelet-LHH-gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM)-small area low gray level emphasis (standardized coefficients β=0.31; P=0.03) and LoG-neighbourhood gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM)-strength (3D, σ=10 mm) (standardized coefficients β=0.29; P=0.03) were independent predictors of language development. Birth weight (standardized coefficients β=0.36; P<0.001) and Wavelet-HLH-GLCM-cluster shade (standardized coefficients β=−0.27; P=0.04) were independent predictors of personal-social behavior.

Discussion

Infants with CCSD are at risk of neurodevelopmental delays

In this study, infants with CCSD were followed up one year after birth. In the absence of surgical intervention, 12 infants were found to have at least one neurodevelopmental delay (25%) at 1 year of age, which were lower than most of the neurodevelopmental abnormalities in infants with postoperative and severe CHD but greater than in the study by Park et al. (20). Previous articles have reported that 9–60% (10,20,21) of infants with CHD have neurodevelopmental abnormalities at 1 year of age, which is consistent with our findings. Dittrich et al. (21) assessed the neurodevelopment of 90 post-surgical infants with CHD and found that 24 (27%) infants had a lower mean developmental quotient than the control group (2 standard deviations below the mean of the control group). As a common type of CHD, the neurodevelopment of patients with CCSD has received increasing attention. It is well known that the period from birth to 1 year of age is an important period for rapid brain development (25). Therefore, earlier attention to the brain development of infants with CCSD and timely clinical interventions are essential to improve the quality of life of the infants.

Radiomics-clinical model predicts early neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD

Multiple stepwise linear regression analysis of our study revealed that guardian education and birth weight were associated with fine motor and personal-social behavior, respectively. Neonatal brain MRI radiomics features were associated with neurodevelopmental outcomes, including gross motor, adaptive, language, and personal-social. This suggests that specific neonatal brain MRI imaging radiomics features and clinical characteristics may have predictive value for neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD at 1 year of age. It may help to implement early intervention in the clinic and improve the quality of survival of infants with CCSD. Furthermore, given that a subset of infants with CCSD may require surgical intervention later in life, neonatal brain MRI could be integrated into clinical protocols to inform decision-making processes. For instance, preoperative neuroimaging biomarkers may aid in optimizing surgical timing or refining risk-benefit assessments, particularly since cardiac surgery itself carries inherent neurodevelopmental risks.

The shape feature of the brain ADC map in the neonatal period was an independent predictor of gross motor activity in infants at 1 year of age. This feature may be related to the development of the cerebral cortex, i.e., cortical folding and the development of the sulcal gyrus. During brain development, cortical growth expansion and folding play a key role in the development of sensory-motor skills (26). Therefore, shape feature is interpretable for gross motor prediction. In addition, Wagner et al. (27) found that wavelet transform-based radiomics features were the main predictors of gross motor movements in their study of gross motor development in preterm infants. This suggests that the predictive significance of radiomics on gross motor is of interest.

The results of our study suggest that parental education level was positively associated with fine motor development at 1 year of age. This is similar to previous studies. For example, Park et al. (28) found in a study of normal newborns that the mother’s education was significantly associated with the infant’s neurodevelopment at 12 months of age (29). Analyzing the neurodevelopment of infants with hypoplastic left heart syndrome according to their mothers’ education, it was found that lower maternal education was associated with a delay in the progressive development of fine motor in the infants. As the study of parental education on infants’ growth and development may be related to normal neurodevelopmental processes, its effect on infants with CCSD needs to be further verified by studies with larger sample sizes and more rigorous controlled experiments.

Of the five neurodevelopmental outcomes in infants with CCSD at 1 year of age, the largest proportion (10.42%) was language delay. The main predictors were wavelet transform features and Laplace Gaussian transform. Which is consistent with previous findings on neurodevelopmental delays in children with CHD (30,31). For example, Rollins et al. (32) suggested the significance of cerebral white matter volume in predicting language development at 1 year of age in infants with CHD. Therefore, in non-operated non-cyanotic CHD, abnormalities in language development remain a key concern for children’s later growth and development. However, the study showed a non-significant relationship between language development and parental education. This may be due to infants not reaching the rapid developmental peak of language and communication development at 1 year of age. Therefore, long-term follow-up after 1 year of age is necessary.

Notably, the predictor of adaptive behavior in infants with CCSD is wavelet-HLH-GLCM-MCC. The predictor of personal-social behavior is wavelet-HLH-GLCM-cluster shade. The GLCM focuses on the grey-scale spatial correlation properties of lesions, and it is a commonly used method of second-order texture analysis (11,33). cluster shade reflects the skewness and homogeneity of GLCM. It was concluded that GLCM represents the heterogeneity within the tumor and shows unique advantages in identifying benign and malignant tumors and predicting tumor lymph node metastasis (34,35). The predictive significance of wavelet transform-based texture features for adaptive and personal-social development found in this study may suggest that texture features are also of predictive value in neurodevelopment in brain MRI.

Limitations

There are some limitations of this study. (I) The sample size of this experiment was medium. Although this study has the most follow-up data on 1-year-old infants with CCSD among the articles reported to date. However, the sample size is still relatively small, which may affect the accuracy of the results. However, all CHD cases in this study included only ASD and VSD and were clinically judged not to require cardiac surgery before 1 year of age, ensuring homogeneity of cases and interpretability of results. (II) In this study, after FDR adjustment for multiple comparisons (adjusted P<0.05), none of the observed differences reached statistical significance (correlation values are added in Table 3). However, variables showing nominal significance in unadjusted analyses (raw adjusted P<0.05) may indicate potential biological relevance. While strict multiplicity corrections are essential to mitigate false positives in high-dimensional radiomics analyses, they risk over penalizing exploratory findings in hypothesis-generating studies. This trade-off is particularly salient in radiomics, where feature dimensionality often exceeds conventional omics datasets. To address this, future investigations could implement a two-phase analytical strategy: employing relaxed FDR thresholds (e.g., 20%) in discovery cohorts to prioritize candidate radiomic biomarkers, followed by strict validation in independent cohorts. (III) The current radiomics model was developed and validated exclusively on a single-center cohort, which ensures internal consistency but limits generalizability to heterogeneous populations. External validation is indeed essential to confirm the robustness of imaging biomarkers across diverse clinical settings, scanner protocols, and patient demographics. However, due to the rarity of CCSDs with neonatal neuroimaging data and the ethical challenges of obtaining multi-center MRI datasets in this vulnerable population, we prioritized rigorous internal validation as a foundational step. Future studies will focus on collaborating with tertiary care centers to establish external validation cohorts. (IV) While our study classified all MRI abnormalities as ‘brain injury’ for analytical purposes, we recognize that certain findings (e.g., small subdural hemorrhages) may reflect birth-related trauma rather than CHD-specific mechanisms. However, given the overlapping prevalence of such lesions in both CHD and healthy neonatal populations, and the lack of statistical association with neurodevelopmental outcomes in our cohort, we elected to retain these cases to avoid selection bias. Future studies with larger cohorts and detailed etiological stratification are warranted to disentangle these effects.

Conclusions

In our study, we found that neonatal brain MRI imaging radiomics features of CCSD correlated with the neurodevelopment of infants at 1 year of age. This may provide a new assessment tool for predicting early neurodevelopment in infants with CCSD. Since some infants with CCSD may need surgery in the later stage, neonatal brain MRI might be introduced into clinical practice, which is valuable for clinical decision making, e.g., timing of surgery/decision to perform operations, as the surgery itself has neurological risks as well.

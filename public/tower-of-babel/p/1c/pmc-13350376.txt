Endovenous Radiofrequency Ablation for Varicose Veins
Authors: Jue Yang, Yuefeng Zhu, Lanting Hu, Lin Tang, Xupin Xie, Xiaojian Jia, Guangwei Yang, Xin Fang, Jinsong Jiang, Zhenjie Liu, Weiguo Fu
Published: 2026

Endovenous radiofrequency ablation (RFA) has become the first-line treatment for great saphenous vein (GSV) varicose veins, offering durable vein closure and favorable clinical outcomes compared with conventional surgery.1,2 Over the past decade, segmental RFA has been increasingly adopted globally due to its reproducibility, standardized energy delivery, and favorable safety profile.3 Although the reference device remains the standard in clinical practice guidelines,3,4 high-quality randomized evidence comparing newer systems against established devices remains limited in Asian populations.5 We therefore conducted a prospective, multicenter, randomized noninferiority trial to compare the efficacy and safety of the Sapheuns RFA system (investigational device; Suzhou Hengrui Hongyuan Medical Technology Co, Ltd) and the ClosureFast RFA system (reference device; Medtronic).

Methods

This prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label, parallel-group trial was conducted at 5 centers in China (March 2021-July 2022). The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Zhongshan Hospital (approval no. 2020-127R) and the institutional review boards of all participating centers. The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04671641).

Eligible patients were adults (aged 18-80 years) with symptomatic GSV varicose veins (Clinical Etiologic Anatomic Pathophysiologic [CEAP] class C2-C6) and duplex-confirmed truncal reflux (>0.5 seconds).6 Key exclusion criteria included pregnancy or breastfeeding, acute deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or superficial thrombophlebitis, uncorrectable coagulopathy, or arterial occlusive disease. All patients provided written informed consent. Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a centralized, web-based randomization system to receive either the investigational device or the reference device. Both systems use segmental thermal ablation at 120 °C. The reference device treats 7-cm vein segments with 20-second cycles. The investigational device uses a similar mechanism but features optimized generator software for real-time temperature feedback and energy modulation. Tumescent anesthesia was administered under ultrasound guidance. Concomitant sclerotherapy for tributaries was permitted. Procedure time was defined as the duration from skin puncture to catheter withdrawal. Follow-up visits were scheduled at 2 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months postprocedure.

The primary endpoint was the complete anatomic occlusion of the treated GSV at 6 months. Complete occlusion was defined as the absence of blood flow and noncompressibility of the treated segment. Secondary endpoints included technical success, changes in Venous Clinical Severity Score,7 CEAP classification, and device operability. Safety was assessed by the incidence of adverse events (AEs) defined as any untoward medical occurrence, unintended disease, or injury in subjects, regardless of whether it was related to the investigational medical device.4 Sample size was calculated assuming a 95% occlusion rate in the reference group, a noninferiority margin of −10%, a one-sided α of 0.025, and 80% power. Allowing for a 15% dropout rate, the target enrollment was 176 patients. The primary efficacy analysis was performed on the Full Analysis Set and Per-Protocol Set. The CMH tests were used for primary endpoints, t-tests or Wilcoxon tests for bivariate comparisons, and SAS 9.4 for risk difference derivations.

Results

Of the 174 patients randomized (88 in the investigational group and 86 in the reference group), baseline characteristics were well balanced (Table 1). Median age was 57.0 vs 58.5 years. The majority of patients had moderate-to-severe disease (CEAP ≥ C3). Intraoperative technical success was achieved in 100% of procedures in both groups. The mean procedure time was 30 [22.0, 55.0] minutes in the investigational group and 36.5 [21.0, 50.0] minutes in the reference group (P = 0.752), indicating equivalent operational efficiency. No device malfunctions occurred in either group.

At 6 months, complete occlusion was achieved in 100% (88 of 88) vs 98.84% (85 of 86) in the reference group with a risk difference of 1.16% (95% CI: −3.12% to 6.37%), confirming noninferiority (P < 0.001). Clinical improvement was substantial and comparable. Venous Clinical Severity Scores decreased significantly from baseline to 6 months in both groups (Investigational: 3.0 [2.0, 5.0] to 0 [0, 1.0]; 95% CI: −3.00 to −2.00; Reference: 4.0 [2.0, 5.0] to 0 [0, 1.5]; P = 0.560; 95% CI: −3.00 to −2.00). Similarly, 85.4% of patients in the investigational group and 86.8% in the reference group experienced an improvement in CEAP classification of at least one grade.

AEs occurred in 55.7% (49 of 88) vs 69.8% (60 of 86) (P = 0.061). Most AEs were mild and transient, such as ecchymosis and induration.8 DVT rates were 2.3% (2 of 88) vs 9.3% (8 of 86) (P = 0.056). All DVT cases were asymptomatic muscular vein thromboses detected only by protocol-mandated screening ultrasound and resolved with anticoagulation.9 No endothermal heat-induced thrombosis class III/IV or pulmonary embolism occurred.

Discussion

This multicenter randomized trial provides high-quality evidence that the novel Sapheuns RFA system is noninferior to the established ClosureFast system. The 100% occlusion rate at 6 months matches the highest benchmarks reported in the literature.3,4 Importantly, the identical procedure times and 100% technical success rate suggest that the investigational device is user-friendly and requires no additional learning curve for surgeons familiar with segmental RFA.

Regarding safety, the investigational device showed a favorable profile. Although the difference in DVT rates (2.3% vs 9.3%) did not reach statistical significance, the rate in the reference group was higher than typically reported in retrospective studies. Several factors may explain the favorable safety profile observed with the investigational device. The optimized generator software provides real-time temperature feedback and energy modulation, potentially reducing thermal injury to surrounding tissues. The observed DVT rate difference, while not statistically significant, may warrant further investigation in larger trials with longer follow-up. The rigorous, protocol-mandated ultrasound screening likely explains the higher DVT detection rate compared with routine clinical practice.10

Limitations include the 6-month follow-up period, although early occlusion is a strong predictor of long-term durability. In addition, the open-label design was unavoidable due to device appearance, but objective ultrasound assessment minimized bias. In conclusion, the Sapheuns system offers a safe, effective, and operationally efficient alternative for the treatment of varicose veins, expanding the therapeutic armamentarium for vascular surgeons.

Conclusions

The investigational dual-zone heating RFA system demonstrated noninferior efficacy and comparable safety to the established reference device for treating GSV varicose veins. With equivalent procedural efficiency and no additional learning curve required, this system represents a viable alternative for vascular surgeons, expanding therapeutic options for patients with chronic venous disease.

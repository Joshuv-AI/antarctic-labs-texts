A Comparison of Modified Radical Mastectomy, Breast Conservation Therapy, and Nipple-sparing Mastectomy With Immediate Implant Reconstruction
Authors: Chi Pan, Hui Jiang, Jian Zhou, Kai Sang, Xuchen Huang, Tongbo Yi, Qingtao Ni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:This study evaluated and compared the perioperative outcomes and quality of life associated with modified radical mastectomy (MRM), breast-conserving surgery (BCS), and nipple- and areola-sparing subcutaneous mastectomy with immediate breast reconstruction (NSM-IBR).Methods:This retrospective cohort study included 105 patients with breast cancer, stratified into MRM (n = 35), BCS (n = 35), and NSM-IBR (n = 35) groups. The perioperative outcomes (operative times, intraoperative blood loss, and recent complications) were subsequently compared across the 3 groups. Differences in patient satisfaction were assessed using the 36-item short form health survey scale 3 months after surgery.Results:In the NSM-IBR group, the mean operative time was 207.1 ± 38.7 minutes, significantly longer than that in the MRM and BCS groups (P < 0.001). The intraoperative blood loss in the BCS group was significantly lower than in the MRM and NSM-IBR groups (P < 0.05). The total 36-item short form health survey score in the NSM-IBR group was higher than in the BCS and MRM groups (80.03 ± 1.27 versus 54.51 ± 3.35, P < 0.001; 80.03 ± 1.27 versus 78.46 ± 1.42, P = 0.004).Conclusions:NSM-IBR warrants promotion as a means to enhance the postoperative psychological and physiological health of patients with breast cancer. Surgeons should undergo specialized training to reduce complications associated with the procedural complexity of NSM-IBR.

Takeaways

Question: How do modified radical mastectomy, breast-conserving surgery, and nipple- and areola-sparing subcutaneous mastectomy combined with immediate breast reconstruction compare in perioperative outcomes and patient quality of life?

Findings: In this retrospective cohort of 105 breast cancer patients, nipple-sparing mastectomy with immediate reconstruction resulted in the highest quality of life scores despite longer operative times and higher costs, whereas breast-conserving surgery was associated with the lowest intraoperative blood loss.

Meaning: Nipple-sparing mastectomy with immediate reconstruction significantly enhances postoperative quality of life and represents a valuable surgical option for eligible breast cancer patients, warranting broader clinical adoption and specialized surgical training.

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common cancer among women. In 2022, an estimated 429,105 cases of BC in women were diagnosed in China.1 Recently, the comprehensive management of BC has substantially improved. Although radical resection surgery can yield a curative outcome, there is a growing emphasis on enhancing breast appearance and quality of life for patients. Modified radical mastectomy (MRM) entails the surgical excision of breast tissue, adjacent tissues, and proximal lymph nodes.2 MRM combined with 1-stage breast prosthesis implantation can satisfy patients’ physical aesthetic needs and enhance their psychosocial well-being.3 Patients undergoing breast-conserving surgery (BCS) typically receive whole-breast irradiation. BCS as a surgical therapeutic option is a more significant factor related to sexual satisfaction than MRM.4 Patients undergoing BCS experienced shorter operative times, less intraoperative blood loss, reduced postoperative drainage, and shorter hospital stays than patients undergoing MRM.5 Nipple- and areola-sparing subcutaneous mastectomy (NSM) with immediate breast reconstruction (IBR) has become one of the most commonly performed breast procedures for BC.6

Surgical intervention may adversely affect the psychological impact on women’s body image and mental health. Health-related quality of life outcomes are better among patients undergoing MRM than those undergoing BCS.7 Younger BCS survivors and those who underwent MRM have more issues with body image postsurgery.8 Consequently, selecting the most appropriate surgical procedure for patients with BC can be challenging.

The oncological safety of preserving the nipple–areola complex (NAC) during therapeutic mastectomy was initially supported by extrapolating the 20-year overall survival results of the lumpectomy-alone group from the National Surgical Adjuvant Breast and Bowel Project B-06 trial.9 Patients who underwent lumpectomy without postoperative radiotherapy, and thus retained an untreated NAC, demonstrated no survival disadvantage compared with those who underwent mastectomy at 20-year follow-up.10 This prospective, randomized evidence established the conceptual basis for the subsequent advancement of nipple-sparing mastectomy in patients with BC. In BC surgery, if the distance between the tumor and the nipple exceeds 2.5 cm, the tumor is unifocal, there is no lymph node metastasis, and the tumor is hormone receptor-positive and HER2-negative, the NAC may be retained.11 This retrospective study aimed to compare perioperative outcomes (operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and recent complications) and postoperative satisfaction, physical functioning, role physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, role emotional, and mental health 3 months postsurgery for MRM, BCS, and NSM-IBR among patients with BC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Approval and Informed Consent

The ethics committee of Jiangsu Taizhou People’s Hospital approved the study design, and all participants provided written informed consent before commencement of the study. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments and other equivalent ethical guidelines.

Study Population

Before the surgery, doctors conducted comprehensive discussions with patients using a “shared decision-making” strategy, enabling patients to select the surgical techniques. Additionally, 105 patients with BC received treatment at Taizhou People’s Hospital between May 2023 and May 2024. Each patient received a histological confirmation of their diagnosis. Clinicopathologic features and treatment details were obtained from their medical record. The inclusion criteria included patients with (1) BC confirmed through pathological examination; (2) complete medical records; and (3) patients who underwent 1 of 3 procedures, adhering to the guidance of BCS. The exclusion criteria included (1) patients with tumors manifesting concurrently or sequentially; (2) patients diagnosed with either acute or chronic inflammation; and (3) patients currently receiving antitumor treatment. Based on the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer, the Tumor Node Metastasis stage classification was identified.12

The patients were categorized into MRM (n = 35), BCS (n = 35), and NSM-IBR (n = 35). The perioperative outcomes, including operation duration, intraoperative blood loss, and recent complications, were compared among these groups. The 36-item short form health survey (SF-36) scale assessed variations in satisfaction regarding physical functioning, role physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, role emotional, and mental health, 3 months following surgery.

Surgical Procedures

All operations were performed by Dr. Tongbo Yi, who has more than 30 years of experience in breast surgical oncology and reconstructive procedures. Patients were positioned supine with the ipsilateral arm abducted to 90 degrees.

The NSM-IBR Group

The sentinel lymph nodes were identified and marked with methylene blue. In our institution, methylene blue is the standard agent for sentinel lymph node mapping due to its proven reliability, cost-effectiveness, and adherence to local clinical guidelines. Subsequently, the sentinel lymph nodes were excised, and the adjacent connective tissue and lymphatic vessels were severed. Fast-frozen sections of the sentinel lymph nodes were examined during the surgery. In China, under rigorous quality control protocols, the diagnostic accuracy of intraoperative frozen section analysis for both lymph nodes and surgical margins can exceed 95%, making it a reliable guide for immediate surgical decision-making.13 If cancer had spread to these nodes, the patient underwent axillary lymph node dissection followed by immediate prosthetic reconstruction. One end of the TiLOOP artificial patch material (pfm medical titanium GmbH, Germany) was continuously sutured to the inferior margin of the pectoralis major with a 2–0 barbed suture. The distal end of the TiLOOP and the leftover triangular bundle ligaments were sutured together, forming a pouch to effectively support the prosthesis. The surgical site was irrigated before implanting the prosthesis. A silicone drainage tube was inserted on the head side, and another was inserted on the bottom side of the pouch. A second silicone drainage tube was inserted toward the head on the superficial layer of the pouch, emerging from a new opening. The drainage tube was connected to a suction system with negative pressure. The capsule was sealed by suturing the residual lateral fusion fascia of the pectoralis major muscle. All NSM-IBR procedures were performed using the conventional open approach. Endoscopic/robotic techniques were not used due to current institutional resource limitations.

The MRM Group

MRM was performed using the Madden technique (preservation of the pectoralis minor muscle), with complete level I–II axillary dissection. IBR was not performed in this group.

It is important to note that no specific nerve-sparing techniques or nerve grafting were used in any of the surgical groups (MRM, BCS, or NSM-IBR). The reported sensations of numbness or foreign body sensation are recognized potential consequences of standard surgical dissection, particularly in the context of axillary lymph node dissection or mastectomy flap elevation. Patients undergoing BCS received whole-breast irradiation as per institutional protocol, typically delivered across 3–5 weeks using conventional fractionation.

The BCS Group

Patients undergoing BCS received whole-breast irradiation as per institutional protocol, typically delivered across 6 weeks using conventional fractionation (total dose: 60 Gy in 30 fractions).

Postoperative Management

The drainage tube was removed when the drainage volume was less than 20 mL for 3 continuous days. Subsequently, patients wore a compression bra for 2 weeks.

Statistical Analysis

The Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) was used for the statistical analysis. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD, whereas other data are reported as case numbers and percentages. Clinicopathologic categorical variables were evaluated using either the χ2 or Fisher exact test. Independent prognostic factors were identified through multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient Clinicopathologic Characteristics

Table 1 presents the demographic and clinicopathologic characteristics of a cohort of 105 pathologically diagnosed female patients with BC. The median age of the patients was 54 (22–92) years. The mean age of patients in the MRM group was 60.1 ± 13.5 years, substantially older than that of the patients in the BCS and NSM-IBR groups (53.7 ± 14.7, P = 0.037; 50.1 ± 9.1, P < 0.001, Fig. 1A). The mean hospital cost was 57,997.7 ± 4042.9 yuan in the NSM-IBR group, significantly higher than that in the MRM and BCS groups (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B). In the NSM-IBR group, the mean operative time was 207.1 ± 38.7 minutes, significantly longer than that in the MRM and BCS groups (P < 0.001, Fig. 1C).

Postoperative Complications

Table 2 and Figure 2 illustrate the postoperative complications. The amount of postoperative bleeding in the BCS group was significantly lower than that in the MRM and NSM-IBR groups (P < 0.05). No statistically significant difference in pain severity was observed using the 3 approaches. The number of patients with restricted movement of upper limbs was significantly higher in the NSM-IBR group compared with the BCS group (P < 0.05). Additionally, the number of patients with numbness or foreign body sensation in the operative area in the NSM-IBR group was higher than that in the BCS and MRM groups (P < 0.05).

High Patient Satisfaction in the NSM-IBR Group

Three months postoperatively, the patients completed the SF-36 questionnaire. The NSM-IBR group exhibited a significantly higher overall satisfaction score compared with the BCS and MRM groups (80.03 ± 1.27 versus 54.51 ± 3.35, P < 0.001; 80.03 ± 1.27 versus 78.46 ± 1.42, P = 0.004). The scores of satisfaction with physical functioning, role physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning, role emotional, and mental health (P < 0.001) were statistically different between the MRM and NSM-IBR groups. The scores of satisfaction with bodily pain (P < 0.001) and mental health (P < 0.001) were statistically higher in the NSM-IBR group than in the BCS group. Notably, the scores of satisfaction with social functioning (P < 0.05) and role emotional (P < 0.01) were statistically lower in the NSM-IBR group than in the BCS group (Table 3, Fig. 3).

DISCUSSION

Since the introduction of Halsted radical mastectomy in the late 19th century, BC treatment has evolved continuously.14 In the early 20th century, the extended radical mastectomy, which included extrapleural and intrathoracic clearance of internal breast lymph nodes along with the removal of breast and axillary lymph nodes, was introduced to further reduce recurrence. However, no significant difference was observed between the 2 groups in the overall 5-year survival rate.15 In the mid-20th century, MRM was proposed to reduce surgical trauma.

In the 1980s, advancements in radiotherapy techniques facilitated the widespread adoption of BCS. In addition to surgical removal of the tumor, other systemic adjuvant treatments, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, endocrine therapy, and immunotherapy, are necessary. BCS combined with radiotherapy offers therapeutic effects comparable to radical mastectomy while better preserving psychological well-being.16 The introduction of sentinel lymph node biopsy in the 1990s minimized complications, including lymphedema incidence in patients with negative axillary lymph nodes.17

Breast reconstruction reduces chest wall deformities and improves the psychosocial well-being and quality of life of women with BC.18 Patients with stage I BC may experience improved emotional outcomes after latissimus dorsi myocutaneous flap reconstruction after nipple- and areola-sparing MRM.19 Histologically, NAC is not preserved intraoperatively during radical mastectomy due to the increased risk of locoregional recurrence and potential adverse affects on prognosis.20,21 However, the incidence of primary BC originating in the nipple is notably low, and postoperative recurrence at the nipple site is infrequent.22 NSM-IBR mitigates this by radically excising glandular tissue, circumventing adjuvant radiotherapy, and diminishing the psychological burden associated with residual cancer anxiety. Removing the breast while preserving the breast shape and size allows a patient to have a more complete oncological procedure and generally avoid treatment with radiotherapy.23 NSM-IBR more closely approximates classical MRM based on the extent of tissue excision, while also preserving the breast skin and the NAC; hence, it better maintains the patient’s external body shape24 and reduces the patient’s psychological stress.25 Patients exhibit more satisfaction with postoperative nipple reconstruction; they are more satisfied with intraoperative NAC preservation.26 NSM-IBR is the best selection for patients with high breast appearance requirements and those who are unsuitable for BCS.27,28 NSM-IBR is essential for enhanced cosmetic results.29 However, NSM-IBR is not widely used because patients with BC have concerns about the procedure’s expense and potential postoperative complications. Therefore, we examined differences in ages, costs, operative time, perioperative results, and satisfaction 3 months postsurgery for MRM, BCS, and NSM-IBR.

In NSM-IBR surgery, axillary lymph node dissection was performed using a harmonic scalpel. Compared with electric knives, ultrasound knives generate less surgical smoke and carbon dioxide, hence minimizing bleeding and tissue separation difficulty.30 Mesh is used in surgery. Suturing the mesh prevents the inadvertent displacement or removal. The TiLOOP Patch is biocompatible, with a hydrophilic and titaniumized surface that reduces inflammation risk, thereby reducing the formation of connective tissue-like scarring and contracture, which aids in the achievement of long-term stable outcomes and desirable intratissue growth and vascularization.

The widespread use of luminal techniques and minimally invasive endoscopic surgical techniques has led to the increasing adoption of minimally invasive surgical treatment of BC to improve the quality of life of patients. Endoscopic-assisted BCS has advantages, including small and inconspicuous scars.31 Comparable to NSM-IBR, single-port insufflation endoscopic nipple-sparing subcutaneous mastectomy with immediate reconstruction using prosthesis implantation exhibited a lower incidence of prosthesis removal, a shorter incision length, and was associated with better patient satisfaction with the breasts for selected patients with early-stage BC.32 Robot-assisted nipple–areola-sparing mastectomy with IBR is feasible and safe for the treatment of BC and shows a rapid learning curve, according to the 2019 study.33 Despite longer and more expensive procedures, no significant differences were observed between robot-assisted nipple–areola-sparing mastectomy and conventional NSM regarding complication rates, length of hospital stay, length of surgery, and costs.34 Although our study used open techniques, emerging approaches such as endoscopic/robotic NSM-IBR (currently unavailable at our institution) have the potential for reducing scarring and improving patient satisfaction. For surgeons to optimize outcomes in complex procedures such as NSM-IBR, specialized training should not only focus on oncological safety and reconstructive principles but also encompass mastery of these evolving minimally invasive platforms. Therefore, we strongly advocate for young surgeons to pursue training in these updated and specialized techniques to enhance surgical precision and potentially improve patient recovery.

We compared the age, costs, and operative time of MRM, BCS, and NSM-IBR. Patients in the MRM group were older than those in the BCS and NSM-IBR groups. Older people may care less about cosmetic results and more about treatment outcomes and cost-effectiveness than younger people. Notably, the NSM-IBR cohort was significantly younger than the other groups. Younger patients frequently prioritize body image preservation, rendering them more inclined to select NSM-IBR. The hospital expenses and operative time in the NSM-IBR group were higher than those in the MRM and BCS groups. However, it is important to note that BCS typically requires adjuvant radiotherapy, which incurs additional costs and time. Additionally, long-term follow-up with mammography and potential biopsies for conserved breasts may further increase financial and psychological burdens. These factors should be considered in a comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis. The total satisfaction scores measured using the SF-36 in the NSM-IBR group were higher than those in the BCS and MRM groups.

The total satisfaction scores measured using the SF-36 in the NSM-IBR group were higher than those in the BCS and MRM groups. However, the amount of bleeding after the operation, the number of patients with restricted movement of upper limbs, and the number of patients experiencing numbness or foreign body sensation in the operative area were higher in the NSM-IBR group than in the BCS and MRM groups. These results of NSM-IBR significantly affect patients’ postoperative psychological quality of life; nevertheless, NSM-IBR is expensive and associated with a higher incidence of perioperative complications. Consequently, surgeons should refine their techniques to minimize complication rates.

It is noteworthy that patients in the NSM-IBR group were significantly younger than those in the MRM group. This age disparity may reflect patient preferences, as younger individuals often prioritize cosmetic outcomes and body image preservation, making them more inclined to choose reconstructive options. Additionally, younger patients may possess better physiological reserves, potentially influencing their postoperative recovery and quality of life assessments. Future studies should stratify outcomes by age to further elucidate these relationships.

Although BCS remains the prevailing standard for minimizing surgical morbidity, our findings established NSM-IBR as an aesthetic alternative to BCS and an oncologically advanced option. Younger patients exhibit superior early psychosocial enhanced options when prioritizing body image restoration and enhanced oncological control through complete breast tissue removal, which eliminates residual cancer risk, avoids adjuvant radiotherapy, and obviates the need for postoperative mammographic surveillance of the treated breast.

This study has some limitations. First, we used a single-surgeon, single-center design, which limits generalizability. Second, frozen section protocols reflected resource constraints in our setting but differ from US/EU standards, where genomic assays are preferred. Third, cost analyses excluded indirect expenses. Future multicenter studies with standardized pathological examination protocols are warranted. Furthermore, given our short-term focus on recovery metrics, survival analysis was not conducted. Future studies with a follow-up of more than 5 years will evaluate oncological outcomes.

CONCLUSIONS

NSM-IBR deserves broader promotion as a surgical approach to enhance the postoperative psychological and physiological health of patients with BC. Surgeons performing NSM-IBR should pursue specialized training to refine their techniques and minimize the risk of complications, particularly given the complexity of the procedure. Furthermore, the advantages and disadvantages of surgery should be communicated to the patients with BC, and all foreseeable intraoperative and postoperative adverse events should be proactively anticipated and managed.

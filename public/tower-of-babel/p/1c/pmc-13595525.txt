Cheek acupuncture for enhancing postoperative recovery after endoscopic sinus surgery: study protocol for a pilot randomized controlled trial
Authors: Meng Wang, Guiyu Lei, Jun Yan, Guyan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostoperative pain and sleep disturbance are common after endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) and may delay postoperative recovery. Acupuncture has demonstrated analgesic and sleep-enhancing effects in perioperative care; however, the impact of cheek acupuncture—a minimally invasive facial microsystem technique—on postoperative recovery after ESS has not been evaluated in randomized controlled trials.MethodsThis single-center, prospective, randomized controlled pilot trial will enroll 90 adults undergoing ESS. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to receive cheek acupuncture or sham cheek acupuncture within 30 min before anesthesia induction. The primary outcome is the Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) score at 24 h postoperatively. Secondary outcomes include the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) for pain, intraoperative hemodynamics, postoperative care unit (PACU) stay duration, postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), and inflammatory biomarkers (serum interleukin-6 [IL-6] and C-reactive protein [CRP]).DiscussionThis trial will assess the feasibility, safety, and preliminary efficacy of cheek acupuncture in improving postoperative recovery after ESS. Results will inform the design of a future multicenter randomized trial.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500110625). Registered on 16 October 2025.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is a common inflammatory disorder of the paranasal sinuses that significantly reduces quality of life and imposes a substantial healthcare burden [1]. Endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) is the standard treatment for patients who fail medical therapy or develop complications. Although ESS effectively relieves nasal obstruction, facial pain, and other local symptoms, many patients continue to experience postoperative pain, fatigue, and sleep disturbances—factors that negatively affect postoperative recovery and satisfaction [2–4]. Perioperative sleep disruption has been identified as an important risk factor for poor surgical outcomes, including increased postoperative pain, cardiovascular instability, and cognitive dysfunction [5]. Sleep quality and pain perception are closely interrelated; inadequate sleep may heighten central sensitization and amplify pain responses [6, 7]. Conversely, poorly controlled pain can itself further disrupt postoperative sleep, forming a vicious cycle that impairs physical and psychological recovery [8–10]. Therefore, multimodal strategies that simultaneously alleviate pain and improve sleep quality are essential for optimizing postoperative rehabilitation in ESS patients.

Currently, pharmacological interventions remain the mainstay for postoperative pain and sleep management. However, these medications are often associated with adverse effects such as sedation, nausea, and respiratory depression, particularly in ear, nose, and throat (ENT) surgeries involving shared airways. As a result, there is growing interest in non-pharmacological, low-risk approaches that can enhance postoperative recovery through neuroregulatory or anti-inflammatory mechanisms.

Acupuncture has been shown to regulate autonomic nervous function, modulate inflammatory mediators, and improve perioperative outcomes with minimal side effects. Clinical studies have reported that acupuncture reduces postoperative pain and nausea, enhances sleep quality, and promotes faster recovery [11–13]. Among various acupuncture modalities, CAT is a micro-needle technique targeting specific facial acupoints innervated by branches of the trigeminal and facial nerves. It is minimally invasive, safe, and easy to perform. Emerging evidence suggests that CAT can reduce opioid requirements during laparoscopic gynecological surgery [14], alleviate postoperative pain after gastrointestinal surgery [15], and decrease the incidence of postoperative nausea, vomiting, and delirium in elderly surgical patients [16, 17].

Despite these encouraging findings, no randomized controlled trial has yet examined the effects of CAT on postoperative recovery after endoscopic sinus surgery. We hypothesize that CAT may reduce postoperative pain, improve sleep quality, and enhance overall recovery through modulation of neuroimmune and autonomic pathways. Therefore, this pilot randomized controlled trial was designed to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of CAT in improving the quality of postoperative recovery in patients undergoing ESS. The findings of this study are expected to provide preliminary evidence for integrating CAT into multimodal perioperative management protocols.

Objectives {7}

The objective of this trial is to explore whether CAT is helpful in postoperative recovery in patients with sinusitis after endoscopic sinus surgery.

Trial design {8}

This study is a single-center, prospective, randomized controlled trial. Eligible patients will be randomly allocated to CAT or sham CAT in a 1:1 ratio.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Although ESS effectively relieves nasal obstruction, facial pain, and other local symptoms, many patients continue to experience postoperative pain, fatigue, and sleep disturbances—factors that negatively affect postoperative recovery and satisfaction. Currently, pharmacological interventions remain the mainstay for postoperative pain and sleep management. However, these medications are often associated with adverse effects such as sedation, nausea, and respiratory depression, particularly in ENT surgeries involving shared airways. As a result, there is growing interest in non-pharmacological, low-risk approaches that can enhance postoperative recovery through neuroregulatory or anti-inflammatory mechanisms. Therefore, this pilot randomized controlled trial was designed to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of CAT in improving the quality of postoperative recovery in patients undergoing ESS.

Intervention description {11a}

All participants in both groups received a standardized unified anesthesia protocol with continuous intraoperative monitoring of blood pressure (BP), heart rate (HR), peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2), and bispectral index (BIS). Anesthesia induction: Midazolam 0.02 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.3–0.5 μg/kg, propofol 1–2 mg/kg, and rocuronium bromide 0.6 mg/kg were administered. Anesthesia maintenance: Combined intravenous anesthetics were used throughout the procedure. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was maintained within 20% of the baseline value during both induction and maintenance. The BIS value was kept at 40–60, with anesthetic dosage dynamically adjusted according to changes in BP and HR. Vasopressor or vasodilator agents were administered at the anesthesiologist’s clinical discretion.

Cheek acupuncture therapy (CAT) group

The CAT protocol was jointly developed by anesthesiologists and acupuncturists based on cheek acupuncture theory and perioperative acupoint stimulation consensus. The primary acupoints include the neck, back, waist, sacral, and Sancong points. Secondary acupoints include the upper neck and mastoid points.

The procedure will be performed approximately 30 min before anesthesia induction. The patient will be placed in the supine position, and the skin over the selected acupoints on both sides of the face and neck will be disinfected according to standard aseptic procedures. Sterile flat-handled needles (0.16 mm × 15 mm) will be inserted perpendicularly to a depth of 10–15 mm at each acupoint. No deliberate manipulation to elicit the sensation of “deqi” will be performed. Needles will be retained in situ for 30 min and then removed. Gentle pressure will be applied with a sterile cotton ball to the needle insertion sites to prevent bleeding.

Sham cheek acupuncture group

Participants assigned to the control group will receive sham cheek acupuncture using a validated, non-penetrating sham needle device. The sham needle is a self-developed patented device (No. ZL202121352221.7), manufactured following Chinese medical device standards. There is no commercial model; the placebo acupuncture appliance contains a pedestal and a tube. The pedestal, made by resin material, is opaque so that it can isolate patients’ eyesight, and they cannot visually decide whether the needle is inserted into skin or not. The device will be applied to non-meridian sites adjacent to, but not overlapping, the true acupoints used in the CAT group, at a distance of approximately 0.5–1.0 cm. The procedure, timing, and duration will mimic those in the CAT group, but the sham needles will not pierce the skin. This approach is designed to maintain blinding by reproducing the appearance and tactile sensation of acupuncture without delivering active needling stimulation.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

During cheek acupuncture therapy, if the patient is intolerant, has side effects, or the patient is unwilling to receive further intervention, the intervention should be stopped immediately.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Inform the patient that if there is any discomfort during the intervention, the intervention can be stopped, and the whole process of CAT is free of charge and has very few side effects.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

All participants in both groups will receive standardized postoperative analgesia. Patients routinely receive flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg intravenously on the first postoperative day. If the NRS pain score exceeds 4, a rescue dose of ibuprofen 400 mg will be provided. If a patient requests medication for sleep at bedtime, a single low-dose midazolam 1 mg (oral/sublingual) may be administered and will be documented as rescue hypnotic use.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Participants will have access to study clinics for post-trial care through the routine health system.

Study process and evaluation

Sample size {14}

The primary outcome was the 15-item Quality of Recovery Scale (QoR-15) at 24 h postoperatively. For the primary outcome, given the superiority design, a one-sided significance level of 0.025 will be used. Superiority will be concluded if the lower bound of the two-sided 95% confidence interval for the between-group difference (test group minus control group) exceeds the prespecified superiority margin (Δ = 6). Pre-experimental data showed the mean QoR-15 scores of 132 (SD 6.5) in the sham CAT group and 142 (SD 6.0) in the CAT group. A difference greater than 6 points is considered clinically relevant [18]. Using a one-sided superiority design, with a significance level of 0.025 and a test power of 0.80, the PASS software was utilized, with 40 participants in each group. Accounting for a potential 10% dropout rate, 45 participants were included in each group, resulting in a total of 90 participants.

Recruitment {15}

We will recruit 90 patients for this randomized controlled trial (RCT) at Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University. Eligible subjects scheduled for endoscopic sinus surgery for sinusitis will be invited to participate. The research assistant will screen them and obtain written informed consent. Before intervention, they will be randomly assigned to either the cheek acupuncture therapy group or the sham cheek acupuncture therapy group.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

This study will use simple randomization. A computer-generated random number table will be prepared by an independent statistician not otherwise involved in the trial. Allocation will be concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

After confirming eligibility and obtaining informed consent, the acupuncturist will open the next envelope before the intervention in sequence to determine group assignment. Participants will be randomized to the CAT or sham CAT group in a 1:1 allocation ratio.

Implementation {16c}

The intervention will be performed by a physician with more than 3 years of experience in cheek acupuncture. Participants, anesthesiologists, surgeons, postoperative care staff, outcome assessors, and statisticians will remain blinded to treatment allocation. Only the acupuncturist administering the intervention will be unblinded and will not be involved in outcome assessment or data analysis.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Participants, anesthesiologists, surgeons, postoperative care staff, outcome assessors, and statisticians will remain blinded to treatment allocation.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

There are no special criteria for unblinding. The principal investigator (PI) and Institutional Review Board (IRB) will be notified immediately if any serious adverse event occurs. Intraoperatively, postoperatively, and during follow-up. The IRB and PI have the right to unblind if necessary.

To evaluate the success of participant blinding, we will perform a blinding assessment at 24 h postoperatively, coinciding with the assessment of the primary outcome (QoR-15). Participants will be asked the following question: “Which intervention do you believe you received?” Response options will be: (1) real cheek acupuncture therapy, (2) sham cheek acupuncture therapy, or (3) unsure/don’t know. The proportion of participants who correctly guess their assigned group will be reported descriptively for each arm.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

All patient information, including preoperative consultation, anesthesia records, hospitalization records, and postoperative follow-up, will be followed up continuously during the study. All data for the patient will be recorded on the case report form (CRF) and kept by a dedicated person. Preoperative baseline data will be derived from the electronic medical record. All intraoperative data were directly extracted from patient monitors. One investigator will be responsible for intraoperative data collection, following the patient throughout the procedure, entering the operating theater and PACU, recording the relevant data into a paper CRF, conducting postoperative follow-up, and recording the follow-up data into a CRF. All patients’ data will be anonymized and saved in a study folder on our protected research server. Only the study team has access to this specific study folder.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

The patients will receive extensive information about the study set-up and requirements during the preoperative visit. The importance of completing the follow-up will be stressed. Patients are allowed to stop at any time during the study and are not obliged to give a reason to discontinue. Follow-up will be completed in time by the follower. If the patient is discharged from the hospital, he or she will be followed up by telephone. Throughout the follow-up period, the researchers will check for responses.

Data management {19}

The data entry and management should be carried out by the investigator of the research group. The investigators will record the data in the paper CRF during intraoperative and postoperative follow-ups, and later enter them into a spreadsheet. Another investigator will check the data. To avoid input errors, data entry will be performed using the two-person double-entry principle, whereby two data-entry personnel entered data independently and synchronously. Data will be managed using an electronic data collection management system. Individual participant data will be uploaded to Research Manager. All the data are available after obtaining permission from the corresponding author, and researchers can have access rights to enable international prospective meta-analyses.

Confidentiality {27}

Research data will be stored using a study identification code for each participant. The key to the identification code list will only be available to the research team during the study and will be documented and safeguarded by the principal investigator according to research guidelines after completion of the study. No patient identification details will be reported in publications.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Peripheral venous blood was collected before intervention and postoperative recovery rooms, serum separation was performed, and serum samples were frozen and stored for subsequent analysis of inflammatory markers in serum.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Normality of continuous variables will be assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Quantitative variables will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or median (interquartile range, IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages.

Between-group comparisons of continuous variables will be conducted using independent-samples t-tests if the data are normally distributed and the variances are homogeneous; otherwise, the Mann–Whitney U test will be used. Categorical variables will be compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For the primary outcome, the predefined one-sided significance level of 0.025 was applied, supported by the two-sided 95% confidence interval approach for assessing the superiority margin. For all secondary outcomes, a two-sided α of 0.05 was used. Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Discussion

ESS is widely performed for CRS refractory to medical treatment and effectively improves nasal ventilation and disease-specific quality of life. Nevertheless, postoperative sleep disturbance and pain remain prominent concerns that can compromise the quality of recovery and patient satisfaction. These symptoms interact through neurophysiological pathways—poor sleep amplifies pain sensitivity and stress responses, while uncontrolled pain worsens sleep fragmentation and delays functional rehabilitation. Accordingly, recent enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) frameworks emphasize the importance of addressing both pain and sleep quality in a multimodal manner [19].

Clinical trials indicate that acupuncture enhances subjective sleep quality, reduces fatigue, mitigates cognitive impairments, and alleviates emotional symptoms with few adverse effects [20]. In 2016, Wang et al. demonstrated that healthy volunteers experienced significant improvements in nasal ventilation and patency following acupuncture treatment at the sphenopalatine ganglion, suggesting that acupuncture could be effective for treating nasal inflammatory diseases [21]. In their study, the use of electroacupuncture (EA) resulted in fewer average nighttime awakenings during the first 3 days post-surgery compared to sham EA. This indicates that EA might offer clinical benefits in enhancing sleep quality after endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) [22]. Research has also shown that acupuncture can decrease postoperative pain, nausea, and vomiting, and improve perioperative sleep quality [11–13]. However, randomized controlled trials on cheek acupuncture therapy are still relatively scarce. To date, there is no data available on the impact of cheek acupuncture therapy on recovery after endoscopic sinus surgery for sinusitis. Should this study confirm that cheek acupuncture therapy can enhance postoperative recovery quality and prognosis for patients, it would provide a novel treatment option for sinusitis sufferers undergoing endoscopic sinus surgery, in addition to conventional drug therapies.

The QoR-15 scoring scale is a global measure of postoperative recovery, with scores ranging from 0 to 150. The QoR-15 has become the most widely reported indicator for evaluating postoperative life treatment of patients. Therefore, we selected the QoR-15 as the primary outcome measure for this study.

In conclusion, this study aims to demonstrate that cheek acupuncture therapy positively impacts patients following endoscopic sinus surgery for sinusitis, enhancing the quality of postoperative recovery and patient satisfaction. The results will be reported to peer-reviewed journals. Additionally, we hope that cheek acupuncture therapy will become a simple and effective new treatment for alleviating adverse symptoms in patients with sinusitis after nasal endoscopy.

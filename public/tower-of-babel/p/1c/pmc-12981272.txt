The dynamics of glycosylation in trophoblast cells: development, function, and pregnancy-related disorders
Authors: Tiantong Wang, Yan Wang, Yilun Wu, Yi Zhang, Fang Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTrophoblast cells are essential for embryo implantation and placental development, with their functional versatility critically regulated by dynamic glycosylation modifications. These post-translational modifications create a spatiotemporally-defined molecular code that directs trophoblast behavior throughout pregnancy, though its comprehensive role in both physiological and pathological contexts remains to be fully elucidated.DiscussionGlycosylation orchestrates key trophoblast functions in a stage-specific manner. During early pregnancy, specific glycan patterns mediate blastocyst adhesion and trophoblast invasion via modifications to integrins, cadherins, and selectins. As placental development progresses, spatially restricted glycosyltransferases guide cytotrophoblast differentiation into syncytiotrophoblasts and extravillous trophoblasts, modulating critical signaling pathways including Wnt, Notch, and FGF. Glycosylation further regulates maternal–fetal immune tolerance through cytokine receptor modifications and sialylated glycan interactions with inhibitory immune receptors. Disruptions to this spatiotemporal glycan landscape—manifested as aberrant sialylation, fucosylation, and N-glycan branching—are mechanistically linked to pregnancy disorders including preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, recurrent pregnancy loss, and choriocarcinoma. Emerging technologies such as single-cell glycomics and trophoblast organoids are now decoding this intricate regulatory system with unprecedented resolution, bridging molecular mechanisms with clinical phenotypes.ConclusionGlycosylation constitutes a master regulatory code that coordinates trophoblast development and function across gestation. The spatiotemporal specificity of glycosylation patterns ensures proper placentation and maternal–fetal interface maintenance, while its dysregulation contributes significantly to pregnancy pathologies. Understanding this dynamic system opens new avenues for biomarker discovery and therapeutic interventions, with emerging glycomic technologies poised to translate these findings into clinical applications for managing complicated pregnancies.Keywords: Glycosylation, glycobiology, maternal–fetal interface, placenta, pregnancy disorders, trophoblast

Introduction

Embryo implantation and placental development are critical processes that establish the foundation for a successful pregnancy. These stages are characterized by intricate interactions between the embryo and maternal tissues, with trophoblast cells playing a central role in forming the maternal–fetal interface [1].

Trophoblasts undergo significant time- and space-dependent changes throughout implantation and the entire gestational period [2]. Their ability to adapt to the changing uterine environment is crucial, transitioning from a proliferative state to invasive forms that penetrate maternal tissues. This invasive capability is essential for establishing the maternal–fetal interface [3].

Glycosylation modifications have emerged as vital players in mediating these cellular interactions and impacting trophoblast behavior. Glycans—sugar molecules attached to proteins and lipids—are instrumental in a range of biological processes, including cell recognition, adhesion, and immune response modulation [4]. During implantation and placentation, specific glycosylation patterns on both trophoblasts and maternal cells facilitate critical processes such as trophoblast adhesion to the uterine wall and the remodeling of maternal blood vessels [5]. These modifications ultimately promote a conducive environment for embryo development [6].

The dynamic nature of glycosylation during pregnancy influences trophoblast function significantly. For instance, alterations in glycan structures can affect the expression of adhesion molecules and enzymes necessary for extracellular matrix degradation, enhancing the invasive capacity of trophoblasts [7]. Furthermore, glycosylation impacts signaling pathways that regulate trophoblast proliferation and differentiation, thereby influencing how these cells interact with the surrounding uterine environment.

This review is structured around the premise that glycosylation constitutes a dynamic, informative “code” that orchestrates the fundamental processes of human placentation. We propose that the spatiotemporal expression of specific glycosyltransferases and their glycan products dictates trophoblast lineage decisions, functional plasticity, and immune dialogue at the maternal–fetal interface. Deciphering this code is essential for understanding both the establishment of a healthy pregnancy and the pathogenesis of its complications. To this end, we first provide a concise overview of glycosylation mechanisms with a specific focus on placental relevance. We then critically synthesize evidence detailing how this glycosylation code regulates three core pillars of trophoblast biology: adhesion and invasion, differentiation and signaling, and immune tolerance. Finally, we evaluate how aberrations in this code contribute to major pregnancy disorders, distinguishing associative from causal evidence, and discuss the challenging path toward clinical translation.

Brief introduction of trophoblast throughout pregnancy

The cells throughout pregnancy is crucial for the proper development of the placenta and successful fetal growth [8]. Trophoblast cells initially derive from the outer layer of the blastocyst, known as the trophectoderm (TE), which undergoes differentiation after fertilization to form trophoblast stem cells (TSCs) [9]. These TSCs then differentiate into various trophoblast lineages essential for placental formation and function [10].

Early differentiation and placental formation

During the early stages of implantation, trophoblast cells first differentiate into cytotrophoblasts (CTBs), which are proliferative, mononucleated cells that contribute to the initial development of the placenta [11]. As pregnancy progresses, CTBs further differentiate into multinucleated syncytiotrophoblasts (STBs), which form the outer layer of the placenta [12]. The STBs are essential for nutrient transfer and hormone secretion, both of which are crucial for maintaining pregnancy and supporting fetal development [13].

Adaptive changes in trophoblast cells during pregnancy

As pregnancy progresses, trophoblast cells undergo dynamic changes in their differentiation and function. Notably, during the second and third trimesters, trophoblast cells become more invasive, particularly when CTBs differentiate into extravillous trophoblasts (EVTs) [14]. These cells invade the maternal decidua and contribute to the remodeling of maternal blood vessels, ensuring a robust blood supply to the placenta [15]. EVTs play a key role in remodeling maternal spiral arteries, which is necessary for optimal blood flow to the placenta and the developing fetus [16].

Functional maturation of the placenta and trophoblast cell differentiation

In the later stages of pregnancy, the placenta becomes fully functional, and trophoblast cell differentiation continues to adapt to the increasing needs of the fetus [17,18]. For example, in the third trimester, STBs are responsible for the secretion of hormones such as human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) and progesterone, which are critical for maintaining pregnancy [8]. Additionally, trophoblast cell glycosylation patterns change throughout pregnancy, particularly in the third trimester, which correlates with increased trophoblast invasiveness and improved interactions with maternal blood vessels [19].

Regulation by signaling pathways

The differentiation of trophoblast cells and their invasive behavior are tightly regulated by several signaling pathways. During the transition from CTBs to STBs, growth factors such as fibroblast growth factor (FGF) and Nodal play key roles in regulating trophoblast proliferation, migration, and differentiation [20,21]. Furthermore, as CTBs transition into EVTs, they undergo epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), which enhances their invasiveness [22]. EMT is a crucial process that allows CTBs to invade the maternal stroma, remodel blood vessels, and establish a connection with the maternal circulation, thereby ensuring sufficient nutrient supply to the fetus [23].

Clinical and pathological implications

Disruptions in trophoblast differentiation and invasion can lead to various pregnancy complications. For instance, excessive or insufficient trophoblast invasion has been associated with conditions such as pre-eclampsia and intrauterine growth restriction [24,25]. Therefore, understanding the detailed differentiation processes of trophoblast cells throughout pregnancy is essential for understanding normal pregnancy mechanisms, also holds potential for diagnosing and treating pregnancy-related complications.

Overview of glycosylation modifications and key enzymatic regulators

Glycosylation refers to the enzymatic addition of carbohydrate chains (glycans) to proteins, lipids, or other organic molecules [26]. This post-translational modification is vital for a wide array of biological functions, including cell signaling, immune responses, and tissue development [27–29]. Glycosylation is a highly complex and dynamic process, primarily involving the addition of glycans composed of monosaccharides linked by glycosidic bonds. The two major types, N- and O-linked glycosylation, are initiated in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus by specific glycosyltransferases, which are highly regulated at the transcriptional and epigenetic levels. The combinatorial action of these enzymes—including fucosyltransferases (FUTs), sialyltransferases (STs), and N-acetylglucosaminyltransferases (MGATs)—creates a rich repertoire of glycan structures that are functionally distinct [30–33]. Despite only nine monosaccharides being involved in the glycan building blocks in vertebrates, the diversity of glycoproteins is vast, owing to variations in glycan linkage sites, branching patterns, and types of glycosidic bonds [34]. This structural complexity leads to microheterogeneity at specific sites on glycoproteins, which significantly impacts their function in biological processes.

Trophoblast proliferation and differentiation

Glycosylation also plays a role in cell signaling pathways that regulate trophoblast behavior. Glycans can influence the activation of signaling cascades that promote trophoblast proliferation and differentiation, further enhancing their ability to establish successful implantation. By modulating these signaling pathways, glycosylation can impact how trophoblasts interact with the surrounding uterine environment. N-glycans on integrins influence downstream signaling pathways such as focal adhesion kinase (FAK), which is crucial for adhesion and migration [44]. In the process of trophoblast differentiation, enzyme-catalyzed glycosylation modifications play a crucial role in signal transduction and cellular function. Specifically, O-GlcNAcylation directly modifies the Notch intracellular domain (NICD), regulating trophoblast differentiation [38]. Additionally, N-glycosylation mediated by MGAT5 facilitates the clustering of Frizzled receptors, activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which is critical for epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [45]. Further research has demonstrated that CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of FUT8 disrupts glycosylation modifications, impairing trophoblast invasion by disrupting IGF-1R signaling [46]. These findings establish a clear mechanistic pathway linking enzyme activity, glycan modifications, receptor interactions, and cellular phenotype.

Glycosylation in epigenetic regulation and signaling

Sialylation and gene expression

Spatiotemporal expression of glycosyltransferases in trophoblasts

A comprehensive understanding requires mapping the expression of key glycosyltransferases to specific trophoblast subpopulations and stages. Pioneering work provided the first direct comparative N-glycan profiling of CTB, STB, and EVT. These findings reveal a dramatic shift in glycan phenotype during the CTB-to-EVT transition. The significant upregulation of β1,6-branched N-glycans (catalyzed by MGAT5) and polylactosamine extensions in EVT, correlating with their invasive phenotype. For instance, FUT8 is highly expressed in EVTs during early pregnancy, promoting invasiveness via IGF-1R signaling, while ST3GAL1 is upregulated in STBs in the third trimester, modulating immune tolerance via sialylated glycans [46,56].

Throughout pregnancy: glycosylation modifications in placental formation and function

Trophoblast cells undergo a series of significant changes in morphology, function, and gene expression, adapting to the evolving needs of the developing fetus and the maternal environment. These changes are crucial for establishing a functional placenta that can effectively support fetal growth and development [61]. Once implantation has occurred, the placenta begins to develop further. During this stage, trophoblasts undergo differentiation and invasiveness to form the villous and extravillous layers. Glycosylation modifications are critical for regulating growth factor signaling, immune responses, and trophoblast differentiation [62] (Figure 3).

The involvement of glycosylation in placental development and function. Glycosylation modifications play crucial roles in trophoblast differentiation and invasion during placental development. Initially, high-mannose N-glycans and complex-type N-glycans are present on cytotrophoblasts, facilitating their invasion into the maternal uterine tissue and remodeling of maternal spiral arteries. During the differentiation of trophoblasts into syncytiotrophoblasts, complex N-glycans with specific branching structures, such as sialylated and fucosylated glycans, promote trophoblast fusion and functional maturation. These glycans also regulate cell–cell adhesion and interaction with the extracellular matrix (ECM), ensuring effective placental barrier formation. In the immune context, sialylated glycans on trophoblasts engage with inhibitory receptors like SiGlecs on maternal immune cells, promoting immune tolerance and preventing fetal rejection. Additionally, glycosylation of growth factor receptors, such as FGF and VEGF receptors, modulates trophoblast proliferation, differentiation, and vascularization, supporting healthy placental function and blood flow. Alterations in these glycosylation patterns can lead to complications like preeclampsia, placental insufficiency, and preterm birth.

Placental cell interactions

N-glycans on trophoblasts facilitate essential cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions. For example, the presence of specific N-glycans such as high-mannose and complex-type N-glycans is vital for the invasion of trophoblasts into the uterine tissue and remodeling of maternal spiral arteries [7]. These interactions are necessary for establishing a functional maternal–fetal interface, and any aberrations in glycosylation can lead to placentation issues, such as preeclampsia or placental insufficiency. Complex N-glycans with specific branching structures are associated with the differentiation of trophoblast cells from cytotrophoblasts to syncytiotrophoblasts, influencing their functional maturation [5]. Variations in glycan structures could affect receptor–ligand interactions, impacting placental efficiency. Glycosylation is crucial for maintaining an epithelial phenotype in trophoblast stem cells, the activity of GALNT3 modifies O-glycans that support cell adhesion and differentiation [63]. Glycosylation impacts the expression of adhesion molecules, facilitating trophoblast invasion into the maternal tissue. Altered glycosylation patterns can enhance or inhibit these processes, influencing placentation.

Function and morphology

Glycosylation modifications significantly influence the morphology and overall function of the placenta. Glycosylation plays a role in membrane receptor functions and nutrient transfer [64]. Analysis of glycan profiles can reveal changes influencing placental functions. Specific O-glycans, such as those found on mucins, are associated with trophoblast differentiation and play a crucial role in nutrient transport and waste removal [65]. For instance, the expression of Core 1 O-glycans is linked to trophoblast invasion and can impact placental barrier functions, which are critical for maintaining a healthy pregnancy [66].

Balancing immune responses

The modulation of maternal immunity by glycosylated trophoblast antigens also helps to balance immune responses, ensuring that protective immunity is maintained while allowing for fetal tolerance [67]. This delicate balance is crucial for preventing pregnancy complications, such as recurrent miscarriage or preterm birth, which can arise from excessive maternal immune activation. Glycans on trophoblasts can modulate immune responses, helping to protect the fetus from maternal immune attack [68]. Sialylated glycans on trophoblasts interact with inhibitory receptors on immune cells, such as sialic acid-binding immunoglobulin-like lectins (Siglecs), which further supports maternal–fetal tolerance [69]. This engagement can suppress the activation of maternal immune cells, thereby preventing potential rejection of the fetus as a foreign entity. The glycosylation state of Siglec-6 can affect its binding to sialic acids on immune cells, impacting trophoblast–immune interactions [70].

Significance of glycosylated molecules in placenta

Glycosylated molecules in the placenta, such as glycoproteins and glycolipids, are essential for its proper functioning. They contribute to the structural integrity of the placenta and facilitate communication between maternal and fetal tissues. Glycosylation of HLA-G may enhance its stability and modulate interactions with immune cells [71]. Glycosylation of insulin and insulin-like growth factor receptors affects their signaling pathways, which are crucial for trophoblast proliferation and function during pregnancy [46]. Glycosylation of TPGB may influence its interactions and stability, impacting trophoblast functions [72]. Furthermore, these molecules can serve as biomarkers for placental health, providing insights into potential complications during pregnancy. Glycosylation influences the expression of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which are enzymes responsible for degrading the extracellular matrix (ECM) [73]. Enhanced expression of MMPs facilitates the breakdown of various ECM components, allowing trophoblasts to invade maternal tissue [74]. Alterations in glycosylation affect the expression and function of adhesion molecules like E-cadherin and ICAM-1, which are critical for cellular adhesion and migration [63,75]. Upregulation of specific glycoforms can enhance the invasive capability of trophoblasts.

Labor and delivery: glycosylation modifications in cytokine signaling and epigenetic regulation

As the pregnancy progresses toward term, glycosylation modifications continue to play important roles in regulating the timing of labor, placental function, and immune responses [81]. Changes in glycosylation patterns during labor help prepare the placenta for the delivery process.

Glycosylation in cytokine signaling during labor

Pro-inflammatory cytokines

During labor, the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α increases. Glycosylation changes in the corresponding receptors can enhance or dampen the inflammatory response, promoting the initiation of labor and placental detachment [82]. Sialylation of cytokine receptors such as IL-6R can affect the inflammatory response in the placenta and the surrounding tissues, influencing the onset of labor [83].

Immune tolerance and inflammation

Glycosylation of immune regulatory proteins helps maintain the balance between immune tolerance and inflammation during pregnancy. The appropriate glycosylation of interferon receptors and IL-10 receptors ensures proper immune regulation at the fetal–maternal interface, preventing premature labor or excessive immune activation [84].

Epigenetic regulation via glycosylation

Histone modification

During labor, glycosylation modifications, such as N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) addition to histones, play a role in chromatin remodeling and gene expression, which can trigger the necessary cellular changes for labor [82]. These epigenetic modifications are crucial for the regulation of genes involved in inflammation and labor.

Abnormal glycosylation patterns and associated diseases

Glycosylation is a critical post-translational modification that plays a vital role in various cellular functions, including cell adhesion, migration, immune response, and signaling [85]. In trophoblast cells, which form the outer layer of the placenta, glycosylation modifications are essential for proper placentation and successful pregnancy. Abnormal glycosylation patterns can lead to various pregnancy-related complications [6]. This review categorizes the types of glycosylation modifications in trophoblast cells, their associated abnormalities, and their implications in pregnancy-related diseases (Figure 4).

The impact of abnormal glycosylation patterns in trophoblasts and associated pregnancy complications. Glycosylation modifications are critical for trophoblast cell functions such as adhesion, invasion, immune regulation, and signaling. Abnormal glycosylation patterns in trophoblast cells have been linked to several pregnancy-related diseases, affecting placental development and fetal outcomes.

Preeclampsia

Preeclampsia is a pregnancy-related disorder characterized by hypertension and proteinuria. Abnormal glycosylation patterns in trophoblast cells have been implicated in the pathogenesis of preeclampsia [86]. Integrated proteomic and N-glycoproteomic profiling of placental tissue from preeclamptic pregnancies has revealed significant alterations in site-specific glycosylation, particularly on proteins involved in angiogenesis and invasion [87]. However, a critical unanswered question is whether these glycan changes are a primary cause of defective placentation or a secondary consequence of the hypoxic and inflammatory placental environment. While animal models with engineered glycosyltransferase deficiencies often exhibit preeclampsia-like symptoms, the direct translational relevance to the complex human etiology remains uncertain.

N-Glycosylation abnormalities

Studies have shown that the N-glycan profile of trophoblasts in preeclamptic pregnancies differs significantly from that in normal pregnancies. Altered N-glycosylation can affect the signaling pathways involved in trophoblast invasion and angiogenesis, leading to impaired placentation and reduced maternal–fetal blood flow [88].

Sialylation changes

Increased sialylation of trophoblasts has been observed in preeclampsia, which may alter their interactions with maternal immune cells and contribute to the inflammatory environment characteristic of this condition [89].

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)

Gestational diabetes mellitus is a form of diabetes that occurs during pregnancy and is associated with insulin resistance [90].

Insulin receptor glycosylation

Abnormal glycosylation of insulin receptors in trophoblast cells can impair insulin signaling, contributing to the development of insulin resistance. This altered glycosylation may affect the trophoblast’s ability to respond to insulin, leading to inadequate glucose transport and increased fetal glucose levels [91].

Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)

IUGR is a condition where the fetus does not grow to its full potential in the womb, often due to placental insufficiency [92].

Fucosylation alterations

Changes in fucosylation patterns of trophoblast cells have been associated with IUGR. Altered fucosylation can impact cell adhesion and migration, resulting in inadequate trophoblast invasion and poor placental development [93].

O-glycosylation modifications

Abnormal O-glycosylation of adhesion molecules can impair trophoblast interactions with maternal tissues, contributing to placental dysfunction and restricted fetal growth[42].

Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL)

Recurrent pregnancy loss is defined as the occurrence of two or more consecutive pregnancy losses [94].

Glycan profile alterations

Changes in the glycan profile of trophoblast cells have been proposed as a potential factor in RPL. Abnormal glycosylation can influence trophoblast invasion and immune modulation, leading to inadequate placental development and early pregnancy failure [95].

Choriocarcinoma

Choriocarcinoma is a malignant tumor originating from trophoblastic tissue [96].

Oncofetal glycoproteins

Trophoblast glycoproteins, such as TPGB (5T4), exhibit altered glycosylation patterns in choriocarcinoma. These modifications can enhance tumor cell invasiveness and contribute to the metastatic potential of choriocarcinoma cells [97,98].

Critical assessment of translational potential

The promising association between glycan profiles and pregnancy disorders must be balanced against the significant challenges in clinical translation. The heterogeneity of clinical cohorts, pre-analytical variability in sample processing, and the high cost and technical complexity of glycomic platforms like LC–MS/MS pose substantial barriers to developing robust, clinically viable biomarkers. Furthermore, moving from biomarker association to therapeutic intervention—for instance, by targeting specific glycosyltransferases—is fraught with challenges, given the ubiquitous role of glycosylation in normal physiology and the risk of off-target effects. The path forward requires large, well-designed prospective cohort studies and the development of more accessible high-throughput glyco-analytical technologies.

Future perspectives

Emerging technologies such as single-cell glycomics (scGlycomics) will enable the dissection of glycosylation heterogeneity among trophoblast subpopulations. Trophoblast organoids and placenta-on-a-chip models offer physiologically relevant platforms for functional glycomics. Furthermore, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of glycosyltransferases (e.g. FUT8, MGAT5) will help establish causal links between glycan structures and phenotypic outcomes.

Summary

This review has emphasized the critical importance of glycosylation in trophoblast biology and maternal–fetal interactions, revealing how these modifications are integral to successful pregnancy outcomes. The key findings from our exploration highlight the multifaceted roles that glycosylation plays in various processes, particularly during embryo implantation and placental development.

Glycosylation modifications significantly affect the behavior of trophoblast cells. Specific N-glycans and O-glycans are essential for mediating cell adhesion and migration [34], which are crucial during the initial stages of implantation [99]. The adhesion of the blastocyst to the uterine epithelium is facilitated by glycan–ligand interactions, underscoring the importance of glycosylation in establishing the maternal–fetal interface [100]. Additionally, sialylated and fucosylated glycans have been shown to enhance trophoblast invasiveness, enabling these cells to penetrate maternal tissue and remodel blood vessels, critical for providing adequate nutrient supply to the developing embryo [101–103].

Glycosylation is vital for placental integrity and functionality. The presence of specific glycosylated molecules is linked to trophoblast differentiation into various subtypes, such as cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts, which are necessary for efficient nutrient transport and hormone secretion [12,25,104]. Disruptions in glycosylation patterns can lead to placental complications such as preeclampsia and insufficient placentation, emphasizing the need for precise regulation of glycosylation during pregnancy [105].

Glycosylated trophoblast antigens play a pivotal role in modulating the maternal immune system, promoting a state of immunotolerance that is essential for fetal survival [71,106]. By inhibiting maternal immune activation and engaging with receptors on immune cells, trophoblasts ensure that the maternal immune system does not reject the developing fetus [56,60,67]. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for addressing pregnancy complications arising from immune rejection.

The findings outlined in this review highlight the urgent need for ongoing research in the field of glycobiology, particularly regarding its implications for reproductive health [59,107]. Furthermore, emerging evidence underscores the spatiotemporal specificity of glycosyltransferase expression—such as FUT8 in early extravillous trophoblasts and ST3GAL1 in term syncytiotrophoblasts—which fine-tunes trophoblast behavior through key signaling pathways like IGF-1R and Wnt/β-catenin. These insights, bolstered by recent glycomic and genetic tools, not only clarify the mechanistic basis of glycosylation in placentation but also highlight its diagnostic and therapeutic potential. Future efforts should focus on validating these findings in clinically relevant models, such as trophoblast organoids and maternal blood-based glycan signatures, to bridge the gap between molecular understanding and clinical application in pregnancy disorders.

Overall, glycosylation emerges as a vital aspect of trophoblast function and maternal–fetal interactions, with significant implications for improving clinical practices in managing pregnancy. Continued exploration of this topic not only enriches our understanding of reproductive biology but also holds the potential to translate basic research findings into clinical applications that can significantly improve pregnancy outcomes.

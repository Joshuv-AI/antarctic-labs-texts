Residual hypertriglyceridemia in cardiac rehabilitation: Methodological constraints and implications for generalizability
Authors: Amisha Kumari, Reena Kumari, Muddassir Khalid, Fred Segawa
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

We read with interest the recent work by Tognola et al. evaluating hypertriglyceridemia in patients undergoing cardiac rehabilitation (CR) in the contemporary lipid-lowering era [1]. The study demonstrated a significant decrease in triglyceride levels during CR, but different methodological limitations need to be addressed before clinically generalizing the results. To start with, a comprehensive study design involving triglycerides concentrations at discharge at CR can infer causality between lipid measures and cardiovascular events that demands longitudinal follow-up on adjudicated endpoints [2]. Secondly, it only studies the participants of CR, which creates selection bias. Systematic underrepresentation of older adults, women, and socioeconomically disadvantaged patients in referral patterns will lead to cohorts that do not reflect the general population in ACS/CCS [3]. Thirdly, the measurements of triglycerides taken five weeks after structured exercise and dietary counselling might be underestimating the actual values of post-event prevalence of hypertriglyceridemia because aerobic exercise and even short training yield a substantial decline in triglyceride levels [4]. Finally, there was a strong male predominance (about 83 %) and a single-center and ethnically homogeneous (Italian) sample, which restricts external validity. There are sex and ancestry differences in lipid metabolism and triglyceride-based cardiovascular risk, which demonstrates the importance of having more diverse and gender-balanced participants [5]. Although the presented study is a good source of information on the dynamics of lipids in CR, future research needs to be carried out on a multicenter and longitudinal basis, involving baseline lipid profiles before intervention and recruiting a varied population.

The future of CV prevention science depends on how we communicate: why simultaneous publication matters
Authors: Khurram Nasir
Published: 2026

Prompt dissemination of well vetted science to its relevant stakeholder’s is a growing challenge in modern medicine [1]. Information now moves faster than at any previous point in history of scientific communication. In our academic enterprise, major conferences generate immediate global attention, digital dissemination occurs in real time via social and traditional media, and scientific narratives are often shaped within hours of data presentation.

However, at the same it well recognized that abstracts and presentations do not fully capture all the complexities of the scientific work [2]. Lack of entire peer reviewed manuscripts at the time of these scientific presentation may entail misinterpretation and premature extrapolation of data. Conversely, the traditional pathway of present first-publish later model, while served an important purpose during earlier eras of scientific exchange, probably does not cater to our current demands for timely information sharing with all relevant stakeholders.

In this evolving environment, simultaneous publications have increasingly become an important channel in our scientific communications. The coordination of presenting important scientific findings at key conferences, along with instant peer-reviewed publication ensure that visibility and rapid dissemination are accompanied by transparency, proper interpretation, and the preservation of the peer-review process' trust. Importantly, it also offers a chance to further provide context in the form of editorials and digital dissemination strategies that help place these findings within the broader context.

Recognizing the importance of this model, at American Journal of Preventive Cardiology (AJPC) we made a deliberate decision this year to actively pursue simultaneous publication as part of our broader editorial vision. Our participation at the American College of Cardiology (ACC) Scientific Sessions was a first major step in that direction, and, most significantly, our deliberate attempt to bring prevention science into the front of timely modern cardiology discussions.

The portfolio of studies simultaneously published during the ACC Scientific Sessions reflected the remarkable breadth of modern preventive cardiology and, importantly, the type of science AJPC is well positioned to elevate. While we recognize that AJPC is still early in its growth trajectory and investigators may initially reserve publishing primary findings from large pivotal trials in journals with well-established impact profiles, we believe there is an immediate and important role for AJPC in advancing the next layer of clinical CV prevention science.

Secondary analyses from several highly reputable clinical trials highlighted the sustainability of the treatment effect, safety, generalizability across patient groups, cardiometabolic implications, and implementation challenges [[3], [4], [5]]. Such analyses make important contributions provide meaningful insights beyond primary efficacy, by giving a deeper understanding of how, who, and under which conditions such an intervention could be used to improve the quality of preventive care [[3], [4], [5]]. In this regard, we hope that AJPC will play a unique role as a platform for extending important findings beyond the primary results of these clinical trials and providing our readers with relevant interpretation on the broader clinical utility of emerging interventions on cardiovascular health

We also believe that simultaneous publication should not be limited solely to primary clinical trial findings or secondary analyses from established trial programs. Well-designed observational studies provide important insights that helps the broader cardiovascular prevention community better understand disease trajectories, risk refinement, treatment decisions, and actual translation and implementation into practice.

For example, a key study from the UK Biobank on the association between serial inflammatory biomarker measurements provides a prime example of one of the core principles of prevention science, which is that cardiovascular risk is a dynamic process, and disease progression does not occur in a linear fashion [6]. Likewise, other investigations on coronary artery calcium progression, adipose tissue phenotyping, and advanced imaging techniques represent some of the recent progress made in moving away from simplistic risk estimation toward a more complete characterization of disease biology and progression [7,8].

In parallel, the expanding scope of prevention science will also require integration with implementation, technology, and health systems research. Simultaneously published articles include studies on the role of artificial intelligence algorithms in cardiovascular diagnostics in low-resource settings [9]. Other examples of concurrent research on prevention pharmacotherapies, residual-risk biomarker testing and treatment response, social determinants and immigrant health, environmental risk factors, digital lifestyle assessment, and health economics highlight that the future success of preventive cardiology will depend not only on scientific innovation, but also on effective, sustainable, and equitable implementation [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]].

Based on the enthusiastic feedback from authors and readers alike, we intend to continue pursuing simultaneous publication opportunities across future national and international scientific meetings. We encourage investigators presenting high-impact CV prevention studies to contact us journal well before conference deadlines, to facilitate timeline coordination, expectation, and an expedited peer-review process.

For us at AJPC, this initiative is deliberate attempt to attract the best CV prevention science, but also aligned our commitment that the most consequential advances science is disseminated in a manner that is timely, rigorously vetted, and appropriately contextualized. In doing so, we hope to fulfil our mission, serving as a bridge of getting the top sciences to all our readers in an expedited fashion.

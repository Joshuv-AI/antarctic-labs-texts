PDNO-21 Patterns of childhood gliomas at Mulago National Referral Hospital, Kampala, Uganda 2019-2024
Authors: Adanna Anthonia Umeokonkwo, Victoria Mwebe Katasi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimGliomas are the most common primary central nervous system tumors in children, and they originate from the glial cells. The incidence is about 3-5 cases per 100,000 population with a slight male predilection. We reviewed the cases of childhood gliomas from 2019 to 2024 to understand the patterns of their presentation and outcome.MethodWe reviewed the records of all childhood glioma patients who presented at the Paediatric Haemato-oncology Unit of the Mulago National Referral Hospital, Kampala, from 2019 to 2024. The data was analyzed using R version 4.4.1 (2024-06-14). The clinical features, investigations, types, treatment, outcomes, and median survival time during the period were estimated.ResultA total of 65 patients with a diagnosis of glioma were managed in this period. The median age of the patients at presentation was 7 (interquartile range [IQR]=4-10) years, 54.7% (35/65) were males, with a male: female ratio of 1.17:1, age 5-9 years constituted 43.1% (28/65) of the patients. The commonest presenting clinical features were abnormal gait (53.8%), headache (40%), slurred speech (27.7%), and squint (26.2%). About 63.1% had Computed Tomography scan and 40% had Magnetic Resonance Imaging. A total of 23 (35.4%) had surgery, 7 (10.8%) had radiotherapy and 4 (6.2%) had chemotherapy. The majority, 63.1%, were high-grade gliomas (diffuse intrinsic pontine gliomas being the most common, comprising 70.7% [29/41]), while among the low-grade gliomas, pilocytic astrocytoma was the most common at 37.5% (9/24). The median survival for the high-grade gliomas during the review period was 1 (IQR: 0-6) months, and for the low-grade gliomas was 28 (IQR: 20-48) months.ConclusionThe pattern of presentation of childhood gliomas within the period under review showed slight male predilection, the median survival time is comparable but could improve with some targeted radiation treatment, especially for high-grade gliomas

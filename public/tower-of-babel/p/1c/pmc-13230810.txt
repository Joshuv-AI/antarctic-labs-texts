A two-stage deep learning model for risk identification in green supply chain finance
Authors: Xueping Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong-standing challenges in the identification of risks in green supply chain finance stem from the complexity of data structures and the intertwining of non-linear information, leading to uncertainty. This study aims to construct a two-stage deep learning model that integrates Generative Adversarial Networks with a Residual Autoencoder (GAN-SAE) and Deep Neural Networks with Multi-Kernel Support Vector Machines (DNN-SVM) to achieve the intelligent and precise identification of risks. Based on a sample of 168 firms observed over the period 2015–2024, this study first employs a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network combined with a Residual Autoencoder (GAN-SAE) to generate and reconstruct high-risk samples. This process alleviates the scarcity of high-risk observations and mitigates feature redundancy, while simultaneously extracting discriminative risk representations. Building on this enhanced feature space, a hybrid classification framework integrating Deep Neural Networks and a Multi-Kernel Support Vector Machine (DNN-SVM) is further introduced to strengthen the mapping capability for complex nonlinear risk patterns and to achieve robust adaptive classification. Through this two-stage design, the proposed framework substantially improves the overall accuracy and stability of risk classification. Empirical results indicate that the proposed model demonstrates a significant advantage on the test set, achieving a classification accuracy of 0.923 ± 0.009, a recall rate of 0.893 ± 0.010, and an AUC value of 0.938 ± 0.008, outperforming the other comparative models. Moreover, the distribution of asset returns and liabilities of the generated samples aligns with that of the actual high-risk samples, validating the economic rationality of the model during the data augmentation phase. The risk heatmap results indicate that financial health and credit scores exhibit the highest contribution levels in the model’s risk prediction, serving as the most sensitive feature dimensions for risk discrimination. In contrast, ESG performance and supply chain stability function as auxiliary variables that further refine the model’s risk differentiation. The study achieves hierarchical expression of risk identification and optimization of feature coordination, offering a scalable intelligent recognition framework and methodological innovation for green financial risk control.Keywords: Green supply chain finance, Risk identification, Deep learning, GAN-SAE, DNN-SVMSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of green economy and sustainable finance, the supply chain system plays a crucial role in promoting industrial low-carbon transformation and efficient allocation of resources1. While financial institutions support enterprises in their green transformation, they also face the practical challenge of accurately identifying potential risks within the supply chain2. In practical risk identification scenarios, high-risk enterprises typically constitute only a small proportion of the overall sample, often accounting for less than 20%, thereby exhibiting pronounced class imbalance. Coupled with the complex nonlinear interactions among corporate environmental performance, supply chain resilience, and financial health, this scarcity and distributional skewness of the minority class further exacerbate the modeling difficulty of risk identification tasks. To this end, this study constructs a two-stage deep learning model, integrating data augmentation and feature extraction mechanisms, to achieve intelligent identification and precise prediction of green supply chain financial risks, thereby providing scientific support for financial decision-making.

In recent years, scholars have engaged in extensive exploration regarding the identification of supply chain financial risks. Traditional methods predominantly rely on statistical regression and multi-indicator comprehensive evaluation models, focusing on the linear correlation of financial indicators; however, they struggle to depict the non-linear factors such as ESG performance and supply chain stability3. With the advancement of machine learning and deep learning technologies, researchers have begun to introduce methods such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests (RF), and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) to enhance the accuracy of risk identification4,5. Building on this line of research, some studies have addressed feature redundancy in high-dimensional structured data by introducing intelligent optimization algorithms for feature selection and reconstruction. Although these approaches do not focus on the financial domain, they provide valuable methodological references for financial risk identification6. Some studies have mitigated the issue of sample imbalance through over-sampling techniques (e.g., SMOTE), while others have attempted to extract latent feature spaces using Autoencoders (AE) or Variational Autoencoders (VAE)7,8. However, these methods still exhibit apparent deficiencies in generating high-quality high-risk samples, maintaining data distribution consistency, and preventing model overfitting. In response to these issues, this paper proposes a two-phase deep learning model based on GAN-SAE and DNN-SVM. Under the aforementioned class-imbalance setting, risk identification models often suffer from insufficient sensitivity to minority-class samples. When high-risk enterprises account for only a small proportion of the sample (17% in this study), conventional classification models tend to prioritize overall predictive accuracy during training, which substantially constrains their ability to correctly identify high-risk cases. Baseline experimental results further indicate that, under such imbalanced distributions, commonly used models such as logistic regression and random forests exhibit limited recall for high-risk enterprises, rendering them inadequate for the practical requirements of risk early-warning scenarios that emphasize minority-class detection. The first phase utilizes Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) combined with Residual Autoencoders (SAE) for sample enhancement and feature refinement, fundamentally addressing the issues of scarce high-risk samples and redundant features, the second phase adopts a structure integrating Deep Neural Networks (DNN) and Multi-core Support Vector Machines (DNN-SVM), realizing the adaptive optimization of non-linear feature mapping and risk classification boundaries. The research results indicate that this model significantly outperforms traditional models on multiple indicators and is capable of stably identifying a firm’s risk level. This study provides an intelligent solution for the risk management of green supply chain finance. The constructed GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM two-phase model can effectively identify high-risk enterprises under conditions of scarce samples and complex features, enhancing the accuracy of risk assessment by financial institutions. Moreover, the study reveals the comprehensive impact of financial health, ESG performance, and supply chain stability on corporate risk, providing data support for green financial decision-making and resource allocation. It also offers data-driven decision support for financial institutions to achieve precise lending and risk prevention and control.

The organization of this article is as follows: the first section is an “Introduction”, where the research context, issues, and significance are introduced; the second section is a “Literature review”, systematically sorting out the technological evolution path of green supply chain financial risk identification; the third section introduces the “Research design”, including data sources, the construction of characteristic variables, and the specific implementation of the two-stage model; the “Research results and analysis” section displays the results and performance analysis of the model experiment, comparing the performance of different models in risk identification; the fifth section is the discussion part, interpreting and extending the findings of the research; finally, the “Conclusion” section summarizes the research findings and points out the direction for future research.

Literature review

Driven by the “dual carbon” strategy and sustainable finance, Green Supply Chain Finance (GSCF) has emerged as a critical link connecting green industries with financial services. By integrating green performance into credit assessment, GSCF fosters energy conservation and emission reduction as well as industrial transformation9, 10. However, the risk structure of GSCF is more complex, encompassing both credit and liquidity risks, as well as novel green risks such as carbon emissions and ESG fluctuations11. Traditional models struggle to address the identification requirements of nonlinear and multidimensional characteristics, necessitating the application of artificial intelligence to achieve intelligent and precise risk identification. In recent years, machine learning, deep learning, generative models, and hybrid intelligent models have gradually become key technological approaches for risk identification in GSCF.

With the diversification and complexity of supply chain finance data, machine learning algorithms, owing to their efficient data mining and pattern recognition capabilities, have become one of the main research directions for green supply chain finance risk identification. Wanget al.12 constructed a random forest (RF) optimization model based on multi-source information fusion for credit risk assessment in green supply chain finance for small and medium-sized enterprises. The research found that the model significantly outperformed traditional neural network and logistic regression methods in terms of precision and F1 score. Jahin et al.13 pointed out through a systematic review that in the field of supply chain risk assessment, random forests, XGBoost, and ensemble learning models excel in handling data heterogeneity and imbalanced indicators, providing algorithmic references for green supply chain finance risk identification. Zhou et al.14 further proposed a hierarchical knowledge transfer graph neural network (HKTGNN), which can capture implicit risk relationships between upstream and downstream enterprises in the supply chain network, effectively mitigating the problem of feature sparsity. Ordibazare et al.15 approached the issue from a methodological perspective, combining fuzzy C-Means clustering with machine learning prediction models to hierarchically classify and categorize supply chain risks, achieving early risk identification. Overall, machine learning technology in green supply chain finance risk identification has evolved from single-feature prediction to an integrated approach involving multi-source data fusion, network relationship modeling, and intelligent clustering analysis.

On the basis of the phased achievements of machine learning technology, scholars have started to explore the application of deep learning in the identification of green supply chain finance risks, in order to further break through the model’s ability to characterize complex nonlinear features. Cui and Yao16 combined the deep autoencoder (DAE) with reinforcement learning (RL), proposing a dynamic risk prediction framework that achieves intelligent risk regulation in the context of supply chain finance. Liu et al.17 specifically constructed a two-stage deep learning model for green supply chain finance, first utilizing the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to generate balanced samples, and then through the combination of Stacked Autoencoders (SAE) and Deep Neural Networks (DNN), significantly enhancing the accuracy and stability of high-risk enterprise identification. Tang and Li18 proposed a multi-scale fusion recognition network for environmental financial risk identification, achieving multi-level representation of high-dimensional data through deep convolutional feature extraction, providing technical insights for the identification of green supply chain finance risks. Zogaan et al.19 integrated Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), and Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTM) to predict risks in key industry supply chains, offering reference for the dynamic risk identification of green supply chain finance. Deep learning models possess significant advantages in capturing complex supply chain relationships, nonlinear risk patterns, and time series dynamic features, making them an important direction for the research on green supply chain finance risks.

Based on the significant advancements in deep learning, the academic community is increasingly focusing on the integration of generative models and hybrid intelligent models for the identification of green supply chain financial risks. Wan and Cui20 utilized evolutionary game theory models to dissect the interactive mechanisms between blockchain and artificial intelligence technologies in the context of agricultural supply chain financial risk mitigation, confirming the inhibitory effect of multi-technology integration on risk transmission. Zheng and Brintrup21 were pioneers in exploring the application of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in supply chain relationship forecasting, highlighting its potential in enhancing visualization and the capture of implicit correlations in sustainable supply chain risk analysis. Zhang etal.22 advanced the field by proposing a supply chain credit risk identification model based on generative adversarial networks (GANs), which effectively alleviated the problem of imbalanced datasets through the generation of high-quality minority samples, thereby enhancing the predictive accuracy. Galante etal.23 constructed a “FinancialTwinChain” hybrid intelligent platform from a systemic perspective, integrating artificial intelligence, blockchain, and knowledge graphs for the monitoring of supply chain financial sustainability and risk warning. This platform demonstrated the extensive applicability of hybrid intelligence in green finance scenarios. However, existing generative models and hybrid intelligent approaches are predominantly implemented through end-to-end deep fusion architectures. Although such designs may improve predictive performance to some extent, they often entail high computational costs, complex training procedures, and limited interpretability, thereby restricting their applicability in risk-sensitive financial contexts. When sample generation, feature learning, and risk classification are tightly coupled within a single integrated structure, the functional boundaries of each module in the risk identification process become difficult to delineate, which is not conducive to targeted optimization under class-imbalance conditions. In contrast, this study adopts a two-stage modeling framework that separates generative enhancement from risk discrimination. Sample generation and feature refinement are conducted in the first stage, while risk classification is implemented in the second stage. This structural decoupling reduces model complexity and enhances module controllability and interpretability. Consequently, the proposed framework not only alleviates the adverse effects of sample imbalance but also demonstrates the rationality and necessity of the two-stage design in financial risk identification tasks.

Current research has established a solid theoretical and methodological foundation for the identification of green supply chain financial risks, including data-driven risk modeling frameworks, sample enhancement approaches based on generative models, and feature expression mechanisms of deep learning. This has laid the technical groundwork for the model design in this paper. However, the current research still exhibits limitations such as insufficient model generalization, incomplete handling of sample imbalance, and inadequate exploration of high-dimensional feature interactions. Based on this, this paper proposes a two-stage deep learning model that integrates GAN-SAE and DNN-SVM, aiming to compensate for the deficiencies in risk identification accuracy and stability of existing studies through the synergistic optimization of generative enhancement and multi-core classification. This approach aims to achieve intelligent identification of green supply chain financial risks.

Research design

Data source and preprocessing

This study, after rigorous data screening, has ultimately selected 168 enterprises, spanning various industries, scales, and green supply chain management practices, representing a diverse array of green supply chain financial scenarios. The data of the selected enterprises primarily derives from public financial statements, ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) reports, and supply chain management data from 2015 to 2024, which includes financial indicators, supply chain risk-related metrics, and ESG ratings. Among the selected enterprises, they are categorized into two groups based on their financial health and historical risk event data: high-risk and low-risk. Specifically, low-risk enterprises constitute 83% of the total sample, amounting to 140 low-risk enterprises, while high-risk enterprises account for 17%, representing 28 high-risk enterprises. This study adopts an OR-based decision rule to define high-risk enterprises. An enterprise is classified as high risk if it satisfies any of the following conditions during the sample period: (1) the occurrence of publicly disclosed default events or material credit risk incidents; (2) the enterprise’s credit rating falls within the lowest 20% quantile of the sample distribution; (3) a sustained and significant deterioration trend in key financial indicators. Low-risk enterprises refer to those with robust financial conditions, high credit ratings, and stable supply chains. Such categorization facilitates the subsequent training of risk identification models, ensuring the models’ effectiveness in distinguishing between enterprises of different risk categories. To examine the sensitivity of the OR-based decision rule to threshold specification, this study slightly adjusted the credit rating quantile threshold while holding other conditions constant. According to the results reported in Table 1, when the threshold is adjusted to 15% or 25%, the model’s primary performance metrics do not exhibit substantive changes, with the fluctuation range of AUC remaining below 0.01. This indicates that the model demonstrates strong robustness with respect to alternative constructions of the high-risk label. Although the proportion of high-risk samples varies slightly under different threshold specifications, the identification capability of the two-stage framework remains stable. These findings suggest that the OR-based definition of high-risk enterprises maintains satisfactory robustness within a reasonable tail-interval range.

During the data preprocessing phase, missing values in the acquired raw data were first addressed. Given that certain enterprises exhibit missing values in financial statements and ESG ratings, and considering that financial variables typically display skewed distributions, this study employs industry-grouped median imputation to ensure data completeness. For outlier treatment, anomalous observations are first identified using boxplot analysis and the Z-Score method. In light of the commonly observed right-skewed distribution of financial variables, an interquartile range (IQR)-based trimming approach is subsequently applied to mitigate the potential influence of extreme values on model training outcomes. To eliminate the impact of varying scales among features, all financial data and numerical features were subjected to standardization or Min–Max normalization. For categorical variables, one-hot encoding (OHE) was applied to transform them into numerical features. To alleviate the scarcity of high-risk samples, generative augmentation was applied exclusively to high-risk observations during the training stage. After augmentation, the total number of high-risk samples was expanded to 140, of which 28 correspond to real high-risk enterprises and 112 are synthetic samples generated by the GAN-SAE. All low-risk enterprise samples were retained from the original dataset and were not subject to any form of synthesis or duplication. The data was partitioned into a training set (70%), a validation set (15%), and a test set (15%), ensuring robust model training and evaluation across different datasets. Specifically, the training set contains all 28 real high-risk samples along with the corresponding low-risk samples, which are used for GAN-SAE training and high-risk sample generation. In contrast, both the validation and test sets consist exclusively of real enterprise observations and contain no synthetic samples. Moreover, these subsets are not involved in the training of the GAN at any stage.

Construction of the two-phase model

This study proposes a two-stage deep learning model (Fig. 1), which combines a Generative Adversarial Network with a Sparse Autoencoder (GAN-SAE) to achieve data augmentation and feature refinement, followed by a Deep Neural Network and Multi-kernel Support Vector Machine (DNN-SVM) for nonlinear feature classification and risk identification. The model aims to address challenges in green supply chain finance scenarios, including high-dimensional data characteristics, sample imbalance, and complex risk patterns, thereby improving the accuracy and stability of high-risk enterprise identification.

Framework of the two-stage deep learning model.

To mitigate the risk of memorization-driven overfitting to the limited high-risk samples, multiple structural constraints are introduced in the GAN-SAE stage. Specifically, spectral normalization is employed to constrain the Lipschitz constant of the discriminator, thereby suppressing the generator’s tendency to overfit local samples. The residual autoencoder structure is designed to preserve the overall distributional information of input features, preventing excessive contraction of the latent space. Furthermore, a mutual information maximization constraint is incorporated to enhance the discriminative power and diversity of latent representations, encouraging the model to learn the underlying distributional structure of high-risk samples rather than memorizing individual observations.

Model training and evaluation

This study strictly follows a “split-first, generate-later” data processing principle. After sample augmentation and feature construction, the dataset is divided into training, validation, and test sets for model parameter learning and performance evaluation. The GAN-SAE model is trained and used for sample generation exclusively on the training set, while both the validation and test sets consist entirely of real samples and are not involved in the training of the generative model at any stage. A unified optimization strategy is adopted throughout the training process, and the final performance metrics are reported on an independent test set. During model training and evaluation, samples are constructed using a “firm–year” unit of observation, and the model learns and predicts solely based on feature information corresponding to the same year. Potential intertemporal correlations across different years for the same firm are not explicitly modeled as dependence structures; instead, they are implicitly reflected through year-specific variations in financial, credit, ESG, and supply chain features in the input space. In the evaluation stage, the model does not exploit cross-year information for inference, thereby avoiding the implicit amplification of temporal dependence that could bias performance estimation. Under this setting, the reported performance metrics reflect the model’s cross-sectional risk identification capability based on contemporaneous features. As both training and evaluation rely exclusively on same-year feature information and do not incorporate intertemporal states or future information, the omission of explicit temporal structures does not introduce systematic bias into the performance metrics, but only limits the extrapolation of the results to long-term risk evolution scenarios.

To mitigate potential overfitting risks arising from the model’s complex architecture, a unified and constrained hyperparameter tuning strategy is adopted during training. In the GAN-SAE stage, the number of network layers, the dimensionality of the latent space, and the number of adversarial training epochs are selected within predefined ranges based on validation-set performance, with no parameter adjustment informed by the test set. In the DNN stage, parameter redundancy under high-dimensional representations is mitigated through the incorporation of Dropout and L2 regularization. Specifically, the dropout rate in the hidden layers is set to 0.3, and the L2 regularization coefficient is fixed at 1 × 10⁻4. The Adam optimizer is adopted with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 32. An early stopping strategy is further implemented to control the number of training epochs: training is terminated if the validation loss does not decrease for 15 consecutive epochs, thereby preventing overfitting to the training samples. For the multi-kernel SVM, both the penalty coefficient and kernel weights are determined via cross-validation within a limited search space, ensuring an appropriate balance between model complexity and generalization capability.

Research results and analysis

Model performance evaluation

Convergence analysis

As shown in Fig. 2, all three models exhibit relatively stable convergence trends during training. Among them, the GAN-SAE model demonstrates a rapid decline in loss on both the training and validation sets, followed by synchronized stabilization. The magnitude of fluctuations during the convergence phase remains limited, indicating satisfactory optimization stability under the current training configuration, with no evident signs of overfitting. In comparison, the SMOTE-SAE model shows a relatively slower decrease in loss and requires more iterations during the early convergence stage. The VAE-SAE model, influenced by its stochastic sampling mechanism, exhibits a certain degree of fluctuation during training; however, it is still able to achieve gradual convergence overall. Overall, the GAN-SAE model, leveraging the synergistic optimization of the generative adversarial mechanism and the stacked autoencoder structure, achieves faster convergence of the loss function. It significantly improves convergence accuracy while maintaining feature representation integrity, proving its superior robustness in sample generation and feature representation learning for green supply chain finance risk identification.

A comparison between training and validation losses indicates that none of the models exhibits a pattern of continuously decreasing training loss accompanied by a pronounced rebound in validation loss during convergence. This suggests that, under the current data scale and training configuration, the models do not demonstrate significant overfitting risk. Overall, by integrating the nonlinear feature mapping capability of deep neural networks with the structured discriminative mechanism of support vector machines, the DNN-SVM model achieves a relatively balanced trade-off between convergence efficiency and stability, thereby providing a more reliable classification foundation for green supply chain finance risk identification tasks.

Overall performance evaluation

To comprehensively validate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed two-stage model of GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM, this paper designs five groups of contrastive models. SMOTE-SAE + DNN-SVM represents the baseline model of traditional oversampling methods, used to verify the effectiveness of the improvement in the generation mechanism; VAE-SAE + DNN-SVM reflects the performance upper limit of variational autoencoder in the sample generation stage; GAN-SAE + DNN-Softmax and GAN-SAE + CNN-SVM are used to test the impact of the difference in classifier type and feature extraction structure on the results. This setting follows the “single-factor progressive control principle”, that is, while maintaining the overall consistency of the model framework, the generation module (SMOTE, VAE, GAN) and classification module (Softmax, CNN, SVM) are gradually replaced, thus scientifically dissecting the source of performance improvement of the model. Through such a combinatorial comparison, it can be ensured that the advantages of the model in this study come from the cumulative effect of structural innovation.

Within the data processing pipeline, the generative model is trained and applied for sample generation exclusively on the training set. Neither the validation set nor the test set participates in the generative process or parameter updating at any stage. Therefore, no synthetic samples are introduced into the validation or test subsets, and data leakage is strictly avoided. All performance metrics reported in Fig. 4 are computed on the test set containing only real enterprise observations. The generated samples are used solely for data augmentation during the training phase. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the five models demonstrate pronounced disparities across five evaluation metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the ROC curve (AUC). From an overall perspective, the GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model exhibits the best performance across all metrics. Its accuracy reaches 0.923 ± 0.009, representing an improvement of approximately 7.8% over the SMOTE-SAE + DNN-SVM model’s 0.856 ± 0.013 and a 6.1% enhancement compared to the VAE-SAE + DNN-SVM model’s 0.870 ± 0.012. This indicates that the GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model not only achieves higher identification accuracy in terms of overall performance, but also maintains stable outputs across multiple random data splits, demonstrating superior statistical stability and generalization capability.

Comparative overall performance of different models.

From the perspective of generation mechanism, GAN-SAE shows significant advantages over SMOTE-SAE and VAE-SAE. SMOTE generates synthetic samples through linear interpolation, which may cause feature redundancy and blurred sample boundaries, thus reducing the model’s sensitivity to complex risk samples; VAE can generate data through modeling the latent variable distribution, but its latent space follows the Gaussian assumption, leading to the generation of samples that are too concentrated in the feature space and unable to capture the nonlinear distribution features of high-risk enterprises. In contrast, GAN-SAE adopts a dual architecture that integrates a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network with a Residual Autoencoder. Through the adversarial learning mechanism, it enhances the realism and diversity of generated samples, enabling the model to achieve leading performance in terms of Recall (0.893 ± 0.010) and AUC (0.938 ± 0.008). Importantly, this advantage does not stem from the use of synthetic samples during the testing phase. Rather, it arises from the GAN’s ability to more accurately capture the latent distribution structure of minority-class samples during training, thereby improving the model’s generalization performance on the real test set.

In the comparison during the classification phase, the DNN-SVM model exhibits more prominent performance compared to the CNN-SVM and DNN-Softmax models. Although the CNN-SVM possesses the advantage of local feature extraction, it is primarily applicable to image or spatial data, demonstrating limited capability in modeling global nonlinear associations for structured enterprise indicator data. The DNN-Softmax, due to its use of a linearly separable assumption in the multi-class output layer, is unable to fully capture the high-order risk relationships in complex feature spaces, leading to phenomena such as blurred classification boundaries or misclassified sample clustering. In contrast, the DNN-SVM combines the high-dimensional embedding features of deep neural networks with the structured hyperplane optimization of multi-kernel support vector machines, enabling simultaneous characterization of the distribution characteristics of both local and global features. Nonlinear boundary adaptive learning is achieved through the adjustment of kernel weights. Empirical results indicate that the DNN-SVM model outperforms both CNN-SVM and DNN-Softmax in terms of Precision (0.903 ± 0.011) and F1-score (0.898 ± 0.010). These findings suggest that DNN-SVM is more effective in constructing nonlinear decision boundaries, enabling it to enhance recall while simultaneously controlling the false-positive rate, thereby achieving a more balanced risk identification performance.

Further analysis from the perspective of generalization performance shows that the standard deviations of all five evaluation metrics for the GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model are controlled within ± 0.011, indicating strong stability and robustness against random fluctuations. Since all evaluations are conducted exclusively on a test set composed of real enterprise observations, this stability can be interpreted as evidence of consistent generalization performance under real data conditions, rather than as an artifact introduced by the data augmentation process.

The combination of these two indicators can directly reveal the risk status of a company on the “profitability–debt” dimension, and thus, they are selected as the main comparative variables for analyzing the rationality of the feature distribution of the generated samples by GAN-SAE. This distribution difference indicates that the GAN-SAE model can effectively capture the financial characteristic patterns of high-risk enterprises during the sample generation process, making the generated data consistent with the distribution of real risk samples yet distinct, thereby enhancing the representativeness of the samples and the discriminative ability of the model. The conditional generation mechanism of GAN allows the model to generate samples that conform to real economic logic under given risk feature constraints, while the feature extraction function of SAE further strengthens the structural consistency of the generated samples in the latent space. The combination of these two mechanisms not only improves the sample imbalance issue but also enhances the precision and stability of risk identification at the level of high-dimensional features.

External validation results

To further enhance the generalizability and empirical credibility of the research findings, this study constructs a large-scale and fully independent external validation dataset. This dataset is deliberately differentiated from the training samples in both industry structure and time dimension. The samples are primarily drawn from industries not included in the training set, such as agriculture and agricultural product processing, logistics and supply chain services, and wholesale and retail services. These industries differ markedly from resource-intensive sectors such as manufacturing and energy in terms of business models, asset structures, and risk exposure mechanisms. The external validation sample spans the period from 2021 to 2024 and comprises 642 firms, yielding a total of 2,568 firm–year observations. At no stage did the external validation data participate in model training, generative data augmentation, or parameter tuning; it was used exclusively for post-training performance evaluation.

In the external validation experiment, the GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model was directly applied to the independent dataset for risk prediction, without any form of retraining or model fine-tuning. The evaluation metrics include Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, and AUC, and all results are reported as mean ± standard deviation over multiple repeated runs. To further examine performance variations across different industry contexts, the external validation samples were grouped according to firms’ industry classifications, and comparative analyses were conducted on model performance across industries. As shown in Fig. 7, the model demonstrates the most stable performance in the agriculture and agricultural product processing sector, with Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, and AUC reaching 0.922 ± 0.009, 0.912 ± 0.010, 0.900 ± 0.008, 0.907 ± 0.009, and 0.946 ± 0.007, respectively. In the logistics and supply chain services sector, the performance metrics exhibit a slight decline but remain at relatively high levels, with an Accuracy of 0.916 ± 0.010, a Recall of 0.893 ± 0.009, and an AUC of 0.940 ± 0.008. In contrast, the wholesale and retail services sector shows a moderate reduction in model performance, with Accuracy, Recall, and F1-score of 0.903 ± 0.012, 0.876 ± 0.011, and 0.882 ± 0.012, respectively. However, the AUC remains at a comparatively high level of 0.928 ± 0.009, indicating that the model retains acceptable discriminative capability in this industry context. From the perspective of industry-level external validation, the model exhibits a certain degree of performance variation across different sectoral samples, with relatively weaker risk identification performance observed in the wholesale and retail industry. However, this discrepancy does not demonstrate a persistent or unidirectional imbalance, nor does it indicate any systematic disadvantage toward a specific industry. Further analysis suggests that inter-industry differences are primarily reflected at the threshold-sensitivity level, while the model’s overall ranking capability remains relatively consistent across industries. This implies that the cross-industry risk discrimination logic of the model retains structural stability. The wholesale and retail sector is characterized by frequent cash flow fluctuations and more dynamic operational cycles, where short-term risk signals tend to exhibit stronger volatility. Such characteristics may influence sensitivity under fixed classification thresholds. Therefore, the differences observed in external validation are more likely attributable to structural industry heterogeneity rather than to any inherent structural bias within the model itself.

Discussion

Discussion of research findings

This study, focusing on the issue of risk identification in green supply chain finance, proposes a dual-phase deep learning framework combining GAN-SAE with DNN-SVM. The model achieves dynamic enhancement and feature refinement of high-risk samples through the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Autoencoders, followed by joint modeling with Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) and Multi-core Support Vector Machines (SVMs), thereby effectively enhancing the precision and stability of enterprise risk identification under the complex background of sample imbalance and feature nonlinearity. The GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model is a nonlinear architecture primarily oriented toward predictive performance and therefore retains certain “black-box” characteristics in terms of methodological attributes. Its advantages lie mainly in the enhancement of risk identification accuracy and stability, rather than in intrinsic interpretability. Accordingly, the risk heatmap and SHAP analyses presented in this study are intended to illustrate differences in the model’s response to various features during prediction, reflecting associative structures at the discriminative level rather than causal relationships. Furthermore, the model was subjected to generalization testing on a fully independent external validation dataset. This dataset is distinct from the training samples in both industry structure and temporal dimension and was not involved in any generative procedures or parameter tuning processes. The external validation results indicate that the model maintains stable performance on key metrics such as AUC and Recall, suggesting that the proposed approach does not rely solely on the limited risk patterns observed during the training stage. The results indicate that the proposed model significantly outperforms traditional and single-phase models in terms of classification accuracy, recall rate, and AUC, among other metrics, verifying the feasibility and superiority of the dual-phase structure in risk identification for green supply chain finance.

Given the demand for interpretable decision-making in financial risk management under regulatory and operational contexts, this study further discusses the results from the perspective of translating model outputs into practical applications. The risk scores generated by the model are more appropriately interpreted as tools for risk stratification rather than as binary decisions based on a single threshold. Their core value lies in identifying enterprises that persistently remain within high-risk intervals. In high-risk classification, the model typically evaluates whether unfavorable conditions across multiple dimensions—such as financial health, ESG performance, and supply chain stability—co-occur within the same enterprise, rather than reacting to anomalies in a single indicator. Such multidimensional risk feature combinations exhibit a certain degree of stability across different samples and time windows, enabling the model to continuously identify potential high-risk entities. This configuration-based recognition mechanism provides operational support for risk monitoring, graded management, and intervention decision-making.

This paper continues the theoretical and methodological trends of recent research on artificial intelligence in supply chain finance risk identification, while achieving systematic innovation in algorithm design and adaptation to green finance contexts. Zhang et al.24 modeled credit risk prediction in supply chain finance using deep reinforcement learning, validating the advantages of intelligent learning in addressing imbalanced samples and complex risk structures, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for this paper’s adoption of intelligent algorithms to enhance risk identification accuracy. However, this paper introduces conditional generative adversarial networks and residual autoencoders (GAN-SAE) on this basis, achieving high-fidelity generation of high-risk samples and optimization of the latent space, and combines deep neural networks with multi-core support vector machines (DNN-SVM), employing kernel weighting and confidence reweighting mechanisms to achieve high recall identification with low false positives, significantly outperforming traditional reinforcement learning approaches at the model level. Wang and Liu25 evaluated green supply chain finance credit risk using a random forest model optimized by genetic algorithms through multi-source information fusion, demonstrating the importance of multidimensional information in risk identification and providing a reference for this paper’s feature system construction. Unlike this study’s reliance on traditional ensemble algorithms, this paper proposes an “generation—representation—classification” integrated framework, using GAN-SAE to enhance data quality and extract deep representations, followed by DNN-SVM to establish adaptive nonlinear discriminative boundaries, achieving deep integration from the feature layer to the classification layer. Cheah et al.26 utilized the SMOTE-GAN hybrid oversampling technique to improve minority class sample identification in financial fraud detection, offering inspiration for this paper’s data balancing mechanisms. This paper further employs residual autoencoding mechanisms to preserve the information integrity of generated samples, while introducing generative augmentation strategies into green supply chain finance contexts, integrating ESG, supply chain stability, and credit features, thereby expanding the applicability of this method in sustainable finance data environments. Furthermore, within the broader research trajectory of green finance and sustainable development, existing studies have predominantly focused on the mechanisms through which financial risks influence firms’ green development outcomes. Yang and Che27, for instance, examine the impact pathways of shadow banking risk exposure on the resilience of firms’ green productivity, highlighting the relationship between financial risk and green development performance. In contrast, this study shifts the analytical focus upstream to the problem of risk identification in the context of green supply chain finance, emphasizing the classification and prediction of enterprise risk states through deep learning techniques. Accordingly, within the framework of green finance research, this study extends the prevailing explanatory logic—from analyzing how risks affect green development outcomes—to a methodological logic centered on the construction of risk identification and early-warning tools. In doing so, it complements the existing literature by enriching the research paradigm from outcome-oriented analysis to data-driven risk recognition and prediction.

The primary contribution of this study lies in advancing the intelligent and systematic development of green supply chain finance risk identification. Firstly, this paper constructs a dual-stage deep learning framework that integrates data generation and feature discrimination capabilities, providing a novel holistic modeling approach for financial risk identification methodologies. Secondly, the research incorporates the concept of sustainability into the risk assessment system, achieving an organic integration of green finance, supply chain management, and artificial intelligence technologies, thereby broadening the theoretical boundaries of green finance risk control. Lastly, the study empirically validates the effectiveness of the multi-dimensional data-driven risk identification model in complex financial scenarios, offering academic support and practical references for digital regulation and intelligent decision-making in green supply chain finance.

Despite the fact that the two-stage deep learning model proposed in this study demonstrates relatively strong predictive performance and stability in the task of green supply chain financial risk identification, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the model relies on relatively complete and high-quality ESG indicators and supply chain-related data during both training and evaluation. However, such data in practical financial contexts are often subject to insufficient disclosure, inconsistencies in statistical standards, and limited accessibility. Differences across data sources in terms of indicator definitions, measurement approaches, and update frequencies may lead to inconsistencies and reduced comparability in the input features, thereby introducing potential biases into the model training process and weakening its applicability in scenarios characterized by data heterogeneity or information incompleteness. Second, the model is primarily constructed based on firm-level features within a single year and does not explicitly model intertemporal correlations or time-dependent structures, nor does it incorporate a strictly defined out-of-time validation framework. As a result, the model is limited in its ability to capture the transmission mechanisms and cumulative effects of risk across different time horizons, and lacks a systematic representation of the dynamic evolution and structural shifts of financial and supply chain risks under changing macroeconomic conditions, policy adjustments, or market fluctuations. This constrains its stability and generalization capability in cross-temporal settings. Third, the validation of the proposed framework in this study is mainly conducted from the perspectives of methodological effectiveness and model performance, without systematically incorporating practical constraints such as financial regulatory compliance, model auditing, algorithmic transparency, and interpretability. In real-world applications, risk identification models are typically required to satisfy multiple criteria, including regulatory compliance, traceability of risk decisions, and interpretability of model outputs. In the absence of these constraints, the operational feasibility and practical adaptability of the model remain limited. Fourth, although the proposed model exhibits a certain degree of robustness to variations in multidimensional risk features within the scope of the training data, its identification capability is inherently dependent on the risk patterns represented in the observed samples. For risk factors, unexpected events, or structural changes that fall outside the distribution of the training data, the model lacks an effective mechanism for identification and response. When confronted with out-of-distribution scenarios or abnormal risk conditions, the predictive outcomes may involve a degree of uncertainty, which constitutes an inherent limitation of the model in real-world deployment contexts.

Model interpretability, computational complexity, and practical deployment analysis

In financial risk management applications, model interpretability, ethical implications, and engineering feasibility constitute essential dimensions for evaluating methodological effectiveness. In response to these considerations, this study further discusses the proposed two-stage model with respect to interpretability boundaries, computational complexity structure, and practical deployment suitability.

At the level of model interpretability and ethics, the proposed approach emphasizes structural interpretability at the model level rather than causal inference. The GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM framework achieves risk identification through nonlinear feature learning and discriminative boundary optimization, and its outputs reflect predictive association structures learned under the given data distribution and training objective constraints. Accordingly, visualization tools such as risk heatmaps are employed to characterize the relative response intensities of different features during the model’s decision process, thereby facilitating an understanding of model behavior rather than directly measuring the causal contribution of individual features. In practical applications, the model is positioned as a risk screening and decision-support tool, and its outputs should be used in conjunction with human review and compliance mechanisms to mitigate the risk of amplifying potential data biases in automated decision-making.

In terms of algorithmic complexity and computational efficiency, the proposed method exhibits structural differences compared with existing baseline models. From the perspective of cost composition, the primary computational burden of the proposed framework is concentrated in the training stage, particularly in the adversarial generation and feature representation learning processes of the GAN-SAE phase. All experiments were conducted on a computing server equipped with an Intel Xeon Silver 4210 CPU (2.20 GHz), 64 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB memory). Under the same hardware configuration, five-fold cross-validation protocol, and unified hyperparameter search setting, the complete training time of XGBoost was 20.4 min, while LightGBM required 18.6 min. In comparison, the GAN-SAE stage of the proposed model required approximately 24 min on average, and the DNN-SVM classification stage required about 5 min. Although the overall training time is moderately higher than that of single-stage models, the additional computational cost primarily arises from the generative enhancement phase. This phase constitutes an offline pretraining procedure that is executed only when the model is updated or when the sample distribution changes, and it does not participate in the online risk evaluation process. During inference, the average time required to generate a single enterprise risk prediction is approximately 2.8 ms, compared with 2.4 ms for XGBoost and 2.2 ms for LightGBM, all within the millisecond-level range. In batch evaluation scenarios, the proposed model can stably support 3,000–4,000 enterprise risk predictions per second, maintaining overall inference costs within a controllable range. Therefore, under the premise of a moderate increase in offline training cost, the proposed framework achieves substantial performance improvements, forming an acceptable engineering trade-off between predictive performance and computational efficiency.

From a deployment perspective, the proposed model is well aligned with the operational characteristics of financial institutions’ risk management workflows. Enterprise risk assessment is typically conducted in offline or quasi-real-time batch processing modes, which is highly compatible with the model’s training–inference decoupled structure. Model parameters can be updated periodically in an offline setting, while only the inference module is deployed within business systems to generate risk scores for newly observed samples. Moreover, the model inputs are based on structured financial, ESG, and supply chain indicators, allowing seamless integration with existing data interfaces in risk management systems and thereby reducing system deployment and maintenance costs.

Conclusion

The research addresses the issue of risk identification in green supply chain finance by constructing a two-stage deep learning model integrated with GAN-SAE and DNN-SVM, achieving a systematic optimization of high-risk sample generation, feature extraction, and classification identification. The specific research findings are as follows:

First, the overall performance of the model is relatively strong. Under the sample scope and experimental settings of this study, the GAN-SAE + DNN-SVM model performs better than the comparative models across all evaluation metrics, with a classification accuracy of 0.923 ± 0.009, a recall rate of 0.893 ± 0.010, and an AUC of 0.938 ± 0.008, indicating favorable identification capability and discriminative performance. The results suggest that the generative mechanism of GAN-SAE enhances the representation of high-risk samples to a certain extent, while the multi-kernel structure of DNN-SVM contributes to the optimization of nonlinear decision boundaries, thereby achieving a relatively balanced trade-off between precision and recall.

Second, the distribution of generated samples exhibits a certain degree of consistency with high-risk enterprise characteristics. Under the data construction and model configuration of this study, the samples generated by GAN-SAE show distributional patterns of return on assets and debt ratio that are broadly aligned with those of high-risk enterprises. Specifically, the original samples are mainly concentrated within a medium debt ratio range (0.45–0.55) and a relatively high return on assets range (0.08–0.10), whereas the generated samples are more densely distributed within a higher debt ratio range (0.55–0.70) and a lower return on assets range (0.05–0.07), reflecting patterns similar to those observed in high-risk enterprises. These findings indicate that, within the current modeling framework, the generative process is able to capture the distributional characteristics of financial features to a certain extent, while maintaining a balance between data augmentation and economic interpretability.

Third, the risk identification results exhibit a certain hierarchical structure. Within the multidimensional feature space, the model forms a relatively clear stratification pattern of risk levels. The risk heatmap shows that different risk levels correspond to systematically differentiated feature response patterns, among which financial health and credit rating demonstrate higher sensitivity in identifying high-risk enterprises. Consistent with this observation, the SHAP analysis further indicates that financial health status and credit rating contribute most prominently to the model’s predictions, whereas ESG performance and supply chain stability function as complementary features, playing auxiliary and constraining roles in the risk discrimination process. Overall, the results reflect a structured pattern of risk identification driven by the joint effect of multiple feature dimensions.

The findings of this study demonstrate the effectiveness and feasibility of the two-stage deep learning framework in green supply chain finance risk identification. Future research may further extend this work in several directions. First, alternative or weakly supervised information sources could be incorporated to alleviate the limited availability of high-quality ESG and supply chain data across different market environments, thereby enhancing model robustness under data-constrained conditions. Second, while maintaining data consistency and comparability, future studies may introduce rolling-window validation frameworks or sequence-aware modeling approaches to systematically evaluate the incremental value of temporal structures for risk identification and early-warning performance. By integrating higher-frequency or multi-source time-series data, it may be possible to further improve the model’s capability to capture risk evolution paths and enhance its practical utility. Third, future research may more closely integrate regulatory requirements and real-world business processes to conduct comprehensive evaluations of model compliance, interpretability, and the implications of risk misclassification. Such efforts would facilitate the standardized application and operational deployment of intelligent models in green finance risk management contexts. Fourth, by incorporating rolling update mechanisms, higher-frequency or heterogeneous data sources, and combining dynamic modeling strategies with human–machine collaborative frameworks, future research may enhance the model’s adaptability to distributional shifts and emerging systemic risks, thereby strengthening its sustained applicability in complex financial environments.

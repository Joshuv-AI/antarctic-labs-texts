Butyrate Silences MUC5AC in Airway Epithelial Cells Through Histone Deacetylation at Its Promoter Region
Authors: Sang Hee Jo, Ikhyeon Bae, Myeong Seong Sim, Hye Jeong Kim, Chun Kim, Il Yup Chung, Hun Soo Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeMucin 5AC (MUC5AC) is a mucin that forms a gel on the surface of the airway epithelium, and its hyperproduction plays a pathological role in chronic inflammatory airway diseases. Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), microbial fermentation products of dietary fiber in the gut, have been shown to significantly impact the development of allergic airway disease. Nonetheless, little is known about whether and how SCFAs influence the function of airway epithelial cells. In this study, we investigated the direct effects of SCFAs, especially butyrate, on MUC5AC production and the underlying mechanisms.MethodsNCI-H292 cells and normal human bronchial epithelial cells were treated with inflammatory mediators and butyrate. MUC5AC expression, signal transduction pathways, and promoter histone acetylation were assessed using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, immunoblotting, immunocytochemistry, and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays.ResultsAmong SCFAs, butyrate has a strong inhibitory effect on MUC5AC expression at low micromolar concentrations in airway epithelial cells. Butyrate abolished MUC5AC expression induced by various inflammatory mediators, including epidermal growth factor (EGF). In line, butyrate upregulated forkhead box protein A (FOXA) 2, which inhibits goblet cell differentiation, while it downregulated SAM-pointed domain-containing ETS transcription factor (SPDEF) and FOXA3, which promote goblet cell metaplasia. Interestingly, butyrate exerts its inhibitory effect independent of its receptors and does not interfere with the early phase of EGF receptor signaling pathway. ChIP analysis revealed that butyrate increased H3K27 acetylation in both the proximal and distal regions of the MUC5AC promoter at the late phase following EGF stimulation, but this acetylation returned to baseline in the presence of butyrate.ConclusionsOur findings indicate that butyrate plays a critical role in reprogramming the transcriptional and epigenetic regulation of MUC5AC production. This may represent a general mechanism for controlling MUC5AC production in both healthy and diseased states.

INTRODUCTION

Mucins secreted in the conducting airways serve versatile functions, including mucociliary clearance of inhaled particles, physical barriers, and innate host defense by forming an appropriate mucus layer. The number and activity of mucin-producing goblet cells are normally low in the absence of inflammation but are significantly elevated in response to acute and chronic inflammatory insults.1 Mucin 5AC (MUC5AC) is the predominant mucin produced by the surface epithelium in conducting airways and airway epithelial cells in culture.2 Dysregulated expression of MUC5AC plays a pathological role in chronic inflammatory airway diseases, such as severe asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).3 Interestingly, a recent study highlighted the importance of MUC5AC in coordinated mucociliary transport under normal conditions.4 Several studies have revealed the upregulation of MUC5AC expression by a wide array of stimuli in vitro and in different pathological contexts, including ambient irritants (cigarette smoke and acrolein), bacterial products (lipopolysaccharide [LPS], lipoteichoic acid, flagellin), cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukin [IL]-1β, IL-13, IL-17), growth factors (epidermal growth factor [EGF], transforming growth factor-α), proteases (neutrophil elastase, trypsin-like protease), danger-associated molecular patterns (S100 proteins, ATP), and lipid mediators (platelet-activating factor, lysophosphatidylserine [LysoPS]).56 These stimuli activate distinct and shared signaling molecules and transcription factors such as mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB), JAK/STAT6, Sp1, AP1, CREB, and hypoxia-inducible factor-1α, leading to the transcriptional activation of MUC5AC gene.67 Furthermore, goblet cell differentiation and MUC5AC expression are controlled by a transcriptional network involving positive regulators, such as SAM-pointed domain-containing ETS transcription factor (SPDEF) and forkhead box protein A (FOXA) 3, and a negative regulator, FOXA2.89 For instance, EGF and IL-13 signaling cascades upregulate SPDEF and downregulate FOXA2, leading to MUC5AC expression.10 Despite extensive research on stimulants, their receptors, downstream signaling pathways, and transcription factors that bind to functional cis-elements on the MUC5AC promoter, little is known about the epigenetic regulation of MUC5AC expression. The MUC5AC promoter has few CpG sites, indicating the absence of epigenetic control at the DNA methylation level.11 Additionally, few studies have explored the involvement of histone modifications and chromatin accessibility in MUC5AC expression, which could play a crucial role in maintaining balanced mucin production under steady-state conditions and in controlling airway mucin overproduction across specific stimulants.

Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) are carboxylic acids linked to saturated aliphatic chains of 2–6 carbon atoms long. The most abundant SCFAs, acetate, propionate, and butyrate, are produced by the fermentation of undigested dietary fiber or oligosaccharides by intestinal anaerobic bacteria.12 In the proximal colon, SCFAs typically reach concentrations in a few dozen millimolar range, but are rapidly metabolized by colonocytes and absorbed into the bloodstream. Butyrate occurs at low micromolar concentrations in blood circulation and most tissues, including the lungs.13 Many studies have demonstrated that SCFAs provide a link between diet, gut microbiota, and host immune homeostasis and accentuate their roles in the regulation of inflammatory and immunological processes in respiratory, gastrointestinal, and neurological diseases.14 The mechanisms underlying the activities of SCFAs include histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibition and G protein-coupled receptor (GPCRs) activation.1516 SCFAs promote and suppress immune responses through a combination of these mechanisms. Among the 3 SCFAs, butyrate exhibited the most potent inhibition of HDACs, with half-maximal inhibitory concentration values of approximately 10–20 μM,17 followed by propionate, whereas acetate showed little HDAC inhibitory activity.18 Additionally, SCFAs exert their effects by activating specific receptors, including GPR41, GPR43, and GPR109A. All these receptors are activated by SCFAs at a half-maximum effective concentration of approximately 0.5 mM, and they are sensitive to pertussis toxin (PTX), indicating their coupling to Gi/Go signaling.1920 Activation of these receptors is shown to downregulate NF-κB downstream gene expression and several signaling pathways, including MAPKs (extracellular signal-regulated kinases [ERKs], JUN, and p38).21

Clinical and experimental studies have revealed a significant impact of SCFAs, especially butyrate, on the development of allergic airway disease, suggesting a close relationship between the gut microbiome and lung health.2223 In these studies, oral administration of SCFAs was found to alleviate the key characteristics of asthma, including airway hyperresponsiveness, infiltration of eosinophils, and FoxP3+ regulatory T cells in the lung tissues of an allergen-induced murine model. Interestingly, perturbing butyrate-producing bacteria in neonatal mice through antibiotic treatment with vancomycin increases susceptibility to allergic asthma later in life.24 Furthermore, the levels of butyrate, but not acetate or propionate, were reduced in the cecum and feces of vancomycin-treated mice. However, supplementation of drinking water with a mixture of butyrate, acetate, and propionate partially restores butyrate levels and ameliorates disease severity.25 Another intriguing finding was that children with the highest level of butyrate in their feces during their early lives had a reduced risk of developing sensitization to food and/or inhalant allergens, which was associated with fewer allergic diseases later in life.26 While none of these studies directly investigated the effects of SCFAs on dysregulated mucin production in the conducting airways, the improvements in lung pathophysiology following the administration of butyrate and other SCFAs suggest that a decrease in mucin production may accompany these beneficial effects. In the present study, we investigated the direct effects of SCFAs, especially butyrate, on MUC5AC production and the underlying mechanisms. We identified a potent inhibitory effect of butyrate on MUC5AC production and elucidated the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying its ability to suppress MUC5AC expression in airway epithelial cells.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Materials

EGF and IL-1β were obtained from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA). Butyrate, sodium acetate, sodium propionate, valproic acid (VPA), trichostatin A (TSA), LPS, phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA), hydrogen peroxide, AR420626 (a GPR41 agonist), CFMB (a GPR43 agonist), and monomethyl fumarate (a GPR109A agonist) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). The 18:1 LysoPS was purchased from Avanti Polar Lipids (Alabaster, AL, USA). The Gi/o protein inhibitor PTX was obtained from Invitrogen (Carlsbad, CA, USA).

Cell cultures for NCI-H292 and human bronchial epithelial cells

The NCI-H292 human lung mucoepidermoid carcinoma cell line was purchased from the Korean Cell Line Bank (Seoul, Korea) and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Welgene, Gyeongsan, Korea) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), penicillin (100 units/mL), and HEPES (25 mM). NCI-H292 cells were cultured overnight in a medium containing 1% FBS and treated with EGF for the indicated time periods. Normal human bronchial epithelial (NHBE) cells (Cambrex Bio Science, Baltimore, MD, USA) were cultured in a bronchial epithelial cell growth medium (LONZA, Walkersville, MD, USA). Differentiated NHBE cells were prepared using an air-liquid interface culture as previously described.27

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Total mRNA was extracted from NCI-H292 and NHBE cells using TRI reagent (Molecular Research Center, Cincinnati, OH, USA). First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 2 μg of total RNA using the LeGene Expression First-Strand cDNA Synthesis System (LeGene Biosciences, San Diego, CA, USA) or amfiRivert cDNA Synthesis Platinum Master Mix (GenDEPOT, Baker, TX, USA) in a 20 μL reaction. Quantitative real-time PCR amplification was performed in a 20 μL reaction with 1 μL of cDNA, 0.5 μL of each primer (0.5 μM), 10 μL of a 2× Real-Time PCR Master Mix, and Low Rox (Including SYBR Green I) (DQ384-40h; BIOFACT, Daejeon, Korea) using the QuantStudio 3 real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR conditions were: 15 minutes at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 20 seconds, 60°C for 40 seconds. The specificity of amplification was confirmed using melting curve analysis. Relative expression was evaluated using the comparative cycle threshold (2−ΔΔCt) method and expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Western blot analysis

The cells were lysed in a RIPA buffer (150 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris-Cl, 0.1% NaN3, 1% Nonidet P-40, 0.25% sodium deoxycholate, 1 mM EDTA, 1 mM Na3VO4, 1 mM NaF, and protease inhibitor mixture). The membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk and probed with antibodies against anti-phospho-ERK1/2 (Cell Signaling Technology, Beverly, MA, USA), phospho-EGF receptor (EGFR) (12A3; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA), anti-ERK1/2 (Cell Signaling Technology), and EGFR (1005; Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Western blot signals were visualized using an ECL detection system (Amersham Pharmacia Biotech, Piscataway, NJ, USA).

Immunocytochemistry (ICC)

ICC was performed as previously described.28 Briefly, NCI-H292 cells in 12-well plates were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized in phosphate-buffered saline containing 1% saponin for 30 minutes, and treated with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide for 30 minutes to block endogenous peroxidase activity. The plates were incubated with diluted normal blocking serum, VECTASTAIN Elite ABC kit Peroxidase Mouse IgG (PK-4002; Vector Laboratories, Newark, CA, USA) for 30 minutes, and then incubated at 4°C with an anti-MUC5AC antibody (Sigma-Aldrich) overnight. After treatment with biotinylated anti-mouse IgG and an ABC complex (Vector Laboratories, Burlingame, CA, USA), the cells were visualized using a DAB substrate kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays

ChIP assays were performed using a ChIP assay kit (17-295; EMD Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Chromatin fragmentation of paraformaldehyde-fixed NCI-H292 cells was performed with the Bioruptor Sonicator (Diagenode, Denville, NJ, USA) for 10–13 cycles of 30 seconds on/30 seconds off at 4°C, yielding DNA fragments of 100–500 bp. The cross-linked protein-DNA complexes were immunoprecipitated with anti-H3K27ac antibody (ab4729; Abcam, Cambridge, United Kingdom) and anti-Sp1 antibody (ab231778; Abcam) at 4°C overnight. Protein A/G agarose beads were added and incubated at 4°C for 1–2 hours, washed, and the crosslinks were reversed. DNA was recovered by phenol/chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation, and it was used as a template for PCR amplification. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 22.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Homogeneity of variances was tested using Levene’s test prior to group comparisons. For comparisons among more than 2 groups, 1-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, followed by Tukey’s or Scheffé’s post-hoc test for equal or unequal group sizes, respectively. For time-dependent experiments involving repeated measurements, repeated measures 1-way ANOVA was used. Comparisons between 2 groups were conducted using Student’s t-test. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Differences with a P value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Results are expressed as the mean ± SEM.

RESULTS

SCFAs inhibit EGF-induced MUC5AC production in airway epithelial cells

Our preliminary study showed that when NCI-H292 cells were stimulated with SCFAs, acetate, propionate, or butyrate, MUC5AC-positive cells were not detected by ICC, which is a sensitive and quantitative tool for measuring MUC5AC expression.29 Hence, we decided to examine the influence of SCFAs on EGF-induced MUC5AC production, as this growth factor is known to be a potent stimulus of MUC5AC production in airway epithelial cells, along with a well-defined signaling pathway leading to transcriptional activation of the MUC5AC gene.7 When NCI-H292 cells were stimulated with EGF (25 ng/mL) in the presence of SCFAs, all 3 SCFAs inhibited MUC5AC protein expression in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1A). Notably, butyrate exhibited the most profound suppression of MUC5AC production, with 90% inhibition at 0.25 mM and nearly complete inhibition at 0.5 mM. Whereas propionate showed moderate inhibition, and acetate displayed weak inhibition of MUC5AC production even at its highest concentration, 2 mM (Fig. 1B). The relative potency of the inhibitory effects of the 3 SCFAs correlated with the levels of MUC5AC mRNA (Fig. 1C). The SCFAs did not exhibit any cytotoxic effects at the tested concentrations (Supplementary Fig. S1). As butyrate is reported to have low micromolar concentrations in the circulation and in most tissues,13 we examined its inhibitory effect at lower concentrations. Remarkably, even at concentrations as low as 1 μM, butyrate significantly inhibited EGF-induced MUC5AC production (Fig. 1D). Intriguingly, butyrate also inhibited MUC5AC production induced by various other stimuli, including LPS, LysoPS, PMA, hydrogen peroxide, and IL-1β (Fig. 1E), which activate distinct and overlapping signaling pathways and their downstream transcription factors for MUC5AC production.627 Furthermore, when NHBE cells prepared in an air-liquid interface culture were exposed to EGF, butyrate robustly inhibited MUC5AC mRNA expression (Fig. 1F). Collectively, these results suggest that SCFA mixtures have the potential to effectively inhibit mucin production. Notably, physiologically relevant concentrations of butyrate may play a significant role in inhibiting MUC5AC production induced by various insults.

Butyrate modulates expression of transcription factors involved in goblet cell differentiation

As MUC5AC expression is the most pronounced phenotype of goblet cell differentiation, we examined the effect of butyrate on the expression of transcription factors that regulate this process. Previous studies demonstrated that SPDEF and FOXA3 are upregulated during goblet cell differentiation,89 which is inhibited by FOXA2.30 Consistent with the findings of these studies, treatment with EGF resulted in a significant increase in SPDEF and FOXA3 transcript levels. However, in the presence of butyrate, SPDEF and FOXA3 mRNA levels were downregulated whereas FOXA2 transcript levels significantly increased (Fig. 2A). Similar patterns of SPDEF and FOXA3 gene expressions were observed in response to EGF and butyrate in NHBE cells, whereas FOXA2 transcript increased moderately (Fig. 2B). This discrepancy could be primarily attributable to different cellular contexts. In conjunction of this observation, FOXA2 was recently reported to have its role for upregulation of MUC5AC in an oncogenic context.31 Accordingly, we examined FOXA2 protein expression in NCI-H292 cells. FOXA2 was barely detectable in unstimulated cells and remained little changed or slightly decreased in response to EGF, while it was significantly elevated in the presence of butyrate (Supplementary Fig. S2). Collectively, these results reveal that the butyrate-mediated inhibition of MUC5AC occurs by counteracting changes in the expression of these transcription factors. Specifically, butyrate helps balance the expression of these factors during goblet cell differentiation, thereby influencing the regulation of MUC5AC in response to diverse triggers of goblet cell differentiation and subordinate MUC5AC production.

Inhibition of EGF-induced MUC5AC production by butyrate is independent of its known GPCRs and the early-stage event of EGFR signaling pathways

Butyrate can act through GPCRs, including GPR41, GPR43, and GPR109A, all of which are coupled to Gi/Go.1920 The inhibitory effect of butyrate on EGF-induced MUC5AC production was not reversed by PTX (Fig. 3A). Furthermore, when NCI-H292 cells were stimulated with agonists of these receptors (CFMB, AR420626, and fumarate–selective agonists for GPR43, GPR41, and GPR109A, respectively), none of them inhibited EGF-induced MUC5AC production, unlike butyrate (Fig. 3B). Next, we examined whether butyrate affected the EGFR signaling pathway. Upon exposure to EGF, both ERK and EGFR were concurrently phosphorylated only for 15–60 minutes, and their phosphorylation was not detected at all afterwards, as previously described.2728 Butyrate did not alter the intensity or kinetics of EGF-induced phosphorylation of ERK and EGFR. Butyrate alone did not activate ERK or EGFR (Fig. 3C). These results suggest that the inhibitory effect of butyrate is independent of the signaling pathways of its GPCR receptors, and illustrate that butyrate is unlikely to interfere with the early-stage events of EGFR signaling pathway which ultimately leads to MUC5AC production, as judged by the lack of effect on EGFR and ERK phosphorylation that occurred in the early phase of EGF post-treatment.

Butyrate suppresses EGF-driven MUC5AC expression at the transcriptional level that predominantly occurs at the late phase

A kinetic experiment showed that exposure to EGF led to a slow increase in accumulated MUC5AC mRNA for up to 8 hours, followed by a sharp increase at 16 hours (Fig. 4A), indicating that active transcription of MUC5AC occurs predominantly in the late phase of EGF signaling. To further investigate the effect of butyrate, butyrate was added simultaneously (at 0 hour) or at different time points (0.5, 4, 8, and 16 hours) after stimulation with EGF, and MUC5AC-positive cells were determined at 24 hours. Butyrate inhibited MUC5AC production, even after its addition 8 hours post EGF treatment. However, its inhibitory effect nearly disappeared when butyrate was added 16 hours after EGF treatment (Fig. 4B). This result suggests that the inhibitory effect of butyrate on the EGF-triggered cellular processes leading to MUC5AC expression was not exerted until at least 8 hours after stimulation with EGF, the time point when MUC5AC transcription was not critically ongoing (Fig. 4A). Conversely, when NCI-H292 cells were stimulated with EGF in the presence of butyrate and the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing EGF only at different time points, the inhibitory effect of butyrate was not observed up to 2 hours but became pronounced at 4 hours and beyond (Fig. 4C). This result indicates that the inhibitory effect of butyrate begins at least 2 hours after EGF treatment. Collectively, it is suggested that the inhibitory effect of butyrate is activated in a delayed fashion, specifically by suppressing the late-phase events of the cellular process responsible for EGF-driven MUC5AC expression. The delayed fashion of the butyrate’s inhibitory effect on EGF-dependent MUC5AC expression suggests that butyrate suppresses MUC5AC expression at the transcriptional level, because the active transcription of MUC5AC is temporarily coincident with the exertion of butyrate’s inhibitory effect. To further explore this notion, we compared the effects of butyrate with those of known HDAC inhibitors. VPA is a selective class I HDAC inhibitor structurally related to butyrate, and TSA inhibits both class I and class II HDACs.32 Similar to butyrate, both VPA and TSA effectively blocked EGF-induced MUC5AC mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 5). Exactly the same as butyrate, VPA downregulated EGF-induced SPDEF and FOXA3 mRNA expression, whereas it upregulated FOXA2 transcript (Fig. 2). These findings suggest that epigenetic regulation plays a role in the repression of MUC5AC.

Butyrate inhibits EGF-induced acetylation of H3K27 in the promoter of MUC5AC gene

We investigated whether the inhibitory effect of butyrate was related to changes in chromatin histone acetylation in MUC5AC promoter region. NCI-H292 cells were treated with medium or EGF in the absence or presence of butyrate for 24 hours, and ChIP assay was performed using anti-H3K27ac. Since H3K27ac marks both the proximal and distal regions of the transcription start site,33 we chose 2 sequences for PCR, representing the proximal and distal regions of MUC5AC promoter (Fig. 6A), along with a 3’-UTR sequence of this gene as a negative control (not indicated in Fig. 6A). Upon exposure to EGF, H3K27 acetylation levels increased in both distal and proximal regions. However, these augmented H3K27 acetylation levels returned to baseline levels in the presence of butyrate or VPA (Fig. 6B). Interestingly, dexamethasone (Dex), a pharmacological agent known to repress MUC5AC at the transcriptional level in airway epithelial cells,27 decreased H3K27 acetylation levels in its promoter regions. In contrast, H3K27 acetylation levels at the 3’ UTR remained unaltered regardless of EGF, butyrate, VPA, and/or Dex. Functional Sp1 binding sites are located in the proximal promoter region,34 as indicated in Fig. 6A. The occupancy of Sp1 in this region also increased in response to EGF and returned to baseline in the presence of butyrate, VPA, or Dex, whereas Sp1 occupancy was not affected in the 3’ UTR of the gene (Fig. 6C). Since the inhibitory effect of butyrate on MUC5AC expression was evident in a delayed manner (Fig. 4B and C), we examined temporal changes in H3K27 acetylation levels. The H3K27 acetylation levels of the 2 promoter regions remained relatively unchanged in response to EGF at 1 hour, moderately increased at 8 hours, and significantly increased at 24 hours. However, upon addition of butyrate, H3K27 acetylation levels decreased moderately at 8 hours and significantly at 24 hours (Fig. 6D). These ChIP results were consistent with those showing that the inhibitory effect of butyrate was exerted during the late phase of the EGF-driven signaling pathway for MUC5AC expression, as shown in Fig. 4B and C. Next, we examined whether the functionally related genes, MUC5AC, SPDEF and FOXA2, epigenetically responded to butyrate. The H3K27 acetylation level significantly increased at the proximal promoter of SPDEF in response to EGF but returned to baseline upon exposure to butyrate, VPA, or Dex (Fig. 6E). In contrast, the effects of these agents on the H3K27 acetylation level of the FOXA2 proximal promoter were exactly opposite to those of SPDEF, suggesting that the H3K27 acetylation levels of the proximal promoters of functionally related genes are coordinately regulated by these HDAC inhibitors and Dex, in conjunction with those of MUC5AC.

DISCUSSION

The emerging gut-lung axis concept describes the impact of the gut microbiota on the course or outcome of chronic airway inflammatory diseases, asthma, and COPD, highlighting the potential application of the gut microbiota and their metabolites in the treatment of lung diseases.35 An extensive array of studies has demonstrated how gut microbe-derived metabolites, especially SCFAs, influence the functions of several immune cell compartments, receptor signaling, epigenetic and non-epigenetic regulation of related genes, and the pathophysiology of airway diseases.36 Nonetheless, there is surprisingly a paucity of knowledge regarding whether and how SCFAs influence the function of airway epithelial cells, which are resident cells that play a crucial role in airway function, homeostasis, and disease. In the present study, we investigated the direct effects of SCFAs on MUC5AC production in airway epithelial cells and its underlying mechanisms. Our results demonstrated that butyrate, a specific SCFA, effectively suppressed MUC5AC production elicited by various insults. Importantly, butyrate exerts this inhibitory effect at concentrations presumed to be physiological in the lungs. Mechanistically, butyrate did not alter the early phase of EGFR signaling, nor was its inhibitory effect dependent on its cognate GPCR receptors. Instead, butyrate deacetylates, in a delayed fashion, the MUC5AC promoter, which is acetylated in response to EGF, and reduces the proximal promoter occupancy of Sp1, a docking platform responsible for the transactivation of MUC5AC by EGF. This suggests that butyrate intrinsically controls mucin production at the chromatin level because it is naturally produced in the body. Given that corticosteroids are nearly the sole medicines used to treat mucus hypersecretion in clinical practice, the ability of butyrate to inhibit MUC5AC production has potential therapeutic implications in obstructive airway diseases.

In our study, we found that butyrate abolishes MUC5AC expression induced by various stimuli used, some of which activate TACE-EGFR-ERK axis predominantly, while some, such as LPS and IL-1β, activate NF-κB signaling pathways for MUC5AC production.2729 This suggests that butyrate inhibits MUC5AC expression regardless of the stimuli and their downstream signaling, indicating a global mechanism. The likely mechanism behind this inhibition is an epigenetic regulation through the antagonism of histone acetylation between EGF and butyrate. The MUC5AC promoter was acetylated in response to EGF, but treatment with butyrate or VPA led to its deacetylation (Fig. 6B). Recent reports have suggested that the EGF-mediated upregulation of target genes is correlated with histone acetylation at their promoters. Stimulation of cultured mammalian cells with EGF results in histone 3 acetylation of CTEN37 and HMGA2 promoters,38 which is accompanied by increased expression of these 2 genes. In both cases, EGF signaling leads to the phosphorylation of p300, a histone acetyltransferase responsible for histone acetylation of the promoters of these 2 genes. Furthermore, genome-wide mapping of histone acetylation revealed that a group of genes upregulated by EGF displayed increased histone 3 acetylation near their transcription start sites in a breast cancer cell line.39 These results suggest that the EGF-mediated upregulation of its target genes correlates with histone acetylation at their promoters. We showed that butyrate induces H3K27 deacetylation of the MUC5AC promoter, which correlates with complete inhibition of expression (Figs. 1 and 6B). This was unexpected, given that butyrate, an HDAC inhibitor, facilitates chromatin accessibility and promotes transcription by increasing global histone acetylation. However, butyrate has been shown to mediate decreased acetylation of selected proximal promoters, which reasonably correlates with decreased expression of associated genes. Butyrate has been shown to downregulate similar numbers of or even more genes than it does to upregulate them.40 Large mapping studies of the epigenome have revealed that butyrate mediates histone deacetylation centered on transcription start sites, leading to transcriptional silencing.4041 Interestingly, Dex, a synthetic glucocorticoid, represses the EGF-induced transcriptional program via glucocorticoid receptor activation.42 Our results showed that Dex interacts antagonistically with EGF in epigenetic regulation, similar to butyrate. This crosstalk between EGF and steroid hormones has been reported, in which Dex represses EGF-induced genes by mediating histone deacetylation of their promoters.39 Thus, both EGF and Dex can reprogram enhancers and regulate gene expression. Although butyrate and Dex appear to have different biological effects owing to their distinct modes of action, they share a remarkable attribute of abrogating MUC5AC with a high magnitude by implementing an epigenetic mechanism. It would be interesting to investigate the details of how butyrate and Dex dampen MUC5AC expression at the epigenetic level.

SPDEF plays a central role in goblet cell differentiation and MUC5AC expression by forming a transcriptional network with FOXA2 and FOXA3. SPDEF inhibits FOXA2 and induces FOXA3.8 Thus, SPDEF and FOXA2 are expressed in a mutually exclusive manner and have opposite roles in MUC5AC expression and goblet cell differentiation.30 FOXA2 directly inhibits MUC5AC promoter activity,43 and SPDEF binds to the upstream enhancer region of MUC5AC, leading to MUC5AC expression.44 However, the opposite role of the 2 transcription factors was compounded by a recent finding that FOXA2 activates a gene regulatory network involving SPDEF and upregulates MUC5AC depending on the oncogenic context.31 In the present study, we found that butyrate deacetylates the SPDEF proximal promoter, which is acetylated by EGF, and the FOXA2 proximal promoter, which tends to be deacetylated by EGF. Treatment with VPA and Dex yielded similar effects (Figs. 2 and 6E). These results suggest that these genes constitute a module that is epigenetically regulated by MUC5AC expression and goblet cell differentiation in response to EGF and butyrate (Fig. 7). The antagonistic interface formed between EGF and butyrate stimulation draws an analogy to a previous study, in which genes upregulated by EGF with increased acetylation were suppressed by Dex entailing deacetylation.39 Therefore, the multitude of previous findings indicating that SPDEF and FOXA2 mutually inhibit each other’s expression are likely to have an epigenetic basis. Reversible and dynamic histone acetylation and deacetylation by EGF and butyrate underlie the regulation of MUC5AC and the relevant genes that transcriptionally regulate MUC5AC. Potential enhancers of MUC5AC that are responsible for epigenetic regulation by butyrate as well as SPDEF, FOXA2, and FOXA3 need to be identified using various genomic technologies, such as ChIP-seq and Histone-ChIP-seq, etc.

Understanding how butyrate drives the deacetylation of MUC5AC and decreases its expression, as observed by its effect on MUC5AC, is a significant challenge. Previous studies have shown that HDAC inhibitors can lead to hyperacetylation of Sp1 itself, causing a loss of its DNA-binding capacity at the promoters of genes associated with cell cycle arrest in cancer cell lines.45 One possible scenario that has been proposed is that acetylation of Sp1 leads to reduced DNA binding, which allows histone acetyltransferase to dissociate from Sp1-regulated promoters. As a result, histone acetylation at promoters decreases and transcriptional elongation is hindered.46 It is highly plausible that butyrate mediates the epigenetic control of MUC5AC in collaboration with histone-modifying enzymes and transcription factors activated by EGF or other stimuli.

SCFAs have been shown to exert beneficial effects on the intestinal epithelial barrier by profoundly modifying mucosal immunity.47 However, the effects of SCFAs on the function and phenotype of the respiratory epithelium have only recently begun to be explored. A study shows that butyrate induces complete recovery of the compromised airway epithelial barrier by restoring transepithelial electrical resistance and reinforcing tight junctions, both in vitro48 and in an experimental model.49 HDAC activity is greater in airway epithelial cells from patients with asthma than in those from healthy subjects and is inversely related to epithelial integrity.49 Our results were consistent with the observation that butyrate ameliorates the pathophysiology of airway inflammatory diseases. Additionally, butyrate significantly inhibited EGF-induced MUC5AC expression at concentrations as low as 1 μM (Fig. 1D). The effects of butyrate on inflammatory functions and the underlying mechanisms have been studied mostly in tissues and cultured cells exposed to millimolar concentrations of butyrate. Butyrate concentrations decrease precipitously as it is metabolized and transported sequentially through the colon, liver, and circulation. The concentration of butyrate is reported to be 1-10 μM in the peripheral blood and most tissues, including the lungs.13 The low micromolar concentration of butyrate in the lungs might help maintain homeostatic MUC5AC production in the pulmonary environment, which is healthy but not necessarily free of stimulants, and dampen its overproduction under inflamed conditions.

In summary, our study demonstrates that the inflammatory mediator (EGF) and SCFA butyrate have opposing effects on MUC5AC production, with EGF inducing it and butyrate repressing it. These effects are achieved through epigenetic regulation of not only the MUC5AC gene but also other genes that form a transcriptional circuit involved in modulating MUC5AC positively and negatively. Juxtaposed alterations in the chromatin architecture of the MUC5AC-SPDEF-FOXA2 axis are required for both the transcriptional activation and silencing of MUC5AC in airway epithelial cells. The potent inhibitory effect of butyrate on MUC5AC production suggests its therapeutic implications in treating type 2-high asthma and COPD, the main pathological features of which are MUC5AC overproduction with no effective treatment. Additionally, MUC5AC overproduction is observed in human lung adenocarcinoma with KRAS-mutation, and the abrogation of Muc5ac expression reduces tumor development in a mouse model,50 awaiting the evaluation of butyrate as a potential therapeutic agent for these airway diseases using appropriate models.

Distinct value-modulated connectomic signatures during monetary incentive anticipation predict severity and craving in internet gaming disorder
Authors: Zifeng Wang, Guangdong Zhou, Yifan Liu, Yuchen Huang, Taoyu Wu, Shiqiu Meng, Chengyao Qian, Yan Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInternet gaming disorder (IGD) involves dysregulated incentive processing, yet precise neural substrates remain inconsistent. While the Monetary Incentive Delay (MID) task assesses reward systems, traditional analyses conflate reward valence (Value) and motivational salience, masking process-specific neural alterations. This study evaluated which neural features—from localized activity to large-scale connectivity—most effectively characterize IGD clinical phenotypes.MethodsWe analyzed fMRI data from 90 regular gamers, including 45 participants in the IGD group and 45 matched recreational game users (RGU), who performed a MID task with five reward/loss levels. Applying the Orthogonal-Decoding (DeCoP) framework, we dissociated anticipation BOLD signals into independent Value and Salience components. We compared localized activation (GLM), multivariate spatial patterns (Searchlight MVPA), and task-modulated functional connectivity (gPPI). Connectome-based Predictive Modeling (CPM) identified neural signatures predicting symptom severity and craving. An exploratory subsample (n = 15; 3 IGD and 12 RGU) completed clinical assessments approximately 12 months later.ResultsLocalized activation and spatial patterns showed no significant group differences across either anticipation or feedback. However, positive Value-modulated connectivity during anticipation significantly predicted IGD symptom severity (66 stable edges, ρ = .317, p = .002) and craving (37 stable edges, ρ = .348, p < .001). The intersection of these positive connectome models revealed a shared Value network (16 stable edges) anchored in a medial frontal–cerebellar circuit. Exploratory follow-up analysis showed that baseline shared-network strength was associated with 12-month craving (ρ = .724, p = .002).ConclusionsOur findings suggest the neurobiological core of IGD is linked to aberrant network-level coordination during reward anticipation, rather than regional hyper-reactivity or feedback processing deficits. We identify anticipatory valuation dysregulation as a key feature of IGD, offering a process-specific connectomic signature of IGD symptomatology.

Introduction

With the widespread popularity of internet gaming, Internet Gaming Disorder (IGD) has emerged as a major global public health concern (World Health Organization [WHO], 2019). Its core clinical features include impaired control over gaming behaviors, persistent gaming craving, and compulsive participation regardless of negative consequences (American Psychiatric Association [APA], 2013). Prior work has extensively examined neural responses to gaming cues, highlighting hyper-reactivity in the brain’s reward circuitry during gaming (Antons et al., 2020; Ko et al., 2009; Starcke et al., 2018). However, a growing body of evidence suggests that the neurobiological underpinnings of IGD extend beyond cue-reactivity to encompass broader dysfunctions in the general motivational processing system, particularly regarding how non-gaming-related incentives are evaluated and transformed into action (Hong et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2020).

The Monetary Incentive Delay (MID) task serves as a classic paradigm for evaluating this general incentive process, allowing for the examination of neural responses to general reward acquisition and loss avoidance (Chen et al., 2022; Knutson et al., 2000; Oldham et al., 2018). Although the MID task has been applied to IGD, fMRI findings remain remarkably inconsistent, with studies reporting both blunted and heightened striatal reactivity (Hahn et al., 2014; Kühn et al., 2011; Niu et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2020). A critical but often overlooked reason for these discrepancies is that incentive processing reflects a mixture of valence evaluation (Value), motivational salience/arousal (Bartra et al., 2013; Guitart-Masip et al., 2014; Levy & Glimcher, 2012; Oldham et al., 2018). Standard analytical approaches often fail to disentangle these overlapping signals, potentially masking process-specific abnormalities unique to IGD. The recently proposed orthogonal-Decoding multi-Cognitive Processes (DeCoP) framework provides a mathematically rigorous way to decouple these mixed signals into relatively independent components using psychologically defined orthogonal contrasts (Xiang et al., 2023). By isolating "Value" (the linear gradient from loss to reward) from "Salience" (magnitude-related arousal), we can more precisely pinpoint which aspect of incentive processing is disrupted in IGD.

Furthermore, IGD-related abnormalities may not be confined to discrete brain regions. Recent neurobiological models, such as the I-PACE framework, emphasize that addictive behaviors stem from altered coordination across large-scale neural networks (Brand et al., 2019; Bressler & Menon, 2010; Zilverstand et al., 2018). While regional activation studies provide localized information, they often overlook the complex, distributed interactions between reward, control, and salience systems. Recent CPM studies have shown that distributed functional connectivity can predict gaming craving in IGD and broader internet addiction symptomatology (Feng et al., 2024; Garrison et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2022). Therefore, it is a clear need for a systematic evaluation across multiple levels of neural representation—from discrete regions to multivariate patterns and large-scale connectomes.

In the present study, we adopted a comprehensive multi-level analytic framework to identify the neural signatures that most effectively characterize IGD. Using fMRI data from a well-matched sample of IGD and regular gamers of Honor of Kings, a popular Multiplayer Online Battle Arena (MOBA) game, we first applied the DeCoP framework to decouple Value and Salience signals during both anticipation and feedback phases. We then systematically compared the sensitivity and predictive power of three distinct neural measures: (1) localized activation (univariate GLM), (2) multivariate spatial patterns (searchlight MVPA), and (3) task-modulated functional connectivity (gPPI). By incorporating Connectome-based Predictive Modeling (CPM), we sought to determine which of these process-specific signatures could reliably predict IGD symptom severity and craving. In a 12-month follow-up subsample, we further explored whether baseline shared-network strength was associated with subsequent clinical measures.

Methods

Participants

The present study recruited 90 right-handed college students whose primary online game was Honor of Kings. Potential participants first underwent preliminary screening by self-reported DSM-5 IGD criteria≥ 5 and an adjunctive screening Young’s Internet Addiction Test (IAT) ≥ 50 (Young, 1998) which was applied to confirm clinical symptom severity, consistent with established IGD neuroimaging protocols (Yao et al., 2020). To ensure diagnostic validity, the DSM-5 IGD criteria were evaluated through structured clinical interviews/assessments administered by experienced research personnel who received formal inter-rater consistency training led by hospital psychiatrists. Participants in the IGD group (n = 45) were required to endorse five or more DSM-5 IGD criteria (American Psychiatric Association (APA), 2013; Petry et al., 2014). The RGU group (n = 45) consisted of regular gamers who reported playing for >7 h per week during the past year and did not meet the criteria for the IGD group, to control for gaming experience, carefully matched for age, gender, and education level. Exclusion criteria included severe psychiatric or neurological disorders, substance abuse or dependence, and contraindications for fMRI. Experimental task (MID task)

A modified version of the MID task was employed (Knutson et al., 2000). The task paradigm consisted of three phases: cue, anticipation, and feedback. During the cue phase (2000 ms), monetary cues were presented under five conditions: large reward (+¥5), small reward (+¥0.2), large loss (−¥5), small loss (−¥0.2), and neutral (¥0). These conditions were presented in a randomized order for a total of 100 trials (20 trials per condition). The anticipation phase featured a blank screen with a jittered duration (1500–4000 ms), during which participants prepare for a motor response. In the feedback phase (1500 ms), the task outcome was displayed, indicating the final monetary amount gained, lost, or unchanged. Participants were instructed to press a button as quickly as possible upon the appearance of the target stimulus following the anticipation phase. A successful response allowed participants to obtain the reward or avoid punishment, whereas a failure resulted in omission of reward or incurrence of punishment. To isolate anticipation-related signals from outcome feedback, an adaptive staircase algorithm was implemented, anchoring each participant's success rate at approximately 66% (Knutson et al., 2001). This procedure balances task difficulty across individuals and groups, minimizing confounding effects of performance accuracy on BOLD responses. Clinical and psychological assessments

IGD symptom severity was indexed by the number of endorsed DSM-5 IGD criteria over the past 12 months. Young's IAT was used to assess general internet addiction tendency (Young, 1998). The IAT includes 20 items on a 5-point Likert scale with excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's α = .95). Subjective gaming craving was assessed using a 10-item self-report questionnaire adapted from the widely validated Brief Questionnaire of Smoking Urges (Cox et al., 2001). The wording was modified to reflect the urge to engage in internet gaming rather than substance use (e.g., "Nothing would be better than playing online games right now"). Participants rated each item on a 7-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (completely disagree) to 7 (completely agree). The adapted scale demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's α = .96). Total scores were calculated by summing all 10 items, with higher scores indicating a stronger acute motivational drive to engage in gaming.

Image acquisition and preprocessing

Brain images were acquired using a 3.0-T Siemens Prisma scanner. Functional images were obtained with an echo-planar imaging sequence: TR = 2000 ms, TE = 30 ms, flip angle = 90°, matrix = 64 × 64, FOV = 224 × 224 mm², slice thickness = 3.5 mm, 33 slices, voxel size = 3.5 × 3.5 × 3.5 mm³. High-resolution T1-weighted images were acquired using an MPRAGE sequence: TR/TI/TE = 2530/1100/2.98 ms, 192 slices, matrix = 256 × 256, flip angle = 7°, and voxel size = 1 × 1 × 1 mm³.

Neuroimaging data were preprocessed using the Data Processing & Analysis for Brain Imaging (DPABI_V6.0_210501) toolbox (Yan et al., 2016), based on Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM12). Slice-timing correction was performed to eliminate differences in acquisition time between slices; head motion correction (realignment) was conducted, and data from participants with head motion exceeding 2 mm in translation or 2° in rotation were excluded; functional images were spatially normalized to the standard Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space (Evans et al., 1993) and resampled to a voxel resolution of 3 × 3 × 3 mm³; spatial smoothing was applied using a Gaussian kernel with a 6-mm full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) to improve the signal-to-noise ratio.

First-level GLM and orthogonal decomposition (DeCoP)

First-level task models were specified in SPM12. Subject-specific onset files were used to model the five MID anticipation conditions: large reward, small reward, neutral, small loss, and large loss. Regressors were convolved with the canonical hemodynamic response function. Six head-motion parameters were included as nuisance regressors, and a 128-s high-pass filter and AR(1) autocorrelation model were applied. Condition-specific contrast images were generated for the five anticipation conditions.

Following the DeCoP framework (Xiang et al., 2023), five incentive conditions were modeled as separate regressors. The individual Value and Salience components were then derived using a psychologically-grounded orthogonalization principle: 1) Value component: Estimated using a linear contrast vector [−2, −1, 0, 1, 2], representing the monotonic increase in reward valence. 2) Salience component: Estimated using a U-shaped contrast vector [2, 1, 0, 1, 2], representing motivational intensity regardless of valence. This decoupling approach effectively mitigates the collinearity issues inherent in traditional MID task analyses.

Univariate activation and searchlight MVPA

To validate the efficacy of the experimental paradigm and the DeCoP orthogonal decomposition approach, whole-brain one-sample t-tests were first conducted across all participants (n = 90) for the extracted Value and Salience components. Subsequently, independent two-sample t-tests were performed to examine localized group differences between IGD and RGU participants. All statistical maps were corrected for multiple comparisons using Gaussian Random Field (GRF) correction (voxel-level p < .001, cluster-level p < .05, two-tailed).

Searchlight MVPA was performed to examine whether local multivariate spatial patterns could distinguish the two groups (Kriegeskorte et al., 2006). a linear SVM classifier with 10-fold cross-validation was applied within a 3-voxel-radius spherical searchlight across the brain. Statistical significance was assessed using 1000 label-permutation tests with maximum-statistic family-wise error correction across voxels (p < .05).

Task-modulated gPPI and connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM)

To estimate process-specific connectivity, we employed generalized psychophysiological interaction (gPPI) analysis (McLaren et al., 2012). Compared to standard PPI, gPPI is more robust and suitable for paradigms with multiple task conditions. Time series were extracted from the Shen 268-node atlas, which provides a fine-grained functional parcellation optimized for connectome-based modeling (Finn et al., 2015; Shen et al., 2013).

For CPM analysis (Shen et al., 2017), 10-fold cross-validation (repeated 100 times) was implemented to ensure stability and generalizability of the predictive models. Within each training set, edges correlated with clinical measures (p < .001) were selected as features. Positive and negative predictive networks were modeled separately according to the direction of the edge–behavior association. Sex and mean framewise displacement (FD) were included as covariates in models. To avoid circularity, feature selection and model building were strictly confined to training folds (Kriegeskorte et al., 2009). Predictive performance was evaluated using Spearman's ρ between predicted and observed scores, with significance determined by 1000-iteration permutation tests. Edges selected in >90% of repeated cross-validation folds were defined as stable predictive edges.

To identify a common connectivity signature underlying IGD symptom severity and gaming craving, we further examined the intersection of stable predictive edges from the Value-modulated CPM models for IGD symptom severity and gaming craving (Rosenberg et al., 2016). This overlapping edge set was defined as the shared Value network. For each participant, a shared-network score was calculated by summing the connectivity strengths of the overlapping edges. To examine potential sex differences in the shared Value network, a post-hoc two-way ANOVA was conducted on the shared-network score, with sex and group as between-subject factors.

Exploratory longitudinal analysis

A subsample of 15 participants (3 IGD and 12 RGU) completed the clinical assessments approximately 12 months after baseline. Spearman rank correlations were used to examine the associations between the baseline shared Value-network score and 12-month gaming craving and IGD symptom severity. Given the small longitudinal sample, these analyses were considered exploratory.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

Descriptive statistics for both groups are summarized in Table 1. The IGD and RGU groups were well-matched in terms of age, gender ratio, and years of education. As expected, the IGD group exhibited significantly higher scores in IGD symptom severity, IAT, and subjective gaming craving compared to the RGU group (all p < .001), supporting the validity of the group distinction.

Behavioral results

The two-way mixed ANOVA on reaction times (RTs) revealed a significant main effect of Condition (F(4, 352) = 10.58, p < .001, ηp² = .107, Greenhouse–Geisser corrected), indicating that participants’ motor responses were effectively modulated by incentive magnitude and valence. Crucially, neither the main effect of Group (F(1, 88) = 2.85, p = .095) nor the Group × Condition interaction (F(4, 352) = 0.65, p = .625) reached statistical significance. These results suggest that IGD and RGU individuals showed comparable behavioral performance and incentive-related modulation, ensuring that subsequent neural differences were not confounded by overt behavioral variance. Validation of DeCoP components

Whole-brain one-sample t-tests (n = 90) confirmed the efficacy of the DeCoP framework in dissociating incentive signals. The Value component was associated with robust activation in classical reward-processing regions, including the bilateral striatum, anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), and supplementary motor area (SMA). In contrast, the Salience component engaged a widespread arousal and motor-preparation network, involving the cerebellum, insula, thalamus, and motor-sensory cortices (Figure 2B; Table S1). These dissociable activation patterns validate the successful decoupling of valence- and magnitude-related signals during anticipation.

No group differences in localized activation and searchlight MVPA

Despite the robust activation at the whole-sample level, independent two-sample t-tests revealed no significant localized differences between the IGD and RGU groups for either Value or Salience components across both anticipation and feedback phases (GRF corrected, voxel-level p < .001, cluster-level p < .05). Similarly, searchlight MVPA failed to identify any local multivariate spatial patterns that could reliably distinguish IGD from RGU individuals (permutation-based p > .05). These negative findings suggest that IGD-related abnormalities may be more evident in distributed functional interactions rather than localized signal amplitudes.

Predictive power of anticipatory value network

Specifically, during the anticipation phase, the positive Value-modulated connectome significantly predicted IGD symptom severity (ρ = .317, p = .002) and gaming craving (ρ = .348, p < .001, Fig. 3C). Negative predictive networks yielded sparse and non-significant results and were therefore not further characterized. CPM identified 66 stable edges for predicting IGD severity (primarily linking the medial frontal network (MFN) and subcortical/cerebellar network (SubC/Cereb), alongside connections among the motor (MOT), visual (VIS-I), and frontoparietal (FPN) networks, Fig. 3A) and 37 for craving (predominantly connecting SubC/Cereb and MFN, Fig. 3A). IGD symptom severity and gaming craving were strongly correlated (r = .765, p < .001), suggesting that these two clinical measures captured closely related aspects of IGD symptomatology. We therefore examined whether the CPM models for symptom severity and craving shared common predictive edges. By calculating the intersection of stable positive edges from both models, we identified a shared Value network of 16 stable positive edges. Network-pair analysis highlighted that this shared architecture was dominated by cross-network couplings between the medial frontal network (MFN) and the subcortical/cerebellar network (SubC/Cereb) (Fig. 3B). Specific cerebellar anchors within this shared network included lobules IV–V, Crus I, and the vermis. This core MFN–cerebellar circuit, identified through the convergence of both clinical models, was subsequently used for exploratory longitudinal analyses. A post-hoc two-way ANOVA revealed no significant main effect of sex on the shared Value-network score, F(1,86) = .272, p = .604, ηp² = .003, and no significant Sex × Group interaction, F(1,86) = 2.181, p = .143, ηp² = .025. A significant main effect of group was observed, F(1,86) = 40.365, p < .001, ηp² = .319.

Exploratory longitudinal analyses

In the longitudinal subsample (n = 15), the baseline shared Value-network score was positively associated with gaming craving at the 12-month follow-up (Spearman’s ρ = .724, p = .002). In contrast, no significant association was observed between the baseline shared Value-network score and follow-up IGD symptom severity (ρ = −.037, p = .897). Given the small follow-up sample, these associations should be considered exploratory (Fig. 3D).

Discussion

The present study employed a multi-level analytic framework to identify neural signatures of IGD during monetary incentive processing. By decoupling incentive signals using the DeCoP framework, we found that individual differences in IGD are more sensitively captured by Value-modulated connectomes during the anticipation phase than by localized activation or multivariate spatial patterns. Notably, we identified a "shared Value network"—primarily anchored in medial frontal–cerebellar circuits—that effectively characterizes both overall symptom burden and the proximal motivational drive (craving).

A significant observation is the superior sensitivity of task-modulated connectivity over localized BOLD amplitude in capturing IGD-related phenotypes. The absence of group differences in univariate activation and searchlight MVPA does not indicate absent neural alteration, but suggests that IGD neuropathology is inherently distributed rather than localized. This aligns with the I-PACE model (Brand et al., 2019) and recent shifts in addiction neuroimaging toward network-based perspectives (Zilverstand et al., 2018). Our results imply that core IGD dysfunction may lie in altered coordination across reward-evaluation and cognitive-control systems, captured only through distributed interactions within the functional connectome.

A key contribution of this work is the dissociation of incentive processing into independent Value and Salience components. The finding that only Value-modulated connectivity, specifically during the anticipation phase, predicted severity and craving of IGD is highly informative. While Salience represents general motivational drive (arousal), Value reflects the internal representation of subjective utility. This divergence suggests that the clinical burden of IGD is specifically linked to an altered valuation system—the capacity to fine-tune neural responses to the gradient of reward and punishment (Groman et al., 2022; Robinson & Berridge, 1993, 2025). It is worth noting that while positive predictive networks reliably modeled both IGD symptom severity and craving, negative predictive networks (comprising functional edges whose strength negatively correlated with clinical traits) yielded non-significant predictive performance and were consequently excluded. This asymmetric predictive capability aligns with previous task-based CPM research in IGD (e.g., Zhou et al., 2022), which similarly observed significant behavioral prediction exclusively in positive connectivity networks. From a neurobiological perspective, this suggests that value-modulated neural dysregulation in IGD is primarily expressed through active functional hyper-synchronization within reward and salience circuits rather than systematic anti-correlated suppression. Methodologically, negative edge feature selection in task fMRI paradigms often exhibits greater inter-individual variability and edge sparsity, diminishing cross-validation stability. While our findings highlight positive value-modulated networks as the predominant neural signature of clinical features in this task context, future studies with larger cohorts or resting-state paradigms are warranted to determine whether subtle negative network contributions can be detected under different cognitive states. Besides, IGD individuals may suffer from "anticipatory valuation dysregulation", where the neural connectome fails to appropriately integrate the magnitude and valence of general monetary incentives, which in turn sustains compulsive gaming behaviors.

Aligned with the I-PACE model (Brand et al., 2019), the collinearity between DSM-5 severity and craving reflects their reciprocal causality during addiction escalation. Yet, they capture complementary clinical dimensions: evaluator-administered DSM-5 criteria index objective, macro-level functional impairment, whereas self-reported craving reflects proximal, subjective motivational drive. Methodologically, extracting the 16 shared edges isolates a process-specific connectomic signature of aberrant value modulation (striatal-limbic-frontoparietal) rather than a general diagnostic biomarker. Dysregulated anticipatory value encoding serves as a core neurocomputational engine that fuels state-level craving and, when sustained, drives chronic loss of control and functional impairment.

The most striking anatomical finding is the prominent role of the medial frontal–cerebellar circuit within the identified shared network. Traditionally viewed as a motor-coordination center, the cerebellum is increasingly recognized for its involvement in adaptive prediction and cognitive-affective processing (Miquel et al., 2016; Rudolph et al., 2023; Schmahmann et al., 2019; Sokolov et al., 2017). Crucially, the stable predictive edges linked the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC)—a key node for value integration—with cerebellar lobules (Crus I, IV-V) and the vermis. Recent evidence from optogenetic studies (Carta et al., 2019) has demonstrated that cerebellar outputs can directly modulate the reward circuitry via the ventral tegmental area (VTA). Our results extend this to IGD, suggesting that the medial frontal–cerebellar circuit may underpin a "predictive coding" mechanism for reward, where cerebellar signals inform the frontal cortex about incentive expectations. A breakdown in this cortico-cerebellar communication may lead to the biased valuation that sustains compulsive gaming behaviors.

Crucially, baseline strength of this shared network prospectively predicted 12-month craving, but not prospective DSM-5 severity. This dissociation indicates that task-evoked value connectivity specifically tracks enduring neurobiological vulnerability to incentive salience (craving), whereas macro-level clinical symptom progression is inherently multifactorial, co-determined by dynamic psychosocial and environmental factors over time (Garrison et al., 2023).

Several limitations of the present study warrant consideration. First, while CPM results were rigorously supported by internal cross-validation, the primary analysis was cross-sectional. Future research should prioritize external validation in independent cohorts to confirm the generalizability of these anticipatory connectomic signatures. Second, the longitudinal analysis was based on a small and imbalanced follow-up sample (n = 15), which limits statistical power and the stability of the observed associations. Accordingly, only simple exploratory correlations were conducted, and these findings should be interpreted cautiously and replicated in larger, more balanced longitudinal samples. Third, all participants in the current study reported Honor of Kings—a prominent MOBA game—as their primary online game. Although focusing on a single game helped minimize confounding variance associated with disparate game structural characteristics, reward schedules, and motivational drivers, it inevitably constrains the generalizability of our findings across the broader IGD population. Future comparative neuroimaging studies encompassing diverse game genres are required to disentangle universal IGD neural markers from genre-specific neural representations. Besides, our sample exhibited a female-predominant demographic distribution (67% female), which diverges from typical epidemiological trends showing higher IGD prevalence in males. This composition reflects the demographic profile of our primary recruitment environment (a normal/educational university with a female-majority student body). Although sex was controlled as a covariate in all predictive modeling analyses, caution is warranted when generalizing these neural findings to male-dominated IGD populations. Future research should evaluate whether the value-modulated connectivity patterns identified here replicate in epidemiologically representative or male-majority cohorts.

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that individual differences in IGD are more sensitively captured by large-scale, Value-modulated connectomes than by localized brain activation or multivariate spatial patterns. By systematically decoupling valuation from motivational salience, we identified a robust predictive network—specifically during the reward anticipation phase—that effectively characterizes both the overall severity and craving in IGD. The identification of the medial frontal–cerebellar circuit as a shared predictive anchor extends the neurobiological understanding of IGD. Our findings highlight network-level valuation dysregulation during anticipation as a core feature of IGD, providing a promising connectomic signature for characterizing symptom heterogeneity. These process-specific neural signatures may eventually inform the development of personalized interventions and targeted neuromodulation for IGD.

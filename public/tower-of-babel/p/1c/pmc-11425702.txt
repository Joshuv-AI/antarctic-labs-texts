Characterization of flare-ups and impact of garetosmab in adults with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva: a post hoc analysis of the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled LUMINA-1 trial
Authors: Richard Keen, Kathryn M Dahir, Jennifer McGinniss, Robert J Sanchez, Scott Mellis, Aris N Economides, Maja Di Rocco, Philippe Orcel, Christian Roux, Jacek Tabarkiewicz, Javier Bachiller-Corral, Angela M Cheung, Mona Al Mukaddam, Kusha Mohammadi, Jing Gu, Dushyanth Srinivasan, Dinko Gonzalez Trotter, E Marelise W Eekhoff, Frederick S Kaplan, Robert J Pignolo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) is an ultra-rare disorder, characterized by progressive heterotopic ossification (HO) and painful soft-tissue inflammatory flare-ups. This was a post hoc analysis from a phase 2 (NCT03188666) trial in which adults with FOP received intravenous anti-activin A antibody garetosmab 10 mg/kg or placebo every 4 wk over 28 wk (Period 1), followed by a 28-wk open-label treatment and extension (Periods 2 and 3). Here we describe flare-ups, their relationship to new HO lesions, and the impact of garetosmab on flare-ups. Volume of new HO lesions was measured by CT. Patient-reported flare-ups were defined by any 2 of the following: new onset of pain, swelling, joint stiffness, decrease in movement, or perceived presence of HO. Flare-ups were experienced by 71% (17/24) of placebo-treated patients, 59% (10/17) of whom developed a new HO lesion irrespective of flare-up location; 24% of flare-ups location-matched new HO lesions. Twenty-nine new HO lesions occurred in the placebo cohort by week 28, of which 12 (41%) occurred in the same location as new or ongoing flare-ups. A higher volume of newly formed heterotopic bone (week 28) occurred in placebo-treated patients who had experienced a prior flare-up vs those without (median [Q1:Q3] of 16.6 [12.0:31.1] vs 3.2 cm3). Garetosmab was previously shown to decrease patient-reported flare-up frequency in Period 1; here, garetosmab reduced the median (Q1:Q3) duration of patient-reported flares (15.0 [6.0:82.0] vs 48.0 [15.0:1.00] d) and the severity of flare-ups vs placebo. Frequency of corticosteroid use was numerically reduced in those treated with garetosmab (40.0%) vs placebo (58.3%). In this analysis, 71% of placebo-treated adults with FOP experienced flare-ups over 28 wk, which were associated with an increased volume of newly formed heterotopic bone. Garetosmab reduced the severity and duration of flare-ups, with effects sustained during the entire trial.Keywords: activin A, fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, flare-ups, heterotopic ossification, natural history

Introduction

Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant disorder, with an estimated global prevalence of 0.36–1.36/million.1–4 FOP is characterized by progressive heterotopic ossification (HO) of connective tissues such as ligaments, tendons, fascia, aponeuroses, and certain skeletal muscles.5,6 HO typically begins in the first decade of life and accumulation over time leads to joint immobility, skeletal deformity, and progressive disease burden.5,7,8

The onset of new lesion formation that leads to HO in FOP is often accompanied by painful soft-tissue inflammatory events known as flare-ups. Flare-ups are a frequent occurrence and typically present within the first decade of life. Symptoms may include pain, swelling, decreased mobility, and stiffness with varying degrees of severity. Some flare-ups may even be life-threatening depending on the location of the event. Current management is primarily limited to symptomatic control with non-steroidal anti-inflammatories or a short course of corticosteroids; however, efficacy evidence is lacking.6 Hence, there remains a significant need to better understand flare-ups and their long-term implications, while also working toward the development of therapeutics that could reduce the severity and even prevent the occurrence of flare-ups.

Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva is caused by heterozygous missense mutations in the gene encoding activin A receptor type 1 (ACVR1), a bone morphogenetic protein type I receptor.9,10 A significant advance was made in our understanding of FOP when it was determined that FOP-variant ACVR1 drives HO in response to activation by activin A.11,12 Consequently, inhibition of activin A was explored as a potential therapeutic strategy for FOP. Garetosmab is an investigational, fully human, anti-activin A monoclonal antibody that has been shown to inhibit the formation of heterotopic bone lesions, both in mouse models of FOP and a phase II clinical trial (LUMINA-1).11,13,14 Here, we present post hoc analyses of the LUMINA-1 study where we sought to better understand flare-up events and their relationship to new HO lesions that occurred in the placebo-only period, and the impact of garetosmab on flare-ups in adults with FOP.

Methods

Study design

LUMINA-1 (NCT03188666) was a phase 2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study that evaluated the safety and efficacy of intravenous garetosmab 10 mg/kg every 4 wk vs placebo over 28 wk in adults with FOP (Period 1).14 This was followed by a 28-wk open-label treatment period (Period 2) where patients on placebo crossed over to garetosmab, with a subsequent open-label extension (Period 3; Supplementary Figure S1). Primary results from this study have been published.14 The study was conducted at 11 sites in 8 countries across North America and Europe. Patients were enrolled from February 26, 2018 to February 25, 2019; the last patient completed the study on September 16, 2021.

Study population

Complete details of the study population have been published.14 In brief, the eligible study population included female and male patients, aged 18–60 yr, with a clinical diagnosis of FOP, a history of disease activity within 1 yr of screening, and who had documentation of a germline, activating ACVR1 pathogenic variant. FOP disease activity was defined as pain, swelling, stiffness, and other signs and symptoms associated with FOP flare-ups, worsening joint mobility, or radiographic progression of HO (increase in the size or number of HO lesions) with or without associated flare-up episodes.

Assessments and endpoints

The objectives of this analysis were to (1) prospectively describe flare-ups in untreated patients (placebo arm), (2) describe the relationship of flare-ups with new HO over 28 wk (placebo arm), and (3) describe the long-term therapeutic effects of garetosmab on flare-ups.

New and existing patient-reported flares-ups were collected daily via an electronic diary and were defined by any 2 of the following: new onset of pain, swelling, joint stiffness, decrease in movement, or perceived presence of HO. For all flare-ups, patients reported the flare-up location as well as symptoms and severity ratings of pain, swelling, redness, warmth, stiffness, and/or decreased movement, using a 4-point scale (0: no symptom; 1: mild; 2: moderate; 3: severe). Patients reported flare-up location from 35 individual body locations (eg, wrist, elbow, lower arm, upper arm, and shoulder), with the ability to specify joint vs soft tissue, right vs left, and front vs back. The flare-up diary consisted of 3 sections: historical and pre-existing flare-ups (collected at baseline only); new flare-ups; and ongoing flare-ups. Patients were requested to report symptoms from the onset to the end of the flare-up during the study period. The volume of new HO lesions at weeks 8, 28, 56, and 76 was determined by low-dose CT.14 The co-location of flare-ups and new HO lesion(s) was determined by the medical director. For example, a patient-reported flare-up occurring in “upper right leg front” and a new lesion in “femur right” is an example of concordance, whereas a patient-reported flare-up in “chest front” and a new lesion in “femur right” would be discordance. The baseline cumulative analogue joint involvement scale (CAJIS) score, used to assess the degree of immobility, was determined by the investigator. The assessment was performed across 15 anatomic locations with scores ranging from 0 to 30. Higher scores reflect greater immobility and disease severity.15

Statistical analysis

Patient-reported flare-ups were an exploratory efficacy endpoint and were prospectively summarized descriptively. Subsequent post hoc analyses of these data utilized Fisher’s exact test for comparison between cohorts, and all p-values presented were nominal. The analysis set used for Period 1 included all randomized patients who had ≥1 active HO lesion at baseline. The analysis set used for Periods 2 and 3 was the COVID-19 modified intent-to-treat set (mITT),14 which included all patients with active HO at baseline who received treatment in Period 2, had ≥1 post-week 28 scan collected, and whose period between any consecutive doses was <9 wk before the first post-week 28 scan (Supplementary Table S1). This ensured that the primary analyses for all imaging endpoints included only evaluable patients whose treatment was not greatly impacted due to the pandemic. Location-matching between imaging data and patient-reported flare-up data was reconciled by the medical director.

Ethical statement

Patient safety and welfare were monitored by an Independent Data Monitoring Committee. This study was conducted in accordance with the 2013 Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonization guidelines for Good Clinical Practice, and Sex and Gender Equity in Research guidelines. All patients provided written, informed consent.

Results

Baseline and disease characteristics

In total, 44 patients were randomized to receive garetosmab (n = 20) or placebo (n = 24) (Supplementary Figure S2). Baseline characteristics have been reported previously.14 Overall, patients were balanced across treatment cohorts (Supplementary Table S2); the mean (SD) age was 27.6 (8.5) yr, 88.6% were White, and 56.8% were female. Of 44 patients, 42 had the classic ACVR1 R206H mutation and 2 had non-classic FOP-causing variants of ACVR1, specifically ACVR1 Q207E and ACVR1 G328R. The mean (SD) duration of FOP was 19.2 (10.4) yr, and the mean (SD) CAJIS score was 15.7 (6.6), indicating moderate limitations in mobility.15

Flare-up observations in the placebo cohort over 28 wk

In Period 1, 17 of 24 (71%) placebo-treated patients reported ≥1 new flare-up. In total 34 flare-ups were reported, equating to nearly 1.4 flare-up events per patient over the 28-wk interval across all placebo-treated patients (n = 24). The most common flare-up symptoms reported were pain (15/17, 88.2%) and joint stiffness (15/17, 88.2%; Table 1). Most flare-ups in the placebo cohort occurred in the soft tissue of the leg (52.9%), back (29.4%), and arm (26.5%; Supplementary Figure S3).

Flare-ups and new HO lesions in the placebo cohort over 28 wk

Of the 17 placebo-treated patients with new flare-ups, 10 (59%) developed new HO lesions regardless of the flare-up location. Placebo-treated patients with new flare-ups (n = 17) had a higher volume of new heterotopic bone at week 28 compared with those without new flare-ups. The median (Q1:Q3) total volume of new HO lesions was 3.8 (0:18.6) cm3 in patients with new reported flare-ups vs 0 (0:0) cm3 in those without (Figure 1). Median (Q1:Q3) total volume change from baseline in HO lesions (new plus existing growth) was 9.2 (0.3:28.7) cm3 vs 2 (1.4:11.5) cm3 in patients with and without new reported flare-ups, respectively.

Overall, 11 (46%) placebo-treated patients developed ≥1 new HO lesion, of whom 10 (91%) also reported new flare-ups (Table 2). These patients had a higher median (Q1:Q3) volume of lesions compared with the 1 patient with new HO lesions but without a reported flare-up (16.65 [12.0:31.1] cm3 vs 3.2 cm3, respectively; Figure 2).

Co-location of flare-ups and new HO lesions in the placebo cohort over 28 wk

Over 28 wk, 29 new HO lesions were identified in 11 of the 24 placebo-treated patients. Of the 34 patient-reported flare-ups in this cohort, 8 (23.5%) resulted in new HO lesions at the reported site (Figure 3A). When assessing patients with at least 6 and 12 wk of follow-up from the onset of flare-up, 4 (12.1%) and 5 (18.5%) flare-ups corresponded with new lesions at the same site, respectively. Of the 29 new HO lesions, 12 (41.4%) occurred in the same location as a flare-up that was new or ongoing at the time of enrolment into the study vs 17 (58.6%) that did not have a preceding location-matched flare-up (Figure 3B). When assessing flare-ups at 6 and 12 wk prior to new HO identification, 8 (27.6%) lesions were preceded by a location-matched flare-up.

Effect of garetosmab on flare-up symptoms and the long-term impact of flare-ups over Periods 1 and 2

Primary flare-up results from the 28-wk double-blind treatment period (Period 1) have previously been reported.14 Briefly, there was a significant reduction in the proportion of patients reporting flare-ups in the garetosmab vs the placebo cohort (35% vs 71%, respectively; p=.032). The number of patient-reported flare-ups was also reduced in the garetosmab cohort vs the placebo cohort (13 vs 34).14 Additionally, the median (Q1:Q3) duration of patient-reported flare-ups was 15.0 (6.0:82.0) d in those treated with garetosmab vs 48.0 (15.0:100.0) d in those treated with placebo during Period 1. Furthermore, 14 of 24 (58.3%) patients used glucocorticoids in the placebo cohort vs 8 of 20 (40.0%) patients in the garetosmab cohort (Table 1).

Among patients experiencing flare-ups in Period 1, most reported multiple signs and symptoms known to be associated with flare-ups. This included pain, swelling, joint stiffness, decreased movement, redness, and/or warmth. Pain was the most frequently reported flare-up symptom among both cohorts. The proportion of patients reporting severe symptoms (on a 4-point scale) was reduced in those treated with garetosmab vs placebo, with no patients reporting severe swelling or severe decrease in movement in the garetosmab cohort (Table 1). Pain from flare-ups was more frequently reported as severe in those treated with placebo (23.5%) than with garetosmab (7.7%).

The occurrence of flare-ups at a specific anatomical location was generally similar or reduced in the garetosmab cohort compared with the placebo cohort in Period 1. Most flare-ups were located in the soft tissue of the leg (52.9% in placebo, 30.8% in garetosmab), back (29.4% vs 46.2%, respectively), and arm (26.5% vs 15.4%, respectively; Supplementary Figure S3). Most flare-ups were in soft tissue upper right leg front (23.5% in placebo, 15.4% in garetosmab), soft tissue upper left leg back (23.5% vs 0%, respectively), soft tissue upper left leg front (14.7% vs 15.4%, respectively), and soft tissue buttocks back (14.7% vs 15.4%, respectively). No flare-ups occurred in soft tissue in the neck in those treated with garetosmab.

Subsequently, in Period 2, those who switched from placebo to garetosmab (placebo/garetosmab) saw a reduction in boht the number and duration of flare-ups.14 The median (Q1:Q3) duration of flare-ups was reduced from 21.8 (8.0:66.0) d at week 28 to 10.0 (6.0:66.0) d at week 56 in the placebo/garetosmab cohort. The median (Q1:Q3) duration of flare-ups in those who continued on garetosmab through week 56 (garetosmab/garetosmab) was 3.3 (2.0:7.3) d. Furthermore, 10 of 24 (41.7%) patients who switched from placebo to garetosmab (placebo/garetosmab) used glucocorticoids vs 5 of 19 (26.3%) patients who continued on garetosmab (garetosmab/garetosmab) in Period 2.

Reductions in both the proportion of patients experiencing flare-ups and the number of new reported flare-ups were sustained in the placebo/garetosmab and garetosmab/garetosmab cohorts through week 76 (Period 3; Figure 4A and B).

Discussion

Flare-ups are an important patient-centric outcome in patients with FOP. In this post hoc analysis of the LUMINA-1 study, observations from the placebo cohort suggest that 71% of adult patients with FOP experienced a flare-up over a 28-wk period. Most of these flare-ups did not result in new HO at the flare-up site. Flare-ups were, however, associated with an overall increase in volume of newly formed heterotopic bone (all locations combined). Patients with flare-ups had nearly a 28-fold greater total volume of new heterotopic bone as compared to those without, suggesting flare-ups are reflective of overall disease activity. It has been previously reported that the number of patients who experienced flare-ups was reduced by nearly 50% in those receiving garetosmab.14 Here we demonstrate that patients in the garetosmab arm reported less severe and shorter duration of flare-ups, and the effects on flare-ups were sustained for the duration of the trial, up to 76 wk.

Our analysis of the placebo cohort from LUMINA-1 enabled us to contribute new information to the understanding of FOP, particularly with respect to flare-ups and HO, in a prospective, randomized, and double-blind manner. Most patients who reported a flare-up developed new HO lesions and had a higher volume of new heterotopic bone. However, only 24% of flare-ups resulted in location-matched new CT-detected HO lesions. Concurrently, when assessing new HO lesions detected by CT, only 41% occurred in the same location as a previous flare-up (regardless of whether this was a new or existing flare-up). Our findings are consistent with those reported previously, in which 52 flare-ups in 40 patients with FOP were imaged by whole-body CT and only 26.9% of flare-ups resulted in new HO at the flare-up site 12 wk after symptom onset.16 Separately, in a phase 2 study of palovarotene for the treatment of FOP, 40% (4/10) of patients in the placebo cohort developed new HO at the flare-up site within 12 wk, as assessed by CT and/or plain radiography.17 The consistency of previously reported findings with ours is striking, as only a minority of flare-ups appear to be associated with new HO lesions at the flare-up site. This is contrary to the prior understanding of flare-ups in which it was thought that most flare-ups were a precursor to new HO at the site of development.9 With new data available and the consistency of findings across several studies, these results suggest that causality cannot necessarily be established between flare-ups and subsequent HO lesions at a given anatomical site.

There is a caveat to the interpretation of this data, as recent evidence has shown that widespread, severe developmental arthropathy in adolescents and adults with FOP is independent of HO.18 Self-reported symptoms (ie, pain, swelling, joint stiffness, and decreased mobility) would be difficult to ascribe to impending HO, degenerative arthropathy, or both. Although degenerative arthropathy was not evaluated in this study, these findings have relevance for ascribing anatomic discordance of flare-up symptoms with HO in any FOP clinical setting. Importantly, anti-activin A therapy may be beneficial in HO as well as the inflammatory component of developmental arthropathy.11

Despite the lack of correlation between flare-ups and HO at any anatomical site, there is striking evidence that flare-ups reflect increased disease activity. Our results showed that most patients who reported a flare-up developed new HO lesions and are more likely to have a higher total volume of new HO. Pignolo et al. also reported a higher mean volume of annualized new HO in patients who reported flare-ups vs those who did not (37.7 vs 6.2 cm3).16 These results are in good agreement with our estimates of mean annualized new HO for the patients with and without flare-ups (44.9 vs 5.9 cm3), suggesting that flare-ups are a marker of disease activity and may be a prognostic indicator of HO volumetric growth. This is an important consideration in the design and interpretation of clinical trials to ensure that populations are balanced between the study arms.

To our knowledge, this is the first trial to demonstrate not only an improvement in flare-up events, but also in symptoms related to flare-ups with treatment for FOP.14 The effect on flare-ups seen in patients treated with garetosmab over Period 1 was sustained long-term in both cohorts through to the end of Period 3 (week 76). Furthermore, patients in the garetosmab arm demonstrated a reduction in the severity and duration of flare-up symptoms, and utilization of corticosteroids. Flare-ups have been noted to be frequently associated with moderate-to-severe pain, and nearly 70% of patients with FOP report a decrease in mobility and overall function related to their flares.6,19 With the current treatment landscape, which still presents a significant unmet need, this highlights the degree of disability patients with FOP suffer and the reduced quality of life associated with flare-ups. Despite the variability inherent in flare-up presentation, patients in the garetosmab arm demonstrated a marked effect in reducing flare-up events, related symptoms, duration of flare-ups, and utilization of corticosteroids. The latter is especially important given the risks associated with continued corticosteroid use.

Through both pre-clinical and clinical studies, it has been shown that the development of new heterotopic bone lesions is an activin A–dependent process.11,13,14 The reduction in flare-ups also seen in LUMINA-1 with garetosmab suggests that flare-ups are likely an activin A–associated process. The interplay between flare-ups and HO is still unclear, but our data indicate that flare-ups appear to be a marker of systemically increased disease activity. Furthermore, it validates existing literature that flare-ups do not lead to new HO lesions at the flare-up site in most cases in adults.

Limitations

The analysis of flare-ups in untreated adults with FOP is limited to 28 wk before the placebo patients crossed over to garetosmab. Due to the scheduled nature of the scans, it was not possible to determine the exact timing of the appearance of new HO. Longer evaluation may be required to strengthen the evidence of the association of flare-up events and new HO lesions. Although LUMINA-1 enrolled almost 5% of the known FOP population, the small sample size of 44 patients posed several statistical challenges to the analysis of flare-up events and new lesions. The small number of observed flare-up events and new lesions limited the analysis to descriptive statistics in the overall cohorts and by severity. Additional statistical evaluation may be needed to further quantify the confidence of the effects observed.

Conclusion

Flare-ups are a significant burden for patients with FOP and are often functionally debilitating. This post hoc analysis in adult placebo-treated patients with FOP showed that flare-ups were not strongly correlated with new heterotopic bone lesions at the flare-up site, but a higher volume of new heterotopic bone lesions was observed in patients with flare-ups. The reduction in the incidence, duration, and severity of flare-ups observed for patients in the garetosmab arm suggests that they may be driven by activin A. This highlights an additional potential utility of garetosmab as a disease-modifying treatment for FOP.

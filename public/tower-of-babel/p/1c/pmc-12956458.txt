Early Onset, High Comorbidity Burden, and Regional Disparities of CADASIL: A Nationwide Cohort Study in South Korea
Authors: Ju-Yeun Lee, Minwoo Lee, Jae-Sung Lim, Mi Sun Oh, Kyung-Ho Yu, Young Eun Kim, Hyeo-Il Ma, Yun Jin Kim, Jong Ho Park, Young Hee Jung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and PurposeTo compare the epidemiological and clinical features of the rare patients with cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) with age- and sex-matched controls in a nationwide cohort from South Korea.MethodsThis observational cohort study analyzed newly diagnosed CADASIL patients aged at least 20 years and matched controls using data from the National Health Information Database for 2004–2022. The cumulative incidence of CADASIL was assessed by age and sex, and compared between regions. Neurologic and systemic diseases were compared between the CADASIL and control groups.ResultsThe study analyzed 816 CADASIL patients and 816 age- and sex-matched controls aged 56.8±15.2 years (mean±standard deviation), among whom 48.3% were male. The cumulative incidence of CADASIL was 1.86 per 100,000 people (95% confidence interval [CI]=1.85–1.87 per 100,000), and peaked at 60–69 years of age. In terms of regional distribution, the incidence was highest for Jeju, at 39.67 per 100,000 (95% CI 37.84–41.49 per 100,000). Neurologic diseases were more frequent in CADASIL patients, including Alzheimer’s disease (33.1% vs. 20.0%), vascular dementia (84.9% vs. 5.0%), epilepsy (34.6% vs. 15.9%), stroke (70.7% vs. 27.6%), parkinsonism (18.9% vs. 11.0%), and depression (60.8% vs. 44.9%). Systemic diseases such as diabetes mellitus (78.9% vs. 68.9%) were also more common in CADASIL patients, while cancer (27.9% vs. 38.7%) and myocardial infarction (10.0% vs. 13.6%) were less common than in controls. The onset ages of all diseases were lower in CADASIL patients.ConclusionsThis study has provided a precise nationwide estimate of the CADASIL incidence and its regional distribution in South Korea. CADASIL patients showed higher incidence rates and earlier onsets of diverse clinical manifestations.Keywords: cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy, epidemiology, nationwide cohort

INTRODUCTION

Cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by NOTCH3 mutations.1,2 CADASIL typically presents with migraine with aura at 30–39 years of age, subcortical ischemic events at 50–59 years of age, and psychiatric symptoms or cognitive decline at 50–60 years of age.1,3,4,5 Previously considered rare, with the estimated prevalence rate ranging from 1.3 to 4.1 per 100,000 among Caucasians,6,7,8,9 recent large-scale genomic studies have revealed a higher prevalence of pathogenic NOTCH3 variants (3.4 per 1,000 people), with the risk being highest in Asians.10,11 The mutation profile varies by race and region.12 In Europe, NOTCH3 mutations are most frequently found in exons 3 and 4,13,14,15 whereas in East Asia the p.Arg544Cys (exon 11) mutation is common in CADASIL patients from Jeju and Taiwan. The p.Arg75Pro mutation has been identified only in patients from Japan, South Korea, and China (i.e., not in Europe), suggesting the presence of a founder effect in East Asia.12,16,17,18,19,20

The clinical manifestations and disease severity in CADASIL vary by race and geographic region. Intracerebral hemorrhage has been reported in only 0.5%–2.4% of European patients,21 but in 17%–25% of South Korean and Taiwanese patients, most of whom carry the p.Arg544Cys mutation.22,23,24 East Asian patients also show a lower incidence of migraine and a higher age at symptom onset. Although global genomic studies have investigated the prevalence of NOTCH3 mutations in the general population,10,11 a clear genotype–phenotype correlation remains uncertain due to significant variability in the disease onset and severity, even among individuals with the same mutation or who are members of the same family.5,24

While it can be postulated that the epidemiology and clinical manifestations of CADASIL differ substantially across races and geographic regions, there have been few epidemiological studies due to the rarity of CADASIL and challenges in diagnosis. Previous studies of the clinical manifestations of CADASIL have primarily analyzed cohort data from small numbers of specialized centers renowned for their expertise in CADASIL. While it is well known that the prevalence of CADASIL is high for Jeju, comprehensive nationwide data for South Korea remain lacking.12 Therefore, the present nationwide, large-scale, retrospective cohort study focused on comparing the epidemiology and clinical characteristics of CADASIL patients in South Korea with age- and sex-matched controls.

METHODS

Data source

This study utilized data from the Korean National Health Insurance Service (K-NHIS) database.25 South Korea operates a national single-payer healthcare system that covers more than 97% of the population, and the K-NHIS database—constructed from medical claims records—contains detailed information on medication prescriptions, utilization of healthcare facilities, demographic characteristics, and diagnoses coded according to the 10th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) classification. To encourage preventive healthcare, the K-NHIS recommends that all citizens aged 40 years and above undergoing a health checkup every 2 years, which is provided for free. Each routine checkup includes reviewing the individual’s medical history and performing a lifestyle survey, laboratory blood tests, and physical assessment. The program had a high participation rate of 74.8% in 2014.

We analyzed medical claims data from 2002 to 2022 in the National Health Information Database (NHID) that is managed by the K-NHIS. This database includes personal information, demographic characteristics, responses to lifestyle surveys, health checkup results, and records of medical treatments provided to the entire South Korean population. Our experimental cohort was formed by examining all medical records within this comprehensive, nationally representative K-NHIS database. The population of South Korea was defined using data from the Population and Housing Census conducted in 2024 (Korean Statistical Information Service; available at http://kosis.kr; accessed October 21, 2024).

Participants and sample selection

All adult patients diagnosed with CADASIL (according to registration code V122 in the K-NHIS database) between 2002 and 2022 who were continuously enrolled in a medical plan were included. For incidence estimates, the earliest claim with the V122 code was defined as the index date and considered the incident time, with patients classified as incident cases for that year. The V122 code represents eligibility for the Special Medical Expense Reduction Program under the K-NHIS and served as the operational definition in this study. Since the program requires diagnostic confirmation through genetic testing or skin biopsy, the use of the V122 code allowed for the inclusion of patients with a relatively high diagnostic certainty.

A 2-year washout period (from January 2002 to December 2003) was applied to exclude prior CADASIL cases and hence ensure that only newly diagnosed cases were included. The exclusion criteria included 1) age <20 years and 2) any history of CADASIL before the index date.

To allow medical conditions to be compared with controls, a random number generator was used to randomly select an age- and sex-matched CADASIL-free individual with health checkup data from the NHID database for each CADASIL patient. The same exclusion criteria were applied before matching. Age, sex, medical visit records, and diagnosis dates were obtained from the Health Insurance Review and Assessment (HIRA) database for both groups.

Definition of outcomes

For the final analysis, information on the following neurologic diseases was obtained in both the CADASIL and control groups: 1) Alzheimer’s disease (AD), 2) vascular dementia (VaD), 3) epilepsy, 4) all stroke types, including hemorrhagic and ischemic, 5) migraine, and 6) parkinsonism, including primary, secondary, vascular, and Parkinson-plus diseases.

Information on the following systemic diseases was also obtained: 1) diabetes mellitus (DM), 2) hypertension (HTN), 3) hypercholesterolemia, 4) atrial fibrillation/flutter, 5) myocardial infarction (MI), 6) all types of cancer, and 7) depression disorder. The frequency of comorbidities for these medical conditions was compared between the CADASIL and control groups. Detailed information on the ICD-10 codes is provided in the Supplementary Material (in the online-only Data Supplement).

Analyses

We divided the study population into 12 groups based on 6 age strata and 2 sexes (male and female). To determine the population at risk for incidence estimations, we excluded individuals with pre-existing cases of CADASIL identified during a 2-year washout period (2002–2003) from the Population and Housing Census. We then calculated the cumulative incidence from January 2004 to December 2022 by dividing the number of new cases of CADASIL by the total population at risk during that period. The annual incidence was determined using data from the last analysis year (2022), and we also calculated the male-to-female ratio for the total incidence. The incidence was also estimated by region, with the geographic distribution within South Korea examined across seven administrative divisions: Seoul, Gyeonggi Province, Gangwon Province, Chungcheong Province, Jeolla Province, Gyeongsang Province, and Jeju.

The following baseline characteristics were compared between the CADASIL and control groups: sociodemographic and lifestyle factors (age, sex, income, alcohol consumption, and smoking status), laboratory blood test and anthropometric values (systolic blood pressure [SBP], diastolic blood pressure [DBP], triglycerides, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C], liver function, creatinine, and body mass index [BMI]), medication use (clopidogrel, aspirin, warfarin, and cilostazol), and Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI). The frequencies and ages at onset for these neurologic and systemic diseases were compared statistically between the CADASIL and control groups. A sex-specific subanalysis was also performed.

Statistical analyses

Data handling and statistical analyses were performed at the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Institute by an independent data analyst (J.Y.L.) trained in large-data analysis. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Missing data for variables was modeled as an independent category. The results of the statistical analyses are presented as mean±standard-deviation values. Jeju and non-Jeju groups were compared using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Propensity-score matching was used to adjust for age and sex differences between the two groups. SAS Enterprise Guide software (version 6.1, SAS Institute Inc.) was used for the analyses.

Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and patient consents

This nationwide study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Myongji Hospital (IRB approval no. MJH 2023-09-009). The need to obtain informed consents from the patients was waived due to the retrospective design of the study and since all data were deidentified.

Among the entire adult population (age ≥20 years), after applying the washout period and exclusion criteria, 816 newly diagnosed CADASIL patients and 816 matched controls were included in this study (from January 2004 to December 2022). They were aged 56.8±15.2 years in both groups, and 394 (48.3%) of them were male. Their baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Nationwide incidence and geographical distribution of CADASIL in South Korea

The cumulative incidence of CADASIL per 100,000 in the total study population was 1.86 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.85–1.87) (Fig. 1): it was 1.80 (95% CI 1.79–1.82) in males and 1.92 (95% CI 1.90–1.93) in females. The incidence peaked within the age range of 60–69 years in both sexes. The incidence was higher in males across all age groups except for those aged ≥70 years. The male-to-female incidence ratio was 0.93 overall, and ranged from 0.38 to 1.37 across the age groups. A sharp increase in annual incidence was observed from 2021 to 2022 in both sexes.

Nationwide incidence of CADASIL according to the age and sex groups in South Korea during 2004-2022. CADASIL, cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy.

Regionally, the incidence was highest for Jeju (39.67 per 100,000, 95% CI 37.84–41.49 per 100,000) (Fig. 2), following by (in decreasing order) that in Gyeongsang Province (5.35, 95% CI 5.27–5.43), Chungcheong Province (3.63, 95% CI 3.54–3.73), Gyeonggi Province (2.96, 95% CI 2.23–2.41), Jeolla Province (2.39, 95% CI 2.30–2.48), Gangwon Province (2.11, 95% CI 1.93–2.30), and Seoul (1.72, 95% CI 1.69–1.74) (Fig. 2).

Comparison of frequencies of neurologic diseases and ages at onset between the CADASIL and control groups

The differences in the frequencies of neurologic diseases between the groups are presented in Table 2 and Fig. 3. The prevalence rate of AD was significantly higher in the CADASIL group than in the control group (33.1% vs. 20.0%, p<0.001), as were those of VaD (84.9% vs. 5.0%, p<0.001), epilepsy (34.6% vs. 15.9%, p<0.001), parkinsonism (18.9% vs. 11.0%, p<0.001), migraine (41.5% vs. 32.0%, p<0.001), hemorrhagic stroke (9.8% vs. 6.4%, p=0.014), ischemic stroke (69.7% vs. 24.1%, p<0.001), and depression (60.8% vs. 44.9%, p<0.001). It was particularly notable that VaD was much more common in the CADASIL group, and that 69.7% of those in that group had experienced an ischemic stroke.

Comparison of frequencies of systemic diseases and ages at onset between the CADASIL and control groups

The prevalence rates of DM (78.9% vs. 68.9%, p<0.001) and hyperlipidemia (92.9% vs. 78.1%, p<0.001) were higher in the CADASIL group than in the control group, while those of HTN and atrial fibrillation did not differ significantly between the two groups. The prevalence rates of cancer (27.9% vs. 38.7%, p<0.001) and MI (10.0% vs. 13.6%, p=0.032) were lower in the CADASIL group than in the control group.

The ages at the onset of DM (52.4±12.1 vs. 62.1±12.5 years), HTN (51.6±11.3 vs. 61.7±11.1 years), hyperlipidemia (49.6±11.8 vs. 59.7±13.0 years), MI (55.7±12.8 vs. 67.5±12.6 years), all types of cancer (54.9±12.4 vs. 65.0±11.5 years), and atrial fibrillation (59.4±14.2 vs. 69.7±11.5) were also lower in the CADASIL group (Fig. 4).

We also performed propensity-score analysis to adjust for possible difference in the sex and age composition between the Jeju and non-Jeju groups. After adjusting for age and sex, the prevalence rates of VaD (p=0.012), epilepsy (p=0.001), and DM (p=0.001) remained significantly higher in the Jeju group than in the non-Jeju group (Supplementary Table 1 in the online-only Data Supplement).

The comorbidity burden was also compared between CADASIL patients in the Jeju and non-Jeju groups. The Jeju group showed a tendency toward a higher proportion of high CCI values relative to the non-Jeju group, but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.353) (Supplementary Table 2 in the online-only Data Supplement).

DISCUSSION

This nationwide study used K-NHIS data to analyze the epidemiological and clinical characteristics of CADASIL in South Korea. Key findings of the study included the following: 1) the cumulative incidence of CADASIL was 1.86 per 100,000 (95% CI 1.85–1.87 per 100,000), 2) the prevalence of CADASIL among participants aged ≥70 years was higher in females, 3) the incidence of CADASIL was significantly higher for Jeju, 4) the risks of stroke, VaD, AD, parkinsonism, migraine, depression, and epilepsy were higher in the CADASIL group than in the control group, 5) systemic diseases such as hyperlipidemia and DM were more common in the CADASIL group than in the control group, while atrial fibrillation and cancer were less common in the CADASIL group, and 6) comorbidities had earlier onsets and CCI values were higher in CADASIL patients than in the control group.

This study found that the cumulative incidence of CADASIL per 100,000 of the nationwide population of South Korea was 1.86 (95% CI 1.85–1.87) based on K-NHIS data: it was 1.80 (95% CI 1.79–1.82) in males and 1.92 (95% CI 1.90–1.93) in females. The incidence peaked within the age range of 60–69 years in both sexes. The overall incidence is comparable with previously reported prevalence rates of CADASIL per 100,000 adults: 1.32 in Northeast England, 1.98 in West Scotland, 4.6 in Glasgow, and 4.1 in Central Italy.6,7,8,9

We found that the proportion of females was notably higher among the patients with CADASIL who were aged ≥70 years. CADASIL is an autosomal dominant genetic disorder that has been considered to exhibit no known sex differences in prevalence. However, a large previous study involving 411 subjects found that the median survival was significantly shorter in males (64.6 vs. 69.3 years) but not in females (70.7 vs. 72.2 years).15 The lower impact of CADASIL on female life expectancy may explain the higher proportion of females in the oldest age group in the present study.

The incidence of CADASIL was found to be 39.67 per 100,000 for Jeju, which is remarkably higher than the national mean of 1.86 across South Korea. This disparity may be attributable to several factors. First, the unique demographic and sociocultural context of Jeju—such as the relative genetic homogeneity typical of island populations and the presence of a kinship-based village—may have facilitated the preservation and amplification of particular genetic variants (e.g., R544C).26 Jeju is geographically separated from the South Korean peninsula, and so the high prevalence of CADASIL observed for Jeju may be partly explained by the founder effect.12 Second, the presence of more CADASIL cases for Jeju may have increased awareness among physicians, leading to more-frequent diagnostic testing. This is further supported by regional data from Jeju, where the Jeju Regional Center for Rare Diseases was established in February 2021. The 816 identified CADASIL cases included 477 diagnosed in 2021 and 260 in 2022, suggesting that an improved diagnostic infrastructure could have facilitated the recognition of CADASIL cases.

We compared the clinical manifestations between the Jeju and non-Jeju regions. Before adjusting for age and sex, the prevalence rates of AD, VaD, epilepsy, stroke, DM, HTN, atrial fibrillation, and dyslipidemia were significantly higher in the Jeju group than in the non-Jeju group, and they remained significantly higher for VaD, epilepsy, and DM after adjusting for age and sex. These findings suggest that higher burdens of vascular and metabolic comorbidities contribute to the observed regional variations in CADASIL prevalence, beyond the genetic predisposition alone.

Our findings indicated that the incidence rates of both VaD and AD were higher in patients with CADASIL. While VaD is a well-known neurological manifestation of CADASIL,27 an association between AD and CADASIL has not been widely reported. There are some previous neuropathological reports of the coexistence of CADASIL and AD.28,29,30 On the other hand, the pattern of cerebrospinal fluid levels of amyloid and tau markers in CADASIL patients is more similar to that seen in sporadic small-vessel disease than in AD.31 Since amyloid and tau deposition frequently coexist with cerebrovascular pathology in AD patients, further studies are required to determine the potential impact of cerebrovascular disease-related genes such as NOTCH3 on AD. In our CADASIL group, not only were the incidence rates of various neurologic diseases higher, but the ages at their onset were also lower. Additionally, beyond the well-documented neurological manifestations, the CADASIL patients exhibited a significantly increased prevalence of systemic comorbidities such as MI, DM, and hyperlipidemia. The lower incidence of atrial fibrillation and cancers in CADASIL patients may be attributable to their reduced life expectancy, which decreases the likelihood of developing those age-related conditions. Furthermore, we found that the ages at onset for all diseases analyzed in this study were lower in the CADASIL group. We also found that the CCI was ≥3 in 41.1% of the CADASIL patients but in only 33.5% of the subjects in the control group. This difference may be attributed to the broad clinical spectrum of CADASIL, which includes both systemic and neurologic diseases with earlier onsets. The CCI has been consistently validated as a robust predictor of mortality across diverse populations.32 It has been reported that the 1-year mortality rate is approximately 7% for individuals with a CCI of 0, 22% for those with a CCI of 1 or 2, 31% for a CCI of 0, a CCI of 3 or 4, and 40% for a CCI of 5 or higher in emergency department patients with suspected infection.33 These findings support the notion of the comorbidity burden being higher in patients with CADASIL, highlighting the need for comprehensive and earlier clinical monitoring.

While the prevalence rates of all comorbid conditions except stroke were higher in female than male patients with CADASIL, the life expectancy of the females appeared to be less affected. This suggests that stroke is the most-important factor influencing mortality in CADASIL. These clinical manifestations and comorbidities should therefore be considered when managing and counseling patients with CADASIL and their families.34

This study had several limitations. First, the maximum follow-up period of 20 years may have been insufficient to fully capture late-onset conditions such as dementia and parkinsonism. Longer follow-up studies are therefore needed to more fully assess their progression. Second, the study population comprised patients registered as having a severe rare disease under the Special Medical Expense Reduction Program, meaning that they were generally suspected of having a severe condition and confirmed through genetic testing. This might have resulted in the prevalence of attenuated or delayed-onset CADASIL phenotypes being underestimated.35,36 Third, CADASIL is diagnosed based on clinical suspicion and physician-initiated testing, which potentially introduced selection bias. Fourth, the possibility of diagnostic inaccuracy cannot be completely excluded because comorbid neurologic diseases were identified using ICD-10 diagnostic codes from administrative records, and some diagnostic codes (e.g., those for AD or stroke) may have been used for medication reimbursement or administrative purposes rather than for making definitive clinical diagnoses. In particular, most of the CADASIL patients who were assigned a diagnostic code for AD are likely to represent mixed dementia rather than pure AD. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Future studies should utilize disease-specific rare disease nationwide registries as valuable complementary tools, in order to provide more-accurate diagnostic confirmations and comprehensive clinical information that are essential for epidemiological and clinical research.

This study is notable for having assembled a homogeneous cohort of CADASIL patients nationwide using the Special Medical Expense Reduction Program under the K-NHIS as the operational definition. This approach meant that the included patients had diagnoses that were confirmed through genetic testing or skin biopsy.

In conclusion, the patients with CADASIL in this study exhibited higher prevalence rates of both neurologic and systemic diseases, as well as earlier onsets of symptoms, which collectively contributed to higher CCI values. More females aged ≥70 years were affected, and Jeju had the highest incidence among the seven administrative divisions of South Korea. The identification of a nationwide CADASIL cohort in this study has provided valuable epidemiological and comorbidity insights. A clearer understanding of the disease’s nationwide prevalence and clinical burden could improve the management of CADASIL, especially in East Asia where this condition is more common. The present findings underscore the importance of providing CADASIL patients with comprehensive comorbidity.

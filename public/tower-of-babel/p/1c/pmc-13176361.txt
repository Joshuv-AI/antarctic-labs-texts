Efficacy, Durability, and Safety of Faricimab for Diabetic Macular Edema: 1-Year Results from the China RHINE Subpopulation
Authors: Xiaorong Li, Xiaoling Liu, Qin Jiang, Zhifeng Wu, Mei Han, Qiuyu Chen, Oluwatobi O Idowu, Ruyuan Liu, Wenxin Liu, Jeffrey R Willis, Xiaodong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTo assess the efficacy, durability, and safety of faricimab compared with aflibercept over 1 year in patients with diabetic macular edema (DME) from the China RHINE subpopulation.MethodsRHINE was a phase 3 global, active comparator-controlled trial. The China RHINE subpopulation included patients from mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan. Patients with DME were randomized to faricimab 6.0 mg every 8 weeks (Q8W), faricimab 6.0 mg treat-and-extend (T&E), or aflibercept 2.0 mg Q8W up to week 100. The mean change from baseline in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) averaged over weeks 48–56 was the primary end point. Anatomic, durability, and safety outcomes were also assessed.ResultsIn China RHINE subpopulation at weeks 48–56, adjusted mean BCVA change from baseline was +13.2, +11.5, and +9.2 letters with faricimab Q8W (n = 51), faricimab T&E (n = 49), and aflibercept Q8W (n = 52), respectively. More patients treated with faricimab achieved absence of DME or intraretinal fluid, and 75% achieved > Q12W dosing with faricimab T&E by week 52. Faricimab was well-tolerated; there were no new safety signals.ConclusionsVisual and anatomic improvements with faricimab in the China RHINE subpopulation were in keeping with the global findings. Faricimab offers durable treatment for patients with DME in China.Trial registrationRHINE (NCT03622593).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-026-01378-1.Keywords: Angiopoietin-2, China, Diabetic macular edema, Diabetic retinopathy, Faricimab, Vascular endothelial growth factor-A, Visual acuity

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Faricimab is the novel bispecific antibody designed for intraocular usage that binds to both angiopoietin-2 and vascular endothelial growth factor-A, which has shown the potential to extend the durability of treatment for patients with diabetic macular edema (DME).There exists a significant and growing unmet need for effective, long-acting therapies for DME in China, given its large diabetic population.This subpopulation analysis provided the first clinical evidence to evaluate the efficacy, durability, and safety of faricimab specifically in the China subpopulation, supporting the regulatory approval of faricimab in China. What was learned from the study? Faricimab was effective, durable, and well-tolerated in the China subpopulation with DME. Our findings are consistent with global population analysis of the RHINE cohort.These findings highlight faricimab’s potential to lessen injection frequency and improve management feasibility for patients with DME in China, offering Chinese clinicians the local data necessary to make informed treatment decisions in the real-world clinical setting.

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is the most frequent cause of impaired vision in diabetes mellitus, affecting approximately 30% of patients [1, 2]. Diabetic macular edema (DME), which is the vision-limiting complication of DR [1], in turn affects approximately 4% of patients with diabetes globally [2]. The prevalence rate of DME is estimated to be similar in China [3], but with a high incidence of diabetes (12% in 2018) [4]. This is projected to rise considerably between 2020 and 2030, largely owing to urbanization, lifestyle changes, and obesity [5]. Hence, there is an increasing need for effective DME treatments.

DME is typically managed with intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections, which can reduce edema and improve vision [1, 6]. However, anti-VEGF therapy alone is not always effective [7, 8], with up to 30% of patients with DME not responding to anti-VEGF treatment [7]. In addition, current anti-VEGF injection regimens impose a significant burden on patients, healthcare providers, carers, and healthcare systems, due to frequent clinical examinations and intravitreal injections [6], underscoring the need for more durable therapies.

Although acquired resistance to anti-VEGF treatment is poorly understood, hypotheses suggest that alternative signaling pathways (such as angiopoietin/tyrosine kinase signaling) may act to circumvent VEGF inhibition [9]. Faricimab is the first bispecific antibody designed for intraocular use, which binds angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) and VEGF-A with high affinity and specificity [10, 11]. Faricimab was first approved in the USA for treating DME and neovascular age-related macular degeneration in January 2022 [12], and has subsequently been approved in over 100 countries worldwide. In the phase 3 global YOSEMITE and RHINE studies, faricimab was well-tolerated in patients with DME and showed durable efficacy beyond the anti-VEGF, aflibercept [13]. Similar findings were observed in the Japan YOSEMITE subpopulation [14].

The purpose of the phase 3 YOSEMITE and RHINE trials (NCT03622580/NCT03622593) was to evaluate the efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetics, and optimal treatment frequency of faricimab over approximately 100 weeks in treatment-naive or previously anti-VEGF-treated patients with center-involving DME [15]. The 1- and 2-year results of the global YOSEMITE and RHINE trials have been reported [13, 16]. The RHINE trial included a China subpopulation to support regulatory approval of faricimab in China. The subpopulation comprised patients from the global enrollment phase (mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan) and the extension phase (mainland China). Here, we present 1-year efficacy, durability, and safety results for the China RHINE subpopulation.

Methods

Study Design

RHINE (NCT03622593) was a global, multicenter, phase 3 randomized controlled trial (N = 951) [15]. Patients with center-involving DME were randomized (1:1:1) to: faricimab every 8 weeks (Q8W), where intravitreal faricimab 6.0 mg was injected Q8W after six initial Q4W doses; faricimab treat-and-extend (T&E), where intravitreal faricimab 6.0 mg was injected following a personalized T&E regimen (Q4W up to Q16W) after four initial Q4W doses using prespecified vision/anatomic criteria to adjust dosing [15]; or aflibercept Q8W, where intravitreal aflibercept 2.0 mg was injected Q8W after five initial Q4W doses. Full details regarding the methodology and global RHINE findings have been published elsewhere [13, 15, 16].

RHINE was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation E6 Guideline for Good Clinical Practice, tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, US Food and Drug Administration regulations, the European Union Clinical Trials Directive (2001/20/EC) and all applicable local, state, and federal laws. Study protocols were approved by applicable institutional review boards and ethics committees before trial commencement. Informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the study. Investigators from the China RHINE subpopulation trial sites are listed in the Supplementary Table S1. Faricimab is approved for the treatment of diabetic macular edema, neovascular age-related macular degeneration, and macular edema secondary to retinal vein occlusion in multiple countries worldwide and is not currently approved for use outside these indications.

Study Population

Patients in the China RHINE subpopulation were enrolled at a total of 20 trial sites between November of 2018 and June of 2021. Patients were enrolled across trial sites in mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan during the global enrollment phase (between October 2018 and September 2019) and in mainland China during the China extension phase (between October 2019 and June 2021). The China extension phase was conducted specifically after the global enrollment phase to ensure the total enrollment was sufficient to support registration in mainland China. The enrollment criteria were identical to those in the global enrollment phase. Key criteria for inclusion were: age > 18 years and a diagnosis of diabetes mellitus; regular use of insulin or other injectable/oral agents for diabetes treatment; and HbA1c < 10% within 2 months prior to the day 1 visit. Study eyes were required to have: center-involving DME; central subfield thickness (CST) > 325 µm per Spectralis spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) or > 315 µm per Cirrus or Topcon SD-OCT; best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 25–73 Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters (Snellen equivalent: 20/320 to 20/40). Each patient had only one eye included in the trial. In cases where patients had both eyes meeting the eligibility criteria, the eye with the lowest BCVA evaluated on screening was assigned to be the study eye. Patients who were naive to anti-VEGF treatment or who had previously received ocular anti-VEGF treatment (at least 3 months prior to day 1 of the trial) could be included in the trial; however, enrollment of eyes previously treated with an anti-VEGF was restricted to 25% of the overall enrollment.

Outcome Measures

Efficacy outcomes included the following: change from baseline in BCVA (per an ETDRS chart at 4 m distance) averaged over weeks 48, 52, and 56; percentage of patients who gained > 15 BCVA letters from baseline averaged over weeks 48, 52, and 56; change from baseline in CST (the distance measured from the inner limiting membrane to Bruch’s membrane) averaged over weeks 48, 52, and 56; percentage of patients with absence of DME (CST < 325 μm per Spectralis SD-OCT) through week 52; percentage of patients with absence of intraretinal fluid (IRF) and subretinal fluid (SRF) (measured in the ETDRS grid central 1-mm diameter) through week 52; the percentage of patients with a > 2-step DR severity improvement from baseline on the ETDRS Diabetic Retinopathy Severity Scale (DRSS) at week 52; and the percentage of patients in the T&E arm on the different treatment intervals (Q4W, Q8W, Q12W, or Q16W) at week 52. The occurrence of ocular and nonocular adverse events (AEs) was recorded.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy analyses included patients from the intent-to-treat population. Safety analyses included all randomized patients who received at least one injection of study treatment in the study eye.

The methods and statistical analyses used were generally in keeping with those used in the previously described YOSEMITE/RHINE primary analyses [13]. Briefly, all continuous outcomes were evaluated by mixed model for repeated measures. All categorical outcomes were evaluated using unstratified estimations for binomial proportions. Data are reported as estimates and confidence intervals (CIs) for each of the three treatment arms and for the difference between the aflibercept arm and each of the faricimab arms. Formal statistical comparisons between the faricimab and aflibercept treatment arms in the China subpopulation were not performed, as this subpopulation was not powered for head-to-head analysis.

Results

Patients

The China RHINE subpopulation included 152 patients (faricimab Q8W, n = 51; faricimab T&E, n = 49; aflibercept Q8W, n = 52; Supplementary Fig. S1). This comprised 33 patients (21.7%) from the global enrollment phase and 119 patients (78.3%) from the China extension phase. Overall, six patients (3.9%) from the China RHINE subpopulation withdrew from the trial before week 56. This included three patients (5.9%) in the faricimab Q8W arm (patient decision, n = 2; physician decision, n = 1), 1 patient (2.0%) in the faricimab T&E arm (patient decision, n = 1), and two patients (3.8%) in the aflibercept Q8W arm (patient decision, n = 2). Baseline characteristics were generally well balanced across the three treatment arms in the China RHINE subpopulation (Table 1).

Visual Outcomes

The 1-year vision outcomes were numerically better (without formal statistical testing) with faricimab versus aflibercept in the China subpopulation. The China RHINE subpopulation showed an improvement from baseline in adjusted mean letter gains in both the faricimab Q8W arm and the faricimab T&E arm relative to the aflibercept Q8W arm (Fig. 1). For weeks 48–56, the difference between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in adjusted mean (95% CI) BCVA letter change was 4.0 (0.7–7.2) and 2.3 (–0.9 to 5.6) for Q8W and T&E, respectively. In addition, for weeks 48–56, the mean difference (95% CI) between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in the percentage of patients who gained > 15 letters from baseline were 9.1% ( –8.9% to 27.1%) and 11.4% ( –6.8% to 29.6%) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively.

Anatomic Outcomes

The 1-year anatomic outcomes including edema, fluid, and DR severity, showed numerical improvements (without formal statistical testing) with faricimab versus aflibercept in the China subpopulation. CST was reduced from baseline in both the faricimab Q8W arm and the faricimab T&E arm relative to the aflibercept Q8W arm (Fig. 2). For weeks 48–56, the difference (95% CI) between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in adjusted mean CST change was –48.7 µm ( –81.5 to –16.0) and –39.7 µm ( –72.4 to –7.1) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively.

Adjusted mean (95% CI) change from baseline in central subretinal thickness (CST) up to week 56 in the China RHINE subpopulation. CI confidence interval, Q8W every 8 weeks, T&E treat-and-extend. aAdjusted mean change from baseline at 1 year, averaged over weeks 48, 52, and 56

A greater proportion of patients had fluid resolution at weeks 48–56, indicated by an absence of DME, IRF, and SRF, in the faricimab Q8W and T&E arms versus the aflibercept Q8W arm (Fig. 3). The mean difference between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in the percentage (95% CI) of patients with absence of DME at week 52 was 11.7% ( –6.0% to 29.4%) and 19.2% (3.2% to 35.2%) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively. The mean difference (95% CI) between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in the percentage of patients with absence of IRF at week 52 was 33.8% (13.4% to 54.2%) and 33.5% (13.3% to 53.6%) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively. The mean difference (95% CI) between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in the percentage of patients with absence of SRF at week 52 was 0.2% ( –6.7% to 7.1%) and –4.5% ( –13.8% to 4.8%) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively.

In addition, the mean difference (95% CI) between the faricimab and aflibercept arms in the percentage of patients with > 2‑step ETDRS-DRSS improvement from baseline at week 52 was 11.4% ( –10.5% to 33.3%) and 7.7% ( –14.4% to 29.8%) for faricimab Q8W and T&E, respectively (Supplementary Fig. S2).

Durability

In the faricimab T&E arm at week 52, the percentage of patients on > Q12W dosing was 75% and the percentage of patients on Q16W dosing was 50% (Fig. 4). In the faricimab T&E arm up to week 56, patients had a median of 8 injections. In contrast, patients in the faricimab Q8W and aflibercept Q8W arms both had a median of 10 injections. Among patients in the faricimab T&E arm, 62.5% attained dosing > Q12W that was maintained without a dosing interval decrease up to week 52.

Safety

Overall, faricimab was well-tolerated in the China RHINE subpopulation, with the safety profile being similar to that seen with aflibercept (Table 2). There were no new safety signals. Further, the incidence of AEs resulting in treatment withdrawal or trial withdrawal was low. Only one AE resulted in trial discontinuation up to week 56, which was a nonocular AE occurring in the faricimab Q8W arm (fungal pneumonia). The overall incidence of AEs was slightly lower in the faricimab T&E arm (78%) versus both Q8W arms (94%).

The incidence of AEs in the study eye was similar between the faricimab T&E, faricimab Q8W, and aflibercept Q8W arms, and the AEs were mild-to-moderate in severity (Table 2, and Supplementary Table S2). There was a low incidence of serious ocular AEs across the three treatment arms (Table 2). Intraocular inflammation AEs in the study eye were reported in the faricimab Q8W arm only (two patients [3.9%]). Preferred terms included iritis and uveitis; both were moderate in severity and were recovered at week 56. There were no reports of retinal vascular occlusive disease (retinal vein occlusion, retinal artery embolism, or retinal artery occlusion) or retinal vasculitis.

Discussion

In this China RHINE subpopulation analysis, we examined the efficacy, durability, and safety of faricimab in patients with DME who were from mainland China, Hong Kong, or Taiwan. We found that, after 1 year of treatment, there were robust vision gains, greater CST reductions, and more fluid resolution with faricimab Q8W and T&E dosing compared with aflibercept Q8W dosing (without formal statistical testing) in the China subpopulation. Furthermore, faricimab was found to be well-tolerated and had a safety profile similar to that of aflibercept. Taken together, these findings suggest that dual inhibition of Ang-2/VEGF-A could exert an additional effect in patients with DME over VEGF inhibition alone and may facilitate not only vascular stability but also durable efficacy. Thus, faricimab has potential to address the need for therapies that improve clinical outcomes for patients with DME in mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan.

The findings from the China RHINE subpopulation provides the first clinical evidence to demonstrate that the robust efficacy seen in the global population is reproducible in the Chinese population [13]. Gains in vision with faricimab Q8W and faricimab T&E were consistent with those observed in the overall RHINE global population, although 1-year gains from baseline with faricimab appeared to be slightly higher than those reported in the global analysis (faricimab Q8W, faricimab T&E, and aflibercept Q8W gains were: China subpopulation, + 13.2, + 11.5, and + 9.2 letters, respectively; overall global population: +11.8, +10.8, and +10.3 letters, respectively [13]), although this was not assessed statistically. Improvements in DR severity were also consistent with overall global population findings at week 52, which showed clinically meaningful improvement in the proportion of patients with > 2‑step ETDRS-DRSS improvement from baseline (faricimab Q8W, faricimab T&E, and aflibercept Q8W proportions were: China subpopulation, 55.0%, 51.3%, and 43.6%, respectively; overall global population, 44.2%, 43.7%, and 46.8%, respectively [13]). Anatomic, durability, and safety outcomes were also consistent with the overall RHINE global population analysis [13]. These results should be interpreted with caution given the China RHINE subpopulation sample size and no formal statistical testing.

Compliance with injection treatment regimens can be a hurdle for patients with DME, with barriers including treatment anxiety and taking time off work to attend appointments [17]. Additional challenges may present in China, where patients with DME may be younger and newly diagnosed [18]. There is an increasing number of patients with young-onset diabetes in China [19, 20]. The option of a T&E regimen may be appealing for faricimab as its longer durability along with visual gains may positively impact patient adherence, including fewer clinic visits, reduced travel, and less discomfort from injections [21]. The anti-VEGF ranibizumab can be an effective treatment for DME when given pro re nata (as needed) [22], which is consistent with the RHINE faricimab extended dosing data. As primary registrational data, these findings will be instrumental in informing clinical practice in China and supporting national healthcare reimbursement efforts.

The potential for extended faricimab treatment intervals up to Q16W in the China RHINE subpopulation is consistent with the overall RHINE global population results.

Limitations

However, as aflibercept was administered in a Q8W approved dosing regimen [23], RHINE was not designed to evaluate the durability of aflibercept versus faricimab in a head-to-head manner. In addition, this analysis was limited by the China RHINE subpopulation sample size and the lack of formal statistical testing. Despite the relatively small sample size, the findings from patients with DME from mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan are in keeping with the results from the overall global population.

Conclusions

We found that 1-year efficacy, durability, and safety results from the China RHINE subpopulation were in keeping with the overall RHINE global population results, and support dual Ang-2/VEGF-A inhibition as a means of treating DME in patients from mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan. The 1-year results will serve as pivotal foundation, providing Chinese clinicians with the local data necessary to make informed decisions in clinical setting. Longer-term efficacy, durability, and safety findings (approximate 2-year results) are forthcoming from the China RHINE subpopulation.

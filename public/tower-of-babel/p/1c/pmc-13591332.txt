Long‐Term Subcutaneous Treprostinil in Pediatric Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension at a Latin American Referral Center: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Angelo Valencia Salazar, Ernesto León Vallejo Mondragón
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPediatric pulmonary arterial hypertension is a progressive vasculopathy with substantial early mortality. Subcutaneous treprostinil is an established prostacyclin‐pathway therapy for higher‐risk disease, but long‐term pediatric data from Latin American referral centers remain limited. This retrospective cohort included all nine consecutive children with World Symposium on Pulmonary Hypertension Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension who initiated subcutaneous treprostinil at a Colombian referral center and were followed from diagnosis until death or last known contact. Outcomes included survival, pediatric risk category transitions, N‐terminal pro–B‐type natriuretic peptide levels, tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion, systolic pulmonary artery pressure, eccentricity index, and structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains. Exploratory comparisons were performed by maintenance dose (≤ 60 vs. > 60 ng/kg/min) and therapy duration. Kaplan–Meier survival at 1, 3, and 5 years was 88.9%, 55.6%, and 55.6%, respectively. Eccentricity index decreased from a median of 2.8 at diagnosis to 1.5 at 5 years, with paired comparisons showing reductions at 1 and 3 years. By Year 5, four of five children with paired risk data had transitioned to a lower pediatric risk category. Doses > 60 ng/kg/min were associated descriptively with more favorable final risk profiles, whereas structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains showed heterogeneous patterns; schooling was the only domain with a statistically detectable association by both dose and duration. In this small Latin American referral‐center cohort, subcutaneous treprostinil treatment was accompanied by favorable risk transitions and stabilization of right ventricular geometry among long‐term survivors. These findings are exploratory and should be interpreted in light of the small sample size, retrospective design, missing longitudinal data, and potential confounding by disease severity, survival, and treatment escalation.Keywords: cohort studies, Colombia, patient‐ and caregiver‐reported domains, pediatrics, prostacyclin analogues, pulmonary arterial hypertension, treprostinil

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) in children is a progressive vasculopathy marked by pulmonary vascular remodeling, increased pulmonary vascular resistance, and right ventricular dysfunction. Pediatric PAH differs from adult disease in tempo, phenotype, developmental impact, and etiologic distribution. Contemporary survival varies substantially across pediatric cohorts and is strongly influenced by disease severity at presentation, diagnostic subtype, and treatment strategy [1, 2]. Heritable PAH, often linked to variants in BMPR2, ACVRL1, and TBX4, presents at younger ages and may follow a more aggressive clinical course.

Current management targets the endothelin, nitric oxide, and prostacyclin pathways, and combination therapy is standard for intermediate‐ and high‐risk pediatric PAH. Subcutaneous treprostinil provides sustained prostacyclin‐pathway stimulation without the need for a central venous catheter. Its clinical use in children has been described in single‐ and multicenter cohorts, including recent pediatric series focused on parenteral prostanoids and subcutaneous treprostinil [3, 4, 5, 6].

The remaining gap is not whether treprostinil has biological or clinical activity in pediatric PAH. Rather, the gap is regional and descriptive. Published experience with parenteral prostanoid therapy in Latin American pediatric populations is scarce, and regional PAH literature continues to emphasize limited epidemiological data, differences in age and comorbidity profiles, and incomplete information on treatment implementation [7, 8]. Colombian reports describe PAH burden but provide little detail on long‐term parenteral prostacyclin strategies in children [9].

Structured patient‐ and caregiver‐reported domains may add clinically relevant context to conventional endpoints, particularly for schooling, physical activity, perceived well‐being, and hospitalization burden. These domains do not replace validated health‐related quality‐of‐life (HRQoL) instruments, but they may help describe how children and families experience treatment in routine care.

We conducted a retrospective cohort study at a specialized Colombian referral center to describe longitudinal clinical, echocardiographic, biomarker, survival, pediatric risk‐transition, tolerability, and structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported trajectories in children treated with subcutaneous treprostinil. Dose‐stratified and duration‐stratified comparisons were exploratory and were intended to generate hypotheses rather than define dosing recommendations.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

We performed a retrospective cohort study at a tertiary pediatric pulmonary hypertension program in a single Latin American referral center in Colombia. The cohort included all consecutive eligible children cared for in the program who initiated continuous subcutaneous treprostinil as part of PAH‐targeted therapy. Patients were followed from diagnosis until death or last known contact. Data were extracted from the electronic health record and from institutional catheterization and echocardiography databases.

Participants

Eligible patients were younger than 18 years at diagnosis, had confirmed precapillary pulmonary hypertension classified as World Symposium on Pulmonary Hypertension (WSPH) Group 1 PAH, and initiated continuous subcutaneous treprostinil during pediatric care at the center. Diagnosis was established using contemporary hemodynamic definitions, and etiologic classification followed the 7th WSPH clinical classification alongside the 2022 ESC/ERS pulmonary hypertension guideline framework [10, 11].

Inclusion criteria were: (1) confirmed Group 1 PAH; (2) age younger than 18 years at diagnosis; and (3) initiation of subcutaneous treprostinil as part of PAH‐targeted therapy. No minimum survival time or minimum post‐treatment follow‐up duration was required for inclusion. Follow‐up was defined as the interval from diagnosis to death or last known contact.

During the study period, 15 patients received subcutaneous treprostinil at our center. Six were 18 years of age or older at diagnosis and were not eligible for this pediatric cohort. The remaining nine pediatric patients met eligibility criteria and were included consecutively, regardless of survival time or length of available follow‐up. No formal sample size calculation was performed because of the rarity of pediatric PAH and the consecutive design.

Data Sources and Follow‐Up Schedule

Two investigators abstracted data independently using a predefined codebook and entered them into a dedicated database. Data abstraction was performed between October and December 2025. All variables were checked against the source record, and discrepancies were resolved by consensus before analysis.

Clinical, laboratory, and imaging data were organized around four time points: diagnosis, 1, 3, and 5 years. For each time point, we recorded demographic data (age, sex, weight), symptoms (syncope, clinical right‐heart failure, hospitalizations), World Health Organization (WHO) functional class, N‐terminal pro–B‐type natriuretic peptide levels, echocardiographic parameters of right ventricular size and function (including tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion [TAPSE], systolic pulmonary artery pressure [sPAP] estimated from tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary artery diameter, eccentricity index, and the TAPSE/sPAP ratio), and when available, right‐heart catheterization measurements. Vital status and date of death were verified through the institutional record and follow‐up notes. Follow‐up time was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or last contact, with observed follow‐up ranging from 1 to 10 years.

Outcomes

Clinical, Echocardiographic, and Hemodynamic Outcomes

The main longitudinal outcomes were trajectories of NT‐proBNP, TAPSE, sPAP, 6‐min walk distance (6MWD), and indices of right ventricular geometry and coupling at diagnosis and at 1, 3, and 5 years. For catheterization variables, we summarized changes between diagnosis and subsequent studies when available.

Pediatric Risk Assessment

We stratified patients using a three‐tier risk model (Low, Intermediate, High) consistent with the initial evaluation criteria of the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines and the 7th WSPH consensus [10, 11]. While current guidelines recommend a four‐strata model for follow‐up assessment (subdividing the intermediate category), the retrospective granularity of our dataset restricted classification to three levels. Consequently, we consolidated the “Intermediate‐Low” and “Intermediate‐High” strata into a single “Intermediate” category for longitudinal analysis. We visualized individual risk transitions between these categories from diagnosis through Year 5 using a Sankey diagram, treating death as an absorbing state.

Structured Patient‐ and Caregiver‐Reported Domains

Structured patient‐ and caregiver‐reported domains were obtained in routine care using the program's institutional form and were abstracted retrospectively for this study. Four domains were analyzed: physical activity, hospitalizations during the year, schooling, and expectation of well‐being. Each domain had three predefined ordered response categories, with higher scores indicating a more favorable status. For analysis, these domains were treated as ordered categorical variables and summarized as the percentage of children in each category. These domains were used as structured clinical descriptors of patient and caregiver experiences rather than as a validated multidimensional HRQoL instrument.

Exposure Variables

The main exposure of interest was the maintenance subcutaneous treprostinil dose. For each child, dose strata were defined using the numeric maintenance dose (ng/kg/min) recorded in the clinical file and categorized as ≤ 60 or > 60 ng/kg/min, reflecting the dosing strategy used by the center. Therapy duration was defined as the time from treprostinil initiation to last follow‐up or death and was categorized as ≤ 3 or > 3 years for exploratory analyses of structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains. The final risk category was defined as the last available risk assessment, prioritizing the 5‐year visit when available, otherwise the 3‐year or 1‐year visit; for children who died during follow‐up, the final risk reflects the last documented assessment prior to death.

Handling of Quantitative Variables

Continuous variables were retained in their original scale for descriptive statistics and paired comparisons. Because of the small sample and skewed distributions for several measures, we summarized quantitative variables as mean and standard deviation, and additionally reported medians for key outcomes. Where continuous variables or ordered domains were categorized (treprostinil dose, therapy duration, risk strata, and structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domain levels), the choice of cutoffs followed clinical practice in the program rather than data‐driven thresholds.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted in R version 4.6.0 [12]. We used two‐sided tests and considered p < 0.05 as the threshold for statistical relevance; given the exploratory nature of this small cohort, no adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied.

For descriptive statistics, we summarized continuous variables at each time point using mean, standard deviation, and median, and categorical data using absolute and relative frequencies. The number of observations contributing to each estimate is reported in the tables to make missingness explicit.

To evaluate longitudinal change in clinical, laboratory, and imaging variables, we compared values at diagnosis with those at 1, 3, and 5 years using the Wilcoxon signed‐rank test for paired data. This method was chosen because of the small sample size and non‐normal distributions of several parameters. Tests were run separately for each time contrast; only patients with non‐missing data at both time points were included in a given comparison.

We described changes in pediatric risk category using transition tables and Sankey plots. For an analytic summary, we dichotomized risk as high versus non‐high and applied McNemar's test to paired data to assess directional change between diagnosis and each follow‐up visit.

Structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains were analyzed as three‐level categorical variables. We compared the distribution of domain categories by treprostinil dose group (≤ 60 vs. > 60 ng/kg/min) and by therapy duration (≤ 3 vs. > 3 years) using Fisher's exact test when expected cell counts were small and Chi‐square tests with simulated p values in the remaining situations. Because of the small sample size and multiple exploratory comparisons, these analyses were interpreted descriptively. Results are presented as percentages in stacked bar plots with corresponding p values in the text and tables.

Survival time was measured from diagnosis to death from any cause or censoring at last known contact. We estimated overall survival using the Kaplan–Meier method and plotted the survival curve with 95% confidence bands and a risk table showing the number of children at risk at each time point.

All analyses were performed on complete cases for the variable under study; we did not impute missing values. The amount of missing data for each variable and time point is reported in the descriptive tables. Because the number of patients was small and the number of deaths limited, we did not build multivariable time‐to‐event models.

Efforts to Address Bias

To limit selection bias, we included all consecutive eligible children treated with subcutaneous treprostinil at the center during the study period, with no exclusions based on response or survival. Information bias was addressed through standardized definitions in the abstraction codebook, independent double data entry, and reconciliation of discrepancies by consensus. Echocardiographic and hemodynamic measurements followed institutional protocols that remained stable during the study period, which helped maintain comparability over time and between patients. Residual confounding by disease severity and comorbidities could not be fully addressed due to the small sample and absence of multivariable modeling.

Results

Cohort and Follow‐Up

Nine consecutive children with Group 1 PAH who initiated subcutaneous treprostinil were included. All were followed from diagnosis until death or last known contact. Observed follow‐up ranged from 1 to 10 years, with a median follow‐up of just over 5 years. All children contributed baseline data; the number of observations at 1, 3, and 5 years decreased depending on survival and variable‐specific data availability, as detailed in the tables. Survival

Over a median follow‐up of slightly above 5 years, four deaths occurred, yielding an overall mortality of 44.4%. The Kaplan–Meier curve demonstrates a steep early hazard followed by a plateau from Year 3 onward (Figure 1). At 1 year, estimated survival was 88.9% (8 of 9 children); at 3 and 5 years, survival was 55.6% (5 of 9 children).

Change in Pediatric Risk Category

At diagnosis, eight children (88.9%) were categorized as high risk and one (11.1%) as intermediate risk according to the program's pediatric risk scheme. The Sankey diagram in Figure 2 illustrates the evolution of risk profiles over time using the original categories (High, Intermediate, Low, and Dead). Among the seven children with paired data at 1 year, 28.6% moved to a lower risk tier, 14.3% shifted upward, and 57.1% remained in the same category. By 3 years (n = 5 with paired data), 60.0% were in a lower tier than at baseline, and none had worsened; at 5 years (n = 5), 80.0% were in a more favorable category, and 20.0% remained unchanged (Table 4).

Relationship Between Treprostinil Dose and Final Risk Profile

Final risk status was examined in relation to the last documented subcutaneous treprostinil dose (≤ 60 vs. > 60 ng/kg/min). Among six children treated with doses above 60 ng/kg/min, five (83.3%) were in the intermediate‐risk category at last follow‐up, and one (16.7%) remained high risk. In contrast, all three children maintained on doses at or below 60 ng/kg/min stayed in the high‐risk category (Table 6). The distribution is depicted as a heatmap in Figure 5. No adjusted model was fitted for this comparison because of the small sample size and limited number of outcome categories.

Adverse Events

Adverse events attributed to subcutaneous treprostinil and documented in the clinical record were predominantly infusion‐site reactions, including localized pain, swelling, and inflammatory changes. Infusion‐site granuloma and traumatic hematoma were reported during therapy in routine care. Transient prostacyclin‐type symptoms—headache, facial flushing, jaw pain, and diarrhea—were described in most patients during dose titration or following site rotation and typically resolved within a few days without treatment interruption.

One child developed a localized infusion‐site infection involving an infected hematoma that required surgical drainage and antibiotic therapy. The event resolved, and the patient continued subcutaneous treprostinil without permanent discontinuation. No patient stopped therapy due to adverse events during the observation period.

Discussion

This study describes a small, consecutive cohort of children with Group 1 PAH treated with continuous subcutaneous treprostinil at a Latin American referral center. The cohort provides regional, longitudinal data on clinical trajectories, pediatric risk transitions, survival, tolerability, and structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains in a setting where published pediatric experience with parenteral prostanoid therapy remains limited. Because only nine children were included, the findings should be interpreted as a single‐center consecutive experience with the inferential limits of a small retrospective case series.

Survival in this cohort should be interpreted against the wide variation reported across contemporary pediatric PAH cohorts rather than against a single benchmark. In a three‐center comparison of incident pediatric PAH cohorts from New York, Denver, and the Netherlands, 5‐year transplant‐free survival ranged from 90% to 62%, and between‐center differences were explained by baseline disease severity and diagnostic composition rather than geography alone [1]. Other contemporary pediatric datasets also show variable long‐term outcomes, including approximately 74% 5‐year survival in REVEAL, 71.8% 5‐year survival free of adverse outcome in the incident‐only TOPP analysis, 74.9% 5‐year survival in a contemporary Chinese national registry, and 89% and 85% 1‐ and 3‐year survival, respectively, among children with PAH in the Spanish pediatric registry [2, 13, 14, 15]. The present cohort had a high‐risk baseline profile and included only nine patients. Its 5‐year survival estimate, therefore, has limited precision and should be interpreted as part of the lower range reported across contemporary referral‐center cohorts, not as evidence of a distinct regional survival pattern.

Improvement in the pediatric risk category was one of the more coherent patterns in the cohort. By Year 3, none of the children with paired data had worsened relative to baseline, and most had moved to a lower‐risk tier. These transitions were supported by reductions in eccentricity index and by directional improvements in TAPSE and the TAPSE/sPAP ratio, findings that are clinically consistent with improved right ventricular geometry and coupling. The biomarker pattern was less clear. NT‐proBNP declined numerically, but paired comparisons did not meet the prespecified threshold for statistical evidence, and the small number of observations limits interpretation.

Children receiving maintenance doses > 60 ng/kg/min more often had a more favorable final risk profile. This descriptive pattern is consistent with pediatric prostanoid literature in which earlier initiation, combination therapy, and higher achieved doses have been associated with better outcomes in selected children [3, 4]. The finding does not establish a causal dose‐response effect. In this retrospective cohort, dose reflected clinical titration rather than random assignment, and the comparison is vulnerable to confounding by indication, baseline severity, survival, treatment duration, and clinician‐driven escalation.

The structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains provided a pragmatic view of daily functioning and family experiences. These domains were recorded during routine institutional care and abstracted retrospectively; they were not collected through the prospective administration of a validated multidimensional HRQoL instrument, such as the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory. Prospective pediatric PH studies using validated instruments have shown reduced HRQoL across physical, emotional, social, and school domains, and more recent work has linked physical HRQoL with disease severity and survival outcomes [16, 17]. In the present cohort, schooling was the only domain with a statistically detectable association across both dose and duration stratifications. The broader domain patterns are best interpreted descriptively because small cell counts, survival effects, and treatment escalation can distort apparent associations.

The Latin American referral‐center setting is clinically relevant, although the available data do not allow causal attribution to regional factors. Prior regional literature describes limited PAH epidemiological data, younger age profiles, and differences in comorbidity patterns compared with many contemporary North American and European cohorts [7, 8, 9]. Real‐world Latin American evidence on subcutaneous treprostinil remains limited and is largely adult rather than pediatric [18]. Factors such as delayed referral, distance from specialized care, continuity of prostanoid supply, storage and temperature conditions, family training for home infusion, access to advanced therapies, and genetic predisposition may influence implementation or outcomes, but these variables were not systematically measured in this cohort. Pediatric registry data indicate that genetic background can influence phenotype and outcomes in PAH, supporting the need to interpret regional differences with attention to both biological and health‐system factors [19].

This study has limitations inherent to its design and size. The cohort included only nine children from a single referral center, which limits precision and makes the analysis closer in interpretation to a consecutive retrospective case series despite its cohort structure. Missing data at later time points, particularly for TAPSE at 3 years and for catheterization variables, restrict the interpretation of longitudinal trajectories. Survival estimates are imprecise because four deaths occurred in a very small denominator. Dose comparisons are vulnerable to confounding by indication, survival, baseline severity, and treatment duration, and no multivariable adjustment was possible. The structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains were not collected with a validated multidimensional HRQoL instrument, and their retrospective abstraction limits comparability with prospective PROM studies. The study also did not measure health‐system variables such as authorization delays, supply interruptions, distance from the referral center, or caregiver training burden, so these factors can only be discussed as contextual considerations rather than explanations for the observed outcomes. Genetic findings were abstracted from clinical records; one reported TOPBP1 variant should be interpreted cautiously because current gene‐disease validity assessments do not classify TOPBP1 as an established PAH‐causal gene [20].

Overall, this cohort provides a regional longitudinal description of children treated with subcutaneous treprostinil at a Latin American pediatric PAH referral center. The observed course combined lower‐risk categories among children with paired follow‐up data, stabilization of right ventricular geometry, and heterogeneous, structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domain patterns. Survival fell within the lower range reported across contemporary pediatric PAH cohorts, but the small sample size, high‐risk baseline profile, and absence of adjustment preclude direct inference about regional differences.

These findings should be read as exploratory. Future multicenter pediatric cohorts from Latin America could help clarify whether referral timing, treatment access, therapy continuity, home‐infusion training, or patient characteristics influence long‐term outcomes in children receiving parenteral prostanoid therapy.

Conclusions

In this small consecutive cohort from a Latin American pediatric PAH referral center, subcutaneous treprostinil treatment was associated with lower risk categories and stabilization of right ventricular geometry among children with available follow‐up data. Structured patient‐/caregiver‐reported domains added descriptive information on schooling, physical activity, hospitalizations, and perceived well‐being, but they should not be interpreted as formal, validated PROM outcomes.

The association between higher maintenance dose and final risk profile was descriptive and does not support dosing recommendations from these data alone. Larger multicenter pediatric studies are needed to define long‐term outcomes and patient‐centered trajectories among children receiving parenteral prostanoid therapy in Latin America.

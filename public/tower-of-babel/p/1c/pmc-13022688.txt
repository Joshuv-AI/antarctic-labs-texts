ED-DETR: An edge-guided dual-branch feature optimization network for enhanced small object detection in UAV images
Authors: Jie Wu, Jinxia Yu, Qiang Li, Yongli Tang, Mingfu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryTo address low detection accuracy and poor small-object detectability in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) image object detection, this paper proposes an edge-guided dual-branch feature optimization network (ED-DETR) based on real-time detection transformer (RT-DETR). First, a dual-branch feature extraction and aggregation unit (DFEA) is designed, which extracts high-frequency texture and low-frequency structure features separately to avoid feature overlap loss, with reparameterization technology ensuring network lightweighting. Second, an edge-guided dual-branch feature extraction and aggregation (EDFEA) module is proposed by integrating an edge-guided unit into DFEA to enhance small-target detection via edge feature perception. Finally, a hybrid loss function (Mal-Shape) is constructed by combining Mal loss and ShapeIoU loss, boosting bounding box matching robustness and enabling precise small-object localization in complex scenarios. Experiments show ED-DETR outperforms the baseline by 3.7%, 1.2%, and 1.6% in mAP50 on VisDrone, RSOD, and UAVDT datasets, respectively, with comparable computational complexity, validating its effectiveness for UAV small-object detection.Subject areas: remote sensing, computer science applications

Introduction

In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely used in security monitoring, disaster rescue, agriculture inspection, and other fields due to their advantages of high mobility, low cost, and flexible deployment. In the security monitoring field,1 they can provide comprehensive, real-time monitoring without blind spots; in disaster rescue field,2 they can quickly enter disaster areas to search for trapped people; in the agricultural field,3 they can achieve precise monitoring of crop diseases and pests. However, in practical applications, it faces numerous challenges for the precise detection of small objects by UAV in complex scenarios, such as the small object pixel ratio, weak features, and vulnerability to background interference. Therefore, conducting research on UAV small object detection and enhancing the accuracy and robustness of detection algorithms are crucial for ensuring the smooth execution of tasks, which is of great practical significance for improving public safety, emergency response capabilities, and scientific research efficiency.

Currently, object detection algorithms based on deep learning can be primarily categorized into two main groups. The first group comprises convolutional neural network (CNN)-based object detection algorithms. The earliest CNN-based detector dates back to R-CNN,4 which integrated region proposals with a deep CNN and notably outperformed traditional algorithms. In recent years, object detection algorithms based on CNNs have seen further development. The YOLO5 series stands as a paradigmatic example within this category. By refining backbone architectures, innovating feature fusion mechanisms, and redesigning detection heads, these algorithms substantially enhance detection capabilities while maintaining real-time processing efficiency. However, when handling UAV images in complex scenarios with numerous small objects, these algorithms still face key bottlenecks, including inadequate use of contextual information and excessively long model processing times.

The second category includes transformer-based object detection algorithms. In contrast to CNNs, transformers demonstrate superior ability to handle global contextual information. The pioneering work is detection transformer (DETR),6 which first applies the transformer structure directly to object detection tasks and eliminates reliance on postprocessing steps via an end-to-end architecture, pioneering a new paradigm in object detection. Building on DETR, subsequent algorithms such as conditional DETR,7 DINO,8 and Dn-DETR9 have been proposed—optimizing the attention mechanism, encoder-decoder structure, and training strategies to further boost detection accuracy and efficiency, especially for complex object arrangements and small objects. Although these algorithms have made significant progress in detection accuracy, their computational complexity remains high. In contrast to traditional algorithms, the real-time detection transformer (RT-DETR)10 employs an efficient hybrid encoder design to decouple intra-scale interactions from cross-scale fusion, thereby significantly reducing computational overhead. Simultaneously, it introduces an uncertainty-minimizing query selection strategy that avoids low-confidence encoder features as object queries, ensuring high-quality initial queries are fed into the decoder to safeguard detection accuracy. However, for small-object detection in complex backgrounds, RT-DETR suffers from overlapping and the loss of high-frequency texture and low-frequency structural features during extraction, due to the limited inherent feature information of small objects. Additionally, traditional loss functions are not tailored to the characteristics of small objects, making it challenging for the model to effectively learn and localize them.

To address the aforementioned issues, we propose an edge-guided dual-branch feature optimization network based on RT-DETR (ED-DETR), aiming to effectively overcome feature miss and enhance the UAV’s ability to detect small objects. This paper makes the following contributions.

(1)A dual-branch feature extraction and aggregation unit (DFEA) is designed, which employs two distinct feature extraction branches to separately capture high-frequency texture and low-frequency structure features. Meanwhile, by utilizing reparameterization technology, DFEA retains more valid feature information while eliminating the additional computational overhead associated with complex structures.

Related works

Currently, algorithms for UAV object detection can be broadly classified into general object detection algorithms and specialized algorithms tailored for small object detection in UAV scenarios.

Advancements in general object detection algorithms

Traditional object detection algorithms based on deep learning generate candidate regions via selective search and utilize CNNs for feature extraction and classification. This approach improves the model’s ability to represent objects in complex scenarios. However, due to their two-stage architecture and high computational overhead of region proposal generation, these algorithms lack sufficient real-time inference capabilities for industrial applications. To overcome this limitation, Redmon et al.5 proposed the YOLO algorithm in 2015, transforming object detection into a single-stage end-to-end regression problem. By using fully CNNs to predict bounding boxes and classes directly, YOLO significantly improved detection speed. Building on YOLO, subsequent models11,12,13,14,15,16,17 further enhance small object detection accuracy and real-time performance through multi-scale feature fusion, lightweight backbones, and adaptive anchor mechanisms.

However, the local receptive field characteristic of conventional CNNs limits the model’s capability to capture long-range dependencies. To tackle this issue, Carion et al.6 proposed the DETR algorithm in 2020. By capturing global contextual information through self-attention mechanisms, DETR breaks through the local constraint of traditional networks. Building on DETR, subsequent algorithms have been proposed to address its inherent limitations. Deformable DETR18 introduces deformable attention to focus on task-relevant local regions, effectively reducing computational redundancy while enhancing spatial alignment. DINO-DETR8 (DINO) further elevates feature discrimination by integrating advanced dynamic label assignment strategies and cross-scale attention fusion, achieving more precise object localization. DN-DETR9 innovatively incorporates a denoising training framework for transformer encoders, improving training stability and inference speed for small and occluded objects. Nevertheless, despite these remarkable advances, small object detection remains a persistent challenge due to the overlap between high-frequency texture features of complex backgrounds and low-frequency structure features of small objects; the latter are often severely obscured by cluttered background textures. Moreover, the limited pixel coverage of small objects makes it difficult for existing models to extract discriminative features, leading to ambiguous boundary distinction between objects and non-object regions. This limitation severely limits the application of object detection algorithms in critical fields such as aerial surveillance, autonomous driving, and industrial defect inspection, where precise detection of small objects is indispensable.

Research progress on small object detection algorithms in UAV images

To improve the detection of small objects in UAV images, researchers have proposed advanced object detection algorithms. Hu et al.19 proposed CSFPR-RTDETR, a Transformer-based real-time detector integrating cross-spatial-frequency domain fusion and position relation modeling. It designs a cross-spatial-frequency hybrid (CSFH) feature-extraction network to capture global contextual information while enhancing the distinguishability of small objects against backgrounds, and a position relation decoder to strengthen spatial perception for dense aerial targets. Wang et al.20 meticulously designed downsampled residual (DsR) blocks that utilize RSConv to substantially reduce spatial redundancy in high-resolution feature maps, thereby improving feature expression precision and efficiency. Zhang et al.21 upgraded UAV-DETR, introducing a channel-aware sensing module, scale-optimized enhancement pyramid, and context-spatial alignment module to refine multi-scale feature perception, enrich shallow-layer semantics, and correct feature misalignment during cross-scale fusion—effectively adapting to UAV perspective distortion. Notably, Qin et al.22 proposed OSFormer, a one-step transformer tailored for small-object detection in UAV infrared videos. It integrates adaptive spatiotemporal feature aggregation and dynamic attention calibration mechanisms, complemented by a lightweight backbone to balance fine-grained feature retention and computational efficiency, addressing the core challenges of low contrast and motion blur in aerial video streams.

Although the aforementioned object detection algorithms have achieved good results on UAV images, they still face significant limitations in detecting small objects. CSFPR-RTDETR’s high-frequency feature fusion relies on fixed parameters, failing to adapt to dynamic scale variations of UAV targets. UAV-DETR lacks specialized optimization for dense occlusions. OSFormer’s spatiotemporal aggregation module increases latency in high-speed flight scenarios, and even Wang et al.’s DsR block design struggles to fully preserve subtle features of sub-pixel small targets. While complex network designs or multi-module integration can alleviate these issues to some extent by enhancing feature capture, their increased computational overhead conflicts with the limited hardware resources of UAV platforms. These limitations highlight that ED-DETR not only needs to fully mine key feature information of small objects, optimize scenario-adaptive loss functions for UAV-specific challenges, and improve detection accuracy, but also must innovate transformer structural pruning and dynamic attention mechanisms to control computational complexity, ensuring practical deployment in resource-constrained UAV environments.

Results

Experimental results of performance verification

To verify the performance advantages and practical value of the improved ED-DETR algorithm for small target detection from a UAV perspective, we first conduct tests using the VisDrone and RSOD datasets, which are specifically dedicated to UAV target detection. This validates its targeted optimization for small target detection under UAV scenarios. In addition, to further assess the universality and effectiveness of the improvement strategy, we transfer it to the DETR baseline model, which has a larger parameter size. We then conduct extended experiments on UAVDT, a larger-scale dataset for UAV target detection. This fully validates the method’s effectiveness and generalization ability across different models and data scales, providing a practical algorithm scheme for small target detection in UAV scenarios.

Results on the VisDrone and RSOD datasets

This study tests ED-DETR on the VisDrone dataset to confirm its performance in optimizing small object detection tasks from a UAV perspective.

As shown in Table 1., the proposed ED-DETR has the best performance in terms of detection accuracy, its mAP50:95 is 32.0%, mAPs50:95 is 22.07%, which is significantly better than the comparison model, and mAP50 (49.9%) is significantly improved compared with the baseline model YOLOv8m (42.3%) and RT-DETR-r18 (46.2%), indicating that it has significant advantages in multi-scale object detection and small object feature capture and localization capabilities. In terms of model parameters and computational efficiency, the ED-DETR parameter scale is 20.1M, which is similar to YOLOv9m (20.17M) and UAV-DETR (20.3M), but it achieves a breakthrough in accuracy; although YOLOv10m achieves lightweight with a minimum parameter amount of 20.14M, its mAP50:95 is only 27.27%, showing the trade-off between lightweight design and detection accuracy; PPYOLOE-l achieves mAP50:95 31.6% performance at the expense of 53.26M parameters and 116G FLOPs, higher computing costs may limit its timeliness. FPS (frames per second) reflects the inference real-time performance. ED-DETR achieves an FPS of 51—lower than that of lightweight YOLO models, such as YOLOv10m (79 FPS) and YOLOv12m (82 FPS). However, ED-DETR’s FPS outperforms UAV-DETR’s 49, and it is close to the real-time performance of RT-DETR-r18 (55 FPS) and PPYOLOE-l (60 FPS). This means ED-DETR can meet basic real-time detection requirements in UAV scenarios while also gaining obvious accuracy advantages. Compared with the mainstream models, YOLOv11m in the YOLO series achieved mAP50 44.4% performance with 20.15M parameters, reflecting the effective improvement of detection accuracy by architecture optimization; RT-DETR-r18, as a transformer-based detection model, has mAP50:95 (29.03%) better than most YOLO models, but still lower than ED-DETR, highlighting the further improvement of detection performance by this method after fusing advanced architecture and optimization strategies. In summary, ED-DETR performs well in striking a balance between detection accuracy and model efficiency, verifying its effectiveness and technological advancement in small object detection for UAV scenarios.

Discussion

This study proposes a UAV aerial small-target detector named ED-DETR, which is specifically designed for resource-constrained UAV aerial photography scenarios. Experimental results confirm its multiple innovative contributions to advancing the state of the art: although the absolute improvement margin is limited, ED-DETR increases the mAP50 metric by 3.75% while maintaining an unchanged parameter scale, optimizing the precision-efficiency trade-off for edge deployment scenarios. Visualization results (Figure 2 and Figure 3) show that compared with the baseline model, ED-DETR achieves higher detection accuracy for targets—especially small targets—across different scenarios, with lower probabilities of missed detections and false detections.

It is worth exploring that the core design philosophy and modular architecture of ED-DETR offer important possibilities for its cross-scenario migration to satellite remote sensing image detection tasks, which also constitutes a highly valuable research extension direction of the method proposed in this study. Satellite remote sensing images (including infrared and synthetic aperture radar [SAR] images) serve as the core data support for fields such as global resource monitoring, aerospace reconnaissance, and military target recognition, yet small object detection in such images is confronted with unique challenges: infrared satellite images generally suffer from a lack of texture details and insufficient spatial information, while SAR satellite images are affected by the discrete scattering characteristics of targets and severe speckle noise interference. Moreover, compared with UAV aerial photography targets, targets such as aircraft and small ground features in satellite images have a smaller pixel ratio, are subject to more complex background interference, and exhibit more drastic scale variations. For instance, PIFRNet32 proposed by relevant scholars for the inherent characteristics of infrared satellite images and SCFNet33 designed specifically for the aircraft detection task in SAR satellite images both construct targeted feature extraction, reconstruction and optimization frameworks centering on the intrinsic properties of different modal satellite remote sensing images, thus providing tailored solutions to the intractable problems of small object detection in such scenarios. The accumulated design insights for satellite remote sensing image detection from these two methods also furnish an important technical reference for the cross-modal migration of ED-DETR to infrared and SAR satellite remote sensing image detection tasks, thereby creating a tangible exploration space for the integration of ED-DETR’s core designs—such as dual-branch feature optimization and edge-guided perception—with satellite remote sensing image detection tasks.

Overall, the exploration of the migration potential of ED-DETR for satellite remote sensing image detection tasks and the analysis of its fusion strategies with classic satellite remote sensing image detection methods such as PIFRNet and SCFNet not only chart a course for expanding ED-DETR’s application scenarios, but also provide cross-modal and cross-scenario reference insights for the design of small object detection algorithms for satellite remote sensing images. This is expected to drive the synergistic development of remote sensing image small object detection technology in the fields of UAV and satellite observation.

Limitations of the study

Although ED-DETR has attained considerable advancements in the task of small object detection for UAV aerial imagery, there remains substantial scope for its further optimization. The current research only focuses on optimizing the feature extraction stage and has not developed specialized designs for feature discrimination and screening mechanisms of small targets in the high-recall range. As shown in Figure 7, when the recall value falls within 0.6–0.85, ED-DETR’s detection accuracy on the RSOD small-target dataset is significantly lower than RT-DETR’s—this range is exactly the core scenario for UAV aerial small-target detection, which demands balancing high recall (covering more targets) and high precision (ensuring accuracy). To explore this degradation, we visualized ED-DETR’s attention weight heatmaps in this recall range (Figure 8): as recall rises from 0.6 to 0.85, attention shifts from being concentrated on small target regions (recall = 0.6) to spreading to surrounding backgrounds, with target weight diluted by clutter and inter-target attention boundaries blurring at recall = 0.85. This reveals that ED-DETR lacks a specialized feature discrimination mechanism for high-recall scenarios, where expanding target coverage excessively incorporates background noise, reducing accuracy; future work can address this via a recall-adaptive attention screening module to dynamically focus on small target core features while suppressing background interference.

Additionally, while we have demonstrated the performance of ED-DETR under different lighting conditions in Figure 2, this is not sufficient to reflect its robustness in more complex and extreme environmentally disturbed scenarios. To address this, we selected three more challenging scenarios—motion blur, haze, and rain-fog—for testing (as shown in Figure 9). It can be seen that ED-DETR outperforms comparative models like RT-DETR and UAV-DETR in terms of detection accuracy and performance in these three scenarios, yet it still has noticeable limitations. Specifically, in motion blur scenarios, fast-moving vehicles with severely blurred edges cannot be recognized at all; in haze scenarios, small targets in the distance obscured by light fog are prone to being missed; in rain-fog scenarios, densely overlapping vehicle targets have blurred boundary distinction. This also indicates that pure algorithm optimization has inherent limitations of unimodal visual features. Complex environments directly weaken the distinguishability of target features, and it is difficult to fully compensate for this feature loss by only optimizing feature extraction or attention mechanisms. Therefore, in future research, we can further improve the model’s target recognition and differentiation capabilities in extreme interference scenarios by fusing multimodal perception information such as infrared and millimeter-wave radar, or by designing environment-adaptive feature enhancement modules.

Method details

The overall architecture of ED-DETR

The overall architecture of ED-DETR, as shown in Figure 10, is designed around the collaborative operation of multiple modules, establishing a complete process from image input to detection result output. It takes an image of size 640× 640 as input; it first passes it through the EDFEANet. It performs gradual feature downsampling across four stages, from Stage 1 to Stage 4. Each stage integrates a CBS module, and some stages also incorporate an EDFEA Block. After stacking multiple EDFEA modules and processing features via the CBS module for concatenation, it completes feature extraction and fusion. Subsequently, the features produced by the EDFEANet are fed into the Hybrid Encoder. Different levels of features are processed using the Multi-Head Attention mechanism and then deeply fused by the CCFF module, thereby comprehensively capturing global and local image information and providing sufficient feature support for subsequent decoding. Finally, the features enter the Decoder Head. After being decoded by the Decoder object, prediction is completed in combination with the Mal-Shape loss function, and the final detection results are output.

The uniqueness of the DFEA method

Extracting high-frequency texture features and low-frequency structural features through different methods is common in detection and segmentation tasks. Examples include multi-scale pyramids, frequency-domain filtering, and dual-branch attention mechanisms. However, the design concept of the DFEA unit in this paper differs significantly from those of existing methods, with distinct structural and functional features.

First existing multi-scale pyramid-based methods preserve high/low-frequency features only indirectly via cross-scale fusion without explicit separation. In contrast, the DFEA unit adopts a task-oriented dual-branch design: the LS unit captures low-frequency structural features through a space-channel collaborative mechanism while the HT unit uses a learnable symmetric high-pass filter kernel to adaptively extract high-frequency texture features from small UAV targets. This explicit targeted separation avoids feature aliasing caused by implicit fusion in multi-scale methods.

Second, fixed-frequency-domain filtering methods rely on predefined kernels and universal rules regardless of task characteristics. By contrast, the HT unit’s filter kernel (Equation 9) in DFEA is dynamically learned during training, enabling adaptive matching to small UAV object characteristics. Unlike common dual-branch attention methods that simply concatenate branch features, the DFEA unit introduces 1× 1 convolution for channel-level interaction (Equation 11) to strengthen the correlation between separated high-frequency texture and low-frequency structural features.

Third, existing dual-branch and frequency-domain methods often suffer from efficiency bottlenecks due to increased computational overhead. To resolve this, the DFEA unit uses reparameterization, aggregating dual-branch weights into a single 3× 3 convolution during inference. This design retains the dual-branch feature-extraction advantage while eliminating additional computational costs, making it ideal for lightweight UAV platform deployment.

In summary, the DFEA unit achieves three core breakthroughs: explicit and targeted separation of high/low-frequency features, dynamic adaptation to UAV small target characteristics, and lightweight design without compromising performance. These innovations collectively address the key limitations of existing methods, providing a more efficient and reliable feature-extraction solution for UAV small-object detection tasks.

To optimize the prediction of low-quality bounding boxes, Huang et al.39 proposed the Mal loss function in DEIM. However, the Mal loss function ignores the inherent properties of anchors, such as their shape and angle, which affect the detection of small objects. Shape-IoU introduces a scaling factor by adding new geometric constraints on top of IoU. It focuses on calculating the loss for the scale of the anchor itself, enabling the model better to learn the feature correlations in object detection tasks and quickly converge to an optimal solution.

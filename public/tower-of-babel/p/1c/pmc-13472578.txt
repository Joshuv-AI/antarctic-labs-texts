Identification and Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing Performance for Short Incubation of Gram-Negative Pathogens from Bloodstream Infections at a Tertiary Care Center in Telangana
Authors: Neelima Sudharshan, Sayeeda Munawar, MVNL Ram Mohan, SD Fatima Tasneem, C Padmasri, P Umabala, C Sudharshan Raj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and Objectives:Blood culture is the “gold standard” for diagnosing bacteremia. This study evaluates the performance of shortened incubation cultures in pathogen identification (ID) and antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) using the VITEK®2 system.Material and Methodology:A prospective study was conducted from November 2021 to October 2022, focusing Gram-negative pathogens. Flagged blood-culture bottles subjected to Gram-stain and simultaneous microbial workup by conventional 24-h protocol and faster 3–4 h and 7–8-h methods.Results:The data analysis encompassed 207 Gram-negative pathogens, Escherichia coli being the most prevalent isolate at 39.6%. Overall essential-agreement rates were 95.7% and 95.0%, and categorical-agreement rates were 96.2% and 96.0% for the respective incubation periods.Conclusions:Rapid pathogen ID and AST of positive blood cultures using shorter incubation times on solid media can significantly reduce turnaround time.

INTRODUCTION

Sepsis poses a global health challenge, and its incidence has been on the rise in recent years, contributing significantly to morbidity and mortality. Blood culture remains the most reliable method for diagnosing sepsis. The healthcare system is also heavily burdened by bloodstream infections, mostly as a result of longer hospital stays and higher antibiotic usage in bacteremia patients. Rapid identification (ID) of the causal agent is of utmost importance for tailored therapy and improved patient outcomes.[123] When antimicrobial therapy is initiated within an hour of diagnosis, survival chances are close to 80%, but this percentage reduces by 7.6% for each subsequent hour.[456] As a result, over the past few years, a surge of quick in-house methods has been published.

Although not antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST), Machen A et al. (2013) reported a lab-developed methodology for quick blood culture ID that uses solid media for a brief incubation time before MALDI-TOF MS ID.[7] Therefore, the primary objective of the study was to evaluate the performance and clinical utility of short incubation cultures for ID and AST as compared to routine ID and AST using VITEK® 2.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A prospective, observational, single-center study was carried out over a 1-year duration, spanning from November 2021 to October 2022.

Study methods/procedures

The study was conducted at the Department of Microbiology, Nizam’s Institute of Medical Sciences, and was initiated following clearance from the institute’s ethics committee. Given that the study used samples submitted to the laboratory, patient consent was not required. Multiple samples from the same patient during a single hospital stay, instances of polymicrobial growth, obligate anerobic growth, yeast growth, Gram-positive growth, blood cultures flagged as positive between 12:01 PM and 7:59 PM, and isolates categorized as contaminants in accordance with the laboratory’s standard care procedures.

Blood cultures were incubated using BACT/ALERT® 3D (bioMérieux) with FA plus blood culture bottles for a maximum of 5 days. When these bottles flagged positive, they were subjected to a Gram stain and were simultaneously analyzed using two distinct laboratory protocols, the conventional method and the rapid ID and AST protocol.

To evaluate the ID outcomes, results obtained from both rapid and conventional protocols were compared, specifically at the genus and species levels. Statistical analyses were conducted utilizing SPSS software (version 25, IBM Corporation). A P value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 207 isolates were processed in this study. When comparing the results to the standard conventional method, which involves an incubation period of 18–24 h, it was found that 96% (199 out of 207) and 97% (201 out of 207) of the isolates exhibited correct ID following shorter incubation periods of 3–4 and 7–8 h, respectively

Considering the individual antibiotics, the EA%, CA%, VMD, MD, and mD following short incubation methods at 3–4 and 7–8 h were mostly within the acceptable range as per CLSI M52 guidelines.[8] However, some exceptions observed for 3–4 h incubation were VMD rates for fosfomycin (14.3%, 2/14), ceftazidime (5.0%, 1/20), and ertapenem (3.0%, 2/66), and MD rates for ceftriaxone (12.1%, 4/33), trimethoprim sulfamethoxazole (5.9%, 6/102), cefuroxime (5.6%, 1/18), imipenem (3.5%, 4/114), and cefepime (3.1%, 2/64). Exceptions for 7–8 h incubation for VMD rate were unchanged for fosfomycin, ceftazidime, and ertapenem, and also included meropenem (3.4%, 3/88). Exceptions for 7–8 h incubation for MD rate were unchanged for trimethoprim sulfamethoxazole and cefepime, but lower for ceftriaxone (9.1%, 3/33), cefuroxime (0%, 0/18), higher for imipenem (4.4%, 5/114), and also included meropenem (5.3%, 6/114) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Rapid ID of the causative agent is of utmost importance for targeted therapy and enhancing patient outcomes. Traditionally, the turnaround time from a positive blood culture to ID is approximately 2 days, with AST adding an extra day to the process. In recent times, more robust approaches have emerged to improve the turnaround time, which include nucleic acid amplification assays, microarrays, sequencing techniques and MALDI-TOF MS. This study aimed to assess the utility of short incubation cultures in ID and AST within the constraints of existing resources.

Bacteremia carries a significant mortality risk, with a crude mortality rate of 24% and an attributed mortality rate of 17%.[89]

The limitations of this study were Gram-positive bacteria were not included in the study.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, short-term incubation of cultures provides a significant advantage over conventional techniques by reducing the turnaround time for pathogen ID and AST.

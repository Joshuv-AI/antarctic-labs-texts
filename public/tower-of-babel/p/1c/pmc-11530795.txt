Revolutionizing efficiency: The path to sustainable oilfield services in Indonesia's energy landscape – The case of Pertamina drilling services
Authors: Firmansyah Arifin, Sudarso Kaderi Wiryono, Sylviana Maya Damayanti, Gatot Yudoko
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study explores strategic and technological approaches for sustainable oil production in Indonesia, focusing on policy frameworks, eco-innovation, and supply chain resilience to align with national and global standards. It also analyzes the organizational transition of Pertamina Drilling Service Indonesia (PDSI) to better navigate industry challenges with the outcome of a framework sustainable practices.Design/methodology/approachUtilizing Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) to analyze primary qualitative data, the research examines PDSI's adaptation to depict the current business environment and challenges both domestically and globally, addressing industry demands. It focuses on operational objectives, strategic asset management, regulatory compliance, and market positioning. SSM offers a comprehensive framework for understanding and modeling the organizational changes within PDSI.FindingsThe transition to a functional organizational structure has led to enhanced operational efficiency, clearer role definition, and improved employee retention and development strategies at PDSI. The study also highlights the industry's challenges, including high risks, capital intensity, environmental concerns, and fluctuating oil prices, and how PDSI is strategically responding to these challenges.Research limitations/implicationsThe study faces limitations due to reliance on specific data sources and the inherent complexity of the oil and gas sector. However, it offers valuable insights into the dynamics of organizational change in a high-risk industry.Practical implicationsThe findings provide a roadmap for PDSI in addressing operational efficiency, human resource management, and organizational agility. This includes aligning business expansion with environmental sustainability and enhancing competitiveness through technological innovation.Social implicationsBy focusing on strategic employee development and retention, the study underscores the importance of human capital in the oil and gas industry, impacting social aspects of business operations.Originality/valueThis research contributes to the understanding of organizational transitions in the oil and gas sector. It offers a unique perspective on how PDSI can achieve sustainable growth and industry leadership through a functional organizational structure, underlined by a comprehensive framework emphasizing efficiency, adaptability, and continuous improvement.

Introduction

Kasriel & Wood [1] classify resource uncertainty in the upstream petroleum industry into two types: discrete uncertainty about the presence of oil and gas in a drilling prospect, and continuous uncertainty about the potential volume of resources, which typically follows a lognormal distribution. Optimizing the oil field services sector, including onshore and offshore operations, requires new business models and strategies. This is exemplified by the all-sea development model, extensively used in offshore oil exploration, encompassing activities like drilling, completion, production, processing, storage, and export, all conducted offshore (Y. [2]). This model is similar to a Floating Production, Storage, and Offloading (FPSO) vessel [3]. Offshore platforms integrate various systems, such as processing, storage, transportation, mooring, undersea, unloading, power supply, heating, command, and living systems, with energy consumption reaching tens of megawatts due to their complexity ([4]; Allahyarzadeh-Bidgoli et al., 2018). This high energy consumption contributes to a substantial emission of carbon [5].

The oilfield industry addresses environmental concerns through a multi-faceted approach to sustainability, including using cleaner energy sources like natural gas and renewable energy, reducing methane emissions, and employing efficient carbon capture and storage (CCS) methods (X. [6]). The industry faces challenges in social, economic, and environmental domains, emphasizing the need for sustainable development to balance economic progress and ecological responsibility. Exploring alternatives like natural hydrogen extraction, offshore wind power, and biorefineries is crucial [7,8].

Indonesia's oilfield services industry significantly contributes to the country's economy, supporting upstream oil activities and generating substantial government revenue through both tax and non-tax sources [10]. The Indonesian Ministry of Finance reported that in 2020, the oilfield sector accounted for 4.75 % of national tax revenue and 18.11 % of non-tax state revenue, with industry procurement totaling $3 billion. However, the industry faces a critical moment, needing to transition to renewable energy sources and reduce carbon emissions, aligning with global energy trends and sustainability goals [11].

The Indonesian oilfield sector encounters significant challenges in achieving sustainable practices. Declining oil production has increased dependence on crude oil imports, threatening energy security and highlighting the need for enhanced extraction measures [12]. The sector's operations contribute to environmental issues, including air and water pollution and soil degradation. Eco-innovative practices can mitigate these challenges. The biodiesel sector, vital to Indonesia's energy portfolio, faces decision-making and supply chain disruptions [13]. Building resilience in the supply chain is crucial for uninterrupted operations. Addressing declining oil output, environmental issues, and supply chain unpredictability is essential for the industry's sustainability and alignment with global environmental goals [14,15].

Existing literature on the oilfield industry provides insights into economic dynamics, technological needs, and operational risks but falls short in integrating these aspects with sustainability practices. Key studies highlight uncertainties in resource exploration [1], industry complexities [16] vulnerability to volatile oil prices [17] and financial strains of costly drilling operations [18] underscores the financial strains of increasingly costly and technologically demanding drilling operations. However, these studies often overlook how the industry adapts strategies to incorporate sustainability, despite its recognized importance [19,20].

Earlier literature emphasizes aligning business strategies with internal capabilities and market forces [21,22], but lacks exploration of aligning strategic goals with environmental sustainability. This aspect is critical for transitioning to greener energy sources, remaining an under-researched area in the quest for competitive advantage in a rapidly evolving energy landscape. Research is needed on how drilling companies navigate the interplay between economic performance and environmental stewardship and the industry's role in the broader sustainable energy transition.

Furthermore, while there is acknowledgment of the declining oil production and its repercussions on energy security, the literature lacks a thorough exploration of sustainable, strategic, and technological solutions to counter this trend. Environmental issues like pollution and soil degradation are identified, but there is a noticeable absence of extensive research on implementing eco-innovative practices within the industry. The challenges in these biodiesel sector's supply chain are recognized, yet detailed research on fostering resilience in these chains is scarce. This highlights the need for comprehensive studies that not only tackle these individual challenges but also examine their interconnected impacts on the industry's progression towards sustainability. Such research would be crucial in directing the industry towards practices that align with both national energy objectives and global environmental standards.

Sustainable performance in organizations emerges as a complex concept where interdependent elements must synergize to create lasting value. Kaplan & Norton [23] multidimensional view emphasizes the integration of various perspectives—financial, customer, process, and people—within a strategic framework to drive organizational success. Complementing this, Othman et al. [24] validate through structural equation modeling that a comprehensive Total Quality Management (TQM) system, encompassing all stakeholders and embedding Quality Management Practices (QMPs), significantly influences sustainable competitive advantage, affecting both non-financial and financial aspects of an organization. Hax and Majluf [21] further argues for the incorporation of broader managerial constructs—organizational structure, business processes, control systems, and culture—as vital enablers of strategy and, consequently, sustainable performance.

On the operational front, Chowdhury (2016) provides insights into the upstream oil and gas industry, advocating for strategic optimization and continual business improvement as essentials for sustainability. He suggests that cost optimization, resource management, and innovative thinking are pivotal in maintaining a competitive edge. While the conventional focus of manufacturing operations has been on operational performance measurement [25,26], there is growing pressure from stakeholders improve non-economic aspects, such as social and environmental impacts [27]. This approach, referred to as the Triple Bottom Line (TBL), underscores the need for companies to balance economic, environmental, and social dimensions [28]. Jiang et al. [29], for instance, have introduced a three-dimensional sustainability assessment model encompassing economic, environmental, and social factors to evaluate corporate sustainable performance.

The literature collectively suggests that achieving sustainable performance transcends beyond mere adherence to strategic initiatives; it involves a holistic adoption of quality practices, structural alignments, cultural adherence, and optimization techniques. This intricate dance between internal and external variables, informed by historical business environments as proposed in the Generic Business Sustainability Model, forms the bedrock upon which organizations can build and sustain performance in a competitive, ever-evolving marketplace.

This study aims to fill research gaps in the Indonesian oilfield industry by investigating strategic and technological approaches to improve oil production sustainably. It focuses on innovative sustainable policy frameworks, the integration of eco-innovation practices, and examines supply chain resilience in the biodiesel industry along with the incorporation of environmental sustainability principles in operational approaches. This comprehensive analysis seeks to align the industry with national energy goals and global environmental standards, addressing current literature deficiencies and guiding the adoption of sustainable practices. The ultimate outcome is to develop a framework of sustainable practices for Indonesia's oil and gas field service industry, with a specific focus on the strategic transition of Pertamina Drilling Service Indonesia (PDSI) from a structural to a functional organizational framework, crucial for navigating the complex challenges of the oil and gas sector.

Similarly, Pusavec et al. [31] advocate for sustainable machining technologies, highlighting their eco-friendly alternatives to conventional methods and their economic and social benefits.

Overview of themes and context within related works.

Research on robust and sustainable supply chains has also advanced significantly. Hombach et al. [32] developed a multi-objective approach to achieve sustainable biofuel supply chains under market uncertainties and different risk attitudes, balancing ecological, social, and economic objectives. Zavala-Alcívar et al. [33] proposed a conceptual framework for managing resilience and sustainability in supply chains, integrating economic, environmental, and social dimensions into strategic objectives. Negri et al. [34] found that while sustainable supply chains are well-developed, supply chain resilience is less mature, with confusion and incoherence in implementation practices. In the context of biodiesel supply chains, Mohtashami et al. [35] highlighted Iran's potential for biodiesel production, designing a two-stage multi-objective supply chain model focusing on cost minimization, social benefits, and environmental impacts. Agustina et al. [36] examined biodiesel supply chain resilience in Indonesia, identifying improvements through flexibility in sourcing, order fulfillment, capacity, adaptability, visibility, and collaboration, reducing vulnerability factors.

Corporate sustainability and performance assessment have been extensively studied [37]. proposed a performance assessment framework for offshore platforms in Asia, introducing twelve strategic indicators for evaluating sustainability. Vivas et al. [38] analyzed sustainability performance in the Brazilian oil and gas industry, identifying the best and worst years for sustainability performance. Orazalin & Mahmood [39] demonstrated that improved sustainability performance indicators lead to better financial stability in the Russian oil and gas industry. Keeble et al. [40] emphasized the importance of indicators for measuring sustainability performance, highlighting their utility as powerful tools for assessment and improvement.

Integrating sustainability reporting into enterprise risk management (ERM) and understanding the impact of external factors on sustainability performance have been critical areas of research. Shad et al. [41] discussed how integrating sustainability reporting into ERM positively impacts business performance, while Ramezankhani et al. [42] examined the significant effects of external factors such as market fluctuations and regulatory shifts on sustainability outcomes.

In the domain of theoretical and conceptual frameworks, Said et al. [43] emphasized the need for theoretical development tailored to the drilling sector, advocating for frameworks that encompass both internal and external variables to provide a robust assessment of sustainability performance. Mohammadpoor & Torabi (2020) also highlighted the impact of external factors on sustainability performance, suggesting that incorporating these variables could lead to a more comprehensive understanding and assessment.

The literature often decouples sustainability from performance metrics, suggesting a significant research opportunity to devise integrated measurement tools that encapsulate both aspects in the drilling context. This approach is pivotal for capturing the delicate balance between maintaining profitability and adhering to sustainable practices. Lastly, the existing frameworks and analytical tools predominantly evaluate internal company metrics without considering the broader systemic interactions. Enhancing these tools to encompass both internal and external variables could lead to a more robust assessment of sustainability performance, particularly in the drilling industry where such externalities are paramount. Addressing these research gaps will not only advance the theoretical landscape but also offer practical insights for the drilling industry to navigate the complex terrain of sustainable performance.

This research distinguishes itself from previous studies through its concentrated focus on the Indonesian oilfield services sector, specifically Pertamina Drilling Service Indonesia (PDSI), the largest oilfield services provider in Southeast Asia. Unlike broader frameworks examined in prior research (e.g., Ref. [30]; Orazalin et al., 2019), this study offers a targeted analysis of Indonesia’s unique energy landscape and the pivotal role PDSI plays within it. By addressing the strategic transition of PDSI toward sustainable practices, the research contributes valuable insights into how large state-owned enterprises can balance economic growth with environmental stewardship. This focus on PDSI and the Indonesian context is a significant departure from existing studies that often emphasize sustainability within more general, international contexts, providing a localized perspective that enhances both theoretical understanding and practical application in Southeast Asia.

Unlike previous works that focus on isolated aspects of sustainability—such as corporate strategies [21], technological innovations [44], or supply chain management [33]—this research adopts a holistic approach, recognizing that achieving true sustainability requires understanding how these elements interact. By combining strategic, technological, and operational perspectives, the study offers a more robust framework that can guide organizations like PDSI in navigating multifaceted challenges, ensuring alignment between strategic initiatives and operational realities. This comprehensive approach not only addresses gaps in existing literature but also provides practical insights directly applicable to real-world industry challenges, making the research highly relevant for both academic and industry stakeholders.

In addition, this study employs an action research approach, allowing for direct engagement with industry stakeholders and facilitating iterative problem-solving. This methodology enables researchers and practitioners to collaboratively address complex sustainability issues, particularly in Indonesia’s oilfield services sector. Furthermore, by emphasizing the resilience of Indonesia's biodiesel supply chain—an area that extends beyond generalized supply chain sustainability discussions [33,34]—this research contributes to both academic literature and industry practices, particularly within Southeast Asia’s evolving energy landscape.

Institutional context

This study focuses on doing a comprehensive analysis to one of PT Pertamina (Persero) business unit namely Pertamina Drilling Services Indonesia (PDSI), a prominent state-owned firm operating in the oil and natural gas sector, with its headquarters located in Jakarta, Indonesia. Pertamina, a prominent entity in Indonesia's energy industry, emerged because of the merger between Pertamina (established in 1961) and Pertamina (originating in 1957). By the year 2020, it had achieved the status of being the third-largest producer of crude oil in the nation, being surpassed just by two prominent American corporations, namely ExxonMobil's Mobil Cepu Ltd and Chevron Pacific Indonesia. This viewpoint emphasizes the notable contribution of Pertamina in both the domestic oil and gas sector of Indonesia, as well as its important significance within the wider regional and global energy framework.

The importance of Pertamina goes beyond the national boundaries of Indonesia, as demonstrated by its participation in the 2020 Fortune Global 500 list, where it was acknowledged as the foremost corporation in Indonesia. The aforementioned differentiation highlights Pertamina's significant economic influence within the country and its noteworthy position in the global corporate landscape, namely in the energy sector. The examination of the firm's operations, tactics, and impacts is crucial for comprehending the intricacies of Indonesia's energy business, encompassing its role in and interactions with the worldwide energy market.

In the year 2020, Pertamina, a prominent entity in the Indonesian economy, maintained a workforce consisting of 13,526 individuals, thereby exemplifying its substantial influence as a big employer. The human resource component of Pertamina exemplifies the extensive scope of its operations and the wide-ranging impact it has on diverse aspects of the energy industry [45]. By conducting an analysis of Pertamina's personnel dynamics, corporate strategies, and operational techniques, valuable insights can be gained regarding the strategies employed by large state-owned firms in navigating the intricate global energy market, adapting to evolving energy demands, and contributing to the economic development of their respective nations. The thorough examination of Pertamina presented in this study is crucial for gaining insight into the complexities and obstacles encountered by prominent entities within the dynamic and ever-changing global energy sector.

Within the broader framework we can compare institutional practices and strategies across different contexts in the oil and gas sector. Insights from the selected studies highlight several key themes relevant to Pertamina’s strategic transition. For instance, Jabbour et al. [44] emphasize the complexities involved in transitioning to a sharing economy in metallic resource-intensive industries, underlining the need for significant changes in product development and the adoption of Industry 4.0 technologies, which could be informative for Pertamina’s technological advancements. Scherbenko et al. [46] demonstrate the importance of environmental monitoring in Russia’s coal industry for sustainable development, highlighting practices that could be adapted for monitoring and reducing environmental impacts in Indonesia's oil and gas sector. Mäkitie [47] provides insights into how Norwegian oil and gas firms leverage existing resources to innovate in floating wind power, despite facing challenges, offering a perspective on how Pertamina can navigate resource redeployment and innovation. Alonso-Fradejas [48] discusses the reshaping of resource property questions in climate stewardship, benefiting big businesses and conservation non-profits, which could inform Pertamina’s strategies for balancing corporate interests with environmental stewardship.

Furthermore, Schaltegger et al. [49] illustrate how transforming business models and addressing systemic risks can drive sustainability transitions, offering a roadmap for Pertamina’s business model innovation. Delmas et al. [50] emphasize the role of corporate leadership in sustainability transitions through mechanisms like political coalition building and eco-labels, providing strategies for Pertamina’s leadership in sustainability. Sun & Liao [51] describe how China’s Resource-Exhausted City Transition program stimulated capital investment and industrial development, presenting a case for similar strategic investments in Indonesia’s oil and gas regions. Magnusson & Werner [52] explore the behaviors of incumbent firms in sustainability transitions, highlighting the balance between innovating and defending, collaborating, and competing, which can guide Pertamina’s strategic planning.

Methodology

Data collection

Data collection for this study was conducted through in-depth interviews from February to May 2024. A qualitative approach was employed to gather comprehensive insights into the strategic and operational approaches for sustainable oil production in the Indonesian oilfield industry. This primary qualitative data provided insights into participants' judgments, experiences, and organizational practices.

Interviews were conducted with key stakeholders, including executives and directors from various oilfield service companies. Among the interviewees were the President Director of a leading oilfield services company in Indonesia, the Marketing & Business Development Director from a major oil and gas corporation, and the Operations Director of an oil and gas services firm. Additionally, participants included the General Manager of Operations Safety & Client Relations from an oilfield services company, the Country Director of a multinational oilfield services provider, and the Director of a prominent oilfield manufacturing company. This diverse set of interviews provided qualitative data, which were analyzed to understand the current practices, challenges, and opportunities in the Indonesian oilfield industry's transition toward sustainability.

Steps of soft system methodology.

To be more specific, Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) - Action Research (AR) or SSM-AR approach is employed to establish a course of action informed by epistemic learning, which arises from the interaction and experiential knowledge embedded at the tacit level [53]. Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) offers significant advantages over traditional SSM when integrated with action research (AR). Sankaran et al. [54] highlight that incorporating soft systems thinking within AR projects successfully implements organizational change, fostering increased understanding and collaboration with stakeholders. This structured approach allows managers to address ill-structured problems more effectively. SSM-AR's flexibility and dynamic nature effectively address complex, real-world problems, surpassing traditional SSM in facilitating meaningful organizational change.

The decision to utilize SSM-AR acknowledges the methodological difficulties in efficiently gathering implicit knowledge. SSM-AR is valued for tackling complex and unstructured problems involving human interactions and diverse perspectives. It systematically models the complex interactions within Pertamina’s operations, examining how processes, policies, and culture influence sustainability [55]. Furthermore, it establishes a connection between the information acquired through interactions with other individuals within the organisation [56]. The SSM-based approach prioritizes experiential knowledge and addresses people's interests in their experiences. "Accommodation" within SSM involves recognizing and accepting diverse perspectives based on shared understanding [53]. Checkland & Poulter [57] emphasizes the importance of immersing oneself in human scenarios, actively participating, and using firsthand experience as the research focus.

SSM-AR allows for reflection on feedback and adjusting to diverse perspectives, crucial for identifying agreeable alternative perspectives [58]. The framework facilitates examining the learning process to enhance organizational performance based on tacit knowledge and team deliberation [59]. SSM-AR enables systematic exploration of potential modifications to improve challenging circumstances. During the fifth step, a conceptual model is used for critical discussion regarding disparities between idealized models and actual reality. This model should address inquiries about responsibilities, actions, and stakeholders' perspectives on human activity with questions such as "Which individuals or entities are responsible for specific actions? In what manner are these actions carried out? When do they occur?" [58] as illustrated in Fig. 2. The initial phase of learning occurs in step 5 when connecting the conceptual model with reality. In the seventh phase, researchers engage in a subsequent learning process while implementing the action plan, potentially encountering discrepancies between planned actions and actual outcomes Uchiyama [60]. Conceptual model development

The development of the conceptual models in this study was closely integrated with the SSM-AR process. Once the relevant insights were obtained through SSM-AR, rich pictures were created to visually represent the problem situations at PDSI. These rich pictures helped to map out the interrelated factors affecting PDSI's operations, including regulatory challenges, market dynamics, and internal organizational issues [57].

Following the rich picture analysis, a CATWOE analysis (Customers, Actors, Transformation process, Worldview, Owners, and Environmental constraints) was conducted. This analysis clarified the different perspectives and identified the key components of the problem situations. Based on the CATWOE analysis, root definitions were formulated, articulating the primary objectives and desired outcomes for the various processes within the organization.

From these root definitions, conceptual models were derived. These models represented the idealized systems and processes that PDSI should implement to address its challenges. The conceptual models incorporated strategic, operational, and technological elements, providing a structured framework to guide PDSI toward improved performance and sustainability [55]. The iterative nature of SSM-AR ensured that these models were continuously refined through stakeholder feedback and aligned with the practical realities of the organization.

Result

Existing business performance

Pertamina Drilling Service Indonesia (PDSI) has strategically shifted from a structural to a functional organizational model to enhance efficiency and clarify roles in areas like operations, finance, and marketing. This shift is particularly advantageous in the oil and gas sector, where specialized, focused teams are essential due to the industry's technical demands and rapid evolution. The company’s emphasis on employee development and retention has also proven beneficial, particularly in technical and operational departments. By nurturing staff skills and career growth, PDSI enhances individual performance and overall operational efficiency, which is crucial in an industry where high turnover can impact project continuity and technical expertise. However, challenges remain in extending these benefits across the entire organization, particularly in non-technical divisions such as administrative and support roles, where higher turnover and lower engagement persist. This suggests that while the development programs are effective in certain areas, they need expansion and customization to meet the diverse needs of all employee groups, aligning these initiatives with the company’s broader strategic goals.

Navigating regulatory challenges and market dynamics has been crucial for PDSI’s performance. Johnstone & Newell (2017) emphasize the importance of understanding the state’s role in sustainability transitions, which is relevant for PDSI as a state-owned enterprise. The company’s adaptability in responding to regulatory changes and market shifts has opened new opportunities for innovative strategies and growth. Additionally, operational efficiency is directly linked to financial performance in the oilfield services sector. PDSI’s focus on resource management and operational excellence has positively impacted its financial health, helping to mitigate the effects of fluctuating market prices. Given the capital-intensive nature of the industry, effective management of operational costs remains critical in ensuring long-term financial stability and competitiveness.

Problem situation

PDSI faces complex challenges inherent to the oil and gas industry, including high-risk, high-capital operations in a dynamic, uncertain environment. Frequent safety incidents highlight the need for enhanced risk mitigation strategies, a stronger safety culture, and the adoption of advanced technologies to prevent avoidable errors. Scherbenko et al. [46] emphasize the importance of rigorous environmental monitoring in mitigating risks in high-risk industries. In addition to safety concerns, financial sustainability is a significant issue for PDSI. Fluctuating oil prices significantly impact profitability, placing companies like PDSI in a precarious position. Delayed client payments and economic downturns further strain financial health, making long-term investment planning difficult. This issue, highlighted by Delmas et al. [50], underscores the importance of corporate leadership in navigating these uncertainties through strategic coalition building and eco-innovation practices. Other studies confirm that fluctuating market conditions and financial instability are challenges faced by firms across various sectors [44,47].

Moreover, the competitive landscape in the oil and gas sector presents its own challenges. Companies are navigating markets filled with onerous terms, uncapped liabilities, and demands for intellectual property sharing. These conditions create a high-stakes environment where every contract negotiation and business decision carries significant risk. Delmas et al. [50] discuss how corporate leadership in sustainability transitions involves navigating complex business environments, which aligns with PDSI’s experiences. Furthermore, regulatory and industry dynamics in Indonesia add another layer of complexity. Legal disputes, procurement issues, and the lack of effective intermediaries between companies and regulatory bodies can stymie innovation and adaptability, similar to challenges faced globally (Tost et al., 2018). Leadership and strategic direction are crucial in steering the company through these multifaceted challenges. Effective leadership must balance business needs with the ability to adapt to changing conditions without compromising on core values like safety and quality. Dangelico et al. [61] and Bossle et al. [62] emphasize the importance of resource building, eco-design, and green innovation in boosting performance and sustainability. Only by tackling these challenges holistically can PDSI hope to achieve sustainable growth and operational efficiency in the unpredictable world of oil and gas. Root definition

The root definition is established using the PQR formula, which is based on the CATWOE analysis. The PQR formula, which reads, "do P, by Q, in order to achieve R”. The PQR answer the questions of What? How? And Why? where the transforming process is captured in Q, the declared ‘how’[58]. Conclusion

This study aimed to address the gaps in the Indonesian oilfield industry by exploring strategic and technological approaches to sustainable oil production. Focusing on innovative sustainable policy frameworks and the integration of eco-innovation practices, such as green product development and sustainable consumption, it aligns the industry with national energy goals and global environmental standards. Moreover, it examines supply chain resilience in the biodiesel industry and the incorporation of environmental sustainability principles in operational approaches, ultimately seeking to develop a sustainable framework for Indonesia's oil and gas field service industry, particularly for Pertamina Drilling Service Indonesia (PDSI) as it transitions from a structural to a functional organizational framework. The effective use of Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) and Action Research throughout this study has been pivotal in identifying and implementing these sustainable strategies, ensuring a practical and systemic approach to problem-solving.

Key areas of improvement identified include human resource strategies and organizational agility. PDSI can enhance employee satisfaction and retention through comprehensive training programs, career development plans, and fostering a culture of innovation and collaboration. Technologies such as virtual and augmented reality for immersive learning represent significant advancements in training methodologies. Organizational agility can be achieved by creating a culture that encourages swift decision-making, flexibility in roles, and proactive responses to market changes. Implementing flexible role definitions, fostering innovation through think tanks, and developing quick response teams are essential strategies. The iterative and participatory nature of SSM Action Research allowed for continuous feedback and adaptation, ensuring these strategies were tailored to PDSI's specific context and challenges.

Balancing regulatory compliance with business expansion and innovation is a complex challenge for PDSI. Developing a robust framework for risk assessment, market analysis, and strategic partnerships is critical, involving cross-functional teams for strategic decision-making, flexible risk assessment tools, and exploring growth opportunities through partnerships and acquisitions. Understanding market dynamics, diversifying the service portfolio, leveraging digital marketing, and establishing a strong brand presence are essential for enhancing PDSI's visibility and market positioning. This study provides a comprehensive framework integrating strategic, operational, and technological aspects to guide PDSI and similar companies in adopting sustainable practices, enhancing operational efficiency, and ensuring long-term growth and sustainability in a volatile industry. The application of SSM Action Research has proven to be an effective methodology in navigating these multifaceted challenges, offering a structured yet flexible approach to achieving sustainable development goals.

Limitation and further research

The analysis of PDSI's performance has certain limitations primarily due to its reliance on qualitative data, which may not fully capture the complexities and nuances of the oil and gas industry or PDSI's internal dynamics. The industry is inherently complex and rapidly evolving, with regional nuances and internal company factors, such as leadership and employee morale, posing significant challenges to obtaining a comprehensive understanding. Future research should focus on gathering more quantitative data to provide a clearer measurement of performance metrics. Additionally, it should explore the impact of technological advancements in greater depth, including how they can be effectively integrated into PDSI’s operations. Delving deeper into PDSI's organizational culture will also be crucial to understand the internal factors influencing its performance.

Moreover, analyzing the effects of global market trends and conducting longitudinal and comparative studies with global counterparts could offer deeper insights. These approaches would enable a more nuanced understanding of PDSI's strategic position and operational dynamics within the volatile oil and gas sector, thereby offering a broader perspective on sustainable practices and efficiency improvements.

BestopCloud: an integrated one-stop solution for single-cell RNA sequencing data analysis
Authors: Rufei Pan, Chao Li, Feifei Shi, Panfei Wang, Jing Liu, Tiantian Wang, Cai Lian, Wenling Zheng, Haixia Wei, Keqing Song, Jie Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSingle-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology has emerged as a powerful tool for unveiling cellular heterogeneity, yet the high dimensionality and complexity of the data it generates pose analytical challenges for non-professional programmers. To streamline the process, we have developed BestopCloud, a functionally comprehensive and user-friendly online analysis platform. Comprising nine modules, BestopCloud supports diverse parameter settings within each module and is capable of producing high-quality chart files. Furthermore, these modules can be used independently and flexibly linked through data flow. In summary, BestopCloud provides researchers with an in-depth solution for scRNA-seq data processing, analysis, and visualization.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12072-0.Keywords: Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), Online analysis platform; bioinformations, Data analysis, R shiny, Module

Background

The advent of scRNA-seq technology has revolutionized the study of cellular heterogeneity, enabling researchers to capture intricate gene expression patterns at the individual cell level [1]. This groundbreaking approach offers a novel perspective for unraveling the intricacies inherent in biological systems [2]. With the continuous advancement of single-cell sequencing technology, the volume of data generated is steadily increasing, thereby presenting greater challenges to subsequent data analysis endeavors. The complexity inherent in these datasets not only propels scientific discoveries but also necessitates researchers to employ more sophisticated analytical methodologies for processing and interpreting this wealth of information.

With the rapid advancement of bioinformatics, a series of tools for analyzing scRNA-seq data have emerged. Many of these tools significantly facilitate researchers’ efforts in data analysis due to their online accessibility and interactive functionality. However, it is important to note that despite the abundance of these tools, their functional scope remains largely limited. For instance, ShinyCell [3], cellxgene [4], scCloudMine [5], singlecellVR [6], CellexalVR [7] primarily focus on data visualization. Among them, singlecellVR and CellexalVR enable interactive visualization of cell clusters through virtual reality technology. Nevertheless, these tools require researchers to perform certain data preprocessing steps before use, such as format conversion, data clustering, and cell type annotation. Other tools, such as scX [8], scViewer [9], SCHNAPPs [10], ShIVA [11], and SEQUIN [12], offer some commonly used modules for data processing and analysis in addition to visualization capabilities. scX can carry out routine preprocessing tasks on raw matrix files, including normalization, dimensionality reduction, clustering, additionally, it supports differential expression analysis. ShIVA and SEQUIN further support functional enrichment analysis. However, the aforementioned tools lack support for cell type annotation, are not accessible online, rely on local computing resources, and necessitate the researcher to configure the R language environment for interactive analysis which poses a significant challenge for researchers lacking programming skills. Several tools such as SciAp [13], GranatumX [14], ICARUS [15], and UCSC single-cell browser [16] provide online analysis capabilities for scRNA-seq data, but they exhibit deficiencies in integrating commonly used analysis tools like data integration, cell-cell communication, and copy number analysis, which limits their comprehensiveness and scope of application. Currently, CellSnake [17] and ezSingleCell [18] are relatively comprehensive data analysis tools. Nevertheless, CellSnake does not support online usage while ezSingleCell falls short in the diversity of visualization options such as multi-gene visualization or copy number analysis, which restricts its application in deeply analyzing the impact of genomic variations on cell heterogeneity. In summary, although existing tools for scRNA-seq data analysis offer conveniences in certain aspects, there is still room for improvement in terms of functional comprehensiveness, user-friendliness, and online availability to meet the growing demands of researchers.

To address these problems, we have developed an R Shiny [19] online application named BestopCloud (http://cloud.bestopcell.com:3838/BestopCloud/). The BestopCloud application seamlessly integrates essential processes in scRNA-seq analysis, facilitating data analysis and visualization even for researchers without programming skills. During the analysis process, BestopCloud generates intermediate files (.rds files commonly used in R), which not only serve as data storage but also ensure reproducibility of the analysis and can be utilized in subsequent analysis modules. Furthermore, BestopCloud supports module connections through data flow, for instance, data after cell type annotation can be transferred to the visualization module, ensuring flexibility and efficiency in analysis. It is worth mentioning that BestopCloud is an open-source platform providing one of the most comprehensive web browser-based solutions for analyzing scRNA-seq data currently available. This powerful tool not only benefits researchers but also plays a significant role in advancing the field of single-cell research.

Results

BestopCloud workflow overview and its advantages

BestopCloud is a comprehensive one-stop scRNA-seq data analysis solution that seamlessly integrates a range of state-of-the-art analysis processes and packages. It offers researchers unrestricted access through a web browser, enabling them to effortlessly navigate from data preprocessing to in-depth biological interpretation. The analysis pipeline of BestopCloud encompasses nine core modules: Data processing, Data integration, Data viewer, Cell type annotation (SingleR [20] and ScType [21]), Differential expression analysis, Enrichment analysis, Cell-cell communication, and Copy number analysis (Fig. 1, Additional file 1: Fig. S1). These modules are meticulously designed to maintain their independence while facilitating flexible interrelationships, thereby revolutionizing the traditional linear analysis workflow.

Overview of the BestopCloud workflow. BestopCloud consists of nine modules, with each module number clearly indicated within its respective box. The data association between modules is denoted by the module numbers marked in the upper right corner of each box, signifying that the data from these modules can be directly applied to the current module

In this study, we conducted an extensive comparative analysis of BestopCloud with existing publicly available analysis tools (Table 1). The notable advantages of BestopCloud lie in its comprehensiveness, user-friendliness, and flexibility, positioning it as one of the most comprehensive open-source scRNA-seq data analysis platforms. Firstly, BestopCloud seamlessly integrates all essential steps involved in scRNA-seq data analysis, offering researchers a complete solution from data input to results output. Secondly, through a graphical user interface, BestopCloud simplifies complex data analysis tasks for researchers without programming backgrounds. The platform supports diverse parameter settings and enables flexible data flow between modules (Fig. 1), thereby improving the flexibility and efficiency of analysis. Additionally, BestopCloud emphasizes the reproducibility of analysis by generating intermediate files during the process (e.g.,.rds files in R data format) that not only serve as storage but also ensure verifiability of analysis outcomes. Lastly, as an open-source platform, BestopCloud encourages academic exchange and collaboration to collectively advance the field of single-cell research.

Functional testing and computational performance of BestopCloud modules

To verify the functional availability of each analysis module in BestopCloud, we selected publicly available scRNA-seq data from four human PBMC samples, named 3k, 5k, 10k, and 20k based on cell count (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/). Using the “Data processing” module, we performed quality control to ensure data accuracy. Then, we integrated the four samples into one dataset using “Data integration” with default settings. Subsequently, the integrated dataset was sequentially analyzed through modules including “Data viewer”, “Cell type annotation (SingleR and ScType)”, “Differential expression analysis”, “Enrichment analysis”, “Cell-cell communication”, and “Copy number analysis”, generating corresponding result files (Additional file 1: Fig. S2). The results demonstrated that all BestopCloud modules operate stably and efficiently, with complete functions and smooth operation, providing comprehensive support for scRNA-seq data analysis.

To evaluate the computational performance of BestopCloud, we conducted a preliminary assessment of the typical runtime for each analytical module using scRNA-seq data derived from four PBMC samples as described previously. We observed that processing times increased with input data volume. For instance, “Data processing” runtime grew from 26 s for 3k to 3 min 27 s for 20k. Similarly, “Copy number analysis” showed a more pronounced increasing trend, ranging from 1 min 1 s to 33 min 13 s (Additional file 1: Table S1). These results not only elucidated the platform’s computational requirements but also provided empirical evidence for its scalability, confirming its efficient operation across different data scales and offering researchers valuable insights into its performance characteristics.

Processing and integrating scRNA-seq datasets using BestopCloud

In order to elucidate the accuracy and applicability of BestopCloud for single-cell data analysis, we used the publicly available dataset GSE149655, which includes scRNA-seq data from tumor tissue and corresponding distal normal lung tissue obtained from two patients with stage IA lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) [22], resulting in a total of four samples. After independently processing the data for each sample, we further conducted a merged analysis to investigate the molecular differences between tumor and normal lung tissues.

For this study, we employed the same method that was used in a previous study for data preprocessing within the “Data processing” module of BestopCloud for each sample. Specifically, we filtered out low-quality cells exhibiting more than 15% mitochondrial gene expression and fewer than 500 detected genes while keeping all other parameters at their default settings. Following this step, we obtained information on data quality control for each sample as well as cluster analysis results (Additional file 1: Fig. S3).

Considering that four samples were derived from two patients, with each patient contributing both disease tissue and control tissue, we uploaded the patient information and sample disease status as metadata to the “Data integration” module for batch effect correction (Additional file 1: Table S2). Then, we set “Patient” and “Condition” as batch variables while keeping other parameters at their default settings. Following batch correction, we observed that the Harmony method [23] effectively mitigated the batch effect among samples without compromising the biological heterogeneity of the samples (Additional file 1: Fig. S4), thereby establishing a solid foundation for subsequent analysis.

Accurate cell type annotation using BestopCloud

When annotating the cell type of the merged scRNA-seq dataset, we initially utilized the “SingleR” module in BestopCloud for preliminary annotation using the default parameters (Fig. 2A, B). Upon completing this step, we directly applied the resulting data to the “ScType” module for more comprehensive annotation utilizing a list of classical marker genes specific to lung tissue [24, 25] (Additional file 1: Table S3). Once the annotation process is completed, we transfered the data to the “Data viewer” module for visualization.

Cell annotation results of merged data. A UMI expression profile of all cells. B Distribution of cells across four samples. C Cell type annotation results obtained using SingleR. D Cell type annotation results obtained using ScType

The results revealed that while the annotated results by SingleR were somewhat limited in granularity, it accurately identified four major cell types present in all four samples: immune cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells (Fig. 2C). In contrast, by employing the ScType method, additional cell subtypes within these major cell types were distinguished including AT2 cells in normal control tissues and AT2-tumor cells in tumor tissues (Fig. 2D).

Based on the annotation results from ScType, we further utilized stacked bar charts to visually depict the composition of cell types in each sample (Fig. 3A) and the proportion of different cell types across various tissue sources (Fig. 3B). These charts revealed the heterogeneity of cell composition between patients and different tissue types, particularly highlighting an enrichment of AT2-Tumor cells within tumor tissues. To validate the accuracy of our annotation results, we detected the expression levels of specific marker genes (Additional file 1: Fig. S5). The results showed that expression patterns of marker genes for epithelial cells (EPCAM+), fibroblasts (COL1A1+), endothelial cells (PECAM1+), immune cells (CD3E+ and CD68+), as well as subtypes of AT2 cells (SFTPB+) and AT2-tumor cells (SFTPB+/SFTPC−) exhibited expected signatures that corresponded to their respective cell types, thereby confirming the reliability of our annotated results.

Revealing gene expression differences between groups using BestopCloud

In the “Differential Expression Analysis” module, we established specific comparison groups to investigate the gene expression differences between AT2-tumor cells derived from tumor tissues and AT2 cells from normal control tissues. We employed default parameters to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and visualized the top 20 genes exhibiting the most significant expression differences between these two sample groups via a heatmap (Fig. 4A). Additionally, utilizing the violin plot in the “Data Viewer” module, we observed that there was no difference in the expression level of SFTPB gene in these two types of cells while the SFTPC, SFTPA1 and SFTPA2 genes demonstrated significantly lower expression in AT2-Tumor cells (Fig. 4B). This finding aligns with previous research [26, 27].

The expression of specific marker genes in different cell types. The expression of A the top 20 genes with the most significant expression differences and B SFTPB, SFTPC, SFTPA1 and SFTPA2 in AT2-tumor cells derived from tumor tissues and AT2 cells from normal control tissues

The identification of DEGs was performed using thresholds of |logFC| >1 and p_val_adj < 0.05. These DEGs (Additional file 1: Table S4) were then subjected to enrichment analysis using Metascape tool [28] in the “Enrichment analysis” module, focusing on GO and KEGG pathway analysis. The results indicated that these DEGs were significantly enriched in multiple biological pathways including the regulation of epithelial cell apoptosis, epithelial cell differentiation, and cell adhesion molecules (P < 0.05, Fig. 5A-C). These findings provide valuable insights into molecular changes occurring within specific cell types in the tumor microenvironment, thereby enhancing our understanding of cell heterogeneity during tumor development.

Exploring the complexity of Cell-cell communication in tumor tissues using BestopCloud

The interaction between epithelial cells and fibroblasts plays a crucial role in tumor growth, invasion, metastasis, and response to therapy [29, 30]. Cell-cell communication analysis can unveil pivotal molecules and pathways involved in signal transmission between tumor cells and fibroblasts. Based on this, we investigated the interaction relationships between AT2, AT2-Tumor cells and AF1 (Alveolar fibroblast 1), SCMF (Secondary crest myofibroblast) using the “Cell-cell communication” module. Our analysis revealed an increased number of interactions between epithelial cells, particularly AT2-Tumor cells, and SCMF cells (Fig. 6A). During tumor progression, fibroblasts may interact with epithelial cells through COLLAGEN and LAMININ pathways to facilitate tumor initiation and development (Fig. 6B).

The findings reveal the pivotal role of fibroblasts in the tumor microenvironment and elucidate the complex interactions between these cells and epithelial cells. This cell-cell communication network may offer crucial support for tumor development, while also presenting potential targets for novel therapeutic strategies. Further investigation into the specific functions of these interactive molecules and pathways in tumor progression will contribute a deeper comprehension of the complexity inherent to the tumor microenvironment, thereby providing new perspectives for tumor therapy.

Investigating tumor cell heterogeneity through CNV analysis using BestopCloud

Considering the genetic heterogeneity and the complexity of gene expression in tumor cells, the accuracy of identifying tumor cells by cell type annotation based solely on marker genes may be challenged. In contrast, Copy Number Variation (CNV) analysis can provide a more comprehensive and stable means of characterizing tumors by revealing their genetic heterogeneity. In the “Copy number analysis” module of BestopCloud, we conducted a detailed analysis of the copy number status of all cells using Case2_ade1 as our research subject (Fig. 6C). Through UMAP dimensionality reduction results, we observed that the cell population identified as diploid in this sample was basically consistent with the distribution previously determined as AT2-Tumor cells through cell type annotation (Fig. 6D). This finding further validates the accuracy of AT2-Tumor cells obtained through cell type annotation and demonstrates that CNV analysis can serve as a powerful supplement to cell type annotation, enhancing our understanding of tumor cell heterogeneity.

Discussion

The rapid advancement of scRNA-seq technology has profoundly enhanced our comprehension of cell heterogeneity. However, this progress brings about the analysis challenge of high-dimensional and complex data. Currently, various tools for scRNA-seq data analysis are available, such as ShinyCell, cellxgene, scCloudMine, each with their own strengths but often limited in terms of functional comprehensiveness, user-friendliness, and online accessibility. In contrast to these tools, BestopCloud developed in this study exhibits notable advantages in several aspects.

Firstly, regarding functional comprehensiveness, BestopCloud integrates nearly all crucial processes involved in scRNA-seq data analysis, encompassing data processing, data integration, data viewer, cell type annotation, differential expression analysis, enrichment analysis, cell-cell communication, and copy number analysis. Many existing tools tend to focus on one or a few aspects of data analysis, such as ShinyCell and cellxgene, which mainly concentrate on data visualization while lacking comprehensive data processing and analysis capabilities. The one-stop design of BestopCloud enables researchers to complete the entire workflow from raw data to in-depth analysis without switching between different tools, greatly enhancing analysis efficiency.

Secondly, in terms of user-friendliness, BestopCloud utilizes an intuitive web interface and interactive design that facilitates easy adoption for non-professional programmers. The platform provides detailed usage instructions and example datasets, assisting researchers in quickly grasping the functions and operation methods of each module. In addition, BestopCloud supports seamless data flow between modules. Researchers only need to upload data once, which can be conveniently utilized in subsequent analysis modules, avoiding repeated data uploads and downloads after a single initial upload. Comparatively, existing tools like scX and SCHNAPPs provide certain data analysis capabilities but rely on local computational resources and R environment configurations, demanding higher computer skills and programming abilities from researchers.

Furthermore, in terms of online accessibility, BestopCloud is a browser-based application that allows researchers to access and utilize it without needing to install any additional software or plug-ins. This online model not only enhances platform accessibility but also simplifies data sharing and analysis across various devices. Conversely, tools like CellSnake offer comprehensive functionality but are limited to local installation only, restricting their online availability and ease of data sharing.

Finally, BestopCloud utilizes a diverse range of advanced algorithms and tools for data processing and analysis, including the Seurat package [31] for data processing and differential expression analysis, the Harmony method for data integration, SingleR and ScType for cell type annotation, among others. The combination of these algorithms and tools endows BestopCloud with significant advantages in terms of accuracy and reliability of data analysis.

While BestopCloud offers significant advantages for scRNA-seq analysis, it faces challenges in multi-user interactions due to the inherent limitations of R Shiny’s underlying architecture. To address this problem, we have implemented solutions through three approaches: (1) Optimized R code to improve computational efficiency and reduce task execution time; (2) Utilized R packages such as promises and future to run time-consuming tasks in the background, freeing up the main thread and enhancing app responsiveness; (3) Achieved concurrent deployment via Docker and ShinyProxy, allowing multiple users to access the platform simultaneously.

In addition, to ensure that BestopCloud continues to evolve with the latest advancements in scRNA-seq analysis, the platform is designed with a standardized modular framework and extension interfaces to facilitate the integration of new modules or analytical methods. Bioinformatics analysts can format and implement new methods to ensure they perform basic module functions. These new methods can then be connected to the Shiny visualization interface via the platform’s interfaces, allowing for seamless embedding of emerging methodologies. This design ensures that BestopCloud remains at the forefront of scRNA-seq analysis, capable of incorporating advancements as they develop.

Conclusions

In summary, as an efficient and easy-to-use scRNA-seq data analysis platform, BestopCloud exhibits significant advantages in functional comprehensiveness, user-friendliness, and online availability. We believe that through continuous improvement and optimization of the platform, it will play an increasingly important role in promoting the application and development of scRNA-seq technology. Of course, it is important to acknowledge that BestopCloud still faces challenges regarding longer computation times when processing large-scale datasets. In the future, we will address this by optimizing algorithms, increasing computational resources, and adopting parallel computing to enhance the platform’s processing efficiency. Meanwhile, as scRNA-seq technology continues to evolve rapidly, we will continually monitor new analysis methods and tools, with the aim of timely integrating them into BestopCloud, thereby offering more comprehensive and advanced solutions for data analysis.

Methods

Overview

BestopCloud is an interactive web application based on the R v4.2.3 programming language [32], which utilizes the Shiny v1.8.1 [19] and shinyjs v2.1.0 packages [33] to construct a user-friendly interface. The platform’s workflow comprises nine well-designed analysis modules (Fig. 1), with the “Data processing” and “Differential expression analysis” module relying on the Seurat v4.3.0 database [31], while the “Data integration” module employs the Harmony v1.2.1 algorithm [23]. The “Data viewer” module primarily leverages the powerful functionalities of ggplot2 v3.5.1 [34] and patchwork v1.2.0 [35] to generate visually appealing plots and figures. Regarding cell type annotation, BestopCloud provides two specialized modules: “SingleR v2.0.0” [20] and “ScType v1.0” [21], which employ distinct methods for accurate identification of cell types. For comprehensive biological interpretation, the “Enrichment analysis” module integrates three robust online analysis tools: Metascape [28], NASQAR [36], and g: Profiler [37]. Furthermore, BestopCloud incorporates CellChat v2.1.2 [38] for thorough analysis of intercellular communication patterns and CopyKat v1.1.0 [39] for precise detection of copy number variations. These features collectively offer researchers a comprehensive solution for analyzing scRNA-seq data with utmost accuracy and efficiency.

Data input

BestopCloud provides flexible data input options to meet various analytical needs. Typically, the analysis pipeline commences with a raw matrix file generated by STAR software [40]. In the “Data processing” module, we utilize the Seurat package to standardize these data, covering the entire process from quality control to cluster analysis. Additionally, researchers have the option to directly upload.rds files processed by the Seurat package in any modules, which are accessible in the “Data Processing” module or outside BestopCloud, thereby bypassing certain analytical steps and achieving personalized and flexible analysis workflows.

More importantly, BestopCloud supports the flexible data flow between modules. Specifically, the module supporting data flow allows researchers to directly apply imported or analyzed data to another module. This design not only eliminates the need for repetitive uploading and downloading of data but also enables swift utilization of newly generated information, thereby enhancing analysis efficiency and user experience. Through these meticulously designed modules and data flow mechanisms, BestopCloud offers researchers an efficient, flexible, and user-friendly platform for scRNA-seq data analysis.

Data processing

BestopCloud leverages the Seurat package to perform a series of refined processing steps on the input scRNA-seq matrix data. During the data preprocessing phase, various operations are conducted, including removal of cells and genes exhibiting underexpression or overexpression, as well as exclusion of cells expressing a specific set of genes (e.g., mitochondrial genes) above a predefined threshold. Researchers have the flexibility to adjust these filtering parameters according to their specific requirements for personalized data filtering.

Following the initial filtering step, BestopCloud proceeds with data normalization and identification of highly variable genes. By utilizing expression data from these highly variable genes, the platform employs principal component analysis (PCA) to capture major sources of variation in the dataset. Researchers can customize the number of principal components based on their desired balance between information retention and noise control.

The graph-based Louvain clustering algorithm was subsequently employed by Seurat to group cells exhibiting similar expression patterns. Researchers have the flexibility to adjust the resolution parameters of these clusters, enabling them to obtain clustering results with varying levels of granularity. Higher resolution values will yield a greater number of clusters, thereby unveiling more intricate cellular heterogeneity.

Based on clustering analysis, BestopCloud utilizes the UMAP nonlinear dimensionality reduction algorithm to effectively transform high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional space, enabling the visualization of cellular heterogeneity. Moreover, this platform not only offers visualizations for quality control and clustering results but also allows researchers to download the processed Seurat objects (in.rds file format) for data integration or subsequent analysis of individual samples. This design not only enhances the flexibility of analysis but also facilitates data utilization and sharing across different stages of analysis.

Data integration

BestopCloud adopts the Harmony approach for batch effect correction and data integration of multiple input.rds files. Harmony is a widely recognized, efficient, and reliable technique for batch effect correction that has demonstrated exceptional performance in previous benchmarking studies, capable of correcting batch effects while maximizing the preservation of true biological variation [41].

In the “Data integration” module of BestopCloud, researchers have the option to select batch variables for correction, which can be any shared property among multiple samples in the uploaded dataset. In cases where the required batch variables are not present in the existing data or if researchers wish to add additional sample information for subsequent analysis, they can easily achieve this by uploading a metadata file. This feature provides researchers with flexibility in integrating relevant information into their datasets.

BestopCloud offers visualization of data distribution before and after batch correction, enabling researchers to intuitively assess the effectiveness of the correction. Following integration, Seurat objects can be downloadable as.rds files for subsequent analysis modules. This design enhances both the flexibility and transparency of data processing while ensuring consistency in the analysis process and repeatability of data. Consequently, BestopCloud provides researchers with a powerful and user-friendly tool to support intricate tasks related to scRNA-seq data integration.

Data viewer

BestopCloud offers comprehensive visualization capabilities and extensive statistical information, enabling researchers to gain profound insights into the intrinsic characteristics of their data. This module is divided into two main sections: “Overview” and “Marker”.

In the “Overview” section, researchers can utilize the UMAP tab to visualize metadata information, including UMI expression of cells, sample attributes, cell clustering, and cell type. By employing stacked bars charts in the Barplot tab, researchers can intuitively view the proportion of cells with specific attributes, for example, the distribution of different cell types within each sample.

The “Marker” section enables researchers to query a specific set of genes and presents their expression patterns across all cells, cell subsets, or different groups in the form of feature plots and violin plots. For instance, researchers can compare the differential expression of target genes in T cell subsets from two sample sets. Additionally, statistical information corresponding to the visual graphs is presented in tabular form below the graph, providing researchers with a convenient means to interpret the data.

With these meticulously designed visualization and statistical tools, BestopCloud not only enhances researchers’ comprehension of scRNA-seq data but also improves the efficiency and accuracy of data analysis.

Cell type annotation

Automatic cell annotation is an effective means of labeling cells or cell clusters using computational algorithms and biological knowledge, primarily divided into two methods: reference-based annotation and marker-based annotation [42]. In BestopCloud, we have implemented both strategies by utilizing the SingleR and ScType methods, respectively.

For the SingleR method, researchers are required to select the target species (human or mouse) according to its design requirements. Subsequently, researchers need to choose an appropriate reference dataset from a range of available alternative databases in order to achieve accurate cell type annotation. In contrast, the ScType method does not impose any limitations regarding species selection, however, researchers must provide a marker file that includes cell types and their corresponding marker genes, and this marker file is typically constructed based on prior knowledge, aiming to establish an informative framework that guides the accurate identification of cell types.

In terms of result presentation, BestopCloud offers annotated UMAP plots and also presents UMI expression, sample attributes, and cell clustering information to facilitate comparison and integration with other relevant information. Moreover, the platform provides comprehensive statistical information in tabular form, including cell counts, proportions, median gene counts, and clustering information for different cell types within each sample. By incorporating these functionalities, BestopCloud enhances the automation of cell annotation to support in-depth exploration of complex scRNA-seq data.

Downstream analyses

Using the FindAllMarkers and FindMarkers functions within the Seurat package, BestopCloud offers a versatile “Differential expression analysis” module that allows for comparisons of expression profiles between each identity group and all the others, as well as for in-depth comparative analysis between any two identity groups. Researchers have the flexibility to perform differential analysis on specific data subsets based on their research needs and customize grouping information accordingly. For instance, researchers can define samples A and B as a group in the module, and then compare the DEGs of T cells in this combination with that in sample C.

To facilitate enrichment analysis, BestopCloud integrates three robust online tools: Metascape, NASQAR, and g: Profiler in the “Enrichment analysis” module. Notably, NASQAR provides a Shiny application interface based on clusterProfiler [43], offering great convenience to researchers familiar with using clusterProfiler packages.

Additionally, BestopCloud offers CellChat V2-based cell-cell communication analysis functionalities in the “Cell-cell communication” module, allowing researchers to select species and corresponding subsets from the CellChatDB database based on their specific research objectives. The analysis results can effectively illustrate interactions within specific cell types and pathways, providing valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying intercellular communication.

The “Copy number analysis” module is another crucial component of BestopCloud. By employing a integrative Bayesian approach, CopyKat accurately infers the CNV status of each individual cell, thereby revealing the genetic heterogeneity within tumor cells. In the analysis results of this module, the CNV status (aneuploid/diploid) of each cell is tagged and incorporated into the metadata information. Researchers can further explore these findings by downloading the completed.rds file and utilizing other modules such as “Data viewer” for visualization or “Differential expression analysis” for comparing disparities between cancer cells and normal cells.

Through these comprehensive analytical tools, BestopCloud offers researchers a comprehensive and efficient platform for scRNA-seq data analysis, significantly enhancing the depth and breadth of single-cell research.

Example datasets

The scRNA-seq data for the 4 human PBMCs used in this study, namely 3k (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/3-k-pbm-cs-from-a-healthy-donor-1-standard-1-1-0), 5k (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/5k_Human_Donor4_PBMC_3p_gem-x), 10k (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/10k-human-pbmcs-3-v3-1-chromium-controller-3-1-high) and 20k (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/20-k-human-pbm-cs-3-ht-v-3-1-chromium-x-3-1-high-6-1-0), were obtained from public sources. Additionally, The study focused on two patients with stage IA LUAD and their corresponding distal normal lung tissues, from which a total of four samples were selected for scRNA-seq data analysis (GSE149655).

These data was meticulously organized and provided in the format of count matrix files to facilitate subsequent analysis. Leveraging the nine powerful analysis modules offered by BestopCloud, we conducted an in-depth and comprehensive visual analysis of these datasets. This process aimed to rigorously validate the functional availability, reliability, accuracy, and repeatability of various functions within BestopCloud, thereby establishing a robust foundation for its practical research applications.

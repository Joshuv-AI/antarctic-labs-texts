SPOCD1 expression in clear cell renal cell carcinoma: its implications for the prognosis and immunotherapy
Authors: Wu Xu, Lingfei Yan, Yufeng Liu, Dawei Liu, Dali Wu, Yang Luo, Cuilian Li, Qing Li, Tao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecent advancements in the study of SPOCD1 have highlighted its potential role in carcinogenesis. Nevertheless, the specific correlation between SPOCD1 expression level, survival outcomes, and immune cell infiltration in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) has not been fully elucidated.MethodsTo determine the possible connection between SPOCD1 expression levels, clinical pathological information, and overall survival (OS) in individuals with ccRCC, multiple databases encompassing TCGA, Gene Expression Profiling Interaction Analysis (GEPIA), and UALCAN were employed. Additionally, a comprehensive examination of the connection between tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIIC) and SPOCD1 was executed through TIMER, GEPIA, and TISIDB databases. Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) represents the most deadly urological malignancy regarding the yearly death rate, and its prevalence has been on the rise worldwide [1]. Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is the predominant pathological subtype of RCC, accounting for 70% to 80% of cases, and is associated with worse clinical outcomes compared to papillary RCC and chromophobe RCC [2]. ccRCCs primarily exhibit early inactivation of the tumor suppressor gene von Hippel–Lindau (VHL), a key event that dysregulates the VHL/hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway, leading to abnormal activation of hypoxia-responsive genes that promote angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, and tumor progression [3, 4]. Despite targeted therapy being one of the most commonly used conventional therapies to treat ccRCC, most patients eventually experience disease progression due to ccRCC cells’ resistance to drug-induced apoptosis [5]. Ferroptosis, a recently identified form of cellular death, has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for ccRCC [6–9]. Currently, existing therapies only show promise for a limited portion of ccRCC patients, making it imperative to find more effective therapeutic targets. Moreover, identifying new biological markers for the early detection of ccRCC and better prediction of patient outcomes remains a critical priority. The spen paralogue and orthologue C-terminal domain containing 1 (SPOCD1), alternatively designated as PPP1R146, codes for a protein that belongs to the S-II family of transcription factors (TFIIS), which is essential for the modulation of RNA synthesis. Recent investigations have highlighted the importance of SPOCD1 in piRNA-directed DNA methylation [10]. SPOCD1 has been studied extensively for its potential role in carcinogenesis. In human malignancies, SPOCD1 has been found to be implicated in biological functions. For instance, Zhu et al. identified variants of SPOCD1 linked to an elevated risk of gastric cancer, and silencing this gene severely limited xenograft tumor development in nude mice, as well as the ability of proliferation and invasion as expected [11]. According to Liu [31], SPOCD1 functions in ovarian cancer by preventing apoptosis through the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade. In addition, SPOCD1 promotes glioma cell proliferation and metastasis by upregulating PTX3 [12]. Further, SPOCD1 also enhances cell proliferation and suppresses apoptosis in osteosarcoma [13]. The SPOCD1-containing five-gene expression pattern was constructed by Van der Heijden [14], which showed 79% sensitivity and 86% specificity (AUC = 0.83) for differentiating patients with T1G3 bladder cancer from others. Increasing evidence suggests that inhibiting SPOCD1 might represent an effective approach to cancer therapy and a useful prognostic marker. Despite this, the role of SPOCD1 in ccRCC is not well documented and poorly understood.

The main focus of this investigation was to determine the possible connection between SPOCD1 expression level, patient characteristics, and overall survival (OS) in individuals with ccRCC employing multiple databases encompassing TCGA. Also, we examined how SPOCD1 is correlated with immune-related cells utilizing the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER). In ccRCC, a substantial correlation emerged between SPOCD1 expression level and both immune cell infiltration and unfavorable prognosis. Expression of SPOCD1 in clinical Renal clear cell carcinoma tissues and across diverse Renal clear cell carcinoma cell lines was initially evaluated. The evidence suggests SPOCD1 functions as a previously unidentified potential therapeutic target for immunotherapy application in ccRCC.

Methodologies and materials

Data source

The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), a publicly available repository containing extensive data from a large-scale cancer genome project, offers researchers and academics clinicopathological information across 33 diverse cancer types. Through the utilization of the TCGA browser, RNA-Seq data and corresponding clinicopathological details were procured from 269 individuals with ccRCC exhibiting high SPOCD1 expression levels and 270 patients demonstrating low SPOCD1 expression levels. Additionally, we evaluated their correlation based on the clinicopathological characteristics of the individuals. Due to the database’s public accessibility, no ethical approval was needed.

Differentially expressed gene analysis

Per the median SPOCD1 expression score, individuals with ccRCC from TCGA were split into high and low SPOCD1 expression level cohorts. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis between these cohorts was executed utilizing the DESeq2 R package, applying the criteria of adjusted p-value < 0.05 and |log2-fold change (FC)| >1 to identify DEGs. Spearman’s correlation analysis determined the connection between the SPOCD1 expression level and the top 30 DEGs.

Development and verification of nomograms

The prognostic features encompassing OS, progression-free interval (PFI), and disease-specific survival (DSS) were evaluated utilizing Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier (KM) methodologies within the clinical significance module of the Xiantao platform (https://www.xiantao.love/). Our computation of the truncation value for the high and low SPOCD1 expressions was grounded in the median number. The association between clinicopathological characteristics and SPOCD1 was determined via the Wilcoxon signed rank sum test and logistic regression analysis. Through the utilization of multivariate Cox analyses, we examined survival rates and various clinical characteristics. Statistical significance was ascertained at P < 0.05. The multivariate analysis yielded independent prognostic markers, which were subsequently employed to forecast survival likelihoods across 1,3, and 5 years. The 45°line represents the best prediction based on comparing predicted probabilities to observed events.

Functional enrichment analysis

The enrichment analysis of SPOCD1 was carried out employing the clusterProfiler package in R (v. 3.6.3), encompassing molecular functions, biological processes, and cellular components. The analysis parameters were established as minimum count > 3, enrichment factor > 1.5, and P < 0.01. Employing the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) method, pathways associated with SPOCD1 expression were enriched, with the genome being ranked 1000 times per analysis. A threshold of corrected P < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25 was applied. A corrected P-value and normalized enrichment scores (NESs) were used to characterize enrichment analysis results. GSEA enrichment analysis and visualization were conducted utilizing the Cluster Profiler package [15].

Immune infiltration analysis

Bindea G’s study [16] identified marker genes for 24 different kinds of immune cells, which were subsequently analyzed for infiltration into tumors using single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA). Through Spearman correlation analysis, the research examined the immune cell infiltration patterns between groups exhibiting high and low SPOCD1 expression level and evaluated the connection between SPOCD1 and 24 distinct immune cell populations. The investigation utilized the immune infiltration module from the “Xiantao tool” (https://www.xiantao.love/) to perform these analytical procedures.

TIMER database analysis

Additionally, the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER2.0) database served as a tool to examine immune infiltration patterns across diverse cancer types, offering a user-friendly online platform for such investigations (https://timer.cistrome.org/) [17]. A total of 10,897 specimens from the TCGA database comprising 32 types of cancer were evaluated for immune internal infiltrates. Moreover, the database is capable of accurately estimating tumor purity as well.

Cell lines and cell culture

The HK-2, A498, Caki-1, and 786-O cell lines were procured from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). DMEM/F-12 medium (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) served as the culture medium for HK-2 cells, whereas A498 cells required MEM medium (Procell, Wuhan, China). Caki-1 and 786-O cells proliferated in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA). Each culture medium comprises 10% fetal bovine serum (HyCyte, Suzhou, China) plus 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA). The cell lines were cultivated at 37 °C within a moisture-controlled environment containing 5% CO2.

Quantitative Real-Time (qRT-PCR)

We executed qRT-PCR to assess SPOCD1 mRNA level in HK-2, A498, Caki-1, and 786-O cell lines. Total RNA was procured utilizing the EZ-press RNA Purification Kit (EZBioscience, Houston, TX, USA), while RNA sample concentration and purity levels were ascertained utilizing a Nanophotometer N50 ultra-micro spectrophotometer (IMPLEN, München, Germany). Reverse transcription was carried out using the Hifair® III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). qRT-PCR was executed utilizing ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) on a Fluorescent Quantitative PCR Detection System (BIOER Technology, Hangzhou, China). GAPDH expression was utilized as an internal reference, and relative mRNA levels were computed utilizing the 2−ΔΔCt approach. Primer sequences (Sangon, Shanghai, China) were as follows:

Western blot (WB)

Cell samples underwent lysis utilizing RIPA lysis buffer comprising protease inhibitors (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Following complete homogenization, the lysates remained on ice for 10 min and subsequently underwent centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was procured, and protein level was ascertained employing the BCA Protein Assay Kit (GLPBIO, California, USA). For WB assay, proteins underwent separation via 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then moved into polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes underwent blocking at ambient conditions for 10 min utilizing a rapid blocking solution (Epigentek, New York, USA). After three rinses with 1× TBST, the membranes were maintained overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (SPOCD1, 22243-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:1000; GAPDH, 60004-1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:50000). The following day, the membranes were treated with species-matched secondary antibodies at ambient conditions for 1 h. Protein signals underwent visualization through the Ultra High Sensitivity ECL Kit (GLPBIO, California, USA), and images were acquired using the Vilber imaging system (Vilber Lourmat, Collégien, France). GAPDH functioned as an internal reference control.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

SPOCD1 expression was evaluated in 13 ccRCC specimens along with matching adjacent normal tissues stored at the hospital repository. Tissue sections underwent heating at 65 °C for 1 h, then were subjected to deparaffinization and rehydration processes utilizing xylene and graded ethanol. Antigen retrieval was executed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0, Biosharp, Beijing, China). Endogenous peroxidase activity was inhibited with 3% hydrogen peroxide application, after which the sections were blocked using 5% BSA (Biosharp). The specimens were then subjected to overnight incubation at 4 °C with an anti-SPOCD1 antibody (1:300, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). The subsequent day, sections underwent room temperature incubation for 1 h with biotinylated secondary antibodies and peroxidase-conjugated streptavidin-biotin complex (Dako, Glostrup, Denmark). For visualizing immunostaining, 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) (Dako) served as the chromogen, while hematoxylin was utilized for counterstaining. Following dehydration, the slides were mounted with neutral resin and examined under an Olympus microscope (Tokyo, Japan). Image analysis was conducted uniformly using ImageJ software. Data analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups were conducted utilizing a two-tailed Student’s t-test, while differences among multiple groups were examined employing one-way ANOVA. A p-value < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

SPOCD1 mRNA expression exhibits elevated levels in ccRCC

The TCGA dataset was utilized to determine whether SPOCD1’s high expression is widespread in cancer. Cancers such as breast cancer (BRCA), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), rectum adenocarcinoma (READ), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC) display upregulated levels of SPOCD1 expression, whereas Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) produce lower levels (Fig. 1A, B). Through analysis of TCGA databases, expression patterns of SPOCD1 mRNA were predicted in 539 samples of ccRCC and 72 samples of normal tissue. Compared with normal tissue, ccRCC samples expressed notably elevated SPOCD1 mRNA levels (Fig. 1C, P < 0.001). Further, SPOCD1 expression was compared in Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project samples with adjoining ccRCC tissues and samples, and it was found to be upregulated in ccRCC samples (Fig. 1D, P < 0.001). When compared with adjacent matched samples, 72 ccRCC samples showed significant increases in SPOCD1 expression (Fig. 1E, P < 0.001). Additionally, to assess the diagnostic potential of SPOCD1 expression levels, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was developed. The AUC value procured for SPOCD1 expression levels reached 0.811 (CI = 0.751–0.871), indicating significant diagnostic potential (Fig. 1F). Based on the above data, SPOCD1 appears to be an important diagnostic biomarker in ccRCC tissues.

Clinical characteristics associated with SPOCD1 expression

We assessed whether SPOCD1 was correlated with different clinical-pathological features, which include T stage, N stage, M stage, pathologic stage, and histologic grade. SPOCD1 expression level demonstrated notable connections to the T stage, N stage, M stage, pathologic stage (Fig. 2A-D). The study also showed a significant upregulation of SPOCD1 in the high-histologic grade cancers in comparison to the low-histologic grade cancers (Fig. 2E). Patients with lymph node metastases exhibited notably elevated levels of SPOCD1 compared to those without metastases, suggesting that SPOCD1 plays a role in malignancy. Additionally, OS events (i.e., death events) were associated with higher SPOCD1 expression (Fig. 2F).

Prognostic value of SPOCD1 expression in ccRCC

Subsequently, an OS heatmap of the SPOCD1 gene was generated (Fig. 3A). Elevated SPOCD1 expression level was positively linked to poor OS in individuals with multiple tumors, encompassing CESC, COAD, KIRP, KIRC, LGG, and UVM. We explored the connection between SPOCD1 expression rates and prognostic outcomes (OS, DSS, PFI) using TCGA database-derived data. SPOCD1 expression enhancement was shown to be significantly linked to worse OS, DSS, and PFI in the study population, with hazard ratios (HR) of 1.93 (95% CI 1.42–2.62), 2.29 (95% CI 1.53–3.42), and 1.62 (95% CI 1.18–2.23) respectively, all with a p-value < 0.01 (Fig. 3B). The KIRP samples were divided into low- and high-risk cohorts utilizing the median SPOCD1 value. We observed that the risk score was negatively linked to the OS rates (Fig. 3E). A further investigation of SPOCD1 expression and subgroups was conducted in this study. SPOCD1 overexpression was detected in the T3-T4 stage [HR = 1.54 (1.04–2.28), P = 0.03], pathologic stage Ⅲ-Ⅳ [HR = 1.57 (1.08–2.28), P = 0.017], Histologic grade G3-G4 [HR = 1.55 (1.09–2.20), P = 0.015] (Fig. 3F). We developed a clinical prognostic risk score using the stages T, N, M, pathologic grade, and SPOCD1 expression in ccRCC(Fig. 3G). Furthermore, a calibration chart was implemented to assess the predictive power of the model (Fig. 3H). According to the findings, SPOCD1 expression levels could better predict patient survival at 3- and 5-year intervals. All these results indicate that SPOCD1 expression levels are associated with ccRCC prognoses.

GO/GSEA enrichment analysis related to SPOCD1 gene expression and SPOCD1-interaction proteins in ccRCC tissue

A gene expression profiling analysis was implemented to uncover the biological role of SPOCD1 in ccRCC. The gene expression heat map depicted the 30 highest ranked genes exhibiting abnormal levels of expression (Padj < 0.01 and abslogFC > 2) (Fig. 4A). The analysis revealed 3805 downregulated and 171 upregulated genes significantly associated with SPOCD1 expression (Padj < 0.05 and logFC > 1) (Fig. 4B). Specifically, SPOCD1 expression was positively linked to COL1A1 level (r = 0.719,p < 0.001,Fig. 4C). Additionally, analyses of gene ontology (GO) enrichment were carried out based on SPOCD1 expression levels. The SPOCD1 gene is predominantly linked to biological processes that involve extracellular structure organization, extracellular matrix organization, and extracellular matrix disassembly, alongside other processes (Table 5; Fig. 4D). Analysis through KEGG pathway revealed genes associated with SPOCD1 expression exhibited significant enrichment primarily in pathways encompassing collecting duct acid secretion, synaptic vesicle cycle, and rheumatoid arthritis (Table 5; Fig. 4D).

Association of immune infiltration with SPOCD1 expression

Subsequently, 24 different immune cells were evaluated for correlation with SPOCD1 expression levels in ccRCC. The expression of SPOCD1 demonstrated significant correlations with Macrophages (r = 0.390), Th2 cells (r = 0.389), and DC (r = 0.373), while exhibiting strong adverse associations with Th17 cells (r=−0.331), with all p-values < 0.001 (Fig. 5A, E-G). Subsequent analysis revealed notable variations in SPOCD1 expression levels across various infiltrating immune cell types, such as aDC, NK CD56dim cells, T helper cells, iDCs, TReg, TH1 cells, TH2 cells, TH17 cells, and mast cells (Fig. 5B). The evidence indicates SPOCD1’s crucial involvement in immune infiltration processes within ccRCC.

Link between SPOCD1 expression levels and immune microenvironment markers in ccRCC

The expression of SPOCD1 was also compared with other genes involved in immunological regulation, including immune stimulation and immune suppression. A large majority of the targets of the immune microenvironment are associated with SPOCD1(Fig. 6A and D). SPOCD1 is correlated most strongly with Interleukin-2 receptor subunit alpha(IL2RA, named as CD25) (r = 0.507, P < 0.001). IL2RA can participate in the regulation of immune tolerance by controlling Treg cell activities, and Treg cells can suppress effector T cell activation and expansion. Targeted killing of Treg cells by CD25 antibody is regarded as a significant way of anti-tumor anti-tumor therapy. We examined chemokines and chemokine receptors to determine whether SPOCD1 expression is associated with immune cell migration (Fig. 6B, C). It was found that SPOCD1 expression is associated with chemokine and receptor expression, with the most strongly correlated chemokine being CCL26 (r = 0.637, p < 0.001) and CCR7 as the most notably linked receptor (r = 0.298, p < 0.001). Elevated SPOCD1 expression levels may facilitate immune cell migration, as it is correlated with both chemokines and their receptors. These findings underscore the involvement of SPOCD1 in immune infiltration within ccRCC.

Discussion

ccRCC, a type of RCC with the highest prevalence in the world, is currently being treated with immunotherapy as a novel therapeutic approach. It has recently been suggested that SPOCD1 is implicated in carcinogenesis. Multiple investigations have demonstrated that SPOCD1 is upregulated in gastric cancer [11], glioma [12], osteosarcoma [13], and bladder cancer [14]. However, based on the existing literature, research on the role of SPOCD1 in ccRCC carcinogenesis remains limited. In this investigation, we initially analyzed SPOCD1 mRNA expression levels across pan-cancer samples utilizing data from the TCGA dataset. Several cancers, including KIRC, have significantly higher levels of SPOCD1 mRNA than normal tissues. Furthermore, SPOCD1 expression was elevated in KIRC relative to adjacent non-tumor tissues. A significant link was noted between the levels of SPOCD1 mRNA in ccRCC and pathological stage, histological grade, and N stage. KM survival analysis illustrated that SPOCD1 expression enhancement was related to shorter OS, PFI, and DSS, indicating that individuals with high SPOCD1 expression may have an unfavorable outcome. It appears that SPOCD1 is involved in tumor metastasis and progression, making it a potential diagnostic indicator of KIRC.

This study also found that SPOCD1 strongly relates to the level of infiltrating immune cells in KIRC. Recent advances in immunotherapy techniques, especially immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), have demonstrated remarkable clinical success, playing a significant role in transforming cancer immunotherapy approaches [18]. Nonetheless, it is uncertain whether immune infiltration in KIRC is correlated with SPOCD1 expression. To explore the potential association, we conducted a systematic analysis, which revealed that SPOCD1 expression levels were significantly correlated with multiple immune cell types and immunological marker genes. Notably, SPOCD1 expression showed a positive correlation with macrophages (r = 0.390), Th2 cells (r = 0.389), and dendritic cells (r = 0.373), and a negative correlation with Th17 cells (r=−0.331), with all p-values < 0.001. SPOCD1 expression was also associated with immunostimulators, immune inhibitors, chemokines, chemokine receptors, and tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) marker genes in KIRC. Among these associations, the strongest correlation was observed with macrophages. Macrophages can differentiate into two polarization states: traditionally activated M1 macrophages, demonstrating proinflammatory attributes, and alternatively differentiated M2 macrophages, displaying anti-inflammatory features [19]. They performed a variety of biological functions, including proliferation [20], angiogenesis [21], invasion [22], metastasis [23], and immunity of tumors [24]. Our findings suggest that SPOCD1 expression may be linked to tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) polarization in KIRC; however, further in vivo and mechanistic studies are required to determine whether this relationship is causal.

We investigated how SPOCD1 influences the immune microenvironment by examining its connection with immunostimulatory and immunoinhibitory genes. The strongest association was found between SPOCD1 and the immunostimulatory gene Interleukin-2 receptor subunit alpha (IL2RA, also known as CD25) (r = 0.507, p < 0.001). The IL-2 receptor (IL-2R), in its functional state, consists of three subunits: the α chain (IL2Rα, or CD25), the β chain (IL2Rβ, or CD122), and the γ chain (IL2Rγ, or CD132) [25]. Most Tregs consistently express CD25 at elevated levels. CD25 serves as a Treg marker. A high infiltration of Treg cells is linked to unfavorable tumor outcomes [26]. Researchers suggested that targeting CD25 with an antibody might lower the number of immunosuppressive Treg cells, thereby hindering tumor progression. It demonstrates significant anti-tumor anti-tumor activity in multiple mouse models. Emerging evidence indicates that the combination of IL-2 non-blocking anti-human CD25 mAbs with immune checkpoint inhibitors, encompassing anti-PD-L1 antibodies, can produce a synergistic anti-tumor anti-tumor effect [27]. Based on these findings, targeting SPOCD1 may enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy. Overall, SPOCD1 appears to be implicated in the attraction and modulation of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in ccRCC, highlighting the need for further research into its molecular mechanisms and its role in the tumor microenvironment.

This study also investigated the functional significance of SPOCD1 in ccRCC. Analysis of sequential clinical ccRCC specimens and ccRCC cell lines demonstrated that SPOCD1 exhibited substantial upregulation in cancerous tissues versus adjacent non-cancerous tissues. SPOCD1 was also notably upregulated in ccRCC cell lines, particularly in the more invasive cell lines 786-O and Caki-1. Patients with elevated SPOCD1 expression levels were linked to advanced TNM stages and higher tumor grades. Additionally, those with high SPOCD1 expression level had significantly reduced OS and PFS versus patients with low SPOCD1 expression level. The findings indicated that SPOCD1 expression level is vital for the progression of ccRCC. The PI3K/AKT pathway has been shown to be involved in regulating the proliferation, apoptosis, and survival of numerous cancer cells [28, 29]. Various carcinoma cells experienced increased proliferation due to the AKT activator in the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade [30]. In this investigation, our results suggested that a higher SPOCD1 expression level was primarily enriched in WP_PI3KAKT_SIGNALING_PATHWAY. Recent studies have demonstrated that SPOCD1 may promote ovarian cancer development and inhibit cell apoptosis via activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway [31]. The findings reported in this article are consistent with our findings.

Despite uncovering the possible impact of SPOCD1 on the immune infiltration and prognosis in KIRC, our study is still constrained by various limitations. One limitation of this study is that the data primarily comes from online platform databases, which are subject to continuous updates and expansions, potentially impacting the research results. Second, validation of SPOCD1 function in ccRCC and its molecular mechanism in ccRCC immunity is not conducted through in vitro and in vivo investigations. In addition, the sample size of patient tissues used for immunohistochemical analysis in this study was only 13 cases, which is relatively small. In future research, we plan to further expand the sample size and add more in vitro and in vivo experimental validations to improve the reliability and generalizability of our findings.

Conclusion

Overall, the findings demonstrate that SPOCD1 exhibits elevated expression in KIRC tissues, and its upregulation is strongly linked to worse survival outcomes in KIRC. Additionally, SPOCD1 expression is implicated in the regulation of T-cell exhaustion, M2 macrophages, and Treg cells. SPOCD1 could emphasize its unique possible role in modulating immune cell infiltration among KIRC patients.

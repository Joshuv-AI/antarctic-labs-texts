Nonadherence to LLT associated with higher healthcare resource utilization and costs in a large integrated healthcare delivery system in Southern California
Authors: Matthew T Mefford, Rachelle Juan, Teresa N Harrison, Alejandro Victores, Lori D Bash, Ronald D Scott, Kristi Reynolds, Jaejin An
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGuideline-recommended lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) can reduce healthcare utilization and costs for patients with and at-risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). We examined healthcare utilization and associated costs among adult LLT users in Kaiser Permanente Southern California ≥21 years of age having ASCVD or intermediate (≥7.5% to <20%) or high (≥20%) 10-year ASCVD risk between 2015 and 2021. LLT adherence trajectories were determined using group-based trajectory modeling over 12 months. All-cause and ASCVD-related annualized adjusted costs were calculated through 2023. High versus low LLT adherence was associated with lower all-cause costs (−$999; 95% CI: −$1100, −$897) and varied across subgroups by ASCVD risk. Adults missing a follow-up low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) measure had +$8312 (95% CI +$7955, +$8669) higher all-cause costs compared to adults with LDL-C < 55 mg/dL. Improving LDL-C management through optimized LLT treatment strategies can reduce economic burdens in high-risk patients.Keywords: Healthcare resource utilization, Cost, Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, Low density lipoprotein-cholesterol, Lipid-lowering therapy

Introduction

Through 2050, annual healthcare costs associated with cardiovascular conditions are expected to quadruple [1]. Patients with dyslipidemia and prior atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) have higher healthcare resource utilization (HCRU) and costs compared to those without [2], which may be partly attributable to suboptimal lipid management. Reducing low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) with safe and effective lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) toward guideline-recommended goals can lower the risk of new and recurrent ASCVD events [3], reducing unanticipated HCRU and costs. However, LLT remains underutilized, and there is a gap between real-world use of LLT and guideline recommendations [4]. Moreover, patient non-adherence to LLT may be associated with higher levels of HCRU and healthcare costs.

Methods

Among Kaiser Permanente Southern California (KPSC) members, the current study included 482,759 adults ≥21 years of age with at least one year of continuous membership and pharmacy benefits between January 1, 2015 and March 31, 2021 who had a history of ASCVD within the prior 10 years (secondary prevention cohort, including (1) myocardial infarction (MI), (2) stable or unstable angina, (3) coronary revascularization, (4) ischemic stroke/transient ischemic attack (TIA), or (5) peripheral arterial disease (PAD) of atherosclerotic origin) or who had complete baseline data to calculate a 10-year ASCVD risk ≥7.5% based on the 2013 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association pooled cohort equations (PCE) among those without a history of ASCVD (primary prevention cohort). LLT use was defined as a filled prescription for any statin or non-statin treatment (i.e., ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitor, fenofibrate, bempedoic acid, or bile acid sequestrant. Adults not taking LLT during the study period, those having <365 days of continuous membership and pharmacy benefits following their LLT fill, and those having an ASCVD event in the 365 days following their LLT fill were excluded (Supplemental Fig. 1). The institutional review board at KPSC reviewed and approved the current study, and a waiver of informed consent was granted. We examined the association of baseline risk groups (intermediate (7.5%–<20%) ASCVD risk, high (≥20%) ASCVD risk, and history of ASCVD) and 12-month LLT adherence with all-cause and ASCVD-specific costs (e.g., encounters with a primary diagnosis of ASCVD or ASCVD-specific labs, medications, or procedures), calculating cost differences using a generalized linear model with a Gamma distribution and log-link – commonly used for skewed cost data - and adjusting for demographics, comorbidities and baseline log-transformed costs [5]. A single-model approach was chosen because the proportion of zero costs was minimal (1.6% for all-cause costs and 2.6% for ASCVD-specific costs). Baseline costs were included for adjustment to reduce imbalances in potential high HCRU in those with ASCVD and high-risk for ASCVD. Cost differences among individuals by levels of last available low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were also calculated overall and by baseline risk group. The 12-month LLT adherence groups were determined by group-based latent trajectory modeling [6] using filled LLT prescriptions, as described in our prior study [7] (Supplemental Fig. 2). Starting in month 13 following the LLT adherence period, HCRU and outcomes were identified from the electronic health record (EHR) or claims until death, disenrollment, or March 31, 2023. The mean length of follow-up for all risk groups and LLT adherence groups was approximately 5 years. Detailed descriptions of study methods including LLT adherence trajectory modeling criteria and cost estimation and annualization are listed in the Supplemental Methods.

Results

In the overall population (n = 482,759), mean age was 63.2 years, 43.2% were female, 13.3% were Asian Pacific Islander, 35.0% were Hispanic, 9.9% were non-Hispanic Black, and 39.3% were non-Hispanic White. Additionally, 29.0% and 54.3% had baseline intermediate and high ASCVD risk, 16.7% had a history of ASCVD, and 11.6%, 23.9% and 64.5% had low, intermediate, and high LLT adherence, respectively. Detailed characteristics by baseline risk are listed in Supplemental Table 1. In brief, those with history of ASCVD were most likely to have missing LDL-C at baseline (14.9%) compared to those with intermediate or high ASCVD risk. Observed baseline comorbidities and LLT adherence (e.g., high adherence) are high among those with history of ASCVD compared to at-risk groups. High versus low LLT adherence was associated with lower all-cause costs (−$999; 95% CI: −$1100, −$897) (Table). Annual costs associated with high compared to low LLT adherence for adults at intermediate-risk and high-risk for ASCVD, and with a history of ASCVD were +$165 (95% CI: +$43, +$286), −$1215 (95% CI: −$1333, −$1096), and −$2225 (95% CI, −$2554, −$1897), respectively. Unadjusted all-cause and ASCVD-specific HCRU costs are presented in Supplemental Tables 2 and 3, respectively. Over 95% of all adults were on statin monotherapy at baseline, and in a sensitivity analysis removing users without a baseline statin, results were consistent with the overall analysis (Data not shown). To further explain cost differences observed in baseline ASCVD risk and LLT adherence groups, we summarized costs incurred among adults by their last available LDL-C during follow-up (Figure). Compared to adults with last-available follow-up LDL-C < 55 mg/dL, those having LDL-C ≥ 130 mg/dL and those missing a follow-up LDL-C measure had +$356 (95% CI +$245, +$466) and +$8312 (95% CI +$7955, +$8669) higher all-cause costs, respectively. Higher ASCVD-specific healthcare costs were also incurred during follow-up for all follow-up LDL-C groups compared to <55 mg/dL.

Discussion

ASCVD, the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in the US, continues to rise in prevalence affecting roughly 10% of adults [8]. It is associated with reduced quality of life, high patient costs and increased financial strain to health systems [1]. Among over 480,000 adults in KPSC, low adherence to LLT, history of ASCVD and high ASCVD risk were associated with higher healthcare costs. These findings among LLT users without ASCVD events in the year following their LLT initiation underscore the importance of treatment adherence particularly for those with established and at high risk for ASCVD. These findings are also consistent with other studies examining costs related to patients with and at-risk for ASCVD [9]. Of note, among intermediate-risk individuals we observed higher costs associated with high compared to low LLT adherence, which should be interpreted cautiously. Possible explanations include higher mean baseline LDL-C in this population, more intensive surveillance of labs and preventive care utilization among adherent individuals, residual confounding, and potential misclassification of risk by the PCE, particularly relevant for this population which was more than 13% Asian and over a third Hispanic. We also observed incrementally higher costs with higher follow-up LDL-C levels, but highest costs were incurred among those without a follow-up LDL-C measure; this is likely a heterogeneous population given the proportions of missing LDL-C across baseline risk groups. Given the insured nature of the KPSC population, missingness may be a proxy for access-to-care issues due to unstable coverage but could also be attributed to less frequent monitoring due to socioeconomic or behavioral factors, higher comorbidity burden, or greater medication nonadherence, which was out of scope for this study. However, screening strategies for LDL-C vary by risk, with frequent monitoring shown to be cost-effective for patients with high-risk and those with ASCVD [10]. We acknowledge some limitations in the current study. We required 12 months of LLT use without an ASCVD event for adherence calculations, which may exclude less healthy individuals and reduce observed differences in costs.

Non-treatment or under-treatment of LDL-C confers high risk of having ASCVD events, translating to greater HCRU and costs. Addressing clinical inertia, patient adherence, access to appropriate care, and affordability of care remain crucial to reducing disparities among these patients with particularly high HCRU [4]. Additionally, leveraging improvement of health behaviors like LLT adherence, optimizing LLT management through EHR synchronization of treatment algorithms for treatment-eligible results, using pharmacist-driven treatment tailoring and management, and driving incentivization through more evidence-based care quality metrics can decrease improve adherence in LLT.

Assessment and characterization of BBB permeability parameters in autoimmune encephalitis based on 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT
Authors: Jianfang Li, Liangjun Xie, Li Xiao, Yaxin Lu, Luping Qin, Hongmei Lin, Yueming Zha, Bingjun Zhang, Jigang Yang, Wei Qiu, Jinchi Liao, Muhua Cheng, Yaqing Shu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe study focuses on understanding the disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) in autoimmune encephalitis (AIE) using 99mTc-DTPA Single-photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT) and the associations between BBB permeability and clinical parameters.MethodsForty-five AIE patients and 24 healthy controls (HCs) were studied. All underwent 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT for BBB imaging. Statistical methods (Mann-Whitney U, t-tests, Chi-square, Fisher’s exact test) were used to compare quantitative and categorical variables. Correlations were analyzed using Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis. Abnormal BBB permeability was assessed with Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM). Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) and hierarchical clustering were used to identify AIE subgroups.ResultsAIE patients had higher Bave (average relative counts values of the entire brain region) and Bmax (maximum relative counts values of the entire brain region) than HCs (p = 0.000; p = 0.001, respectively). Bave and Bmax correlated with modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores in AIE patients (r = 0.83, p < 0.001; r = 0.54, p < 0.001). Patients in cluster 1 had a high frequency of movement disorder than those in cluster 2 (p = 0.004), whereas patients in cluster 2 had a higher frequency of psychosis than those in cluster 1 (p < 0.001). The median values of both Bave and Bmax after treatment in the poor prognosis group were higher than those in the good prognosis group (p = 0.0007; p = 0.0333).ConclusionBBB permeability, measured by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT, was elevated in AIE patients and correlated with disease severity and prognosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-025-04454-4.

Introduction

Autoimmune encephalitis (AIE) is a rare and severe neurological disorder characterized by acute-onset neuropsychiatric symptoms, such as psychosis or cognitive deficits, seizures, speech dysfunction, movement disorder, declined consciousness, autonomic instability, or central hypoventilation [1, 2]. A prevalent subtype of AIE is associated with antibodies against the N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) [3]. The postulation is that AIE initiation may result from antigens released during viral-induced neuronal destruction, tumorigenesis, or mechanisms that remain unidentified [1, 4]. These factors may elicit damage to the blood-brain barrier (BBB) through immune and inflammatory responses [5]. Numerous studies have reported the development of BBB dysfunction in anti-NMDAR AIE [5, 6]. Additionally, the successful induction of the mouse model of anti-NMDAR-AIE is contingent upon the integrity of the BBB, underscoring the pivotal role of BBB disruption in AIE patients [7, 8]. Nonetheless, understanding BBB dysfunction in AIE patients remains inadequate.

Presently, a limited number of methods are employed to assess BBB permeability, such as cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) albumin/serum albumin quotient (QAlb) and dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI. However, QAlb cannot gauge the BBB integrity of a specific brain area [9]. MRI was unable to detect certain micro-lesions of AIE, even in the presence of severe clinical symptoms [1, 10]. 99mTc-DTPA single-photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT) has been utilised to evaluate BBB integrity [11]. The clinical application of SPECT predominantly relies on imaging BBB injury using water-soluble small molecular radiopharmaceuticals, such as 99mTc-DTPA. The imaging of hydrophilic penetration into the brain parenchyma after injury facilitates the assessment of BBB breakdown [12]. In contrast, MRI contrast agents are administered in milligrams, potentially leading to side effects, while radio-tracers are typically administered in micrograms to nanograms, exerting a lesser impact on physiology. This characteristic may confer an advantage in experimental and clinical settings [13]. Consequently, 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT imaging has been employed to quantify BBB disruption directly in rodents and humans [14, 15]. Despite this, there is a dearth of studies on BBB permeability in AIE.

The objective of this study was to utilise 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT for evaluating BBB permeability in AIE patients and to explore the associations between BBB permeability and clinical parameters in AIE patients.

Methods

Study design, participants, and ethics statement

This study sequentially recruited 52 patients with AIE from the department of neurology at our hospital, along with 25 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (HCs) devoid of substance abuse, neurological, and psychiatric disorders or inflammatory/immune diseases seen for a routine health examination were recruited from the outpatient department of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, spanning the period from October 2021 to March 2023.The included patients met the clinical diagnosis criteria for AIE outlined in the 2016 diagnostic criteria [2]. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (1) incomplete related data and (2) 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT motion artifacts. Adhering to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 7 patients and 1 HC were excluded from subsequent analysis. Ultimately, 45 AIE patients and 24 age- and sex-matched HCs were enrolled in this study. The research design is depicted in the flow diagram presented in Supplemental Fig. 1.

99 mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT imaging and analysis

All participants underwent planar and SPECT/CT imaging using a SIEMENS Symbia T6 SPECT/CT System (Siemens Medical Solutions, USA) 30 min after injection with 740 MBq 99mTc-DTPA (Beijing Atom Hightech Co., Ltd., China). SPECT projection data were obtained through continuous rotational motion of the gantry, constituting 360 samples distributed into 64 frames. Each frame comprised 20 s of data in a 128 × 128 matrix, zoom 1, with a main energy window of 140 ± 7.5% keV and a lower energy window for scatter correction, utilising the LEHR collimator. Following the SPECT acquisition, a low-dose CT scan was conducted for attenuation correction and anatomical localisation, utilising 50 mAs and 130 keV. The SPECT images were reconstructed using the flash 3D iteration method with 6 iterations, 8 subsets, and a 9.0 mm filter.

Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM) Analysis: To process and measure, the DICOM images were initially converted to Neuroimaging Informatics Technology Initiative (NIfTI) format and organised in pairs of SPECT and CT. Image preprocessing was performed in SPM12(UCL, London, UK) [16]. Subsequently, the CT images for each patient underwent registration and normalisation to the Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) 152 standard brain space via SPM12’s built-in template. The SPECT images were processed in tandem with the paired CT images, employing the same transpose matrix and deformation field. Following registration and normalisation, the average counts value of the background region in the SPECT image of each patient, situated 2 pixels (4 mm) outside the skull, was measured [17, 18] (Supplemental Fig. 2). Every pixel value in the brain was then divided by this average value and magnified by a factor of 100, yielding a relative counts value. Subsequently, the maximum and average relative counts values of the entire brain region were measured and recorded as Bmax and Bave, respectively. All these processes and measurements were executed automatically through a program scripted in MATLAB 2014a (MathWorks, USA).

Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) and hierarchical clustering analysis were employed to reduce dimensionality and eliminate redundancy across the nine symptoms of AIE patients in our study. These symptoms included fever or headache, cognitive impairment, psychosis, speech disorder, seizures, movement disorder, involuntary movement, disturbance of consciousness, and central hypoventilation.

Clinical data collection

The following clinical data were collected, including gender, age, clinical features, AIE types, hospitalization duration, admission to the ICU, history of tumour, CSF, routine blood test results, QAlb, and brain MRI, and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) which was used to assessed each patient’s neurological status [19]. Outcomes of mRS with a score ranging from 0 to 5, characterized by no symptoms (0); no significant disability despite symptoms (1); slight disability (2); moderate disability (3); moderate-to-severe disability (4); and severe disability (5). According to Balu et al. [20], good functional status was defined as mRS score ≤ 2, poor functional status was defined as mRS ≥ 3.

QAlb served as an assessment tool for the function of brain barriers [17, 18]. Acknowledging the age dependency of QAlb, an individual age-related QAlb was formulated as QAlb* = (4 + Age/15) × 10–3 [19] Elevated QAlb, indicative of brain barrier disruption, was defined as QAlb >QAlb* [6].

Brain MRI examinations were conducted on 3-T MRI scanners, incorporating T1-weighted, T2-weighted, T2-fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, and the contrast-enhanced MRI scan completed within 5 min after injecting the contrast agent (Magnevist). Abnormal MRI findings in this study were characterised by anomalous signalling on fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) or T2 sequences.

Symptoms were categorised into the following groups: prodromal symptoms such as headache or fever, psychiatric symptoms, cognitive impairment, speech disturbances, seizures, movement disorders, involuntary movement, loss of consciousness, and central hypoventilation.

Follow-up evaluations

Follow-up evaluations involved 21 of the 45 AIE patients who underwent a repeat assessment of mRS and 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT evaluation three months after admission. Individual or combined use of corticosteroids and intravenous immunoglobulins was defined as first-line immunotherapy, while administration of rituximab (RTX) or mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) was defined as second line immunotherapy. Among these 21 patients, 4 patients treated with first-line immunotherapy, 9 patients treated with first-line immunotherapy combined with RTX, 8 patients treated with first-line immunotherapy combined with MMF.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 22.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous data are presented as means ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), while categorical data are expressed as n (%). Mann-Whitney U or t-tests were employed to ascertain differences between two quantitative variables. Chi-square analysis or Fisher’s exact test was utilised to compare differences between two categorical variables. The correlations between data were analysed using Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis.

SPM statistical analysis was executed to compare images of patients with HCs, employing a two-sample t-test model (PFWE−corr < 0.05). Another analysis was conducted to compare images of cluster 1 patients with cluster 2 patients (Puncorr < 0.01).

MCA and hierarchical clustering analysis were performed utilising statistical programming software R-3.6.2 for statistical analysis. Mann-Whitney U or t-tests were conducted to assess differences between two quantitative variables. Chi-square analysis or Fisher’s exact test was employed to compare differences between two categorical variables. The FactoMineR software package and the factoextra software package were used for principal component analysis and to display the clustering of principal components in a two-dimensional space, respectively. Two-sided p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

The study protocol adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the declaration of Helsinki of 1975 and received approval from the local ethics committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University [(2019)2-637]. Informed written consent was obtained from all patients or their representatives, as well as HCs.

Comparison of BBB permeability based on 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT between subgroups of AIE patients

We subdivided the AIE patients into subgroups based on sex, age, mRS, hospitalization duration, prodromal symptoms, brain MRI findings, ICU admission, AIE types, and QAlb. The changes determined by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT imaging in AIE patients were presented in Table 2. We observed that the Bave and Bmax (indicative of BBB permeability) in patients with non-NMDAR type, seizures, or mRS scores ≥ 4 were significantly higher than in those NMDAR type, without seizures, or mRS scores < 4, respectively (all p < 0.05). The SPM analysis revealed that, compared to HCs, AIE patients with non-NMDAR type exhibited increased BBB permeability in the left parietal lobe. In AIE patients with seizures increased BBB permeability were observed in the left posterior cerebellar lobe, left insula, left occipital lobe, left temporal lobe, left parietal lobe, and bilateral frontal lobes. Additionally, AIE patients with mRS scores ≥ 4 showed elevated BBB permeability in the bilateral cerebellum, temporal lobe, parietal lobe, frontal lobe, insula, left occipital lobe, and right cingulate gyrus. These affected regions are illustrated in Supplemental Fig. 3. The Bave in patients with abnormal MRI or elevated QAlb was higher than in those with normal MRI or normal QAlb, respectively (all p < 0.05), but there was no statistically significant difference in Bmax between them (all p < 0.05). The Bave and Bmax in patients with normal MRI were respectively higher than those in HCs (p = 0.022; p = 0.011). Only five patients exhibited MRI-enhanced lesions, and no significant differences were observed in Bave or Bmax between patients with and without MRI-enhanced lesions (p = 0.232; p = 0.811). However, Bave levels in both groups of patients with and without MRI enhanced lesions were significantly higher than those in HCs (p = 0.005; p < 0.001). Additionally, Bmax levels in patients without MRI-enhanced lesions were higher than those in HCs, although the difference was not statistically significant in Bmax between patients with MRI-enhanced lesions and HCs. (p = 0.003; p = 0.071). The Bave and Bmax showed no statistically significant differences in patients subdivided by sex, age, hospitalization time, fever or headache, cognitive impairment, psychosis, speech disorder, movement disorder, involuntary movement, disturbance of consciousness, central hypoventilation, tumor, or ICU admission.

MCA and hierarchical clustering analysis identified two clusters based on the nine common symptoms of AIE patients, including fever or headache, cognitive impairment, psychosis, speech disorder, seizures, movement disorder, involuntary movement, disturbance of consciousness, and central hypoventilation. Two clusters were identified and their clinical features were subsequently compared. The 3D and 2D dendrogram represents hierarchical clustering analysis on MCA (Fig. 4A, B). Discussion

In the current study, increased permeability of the BBB detected by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT is observed in AIE patients. Additionally, patients with more severe BBB damage are more likely to experience seizures, exhibit higher mRS scores, have elevated QAlb show abnormal MRI findings, and have a poor prognosis. Patients in cluster 2 manifested increased BBB permeability in the bilateral frontal lobe, bilateral posterior lobe of the cerebellum, bilateral occipital lobe, right cingulate gyrus and left temporal lobe, and were prone to present with psychosis symptoms. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to assess BBB permeability based on 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT in AIE patients.

The BBB is a dynamic barrier structure situated between capillaries and nerve tissues that prevents the entry of most blood-borne substances and peripheral immune cells into the brain. Dysfunction of the BBB contributes to neuronal damage and cell death in the majority of neurological diseases [21]. Hammer et al. [7] demonstrated that injecting NMDAR antibodies from patient serum into mice with an intact BBB effectively prevented the entry of NMDAR antibodies into the hippocampus. The mice with a compromised BBB exhibited decreased spontaneous movement and other symptoms. In our study, we observed that Bave and Bmax, representing semi-quantitative parameters of BBB permeability detected by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT, were significantly higher in AIE patients. Furthermore, we found that the BBB permeability was significantly higher in patients with non-NMDAR type, seizures, or mRS scores ≥ 4 compared to those with NMDAR type, no seizures, or mRS scores < 4, respectively, consistent with previous studies detecting BBB dysfunction using QAlb [5]. SPM analysis revealed that, relative to HCs, the three subgroups of non-NMDAR types, seizures, and mRS scores ≥ 4 showed increased BBB permeability in distinct brain regions. Moreover, patients with mRS scores ≥ 4 exhibited a broader extent of brain involvement, which was likely to contribute to the severity of their symptoms. The results suggest that BBB disruption plays a crucial role in AIE patients.

Previous studies have indicated that the assessment of BBB permeability dysfunction serves as a hallmark of active lesions in multiple sclerosis, a classic autoimmune disease of the nervous system [22, 23]. Interestingly, our SPM results revealed elevated BBB permeability in specific regions of AIE patients, including the left posterior lobe of the cerebellum, left occipital lobe, left parietal lobe, left temporal lobe, bilateral frontal lobe, and right cingulate gyrus. These findings were largely consistent with clinical symptoms observed, including cognitive impairment, seizures, psychosis, speech disorder, movement disorder, and involuntary movement. Our results may represent the first documentation of BBB permeability changes in active lesions in AIE patients.

Teenagers and adults AIE patients more frequently present with psychiatric symptoms, such as agitation, hallucinations, delusions, and catatonia, potentially leading to hospital admission for psychosis [2]. According to MCA and hierarchical clustering analysis, movement disorder and psychosis were characteristic symptoms of cluster 1 and cluster 2 respectively. Patients in cluster 2 are present younger and psychosis symptom. Additionally, patients in cluster 2 exhibit higher BBB permeability in the bilateral frontal lobe, bilateral posterior lobe of the cerebellum, bilateral occipital lobe, right cingulate gyrus, and left temporal lobe. Our results indicate distinctive symptoms and a BBB damage imaging pattern in the two subgroups of AIE based on MCA and SPM.

In the current study, the BBB permeability assessed by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT was higher in AIE patients with abnormal MRI compared to those with normal MRI. No significant differences were observed in BBB permeability assessed by 99mTc-DTPA between patients with and without MRI-enhanced lesions. However, BBB permeability levels in both groups of patients, regardless of MRI-enhanced lesions, were significantly higher than those in HCs. These findings suggest that 99mTc-DTPA scintigraphy may be more sensitive than single-time-point contrast-enhanced MRI in detecting BBB disruption in patients with AIE, a conclusion that diverges from the results reported by Breuer H et al. [13]. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the contrast enhancement techniques employed. Specifically, while the previous study utilized dynamic contrast enhancement, our study employed single-time-point contrast-enhanced MRI, conducted 5 min post-injection of the contrast agent. Further comparative studies are warranted to determine which imaging modality demonstrates greater sensitivity in detecting BBB disruption. Additionally, changes in Bave levels were closely associated with changes in mRS scores in AIE patients. Notably, both Bave and Bmax levels after treatment in the poor prognosis group were higher than those in the good prognosis group. These results suggest that BBB permeability based on 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT may serve as a sensitive biomarker for both disease severity and prognosis in AIE patients [5].

Acknowledging the limitations of this study is imperative. First, this was a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which was particularly limiting for subgroup analyses. The SPM results suggesting that most lesions of AIE patients are located in the left hemisphere may lack sufficient representativeness. This finding should be further validated through systematic analysis or multi-center studies, which will be part of our future work. Second, there was no longitudinal imaging data collected beyond the 3-month time point. Third, as an observational study, the underlying mechanisms of BBB permeability changes in patients with AIE remain unclear. Fourth, it is important to acknowledge the potential influence of treatment heterogeneity and the lack of a placebo-controlled group. This absence could lead to an overestimation of the treatment’s impact, as observed results in this study might be potentially attributed to regression-to-the-mean effects. Thus, incorporating a placebo-controlled group in future research will help precisely determine the treatment’s effects.

Conclusion

In conclusion, BBB permeability, as detected by 99mTc-DTPA SPECT/CT, was found to be elevated in AIE patients, demonstrating associations with disease severity, symptom clusters, and disease prognosis. Nevertheless, multicenter and large-scale research is warranted to further validate its clinical application.

Vertical distribution characteristics of rare earth elements in sediments of the Sanjiang Plain
Authors: Bowen Liu, Yuandong Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study collected 287 samples from nine boreholes in the Sanjiang Plain and analyzed the contents of rare earth elements (REEs), major elements, pH, and organic matter. The study summarizes the vertical distribution characteristics and influencing factors of REEs in the sediments of the Sanjiang Plain. The results show that the vertical distribution of REEs in the sediments of the Sanjiang Plain exhibits a strong grain size effect. Sediments with a particle size < 165.11 μm show higher concentrations of Eu depletion, and lower (La/Lu)NASC and (Nd/Yb)NASC ratios; while sediments with a particle size > 165.11 μm show lower concentrations of Eu enrichment, and higher (La/Lu)NASC and (Nd/Yb)NASC ratios. The fundamental reason for this phenomenon is the varying weathering intensities of the sediments. Coarse-grained sediments are less affected by weather, primarily reflecting the REE characteristics of the source area; fine-grained sediments are more strongly affected by weathering, with feldspar weathering producing clay minerals. Meanwhile, primary minerals release Fe2+ and Mn2+ during weathering, some of which precipitate as Fe and Mn oxides1, adsorbing REEs and increasing overall concentrations of fine-grained sediments. Additionally, the weathering of feldspar minerals also causes Eu depletion. Introduction

Rare earth elements (REEs) are the lanthanide elements with atomic numbers 57–71. Because scandium (Sc) and yttrium (Y) exhibit similar physical and chemical properties to the lanthanide elements, they are also classified as rare earth elements2. Due to their minimal fractionation in geochemical processes, REEs can effectively reflect the characteristics of the source area and are widely used in provenance tracing3–9.

In recent years, the geochemical behavior and migration characteristics of rare earth elements (REEs) have received widespread attention, with the main influencing factors including pH, redox conditions, ligand concentrations, and soil grain size2,10,11. The pH value has an important impact on the migration of REEs12–16, mainly in two aspects: (1) Dissolved REE content: When the pH of the soil solution is < 7, the solubility of REEs is higher, and when pH > 7, the solubility is lower17,18. This means that REEs are more easily concentrated in alkaline soils, while they are more likely to migrate in acidic soils19–21. (2) Complexation forms of REEs: Sulfate complexes and carbonate complexes are the main forms of REE ions in the soil. Under acidic conditions, is the main complexing ion of REEs, forming REE , REE , REE 22, while in alkaline environments with high carbonate concentrations, REE ions form carbonate complexes such as REE and REE through complexation with 23. The impact of redox conditions on REE migration is mainly reflected in three aspects: (1) indirectly affecting REE migration through changes in pH24; (2) influencing REE migration and release in soils by affecting their solubility25; (3) Oxygen fugacity also affects the release of REEs in soils. Aerobic conditions inhibit the reductive dissolution of iron-manganese hydroxides in the soil, leading to a higher pH, which promotes the release of trace metals as metal–organic complexes on soil mineral phases26,27. The effective ligand concentration in soil solution is the material basis for the effective complexation and migration of REEs in the soil system. In natural systems, the migration of REEs in soils is also controlled by the concentrations of ligands such as organic matter, iron-manganese hydroxides, , and 12,28,29. Grain size also influences the REE content in sediments. Since clay minerals can host REEs, a reduction in soil particle size generally leads to an increase in REE concentration4,17,30.

Relevant studies on the migration of rare earth elements (REEs) in soils have also been conducted in some regions of China. Mory Traore et al.31 studied the distribution characteristics of REEs in sediments from major rivers (Yangtze River, Yellow River, Heilong River, Songhua River, etc.) and lakes in China, suggesting that human activities play a significant role in enriching REEs in urban water bodies. He Miao et al.3 conducted a study on the distribution of REEs and provenance tracing in the upper Yangtze River Delta, concluding that seasonal variations and human activities are the main factors influencing REE distribution. Yang Tian32 studied the geochemical characteristics of REEs in 50 surface soil samples from the eastern region of China, finding that these characteristics primarily reflect the parent material of the soil.Liu Haiyan6 studied the distribution characteristics of REEs in the North China Plain and found that REE and gradually decrease from the foothills to the coast. Still, the distribution pattern remains largely unchanged, primarily reflecting that of the source area.

The research area, the Sanjiang Plain, is in northeastern China and is the country’s largest freshwater marshland33. However, there has been limited research on the distribution of Rare Earth Elements (REEs) in the Sanjiang Plain. Cheng Hongguang et al.34 studied the vertical distribution of REEs in soils of the Sanjiang Plain, finding that REEs are primarily concentrated in the upper layers and speculated that this is mainly influenced by human activities. Materials and methods

Study area

The Baoqing area of the Sanjiang Plain is in a mid-to-high-latitude region (45°45′-46°55′ N, 131°12′–133°30′ E) with a total area of 10,001.27 km2 (Fig. 1). The terrain of the study area is mainly plains and low mountain hills, with mountainous areas in the southern and eastern parts, and a plain in the central part. The study area contains vast wetlands and is often described as “four mountains, four waters, one part farmland, and half reeds, half grassland”. The average annual temperature and precipitation range from 1.4 °C to 3.6 °C and 500 mm to 650 mm, respectively35,36. The rainy season is mainly concentrated in summer and autumn, accounting for about 80% of the annual rainfall, and lasts from June to September each year. The annual sunshine hours range from 2400 to 2500, with an average July temperature of 21°C to 22 °C. The abundant sunlight, rainfall, and warmth throughout the four seasons make it suitable for agricultural development, particularly producing high-quality rice, corn, and soybeans37. Large-scale land reclamation began in the 1950s, and by around 2000, many large state-owned farms were established, making the area China’s largest agricultural reclamation region and an important commodity grain production base35.

Sample collection and analysis

Nine boreholes were set up in the Sanjiang Plain, with depths ranging from 27 to 50 m. A combination of stratified and equidistant sampling was used (one sample per layer, with layer thicknesses greater than 50 cm and samples collected every 50 cm), totaling 287 samples. The distribution of the boreholes is shown in Fig. 1. The analysis of major elements, pH, organic matter, and other test items was conducted by the laboratory of the Mudanjiang Comprehensive Natural Resources Survey Center. Additionally, grain-size analysis was conducted by the Heilongjiang Provincial Geological and Mineral Research Center. 81 samples were collected from boreholes FJZK05 and FJZK06 for X-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments to test their mineral composition.

Standardization of rare earth elements

The concentration of rare earth elements (REEs) in all sediment samples was normalized to the average REE concentration of North American Shale (NASC). The corresponding normalized REE values were calculated using the method of Taylor and McLennan (1985). Fractionation indices [(La/Lu) NASC and (Nd/Yb)NASC] were used to quantify the fractionation between LREEs and HREEs. The normalized values of (Lu) NASCs of adjacent rare earth elements were extrapolated to calculate Ce (Ce/Ce*) and Eu (Eu/Eu*) anomalies. Results and discussion

Borehole characteristics

The bottom of the borehole consists of a gravelly sand layer with coarse sediment particles, reflecting a strong hydrodynamic environment, typical of fluvial deposits. Above this, there is often a layer of loam and sandy loam with fine sediment particles, reflecting a weaker hydrodynamic environment, indicative of lacustrine deposits. Above the lacustrine deposits, there are alternating layers of sandy loam, loam, and sand, reflecting fluctuating hydrodynamic environments characteristic of river-floodplain-lacustrine deposits. Variation in sedimentary facies in the borehole reflects differential uplift of the Sanjiang Plain since the Neogene. The specific sedimentary stratigraphy of the borehole is shown in Fig. 2.

Quaternary stratigraphic correlation and sedimentary facies distribution of nine boreholes across the Sanjiang Plain.

Physical and chemical properties

The sediments from the nine boreholes can be divided into two parts based on average particle size: < 165.11 μm and > 165.11 μm (Fig. 3). Sediments with particle size < 165.11 μm are mainly distributed in the upper part of the boreholes in lacustrine and river-floodplain-lacustrine sedimentary layers, occasionally found in fluvial facies. The particle size is mainly concentrated below 80 μm, with textures primarily consisting of loam, silty loam, sandy loam, and colors ranging from black to yellow. Compared to coarse-grained sediments, the content of organic matter, Al2O3, Fe2O3, MgO, TiO2, P2O5, MnO, and other components is higher, while the content of SiO2, Na2O, K2O, and other components is lower. The pH is slightly acidic (Fig. 3).

Violin plot of particle size and major element distribution.

Sediments with particle size > 165.11 μm are mainly distributed in the lower part of the boreholes in fluvial facies and are interbedded with sediments < 165.11 μm in the river-floodplain-lacustrine sedimentary layers. They are occasionally found in the upper lacustrine sedimentary layers. The particle size is mainly concentrated between 300 and 500 μm, with a texture primarily consisting of sandy soil, and the color is light grayish-white and light yellow. Compared to fine-grained sediments, the content of SiO2, Na2O, K2O, and other components is higher, while the content of organic matter, Al2O3, Fe2O3, MgO, TiO2, P2O5, MnO, and other components is lower. The pH is slightly alkaline (Fig. 3).

In the sandstone classification diagram38 (Fig. 4), sediments with particle size > 165.11 μm are distributed at the junction of lithic sandstone, detrital sandstone, and feldspathic sandstone, while sediments with particle size < 165.11 μm are mainly found in the lithic sandstone zone. This may be due to strong weather during sedimentation, which depleted feldspar minerals. In the A-CN-K ternary diagram39 (Fig. 5), sediments with particle size > 165.11 μm are concentrated in the center of the diagram, with CIA values below 60. Some samples shift along the CN-K line, indicating that they were almost unaffected by chemical weathering39 and were influenced by potassium (K) alteration after deposition. Sediments with particle sizes < 165.11 μm generally plot along the A-CN line, with CIA values mostly > 60, indicating strong chemical weathering40. Plagioclase is weathered to form illite, kaolinite, and montmorillonite, and the end of the line shifts toward the Al2O3 apex, suggesting that potassium feldspar and illite were decomposed, and alkalis were lost. The A-CN-K ternary diagram shows that sediments of different particle sizes have similar original rock compositions but differ in the intensity of weathering during sedimentation.

Mineralogical characteristics

The mineral composition was determined by comparing the XRD test results with standard mineral PDF cards. The primary minerals are mainly quartz, potassium feldspar, and sodium feldspar, with a small amount of tremolite. The secondary clay minerals include illite, chlorite, kaolinite, and montmorillonite (Table 1 in the supplementary materials). Compared with grain-size data, the clay mineral content in fine-grained sediments is significantly higher than in coarse-grained sediments (Fig. 6), indicating that weathering has indeed caused feldspar minerals in fine-grained sediments to be more extensively converted to clay minerals. In contrast, coarse-grained sediments are less affected by weather and more strongly reflect the mineral composition of the source area.

Rare earth fractionation patterns

From the NASC normalization diagram (Fig. 9), it can be seen that the distribution patterns of rare earth elements in fine-grained sediments and coarse-grained sediments are quite different: The rare earth element distribution pattern in fine-grained sediments is similar to that of the Sanjiang Plain wetland sediments34, with higher overall REE content, a flatter curve, slight Ce depletion, and a negative Eu anomaly. The rare-earth element distribution pattern in coarse-grained sediments is similar to that of the widely distributed biotite granites in the Sanjiang Plain41, with lower overall REE content, a rightward-sloping curve, Eu enrichment, and depletion of Dy and Er.

Rare earth element (REE) distribution patterns of the studied sediments normalized to the North American Shale Composite (NASC).

Although the rare earth element distribution patterns in sediments of different particle sizes have some differences, the two types of sediments are interbedded. Since no large-scale tectonic movements have occurred in the Sanjiang Plain since the Quaternary, with only differential uplift in the surrounding mountains and intermittent subsidence in the plains, the source area of the basin sedimentation has not changed42, the sediments of both particle sizes should have the same source, and the differences in their rare earth element distribution patterns should be the result of migration during the weathering process.

Coarse-grained sediments better preserve the rare-earth-element characteristics of the source area. Although the Eu positive anomaly is slightly weaker, the value remains relatively high, likely due to partial feldspar weathering, which results in Eu loss43. Fine-grained sediments are more strongly weathered, with most feldspar undergoing weathering. The resulting clay minerals easily absorb rare earth elements, leading to a higher overall in fine-grained sediments. Additionally, weathering of feldspar minerals also results in Eu depletion. It is worth noting that the fractionation of LREEs and HREEs in fine-grained sediments is significantly smaller than in coarse-grained sediments. This may be related to the different geochemical behaviors of LREEs and HREEs. The geochemical behavior of LREEs is more like that of Ca, making them relatively easy to dissolve and not easily adsorbed. In contrast, the geochemical behavior of HREEs is more like that of Al, making them more easily adsorbed and less likely to dissolve44. As a result, during the weathering process, LREEs are lost to a greater extent, while HREEs are relatively enriched.

Enrichment factors of rare earth elements

A correlation analysis between soil physicochemical properties and total rare earth element content reveals a strong positive correlation between and the contents of TiO2 and Al2O3, and a strong negative correlation with Na2O, K2O, and sediment particle size. Ti and Al are less mobile elements, while Na and K are more mobile. During the weathering process, feldspar minerals decompose to form clay minerals, and the particle size of the sediments decreases, resulting in the loss of Na and K. Al2O3 is a major component of clay minerals, and less is easily adsorbed onto clay minerals, leading to the enrichment of TiO2 and Al2O330. Conclusions

The study found that the vertical distribution of rare earth elements in the sediments of the Sanjiang Plain exhibits a “grain size effect”, with sediments of different grain sizes showing distinct rare earth element distribution characteristics: In sediments with particle size < 165.11 μm, is higher, Eu is depleted, and (La/Lu)NASC and (Nd/Yb)NASC are lower. In sediments with particle size > 165.11 μm, is lower, Eu is enriched, and (La/Lu) NASC and (Nd/Yb) NASC are higher.

Sediments of different grain sizes have the same source, and the primary reason for the different rare earth element characteristics is the varying intensity of weathering. Coarse-grained sediments are less affected by weather, primarily reflecting the rare-earth-element characteristics of the source area. Additionally, because they contain more feldspar minerals, Eu shows a positive anomaly. Fine-grained sediments are more strongly weathered, with feldspar weathering producing clay minerals that readily adsorb rare earth elements30. Primary minerals release Fe2+ and Mn2+ during weathering, and some of these ions reprecipitate as Fe and Mn oxides1, leading to higher overall in fine-grained sediments. Additionally, the weathering of feldspar minerals also results in Eu depletion.

The different geochemical behaviors of LREEs and HREEs result in the degree of fractionation of LREEs and HREEs in fine-grained sediments being smaller than in coarse-grained sediments. The geochemical behavior of LREEs is more like that of Ca, making them relatively easy to dissolve and not easily adsorbed. In contrast, the geochemical behavior of HREEs is more like that of Al, making them more easily adsorbed and less likely to dissolve44.

Human activities have increased the organic matter content in the surface soils of the Sanjiang Plain, thereby affecting the vertical distribution of rare earth elements.

Towards million-token context windows: a topology-preserving framework for adaptive transformer sparsification
Authors: Anderson R. Santos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Transformer self-attention and billion-node network analyses share a key limitation: all-to-all evaluation creates an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} computational cost. Existing methods address this by either distributing the workload across hardware or substituting recurrent operators. This trades associative recall for efficiency. We present Reduced Interaction Sampling (RIS), a stochastic sparsification framework. RIS computes only a fraction of possible pairwise interactions. By leveraging topological redundancy in real-world networks, RIS separates structural accuracy from computational expense. For example, on the com-LiveJournal graph with 4 million nodes, RIS preserves the degree centrality rank (ρ = 0.96) while using only 10% of the edges. A partition-based setup, RIS-Structural, identifies twice as many hubs as sliding-window methods under heavy sparsity (1.00% vs 0.50%, p=0.033). In TinyLlama-1.1B attention tests (0.5k–65k tokens), RIS achieves a geometric reach of about 21k tokens at 65k–outperforming Longformer (≈2k) and BigBird (≈17k). Window-based models surpass \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^5$$\end{document} Cumulative Attention Mass but lose 98% of hub recovery. This shows that dense scalar weights poorly reflect long-range geometric reach. RIS maintains a stable Hub Recall with up to 128 times longer sequences and an edge budget below 0.01%. The quadratic bottleneck in modern computing

The Transformer’s self-attention mechanism 1 evaluates all N × N token pairs. At \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^5$$\end{document} tokens 2, a single attention layer requires tens of gigabytes; doubling the sequence length quadruples the cost. From Gemini 1.5 3 to Gemini 3.1 Pro 4, and Claude 3 5 to Claude 4.6 6, context windows have been extended to the millions, but primarily through Ring Attention 7—splitting the sequence across hundreds of TPUs in a communication ring—and through KV-cache compression. The quadratic cost is not eliminated; it is sharded across hardware budgets unavailable to the broader research community. That the “Lost in the Middle” degradation 8 persists even in frontier models suggests that high cumulative Attention Mass does not guarantee structural retrieval fidelity.

The same barrier recurs across domains. Pangenomic network construction requires all-to-all genome comparisons to resolve coevolutionary modules and horizontal gene transfer events 9; at N=10,000 bacterial strains, the comparison space exceeds 49 million pairs. In financial monitoring, detecting money laundering rings requires global transaction-flow analysis—the cost forces regulators to rely on local heuristics that miss cross-network structures. Epidemic super-spreader identification faces the same limit at the population scale.

Sequenced genomes double every 18 months; language model training corpora grow faster. A single large Transformer can emit as much CO\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_2$$\end{document} as five automobiles over their lifetimes 10.

Existing approaches and their limits

Hardware optimization (FlashAttention 11 and its successors) recomputes attention blocks on-the-fly to avoid materializing the full N × N matrix. Speedups of 2–4× are real, but complexity is unchanged: doubling sequence length still quadruples cost.

Architectural replacement—Mamba 12, RWKV 13, Retentive Networks 14—runs in O(N) by substituting recurrent or convolutional operators for self-attention. The concession is global associative recall; long-range multi-hop reasoning suffers 15. Hybrid approaches such as Titans 16 embed external neural memory to recover some of that capacity at O(N) cost, though large-scale empirical evidence remains sparse.

Predefined sparsity—Sparse Transformers 17, Longformer 18, BigBird 19—applies fixed sliding windows, strided patterns, or random connections. These reduce the cost for a single domain at a time but require manual re-engineering when the problem topology changes.

Commercial infrastructure scaling: Equivalent frontier models (from Gemini 1.5 3 scaling to Gemini 3.1 Pro 4, and Claude 3 5 to 4.6 6) reach million-token windows through Ring Attention 7 and undisclosed sparsity enhancements. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} complexity is not reduced – it is sharded across prohibitively expensive hardware. The “Lost in the Middle” effect 8 demonstrates the residual cost: retrieval fidelity degrades for information embedded at mid-context even in systems with nominally unlimited windows.

Parallel to our structural sparsification approach, recent work on quantization of the KV cache – such as PolarQuant 20 and the subsequent TurboQuant framework 21 – demonstrates that compressing individual vector representations can be combined with topological sampling to achieve compounded gains in memory efficiency and throughput.

Graph-theoretic insight: the redundancy hypothesis

Erdős and Rényi 22 established that a random graph on N nodes becomes connected with high probability when its average degree exceeds łog N. It requires only O(N łog N) edges, far fewer than a clique would require.

Dense interaction matrices are topologically over-specified. In scale-free networks (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P(k) \sim k^{-\gamma }$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma \in [2,3]$$\end{document}), a few high-degree hubs carry most of the connectivity. These hubs and the bridging edges between them determine degree centrality, betweenness, and community structure 23. Recent advances in identifying influential nodes have introduced sophisticated metrics based on structural diversity and laplacian energy to better rank these critical hubs 40,42,44. Sparse sampling preserves this backbone while discarding the redundant mass. RIS is built entirely around this structural asymmetry, proving that topological charge—not raw Attention Mass—is the primary driver of long-range semantic coherence.

The RIS framework

We originally developed RIS to navigate the combinatorial explosion of protein-protein interaction prediction in the GenPPi software 24. Here, we generalize it as a Stochastic Clique Decomposition framework with two operational configurations: RIS-Stochastic A pure stochastic sampler establishing the O(N) or O(N łog N) complexity guarantee. It treats the input as a structureless complete graph beyond the immediate pivot, maximizing computational reduction.RIS-Structural A partition-based configuration that organizes nodes into dense intra-block sublists before sampling global edges. This design preserves local community structure in biological and linguistic data, improving hub recovery in real-world networks at the cost of a modest overhead relative to RIS-Stochastic.Both configurations share the Heuristic Adaptive Function (HAF), which scales sampling density with system size and reduces total complexity to O(N) for N ≥ 3000.

Results

Experimental design

Benchmarking covered five canonical network topologies: Erdős–Rényi (ER) 22, Barabási–Albert (BA) 39, Watts–Strogatz (WS) 31,32, Stochastic Block Model (SBM) 41, and Power-law Cluster (PLC) 43, spanning distinct structural paradigms in network science 25 (see Supplementary Note 3). For each topology and system size N, 30 random instances were generated to evaluate centrality preservation.

Understanding the synthetic-vs-real gap

Synthetic experiments show lower correlations (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rho \approx 0.35$$\end{document}) than real-world networks (ρ > 0.96). The gap reflects the ground-truth definition: synthetic benchmarks are evaluated against the dense complete graph, while real-world networks are naturally sparse. In sparse ground-truth graphs, hubs dominate random samples and centrality rankings align closely (see Supplementary Note 3).

Topological preservation beyond centrality

Degree correlation is one dimension of structural fidelity.

1. Top-K Hub Recall. In synthetic High-Fidelity experiments (N=10,000, 5% density), RIS recovered \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\approx 70\%}$$\end{document} of the top-100 hubs; on com-LiveJournal (q=0.01), recall reached 99.7%. The long tail of low-degree nodes introduces noise into global ranking correlation, while the high-degree skeleton is preserved more reliably.

2. Connectivity and Path Lengths. The largest connected component in com-LiveJournal (q=0.01) retained >98% of the original nodes; average shortest path lengths rose from ≈ 5.3 to ≈ 20, a bounded increase that preserves the small-world property.

3. Clustering Coefficient. RIS reduces local clustering coefficients, as triangles are easily broken by random sampling. At q=0.01, the global clustering coefficient fell to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 60\%$$\end{document} of the original. Community structure remained intact enough to support node classification tasks, indicating that macro-scale modularity is more resilient than micro-scale triangulation.

Why RIS surpasses random edge (RE)

RIS and RE show near-identical global centrality on scale-free networks (Table 2), yet two architectural properties differentiate them: Parallel Decomposability RE requires global access to the full edge list for uniform sampling–infeasible for streaming data or matrices whose \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N^2$$\end{document} pairs have not been materialized. RIS’s partition-based design allows completely independent processing of sub-blocks without global coordination.Adaptive Fidelity RE is a fixed, density-blind sampler. HAF adjusts to local density, preserving community structure in WS-type graphs (Fig. 1) where uniform random sampling tends to disconnect smaller communities.

The stabilization zone and phase transitions

Figure 1 shows a Stabilization Zone for N ≤ 1000, where centrality correlation remains high (ρ > 0.75) across scale-free topologies. This corresponds to HAF’s Dense-Coverage and Transition phases, during which the sampling density captures most of the local structure. Beyond N = 2000, HAF enters Saturation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_{sat}$$\end{document} constant), and correlation declines but persists above ρ = 0.3 for scale-free networks.

The cost-fidelity trade-off shows a phase transition near N = 2000: below this threshold, RIS operates as a precision instrument at moderate savings; above it, hub over-representation preserves the structural skeleton as scalability takes precedence.

The regularity paradox: Why RIS struggles with homogeneity

RIS performs better on purely random graphs (ER) than on structured small-world (WS) or community graphs (SBM):

Watts–Strogatz Networks: Degree centrality is nearly uniform. Low-degree variance makes node ranking sensitive to sampling noise: two nodes with true degree 10 may be observed at degrees 8 and 12, inverting their relative ranks.

Erdős–Rényi Networks: Degrees follow a Poisson distribution with variance equal to the mean. Sufficient degree variability allows random sampling to preserve relative rankings, and the stochastic ground truth itself is forgiving of sampling imprecision.

Stochastic Block Models: RIS treats all nodes uniformly and ignores community structure. In dense communities with sparse inter-community edges, it oversamples inter-community edges relative to their true density, distorting centrality rankings.

RIS is suited for hub-heavy architectures: social networks 27, biological interactomes 28, semantic networks in language 29, and the Internet 30.

Edge recall: the redundancy of dense connections

Figure 3 presents a complementary perspective: edge recall, defined as the fraction of ground truth edges recovered by RIS. The results appear paradoxical at first glance–recall drops to <5% for large N, yet topological preservation (Fig. 1) is maintained. This apparent contradiction resolves when one recognizes that dense cliques contain substantial informational redundancy.

In a complete graph, each edge contributes minimally to global topology because alternative paths abound. Consider the shortest path between nodes A and C: in a complete graph, both the direct edge (A, C) and the two-hop path \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C$$\end{document} exist for every intermediate node B. Removing (A, C) does not disconnect A and C-it merely increases their distance from 1 to 2. As long as sufficient random edges are retained, the network remains connected, and path lengths remain bounded.

This observation aligns with the small-world property: in real-world networks, the average path length grows logarithmically with N 31. RIS leverages this property by sampling sufficient edges to maintain O(łog N) length paths, even when discarding >95% of edges. The retained edges form a “structural skeleton” that captures the network’s essential topology–its hubs, communities, and connectivity patterns–without the redundant edges that inflate computational cost without enhancing topological information.Fig. 3Edge Recall Analysis. Downstream task performance

To assess practical utility beyond topological metrics, RIS was evaluated on two canonical graph learning tasks:

Link Prediction: Using the RIS-sparsified com-LiveJournal graph at q=0.1, a supervised link predictor (logistic regression on common neighbors, preferential attachment score, and resource allocation index) achieved AUC=0.887 on held-out test edges from the original ground truth–versus AUC=0.923 on 100% of ground-truth edges. The 3.6-point gap is modest given 90% edge reduction.

Node Classification (Synthetic Task): A 2-layer GCN trained on RIS-sparsified data achieved an accuracy of 0.78 at q=0.1 on an SBM community detection task (full graph: 0.82). At q=0.05, accuracy dropped to 0.71, suggesting q ≥ 0.05 as a floor for community-sensitive applications.

Empirical validation on large language models

We tested RIS natively on natural language processing tasks by analyzing the self-attention matrices of the TinyLlama-1.1B model 36. The model processed contiguous sequences of English Wikipedia text scaling from 512 to 8192 tokens. To scale beyond the physical hardware limits (Out-Of-Memory) to 65,536 tokens, we supplemented the empirical texts with simulated topological matrices that represent the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} scaling.

Parametric isometry and the sparsity budget

The edge budget across all evaluated architectures was locked to the sparsity ceiling generated by the RIS logarithmic function. At \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N=32{,}768$$\end{document} tokens, the logarithmic strategy (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R \approx 3 \log _2 N$$\end{document}) selects R=45 edges per node from 32,767 possible connections, yielding a local density of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 0.13\%$$\end{document}. The “0.01% budget” cited throughout the scaling analysis is the asymptotic limit as N approaches the million-token regime, where the O(N łog N) cost becomes negligible relative to the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} space. Fixing this budget forces Longformer and BigBird to operate under the same tensor constraint as RIS, establishing methodological equivalence across architectures.

Large contexts and hub degradation

Scaling from 0.5 to 65k tokens reveals a structural divergence that Hub Recall alone does not capture. At 65k tokens, Longformer’s MHD is 1996.7–bounded by its window, it never perceives more than ≈3% of the document’s distal extent. This geometric confinement provides a mechanistic account of the “Lost in the Middle” failure 8: the tokens that determine long-range coherence are simply outside the sampler’s reach. BigBird extends that reach to 17,475.5 tokens at the same budget, as its stochastic attention component introduces distal connections the local window cannot. The gain is real, but it does not equal the 21,614.3 tokens of structural span achieved by RIS. Discussion

Balancing efficiency and fidelity

The synthetic Barab ási-Albert benchmarks return a modest centrality correlation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rho \approx 0.15$$\end{document}–0.36). This results from a strict parameter selection: we prioritized computational scalability over absolute ranking precision.

The default Logarithmic strategy bypasses the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} limitation, cutting-edge evaluations by >99.5% at N=10,000. Sampling just 0.4% of the matrix naturally introduces noise among low-degree nodes.

We relaxed the sparsity constraints on synthetic graphs to test the algorithm’s fidelity ceiling. Bumping the sampling budget to 5% (K=500) pushed the centrality correlation to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rho \approx 0.81$$\end{document}, capturing nearly 70% of the top-100 hubs. Real-world interaction networks typically operate at densities well below 1%. When RIS parameters match the target density, the algorithm preserves relative structure effectively across domains.

Lossy compression of network topology

Figure 3 indicates RIS samples 2–5% of ground-truth edges for N < 2000, dropping to 0.12% at N = 10,000. The algorithm operates as a lossy compression mechanism: discarding 99% of the data still allows the reconstruction of global path lengths and hub hierarchies.

This principle directly translates to LLM attention matrices. An N=10,000 sequence generates nearly 50 million pairs. Our data indicates less than 5% of those pairings hold semantic weight. The rest is topological noise.

The architectural divergence: global topology versus local mass

The large-scale natural language evaluation (Table 5) exposes a critical architectural divergence between scalar Attention Mass and Topological Charge retention.

The Attention Mass vs. Topology Divergence. Continuous window architectures (Longformer) aggregate high volumes of scalar mass by mapping grammatically dense, adjacent loops (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rho \rightarrow 0.94$$\end{document} in local degree). However, this neighborhood density fractures during long-scale retention, correlating weakly with global Hub Recall (dropping to 0.61% at 32k). As evidenced by the budget math, capturing massive local weights–reaching a Cumulative Attention Mass of 103,269 at 32,768 tokens (Table 5)–is an artifact of sequence order; identifying global hubs using only 0.01% of the interaction budget requires a fundamental shift from local heuristics to global topological extraction. RIS operates with nearly 7.5× less mass (13,694) than Longformer at the same scale while delivering superior structural recovery. This shows that the large-scale semantic hierarchy rests on rare distal edges rather than on dense local clusters.

The Stochastic Floor in Giant Uniform Matrices. The physical memory limit forced the generation of surrogate, Random Uniform topology matrices for regimes 16k to 65k. In this synthetic vacuum–void of the natural distributive preference of language–mechanisms based on probabilist pseudo-random connections (p=1/N), such as BigBird, naturally reach their stochastic floor (1.03%). Methodological convergence (1.07% for RIS) illustrates the statistical asymptote of sampling in white-noise distributions. While random connections perform adequately within the isolated spatial noise of synthetic tools, only adaptive sampling (RIS) demonstrates concurrent dominance across the empirical semantic structures observed in natural text (N ≤ 8192).

Structural Computational Cost (Sliding Windows vs. Linear Efficiency). The parametric disadvantage of continuous windows against an extreme locked budget (<0.01%) lies in their algorithmic striding traversal cost. To stabilize global recovery under major scalar restrictions, fixed patterns demand proportional expansion of their diagonal bandwidth, inducing a secondary geometric recrudescence of complexity. Pure stochastic sampling (RIS) bypasses contiguous spatial continuity, recovering hub features in strict O(N) linear time by slicing the structural gradient position-agnostically. Limitations and application boundaries

RIS-Structural, in its current form, is strictly bounded to static, unweighted, and undirected networks. For directed structures—such as citation or metabolic networks—the current sampling logic is not natively designed to capture asymmetric flow (in- vs out-degree). While extensions utilizing directed centrality measures like PageRank 45 or Katz centrality 46 might be theoretically trivial, they are beyond the current framework. Similarly, while RIS can technically sample weighted networks by carrying over existing edge weights, it does not natively utilize these weights to guide the stochastic selection process. Temporal dynamic networks are fundamentally out of scope for the current static implementation, as inserting new nodes in a streaming setting would mandate continuous resampling to preserve global connectivity.

On the theoretical side, bounding the centrality-preservation error via concentration inequalities is the nearest open problem. Architecturally, supporting weighted directed edges or eigenvector-based centrality variants 47 remains an area for future architectural expansion, not a feature of the present design.

Conclusion

RIS exploits the topological redundancy of dense networks to achieve >99% computational reduction while preserving structural properties–particularly in the scale-free topologies that characterize natural and social systems.

Three results stand out. The Heuristic Adaptive Function smoothly transitions from high-fidelity local sampling to constant-cost global connectivity, mirroring the phase transition from small to large systems. Hub structure is preserved through statistical over-representation: in heterogeneous networks, high-degree nodes appear in random samples with disproportionate frequency. And RIS recovers only 2–5% of ground-truth edges at moderate scales (N ≈ 1500) and 0.12% at N = 10,000, yet degree centrality correlations remain positive (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rho \approx 0.15$$\end{document}–0.35) for scale-free graphs–which suggests dense connectivity carries substantial informational redundancy.

The framework applies beyond its proteomic origins. Transformer architectures, pangenomic analysis, financial monitoring, and sustainable AI all face variants of the same \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} barrier. RIS reframes the problem as topological compression: retaining the structural skeleton while discarding the redundant edges that inflate cost without contributing information.

Four directions remain open: formal probabilistic bounds on topological preservation, extension to weighted and dynamic networks, structure-aware variants, and integration of RIS-based attention into production-scale language models. Scalability need not require larger hardware budgets; it can come from recognizing the structural redundancy already present in complex systems.

Problem formulation

Graph Representation and Edge Counting. Throughout this work, pairwise relationships are modeled as undirected graphs without self-loops, appropriate for symmetric relationships (e.g., protein-protein interactions, social networks, co-occurrence in text). Let G = (V, E) be such a graph with N = |V| entities. In the extreme case of a complete graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_N$$\end{document}, the number of edges is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|E| = \left( {\begin{array}{c}N\\ 2\end{array}}\right) = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$$\end{document}, which is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} but strictly less than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N^2$$\end{document}.

Note on Attention Matrices: When discussing Transformer self-attention as a motivating application, the attention matrix is N × N and includes diagonal entries (self-attention), yielding \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N^2$$\end{document} scalar values. However, for the graph-theoretic analysis that follows, the focus is on the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {\begin{array}{c}N\\ 2\end{array}}\right)$$\end{document} unique pairwise interactions, treating self-attention as a distinct local operation. Reproducibility and stochastic seeding

Both synthetic graph generation and RIS sampling use deterministic pseudo-random seeds derived from configuration parameters. Synthetic topology sweeps utilized 30 replicates; large-scale real-world benchmarks (com-LiveJournal) were escalated to 500 and 20,000 replicates as specified in their respective results sections. All figures plot the mean across replicates. A –randomize flag perturbs the seed base and can be used to confirm that findings are not artifacts of a particular random sequence.

Algorithmic core

RIS approximates the complete pairwise comparison space over N entities with a sparse graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'_{RIS} = (V, E')$$\end{document} through three stochastic phases:

Phase 1: Stochastic Shuffling. Input entities are randomly permuted to eliminate ordering artifacts. Real-world datasets often exhibit systematic ordering (e.g., genomes by phylogeny, documents chronologically) that can introduce spurious correlations.

Phase 2: Mode Selection. Stochastic Mode treats the shuffled set as a single pool and draws R(N) neighbors per pivot globally. Structural Mode (Stochastic Clique Decomposition) first partitions the shuffled set into blocks of size \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B = \min (0.1N, B_{max})$$\end{document}, fully connects each block, then adds global redundant edges. The ceiling \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B_{max}$$\end{document} (e.g. 2048, consistent with FlashAttention SRAM tile sizes and common Transformer window widths) bounds the intra-block clique cost to a constant, keeping the overall complexity at O(N) as N grows. Without this cap, block size scales with N and the clique construction regresses to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document}.

Phase 3: Heuristic Adaptive Sampling. For each pivot, R(N) neighbors are drawn uniformly from the remaining nodes to maintain long-range connectivity. R(N) is governed by the HAF (Equation 1), ensuring O(łog N) or O(1) samples per node depending on the chosen strategy.

Complexity analysis

The sampling phase dominates the computational complexity of RIS. As R(N) saturates to a constant \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_{sat}$$\end{document} for large N, the total complexity scales linearly as O(N). For RIS-Structural, the intra-block clique construction is bounded by a constant \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B_{max}$$\end{document} (e.g., 2048), ensuring that the quadratic cost of fully connecting a block does not scale with N. Thus, the total complexity remains \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N \cdot B_{max} + N \cdot K_{sat}) \approx O(N)$$\end{document}. Detailed complexity proofs and a formal cost breakdown for both modes are provided in Supplementary Note 2.

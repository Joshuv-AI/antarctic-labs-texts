Comparison of cyclic fatigue properties of four nickel-titanium single-file systems in curved artificial canals
Authors: Jian Wang, Zhaowei Tai, Xuechun Wang, Junli Han, Jiang Tao
Published: 2026

Introduction

Nickel-titanium (NiTi) rotary instruments have revolutionized endodontic treatment due to their superior flexibility, superelasticity, and cutting efficiency compared with traditional stainless steel files [1–3]. These properties enable NiTi instruments to navigate the complex and curved anatomy of root canals more effectively, thereby reducing the risk of canal transportation and perforation [4]. However, despite these advantages, the risk of instrument fracture remains a significant concern in clinical practice [5, 6]. Fractures can occur through two primary mechanisms: cyclic fatigue and torsional failure [5]. Of there, cyclic fatigue is particularly critical, as it results from repeated bending and compression of the file within curved canals, which leads to the formation of microcracks and eventual separation [2].

Resistance to cyclic fatigue is influenced by several factors, including the design, alloy composition, and heat treatment of the instrument [7–9]. Recent advancements in NiTi technology have introduced various heat-treated alloys, such as M-Wire, Gold-Wire, and Blue-Wire, which improve the mechanical properties of the instruments, including flexibility and fatigue resistance [1, 3, 10–12]. Additionally, the geometry of the instrument, such as its cross-sectional design and taper, plays a crucial role in determining its performance in curved canals [10, 11, 13]. Studies have shown that instruments with smaller tapers and thinner shafts may reduce stress on the root dentin, thereby preserving peri-cervical dentin and improving the long-term prognosis of endodontically treated teeth [5, 14].

Single-file systems have been introduced in dental clinics to simplify procedures during the root canal therapy. Reciproc Blue (VDW, Germany) (REC), ORODEKA ONEPLEX (ORODEKA, China) (ORO) and M3-L 2018 (UDG, China) (M3L) gained great popularity as the prevailing single-file systems among Chinese dental communities. TruNatomy Prime (Dentsply Sirona, Germany) (TRU) is a newly-introduced system that is not widely used in China. Combined with the three aforementioned single-file systems, these instruments feature variable taper designs and unique features that enhances the efficiency, safety, and precision of endodontic treatment. Despite the development of these advanced instruments, comparative data on the cyclic fatigue resistance of these single-file systems in artificial canals with varying curvatures remains limited. Previous research has primarily focused on the performance of multifile systems or single-file systems with specific heat treatments and has rarely examined domestic single-file systems [12, 15]. However, the increasing popularity of single-file systems in clinical practice necessitates a comprehensive evaluation of their cyclic fatigue properties, particularly in simulated curved canals that replicate the challenging anatomy of human root canals. Such evaluations can provide valuable insights into the clinical performance and durability of these instruments and help dental practitioners make informed decisions when selecting the appropriate file system for specific cases.

Therefore, this study aims to compare the cyclic fatigue resistance of four commercially available NiTi single-file systems (Reciproc Blue, ORODEKA ONEPLEX, M3-L 2018 and TruNatomy) in curved artificial canals with different radii of curvature. By assessing the number of cycles to failure (NCF) and analyzing the fracture surfaces, this study seeks to identify the most fatigue-resistant single-file system and provide evidence-based recommendations for clinical use. The findings of this study will contribute to the ongoing efforts to optimize endodontic instrumentation and enhance the safety and efficacy of root canal treatment.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study evaluated four types of single-file systems: Reciproc Blue (VDW, Germany), ORODEKA ONEPLEX (ORODEKA, China), and M3-L 2018 (UDG, China) and TruNatomy (Dentsply Sirona, Germany). No humans or animals were included in the study; therefore, approval from an ethics committee was not required. In accordance with similar published studies, the sample size was calculated using G*Power 3.1.9.6 software (Heinrich-Heine-Universitat, Düsseldorf, Germany). Using an ANOVA test with an alpha error rate of 0.05, beta level of 0.2, and an effect size of 0.8, the analysis indicated that 15 samples per group would be sufficient to detect statistically significant differences [3, 16].

Before the cyclic fatigue tests, all the files were examined at x20 magnification under a dental operating microscope (Zumax OMS2380, Suzhou, China) to exclude any defects or deformations. All files from the four brands were 25 mm in length, size 25/06 with 15 instruments in each group.

Establish static artificial root canal in vitro

The customized device was built based on a computer-aided design (CAD) blueprint (Fig. 1), based on a previous study by Tong Zhongchun et al. [17]. Artificial canal models were then manufactured for cyclic fatigue testing by a stainless steel and metal product company (Yuhong Taizhou, China). Three curved canal models were designed and generated at angles of 30°, 45° and double-curved canals (S-shaped). The parameters and picture were listed in Fig. 1.

CAD design of 3 canals with different angles of curvature (A), established stainless steel artificial canal models (B) and 4 single-file systems under a dental operating microscope (C)

Cyclic fatigue fracture test

The files were operated in an endodontic motor with the recommended settings. REC, ORO, M3L, and TRU were rotated in an electric motor (VDW; Germany) according to the manufacturer’s recommendations as follows: ORO at 400 rpm and 300 g/cm torque; M3L at 400 rpm and 2.0 N / cm torque; TRU at 500 rpm and 1.5 N༏cm torque; REC used the preset Reciproc ALL mode. All tests were performed at room temperature during the day in March in Shanghai, China. To minimize instrument friction and simulate the procedure of root canal therapy in vivo, the artificial root canal was coated with a consistent lubricant (glycerin) before the nickel-titanium instrument was inserted [10], as previously described. The file was inserted into the working length and then the motor was initiated. Time stopped when fracture was detected visually and/or audibly. The time to fracture (FT) (in seconds) was recorded, and the NCF value was calculated using the formula: NCF= time (s) to fracture × rotational speed (rpm) / 60 (s).

SEM analysis

The fractured surfaces of the four types of files were collected and ultrasonicated before SEM testing. The fractured segments were prepared for SEM (ZEISS, Sigma 300, Germany) analysis to examine topographic features. SEM imaging was performed at magnifications of X100, X200, X500, and X1000 to capture detailed fracture surfaces images.

Statistical analysis

The normality of the distributions was confirmed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Statistical analysis was performed using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for comparisons within the group, and Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) test was employed to analyze the differences between the groups. Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation values. The significant level was set at p < 0.05 using the statistical software SPSS version 27 (IBM, U.S.A).

Discussions

Ni-Ti single-file systems have become a cornerstone of modern root canal therapy, gaining widespread popularity among clinical practitioners [18, 19]. However, similar to traditional sequential Ni-Ti files, these single-file systems are also prone to cyclic fatigue, which can lead to instrument unwinding and eventual separation—a common intraoperative complication in endodontic treatment [14, 20]. Several factors influencing cyclic fatigue has been identified, including the instrument’s size, cross-sectional design, and manufacturing technology [14, 16, 20, 21]. Additionally, studies have highlighted other potential variables, such as the angle of instrument entry [22], temperature [11, 23], repeated autoclave cycles [24], and motion style (rotational versus reciprocating motion) [9], all of which can affect the mechanical behavior of these instruments.

In curved root canals, Ni-Ti instruments are particularly susceptible to cyclic fatigue. While numerous studies have explored the in vitro cyclic fatigue resistance of various curved root canal systems [10, 16, 17, 25], there is a lack of comparative analysis between two widely used domestic single-file systems (ORODEKA and M3L) and their international counterparts available in the domestic market. This study is the first conduct such a comparative analysis, aiming to provide an evidence-based evaluation of the performance of domestic and international single-file systems in different curved root canals.

Extracted teeth are considered the ideal specimens for in vitro cyclic fatigue resistance studies. However, inherent variations in canal anatomy make it nearly impossible to achieve homogeneity among extracted teeth [9, 26]. Consequently, researchers have adopted customized and standardized in vitro testing equipment as a viable alternative [5, 21, 27]. Building on the relevant literature, particularly that of Tong et al. [17], we designed three types of curved artificial root canal models—45°, 60°, and double 45° (S-shaped)—using stainless steel to provide a consistent and controlled testing environment.

TruNatomy (TRU), a newly launched NiTi file system in 2020, is manufactured using a specialized heat treatment process and slender NiTi wires with a maximum fluted diameter of 0.8 mm and an off-centered parallelogram cross-sectional design. These characteristics allow the instruments to maintain high flexibility while efficiently removing dentin debris during canal preparation. In this study, the performances of four single-file systems—TRU, REC, ORO, and M3L—were evaluated across three different curvature angles. TRU demonstrated excellent resistance to cyclic fatigue, consistent with previous studies [1, 5, 13, 14, 16, 24], in which it outperformed other instruments under various conditions, including autoclave sterilization and temperature variations. Although TRU operates at a higher rotation speed and lower torque, its smaller taper and reduced cross-sectional area, as observed in SEM images, likely contribute to its inherent flexibility and superior cyclic fatigue resistance.

Reciproc Blue is a reciprocating instrument equipped with specialized Blue Wire technology, which is characterized by lower transformation temperatures and a greater proportion of stable martensite compared to M-Wire [23, 28]. This resulted in a softer and more flexible NiTi file. In the present study, although Reciproc Blue showed slightly lower performance than TRU across all three subgroups, it still outperformed the other two single-file systems. Notably, there was no significant difference between Reciproc Blue and TRU in the 60° subgroup (p > 0.05). The high number of cycles to failure (NCF) observed for Reciproc Blue can likely be attributed to its S-shaped cross-sectional design (Fig. 3) and the aforementioned Blue Wire technology. These findings are consistent with several peer-reviewed studies that have reported similar results when using Reciproc Blue instruments [3, 7, 11, 12, 15, 20–23, 25, 29, 30].

ORO and M3L, two widely used domestic single-file instruments, were included in this study to provide a comprehensive comparison with two international systems (REC and TRU). To date, no studies have compared the cyclic fatigue resistance of M3L or ORO with that of REC or TRU. Consequently, this study is the first report to analyze the cyclic fatigue resistance performance of these four systems. Both ORO and M3L utilize a Controlled Memory (CM) wire process, which is known for its flexibility and durability. Among the four systems, ORO demonstrated a softer touch and greater pre-bending capability, both of which are advantageous in clinical applications. Dongsheng Yu et al. reported that Orodeka Plex 2.0, a multi-file system from ORODEKA, produced the lowest extrusion of apical debris, while Reciproc Blue yielded the highest [19]. Interestingly, in the present study within 60° subgroup, ORO exhibited exceptionally high NCF values (295.17 ± 63.48), closely matching those of the REC system. This finding suggests that ORO has unique strengths in heavily curved canals. In addition, ORO incorporates an optimized geometric design. Its distinctive features—variable pitch and a progressive taper structure—further enhance the file’s resistance to fracture. On the other hand, M3L’s lowest NCF values indicate that it is better suited for straight canals rather than for curved ones. M3L’ s metallurgical characteristics may affect have influenced the experimental results. One study reported M3 exhibited a higher Al-peak Than Pro Taper systems (2.54%) in the element analysis, despite the fact that Al is more accessible to gain a more flexible and fatigue-resistant properties [2]. Therefore, for the preparation of curved canals, M3L may not be the optimal choice, and practitioners may consider alternatives such as TRU, REC, or ORO. In conclusion, when selecting instruments for root canal preparation, the following recommendations should be considered based on different canal anatomy: (1) Straight root canals: Suitable systems include REC, ORO, M3L, or TRU. (2) Moderately curved canals: As the curvature increases, the TRU, REC, and ORO systems are preferred. (3) Severely curved canals (such as S-shaped canals): The TRU system is the optimal choice for preparation.

Although the M3L and ORO systems have demonstrated impressive domestic performance, their adoption in global markets remains relatively low. Currently, dental professionals worldwide have limited familiarity with these two single-file systems and may find it challenging to compare the clinical differences between the four major single-file products. This study holds significance for two key reasons: First, it serves as a valuable reference for international practitioners seeking insights into product diversity within emerging markets. Second, it establishes essential performance evaluation criteria for nickel-titanium instrument systems that have not yet achieved widespread global recognition. Although this study provides valuable insight into the cyclic fatigue properties of NiTi single-file systems, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, although artificial canals were used as a standardized model, the study was conducted from a static perspective and didn’t incorporate dynamic investigations to replicate the complex, multidirectional movements encountered in natural root canal treatment [9]. It is important to recognize that artificial root canal models created in laboratory settings cannot perfectly reproduce the complex anatomical structures of natural tooth root canals. Furthermore, the thermal dynamics observed in living root canal systems differ significantly from those observed in artificial models, and these inherent differences cannot be completely standardized. Second, future research should focus on validating other potential influencing factors, such as rotational speed and various irrigation solutions, to further elucidate their impact on cyclic fatigue resistance. Third, the experiments were performed at room temperature. although the previous studies indicated that the ideal austenitic finishing (Af) temperature (transformation temperature) of the Ni-Ti file was about 29–32 °C [1], slightly lower than the body temperature. Otherwise, during movements in artificial canals, the ambient temperature will rise and has a negative impact on the fatigue resistance of the Ni-Ti files [23]. Therefore, essential strategies such as preoperative cooling may help extend life span.

Conclusion

In summary, this study provides a comparative evaluation of the cyclic fatigue resistance of four single-file endodontic systems. The results indicate that TruNatomy demonstrated significantly greater fatigue resistance than the other three systems, highlighting its enhanced performance in curved root canals. These findings indicate that TruNatomy can serve as a raliable choice for the treatment of intricate root canal anatomies. Nevertheless, additional studies are required to explore other factors affecting the mechanical properties of NiTi instruments and refine their clinical use in endodontics.

IFPHPOS0035 Public Health Learning Hubs in a Local Authority, A Forum for Multidisciplinary Learning and Problem-Solving
Authors: Gethin P Hopkin, Muna Abdel Aziz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSharing multidisciplinary perspectives to address challenges is integral to effective public health practice. Using Kline’s “Time to Think” principles and adapting a programme used elsewhere in public health registrar training and consultant professional development, we established multidisciplinary Public Health Learning Hubs in a local authority public health team. Here, we share our reflections from developing the programme to tackle practical public health problems in a local authority.MethodsLearning Hubs were a monthly meeting in all work diaries. Conducted online, a single volunteer presented a topic or dilemma, followed by another attendee offering a different perspective on the topic in response. In breakout rooms, colleagues spoke individually on the topic or built on the original perspective shared, providing a diverse set of perspectives on the topic.ResultsIntegration as standing meetings protected engagement with Learning Hubs as core business. Attendance and presentations by speakers from a range of different professional groups, including placement students and external partners, appeared to strengthen links across different teams and organisations. Sessions were well attended and we suggest this demonstrates the desire for multidisciplinary and interactive thinking in the public health workplace. Virtual breakout rooms worked effectively, demonstrating the model’s ongoing applicability in the hybrid age.ConclusionOur experience suggests that by fostering an active learning environment, Learning Hubs in Local Authorities can be an effective platform for multidisciplinary knowledge exchange. By offering diverse perspectives on topics, they can illuminate different approaches to tricky problems, stimulate new lines of enquiry or uncover new solutions.

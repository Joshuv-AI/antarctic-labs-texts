Obesity and Keratoconus: A Mediation Study Based on the Chinese Keratoconus Cohort
Authors: Kaili Yang, Anqi Chang, Runqi Tu, Liyan Xu, Yuwei Gu, Qi Fan, Shanshan Yin, Yi Yuan, Yifan Wang, Chenchen Yin, Yonghao Zang, Changxiu Li, Chenjiu Pang, Daniela Oehring, Yibin Hao, Shengwei Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeGrowing evidence indicates a strong association between obesity and keratoconus (KC), although the potential mediating factors remain largely unknown. This study aimed to explore the potential pathways through which corneal biomechanical and topographic parameters might link obesity and KC using structural equation modeling (SEM).MethodsThis case-control study included 759 participants (363 patients with KC and 396 controls) from the Chinese Keratoconus (CKC) Cohort Study. Associations among body mass index (BMI), corneal biomechanical parameters, topographic parameters, and KC were analyzed using generalized linear regression models. SEM was used to construct latent variables for corneal biomechanical and topographic parameters. Three mediation models (single, parallel multiple, and serial multiple mediation) were used to examine the mediating roles of these parameters in the association between BMI and KC.ResultsAfter adjusting for confounders, the odds ratio (OR) for BMI and KC was 1.14 (95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.07–1.22). All corneal biomechanical and topographic parameters showed significant associations with KC (P < 0.05). In the single mediation model, the indirect effect of corneal biomechanical parameters was 0.023 (95% CI = 0.002–0.045), whereas that of topographic parameters was 0.049 (95% CI = 0.023–0.073). The parallel multiple mediation model yielded a total indirect effect of 0.033 (95% CI = 0.013–0.053), with significant pathways through corneal biomechanical parameters (effect = 0.011, 95% CI = 0.000–0.022, 17.2% of total effect) and topographic parameters (effect = 0.022, 95% CI = 0.010–0.038, 35.4% of total effect). A serial mediation pathway was also identified, in which BMI correlated with corneal biomechanical parameters, which subsequently linked to topographic parameters and then KC (effect = 0.021, 95% CI = 0.002–0.039, 32.3% of total effect).ConclusionsHigher BMI was associated with KC. The association between BMI and KC may be partially explained by alterations in corneal biomechanical and topographic parameters, providing a statistical model for understanding potential underlying links in the disease.Translational RelevanceFor patients with obesity, KC-related screening should be emphasized, and health management for patients with KC and obesity should be strengthened in clinical practice.

Introduction

Obesity is a growing public health issue affecting over one billion persons worldwide.1 In addition to various health risks, there is also epidemiological evidence linking obesity to keratoconus (KC).2 KC is a bilateral and asymmetric ocular disease that involves progressive thinning and steepening of the cornea, leading to visual impairment.3–5 It is most prevalent in Middle Eastern and Asian ethnicities, and typically occurs in individuals aged 20 to 39 years.6 There is ample evidence that genetics,7 cellular biochemistry,8,9 and corneal biomechanical factors10,11 play a causal role in the development of KC. However, the specific mechanism by which body mass index (BMI) contributes to KC is largely unknown.

With the development of ophthalmic examination techniques, especially for the Corneal Visualization Scheimpflug Technology (Corvis ST) and Pentacam HR, it is now possible to measure early structural changes in the cornea.12,13 The Corvis ST software can provide new parameters, such as Max Inverse Radius, Deflection Amplitude Ratio Maximum (2 mm zone; DA Ratio Max [2 mm]), Pachymetry Slope, Deflection Amplitude Ratio Maximum (1 mm zone; DA Ratio Max [1 mm]), Ambrosio Relational Thickness (ARTh), Integrated Radius, and Stiffness Parameter at first applanation (SPA1).14 In our previous study, it has been providing evidence that these parameters could effectively diagnose KC.15 The most commonly used topographic parameter measured by the Pentacam HR is the maximum keratometry value (Kmax).16,17 A higher value of Kmax represents a more severe stage of the disease.18 In this study, we chose to explore the relationship between BMI and KC using the parameters of Flat Keratometry (Kf), Steep Keratometry (Ks), Pachymetry at the Corneal Apex (ACT), Pachymetry at the Pupil Center (PCT), Minimum Pachymetry (TCT), and Maximum Keratometry (Kmax).

Recent Mendelian randomization studies provide evidence supporting a potential causal relationship between elevated BMI and KC.19,20 However, the specific mechanistic pathways linking systemic obesity to local corneal pathology remain poorly understood. Investigating such complex “system-to-local” relationships requires an analytical approach capable of modeling multidimensional data and testing indirect effect pathways. Structural equation modeling (SEM) offers a suitable framework for this purpose. Compared with traditional associative analyses, SEM provides distinct advantages for elucidating complex interdependencies among variables by integrating measurement and structural models into a comprehensive analysis.21 As a comprehensive multivariate technique, SEM allows for the testing of theoretical models involving latent variables—unobserved constructs inferred from multiple observed indicators.22 In the measurement model, SEM integrates several correlated clinical parameters (e.g. various biomechanical metrics) into a more robust latent variable (e.g. corneal biomechanical status), effectively representing complex traits while accounting for measurement error.23 Subsequently, the structural model enables the formal specification and testing of hypothesized pathways among these latent and observed variables. Within this SEM framework, mediation analysis can be systematically used to quantify indirect effects. Therefore, this study utilized SEM to explore the potential mediating roles of corneal properties in the BMI-KC association. Specifically, we constructed latent variables for corneal biomechanics and topography, and then tested and compared simple, parallel, and serial multiple mediation models. This analysis aims to provide a statistically grounded, testable model for the potential pathways underlying this association, offering a novel perspective for future mechanistic research.

Materials and Methods

Participants

The KC participants in this study were from the Chinese Keratoconus (CKC) Cohort Study, which has been described elsewhere.24 Control individuals were included in the study based on their gender and age (within 3 years) to ensure comparability with the KC participants. A total of 759 participants (363 patients with KC and 396 control participants) were recruited in the case-control study, after excluding the individuals with missing data on BMI (n = 33; Supplementary Fig. S1).

The standard of diagnostic criteria for KC used in present study were as follows25: presence of at least one positive sign on slit-lamp examination (Munson's sign, Fleischer's ring, Vogt's striae, or corneal scar), a Belin Ambrosio enhanced ectasia total deviation (BAD-D) index ≥2.6 and an asymmetric bowtie pattern with or without skewed axes showed on a corneal topography map.

The inclusion criteria for the control group were participants with a spherical equivalent <8.00 diopters (D), corrected distance visual acuity in LogMAR ≤0.1, and no history of refractive surgery with corneal astigmatism <1.50 D. Informed consent was obtained from each subject or their legal guardians before participation. This study was approved by the institutional review board of our institute (HNEECKY-2019 (5)) and was registered in the clinical trial center (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, ChiCTR1900023046), and followed the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Analysis Parameters

The information about demographic characteristics were collected through face-to-face interviews. The educational levels of the individuals were classified into middle school or below and high school or above. The occupation levels were divided into student and others. The time of using the eye for the subjects according to the question of how much time did you spend using electronic products (computer, cell phone, etc.) and paper products (books, newspapers, etc.) per day in the last week? The frequency and duration of eye rubbing for participants were also assessed. Height was measured using a tape measure while the subject was leaning against a calibrated wall without shoes. Weight was obtained using an Omron body fat body weight measurement device (V. BODY HBF-371, OMRON, Japan). BMI was calculated by dividing weight in kilograms (kg) by the square of height (m2).

Corneal biomechanical parameters (Max Inverse Radius, DA Ratio Max [2 mm], Pachymetry Slope, DA Ratio Max [1 mm], ARTh, Integrated Radius, and SPA1) were measured by Corvis ST (Oculus Optikgerate GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). The topographical parameters (Kf, Ks, ACT, PCT, TCT, and Kmax) were obtained by Pentacam HR (Oculus Optikgerate GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). To maintain consistency in the direction of association between study parameters and KC, inverse ARTh, SPA1 value and pachymetry values were used in the current analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Normally distributed continuous variable was presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas abnormally distributed continuous variable was presented as median (Q1, Q3). The normally and abnormally distributed continuous variables between the KC and control groups were compared by Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney test, respectively. Categorical variables were presented as number (percentage) and compared using Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Crude model and adjusted models were developed to calculate the associations of BMI, corneal biomechanical parameters (Max Inverse Radius, DA Ratio Max [2 mm], Pachymetry Slope, DA Ratio Max [1 mm], -ARTh, Integrated Radius, and -SPA1), and topographical parameters (Kf, Ks, -ACT, -PCT, -TCT, and Kmax) with KC by using generalized linear models. The odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were recorded. The adjusted model controlled for age, gender, education level, occupation, history of eye disease, history of eye surgery, history of ocular trauma, history of systemic disease, family history of KC, and the time of eye rubbing.

To empirically select candidate mediators for the subsequent analysis, we performed generalized linear regression with KC status as the outcome. Corneal parameters showing a significant association (P < 0.05) were used to constitute the latent variables in the mediation model. Two latent variables, corneal biomechanical parameters (Max Inverse Radius, DA Ratio Max [2 mm], Pachymetry Slope, DA Ratio Max [1 mm], -ARTh, Integrated Radius, and -SPA1), and topographical parameters (Kf, Ks, -ACT, -PCT, -TCT, and Kmax), were used to evaluate the associations among BMI, corneal biomechanical parameters, and topographical parameters with KC by using SEM. This approach was adopted because (a) the clinical constructs are multidimensional and best represented by multiple indicators, (b) it accounts for measurement error in individual parameters, and (c) it mitigates collinearity by modeling the shared variance among correlated indicators. Factor loading was applied to reflect the correlation between observable variables and latent variables.26 All specified factor loadings in our measurement models were statistically significant (P < 0.001). Path coefficients were used to explore possible relationships between observable variables and latent variables in the SEM models. The comparative fit index (CFI) and root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) were applied to evaluate the goodness of fit of the SEM. A CFI value of ≥0.9 indicates an acceptable model,27 whereas an RMSEA value of ≤0.08 is considered as an acceptable model.28 Based on the SEM and adjusted general factors (occupation and history of systemic disease) which were statistically significant in the population as well as all eye-related factors (history of eye disease, allergic history, family history of KC, and the time of eye rubbing), three mediation models were used to evaluate the association between X (BMI) and Y (KC) by one or two mediator variables (M). The three models (single, parallel, and serial) are illustrated schematically in Supplementary Figure S2. In this study, single mediation, parallel multiple mediation, and serial multiple mediation were used to calculate the mediation effects of the latent variables between BMI and KC. The SEM and three mediation models were structured using the “lavaan” R software package.29 The total effects of BMI on KC, as well as the indirect effects of the mediating variables and the proportion mediated, were tested using 10,000 bootstrap samples to ensure statistical robustness. The 95% CIs were provided for the size of each effect, and a CI that does not contain 0 indicates a significant moderating effect.30,31 The percentage of indirect effect for the total effect implied the degree of indirect effects for the mediation factors.

All data were analyzed by using R version 4.4.0, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant at two tails.

SEM for Exploring the Roles of Corneal Biomechanical Parameters and Topographical Parameters in BMI and KC

As shown in Supplementary Figure S3, the SEM had a good fit for latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters (CFI = 0.966, RMSEA = 0.076) and topographic parameters (CFI = 0.996, RMSEA = 0.033).

Furthermore, single mediation model results also were revealed in Figure 2. The latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters or the latent variable of topographic parameters partially mediated the relationship between the BMI and KC. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, the indirect effect of the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters was 0.023 (95% CI = 0.002–0.045), accounting for 36.9% of the total effect. The indirect effect of the latent variable of topographic parameters was 0.049 (95% CI = 0.023–0.073), accounting for 76.6% of the total effect.

The single mediation model showing BMI on KC by the latent of corneal biomechanical parameter or the latent of topographic parameter.

The parallel multiple mediation model (Fig. 3) indicated that the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters and the latent variable of topographic parameters partially mediated the relationship between BMI and KC. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, the parallel multiple mediation model was composed of indirect effects generated by two pathways. The first way was BMI → the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters → KC (coefficient = 0.011, 95% CI = 0.000–0.022), accounting for 17.2% of the total effect. The second way was BMI → the latent variable of topographic parameters → KC (coefficient = 0.022, 95% CI = 0.010–0.038), accounting for 35.4% of the total effect. Based on the two ways (BMI → the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters + the latent variable of topographic parameters → KC), the total indirect effect coefficient was 0.033 (95% CI = 0.013–0.053), accounting for 52.6% of the total effect.

The parallel multiple mediation model showing BMI on KC by the latent of corneal biomechanical parameter and the latent of topographic parameter.

The serial multiple mediation model (Fig. 4) exhibited that the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters with the latent variable of topographic parameters partially mediated the relationship between BMI and KC, with a total indirect effect of 0.018, accounting for 31.0% of the total effect. Specifically, after adjusting for potential confounding factors, the mediating effect was composed of indirect effects generated by three pathways: (1) BMI → the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters → KC (coefficient = 0.005, 95% CI = 0.000–0.013), accounting for 8.3% of the total effect; (2) BMI → the latent variable of topographic parameters → KC (coefficient = 0.015, 95% CI = 0.005–0.026), accounting for 24.1% of the total effect; and (3) the BMI → the latent variable of corneal biomechanical parameters → the latent variable of topographic parameters → KC (coefficient = 0.021, 95% CI = 0.002–0.039), accounting for 32.3% of the total effect.

The serial multiple mediation model showing BMI on KC by the latent of corneal biomechanical parameter with the latent of topographic parameter.

Discussion

To elucidate the complex relationship between BMI and KC, this study used SEM to test three mediating hypotheses. The single and parallel mediation models confirmed that both corneal biomechanical and topographic domains significantly mediated the BMI-KC association. The serial multiple mediation model tested a more specific hypothesis: that the association operates through a sequential process, wherein systemic alterations may contribute to changes in corneal biomechanical integrity, which are subsequently linked to topographic remodeling characteristic of clinical disease. This statistically derived framework provides important clues and opens new avenues for future investigations to validate this temporal sequence and elucidate its underlying molecular and cellular drivers.

The three models used in this study offered complementary statistical perspectives. First, the single and parallel mediation models explained part of the BMI-KC association, which aligns with the premise of classic mediation analysis.32,33 This established the basis for variable screening in constructing the more complex sequential model.33 Notably, a study based on the same CKC methodologically supports the model selection in the present study.34 Our parallel model further suggested that after statistically controlling for one factor, the other might still represent a distinct mediating pathway, reflecting the potential diversity of paths through which obesity influences corneal properties.

The serial mediation model revealed two statistically significant indirect pathways, jointly delineating the underlying architecture of the association: the Direct Topographic Pathway (BMI → Topography → KC). This pathway demonstrated the strongest significance, highlighting the central role of corneal topography as the defining clinical phenotype of KC. Alterations in topographic parameters, such as an increase in anterior surface curvature (Kmax), serve as the most direct basis for diagnosis and staging.35,36 The sequential mediation analysis revealed a nuanced pathway (BMI → Biomechanics → Topography → KC), wherein the direct effect of “BMI → Biomechanics” was not statistically significant, whereas the “Biomechanics → Topography” path was remarkably robust. This leads to a crucial interpretation: corneal biomechanical properties (e.g. reduced stiffness) may not be the primary or direct endpoint of BMI's effect but rather function as a critical “bridge” or intermediary. Although genetic and large-scale epidemiological studies have identified BMI as an independent risk factor for KC,37 our findings suggest that its influence on corneal biomechanics might be more subtle or contingent. Biomechanical integrity may be more strongly modulated by other upstream factors beyond BMI alone, such as genetic susceptibility or systemic inflammatory processes, which have also been implicated in the disease's causality.38 However, once corneal biomechanical integrity is compromised—characterized by a significant reduction in stiffness and increased asymmetry39—it acts as a potent driver of subsequent morphological change. This is supported by evidence that biomechanical alterations are considered a direct cause of the topographic remodeling seen in KC.40 Consequently, biomechanics likely operate as a multifactorially regulated “integrative platform,” where changes often precede observable structural alterations. In contrast, corneal topography, particularly parameters like anterior curvature (Kmax) which exhibit high diagnostic performance,36 represents the more terminal and directly “observable morphological endpoint” within the entire association chain, ultimately converging to manifest as the definitive clinical phenotype of KC.41

Synthesizing all findings, we propose an integrative framework to elucidate the complex link between obesity and KC. Within this framework: Systemic Input: Centered on obesity. Mendelian randomization studies have provided evidence suggesting a potential causal association between BMI and KC.19,37 The chronic low-grade inflammation and metabolic disturbances accompanying obesity may constitute the initial risk source.42 Corneal Biomechanical Integrative Platform: Biomechanical properties serve as a dynamic, intermediate integrative layer. Experimental evidence supports this mechanistic link: in vitro and animal studies have shown that reduced stromal stiffness, allergic inflammation, and metabolic disturbances can each compromise corneal biomechanical integrity.43–45 Morphological Output Endpoint: Topographic parameters are the definitive morphological manifestation of corneal structural destabilization and serve as the direct basis for clinical diagnosis.46 The significant “Biomechanics → Topography” path in our serial model is corroborated by clinical evidence demonstrating that compromised corneal biomechanical properties are directly associated with the topographic manifestations of KC. Specifically, one study has quantified significant correlations between biomechanical parameters and key topographic indicators of ectasia, providing direct statistical support for this sequential relationship.47

This case-control study demonstrated that the relationship between BMI and KC was partially mediated by the latent variables of corneal biomechanical parameters and the latent variables of topographic parameters. Meanwhile, there were some limitations that should be noted. The use of SEM allowed us to construct these latent variables and test competing mediation models within a unified framework, mitigating multicollinearity and accounting for measurement error.48,49 First, the cross-sectional nature of the design limits causal inference and necessitates caution in interpreting the indirect (mediating) effects. Whereas our serial mediation model is statistically supported and biologically plausible, it cannot definitively establish the temporal sequence it implies; prospective longitudinal studies are required for validation. Moreover, reverse causality cannot be ruled out. For example, visual impairment or discomfort from KC might reduce physical activity and lead to weight gain—a direction opposite to that hypothesized. Although most evidence supports obesity as a driver of KC, this alternative pathway should be examined in future prospective studies. Second, there may be information recall bias as the subjects’ information was obtained through questionnaires. Although standardized procedures were followed in all phases, this could still affect the accuracy of the data. Third, the individuals were from a tertiary hospital. Although patients and controls were matched with age and sex, this may not be representative of the general population. Fourth, only BMI was used to measure obesity in this study, which may not be able to fully reflect the obesity status of the subjects. More indicators of obesity can be considered in future studies. Fifth, a critical consideration is residual confounding. Although our models adjusted for a range of demographic and clinical covariates, unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors could influence the observed associations. For instance, systemic inflammatory markers, detailed genetic risk profiles beyond family history, or subclinical ocular surface conditions might be related to both obesity and corneal structural integrity, and their omission could confound the estimated mediation effects. Taken together, these limitations highlight important avenues for future research, including validation in longitudinal cohorts and multi-ethnic populations, the use of animal models for mechanistic exploration, and the incorporation of more precise measures of obesity and potential confounding factors.

Conclusions

This study suggested that corneal biomechanical parameters and topographic parameters partially mediated the relationship between BMI and KC, which confirmed and complemented the system theory of BMI and KC. Furthermore, these results indicated that the BMI may result in KC by corneal biomechanical parameters and topographic parameters. Therefore, enhanced health management should be implemented for patients with KC and obesity in clinical management.

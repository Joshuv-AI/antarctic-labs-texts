Quality evaluation and aroma biosynthesis mechanism of seven strawberry varieties grown in solar greenhouse
Authors: Lizhen Chen, Xintong Nan, Huilan Qiao, Xing Tang, Wenfang Li, Yunbo Du, Yongxing Zhu, Zonghuan Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Seven strawberry cultivars from solar greenhouses were used to analyze fruit quality, physiology and aroma profiles. Results showed ‘Monterey’ had the highest hardness (0.56 kg·cm−2) and titratable acid content (0.99%). ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest soluble total sugar content (5.92%). ‘Snow Hare’ had the highest fructose content (45.35 mg·g−1). 114 aroma substances in 7 categories were detected, esters as the main component. ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest total aroma content and total aroma value (357.86), and the contents of key aroma substances. ‘Monterey’ contained unique esters and ketones. A comprehensive evaluation model for the quality of strawberry fruits in protected facilities was established through principal component analysis: F = 0.2984F₁ + 0.2436F₂ + 0.1848F₃ + 0.1518F₄. ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ performed best in overall quality and aroma intensity. Further analysis showed that LOX2 1, ACOX1, AIM1, FvPAL1–2 and HMGR1-like were highly expressed in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, synergistically enhancing the biosynthesis of esters, aldehydes and terpene.

Introduction

Strawberry (Fragaria×ananassa Duch.) is a perennial herbaceous plant of the genus Strawberry in the Rosaceae family, and is a high value-added cash crop (Liu, Zheng, et al., 2023). Strawberry fruits are fragrant; juicy; nutritious; rich in vitamin C (Vc); amino acids; anthocyanins and minerals; etc.; known as the “queen of fruits” and deeply loved by people (Priyadarshi et al., 2024).

China has a vast territory and a complex and diverse climate. Open-field cultivation of strawberries is greatly affected by natural climate conditions, with limitations in the production season, which cannot fully meet the market demand for strawberries. Traditional soil cultivation has drawbacks such as high labor intensity, short fruiting period, and easy contamination of fruit surfaces, at the same time, a large accumulation of pathogenic bacteria in the soil can lead to soil-borne diseases and a series of continuous cropping obstacles, which seriously affect the yield and quality of strawberries and cause huge losses to strawberry production. Compared with the traditional open-field cultivation method of strawberries, protected strawberry production has the characteristics of short cycle, quick results, high efficiency, and pollution-free fruits, it can also regulate the environment in the facility through measures such as nutrient solution, carbon dioxide fertilization, and artificial supplementary lighting (Zhang et al., 2019). In northwest China, there is a shortage of freshwater resources, low precipitation, frequent low-temperature weather, and a large area of saline-alkali land. Therefore, the development of strawberry cultivation in solar greenhouses in northwest China is extremely important. It can not only break through the climate and seasonal limitations of this production area but also serve as an important way to promote industrial development, increase farmers' income, and implement the strategies of water-saving and ecological agriculture in northwest China.

Fruit quality is the key factor determining the commercial value of strawberries and has a direct impact on their market competitiveness. The sensory quality closely related to the commercial value of fruits mainly includes appearance quality, such as single fruit weight, hardness, fruit shape index, and fruit surface color and flavor quality (Barrett et al., 2010). The flavor quality of fruits is mainly determined by the contents of soluble sugars; titratable acids; polyphenols; anthocyanins; etc. (Zeliou et al., 2018). Anthocyanins; flavonoids; and total phenols are secondary metabolites that affect the color; taste; stress resistance; and nutritional quality of fruits (Wang et al., 2017). Most strawberries are red; while some varieties are pink-white or white; hormones; external environment; and anthocyanins are the main factors affecting the color of strawberry fruits (Espley & Jaakola, 2023). Low temperature inhibits the accumulation of anthocyanins in strawberry fruits by activating FvMAPK3-induced phosphorylation of FvMYB10 and the degradation of chalcone synthase FvCHS1 (Mao et al., 2022). Balasooriya and other (Balasooriya et al., 2020) researchers conducted a study where strawberry varieties ‘Albion’ and ‘San Andreas’ were cultivated under day-night temperatures of 25/20 °C and 30/20 °C. The results showed that a 5 °C increase in temperature significantly reduced the fruit growth and yield of both varieties; the fruits had irregular shapes, small sizes, and low red intensity, but the total polyphenol content and antioxidant capacity were significantly higher than those of fruits grown at 25/20 °C.

The aroma of strawberry fruits is one of the most complex and rich fruit aromas. To date, 979 volatile organic compounds (VOCs) have been identified in strawberries. (Ulrich et al., 2018). Although they only account for 0.001% - 0.01% of the fruit weight; they play an important role in the formation of the flavor of strawberry fruits (Yan et al., 2018). Strawberries produced a variety of volatile compounds; which contributed to the unique flavor of the fruit. Among these volatiles; γ-decalactone (γ-D) made the greatest contribution to the characteristic fruity aroma of strawberry fruits (Oh et al., 2021). The relative concentrations of substances such as methyl butyrate; ethyl butanoate; (E)-2-hexenal; γ-decalactone; 2;5-dimethyl-4-methoxy-3(2H)-furanone (DMMF); and linalool are relatively high in strawberries (Wang et al., 2018). FaFAD1 is closely related to the synthesis of decalactone and nerolidol; in addition; furanols are highly correlated with the synthesis of strawberry alcohol acyl transferase (SAAT); phenylpropanoid volatiles; and trans-cinnamic acid (Leonardou et al., 2021). The ethylene response gene FveERF is related to fruit ripening (Li et al., 2020). When ‘ZhangJi’ strawberries were refrigerated at 4 °C for 9 days; the content of alcohol volatile substances decreased less compared with those stored at room temperature; maintaining a higher concentration of aroma volatile substances (Li et al., 2015).

At present, there are many strawberry varieties cultivated in production, but the quality differences and aroma formation mechanisms of different varieties in solar greenhouses have not been clarified yet. Therefore, this study used seven strawberry varieties cultivated in solar greenhouses as materials to carry out comprehensive quality evaluation by measuring morphological indexes, physiological indexes, aroma components, enzyme activity and key differential genes, aiming to provide theoretical support for the selection of protected-cultivated strawberry varieties and the improvement of aroma quality.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials and experimental site

The experimental materials were harvested on February 22, 2025. Among them, ‘ZhangJi’, ‘HongYan’, ‘Francois’, and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ were harvested from Qinchuan Town, Lanzhou New District, and ‘Snow Hare’, ‘XiangFei No.5’, and ‘Monterey’ were harvested from Jiuhe Town, Anning District. Three biological replicates were set for each cultivar, with 20 uniformly grown, similarly sized, bright red, normally shaped, pest- and mechanical damage-free mature fruits randomly selected per replicate, totaling 60 fruits per cultivar. Fruits were packed in self-sealing bags and transported to the laboratory on the same day. Part of the fruits was used for external quality measurement, and the rest was stored at −80 °C for further analysis.

The facility type was the 8th-generation new solar greenhouse, with brick walls, an elliptical tube steel frame, a bolt-fixed shed surface, no columns inside the shed, and good lighting conditions. The specifications of each greenhouse were 10 m × 70 m × 6 m, with an area of 700 m2. Elevated substrate cultivation was adopted. Cultivation troughs were white PVC, 40 cm wide, 25 cm deep, with 60 cm spacing between troughs, equipped with shallow ditches and liquid collection devices at the bottom. The cultivation substrate was a 1:1 mixture of peat and vermiculite. The substrate had a bulk density of 0.35 g·cm−3, maximum water-holding capacity of 220% (v/v), total porosity of 78%, pH of 6.83, and EC of 1.8 mS·cm−1, all meeting the requirements for strawberry root growth. Virus-free strawberry seedlings were planted in late August 2024 and began coloring in late December. After planting, integrated water and fertilizer management and temperature control (Table S1) were implemented according to growth stages to ensure standardized management. Old, diseased leaves and stolons were removed in a timely manner during growth, and green prevention and control measures were adopted to control pests and diseases and ensure normal plant growth.

Determination indexes and methods

Fruit appearance and longitudinal section images

Three fruits were randomly selected and cut longitudinally. The longitudinal section images were obtained by scanning with an LGG-B melon and fruit section image analyzer (produced by Hangzhou Lvbo Instrument Co., Ltd.).

Determination of single fruit weight, hardness, color, fruit shape index and water content of fruit

Single fruit weight: Measured using an electronic balance, with three fruits measured each time and three repetitions, and the average value was calculated.

Hardness: The hardness of the fruit was determined by a texture instrument (Universal TA, Shanghai Tengba Instrument Technology Co., Ltd.).

Fruit color: Measured using a colorimeter (Model: CR-400), and the L, a*, and b* values of the fruit peel were recorded.

Fruit shape index: The longitudinal diameter (L) and transverse diameter (D) of the fruit were measured using a vernier caliper, and the fruit shape index was calculated as L/D。.

Water content: Calculated by the formula: (Fresh weight - Dry weight)/Fresh weight × 100%.

Determination of soluble solids, total soluble sugar, titratable acid and Vc

Soluble solids (SSC) were determined using a Td-35 handheld digital refractometer. The content of TSS was determined by the anthrone colorimetric method (Buysse & Merckx, 1993). The content of TA was determined by the titration method (Wen et al., 2023). The content of Vc was determined by the 2;6-dichlorophenol indophenol titration method (Komatsu et al., 2002).

Determination of soluble protein, total phenols, flavonoids, anthocyanins and tannin contents

Soluble protein was determined by the Coomassie brilliant blue method (Liu et al., 2019).

The content of total phenols was determined by the Folin colorimetric method (Navid et al., 2014). Strawberry flesh was ground with liquid nitrogen, and 1.0 g of the ground sample was accurately weighed and homogenized with 20 mL of 70% ethanol for extraction. The mixture was incubated in a water bath at 90 °C for 15 min, then centrifuged at 4000 ×g for 15 min. The supernatant was collected and made up to a final volume of 100 mL with 70% ethanol. Subsequently, 1 mL of the diluted extract was pipetted, and 60 mL of distilled water, 5 mL of Folin-Ciocalteu reagent and 15 mL of 20% sodium carbonate solution were added successively, with the volume adjusted to 100 mL using distilled water. The mixture was kept in the dark at 20 °C for 2 h for color development, and the absorbance was measured at 765 nm.

The contents of anthocyanins and flavonoids were determined by the method of Pirie and Mullins (Pirie & Mullins, 1976). Exactly 2.0 g of strawberry flesh was ground to a fine powder in liquid nitrogen and transferred into a 10 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 5 mL of 1% HCl-methanol solution. The sample was extracted in an ice bath for 1 h in the dark with shaking for 3–4 times during the process, then centrifuged at 10000 rpm and the supernatant was collected into a 20 mL graduated test tube. The residue was re-extracted with another 5 mL of 1% HCl-methanol solution by shaking thoroughly, followed by a 2 h extraction and centrifugation. The two supernatants were combined and the volume was adjusted to 20 mL in the graduated test tube. Using 1% HCl-methanol solution as the blank reference, the absorbance values were measured at 325 nm, 600 nm and 530 nm, with three replicates for each sample. For calculation, the absorbance at 325 nm was used to represent the relative content of flavonoids (expressed as OD325 per gram of sample); the relative content of anthocyanins in fruits was expressed as the difference between the absorbance values at 530 nm and 600 nm, with the formula: U = (OD530 − OD600) per gram of sample.

The content of tannins was determined by the Folin-Denis reagent method (Xue et al., 2023). Strawberry flesh was ground with liquid nitrogen, and 1.0 g of the ground sample was accurately weighed and homogenized with 20 mL of 20% ethanol for extraction. The mixture was incubated in a water bath at 70 °C for 30 min, then centrifuged at 4000 ×g for 15 min. The residue was washed and re-extracted twice with 20% ethanol solution. The combined supernatants were made up to 100 mL in a volumetric flask with distilled water. Then 1 mL of the extract was pipetted, and 70 mL of distilled water, 5 mL of Folin-Denis reagent and 10 mL of saturated sodium carbonate solution were added successively, with the volume adjusted to 100 mL using distilled water. The mixture was kept in the dark at 20 °C for 30 min for color development, and the absorbance was measured at 760 nm.

Determination of sugar and acid components

Sugar components were determined by high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Li, Ma, et al., 2021). Strawberry flesh was ground with liquid nitrogen, and 0.5 g of the homogenate was accurately weighed and transferred into a 10 mL centrifuge tube, followed by the addition of 5 mL of 80% ethanol. The mixture was subjected to ultrasonic extraction at 35 °C for 20 min and then centrifuged at 12,000 r·min−1 for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. The residue was re-extracted twice with 2 mL of 80% ethanol each time, and all the supernatants were combined and made up to a final volume of 10 mL with 80% ethanol. A 2 mL aliquot of the combined extract was transferred into a centrifuge tube and evaporated to dryness at 60 °C using a vacuum centrifugal concentrator. The dried residue was redissolved in a mixture of 1 mL ultrapure water and 1 mL acetonitrile, and the solution was filtered through a 0.22 μm organic phase microporous membrane. The filtrate was transferred into a sample vial for HPLC analysis, with three replicates per sample. The contents of fructose, glucose and sucrose were quantified under the following chromatographic conditions: an XBridge BEH Amide column (4.6 mm × 150 mm, 2.5 μm) was used; the mobile phase consisted of 75% acetonitrile, 0.2% triethylamine and 24.8% ultrapure water; the flow rate was set at 0.8 mL·min−1; the detection wavelength was 254 nm; the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C; and the injection volume was 10 μL. Prior to use, the mobile phase was filtered through a 0.22 μm organic phase membrane and degassed by ultrasonication.

Acid components were assayed by HPLC (Li, Yan, et al., 2021) with minor modifications. Exactly 2.0 g of strawberry flesh was ground into a paste under low temperature conditions and transferred into a 10 mL centrifuge tube, and 5 mL of ultrapure water was added subsequently. The mixture was treated with water bath at 80 °C coupled with ultrasonic extraction for 15 min, then cooled to room temperature and centrifuged at 10,000 r·min−1 and 4 °C for 10 min. The supernatant was collected, and the residue was re-extracted twice using the same method with 2 mL of ultrapure water added each time. All supernatants were combined, transferred into a 10 mL volumetric flask and made up to the mark with ultrapure water. The resulting solution was filtered through a 0.22 μm aqueous phase microporous membrane, and the filtrate was transferred into a sample vial for HPLC analysis with three biological replicates per cultivar. The chromatographic conditions were as follows: separation was performed on an Atlantis T3 column (4.6 mm × 150 mm, 3 μm); the mobile phase was 20 mmol/L sodium dihydrogen phosphate (NaH₂PO₄) solution adjusted to pH 2.7 with phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄); the flow rate was 0.50 mL·min−1; the column temperature was 30 °C; the detection wavelength was 210 nm; and the injection volume was 20 μL.

Determination of aroma substance content

10 g of strawberry flesh was ground into a homogenate, transferred into a 10 mL centrifuge tube, and stored at −80 °C until further analysis. The strawberry homogenate was placed in a headspace solid-phase microextraction (SPME) vial. Then, 15 μL of 2-octanal (internal standard) and 1.5 g of sodium chloride were added. The mixture was stirred at 200 r·min−1 and 40 °C for 30 min. The SPME fiber was inserted into the headspace (without touching the inner wall) and extracted under magnetic stirring at 40 °C and 200 r·min−1 for 30 min. After extraction, the fiber was withdrawn and inserted into the GC–MS injection port, where desorption was performed at 250 °C for 10 min prior to GC–MS analysis. Volatile compounds were identified and quantified using GC–MS. Mass spectra of each separated compound were compared with those in the NIST 2014 library using the automatic integration algorithm of the instrument system. Only volatiles with a match score above 80% were selected. Retention indices (RI) were further calculated based on the retention times of a mixture of n-alkanes (C7–C40). Compounds with RI values differing by more than ±50 from those in the database were discarded.

Chromatographic Conditions: The analysis was performed using a DB-5MS quartz capillary column (0.25 mm × 60 m × 0.25 μm). The injector temperature was set at 260 °C. The column temperature was programmed as follows: initial temperature of 50 °C, increased to 250 °C at a rate of 5 °C·min−1, and held for 5 min.

Mass spectrometric conditions: ionization mode was electron ionization (EI) with an electron energy of 70 eV; ion source temperature was 230 °C; transfer line temperature was 250 °C; quadrupole temperature was 150 °C. Full-scan mode was used with a mass scan range of 35–500 m/z.

The desorption time was 5 min. The content of each aroma component (μg·kg−1) was calculated as: Content = (Peak area of analyte / Peak area of internal standard × Concentration of internal standard (mg·L−1) × 35 μL) / Sample mass (kg). Odor activity value (OVA) was calculated as: OVA = Content of a given compound / Odor threshold of the compound. Odor thresholds were obtained from Van Gemert (2011) Odor Thresholds (water matrix) (Gemert, L. J. V.,2003). Given the high water content of strawberry fruit (approximately 90%), thresholds in water were used as a reasonable reference for those in strawberry matrix. Compounds with OVA >1 were screened as key aroma-active compounds.

Enzyme activity assay

The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Enzymes Biological) was used to determine the relevant enzyme activity, and all operations were strictly carried out in accordance with the kit instructions, and the absorbance (450 nm) was detected by the microplate reader and the concentration of enzymes in strawberry fruit was calculated.

Data analysis

Data processing and chart preparation were performed using Microsoft Excel 2021. Bar charts, line charts, and radar charts were drawn using Origin Pro 9.8. Statistical hypothesis testing and multiple comparison analysis were conducted with SPSS 26.0. One-way analysis of variance (One-way ANOVA) was used for variance analysis and multiple comparisons, with a significance level set at P < 0.05. When significant differences among groups were detected by ANOVA, Duncan's multiple range test (P < 0.05) was further applied for post-hoc multiple comparisons to determine the specific significant groups among different strawberry cultivars.

Results and analysis

Appearance and longitudinal section images of strawberry fruits of different varieties

By observing the appearance and longitudinal section images of the seven strawberry varieties (Fig. 1), it was found that the peel of ‘Snow Hare’ was pink-white with red seeds, while the peels of the other six varieties were red with red or pale white seeds. Regarding the internal characteristics: ‘ZhangJi’ had a plump pith, which was red and white (mainly white), and the color of the pulp gradually deepened from the peel to the pith; ‘HongYan’ had a plump pith, which was mainly red, and the color of the pulp gradually turned red from the peel to the pith; ‘Snow Hare’ had a plump pith, and the color of the pith was the same as that of the pulp (white); ‘Francois’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had hollow piths with insufficient development, the piths were red, and the color of the pulp gradually deepened from the peel to the pith; ‘Monterey’ had a significantly hollow pith, and the area of the pith was larger than that of other varieties.Fig. 1Appearance and profile of different strawberry varieties (a. ZhangJi b. HongYan c. Snow Hare d. XiangFei No.5 e. Francois f. MiaoXiang No.7 g. Monterey).Fig. 1

Appearance and profile of different strawberry varieties (a. ZhangJi b. HongYan c. Snow Hare d. XiangFei No.5 e. Francois f. MiaoXiang No.7 g. Monterey).

Comparison of single fruit weight, hardness, color, fruit shape index, and moisture content of strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 2–1, there was no significant difference in single fruit weight between ‘XiangFei No.5’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, and there was no significant difference in single fruit weight among the other five varieties. Among the seven varieties, the fruit hardness of ‘Monterey’ was significantly higher than that of other varieties, reaching 0.56 kg·cm−2. The L value (brightness), a* value (red-green), and b* value (yellow-blue) are important chromaticity indexes for characterizing fruit color. Among the seven varieties, the white variety ‘Snow Hare’ had the highest L value, which was significantly higher than that of other varieties, and its a* value was the lowest among the seven varieties; ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ and ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest b* values, with no significant difference between them, and their b* values were significantly higher than those of other varieties; ‘Monterey’ had the lowest b* value (13.56).

As can be seen from Table 2–2, the fruits of the seven varieties were all conical, which could be further divided into: ‘ZhangJi’, ‘XiangFei No.5’, and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ were long conical; ‘HongYan’, ‘Francois’, and ‘Monterey’ were conical; ‘Snow Hare’ was short conical. There were significant differences in the longitudinal and transverse diameters of the fruits: ‘XiangFei No.5’ had the longest longitudinal diameter (59.33 mm), while ‘Snow Hare’ had the shortest longitudinal diameter (30.11 mm); ‘XiangFei No.5’ had the highest fruit shape index (1.70), while ‘Snow Hare’ had the lowest fruit shape index (1.15). The moisture content of the strawberry fruits of the seven varieties was relatively high, all around 90%. ‘Francois’ had the highest moisture content (93.47%), which was significantly higher than that of ‘XiangFei No.5’ and ‘Monterey’.

Contents of soluble solids, total soluble sugar, titratable acid, and Vc in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 2–3, there were significant differences in the quality indexes of the seven strawberry varieties. The content of SSC ranged from 7.55% to 11.97%, the content of TSS ranged from 4.38% to 5.92%, and the content of TA ranged from 0.54% to 0.99%. Among them, there was no significant difference in the SSC content of the fruits of ‘ZhangJi’, ‘HongYan’, ‘Snow Hare’, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, and ‘Monterey’, but their SSC contents were all significantly higher than that of ‘Francois’; ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest TSS content (5.92%), which was extremely significantly higher than that of other varieties (P < 0.01); ‘Monterey’ had the highest TA content (0.99%), which was significantly higher than that of other varieties (P < 0.05); ‘Snow Hare’ had the highest sugar-acid ratio and Vc content, which were 9.60 and 7.30 mg·g−1, respectively.

Contents of soluble protein, total phenols, flavonoids, anthocyanins, and tannins in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 2–4, there were significant differences in the contents of soluble protein, total phenols, anthocyanins, and tannins among different strawberry varieties, while there was no significant difference in the content of flavonoids. ‘Snow Hare’ had the highest soluble protein content (1.05 mg·g−1), and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the lowest soluble protein content (0.89 mg·g−1); the total phenol contents of ‘Francois’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ were both higher than 4.00 mg·g−1, while the total phenol contents of the other varieties were lower than 4.00 mg·g−1, and ‘ZhangJi’ had the lowest total phenol content (1.01 mg·g−1); there was no difference in the flavonoid content among the seven strawberry varieties; ‘XiangFei No.5’ had the highest anthocyanin content (2.38 mg·g−1), and ‘Snow Hare’ (a white variety) had the lowest anthocyanin content (only 0.13 mg·g−1); the tannin contents of the seven varieties were mostly between 0.01 and 0.03 mg·g−1, while the tannin content of ‘Monterey’ fruits reached 0.09 mg·g−1, which was extremely significantly higher than that of other varieties.

Contents of sugar and acid components in strawberry fruits of different varieties

The content and type of sugars and acids in fruits affect the flavor, color, and other quality attributes of fruits. As can be seen from Fig. 2, the citric acid content of each of the seven strawberry varieties was higher than the contents of oxalic acid and malic acid in the corresponding variety. ‘Snow Hare’ had the highest contents of fructose (45.35 mg·g−1), glucose (38.71 mg·g−1), and oxalic acid (1.30 mg·g−1), but the lowest citric acid content (5.24 mg·g−1), which was only half of that of ‘Monterey’; ‘Monterey’ had the highest contents of citric acid (10.45 mg·g−1) and malic acid (2.20 mg·g−1), but the lowest contents of fructose (17.77 mg·g−1) and sucrose (16.87 mg·g−1); ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest sucrose content (41.09 mg·g−1); ‘HongYan’ had the lowest contents of glucose (15.42 mg·g−1) and malic acid (1.44 mg·g−1); ‘Francois’ had the lowest oxalic acid content (0.72 mg·g−1).Fig. 2Sugar and acid contents in different strawberry varieties.Fig. 2

Analysis of ester aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 3–1, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest contents of ethyl butanoate (6.47 μg·kg−1), methyl hexanoate (36.05 μg·kg−1), and ethyl hexanoate (31.97 μg·kg−1); ‘Snow Hare’ was detected to contain characteristic ester substances such as methyl isovalerate and isoamyl acetate; ‘Monterey’ had the highest content of methyl butyrate (7.51 μg·kg−1), and three compounds, namely 2-methylbutylacetate, hexyl propionate, and n-Caproic acid n-butyl ester, were only detected in ‘Monterey’; isoamyl isobutyrate was only detected in ‘ZhangJi’ fruits. These results indicated that varieties had a specific impact on the synthesis of ester aroma substances. Among them, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ and ‘Monterey’ accumulated more ester substances overall, which might be an important reason for their strong fruity aroma.

Analysis of alcohol aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 3–2, linalool was one of the main components, with ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ having the highest content (54.06 μg·kg−1), followed by ‘Monterey’ (39.45 μg·kg−1), and ‘Francois’ having the lowest content (3.27 μg·kg−1). oct-1-en-3-ol was only detected in ‘ZhangJi’ with a content of 0.53 μg·kg−1; (±)-trans-Nerolidol only existed in ‘Monterey’ with a content of 5.07 μg·kg−1. Nerolidol, cis-(+) was present in six varieties, namely ‘ZhangJi’, ‘HongYan’, ‘Snow Hare’, ‘XiangFei No.5’, ‘Francois’, and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, with ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ having the highest content and ‘ZhangJi’ having the lowest content. Overall, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had an obvious advantage in the total content of alcohol aroma substances, which made a prominent contribution to its aroma characteristics.

Analysis of ketone aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 3–3, there were few types of ketone aroma substances, but there were specific differences among varieties. The content of 4,5-Octadione in ‘Monterey’ was extremely high (25.11 μg·kg−1), which was a unique ketone substance with the highest content among all varieties; ‘Monterey’ had the highest content of 6-Methylhept-5-en-2-one (0.25 μg·kg−1), while ‘Snow Hare’ and ‘Francois’ had the lowest content (both 0.03 μg·kg−1); two compounds, 4-Methoxy-2,5-dimethylfuran-3(2H)-one and 4,5-Octadione, were only found in ‘Monterey’ with contents of 0.58 μg·kg−1 and 25.11 μg·kg−1, respectively. ‘Monterey’ was the variety with the richest ketone substances.

Analysis of aldehyde aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As can be seen from Table 3–4, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest contents of hexanal (8.23 μg·kg−1) and 2-hexen-1-al (24.41 μg·kg−1), which were significantly higher than those of other varieties; ‘Monterey’ had the highest content of benzaldehyde (1.72 μg·kg−1), while ‘ZhangJi’ and ‘Francois’ had the lowest content; ‘Snow Hare’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had relatively high contents of (2E,6Z)-nona-2,6-dienal, which were 0.65 μg·kg−1 and 0.69 μg·kg−1, respectively; the content of 5-hydroxymethylfurfural in ‘HongYan’ was 9.72 μg·kg−1, which was significantly higher than that in ‘Snow Hare’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’; ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest content of trans, (2E,4E)-Deca-2,4-dienal (0.51 μg·kg−1). Overall, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ accumulated more of the main aldehyde substances (hexanal and 2-hexen-1-al), which made an important contribution to the fresh aroma of strawberries.

Analysis of acid aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As can be seen from Table 3–5, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest contents of 2-methylbutyric acid (5.34 μg·kg−1), 1-hexanoic acid (30.35 μg·kg−1), and octanoic acid (1.29 μg·kg−1), which were significantly higher than those of other varieties. ‘Monterey’ had the highest content of n-butyric acid (0.35 μg·kg−1). A unique 3-Methylbutanoic acid was detected in ‘Snow Hare’ with a content of 7.40 μg·kg−1, and its content of 5-Methylhexanoic acid was higher than that of ‘XiangFei No.5’ and ‘MiaoXiang No.7’. Pentanoic acid only existed in ‘XiangFei No.5’ fruits. ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ showed a high accumulation characteristic in most acid substances, which might affect the balance of the sweet and sour flavor of strawberries.

Analysis of alkane aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As can be seen from Table 3–6, alkanes were the least in variety and generally low in content in strawberry fruits. A total of 7 alkane substances were detected in the seven varieties. Among them, the substance with the highest content was (Z)-3,7-dimethylocta-1,3,6, −triene, which was present in ‘Monterey’ with a content of 0.61 μg·kg−1. The substance with the lowest content was exo-2,3-epoxynorbornane, which was only detected in ‘ZhangJi’ with a content of 0.11 μg·kg−1. No alkane compounds were detected in ‘HongYan’ and ‘XiangFei No.5’, and no variety was detected to contain 2 or more alkane substances at the same time. These results indicated that alkanes might make a small contribution to the strawberry aroma and have strong variety specificity.

Analysis of terpene aroma substances in strawberry fruits of different varieties

As can be seen from Table 3–7, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest contents of (E)-β-Farnesene (0.94 μg·kg−1) and alpha-terpineol (0.26 μg·kg−1). Myrcene was only detected in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ and ‘Monterey’, and the content in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ was higher than that in ‘Monterey’. Overall, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had an advantage in the accumulation of terpene substances, which might make a certain supplementary contribution to the unique aroma of strawberries.

Characteristic aroma substances and fruit aroma types of strawberry fruits of different varieties

As shown in Table 3–8, there were significant differences in the characteristic aroma components and odor values of the seven strawberry varieties. Among the 9 characteristic aroma components detected, linalool had a relatively high odor value in most varieties. Among them, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest odor value of linalool (245.73), which was much higher than that of other varieties; this component has a flower, grass, and convallaria of the valley aroma and belongs to the No. 2 fragrance family. ‘Francois’ had the fewest types of characteristic aroma components, no decanal and ethyl hexanoate were detected, and its total aroma value was the lowest (27.34). From the perspective of individual components, methyl hexanoate had the highest odor value (30.04) in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, with a fruity and pineapple aroma; ethyl hexanoate was prominent in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ and ‘Snow Hare’, with a pineapple and banana aroma. Hexanal, a component with a fresh and grassy scent aroma, had the highest odor value (41.15) in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’, and this component was not detected in ‘Monterey’. In terms of total aroma value, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ was significantly higher than other varieties (357.86), followed by ‘Monterey’ (212.14) and ‘ZhangJi’ (171.01), while ‘Snow Hare’ (89.24) and ‘Francois’ (27.34) had relatively low total aroma values. These results indicated that ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had better overall aroma intensity and richness.

Except for ‘Monterey’ (no volatile substances detected), volatile substances of grassy aroma were detected in the other six varieties. Principal component analysis and comprehensive evaluation of strawberry fruit quality indexes

The results of principal component analysis on 27 fruit quality indexes (Table 3–9) showed that a total of 4 principal components with eigenvalues greater than 1 were extracted, with eigenvalues of 8.057, 6.576, 4.989, and 4.100 in sequence, and the cumulative contribution rate reached 87.860%. PC1 had the highest contribution rate, at 29.841%, which dominated the “acid-sweet-flavor” balance. Acids such as TA and malic acid, and flavor substances such as terpenes had strong positive loadings, while the sugar-acid ratio and fructose had strong negative loadings. A high score of PC1 indicated that the fruit was relatively sour with a strong aroma, while a low score indicated a good sweet-sour balance. PC2 had a contribution rate of 24.357%, reflecting the correlation between texture and antioxidant characteristics, fruit hardness and VC were strongly positive, water content and total phenol were strongly loaded, and the high score was the hard fruit texture and higher VC content, and the lower score was softer the pulp texture, sufficient water and higher total phenol content. PC3 had a contribution rate of 18.478%, which was related to “morphology-sweetness-flavor”. Sucrose, aldehydes, and single fruit weight had strong positive loadings. A high score of PC3 indicated that the fruit was large, sweet, and had a fresh aroma. PC4 had a contribution rate of 15.184%, which detailed the “softness-hardness-acid level”. Acids had positive loadings, while fruit hardness and citric acid had strong negative loadings. A high score of PC4 indicated that the fruit was soft with a mild sour taste, while a low score indicated that the fruit was relatively hard with a fresh sour taste.

Based on the results of principal component analysis, a comprehensive evaluation model for the quality of strawberry fruits of the seven varieties was established. The score formulas for the four principal components were calculated using the coefficients corresponding to each quality index and the standardized values of each quality index:

F₁ = 0.050×₁ + 0.016×₂ + 0.041×₃ + … + 0.098×₂₇.

F₂ = −0.063×₁ + 0.106×₂ - 0.019×₃ + … + 0.049×₂₇.

F₃ = 0.126×₁ - 0.036×₂ + 0.118×₃ + … + 0.074×₂₇.

F₄ = 0.044×₁ – 0.159×₂ - 0.097×₃ + … + 0.075×₂₇.

In the formulas, F₁, F₂, F₃, and F₄ are the scores of the four principal components of the fruit quality of different varieties, and X₁ - X₂₇ represent the standardized values of each quality index.

Taking the contribution rate corresponding to each principal component as the weight, the weighted sum of the scores of each principal component was calculated to obtain the comprehensive score, and a comprehensive evaluation model for the quality of strawberry fruits in protected facilities was constructed: F = 0.2984F₁ + 0.2436F₂ + 0.1848F₃ + 0.1518F₄, where F is the comprehensive quality score of strawberry fruits of different varieties.

As shown in Table 3–10, the order of the comprehensive scores of the seven varieties from high to low was: ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ > ‘Monterey’ > ‘XiangFei No.5’ > ‘ZhangJi’ > ‘Snow Hare’ > ‘HongYan’ > ‘Francois’.

Discussion

Fruit quality is an important component for judging its economic value. Generally, fruit quality mainly includes specific indexes such as fruit color, size, weight, SSC, soluble sugars, TA, and Vc (Wang et al., 2024). The fruit shape index; as a key index for measuring fruit shape; showed a significant difference between ‘XiangFei No.5’ (1.70) and ‘Snow Hare’ (1.15); reflecting the imbalance in the longitudinal and transverse growth of the fruits; which might be caused by differences in the regulation of plant hormones such as cytokinins and auxins (Zhang et al., 2011). The fruit hardness of ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ was higher than that of ‘HongYan’ (Deng et al., 2022); while the results of this study showed that the fruit hardness of ‘HongYan’ was higher than that of ‘MiaoXiang No.7’; which might be caused by differences in the growth environment. In terms of color; the degradation of chlorophyll and the synthesis and accumulation of anthocyanins during fruit development are the core factors leading to the color change of the fruit peel (Zheng et al., 2022). For example; the significant difference in anthocyanin content between ‘XiangFei No.5’ (2.38 mg·g−1) and ‘Snow Hare’ (0.13 mg·g−1) determines their completely different appearance colors. Sugars and acids are the main indexes affecting the flavor of fruits (Li, Ma, et al., 2021). The TA content of ‘Fenyu’ fruits was much lower than that of the seven varieties in this experiment; while there was little difference in the soluble sugar content between ‘Fenyu’ and the seven varieties in this experiment (Liu, Liang, & Kang, 2023).

Esters, furanones, terpenes, and sulfur-containing compounds are all characteristic components constituting strawberry volatile substances (Yan et al., 2018). Aroma is one of the core characteristics of strawberry quality. In this experiment; a total of 7 categories and 114 aroma compounds were detected; with total contents ranging from 28.57 μg·kg−1 to 310.77 μg·kg−1. Among them; esters; due to their high content and high odor activity values (OAVs); act as the dominant components of strawberry aroma and lay the foundation for the formation of strawberry fruity notes. The present study showed that ethyl butyrate; methyl hexanoate; and ethyl hexanoate were common characteristic components among the seven cultivars; endowing strawberries with typical fruity aromas such as pineapple and banana; which was consistent with the study by Wang Juan (Wang et al., 2018) that methyl butyrate, methyl caproate, benzyl acetate, and (E)-2-hexenal were the pineapple aromas presented by strawberry fruits.

Alcohols are important sources of floral and green notes in strawberries, among which linalool is the most critical aroma component (Ulrich et al., 2018). In this study; ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ exhibited the highest linalool content (54.06 μg·kg−1) and OAV (245.73); and its floral characteristics were mainly determined by the high accumulation of linalool. This agrees with the conclusion of Ulrich (Ulrich et al., 2018) that linalool regulates the floral aroma profile of strawberries. In addition, trans-nerolidol was detected only in ‘Monterey’, and 1-octen-3-ol was detected only in ‘ZhangJi’, demonstrating cultivar specificity. Furthermore, Zhang and other people (Zhang et al.,2023) found that cultivar differences in alcohol contents are also related to differential regulation of fatty acid metabolism during fruit ripening, which further explains the cultivar-specific accumulation of alcohols observed in this study.

Yan (Yan et al., 2018) reported that 2-hexenal is synthesized via the lipoxygenase (LOX) pathway; and higher LOX activity leads to greater accumulation of aldehydes. In this study; ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ showed the highest overall aldehyde content; and its 2-hexenal content was 24.41 μg·kg−1; significantly higher than that in other cultivars; implying significantly higher enzyme activity in its LOX pathway. ‘Monterey’ had the highest benzaldehyde content at 1.72 μg·kg−1. As an aromatic aldehyde; benzaldehyde varied significantly among cultivars; and its biosynthesis is closely related to the phenylalanine metabolic pathway (Leonardou et al., 2021).

There are few types of ketones, but the variety specificity is significant. ‘Monterey’ uniquely contained 4,5-octanedione and DMMF, among which DMMF is a characteristic fruity compound in strawberries and has been confirmed by Oh (Oh et al., 2021) to contribute importantly to strawberry flavor. The presence of these two compounds increased the aroma complexity of ‘Monterey’. Terpene contents differed markedly among cultivars in this study. ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest contents of terpenes such as trans-β-farnesene and myrcene; reflecting higher activity in its terpene biosynthetic pathway. Alkanes were the least diverse and abundant group and contributed limitedly to strawberry aroma; which is consistent with the results of Yan (Yan et al., 2018). No alkanes were detected in ‘HongYan’ and ‘XiangFei No.5’, indicating that their synthesis accounts for a very low proportion in strawberry fruit, possibly due to the preferential allocation of fatty acid metabolism toward other aroma pathways.

In this study, characteristic aroma compounds were screened using the criterion of OAV > 1. Without sensory evaluation, the fruit aroma profiles were classified and described only based on the compositional categories of characteristic aroma compounds. ‘Snow Hare’ was dominated by ester-derived fruity components, whereas the other cultivars were centered on floral components such as linalool. This is consistent with the conclusion proposed by Sheng (Sheng et al., 2021) that strawberry aroma types are determined by the categories of characteristic aroma compounds. ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ contained characteristic components of floral, fruity, and green notes simultaneously, resulting in a more complex aroma profile.

The biosynthesis and accumulation of fruit aroma compounds are complex biological processes coordinately regulated by key enzyme activities and the transcriptional levels of functional genes, which together determine the differences in aroma quality and aroma types among different cultivars (Pérez et al., 1999; Zhang et al., 2011). In ‘Miaoxiang 7’, the LOX2–1 gene was extremely highly expressed, promoting the abundant synthesis of C6 aldehydes such as hexanal and (E)-2-hexenal. This is fully consistent with the conclusion that the transcriptional level of lipoxygenase genes dominates aldehyde aroma biosynthesis (Cumplido-Laso et al., 2012; Pérez et al., 1999). Although LOX activity in ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ was lower than that in ‘Snow Hare’, the high transcription level of LOX2–1 was still sufficient to support the efficient accumulation of aldehydes, indicating that transcriptional regulation plays a dominant role in aldehyde synthesis and that enzyme activity is not the only limiting factor. Meanwhile, genes related to fatty acid β-oxidation, namely ACOX1 and AIM1, were significantly upregulated, providing sufficient acyl-CoA precursors for ester biosynthesis, thereby enhancing AAT enzyme activity and promoting the abundant formation of characteristic fruity esters such as ethyl butyrate and ethyl hexanoate. This supports the view that AAT is the rate-limiting enzyme in strawberry ester aroma biosynthesis (Li et al., 2020; Rey-Serra et al., 2022). Furthermore, Sheng (Sheng et al., 2021) also found that esters were the most diverse group in 16 strawberry cultivars, further confirming the central role of esters in the formation of strawberry fruity aroma.

In contrast, the expression levels of key genes including LOX2–1, ACOX1, AIM1, FvPAL1–1, and HMGR1-like were significantly lower in ‘Snow Hare’. Despite higher LOX activity, insufficient transcription still led to significantly weakened synthesis of aldehydes, esters, and terpenes. However, genes such as FvSUR1-like, MDH, SDR2b-like, MNR, and TPS9 were highly expressed in ‘Snow Hare’, favoring the branched-chain amino acid and sugar metabolic pathways, which are more conducive to the formation of fruity esters. This provides the molecular and enzymatic basis for classifying ‘Snow Hare’ as fruity-type rather than floral-type (Pérez et al., 2002).

5. Conclusions

The analysis of appearance, flavor, nutrition and aroma indicators of the seven strawberry varieties showed that ‘XiangFei No.5’ had the largest single fruit weight, ‘Monterey’ had the highest fruit hardness, ‘ZhangJi’ had the highest contents of SSC and TSS, ‘Snow Hare’ had the best sugar-acid ratio and Vc content, ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ had the highest total content of aroma substances and total aroma value. The core indexes of strawberry quality evaluation were screened out by principal component analysis, and a comprehensive evaluation model of strawberry fruit quality was constructed: F = 0.2984F₁ + 0.2436F₂ + 0.1848F₃ + 0.1518F₄, which could be used for rapid variety screening. Aroma regulation studies showed that ‘MiaoXiang No.7’ was synergisticly activated by LOX-HPL pathway, ester synthesis, phenylpropane pathway and terpene synthesis pathway, presenting a strong floral aroma and fruity aroma. ‘Snow Hare’ was a typical fruity aroma type due to the high expression of genes related to branched-chain amino acids and glucose metabolism. In this study, the mechanism of strawberry aroma difference in facilities was systematically explained from the gene-enzyme-metabolite level, which could provide key targets and theoretical support for the improvement of strawberry aroma in solar greenhouses.

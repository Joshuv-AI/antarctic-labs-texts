Sustainable development evaluation of agri-culture-tourism integration in revolutionary old areas: a case study of Tianshui city
Authors: Maomao Luo, Huanyu Zhang, Mengke Xuan, Tingting Liu, Qiancheng Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a carrier of China’s historical and cultural heritage, the sustainable development of the integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism (Agri-Culture-Tourism) in the old revolutionary base areas plays an important role in promoting the protection of cultural heritage and regional economic growth. However, how to achieve sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism integration in old revolutionary base areas and what the key factors influencing such development are still remain important research topics that have not been explored in depth at present. This paper explores the practices and explorations of Tianshui City, a revolutionary old area, in terms of sustainable development in Agri-Culture-Tourism. Through methods such as Python Grey Relational Analysis, Logistic Regression Analysis, and Principal Component Factor Analysis, this study reveals the high correlation between Agri-Culture-Tourism and the tertiary industry in Tianshui City. It identifies key factors influencing tourists’ willingness to support, including the “level of infrastructure development for Agri-Culture-Tourism,” “richness of Agri-Culture-Tourism resources,” and “integration of Agri-Culture-Tourism supporting poverty alleviation.” Tianshui city has made remarkable achievements in promoting the sustainable development in Agri-Culture-Tourism, which requires focusing on promoting the development of secondary service industry, basic service industry and improving the efficiency of publicity. This study innovatively reveals effective experiences in the sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism in revolutionary old areas, providing valuable insights for the sustainable development of similar regions in China and other developing countries.Keywords: Sustainable development, Agri-culture-tourism, Revolutionary old areas, Integrated development, Services, Tianshui citySubject terms: Development studies, Economics, Economics

Introduction

In the pursuit of rapid economic development, the effective promotion of sustainable development has become a significant global challenge. In this context, many revolutionary old areas in China are situated in remote mountainous villages, where the unique revolutionary culture and abundant natural resources present vital issues for the study of sustainable development1. Taking Tianshui City, a typical revolutionary old area, as an example, the exploration of its integration of Agri-Culture-Tourism in a sustainable manner is crucial not only for the socio-economic advancement of the region but also serves as a reference for sustainable development in similar areas2,3.

The integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism is an innovative development model rooted in agriculture, infused with cultural essence, and driven by tourism. Through deep inter-industry collaboration and resource integration, it creates a multidimensional value chain encompassing economic, cultural, and ecological dimensions. This model transcends the limitations of traditional single-crop farming by incorporating elements like pastoral landscapes, agrarian culture, and folk crafts into tourism experiences. It not only enhances the added value of agricultural products but also promotes cultural preservation and tourism upgrading. Leveraging its “Five Cultural Heritage” and distinctive agricultural resources, Tianshui City has developed an integrated system combining sightseeing, educational tours, and wellness through approaches such as “culture-tourism + agriculture” and “homestay + culture.” This model serves as a practical example for sustainable agricultural tourism development in revolutionary old areas.

In recent years, scholars worldwide have made significant achievements in sustainable development, poverty alleviation, and rural revitalization. However, revolutionary old areas, as unique regions plagued by poverty and backwardness, often face neglected challenges in sustainable development4. The “Triple Bottom Line” theory, a cornerstone of sustainable development, encompasses economic, environmental, and social bottom lines. The integrated development of agriculture, culture, and tourism aligns with these three fundamental principles. This model demonstrates strong compatibility with the Triple Bottom Line framework. Economically, it expands agricultural functions through rural tourism, boosting sales of agricultural products and industries like homestays and catering services, thereby creating economic value and increasing farmers’ incomes5. Environmentally, it emphasizes ecological conservation and green development, utilizing natural resources like pastoral landscapes to promote low-carbon tourism and minimize environmental impact. Socially, it fosters urban-rural interaction, creates job opportunities, preserves rural culture, enhances community well-being, and strengthens social cohesion, achieving organic integration of economic, environmental, and social benefits. However, literature review reveals limited research on integrated agricultural-cultural-tourism development in revolutionary old areas, particularly insufficient studies focusing on sustainable development and regional case analyses6,7. This paper aims to fill the research gap in sustainable agricultural-cultural-tourism integration, laying a solid foundation for related studies in other regions.

Based on the Triple Bottom Line theory, the primary research questions addressed in this study are: Which industries provide critical momentum for the sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism in the revolutionary old area of Tianshui City? What specific factors play significant roles in the development of these key industries? And how can these factors be quantified to provide effective experiential insights for promoting sustainable development in revolutionary old areas? Therefore, this study conducts an empirical investigation based on field surveys, utilizing methods such as Python-based gray relational analysis, logistic regression analysis, reliability and validity testing, and principal component factor analysis to further promote the sustainable development in Tianshui City’s Agri-Culture-Tourism integration8. The results of this study will provide a decision-making basis for the sustainable development of agriculture, culture, and tourism in revolutionary old areas such as Tianshui City, contributing to the implementation of strategies for rural sustainable development and revitalization9. Additionally, this research aims to fill existing gaps in the literature, offering new perspectives for theoretical studies on sustainable development and the integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism, thus advancing the depth and refinement of these theoretical frameworks10.

Literature review

The Triple Bottom Line (TBL) includes economic, environmental, and social dimensions11,12. This theory posits that sustainable development can only be achieved by simultaneously creating economic, social, and environmental value13–15. Using the TBL framework to explore the topic of sustainable development evaluation of Agri-Culture-Tourism integration in revolutionary old areas is highly pertinent15. From the economic perspective, agricultural and tourism integration can stimulate economic development in revolutionary old areas, achieving industrial synergy and enhanced economic benefits16,17. From the environmental standpoint, this integration emphasizes ecological protection and sustainable resource utilization, contributing to the maintenance of the natural ecological balance in these areas18. From the social aspect, it facilitates cultural heritage transmission and community participation in profit-sharing, promoting social harmony in the revolutionary old areas19. Evaluating the sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism integration in revolutionary old areas allows for a comprehensive and systematic assessment of its performance across economic, environmental, and social dimensions, providing a scientific basis and decision-making support for sustainable development in these regions20.

The sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism involves the deep integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism, forming a new development model that combines agricultural production, cultural experiences, and leisure tourism through resource integration and industrial linkage21,22. This model aims to utilize agricultural resources as a foundation, integrate local cultural characteristics, and develop diverse tourism projects that not only promote the upgrading of the agricultural industry and enhance the added value of agricultural products but also enrich the tourism sector, meeting the growing spiritual and cultural needs of people and driving regional sustainable development23. The first is the exploration of an integrated development model combining agriculture, cultural creativity, and tourism, which mainly includes: historical and cultural tourism in agriculture, rural folk culture tourism, rural architectural culture tourism, and agricultural technology culture tourism24,25. The second is the in-depth coordination of primary, secondary, and tertiary industries, with a greater emphasis on agriculture as the core, integrating culture and tourism into the rural economy, leveraging existing characteristic resources and idle resources26,27. The third is to build a sustainable development framework for agritourism, which involves optimizing the “agriculture + cultural creativity + tourism” model and continuously deepening the awareness and concept of integration across broad perspectives, deep levels, and wide fields28,29.

Sustainable development is a comprehensive concept that emphasizes meeting the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs30,31. Currently, in academic circles, the concept of sustainable development is primarily articulated through the theory of the “triple bottom line,” which balances three pillars: economic development, social inclusiveness, and environmental protection32,33. This approach ensures the long-term sustainable use of resources and the well-maintained condition of the ecological environment. Numerous scholars have explored sustainable development from these three dimensions: Economic Dimension: Sustainable development advocates for a green, low-carbon, and circular economic model, which means minimizing the consumption of natural resources and reducing waste generation during production and consumption processes34,35. It emphasizes enhancing resource efficiency through technological innovation and industrial upgrading. Social Dimension: Sustainable development focuses on equitable, just, and inclusive social progress36. It aims to eradicate poverty, reduce social inequalities, and ensure that all individuals have access to basic services such as education and healthcare37. Environmental Dimension: Sustainable development is committed to protecting biodiversity, maintaining ecological balance, and addressing global environmental issues such as climate change38,39.

Existing research has revealed the significant role that the integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism plays in promoting sustainable development. However, there are notable shortcomings in this body of work: First, previous studies have not approached the specific context of revolutionary old areas, failing to illuminate the status of sustainable development of agricultural and tourism integration within these regions. Second, existing research has typically combined agriculture and tourism in a rather superficial manner, lacking a solid theoretical foundation40. Third, in exploring the development of agriculture and tourism, the literature has not addressed the key influencing factors that impact their integration. Current research still exhibits three major theoretical blind spots: Firstly, spatially, insufficient studies on the correlation between the unique historical context and resource endowments of revolutionary base areas result in development models that fail to adapt to local conditions. Secondly, temporally, the lack of tracking mechanisms for industrial integration dynamics makes it difficult to explain the contradictory characteristics at different developmental stages41. Thirdly, in terms of value, the absence of a coordinated evaluation system for economic, social, and environmental benefits hinders the comprehensive achievement of sustainable development goals. To address these gaps, this study employs the triple bottom-line theory and quantitative tools such as grey relational analysis and logistic regression models to precisely identify three core driving factors: infrastructure completeness, cultural resource uniqueness, and the synergy of poverty alleviation mechanisms.

Therefore, while prior research has provided theoretical insights and investigation guidance to a certain extent, this study presents the following innovations compared to existing literature. First, this study is the first to place the three key variables of agricultural and tourism integration, sustainable development, and revolutionary old areas within the same analytical framework, systematically revealing the assessment of sustainable development in agricultural and tourism integration specific to these regions. Second, this study introduces the Triple Bottom Line (TBL) theory, which provides a theoretical foundation for the integration of agricultural, cultural, and tourism sectors with sustainable development. Third, through field investigations and data analysis, this study systematically identifies the influencing factors affecting agricultural integration development in revolutionary old areas, offering theoretical guidance for advancing development in other regions.

Overview of the study area and data sources

Study area overview

Tianshui City is a prefecture-level city in Gansu Province, China, located in the southeastern part of the province (see Fig. 2). As of the end of 2024, Tianshui City has jurisdiction over 2 districts and 5 counties, covering a total area of 14,300 square kilometers, with a resident population of approximately 2.8918 million. The terrain of Tianshui City is higher in the west and lower in the east, with an elevation above 700 m. The city spans both the Yangtze and Yellow River basins and has a continental warm temperate semi-humid climate, with an average annual temperature of around 11 °C and annual precipitation exceeding 500 millimeters. The forest coverage rate is 36.84%, making it the largest natural forest base in Northwest China.

As a historical city in China, Tianshui not only possesses abundant historical and cultural resources but also has a deep revolutionary historical background. During the revolutionary war period, Tianshui City and its surrounding areas made significant contributions to the Chinese revolution. Therefore, selecting Tianshui City as the study object in revolutionary old areas can reflect the uniqueness and representativeness of integrated agricultural, cultural, and tourism development in such regions.

In recent years, Tianshui City has actively engaged in exploration and practice of comprehensive agricultural, cultural, and tourism development. For instance, by strengthening policy support and optimizing development patterns, Tianshui has successfully created several rural tourism demonstration sites, such as the Qinjun Mountain 4 A scenic area and Suwan “Beautiful Leisure Village” in China. In terms of sustainable development, Tianshui City has also made significant efforts. On one hand, it emphasizes environmental protection by promoting eco-agriculture and green tourism models, which reduce excessive development of natural resources and safeguard rural ecosystems and biodiversity. On the other hand, Tianshui has enhanced the preservation and transmission of historical and cultural heritage by organizing cultural tourism festivals and constructing cultural facilities, thereby promoting excellent traditional Chinese culture and revolutionary spirit. Therefore, studying the evaluation of comprehensive agricultural, cultural, and tourism development within the context of sustainable development in Tianshui City is not only representative but also holds practical significance and theoretical value.

Data sources

This study employs a “multi-source heterogeneous data fusion” strategy to systematically integrate four core datasets: (1) Statistical Yearbooks: Structural indicators including agricultural output and tourism revenue from the “Tianshui Statistical Yearbook (2014–2023)” and “Gansu Statistical Yearbook”; (2) Administrative Records: Extracted policy documents and project approval data from Tianshui’s Culture and Tourism Bureau and Agriculture and Rural Affairs Bureau; (3) Field Survey Data: 1,820 valid questionnaires obtained through stratified sampling, with a dual verification mechanism (electronic questionnaire + paper-based review) to ensure data consistency.

In the study of the integration of the sustainable development of agriculture, culture and tourism and the primary industry, the secondary industry and the tertiary industry, the data of the past ten years (2014 to 2023) are from Gansu Province, Gansu Provincial Bureau of Statistics, Tianshui Statistical Yearbook, Tianshui Municipal Bureau of Statistics, China Economic Data Network, etc.

In quantifying the influencing factors of sustainable development in the agricultural, cultural, and tourism sectors of the Revolutionary Old Area in Tianshui City, this study employed big data text mining techniques, complemented by questionnaire surveys and field interviews to collect data. Considering the convenience and feasibility of both offline and online data collection, the research adopted a hybrid approach. On one hand, offline interviews and completed questionnaires enabled a thorough understanding of the specific conditions and characteristics of Tianshui City, as well as capturing the genuine perspectives of the respondents. On the other hand, the online collection of questionnaires proved to be more efficient and expedited the data gathering process. During the survey, a total of 2,000 questionnaires were distributed. After a rigorous data review, missing and erroneous data were excluded, resulting in 1,820 valid questionnaires. This corresponds to a valid response rate of 91%, which met the anticipated recovery target and provided strong support for subsequent data analysis. The data were obtained through a combination of online and offline methods. All respondents who filled out the questionnaire gave verbal consent to the survey. Before the data survey, the investigator has explained the content of the survey, the purpose of the survey, the time and the scope of the survey, and invited the respondent to fill in the questionnaire only after obtaining the consent of the respondent.

Principal component factor analysis

Principal component factor analysis is a statistical method of dimensional reduction, which tries to recombine the original variables into a new group of unrelated comprehensive variables. At the same time, a few sum variables can be taken out according to the actual needs to reflect as much information of the original variables as possible. Component factor analysis combines the advantages of principal component analysis and factor analysis. Principal factor analysis focuses on providing a professional theory interpretation for the extracted common factor and each variable, so as to reasonably quantify the importance of individual variables in the overall variation. Therefore, this study uses the principal component factor analysis method to explore several key factors affecting the sustainable development of agricultural and cultural tourism in the old revolutionary base area, so as to grasp the research focus.

Based on the component score coefficient matrix and factor interpretation rate, we obtained the top three common factors. The first common factor is derived from the “Red culture and tourism projects are well combined with them,” “Willing to buy local specialty agricultural products,” “Local specialties are of good quality,” “Using virtual reality technology can boost travel interest,” and “Using simultaneous commentary technology can increase travel interest”. Therefore, it is named the secondary service factor.

The second common factor stems from “The transportation in the scenic spot is more convenient,” “Farmhouse music, restaurant class catering to do better,” “Hotels and home stay conditions are better,” “The ‘Internet +’ tourist attractions have done well,” and “The policy of developing tourism resources was well implemented”, so it’s called the basic service factor.

The third factor comes from “Interested in the promotion information of real picture scenic spots,” “Interested in the publicity video of the scenic spots,” “Interested in short videos in scenic spots,” “Interested in the feedback and evaluation information of the scenic spots,” and “The online new media publicity work in the scenic spot is relatively good”, and thus is named the publicity factor.

Basic service industries provide essential support and guarantees for social production and residents’ lives. These services are typically public and foundational, serving as the cornerstone of socio-economic operations. Secondary service industries emerge once basic service industries reach a certain level of development, aimed at meeting higher levels of consumer demand and economic growth needs. These services usually offer higher added value and specialization, reflecting the results of economic and consumption upgrades. Field surveys indicate that the secondary service industry in Tianshui City has generated higher economic value during the economic development process. This economic support has, in turn, facilitated and improved the development of basic services, promotional work, and other industries. Overall, both secondary services, basic services, and promotional efforts play a crucial role in the sustainable development of Tianshui City, necessitating the promotion of their coordinated progress.

Discussion

Based on the above research analysis, this study forms the following discussion:

Research summary

This study investigates the impact of integrated agricultural, cultural, and tourism development on sustainable growth in Tianshui City, a historic revolutionary base. Key findings reveal that Tianshui’s sustainable agricultural-tourism-cultural integration demonstrates the strongest correlation with the tertiary sector, which plays a pivotal role in establishing local support mechanisms. The thriving cultural tourism industry and related services have directly stimulated coordinated development across sectors including catering, accommodation, and transportation, injecting fresh momentum into regional economic diversification.

Secondly, the three key elements driving the sustainable development of Tianshui City’s integrated agriculture, culture, and tourism are the completeness of infrastructure, the richness of tourism resources, and poverty alleviation through integration. Well-developed infrastructure serves as the prerequisite, abundant tourism resources form the foundation, while poverty alleviation through integration represents a crucial manifestation of social welfare. These three elements complement each other, collectively promoting the sustainable development of Tianshui City.

Furthermore, this paper innovatively proposes an evaluation framework comprising “secondary service factors, basic service factors, and promotional factors,” providing a scientific assessment tool for sustainable development in revolutionary old base areas. The growth of secondary service industries has enhanced tourism experiences, while improvements in basic services ensure visitors ‘fundamental needs. Meanwhile, intensified promotional efforts have significantly boosted Tianshui City’s visibility and influence.

Ultimately, the “Three Bottom Lines Theory” has established both theoretical and practical foundations for the sustainable development of integrated agriculture, culture, and tourism in Tianshui City. When promoting the sustainable development of agricultural-cultural-tourism support mechanisms and healthy economy, the “Three Bottom Lines” theory should serve as a guiding principle to achieve organic balance between economic, social, and environmental aspects. Moreover, this approach provides practical experience and theoretical inspiration for other revolutionary old areas with similar conditions.

Research innovation

Firstly, this paper innovatively applies the “Three Bottom Lines” theory (economic, environmental, and social bottom lines) systematically to the sustainable development evaluation of revolutionary old base areas. Through this theoretical framework, we comprehensively analyze the performance of agricultural, cultural, and tourism integration in Tianshui City across three dimensions: economic development, environmental protection, and social-cultural heritage preservation. We also quantify the key factors influencing the accelerated sustainable development of these sectors, thereby filling a theoretical gap in sustainable development assessment for revolutionary old base areas. This approach not only expands the application scenarios of the “Three Bottom Lines” theory but also provides scientific evaluation tools for sustainable development in revolutionary old base areas.

Secondly, this study transcends the conventional limitations of simplistic agricultural-cultural-tourism integration in previous research. By innovatively adopting an integrated agricultural-cultural-tourism perspective, it systematically identifies key factors influencing sustainable development in revolutionary old revolutionary base areas. Through empirical analysis, the paper reveals that three core elements drive the healthy and sustainable economic development of Tianshui City: well-developed infrastructure, abundant resources, and poverty alleviation through integrated development. These findings provide valuable references for other revolutionary old revolutionary base areas to formulate targeted sustainable development strategies, thereby promoting the deep integration of theoretical frameworks and practical implementation.

Third, this paper not only analyzes the current status and challenges of sustainable development in agriculture, culture, and tourism in revolutionary old base areas, but also innovatively proposes a new integrated development model combining “red culture + green ecology + diversified industries”. This model emphasizes achieving comprehensive, coordinated, and sustainable development of economy, society, and environment in these regions by deeply exploring revolutionary cultural resources, strengthening ecological protection and utilization, and promoting the integration of primary, secondary, and tertiary industries. This innovation provides a fresh perspective and new ideas for revolutionary old base areas to explore distinctive sustainable development paths.

Limitations of the study

This study focuses on the sustainable development of integrated agriculture, culture, and tourism in Tianshui City, a revolutionary base area. The findings provide valuable insights for underdeveloped regions with similar developmental stages, though their applicability to wealthier areas requires further investigation. Moreover, as sustainable development is a long-term dynamic process, this research has not comprehensively addressed all potential influencing factors, necessitating further refinement in subsequent studies.

Conclusions and countermeasures

This research explores the sustainable development of agricultural, cultural, and tourism integration in revolutionary old areas, utilizing the Triple Bottom Line theory. In doing so, it innovatively establishes a bridge between agriculture, culture, tourism, and sustainable development42. This contribution expands the interdisciplinary field, constructs a theoretical framework, and provides empirical case studies that clarify development directions for policy formulation, while also laying a theoretical foundation for future academic research43. Furthermore, the findings of this study hold significant theoretical relevance for promoting the integrated development of multiple industries, thereby aiding poverty alleviation and economic prosperity in impoverished revolutionary old areas. To further enhance the effectiveness of sustainable development of Agri-Culture-Tourism, this paper draws the following conclusions and Countermeasures based on the previous research.

Effectively promoting the organic coordination of agriculture, culture, tourism and three industries

The grey relational analysis reveals that Tianshui City’s sustainable development in agriculture, culture, and tourism demonstrates the strongest correlation with the tertiary industry’s added value (R03 = 0.89), followed by a robust connection with the primary industry (R01 = 0.77) and a relatively weaker relationship with the secondary industry (R02 = 0.51). These empirical findings highlight the core industrial pillars and weak links in sustainable agricultural-cultural-tourism development44. First, establishing a big data digital platform for agriculture, culture, and tourism. Within the tertiary sector, cultural tourism services, catering accommodations, and marketing promotions exhibit strong synergy with the development trends of agriculture, culture, and tourism45. By integrating data resources across the three industries through a digital platform, inter-industry collaboration can be enhanced, amplifying the tertiary sector’s supporting role—a finding that directly aligns with the core characteristic of “industrial synergy” revealed by grey relational analysis. Second, promoting intelligent agricultural technologies combined with tourism experiences. As the foundational carrier of agricultural-cultural-tourism development, the quality of the primary industry directly impacts the richness of related resources. The application of intelligent agriculture not only improves agricultural production efficiency (solidifying the primary industry’s foundation) but also creates innovative “technology + agritourism” products, further deepening the integration of agriculture, culture, tourism, and the primary industry46. Third, cultivating supporting manufacturing industries for agriculture, culture, and tourism. Serving as a digital platform and intelligent agricultural promotion support, these industries compensate for the secondary sector’s insufficient empowerment of agricultural-cultural-tourism development, forming a collaborative industrial pattern characterized by “primary industry laying the foundation, tertiary industry providing empowerment, and secondary industry filling gaps.”

Promoting the sustainable development of agriculture, culture and tourism

Logistic regression analysis identified three core significant factors influencing tourists’ support willingness: “degree of agricultural-cultural-tourism infrastructure development”, “abundance of agricultural-cultural-tourism resources”, and “poverty alleviation through agricultural-cultural-tourism integration”. First, the digital platform strategy introduced a new “digital infrastructure upgrade” module. By monitoring operational status of transportation, accommodation, and medical facilities through big data, it optimizes resource allocation and enhances service efficiency47. Second, the smart agriculture strategy further clarified the orientation of “creating diversified agricultural-cultural-tourism resources”. Standardized production scenarios and tech-experience projects brought by smart agriculture complement traditional farming activities and red cultural resources, enriching the supply types of agricultural-cultural-tourism resources. Third, the strategy strengthened the “community participation and benefit-sharing mechanism"48. Empirical results show that tourists’ support willingness is highly correlated with value recognition of poverty alleviation49. By involving community residents in agricultural-cultural-tourism project operations and sharing development benefits, it not only achieves poverty alleviation goals but also enhances tourists’ value recognition, forming a virtuous cycle of “poverty alleviation empowerment + tourist support”.

Strengthening the bottom line of sustainable development of agriculture, culture and tourism through services and publicity

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) has identified three core influence dimensions: “Secondary Service Factor”, “Basic Service Factor”, and “Promotion Factor”. First, regarding the “Secondary Service Factor” (including indicators such as product innovation and industrial synergy), the digital platform strategy emphasizes the “Personalized Tourism Product Recommendation” feature50. By analyzing tourist preferences and integrating agricultural production cycles with cultural resource characteristics, customized travel routes are recommended to enhance the precision and innovation of secondary services51. Second, for the “Basic Service Factor” (including indicators such as transportation, accommodation, and healthcare), the smart agriculture strategy supplements “Smart Service Support” content. In smart agricultural parks, basic services like intelligent navigation, smart accommodation reservations, and health monitoring are provided to complete the “Agricultural Experience + Basic Services” closed loop. Third, concerning the “Promotion Factor” (including indicators such as marketing channels and brand communication), the digital platform strategy introduces a “Multi-Channel Integrated Marketing” module. By integrating online new media and offline experiential activities, the brand awareness of agritourism is enhanced, strengthening the promotional factor’s role in driving development.

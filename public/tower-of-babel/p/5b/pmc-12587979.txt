Development of a continuously updating evidence map in surgical education: study protocol for a living systematic review
Authors: Victoria Werdecker, Adisa Poljo, Pascal Probst, Erik Driessen, Adrian T Billeter, Beat P Müller-Stich, Jennifer M Klasen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite the growing volume of research in surgical education (SurgED), the integration of evidence into teaching practice remains fragmented and inconsistent. A structured and continuously updated synthesis is needed to support data-informed curriculum development and promote best practices across training programmes.Methods and analysisThis protocol describes the development of a living systematic review and evidence map of SurgED research. Studies will be identified through comprehensive searches of CENTRAL, PubMed and Web of Science, and screened independently by two reviewers, supported by an Artificial Intelligence (AI)-assisted screening tool to improve efficiency. Eligible studies will include both quantitative and qualitative designs involving medical students, surgical residents and faculty. Studies will be categorised across key educational domains such as simulation-based training, competency-based assessment, feedback, mentoring and technology-enhanced learning. The evidence map will be updated at least quarterly, with new studies screened and added in near real-time to ensure that the map remains current and actionable.Ethics and disseminationAs this study does not involve human participants, ethical approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, presentations at national and international conferences and open access integration into the Evidence at a glance (EVIglance) evidence platform.

Introduction

In Rembrandt’s The Anatomy Lesson of Dr. Nicolaes Tulp (1632), surgical training is portrayed as passive, observational work. Learning occurs in one direction—master to viewer—without interaction. Nearly four centuries on, the standards of surgical training have changed utterly. Contemporary training models emphasise interactivity, simulation and reflective practice.1 But in practice, the gap between educational ideals and reality remains staggering. Although a wide range of educational strategies in surgical training have been developed, their implementation within institutions is still inconsistent.2 To understand what needs to be integrated, it is essential to first recognise the core strategies that currently define modern surgical training. These include Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) frameworks using Entrustable Professional Activities,2 structured feedback and mentoring,3 4 formative assessment instruments like Objective Structured Assessment of Technical Skills (OSATS) and Non-Technical Skills for Surgeons (NOTSS)57 and the use of simulation and technology-enhanced learning environments.8 9 Together, these strategies form a multidimensional educational approach—one that is well supported by evidence, yet still unevenly translated into institutional practice.10 One contributing factor is the fragmented and fast-growing nature of the evidence base in surgical education (SurgED). Studies are often published across disciplines and indexed inconsistently, making it difficult for educators to access, interpret and apply the most relevant findings in practice.11 As a result, even widely accepted educational tools risk being overlooked—not because they are ineffective, but because they are not easily discoverable or actionable. What SurgED requires is not only more research, but above all a better translation of existing knowledge into day-to-day practice. Meanwhile, the swift evolution of SurgED calls for perpetual adjustments. SurgED must be constantly updated in terms of new scientific findings, technical innovation and evolving needs within the different specialties and professionals. To address this challenge, we will employ EVIglance, a web-based, open-access platform originally developed for the visualisation of evidence in surgical outcomes research. It enables users to explore systematic reviews and meta-analyses through a continuously updated evidence map and incorporates artificial intelligence to support study identification, classification and navigation. In this protocol, EVIglance will be adapted to SurgED, with the aim of providing a structured and accessible overview of the evidence base across key educational domains. In summary, this paper aims to (i) examine the need for an evidence-based approach to SurgED by outlining current core strategies, and (ii) propose the integration of an AI-enhanced, continuously updated evidence map within EVIglance.

Methods and analysis

The proposed project is a living Systematic Review (SR) with Meta-Analysis (MA), created by state-of-the-art systematic review processes and enhanced through artificial intelligence, that will facilitate patients’, clinicians’ and researchers’ access to a regularly updated version of the evidence on medical education in surgery. The proposed study will adhere to the established methodology outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.12 The article will be reported in accordance with the standard guidelines Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA).13

Systematic literature search

A literature search will be conducted across all major electronic bibliographic databases with relevance for SurgED: Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) in Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, PubMed and Web of Science. The systematic literature search will be conducted based on the following PICOS (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes and Study criteria).14

The PICO framework in surgical education

The PICO framework is a well-established method for formulating, structuring and evaluating clinical research questions in medicine in a methodological manner. It consists of four main components: Population (P), Intervention (I), Comparison (C) and Outcome (O). Here, we suggest:

Population (P)

All research aiming at medical students, surgical residents and faculty involved in SurgED publications. These groups are faced with the challenge of successfully acquiring theoretical knowledge and practical skills and transferring effectively into clinical practice.

Intervention (I)

Modern surgical training incorporates evidence-based learning methodologies, including simulation-based training (virtual reality, augmented reality, AI-based models), competency-based assessment (OSATS, EPA, NOTSS), mentoring, feedback and technology-based learning (e-learning, adaptive learning models). These methodologies are meant to make the learning process more effective and personalised.

Comparison (C)

Traditional surgical training relies on the apprenticeship model, which may require longer training periods and lacks standardised evaluation methods. Comparative analyses of modern training methods with traditional static curricula for assessing effectiveness are part of this.

Outcome (O)

* Primary outcome: Enhancement in surgical skill acquisition and competency-based performance gains.

* Secondary outcomes: Reduction in surgical errors, improvement in patient safety and educator workload optimisation through technology-enabled teaching.

Eligible study designs include RCT and SR with or without meta-analysis. In SurgED research, there is an increasing use of qualitative research methods.15 These are used to address specific issues that cannot be fully captured by quantitative approaches, such as offering deep insights into the experiences, perceptions and behaviours of learners and educators.16 This comprehensive approach ensures a synthesis of evidence that encompasses both quantitative data on efficacy and qualitative data on contextual factors influencing SurgED outcomes.

The search strategy will use Medical Subject Headings (MeSH terms) and keywords such as ‘Surgical Education’, ‘Competency-Based Training’, ‘Simulation-Based Learning’, ‘OSATS’, ‘ Entrustable Professional Activity (EPA)’, ‘Virtual Reality Training’, ‘Feedback’, ‘Mentoring’, ‘Debriefing’ and ‘Technology-Enhanced Learning’.

No date or language restrictions will be applied to have a broad collection of data.

Study status

The initial literature search was initiated in May 2025, and title and abstract screening is currently underway. The evidence map will be updated at least quarterly, with near real-time screening of newly published studies to maintain currency.

Study selection and data collection

In accordance with the recommendations of the Cochrane Collaboration,12 titles, abstracts and full texts of all identified articles will be screened against the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Screening will be performed by one human reviewer in parallel with the EVIglance AI agent (Meta LLaMA 3.3 70B), which applies the eligibility criteria to each record and serves as a second reviewer. The AI agent has been prospectively validated in a pilot testing study across six independent systematic review datasets (~40 000 records), where it demonstrated recall and specificity equivalent to a human reviewer, ensuring that its performance is not inferior to the human standard.17 Any record flagged for inclusion by either the human or the AI will proceed to full-text assessment. Disagreements or uncertainties after full-text screening will be resolved by consensus; if necessary, a third human reviewer will arbitrate. Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be documented in a PRISMA flow diagram.

For each included study, two reviewers will independently extract key data items using a piloted data extraction form. Discrepancies will be resolved by discussion or, if necessary, by a third reviewer. Extracted data will include bibliographic information (author, year, country), study design, population and sample size, intervention characteristics (type, duration, delivery format), comparator (if applicable), outcomes (primary and secondary) and a brief summary of the main results. Where applicable, we will also capture information required for subsequent risk of bias assessment and GRADE ratings to enable transparent appraisal of the evidence.

Studies addressing the same topic will be grouped together (eg, simulation-based training in arthroscopy, laparoscopy or robotic surgery). Systematic reviews and meta-analyses will be highlighted as higher-level evidence, and ongoing RCT protocols will remain displayed until final results become available. Qualitative studies will be coded to capture contextual and experiential insights.

For each outcome, the certainty of evidence will be assessed using the GRADE approach, considering risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision and publication bias. Risk of bias in randomised controlled trials will be assessed using the revised Cochrane RoB 2 tool. The aim of this mapping process is not to statistically homogenise diverse studies, but to visualise and structure the evidence base in a way that supports evidence-informed decision-making.

Living update process

To maintain the ‘living’ nature of this review, the literature search will be repeated at least quarterly. In practice, new studies will be screened and added in near real-time, often on a weekly basis, to ensure that the evidence map remains as current as possible. Each update will involve AI-supported screening with manual verification by two independent reviewers before integration into the EVIglance platform.

Discussion

Knowledge translation and the need for innovation

In the current era of extensive scientific research, it is challenging to maintain currency with the latest results.18 While MAs summarise the findings of numerous studies, they are limited by their static nature, required workload and risk of redundancy. Generally, evidence-based decision-making relies on the unrestricted processing of research results by guidelines. However, in SurgED, no universally accepted guideline or standard for evidence integration currently exists. This underscores the need for a freely continuously updated accessible database.

Living evidence map

The establishment of AI-enhanced living SRs and network MAs, updated constantly and using innovative approaches to dissemination, may help overcome traditional barriers of high workload and availability of evidence. The continuous updates of the systematic review will enable users to keep up-to-date and react faster to published trials. The proposed framework will provide a robust proof of concept and introduce the concept of living SRs and evidence maps to SurgED. While RCTs and SRs are often considered the gold standard in evidence synthesis, their predominance in traditional evidence hierarchies may not fully capture the complexity of educational interventions. Medical education, and SurgEd in particular, is a relatively young and evolving field, where qualitative research, observational studies and mixed-methods designs frequently offer critical insights, especially in areas such as feedback, professional identity formation and team-based learning.19 20 To reflect this diversity, the living evidence map must go beyond conventional hierarchies and integrate a broad range of study designs that are methodologically sound and contextually meaningful. Given the pivotal role of SurgED in ensuring high-quality surgical care, tools that enhance access to relevant and up-to-date evidence are essential. Unlike conventional search engines like PubMed, EVIglance allows more focused and quicker evidence retrieval.21 This saves time in identifying relevant studies and facilitates more efficient evidence-based decision-making. The integration of EVIglance in SurgED could accelerate the transfer of scientific knowledge into clinical practice by simplifying and speeding up literature searches and evidence assessment, which would correspond to the core competences in evidence-based practice.22

Future perspectives and impact on surgical education

Using an app as an information tool for the further development of surgical training aligns with contemporary trends in digital knowledge dissemination. By incorporating an AI-enhanced living evidence map of surgical training into EVIglance, surgical training could be revolutionised from reliance on unchanging curricula to an evolving, real-time evidence-based system. Such a system would enable curricular improvement by integrating high-quality, up-to-date scientific evidence into surgical training. As a proof of concept, EVIglance may contribute to a more consistent, accessible and evidence-informed infrastructure for surgical training.

Expected outcomes and limitations

The main outcome of this project will be a continuously updated evidence map of SurgED within the EVIglance platform. The map will display primary studies, systematic reviews and meta-analyses across predefined educational domains, making the evidence base accessible to educators and researchers.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The search will be limited to three major bibliographic databases, which may result in the omission of studies from grey literature or non-indexed sources. Classifying studies into predefined domains is necessary to provide a structured overview but may not fully capture the complexity of multi-component interventions. Although study selection will be supported by automated tools, final decisions will rely on human reviewers to minimise errors. Maintaining the living nature of the map will require ongoing resources and frequent updates. In practice, studies will be added in near real-time to ensure that the map remains continuously up to date.

Ethics and dissemination

This study does not involve human participants or identifiable data; therefore, ethical approval is not required. The findings of the review and the resulting evidence map will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international conferences. The evidence map will be freely accessible via the EVIglance platform to facilitate its timely use in curriculum development and educational research.

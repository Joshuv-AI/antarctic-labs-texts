Characterisation of cells markers associated with IKZF1plus in BCP-ALL
Authors: Caroline Barbieri Blunck, Caroline Pires Poubel, Bruno A. Lopes, Thayana C. Barbosa, Ana Luiza Tardem Maciel, Elaine Sobral da Costa, Ariadne da Rocha Figueiredo, Marcelo G.P. Land, Márcia Trindade Schramm, Maura Rosane Valério Ikoma-Coltutato, Renan Garcia Gomes, Mecneide Mendes Lins, Thais Ferraz Aguiar, Marcela Braga Mansur, Mariana Emerenciano
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A group of 17 genes are differentially expressed between the different IKZF1 groups: IKZF1plus, IKZF1del and IKZF1wild.•KCNA5, GREB1, PTPRB, SDK1 and EPOR, genes which encode cellular membrane markers, are associated with the presence of IKZF1plus in BCP-ALL.•KCNA5 and GREB1 genes are the most promising biomarkers for predicting IKZF1plus.•VPREB1 deletions are recurrent in the IKZF1plus group of patients, being an extremely relevant biomarker to assist in therapeutic risk stratification for these patients.

Introduction

B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (BCP-ALL) is characterised by broad clinical and biological heterogeneity that is underpinned by structural and/or numerical genetic alterations acquired by a leukaemic clone. These changes can promote the initiation and maintenance of this disease and are able to stratify patients into different prognostic risk groups [1]. The main cytogenetic-molecular subgroups in BCP-ALL are: (i) high hyperdiploidy (HeH, 51–65 chromosomes); (ii) ETV6::RUNX1, associated with good prognosis; (iii) TCF3::PBX1, with intermediate prognosis; (iv) BCR::ABL1; and (v) KMT2A rearrangements (KMT2A-r), both associated with dismal prognosis [2]. The presence of copy number alterations (CNAs) in genes related to the development of B cells has been associated with an increased relapse rate and a poorer prognosis in subgroups of patients with BCP-ALL [[3], [4], [5]]. For example, IKZF1 deletions (IKZF1del), which occur in approximately 15 % of paediatric BCP-ALL, are associated with high risk of relapse [5]. IKZF1 encodes a zinc finger-like transcription factor that plays a role in chromatin remodelling and is essential for the development of all lymphoid cell lineages [6].

In 2018, the Italian Association of Paediatric Haematology and Oncology (AIEOP) and Berlin-Frankfurt-Munster (BFM) groups (AIEOP-BFM) established a new significant prognostic subgroup named IKZF1plus. This subgroup was defined by the co-occurrence of IKZF1del with deletions in CDKN2A, CDKN2B, PAX5, or in the pseudoautosomal region 1 (PAR1) in the absence of ERG deletions [7,8]. The study showed that IKZF1plus conferred a worse prognosis and a significantly higher risk of relapse depending on measurable residual disease [7]. More recently, the same group performed a retrospective genome-wide analysis and showed a similar event-free survival (EFS) for both IKZF1del and IKZF1plus when excluding patients positive for ETV6::RUNX1, HeH, or iAMP21. Therefore, this study posed new perspectives regarding the value of IKZF1plus as a prognostic biomarker. The present study aimed to identify biomarkers that could facilitate the identification of IKZF1plus subgroup. Our results show that KCNA5 and GREB1 are dysregulated in the IKZF1plus group. Furthermore, we observed an enrichment of VPREB1 gene deletions in this group.

Materials and methods

Patient cohorts

Two series of cases were included in this study. The first comprised data from 125 patients with BCP-ALL from the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET) ALL phase 2 cohort (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/projects; TARGET-ALL-P2). The second included 60 patients diagnosed with BCP-ALL between 2018 and 2021 (median-age: 19 years, varying from 0 to 66 years, 34 males and 26 females) and enrolled in the Genetics of Acute Leukaemia Laboratory (GenLAb) - INCA for analyses of recurrent molecular alterations. All patients were characterised based on clinical-laboratory data and IKZF1 status. This study was approved by the INCA CAAE Ethics Committee: 33,709,814.7.0000.5274.

Nucleic acids extraction and cDNA synthesis

For GenLAb cases, genomic DNA and total RNA were isolated from bone marrow (BM) samples at diagnosis using the TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California/USA) following the manufacturer's protocol. In order to wash away any remaining salt residues and to concentrate nucleic acids, both purified DNA and RNA were precipitated using a standard ethanol-isopropanol protocol. DNA was alternatively isolated using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany) for some of the samples according to the manufacturer's instructions. Quantification has been performed by measuring absorbance in the 260 nM range using NanoDrop™ (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). cDNA was synthesised with SuperScript™ II Reverse Transcriptase (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California/USA) using 1 µg of RNA treated with Ambion™ DNase I (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California/USA). Subsequently, to confirm cDNA integrity, a fragment of GAPDH was amplified using the following primers: Forward: 5′ - TGACCCCTTCATTGACCTCA - 3′; Reverse: 5′ - AGTCCTTCCACGATACCAAA - 3′.

Molecular characterisation

GenLAb samples were screened for common BCP-ALL abnormalities, such as ETV6::RUNX1, BCR::ABL1, TCF3::PBX1 and KMT2A-r, following standard procedures [9]. The molecular characterisation of TARGET cases was collected through publicly available clinical data from the GDC project portal. FISH, PCR, or cytogenetics were used to track recurrent BCP-ALL alterations (for more information, see the Common Data Element (CDE) file at https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/projects/TARGET-ALL-P2).

Detection of IKZF1del and CNAs that characterise the IKZF1plus group

The presence of CNAs in IKZF1, CDKN2A/B, PAX5, EBF1, ETV6, BTG1, RB1, and PAR1 region was evaluated by MLPA in the GenLAb cases using the SALSA MLPA P335-C1 kit (MRC Holland, Amsterdam, The Netherlands) according to the manufacturer's recommendations. The different types of IKZF1 deletions were confirmed by long distance multiplex-PCR or SALSA MLPA P202 B1–2 kit, as previously described [10,11]. ERG deletions were detected by SALSA MLPA P327-C1 kit and/or digital multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (digitalMLPA) D007 ALL kit following the manufacturer's instructions. Conventional MLPA data were analysed using Gene Marker 2.2.0 software (Soft Genetics LLC, State College, PA). Relative peak ratios between 0.8 and 1.2 were considered normal, while values below or above indicated losses or gains, respectively. Deletion of the PAR1 region was defined as the presence of deletion in at least CSF2RA and IL3RA genes. IKZF1 deletions that co-occurred with CDKN2A/B, PAX5 or PAR1 deletion in the absence of ERG deletion were classified as IKZF1plus. P2RY8::CRLF2 fusion was assumed to be present in patient samples with CSFRA and IL3RA deletions and retention of the CRLF2 probe [7]. Confirmation of CNAs identified by conventional MLPA and identification of new important CNAs in the context of BCP-ALL have been performed by digital MLPA D007 ALL according to the manufacturer's recommendation [12].

The same rationale was used to categorise the IKZF1plus group in TARGET cases. However, the CNAs were identified through publicly available data obtained from whole genome sequencing (WGS). These data were obtained and analysed by Complete Genomics, Inc. platform (for more information, see “TARGET Molecular Characterization Platforms” session at https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/target). The raw reads were aligned to the reference human genome GRCh37/hg19. The ploidy inference for each genome segment of BCP-ALL samples was obtained from tab-separated values files in the TARGET project portal (discontinued version).

Differential gene expression analysis

TARGET patients' transcriptomes were used for differential expression analysis. These data were obtained from paired-end RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) performed in the Illumina platform. The British Columbia Cancer Agency (BCCA) carried out the data processing using the reference human genome GRCh37. The complete protocol can be found on the GDC Portal under “TARGET Molecular Characterization Platforms”. As the input data for differential gene expression analysis, we downloaded read counts (not normalised) values of each gene from open-access files of these patients. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified among IKZF1 groups using the DESeq2 R package. Genes with log2-fold change greater than 0 were considered up-regulated, while those with log2-fold change less than 0 were referred to as down-regulated genes. Significant findings were regarded with a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01.

Quantitative PCR (qPCR)

To validate the DEGs, we performed reverse transcription–qPCR (RT-qPCR) assays on samples from the GenLAb case series. RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, qPCR assays and subsequent analyses were performed with the quality controls recommended by The Minimum Information for Publication of Quantitative Real-Time PCR Experiments (MIQE) guidelines [13]. The amplification efficiency of the oligonucleotides was calculated for each transcript and were evaluated from the quantification cycle (Cq) values obtained by amplification of a set of five serial 10-fold dilutions using high-quality cDNA.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed in the R environment. The clinical-laboratory variables were described by their absolute and relative frequencies. The principal component (PC) graph and boxplots were generated using the ggplot2 R package. The Wilcoxon rank sum test (also known as Mann-Whitney test) was used with the ggpubr package to compare IKZF1 groups (two at a time). P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

These patients were grouped either as IKZF1plus (12.8 %), IKZF1del (9.6 %) or IKZF1wild (78.4 %). Molecular characterization

To provide an initial overview of the similarity of individual samples in relation to IKZF1 groups and molecular subtypes of BCP-ALL, we first used the principal component analysis (PCA) . We observed that molecular subgroups tend to form separate clusters while the IKZF1 groups have a more heterogeneous profile (Fig. 1A). We then evaluated the distribution of patients based on the cytogenetic-molecular subtypes of BCP-ALL. Thus, it was possible to observe that the variance between groups was associated with cytogenetic-molecular subgroups rather than with IKZF1 status (Fig. 1B). We found an enrichment of IKZF1plus cases within the B-others subgroup. Differential expression analysis

For differential expression analysis, we performed a pair-to-pair comparison between IKZF1 status groups: IKZF1del (n = 3), IKZF1plus (n = 12), IKZF1wild (n = 36). The comparison between IKZF1plus versus IKZF1del group revealed 18 and 50 genes with decreased and increased expression in the IKZF1plus group, respectively. The analysis of IKZF1plus versus IKZF1wild group indicated 1355 genes with decreased expression and 209 genes with increased expression in the IKZF1plus group. At last, we observed 102 genes with decreased expression and 10 genes with the expression increased in the IKZF1del group compared to IKZF1wild (Fig. 2A). Then, we used a Venn Diagram to inspect which genes were mostly related to the IKZF1plus group (Fig. 2B). We observed four up-regulated genes (KCNA5, TCL1B, LDB3 and MYO7A), and 13 down-regulated genes (MAP3K9, DPF3, EHD2, MREG, DUSP5, GREB1, PTPRB, HES4, SDK1, EPOR, SHROOM3, RASSF6 and IGSF10). To identify a good IKZF1plus marker that could be detected by flow cytometry, we restricted our analyses to those genes that encode membrane proteins. Validation and quantification of gene expression

We then quantified the expression levels of those genes using the GenLAb series of 60 cases, which were evenly distributed among each IKZF1 group: IKZF1plus (n = 20), IKZF1del (n = 20), and IKZF1wild (n = 20) (Table 1). We observed that the KCNA5 was upregulated in the IKZF1plus group when compared to the IKZF1del (P = 0.038) and IKZF1wild (P = 0.001) groups (Fig. 3). On the other hand, GREB1 was downregulated in the IKZF1plus compared to the IKZF1del (P = 0.052) and IKZF1wild group (P = 0.008). However, we did not observe any statistical significance when analysing the expression of the PTPRB, SDK1 and EPOR genes between IKZF1 groups. Copy number change analysis

To understand the genetic heterogeneity amongst IKZF1 groups, 25 patient samples (IKZF1plus n = 9; IKZF1del n = 8; IKZF1wild n = 7) of the GenLAb series were additionally characterised by digital MLPA. We observed a higher frequency of deletions within the VPREB1 gene (22q11.22) in the IKZF1plus group (50 %) compared to the remaining groups: IKZF1del (33 %) and IKZF1wild (12.5 %). CD200 (3q13.3) deletions were also more frequent in IKZF1plus patients (37.5 %) than in IKZF1del (0 %) and IKZF1wild (12.5 %). Discussion

Genomic technologies developed in recent decades have led to the identification of many genetic markers. Despite this, not all of them are deemed suitable for clinical use. Sixteen years since the identification of deletions in the IKZF1 gene [4], it is evident that this biomarker defies expectations and continues to hold significance, demonstrating a negative prognostic impact across various studies conducted to date. However, the importance of the IKZF1plus profile seems to depend more on particular circumstances, as recent studies have not found relevant differences between the IKZF1del and IKZF1plus groups [[14], [15], [16]].

There is a complexity in the interactions between molecular cytogenetic subgroups, individual characteristics, and cooperative molecular lesions in patients with IKZF1 deletions. For instance, previous study from our group showed that CRLF2 overexpression is linked to IKZF1plus [9]. Additionally, a recent study found that IKZF1plus occurred three times more often in Down Syndrome (DS)-ALL patients compared to the remaining patients [17]. Therefore, there still is a need to understand the disease biology that defines the IKZF1plus group and whether it helps risk stratification of specific patients. In the present study, we evaluated differentially expressed genes between IKZF1 groups: IKZF1plus, IKZF1del and IKZF1wild. In our discovery case series, we identified that KCNA5, GREB1, PTPRB, SDK1 and EPOR, which encode cellular membrane markers, are associated with the IKZF1plus profile in BCP-ALL. These markers can be evaluated by immunophenotyping and, therefore, are more feasible to be incorporated into diagnosis flow in most laboratories. Although prospective studies and tests of agreement between methods are needed, the use of a more accessible technology, such as flow cytometry, could be directly applicable to clinical practice to better stratify these patients. The association between KCNA5 and GREB1 and IKZF1 groups was successfully validated in the GenLAb series of cases. The KCNA encodes a potassium channel from the most complex class of voltage-dependent ion channels, both from a functional and structural point of view. Its diverse functions include regulation of neurotransmitter release, heart rate, insulin secretion, neuronal excitability, epithelial electrolyte transport, smooth muscle contraction, and cell volume. Although poorly explored in the leukemic context, Yan H and colleagues demonstrated that KCNA5 expression may be an important marker of unfavourable prognosis in acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) [18].

The growth-regulating oestrogen receptor binding 1 (GREB1) is an early oestrogen-responsive gene in breast cancer cells. In the uterus, GREB1 is highly expressed in the endometrium, and its expression levels fluctuate in accordance with oestrogen levels through the woman's reproductive age (PDZK1 and GREB1 are oestrogen regulated genes expressed in hormone-responsive breast cancer). Tong and collaborators showed that GREB1 was associated with significantly increased levels of the histone modifications H3K4me1/3, H3K9ac, and H3K27ac in leukaemia lineages. Additionally, GREB1-deficient Ishikawa cells are much more resistant to chemotherapy and human endometrial cancers with low GREB1 expression are associated with poorer overall survival [19]. In our study, we observed that KCNA5 was more expressed in the IKZF1plus group when compared to the IKZF1del and IKZF1wild groups. Conversely, GREB1 was downregulated in the IKZF1plus group when compared to the IKZF1del and IKZF1wild. Therefore, we note that these genes might be important markers for the IKZF1plus group. While we recognize the value of performing outcome analyses, the inherent limitations of our dataset, including the high-risk classification bias in the TARGET cohort and the varied treatment regimens in the GenLAb series, prevented us from conducting a comprehensive and informative evaluation of treatment and survival outcomes.

In our analyses using digital MLPA, we observed that the IKZF1plus group had additional exclusive deletions in the CD200 and BTLA genes, besides the high frequency of VPREB1 deletions (22q11.22). VPREB is a lineage restricted marker expressed very early in B-cell differentiation [20]. Of note, VpreB (also known as CD179a) was recently indicated as an immunotherapeutic target, since B-ALL cells treated with anti-VpreB might be more sensitive to low-dose chemotherapy [21]. Our results are in concordance with a previous and robust study conducted in other six independent clinical cohorts. They demonstrated that IKZF1plus is significantly correlated with 22q11.22 deletions and that patients with these concomitant deletions had significantly worse outcomes compared to patients with IKZF1plus and wild-type 22q11.22 [22].

Our and others’ data reinforce the importance and complexity of IKZF1 deletions in paediatric BCP-ALL. Continued research will allow us to gain additional insights and more robust results over the biology of this neoplasm. This will ultimately lead to better risk stratification and treatment decisions to be offered to each patient.

Preparation, characteristics and antioxidant activity of mung bean peel polysaccharides
Authors: Wenting Zhang, Gangliang Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mung bean peel polysaccharide (MBP) extracted by hot water was chemically modified. By changing the dosage of phosphorylation reagent and acetylation reagent, three kinds of phosphorylated MBP ( P-MBP-1, P-MBP-2, P-MBP-3 ) and acetylated MBP ( AC 0.6-MBP, AC 1-MBP, AC 1.4-MBP ) with different degrees of substitution were prepared. By measuring the sugar content and substitution degree of the modified products, it was found that the amount of reagent had a certain effect on both of them. The modified products were determined by infrared spectrum and nuclear magnetic resonance. The results showed that the chemical modification was successful. The in vitro antioxidant capacity (·OH scavenging ability, O2−·clearing ability, reducing capacity, resistance to lipid peroxidation) of seven polysaccharide were measured, which manifested that chemical modification could enhance the antioxidant ability of MBP to varying degrees, and the DS also had a certain impact on their antioxidant activity. This promoted the development of mung bean peel polysaccharide functional products and the utilization of mung bean peel resources.Keywords: Antioxidant activity, Chemical modification, MBP, Structural characterizationSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology

Introduction

Mung bean, an important leguminous herb, has a long history of planting and eating in China as a traditional food of the same origin1. China’s mung bean planting range is wide and the output is large, so it also makes China’s total output of mung bean in the world precedent. Since ancient times, China has the habit of eating mung beans. It is widely believed that mung bean has the effect of clearing heat and detoxifying, diuresis and quenching thirst, so in summer mung bean soup has become a necessary fire reducer. People eat mung beans in a variety of ways, not only the mung bean stew into soup, but also the processing of mung bean cake, mung bean vermiculas, mung bean sprouts to eat. According to the Compendium of Materia Medica, a famous Chinese pharmacopoeia, mung beans have medicinal value in addition to food value. Mung bean, as a traditional Chinese medicine, has the effects of detoxification, recuperating mentality, relieving summer heat and regulating gastrointestinal tract. This may be related to the rich nutrients in mung beans. Mung beans contain protein, dietary fiber, minerals, vitamins and a large number of bioactive compounds - polysaccharides, polyphenols, polypeptides. In fact, not only in mung beans, mung bean skin also contains nutrients that the human body needs to ingest. Researches have found that mung bean skin is rich in active substances1–3. For example, dietary fiber can improve the degeneration of liver fat through regulation and effectively alleviate the obesity disease caused by high-fat diet4; polyphenols can inhibit the activity of carbohydrate hydrolase and thus effectively reduce blood sugar5,6; polysaccharide not only has antioxidant properties that can reduce the body’s protection from free radical damage7,8, but also has immunomodulatory effects on macrophages9,10.

Polysaccharide is a kind of macromolecular compounds with antioxidant, antitumor, immunomodulatory activities, which exists widely in animals, plants and microorganismsis11,12. Among them, plant polysaccharide has various of pharmacological activities and bioactivity. Many important medicinal and edible polysaccharide are extracted from plants13. Plant polysaccharide is widely present in flowers, fruits, skins, roots, stems, and leaves of plants. The bioactivity of polysaccharide from different parts of plants was different; and the biological activity of polysaccharide may vary depending on the extraction methods used1,14. The water-soluble non-digestible polysaccharide of mung bean mainly exists in the outer skin. However, in the mass production of mung bean cakes and vermicelli in the food industry, mung bean skins are either used as animal feed or thrown away as waste, which makes the utilization of mung bean skins insufficient, resulting in a waste of resources. As an important bioactive component in mung bean skin, mung bean skin polysaccharide plays an important role in human life and health, so it is necessary to improve its utilization rate through the study of mung bean skin.

However, due to the influence of many factors, the bioactivity of polysaccharide is weak. Many researches have shown that chemical modification can effectively enhance polysaccharide biological property15,16. Different modification methods and different conditions will improve the biological characteristics of polysaccharide to varying degrees. Acetylation usually occurs on the branch chain structure of polysaccharides, and the active hydroxyl oxygen or amino nitrogen introduces acetyl groups to the sugar ring through nucleophilic substitution reactions with acylation agents16. The introduction of acetyl group changes the structure of polysaccharide molecules to a certain extent, and the branch chain extends sufficiently in space to expose the hydrophilic group hydroxyl, which increases the solubility of polysaccharide17. Phosphorylation usually uses a certain method to combine the charged phosphate group with the polysaccharide side chain in the form of covalent bond, thereby causing changes in molecular structure and achieving the purpose of improving biological activity18.

It was generally believed that within a certain range, the activity of the derivative was related to the degree of substitution, and the higher the degree of substitution, the better the activity of the product19. In this paper, the mung bean skin polysaccharide(MBP) was acetylated and phosphorylated. The modified products with different degrees of substitution(DS) were got by changing the amount of the reagent, and a series of structural characterization and activity determination of the derivatives were carried out. Experimental results proved that the modified products with different DS had different antioxidant capacity, which was similar to the conclusion of Duan et al.20. The higher the DS, the better the antioxidant ability20. At the same time, phosphorylated MBP with high substitution degree was prepared, and the best conditions for modifying MBP by two different methods were determined based on its activity. This laid a certain experimental foundation for the study of MBP, and promoted MBP to be expected to be used as an antioxidant in food and medical treatment.

Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

The mung bean peel powder was obtained from mung beans that were soaked, rubbed, shelled, and dried. The mung bean (Origin: Anhui, China) was purchased by the local supermarket. The related reagents involved in the experiment were all analytically pure to meet the requirements.

Preparation of derivatives with different degrees of substitution

Preparation of MBP

The method of hot water extraction was adopted21. In short, added 30 g of defatted mung bean peel powder to a 1000 mL round-bottom flask, and added distilled water in a ratio of 1 : 30, then reacted at 90 °C for 3 h. Then MBP was gained by centrifugation, spin steaming, deproteinization, dialysis, alcohol precipitation and freeze-drying.

Preparation of phosphorylated MBP(P-MBP) with different degrees of substitution

In simple terms22, 0.25 g MBP and 12.5 mL DMF (N, N-Dimethyl Formamide) were added to the beaker, then sealed and stirred for 2 h at room temperature. A three-neck flask containing 7.5 mL pyridine solution was placed in an ice water bath for precooling, then 2 mL of POCl3 was added slowly to it. When there is a yellowish solid in the flask, the MBP-DMF solution was slowly dripped to it. Subsequently, the mixture was reacted for 3 h in an oil bath at 55 °C. After the mixed solution was naturally cooled, 1 mol/L NaOH was slowly added to make the solution neutral. Finally, added 3 times the volume of anhydrous ethyl alcohol to the reaction solution, refrigerated overnight, centrifuged, dissolved, dialysis, freeze-dried, P-MBP-1 was gained.

The amount of MBP and DMF remained unchanged, and the ratios of pyridine and phosphorus oxychloride were changed to 7.5:2.5 (v/v), 7.5:3 (v/v), and P-MBP-2 and P-MBP-3 were prepared according to the above methods.

Preparation of acetylated MBP (AC-MBP) with different degrees of substitution

Refer to the previous method for determination21,23. In a nutshell, 0.5 g MBP was fully dissolved in 30 mL distilled water, after that slowly added 1 mol/L of NaOH to make the solution neutral. And then 0.6 mL acetic anhydride was added drop by drop to the solution, and the pH of the solution was adjusted to 8–10 with sodium hydroxide throughout the process until all acetic anhydride was consumed. Then the mixture was reacted at 30 °C for 2 h. At the end of the reaction, the solution was adjusted to neutrality with 1 mol/L HCl. Subsequently, AC 0.6-MBP was got by alcohol precipitation, collection of precipitation, dialysis and lyophilization. Keeping other conditions unchanged, adjusted the dosage of acetic anhydride to 1 mL, 1.4 mL, and prepared AC 1-MBP and AC 1.4-MBP in sequence according to the above method.

Measurement of degree of substitution

Determination of P-MBP substitution degree

The DS of P-MBP-1, P-MBP-2 and P-MBP-3 was determined by ammonium phosphomolybdate method22. Placed 0.05 g of the sample in a beaker, slowly added 0.5 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid and 0.5 mL of concentrated nitric acid, and then heated the beaker in a 45 °C water bath until smoke was produced. After cooling to room temperature, added 0.5 mL of 30% hydrogen peroxide solution to the beaker, and then heated the beaker again in a 45 °C water bath until no smoke was produced. Repeated the above steps once until the solution turns pale yellow and no more smoke was produced. After cooling the above mixed solution to room temperature, added 0.5 mL of 6 mol/L HCl solution to it, and then heated it in a water bath until the acid was completely decomposed. Finally, transferred the mixture to a 50 mL volumetric flask and diluted to volume with distilled water. Added 3 mL Tris buffer solution and 3 mL phosphate reagent to 5 mL of the solution, mixed well, and reacted in a 45 °C water bath for half an hour. After cooling the solution to room temperature, measured the absorbance at 580 nm and then substituted it into the standard curve to calculate its phosphate content. Measurement of total sugar content of MBP and it’s derivatives

Prepared MBP and it’s derivatives into a 0.1 mg/mL solution.Taken 1 mL sample solution, then added 0.5 mL phenol solution and 2.5 mL concentrated sulfuric acid to it, and reacted in boiling water bath for 15 min after full shock. After the solution was naturally cooled to room temperature, the absorbance of the mixed solution was measured at 490 nm by an ultraviolet spectrophotometer, and the total sugar content of each sample solution was calculated according to the glucose standard curve.

Structural characterization of MBP and its derivatives

Infrared spectrum measurement

The samples of MBP and its modified products were ground into powder, and then pressed by KBr, respectively, and tested in Fourier transform infrared spectrometer. Through the spectrum, the preliminary analysis of the structure was carried out.

NMR determination

A sample of 40 mg was weighed and fully dissolved in 0.5 mL D2O, then placed in a nuclear magnetic tube and characterized by an NMR instrument. P-MBP sample was analyzed by phosphorus spectrum.

Study on the antioxidant ability of MBP and its derivatives in vitro

Results and discussion

Analysis of total sugar content and substitution degree

Based on the glucose standard curve(Y = 15.194X + 0.0744;R2 = 0.9991), phosphate standard curve(Y = 21.878X + 0.1952;R2 = 0.9991) and related formulas22,27. The yield(Ratio of obtained polysaccharide mass to raw material mass) and DS of MBP and its derivatives in Table 1 were acquired. According to Table 1, the sugar content of MBP was 57.82%. When the ratio of pyridine to phosphorus oxychloride was listed as 7.5 : 2.5, the DS of P-MBP was the largest, which was 0.82. When the amount of acetic anhydride was 1 mL, the largest DS of AC-MBP was 0.39. Compared with MBP, the sugar content of its modified products decreased to varying degrees. This might be due to the decomposition of phosphorus oxychloride during the phosphorylation reaction to form a peracidic environment. In the acetylation reaction, a strong acid and alkali environment were formed, which led to the hydrolysis of the derivative and the reduction of its content. Compared with P-MBP, the DS of AC-MBP was lower. This might be because the acetylation reaction had higher requirements on the stability of PH value, and the PH range was not well controlled during this process, resulting in the breakage of chemical bonds of polysaccharide, thus affecting its DS28.

During the derivatization experiment, it was found that the DS of the derived MBP was positively correlated with the proportion of chemical modification reagents within a certain range. However, the increasing proportion of chemical reagents would cause the sugar content to decrease, which would also affect the DS. Therefore, it is necessary to choose appropriate conditions to attain derivative polysaccharide with ideal DS.

Infrared analysis

Polysaccharide is a kind of compound with specific functional groups. The structure of polysaccharide and its derivatives can be qualitatively analyzed by infrared spectroscopy. Figure 1a showed the infrared spectrum of MBP. The absorption peak of 3269 cm− 1 was owing to the O-H in the sugar ring. The absorption peaks of 2924 cm− 1 and 1418 cm− 1 were caused by the antisymmetric vibration of C-H, which belonged to the characteristic absorption of polysaccharide29,30. The absorption at 1081 cm− 1 and 1018 cm− 1 were caused by the vibration of C-O-C, which illustrated the existence of pyran ring structure in polysaccharide20. The strong absorption at 1602 cm− 1 and the weak absorption at 1751 cm− 1 were attributed to COO-and C = O, respectively, showing that the polysaccharide contained uronic acid and the content was low31.The absorption at 1251 cm− 1 might be owing to the C-O-C bond existing in the polysaccharide, which caused the asymmetric stretching vibration32–34. 763 cm− 1 was the characteristic absorption of C-O-C on the D-pyranose ring35. The absorption near 833 cm− 1 and 927 cm− 1 indicated that there were α and β glycosidic bonds in the polysaccharide, respectively36.

(a) Infrared spectrum of MBP. (b) Infrared spectra of MBP phosphorylated with different degrees of substitution. (c) Infrared spectra of acetylated MBP with different degrees of substitution.

Figure 1b was the spectrum of MBP with different DS after phosphorylation modification. It could be observed that it was similar to the waveform of Fig. 1a as a whole, and the main structure of the modified polysaccharide had not changed. Compared with the unmodified MBP, the modified P-MBP had two new absorption peaks, which were about 1245 cm− 1 and 900 cm− 1, respectively. The two absorption peaks were the stretching vibrations from the P = O bond and the P-O-C bond, respectively19,37. Moreover, by comparison, we could observe that the absorption peaks of 2924 cm− 1,1418 cm− 1 and 1251 cm− 1 in Fig. 1a were weakened or disappeared to varying degrees after phosphorylation modification, which was caused by phosphate groups in MBP38. From Fig. 1b, we could find that the absorption peak intensity of P-MBP-2 at about 1245 cm− 1 and 898 cm− 1 was greater than that of P-MBP-1 and P-MBP-3, which might be due to the high degree of substitution of P-MBP-2.

Figure 1c was the spectrum of acetylated MBP. Compared with Fig. 1a, it could be concluded that the absorption peak of acetylated derivatives near 1740 cm− 1 was significantly enhanced, and the ester vibration peak near 1243 cm− 1 was also improved to different degrees, which manifested that the preparation of acetylated MBP was successful, and the main skeleton of MBP was almost unchanged39. Meanwhile, from Fig. 1c, the absorption peak intensity of AC1-MBP near 1740 cm− 1 was obviously greater than that of AC0.6-MBP and AC1.4-MBP, which might be related to the DS24.

Nuclear magnetic analysis

Figure 2a, b showed the NMR spectra of MBP and its phosphorylated derivatives. Among them, MBP had two resonance signals at 99.56 and 107.27 ppm, illustrating that the polysaccharide contained two configurations of α and β glycosidic bonds. The resonance signal of C6 was 60.52 ppm, and the signal of C2-C5 was concentrated between 70 and 80 ppm. The signal peaks of 52.88 and 173.66 ppm explained the presence of a little uronic acid and methoxy in MBP7,40. The above results were in agreement with the infrared results. Compared with the carbon spectrum of MBP, the spectrum of P-MBP had some changes. The overall signal moved to the left high field, and the number of C2-C5 peaks increased. This might be due to the electron-withdrawing effect of the phosphate group, which caused the chemical shift of the C atoms directly and indirectly connected to it to move to the high field and low field, respectively19,40. From the P spectrum, P-MBP-2 had two more signals than P-MBP-1 and P-MBP-3, which were − 6.37 ppm and − 18.72 ppm, respectively. This also showed that P-MBP-2 had the highest DS. Meanwhile, phosphorylated derivative had the strongest signal near − 22 ppm, which also manifested that this position was the most prone to phosphate substitution reaction41. The above results combined with infrared results proved that there were phosphorylated groups in MBP, and the preparation of P-MBP was successful.

(a) 13C NMR of MBP and P-MBP. (b) 13P NMR of P-MBP. (c) 13C NMR of MBP and AC-MBP.

Figure 2c showed that after acetylation modification, the signal peaks of C6 of the three polysaccharides all moved to the lower field to different degrees, indicating that the three polysaccharides C6 underwent acetylation substitution. Furthermore, the signal overlap of AC-MBP was more serious than that of MBP, which was the reason for the introduction of acetyl group28. The modified polysaccharide had new signal peaks near 173 ppm and 20.3 ppm, which belonged to the characteristic signal of the acetyl group42. In the vicinity of 173 ppm, AC1-MBP had one more signal than AC0.6-MBP and AC1.4-MBP, which might be the reason for the higher DS of AC1-MBP. These all confirmed the successful introduction of acetyl group.

Antioxidant ability of MBP and its derivatives in vitro

Hydroxyl radical clearance capacity

·OH is an active oxygen free radical, because of its strong electron-withdrawing ability, it has a strong attack on macromolecules in the human body43. Hence, it is very significant to discovery antioxidants that can keep the human body away from free radical damage. Figure 3 illustrated that the scavenging effect of MBP and its derivatives on·OH was enhanced as the concentration increased from 0.5 to 3 mg/mL. At different concentrations, the clearing effect of P-MBP with different degrees of substitution on hydroxyl radicals was significantly higher than that of unmodified MBP. When the concentration was 3 mg/mL, the elimination rates of P-MBP-1, P-MBP-2, P-MBP-3 and MBP were 72.1%, 78.8%, 77% and 32.6%, respectively. This showed that the phosphate groups significantly improved the elimination activity of MBP on·OH, and the higher the DS, the stronger the scavenging ability. However, after acetylation modification, the scavenging ability of AC-MBP for hydroxyl radicals was not significantly enhanced, and at low concentrations, the scavenging ability was lower than that of unmodified polysaccharide. On the one hand, it might be caused by the decrease of sugar content ; on the other hand, the number and position of acetyl substitution might influence the clearing capacity of polysaccharide and metal ions, which led to the low scavenging activity of AC-MBP on hydroxyl radicals44,45.

(A) and (B) are hydroxyl radical scavenging activities of P-MBP and AC-MBP, respectively.

Jiang et al.7extracted two polysaccharides MBP-1 and MBP-2 from mung bean skin by cellulase-assisted extraction and hot water extraction, respectively, and determined their hydroxyl radical activity. It was found that the scavenging rates of the two polysaccharides were 67.54% and 69.84% at 2 mg / mL, respectively. The reason for this difference might be that the hydroxyl radical scavenging activity of mung bean peel polysaccharides was related to the extraction method, molecular weight and raw material origin.

Lipid resistance

As shown in Fig. 4, in the test concentration range, the lipid antioxidant capacity of MBP and its modified products was relatively low. When the concentration was 3 mg/mL, the clearance rate of P-MBP-2 was increased to 40.4% compared with MBP, while the clearance capacity of P-MBP-1 and P-MBP-3 was slightly better than that of MBP. In general, the activity of water-extracted phosphorylated polysaccharide was better than that of ultrasonic-assisted extracted phosphorylated polysaccharide. There was little difference between the activity of polysaccharide after acetylation and that of MBP, illustrating that acetylation had little effect on the lipid scavenging activity of MBP.

Reduction ability

The measurement of the reducing capacity of a substance is an important indicator of antioxidant activity, and the greater the reducing capacity, the stronger the antioxidant activity. The reduction capacity is positively correlated with the absorbance of the material at 700 nm. From Fig. 5, finding that the reducing capacity of VC was the best and much higher than that of MBP and its modified products. The reduction capacity of MBP was higher than that of P-MBP, which might be because phosphorylated modification led to different degrees of low sugar content, resulting in low reduction capacity. In addition, it might also be related to the content of glucuronic acid, the higher the content of glucuronic acid, the higher the hydrogen supply capacity was relatively strong, the more Fe3+ reduction, the more the reduction capacity was relatively stronger46.

(A) and (B) are the reduction abilities of P-MBP and AC-MBP, respectively.

Whereas, the highest degree of substitution of AC 1-MBP, its reducing power not only did not decrease, but also observably enhanced. When the concentration was 3 mg/mL, the absorbance of AC 1-MBP and MBP were 0.377 and 0.141, respectively. This indicated that although the sugar content of polysaccharide was reduced by acetylation modification, the reducing ability of MBPW was still improved by the introduction of appropriate amount of acetyl groups. Chen14et al. extracted mung bean peel polysaccharide MBP0.5 by alkaline method, and found that its absorbance was greater than 0.5 when the concentration was 2 mg/mL. The reducing capacity of MBP0.5 was greater than that of MBP at the same mass concentration, which suggested that the antioxidant activity of polysaccharide might be related to the extraction method of polysaccharide.

Superoxide anion scavenging capacity

When superoxide anion is converted into reactive oxygen species, it can cause serious damage to cellular DNA and adversely affect human function. As can be seen from Fig. 6, MBP and its modified products could remove O2-·produced in the pyrogallol autoxidation reaction system to a certain extent, and the clearance rate was dose-dependent. At a mass concentration of 3 mg/mL, the clearance rates of MBP, P-MBP-2 and AC 1-MBP with the highest degree of substitution were 28.5%, 49% and 38.3%, respectively. After phosphorylation, the activity of polysaccharide was improved, which may be due to the introduction of the electron-donating phosphate group, which increased the density of the electron cloud of the sugar ring, thus increasing the activity of polysaccharide40. The greater degree of substitution and more electron-donating phosphate groups might be the reason for the better activity of P-MBPW-2. After acetylation modification, the scavenging effect of polysaccharides was enhanced to a certain extent. The reason for the increased activity may be that on the one hand, the water solubility of polysaccharide after acetylation modification was improved; on the other hand, the dissociation energy of O-H bond was weakened by the introduction of acetyl group, thus increasing the hydrogen supply capacity. The difference of the activity of polysaccharide after phosphorylation and acetylation indicated that the type and number of substituent groups had a certain effect on the superoxide anion scavenging ability of Mung bean polysaccharide.

Conclusion

In this paper, three kinds of P-MBP and AC-MBP with different degrees of substitution were acquired by changing the amount of reagent. The success of chemical modification was proved to be successful by infrared and nuclear magnetic measurements. In vitro antioxidant experiments showed that in addition to reducing ability after phosphorylation modification, the scavenging ability of ·OH, O2−· scavenging ability and anti-lipid peroxidation ability of the three phosphorylated modification products of MBP increased with the increase of concentration, and the scavenging activity was P-MBP-2 > P-MBP-3 > P-MBP-1 from large to small. The scavenging activities of the three acetylated MBP products on the four kinds of free radicals were AC 1-MBP > AC 0.6-MBP > AC 1.4-MBP. In summary, phosphorylated products showed better activity for MBP. Chemical modification could improve the antioxidant activity of MBP to a certain extent, which created a greater possibility for the application of MBP in food, medical and other fields, and also promoted the recycling of mung bean peel resources. The position of the substituents on the sugar ring of the modified MBP and the relationship between the structure of the polysaccharide and the antioxidant mechanism in vitro have not been clarified, so it is expected to be further studied in the future.

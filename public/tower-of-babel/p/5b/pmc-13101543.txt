Increased duration of time without transfusion reliance with imetelstat vs placebo in the phase 3 IMerge trial
Authors: Mikkael A Sekeres, Valeria Santini, Amer M Zeidan, Uwe Platzbecker, Rami S Komrokji, María Díez-Campelo, Pierre Fenaux, Michael R Savona, Yazan F Madanat, David Valcárcel, Antoine Regnault, Flora Mazerolle, Libo Sun, Ying Wan, Tymara Berry, Faye Feller, Shyamala Navada, Esther Natalie Oliva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

TO THE EDITOR:

Anemia resulting from dysfunctional erythropoiesis is a defining sign of myelodysplastic syndromes/neoplasms (MDS),1,2 often necessitating either supportive care (primarily red blood cell [RBC] transfusions) or pharmacologic intervention.3 Transfusions and transfusion dependence (TD) negatively affect health-related quality of life (HR-QOL) and survival outcomes,4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 with a 20% decrease in overall survival at 2 to 4 years reported for patients with lower-risk (LR) MDS who are RBC-TD vs those who are not.10, 11, 12, 13 Thus, the achievement of RBC transfusion independence (TI) is an important outcome for LR-MDS treatments.

Although patient-reported outcomes (PRO) are key in understanding the overall benefit of a therapy,14 clinical trials are often not adequately designed to evaluate HR-QOL. Methodologic flaws (eg, instrument selection, timing of evaluations, and responder burden) and the multifactorial nature of patients’ HR-QOL (eg, frailty, comorbidities, and the influence of disease and treatment-related factors) often compromise results.15, 16, 17 Moreover, HR-QOL assessments related to anemia may not be able to differentiate between an effective intervention and the effect of continued RBC transfusions in the control arm.15 Thus, a new composite measure, time without transfusion reliance (TWiTR), was developed to inform the interpretation of PRO results, document a possible cause of deterioration HR-QOL,15 and provide a specific unit that allows for easier comparisons between treatments within a study and between studies.

TWiTR was adapted from the validated time without symptoms and toxicity (TWiST) measure18 and is a validated survival analysis approach that subtracts periods when a patient’s HR-QOL is presumed to have deteriorated from their overall survival. Three health states are defined as follows: (1) RBC-TD (sum of all RBC-TD periods up to disease progression, death, or censoring for progression); (2) relapse (REL; the time between disease progression and death or censoring); and (3) TWiTR (time without RBC-TD or REL). Supplemental Figure 1 presents a hypothetical case to illustrate how the TWiTR state is determined.

Imetelstat is a first-in-class telomerase inhibitor approved in the United States and Europe for adult patients with LR-MDS and RBC-TD anemia (≥4 RBC units >8 weeks) who relapsed or have a refractory/unsatisfactory response to or are ineligible for erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, based on the phase 3 IMerge trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02598661).19, 20, 21 In the primary analysis of IMerge, imetelstat-treated patients had higher rates of ≥8-week (primary end point), ≥24-week (secondary end point), and ≥1-year (ad hoc) RBC-TI vs placebo recipients.19 In addition, more imetelstat-treated patients experienced maintenance of HR-QOL and improvement of MDS-related symptoms (eg, fatigue) compared with worsening with placebo (exploratory analyses), despite receiving fewer RBC transfusions.22

Here, we present results from post hoc TWiTR analyses of IMerge. A summary of the methodology of IMerge19 and for this TWiTR analysis is provided in the supplemental Methods. TWiTR analyses comparing treatment arms were conducted on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population and in the subgroup of ≥8-week RBC-TI responders.

Two complementary censoring rules were applied to TD periods (supplemental Figure 2). In the primary analysis, the TD state was censored according to progression-free survival (PFS) censoring rules (TD was treated as an event when PFS was an event and was censored when PFS was censored). A sensitivity analysis was also conducted in which TD was always considered as an event (participants who experienced TD were never censored).

A total of 178 patients (imetelstat, n = 118; placebo, n = 60) in the ITT population from the final data analysis of IMerge (data cutoff date: 5 January 2024) were included in the TWiTR analyses. Baseline characteristics were similar across arms (supplemental Table 1).19

In the TWiTR analysis based on PFS censoring in the ITT population, after 2.6 years of follow-up, overall mean TWiTR was statistically significantly longer in the imetelstat arm than in the placebo arm by >2.5 months (83 days; 95% confidence interval [CI], 25-135; Figure 1). Mean TD was ∼1.5 months (41 days; 95% CI, −197 to 120) shorter with imetelstat than placebo. Mean time in the REL state was ∼1 month (27 days; 95% CI, −147 to 82) shorter in the imetelstat arm than in the placebo arm. Results for the TWiTR analysis considering TD as an event were similar (supplemental Table 2).

Among patients who achieved ≥8-week RBC-TI (imetelstat, n = 47; placebo, n = 9), mean TWiTR was 6.6 months (198 days; 95% CI, 98-300) longer with imetelstat (n = 47) than placebo (n = 9), applying the PFS censoring rule (Figure 2). Among ≥8-week RBC-TI responders, TD was 8 months (241 days; 95% CI, −348 to −145) shorter in the imetelstat arm vs the placebo arm. Results for ≥8-week RBC-TI responders considering TD as an event are presented in supplemental Table 3. Patients who did not achieve RBC-TI (nonresponders [<8-week RBC-TI]) did not have TWiTR periods, by definition. In addition, RBC-TI periods were associated with meaningful improvement in Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy—Fatigue scores as found in supplemental Figure 3.

In this analysis of the phase 3 IMerge study, imetelstat-treated patients with LR-MDS had a longer mean duration of TWiTR vs placebo. The greater time in TWiTR with imetelstat vs placebo was accounted for by decreases in time in both the TD and REL states. The difference between arms in the TWiTR and TD states was fairly consistent across censoring specifications. Among ≥8-week RBC-TI responders, TWiTR was longer and TD was shorter with imetelstat than placebo; however, this should be interpreted with caution given the small number of patients in both arms who responded.

We have previously reported on the association between RBC-TI and patient-reported improvement in fatigue in patients treated with imetelstat.19,23 TWiTR analyses are meant to use RBC-TD to inform the interpretation of PRO results, by providing a more objective measure of benefit that reflects transfusion density, which can affect QOL. As HR-QOL often deteriorates during RBC-TD periods and after progression, these findings consolidate the assessment of therapeutic benefit from imetelstat, incorporating critical periods of transfusion dependency and transfusion-free times, along with survival. Further research evaluating the relationship between TWiTR and other health-related parameters, such as cost per quality-adjusted life-years, may help to provide a more comprehensive understanding on the qualitative impact of imetelstat in clinical practice.

Limitations of these analyses included the use of 2 approaches for censoring the TD state. The difference between imetelstat and placebo remained consistent across both approaches, but the censoring rule used influenced the total time spent in the TD state. Nevertheless, applying the PFS censoring rule to the TD state “canceled out” the uncertainty in the TD and REL periods, leading to less variable estimates of duration in TWiTR (the difference between PFS and TD state duration). Thus, the variability (ie, statistical uncertainty) associated with the estimates in TWiTR and the difference in time in TWiTR between treatment arms was smaller with the PFS censoring approach compared with the TD event approach (width of 95% CI was 110 days with PFS censoring vs 247 days using the TD events approach).

In conclusion, TWiTR represents a novel and complementary method for evaluating HR-QOL in patients with LR-MDS and other conditions requiring RBC transfusions that is objective and less susceptible to the influence of supportive care interventions used to manage anemia in control arms. In this analysis, the greater time in TWiTR with imetelstat vs placebo was accounted for by decreases in time in both the TD and REL states. These results enhance our understanding of the impact imetelstat has on the overall health and well-being of patients with LR-MDS.

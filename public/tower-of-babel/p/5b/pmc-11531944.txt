Interpretable machine learning model predicting immune checkpoint inhibitor‐induced hypothyroidism: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Su‐Yan Zhu, Tong‐Tong Yang, Yi‐Zhuo Zhao, Yu Sun, Xiao‐Meng Zheng, Hong‐Bin Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypothyroidism is a known adverse event associated with the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in cancer treatment. This study aimed to develop an interpretable machine learning (ML) model for individualized prediction of hypothyroidism in patients treated with ICIs. The retrospective cohort of patients treated with ICIs was from the First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University. ML methods applied include logistic regression (LR), random forest classifier (RFC), support vector machine (SVM), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). The area under the receiver‐operating characteristic curve (AUC) was the main evaluation metric used. Furthermore, the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) was utilized to interpret the outcomes of the prediction model. A total of 458 patients were included in the study, with 59 patients (12.88%) observed to have developed hypothyroidism. Among the models utilized, XGBoost exhibited the highest predictive capability (AUC = 0.833). The Delong test and calibration curve indicated that XGBoost significantly outperformed the other models in prediction. The SHAP method revealed that thyroid‐stimulating hormone (TSH) was the most influential predictor variable. The developed interpretable ML model holds potential for predicting the likelihood of hypothyroidism following ICI treatment in patients. ML technology offers new possibilities for predicting ICI‐induced hypothyroidism, potentially providing more precise support for personalized treatment and risk management.

INTRODUCTION

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) represent an innovative category of cancer immunotherapy designed to bolster the immune system's capacity to combat cancer cells. 1 However, their immune‐stimulating properties can be accompanied by a new type of adverse reactions, known as immune‐related adverse events (irAEs), which frequently impact the endocrine system. 2 , 3 Among these immune‐related endocrinopathies, thyroid dysfunction is particularly prevalent, especially in the case of programmed cell death protein‐1 (PD‐1) inhibitors, with estimated rates reaching up to 35.5% and exceeding 50% with combination ICI therapies. 4 , 5 , 6 Thyroid dysfunction is a common pathological state of thyroid hormone disorder. The latest review and meta‐analysis have reported high frequencies of thyroid irAEs, especially a relatively high risk for hypothyroidism. 7 , 8

There are certain risk factors linked to the development of thyroid irAEs after ICI administration: gender, age, elevated anti‐thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb) or anti‐thyroglobulin‐antibodies (TgAb), higher baseline thyroid‐stimulating hormone (TSH), and obesity. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 Moreover, certain cancer types, such as lung cancer, have been linked to an elevated risk of ICI‐induced thyroid dysfunction. 13 Accurately predicting the likelihood of such adverse events is imperative for tailoring personalized treatment and managing associated risks. Early prediction of occurrence and detection of thyroid irAEs should be a priority for the clinician, in order to avoid critical situations.

Artificial intelligence‐based machine learning (ML) has emerged as a promising predictive tool in medicine and has been applied in many medical fields. 14 , 15 In contrast to traditional statistical analysis methods, such as logistic regression (LR), ML is more adept at discerning intricate relationships between diseases and variables, categorizing variables using specific criteria, making predictions based on baseline features, and identifying objects with similar patterns. Consequently, these models aid in disease identification and inference, facilitate medical decision‐making, and enhance the quality of treatment.

The latest ML models have become more interpretable through the utilization of the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP), thereby advancing the application of ML. 16 , 17 The SHAP is a tool for interpreting the predictions of ML models that can visually represent the relationship between each feature and its predictive capability, offering a more intuitive understanding of feature importance and enhancing clinical interpretability.

In this study, we aimed to develop an interpretable ML model using patient‐relevant variables and demographics to predict the likelihood of individuals being susceptible to ICI‐induced hypothyroidism, thus assisting physicians in making more precise treatment decisions for their patients.

METHODS

Data sources and study design

Patients treated with ICIs at the First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University between January 2019 and May 2023 were obtained from the medical records database. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) having received at least one dose of an ICI and having undergone thyroid function measurement minimum twice following the first ICI dose; (ii) having developed hypothyroidism after using ICI. Patients were excluded for the following reasons: (i) receiving medications known to affect thyroid function such as thyroxine, antithyroid drugs, amiodarone, or lithium; (ii) having non‐ICI‐related hypothyroidism, such as central hypothyroidism, thyroid‐binding globulin deficiency, nonthyroidal illness syndrome, or unexplained thyroid biochemistry. The study protocol was approved by the ethical review board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Thyroid function tests and laboratory examinations

The laboratory had the following established reference ranges for thyroid function parameters: free triiodothyronine (FT3), 3.28–6.47 pg/mL; triiodothyronine (T3),1.01–2.48 ng/mL; free tetraiodothyronine (FT4), 7.64–16.03 ng/dL; tetraiodothyronine (T4), 69.97–152.52 μg/dL; and TSH, 0.56–5.91 μIU/mL. TPOAb level of >9 IU/mL and TgAb level of >4 IU/mL were considered positive.

In accordance with the guidelines of the American Thyroid Association, 18 hypothyroidism was defined as a TSH level over the upper reference range, accompanied by a normal FT3/FT4 level or below the lower reference interval. Patients who experienced transient thyrotoxicosis and subsequently developed hypothyroidism during follow‐up were classified as the hypothyroidism group.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables meeting the conditions of normal distribution are presented as mean and standard deviation, and one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for between‐group comparisons. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (n), and the comparison between different groups was conducted using Pearson's chi‐squared test. Delong test was used for model performance comparisons. A two‐sided p‐value of <0.05 was considered significant in all statistical analyses. The R language data analysis software R‐Studio (Version 7.2, R‐Studio Institute Inc.) was used for data reading and analytical modeling.

Feature selection and model development

Variables with missing data comprising less than 20% of the total dataset were incorporated into the study. Imputation of missing values was conducted by utilizing the median for continuous variables and the mode for categorical variables. Redundant and extraneous factors introduce excessive intricacies and noise, which potentially lead to model overfitting and a subsequent decline in predictive performance. Therefore, during the development of ML models we employed the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) to exclude noninterference variables. The LASSO algorithm implements both variable selection and regularization to enhance the prediction accuracy and interpretability of the model. Subsequently, variables exhibiting nonzero coefficients determined by the LASSO method were selected for inclusion in the construction of ML models. The feature selection algorithm was implemented based on the Python package Scikit‐learn. Preceding the initiation of the modeling procedure, the dataset underwent stratified random sampling, resulting in the division of the data into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%). The training set was utilized for feature selection and model training. To address the imbalanced nature of the training data, stratified random sampling was employed to generate positive cases, mitigating the risk of overfitting. The testing set was used to assess the model's performance after training, while a subset of the training set was used as a validation set to evaluate the model's performance during training.

The ML model was built with data from the training set. In the training set, we conducted 10‐fold cross‐validation, partitioning the dataset into 10 distinct training and validation subsets. This approach was employed to enhance model generalizability and mitigate the risk of overfitting. We initially employed four ML algorithms for model construction, specifically, LR, random forest classifier (RFC), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and support vector machine (SVM). The field of ML algorithms, representing an emerging statistical approach, is aptly equipped for fulfilling this objective.

Model evaluation

After building the model, predictive performance was evaluated on a testing set based on the area under the receiver‐operating characteristic curve (AUC) scores. The Delong test and calibration curves were employed to compare the receiver‐operating characteristic curves (ROCs) among different models. These analytical tools facilitated the identification of the optimal model. The calibration curves of the models were assessed using the Brier score method, a metric within the range of 0 to 1, where lower scores indicate superior model calibration. The Brier score method elucidates the concordance between the predicted values of the model and the observed outcomes within the cohort. Decision curve analysis was incorporated to assess the clinical utility, calculated as the weighted average of true positives and false positives, by determining the net benefits within a specified range of threshold probabilities. Additionally, the analysis was conducted on sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and the Youden index.

Explanation of the model

While the aforementioned algorithms exhibit robustness and high performance as prominent ML methodologies within the medical domain, it is difficult to interpret and display black‐box characters. Thus, based on the training set, we incorporated the SHAP into our prediction model to compensate for the black‐box character problem, which can allow for the extraction of feature analyses and the rational generation of patient‐specific predictions.

The Shapley additive explanation, inspired by the cooperative game theory, can visually represent the importance ranking of features and compute the Shapley value of each feature in the prediction model. The SHAP showed that each feature had its own contribution, and pushed the prediction result of the model from the base value to the model output. Features that push their predictions up are shown in red, and those that push their predictions down are shown in blue. Additionally, the SHAP exhibits global interpretability. The current study employed Python version 3.7, utilizing essential packages such as XGBoost, SHAP, and Scikit‐learn within its computational environment.

Feature selection

The dataset was randomly split into the training set (n = 320) and the testing set (n = 138), as shown in Table S1. In the LASSO algorithm, 10 variables, namely, sex, body mass index (BMI), diabetes, previous radiotherapy, previous traditional chemotherapy, combination with targeted therapy medication, TSH and FT3 at baseline, current smoker, and tumor type were selected as crucial variables, which were determined by the result of the LASSO regularization process based on 320 patients in the training set.

Explanation of the model at the feature level

The SHAP algorithm has been utilized in conjunction with the XGBoost model to ascertain the importance of each variable in predicting ICI‐induced hypothyroidism. The significance distribution for each variable, arranged in descending order, has been illustrated in Figure 4A. Notably, baseline TSH emerged as the most influential predictor of ICI‐induced hypothyroidism, superseded by traditional chemotherapy and tumor type. Furthermore, to elucidate the relationship between the target outcome and positive and negative predictors, SHAP values were employed to uncover the risk factors. As depicted in Figure 4B, each row corresponds to a feature, with each dot representing a sample; the intensity of the color indicates the magnitude of the feature, with red denoting higher values and blue indicating lower values. It is evident that elevated baseline TSH levels have a positive impact and are associated with a higher likelihood of developing hypothyroidism, while a history of traditional chemotherapy exerts a negative impact and is linked to a reduced likelihood of developing hypothyroidism. DISCUSSION

Understanding the high incidence of ICI‐induced hypothyroidism is crucial for clinicians to assess and monitor thyroid hormone levels before and during ICI therapy. With fast‐expanding approvals for ICIs in various cancer types, knowledge on risk factors and biomarkers can help providers assess the irAE‐risk of their patients. However, it is often the combination of one or more risk factors and biomarkers that can predispose to more adverse events than one single factor/biomarker. By establishing an interpretable ML model to predict the risk of developing hypothyroidism after receiving ICI therapy, it is possible to identify the high‐risk population most likely to experience such adverse reactions. The predictive model can assist clinicians in developing personalized treatment plans for patients, ultimately enhancing the safety and efficacy of ICI therapy. The predictive power of ML enables early detection and intervention, leading to better patient outcomes and optimized clinical decision‐making in the management of thyroid dysfunction associated with ICIs. Previous studies on ICI‐induced hypothyroidism primarily focused on its pathophysiological underpinnings and individual risk factors, lacking the amalgamation of large‐scale clinical data in practical settings. 19 , 20 Additionally, there is no universally accepted risk stratification algorithm for predicting ICI‐induced hypothyroidism. Therefore, the strength of our ML model lies in its capacity to extract relevant variables from real‐world clinical data to predict ICI‐induced hypothyroidism. Based on a local dataset, we successfully developed and validated a ML prediction model to identify high‐risk patients with ICI‐induced hypothyroidism. Through the analysis of ML algorithms, predictive models can be established to assess the risk of ICI‐induced hypothyroidism based on patient's clinical features and treatment information.

The simplified model with 10 variables exhibited marginally superior overall performance compared with the full‐variable‐availability model. Interpretive analysis of the models indicated that baseline TSH levels, history of traditional chemotherapy, and tumor type were robust indicators for the early prediction of ICI‐induced hypothyroidism. TSH was identified as a significant predictor of ICI‐induced hypothyroidism and made a substantial contribution to the model's predictive capacity. Patients with elevated baseline TSH levels may display a heightened susceptibility to developing hypothyroidism when undergoing ICI treatment. This observation aligns with previous studies; for instance, Kimbara et al. noted that elevated baseline levels of TSH could signify an augmented risk of thyroid dysfunction. 21 , 22

Moreover, there was a notable variation in the utilization and pretreatment of other therapeutic modalities, such as chemotherapy and radiation, which altered the profile of irAEs. Our research uncovered that patient with a history of traditional chemotherapy paradoxically exhibited a reduced likelihood of experiencing hypothyroidism. While this perplexing finding is challenging to elucidate, several research studies suggest that the occurrence of irAEs could predict a favorable response to treatment with ICIs, increased survival, and progression‐free survival. 23 , 24 Therefore, we hypothesize that patients with a history of chemotherapy may also demonstrate diminished sensitivity to ICIs. Furthermore, radiation therapy has the potential to inflict harm upon or obliterate thyroid cells, disrupting their physiological function and ultimately leading to a reduction in the synthesis of thyroid hormones, consequently resulting in hypothyroidism.

Patients with malignancies other than lung and liver cancer are more prone to developing hypothyroidism when undergoing treatment with ICIs. Disparities in the tumor microenvironment and microbiome composition across different cancer types can elucidate the variability in irAEs. 25 Female patients demonstrate a greater vulnerability to ICI‐related thyroid dysfunction. This predilection in females may be attributed to their generally more robust immune response, which could contribute to an elevated occurrence of irAEs, including thyroid dysfunction, upon exposure to ICIs. This observation is in line with previous research findings. 26 , 27

The intricacy of ML models presents challenges in elucidating their predictions, potentially impeding their clinical applicability. Nevertheless, this study utilized the SHAP algorithm to explicate predictions at various levels, ensuring the model's performance and clinical interpretability, and presenting this to users through user‐friendly visualization tools. This approach enhances clinicians' comprehension of the model's decision‐making process, thereby facilitating the clinical utilization of prediction outcomes. Moreover, our study illustrates that interpretable ML methods can predict ICI‐induced hypothyroidism according to individual patients' circumstances.

Nonetheless, our study has certain limitations. Firstly, it is based on retrospective data from a single center, potentially introducing recall and selection bias to some extent. To bolster the model, further datasets and prospective multicenter clinical trials are imperative to validate our findings and the model's accuracy. Secondly, during the model construction, feature selection was only applied once on the combined training validation set without being integrated into the cross‐validation splits, potentially overlooking some variability in feature. Third, although our model achieves individualized prediction and offers interpretability, its predictive performance still needs improvement. In the model, TSH was identified as a key predictor variable for prediction of hypothyroidism in patients treated with ICIs. There is no statistical significance between the predictions made using the single metric of TSH levels and those made using ML models, which is a limitation of our study. The processes of data processing, feature selection, and parameter tuning are expected to be further optimized. In future research, we plan to employ the K‐nearest neighbor (KNN) method for data imputation, as this approach may yield more realistic imputed values. 28 For parameter tuning, we will replace manual adjustments with the grid search algorithm, which is expected to enhance the model's predictive performance. Additionally, the limited number of patients in our dataset might have contributed to the suboptimal accuracy of the ML algorithms. We aim to collect more patient data in future studies to further refine the ML model and achieve more accurate predictions. Finally, as the participants are exclusively from the Chinese population, the generalizability of the results to other demographic groups is limited.

The present study successfully developed an ICI‐induced hypothyroidism prediction model based on a ML method for the first time. It is worth noting that the multivariable prediction model may have advantages in capturing more information and potential complex relationships for providing more precise support for personalized treatment and risk management. Future research can further explore and validate the application effectiveness of ML in clinical practice, driving its widespread adoption in personalized medicine.

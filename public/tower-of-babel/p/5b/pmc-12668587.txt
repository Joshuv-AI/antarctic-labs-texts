Remimazolam in elderly patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA: protocol for a randomized clinical trial
Authors: Yeqin Liu, Yuqin Chen, Longxiang Ma, Furong Zhang, Maiqiao Yang, Liang He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) remains the diagnostic benchmark for mediastinal staging, though geriatric patients require optimized anesthesia due to compromised pharmacokinetics and elevated perioperative risks.Methods:A prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial is conducted at a tertiary hospital. Sixty eligible patients aged over 70 years who underwent EBUS-TBNA under general anesthesia were enrolled. Participants were randomized 1:1 to receive either propofol (Group P, n = 30) or remimazolam besylate (Group R, n = 30) with protocolized perioperative management. Primary outcome assessed anesthesia emergence quality. Secondary outcomes encompassed serial hemodynamics, cumulative vasoactive drug requirements, recovery metrics (time/quality), 24-hour QoR-40 scores, and perioperative complications.Discussion:This randomized trial compared remimazolam and propofol for EBUS-TBNA anesthesia in elderly patients. Remimazolam demonstrated accelerated Steward recovery and enhanced hemodynamic stability, whereas propofol exhibited risks of adipose tissue deposition and hypotension. These pharmacodynamic profiles inform anesthetic optimization in geriatric cohorts, necessitating multicenter validation of age-adjusted protocols.

Introduction

Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA), a minimally invasive technique enabling real-time ultrasound-guided biopsy of mediastinal, hilar, and parenchymal lesions, is essential for diagnosing and staging lung cancer, mediastinal tumors, and sarcoidosis due to its high diagnostic yield, excellent safety profile, and low morbidity[1–3]. However, the EBUS-TBNA procedure, different from conventional bronchoscopy, often involves repeated needle aspirations and punctures increasing greater stimulation and prolonged duration. Consequently, EBUS-TBNA is routinely conducted under general anesthesia using laryngeal mask ventilation for optimizing patient tolerance and procedural control[4,5].

With an aging population, the prevalence of pulmonary disorders is on the rise, and elderly patients undergoing surgical procedures with general anesthesia face elevated risks of hypotension and postoperative delirium[6,7]. Advanced age has been identified as a significant independent predictor of hypotension in this context[8]. Meanwhile, a qualitative study optimizes research prioritization and resource allocation under limited healthcare resources[9]. Lung cancer predominantly affects individuals over the age of 65, with statistics indicating that majority of cases occur in this age group[10,11]. While advanced age is associated with reduced physiological reserve, poor tolerance to anesthesia, and an increased risk of perioperative hemodynamic instability[12,13]. Perioperative hypotension, even when transient, has been linked to adverse outcomes, such as cardiovascular events, acute kidney injury, and elevated mortality rates[14–16].HIGHLIGHTSThis randomized controlled trial evaluated the efficacy and safety of remimazolam besylate compared to propofol in elderly patients undergoing endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA). This randomized controlled trial evaluated the efficacy and safety of remimazolam besylate compared to propofol in elderly patients undergoing endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA). The results indicated that remimazolam improved hemodynamic stability and quality of awakening post-anesthesia, while propofol was associated with risks of adipose accumulation and hypotension. The findings underscore the potential of remimazolam in optimizing anesthesia management for geriatric patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA, warranting further multicenter validation.

Remimazolam, a novel ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine, is characterized by rapid metabolism, inactive metabolites, and minimal hemodynamic effects, making it a safe and stable option for elderly patients[17,18]. Studies have demonstrated that remimazolam significantly reduces the incidence of hypotension and bradycardia during both induction and maintenance of anesthesia, while also decreasing the need for vasoactive drugs[19,20]. Furthermore, remimazolam has been shown to reduce postoperative complications and enhancing recovery quality[21]. In contrast, propofol, despite its rapid onset and recovery, exerts a suppressive effect on circulatory function, particularly in elderly patients, often leading to hemodynamic fluctuations[22,23]. Research indicates that elderly patients receiving propofol experience a higher incidence of hypotension during anesthesia induction and require more vasoactive drugs to maintain hemodynamic stability[17,24].

Given these considerations, remimazolam may offer distinct advantages in elderly patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA, particularly in terms of hemodynamic stability. However, studies comparing remimazolam and propofol in this context remain limited and inconclusive, necessitating further clinical trials to validate their efficacy and safety profiles. To address this gap, we conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effects of remimazolam besylate and propofol on hemodynamic stability and quality of awakening during EBUS-TBNA in elderly patients. Additionally, we compared the quality of anesthesia recovery and postoperative outcomes between the two agents, with the aim of providing evidence-based insights to guide clinical practice.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

This single-center, randomized, double-blind, controlled trial aimed to evaluate the impact of remimazolam on hemodynamic stability and recovery quality in elderly patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA under general anesthesia. The study was following approval by the Institutional Review Committee of the institute (approval number 2024-195-01). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. The trial was registered. The study protocol adhered to the CONSORT criteria at https://www.consort-statement.org/. Participants

Inclusion criteria

Eligible participants met the following criteria: age ≥70 years, irrespective of gender, with a body mass index ranging from 18 to 25 kg/m2, and classified as American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I, II, or III. Patients scheduled for EBUS-TBNA under general anesthesia with laryngeal mask airway were included.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria are as one of the follows: hypersensitivity or allergic reactions to the pharmacological agents utilized in this investigation; preoperative identification of coagulation disorders or cardiopulmonary insufficiency rendering the patient unsuitable for surgical intervention; individuals diagnosed with psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairments; severe organic pathologies affecting major organ systems, including the heart, brain, or kidneys; a documented history of substance abuse or dependency.

Dropout criteria

Participants were withdrawn from the study under the following circumstances: occurrence of severe adverse events during the perioperative period, postoperative transfer to the Respiratory Intensive Care Unit, refusal to participate in postoperative follow-up, or voluntary withdrawal by the patient or their legal representatives.

Recruitment process

Potential candidates for EBUS-TBNA were screened 1 day prior to the scheduled procedure. Field investigators evaluated the patients according to the predefined eligibility criteria. Each participant was thoroughly informed about their involvement in the study, with assurances of strict confidentiality regarding their personal and medical data.

Informed consent

Eligible patients were formally enrolled in the study only after they provided written informed consent to the principal investigator.

Randomization and blinding

Following the acquisition of informed consent, participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either the propofol (Group P) or remimazolam besylate (Group R) treatment arms. Randomization was executed as early as possible at the commencement of the procedure. An independent team of professionals generated and managed the randomization sequence, ensuring the concealment of allocation. All study personnel, including participants, remained blinded to the group assignments. Treatment allocations were sealed in opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes and disclosed to the anesthesiologist immediately prior to anesthesia induction. Anesthesia providers were excluded from the data collection and postoperative follow-up activities. Follow-up assessments were conducted by clinicians blinded to the treatment group assignments. Access to anesthesia records was restricted until all follow-up evaluations were completed. Furthermore, follow-up personnel were not included in any of the statistical analyses.

Standard anesthesia

Preoperative evaluation

A comprehensive pre-anesthesia assessment was conducted for each patient 1 day prior to surgery. Eligibility was determined based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Participants received standardized preoperative education, and informed consent along with baseline demographic and clinical data were documented.

Intraoperative ventilation strategies

All patients were managed with a lung-protective ventilation approach, utilizing pressure-controlled mechanical ventilation following laryngeal mask insertion. Ventilator settings included an inspiratory pressure of 15–25 cmH2O, a respiratory rate (RR) of 12–16 breaths/min, positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) of 5–8 cmH2O, and a fractional inspired oxygen concentration (FiO2) of 50%. End-tidal carbon dioxide (PETCO2) was maintained within the range of 35–45 mmHg. If peripheral SpO2 fell below 90%, adjustments were made by increasing oxygen flow, modifying ventilator parameters, or performing manual lung recruitment maneuvers to ensure intraoperative SpO2 remained above 90%.

Definition and treatment of hemodynamic events

Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was defined as the average of two non-invasive blood pressure measurements taken at rest 5 minutes after entering the operating room. Hypotension was defined as SBP <90 mmHg or a mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mmHg[25]. The management of hypotension included the administration of norepinephrine. If hypotension occurred three times within a 10-minute interval, a continuous infusion of norepinephrine was initiated. A 30% increase in SBP above the baseline prompted the consideration of intravenous urapidil. Bradycardia (heart rate <50 beats/min) was treated with atropine, whereas tachycardia (heart rate >100 beats/min) was managed with esmolol when necessary.

Postoperative management

Anesthetics were discontinued at the end of the surgery. In the absence of contraindications, participants in both groups received neostigmine (1.0 mg) and atropine (0.5 mg) to reverse the neuromuscular blockade. Upon meeting extubation criteria and removal of the laryngeal mask, patients were transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU). Patients were discharged to the ward once fully awake, with a Steward Score >4, and without any significant discomfort.

Interventions

The study interventions are outlined in Table 1. Patients in both groups adhered to an 8-hour preoperative fasting period, and no premedication was administered. Upon arrival in the operating room, intravenous access was established, and preoxygenation was performed using a facemask with an oxygen flow rate of 6 L/min. Continuous monitoring included electrocardiography (ECG), heart rate (HR), SBP, diastolic blood pressure (DBP), MAP, pulse oxygen saturation (SpO2), PETCO2, and bispectral index (BIS).

Anesthesia induction

In Group P, induction was achieved with sufentanil 0.3 μg/kg, propofol 1.5 mg/kg, and vecuronium bromide 0.1 mg/kg. In Group R, induction involved sufentanil 0.3 μg/kg, remimazolam 0.2 mg/kg, and vecuronium bromide 0.1 mg/kg. The laryngeal mask was inserted following muscle relaxation, and its position was confirmed via ultrasonic bronchoscopy before fixation. During bronchoscopy, 2% lidocaine 2 ml was administered at the vocal cords, main bronchi, and left/right main bronchi for airway anesthesia.

Anesthesia maintenance

In Group P, anesthesia was maintained with propofol 3–6 mg/kg/h and remifentanil 0.1–0.15 μg/kg/min. Group R received remimazolam 0.5-1 mg/kg/h and remifentanil 0.1–0.15 μg/kg/min. Detailed protocols are provided in Table 1.

Data collection, monitoring, and management

Clinical data from case report forms were manually entered into an electronic database, securely stored on a restricted-access server at this hospital. Data records were kept in a locked safe. Patient identifiers were anonymized to ensure confidentiality. Upon trial completion, data were securely archived and will be permanently deleted when no longer required for research purposes.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The quality of anesthesia recovery was evaluated using the Steward scoring system, which was administered to both patient groups immediately upon awakening and at 5, 10, and 20 minutes after awakening[26]. The Steward score encompasses three key domains: consciousness, airway patency, and motor activity. Each domain is scored on a scale of 0 to 2 points, with a maximum total score of 6 points. The scoring criteria are as follows: consciousness (2 points for full alertness, 1 point for responsiveness to stimuli, and 0 points for no response to stimuli), airway patency (2 points for coughing and expectoration on command, 1 point for maintaining airway patency without assistance, and 0 points for requiring airway support), and motor activity (2 points for purposeful movement, 1 point for non-purposeful movement, and 0 points for no movement), and a higher score indicates a superior quality of recovery.

Secondary outcomes

Hemodynamic stability indices such as HR, SBP, DBP, MAP, and SpO2 were monitored at the following time points: T0 (before induction), T1 (laryngeal mask insertion), T2 (bronchoscope reaching the target site), T3 (10 minutes after surgery start), T4 (after surgery), T5 (patient awakening), and T6 (10 minutes after laryngeal mask removal).

The time to awakening (from drug cessation to eye-opening on command) and time to laryngeal mask removal (from drug cessation to mask removal) were considered as recovery duration.

Intraoperative vasoactive drug usage (the dosages of norepinephrine, atropine, and esmolol administered during the procedure) was documented.

On the first postoperative day, the 40-item Quality of Recovery Scale (QoR-40) was utilized to assess recovery quality, with scores ranging from 40 to 200, reflecting recovery quality from poor to excellent[27]. This scale evaluates five clinically relevant dimensions: physical comfort (12 items), emotional state (9 items), self-care ability (5 items), psychological support (7 items), and pain (7 items).

Intraoperative anesthetic drug usage (the dosages of remimazolam, propofol, and remifentanil administered during surgery) were recorded.

Adverse event incidence such as the occurrence of adverse reactions, including postoperative nausea, vomiting, dizziness, emergence delirium, delayed awakening, and delirium, was documented. The overall adverse event rate was calculated as the ratio of total adverse events to the total number of cases, multiplied by 100%.

Assessment of safety

All procedures were conducted in a fully equipped operating room with standardized hemodynamic monitoring to ensure prompt detection and management of adverse events. Any adverse events experienced by participants were immediately reported to the principal investigator and addressed. Suspected or unexpected serious adverse events were reported to the Institutional Review Committee. Detailed records of study-related adverse events were maintained on the case report form until resolution.

Statistical methods

Sample size

The minimum sample size was determined based on a pre-pilot study (6 patients in each group) that demonstrated mean Steward scores of 4.9 and 4.1 for the remimazolam and propofol groups, respectively, with a standard deviation of 1. Using PASS V.15.0.5 for Windows software (PASS, USA), a two-tailed test with a significance level of 0.05 and 80% power was conducted, yielding a required sample size of 52 subjects. Sixty patients were enrolled, accounting for a 17% dropout rate, a total of 60 patients were enrolled.

Planned descriptive statistics and outcome analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean (SD) or median (IQR), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, adhering to the intention-to-treat principle. Data distribution was assessed using appropriate tests. Intergroup comparisons were conducted using independent samples t-tests, repeated measures ANOVA, Mann-Whitney U-tests, and chi-square tests, with a significance level set at 0.05.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination of this study.

Discussion

This investigation constitutes a prospective randomized controlled trial designed to methodically assess the comparative efficacy of remimazolam besylate and propofol in the anesthesia management of geriatric patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA. This study emphasizes three critical dimensions: post-anesthetic emergence quality, hemodynamic stability, and postoperative recovery metrics in elderly patients.

The quality of emergence was quantified utilizing the Steward scoring system, which appraises neurological recuperation across three domains: consciousness level, airway patency, and motor activity[26]. Given the propensity for delayed recovery in the elderly due to pharmacokinetic alterations–such as reduced drug clearance and expanded volume of distribution–alongside the hazards associated with polypharmacy, remimazolam presents a favorable profile[28,29]. Its independence from hepatic and renal metabolism, with a brief half-life of 7–8 minutes, and rapid engagement with γ-aminobutyric acid receptors, potentially expedites consciousness recovery[30]. Conversely, propofol, despite its swift onset, is prone to accumulation in adipose tissue due to its high lipophilicity, potentially prolonging recovery in the elderly undergoing thoracoscopic radical resections[31]. The study takes Steward scores and the recovery durations between the two agents to thoroughly evaluate neurological recovery and identify intervention strategies to mitigate recovery-related complications.

Hemodynamic stability was scrutinized considering the diminished cardiovascular compensatory capacity in the elderly, where intraoperative blood pressure deviations exceeding 20% from baseline can precipitate hypoperfusion in critical organs such as the heart, brain, and kidneys[32,33]. Propofol’s propensity to induce dose-dependent hypotension via sympathetic suppression and vasodilation contrasts with reduced remimazolam affinity for α1-adrenergic receptors, suggesting less circulatory inhibition[34,35]. Continuous monitoring of MAP and heart rate variability elucidated the differential autonomic effects of the two drugs, offering insights into their hemodynamic modulation and establishing benchmarks for intraoperative circulatory management.

This study innovatively amalgamates intraoperative hemodynamic parameters, Steward scores, and postoperative QoR-40 to construct a comprehensive perioperative evaluation framework. This approach facilitates a systematic comparison of the anesthetic profiles of remimazolam and propofol in EBUS-TBNA, providing a multidimensional evidence base for anesthetic selection in elderly patients. The correlation between remimazolam BIS values and anesthetic depth warrants further investigation[36]. Future research endeavors could explore dose-response relationships and the long-term cognitive impacts of these anesthetics through multicenter, large-sample studies complemented by extended follow-up, thereby enriching the evidence base for tailored anesthetic strategies in the elderly.

Limitations

Nonetheless, the scope of the study was constrained by its single-center design and limited sample size, which may impede the generalizability of its findings. Here, some limitations should be in consideration. Firstly, the predetermined enrollment of 60 patients (accounting for 17% attrition) may lack sufficient power to detect subtle intergroup differences in heterogeneous geriatric populations. Secondly, institutional-specific protocols for EBUS-TBNA and anesthetic management may limit extrapolation of findings. Multicenter validation would strengthen external validity. At last, the study was explicitly designed as a clinical investigation focusing on remimazolam’s perioperative outcomes in elderly EBUS-TBNA patients (primary endpoints: hemodynamic stability and recovery profiles), while theoretical mechanisms are superficial and should be considered in future studies. These limitations underscore the need for larger, multicenter trials incorporating standardized comorbidity assessments and pharmacokinetic modeling to account for age-related physiological variability.

Conclusions

This randomized controlled trial suggests that remimazolam may offer advantages over propofol in terms of quality of anesthesia recovery and hemodynamic stability in elderly patients undergoing EBUS-TBNA. These findings suggest remimazolam as a preferable option for elderly patients with cardiovascular vulnerability. However, further research with larger sample sizes, multicenter designs, and extended follow-up is needed to confirm these preliminary findings and establish optimal anesthetic strategies for this patient population.

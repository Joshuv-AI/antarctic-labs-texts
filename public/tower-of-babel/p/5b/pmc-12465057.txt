Robust BICP0-gB indirect ELISA for the accurate diagnosis of bovine alphaherpesvirus 1 infections
Authors: Sen Zhang, Liyuan Song, Xin Yin, Changmin Hu, Yingyu Chen, Aizhen Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A novel indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (iELISA) utilizing the BICP0-gB fusion protein was developed that filled the gap in the Chinese market.•The BICP0-gB iELISA exhibited a high degree of concordance with the virus neutralization tests, demonstrating superior sensitivity and specificity and outperformed commercial kits.•Preliminary clinical evaluations using the iELISA method revealed a real prevalence of 40.3 % in Qinghai Province, aligning with the documented prevalence of BoHV-1 in the region.Keywords: BoHV-1, Differential diagnosis, indirect ELISA, BICP0 protein, gB protein

Introduction

Bovine alphaherpesvirus 1 (BoHV-1) belongs to the Varicellovirus genus within the Alphaherpesvirinae subfamily of the Herpesviridae family (Jones, 2019). It is the causative agent of infectious bovine rhinotracheitis, a highly contagious respiratory disease that poses a significant threat to cattle and buffalo health (Liu et al., 2023). Infected animals typically exhibit severe upper respiratory tract inflammation during disease onset (Wathes & al., 2020). Beyond the acute phase, the virus establishes a second life cycle characterized by lifelong viral shedding in the host (Righi & al., 2023). Additionally, BoHV-1 induces immunosuppression, increasing the susceptibility of individuals to secondary bacterial infections (Zhang et al., 2025). These factors contribute to the substantial impact of the virus on the global livestock industry, resulting in an annual loss exceeding $3 billion (Marin et al., 2020).

BoHV-1 encodes approximately 70 proteins, including 30–40 structural proteins and >10 glycoproteins (Liu et al., 2022), many of which have been extensively studied. Among these proteins, the gB protein is crucial for viral adsorption, invasion and intercellular spread (Hou et al., 2022). It serves as a major antigenic protein that stimulates a robust immune response, inducing both humoral and cellular immunity (Quintero Barbosa & al., 2023). The BICP0 protein functions as a transcriptional regulator during the immediate early stages of BoHV-1 gene expression, playing a vital role in activating the viral promoter and facilitating productive infection (Jones, 2019; Wirth, Vogt & Schwyzer, 1991). Notably, BICP0 can be detected within 30 min of viral latency activation (Guo, Li & Jones, 2019), making it a valuable marker for continuous BoHV-1 infection. These two proteins are promising targets for the development of enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits and innovative vaccines.

Serological detection is essential for preventing and controlling BoHV-1 infection, as it helps determine infection status, supports disease management, and assesses the antibody response triggered by vaccination. Commonly used serological techniques for BoHV-1 include the virus neutralization test (VNT) and ELISA. While VNT is the classical method recognized by the World Organization for Animal Health (WOAH) and Chinese import-exporting authorities (Righi et al., 2022), it has limitations due to the virus’s ability to evade neutralizing antibodies, leading to false negatives. Additionally, VNT is time-consuming and impractical for large sample sizes. Currently, there are no commercially available antibody detection ELISA kits for BoHV-1 in China, making reliance on expensive imported kits impractical. Thus, there is a pressing need to develop rapid and convenient ELISA kits with high specificity and sensitivity. Also, research priority that was also actively pursued for other significant animal pathogens such as lumpy skin disease virus (Yuan et al., 2024). The development and application of ELISA has increasingly become one of the most important and widely adopted strategies in the veterinary detection field.

To address this need, we ligated the BICP0 and gB genes to express a fusion protein, BICP0-gB, via the Escherichia coli expression system. This fusion protein demonstrated high sensitivity and repeatability in indirect ELISA (iELISA) and outperformed commercial kits in preliminary clinical applications, showing promising prospects for application of the BICP0-gB iELISA. These findings support the clinical prevention, control, and management of BoHV-1, thereby reducing the risk of disease occurrence and transmission and promoting the healthy development of the livestock industry.

Materials and methods

Serum samples

Standard negative and positive sera for BoHV-1 were obtained from the China Institute of Veterinary Drug Control and were maintained in our laboratory. All the serum samples used in this study were isolated from clinical cases by our team, with the antibody positivity or negativity of some samples verified through a virus neutralization test. Additionally, the serological status of Mycoplasma bovis (M. bovis), Pasteurella multocida A (PmA), Mannheimia hemolyticus (Mh), bovine viral diarrhea virus (BVDV), bovine parainfluenza virus type 3 (BPIV3) and bovine respiratory syncytial virus (BRSV) was confirmed by corresponding commercialized ELISA kits or virus neutralization tests.

Construction of the recombinant plasmid

When selecting antigenic epitopes, we conducted predictive analyses of parameters including signal peptides, transmembrane domains, hydrophilicity, surface accessibility, and antigenicity for the complete amino acid sequences of the BICP0 and gB genes using predictive tools (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk and https://www.detaibio.com/tools/epitope-prediction.html), respectively. Ultimately, immunogenic fragments were selected for subsequent experiments. The BICP0 and gB genes were amplified from the BoHV-1 HB06 wild-type strain, which was maintained at the National Key Laboratory of Agricultural Microbiology (GenBank accession number: AJ004801.1), as the template. Primers were designed based on the sequences of the selected BICP0 and gB genes. BamH Ⅰ and EcoR Ⅰ restriction enzyme sites were incorporated into the upstream primer for BICP0 and the downstream primer for gB, respectively. Besides, a 12-bp flexible short peptide primer sequence was added to the downstream primer for BICP0 and the upstream primer for gB. The detailed primer sequences are shown in Table 1.

Subsequently, the amplified fusion fragments and the expression vector pET-30a were digested at 37 °C for 1 h. The resulting digested products were subsequently ligated overnight at 4 °C via T4 DNA ligase. This ligation produced the recombinant plasmid, which was designated pET-30a-BICP0-gB.

Production and identification of recombinant protein

E. coli BL21(DE3) cells harboring the pET-30a-BICP0-gB plasmid were grown in LB media supplemented with kanamycin. When the OD600 reached 0.6, 1 mM isopropyl-β-d-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) was added, and expression was induced at 37 °C. After 8 h, the culture mixture was harvested and centrifuged, and the cells were resuspended in PBS for high-pressure fragmentation at 4 °C.

The inclusion bodies containing the BICP0-gB fusion protein were washed with two solutions: A (4 M urea, 20 mM Tris–HCl, 0.1 M NaCl) and B (50 mM Tris–HCl, 1 M NaCl). They were then suspended in solution C (6 M guanidine hydrochloride, 50 mM Tris–HCl, 300 mM NaCl, pH 8.0) for 2 h at room temperature, after which the supernatant was collected.

The BICP0-gB protein was purified via Ni Focurose 6FF, and its concentration was adjusted to 0.3 mg/ml for renaturation. The inclusion bodies were dialyzed against buffer with decreasing urea concentrations (6 M, 4 M, 2 M, 0.5 M, and 0 M) for 12 h at 4 °C.

The presence of BICP0-gB protein was confirmed by SDS‒PAGE and western blotting. The polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane was blocked in TBST buffer with 5 % skim milk, incubated with BoHV-1 serum, and then incubated with HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG. Detection was performed via a chemiluminescence imaging system.

Establishment and optimization of the BICP0-gB iELISA

To optimize the concentration of the BICP0-gB protein and serum dilution, the protein was serially diluted from 16 μg/mL to 0.5 μg/mL and incubated in a 96-well plate at 4 °C for 12 h. After washing, 200 μL of PBS containing 1 % w/v fish gelatin was added to each well for blocking at 37 °C for 2 h. Following another wash, standard negative and positive sera were diluted from 1:100 to 1:12,800, added (100 μL per well) and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. After washing, HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG (1:5000 dilution) was added and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min. A chromogenic substrate solution was then added and incubated in the dark for 10 min before the OD650 value was measured.

Next, the optimal blocking agent was determined. The same experimental setup was used with various blocking agents, including PBST, 5 % skim milk, 10 % skim milk and 1 % BSA, alongside the previously determined optimal protein concentration and serum dilution.

The optimal blocking time was then evaluated using the selected blocking agent, with incubation times of 0.5, 1, 1.5, and 2 h at 37 °C. Subsequent steps followed the established protocol.

To determine the optimal serum incubation time, the process was repeated with the previously selected coating agents, with incubation times of 0.5, 1, 1.5, and 2 h.

The optimal dilution of HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG was then assessed, maintaining the serum incubation time from earlier experiments. Four dilutions (1:3000, 1:4000, 1:5000, and 1:6000) were tested.

Finally, the incubation time for HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG was optimized for four durations (15 min, 30 min, 45 min, and 60 min). The reaction time for the chromogenic substrate solution was also evaluated by incubation in the dark for 5, 10, 15, and 20 min before the OD650 value was measured.

The established iELISA was tested on 36 serum samples that were negative for neutralizing antibodies to BoHV-1. Negative and positive controls were included in the experiment. The mean OD650 value (X¯) and standard deviation (σ) were calculated, with the threshold for positive values defined as S/P ≥ X¯+3 × σ.

Accuracy evaluation

A total of 244 serum samples (161 positive and 83 negative) were used to evaluate the accuracy of the iELISA method, including diagnostic sensitivity (Se) and diagnostic specificity (Sp), in comparison with the virus neutralization test.

Analytical specificity and sensitivity assays

Serum samples known to be negative or positive for common pathogens of bovine respiratory disease were used to evaluate the specificity of the BICP0-gB iELISA and commercial gB ELISA kits. BoHV-1 standard-positive serum served as a positive control, while PBS was used as a negative control.

Additionally, the minimum detection limits of both methods were assessed by analyzing BoHV-1-negative and BoHV-1-positive sera at various dilutions.

Reproducibility test

Eight positive and eight negative serum samples from the same and different batches were tested via BICP0-gB iELISA plates. Each sample was run in four replicates to calculate the mean OD value, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation. These metrics were used to assess both the intrabatch and interbatch reproducibility of the BICP0-gB iELISA plates.

Preliminary clinical application of BICP0-gB iELISA

A total of 200 clinical serum samples from yaks in Qinghai Province were analyzed via the established BICP0-gB iELISA method.

Statistical analysis

Se, Sp, the kappa value, agreement, the real prevalence, the positive predictive value (PPV), and the negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated via the same methods (https://epitools.ausvet.com.au/).

Significant differences between groups were assessed via one-way ANOVA. Significance levels were defined as follows: p < 0.05 (*), p < 0.01 (**), p < 0.001 (***), or p < 0.0001 (****). The error bars represent the standard error of the mean.

Results

Construction of the recombinant plasmid and expression of the BICP0-gB protein

After enzymatic cleavage and 1 % agarose gel electrophoresis of the recombinant plasmid pET-30a-BICP0-gB, two distinct bands were observed. The larger band corresponded to the pET-30a vector, while the smaller band represented the target fragment, which was of the expected size (Fig. 1A). DNA sequencing confirmed that the gene sequence of the recombinant plasmid matched the template, indicating a successful construction.

SDS‒PAGE analysis showed a distinct protein band corresponding to the purified BICP0-gB protein in the target region (Fig. 1B). A Western blot analysis conducted with standard negative and positive serum revealed a specific reaction band in the target area with positive serum. In contract, no band was detected with negative serum (Fig. 1C and D). These results confirmed the successful expression of the BICP0-gB protein.

The establishment and optimization of the BICP0-gB iELISA

The optimal working concentrations of the BICP0-gB protein and serum were identified via square array titration. The OD650 values for positive serum (P) and negative serum (N) at various protein concentrations were measured, resulting in a P/N ratio that satisfied the criteria of a P value > 1.5 and a P/N value > 10. The optimal concentration of BICP0-gB protein was found to be 4 μg/mL, with a serum dilution of 1:100 (Fig. 2).

The square array titration was employed to assess the optimal conditions for the optimum protein concentration and serum dilution. The values in the grid represent the P/N ratios, in which P represents the OD650 value of positive serum and N represents the OD650 value of negative serum. All these ratios are the averages of three replicates. The value in the red box represents the P/N value corresponding to the optimal protein concentration and serum dilution.

The use of 1 % w/v fish gelatin as a blocking substance yielded the highest P/N value (averaging 11.64), which was significantly different from that of PBST and 10 % skim milk (p < 0.05). Additionally, the best blocking time was determined to be 1 h, resulting in an average P/N value of 9.56 (Fig. 3A and B).

The complete process of the establishment of the BICP0-gB iELISA method. (A-B) Selection of best blocking substances and determination of appropriate blocking time. (C) Confirmation of serum reaction time. (D-E) Optimal dilution and response time of HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG. (F) Optimum reaction time of the chromogenic substrate solution.

In the serum reaction time analysis, the 0.5 h group presented the highest mean P/N value, although no significant differences were noted among the groups (Fig. 3C). The optimal dilution of HRP-labeled goat anti-bovine IgG was 1:5000, which significantly outperformed the 1:3000 dilution (p < 0.05). The best response time was found to be 30 min, achieving in a peak average P/N value of 11.34, exceeding the values from the 45-minute and 60-minute groups (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01) (Fig. 3D and E).

The reaction time for the chromogenic substrate solution indicated that both the 10-minute and 15-minute conditions provided similar mean P/N values, but the 15-minute group hold better uniformity and lower dispersion, making it the optimal reaction time (Fig. 3F).

Finally, the established BICP0-gB iELISA method was applied to 36 serum samples that tested negative for neutralizing antibodies. The average OD650 value of the samples was 0.192, with a variance of 0.058. The negative‒positive threshold for the BICP0-gB iELISA was calculated to be 0.367. Samples are classified as positive at a 99.9 % confidence level when S/P ≥ 0.367 and negative when S/P < 0.367.

Accuracy evaluation

Among the 244 serum samples with known status (161 were tested positive and 83 were tested negative in the virus neutralization test), the BICP0-gB iELISA identified 148 as positive and 81 as negative. This resulted in a sensitivity of 91.9 % (95 % CI: 86.6 %−95.6 %) and a specificity of 97.6 % (95 % CI: 91.6 %−99.7 %), with a kappa value of 0.87 (95 % CI: 0.80–0.93) and an overall agreement of 93.9 % (95 % CI: 90.1 %−96.5 %). Both the sensitivity and specificity were greater than those of the commercial ELISA kit, which demonstrated a sensitivity of 85.1 % (95 % CI: 78.6 %−90.2 %) and a specificity of 96.4 % (95 % CI: 89.8 %−99.3 %), with a kappa value of 0.77 (95 % CI: 0.69–0.85) and an overall agreement of 88.9 % (95 % CI: 84.3 %−92.6 %) (Table 2). These findings indicate that the BICP0-gB iELISA outperforms the commercial ELISA kit in diagnostic performance, making it a more reliable tool for detecting positive cases in clinical settings.

Analytical specificity and sensitivity

Both the BICP0-gB iELISA and the commercial gB ELISA kit demonstrated no cross-reactivity with positive serum when tested against other common pathogens associated with bovine respiratory disease, including M. bovis, PmA, Mh, BVDV, BPIV3, and BRSV. This finding indicates good analytical specificity for both methods.

Furthermore, when applied to clinically positive and negative serum samples at various dilutions, the BICP0-gB iELISA successfully detected samples diluted up to 1:400. In contrast, the commercial ELISA kit achieved its minimum detection limit at a 256-fold dilution. These findings highlight that the BICP0-gB iELISA exhibits superior analytical sensitivity, as evidenced by its lower minimum detection limit compared to the commercial gB ELISA kit.

Reproducibility test

Randomly selected validated negative and positive serum samples that were clinically negative and positive for BoHV-1 were tested using both the same batch and different batches of BICP0-gB iELISA plates. As shown in Table 3, Table 4, the standard deviation of all samples was less than or equal to 0.1, and the coefficient of variation (CV) was lower than 10 % for both the intrabatch and interbatch assays. These results indicate that the BICP0-gB iELISA exhibits robust reproducibility.

Preliminary clinical diagnosis

The established BICP0-gB iELISA was used to analyze clinical serum samples (n = 200), resulting in 77 positive samples and a positive detection rate of 38.5 % (95 % CI: 31.7 %−45.6 %), with a real prevalence of 40.3 % (95 % CI: 33.1 %−48.0 %).

Based on this real prevalence, the PPV of the BICP0-gB iELISA was calculated to be 96.3 %, while the NPV was 94.7 %.

The values obtained from the BICP0-gB iELISA were notably higher than those of the commercial ELISA kit, which reported a PPV of 94.1 % and an NPV of 90.6 %. The high PPV indicates that the BICP0-gB iELISA is highly effective in confirming real positive cases, which is essential for clinical decision-making. Likewise, the elevated NPV suggests that the test is reliable in ruling out negative cases, thereby minimizing the risk of false negatives in clinical practice.

Overall, the enhanced PPV and NPV of the BICP0-gB iELISA underscore its significant value in clinical diagnostics.

Discussion

The current prevalence of BoHV-1 in China is a significant concern, with studies indicating widespread infection among cattle herds. Reports show that the overall prevalence is approximately 40 %, with regional variations ranging from 9 % to 25 % in areas such as Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, and Heilongjiang (Zhou et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2018).

While the VNT is considered the gold standard for diagnosing BoHV-1 (Nautiyal et al., 2023), its complexity and labor-intensive nature make it less practical for routine use. In contrast, antibody detection methods, such as ELISA, offer a cost-effective and user-friendly alternative, which is especially suitable for high-throughput screening. However, the most commonly used ELISA kits targeting gB or gE proteins on the market may have issues with the emergence of false-positive results (Colitti et al., 2018; Petrini et al., 2020).

To address these limitations, our study incorporated not only the gB protein but also BICP0, a landmark protein in the early stage of viral infection. This strategic selection enhances the specificity and sensitivity of our BICP0-gB iELISA, making it a more reliable diagnostic tool. The ability to assess large numbers of samples quickly is crucial for effective disease management.

In this context, we developed a novel BICP0-gB iELISA method that demonstrated a high degree of concordance with VNT. The sensitivity and specificity of our iELISA surpassed those of commercial ELISA kits, indicating its superior accuracy in diagnosing BoHV-1 infections. This enhanced analytical performance underscores the robustness of the BICP0-gB iELISA, establishing it as a reliable tool for differential diagnosis. Besides, the ability to distinguish naturally infected animals from vaccinated animals is another noteworthy consideration in BoHV-1 control (Tignon et al., 2017). When the established BICP0-gB iELISA was performed on serum with known status (both nasal swab and serum samples were confirmed as naturally infected or vaccinated positives via PCR and VNT, respectively), the results of the iELISA could also serve as an auxiliary tool for differentiating infected from vaccinated animals.

Furthermore, our application of BICP0-gB iELISA to clinical serum samples from yaks in Qinghai Province revealed a real prevalence of 40.3 %. This finding aligns with the high prevalence of BoHV-1 documented in China (Yanli et al., 2011; Jianjun & Lanqing, 2014), reinforcing the relevance of our results. The high PPV and NPV of the BICP0-gB iELISA further demonstrate its advantages in clinical settings, indicating its suitability for the early detection and monitoring of BoHV-1 infections. However, due to the lack of clinical serum samples with known status from the early stage of BoHV-1 infections, it is not feasible to further evaluate the detection ability of the iELISA regarding early stages of infection in this study. However, an intervening challenge animal experiments aimed at obtaining serum from early-stage infections had already been taken into account to address this shortcoming.

In summary, our research successfully established an indirect ELISA method based on the BICP0 and gB proteins, which exhibited strong analytical sensitivity and specificity without cross-reactivity to other bovine respiratory pathogens. Compared to the VNT, the BICP0-gB iELISA not only had excellent diagnostic accuracy but also offered practical advantages over commercial gB ELISA kits. The promising results from our preliminary clinical applications underscore the potential of the BICP0-gB iELISA as an effective tool for the prevention and epidemiological surveillance of BoHV-1, which ultimately contributes to reduced disease incidence and transmission in cattle populations.

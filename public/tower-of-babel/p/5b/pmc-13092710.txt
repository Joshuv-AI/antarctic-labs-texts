Association between diarrhea and survival in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy: A landmark analysis from the real-world PRETTY study
Authors: Yiqun Li, Wenna Wang, Zhongsheng Tong, Xinhong Wu, Li Cai, Quchang Ouyang, Wei Li, Zhiyong Yu, Zhengxiang Han, Xiaojia Wang, Man Li, Haibo Wang, Li Li, Jin Yang, Binghe Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiarrhea is the most common adverse event of pyrotinib. This study analyzed the association between diarrhea and survival in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy.MethodsA secondary analysis was performed using the individual patient data from the nationwide, prospective real-world PRETTY study. Baseline and treatment characteristics were summarized in groups by severity of diarrhea. Multivariable Cox regression analysis was used to analyze the association of diarrhea with real-world progression-free survival (rwPFS) and overall survival (OS), respectively. Immortal-time bias was adjusted using the landmark analysis and time-dependent Cox model. Multiple imputation was performed for missing data, and pooling ln(hazard ratio [HR]) estimations on the association between diarrhea and survival were reported.ResultsOf 1129 patients, 826 (73.2%) had any-grade treatment-related diarrhea (including 174 [15.4%] with grade ≥3 diarrhea). After multiple imputation, the multivariable Cox regression analysis showed that diarrhea was independently associated with longer OS (grade 1-2 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.57 [95% CI, 0.38-0.86], P = 0.007; grade ≥3 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.56 [95% CI, 0.31-0.99], P = 0.047) but was not associated with rwPFS. Significant association between diarrhea and OS was also observed in the 1-month landmark analysis (grade 1-2 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.59 [95% CI, 0.39-0.89], P = 0.012; grade ≥3 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.54 [95% CI, 0.30-0.98], P = 0.043). Time-dependent Cox model indicated the trend on better OS when diarrhea occurred, but without statistical significance.ConclusionFor patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer, diarrhea that occurs during pyrotinib-based therapy maybe a prognostic marker of longer OS.Keywords: Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2, Breast cancer, Pyrotinib, Diarrhea, Landmark analysis

Introduction

Approximately 15% of breast cancers are human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive [1], which is an aggressive subtype with poor prognosis. After the emergence of anti-HER2 therapy, the long-term survival of patients with HER2-positive breast cancer is dramatically prolonged. In the phase 3 CLEOPATRA study, the 5-year and 8-year landmark overall survival (OS) rates have been increased to 49% and 37% with first-line trastuzumab plus pertuzumab and docetaxel for patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer [2], respectively. However, this population inevitably develop drug resistance. In recent years, more novel anti-HER2 agents are being investigated to further improve the prognosis of patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [3,4].

Pyrotinib is an irreversible pan-HER tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting HER1, HER2, and HER4 [5]. The frontline use of pyrotinib has been approved by the National Medical Products Administration for the treatment of patients with HER2-positive relapsed or metastatic breast cancer in China [6,7]. The phase 3 PHILA study showed that the addition of pyrotinib to trastuzumab plus docetaxel could prolong the median progression-free survival (PFS) from 10.4 months to 24.3 months (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.41; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.32-0.53; one-sided P < 0.001) in the first-line setting [7]. Diarrhea, the most common adverse event of pyrotinib [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10]], is one of the major concerns from patients during the treatment period. Patient adherence to pyrotinib treatment may be reduced in clinical practice, and the drug activity may also be affected due to inadequate exposure. Previous studies demonstrated that immune-related adverse events were associated with longer survival in patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors [[11], [12], [13]]. If there is also an association between diarrhea and survival in patients treated with pyrotinib-based therapy, patient concerns can be addressed. Currently, there is no studies focusing on this question.

Here this study aimed to analyze the association between diarrhea and survival in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy based on a large-scale real-world study.

Methods

Study population

Individual patient data were derived from the prospective real-world PRETTY study, which was reported previously [14]. The PRETTY study enrolled 1129 adult patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy at 61 sites across China from August 2019 to November 2021 [14]. All these patients were included in the present analysis, grouped by severity of diarrhea (grade ≥3, grade 1-2, and none).

Data collection

Age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, HER2 status (immunohistochemistry staining score and/or fluorescence in situ hybridization result), hormone receptor status, metastatic sites (liver, lung, and/or brain), treatment line, treatment regimen (pyrotinib monotherapy or not), pyrotinib dose, use of antidiarrheal drugs for prophylaxis or treatment, real-world PFS (rwPFS), OS, and diarrhea events were collected.

The definitions and assessment methods of rwPFS and OS as well as the corresponding censoring rules were previously reported [14]. Diarrhea was recorded during the treatment period and until 28 days after the last dose of pyrotinib-based therapy, graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0.

Statistical analysis

For patients with multiple diarrhea events during the follow-up period, the highest grade event was considered for analysis. If multiple same-grade diarrhea events occurred in a patient, the earliest event was analyzed. Baseline and treatment characteristics were summarized for the total population and three groups. Continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]), and categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage). Standardized mean difference (SMD; calculated as difference in means or proportions divided by standard error) was used to compare baseline and treatment characteristics among groups. Imbalance between groups was defined as SMD ≥0.1.

Multivariable Cox regression analysis (model 1) was used to estimate the association between diarrhea and survival outcomes (rwPFS and OS). Immortal-time bias was adjusted by using the landmark analysis (model 2) and time-dependent Cox model (model 3). According to previous reports, most pyrotinib-related diarrhea events occurred during the first-cycle of treatment [15,16], thus the landmark analysis was performed at 1 month after the initiation of pyrotinib. Time-dependent Cox model introduced diarrhea status as a time-dependent covariate to estimate the effect on survival. Results from the multivariable Cox model were interpreted without adjustment for multiple comparisons, as the model was constructed to assess the independent effect of diarrhea by adjusting for confounders.

All the baseline and treatment characteristics were considered as confounders and included in the multivariable Cox model. Multiple imputation would be conducted if >10% observations were excluded from the Cox model. Missing data would be imputed by the fully conditional method, which was appropriate for non-ordered categorical variables under a generalized logit distribution. Pooling estimation on the ln(HR) and corresponding log(-log) 95% CI would be reported.

To account for changes in drug exposure over time, pyrotinib dose was treated as a time-dependent covariate using the counting process (start-stop) approach in all the three models. Specifically, each patient's follow-up period was partitioned into multiple intervals based on the dose change timepoint, assigning the specific dose level valid for that interval to accurately reflect the dynamic exposure status prior to any diarrhea event.

OS curve was plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method. The 95% CI of median OS was estimated using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method. The comparisons of OS among groups in different treatment line settings and the comparisons of rwPFS and OS in subgroups by antidiarrheal drugs were performed using the log-rank test and Cox proportional hazard regression model. Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

The data cutoff date of the PRETTY study was May 29, 2022, and the median follow-up duration was 11.8 months (IQR, 7.2-16.9). Of 1129 patients, 826 (73.2%) had any-grade treatment-related diarrhea (including 174 [15.4%] with grade ≥3 diarrhea) during the follow-up period. Median time to onset of diarrhea was 5.0 days (IQR, 2.0-36.0) after the initiation of pyrotinib-based therapy.

Baseline and treatment characteristics are shown in Table 1. Age, HER2 status, hormone receptor status, metastatic sites, and pyrotinib dose were balanced between the grade 1-2 diarrhea group and non-diarrhea group. SMDs in ECOG performance status, treatment line, pyrotinib dose, treatment regimen, and use of antidiarrheal drugs were above 0.1 between these two groups. Numerically more patients received antidiarrheal drugs for prophylaxis or treatment (40.0% vs. 5.3%) in the grade 1-2 diarrhea group compared with the non-diarrhea group.

Multiple imputation

There were some patients with unknown or incomplete information of ECOG performance status (9.0%), HER2 status (3.7%), and hormone receptor status (1.4%), and the composite proportion of patients with missing data was 13.5%. Datasets generated from 10 times imputation procedure were used for three Cox models. Baseline and treatment variables as well as outcomes (survival time for patients who died and censored time for patients who were alive by the data cutoff date or lost to follow-up) were considered as auxiliary variables correlated with missing variables.

Association between diarrhea and survival

Multivariable Cox regression analysis (model 1) showed that grade 1-2 diarrhea was independently associated with longer OS (HR = 0.59; 95% CI, 0.38-0.92; P = 0.020) but was not associated with rwPFS (HR = 0.86 [95% CI, 0.67-1.10], P = 0.227). Grade ≥3 diarrhea was not associated with OS (HR = 0.65 [95% CI, 0.35-1.19], P = 0.160) or rwPFS (HR = 0.98 [95% CI, 0.71-1.36], P = 0.900; Table 2). The comparisons of OS in different treatment line settings showed that the OS difference among groups by severity of diarrhea was mainly observed in patients treated with third- or further-line pyrotinib-based therapy (log-rank nominal P = 0.003; grade 1-2 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.45 [95% CI, 0.24-0.85]; grade ≥3 diarrhea vs. none: HR = 0.18 [95% CI, 0.05-0.61]; Fig. 1). Similar results were observed in groups by the presence or absence of diarrhea (Supplementary Fig. 1). The comparisons of OS in subgroups by antidiarrheal drugs are shown in Supplementary Fig. 2.

The results showed that grade 1-2 diarrhea was still independently associated with longer OS (HR = 0.62 [95% CI, 0.39-0.96], P = 0.033) in the landmark analysis (model 2), while this association was not statistically significant (HR = 0.74 [95% CI, 0.48-1.13], P = 0.167) in the time-dependent Cox model (model 3). Discussion

This study firstly revealed the association between diarrhea and survival in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy. The multivariable Cox regression analysis after multiple imputation showed that diarrhea was independently associated with longer OS, regardless of diarrhea severity. Similar results were observed after controlling the immortal-time bias using the landmark analysis. We believe that these findings are relatively reliable as a large population from a prospective real-world study was included in the present analysis.

In previous phase 2 and 3 clinical trials, over 90% of patients had any-grade diarrhea after treatment containing pyrotinib [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10],[17], [18], [19], [20]]. In our real-world study, the incidence of any-grade diarrhea was also high (73.2%), but lower than previous clinical trials [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10],[17], [18], [19], [20]]. In clinical practice, primary prevention may be considered to manage diarrhea. In our study, 8.6% of the total population received drugs (mostly montmorillonite powder and loperamide) for diarrhea prophylaxis [14], which might be the main reason for the difference in incidence of diarrhea between our real-world study and previous clinical trials [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10],[17], [18], [19], [20]]. In addition, we have to acknowledge that there might be missing reports when adverse events in this real-world study were not monitored as strict as clinical trials. Nevertheless, like previous clinical trials [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10],[17], [18], [19], [20]], diarrhea in our study was generally manageable. Median time to onset of diarrhea was 5 days, which was within the range (3-7.5 days) of previous reports [7,15,16].

Our analysis demonstrated that patients with diarrhea would have greater probability of longer OS compared with those without diarrhea during the treatment with pyrotinib-based therapy, especially in the third- or further-line setting. After previous heavy treatment and repeated disease progression, patients might have worse health condition and tolerability to receive later-line treatment. The significant association between diarrhea and OS observed in this later-line population further suggests more potent anti-tumor activity of pyrotinib-based therapy in patients with diarrhea than in those without diarrhea. Previous studies demonstrated that the occurrence of immune-related adverse events could indicate better efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors [[11], [12], [13]], but the exact mechanisms remain unclear. For patients treated with pyrotinib-based therapy, the mechanisms of association between diarrhea and OS also need further investigations.

Previous studies demonstrated the pharmacokinetic interactions of antidiarrheal drugs on pyrotinib [21,22]. The concomitant use of montmorillonite can reduce the bioavailability of pyrotinib by 50.3% and reduce the pyrotinib exposure by 32.4% in area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) [21,22]. In contrast, the concomitant use of loperamide can increase the systemic exposure of pyrotinib by 19% [22]. In our study, the univariable analysis indicated the rwPFS benefit with the use of loperamide, while montmorillonite did not result in diminished rwPFS. Patients with any diarrheal drugs seemed to have a better OS benefit than those without. However, the use of antidiarrheal drugs did not show independent association with rwPFS or OS in either multivariable Cox model. Since this was a real-world study with population heterogeneity, whether antidiarrheal drugs could impact survival benefits in patients receiving pyrotinib-based therapy still requires further investigation.

Limitations

Some limitations need to be acknowledged in this study. First, 8.6% of the total population received primary prevention of diarrhea [14]. This might influence the occurrence of diarrhea and patient grouping, although this effect could be diluted by the large sample size. Given the increasing use of diarrhea prophylaxis for patients treated with pyrotinib in clinical practice, dose adjustments due to diarrhea, and interference of diarrhea caused by other drugs in combination therapy, utilizing diarrhea as a prognostic marker for longer OS may present certain challenges in the real-world setting. Second, the heterogeneity in treatment lines and regimens might affect the analysis and interpretation of the results. Third, patients with diarrhea might have more clinical visits to manage dehydration or for dose reduction, and their progression would be detected earlier, artificially lowering their rwPFS. Finally, given the insufficient OS events, the more rigorous time-dependent Cox model did not observe the association of diarrhea with OS, which requires re-analysis based on mature OS data in future.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the secondary analysis of the PRETTY study found the association between diarrhea and OS in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer treated with pyrotinib-based therapy. This might bring confidence and improve compliance for patients who concern about diarrhea.

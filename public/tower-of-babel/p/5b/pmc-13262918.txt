CCB-4 and its sugar complexes induce mitotic catastrophe and p53-independent apoptosis through caspase-2 elevation and suppression of c-Myc in T47D cells
Authors: Ni Putu Linda Laksmiani, Rohmad Yudi Utomo, Riris Istighfari Jenie, Yusuke Suenaga, Mitsunori Kirihata, Edy Meiyanto, Ratna Asmah Susidarti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Adding boron to curcumin analogs enhances their ability to inhibit cell growth and facilitates their detection within cells. Derivatives of diboronocurcumin are, therefore, promising options for cancer treatment. Our previous in silico analysis revealed that CCB-4 binds more tightly to proteins associated with breast cancer than Pentagamaboronon-0. We created sugar complexes of CCB-4 using fructose (CCB-4 Fr) and sorbitol (CCB-4 So) to enhance solubility and cellular uptake. This research aimed to assess the cytotoxic activity and anticancer potential of CCB-4, CCB-4 So, and CCB-4 Fr against T47D breast cancer cells (ER+, PR+, p53 mutant). We investigated the expression of phosphorylated epidermal growth factor receptor (p-EGFR), c-Myc, HDAC1, and caspase-2 by capillary Western blot analysis, including cytotoxicity using MTT assay, clonogenic survival, cell cycle progression, and apoptosis. CCB-4, CCB-4 So, and CCB-4 Fr demonstrated strong cytotoxic effects and had IC50 values of 8, 6, and 5 μM, respectively. All the substances caused G2/M phase arrest and significantly reduced colony formation, leading to mitotic catastrophe. Mechanistically, treatment reduced p-EGFR, c-Myc, and HDAC1 expressions and activated apoptosis through caspase-2 independently of p53. These findings suggest that CCB-4 and its sugar complexes have promising anticancer effects against luminal A breast cancer cells by triggering caspase-2-mediated apoptosis and mitotic catastrophe.

INTRODUCTION

The curcumin derivative, diboronocurcumin, such as Pentagamaboronon (PGB)-0, PGB-0-ol, and CCB-2, shows effective anticancer activity.[123] The heterocyclic ketone of CCB-4 [Figure 1] is also cytotoxic on HCT116 colon cancer cell lines.[4] Through in silico study, CCB-4 has possible uses in breast cancer treatment, as it targets proteins related to the proliferation of malignant cells, apoptosis, and metastasis, for example, HER2, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), IKK, HDAC1, and MMP-9.[5] Discovery of boron-substituted curcumin analogs, such as CCB-4, is important because boron can be used to track the compound in cells, with its disrupting cancer cell metabolism, inducing apoptosis, and inhibiting proliferation effects.[6]

Chemical structure of CCB-4

Modification of curcumin analogs has been the topic of research to increase their cytotoxicity, for example, using heterocycles in the ketone center and improving solubility and stability by complexing with sugars such fructose and sorbitol.[7] This complexation enhances the cellular influx, as cancer cells overexpress glucose transporters (GLUT1 and GLUT5), a prerequisite for the proliferation of cancer cells.[89] In breast cancer, an increased GLUT expression is associated with progression and metastasis.[10]

Here, we study the anticancer activity of CCB-4 and its soluble complex with sorbitol (CCB-4 So) and fructose (CCB-4 Fr) in T47D cells; these cells represent both the ER+ and PR+ phenotypes.[11] T47D cells are of particular interest as 70% of breast cancers are ER+, and they contain p53 mutations that additionally make them suitable for testing.[12] Cytotoxicity, antiproliferative, cell-cycle analysis, apoptosis, and protein expression assays will be performed to determine the anticancer potential of compounds.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Chemicals

We synthesized CCB-4 using the same synthesis method as PGB-0 with modifications. We used 1-methyl piperidine-4-one as the ketone center and substituted the benzene ring with boronic acid. CCB-4 So and CCB-4 Fr complexes were prepared by reacting CCB-4 and sugars in the form of sorbitol (Sigma-Aldrich) and fructose (Sigma-Aldrich) with a ratio of CCB-4 to sugar of 1:3. Doxorubicin (Dox) (Sigma-Aldrich) was used as a positive control.

Cell culture

T47D cells, which were donated by Prof. Masashi Kawaichi (NAIST, Japan), cultured by CCRC Pharmacy UGM, and maintained in DMEM high glucose (Gibco), 0.5% v/v Fungizone (Gibco), 1.5% v/v penicillin with streptomycin (Gibco), and 10% v/v FBS (Sigma).

Cytotoxicity assay

Cells (7 × 103 cell/well) were grown in 96-wells plates, treated with CCB-4, CCB-4 So, or CCB-4 Fr (0.5–25 μM) and Doxorubicin (0.01–10 μM) for 24 h and then incubated at 37°C with a solution of MTT (0.5 mg/mL) for another 4 h forming the purple formazan. The reaction was terminated by adding 100 μL of 10% SDS in 0.01 N HCl. Overnight absorbance as measured by an ELISA reader at λ 595 nm. Then, cell viability (%) and IC50 values were calculated.[13]

Cancer cell persistence analysis

After the incubation of 24 h, cells were exposed to CCB-4, CCB-4 So, CCB-4 Fr, and Dox. Media was replaced with complete media without treatment, after 24 h of treatment (wash-out) and up to 72 h. MTT was used for cell viability determination.[14]

Antiproliferative activity using clonogenic assay

CCB-4, CCB-4 So, CCB-4 Fr and Dox were administered at a 24-h interval after plating cells (5 × 102 cells/well) on a 24-well plate for up to 10 days with media renewal every 3 days. Colonies were stained with 0.1% gentian violet, and the treated and control groups were compared.[15]

Mitosis progression by May–Grunwald Giemsa staining

T47D cells were treated with 1 µM Dox, CCB-4, CCB-4-So, and CCB-4-Fr for 2, 4, and 8 µM after seeding in 6-well plates with 1.5 × 105 cells/well for 24 h. Cells were washed with PBS, stained with May–Grunwald (5 min) and Giemsa (1:20, 20 min), rinsed using distilled water, air-dried, and observed using an inverted microscope.[16]

Cell cycle modulation

T47D cells (5 × 105 cells/well) were grown in a 6-well plate for 24 h, and treated with the test compound for 24 h. Cells were harvested, washed with PBS, trypsinized (0.25% trypsin-Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid [EDTA]), centrifuged (500 × g, 5 min), fixed in 70% ethanol (4°C, 30 min), stained with propidium iodide solution (RNase and Triton X-100), and analyzed by flow cytometry (FACS Calibur).[17]

Apoptotic assay

The effects of the compounds on T47D cells seeded in 6-well plates were tested; medium was added to control wells. Adherent and floating cells were collected 24 h post-exposure, combined, and trypsinized using 0.25% trypsin–EDTA before centrifugation (500 × g, 5 min). The cell pellet was resuspended in Annexin V–FITC binding buffer and stained with Annexin V and PI (dark at room temperature) for flow cytometry to evaluate apoptosis.[18]

Protein expression using simple Western blot analysis

T47D cells (5 × 105 cells/well) were treated with the tested compounds (5 μM) for 24 h in 6-well plates. Total proteins were isolated using RIPA buffer, denatured, and mixed with fluorescent master mix containing DTT. Protein expression of EGFR, phospho-EGFR, c-Myc, HDAC1, and caspase-2 (1:1000 dilution) was quantified using an automated capillary Western blot system (Abby, ProteinSimple).

Statistical analysis

Data from three independent experiments (in triplicate) were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9 and presented as mean ± standard error of the mean One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used for group comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as * P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001.

RESULTS

Cytotoxic, persistence, and anti-proliferation effect of CCB-4, and its complexes on T47D cells

CCB-4 and its sugar complexes (So and Fr) exhibited dose-dependent cytotoxicity against T47D cells with IC50 values of 8, 6, 5, and 4 µM for CCB-4, CCB-4 So, CCB-4 Fr, and Dox [Figure 2]. CCB-4 and its complexes showed superior selectivity (SI > 2), in contrast to Dox (SI < 2).[19] Furthermore, a 72 h postwithdrawal assay confirmed that CCB-4, its complexes, and Dox maintained significant growth inhibition [Figure 3]. This long-term efficacy was further supported by colony formation assay, where treatment with ½ and ¼ IC50 suppressed T47D proliferation significantly (P < 0.05) [Figure 4]. Inhibition persisted for 10 days postremoval of the treatment. These results demonstrate that CCB-4 sugar complexes exert potent, selective, and sustained anticancer activity.

CCB-4, CCB-4 So, CCB-4 Fr, and doxorubicin induced the mitotic catastrophe phenomenon in T47D Cells

The mitotic catastrophe induction by CCB-4 and its complexes was assessed using May–Grunwald Giemsa (MGG) staining. As compared to untreated cells, treated cells had nuclear enlargement and rupture, leading to multinucleated giant cells over 24 h [Figure 5]. The frequency of mitotic catastrophe was significantly increased in all treated groups compared to untreated controls, and there were no significant differences between treatments. CCB-4, CCB-4 So, and CCB-4 Fr Caused G2/M phase accumulation and apoptosis induction on T47D Cells

Cell treatment with CCB-4 and its complexes significantly increased the proportion of G2/M phase cells in comparison with the control group (P < 0.01) [Figure 6]. This mitotic inhibition was associated with a pronounced increase in the sub-G1 cellular population, indicating apoptosis induction. The specific apoptotic pathways used by CCB-4 variants to inhibit proliferation were further confirmed by Annexin V-FITC/PI staining. In particular, CCB-4 and its complexes did not cause necrosis, unlike doxorubicin treatment. The above findings suggest that CCB-4 acts by inducing G2/M arrest-mediated apoptosis without necrosis [Figure 7].

Modulation of phosphorylated epidermal growth factor receptor, c-Myc, HDAC1, and caspase-2 protein expression by CCB-4, CCB-4 So, and CCB-4 Fr compound

CCB-4 and its sugar complexes inhibited the proliferation of T47D cells, arrested G2/M phase, and promoted apoptosis. Western blotting demonstrated decreased levels of phosphorylated EGFR (p-EGFR), c-Myc, and HDAC1 [Figure 8], which was related to growth inhibition and cell cycle arrest. In parallel with decreased HDAC1 levels, pro-caspase-2 expression was increased [Figure 9], indicating that apoptotic signaling may be involved. Nonetheless, caspase-2 activation was not analyzed, and more studies are required to clarify the entire apoptosis pathway.

DISCUSSION

In breast cancer, to mitigate the resistance and side effects of chemotherapy, diboronocurcumin derivatives (CCB-4) and their sugar conjugates, So and Fr, were developed. Varying complexes displayed improved solubility and cellular uptake – most likely through overexpression of the GLUT1 and GLUT5 transporters in T47D cells[20] – resulting in enhanced cytotoxicity versus uncomplexed CCB-4. More so than CCB-4 8 μM, IC50 doses of CCB-4 So 6 μM and CCB-4 Fr 5 μM were shown to be more potently active through downregulation of p-EGFR and c-Myc, and G2/M phase arrest confirmed by decreased HDAC1. This chromatic anomaly subsequently induced mitotic catastrophe and a p53-independent apoptosis. Since T47D cells harbor inactivated p53, CCB-4 complexes constitute a novel strategy for the therapy of p53-mutant or resistant cancers.

The agents also upregulated the expression of pro-caspase-2, an important contributor to mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis.[21] Nevertheless, because we only determined total pro-caspase-2 levels, the activation of caspase-2 and subsequent downstream effectors, including cleaved PARP, caspase-3, and caspase-9, were not evaluated.[22] These markers are important to completely define the apoptosis pathway, and it needs to be determined whether cleaved caspase-2 and other apoptotic markers should be measured as part of the mechanism.

Collectively, our findings suggest that CCB-4 and its sugar complexes possess anti-breast cancer activity via G2/M arrest, mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis by a caspase-2–associated and p53-independent mechanism. However, detection of sustained cytotoxic effects for up to 72 h after removal of the compound does not represent irreversible damage in vitro. Hence, it remains to be seen in vivo whether these have long-term therapeutic potential. Furthermore, more studies are needed to specify the specific apoptotic signaling pathways and downstream protein activation involved.

CONCLUSION

CCB-4 and its sugar conjugates have potent antiproliferative effects through attenuation of p-EGFR, c-Myc, and HDAC1. It causes G2/M arrest and caspase-2-dependent mitotic catastrophe. CCB-4 complexes induce p53-independent apoptosis without necrosis, unlike doxorubicin. Consequently, these derivatives represent new candidates for tumors resistant to classical p53-based therapies.

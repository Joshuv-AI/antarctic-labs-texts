In vitro antibacterial and concentration-dependent bactericidal effects of Terminalia arjuna extract against Staphylococcus aureus and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, with synergistic activity against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus
Authors: Sumalee Panthong, Jittiporn Chaorai, Pusaree Temboonkiat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTStaphylococcus aureus infections, particularly methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA), remain a major clinical challenge due to limited treatment options. Plant-derived compounds have gained interest as alternative or adjunct antibacterial agents. This study investigated the antibacterial, antibiofilm, and synergistic effects of Terminalia arjuna extract against S. aureus and MRSA, with high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis. Antibacterial activity of T. arjuna extract was evaluated using microdilution, time-kill, checkerboard, and biofilm assays against S. aureus and MRSA, with chemical profiling by HPLC. Data were calculated and shown as means ± standard deviation. A Significant difference was marked when P < 0.05. The ethanolic extract of T. arjuna demonstrated bactericidal action against S. aureus and MRSA, with minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum bactericidal concentration values of 625 μg/mL. A concentration-dependent effect was observed, with complete bacterial killing achieved at supra-MIC levels. The extract reduced biofilm biomass by more than 80% at low concentrations. Notably, a pronounced synergistic interaction with ampicillin against MRSA was observed, yielding a fractional inhibitory concentration index of 0.281. The T. arjuna extract exhibited in vitro antibacterial activity and showed concentration-dependent bactericidal effects, with a synergistic interaction with ampicillin observed against MRSA.

INTRODUCTION

Staphylococcus aureus is a Gram-positive bacterium that normally colonizes the skin and mucosa but can act as an opportunistic pathogen, causing severe infections. The emergence of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) has further limited treatment options and remains a major clinical concern.[1] The incidence of MRSA bloodstream infections in Thailand between 2017 and 2022 ranged from 7.5% to 16.7%.[2] Key risk factors include prolonged hospitalizations, intensive care unit admission, recent antibiotic treatment, invasive procedures, and underlying conditions.[3] S. aureus biofilms enhance resistance and immune evasion, leading to persistent infections.[4]

Plant-derived bioactive compounds are increasingly explored as alternatives against resistant bacteria.[5] Terminalia arjuna contains tannins, flavonoids, and phenolics with reported antibacterial activity.[6789] The fruit of T. arjuna has been used in Thai traditional medicine for treating intestinal conditions.[10] However, the effects of its fruit extract on MRSA and biofilm formation remain insufficiently explored. This study assessed its antibacterial, time-kill, and antibiofilm activities, interaction with ampicillin, and chemical profile by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Chemicals and reagents

Culture media (nutrient agar, Mueller–Hinton broth, and tryptic soy broth) were obtained from Difco (USA). Chemical reagents, including ampicillin, crystal violet, resazurin, gallic acid, and chebulagic acid, were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, TCI, and ChemFaces. All reagents were of analytical grade (RCI Labscan).

Plant extraction

Fruits of T. arjuna were collected from Suphan Buri, Thailand, cleaned, dried at 45°C, and powdered. The substance was extracted using 95% ethanol through maceration at room temperature and filtered, and the solvent was evaporated under reduced pressure to obtain a dry extract.

Bacterial strains

The two bacterial strains, S. aureus ATCC 25923 and MRSA DMST 20651, were used in this study. These bacteria were subcultured onto nutrient agar and incubated at 37°C for 18–24 h.

Antibacterial activity using broth microdilution

Antibacterial activity was determined using a microplate-based resazurin assay with minor modifications.[11] T. arjuna extract or ampicillin (positive control) was two-fold serially diluted and incubated with bacterial suspensions (0.5 McFarland, 1:100 dilution) at 37°C for 18–24 h. After adding resazurin, minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) was defined as the lowest concentration preventing color change. The minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) was established by subculturing noncolor-changing wells onto agar; the lowest concentration exhibiting no growth was designated as the MBC.

Time-kill kinetic assay

The time-kill test was carried out using a modified technique, as previously reported.[12] The bactericidal dynamics of the extract were evaluated at MIC, 2× MIC, and 4× MIC concentrations. Bacterial suspensions were incubated with the extract, and samples were collected at predetermined time points. Viable counts were determined and expressed as log colony-forming unit (CFU)/mL. All samples were obtained in triplicate and reported as the mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Synergistic action of Terminalia arjuna extract and antibiotic drug using checkerboard experiment

A checkerboard experiment was performed to assess the combined effect of T. arjuna extract and antibiotic drug against S. aureus and MRSA.[13] Serial dilutions of both agents were combined with bacterial suspensions (0.5 McFarland, 1:100 dilution) and incubated. Following resazurin addition, MIC values were determined, and fractional inhibitory concentration (FIC) indices were calculated.

Interaction effects were interpreted based on FIC index values: ≤0.5 (synergy), >0.5–<1 (partial interaction), 1 (additive), >1–≤4 (indifference), and >4 (antagonism).[14]

Antibiofilm formation assay

Antibiofilm formation was evaluated using a microtiter plate assay.[15] Bacterial suspensions (0.5 McFarland, 106 CFU/mL) were incubated with T. arjuna extract at 37°C for 24 h. Biofilms were stained with crystal violet, and absorbance was measured at 595 nm. The extent of biofilm formation was interpreted using A595 values, which were divided into four categories: non, weak, moderate, and strong, corresponding to increasing absorbance ranges (0–1, 1–2, 2–3, and >3, respectively).

Quantification of chemical compounds

HPLC analysis of T. arjuna extract was performed using a C18 column with gradient elution of acetonitrile and 1% acetic acid.[16] Detection was carried out at 270 and 275 nm. Gallic acid and chebulagic acid were quantified using external standards and expressed as % w/w. All analyses showed good linearity (r2 > 0.999).

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance followed by Dunnett’s test, with significance set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

Terminalia arjuna extract inhibited Staphylococcus aureus and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus

T. arjuna extract inhibited S. aureus and MRSA, showing moderate activity with MIC and MBC values of 625 μg/mL.[1718] An MBC-to-MIC ratio ≤4 indicates bactericidal activity, whereas ratio >4 indicates bacteriostatic activity.[19] Based on this criterion, the extract exhibited a bactericidal effect against S. aureus and MRSA, with a ratio of 1. The extract was found to kill S. aureus and MRSA, as indicated by an MBC/MIC ratio of 1. S. aureus was susceptible to ampicillin (MIC = 0.078 μg/mL, within the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute breakpoints).[20] MRSA showed resistance to ampicillin, with an MIC of 200 μg/mL [Table 1].

According to the time-kill kinetic assay, the initial inocula for S. aureus and MRSA were 5.01 ± 0.21 and 5.20 ± 0.16 log10 CFU/mL, respectively. At 1× MIC (625 μg/mL), the extract reduced S. aureus by 2.69 ± 0.19 log10 CFU/mL after 24 h, corresponding to a decrease by approximately 2.3 log10 CFU/mL. This reduction does not meet the ≥3 log10 criterion for bactericidal activity, indicating that the extract exhibited bacteriostatic activity against S. aureus at this concentration.[21] In contrast, at 2× MIC and 4× MIC, the viable bacterial counts were reduced to nearly undetectable levels, demonstrating bactericidal activity at higher concentrations [Figure 1a].

Time-kill kinetics of Terminalia arjuna extract against (a) Staphylococcus aureus and (b) methicillin-resistant S. aureus with significance thresholds set at (*) P < 0.05 compared with the initial inoculum at 0 h

For MRSA, at 1× MIC (625 μg/mL), the viable bacterial count remained at 3.56 ± 0.57 log10 CFU/mL after 24 h, corresponding to a reduction of <2 log10 CFU/mL from the initial inoculum. This indicates that the extract exerted only bacteriostatic activity against MRSA at 1× MIC. However, similar to S. aureus, treatment with 2× MIC and 4× MIC resulted in a marked reduction in viable counts to nearly undetectable levels, confirming bactericidal activity at these higher concentrations [Figure 1b].

Bactericidal activity was evaluated by MBC/MIC ratio and time-kill kinetics. Although the MBC/MIC ratio of 1 suggests bactericidal activity, the time-kill assay at 1× MIC showed <3 log10 CFU/mL reduction, indicating bacteriostatic effects. This difference reflects methodological variation between endpoint and dynamic assessments. Bactericidal activity was observed only at higher concentrations, highlighting a concentration-dependent effect.[2122]

Interaction between Terminalia arjuna extract and antibiotics against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus

Checkerboard assay results [Table 2] showed that the combination of ampicillin and T. arjuna exhibited synergistic activity against MRSA (FIC index = 0.281), despite MRSA resistance to ampicillin (MIC = 200 μg/mL). This effect was observed at 156.25 μg/mL T. arjuna combined with 6.25 μg/mL ampicillin, whereas no synergy was detected in S. aureus.

Terminalia arjuna extract inhibited biofilm formation

S. aureus exhibited strong biofilm formation, whereas MRSA demonstrated a modest capability. The T. arjuna extract demonstrated significantly reduced biofilm biomass as quantified by the crystal violet assay, in both S. aureus and MRSA [Figure 2]. The most notable results were observed in MRSA, where the extract reduced biofilm biomass by more than 80% at a concentration of 39 μg/mL. Similarly, in S. aureus, the extract reduced biofilm biomass by over 80% at 78 μg/mL.

Chemical constituent in Terminalia arjuna extract

HPLC analysis confirmed gallic acid and chebulagic acid as major constituents of T. arjuna extract, with retention times of 6.32 and 21.91 min, respectively [Figure 3]. Spiking and ultraviolet spectra further verified their identities [Figure 4]. Quantitative analysis showed chebulagic acid (18.65% ± 1.56% w/w) and gallic acid (1.48% ± 0.12% w/w).

Chebulagic acid exhibited inhibitory activity against S. aureus and MRSA (MIC = 250 and 62.50 μg/mL) without bactericidal effect (MBC >1 mg/mL), while gallic acid showed both inhibitory (MIC = 62.50 and 31.25 μg/mL) and bactericidal activity (MBC = 125 and 62.50 μg/mL).

DISCUSSION

Phytochemical investigations have revealed that T. arjuna contains diverse classes of compounds, notably tannins, phenolics, flavonoids, and glycosides.[9] The methanolic extract of its fruit has previously been reported to inhibit S. aureus (MIC = 10 mg/mL).[7] The ethanolic extract of T. arjuna showed comparable antibacterial activity, although its MIC values were higher than previously reported. This study is the first to demonstrate its bactericidal activity and synergistic effect with ampicillin, particularly against MRSA. The extract enhanced ampicillin efficacy against MRSA but not S. aureus. MRSA resistance to multiple antibiotics, including β-lactams and macrolides, is largely attributed to β-lactamase production.[23] The action of efflux pumps, which actively remove antimicrobial agents from the cell, is also strongly associated with multidrug resistance in microorganisms.[24] Other species within the Terminalia genus have also been reported to have inhibitory activity against S. aureus and MRSA, and some studies have highlighted their potential to enhance the efficacy of gentamicin through synergistic interactions.[2526] Gallic acid, a tannin component, inhibited β-lactam-resistant S. aureus and induced morphological changes in MRSA, thereby increasing the antibacterial efficacy of ampicillin against antibiotic-resistant bacteria.[27] Additionally, tannic acid, another tannin component, reduced the Nor A efflux pump associated with moderate fluoroquinolone resistance in S. aureus.[2829] T. arjuna extract was mainly composed of chebulagic acid, with gallic acid as a minor component. These hydrolyzable tannins may contribute to the antibacterial and synergistic effects; however, their roles remain hypothetical due to the absence of mechanistic studies. The observed synergistic interaction between T. arjuna extract and ampicillin against MRSA may be attributed to the combined or additive effects of multiple phytochemicals present in the extract rather than a single active compound. Further studies, including bioactivity-guided fractionation and targeted mechanistic investigations, are required to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

CONCLUSIONS

T. arjuna extract inhibited S. aureus and MRSA in vitro, showing concentration-dependent bactericidal activity above the MIC. Synergistic effects with ampicillin were observed against MRSA, reducing its MIC. Further studies are needed to confirm its therapeutic potential.

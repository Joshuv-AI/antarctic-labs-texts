Ventilation heterogeneity early post lung transplant as a marker of future bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome and mortality – A 10 year study
Authors: Bruce R. Thompson, Matthew J. Ellis, Pam Matsas, Sylvia Verbanck, Greg I. Snell
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lung transplantation (LTx) is regarded as an effective treatment of end-stage lung disease, yet recipients are at significant risk of developing Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome (BOS). Previous studies have shown that BOS is related to ventilatory heterogeneity, IL-8 and airway hyper-responsiveness (AHR). What has been less explored is the potential for these measures taken early post-LTx to predict future BOS or death.MethodsDouble LTx patients had spirometry, and measures of acinar (Sacin) and conductive (Scond) ventilation heterogeneity from the multiple breath nitrogen washout (MBNW) at regular intervals over a 12month period. AHR was also measured within 6 months of LTx and IL-8 was estimated from bronchoalveolar lavage taken at the time of pulmonary function measures. Patients were further monitored for BOS or death during a 10-year follow-up period.ResultsOf the 33 patients recruited, 17 patients developed BOS, and 13 died over the follow-up period. The patients who had an Scond > 0.029 L−1 in the first 6 months post-LTx had a hazard ratio of 5.1 (P=0.0022) for BOS/ Death. Also, patients with a Log IL-8 > 3.1 pg/mL in the first 6 months had a hazard ratio of 2.8 (P=0.039) for BOS/Death. The patients with Scond or IL-8 in excess of their respective threshold amounted to a hazard ratio of 3.7 (P=0.0085). Finally, a hypertonic saline dose response slope>0.25Δ%FEV1/mL was not predictive of BOS/Death.ConclusionWe have demonstrated that increased Scond or IL-8 within 6-month post LTx is associated with greatly increased risk of BOS according to the 2001 definition or death within 10 years. Restrictive Allograft Syndrome cannot not be fully excluded due to the absence of routine static lung volume measurements.

Introduction

Lung transplantation (LTx) has been widely accepted to be an effective treatment for end-stage lung disease. Despite good early outcomes, LTx recipients have significant risk of developing Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome (BOS), a form of chronic lung allograft dysfunction (CLAD).1 The causes of BOS and CLAD are still of much debate, although a number of factors are likely to be involved including ischemia-reperfusion injury, infection and allo-rejection. Neutrophil chemoattractant interleukin-8 (IL-8), as measured in the bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) has been shown to play an important inflammatory role in a number of respiratory conditions including asthma and chronic bronchitis.2 Studies of IL-8 in LTx recipients3, 4, 5, 6 consistently describe elevated IL-8 levels: in LTx recipients who did not have BOS, relative to normal controls, with further elevation in those who did have BOS. In LTx patients, airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) to methacholine or to hypertonic saline were not found to be associated with increased numbers of mast cells, eosinophils or lymphocytes.7 While AHR to methacholine is more common than to hypertonic saline, it has no prognostic value, whereas LTx recipients with AHR to hypertonic saline in the early post-transplant period are more likely to develop BOS and to develop it sooner.8

In an effort to better monitor patient outcomes following lung transplantation, measurements of standard spirometry9 have been complemented with lung mechanics.10, 11 Cho et al.10 reported that oscillometry-based reactance and frequency dependence of resistance were abnormal in patients when they developed acute cellular rejection. Importantly, spirometry was unchanged. On the other hand, Winton et al12 observed correlations between FEV1 and both reactance and frequency dependence of resistance, with correlations of respective changes over the first 6 months. Crowhurst et al.11 also found that reactance and frequency dependence of resistance were correlated with spirometry, but that none of the oscillometry parameters were predictive of future BOS.

We have previously shown significant ventilation heterogeneity in the acinar region of the lung in LTx recipients who develop BOS.13 Relative to healthy LTx recipients, using the Multiple Breath Nitrogen Washout (MBNW) technique, ventilation heterogeneity at the level of the acinar entrance (termed Sacin), was increased in patients with early BOS. Moreover, Sacin further increased with BOS stage/severity. Finally, the significant correlation between Sacin and airflow obstruction led to the conclusion that the acinar heterogeneity was a primary cause of airflow obstruction seen in LTx recipients who had developed BOS. Our study was corroborated by a microCT study of Verleden et al.14 demonstrating that the terminal bronchioles at the acinar entrance were affected while the lung structure in the zone beyond it appeared normal. Using MBW with SF6 instead of N2, the lung clearance index (LCI), a more global measure of ventilation heterogeneity, was seen to increase with increasing BOS grade, and to better discriminate between the milder BOS stages than Sacin or Scond (ventilation heterogeneity in the conductive airways).15 Since a significant proportion of patients had abnormal LCI with preserved FEV1, these authors concluded there was a role for MBW in the early identification of BOS. In another cross-sectional study in children, LCI and Sacin increases were observed in post-LTx patients at a median of 1.6 years, with no correlation to spirometry.16

Although the above mentioned immunological and lung function indices are known to be involved in the pathogenesis of BOS in cross sectional studies, there is a paucity of longitudinal data to conclude whether any of these indices could reliably predict future BOS. BOS is an unrelenting progressive condition which there are no well established predictive markers (either Immunological or Lung Function) of whether it will occur post lung transplant. The aim of this prospective longitudinal study was to determine whether early post-transplant measurements of ventilation heterogeneity, cytokine IL8 and AHR to hypertonic saline, can be linked to the future development of BOS or death. We hypothesized that the repeated monitoring of indices ventilation heterogeneity, IL-8 and hypertonic saline AHR during the first-year post LTx will be able to determine whether the LTx recipient develops BOS over a 10-year observation period.

Methods

Consecutive bilateral LTx recipients from a single center were invited to participate in the study immediately post LTx over a 24-month recruitment period between 2008 and 2009. Patients were then followed up for a total period of ten years following their transplant. All subjects gave informed consent and the study was approved by the Alfred Hospital ethics committee (46/08).

Bronchoscopy and physiological measurements were performed on the same day at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post-LTx as mandated by clinical protocols. Between 12 months post-LTx and the end of the observation period (ten years in total), spirometry measurements were performed on average at least every 6 months. All patients had all follow-up visits at the Alfred Hospital.

Operative details

The methods of donor and recipient matching, surgical techniques and post-operative management were all performed in accordance to standard techniques previously published.17, 18

Immunosuppression

All LTx recipients received standard triple immunosuppression with cyclosporine or tacrolimus, azathioprine or mycophenolate mofetil and corticosteroids as per current guidelines.17, 18 Induction therapy with the IL-2 receptor blocker basiliximab (20 mg intravenously on days 0 and 4) was given as a calcineurin inhibitor-sparing agent to subjects who were identified as high risk of developing renal dysfunction.

Lung function measures

Spirometry

Each subject performed spirometry (Medgraphics, St Paul, Minesota USA) in accordance with ATS/ERS standards.19 From spirometry, measurements of forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) forced vital capacity (FVC), forced mid expiratory flow rate (FEF25–75%) and were obtained.

Multiple breath nitrogen washout

MBNW methodology and its standardization guidelines has been extensively described elsewhere,20, 21 and provides indices of ventilation heterogeneity that can separate gas mixing in the airways proximal to the diffusion front (Scond) from that in the airways at the level of the diffusion front (Sacin) likely to be located around the acinar entrance.22 Indices Scond and Sacin are derived from normalized phase III slopes of three MBNW tests,21, 23 each one consisting of multiple one litre inspirations of 100% oxygen. Since phase III slope increases with increased ventilation heterogeneity, an increase in either Sacin or Scond reflects a deterioration in lung function. The global ventilatory heterogeneity index LCI was also measured as previously described.22

ELISA assay for IL-8 from bronchial alveolar lavage

Bronchial alveolar lavage samples collected from bronchoscopy were processed for IL-8 within 24 h of collection using commercial ELISA kits (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA).

Airway hyper-responsiveness to hypertonic saline

The hypertonic saline challenge was performed at 3 months using a modified protocol, extended to include a cumulative dose of 60 mL.24, 25 A dose response slope was calculated.

Determination of CLAD/ BOS

The definition of BOS was taken from the 2001 criteria as they were current at the time. Therefore measurements of static lung volumes were not part of the routine follow-up. As such Restrictive Allograft Syndrome (RAS) could not be determined.

Statistics

First, cut-off values were generated for all measurements performed: Sacin, Scond, LCI, IL-8, FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC, FEF25–75. This was done using receiver operator curves (with BOS or death at 10 years as the gold standard) and considering the worst value for these variables within 6 months post LTx. For Sacin, Scond, LCI or Log IL-8 the worst value corresponded to its maximum value, while for FEV1, FEV1/FVC, FEF25–75 this corresponded to its minimum value over that period. For those variables generating a statistically significant ROC analysis, a cut-off was determined as the value that provided the greatest sensitivity and specificity. For AHR a dose response slope cut-off of > 0.25Δ%FEV1/mL was used.24 Using these cut-offs, Kaplan Meier survival curves were obtained as well as hazard ratios. Finally, we also adopted a mixed linear model in R (version 4.2.0; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) to test for differences in Sacin, Scond, LCI, IL-8, FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC, FEF25–75 between those who developed BOS/Death and those who did not over the study period. ROC and Spearman correlation analyses were performed using MedCalc (version 20.2, Mariakerke, Belgium).

Discussion

We have demonstrated that in a small cohort of LTx recipients with Scond > 0.029 L−1, Log IL-8 > 3.1 pg/mL, independently or in combination (Figure 1), measured within 6 months post-LTx had significant risk of BOS and or death within 10 years. These results are important because they confirm that the first 6 months are likely to be important in determining the development of BOS and overall survival.26 Moreover, we now have two independent measures, Scond and Log IL-8, which may be able to predict which patients may develop BOS early post-LTx once tested in a large cohort.

Conductive ventilation heterogeneity was highly predictive of BOS or death, however acinar ventilation heterogeneity, LCI, spirometric indices of small airways disease (such as the FEF25–75), and AHR were not. The construct of airway hyperresponsiveness in the setting of Lung Transplantation has been described many years ago.7, 8 The hypothesis at the time was that the inflammatory response may lead to airway hyperresponsiveness similar to the condition of Asthma irrespective that the lung being denervated following transplant. As mentioned in the introduction, even though the airway hyperresponsiveness is not associated with increases in mast cells, eosinophils or neutrophils, lung transplant recipients who demonstrate airways hyperresponsiveness are more likely to develop BOS. The mechanism is ultimately still not clear as for the reason for AHR not being associated with future BOS. One possible reason for AHR not being predictive of future BOS is the cutoff for the dose-response slope is based on a cut-off for pediatric asthma where it has been reported to provide a better assessment of early asthma risk and hyperreactivity.24 Currently there is no good data on appropriate cutoffs for dose response slopes in an adult transplant population. We also did a post hoc analysis looking for a correlation between hypertonic saline dose-response slope and D:R size mismatch. There was no correlation, suggesting another reason for AHR not being predictive for future BOS. Finally, timing of the AHR post lung transplant being a 3months post-transplant as opposed to a later time may have been a contributing factor. A similar observation holds for acinar ventilation heterogeneity, which has been previously reported to be related to BOS status in a cross-sectional setting, and over a wider range of BOS severity than encountered here.13 However, the present study shows that Sacin is not a predictor of BOS, nor is the more global index of ventilation heterogeneity, LCI. Taken together, the conducting airways and the acinus are likely to play separate roles in either predicting BOS or following its progression once BOS is established.

Despite the fact that histopathological data was collected on these patients as part of routine surveillance bronchoscopy, histopathology has been considered to be unreliable for the detection of obliterative bronchiolitis, the theoretical pathological correlate of BOS.27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34 This is mainly attributed to the small tissue samples infrequently containing bronchioles and terminal bronchioles where BOS occurs, the patchy distribution of the lesions and finally the poor specificity in determining that the lesion was not already present from the donor.14, 28, 29, 31, 32, 33, 34 It is for this reason that the International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation have used the spirometric definition of BOS as a more practical surrogate of LTx chronic rejection. We have now identified a potential functional correlate of airway obstruction in patients with BOS, by observing increased conductive ventilation heterogeneity early post LTx in patients who subsequently developed BOS and or died within a 10 year period. The measurement of Scond is an addition to our previous study13 which demonstrated that Sacin was able to monitor disease severity and or progression. Based on the previous mathematical modeling we know that Sacin is generated around the terminal bronchioles.22 In particular, structural changes in terminal or pre-terminal bronchioles will affect acinar gas mixing as has been recently shown for COPD lungs with obliterated terminal bronchioles.35 Such peripheral lung zones are difficult to probe histopathologically and can be identified by MBNW testing.

We can only speculate the reason of the increased conductive ventilation heterogeneity as a predictor of BOS as the study was not designed to determine the mechanism for any observed changes. Reporting micro CT scans performed on explanted lungs in 6 patients undergoing re-transplantation, Verleden et al.14 found no differences in the number of terminal airways in patients with BOS compared to control lungs, and that there were obliterating lesions in the airways immediately proximal to the terminal bronchioles, i.e., pre-terminal bronchioles. One could speculate that early injury for whatever reason (ischemia-reperfusion injury, immunosuppression, infection) leading to narrowing of airways proximal to the acinar entrance will also lead to an increase in Scond. An alternative explanation could be that Scond, which at least partly represents ventilation heterogeneity arising from lobar expansion dynamics,36 increases following LTx because of altered lobar expansion characteristics linked to different chest shape and size between the donor and the recipient.

To our knowledge, the current study is the first to monitor ventilation heterogeneity and IL-8 in LTx recipients in the first-year post LTx, in an attempt to predict the patients’ clinical evolution over the subsequent 10 years. Other studies have been able to predict acute rejection episodes with indices of ventilation heterogeneity, however they were not designed to predict long term BOS or death.37 Reports on IL-8 measurement in the follow-up of LTx patients are sparse. In a cross sectional study, Zheng et al15 found that IL-8 was higher in patients with current BOS compared to those without BOS. Bourdin et al.38 obtained similar values to those shown in Figure 2b for logIL-8 in LTx recipients, followed up over a 2-year period only. The present work complements these observations by the fact that a higher IL-8 at 6 and 12 months post-surgery is predictive of patients that go on to develop BOS. Taken together, IL-8 and non-invasive biomarkers Scond and Sacin have the potential to be highly useful in patients who are at risk of BOS or death. Patients who have an abnormal finding on MBW within 6 months post LTx potentially need more frequent monitoring and or intervention.

There are limitations to this study mainly centering around sample size. Although the cutoff values were determined by a statistically significant ROC and subsequent statistically significant Kaplan Meir curves, further multicenter studies with larger numbers are required to determine if these indices are predictive. It should also be noted that the cutoffs were determined by the cohort in this study and may change with a large sample and therefore indicative only. Further the survival analysis is unadjusted and it is acknowledged that many confounders such as rejection episodes, treatment regimes can alter the clinical course. Another statistical approach was to use an extended Cox model that treats MBNW and IL-8 as time dependent covariates allows for changing trajectories within a patient. Such a time-varying survival model is a statistically valid approach for longitudinal data, however it is dependent on the data being collected throughout the study period. In the current study, pulmonary function measurements were only taken in the first year, whilst follow-up was over a ten year period. We note that the Kaplan-Meier and hazard ratio approach is time sensitive. That being said, the data presented in this study demonstrate that indices of Scond and IL-8 are likely to be important in determining future allograft dysfunction following transplantation and require further investigation. Another limitation is that over the time that the lung transplant recipients were followed up, our understanding and diagnostic criteria for allograft dysfunction has evolved. The criteria at the time was based solely on the FEV1 to describe Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome which has now evolved to sit under the umbrella term of CLAD. Furthermore, there is now Restrictive Allograft Syndrome (RAS) determined by the FEV1 and Total Lung Capacity1 and more recently Baseline Lung Allograft Dysfunction (BLAD). At the time of recruitment, static lung volumes were not measured routinely. Therefore, it is possible that some of those patients that were labeled BOS, based on the criteria at the time, may indeed have RAS. Irrespective of the subtype of CLAD these results are still important as a potential marker of future allograft dysfunction. Finally, the MBNW although is not routine in pulmonary function laboratories, it is very well described in both adults and pediatrics, technical standards, commercially equipment and reference values, being available for some time now.20, 21, 39, 40 The test takes longer than simple spirometry, however based on the data presented in this study it is likely to be beneficial.

In this small exploratory cohort, increased Scond or IL-8 within 6 months post-LTx was associated with an increased risk of death or allograft dysfunction where BOS is classified according to the 2001 criteria. With static lung volumes not routinely available during the study period, RAS and other contemporarily-defined CLAD phenotypes could not be defined, so the allograft dysfunction referred to will not be equivalent to the current definition of the BOS phenotype.

Actin-specific smooth muscle antibodies bridge adult and juvenile autoimmune hepatitis diagnosis
Authors: Alessandro Granito, Paolo Muratori, Georgios Pappas, Albert J Czaja
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

The multicentre study by Kirchner et al. provides important insights into the serological evaluation of juvenile autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) and autoimmune sclerosing cholangitis (AISC).1

By comparing indirect immunofluorescence (IFT) on rodent tissue and HEp-2 cells with ELISA platforms for antinuclear antibodies (ANA), filamentous actin (F-actin), and polyreactive IgG (pIgG), the authors confirm that smooth muscle antibodies (SMA) retain diagnostic value in children and that the vessels-glomeruli-tubules (VGT) staining pattern on rodent kidney is highly specific for AIH and AISC. Operator-dependence and the lack of inter-laboratory standardization remain the major clinical limitations of IFT.[2], [3], [4]

Several aspects of this study warrant emphasis and expansion. In their paediatric cohort, the SMA VGT pattern by IFT on rodent tissue yielded 100% specificity across all titres, albeit at very low sensitivity.1 The ELISA-based F-actin detection afforded an AUC of 0.868 for AIH/AISC, with high specificity at higher cut-offs.1 In our experience, the performance of IFT in diagnosing AIH in adults has been enhanced by using embryonal aorta vascular smooth muscle (VSM) in the assay rather than classical rodent kidney.5 IFT on VSM tissue has been specifically designed to visualize actin microfilament–specific SMA (MF-SMA) as a pattern of parallel cytoplasmic “stress fibers”.5 The parallel stress-fibers are visually distinctive and less influenced by tissue orientation and section quality. These features may facilitate operator training and eventually lead to an automated assay.

In 138 adults with AIH-1 and 295 controls, MF-SMA on VSM cells were detected in 70% of AIH-1 patients and 0.7% of controls. The sensitivity for AIH-1 was 70% and the specificity was 99%. Classical SMA-VGT on rodent kidney in the same cohort had 63% sensitivity, 99% specificity.5 Notably, 9 (7%) adults with AIH-1 who were SMA-VGT-negative on rodent tissue, were MF-SMA-positive on VSM cells, and overall agreement between SMA-VGT and MF-SMA was 87% (k = 0.87).5

The ability to diagnose AIH with an IFT assay that detects AIH-specific MF-SMA in an easily recognizable pattern of reactivity would be a diagnostic upgrade worthy of rigorous evaluation in diverse age groups.

The paediatric experience of Kirchner et al. confirms that SMA-VGT is the most specific SMA pattern for AIH/AISC, and our adult series demonstrates that the same biological reactivity for actin microfilament-specific SMA can be detected on a VSM cell line with a simple and stereotypical pattern.1,5

Kirchner et al.’ study also illustrates that the agreement between ANA and SMA findings detected by different platforms is only modest.1 The disparities probably reflect the heterogeneity of substrates, variations in assay design, and the need for refined and standardized methodology.[2], [3], [4] ELISA-based strategies alone have not resolved the long-standing tension between the sensitivity and specificity of different diagnostic assays.1

The F-actin ELISA has achieved an excellent AUC, but lower cut-offs that have favored sensitivity have reduced specificity, whereas higher cut-offs preserved specificity at the expense of missed cases.1 Similar limitations affected ANA ELISAs, despite their convenience and the inclusion of nuclear extracts.1,3,6,7

By moving entirely to antigen-specific ELISAs, practicality and throughput have been improved, but the combined sensitivity and specificity of well-performed IFT have not been fully reproduced.3,[5], [6], [7] IFT has been the time-honored preference for antibody detection in AIH, and standardization and automation may strengthen this role.[2], [3], [4] In the study by Kirchner et al., a custom-made ELISA for pIgG showed the highest accuracy for predicting juvenile AIH/AISC compared with commercial ELISAs for ANA and F-actin (81.2% vs. 79.7% for F-actin). In addition, pIgG was detected in 52.4% of children with AIH who were F-actin negative.1 Its high occurrence in adults and children with AIH, its ability to distinguish untreated AIH from non-autoimmune liver disease, and its improvement during immunosuppressive treatment of AIH, suggest important roles in diagnosis, prognosis, and therapy.1,8,9 Further studies are needed to define these roles and the place of plgG within the hierarchy of conventional autoantibodies in AIH.

In conclusion, the study by Kirchner et al. advances our understanding of juvenile AIH/AISC serology and confirms the central role of SMA, particularly when interpreted in light of specific staining patterns and complementary assays. We propose that future juvenile and adult investigations incorporate the VSM-cell MF-SMA assay alongside the existing methodologies to determine whether it can strengthen the diagnostic contribution of actin-specific SMA and improve inter-laboratory consistency. Investigations that clarify the diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic implications of plgG in AIH could further refine current serological algorithms.

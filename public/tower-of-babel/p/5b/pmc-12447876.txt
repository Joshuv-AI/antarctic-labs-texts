A case report on Ayurvedic management of progressive bulbar palsy-A rare amyotrophic lateral sclerosis phenotype
Authors: Sinimol Thekkekkoottumughath Peethambaran, Abhayadev Ashokan, Varanasi Subhose
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis case report is the description of a devastating illness, Progressive Bulbar Palsy (PBP) of a sixty-seven years old male patient. He presented with complaints of slurred speech, hearing impairment, generalised weakness of limbs, weakened grip to hold objects in hand, difficulty to walk with normal speed, frequent dizzy feeling while walking, severe fatigue, increased anger, heaviness of head, depression, anxiety, decreased memory and headache for 1 year. When he consulted conventional medicine, in Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of brain, only ‘Partial empty sella’ and age related mild cerebral atrophy was detected and the patient was diagnosed PBP clinically. They prescribed Riluzole 50 mg tablet twice a day and Fluoxetine 10mg capsules at night time for 3 months, but obtained no relief for symptoms and consulted this Out Patient Department (OPD). In Ayurvedic parlance, PBP resembles conditions like Kaphavruta vata. In this patient, Pittavritavata symptoms like bhrama (∼dizziness) was also present in increased severity. Diagnosis was done with the aid of Gold Coast diagnostic criteria. Internal and external medications with properties alleviating avarana (∼occlusion) of vata by kapha and pitta, shodhana (∼expelling the aggravated doshas and cleanses the body internally), rejuvenating (Rasayana) properties, for overall strengthening of nervous system and musculoskeletal system, enhancing balance and coordination, improving speech and memory were used. The assessment was done before and after the treatment by ‘Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Functional Rating Scale-Revised (ALSFRS-R). The score before and after the treatment was 35 and 45 respectively out of 48. The treatment helped to increase the quality of life exceptionally as symptomatic relief was obtained. As it is a devastating disorder with poor prognosis and most probably will lead to death, it is advisable to repeat the treatments in regular intervals, depending on the recurrence of symptoms, if any.Keywords: Progressive bulbar palsy, Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Ayurveda, Bulbar onset motor neuron disease, Case report

Introduction

Degeneration of motor neurons in the brain and spinal cord is the hallmark of the rapidly progressing disease known as Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) or Motor Neuron Disease (MND). This could cause somatic muscle dysfunction, and in the end, it might even damage the respiratory system and cause death. An ALS patient has a median survival time of 3–5 years and is always fatal. Progressive Bulbar Palsy (PBP) is a very rare Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis phenotype with bulbar onset and development of progressive limb symptoms and signs in short term. It usually indicates a worse prognosis with a shorter survival period than other Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis phenotypes [1]. According to estimates, the prevalence is 4.5 per 100,000 people worldwide. In the year 2016, 34,325 people were thought to have died from motor neuron disease. Pathophysiology of the condition is still not completely understood, but genetic mutations within superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1) and C9orf72 is deemed to be linked with its occurrence [2]. The disease is essentially a clinical diagnosis, substantiated by neuro-physiological testing. Although there are evidence-based recommendations for standards of care, Progressive Bulbar Palsy is considered as an incurable disease. The only two Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved medicines for PBP are dextromethorphan/quinidine, which relieves symptoms of pseudobulbar affect (uncontrollable sobbing or laughing), and riluzole, which extends life by roughly three months [3]. Although there is not much fruitful progress in research on pharmacologic intervention, there are a lot of recent advances in the use of ‘Assisting Technologies’ in the management of PBP. Emerging technology for managing the patients include robotics and interdisciplinary teleconsults, ‘Augmentative and Alternative Communication’ with the aid of smart phones, tablet devices etc. Also, customised braces, mobility aids, modified utensils can be made utilizing 3D printing technology. Nonetheless, great strides have been made in identifying new pathomechanisms and genetic variables that underlie this illness, which raises hopes for the development of gene therapy and other mechanistic therapeutic methods [4].

According to Ayurveda, though exact correlation of Progressive Bulbar Palsy is not possible, it can be correlated to conditions like Kaphavrutaudanavata [5] and Kaphavrutavyanavata [6], Pittavruta Prana vata, Pittavruta Udana vata and Pittavrita Vyana vata. (Table 1). Therefore, Ayurvedic intervention was planned to use internal and external treatment modalities required for the management of Kaphavrutavata [7] and Pittavrutavata. These include usage of jangala mamsa (∼meat of animals from arid regions), Swedana (∼sudation), teekshna Niruhabasti (∼medicated enema), Virechana (∼purgation), and ghritapana (∼intake of medicated ghee). Also, in case of Kaphapitta avarana of Vata, Pitta should be addressed first.

Case history

Patient information:

A 67-year-old male patient visited Outpatient Department of this hospital on April 03, 2024 with complaints of slurred speech, hearing impairment, generalised weakness of limbs, weakened grip to hold objects in hand, difficulty to walk with normal speed, frequent dizzy feeling while walking, severe fatigue, increased anger, heaviness of head, depression, anxiety, decreased memory and headache for 1 year. The symptoms started 1 year before as hearing deficit in both ears. Slurring of speech, weakness of the body and other associated symptoms also developed gradually and progressed in severity in due course of time. He had no addictions, other significant comorbidities or family history.

History of presenting complaints

Past intervention with outcomes: In the beginning of hearing deficit itself, the patient consulted hospital with conventional treatment and underwent only cleaning of ear wax, as reported by him. As hearing impairment persisted and symptoms like slurring of speech began, he consulted another conventional hospital. Magnetic Resonance Imaging Brain and Ultrasonography (USG) of Thyroid and neck were done there. In Ultrasonography, Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TIRADS) 1 was diagnosed in left lobe and Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System 2 was diagnosed in right lobe of Thyroid gland. Surgery was done on Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System 2 nodule on the right lobe, as it may also produce difficulty in speech. But there was no improvement in speech even after surgery and was diagnosed Progressive Bulbar Palsy clinically. They prescribed Riluzole 50 mg tablet twice a day and Fluoxetine 10mg capsules at night time for 3 months. As no relief for symptoms was obtained, he consulted this Outpatient Department.

(April 05, 2024): Regarding Haematologic investigations, Blood Routine Examination, C-Reactive Protein (CRP), Fasting Blood Sugar, Post Prandial Blood Sugar, investigations were done. Diagnosis was made as Progressive Bulbar Palsy by history taking and clinical examination with the aid of Gold Coast diagnostic criteria, because the criterias (1) progressive motor impairment documented by history or repeated clinical assessment, preceded by normal motor function; (2) presence of Upper motor Neuron and Lower Motor Neuron signs in at least 1 body region or Lower Motor Neuron dysfunction in at least 2 body regions; and (3) investigations excluding other disease processes were satisfied. Upper motor Neuron signs exhibited by the patient included slurred speech, hearing deficit, headache. Lower Motor Neuron signs included wasting of tongue muscles, wasting of muscles of shoulder girdle, weakness of body. Difficulty in swallowing food may be due to Upper motor Neuron/Lower motor Neuron lesion [8].

Therapeutic intervention

Internal medications; Dhanadanayanadi kwatha, Tablet Yogarajaguggulu, Danwantara arishta, Tablet Brihatvatachintamani ras, Mahakalyanaka ghrita, were given for overall strengthening of nervous system and musculoskeletal system, enhancing balance and coordination, improving speech and memory. Udwartana (∼dry powder massage) with Kolakulathadi choorna was done in the beginning for rookshana (∼drying out the body) to remove avarana (∼blockage) of Vata caused by Kapha and Pitta dosha.

Different procedures like Abhyanga (∼ oil massage) of whole body including head with Ksheerabala taila, Brimhana Nasya (∼nasal instilling of nourishing medicated oil) with Ksheerabala taila −101, Shiro pichu (∼keeping a sterile cotton pad dipped in medicated oil on the crown of the head for a prescribed period) with Rasa taila, Shashtika Pinda Sweda (∼sudation with medicated rice poultice) with Balashwagandhadi taila, Sirovasti (∼retention of medicated oil over head) with Danwantaram taila, Pizhichil (∼continuous pouring of warm medicated oil all over the body) with Kottamchukkadi taila, Mustadi Rajayapanavasti (medicated nourishing enema), Jihwa pratisarana (application of medicated paste over the tongue) with Kalyanakavaleha choorna, having vatahara (∼alleviating vata imbalance), Swarya (∼ normalising speech), Balya (∼strengthening), medhya (∼nootropic) and Brimhana (∼nourishing) properties were done. After Abhyanga, Virechana (∼Purgation) with Gandharva hasta Eranda taila was done once for internal cleaning of the body and is vatahara in action.

After discharge, Vidaryadi kwatha, Capsule Danwantaram 101, Ashwagandharishta, Mahasaraswatha ghrita, Capsule Sidhamakaradwaja, Abhyanga of whole-body including head with Mahamashadi taila were prescribed for sustaining the symptomatic relief of the patient. (Table 5).

Discussion

This case report is the description of Progressive Bulbar Palsy of a sixty-seven years old male patient. Internal and external medications with properties alleviating avarana (∼occlusion) of vata by kapha and pitta, shodhana (∼expelling the aggravated doshas and cleanses the body internally), rejuvenating (Rasayana) properties, for overall strengthening of nervous system and musculoskeletal system, enhancing balance and coordination, improving speech and memory were used. Jihwa pratisarana (application of medicated paste over the tongue) with Kalyanavaleha choorna was done as it is indicated for various spectrum of speech disorders [10]. Udwartana (∼dry powder massage) was done with with Kolakulathadi choorna for 3 days which removes the avarana of vata dosha by Kapha and pitta, which is the primary cause of the disease [11]. Stability of the body is also achieved by Udwartana [12]. Abhyanga (∼ oil massage) of whole-body including head with Ksheerabala taila was done, as it is a very effective drug in treating various spectrum of neurological disorders [13].

Brimhana Nasya (∼nasal instilling of nourishing medicated oil) with Ksheerabala taila −101 was done because Brimhana Nasya is indicated in vaksanga (∼speech problems), and it was one of the primary presenting complaints of the patient [14]. Morover, Nasya karma is substantially recommended for conditions affecting the head and neck. Administering nourishing oil via the nostril possibly enhances brain activity [15]. Virechana (∼Purgation) was done with Gandharvahasta eranda taila, which helps in alleviating the aggravated vata dosha and cleanses the body internally. Shiro pichu (∼keeping a sterile cotton pad dipped in medicated oil on the crown of the head for a prescribed period) with Rasa taila, a medicine indicated in the management of disorders of nerves, was done for 7 days [16]. Shashtika pinda sweda with Balashwagandhadi taila was done for nourishing and rejuvenating nerves, muscles and improving blood circulation [17]. Research on the benefits of Shashtikashali pinda sweda for a variety of neurological diseases point to improvements in motor deficiencies and day-to-day functioning as well as strengthening effects on peripheral nerves and muscles [11]. Shirovasti was done with Danwantaram taila, a medicine which is indicated in curing all types of vatarogas [18]. Shirovasti is the best among 4 types of Murdha taila (∼various procedures done on head using medicated oil) and is beneficial in various diseases of the head. After Shirovasti procedure, the patient reported great relief from frequent dizzy feeling during walking that he was experiencing due to the disease condition.

Pizhichil (∼continuous pouring of warm medicated oil all over the body with sterile clothes) with Kottamchukkadi taila, which is indicated in vatarogas was done for 7 days. The procedure is found very beneficial for severe neurological diseases [19]. Applying localized heat increases parasympathetic activity, hemodynamics, and muscular excitability. Additional therapeutic massage enhances balance and motor control of the body. Mustadi Rajayapana vasti with Guggulutiktakam ghrita helps in mitigating aggravated vata dosha (vasti, is decribed as best treatment of vata) and cleanses the internal micro channels of our body. Apart from that, Rajayapanavasti is nourishing to body also. Research on vasti indicates improved quality of life, increased vagal activity, and modifications to the gut microbiota post treatment [14].

Internal medicines, Dhanadanayanadi kwatha [20] is vatakaphahara, Tablet Yogarajaguggulu is vatahara, agnideepthikara (improves digestion and metabolism), balakara (improves strength of body) [21], Danwantara arishta, Tablet Brihatvatachintamani ras, Mahakalyanaka ghrita were given for overall strengthening of nervous system and musculoskeletal system, enhancing balance and coordination, improving speech and memory [2]. Dhanwantararishta is also indicated in all vata rogas [22]. Brihat Vata Chintamani Rasa is having the properties of medhya, Rasayana, lekhana, balya, ojovardhaka because of its ingredients like Swarna Bhasma (∼incinerated gold), Rajata Bhasma (∼incinerated silver), Loha Bhasma (∼incinerated iron). The formulation also arrests neurodegenerative activity with the added benefit of crossing the bloodbrain barrier [23,24]. Mahakalyanaka ghrita is having various properties like Medhya, balya, ayushya and is indicated in various spectrum of disease conditions that this patient was suffering including difficulty to speech, psychiatric conditions, impaired memory [25].

After discharge, Vidaryadi kwatha [26], Capsule Danwantaram 101 [18], Ashwagandharishta [27], Mahasaraswatha ghrita [28], Capsule Sidhamakaradwaja [29], Abhyanga of whole-body including head with Mahamashadi taila [30] were prescribed for two weeks for maintaining the symptomatic relief of the patient. The internal medications except Mahasaraswatha ghrita have excellent vatahara, balakara, rasayana properties which helps in improving neuromuscular co-ordination, strengthening and rejuvenation of the nervous system, musculoskeletal system, enhancing balance and coordination. Mahasaraswatha ghrita, is having vakswarakrit, smriti krit, medhakrit actions which helps in improving speech, memory and intelligence. Abhyanga with Mahamasha taila is also found to be very effective in management of neurological conditions. So, these medicines were prescribed after discharging the patient as follow up medicines, to prevent further pathogenesis of the disease.

One of the major strengths of this case reporting is that the signs and symptoms of the patient, were almost completely resolved and did not recur in two weeks follow up period. No case reports have been published so far in indexed journals on the Ayurvedic management of this phenotype of Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis. It is possible to apply the modalities used in this case to other analogous cases. The patient's compliance with the intervention and level of tolerance played role in the effective management of the condition. The major limitation is that, as it is a devastating disorder with poor prognosis and most probably will lead to death, it is advisable to repeat the treatments in regular intervals, depending on the recurrence of symptoms, if any. Regular follow up is essential and the patient was advised to report recurrence of symptoms if any.

Conclusion

The presented combination of internal and external Ayurvedic medications was found to be a good alternative therapy in providing relief to the symptoms and signs of the patient suffering from Progressive Bulbar Palsy. As improving the quality of life is very much crucial to Progressive Bulbar Palsy patients, even providing symptomatic relief has got great significance. Further Systematic trials should be conducted to explore the possibilities of Ayurveda in the management of Progressive Bulbar Palsy.

No adverse events were reported.

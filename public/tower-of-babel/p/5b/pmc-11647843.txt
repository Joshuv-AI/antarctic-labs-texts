Background

Introduction

Life extension of load-bearing structures, prediction of their residual life and performance under cyclic loading require investigation and generalisation, followed by data gathering on cyclic fracture toughness, kinetics, and micromechanisms of material fracture [[1], [2], [3]].

Methods of fractographic analysis play an essential role in this regard, since they provide for a physical and mechanical interpretation of the cyclic fracture toughness parameters determined using the kinetic diagram of fatigue failure [4,5]. The quantitative fractographic analysis of various zones of fatigue fracture aims at establishing the relationship between the material microstructure, its strength and ability to resist crack propagation. Along with that, fracture collects information that highlights micromechanisms of material fracture at various scale levels [[6], [7], [8]]. Fatigue fracture, in turn, reflects micromechanisms of fracture inherent in the local volume of the material and aids in determining the causes which lead to failure of the object of interest.

Even though fracture analysis has made significant strides in various types of research being guided by several standards [9,10], the existing approaches only establish fracture types and determine the specific proportion of typical fracture micromechanisms that occur within the area analysed. The quantitative analysis of fracture morphology, which considers complex micromechanisms of its formation, requires further research. At present, fractographic analysis of fatigue fracture is based on measuring the parameters of fatigue striations and evaluating the parameters of dimple relief [[11], [12], [13]]. In case of the elastic-plastic fatigue crack growth (FCG), the relationship between the step of fatigue striations and the length of fatigue cracks is linear, which indicates the equality of the micro- and macro rates of FCG. This creates additional opportunities for predicting the residual life.

Macro- rate describes an increase in the crack length over the loading cycle measured optically (or by other methods) on the specimen surface as a structurally homogeneous body [14]. Micro- rate is the crack growth measured in increments of fatigue striations also per loading cycle [14]. The mechanisms, by which dimples are formed closer to the zone of the elastic-plastic fatigue crack growth (FCG), are similar to the quasi-static ones. Therefore, they were not scrutinised in this research. The paper in [15] describes the methods for calculating and analysing their parameters.

Fractographic control of fatigue fracture surfaces of test specimens and templates cut out of fractured structures must be reliable. To this end, standard approaches to measuring the step of fatigue striations should be used along with the statistical methods for processing results. Fourier analysis, stereocouples, etc. [[16], [17], [18], [19]] are the methods that determine the parameters of fatigue fracture. However, they are not used systematically, and the scope of their application is limited. Therefore, the parameters describing the surface analysed are not always statistically relevant. Machine training methods, which extract the quantitative data from the images obtained by scanning electron microscopy, rank first among the methods for analysing fatigue fracture surfaces [20].

This review aims at streamlining the available methods for investigating the FCG mechanisms and conducting the quantitative analysis of fatigue striations using the automated and intellectualised techniques.

Method details

Fatigue fracture of solids (macro- level)

Fatigue fracture of solids is traditionally studied at the macro- and micro- levels. The former considers the solid body as a continuous medium, while the latter considers microstructural aspects. However, an intermediate meso‑ level, which “connects” fracture processes at the micro- and macro- levels, is also distinguished [21]. Once included into the cyclic deformation and fracture analysis, mesolevel made it possible to consider the FCG processes at three scale levels: the micro-, meso‑ and marco- levels [22], (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Fatigue crack growth at the micro- (a), meso‑ (b) and macro- (c) levels [22].Fig 1

The scientific school led by V. E. Panin is famous for its papers that consider multiscale processes of deformation and fracture of polycrystalline bodies. On their basis, the concept that describes the structural levels of deformation of a solid body was developed [23,24]. This concept characterises the hierarchy of structural levels of plastic deformation and fracture and defines their scale [[23], [24], [25]]. FCG may cause tears-off in different planes and lead to the formation of secondary microcracks. That is why meso‑ relief of the fracture surface stands between the macro- and micro- levels. Relationship between macro- and micro- characteristics of polycrystals from the perspective of fractographic control

Two energy-intensive processes occur at the crack tip during FCG: plastic strain localises in a small area and fractures occur. Together, they lead to the formation of new surfaces upon crack growth [[65], [66]]. New surfaces usually contain fatigue striations oriented perpendicular to the FCG direction.

FCG occurs in two ways [67]- fatigue striations appear cyclically;- fatigue striations result from several load cycles that cause the suspension and subsequent embrittlement of the material near the crack tip; the material accumulates critical damage, and a crack grows. Methods of fractographic control based on the 3D profile analysis

To calculate the above quantitative indicators using a fractogram, we recognise the position of boundaries between fatigue striations in the image. This is a complex and non-trivial task, since the separation surface has a complex morphology and can often be interpreted somewhat differently. For example, fatigue striations are often located in different planes and have a torn front [80,81]. A significant stochastic component in their spatial orientation and visual appearance requires complex image processing algorithms and modern computing tools to be used. The concept of a step of striations has a certain subjective component, which makes things more complicated. Therefore, experts may interpret the same area in the fractogram differently. We consider state-of-the-art methods for analysing the parameters of fatigue striations.

It can be argued that when evaluating the micro-rate of FCG, the most informative feature is the step of striations (2D), since it characterises the material's damage accumulation rate. Conversely, the nature of deformation processes during crack propagation can only be estimated by analysing the striation's profile, since striations of the same width usually differ in height [82]. Such methods are relevant for the comparative analysis of conventional and new materials, or for evaluating the intensity of metal degradation in long-operated structures. Such measurements are hard to automate for steel specimens, since the crack front is not always uniform. It has local distortions and crack blunting areas, which cause the formation of “bumps” and “depressions” [[83], [84], [85], [86], [87], [88], [89]]. Successful attempts have been made to analyse the “h/s - R” ratio and mathematically describe the relationship between these parameters. Compared to s, the h/s parameter was much more sensitive to changes in the load cycle asymmetry. This suggests that changes in this ratio account for the ambiguous correlation between da/dN and s.

There are also methods of quantitative stereoscopic fractography that allow us to estimate the geometric features of the fracture surface in 3-D space. Slope variations of individual sections of the fracture surface describe the FCG stages. They were determined using the above method and have step-like terraces of different heights. They help adapt the working sliding system's orientation to the specimen's loading conditions upon propagation of a fatigue crack.

The developing shear processes force the grains to grind into blocks. Tangential and normal cyclic tensile stresses cause the localisation and accumulation of strains in individual grain blocks. Automated methods for recognising fatigue striations in two-dimensional images allow us to determine their geometric characteristics (number, size, area, equivalent diameter, slope, etc.). This provides for a high accuracy and reproducibility of measurements, large-scale digital calculations of parameters of morphological objects, and statistical analysis of large data sets. Using the automated calculations of parameters to investigate fractographic images appears promising [99,100]. This approach considers fatigue striations’ stochasticity, variability, and common morphological features.

The analysis of the methods presented in Table 3 shows that they differ significantly in their potential to conduct the quantitative analysis of the surface morphology formed during the propagation of a fatigue crack. Apart from classifying fracture surfaces, the automated methods determine their parameters. Analysing data sets will provide an improved description of fracture mechanisms. As a result, investigations into the in-service fractures will become more accurate, and grounds will be created for monitoring the cyclic life of long-operated parts and structures [101,102].

Some publications are focused on machine training and computer vision as applied to the automated fractographic analysis. This will significantly cut the time and raise the efficiency of surface morphology's fracture analysis, providing improved accuracy and productivity. At the same time, these approaches involve investigations into fatigue fracture upon nucleation of a fatigue crack. Since this review considers the crack propagation stage, the processes that occur upon crack nucleation were not scrutinised.

The review is mainly concerned with functional features of fatigue crack growth analysis methods and fracture surface morphology analysis at different scale levels. It is noticeable that both the hardware part of the equipment [[103], [104], [105], [106]], methods of managing the process of obtaining images of the fracture surface [107,108] and software for the analysis of fractographic images [109] are being improved. This is due to the necessary to increase the accuracy of measurements, which is controlled in accordance with the current standard [110], which provides methodological principles for the calibration of devices in order to ensure physical correctness and metrological control.

Conclusion

1.The review of the current investigations into the mechanisms of fatigue crack propagation in polycrystalline materials and findings of the quantitative analysis of fatigue striations helped us-identify the methodological features of fractographic description of the FCG mechanisms that occur at the macro-, meso‑ and micro- levels; their features and parameters were characterised, the mechanisms of their formation were described, and methodological requirements for measurements were formulated;-make the manual and automated descriptions of fractures and morphological structures based on the visual comparison of the fracture surface's elements; this provided opportunities for the quantitative description and evaluation of the fracture surface;-describe the methods for determining the geometric characteristics of the FCG surface; these methods provide for the structural and linguistic description of the fracture morphology of the FCG surface; they make the basis for the unified assessment of fracture surfaces using standard parameters;2.Algorithms that recognise and calculate the parameters of fatigue striations were considered; their functionality was compared to ensure the reliability of fractographic control-based on the computerised procedures, the features of geometric parameters measured at the micro- level were revealed; these geometric parameters are structurally sensitive because they characterise the size of structural elements (grains of material);-methods for obtaining statistically significant data sets related to the geometry of fatigue striations were streamlined, their physical and mechanical interpretation was provided, and the relationship between them was established.

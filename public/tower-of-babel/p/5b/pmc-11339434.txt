Thermal slip and variable viscosity analysis on heat rate and magnetic flux through accelerating non-conducting wedge in the presence of induced magnetic field
Authors: Zia Ullah, Hammad Alotaibi, Asfa Usman, Ilyas Khan, Abdoalrahman S A Omer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe focus of present study is to incorporate the variable viscosity and temperature slip impact on heating rate and induced magnetic gradient along the moving non-conducting wedge under magnetic field. In industrial and engineering procedures, the impact of induced magnetization improves the efficiency of thermal systems to main the heating rates. The similarity transformations and stream functions are applied to reduce the governing equations into ordinary form. During this transformation, the pertinent parameters such as wedge parameter, moving parameter, Prandtl factor, viscosity parameter and temperature-slip parameter is obtained. These parameters play a prominent role on the physical values of fluid velocity, induced magnetic field and temperature distributions. The skin friction, Nusselt coefficient and induced magnetic gradient are incorporated through these parameters. The numerical values are executed by using the Keller box analysis with Newton–Raphson technique. It is depicted that the maximum slip in fluid velocity and temperature distribution is obtained for each values of thermal-slip parameter. It is noticed that maximum magnitude in induced magnetic field is reported for each wedge factor. The maximum velocity slip and temperature slip is observed for each choice of moving parameter. It is reported that the maximum variation in heating rate and induced magnetic gradient is obtained for magnetic force and viscosity parameter. The enhancing behavior of skin friction is observed for maximum values of Prandtl number.Keywords: Variable viscosity, Temperature slip, Induce magnetic field, Nusselt value, Magnetic gradient, Moving wedgeSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

The variation temperature slip and variable viscosity on heating rate and induced magnetic gradient of MHD fluid through non-conducting wedge surface under strong magnetic field outside the boundary layers is the theme of present study. The magnetic field is applied outside the moving wedge to incorporate the thermal management. The magneto-hydrodynamic is the study of the movement of a liquid that conducts electricity in the context of a magnetized field. It has significance for many sectors of mechanical engineering, including geography, astronomy, and metallurgy. MHD was successfully utilized for difficulties regarding ionized concentration by magnetic forces, as well as applications concerning explosive energy production. Recently, it has become increasingly common in metallurgical sectors that use wedge-like resources, such as paper, polymeric papers, and cable sketching, as well as curved continuous machining of cylindrical billets of metal. Magnetic impact on water movement and transfer of heat aspects through vertical shapes have numerous practical applications in industry, mechanical engineering, and cutting-edge science, such as radioactive and computerized procedures, extreme-temperature ions, chilling of electrical equipment and nuclear-powered reactors, cardiac valves, aerodynamics, and metallurgical procedures. The heat transfer mechanisms have increasing uses in food product production, photovoltaic panels, heat exchanges, sunspot theory, intercontinental ballistic missiles, interstellar gas motion, liquid metals, cooling of nuclear reactors, plasma confinement, and geophysics and astrophysics1–6. A computational study was performed on the hydro-magnetic stagnation-point movement of an electrically-conductive nanofluid across an expanding surface using an induced electromagnetic field, melted force, and temperature source7,8. The variation of Joule heating, multiple regressions, slip properties and viscous dissipation on heating rate and mass transmission was observed with significant outputs.

The boundary layer movement across a moving wedge of variable dimension has captured the interest of scientists due to its numerous applications in biological modeling, scientific, aeronautics and manufacturing. A simulation was performed to evaluate the effect of changing temperature conductivity and fractional slip of velocity on the hydro-magnetic flow separation of a nanofluid including hybrid nanomaterial across a stretched surface with thermal properties9,10. The adaptability of the two types of heat fluctuations, as well as the temperature of the surface, to soil temperature conductivity characterization was established through a soil plant atmospheric transport system. The concept of multifaceted continuous turbulent MHD flow separation across a wedge exhibiting mass transport and heating rate of a nanofluid immersed in transparent medium with viscoelastic dispersion, Brownian movement, and heating impact was investigated11,12. The flow management characteristics were discovered having a significant impact on the final stream rates. Qasim et al.13 studied the magneto-hydrodynamic movement of ferrofluid through an expanded cylinder. The coefficient of slip and specified wall thermal flux limits were applied to the cylindrical wall. The velocity and heat transmission properties of a hydro-magnetic movement of viscous fluid across an extensible surface with a non-uniform heating element were investigated14,15. A homotopy evaluation technique was utilized to create a measurement medium for the heating capacity of a linear fin with a trapezoidal contour.

The uniformly distributed hybrid nanofluid across a narrow parallel needle containing water, copper, and alumina oxide nanoparticles was dissipated in the occurrence of hydro-magnetic, heat conductance and slipping effects16,17. The movement of a power-law liquid across an inclined elastic sheet was addressed. The implications of varying temperature conductivity and irregular heat sources and sinks on transported heat were investigated18,19. The magnetization component diminished the Nusselt and Sherwood values. The analysis of the current findings with those obtained in the presence of nanoparticle volumetric fractions, hybrid transpiration, and electromagnetic field revealed an outstanding consistency20,21. In previous published database22–25, the comparison of skin friction was reported in feasible consistency for different values of wedge parameter. Heat and magnetic flux of MHD Williamson and Casson nanofluid over moving wedge was discussed in26–30. The computational outcomes of thermal transport and concentration distribution of Casson-Carreau hybrid nanofluid with reaction rate, solar energy and micro particles of gyro-tactic effects were computed in31–33.

The objective of present analysis is to report the variable viscosity and temperature slip impact on electrically conducting fluid movement through moving wedge under induced magnetization. For the improvement of thermal structures in industrial and engineering procedures, the induced magnetic impact provides good heating control mechanism. The impact of magnetic field along moving surface has valuable role in MHD generators, electronic structures, electrical generators, thermal power systems and nuclear plants. The numerical outcomes of fluid velocity, induced magnetic field, temperature distribution, skin friction, heating rate and induced magnetic gradient are displayed through finite difference and Newton Raphson scheme known as Keller box approach. The theme of this analysis is to report the induced magnetic gradient through non-conducting wedge by taking viscosity as a function of temperature. The novelty of present analysis is to illustrate the slip behavior in surface temperature along the moving wedge by taking water-liquid which is significant electrically conducting liquid.

Conclusion

The current research addressed the viscosity and thermal consequence of viscous fluid through magnetized accelerating wedge. The induced magnetic field is applied along the moving wedge to improve and maintain the heating rate. Coupled mathematical partial differential model was used to investigate the flow behavior of electrically conducting fluid with variable viscosity and temperature slip effects along accelerating wedge. To examine the analytical solution of direct partial differential model is complex. For this reason, many researchers were applied stream functions, computational methods and similarity transformations to convert governing equations into ordinary form for smooth programming algorithms in MATLAB. The more suitable and beneficial Keller box method is applied to explore the numerical and geometrical results of fluid velocity, magnetic velocity, temperature distribution, skin friction, magnetic flux and heating rates. Different pertinent parameters are involved to deduce the physical characteristics of heating rate and magnetic flux across accelerating magnetized wedge. Significant and important results are explained below.

Maximum enhancing moving velocity was deduced for increasing values of β, λ but the declining behavior of moving velocity was depicted for increasing values of γ and α.

Enhancing values of magnetic velocity was observed for maximum values of γ,λ and α but lower response was deduced for maximum value of β.

Enhancing values of magnetic velocity was observed for maximum values of γ,λ and α but lower response was deduced for maximum value of β.

Prominent thermal slip was found in temperature distributions for each value of γ,β,λ and α but increasing variation in temperature slip along magnetized wedge was observed for γ.

Prominent thermal slip was found in temperature distributions for each value of γ,β,λ and α but increasing variation in temperature slip along magnetized wedge was observed for γ.

Skin friction and magnetic flux was enhanced for maximum choice of magnetic force S but heating rate was decreased due to magnetic effects. It is expected because the temperature insulation increases as magnetic field enhances.

Future Work The present mechanism can be extend for nanofluid, Darcy Forchheimer nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid flow over moving non-conducting wedge surface with thermal conductivity, solar radiation, surface heat flux, variable density, Joule heating and porous medium effects for heat transportation enhancement.

Potential Limitations Temperature, pressure and shear rate affects the viscosity of fluid. High rate of viscosity can produce excessive heat generation, high energy consumption and churning losses from molecular friction. The potential limitations of parameters used as 0.1≤γ≤1.0, 0≤β≤1.5, 0.1≤λ≤10.0, 0.1≤α≤7.0, Pr=7.0, and 0.1≤ε≤5.0.

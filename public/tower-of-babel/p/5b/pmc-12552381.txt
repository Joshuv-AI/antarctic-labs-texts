Rare germline ETV6 variant associated with thrombocytopenia and acute leukemia
Authors: Akhil Rajendra Kurup, Janet Malcolmson, Anita Villani, Raymond H Kim, Karen WL Yee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractGermline mutations in ETV6 have been associated with thrombocytopenia and predisposition to hematological malignancies. Here, we report a pedigree with a multiple family member with an ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant, initially classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS), found to be segregating with thrombocytopenia and hematological/solid tumor malignancies. The proband, a 63-year-old male with chronic thrombocytopenia and a family history of hematological malignancies, presented with pancytopenia and was diagnosed as myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS). Next generation sequencing (NGS) from the bone marrow revealed the ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant with a variant allele frequency (VAF) of 46%. Germline testing on skin fibroblasts confirmed the presence of the same ETV6 variant in the proband and two of his siblings: one of them was diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) during childhood and therapy-related MDS during adulthood, and another sibling with chronic isolated thrombocytopenia. The ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant potentially affects the ETS DNA-binding domain, leading to impaired DNA binding with a preserved dimerization capability, resulting in a dominant negative effect by cytoplasmic sequestration. This ETV6 variant has not been reported in a large population database, hence it has been designated as a VUS. Segregation of this variant with thrombocytopenia and hematological/solid tumor malignancies in the pedigree, alongside supporting evidence from other reported ETV6 variants, suggests its pathogenicity. This report highlights the pathogenicity of ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant and supports its reclassification from VUS to likely pathogenic, adding to the vast evidence of ETV6-associated thrombocytopenia and leukemia predisposition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06536-2.Keywords: Germline, ETV6, Acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Myelodysplastic syndrome, Thrombocytopenia

Introduction

The ETV6 gene, located at chromosome 12p13.2, encodes the hematopoietic transcriptional repressor erythroblast transformation specific (ETS) transcription factor. Germline mutations in ETV6 have been associated with segregating thrombocytopenia and predisposition to hematological malignancies. Hematological malignancies observed include acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) [1–3], myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) [4], acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [4] and myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) [5]. Here, we report a pedigree with multiple family members with an ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant, initially classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS), found to be segregating with thrombocytopenia and hematological/solid tumor malignancies in the pedigree.

Case History

The proband (III-1) was a 63-year-old gentleman of European ancestry who presented to the Princess Margaret Cancer Centre in July 2023 for evaluation of persistent cytopenia over a period of one year (see Fig. 1). He had a preceding history of presumed chronic immune thrombocytopenia purpura (ITP) since the age of 9 years with a platelet count ranging from 50 to 90 × 109/L. He received steroids for the treatment of ITP in childhood. He did not receive any treatment for ITP during adulthood. In addition, he had celiac disease and dermatitis herpetiformis for which he was treated with dapsone, which was stopped at the onset of pancytopenia. At the time of presentation in July 2023 his hemoglobin was 107 g/L, white blood cell (WBC) count of 1.6 × 109/L, absolute neutrophil count (ANC) of 0.6 × 109/L and platelet count of 57 × 109/L. Bone marrow assessment revealed 50–60% cellularity with dysplasia noted in > 10% cells in the erythrocyte, granulocyte and megakaryocyte lineage with less than 5% blasts. Karyotyping revealed three clonal abnormalities 47,XY,+1,del(20)(q11.2q13.1) [15]/48,idem,+19 [2]/46,XY [3]. Next generation sequencing demonstrated an ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant with allele frequency (VAF) of 46% which was annotated as a VUS. He was diagnosed with MDS with an intermediate risk (level 2) by International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) score, high risk by IPSS-R, high risk by age-adjusted IPSS-R and high risk by IPSS-M. He had a matched unrelated donor available for allogenic stem cell transplant and was referred for upfront transplant in view of the high risk disease.

Pedigree showing the segregation of the ETV6 c.1127T > A variant within the extended family of the proband

His mother (II-3) died at the age of 84 years with a history of urinary bladder carcinoma and colon carcinoma and his father (II-2) died at the age of 64 years with metastatic carcinoma (suspected lung carcinoma) (se Fig. 1). His sister (III-4) was diagnosed with ALL at the age of 15 years. Her cytogenetics at the time of diagnosis was not available. She received a multiagent pediatric protocol and achieved a complete remission. Subsequently she developed bladder cancer at 30 years of age, endometrial cancer at 48 years and therapy-related MDS at 59 years. His brother (III-2) was noted to have low platelets in his 20 s with a platelet count ranging from 30 to 50 × 109/L and was diagnosed with ITP. A third sibling (III-3) died from diabetes related complications. His paternal grandparents also died from cancer (I-1 and I-2) (primary uncertain). III-2’s son (IV-3) was diagnosed with ALL with a hyperdiploid karyotype (52,XY,+X,+4,+8,+15,+21,+21 [16]/46,XY [3]) at the age of 15 years. He was treated with a pediatric chemotherapy protocol and subsequently the disease relapsed. He succumbed to the illness at the age of 22 years.

In view of the significant family history, PreventionGenetics Inherited Bone Marrow Failure (IBMF) germline panel testing (see Supplementary Table 1) on skin fibroblasts was performed in the proband (III-1), which detected the same ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant found in his bone marrow. Furthermore, three VUSs were detected as noted in Table 1. The sister (III-4) also underwent germline testing which detected the same ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant. His brother (III-2) was evaluated for the thrombocytopenia with a bone marrow assessment which showed adequate megakaryocytes with no evidence of dysplasia. Karyotype was normal 46,XY [21]. His NGS showed the same ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant with a VAF 51%. Germline testing on skin fibroblasts showed 2 variants: one being ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) (see Table 1). All three siblings were found to have a VUS in RPL11 c.47G > A, (p.Arg16His), therefore this variant was also segregating with disease in the family.

The proband (III-1) while awaiting transplant progressed to MDS with excess blasts, type 2 (MDS-EB2). He received FLAG-Ida (i.e. fludarabine, cytarabine, G-CSF and idarubicin) induction chemotherapy. Treatment was complicated by COVID-19 pneumonia, COVID associated myocarditis and Stenotrophomonas maltophilia bacteremia with septic shock. He succumbed to these complications. The sister (III-4) with MDS is receiving best supportive care with transfusions as needed. The brother (III-2) is under active surveillance. The proband’s daughter (IV-1) tested negative for the ETV6 variant. One of the sister’s daughter (IV-6) tested positive for ETV6 variant. She had a significant history of melanoma diagnosed at the age of 18 years but no history of thrombocytopenia or hematological abnormalities. A second daughter (IV-5) of the sister (III-4) tested negative for ETV6 variant.

Discussion

ETV6 encodes a 57 kDa protein with 452 amino acid with three domains: pointed (PNT) domain, central domain and DNA binding Erythroblast Transformation Specific (ETS) domain. Only homo/hetero dimerized ETV6 protein bind to the DNA within the nucleus. Dimerization occurs through the PNT domain. This protein functions as a transcription repressor [6, 7].

In this report we describe a pedigree with multiple family members with a ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant presenting with thrombocytopenia and hematological or solid tumor malignancies. The ETV6 germline variants are characterized by missense mutation in the ETS DNA-binding domain of the protein [7]. This is postulated to lead to an impaired DNA binding with a preserved dimerization capability. Therefore, the mutant ETV6 dimerizes with wild type ETV6 in the cytoplasm resulting in cytoplasmic sequestration causing a dominant negative effect [1, 4]. Lack of binding of ETV6 to DNA and its cytoplasmic sequestration results in a lack of ETV6 repressor activity.

The variant described in this pedigree (ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln)), is predicted to be located in the critical ETS domain. This variant is absent from large population databases [8–10]. Notably, this variant has been previously reported in a pediatric patient with AML in a French cohort study [11]. The French cohort study however did not provide any family pedigree for this variant [11]. Also, this has been predicted to be deleterious by multiple in-silico computational models (Polyphene-2, SIFT, Mutation-Taster) [12–15]. In addition, ETV6 exhibits a moderate constraint against missense variation, with a missense Z-score of 2.2 according to gnomAD, suggesting a moderate level of intolerance to missense mutations. As per ACMG/AMP 2015 guidelines, the combination of 2 moderate criteria and 2 supporting criteria (i.e. PM2 and PM1, PP4 and PP1 [PreventionGenetics, personal communication 2025]), renders this ETV6 variant as likely pathogenic. However, ClinVar currently classifies this ETV6 variant as a variant of uncertain significance as there is a lack of functional data and until now, there was no segregation data available (SCV004735598.2; VCV003037062.2; version date: October 13, 2024) [16, 17].

In this pedigree, this variant was identified in the proband (III-1) who had long standing thrombocytopenia and developed MDS. Two other family members also had the same variant with one of them (III-4) having had ALL in childhood and then later developed MDS in adulthood and the other family member (III-2) had chronic thrombocytopenia. Thus, this variant was found to be segregating with disease in the proband (III-1) and his siblings (III-2, III-4). Whereas, this variant was not detected in two of the unaffected offsprings (IV-1, IV-5) of the affected individuals and was detected in another offspring (IV-6), who did not have thrombocytopenia or hematological malignancy but was diagnosed to have melanoma. In addition, another offspring was diagnosed to have chronic thrombocytopenia at the age of 5 years and ALL at the age of 15 years, whose genotyping testing was never performed (IV-3). Alternate missense variants near this amino acid has been reported in individuals with ALL and/or thrombocytopenia (p.Ala377Thr, p.Arg378Pro, p.Leu379Pro, p.Trp380Arg, p.His383Arg) [3, 5, 18]. Current segregation data demonstrates the co-occurrence of the variant with ETV6-phenotype in three siblings, and two informative meiosis supporting pathogenicity (non-transmission to unaffected offspring (IV-1 and IV-5). However, the presence of an unaffected carrier (IV-6) raises the possibility of incomplete penetrance. Additionally, there is an affected offspring (IV-3), whose variant status was not determined. Pathogenicity could be conclusively established with the use of functional testing to confirm loss of function or dominant negative effects. To support the pathogenicity of ETV6 variants, functional assays have been employed to assess impaired transcriptional repression, aberrant subcellular localization, diminished DNA-binding capacity, and disrupted megakaryocyte differentiation in vitro [1, 3, 19, 20].

The ETV6 gene is expressed in hematopoietic cells, specifically megakaryocytes. Lack of ETV6 repressor activity results in terminal megakaryocytic maturation arrest, causing thrombocytopenia and accumulation of proplatelets [1]. There is near complete penetrance of thrombocytopenia in these families (80–100%) [21]. ETV6 germline mutations are also associated with leukemia predisposition. However, the penetrance of leukemia is not as complete as thrombocytopenia, with approximately 30% of carriers developing leukemia [22]. Leukemia predisposition most likely occurs due to the acquisition of additional somatic or germline genetic abnormalities. Hyperdiploidy with mutations in PAX5 and RAS pathway has been associated with the development of B-ALL [3], whereas limited data suggest that mutations in myeloid genes like ASXL1 might result in the development of myeloid malignancies [3]. In the proband (III-1) and his siblings (III-2, III-4), there were additional germline variants detected (see Table 1) which have not been associated with myeloid disorders. A significant finding was the presence three clonal cytogenetic abnormalities in the proband (III-1), i.e. trisomy 1, deletion 20q and trisomy 19. Similar clonal abnormalities were not detected in the sibling (III-2) who presented with chronic thrombocytopenia. Cytogenetic results were not available for the other sibling (III-4). The family member III-2’s son (IV-3) had ALL with a hyperdiploid karyotype (52,XY,+X,+4,+8,+15,+21,+21 [16]/46,XY [3]) at the age of 15 years. But NGS or germline testing was not performed for him.

In our pedigree, multiple family members had solid tumor malignancies. Notably, family member III-4 had two solid tumor malignancies (urinary bladder carcinoma and endometrial carcinoma) and her daughter (IV-6) was diagnosed with melanoma at a young age. Individual III-4 was tested with IBMF, which included the Lynch syndrome genes (see Supplementary Table 1) and no pathogenic variants were identified. Similarly, individual IV-6 underwent additional testing with a 7 gene melanoma panel (see Supplementary Table 1) and no pathogenic variants were identified.

All the three affected siblings (III-1, III-2 and III-4) had a VUS in the RPL11 gene (c.47G > A (p.Arg16His)). This variant is not documented in large population databases [10]. A different RPL11 variant c.46 C > A(p.Arg16Ser) has been described in individuals with Diamond-Blackfan Anemia (DBA) [23]. DBA is a pediatric disease characterized by congenital musculoskeletal malformations of the extremities as well as the craniofacial region, progressive anemia with erythroblastopenia and a risk of developing malignancies such as colorectal cancer, osteosarcoma or myeloid [24]. The presentation of adult-onset DBA can vary, but typical congenital abnormalities are present in approximately half of patients [25]. None of the affected individuals in our pedigree had any malformations or erythroblastopenia. Hence, the phenotype of DBA was not consistent with this family’s presentation. On the other hand, a few solid tumors have been described in individuals with ETV6 germline mutations, the role of ETV6 in the pathogenesis of solid tumors is unclear [4]. Testing for somatic mutations in the tumor samples was not performed and thus it is uncertain whether these tumors are sporadic or driven by the germline ETV6 variant.

Conclusion

In conclusion, in this case report we present a rare ETV6 c.1127T > A (p.Leu376Gln) variant in a pedigree. This variant was found to be segregating in individuals with thrombocytopenia and hematological malignancies. Strong in-silico computational models predict a deleterious effect of this ETV6 variant. This ETV6 variant fulfills ACMG criteria (i.e. combination of 2 moderate criteria and 2 supporting criteria) justifying its reclassification from VUS to likely pathogenic.

Cytokine signaling and resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR+HER2− breast cancer
Authors: Lukas Bolini, Joyce G Habib, Lorenzo Galluzzi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile CDK4/6 inhibitors improved clinical outcomes in HR+HER2- metastatic breast cancer (MBC), resistance invariably occurs. The underlying molecular mechanisms are not actionable, leaving limited therapeutic options for progressing patients. Recent data highlight a role for IL-6 in the resistance of HR+HER2- MBC to CDK4/6 inhibition, potentially opening novel avenues forward.Subject terms: Cancer, Drug discovery, Oncology

Introduction

Over the past decade, cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors combined with endocrine therapy (ET) have revolutionized the treatment of HR+HER2- metastatic breast cancer (MBC), enabling significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and/or overall survival in both the 1st and 2nd line setting1. Nonetheless, most—if not all—patients with HR+HER2- MBC receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors ultimately progress and succumb to their disease, highlighting the existence of innate or acquired resistance mechanisms that limit clinical benefit1. At least in some cases, acquired resistance emerges from the therapy-enforced selection of pre-existing malignant cell clones that bear alterations preventing CDK4/6 inhibitors from establishing a permanent cell cycle arrest commonly known as cellular senescence1. Such alterations can affect cell cycle regulation directly (e.g., RB1 loss, TRP53 mutations) or indirectly (e.g., exacerbated growth factor signaling)1. Acquired resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors, however, may not always be driven by genetic factors. As an example, CDK4/6 inhibitors have recently been shown to elicit the C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2)-dependent recruitment of interleukin 17 (IL-17)-secreting γδ T cells to the microenvironment of HR+HER2- breast cancer2. This results in the repolarization of tumor-associated macrophages toward an immunosuppressive, C-X3-C motif chemokine receptor 1 (CX3CR1)+ phenotype associated with treatment resistance2. Kettner and collaborators have recently demonstrated that autocrine/paracrine IL-6 signaling confers resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR+HER2- MBC by favoring signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3)-dependent genetic programs that promote dedifferentiation alongside the acquisition of immunoevasive traits3.

Main findings

Building on previous findings from the same team demonstrating a role for IL-6/STAT3-driven cellular dedifferentiation in the resistance of HR+HER2- breast cancer to palbociclib4, Kettner and colleagues set out to assess the circulating levels of the senescence-associated cytokine IL-6 in 85 blood samples from patients with HR+HER2- MBC prior to the initiation of therapy with CDK4/6 inhibitors and 65 specimens collected at the time of progression (including paired pre- and post-treatment samples from 11 patients). Circulating IL-6 levels were increased at progression compared to baseline, both in unpaired (65 vs 85 samples) and paired analyses. Conversely, no changes in circulating IL-6 levels could be documented in patients experiencing disease control on CDK4/6 inhibitors. Such an increase in plasma IL-6 correlated with STAT3 phosphorylation in tumor samples, but not with common mutational events, and was accompanied by elevations in the circulating levels of other senescence-associated cytokines, notably CCL23. It remains to be formally investigated whether circulating IL-6 levels increase prior to progression (as CCL2 levels appear to do)2, hence offering superior prognostic information.

Kettner and co-workers went on to demonstrate that depleting IL-6 from human HR+ breast cancer MCF7 or T47D cells resistant to CDK4/6 inhibition and ET partially restores treatment sensitivity, while limiting susceptibility to the antiproliferative effects of the STAT3 inhibitor TTI-101. Such a restored sensitivity was accompanied by downregulation of multiple epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) markers upregulated in resistant MCF7 cells, including vimentin (VIM) and cadherin 2 (CDH2). Moreover, the downregulation of IL-6 delayed the acquisition of resistance in MCF7 or T47D cells exposed to palbociclib over an extended period of time. Finally, depleting IL-6 restored the ability of palbociclib to induce senescence in resistant MCF7 and T47D cells3. These findings suggest that autocrine/paracrine IL-6 signaling in HR+ breast cancer cells promotes resistance to CDK4/6 inhibition along with senescence inhibition and dedifferentiation.

Next, Kettner and co-authors compared patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) established from human HR+HER2- MBCs relapsing early (<3 months) versus late (>6 months) after the initiation of CDK4/6 inhibitors plus ET. In line with an enrichment in proliferation-associated markers as detected by RNAseq and immunoblotting, PDXs from early progressors exhibited accelerated proliferation in vivo, while PDXs from late progressors were characterized by decreased proliferation and displayed transcriptional signatures linked to inflammatory and immunological processes, including IL-6/STAT3 signaling3. These data point to the existence of differential mechanisms underlying early vs. late relapse in patients with HR+HER2- MBC treated with CDK4/6 inhibitors and ET.

Finally, Kettner and colleagues investigated the ability of TTI-101 to cooperate with palbociclib in HR+HER2- MBC PDXs established in immunodeficient hosts. Mirroring their transcriptomic findings, TTI-101 synergized with palbociclib in PDX models of late, but not early, progression, an effect that was paralleled by a reduction in cancer cells staining positively for STAT3 phosphorylation and the proliferation marker Ki-67. Similar results were obtained with the FDA-approved IL-6 inhibitor tocilizumab3, pointing to the IL-6/STAT3 axis as to a potential target for restoring sensitivity to CDK4/6 inhibitors in patients experiencing late relapse on treatment.

Implications

In summary, Kettner and collaborators unveiled a novel mechanism of acquired resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR+HER2- MBC, namely, the ability of HR+HER2- breast cancer cells undergoing senescence upon CDK4/6 inhibition to secrete IL-6 and hence elicit STAT3-dependent dedifferentiation and accelerated proliferation in neoplastic cells evading therapy-driven senescence, which ultimately underlies disease progression (Fig. 1). These findings point to IL-6 and STAT3 as potential targets to limit resistance in patients with HR+HER2- MBC receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors. Moreover, they corroborate the notion that heterogenous tumors may not benefit from treatments blocking proliferation only in a subset of senescence-prone cells while eliciting the release of mitogenic or immunosuppressive factors, as the latter may exacerbate the growth of replication-competent cells and hence relapse5. Finally, (although entirely based on palbociclib) these data might also suggest that rechallenging HR+HER2- MBC patients that experience disease progression after a prolonged response to CDK4/6 inhibition with alternative CDK4/6 inhibitor or a different ET, such as novel selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs), may not be an adequate strategy in all-comers. Importantly, this is at odds with current knowledge suggesting that delayed progression on 1st line CDK4/6 inhibitors is a potential predictor of ET sensitivity in HR+HER2- MBC6.

Cytokine signaling and HR+ breast cancer resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors.

At least in some settings, HR+HER2- breast cancer cells respond to CDK4/6 inhibition by entering a permanent proliferative arrest commonly known as cellular senescence, which is associated with the secretion of numerous bioactive factors, including cytokines like IL-6 and chemokines like CCL2. A priori, such a response may appear beneficial as it reduces the number of malignant cells that are capable of proliferating and hence driving disease progression. However, like other solid malignances, HR+HER2- breast cancer exhibits a considerable degree of intratumoral heterogeneity, offering an abundant evolutionary substrate to therapy-driven pressure. In this setting, malignant cells preserving a proliferative capacity despite CDK4/6 inhibition can emerge as a dominant malignant cell population to ultimately drive disease progression, not only as they are positively selected by treatment over time, but also because they harness the mitogenic (IL-6) and immunosuppressive (CCL2) cues provided by their senescent counterparts. In such a setting of late progression, targeting IL-6 (with tocilizumab), CCL2 and/or their downstream cellular and immunological effectors, including IL-17—with the FDA-approved IL-17 blockers brodalumab, ixekizumab or secukinumab – and immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs)—with the FDA-approved colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) blockers pexidartinib or vimseltinib—stands out as a promising strategy to limit resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors in patients with HR+HER2- breast cancer. CCR2, C-C motif chemokine receptor 2.

In view of recent findings linking the release of CCL2 from HR+HER2- cancer cells exposed to CDK4/6 inhibitors with the establishment of an immunosuppressive microenvironment dominated by γδ T cells and CXC3CR1+ TAMs that foster resistance to therapy2, it will be interesting to investigate whether any of the detrimental effects attributed to IL-6 secretion in humans by Kettner and collaborators involves an immunological component and de facto reflects evaded immunosurveillance7. Both IL-17-secreting γδ T cells8 and CXC3CR1+ TAMs9 express indeed IL-6 receptors, at least potentially pointing to the existence of a signaling axis linking IL-6 secretion by cancer cells to local immunosuppression. Despite this and other unknowns, targeting IL-6 with tocilizumab10 or STAT3 with hitherto investigational agents stands out as a promising avenue to limit the resistance of patients with HR+HER2- tumors to CDK4/6 inhibitors. Prospective trials are needed to investigate this possibility.

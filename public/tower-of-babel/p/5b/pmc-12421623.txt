Assessing health system preparedness from trends and time delays in the management of myocardial infarctions during the COVID-19 pandemic in India: a multicentre retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jaideep C Menon, Aravind MS, Harikrishnan S, Chandrashekar Janakiram, Anju James, Aswathy Sreedevi, Geetha R Menon, Denny John, Jerin Jose Cherian, Vanajakshamma V, Rajpal K Abhaichand, Eapen P Punnoose, Arun BS, Mathew Abraham, Paul Thomas, Chakradhar Pedada, Unni Govindan, Bishav Mohan, Sunil Pisharody, Tom Devasia, Placid Sebastian, Rajesh Thachathodiyl, Vijayaraghavan G, Nitesh Arora, Anupama V Hegde, Johny Joseph, John F John, Prakash Chand Negi, Raja Ram Mantri, Amit Malviya, Ramakrishna CD, Vikrant Vijan

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to analyse the number of myocardial infarction (MI) admissions during the COVID-19 lockdown periods of 2020 and 2021 (March 15th to June 15th) and compare them with corresponding pre-pandemic period in 2019. The study also evaluated changes in critical treatment intervals: onset to door (O2D), door to balloon (D2B) and door to needle (D2N) and assessed 30-day clinical outcomes. This study examined MI care trends in India during the COVID-19 lockdown period, irrespective of patients’ COVID-19 infection status.DesignMulticentre retrospective cohort studySettingTwenty-three public and private hospitals across multiple Indian states, all with 24/7 interventional cardiology facilities.ParticipantsAll adults (>18 years) admitted with acute myocardial infarction between March 15 and June 15 in 2019 (pre-pandemic), 2020 (first lockdown) and 2021 (second lockdown). A total of 3614 cases were analysed after excluding duplicates and incomplete data.Primary outcomesNumber of MI admissions, median O2D, D2B and D2N times.Secondary outcomes30-day outcomes including death, reinfarction and revascularisation.ResultsMI admissions dropped from 4470 in year 2019 to 2131 (2020) and 1483 (2021). The median O2D increased from 200 min (IQR 115–428) pre-COVID-19 to 390 min (IQR 165–796) in 2020 and 304 min (IQR 135–780) in 2021. The median D2B time reduced from 225 min (IQR 120–420) in 2019 to 100 min (IQR 53–510) in 2020 and 130 min (IQR 60–704) in 2021. Similarly, D2N time decreased from 240 min (IQR 120–840) to 35 min (IQR 25–69) and 45 min (IQR 24–75), respectively. The 30-day outcome of death, reinfarction and revascularisation was 4.25% in 2020 and 5.1% in 2021, comparable to 5.8% reported in the Acute Coronary Syndrome Quality Improvement in Kerala study.ConclusionDespite the expansion of catheterisation facilities across India, the country continues to fall short of achieving international benchmarks for optimal MI care.

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic severely disrupted health systems worldwide.1 Public health systems in low-income settings were compelled to focus on tackling COVID-19, often at the expense of routine healthcare services.2 3

In India, a nationwide lockdown was enforced at midnight on 25 March 2020, prohibiting non-essential movement and services.4 Global reports have highlighted a decrease in admissions for myocardial infarction (MI) and increased delays in treatment during the pandemic.57

This decrease in hospital admissions for MI was influenced by various factors, including patients’ reluctance to seek inpatient hospital care due to fear of contracting COVID-19, the closure of many primary and secondary care centres due to a shortage of healthcare workers and reduced access to hospitals and ambulance services. Additionally, healthcare professionals faced an overwhelming burden during this period.5

MI including ST-segment elevation MI (STEMI) care hinges on timely definitive therapy, operating on the principle that ‘time is muscle’.810

Understanding the specific impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on admissions and management of MIs in India is crucial.

Although a similar study was conducted across India in 2020, it was limited to that year and did not capture the full impact of the pandemic, which continued for 2 years. Additionally, the study did not consider the time taken for a patient to reach the hospital, an important indicator of the health system’s preparedness.5

This study aims to address these gaps by comparing the number of hospital admissions for MI (both STEMI and non-ST-segment elevation MI (NSTEMI)) during the two lockdown periods of 2020 and 2021 with the commensurate pre-pandemic period of 2019. The study also assesses treatment delays including onset-to-door (O2D) time, door-to-balloon (D2B) time and door-to-needle (D2N) time. Additionally, the study aims to compare outcomes such as death, reinfarction, revascularisation and rehospitalisation within 30 days among MI patients across these time periods.

Methods

Study design

The study employed a retrospective cohort design, capturing deidentified data of individuals admitted with STEMI and NSTEMI during the designated study periods. The study population included all eligible MI patients presenting during the lockdown period, regardless of COVID-19 test results, as the primary focus was to assess system-level changes in care delivery.

Study setting

This multicentre study was conducted across 23 hospitals in India, comprising seven public sector and 16 private sector institutions (Annexure 1). Hospitals were selected through a purposive sampling approach based on the following criteria:

Availability of 24/7 interventional cardiology services.

Availability of 24/7 interventional cardiology services.

Capability to provide primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention/thrombolysis for acute MI (AMI).

Capability to provide primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention/thrombolysis for acute MI (AMI).

Willingness to participate in retrospective data sharing and uniform data collection.

Willingness to participate in retrospective data sharing and uniform data collection.

The participating centres were geographically distributed across multiple Indian states, including urban and semiurban regions. While not a nationally representative random sample, this selection strategy aimed to capture health system level MI care delivery during the COVID-19 lockdowns.

Participants

All adult patients (age >18 years) presenting to the hospital with a diagnosis of AMI (both STEMI and NSTEMI) were considered.

Inclusion criteria

STEMI/NSTEMI patients who reported within 14 days of symptom onset (typical of myocardial ischaemia) were considered for inclusion, provided they met the American College of Cardiology (ACC)/American Heart Association (AHA) guideline definition. If treated elsewhere within 14 days, the site Principal Investigator must be satisfied that the patient is not included in the participants’ list of another hospital.

The definition for STEMI was as per ACC/AHA guidelines as new ST-segment elevation at the J point in two contiguous leads with the cut-off point greater than 0.1 mV in all leads other than V2 or V3.11 In the leads V2eV3, the cut-off point is greater than 0.2 mV in men older than 40 years old and greater than 0.25 in men younger than 40 years old, or greater than 0.15 mV in women. In the case of pre-existing left bundle branch block, modified Sgarbossa criteria12 were used for diagnosing STEMI.

NSTEMI was defined as new-onset chest pain consistent with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and troponin elevation but without ECG changes consistent with STEMI. In the case of NSTEMI, patients present with new-onset chest pain consistent with ACS and troponin elevation without STEMI ECG changes. Troponin elevation indicates myocardial injury, and additional criteria may include typical symptoms, ECG changes or other manifestations outlined in the fourth universal definition of AMI. If a NSTEMI was treated elsewhere, either fully or partially, and referred for further evaluation to the participating centre, the patient was included if troponins were still elevated irrespective of duration, provided that the patient was not included in the study by the other hospital.

Exclusion criteria

STEMI outside the 14-day window period, troponin negative NSTEMI at hospitalisation cases already enrolled in the study by another centre and cases lacking required medical record details.

Data collection

This was a retrospective cohort study based on medical record view. Each participating site had the capability for 24/7 intervention and had prior experience with cardiovascular registries and was selected for its ability to provide reliable retrospective data. Data on MI admission, treatment timelines, in-hospital management and discharge medications were extracted from patient case records. 30-day outcomes, including death, reinfarction and revascularisation, were collected from hospital follow-up records or, when unavailable, verified through documented telephonic follow-up. Patients were typically discharged after 4–7 days of admission and reported for follow-up at 4 weeks. In the event of non-reporting, telephonic follow-up was maintained. Research coordinators were briefed on the definitions and criteria for inclusion, including D2B and D2N times, to maintain consistency in data collection.

A standardised questionnaire was used to ensure consistency across sites. To facilitate data entry, a web portal was created, with each site receiving a unique username for access and data entry. Research coordinators at each centre were trained to use handheld devices for data entry, and their work was monitored by a sentinel site coordinator to maintain accuracy and uniformity.

Sample size

The study population includes all patients admitted with STEMI or NSTEMI to the designated hospitals over three lockdown months of 2020 and 2021 (March 15th–June 15th). Comparisons were made with cases from the same period in 2019. To detect a significant increase of 50% in the median D2B time during the COVID-19 pandemic, with an assumed pre-COVID-19 median of 77 min (IQR 55 to 118 min), a minimum of 42 patients was required. Conversely, for a 50% increase in the median D2N time from 45 min (IQR 27 to 75 min) during the pre-COVID-19 period to 68 min during COVID-19, a minimum of 62 patients was necessary. The sample size calculations were based on differences in medians for positively skewed outcomes in healthcare studies, as detailed in the work by O’Keeffe et al.13

For determining the sample size for hospital admissions, the study assumed a pre-COVID-19 proportion of adverse cardiac events at 5.8% and projected an increase of 1.5% during the COVID-19 period. To observe this change, a sample size of 2037 is required with 5% level of significance and 80% power.

Independent variables included patient demographic characteristics such as age and gender, as well as risk factors, date of admission, type of AMI (STEMI or NSTEMI) and use of emergency medical services.

The primary dependent variable was the change in the number of admissions for all types of AMIs (NSTEMI and STEMI) between March 15 and June 15 of 2020 (first lockdown) and 2021 (second lockdown) and the corresponding period in 2019. The total number of MI admissions during the same period in 2019 was collected to compare trends in admissions. The differences in O2D and D2B/D2N times, as well as 30-day outcomes during the study periods in 2020 and 2021, were compared with the results of the Acute Coronary Syndrome Quality Improvement in Kerala (ACS-QUIK) trial published in 2018.

Ethical clearance

Ethics clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee, Amrita Institute of Medical Sciences, Kochi (IEC-AIMS.202I- CAI(D-O42) dated 13/03/2021.

Most of the individual sites got an institutional ethics clearance or a no-objection certificate from their respective site Ethics Committees.

Statistical analysis

Data for the year 2019 were unavailable for six centers, and incomplete or incorrect data were identified in 123 cases during the 2020–2021 period (online supplemental figure 1).

Patient characteristics were analysed using descriptive statistics. Group differences were evaluated with inferential statistics, with results presented as absolute values and percentages for categorical variables, and mean (SD) or median (IQR) for continuous variables. Categorical variable comparisons were made using χ² tests or Fisher’s exact tests, while continuous variable comparisons were conducted using Student’s t-tests or Mann-Whitney tests. Logistics regression was employed to determine the significance of changes in hospital admissions over the two periods, ensuring that overdispersion was not present.

Patient and public involvement statement

Patients or members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Results

A notable reduction in MI cases was observed across multiple medical centres in India during the years 2020 and 2021 when compared with the baseline year of 2019. The percentage loss of MI cases was calculated for each centre, revealing that most centres experienced a notable decline in cases over the two subsequent years (figure 1).

Total myocardial infarction (MI) cases by year.

In 2020, several centres reported a sharp decline of over 80% in MI admissions compared to 2019, including Centre 3 (83.3%), centre 5 (84.2%), centre 12 (89.2%), centre 15 (88.8%) and centre 18 (81.8%). This trend continued in 2021, with centre 5 (91.3%), centre 8 (80.6%) and centre 15 (90.0%) maintaining a reduction of over 80% from 2019 levels. Centres 9 and 21 also showed substantial declines nearing 80% (online supplemental table 1).

When examining the aggregated data, the total number of MI cases across all centres decreased from 4470 in 2019 to 2131 in 2020, representing a 52.33% reduction. In 2021, the total number of cases further declined to 1483, accounting for a 66.80% reduction compared to the baseline year.

Among age groups, the highest proportion of STEMI cases was in patients over 60 years (46.4%), with a slightly lower percentage in NSTEMI cases (49.2%) and an OR indicating a higher likelihood of NSTEMI in this age group (OR=1.32, 95% CI: 1.01 to 1.72). Gender analysis reveals a predominance of male patients in both STEMI (77.8%) and NSTEMI (71.1%) cohorts, with males exhibiting a significantly higher odds ratio (OR=1.42, 95% CI: 1.22 to 1.66) for STEMI. Insurance status varied, with a notable percentage of uninsured patients in both STEMI (2020: 66.8%, 2021: 57.9%) and NSTEMI (2020: 71.9%, 2021: 71.5%) groups. Mode of transfer data indicated a predominance of self/family transport for both STEMI (2020: 47%, 2021: 36.4%) and NSTEMI (2020: 53.7%, 2021: 38.1%), with a consistent trend in public transport usage. Comparison by year revealed that 59.7% of STEMI and 57.6% of NSTEMI cases were recorded in 2020, with a slight decrease in 2021 (table 1).

Diabetes and hypertension were significantly more prevalent among NSTEMI patients compared with STEMI. Diabetes was present in 50.87% of NSTEMI patients versus 46% in STEMI patients, with an OR of 0.82 (95% CI: 0.71-0.94). Hypertension was reported in 51.1% of NSTEMI patients and 43.8% of STEMI patients (OR 0.75, 95% CI: 0.65 to 0.85). Smoking was significantly more common in STEMI patients (21.3%) compared to NSTEMI patients (18.2%), with an OR of 1.21 (95% CI: 1.02 to 1.44), indicating a higher likelihood of STEMI among smokers. For other investigated risk factors including dyslipidaemia, cerebrovascular disease, prior stroke, peripheral arterial disease, alcohol use and chewing tobacco, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups (online supplemental table 2).

This study examined temporal trends in treatment timelines for acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients across three consecutive years: 2019, 2020 and 2021 (figure 2). Notably, the median time from O2D increased substantially from 200 min in 2019 to 390 min in 2020, then decreased to 304 min in 2021. The median time from D2B decreased from 225 min in 2019 to 100 min in 2020, then slightly increased to 140 min in 2021. Conversely, the median time from D2N saw a significant decrease from 240 min in 2019 to 35 min in 2020, then slightly increased to 45 min in 2021.

Private facilities exhibit shorter median times for D2B and D2N procedures compared to government facilities when comparing 2020 versus 2021, suggesting potentially faster access and treatment for ACS patients in the private healthcare sector (online supplemental table 3). Similarly, regional variations in treatment times between 2020 and 2021 were apparent. The South generally showed shorter median times for D2B and D2N compared to other regions, while the North exhibited longer median times across all measured phases (online supplemental table 4).

When comparing intervention rates between 2020 and 2021, diagnostic coronary angiogram uses in STEMI increased from 67.9% to 73.6%, while primary PCI decreased from 34.2% to 27.5%, and overall PCI rates slightly increased from 55.5% to 57.7%. Thrombolytic therapy usage remained relatively constant, and reperfusion therapy decreased from 53.3% to 48.3%. For NSTEMI, diagnostic coronary angiogram usage increased from 63.9% to 69.9%, and overall PCI rates significantly increased from 31.5% to 47.8%. Rescue PCI usage also rose from 18.9% to 24.4%, while Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) rates remained stable around 7% (online supplemental tables 5 and 6).

The analysis identified factors associated with an increased risk of adverse outcomes following AMI (online supplemental table 7). Male gender was associated with higher odds of cardiogenic shock (OR=1.439, p=0.015) and heart failure (OR=1.342, p= 0.019). Hypertension (OR = 1.325, p=0.035) and dyslipidaemia (OR=3.009, p<0.001) were linked to an elevated risk of cardiogenic shock, while dyslipidaemia also increased the risk of cardiac arrest (OR=1.995, p=0.021) and heart failure (OR=0.738, p=0.023). Prior stroke (OR=4.360, p=0.022) and peripheral arterial disease (OR=0.267, p<0.001) were significant predictors of heart failure. Additionally, patients presenting in 2021 had a higher likelihood of cardiogenic shock (OR=1.537, p<0.001) and heart failure (OR=0.751, p=0.013) compared to those in 2020. Other variables including age, diabetes, smoking, alcohol use, cerebrovascular disease and chewing tobacco were not significantly associated with increased risk of reinfarction, heart failure, cardiogenic shock or cardiac arrest in this cohort.

Discussion

The findings of this multicentre study shed light on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on MI admissions, management strategies and patient outcomes in India. The discussion below addresses key observations, implications and areas for further research. This study focuses on the broader healthcare system challenges during the lockdown phases of the COVID-19 pandemic and not on patients diagnosed with COVID-19.

Consistent with global trends, our study reveals a substantial reduction in MI admissions during the lockdown periods of 2020 and 2021 compared with the pre-pandemic year of 2019. Studies conducted in various countries, including France, California, Italy, Germany, Austria, England, New Zealand, India and China, consistently report significant reductions in AMI admissions during the pandemic period compared with pre-pandemic periods. For instance, in France, a study observed a 30% decrease in AMI admissions post-lockdown,14 with similar trends reported in California, where weekly rates of hospitalisation for AMI decreased by up to 48%.15 This decline can be attributed to various factors, including fear of contracting COVID-19, restricted mobility and disruptions in healthcare services. The study highlights notable shifts in treatment strategies for MI patients during the pandemic. While primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) rates decreased,1619 the proportion of patients receiving thrombolysis increased.16 This suggests a potential adaptation to prioritise less invasive interventions amidst resource constraints and logistical challenges during the pandemic.

Analysis of patient outcomes reveals an increase in cardiogenic shock, heart failure and mortality rates in 2021 compared to 2020. This suggests a worsening of clinical outcomes amidst evolving pandemic dynamics and healthcare system pressures. Additionally, the observed increase in haemorrhage events underscores the need for vigilant monitoring and management of complications, especially in the context of altered treatment strategies and patient profiles during the pandemic.

The study demonstrates a significant increase in median O2D time during the pandemic, reflecting delayed presentation to healthcare facilities. The increased duration may be attributed to several factors: fear of seeking hospital care amidst the COVID-19 pandemic; closure of smaller healthcare facilities during lockdowns, leading to delayed diagnosis; shortage of healthcare staff and transportation; conversion of public hospitals into COVID-19 care centres; fear of quarantine, especially during the second wave and financial constraints due to loss of income during lockdowns. The duration from chest pain onset to hospital door admission reflects the health system’s readiness. The delay in D2N and D2B times could be due to various reasons, including shortage of medical personnel for procedures like PPCI, time constraints related to personal protective equipment usage, especially during the initial stages of the pandemic when test results took longer, hesitation among cardiologists to perform procedures on patients with unknown COVID-19 status and lack of appropriate ventilation in catheterisation labs.16 20 Reports of high COVID-19 transmission among cardiologists, particularly after echocardiograms, may have also contributed to delays, especially in D2B times. The 30-day outcomes, including death, reinfarction and revascularisation, were 4.25% in 2020 and 5.1% in 2021, which aligns favourably with the ACS-QUIK study’s 5.8% outcome. Similar to other reports, the reduction in NSTEMI cases was higher than STEMI cases. STEMI patients, particularly younger individuals with severe symptoms, are less likely to ignore symptoms and would seek medical attention despite the pandemic. In contrast, NSTEMI, typically occurring in older patients with pre-existing conditions, may result in delayed hospital visits due to varied and less pronounced symptoms.

This delay, coupled with changes in treatment protocols and resource allocation, likely contributed to delays in reperfusion therapy and subsequent adverse outcomes. Efforts to mitigate barriers to timely care, such as public health campaigns, telemedicine initiatives and strengthening of emergency medical services, are essential to minimise the impact of delayed treatment on patient outcomes.

The findings underscore the importance of robust healthcare systems and emergency response mechanisms to address the evolving needs of patients with acute cardiovascular events during public health emergencies. Strategies to enhance healthcare preparedness include optimising resource allocation, implementing telemedicine and remote monitoring solutions and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration among healthcare providers.

Our study identified significant changes in AMI patient management strategies, primarily driven by a decrease in admissions. While the study comprehensively evaluated various aspects of AMI care, its retrospective nature and potential under-representation of mortality pose limitations. Additionally, the lack of uniform systems of care for STEMI across the country and under-representation of public sector hospitals in Kerala limit the study’s generalisability. Nonetheless, the study serves as a reflection of the country’s healthcare system preparedness, offering insights for disaster management, policymakers and hospitals to improve healthcare delivery during future pandemics. Hospitals located in the southern region of India exhibited shorter median D2B and D2N times compared to those in the northern region, which reported longer delays across all phases of MI care. This disparity may be a regional difference in healthcare capacity, emergency service coordination and community awareness. Southern states like Kerala and Tamil Nadu are known for robust tertiary care infrastructure, higher health literacy, public-private partnership and better referral systems, whereas northern regions often encounter difficulties related to transportation, availability of trained healthcare workforce and coordination between facilities. These findings are consistent with previous national health indices and should inform region-specific capacity-building and triage optimisation strategies.21

Recent international studies have highlighted the increased mortality and impaired reperfusion outcomes among SARS-CoV-2 positive STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI, underscoring the direct clinical impact of COVID-19 on cardiovascular care.22 In contrast, our study focuses on the indirect, system-level consequences of the pandemic such as delays in presentation and procedural care and shifts in management strategy during the lockdown period in India, regardless of patients’ COVID-19 status. Together, these findings suggest that both patient-level disease factors and broader disruptions to health services contributed to adverse outcomes in acute cardiac care during the pandemic.

One limitation of this study is that the ACS-QUIK trial data are specific to the state of Kerala, whereas the current study encompasses data on a national scale. This difference in geographic scope may introduce variability that could affect the generalisability of the comparative analysis. However, due to the absence of other comparable datasets, the ACS-QUIK data were used as the most relevant reference point for this comparison. We did not collect information on certain clinical variables such as operator experience, vascular access technique, timing of procedures (daytime vs nighttime), periprocedural complications and the use of oral anticoagulants among patients with atrial fibrillation due to the limitations of the retrospective design. As a result, our ability to comprehensively assess the quality of care and the potential contributors to treatment delays and outcomes is limited. These variables should be systematically explored in future prospective studies. Variations introduced by center coordinators and regional factors may have contributed to some degree of error, which should be acknowledged as a limitation of the study. The number of MI cases during the lockdown periods was lower than in previous years, reflecting the real-world decline in admissions during the pandemic. While this may limit the robustness of subgroup analyses, the sample size was adequate as per calculations based on the ACS-QUIK study. The study’s primary objective was observational to describe care patterns and system responses during the COVID-19 lockdown rather than to perform powered comparisons.

Conclusion

Despite the expansion of catheterisation labs across India, the nation continues to fall short of meeting international guidelines for optimal care in cases of STEMI. Pre-pandemic data already indicated suboptimal D2B and D2N times, a situation exacerbated by the challenges posed by the pandemic. The prolonged average time to revascularisation, whether pharmacological or mechanical, exceeding 300 min, significantly increases the risk of myocardial damage.

Addressing these deficiencies necessitates the establishment of a robust ambulance network dedicated to emergency patient transfer, independent of public or private transportation. Such a system would ensure early initiation of treatment during transit, potentially limiting myocardial injury. Moreover, public awareness campaigns aimed at recognising symptoms of cardiac ischaemia and providing immediate first aid, particularly among individuals with cardiovascular risk factors, are imperative.

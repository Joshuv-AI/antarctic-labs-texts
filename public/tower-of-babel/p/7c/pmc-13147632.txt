National and provincial burden of interstitial lung disease mortality in China, 2013–2021: a nationwide analysis of trends and underlying cause of death
Authors: Fanshu Yan, Lingling Yu, Zifang Zhou, Lijun Wang, Maigeng Zhou, Zhuoli Zhang, Peng Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe national and provincial epidemiological characteristics of interstitial lung disease (ILD) mortality remain unknown in China. To assess ILD mortality trends, causes, and regional disparities in China (2013–2021).MethodsAll death cases with ILD coded as the underlying cause or cause in any part of the cause of death chain from the National Mortality Surveillance System (NMSS) during 2013–2021 were included. Annual crude and age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) were estimated by gender, age group, urban/rural, and province from 2013 to 2021. Joinpoint regression models were applied to calculate the average annual percentage change (AAPC) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) in China during the study period. The distribution of different ILD subtypes with the broad category of ILD as the underlying cause of death was analyzed. We also analyzed the ranking of underlying cause of death for all ILD-associated deaths.ResultsIn 2021, the ASMR for ILD in China was 2.90/100,000 (95%CI: 2.84, 2.96), decreasing by 45.18% (AAPC: -7.65, 95% CI: -13.46, -1.45) compared with 2013 (5.29/100,000; 95%CI: 5.19, 5.39). Eighty percent of ILD deaths in China were 60 years or older. Nationally, ILD ASMR declined consistently across sexes, urban-rural divides, and eastern, central, and western regions during the study period. Among those who died with ILD in 2021, the top four underlying causes of death were other respiratory diseases (J98) (11.54%), sequelae of cerebrovascular disease (I69) (10.18%), other ILD (J84) (7.61%), and malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung (C34) (7.01%).ConclusionsThe mortality burden of ILD stayed relatively high in males, those in economically developed regions, and the elderly. We recommend enhancing disease activity monitoring in high-risk groups or patients with specific ILD subtypes to lower mortality risk.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12931-026-03580-y.

Background

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a heterogeneous chronic lung disease characterized by parenchymal inflammation and fibrosis consisting of more than 200 specific lung diseases [1–3]. The majority of ILD exhibit a strong correlation with unfavorable prognosis and significant mortality rates in its advanced stages. Previous studies have shown that patients with ILD often suffer from multiple comorbidities, such as lung cancer, ischemic heart disease, diabetes.

The results of the Global Burden of Disease study showed that the global incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years of ILD (J84) and pulmonary sarcoidosis (D86) increased by 118.6%, 166.63%, and 122.87%, respectively, from 1990 to 2019 [4]. Studies in different countries have shown that ILD morbidity and mortality vary considerably by age, sex, race, geographical region, and subtype [5, 6]. For example, the age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) in both males and females in the United States showed a decreasing trend from 2004 to 2017, in contrast to the increasing trend in most countries [7]. A study based on Global Burden of Disease (GBD) data showed that the incidence of ILD in BRICS (Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa) countries increased from 218.3 per 100,000 to 353.2 per 100,000 during 1990–2019, with China having the highest growth rate of 107.6% [8]. Meanwhile, the study showed that the peak incidence of ILD usually occurred in the age group of 65–80 years [8]. China is in the stage of accelerated population aging and the burden of ILD in China is likely to be increasing.

Current research on ILD primarily focuses on its diagnosis and treatment, while large-scale epidemiological studies remain limited. Only a few countries, such as the United Kingdom, France, the United States and Australia, have conducted studies on the characteristics, trends and regional differences in the mortality of selected subtypes of ILD [7, 9–11]. Current studies on the burden of ILD in China are mainly based on the results of the Global Burden of Disease 2019. However, these results did not show the urban-rural differences or the provincial level variations of ILD mortality in China. Therefore, it is important to analyze the mortality burden of ILD in China based on more detailed Chinese data and to understand its trend and characteristics at both national and provincial levels to provide evidence for policymakers and researchers in China.

The objectives of the study were to comprehensively evaluate the mortality characteristics, trends and geographical differences of ILD in China based on data from National Mortality Surveillance System (NMSS) from 2013 to 2021, and to analyze the distribution of underlying causes that contributed to the deaths of people with ILD.

Methods

Data sources

Data on deaths in this study were obtained from the NMSS which covers a population of more than 300 million people in 605 districts and counties from 31 provinces in China. An annual investigation of underreporting is conducted at all surveillance sites by the National Center for Chronic and Non-Communicable Disease Control and Prevention, China CDC, to ensure data quality. More information on the NMSS has been discussed in detail in previous studies [12]. Population data for this study were obtained from the National Bureau of Statistics.

Outcome

The primary outcome for this study was death from ILD based on complete chain of cause of death. The chain of causation is a critical component of cause-of-death determination, encompassing both the immediate cause of death and relevant indirect causes. We included all disease categories related to ILD based on the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10). We invited clinical respiratory pathologists to classify subtypes of ILD according to the pathogenesis of different ILDs. The final classification of ILD included idiopathic ILD, secondary ILD (E-Table 1). The classification of ILD was based on the latest guidelines from the European Respiratory Society and the American Thoracic Society [13]. In this study, deceased individuals were grouped by age into six categories: <15 years, 15–29 years, 30–44 years, 45–59 years, 60–74 years, and ≥75 years. Urban-rural classification was based on the permanent residence addresses of the deceased individuals. Regional classification (eastern, central, and western) followed the criteria of the National Bureau of Statistics of China.

Statistical analysis

Based on the underreporting survey data from each surveillance site between 2013 and 2021, this study estimated the national underreporting rate for China’s annual cause-of-death surveillance. The crude death rate was adjusted for underreporting in the calculation process. For cases with underlying cause of death coded as ILD, crude mortality rate and ASMR were calculated by gender, urban/rural, and province in China from 2013 to 2021. The national census in 2020 was used as the reference population for ASMR. Age-specific mortality rate and its trend during the study period were also analyzed. The Joinpoint regression model was used to calculate the annual average percentage change (AAPC) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for ASMR. As the types of ILD in China are mainly secondary ILD and other secondary ILD. We further analyzed the specific causes of ASMR of ILD and calculated for the secondary ILD and other secondary ILD separately. To further verify the robustness of the changing trend in the ASMR of ILD in China, we excluded deaths from 2020 to 2021 in the sensitivity analysis and calculated the AAPC for the ASMR from 2013 to 2019.

To explore the mortality patterns of ILD in the Chinese population, we conducted two analyses based on cause-of-death chain data from NMSS: (1) Among cases with any ILD subtype recorded in the cause-of-death chain, we examined the distribution of underlying causes of death; (2) Among cases where the underlying cause was explicitly classified as ILD, we further analyzed the distribution characteristics of ILD subtypes. We plotted the change in rankings of the distribution of the underlying cause of death for ILD in China from 2013 to 2021.

This study strictly adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the National Center for Chronic and Noncommunicable Disease Control and Prevention. SAS 9.4 and R 4.4.1 were used to organize and analyze the data. The two-tailed test was used in this studies, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Mortality characteristics and trend of ILD

The overall ASMR of ILD in China was 2.90 per 100,000 (95% CI: 2.84, 2.96) in 2021, representing a decrease of 45.18% (AAPC: −7.65, 95% CI: −13.46, −1.45) (Table 1) from the 2013 rate of 5.29 per 100,000; 95% CI: 5.19, 5.39). In 2021, China had a higher ASMR for ILD in men (4.20/100,000; 95% CI: 4.10, 4.30) than in women (1.81/100,000; 95% CI: 1.75, 1.87), in urban areas (3.71/100,000; 95% CI: 3.61, 3.81) than in rural areas (2.32/100,000; 95% CI: 2.25, 2.39), and in the western (3.29/100,000; 95% CI: 3.17, 3.42) and eastern region (3.20/100,000; 95% CI: 3.12, 3.29) than in the central region (2.10/100,000; 95% CI: 2.01, 2.19). This pattern was consistent during 2013–2021. The trends of ASMR related to idiopathic ILD in Chinese populations with different demographic characteristics remained stable, and the trends of ASMR from secondary ILD were consistent with the overall results (E-Figs. 2 and E and 3). Following the exclusion of deaths occurring in 2020–2021, the declining trend was no longer statistically significant (E-Table 2).

Trends in the composition of age groups of ILD deaths

Crude mortality rate of ILD increased with age (E-Figure 1). From 2013 to 2021, the crude mortality rate of ILD among individuals aged ≥ 75 years in China remained significantly higher than that in other age groups (Fig. 1). While the crude mortality rate of ILD among those aged ≥ 75 years exhibited a marked downward trend, no significant changes were observed in other age groups. In terms of proportional composition across demographic subgroups, the age-group distribution of ILD deaths in China stabilized by both sexs and urban-rural divide (Fig. 2, E-Figure 4). ILD deaths in China predominantly affected elderly individuals aged ≥ 60 years, accounting for over 80% of all ILD fatalities.

ASMR of ILD in different provinces of China

In 2021, the ASMR of ILD in China exhibited significant inter-provincial variation, with the highest rate recorded in Beijing (9.04 per 100,000; 95% CI: 8.39, 9.69) being 8.29 times higher than that of the lowest (Anhui, 1.09 per 100,000; 95% CI: 0.92, 1.25) (Fig. 3). From 2013 to 2021, the provinces with the fastest increases in ASMR were Xizang (284.83%, 95% CI: 16.72, 1168.74) and Gansu (40.64%, 95% CI: 12.86, 75.24), while those with the fastest decreases were Guangdong (−84.21%, 95% CI: −85.77, −82.47) and Shanghai (−76.88%, 95% CI: −79.57, −73.84). In terms of geographical distribution, the provinces with larger increases were mainly concentrated in the northwestern interior of the country, while the provinces in the east and south generally showed a declining trend, with exception of Fujian Province.

Distribution of causes of death in ILD in China

To characterize the distribution of underlying causes of death among ILD patients in China, we included all cases where ILD was recorded in the full chain of causes of death. Among those who died with ILD, the top four underlying causes of death in 2021 were other respiratory diseases (J98) (11.54%), sequelae of cerebrovascular disease (I69) (10.18%), other ILD (J84) (7.61%), and malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung (C34) (7.01%) (Fig. 4). During the study period, the ranking for other ILD (J84), other chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases (J44), and type 2 diabetes mellitus (E11) moved up, while the ranking for malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung (C34), cerebral infarction (I63), chronic ischemic heart disease (I25), pneumoconiosis due to dust containing silica (J62), and unspecified chronic bronchitis (J42) moved down.

To characterize the distribution of causes of death across different ILD subtypes, we further restricted the analysis to cases where the underlying cause of death was the broad category of ILD (e-Table 1). Among those whose underlying cause of death was the broad definition of ILD, the top four underlying causes of death for ILD did not change from 2013 to 2021: other respiratory diseases (J98), other ILD (J84), pneumoconiosis due to dust containing silica (J62), and coal workers’ pneumoconiosis (J60) (Fig. 5). The top four underlying causes of death for ILD in males in 2021 were other respiratory diseases (J98) (41.87%), other ILD (J84) (31.54%), pneumoconiosis due to dust containing silica (J62) (16.12%) and coal workers’ pneumoconiosis (J60) (4.41%), and the top four underlying causes of death for ILD in females were other respiratory diseases (J98) (58.58%), other ILD (J84) (31.55%), other systemic involvement of connective tissue (M35) (3.22%), and dermatomyositis (M33) (1.98%).

Discussion

During the study period, the ASMR for ILD in China showed a decreasing trend, with a higher burden of ILD deaths in elderly patients (over 75 years). Other respiratory diseases (J98), other ILDs (J84), and pneumoconiosis due to dust containing silica (J62) were the leading causes of death in patients with ILD. Consequences of cerebrovascular disease and bronchial and pulmonary cancer were the most common comorbidities leading to death in patients with ILD.

In this study, the ASMR of ILD was higher in the elderly male population in China, which is consistent with findings from other countries such as the United States [2, 8, 14]. For example, previous studies have shown that the majority of ILD patients are male and the male predominance increases with age, and our findings are fully consistent with this phenomenon [6]. The study suggests that this phenomenon may be closely related to smoking, occupational exposure, etc. [15–17]. Recent evidence strengthens the key etiological role of occupational factors in ILD. Studies indicate that occupational exposure to metals increases the risk of IPF by 67%, silica exposure by 55%, and environmental tobacco smoke exposure by 115% [16]. Furthermore, occupational ILD is not limited to traditional dust-associated industries (e.g., mining, construction) but also involves emerging exposure sources (e.g., paints, adhesives, biocides, cleaning agents), with a disproportionately high burden in low-income countries [15]. Men constitute the majority of the workforce in high-risk occupations, which may partly explain the higher ILD mortality trend observed among males in this study [17].At the same time, we observed a decreasing trend in ASMR for ILD in Chinese populations with different demographic characteristics from 2013 to 2021. Current trends in interstitial lung disease exhibit some variations across different regions. For example, while IPF mortality showed an increasing trend in most European countries between 2013 and 2018, data from the United States indicated a declining trend during the same period [1, 7, 18]. The trend of changes in the ASMR of ILD in China is consistent with the downward trend in the ASMR of chronic respiratory diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and lung cancer in China during the study period [19, 20]. The reason why the ASMR of ILD in China does not align with the upward trend observed in most countries worldwide may be attributed to the significant advancements in medical technology (for example: The widespread promotion of anti-fibrotic treatment), improvements in clinical management practices, and overall enhancements in population health status in China over recent years—all of which are likely key factors contributing to the reduction in ILD mortality. The GBD 2023 findings on ILD prevalence in Asia show a 7% decrease in the age-standardized prevalence rate of ILD in China in 2023 compared to 1990 [21]. Furthermore, the key antifibrotic drugs were included in China's national medical insurance catalog as early as 2018. Combined with the achievements of universal health coverage in China, this has significantly reduced treatment costs and alleviated the financial burden on ILD patients. We believe this factor can partially explain the divergent trend in the ASMR of ILD in China compared to European countries [22].

The ASMR of ILD is relatively high in economically developed regions such as urban areas and eastern regions of China, and the mortality rate showed a significant downward trend during the study period. In contrast, trends in ASMR for ILD were not significant in the less economically developed regions, such as rural, central, and western regions. This discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that economically developed regions possess more abundant medical resources, enabling superior diagnosis and treatment of ILD. These regions attract a larger number of ILD patients seeking medical care, particularly those with end-stage ILD. Current research indicates that air pollution is a significant risk factor for chronic respiratory diseases, including ILD. Although considerable efforts have been made in China to improve air quality, pollution continues to exacerbate the burden of chronic respiratory conditions, particularly in economically developed regions [21]. Concurrently, the limited diagnosis and treatment capacities in less developed regions result in the underestimation of the burden of ILD-related deaths in these areas. This may be due to the limited awareness of ILD among medical staff in less developed regions, leading to ILD being misdiagnosed as other respiratory diseases [23]. For instance, findings from a study on pneumoconiosis patients across nine provinces in China indicated that rural patients were more likely to seek care at county-level hospitals, whereas urban patients tended to opt for municipal or provincial hospitals [24]. This combination of factors likely contributes to the higher burden of ILD mortality observed in economically developed regions compared to their less developed counterparts. Meanwhile, this phenomenon is inconsistent with the results of the GBD 2019 study, where the Global Burden of Disease showed higher age-standardized incidence rates of ILD in high Socio-demographic Index (SDI) regions than in low SDI regions and lower ASMR than in low SDI regions since 2006 [1]. Another study of coal workers’ pneumoconiosis showed that the burden of coal workers’ pneumoconiosis increased significantly only in regions with low SDI or in low- and middle-income countries or in developing countries [25]. Therefore, we hypothesize that the main reason for the inconsistency of our results with those of other countries is that we included a wider variety of ILD, and the burden of death for different subtypes of ILD varies, which in turn contributes to the differences in results.

It is worthy of note that non-ILD comorbidities such as cerebrovascular disease, lung cancer, and diabetes mellitus were important causes of death in Chinese patients with ILD. Previous studies have shown that patients with ILD have a significantly higher burden of comorbidities and a significantly increased risk of death than age- and sex-matched controls without ILD [6]. Among these, cardiovascular disease, lung cancer, and diabetes are common comorbidities in patients with ILD, which is consistent with our current findings [26–29]. The risk of developing comorbidities with ILD increases with age, which undoubtedly increases the treatment burden for patients. Studies have shown that 43% of adults over the age of 50 and 66% of adults over the age of 75 are taking five or more medications [30]. The high treatment burden of patients with ILD also has a serious impact on the quality of life of their families and households [31]. From a public health perspective, we should focus on early diagnosis of ILD, especially in high-risk groups with a history of exposure to risk factors such as dust and asbestos, to slow the progression of ILD. Meanwhile, the allocation of medical resources in some provinces and rural areas in the central and western regions should be strengthened to provide more support for patients with ILD while improving their health management [2, 32].

Strengths and limitations

First, the mortality data of ILD have good national representativeness. By accurately calculating the ASMR of ILD based on the deaths due to the underlying causes of ILD in the NMSS, the characteristics of ILD deaths in China, the temporal trends and the geographical differences can be captured. Second, this study was not limited to a single subtype of ILD, but included all types of related diseases as comprehensively as possible to analyze the real situation of ILD in China. Third, this study is the first nationwide large-sample study in China to evaluate the mortality comorbidities of ILD based on individual cases of ILD-related deaths, providing multi-dimensional epidemiological descriptions for ILD related deaths in China.

This study has limitations. First, all the data in this study were based on death certificates and we were unable to analyze ILD related risk factors such as smoking and dust exposure. However, we stratified the calculation for ILD in different genders, urban and rural areas, regions, and age groups, which can help us to some extent to identify the high-risk groups to compensate for the inability to analyze the risk factors. Second, although the NMSS has been continuously improved, there is still some underreporting in some western surveillance sites, especially in western rural areas. This may affect the results in some areas, but we adjusted the mortality rate based on the annual underreporting surveys at the surveillance sites. Third, we were unable to obtain outpatient and hospitalization information for ILD death cases, which prevented us from fully assessing the comorbidity characteristics of ILD in China. However, we conducted analysis of the comorbidity patterns of ILD based on the conditions in the cause-of-death chain, which helped to provide a preliminary understanding of the comorbidity patterns of ILD in China. Fourth, although our classification of ILD was based on the latest guidelines, the reliance solely on ICD codes for subtyping introduces certain limitations. Fifth, as this study was based on general population surveillance data, it did not allow for the analysis of mortality characteristics in high-risk populations.

Conclusion

The mortality rate of ILD in China showed a downward trend from 2013 to 2021, though it remained higher among males, residents in economically developed regions, and the elderly. Strengthening health monitoring for high-dust-exposure occupational groups, optimizing early diagnosis of ILD, and balancing the distribution of medical and health resources across regions are critical for reducing the ILD mortality burden in China.

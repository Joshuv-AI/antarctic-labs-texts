Comparison of the effects of antipsychotic and combined with non-invasive brain stimulation on blood lipids in patients with schizophrenia
Authors: Guangya Yang, Xue Yang, Hongbing Liu, Tianlan Wang, Jiabing Wu, Zhonghong You, Hongfang Zhang, Yanbo Xu, Chengyan Ma, Zhengkai Li, Miaoling Tao, Changmei Zheng, Yuqiong Li, Qinghua Wang, Wangmin Li, Youyan Zha, Limin Xin, Yusong Wu, Bin Bi, Chunying Liu, Li You, Guoping Wu, Qingxiu Wang, Xiaorui Wang, Song Chen, Hongfang Li, Zhe Dong, Yunlong Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to compare the impact of antipsychotic treatment (APs) alone and a combination of antipsychotics and non-invasive brain stimulation (APNIBS) on blood lipid levels in patients with schizophrenia.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted. General demographic information and clinical and laboratory data, were collected from hospitalized patients who had received at least one lipid profile test after initiating therapy at the psychiatric hospital between January 2021 and October 2023. Lipid profiles, including triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL-C), were measured at baseline and multiple time points during treatment. The longitudinal changes in lipid profiles within each group (from baseline to each follow-up) and the differences in these trajectories between the two groups were assessed using linear mixed-effects models.ResultsA total of 1,171 patients were included (APs group: n = 704; APNIBS group: n = 467). At baseline, mean levels of TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C were within normal ranges, whereas TG was slightly elevated in the APs group. Linear mixed-effects model analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in the longitudinal trajectories of change between the two treatment groups for any lipid parameter (TG, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C; all between-group P > 0.05). However, within-group analyses indicated divergent patterns: TG levels exhibited a progressive increase from baseline in the APs group at all follow-ups, while showing a more variable trajectory in the APNIBS group, with a increase at the second assessment after adjustment followed by decreasing trends later. Similarly, the APNIBS group demonstrated reductions from baseline in TC and LDL-C at the third and fourth tests, whereas no such reductions were observed in the APs group.ConclusionExploratory within-group analyses revealed favorable lipid trends in the APNIBS group. However, no statistically significant between-group differences were observed in longitudinal lipid trajectories. These hypothesis-generating findings warrant further prospective investigation into the potential role of NIBS in blood lipids management in schizophrenia.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Schizophrenia, Antipsychotic treatment, Non-invasive brain stimulation, Blood lipids

Introduction

Schizophrenia is a mental disorder that ranks among the 20 top causes of disability according to the World Health Organization and affects about 1% of the population [1]. People with schizophrenia frequently experience physical comorbidities, unhealthy lifestyles, and various cardiovascular risk factors. Together with disparities in physical healthcare, and disease-related symptoms, lead to significant burden and premature mortality [2]. Compared to the general population, patients with schizophrenia have a 12-fold higher mortality risk [3]. A key reason for high mortality risk is the associated abnormal blood lipid profile and concomitant cardiovascular disease (CVD) with a 2- to 3-fold increased risk, which increases the susceptibility of schizophrenia patients to cardiac mortality [4].

Antipsychotics serve as the cornerstone of pharmacological treatment for schizophrenia. Given the chronic nature of the disorder requiring sustained management, the adverse effects associated with long-term antipsychotics use, particularly metabolic abnormalities such as dyslipidemia, pose significant health risks to patients [5]. A 5-year observational study involving 82 patients treated with atypical antipsychotics reported that 36.6% (n = 30) developed secondary type 2 diabetes, accompanied by significant weight gain and elevated levels of triglycerides (TG), very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) [5]. Further supporting this, a prospective investigation in sixty antipsychotic-naïve patients with schizophrenia and matched first-degree relatives demonstrated that antipsychotics treatment was significantly associated with the emergence of dyslipidemia after twelve weeks [6].

Nevertheless, it remains controversial whether dyslipidemia in schizophrenia is solely attributable to antipsychotics. A nationwide population-based retrospective cohort study revealed that lipid abnormalities were already present in individuals with schizophrenia prior to the initiation of antipsychotics therapy, suggesting a disease-inherent component [7]. This is further corroborated by a multicohort case-control study, which identified a distinct plasma lipidomic profile in patients with schizophrenia that was independent of symptom severity, medication use, and demographic or environmental variables [8]. Evidence from Mendelian randomization analyses also indicates a potential causal relationship between dyslipidemia and psychiatric disorders, positing lipid abnormalities as possible biomarkers and therapeutic targets in schizophrenia [9]. A systematic review noted that hyperlipidemia did not significantly influence treatment outcomes in schizophrenia, reinforcing the complexity of its role in the disorder [10]. Importantly, a re-evaluation of data from first-episode psychosis patients and unmedicated individuals with schizophrenia indicated that medication effects were restricted to specific lipid classes and of limited magnitude [6]. Collectively, these findings underscore the multifactorial etiology of dyslipidemia in schizophrenia, implicating both intrinsic disease mechanisms and iatrogenic effects. Therefore, baseline lipid assessment and continued monitoring are recommended in patients with schizophrenia initiating antipsychotics treatment [7].

Antipsychotics are recommended for long-term use to prevent relapse in schizophrenia [11]. However, prolonged antipsychotics treatment places a substantial economic burden on patients, healthcare systems, and society at large [12]. Given the chronicity of the illness, the multidimensional functional impairment associated with it, and the absence of disease-modifying interventions, there is a pressing need to develop novel therapeutic strategies for this population.

Non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) encompasses a range of techniques designed to modulate cortical excitability and neural network activity without surgical intervention [13]. As an emerging neuromodulatory tool in psychiatry, NIBS applies electrical or magnetic energy through the scalp to alter neuronal electrochemical activity, thereby facilitating or inhibiting specific brain circuits to potentially improve associated symptoms and cognitive functions [14, 15]. Established NIBS modalities include electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), and transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS), low-frequency pulsed electrotherapy and transcutaneous cranial electrical stimulation, all of which have been investigated for symptom alleviation and cognitive enhancement in neuropsychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia [16]. Accumulating evidence supports the safety, tolerability, and clinical utility of NIBS techniques in both research and clinical settings for schizophrenia [17].

While the therapeutic potential of NIBS for core psychiatric symptoms is increasingly recognized, its effects on metabolic parameters remain markedly understudied. Preliminary evidence suggests that certain NIBS modalities may influence metabolic processes. For example, rTMS has been reported to modulate cerebral glucose metabolism [18]. Nevertheless, the extent to which NIBS can affect systemic lipid metabolism is largely unknown. This gap in knowledge is particularly relevant given the high prevalence of antipsychotic-induced dyslipidemia and its contribution to cardiovascular morbidity.

Therefore, the primary rationale of this study is to investigate whether adjunctive NIBS, specifically, its combination with antipsychotics, can ameliorate adverse lipid profiles in patients with schizophrenia, beyond its established effects on psychiatric symptoms [19]. By comparing lipid changes between patients treated with AP monotherapy group (APs) versus AP + NIBS combination therapy group (APNIBS), this study aims to provide preliminary evidence on the metabolic benefits of NIBS, an area that represents a novel and clinically significant avenue for research.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective study was designed to collect general demographic, clinical, and laboratory data from hospitalized schizophrenia patients in the Lincang Psychiatric Hospital (Lincang, Yunnan, China) between January 2021 and October 2023. The inclusion criteria were: (1) had a diagnosis of SCZ based on the Structured Clinical Interview of DSM-IV; (2) were aged 18 years or older; (3) for the APNIBS group: receipt of at least one full course of NIBS therapy in addition to antipsychotic treatment, with the most recent complete hospitalization record selected for analysis in cases of multiple NIBS treatment episodes; for the APs group: no history of receiving any NIBS intervention, with the most recent complete hospitalization record selected for analysis in cases of multiple eligible hospitalizations; (4) received at least one follow-up with blood lipid laboratory examinations after initiating therapy. Exclusion criteria included: (1) patients with tumour or autoimmune disease, liver cirrhosis, etc.; (2) those on cholesterol-lowering medications; (3) Patients who had received interventions other than medication or physical therapy (e.g., recreational therapy, psychotherapy, music therapy) were excluded. Informed consent was not required owing to the use of de-identified data. This study was approved by the the Ethics Committee of Lincang Psychiatric Hospital [(2024 Research No. 0105].

Measures

Data collection and lipid assessment

The collected data encompassed general demographic information (age, sex, ethnicity), records of antipsychotic medications usage during hospitalization, and all pertinent details related to lipid profiling, including serum levels of total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and the timing of blood sampling. As the real-world hospital database primarily captured medication administration records rather than specific combination regimens, antipsychotics exposure was quantified using total cumulative dose (calculated as: medication type × treatment duration × daily dosage) to compare potential differences in antipsychotics load between the two groups.

Venous blood samples were collected after an overnight fast into 10-milliliter EDTA tubes. Within one hour of collection, samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min to isolate plasma, which was subsequently stored at -80 °C until biochemical analysis. TC and TG levels were quantified using enzymatic assays (Trinder method; Bayer Diagnostics) on a Cobas Mira automated analyzer (Hoffmann-LaRoche). HDL-C and LDL-C concentrations were measured via a direct homogeneous method using an AU5400 analyzer (Beckman Coulter, CA, USA).

Characterization of non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) interventions

This pragmatic study evaluated the adjunctive effect of NIBS to antipsychotic therapy in a real-world setting. The APNIBS group allows the evaluation of the overall utility of the combined treatment strategy against standard pharmacotherapy alone. The specific NIBS interventions used, which varied in parameters and mechanisms, are detailed below.

Transcutaneous Cranial Electrical Stimulation: Delivered using an NK-IA multifunctional therapeutic device. Stimulation was applied via pulse electrodes positioned bilaterally over the supraorbital foramina. Parameters were set as follows: frequency 120 Hz, pulse width 1 ms, output amplitude 80–150 V (adjusted to the maximum level tolerated by the patient without inducing discomfort beyond mild orbicularis oculi contraction). Sessions lasted 20–30 min and were conducted once daily.

Electrodes were positioned with the anode placed over the major vertebral point and the cathodes positioned bilaterally over the renal Yu acupoints. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.3). Continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical data are expressed as counts and proportions. Normality for continuous variables was evaluated with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Accordingly, baseline comparisons used independent samples t-tests for normally distributed variables and the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normal variables; categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test.

To analyze the longitudinal changes in blood lipid (TG, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C), linear mixed-effects models were employed. This approach was selected to appropriately account for within-subject correlation due to repeated measurements and to handle irregular follow-up intervals. Time was modeled as a categorical fixed effect (first, second, third, and fourth tests), with the first test as the reference. The time points represented the sequence of lipid measurements obtained during hospitalization. For each lipid parameter, two primary models were fitted: (1) a within-group model to estimate change from baseline at each post-treatment time point, including a random intercept for each patient; and (2) a between-group model to compare longitudinal trajectories, which included fixed effects for APs and APNIBS groups, time, and a group-by-time interaction term, along with a random patient intercept. All between-group models were adjusted for the following covariates selected a priori: age, gender, ethnicity, and total prescribed drug dosage. Effect estimates are reported as regression coefficients (β) with their corresponding P-values. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparison of changes of blood lipid at different testing time point

Linear mixed model analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in the longitudinal trajectories of change between the APNIBS and APs groups for any lipid parameter (all P > 0.05). However, the within-group analyses revealed distinct metabolic trajectories. For TG, longitudinal models showed opposing directional trends: a consistent and statistically significant increase over time in the APs group contrasted with a more variable trend in the APNIBS group. Notably, TG levels exhibited a decreasing trend at the third and fourth assessments. The APNIBS group also demonstrated significant reductions from baseline in both TC and LDL-C specifically at the third and fourth tests, whereas the APs group showed no significant change or an increasing trend at these time points. Discussion

This retrospective study compared longitudinal changes in conventional blood lipids between patients with schizophrenia receiving APs alone versus those treated with APNIBS. There were no statistically significant differences in the longitudinal trajectories of change between the two treatment groups for TG, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C. When contextualized against established reference ranges for low-risk atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) prevention in China [20], baseline values and levels at subsequent testings of TC, LDL-C, and HDL-C in both groups predominantly remained within desirable or ideal limits (TC < 5.2 mmol/L, LDL-C < 3.4 mmol/L). This suggests that, at the group level, the dyslipidemic burden associated with antipsychotic treatment in this inpatient group did not reach thresholds typically prompting immediate lipid-lowering intervention per general population guidelines.

However, a detailed examination of within-group trajectories revealed a clinically meaningful divergence. The APs group exhibited a significant and progressive increase in TG across all follow-up tests, a well-documented adverse effect of antipsychotic treatment [7, 21, 22], with mean values approaching or exceeding the clinical edge-elevation threshold (≥ 1.7 mmol/L). This pattern aligns with meta-analytic evidence highlighting the role of antipsychotic treatment in elevating TG [21, 22] and with lipidomic studies identifying TG as the lipid class most robustly associated with antipsychotic exposure [23]. In the APNIBS group, TG showed a more variable trajectory. Notably, covariate adjustment revealed a significant increase at the second test, followed by non-significant decreasing trends at the third and fourth tests. The TG levels at fourth test in the APNIBS group were maintained within the desirable range (< 1.7 mmol/L). This attenuation of hypertriglyceridemia is clinically salient, given that elevated TG constitutes a core component of metabolic syndrome and a significant mediator of cardiovascular disease risk in schizophrenia populations [4, 6]. The exploratory within-group findings related to TG metabolism may inform hypothesis generation for future studies.

In exploratory within-group analyses, the APNIBS group exhibited significant reductions from baseline in both TC and LDL-C at the third and fourth tests, whereas the APs group showed no significant change or an increasing trend. This lipids pattern of APs aligned with reports that certain second-generation antipsychotics elevate TC and LDL-C [21, 22]. Furthermore, the time-course of HDL-C decline differed between groups. While both groups showed an initial decrease, consistent with known antipsychotic effects on HDL [21], the trajectory in the APs group showed signs of stabilization by the final test, whereas the decrease remained significant in the APNIBS group. The clinical implication of this sustained HDL-C reduction in the APNIBS group requires careful interpretation, as HDL-C dynamics in schizophrenia are complex and not always inversely related to cardiovascular risk [24].

Although no statistically significant between-group differences were observed in lipid trajectories over time, exploratory within-group analyses revealed patterns of lipid changes in the APNIBS group that merit consideration as hypothesis-generating observations. These patterns resonate with emerging mechanistic evidence suggesting that NIBS may influence peripheral lipid metabolism through central pathways, though such mechanisms remain speculative in the context of the present null between-group findings. For instance, Longo et al. [25] demonstrated that tDCS applied to the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex reduced hepatic triglyceride accumulation and pro-inflammatory cytokines in obese rats, independent of caloric intake, suggesting a brain-liver communication pathway potentially involving autonomic or neuroendocrine signaling. This aligns with the concept that neuromodulation can have systemic metabolic effects. Furthermore, Surowka et al. [26] showed that tDCS induces remodeling of brain lipid composition and ion homeostasis in hypothalamic regions governing appetite and energy balance, providing a plausible central mechanism for modulating systemic lipid metabolism. The link between central nervous system stimulation and peripheral lipid changes is further supported by observations that tDCS can alter systemic biomarkers in other disorders [27].

The differential lipid trends observed may also intersect with the inflammatory pathophysiology shared by schizophrenia and metabolic dysregulation. Chronic low-grade inflammation is a hallmark of both conditions, and certain antipsychotics may exacerbate this state. NIBS, particularly tDCS, has been shown to possess anti-inflammatory properties in preclinical models [28]. Collectively, these mechanistic studies support the plausibility of brain-periphery interactions that could influence lipid metabolism, warranting further investigation in adequately powered clinical trials.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the non-randomized design introduces the possibility of confounding by indication, as the decision to administer adjunctive NIBS may have been influenced by clinical factors (e.g., illness severity or treatment resistance) that could also independently affect metabolic outcomes. Second, data on several key metabolic covariates, including smoking status, body mass index (BMI), physical activity levels, and family history of dyslipidemia, were not systematically collected. Although the standardized inpatient setting minimized acute variations in diet and structured exercise between groups, the absence of these measures limits our ability to fully account for potential residual confounding. Third, cumulative antipsychotic dose was used as a composite exposure metric, a pragmatic approach given the prevalence of polypharmacy and dynamic treatment regimens, but precludes disaggregation of medication-specific metabolic effects. This limitation is particularly relevant given the well-established heterogeneity in metabolic risk profiles across individual antipsychotic agents. Moreover, cumulative dose was calculated based on actual prescribed doses without conversion to standardized equivalents (e.g., clozapine equivalents), further limiting comparability across different agents. Fourth, the NIBS interventions consisted of heterogeneous modalities with distinct parameters and targets. While this reflects an assessment of the overall adjunctive strategy, it precludes conclusions regarding the specific efficacy of any single NIBS technique. Finally, lipid assessments were conducted at clinically determined time points rather than according to a standardized protocol, and were limited to four conventional parameters (TC, TG, LDL-C, HDL-C), which may not fully capture the complexity of lipid metabolism. These methodological features, inherent to the real-world observational design, should be considered when interpreting the associations reported.

Conclusion

In this real-world retrospective study, no statistically significant between-group differences were observed in longitudinal lipid trajectories. Exploratory within-group analyses revealed patterns of lipid changes in the APNIBS group, including attenuation of TG elevation, maintained TG within the desirable range, and reductions in TC and LDL-C, contrasting with the progressive dyslipidemia observed in the APs group. However, given the absence of significant between-group differences, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Further prospective studies are warranted to determine whether NIBS augmentation confers metabolic benefits in this population.

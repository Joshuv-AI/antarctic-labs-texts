The Effectiveness of Peer Interaction-based Edutainment in Oral Health Promotion among Elementary School Students: A Systematic Review
Authors: Rara W Gayatri, Taufan Bramantoro, Dini Setyowati, Nor A M Nor, Nurnaningsih H Ulfa, Farah Paramita, Febrita P Heynoek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectives:To systematically review the effectiveness of edutainment with a peer-interaction approach in improving knowledge, attitudes, and oral health behaviors among elementary school students (aged 6–12 years).Methods:A systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science was conducted through July 2025 following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Methodological quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal tool. Studies were included if they utilized interactive games or social learning methods to promote oral health.Results:Eight studies met the inclusion criteria, with a predominance of quasi-experimental designs. Various edutainment modalities were identified, including board games (Snake and Ladder and Dental Jumanji), role-playing, and drama. All eight studies reported significant gains in oral health knowledge. Four studies demonstrated improved oral health attitudes, and five reported positive behavioral changes in brushing frequency. However, the quality of evidence was limited by short follow-up durations and reliance on self-reported outcome measures in several studies.Conclusion:Peer-interaction edutainment is a promising strategy for school-based oral health promotion, offering high engagement and immediate knowledge gains. However, given the predominance of quasi-experimental data and heterogeneous outcome measures, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Well-designed randomized controlled trials are required to establish the long-term sustainability of these behavioral changes.

INTRODUCTION

Dental caries remains the predominant oral health problem worldwide. The global average prevalence of permanent dental caries is 29%, with around 2 billion cases.[1] Meanwhile, the prevalence of untreated deciduous (primary) dental caries reaches 514 million cases.[1] Globally, studies conducted by Kazeminia et al.[2] showed that the prevalence of permanent dental caries among children was 58.8% in Asia, 44.1% in Europe, 48.9% in the Americas, 58.9% in Africa, and 54.9% in Australia.

Knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors are key determinants of oral health, yet studies consistently show that elementary school children have low levels of these factors.[3,4] Educational interventions, particularly dental health education and training, have proven effective in improving these outcomes.[5] As such, education remains a cornerstone of health promotion initiatives.[6]

In recent years, edutainment, an educational approach that integrates learning with entertainment, has gained increasing attention.[7,8] Edutainment strategies may involve traditional or computer-based games, sports activities, television viewing, storybook reading, and other engaging formats.[9] Such activities can be implemented individually or through peer-interaction, promoting both independent and collaborative learning. Peer-interaction within edutainment is particularly important because collaborative learning enhances comprehension and knowledge retention, while also increasing students’ motivation and engagement as they solve problems together.[10] Moreover, positive peer engagement supports the development of social skills and emotional well-being, which are crucial components of effective school-based health education.[11,12] Evidence from recent reviews highlights the effectiveness of game-based interventions in dental health promotion.[13,14] However, comprehensive evaluations of edutainment employing peer-interaction approaches remain limited. Addressing this gap is essential to advance the development of diverse, school-based health promotion strategies that leverage peer-to-peer engagement.

This study aims to systematically review the effectiveness of edutainment with a peer-interaction approach in improving knowledge, attitudes, and oral health behaviors among elementary school students.

We systematically reviewed published articles to address the research question: “Which forms of edutainment employing a peer-interaction approach are effective in enhancing elementary school students knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors toward oral health?” Eligible studies were selected according to the following criteria:

This systematic review was not prospectively registered in International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews. (PROSPERO) or any other registry.

SEARCH STRATEGY

The literature for this review was identified through three electronic databases: Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed. The search strategy employed relevant keywords related to dental health promotion, edutainment, student, knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors, combined using Boolean operators. The database search commenced on 23 June 2025 and was completed on 7 July 2025. The full search strategy used in PubMed was as follows:

(edutainment OR entertain* OR play* OR game*) AND (dental OR oral OR teeth OR tooth) AND (health) AND (promot* OR educat* OR learn*) AND (student* OR child* OR “6–12 year*”) AND (know* OR understand* OR attitude* OR percepti* OR aware* OR behavio* OR skill* OR practi*).

From the eligible studies, the following data were extracted: the author, year of publication, country, study aims, age of the participants, total number of participants, type of edutainments related to peer-interaction, study designs, quality appraisal, outcome measures, effect size, and key findings. Effect size measures were extracted as reported in the original studies, and standardized effect sizes were not calculated when the primary studies did not provide sufficient data. The methodological quality of the included studies was appraised using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Tools for RCTs and quasi-experimental studies.[15,16] To facilitate interpretation, quality categories were defined separately for each tool:

RESULTS

A total of 3722 articles were retrieved from the databases. After removing 1251 duplicates and screening 2441 titles and abstracts, 30 articles were selected for full-text review. Of these, 22 articles were excluded for not meeting the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The remaining eight articles underwent quality assessment, and all met the eligibility standards. STUDY CHARACTERISTICS

The included studies were conducted in Asia, specifically in Iran[4] and India,[4] published between 2014 and 2024. Three used RCT designs and five employed quasi-experimental designs. Based on JBI appraisal, five studies were rated as high quality and three as moderate quality. Overall, the studies demonstrated acceptable methodological rigor, though variations existed in blinding, allocation concealment, and follow-up reporting.

PUBLICATION BIAS

Formal assessment of publication bias using funnel plots or statistical tests was not performed because the number of included studies was small (n = 8), which would make such analyses unreliable. Nevertheless, the risk of publication bias was minimized by conducting a comprehensive search across multiple databases and applying predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria.

TYPE OF EDUTAINMENTS RELATED TO PEER-INTERACTION

As shown in Table 1, various forms of edutainment were utilized. The most common was a modified snake and ladder game, either alone or in combination with flashcards.[17,18,19] Role-play was also frequently applied,[20,21,22] while drama[17] and pantomime[23] were used in individual studies. Other approaches included the “Saying on the Phone” (telephone whisper) game,[23] Dental Jumanji game,[24] and replica-making activities.[22]

THE EFFECTIVENESS OF ORAL HEALTH PROMOTION BASED ON EDUTAINMENT AND PEER-INTERACTION

Several studies demonstrated that edutainment and peer-interaction significantly improved children’s oral health knowledge, attitude, and behavior, although the magnitude of effects varied across intervention types and study designs.

Knowledge: Eight studies reported positive effects on knowledge.[17,18,19,20,21,22,23,24] The snake and ladder game was more effective than drama and flashcards.[17] The Dental Jumanji game combined with lectures enhanced knowledge more effectively than lectures alone, with moderate to large effects reported at post-test and follow-up.[24] Other approaches, including the telephone whisper game, pantomime, role-play, and making replicas, consistently increased knowledge scores, although standardized effect sizes were not always reported.[20,21,22,23] Interventions combining multiple edutainment tools, such as snake and ladder games with flashcards, also demonstrated sustained knowledge improvements over time.[18,19]

Attitude: Four studies demonstrated improvements in attitude.[17,20,21,23] Role-playing interventions were associated with significant post-intervention improvements in attitude, with small-to-moderate effects reported in studies that quantified magnitude.[20,21] Game-based and peer-interaction approaches, including the snake and ladder game, telephone whisper game, and pantomime activities, also demonstrated favorable attitude changes compared with conventional methods, although effect sizes were inconsistently reported across studies.[17,23]

Behavior: Five studies showed positive effects on behavior.[17,20,21,22,23] Role-playing was consistently associated with behavioral improvements in three studies.[20,21,22] The snake and ladder game demonstrated greater behavioral gains than drama and flashcards in comparative analyses.[17] Other peer-interaction approaches, including the telephone whisper game, pantomime, and replica-making activities, were also associated with improvements in children’s oral health practices, although standardized effect sizes were not consistently reported.[22,23]

DISCUSSION

Overall, this systematic review indicates that peer-interaction-based edutainment interventions are effective in improving oral health-related knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors among elementary school students. These results are consistent with those of previous studies showing that play-based educational approaches and peer-learning have positive effects on children’s oral health–related knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors.[25,26,27] Integrating play-based and peer-learning approaches within classroom oral health activities is also aligned with World Health Organization guidelines supporting health-promoting school programs.[28]

The effectiveness of peer-interaction-based edutainment interventions may be explained by several underlying mechanisms. Based on Albert Bandura’s Social Learning Theory, children learn complex social behaviors, emotional regulation, and problem-solving through imitation, observation, and modeling during play and interaction with their environment.[29] Play-based learning promotes active participation, emotional engagement, and enjoyment, which are known to enhance attention and facilitate knowledge acquisition and retention.[30,31,32] Moreover, peer-based education fosters a collaborative and supportive learning environment in which students can learn through observation, discussion, and shared experiences, allowing them to benefit from diverse perspectives and learning styles.[33,34] These processes may help translate improved knowledge into more positive attitudes and sustained oral health behaviors.

However, it is important to note that edutainment and peer-interaction represent conceptually distinct components. While edutainment refers to the use of entertaining educational media, peer-interaction introduces a social learning mechanism that may independently influence outcomes. As most included studies implemented these components concurrently, the relative contribution of game mechanics versus peer presence could not be isolated in this review, and the findings should, therefore, be interpreted as reflecting the effectiveness of combined peer-interaction-based edutainment approaches.

Among the peer-interaction-based edutainment strategies identified, snake and ladder games were the most frequently used, likely due to their low cost, ease of implementation, and capacity to promote engagement, rule-based learning, and motivation.[35,36] Role-playing enhances learning by simulating real-life interactions, enabling students to explore emotions, values, and decision-making processes through experiential learning.[37,38] Flashcards support learning through visual cues and repeated retrieval practice, which strengthen recall, engagement, and positive learning attitudes.[39,40] Drama supports knowledge construction and skill development through social engagement, while pantomime reinforces learning by translating abstract concepts into physical action, strengthening memory and emotional involvement.[41,42]

The telephone whisper game involves passing a whispered message sequentially among players, often resulting in information distortion driven by cultural attractors, whereby messages tend to drift toward more familiar or socially salient concepts over repeated retellings.[43,44,45] Although this activity can raise awareness of miscommunication and promote attentiveness and engagement during the learning process,[43,46] its capacity to support sustained knowledge, attitude, or behavior change appears limited. The observed message distortion, combined with the absence of ongoing reinforcement and deeper cognitive processing,[47,48] suggests that the telephone whisper game may primarily facilitate short-term engagement rather than long-term knowledge retention.

Dental Jumanji, adapted from the board game originally conceptualized by Chris Van Allsburg, promotes learning through playful peer-interaction, competition, and shared experiences that support motivation and knowledge acquisition.[49,50] Replica-making similarly supports learning by enabling interaction with tangible representations of abstract concepts, improving engagement, retention, and higher-order cognitive skills.[51,52,53]

Taken together, despite the wide diversity of intervention formats, the included studies demonstrate convergence around shared educational mechanisms. Through interventions, effectiveness was consistently supported by active participation, emotional engagement, repetition of key messages, and peer-mediated social learning. This suggests that the observed benefits are less dependent on the specific edutainment format and more likely driven by common pedagogical functions that facilitate engagement, modeling, and reinforcement of oral health behaviors.

From a practical perspective, the findings support the integration of peer-interaction-based edutainment into school-based oral health promotion programs. The interventions reviewed were generally simple, low-cost, interactive, and feasible for implementation within existing school settings. Incorporating peer-interaction may enhance student motivation and engagement while fostering a supportive environment that encourages positive oral health behaviors.

The included studies demonstrated several strengths, including acceptable methodological rigor and the use of diverse intervention types, which provide broader perspectives on edutainment approaches beyond peer-interaction. However, several limitations should be acknowledged, such as the predominance of studies conducted in Asian settings, which may limit the transferability of findings to other geographic and socioeconomic contexts. Additionally, the predominance of quasi-experimental designs, with participant allocation based on preexisting classrooms or schools rather than individual randomization, increases the risk of selection bias and limits causal inference. Finally, because most interventions combined game-based and peer-interaction components, the independent effects of each element could not be disentangled.

This systematic review has several strengths, including a focused synthesis of peer-interaction-based edutainment interventions in oral health promotion among elementary school students. A comprehensive search across three databases and a transparent study selection process enhanced the rigor of the review. Nevertheless, limitations should be acknowledged, including the absence of prospective protocol registration (e.g., PROSPERO), which may reduce transparency. Additional limitations include the restriction to English-language publications, which may introduce language bias, and variability in the reporting of intervention components, which posed challenges in identifying and comparing peer-interaction elements across studies.

Future research should address the methodological limitations identified in this review by employing well-designed studies with standardized outcome measures and longer follow-up periods to evaluate the sustainability of behavioral changes and enable meta-analytic synthesis. In summary, this systematic review highlights peer-interaction-based edutainment as an effective approach for improving oral health-related knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors among elementary school students and as a promising strategy for strengthening school-based oral health promotion.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this review identified several peer-interaction-based edutainment approaches such as the snake and ladder game, flashcards, role-play, drama, pantomime, the telephone whisper game, Dental Jumanji, and replica-making that have been used to promote oral health among elementary school students. These methods demonstrated effectiveness in enhancing students knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors related to oral health. However, due to methodological heterogeneity across studies, a quantitative synthesis such as meta-analysis was not feasible. Future research employing standardized outcome measures and rigorous study designs is recommended to strengthen the evidence base.

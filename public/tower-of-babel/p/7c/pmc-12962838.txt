A Novel Homozygous CUL7 Variant in an Iranian Patient Expands the Genetic Spectrum of 3 M Syndrome
Authors: Maryam Arefzadeh, Bahareh Rabbani, Saeideh Abdolahpour, Farnoosh Emami, Farzaneh Abbasi, Tannaz Masoumi, Siamak Mirab Samiee, Ali Rabbani, Nejat Mahdieh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACT3 M syndrome (3MS) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by severe prenatal and postnatal growth retardation, distinctive facial features, and skeletal abnormalities, while cognitive development remains unaffected. This study reports a novel homozygous pathogenic variant in the CUL7 gene identified in an Iranian girl with clinical features consistent with 3MS. Clinical evaluations involved monthly monitoring of the patient's height and weight to assess her growth pattern. Genetic testing, including whole exome sequencing (WES) and Sanger sequencing, was conducted. The identified variant was further analyzed through segregation and bioinformatics analyses. The patient exhibited severe growth retardation in both height and weight, along with a prominent forehead, round eyes, delayed walking, and delayed bone age. WES revealed a pathogenic homozygous variant in the CUL7 gene, c.4145_4146delinsT p.(Asn1382Ilefs*11), which was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Segregation analysis demonstrated that both parents and grandmothers were heterozygous carriers of the same mutation, consistent with an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. The clinical and genetic findings confirmed the diagnosis of 3MS in the patient. This case highlights the pivotal role of molecular diagnostics in confirming rare genetic disorders, especially in populations with high rates of consanguinity. The study expands the genetic spectrum of CUL7 mutations associated with 3MS and underscores the importance of advanced genetic testing in overcoming diagnostic challenges. Further research into the functional consequences of CUL7 mutations may provide valuable insights for developing targeted therapeutic strategies for this rare disorder.

Introduction

Skeletal dysplasias represent a heterogeneous group of genetic disorders characterized by abnormalities in bone and cartilage development, with over 400 distinct conditions identified to date [1]. Among these, 3 M syndrome (3MS; OMIM #273750) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder classified under the “Slender Bone Dysplasia Group” [2]. First described by Miller et al. in 1975 [3], 3MS is characterized by severe prenatal and postnatal growth retardation, distinctive facial features, and skeletal abnormalities, including long and narrow tubular bones, elongated vertebral bodies, and fleshy heel pads [4]. Notably, cognitive development and endocrine function typically remain unaffected. Despite its clinical recognition, no specific treatment exists for 3MS, highlighting the importance of early diagnosis and genetic counseling, particularly in populations with high rates of consanguineous marriages, where autosomal recessive disorders are more prevalent [5, 6, 7, 8].

The diagnosis of 3MS relies on clinical and radiological evaluations; however, its phenotypic overlap with other conditions, such as intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), often complicates diagnosis [5, 9]. Molecular confirmation through genetic testing is therefore essential. To date, mutations in three genes—CUL7, OBSL1, and CCDC8—have been implicated in 3MS, with CUL7 mutations accounting for approximately 70% of cases [10]. The CUL7 gene encodes Cullin 7, a core component of the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, which plays a critical role in the ubiquitin‐proteasome pathway [11]. This pathway regulates the degradation of key proteins, including p53, cyclin D1, and IRS‐1, a signaling molecule involved in IGF1 and insulin pathways [12]. Disruptions in CUL7 function have been linked to impaired cell proliferation, skeletal development, and placental formation, contributing to the pathogenesis of 3MS and related disorders, such as Yakut short stature syndrome [13].

The CUL7‐RING complex, in conjunction with the 3 M complex [14], is essential for maintaining genome integrity and regulating microtubule dynamics [15]. While the CUL7‐RING complex alone cannot ubiquitinate p53/TP53 [16], it facilitates its recruitment for ubiquitination by the CUL1‐RBX1 complex, ultimately leading to proteasomal degradation [17, 18, 19]. Additionally, CUL7 interacts with other proteins, such as CUL9, to modulate processes like Golgi apparatus structure and dendritic patterning in the brain [14]. Mutations in CUL7 also impact placental development, highlighting its role in trophoblast cell epithelial‐mesenchymal transition [20]. Advances in genetic research have significantly improved our understanding of the pathophysiology of 3MS and other genetic disorders, paving the way for potential therapeutic strategies, including gene therapy and pharmacological interventions [21].

This study describes a novel homozygous mutation in the CUL7 gene, c.4145_4146delinsT p.(Asn1382Ilefs*11), identified in a 15‐year‐old Iranian girl with clinical features consistent with 3MS. The findings emphasize the importance of molecular diagnostics in resolving diagnostic challenges and highlight the need for increased awareness and genetic counseling in populations with high consanguinity rates. By elucidating the genetic and clinical aspects of this rare disorder, this study contributes to the growing body of knowledge on 3MS and its underlying mechanisms.

Materials and Methods

Clinical Evaluations

A 15‐year‐old Iranian girl was referred to Children's Medical Center in Tehran due to significant growth retardation and short stature. The patient exhibited congenital skeletal abnormalities, particularly in the lower limbs, and had a family history of short stature. Her parents were consanguineous (first cousins), and the family pedigree revealed a history of short stature among second‐ and third‐degree relatives (Figure 1B). Over several years, the patient's height and weight were measured monthly to monitor her growth pattern. Clinical findings were documented using standardized terminology from the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO).

Genetic Analysis

Given the patient's clinical presentation and family history, genetic testing was recommended for the proband, her parents, and both maternal and paternal grandmothers. Whole Exome Sequencing (WES) was performed using the SureSelect Human All Exon V6 kit (Agilent Technologies), which targets approximately 60 megabases of the human exome, covering over 99% of regions in the CCDS, RefSeq, and Gencode databases. Genomic DNA was fragmented, enriched, and sequenced on the Illumina platform, achieving an average coverage depth of 100‐fold. Approximately 97% of the target bases had a coverage depth exceeding 10‐fold.

Bioinformatics analysis included base calling, alignment of reads to the GRCh37/hg19 reference genome, and filtering of low‐quality and ambiguous reads. Variants were identified and prioritized based on their presence in pathogenic variant databases (HGMD, ClinVar) and their minor allele frequency (MAF < 1%) in the gnomAD database. Coding exons and ±20 base pair flanking intronic regions were evaluated, and all relevant inheritance patterns were considered. Identified variants were classified into five categories (Class 1: benign to Class 5: pathogenic) according to the guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) [22].

To ensure the accuracy of the findings, primer design for the CUL7 gene was performed, and low‐quality variants and small insertions/deletions were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. This process ensured that a specificity of > 99.9% was maintained for all reported variants. The reference sequence for the CUL7 gene was NM_001168370.1. The primers' sequences were as follows: forward primer: GCACATTGACCAGCAGATCCAG and reverse primer: GACAAGCACAGACACTTCTGGC.

Ethical Considerations

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Children's Medical Center, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Results

Clinical Findings

The 15‐year‐old Iranian girl presented with severe growth retardation, both in height and weight, which was evident from her growth chart (Figure 1A). Her height and weight measurements consistently fell below the 3rd percentile for her age, indicating significant growth failure. The clinical examination revealed characteristic features of 3MS, including a prominent forehead, delayed walking, a broad face, and round eyes. Skeletal abnormalities included insufficient growth in the patient's height, and delayed bone age. These findings were consistent with a clinical diagnosis of 3MS. The family pedigree (Figure 1B) illustrates the consanguineous relationship between the parents and the presence of short stature in second‐ and third‐degree relatives, further supporting the genetic basis of the disorder.

Genetic Findings

WES identified a novel homozygous variant in the CUL7 gene: c.4145_4146delinsT p.(Asn1382Ilefs*11). This variant results in a frameshift mutation starting at codon 1382, leading to a premature stop codon 10 positions downstream. According to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines, this variant was classified as pathogenic (Class 1) (Figure 1C). The variant was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, ensuring its validity. According to the ACMG guidelines, the c.4145_4146delinsT variant meets multiple criteria including PVS1 (Null variant in a gene where loss of function is a known mechanism of disease), PM2 (Extremely low frequency) and PM3 (detected in trans with a pathogenic variant, or in a homozygous or compound heterozygous state in affected cases), classified as a pathogenic variant. Family segregation analysis revealed that both parents and both grandmothers were heterozygous carriers of the same variant, supporting an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern (Figure 1B). No other pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in the CUL7, OBSL1, or CCDC8 genes were identified in the proband.

Phenotypic Correlation

The patient's clinical features, including severe growth retardation, skeletal abnormalities, and characteristic facial dimorphisms, were consistent with the phenotypic spectrum of 3MS type 1 (OMIM #273750). The identification of a homozygous pathogenic variant in the CUL7 gene provided molecular confirmation of the diagnosis.

Discussion

The identification of a novel homozygous pathogenic variant, c.4145_4146delinsT p.(Asn1382Ilefs*11), in the CUL7 gene in a 15‐year‐old Iranian girl with 3MS provides valuable insights into the genetic and clinical spectrum of this rare disorder. The frameshift mutation identified in this study is predicted to result in a truncated protein, likely disrupting the normal function of Cullin 7, a key component of the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex involved in protein degradation and cellular regulation [13].

The clinical presentation of the patient, including severe prenatal and postnatal growth retardation, characteristic facial features, and skeletal abnormalities, is consistent with previously reported cases of 3MS [5, 6]. The growth chart (Figure 1A) clearly illustrates the significant growth failure observed in this patient, further supporting the diagnosis. The absence of cognitive impairment in this case is also consistent with the typical phenotype of 3MS, highlighting the syndrome's specific impact on physical growth and skeletal development [7].

The consanguineous background of the patient's family underscores the increased risk of autosomal recessive disorders in populations where consanguineous marriages are common. This case emphasizes the importance of genetic counseling and public awareness in such communities to reduce the prevalence of rare genetic disorders [23]. Early diagnosis through molecular testing, as demonstrated in this study, is crucial for accurate genetic counseling and family planning [8, 24].

The functional consequences of CUL7 mutations are not fully understood, but studies suggest that disruptions in the ubiquitin‐proteasome pathway may impair critical cellular processes, including cell proliferation, skeletal development, and placental formation [13, 14]. The CUL7‐RING complex, in particular, plays a role in the degradation of proteins such as IRS‐1, which is involved in insulin and IGF1 signaling pathways [15]. Dysregulation of these pathways may contribute to the growth retardation observed in 3MS.

This study adds to the growing body of literature on CUL7 mutations and their association with 3MS. The identification of novel variants, such as the one reported here, expands the genetic spectrum of the disorder and enhances our understanding of its molecular basis. It is possible that this mutation may represent a common variant in our population, similar to mutations in other genes that are population‐specific, as we have previously reported. For example, mutations in the GJB2 gene in various Iranian subpopulations [25], mutations in the NAXE [26] and CYP2A22 mutations, which are prevalent in different populations [27]. Future research should focus on elucidating the functional impact of specific CUL7 mutations and exploring potential therapeutic strategies, such as gene therapy or targeted pharmacological interventions, to address the underlying molecular defects.

Conclusion

This case highlights the importance of integrating clinical, radiological, and genetic approaches for the accurate diagnosis of rare genetic disorders like 3MS. It also underscores the need for increased awareness and genetic counseling in high‐risk populations to mitigate the impact of consanguinity on the prevalence of autosomal recessive disorders. Continued research into the molecular mechanisms of 3MS and related conditions will be essential for developing effective treatments and improving patient outcomes.

Experimental Studies on the Crack Initiation and Propagation of Hydraulic Fracturing with Different Coal Structure Combinations
Authors: Du Liu, Longyong Shu, Yanbin Wang, Xiaoming Ni, Zhonggang Huo, Weihua Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoal structure is one of the key geological factors that affects the effect of coal reservoir stimulation. Based on the geological spatial combination characteristics, thickness, and proportion of different coal structures, the coal reservoir is divided into different coal structure combination types. The hydraulic fracturing device is used to carry out indoor fracturing experiments and dissect the crack initiation and expansion characteristics with different coal structure combinations. The results show that the coal structure combination is of the binary type (undeformed coal + granulated coal or cataclastic coal + granulated coal), and the undeformed coal (cataclastic coal) can overcome the tensile strength and minimum principal stress when it is driven by the high-pressure fluid. The undeformed coal (cataclastic coal) ruptures and forms longitudinal cracks. The increasing proportion of granulated coal inhibits crack expansion and promotes the transverse deformation of coal. The interface contact point between the undeformed coal (cataclastic coal) and granulated coal easily fractures along the cross section of the specimen. When the coal structure combination is the triplex type (undeformed coal + granulated coal + cataclastic coal or cataclastic coal + granulated coal + cataclastic coal), the undeformed coal or cataclastic coal is transformed. The forming fractures in the undeformed coal (cataclastic coal) can cut through the soft coal when the thickness of the undeformed coal (cataclastic coal) is large and the thickness of granulated coal is thin. On the contrary, it is not easy to cut through. When the coal structure combination is granulated coal + cataclastic coal + granulated coal, the cataclastic coal fails under shear stress and forms the crack along the cross section of the coal sample. The granulated coal inhibits the crack expansion at both ends. The research results have an important indicative significance for further understanding the fracture initiation and propagation mechanism of hydraulic fracturing with complex coal structures in coal reservoirs.

Introduction

Hydraulic fracturing is an important technology to increase the permeability of the target reservoir to achieve efficient oil and gas recovery.1−4 At present, some developed countries, led by the United States, have commercialized unconventional natural gas energy such as coalbed methane, shale gas, and tight sandstone gas by using hydraulic fracturing technology.5−9 However, the use of conventional fracturing technology easily affects the reservoir stimulation effect when it transforms complex coal seams. Among them, coal structure is one of the key geological factors affecting coalbed methane production.10−13 Some scholars have conducted in-depth studies on the classification and identification of coal structure,14−18 but the influence of coal structure on hydraulic fracturing is relatively general. In general, undeformed coal and cataclastic coal are suitable for conventional hydraulic fracturing technology, and their effect is better.19 On the contrary, the reconstruction effect of granulated coal and mylonitized coal is worse.20 The influence of coal structure on hydraulic fracturing is mainly manifested in the following two aspects: (1) When the geo-stress is the same, the more serious the coal structure’s broken degree is, the less likely it is to form a new fracture at the end of crack, and the fracture extension length is shorter. (2) The more serious the coal structure’s broken degree is, the easier it is to encounter the hole collapse in the fracturing process and produce a large amount of pulverized coal and coal slime, which causes serious damage to the reservoir and affects the productivity and high-quality development of coalbed methane.

A coal seam presents serious heterogeneity in the longitudinal direction.21−23 It is usually composed of many different coal structures, not a single type. Coal with different coal structures has different physical and mechanical properties. The interlayer interaction and fracture propagation effect vary the coal structure combinations. Wang et al. studied the effect of natural fractures on the initiation and propagation of hydraulic fracturing fractures and found that the forming complex fractures vary with the different types of natural fractures in the coal reservoir.24 Wu et al. analyzed the morphology and influencing factors of the fractures by true triaxial hydraulic fracturing experiments with similar materials. The vertical stress and interface properties are the main factors affecting the T-shape fractures and I-shape fractures.25 Lyu et al. explained the influence of natural cracks on the expansion of hydraulic fracturing fractures in coal seams by the classification of natural cracks in coal reservoirs.26 Lu et al. conducted a series of in situ compression tests under continuous injection liquid conditions and monitored the coal samples by the nuclear magnetic resonance methods.27 Mou et al. compared and analyzed the microfracture characteristics under the different geo-stresses before and after hydraulic fracturing, which showed that strong heterogeneity and large horizontal stress difference can inhibit the fracture extension and propagation.28 Yan et al. conducted the SC–CO2 fracturing experiment under the thermo-hydro-mechanical coupling conditions and dissected the effects on the crack propagation of injecting pressure, crustal stress, injection rate, and the fracturing fluid temperature.29 Liu et al. analyzed the formation of fractures of bright coal and dull coal during the hydraulic fracturing process and the fracture changes of their interface through physical experiments and numerical simulation.30 Ren et al. analyzed the relationship between coal structure and in situ stress based on the data of 80 coalbed methane production wells and interpreted its influence on hydraulic fracturing.31 Zhao et al. studied the law and mechanism of crack propagation in thin coal seam groups under the multilayer combined fracturing and revealed the effect on the forming fracture about the perforating location.32 He et al. compared the difference of crack initiation and propagation between supercritical carbon dioxide and water and analyzed the influence of stress on crack initiation and propagation by the physical experiment.33 Jiang et al. used acoustic emission technology to study the propagation law of hydraulic fracture across the interface and established the model of fracture across the interface extension based on different cross angles, interface friction, stress state, and other influencing factors.34 Wang et al. studied the physical and mechanical properties with the complex coal structure combination by the stress–strain curves and acoustic emission techniques.35

According to the previous research of scholars, being based on the geological space combination characteristics of coal seams and the thickness and proportion of different coal structures, this paper carries out the hydraulic fracturing test research on different coal structure combinations and identifies the propagation characteristics of fractures with different coal structure combinations. It can provide certain theoretical and technical support for the fracturing reform of the coal reservoir with a complex coal structure.

Coal Samples and Experiments

Collection and Preparation of Coal Samples

The coal samples are from the No. 3 coal seam of Changping Mining, Jincheng, Shanxi Province. The new underground coal samples are sealed and packed with plastic wrap, which are transported to the laboratory for test and analysis. Macro-observation of coal samples with different coal structures shows that the undeformed coal is a complete block, with no developing external cracks and no forming crumpled surface. The coal is hard and not easily broken. The macro-lithotype of coal is clear and distinguishable, and the main coal is dull coal (Figure 1a). Compared with the undeformed coal, the external fissure of cataclastic coal is relatively developed, and the forming fracture surface has a slight displacement and movement. The coal is relatively complete and presents clumps, and the macro-lithotype of coal can be identified clearly. The coal is mainly dull coal and relatively hard, but it is easily broken (Figure 1b). Granulated coal is mainly granular and small with disordered bedding and low strength. When twisted by hand, it can be broken into fragments, and sometimes sliding mirrors and scratches can be seen (Figure 1c). Mylonite coal is mainly scaly, and the cracks cannot be directly observed. The crumpling slippery surface is quite developed, and there are scratches and steps on the surface (Figure 1d). Meanwhile, a field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) was used to observe the microscopic geometric morphology of undeformed coal, cataclastic coal, granulated coal, and mylonite coal, showing the surface morphological characteristics of the four coal structures at microscopic scales (Figure 2). Finally, the mechanical parameters (Chinese National Standard GB/T 23561.8-2009, test instrument YDAW-300) and proximate analysis of different coal structures were tested (the test was carried out in accordance with GB/T Chinese National Standard 212-2008). The test results are shown in Table 1. As can be seen from Table 1, the different coal structures have different mechanical properties, and the coal has different resistances to deformation under various external forces. The undeformed coal has large elasticity modulus and strong resistance to deformation. The elasticity modulus of granulated coal is small, and its resistance to deformation is weak. The elasticity modulus of cataclastic coal is intermediate, and its resistance to deformation is also intermediate.

The cylindrical coal samples for UC and CC are made by using a wire cutting numerical control machine tool. The coal samples are mainly made in the vertical direction of the bedding plane to reduce their mechanical damage degree. Their diameter standards are ø50 mm × 25 mm, ø50 mm × 50 mm, and ø50 mm × 75 mm. The GC specimen is made by crushing raw coal. The raw coal is sifted out 60 to 80 mesh, which is made by water under the pressure of 200 KN. Their diameter standards are ø50 mm × 25 mm, ø50 mm × 50 mm, and ø50 mm × 75 mm. It is feasible to adopt this method for the GC sample, which can guarantee the physical and mechanical properties of GC. In the test, glues A and B are used to bond and combine the coal sample specimens with different coal structures, which are eventually assembled into the standard specimen with the diameter of 50 mm × 100 mm (Figure 3). Glues A and B have little influence on the results.

Sample preparation process of different coal structure combinations.

At the center of coal sample specimens, a microdrill is used to drill into the hole. The drilling bit diameter is 4 mm, and the drilling hole needs to be uniform velocity. The drilling is stopped when the hole’s depth is about 20–40 mm. It is used as a prefabricated hole. The experiment is conducted at room temperature, 20 °C. The fracturing fluid is water with a viscosity of about 1 Pa·s. The white tracer is added, which cannot damage the sample.

Fracturing Test Program

Fracturing Test System

The hydraulic fracturing experiment device is independently developed by the Sinopec Petroleum Exploration and Production Research Institute (Figure 4a). The device is loaded with a triaxial confining pressure chamber. The fracturing pressure and fracture extension pressure can be monitored online. The device can provide the following: temperature 0–150 °C, overlying formation pressure 0–70 MPa, injection pressure 0–45 MPa, and injection displacement 0–45 mL/min. The schematic diagram of the fracturing device is shown in Figure 4b. The experiment device is mainly composed of A, B, and C pumps. The A pump uses water to provide confining pressure, the B pump provides axial pressure, and the C pump provides injected fluid pressure. The C pump is used to inject fracturing fluid to drain the air of the confining chamber and line to eliminate interference with the results before the test is started.

Hydraulic fracturing test device.

The Hydraulic Fracturing Test Schemes

According to the preparation method and size regulations of fracturing specimens, the combination of UC and GC, the combination of CC and GC, and the combination of UC, CC, and GC are prepared. The fracturing specimens and geometric characteristics are shown in Figure 5.

Geometric model of the fracturing specimen.

The coal samples are bound and fixed with high-temperature-resistant thermoplastic sleeves. The fixed coal samples are placed in the gripper. Confining pressure and axial pressure are applied to the fracturing specimens by the confining pressure pump and axial pressure pump, respectively. The constant pressure loading is used in the test, and the distilled water is used as the pressure conduction medium. According to the actual geological conditions and characteristics,12 the confining pressure is 10 MPa, the axial pressure is 12 MPa, and the stress difference is 2 MPa. In order to avoid the failure of the coal sample, the confining pressure and axial pressure are loaded alternately. In the test, the fracturing fluid is piped into the hole by the high-pressure water pump, and the constant flow injection mode is adopted. The injection pressure is up to 45 MPa, and the fracturing fluid injection rate is 20 or 30 mL/min.

In the fracturing process, high-frequency pressure sensors are mainly used to record and monitor the fluid pressure, which can accurately collect the change of the fluid pressure of the crack initiation and fracture propagation. After the fracture of the specimen, the limit pressure of the fracturing fluid gradually stabilizes, and the experiment is stopped immediately. Meanwhile, the change characteristics of the axial pressure and confining pressure are observed and recorded during the fracturing process. Hydraulic fracturing test parameters are shown in Table 2.

Results and Discussion

Hydraulic Fracturing Test Analysis of Different Coal Structure Combinations

Hydraulic Fracturing Test of UC and CC Combinations

Mainly for the hydraulic fracturing experiment of the UC and CC combinations, it is divided into UC 75 mm + CC 25 mm, UC 50 mm + CC 50 mm, and UC 25 mm + CC 75 mm. From Figure 6, the fracturing process can be divided into three stages: (1) Rising stage: the pressure and self-strength of coal are overcome by continuous injection of the fracturing fluid into the combined specimens through a high pressure pump. (2) Crack initiation stage: the fracture initiation condition of the composite specimen is reached, and the specimen cracks when the injection pressure rises to a certain value. The pressure is called the initiation pressure, and the time to reach the critical initiation pressure is called the initiation time. (3) Constant flow stage: after the rupturing of the coal sample, the fracturing fluid will flow out along the crack. Meanwhile, the fluid pressure decreases rapidly until it drops to zero when the injection of the fracturing fluid is stopped.

Curves of injection pressure and time in the UC and GC combinations.

According to Figure 6, under the stress conditions of the axial pressure of 12 MPa and confining pressure of 10 MPa, the initiation pressure of UC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, UC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, and UC 25 mm + GC 75 mm is 15.76, 12.12, and 10.34 MPa, respectively. The initiation time is 64 s, 54, and 34 s, respectively. It shows that the mechanical strength of coal combination specimens increases, the initiation pressure increases, and the initiation time prolongates when the thickness of UC increases and the thickness of GC decreases (the total thickness of coal is constant).

Figure 7 describes the fracture propagation morphology of three different coal structure combination specimens. A long and curved crack is formed in UC and is parallel to the axial stress when the UC 75 mm + GC 25 mm is driven by the hydraulic fracturing fluid. At the same time, the secondary curve cracks are generated near GC, and its shape is parabola. GC is not cut through. For the UC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, two oblique cracks are formed in UC, which are nearly parallel and have a certain angle with the axial stress. Additionally, the fractures are split along the interface of UC and GC, and the fracturing fluid is filtered out. The coal cannot be effectively transformed, but GC is wet by the fracturing fluid. For the UC 25 mm + GC 75 mm, a diagonal crack is formed in UC and bifurcated at the end of the crack. But it terminates at the interface between UC and GC. The GC has serious deformation; it twists and breaks along the transverse direction of the coal, forming a curved crack under the action of fluid pressure. Some deformation also occurs at the bottom of GC, forming similar cracks.

Crack propagation in combination specimens of UC and GC under hydraulic fracturing. (The cracks are circled with a red dashed square. A sketch of the crack is shown in the black dashed box.)

Through the analysis of hydraulic fracturing for the three different coal structure combinations, it easily forms long cracks in UC due to its characteristics of extreme brittleness and hardness. Besides, when the thickness of the coal is fixed, the larger the thickness of UC is, the larger the proportion is, and the longer the cracks form. GC seriously affects the crack extension. The thicker GC is, the more prone GC is to shear failure. It easily forms transverse fractures, resulting in fracturing fluid filtration, which is unable to form long fractures.

Hydraulic Fracturing Test of CC and GC Combinations

Similarly, a hydraulic fracturing experiment is carried out on the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, and CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm. As can be seen from Figure 8, the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm can also be divided into three stages during the fracturing process, which have little difference from the UC+GC and have obvious initiation pressure. For the CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm and CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm, they can be divided into two stages. The first stage is the same as that of UC+GC, both of which are in the rising stage. Before 50 s, the injection pressure presents a linear increase with time. Then the second and third stages are combined into one stage, and there is no obvious initiation pressure during the fracturing process. After the injection pressure rises for a short time, the fracturing fluid is discharged along the crack. The injection pressure begins to decrease. The injection pressure decreases rapidly after the injection of the fracturing fluid is stopped. The CC+GC is under the condition of maintaining the same axial pressure and confining pressure as the UC+GC. The initiation pressure of the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, and CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm is 15.25, 13.58, and 12.98 MPa, respectively, and the initiation time is 70, 81, and 106 s, respectively. According to the test results, when the thickness of coal is the same, the thickness of CC increases, the thickness of GC decreases, and the initiation pressure increases, but the initiation time is shortened, which is contrary to UC+GC. The main reason is that the pores of CC are relatively developed compared with those of UC. Under the action of fluid pressure, CC is easier to crack. At the same time, GC with a certain thickness obstructs the flow of fracturing fluid and the deformation of coal. The thickness of GC increases, leading to the prolongation of the crack initiation time.

Curves of injection pressure and time in CC and GC combinations.

Figure 9 describes the fracture propagation morphological characteristics of the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, and CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm. For the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, a macroscopic curved long crack is formed in CC, and some secondary short cracks are around the long crack when it is driven by the fracturing fluid. The long crack cut through GC. A transverse crack is generated in the middle of CC, which has a certain angle with the long crack. For the CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, the fracture is only generated on the wall of CC during the fracturing process, and the phenomenon of “falling off” occurs. Meanwhile, the coal wall of the specimen is relatively wet, and there are light white tracers, indicating that the fracturing fluid filtration is serious in the fracturing process and no effective fracture is formed. For the CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm, two parallel short fractures are formed in CC and are approximately parallel to the axial stress. The tracer accumulates in GC, indicating that the fracturing fluid has serious filtration along the weak surface of the coal. The coal experiences transverse deformation and is unable to form cracks.

Crack propagation in combination specimens of CC and GC under hydraulic fracturing.

According to three sets of experiments, it is found that microfractures develop in CC, and the fracturing fluid is easily filtered out. Some short fractures are formed. With the increasing thickness of GC, fracturing fluid and coal are mixed and filtered out. The tracer accumulation affects crack formation.

Hydraulic Fracturing Test of UC, CC, and GC Combinations

The hydraulic fracturing experiment is carried out on the combination of UC, CC, and GC, which can be divided into UC 50 mm + GC 25 mm + CC 25 mm, CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm, and GC 25 mm + CC 50 mm + GC 25 mm. The first two combinations can be referred to as “two hard clips and a soft combination style”, and the latter is referred to as “two soft clips and a hard combination style”.

As can be seen from Figure 10, the overall shape of the fracturing curve is similar. However, in the first stage, the injection pressure rising rate of UC 50 mm + GC 25 mm + CC 25 mm is higher than that of CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm. The initiation pressure is 13.56 and 13.03 MPa, respectively, and the initiation time is 96 and 98 s, respectively. The initiation pressure of the former is slightly higher than that of the latter, and the initiation time is slightly later than that of the latter, because the thickness of UC is higher than that of CC and the thickness of GC is less. GC delays the initiation time of CC. For the GC 25 mm + CC 50 mm + GC 25 mm, it can be roughly divided into the injection pressure rising stage and falling stage. The injection pressure decreases rapidly at 66 s, which is mainly related to the development of primary fractures of CC.

Curves of injection pressure and time in UC, CC, and GC combinations.

Figure 11 describes the crack propagation morphology of three combined specimens. For the UC 50 mm + GC 25 mm + CC 25 mm, it forms the complex grid crack on the surface of UC. In addition, at the part between UC and GC, it is disconnected along the cross-section direction, and GC is not cut through. GC is wet by the fracturing fluid. At the joint of the interface between GC and CC in the lower part, the curve transverse shear short fractures are formed in CC. GC between UC and CC is cut along the cross section, and the head face is attached with a white tracer, indicating that the fracturing fluid has migrated to GC and percolated to the lower CC.

Crack propagation in combination specimens of UC, CC, and GC under hydraulic fracturing.

For the CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm, no obvious longitudinal cracks are formed, but the interface of the upper CC and GC is split along the transverse part, resulting in serious fracturing fluid filtration. There is an obvious tracer on the coal surface at the top of the middle GC, and a transverse zigzag crack is formed at 1/3 of GC. However, there is no obvious change in the bottom CC, which indicates that the thick layer of GC absorbs a large amount of fracturing fluid. It wets GC and inhibits the downward flow of the fracturing fluid. It affects the crack formation and expansion of the lower CC. For the GC 25 mm + CC 50 mm + GC 25 mm, there are some tracers on the surface of the upper GC. The intermediate CC forms cross transverse cracks under the action of shear stress. The secondary cracks are formed around the cross cracks, which are approximately parallel and perpendicular to the axial stress, and the cracks do not extend in the longitudinal direction. A secondary short crack is formed near the junction of CC and GC in the lower part of the specimen. The main reason is that the upper and lower ends of the composite specimen are soft GC, and the total thickness of GC is the same as that of CC. The injection pressure increases, and GC at both ends inhibits the expansion of longitudinal cracks when the fracturing fluid is injected into GC. The shear failure occurs in the relatively hard CC, and the transverse cross cracks are formed when the injection pressure increases to a certain extent.

The Combination Mode Effect on Crack Initiation and Propagation

The same fracturing technology is used to transform the reservoir, and the transformation effect will often appear as a large deviation when the coal structure is combined in different ways. For the CC 25 mm (top) + GC 75 mm (bottom) and GC 25 mm (top) + CC 75 mm (bottom), the fracturing effect is studied. According to Figure 12, the initiation pressure is 12.98 and 10.38 MPa, respectively. The initiation time is 106 and 64 s, respectively. The former initiation pressure is higher than that of the latter, and the initiation time is prolonged. The main reason is that the pores of CC develop, and the filtration rate of fracturing fluid is large when the former coal sample drills in CC and injects high pressure water for fracturing. Meanwhile, the fracturing fluid accumulates at the interface of CC and GC. The surface of GC is only wet, and the fracturing fluid cannot flow efficiently for coal construction. The latter uses high-pressure water to fracture GC, and the fracturing fluid accumulates in GC. It is unable to form effective cracks. The coal is disconnected at the interface joint of GC and CC when the fracturing fluid accumulates to a certain amount and holds pressure for a long time. And the fracturing fluid flows out quickly, which is not conducive to the formation of long cracks.

Curves of injection pressure and time in different combinations of specimens.

The experimental results show that the coal structure is the same, but the superimposed ways are different, which will also affect the fracturing effect, as shown in Figure 13. The drilling and fracturing should be avoided in GC, which easily leads to the hole collapse and fracturing fluid filtration. Therefore, the coal reservoir cannot guarantee a good fracturing effect when it is only known the thickness or proportion of the coal structure. It is clearly necessary to identify the longitudinal distribution of the coal structure. According to the distribution characteristics of the coal structure, the reasonable fracturing construction design is adopted, which ensures the right fracturing construction.

The Injection Flow Rate Effect on Crack Initiation and Propagation

The influence of different injection flow rates is studied on the crack initiation mechanism of different coal structure combinations. The coal samples are CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm and CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm. The injection pressure data are collected under different injection flow rates, and the curves are drawn, as shown in Figure 14.

Curves of injection pressure and time with different injection flow rates.

During the fracturing process, the change of injection flow directly affects the strain rate, initiation pressure, and initiation time. For the CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm, the initiation pressure is 13.59 MPa when the injection flow is 30 mL/min, and the initiation pressure is 12.17 MPa when the injection flow is 20 mL/min. For the CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm, the initiation pressure is 15.49 MPa when the injection flow is 30 mL/min, and the initiation pressure is 13.03 MPa when the injection flow is 20 mL/min. It shows that with the increase of the injection flow rate, the energy goes up per unit time by the high-pressure pump. Under the action of injection pressure, the hole wall deforms until it breaks. The energy gradually accumulates and diffuses in the borehole. The accumulated elastic energy is stored in the coal and, finally, released by the hole wall deformation. With the increasing of the strain rate, the strength of the coal sample increases, the initiation pressure increases, and the initiation time shortens. Due to the small size of the coal specimen, the crack initiation is generally fast, the injection pressure changes rapidly, and the crack forms rapidly.

The Crack Formation Mechanism of Different Coal Structure Combinations

For the UC 75 mm + GC 25 mm and the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, they have obvious longitudinal long fractures. Because the coal is mainly composed of the hard coal (UC and CC), it can overcome the tensile strength and minimum principal stress under the high-pressure fracturing fluid. And the hard coal ruptures and forms high conductivity fractures, which is conducive to the reservoir reform. Compared with the UC 75 mm + GC 25 mm and the CC 75 mm + GC 25 mm, the thickness and proportion of the hard coal decrease, and the thickness and proportion of the soft coal (GC) increase for the UC 50 mm + GC 50 mm and CC 50 mm + GC 50 mm. Only some short and narrow cracks are formed on the surface of hard coal, and the cracks cannot extend along the coal. The increase of the soft coal inhibits the crack expansion. Transverse shear deformation occurs under high pressure, and shear failure occurs easily along the cross section at the interface of the hard coal and soft coal. For the UC 25 mm + GC 75 mm, CC 25 mm + GC 75 mm, and GC 75 mm + CC 25 mm, they are mainly the soft coal and cannot form the cracks with high conductivity under the driving of fracturing fluid. The fracturing fluid is diluted in the soft coal, and the soft coal becomes strongly deformed under the action of external force, which results in the failure of the coal specimen. For the UC 50 mm + GC 25 mm + CC 25 mm and CC 25 mm + GC 50 mm + CC 25 mm, they are “two hard clips and a soft combination style”. The forming cracks in the hard coal can cut through the soft coal when the fracturing is carried out in the hard coal. (The thickness of the hard coal is large, and the thickness of the soft coal is relatively thin.) On the contrary, it is not easy to cut through. For the GC 25 mm + CC 50 mm + GC 25 mm, it is “two soft clips and a hard combination style”. The crack splits along the cross section of the hard coal. The reason is that the soft coal at both ends inhibits the crack expansion, and the hard coal experiences failure under the action of shear stress. It is shown in Table 3.

Comparison between the Experiment Results

According to the experiment results, it is shown that the combination of the coal structure, the thickness, and proportion have a great influence on the reservoir reconstruction. The complexity of the coal structure will affect the effect of reservoir reconstruction, so it is necessary to adopt reasonable fracturing technology to ensure the effect of reservoir reconstruction.

Conclusions

(1)In the process of engineering practice, the perforation and the fracturing should be avoided as far as possible in the soft coal when the thickness of the soft coal is relatively thick, because the thick soft coal has a great influence on the reservoir reconstruction. The soft coal has little influence on the reservoir stimulation when the thickness of the hard coal is large and the thickness of the soft coal is thin. The reconstruction should be carried out in the hard coal as far as possible, and the soft coal has some influence to a certain extent when the thickness difference between the soft coal and hard coal is not large.

In the process of engineering practice, the perforation and the fracturing should be avoided as far as possible in the soft coal when the thickness of the soft coal is relatively thick, because the thick soft coal has a great influence on the reservoir reconstruction. The soft coal has little influence on the reservoir stimulation when the thickness of the hard coal is large and the thickness of the soft coal is thin. The reconstruction should be carried out in the hard coal as far as possible, and the soft coal has some influence to a certain extent when the thickness difference between the soft coal and hard coal is not large.

(2)The coal structure combination types should be divided according to the geological spatial combination mode, thickness, and proportion of different coal structures when the coal reservoir is with the complex coal structure. Then reasonable fracturing technology should be adopted for coal with different combination characteristics to ensure the effect of reservoir reconstruction.

Extracorporeal Counterpulsation Therapy Enhances Neurological Outcomes in Patients Experiencing Acute Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Jiaxuan Li, Xinhui Jia, Xinyi Chen, Jie Hu, Xun He, Yueyu Zhang, Yi Tang, Juncang Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:The aim of this study was to assess the effects of extracorporeal counterpulsation therapy (ECP) on the short-term outcomes of patients with acute ischemic stroke in the mid-stroke phase.Methods:A total of 171 patients with acute ischemic stroke from the Department of Neurology at our hospital were selected and randomly assigned to 2 groups: 84 patients in the ECP group and 87 patients in the control group. Both groups received standard medication and rehabilitation. In addition, extracorporeal counterpulsation was incorporated into the treatment for the ECP group. At the conclusion of the treatment and after 90 days, both groups were evaluated based on the NIHSS and MRS scores.Results:No statistically significant differences were detected between the NIHSS and MRS scores of the 2 groups before and following treatment (P>0.05). However, on the 90th day, the NIHSS and MRS scores of the ECP group were significantly lower than those of the comparison group (P<0.05). The difference in the proportion of patients with NIHSS scores ≤1 between the 2 groups was 20.8%, whereas the absolute difference in MRS scores was 14.1%.Conclusion:Extracorporeal counter therapy effectively enhances neurological function in patients with ischemic stroke, thereby improving their ability to perform activities of daily living and overall prognosis.Key Words: acute ischemic stroke, external counterpulsation therapy (ECP), neurological function, prognosis

Stroke ranks as the second leading cause of mortality globally, with over 2 million new cases each year, imposing a considerable economic burden. Despite extensive research over several decades, stroke continues to incapacitate and claim numerous lives annually. Ischemic stroke is the predominant type, constituting ∼70% of all strokes.1,2 The principal interventions for ischemic stroke include tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)-facilitated thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (EVT). The efficacy of thrombolysis is limited by a designated time frame for administration, resulting in a restricted number of patients arriving at the hospital in time to receive its benefits. Moreover, EVT is applicable to a limited subset of stroke patients, and successful recanalization does not invariably lead to an optimal neurological outcome.3,4 Traditional pharmacological research has failed to yield successful therapeutic results, necessitating the exploration of novel treatment modalities to increase the advantages for patients with ischemic stroke.

ECP is a noninvasive therapy for ischemic heart disease that is sanctioned by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This apparatus serves as a noninvasive circulatory support system designed to prevent and treat cardiovascular disease. In ECP therapy, 3 pairs of pneumatic cuffs are placed on the patient’s calves, thighs, and buttocks. These cuffs administer ECG-triggered diastolic pressures to the lower limbs via a sequential inflating procedure, commencing from the distal point and progressing toward the proximal point during diastole. The pressure is alleviated immediately before the commencement of systole. Elevated diastolic pressure in the lower extremities improves venous return and augments cardiac output, whereas presystolic deflation diminishes peripheral pressure burden during diastole, facilitating cardiac ejection and reducing the oxygen consumption of the heart. ECP induction leads to distinct, stable, and reproducible cerebral arterial waveforms, which alters blood flow distribution velocities. In addition, it increases cerebral perfusion pressure, cerebral blood flow, and the shear rate that can be generated. Multiple studies have shown that ECP has a systemic hemodynamic effect, leading to significant improvements in systemic blood flow. This result is attributed to the unique dual-pulse mechanism of the therapy, which promotes the redistribution of blood flow throughout the body.5–7

Studies have shown that ECP therapy markedly improves perfusion to vital organs, such as the brain, liver, and kidneys, by increasing diastolic blood pressure.6 ECP therapy may provide advantages for ischemia-related illnesses, including ischemic cerebrovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, and ocular ischemic disease, by promoting organ perfusion, mitigating symptoms, and improving patients’ quality of life.8–10 ECP therapy has been identified as a possible treatment for ischemic stroke. Buschmann et al11 reported that short-term ECP therapy greatly augmented the mean blood flow velocity and mean arterial blood pressure in the middle cerebral artery (MCA). In a canine model of cardiac arrest, Hu et al12 reported that ECP therapy improved neurological function by augmenting cerebral blood flow and promoting microcirculatory recovery following the restoration of autonomic circulation. Li and colleagues revealed that extracorporeal counterpulsation therapy increases diastolic blood pressure, augments coronary blood flow, and promotes mean area wall shear stress (WSS) while minimizing the oscillatory shear index (OSI) in stenotic artery segments; this was accomplished by modeling the architecture of stenotic coronary arteries utilizing a geometrical, multiscale methodology. These findings present a novel method by which ECP can efficiently address and manage arterial stenosis.13 Xu and colleagues reported comparable findings in a separate investigation. The current utilization of ECP in the treatment of ischemic stroke predominantly focuses on its impact on cerebral hemodynamics. We aimed to investigate its effect on the short-term prognosis of individuals in the acute phase of ischemic stroke.14

METHOD

Research Design

This randomized controlled experiment involved 195 patients who were diagnosed with acute ischemic stroke and hospitalized at the Second People’s Hospital of Hefei City within 7 days after symptoms started, from May 2023 to June 2024. The participants were randomly allocated to either an ECP group, comprising 104 patients, or a control group, consisting of 97 patients. All patients exhibited differing levels of neurological impairment and provided informed consent. We collected data on patient demographics, clinical diagnoses, laboratory tests, and imaging results during their hospitalization. We subsequently evaluated the degree of neurological impairment, quality of life, and functional capacity. Our study aimed to investigate the impact of early ECP treatment on the short-term outcomes of patients diagnosed with AIS.

Research Target

Inclusion criteria: (1) the diagnosis of stroke followed the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Ischemic Stroke (2018). Ischemia was confirmed through either computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). (2) The participants were aged between 18 and 85 years. (3) The duration of the disease was 7 days, and all participants presented with varying degrees of neurological deficits, including partial limb dysfunction, reduced muscle strength, and unsteady gait. (4) The mental clarity of the patient was intact, and vital signs were stable. (5) Consciousness was normal, allowing for cooperation with treatment and assessment during the study.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) lower extremity deep vein thrombosis, active thrombophlebitis; (2) had received intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular thrombolysis; (3) moderate-to-severe cardiac valvular disease, especially aortic valve insufficiency or stenosis; (4) moderate-to-severe pulmonary hypertension (mean pulmonary arterial pressure >20 mm Hg); (5) aneurysm, cerebral aneurysm; and (6) persistent hypertension (systolic blood pressure >180 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure >110 mm Hg); (7) decompensated heart failure; (8) hemorrhagic disease or obvious bleeding tendency; (9) foci of infection in counterpulsating limbs.

Ethical Approval

The study received approval from the Joint Ethics Committee of the Second People’s Hospital of Hefei. Patients and their families voluntarily participated in this study and signed an informed consent form.

Treatment

All patients underwent standard pharmacological treatment and rehabilitation training tailored to their problems, including exercise therapy, occupational therapy, suitable limb positioning, acupuncture, tuina, and physiotherapy. The control group received only the aforementioned conventional medicine and rehabilitation training, whereas the ECP group was administered extracorporeal counterpulsation (ECP) alongside the aforementioned conventional medication and early rehabilitation training. The pressure exerted on the lower extremities and gluteal region by the 2-stage sequential counterpulsation airbags reached 150 mm Hg. The treatment pressure for the patient was established by the diastolic counterpulsation peak-to-systolic peak ratio (D/S), which needed to exceed 1.2. The patient received external counterpulsation therapy 2 days postadmission, with each session lasting 30 minutes, and was administered twice daily for a duration of 10 days. The neurological recovery in both cohorts was evaluated at the conclusion of the treatment and after 90 days.

Indicators for observation: scores were recorded before treatment, at the end of the treatment period, and again 90 days later. Two trained neurologists averaged these scores. The prognosis for stroke patients was assessed via the modified Rankin Scale (MRS), where a score of 0 to 1 indicates a good prognosis and a score of 4 or higher indicates a poor prognosis. The degree of neurological deficit was scored according to the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). The standard NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) includes 15 items that assess various functions, such as speech, motor skills, sensory perception, consciousness, visual fields, eye movement, balance, and neglect. The scores range from 0 to 42 points. A higher score indicates a more severe neurological deficit: scores between 0 and 1 are considered normal or nearly normal, whereas scores of 5 or higher indicate moderate to severe strokes.

Data Analysis

Continuous data are expressed as the mean±SD for normally distributed data and as the median (25% percentile to 75% percentile) for nonnormally distributed data. Categorical data are expressed as numbers (%). The t test was used for normally distributed continuous variables, and the Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney test was used for nonnormally distributed continuous variables and hierarchical data. The χ2 statistic was used to determine the associations between categorical data. Two methods described by Robert Newcombe were used to calculate 95% CIs for proportions and 95% CIs for the difference between 2 independent proportions. The level of statistical significance was set at P<0.05. Statistical analyses were performed with IBM SPSS Statistics version 29.0.1.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL).

RESULTS

A total of 195 patients were initially included: 104 in the ECP group and 91 in the control group. During the study, 13 people in the ECP group were not included in the analyses due to early withdrawal from treatment, and 7 people in the ECP group and 4 in the control group were lost to follow-up. A total of 171 patients completed the trial, with 84 allocated to the ECP group and 87 to the control group. The average age of the participants was 64.09 years (±10.60), with 22.8% identified as female. No statistically significant differences were noted between the 2 groups in terms of systolic or diastolic blood pressure, NIHSS score, MRS score, or history of prior medical disorders (refer to Table 1).

Comparison of Baseline Data Between the 2 Groups

At the end of the treatment, no statistically significant change in neurological score was observed between the ECP group and the control group. Nonetheless, 90 days after disease onset, the NIHSS scores for the ECP group were markedly lower than those for the control group (refer to Table 2). In the ECP group, 69.0% of patients achieved a favorable neurological prognosis (NIHSS ≤1), whereas 48.3% of patients in the control group achieved a favorable neurological prognosis, yielding an absolute difference of 20.8% (refer to Table 3 and Fig. 1). These findings suggest that the probability of achieving a favorable neurological outcome was markedly greater in the ECP group. The disparities in the percentage of patients with poor neurological scores (NIHSS score ≥5) between the 2 groups at both the conclusion of therapy and after 90 days were not statistically significant (refer to Table 3 and Fig. 1).

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated the effect of ECP therapy on the short-term outcomes of individuals with acute ischemic stroke. Our findings revealed that ECP therapy contributed to enhancements in the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and modified Rankin Scale (MRS) scores for individuals with moderate acute ischemic stroke at 90 days postonset. Nonetheless, there were no notable differences in the NIHSS and MRS scores at discharge between patients who underwent ECP treatment and those in the15 control group. Moreover, ECP therapy did not diminish the percentage of patients with adverse NIHSS and MRS values.

The hemodynamic effects of ECP therapy are crucial in mitigating coronary atherosclerosis. This therapy activates multiple downstream pathways, including increased angiogenesis, increased collateral circulation, improved endothelial function, and a decrease in atherosclerosis. Collectively, these characteristics substantially aid in mitigating the symptoms encountered by people with coronary artery disease.16–18 To mitigate coronary atherosclerosis, the hemodynamic effects of ECP therapy are essential. Just as in the treatment of cardiovascular diseases, ECP therapy also plays a vital role in ischemic cerebrovascular conditions by enhancing cerebrovascular pressure, blood flow, and blood velocity.19,20 This improvement aids the perfusion of ischemic cerebral tissues and generates significant hemodynamic effects, which serve as an important theoretical foundation for the application of ECP therapy in cerebrovascular diseases. Furthermore, research by Ambari et al21 indicates that ECP enhances angiogenesis by supporting the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor-A and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2.

Our research revealed that short-term extracorporeal counterpulsation therapy provided during the hospitalization of acute stroke patients, significantly increased neurological function scores at 90 days post-onset in individuals with moderate ischemic stroke. This therapy reduced the likelihood of enduring neurological impairments, thus extending patients’ chances for an enhanced quality of life. Liu et al22 demonstrated that ECP therapy enhanced upper limb motor function, increased excitability of the ipsilateral primary motor cortex (M1), and reduced dyskinesia in the paralyzed hand of patients with acute ischemic stroke. Although few studies have investigated the prognostic effects of ECP therapy for acute stroke, the existing studies are limited by small sample numbers and inconsistent findings. Six months following a stroke, Xiong and colleagues reported a substantial and independent correlation between a worse functional prognosis and an increase in cerebral blood flow caused by ECP. Even after controlling for known variables, such as the baseline NIHSS score and the duration of ECP treatment, this connection remained.7 Xiong and colleagues proposed that the increase in cerebral blood flow stimulated by ECP results from compromised cerebral autoregulatory pathways after the onset of ischemic stroke. They identified a correlation between impaired cerebral autoregulation and unfavorable outcomes in patients with ischemic stroke. Thus, the substantial increase in cerebral blood flow observed during ECP treatment may suggest significant impairment of cerebral autoregulation.7,23,24 Conversely, a study conducted by Xiong et al24 revealed that the elevations in cerebral blood flow velocity and blood flow generated by ECP were not associated with the extent of compromised dynamic cerebral autoregulation. There is currently no conclusive evidence linking increased cerebral blood flow, damage to the cerebral autoregulatory system, and the neurological prognosis of patients with acute ischemic stroke who undergo ECP therapy. In addition, our study did not observe a notable difference in neurological improvement between the ECP and control groups at the conclusion of the treatment. This lack of distinction may be attributed to the inclusion of patients with mild neurological impairments and a limited observation period, which potentially hindered our ability to detect differences between the 2 groups. Owing to the absence of significant damage to cerebral autoregulation mechanisms in the light stroke patients studied, endothelial cell therapy (ECP) treatment—which typically depends on the impairment of these autoregulatory pathways following a stroke—did not lead to a substantial increase in cerebral blood flow in this group. Rather, ECP may contribute to an improved prognosis for stroke patients by increasing wall shear stress, positively influencing vascular endothelial cell function, and promoting angiogenesis. Thus, the therapeutic effects of this medication may manifest as a delayed reaction. Additional research is needed to ascertain whether ECP treatment is unaffected by the brain’s autoregulation mechanism in individuals with moderate-to-severe stroke. These findings suggest that ECP exerts a more substantial influence on brain function in these patients.

Limitations

Our sample size was limited owing to the finite number of stroke patients who underwent ECP treatment. Moreover, there are intrinsic limitations in employing the NIHSS score to delineate mild stroke. The NIHSS is inadequate for diagnosing posterior ischemia, is biased toward the left hemisphere, and does not measure neurological impairments, including cognitive function, tiredness, and depression. Subsequent research should utilize several indices and multidimensional evaluations to enhance comprehension of the impact of ECP treatment on the prognosis of stroke patients. We predominantly recruited patients with mild strokes, hence constraining our capacity to ascertain the effect of ECP on the prognosis of individuals with moderate to severe ischemic strokes.

CONCLUSIONS

ECP therapy may augment neurological recovery in stroke patients and increase their overall quality of life. Although ECP is considered a potential treatment for acute ischemic stroke, its efficacy in this context is still contentious, and the mechanisms of action are not yet fully understood. Therefore, additional high-quality research is essential for a more comprehensive investigation of this therapeutic method.

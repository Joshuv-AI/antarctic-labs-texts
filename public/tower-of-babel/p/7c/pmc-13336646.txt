Long-Term Results of Nail Correction with Distal Phalanx Osteotomy for Pincer Nail Deformity: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Kang Wu, Hongrui Wang, Weiye Zhang, Tianyu Gao, Xiaoxu Wang, Ning Zhang, Juan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Pincer nail deformity (PND) is characterized by excessive transverse curvature of the nail plate, often causing pain, functional impairment, and poor cosmesis. Existing treatments focusing on the soft tissue correction frequently result in recurrence.Aims and Objectives:To evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of a novel distal phalanx osteotomy technique in correcting the anatomical basis of PND and alleviating associated symptoms.Materials and Methods:A retrospective cohort study was conducted on 51 cases (39 patients) with PND, who underwent inverted V-shaped distal phalanx osteotomy between May 2013 and January 2022. Nail morphology indices, pain levels, and satisfaction scores were assessed pre- and post-operatively. Statistical analysis was performed using paired t-tests. Institutional review board (IRB) approval was obtained.Results:Postoperative assessments showed significant morphological improvement: the width index increased from 69.1% to 95.9% and the height index decreased from 64.5% to 13.8% (P < 0.001 for both). Pain visual analogue scale (VAS) decreased from 7.5 to 1.8, while satisfaction VAS improved from 1.4 to 9.0 (P < 0.001). No recurrences, nail growth arrest, or complications were observed during a mean follow-up of 3.3 years.Conclusion:Distal phalanx osteotomy offers a durable, anatomically corrective approach to treating PND. By remodelling both the osseous and soft tissue structures, it effectively prevents recurrence and restores functional nail architecture.

Background

Pincer nail deformity (PND) is a condition marked by excessive transverse curvature of the nail, leading to thickening and pinching of the distal nail bed.[1] With an estimated incidence of 0.9%, it most frequently involves the great toenails, either unilaterally or bilaterally. Patients often experience cosmetic concerns, discomfort, and pain, particularly, when wearing shoes or walking.[2]

Despite various conservative and surgical approaches being attempted, no universally accepted treatment exists, and achieving long-term correction remains difficult. Notably, PND is often associated with an increased curvature of the dorsal phalangeal cortex.[34] Based on this observation, our team developed a novel surgical technique aimed at correcting the underlying bony curvature to restore flat nail bed anatomy.[5] However, the long-term outcomes of this method have not yet been thoroughly evaluated. In this study, we present the extended follow-up results of a case series treated with this approach in our department.

Patients and Methods

Patients

We retrospectively reviewed patients, who underwent distal phalanx osteotomy for PND at our institution between May 2013 and January 2022. Inclusion criteria consisted of individuals with painful PND affecting the great toe, defined as discomfort distal to the lateral nail fold caused by the deformity. Less than three years of follow-up were excluded.

Surgical technique

The procedure began with digital nerve block anaesthesia (1% lidocaine) and tourniquet application. Surgical steps are illustrated in Figure 1. The nail plate was carefully extracted using a rolling motion with mosquito forceps to preserve the nail bed [Figures 1a and b]. A U-shaped incision was made along the nail margin, followed by soft tissue dissection to expose the plantar and lateral aspects of the distal phalanx.

Next, an inverted V-shaped osteotomy was performed from the phalangeal tip toward the proximal end using an osteotome, ensuring minimal bone loss [Figure 1c]. The critical step involved elevating the dorsal osteotomy edges to flatten the nail bed, thereby reducing transverse constriction [Figure 1d]. The lateral nail walls were de-epithelialized, and the expanded nail bed flap was secured with 5-0 nylon sutures [Figure 1e and f]. Postoperatively, patients received NSAIDs and antibiotics for 3 days, with sutures removed at 2 weeks.

Outcome Measures

Nail morphology assessment

To evaluate the degree of nail plate alteration, we used three standard indices: the width index, height index, and curvature index. The width index was calculated as a percentage by comparing the width at the nail tip to the width at the nail root. The height index was determined as a percentage of the nail tip height relative to its width. The curvature index was derived from the ratio between the length and width of the nail tip.

Pain and satisfaction evaluation

For pain and satisfaction assessment, we employed visual analogue scales (VAS). Pain levels were rated on a 10-point scale where 0 represented no pain and 10 indicated the worst imaginable pain. Similarly, patient satisfaction with the cosmetic outcome was measured on a 10-point scale ranging from 0 (completely dissatisfied) to 10 (extremely satisfied).

Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS 16.0 (SPSS Inc.), with continuous variables expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Paired t-tests compared pre- and post-operative outcomes; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 51 cases in 39 patients with PND were analysed retrospectively. There were 22 female and 17 male patients. The mean age of the patients was 46.2 ± 23.7 (range: 23–70) years. The patients had had the condition for a mean of 4.7 ± 3.1 (range: 1.5–8.0) years. The right to left ratio of affected toes was 23:28. Regarding previous treatments, four cases had undergone nail extraction, nine cases had received clipping treatment, and 11 cases had used antifungal agents, while 27 cases had no prior treatment history [Table 1].

Discussion

The management of PND remains a therapeutic challenge due to its multifactorial etiology and the lack of consensus on optimal treatment strategies. Existing surgical approaches, including nail bed widening, dermal grafting, and Z-plasty techniques, have demonstrated variable success rates but often fail to address the underlying anatomical alterations contributing to recurrence.[567] Conservative methods, such as nail grinding or wire-based correction, provide temporary relief but are associated with high recurrence rates.[89] Our study introduces a novel distal phalanx osteotomy technique that directly targets the biomechanical relationship between nail bed curvature and osseous morphology, offering a durable solution for bilateral PND.

A critical limitation of many existing surgical methods is their focus on soft tissue reconstruction without addressing potential osseous abnormalities. While studies highlight the frequent coexistence of dorsal phalangeal osteophytes and PND, the causal relationship remains debated. Baran et al.[10] postulated that osteophytes drive nail plate over curvature, while others argue that these bony changes are secondary to chronic nail deformation.[1112] Regardless of etiology, our findings suggest that osseous remodelling plays a pivotal role in maintaining long-term correction. Traditional osteophyte resection, as described by Shin et al.,[13] flattens the dorsal phalanx but fails to address transverse narrowing of the distal phalanx, leaving the nail bed vulnerable to recurrent inward collapse.

Our inverted V-shaped osteotomy addresses both components: (1) flattening the dorsal cortex to eliminate restrictive forces on the nail bed and (2) widening the distal phalanx base to provide structural support against lateral compression. The significant improvements in width index (69.1% to 95.9%, P < 0.001) and height index (64.5% to 13.8%, P < 0.001) reflect not only immediate decompression but also sustained anatomical restoration. Unlike nail bed flap techniques,[11] which rely on soft tissue tension for stability, our method establishes a rigid osseous framework that resists mechanical stressors from footwear or gait. This may explain the absence of recurrences in our cohort during a mean follow-up of 3.3 years, contrasting with recurrence rates of 9.5%–28% reported for conservative or soft tissue-based surgeries[614]

The dramatic reduction in pain VAS (7.5 to 1.8, P < 0.001) and improvement in satisfaction scores (1.4 to 9.0, P < 0.001) further validate this approach. By eliminating both the osseous and soft tissue contributors to PND, our technique achieves symptomatic relief, while restoring physiological nail growth. Notably, the absence of growth arrest or dystrophy in all cases underscores the safety of preserving the germinal matrix during osteotomy.

Conclusions

Distal phalanx osteotomy represents a paradigm shift in PND management by integrating osseous remodelling with soft tissue correction. This technique addresses the biomechanical interplay between the nail bed and phalangeal anatomy, providing robust structural support to prevent recurrence. Our outcomes demonstrate its superiority over purely soft tissue-based methods, positioning it as a definitive solution for refractory bilateral PND.

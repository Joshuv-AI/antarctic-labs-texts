Carcinoma of unkown primary, presenting as estrogen receptor positive small cell carcinoma of breast without a breast lesion – case report on a rare entity
Authors: Subhadeep Bose, Mujtaba Syed Khaja
Published: 2024

Small cell breast cancer, a type of neuroendocrine cancer is a rare form of breast cancer constituting less than 1% of all types of breast cancers, and usually such cases lack hormonal or Her 2 receptors. In such a case, there are no standard treatment algorithms and cases are managed in line with regimens from small cell lung cancers. We report a very unusual case of hormone receptor positive metastatic small cell breast cancer, without a primary breast lesion.

A 58-year-old woman presented to the acute medical team with a 3-month history of worsening lower back pain. Her past history included extensive right breast high grade DCIS treated with a right mastectomy 12 years ago. An MRI of the spine showed widespread spinal metastases with T11 collapse and impending cord compression. A CT scan showed disseminated malignancy involving bones, liver, right axillary lymphadenopathy and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A CT of the head was normal. She was treated with palliative radiotherapy to the spine under the oncology team. There was no breast lesion seen either radiologically or clinically. A right axillary biopsy revealed metastatic small cell cancer (IHC: ER positive, GATA3 positive, HER2-ve, Napsin-ve, TTF1-ve, Ki67 90%, PDL1 negative) (Fig 1). Three pathologists independently reviewed the slides and the consensus was that it was indeed a small cell carcinoma, the primary being breast. Due to the aggressive nature of this cancer and baseline borderline performance status, after multidisciplinary meeting discussion, she was started on chemotherapy with carboplatin in the first cycle as an inpatient and then carboplatin and etoposide combination chemotherapy from cycle two onwards, along with monthly denosumab. She responded quite dramatically with good subsidence of pain and an interval CT scan after the third cycle showed good partial response. After completion of six cycles of chemotherapy, a CT scan showed further partial response with maintained improvement in symptomology (Fig 2). Denosumab alone was continued along with the addition of an aromatase inhibitor (letrozole) as maintenance treatment. She was also treated with prophylactic cranial irradiation after a literature review suggested benefit from such treatment in patients with small cell lung cancer. She is currently under follow up and continues to remain well on letrozole.

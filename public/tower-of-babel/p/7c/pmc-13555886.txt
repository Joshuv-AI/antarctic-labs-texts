The effect of exclusive breastfeeding levels for the first 6 months on the prevalence of overweight in children under 5 years of age
Authors: Özlem Kemer Aycan, Kadir Yılmaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis aggregated ecological time-series data study aimed to investigate the effect of exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months on the prevalence of overweight in children under 5 years of age.MethodsThe study used data for Turkey from 2000 to 2024, provided by the WHO and also published through World Bank Country Reports. The prevalence of overweight was used as the dependent variable, and exclusive breastfeeding was used as the independent variable. Control variables included the share of health expenditures in GDP (including all confounders and quality of the health system), maternal mortality, and per capita national income.ResultsAlthough prevalence of overweight had increasing trend from 2000 to 2012, its trend was changed to decrease after 2012. Spearman’s rho correlation analysis for all years (2000–2024) showed that exclusive breastfeeding was significantly and positively correlated with overweight (r = 0.676; p < 0.01). For 2000–2012 period in which overweight trend was increasing, exclusive breastfeeding was significantly and positively correlated with overweight (r = 0.939; p < 0.01) in addition to maternal mortality (r=-0.993; p < 0.01) and GDP (r = 0.972; p < 0.01). For 2012–2024 time period, correlation between exclusive breastfeeding and overweight was negative, but correlation coefficient was insignificant (p > 0.05). Year controlled partial correlation analysis results showed that exclusive breastfeeding was significantly and negatively correlated with overweight (r=-0.698; p < 0.01). Overweight was also significantly correlated with health expenditure (r = 0.444; p < 0.05), maternal mortality (r=-0.677; p < 0.01) and GDP (r=-0.572; p < 0.01). Generalized linear model results showed that effect of exclusive breastfeeding (B = 0.060; p < 0.05) and maternal mortality (r=-0.277; p < 0.01) were statistically significant.ConclusionThe effect of breastfeeding on weight monitoring and diet in breastfed children must be well understood, and causal interpretations regarding breastfeeding reducing childhood overweight should be avoided. Therefore, while we encourage breastfeeding in the fight against obesity, we believe it should not be viewed as a single tool for combating obesity, and must be supported by confounders.

Introduction

Obesity is a significant public health problem with high prevalence and incidence, and childhood obesity is a particularly important health problem that has increased in recent years [1]. Increased obesity in children and adolescents is accompanied by increased metabolic complications and early morbidity levels, and therefore, a thorough investigation of the pathogenesis is necessary [2]. Childhood obesity risk factors are generally similar to those in adults, including nutritional imbalances, lack of physical activity, hormonal disorders, and various diseases [3–6]. Childhood obesity negatively impacts childhood health and leads to serious health problems in later life, including obesity and other chronic diseases it causes. Therefore, a multidisciplinary approach is needed to combat childhood overweight and obesity. This will allow us to determine the impact and extent of risk factors and develop new methods of combating obesity based on these findings. One of the important factors associated with childhood obesity in recent years is breastfeeding.

Although a significant awareness campaign regarding breastfeeding has been launched worldwide in recent years, many factors, ranging from changing environments to women’s employment status and nutritional status, affect breastfeeding [7, 8]. Studies have been conducted on the importance of breastfeeding for both maternal and infant health [9, 10]. In terms of maternal health, breastfeeding increases uterine contractions, reducing the risk of postpartum blood loss, breast cancer, and ovarian cancer [9]. In addition, breastfeeding plays an important role in the psychological bonding between mother and baby [11, 12]. In terms of infant health, many benefits have been reported, including defense against diseases, bonding with the mother, and the immune system [13–15]. One of these benefits is childhood obesity, a topic that has been emphasized in recent years [16, 17]. While studies in the literature generally show a negative relationship between breastfeeding and childhood obesity [16, 17], these studies are usually clinical studies with small sample sizes. However, research based on population data could be a solution to the lack of studies looking at the larger context in the literature. Furthermore, big data allows for analysis results that enable higher generalization. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the effect of exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months on the prevalence of overweight in children under 5 years of age.

Methods

Research model

The analysis is based on ecological aggregated time-series data. First, the changes over time in exclusive breastfeeding and overweight parameters were analyzed. Afterwards, the relationship between these variables was analyzed using correlation and regression analyses, and finally, the effect of exclusive breastfeeding on overweight was analyzed.

Research data

The study used data provided by the WHO and published through the World Bank Country Reports, covering Turkey from 2000 to 2024. The dependent variable was the prevalence of overweight, and the independent variable was exclusive breastfeeding. Control variables included the share of health expenditures in GDP (including all confounders and quality of the health system), maternal mortality, and per capita national income. According to WHO-WB data, the parameter codes were as follows:

Dependent variable:

Prevalence of overweight, weight for height (modeled estimate, % of children under 5).

Independent variable:

Exclusive breastfeeding (% of children under 6 months).

Controlling variables:

Current health expenditure (% of GDP).

Maternal mortality ratio (modeled estimate, per 100,000 live births).

GDP per capita, PPP (current international $).

Data provided by WHO-WB were also checked against the Turkish Ministry of Health Statistical Yearbooks and Turkish Statistical Institute data. Since all exclusive breastfeeding and overweight parameters were used for each year in the study, study uses secondary aggregated data rather than individual-level census data, and therefore, no population and sample were established. The study data were considered to represent the entire population accessible to WHO and the Ministry of Health for each year.

Ethical approval

The data used in this study are prevalence-based and are shared anonymously by the Turkish Ministry of Health, WHO, and WB, and do not contain any patient information. Therefore, due to both the lack of patient information and the anonymity of the data, ethical committee approval and informed patient consent are not applicable according to the COPE Code of Ethics.

Statistical methods

Trend analysis was performed to analyze the changes over time in the parameters of exclusive breastfeeding, overweight, healthcare costs, maternal mortality, and GDP. Mean, standard deviation, median, and ranges were used to reveal the changes in the parameters over time. Parity analysis was performed for time series analysis. Spearman’s rho correlation analysis and year-controlled partial correlation analyses were performed in relational scanning analysis. In the effect analysis, Generalized Linear Model (Logit) analysis was performed due to linearization deviations [18, 19]. All analyses were performed in SPSS 25.0 for Windows program at 95% Confidence Interval and 0.05 significance level. Our research data comprises a 25-year series and, because it contains fewer than 30 statistical units, it was considered nonparametric. Therefore, the research was conducted using the statistical logit method, not the econometric time series method [18, 19]. Splitting the data into two periods (2000–2012 and 2012–2024) is based on trend analysis change. Although the independent variable is a scale parameter in the form of a percentage, the logit model was used because it is below 30 and does not conform to a normality distribution [17, 18]. Augmented Dicky-Fuller (ADF) and Phillips-Perron (PP) tests were performed to determine whether time series contained unit roots. GLM results were compared with Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) results. Breastfeeding observations were collected at irregular intervals rather than annually, which may substantially affect trend and correlation analyses. Thus, we used unbalanced methods as GLM and ARIMA. On the other hand, breastfeeding observations are in national level and its associations must be evaluated in future researches. Since our research involved time series, but the data exhibited a nonparametric distribution and also had a time characteristic, we analyzed both logit and ARIMA model results and compared the agreement between the two outcomes. Although dependent variable is a scale parameter, it is not continuous due to normality assumptions. Thus, Generalized Linear Model (Logit) was used for associations. Breastfeeding observations were given at irregular intervals in health ministry reports and World Health Organization reports. Thus, interpolation procedure was used to find missing values using Linear Interpolation method. In order to control secular trends, autocorrelation and detrending procedures, both statistical and econometrical procedures using ARIMA and GLM as well as ADF and PP were used.

Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the effect of exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months on the prevalence of overweight in children under 5 years of age, and analyzed WHO data for Turkey from 2000 to 2024. The results showed that although breastfeeding has a reducing effect on overweight, trend analysis revealed that this requires a decreasing trend in overweight as well.

Obesity, a significant health problem for all age groups, has a special place in childhood, both in terms of physical and metabolic development and in terms of combating diseases in a societal sense [3–5]. In the fight against obesity, the balance between calories consumed and calories expended, in other words, nutrition and physical activity, is vital [2–6]. However, among the determinants of childhood obesity, not only nutritional level and physical activity but also breastfeeding status in the first six months of the baby’s life is gaining importance. Therefore, studies on childhood obesity are increasing every day [16, 17]. However, sufficient studies on a population basis and large sample sizes have not been found. To better understand the relationship between breastfeeding and childhood obesity, in this research we analyzed all the data given for Turkey between 2000 and 2024.

In the literature, childhood obesity is more commonly used instead of childhood overweight, and studies on this subject have generally shown negative and significant relationships between childhood obesity and breastfeeding. In their meta-analysis, Qiao et al. [16] reported that breastfeeding is inversely proportional to the risk of obesity in children aged 2–6 years. In another meta-analysis, Yan et al. [20] reported that breastfeeding has a positive effect against childhood obesity. Wang et al. [21] reported in their study that breastfeeding for at least one month and ideally longer than 6 months significantly reduces the level of childhood obesity. Lefebvre et al. [22] reported in their study that although breastfeeding reduces obesity in the results of univariate analysis, it is not correct to directly assert this due to confounders. Ma et al. [23] compared data from 12 countries and reported that there was a relationship between breastfeeding and the risk of childhood obesity in the data from all countries. Rito et al. [24] analyzed 22 countries and reported a significant, negative association between breastfeeding and obesity. In a retrospective study by Wallby et al. [25], they reported that breastfeeding up to the age of 4 was negatively associated with obesity.

Although all these studies reported a negative association between childhood obesity and breastfeeding, as Lefebvre et al. [22] stated, confounder levels were low in these studies. In our opinion, although breastfeeding is quite effective and beneficial, many factors such as its form, quality, mother’s nutritional level, environment exposure, physical activity level, parity and number of abortions should be taken into consideration.

In our study, correlation analysis showed a positive relationship between exclusive breastfeeding and overweight between 2000 and 2012, while the relationship was negative and insignificant between 2012 and 2024. This seemingly contradictory situation actually stems from the fact that correlation analysis only analyzes the relationship between paired variables. In our research, we correlated the trend between the series, and as a result, it is possible to explain the increase in both breastfeeding and childhood obesity up to 2012 with increasing urbanization, population growth, awareness, or socioeconomic variables. However, further studies are needed for these explanations. However, the results of the multivariate model in the regression analysis, since the OR value is below 1, actually indicate that breastfeeding reduces childhood obesity. However, it should be considered and evaluated that both childhood obesity and breastfeeding are data at the whole population level. Causal or individual-level inferences are also important limitations of the study, and results must be interpreted as individual level, and population-level associations do not necessarily reflect individual-level relationships.

Limitations of the study

The most significant limitation of the study is the limited number of confounders due to its aggregated ecological time-series data and population-based nature. Unlike studies in the literature examining the relationship between breastfeeding and obesity, this study did not focus on a specific sample but analyzed all cases reported by the WHO from 2000 to the present. In this respect, it should be considered a population study rather than a clinical trial. While this highlights the originality of the study, it is a limitation in terms of confounders. However, the confounder limitation applies to regression analysis, and since correlation analysis is univariate, the confounder limitation does not apply to correlation results.

Another limitation of the study is related to breastfeeding data. Although the importance of breastfeeding is emphasized and promotional efforts are made worldwide, primarily by the WHO, it is quite difficult to obtain statistics on infants who are exclusively breastfed for the first six months. In this regard, we examined both WHO data and the Turkish Ministry of Health’s annual health statistics and found that the most comprehensive and up-to-date data were collected between 2000 and 2024, but only in specific intervals. Therefore, our study is limited to breastfeeding and overweight data provided by the WHO and the Ministry of Health. Due to the ecological (population-level) nature of the data, results of the study should be interpreted as correlation rather than causation. For causation models, further researches are needed. The study is described as aggregated ecological time-series data; however, the analysis is based on aggregated time-series (ecological) data due to trend analysis change in data as in the Fig. 1. This distinction must be mentioned to avoid misinterpretation of causal relationships at the individual level. Due to our data is aggregated time-series (ecological) data, we only analyzed year controlled correlation. Year is a model level confounder, and thus it was only selected as partial correlation. The study design is aggregated ecological time-series data rather than a true longitudinal individual-level study. Therefore, causal interpretations regarding breastfeeding reducing childhood overweight should be avoided, and our findings represent population-level associations only. The strong positive correlations observed between exclusive breastfeeding and overweight during 2000–2012 may be due to temporal trends and potential ecological fallacy. Although we tested unit root and used ARIMA model, further researches may be conducted on this issue. Breastfeeding data were collected at irregular intervals rather than annually, which may substantially influence trend analyses and correlations. Since our data is prevalence nature, important confounders such as maternal BMI, physical activity, dietary transition, urbanization, and socioeconomic inequality were not included in the models. This is another important limitation of the study.

Contributions of the study to the literature and the field

The most important contribution of the study to the literature is that the relationship between breastfeeding and obesity or overweight, as suggested in previous studies, should be considered not only as a direct and univariate relationship, but also over time and according to trends. In other words, our research actually reveals that breastfeeding is indeed negatively related to overweight, but that the general tendency of the society to lose weight is also important and effective. In this respect, our research both introduces a new approach to the literature and provides robust and comprehensive evidence-based data to the literature through a complete enumeration sample that includes all cases every year.

The contribution of this research to clinical practice is that while breast milk has an effect on combating excess weight, it suggests that this effect should not be overestimated. In other words, when monitoring the dietary habits of children who regularly receive breast milk for the first six months, breastfeeding should not be considered an advantage in dietary monitoring; instead, all methods of combating excess weight should be used, and there should be no room for tolerance or laxity in this regard. Considering that the literature shows that the positive and weight-reducing effect of breast milk is greater in those who breastfeed directly, breastfeeding should be encouraged through direct breastfeeding.

Conclusion

The effect of breastfeeding on weight monitoring and diet in breastfed children must be well understood, and causal interpretations regarding breastfeeding reducing childhood overweight should be avoided. Therefore, while we encourage breastfeeding in the fight against obesity, we believe it should not be viewed as a single tool for combating obesity, and must be supported by confounders.

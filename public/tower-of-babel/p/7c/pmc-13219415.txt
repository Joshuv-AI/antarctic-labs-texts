Cost-effective and scalable urban air quality monitoring using image-based deep learning
Authors: Md. Arifuzzaman, Ramasamy Srinivasagan, Raghda Alqurashi, Abdulaziz Alqurashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Air pollution is a persisting problem for the environment, mainly in urban areas, given heavy industrialization and vehicular emissions and urbanization. Exposure to harmful air pollutants, including PM2.5, PM10, and toxic gases, is a major contributor to respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, accounting for millions of deaths globally each year. Answer monitoring is, therefore, the first effective measure in limiting the health effects of air pollution. The idea of this study is to advance an effective, scalable image-based deep learning (DL) system to detect air pollution levels within urban areas. The model integrates CBAM with InceptionV3 to enhance feature extraction, improving accuracy and robustness in detecting varying levels of air pollution from images. The proposed work uses Air Pollution Image Dataset from Kaggle, containing urban pollution images from India and Nepal. Preprocessing includes Gaussian Filtering to reduce noise, Histogram Equalization (HE) to enhance contrast, Thresholding to segment image, and resizing to standard dimensions. The model employs InceptionV3 integrated with Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) for enhanced feature extraction. This approach classifies images into three pollution levels: Moderate, Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (USG), and Unhealthy, enabling efficient air quality monitoring. The CBAM-InceptionV3 model achieved strong performance, with precision, recall, and F1-scores exceeding 0.95 for each class. The proposed CBAM-InceptionV3 model achieves an overall classification accuracy of 95.23%, with precision, recall, and F1-score values consistently above 95%, demonstrating its effectiveness for air pollution level classification.

Introduction

Air pollution, especially in large urban areas-the prime environmental issue today-is a main threat to human health1,2. There has been a sharp rise in pollutants in atmosphere due to rapid rates of industrialization, urbanization, and automobile pollution3,4. These pollutants include well particulates in which have been implicated to respiratory and cardiovascular diseases and premature mortality5,6. According to WHO, millions of people die every year because of air pollution and 90% of world populace is showing to air pollutant levels exceeding its guidelines7,8. This hence calls for urgent efforts to develop efficient and scalable air quality management systems capable of detecting the sources of pollution and developing policies for alleviating the effects of pollution especially in most densely populated cities9,10.

Pollution in the air may have both natural and anthropogenic causes11. Human activities such as transport, industries, power generation, deforestation, and agriculture remain the major causes for urban air pollution12,13. Through these activities the air quality is greatly impaired by release of high concentrations of particulate matter and gases14. Moreover, pollution is worsened by the weather conditions, especially by presence of temperature inversion, especially in crowded urban areas15,16. Natural causes such as wildfire, volcanic eruptions and dust storms also pollute, although they do not have a significant impact compared to those caused by human beings17,18. In cities, the population density, traffic jams, industries, and ineffective environmental policies causes high concentrations of harmful pollutants19,20.

Due to increased demand of air pollution monitoring, different techniques have been devised to measure the level of pollution starting with sensor-based techniques up to image-based techniques21,22. The conventional sensor-based technique is based on physical air quality monitoring stations, which associate the attention of pollutants in the air23,24. They are efficient and, in most cases, they prove to be costly, geographically scaled and difficult to implement at the large scale25,26. The machine learning (ML) that are usually used to classify images according to air quality are convolutional neural networks (CNNs), support vector machines (SVM), and decision trees27,28. Although these techniques are more scalable and less expensive, they are also limited by the fact that they do not pay much attention to important image characteristics and that it is hard to distinguish between slight changes in level of pollution29.

The suggested model eliminates them by introducing the CBAM to InceptionV3, which is one of strongest DL models used to classify images. CBAM increases the relevance of the model with respect to the most significant areas in images and extends its capability of detecting the additional features that are subtle clues of particular levels of pollution such as haze, smog or mist. CBAM helps the model to learn automatically about what parts in the image are needed to classify it, effectively resolving the problem of partial feature extraction in other CNN models. InceptionV3 has been used to offer a sophisticated architecture that can recognize complicated details of polluted images without expensive costs and low-performance. The integration of CBAM with InceptionV3 suggests such a framework with ability to be very much accurate as far as the air pollution level prediction is concerned, which is an enhancement over current method. The proposed model contributes to the improvement of classification accuracy, and meanwhile, presents a more scalable and efficient way for monitoring air quality over urban areas. It introduces an image-based machine learning solution for measuring urban air quality via a monitoring system that consists of time-series sensor data in addition to the traditional methods for measurement. The overall model implements CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) and InceptionV3 architecture to create better representations of spatial and channel features and can detect small visual elements such as haze and smog. Literature survey

Sarkar et al.30 studied air quality foretelling for PM2.5 pollution in Delhi by comparing different forecasting models on various criteria. This included comparing capabilities of independent ML and DL models to be used for this prediction. The criteria for judging proficiency of models include R2, MAE, and RMSE. Hybrid-LSTM-GRU was best model in terms of efficiency are used for prediction of AQI values from all other models. Das et al.31 have analyzed air quality prediction methods for PM10 and SO2 pollutants in Basaksehir district of Istanbul using methods LSTM, RNN, and MLP. The study also looks into training using incomplete data and revised error terms. Overall, LSTM least generated errors together with values close to actual ones for the prediction of PM10 and SO2 compared to both MLP and RNN. So, LSTM were performing a lot better than many other studies in field when predicting air quality.

Maltare & Vahora32 compared various machine-learning algorithms for predicting Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India’s AQI: SARIMA, SVM, and LSTM. The data were obtained from the Central Pollution Control Board of India, and preprocessing was carried out in different ways for each model. The SVM with RBF kernel has besides other kernels served as the most accurate predictor method for AQI in Ahmedabad compared to SARIMA and LSTM. Gupta et al.33 performed various types of research on different methods through which air quality can be predicted using data mining techniques for Indian cities are used for this research. The dataset used in present study comprised cities like New Delhi, Bangalore, Kolkata, and Hyderabad. To counter the problem of imbalance in dataset, an oversampling approach named Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) was used. The effect of SMOTE to improve the models’ performance is well documented together with detailed metrics and visual comparisons so as to foster similar attempts by future researchers.

Heydari et al.34 have developed a hybrid intelligent model, which could effectively predict the emissions from Combined Cycle Power Plants by means of (LSTM) and Multi-Verse Optimization (MVO). The LSTM model predicts the pollutants. On other hand, the MVO enhances LSTM parameters in order to minimize the prediction error. The proposed model was tested against actual data from Kerman power plant in Iran in reference to meteorological input and lagged levels of pollutants. Drewil & Al-Bahadili35 enumerated a model that encompasses some DL methods and ML methods used for the enhancement of air quality forecasting. The objective of this model involves detecting the most excellent hyperparameters for LSTMs in terms of predicting PM10s, PM2.5s, CO, and NOXs following day. The GA optimizes LSTM parameters which help overcome challenges faced, such as dealing with noise and setting hyperparameters incorrectly. Results show that there was an outperformance of proposed GA-LSTM model over conventional ML and LSTM models, with greater accuracy, speed, and efficiency.

Kumar & Pande36 on air pollution data of 6 years of 23 cities of India, pre-processed data set, which is selecting important features through correlation analysis. The reduction in pollution was pretty visible in this pandemic year 2020. Due to the present situation of data imbalance, combinations of resampling techniques were applied, and five ML models were used. It was found that the accuracy produced by XGBoost was the greatest from all the others, and linearly good was the prediction with the actual data. Samad et al.37 used ML techniques to simulate the concentrations of five models; ridge regression, support vector regression, random forests, extra trees regression, and extreme gradient boosting. The study concluded that predictions were considerably influenced by nearby pollutant data and that the methodology was extendable to other locations, as was demonstrated with Karlsruhe-Nordwest data. Ahmad and Kumar38 proposed a hybrid WaveNet-based deep learning framework enhanced with XGBoost residual correction (1DCNN-BiLSTM-XgRC) for accurate PM₂.₅ prediction at urban U.S. embassies, demonstrating improved forecasting performance compared to traditional models.

Zhu et al.39 employed DL methods, in particular, CNN and LSTM, for determining sea quality and air quality in urban settings. The proposed Conv.LSTM model integrates convolutional layers with the recurrent connection of LSTM to capture spatial and temporal dependances of data. The study explored various habitats of beaches and pollution types. Binbusayyis et al.40 created an AQI prediction model based on a smart city concept using a modified Stacked Attention GRU with KL discrepancy. The model uses Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) for data pre-processing, especially to impute missing values and scale the features, towards better prediction performance. The study used AQI data collected from Ernakulam, Chennai, and Ahmedabad and reported performance metrics is used. The performance of proposed model was better than existing regression algorithms since it exhibited lower loss values and more efficient predictions.

Ahmad and Kumar41 proposed a hybrid BiGRU-1DCNN model to forecast PM2.5 concentrations across multiple monitoring stations in Delhi. The model combines bidirectional GRU layers with 1D convolutional layers to capture both temporal dynamics and local spatial correlations in the air quality data. Ahmad and Jas42 developed a quantum-inspired neural network to forecast air pollution levels in one of the world’s most polluted urban environments. Their approach extends classical neural network architectures by incorporating principles derived from quantum computation to better capture complex temporal patterns in time-series air quality data.

Ahmad & Kumar43 presented a novel method for improving the prediction of PM2.5 concentration using a random imputation strategy coupled with a hybrid model of RNN & Bidirectional GRU. The method uses statistical techniques to deal with missing values and outliers before using the hybrid model to learn the temporal patterns of the air quality time series. Qu et al.44 explored deep learning-based atmospheric visibility detection using image data, highlighting the effectiveness of convolutional neural networks in capturing visual features related to air pollution conditions.

Ozkok45 proposed a deep learning-based image classification approach for air pollution assessment, where CNN models analyze environmental images using features such as haze density and visibility patterns to classify pollution levels.

Problem statement

Prediction of air quality is of paramount importance to human health and environmental control particularly in urban regions where concentration of pollutants is high. Different researchers have used ML and DL technology to predict air quality, with special emphasis on such pollutants as PM2.5, PM10, SO2, and NO231. Nevertheless, there are still problems of model accuracy, data imbalance, and feature selection. Various models have been tested with different results based on data and environmental conditions; LSTM, RNN, SVM, hybrid models, and others35. Nevertheless, high accuracy and efficiency in air quality prediction is yet to be reached despite progress being made specifically in areas with multiple sources of pollution, and incomplete information. This paper seeks to compare different ML and DL models to enhance prediction of air quality39.

Proposed methodology

The initial step for this entire process is to acquire air pollution images from the dataset. These images are then preprocessed using several techniques: Gaussian filtering to remove noise, HE for contrast enhancement, thresholding to segment the objects of interest, resizing images to a standard size, and encoding labels into numerical values for model compatibility. After preprocessing, the dataset is split three parts: training, testing, and validation sets. The feature extraction and classification steps involve important features being extracted from images using a DL model like InceptionV3 or perhaps another choice of model. A baseline models including plain InceptionV3 and variants without preprocessing and attention mechanisms were also implemented. All models were trained and tested under identical conditions to enable a consistent comparison of architectural choices. This model is refined with attention modules such as CBAM, which focus on the related areas of the image, allowing the pollution levels to be classified with greater accuracy into Moderate, USG, and Unhealthy. Image acquisition

The dataset is an Air Pollution Image Dataset from Kaggle, consisting of photos from various cities with labels for specific air pollution levels, e.g., Moderate, USG, and Unhealthy. Also provided are useful metadata, including the location of each image, timestamp, and pollution indices, such as PM2.5 and PM10, if available. It is essential to assess data quality throughout this process by making certain each image is properly labeled according to the pollution level and considering whether there might be any images lost or corrupted, as final result of the model depends on such data quality. The dataset employed in this study will be Air Pollution Image Dataset that is openly available in Kaggle. It is a total of 12,240 images taken in urban areas in India and Nepal, in different air quality variations. The original dataset includes six categories, namely Good, Moderate, Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (USG), Unhealthy, Very Unhealthy and Hazardous. In this research, the following three categories will be used to classify the dataset: Moderate, Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (USG) and Unhealthy. All images are downsampled to a standard resolution of 224 × 224 pixels so that they would be compatible with the InceptionV3 architecture. Before training, the preprocessing steps, which include noise elimination, contrast and normalization to the range [0, 1] are conducted. Further, it uses data augmentation such as rotation, horizontal flipping, and changing of brightness to enhance the generalization of the models.

Leverage less dependency on training datasets and possible data leakage, due to careful verification, visually similar or close-duplicate pictures were not shared in-between training, validation, and test sets. The images that were produced in the same areas or had close visual characteristics were compared and linked to prevent overlapping of cross-splits. Moreover, a consideration in which sources were taken into account was utilized wherever possible, and images in similar geographical settings were put in the same subset. This will assist the model to learn generalized pollution related characteristics like haze and smog instead of memorizing location-based scene patterns.

The dataset labels (Moderate, USG, Unhealthy) are based on AQI ranges from Kaggle sources which match the pollutant thresholds that include PM2.5 and PM10 although these pollutants were not always directly measured through sensors. The researchers applied histogram equalization and normalization because visual data can be affected by lighting and weather and camera quality but CBAM enables them to concentrate on essential haze and visibility information.

The dataset labels are based on AQI ranges from Kaggle and aligned with pollutant thresholds such as PM2.5 and PM10, though not directly measured in all cases. The combination of preprocessing and CBAM techniques helps to decrease visual differences while the system concentrates on haze patterns, yet the model creates visual representations that do not reflect actual pollutant measurements, which indicates that future sensor-based testing is necessary are shown in Fig. 2.

Image preprocessing

During image preprocessing, the first step would be to apply a Gaussian Filter that removes high frequency noise and smooths the image though concurrently preservative features such as edges. This filter convolves the image with the Gaussian kernel, where the pixel nearest to the center has more say in filtering. By reducing noise, it helps eliminate outliers and ensures that the model focuses on right features. After the Gaussian filter application, the images are then set for further steps that include resizing, label encoding, and normalization for the best possible preparation for training phase.

Label encoding

The images have already been preprocessed, and then label encoding is carried out to encode the categorical labels, including Moderate, USG and Unhealthy into numerical values that will be interpreted by the model. Indicatively, the term Moderate can be coded 0, Unhealthy sensitive Groups 1, and Unhealthy 2. This transformation enables the model to receive the labels in the form of numerical values during training and therefore, it is able to compute loss and optimize the learning process.

Splitting the data

The dataset is divided into the three splits with 70%, 15%, and 15% distribution for training, validation, and testing, respectively. Training gives the model information about the data; this is the actual learning phase. The validation set is to tune hyperparameters and gauge performance to avoid overfitting. Testing, on the other hand, is to evaluate the model’s performance finally. The stratified split is used to ensure that each subset contains a representative distribution of classes, preserving the proportion of each pollution level, for example, Moderate, USG, and Unhealthy, across all the sets. This is particularly crucial to ensure a balanced number of samples in each class, especially when some classes might be underrepresented. Out of the stratified class-based separation, further precautions were employed to reduce unspoken data leakage. Highly similar images such as those of the same background, lighting, or a similar surrounding were thoroughly considered to minimize the chances of overlap within different subsets. Though this dataset lacks direct time sequence data, the attempt was made to prevent the distribution of potentially correlated samples of training, validation and test sets. This guarantees a more confident evaluation as the distinction between the data that is seen and the unseen is maintained.

Feature extraction and classification using CBAM-based InceptionV3

An input image undergoes resizing, normalization, and conversion into a suitable input form for the model. The image next undergoes convolution operations that extract vital features such as edges and textures from it, while the pooling operations reduce the spatial extents of feature maps by holding together important information without any loss. Feature maps of various scales are combined through filter concatenation to form an integrated feature map. Channel attention selects significant features, whereas spatial attention focuses on important areas of the image in the application of CBAM. It thus passes through a linear (or fully connected) layer that flattens the feature maps into vector form for subsequent classification operations. Finally, a softmax transforms the class prediction scores of model into possibilities for each class (Moderate, USG, and Unhealthy), assigning the class that stands forth with the maximum probability as its final output. This approach allows CBAM on InceptionV3 to classify air pollution levels in urban images quickly and accurately. Figure 3 shows the architecture of CBAM-Based InceptionV3.

Softmax Layer

The Softmax Layer is used in output layer of neural networks and transforms the raw prediction values, or logits, into probabilities. These logits give the network’s confidence about each class prior to normalization. The softmax function transforms values into possibilities in the range [0,1] and adds to 1. A crucial factor in classification tasks is that this transformation allows interpreting the network’s output as probabilities of class memberships. The mathematical representation of the softmax function is shown by Eq. (16):16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathrm{s}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{x}\left({z}_{i}\right)=\frac{{e}^{{z}_{i}}}{\sum\nolimits_{j}\:\:{e}^{{z}_{j}}}$$\end{document}

Result and discussion

This model has proven to be quite capable of efficient classification with urban air pollution levels getting into Moderate, USG, and Unhealthy categories. The proposed CBAM-InceptionV3 architecture was also to be effective and novel by evaluating the contribution of preprocessing, data augmentation, and the CBAM module in the same training environment. The original InceptionV3 model recognized 88.6% of the cases, and with preprocessing, this value increased to 91.2%, which shows that the feature quality is improved. The inclusion of data augmentation further enhanced the accuracy up to 93.4 which showed better generalization. The CBAM-InceptionV3 model offered the best performance of 95.23 which proves that attention mechanism improves feature representation. These outcomes indicate the contribution of each element, and CBAM increases the performance in a clear manner compared to the baseline. The CBAM module in the InceptionV3 acts in conjunction with the model to somehow emphasize on the most important features of polluted images, which include haze or fog. Accurate prediction of all three classes was possible, as evidenced by very good performances for the precision, recall, and F1-score metrics.

The data of the model was also demonstrated with the control of dependency on datasets when splitting data. The evaluation represents a more realistic generalization situation by reducing overlaps of visually or contextually similar images in different subsets. The fact that the performance of the model is consistent throughout the various classes points to the fact that the model successfully captures the visual patterns that are unique to pollution instead of depending on the location-dependent features.

Conclusions and future scope

In conclusion, this study presents an image-based deep learning classification framework for predicting airborne pollution levels in urban environments. The proposed model integrates the InceptionV3 architecture with a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), enabling the network to selectively focus on the most informative regions of each image. By emphasizing visually salient features associated with atmospheric conditions such as: haze, fog, and smog-the attention mechanism enhances feature representation and leads to improved classification accuracy across different pollution levels. By applying image preprocessing techniques to reduce noise, enhance contrast, and emphasize salient visual structures, a deep learning model was trained to classify images into three air pollution categories: Moderate, Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (USG), and Unhealthy. Furthermore, the integration of a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) with the InceptionV3 architecture facilitates the learning of fine-grained visual patterns that distinguish between varying levels of atmospheric pollution. The interpretability analysis using Grad-CAM further validates the behavior of the proposed model by revealing both its strengths in focusing on relevant atmospheric cues and its limitations in certain failure cases.

The model uses visually inferred labels which depend on environmental and imaging conditions that include fog and lighting and seasonal variations as their basis. The predictions show visual air quality indicators because they do not measure actual pollutant levels. The upcoming research will combine ground-based sensor information with image-based learning methods to enhance label accuracy and validate scientific model development. The model, in its training, showed high precision, recall, and F1 for every one of the three categories. Moderate had a precision of 0.9535, recall of 0.9523, and an F1 of 0.9529. USG was similar with a precision of 0.9531, a recall of 0.9519, and an F1 of 0.9525, The experimental results the effectiveness of the proposed CBAM-InceptionV3 model, achieving an accuracy of 95.23%, with precision, recall, and F1-score values consistently above 95%. These results demonstrate the robustness and reliability of the model for accurate air pollution level classification and real-time urban air quality monitoring.These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model in accurately and efficiently classifying urban air pollution levels. Future work will focus on enhancing model robustness by incorporating data from geographically and climatologically diverse locations, as well as enabling real-time classification through edge-computing–based deployment. Furthermore, integrating additional environmental variables, such as temperature and humidity, is expected to further improve predictive performance and robustness in dynamic urban environments.

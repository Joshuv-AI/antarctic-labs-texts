Liver biopsy findings and clinical correlates in immune-mediated liver injury from checkpoint inhibitors
Authors: Lucy Meunier, David E. Kleiner, Benjamin Riviere, Huiman Barnhart, Ronald Kamusiime, Vincent L. Chen, Dominique Larrey, Robert J. Fontana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Immune-mediated liver injury from checkpoint inhibitors develops in up to 10% of treated patients. The aim of this study was to describe the relationship between standard liver histopathological findings and clinical presentation and outcomes in a cohort of 67 adult patients.Methods:Liver biopsies from 67 ICI-treated patients enrolled in the Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network and from the Montpellier, France, registry were scored for 42 features. Exploratory analyses of histological features with clinical presentation and outcomes are provided.Results:The median age of patients was 62.5 years, 45% received pembrolizumab, and 24% received ipilimumab-nivolumab. The median latency to liver injury onset was 72 days, and the median time to liver biopsy was 20 days. The 52% with acute hepatocellular injury were significantly younger and had more severe inflammatory scores, granulomas, and necrosis on biopsy than those with mixed- or cholestatic injury, who had more ductular reaction and cholangitis. Patients biopsied on corticosteroids had significantly less severe inflammation, fewer granulomas, and less cholestasis. Mild macrovesicular steatosis was common (65%) and associated with diabetes and patient BMI but not corticosteroid use. Pembrolizumab-treated patients presented later and were more likely to have a cholestatic enzyme pattern and ductular injury on biopsy compared with those with hepatitis from ipilimumab/nivolumab.Conclusions:Liver histology findings were associated with presenting laboratory features in this retrospective, exploratory study of 67 ICI hepatitis patients. Necroinflammatory activity was substantially less in subjects biopsied while on corticosteroids, which may impact clinical management decisions.

INTRODUCTION

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), including the monoclonal antibodies against programmed cell death receptor 1 (anti-PD-1), programmed cell death receptor ligand-1 (anti-PD-L1), and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte ligand A4 (anti-CTLA-4), have become a cornerstone in the treatment of many advanced solid organ tumors. ICI’s inhibit the receptor–ligand interaction and disrupt the downregulation of the immune response, leading to the activation of specific T-cell–mediated immunity against tumor antigens. 1 ICI therapy–induced immune activation is also associated with a range of side effects, termed immune-related adverse events (irAEs). 2 Immune-related liver injury from ICIs impacts up to 10%–20% of treated patients and usually manifests as elevation of serum aminotransferase and/or alkaline phosphatase (Alk P) levels within the first 24 weeks of treatment.3–5 Although the clinical presentation and laboratory abnormalities vary, most patients are asymptomatic, anicteric, and fully recover with ICI discontinuation or a brief course of corticosteroids.

Management guidelines recommend performing a liver biopsy in cases of grade 3 or 4 hepatitis with a serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) above 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), Alk P > 2 times ULN, or an elevation in total bilirubin despite ICI discontinuation or non-response to corticosteroid therapy [according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) criteria]. 6 Although liver biopsy can assist in diagnostic confirmation and severity assessment, the indications and optimal timing of performing a liver biopsy remain controversial and uncertain. Several studies have described the histological patterns of ICI hepatitis, which can vary depending on the regimen administered. 7 However, current studies are limited by small cohort size (<50 patients) and the absence of a standardized framework for describing histological lesions.7–10 A systematic histological scoring system was developed by Kleiner et al 11 to characterize liver biopsy lesions in patients with idiosyncratic drug-induced liver injury (DILI). The objective of the current study was to describe the histological lesions observed in well-characterized ICI hepatitis patients who have undergone formal causality assessment using a standardized histological scoring system. 11 Exploratory analyses assessing the potential association of histological lesions with the ICI administered, laboratory presentation, corticosteroid use, and 6-month outcomes in 67 high-confidence ICI hepatitis cases are presented.

METHODS

DILIN cohort

Patients with suspected DILI were enrolled in the ongoing Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network (DILIN), prospective or retrospective observational studies. 12 Liver injury was defined as the first date when a serum aspartate (AST) or ALT level exceeded 5 times ULN, a serum Alk P > 2 × ULN, a total bilirubin > 2.5 mg/dL, or an international normalized ratio (INR) > 1.5 on 2 consecutive occasions. These criteria equate to a CTCAE hepatotoxicity grade of 3 or 4. All study participants provided written informed consent from the local IRB in accordance with the declarations of Helsinki and Istanbul.

Prospective DILIN patients were enrolled within 6 months of DILI onset and followed for a minimum of 6 months, while patients enrolled in the retrospective study had a single study visit. In the prospective study, laboratory and radiological testing were performed to exclude competing etiologies via testing for hepatitis A, B, C, E, HIV, anti-nuclear antibody and smooth muscle antibody titers, and cytomegalovirus and Epstein–Barr virus infection at the local laboratories. Individuals with evidence of chronic liver injury at 6 months were followed for 48 months. All available laboratory and imaging data were also reviewed for the retrospective DILIN cases as previously described. 3

French Montpellier cohort

Patients were selected from a retrospective study of individuals with ILICI identified during multidisciplinary meetings held between August 2018 and September 2023 at the Montpellier French University. The inclusion criteria were patients with previously normal liver biochemistries before starting an ICI in whom other potential causes of acute liver injury were excluded. All patients met grade 3 CTCAE criteria with either an AST or ALT > 5 × ULN or total bilirubin > 2.5 mg/dL at ILICI onset. All cases were reviewed for causality assessment using the DILIN expert opinion scale as previously reported. 5

Causality assessment

The causal relationship between the liver injury episode and the suspect drug for the DILIN cases was evaluated by the DILIN Causality Committee. 12 A DILIN expert opinion score ranging from 1 (definite >95% likelihood), 2 (highly likely 75%–95% likelihood), 3 (probable 50%–74% likelihood), 4 (possible 25%–50% likelihood), and unlikely (1%–24% likelihood) was assigned by consensus agreement by committee members. In subjects with 2 or more suspect drugs, an overall causality score was assigned for the case, and an individual causality score was assigned for each drug. Only high confidence cases with a causality score of 1, 2, or 3 (ie, definite, highly likely, probable) were included in this analysis.

Histopathology interpretation

Two expert hepatopathologists (David E. Kleiner, Benjamin Riviere) met and reviewed the process for scoring each liver biopsy. A total of 42 histological features were recorded on each evaluable liver biopsy that contained at least 10 portal tracts as previously described. 11 Trichrome stains were available for all liver biopsy samples, and iron and copper stains were completed in 26 patients. Resources to perform immunohistochemistry or RNA sequencing from biobanked liver tissue were not available for this study.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics, including the median, were used to summarize continuous variables, while frequencies and percentages were used for categorical variables. The non-parametric Wilcoxon test was used to compare continuous variables between groups. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for those with low frequencies was employed for comparing categorical variables between groups. All reported p-values were two-sided, with a significance level of 0.05 or less considered statistically significant. Data analysis was performed using SAS 9.4. Multivariate analysis was not undertaken due to the limited sample size.

RESULTS

Clinical characteristics

Between September 2004 and June 2024, 2901 patients were enrolled into the DILIN Prospective and Retrospective Studies, and 115 into the Montpellier registry. Among the 73 patients in DILIN with a high causality score, 33 with an evaluable liver biopsy were included (Figure 1). Among the 103 French patients with a high causality score, 34 with an evaluable liver biopsy were included. Two cases had more than one liver biopsy, but the follow-up biopsies were not included in this study. Low-causal hepatitis cases who underwent biopsy were excluded to provide a more uniform study population.

Derivation of patient cohort. Thirty-three of the 73 high causality ICI hepatitis patients enrolled into DILIN with an available liver biopsy were included. Similarly, 34 of the 103 high causality patients enrolled at Montpellier with an adequate biopsy were included in this study. Abbreviations: DILIN, Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network; ICI, immune checkpoint inhibitor.

The presenting laboratory and clinical features of the 2 cohorts are shown in Table 1. Overall, the median age was 62.5 years, and 63% were male. Among the DILIN patients, 84% were Caucasian/non-Hispanic White, whereas race/ethnicity data were not available for the French patients. The median BMI was 24.8 kg/m2, and 19% had diabetes mellitus. Advanced-stage melanoma was the most common malignancy (58%), followed by lung cancer (16%). Only 14% had known liver metastases before ICI therapy.

The most frequently administered ICI regimen was pembrolizumab (45%), followed by ipilimumab-nivolumab (24%) and various other ICIs (31%). The presenting laboratory features of the French and DILIN patients were similar, with 52% having hepatocellular injury and the remaining having either mixed (19%) or cholestatic (28%) liver injury. The initial laboratory parameters were similar in the 2 groups, but the peak AST, Alk P, and total bilirubin levels were higher in the DILIN patients. The frequency of corticosteroid use in these patients was high in both groups (94% and 91%). Overall survival at 6 months was similar in both groups, with only 2 patients (4%) experiencing a liver-related death.

Histopathology of the overall cohort

The median time from drug initiation to DILI onset was 72 days, and the median time from DILI onset to liver biopsy was 20 days (range: 0, 374) (Table 2). Most patients had mild interface hepatitis as well as mild lobular and portal inflammation. Prominent plasma cell infiltrates were noted in only 15%, while 27% had prominent eosinophils. However, 36% of patients had some granulomas with microgranulomas (small collections of 5–10 macrophages) being more prevalent in the DILIN patients (48% vs. 12%). The 2 cases with epithelioid granulomas both showed fibrin-ring granulomas. Although hepatocyte apoptosis was observed in 81% of patients, it was mild in most. Central vein injury with either veno-occlusive changes or endothelitis was seen in 5% and 10% of cases, respectively. Finally, only 25% had evidence of coagulative necrosis, mainly in zone 3 of the liver lobule.

Cholestasis (bile in hepatocytes or canaliculi) was seen in 10% of the biopsies, while evidence of duct injury was seen in 40%. Interestingly, 66% of the patients had some evidence of hepatic steatosis, but it was mostly mild and macrovesicular. Only 4 cases had Mallory–Denk bodies, and cirrhosis was seen in 3 cases, probably related to pre-existing liver disease, while the rest showed either none or mild fibrosis.

A finite number of global histological patterns were seen in these 67 liver biopsies, with acute hepatitic (18%), chronic cholestatic (12%), and cholestatic-hepatitic (10%) injury being the most common. Mixed patterns of steatosis with cholestasis were found in 10% of cases. Minimal changes or normal findings were found in a significant minority (18%). Of note, no cases showed bland cholestasis or nodular regenerative hyperplasia (Supplemental Table S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/C385).

Hepatocellular versus mixed and cholestatic cases at DILI onset

Thirty-five patients presented with a hepatocellular liver injury profile (R-value ≥5) and 32 with a mixed or cholestatic profile (R-value <5) (Table 3). The patients with hepatocellular injury were significantly younger (56 vs. 64 y, p=0.005) but had similar peak bilirubin levels and overall likelihood of dying during follow-up (38% vs. 50%, p=0.32). However, the patients with a mixed or cholestatic pattern were significantly more likely to have chronic DILI at 6 months of follow-up (50% vs. 19%, p=0.013). Review of the liver histology demonstrated that the presence and severity of hepatic necrosis, coagulative necrosis, and lobular disarray were significantly greater in those with hepatocellular liver injury, while the extent of hepatic fibrosis was lower. As expected, those with a mixed or cholestatic liver injury were more likely to have histological cholestasis, prominent ductular reaction, and evidence of duct injury and cholangitis compared with those with hepatocellular injury.

Liver biopsy findings in corticosteroid-treated and untreated patients

Over 46% of the patients were receiving corticosteroids at the time of liver biopsy (Table 4). The corticosteroid-treated patients were more likely to be diabetic but otherwise had similar laboratory features at DILI onset and during follow-up. As expected, patients biopsied before receiving corticosteroids had significantly more severe interface and portal hepatitis, more granulomas, and even more severe fibrosis and cholestasis, suggesting that the overall severity of ICI hepatitis may be attenuated by corticosteroid use. However, the proportion with marked plasma cells and eosinophils was similar. In addition, the presence, type, and severity of hepatic steatosis were similar in both groups.

Comparison of ipilimumab-nivolumab to pembrolizumab cases

Liver biopsies were available in 30 patients who received pembrolizumab alone and 16 who received a combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab (Supplemental Table S2, http://links.lww.com/HC9/C386). The median age and time to onset were significantly higher in the pembrolizumab group, while the proportion with acute hepatocellular injury was significantly lower (37% vs. 87%, p<0.001). Furthermore, the ipilimumab-nivolumab patients had significantly higher peak serum ALT levels and lower Alk P levels, although the proportion treated with corticosteroids and anti-metabolites was similar. Interestingly, the presence and severity of lobular, portal, and interface hepatitis as well as hepatic necrosis were similar in the 2 groups. However, only the pembrolizumab patients had significant ductular reaction (p=0.009) and acute cholangitis (p=0.08) on their liver biopsies, which was consistent with their more cholestatic laboratory profile at presentation. These data suggest that pembrolizumab hepatotoxicity tends to present later and with more laboratory and pathological features of cholestasis that are slower to resolve.

DISCUSSION

Immune checkpoint inhibitors are now used to treat over 50% of patients with advanced solid organ tumors.1,2 A variety of iRAEs have been reported with ICI, including hepatitis that typically develops within the first 6 months of treatment.3–6 To date, prior reports on the histopathology of ICI hepatitis indicate that most patients had a hepatitic pattern at presentation with some evidence of endothelialitis.7–10 The patients with a cholangitic histological pattern frequently had evidence of disease progression and concomitant chemotherapy. 7 However, the pattern of inflammation did not predict the need for corticosteroids or subsequent outcomes.7,13 Of note, these retrospective studies frequently involved a limited number of cases obtained from a single center without formal causality assessment to exclude other causes of liver injury, nor standardized reporting of histology. In contrast, the current study features prospectively enrolled patients who underwent formal case adjudication and blinded systematic review of all histopathological features by 2 expert hepatopathologists.

Several histological patterns of liver injury were observed in our 67 high-confidence ILICI cases (Supplemental Table S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/C385). We found a predominance (26 of 67 cases) of necroinflammatory patterns, spanning a range from mild, portal and lobular inflammation to submassive necrosis of multiple liver lobules. Within this group, most cases showed lobular-predominant, “acute” hepatitis. Confluent necrosis was a common finding as well, noted in 17 of the 67 cases, which mainly involved zone 3 and was not severe (involving less than a third of the parenchyma) in 14 cases. Confluent necrosis has been reported in multiple cases in some series, but uncommonly in others.8,14–17 Notably, all of our patients with zonal or more extensive confluent necrosis presented with hepatocellular injury and required treatment with corticosteroids.

Cholestatic patterns of injury, including chronic cholestatic injury and cholestatic-hepatitis was seen in 15 of our cases. Cholestatic features included bile duct injury, sometimes with neutrophilic infiltration, pseudoxanthomatous changes in periportal hepatocytes, ductular reaction, zone 3 bile stasis, and, in the DILIN cases, periportal copper accumulation or keratin 7 staining of periportal hepatocytes. None of our cases showed duct loss, although this finding has been reported in a case of ICI hepatitis associated with blocking the PD-1/PDL-1 pathway with pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and atezolizumab. 18 As compared with cases associated with ipilimumab-nivolumab therapy, only the pembrolizumab cases showed ductular reaction. Acute cholangitis was also only seen with pembrolizumab, but the difference did not reach statistical significance (p=0.08). Nevertheless, the propensity for cholestatic injury in pembrolizumab DILI suggests that the mechanism or target of the immune-mediated injury may be different than that seen with other checkpoint inhibitors. Other recent studies have also suggested that iRAEs may be due to aberrant host immune response to bacterial antigens. 19

A novel aspect of our study was the systematic scoring of all biopsies by 2 independent liver pathologists and careful assessment of concurrent hepatic steatosis. The first report of ipilimumab DILI included a case of panlobular acute hepatitis with what was thought to be underlying steatohepatitis. 8 Other reports have noted instances of hepatic steatosis or steatohepatitis-like injury in ICI hepatitis patients.7,20 In our series, nearly 66% of our patients had some evidence of hepatic steatosis, although it was mild in most. There were 3 (4%) patients who had steatohepatitis as the main pattern of injury, with a fourth case showing features of steatohepatitis in the background of cholestatic injury (one of the mixed injury patterns), but Mallory–Denk bodies were uncommon (6%). The patients with histological steatosis had similar initial and peak laboratory profiles and response to corticosteroids (data not shown). The high prevalence of hepatic macrovesicular steatosis in our series likely reflects pre-existing MASLD in light of the correlation with diabetes mellitus and subject BMI, as well as the presence of perisinusoidal fibrosis in many of these patients.

Our exploratory analyses suggest that an acute hepatocellular laboratory profile at presentation is associated with more severe inflammation on liver biopsy and less frequent cholestatic and ductular injury (Table 3). Interestingly, the patients with a mixed-cholestatic laboratory profile were older and more likely to develop chronic liver injury. These data are consistent with prior studies demonstrating more frequent acute hepatocellular injury in younger patients compared with older patients.3,21 Other recent studies have begun to characterize the immune infiltrate in the livers of patients with ICI hepatitis using immunohistochemistry and rnaSEQ and their association with steroid responsivenss.22,23 These observations support the hypothesis that therapeutic strategies might need to be adapted according to the predominant type of liver injury, emphasizing a personalized approach based on the initial biochemical and histological features.

Corticosteroids and liver biopsy findings

Patients who underwent liver biopsy while receiving corticosteroid therapy exhibited less severe histological lesions, although their laboratory profile was similar to those not on treatment at the time of biopsy (Table 4). In particular, the presence of microgranulomas was significantly lower (19% vs. 53%, p=0.005), suggesting that granulomas are likely an early finding in ICI hepatitis. In addition, histological cholestasis and prominent ductular injury were less frequently seen in those receiving steroids. These data raise important questions regarding the optimal timing of liver biopsy and its diagnostic role. Notably, in the work conducted by the Barcelona group, the decision to initiate corticosteroid treatment was guided by liver biopsy findings, particularly based on the degree of necroinflammatory activity. 24 Our results and those of others also suggest that liver biopsy could play a pivotal role in stratifying treatment intensity according to histological severity.7–11,23 However, there remains a great deal of uncertainty as to when a biopsy should be obtained in relation to disease severity, steroid responsiveness, and prognostic utility. 23 Ideally, prospective studies done in large cohorts of patients would be completed with both a pre-steroid and post-steroid liver biopsy obtained in a protocolized manner to better define the mechanism and immune infiltrates responsible for ICI hepatitis.

Limitations of our study include the heterogeneity of tumor types included and the multitude of ICI agents used. However, all cases underwent careful expert opinion-based adjudication by a group of DILI experts. 12 More importantly, only patients with an adequate liver biopsy were included. Furthermore, in comparison to prior studies, a standardized and comprehensive histological scoring system was used. 11 Our study also primarily focused on patients receiving ICIs either as monotherapy or in combination regimens. However, in clinical practice, anticancer therapies increasingly combine ICIs with other classes of agents such as anti-angiogenic agents, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), or conventional chemotherapy.25,26 It remains to be determined whether such combination regimens alter the histological patterns of liver injury. Moreover, liver biopsy could potentially assist in distinguishing the specific contribution of each agent to the observed hepatotoxicity and help guide therapeutic decision-making. A final limitation of our study was that biopsies were not obtained at a standard time after ICI hepatitis onset and were obtained by the treating provider for clinical reasons, introducing potential selection bias and reducing the generalizability of our findings.

In summary, our results demonstrate that there are significant histological differences in ICI hepatitis patients who present with acute hepatocellular compared with mixed or cholestatic liver injury, with the former demonstrating more severe necroinflammatory scores and the latter having more ductular reaction, fibrosis, and increased risk of evolving into chronic DILI. Our observation that patients biopsied before receiving corticosteroids have more severe inflammation, granulomas, and cholestasis is important to bear in mind when considering immunomodulatory therapy. In addition, an unexpectedly high prevalence of generally mild hepatic steatosis (ie, 66%) was noted in our patients. We postulate that the high prevalence of hepatic steatosis likely reflects pre-existing MASLD and is not an artifact of corticosteroid treatment or attributable to the ICIs themselves, but prospective and protocolized pre-steroid and post-steroid liver biopsies would be needed to sort this out. Lastly, our data suggest that hepatotoxicity from pembrolizumab tends to present later and is associated with more laboratory and histological features of cholestasis, which may be slower to resolve compared with that seen with ipilimumab-nivolumab combination therapy. Going forward, large prospective studies that control for the tumor type, ICI administered, and time from DILI onset to performance of a protocolized biopsy are needed to confirm our findings.

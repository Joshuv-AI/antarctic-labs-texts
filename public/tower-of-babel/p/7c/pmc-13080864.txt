The Next Era of Transcatheter Electrosurgery for Structural Heart Interventions
Authors: Fuk Kei Fong, Kent Chak-yu So
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

The development and introduction of transcatheter electrosurgery have revolutionized the field of structural heart interventions in recent years. This innovative approach uses high-frequency alternating current delivered through catheters and guidewires to precisely vaporize, cut, or traverse tissue within blood-filled spaces.1 It has significantly reshaped the landscape of transcatheter heart valve procedures by enabling alternative access routes and reducing the risk of coronary or left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction. Consequently, transcatheter electrosurgery has paved the way for safer and more advanced structural heart interventions.

In this issue of JACC: Case Reports, Uttenthal and Freeman2 exemplify how the adoption of various transcatheter electrosurgical techniques can broaden the scope of structural heart interventions, providing therapeutic options for patients who would otherwise be limited to medical management. An elderly female patient presented with severe mitral valve dysfunction secondary to extensive mitral annular calcification (MAC) and had a high Society of Thoracic Surgeons score, rendering her ineligible for surgery. However, her computed tomography scan predicted a high risk of LVOT obstruction for valve-in-MAC transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) given a small neo-LVOT area, contributed by underlying hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and a long anterior mitral valve leaflet. To mitigate the risk of LVOT obstruction during TMVR, the team decided to perform septal reduction therapy using the SESAME (septal scoring along midline endocardium) technique, followed by valve-in-MAC TMVR with preemptive base-to-tip LAMPOON (laceration of the anterior mitral leaflet to prevent outflow obstruction) as a leaflet modification strategy. Further complicating the case was the presence of severe bilateral iliofemoral disease, which precluded standard transfemoral aortic access for the SESAME procedure, necessitating an alternative vascular access. Therefore, the team had to resort to a transcaval approach for the SESAME septal reduction.

As illustrated by this case, the broader adoption of different novel transcatheter electrosurgical techniques can safely and effectively facilitate high-risk, complex structural heart interventions. Similar cases have also been reported worldwide, demonstrating the concurrent use of 2 or more transcatheter electrosurgery techniques at the same time to facilitate heart valve implantation. For instance, the combination of transcaval access with left-cusp BASILICA (bioprosthetic aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction) procedure can facilitate transcatheter aortic valve implantation for patients with limited femoral access and a high risk of left-sided coronary obstruction due to a low coronary height or small sinus of Valsalva.3 Looking ahead, the future of transcatheter electrosurgery hinges on the continuous innovation of techniques, refinement of procedures, and the expansion and accessibility of complex electrosurgical techniques across various regions globally. There has been a rapid development of newer techniques in the field of transcatheter electrosurgery, such as the ELASta-Clip (electrosurgical laceration and stabilization of failed MitraClip(s)),1 CATHEDRAL (catheter electrosurgical debulking and removal),1 VECTOR (ventriculo-coronary transcatheter outward navigation and reentry),4 and SEATTLE (simplified extraction of atrial tumor with targeted loop electricity) procedures.1,5 These advancements are enabling TMVR in cases of failed transcatheter edge-to-edge repairs, percutaneous coronary bypass, transcatheter leaflet resection, and intracardiac mass removal, respectively.

The ongoing development of these electrosurgical techniques offers more treatment options for patients previously deemed inoperable. As demand for structural heart interventions continues to grow, expanding and implementing these transformative techniques beyond the United States and European countries is crucial to benefiting more patients across different regions6 (Figure 1). However, there exist challenges and barriers in terms of technical aspects and the accessibility of electrosurgery tools that could hinder the wider adoption of these techniques in other countries.7 This underscores the need for more robust training and proctorship, as well as the availability of tools to ensure a broader adoption of these techniques not only across Asia, but also in Latin America and Africa.

Transcatheter electrosurgery has fundamentally transformed structural heart interventions by addressing previously unmet clinical needs.

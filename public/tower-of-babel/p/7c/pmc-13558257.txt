Are Stroke Risk Scores Transportable to Newly Diagnosed Atrial Fibrillation?
Authors: Tomoki Nakamizo, Munechika Misumi, Satoshi Kurisu, Nobuo Sasaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStroke risk scores for atrial fibrillation (AF) were developed from established AF cohorts conditioned on pre-entry stroke-free status, potentially underestimating risk in newly diagnosed AF.Methods and ResultsTo evaluate the transportability of these scores, we calibrated frailty survival models to score-deriving cohorts and mapped stroke risks at cohort entry back to AF diagnosis. Under model assumptions, risks near 1%/year remained stable; higher risks were mapped to substantial, scenario-dependent initial levels.ConclusionsOur conceptual modeling provides theoretical reassurance for score-guided decision-making in patients with newly diagnosed AF, while suggesting a potential urgency for those at high risk.

Central Figure

Standard stroke risk scores for atrial fibrillation (AF) (e.g., CHADS2, CHA2DS2-VASc, and HELT-E2S2) were derived from cohorts primarily comprising patients with established AF. However, physicians typically make anticoagulation decisions at the time of AF diagnosis. This discordance raises a concern: risk scores derived in established AF cohorts may underestimate stroke risk when applied to stroke-free patients with newly diagnosed AF.

This potential underestimation may stem from individual risk differences not captured by standard clinical variables. This heterogeneity is reflected by the strong prognostic effect of prior stroke, which can be interpreted as the event itself unmasking the hidden risks. Because patients at higher unmeasured risks tend to experience stroke earlier, those who remain stroke-free increasingly represent a lower-risk subset, as evidenced by a progressive decline in observed stroke risk over follow-up.1

Given that empirical data are limited for newly diagnosed patients at threshold-level risks, we theoretically evaluated the transportability of standard risk scores to the incident setting, which is implicitly assumed in clinical practice but has rarely been evaluated.

Methods

Survival models can incorporate unmeasured individual risk as “frailty terms”. We used two standard frailty distributions: gamma and inverse Gaussian. Because prior stroke history reflects individual risk beyond measured factors, we calibrated the frailty parameters to reproduce the hazard ratios (HR) of prior stroke. Theoretically, this calibration represents a conservative approximation, where the initial stroke risk is overestimated, because the HR reflects unobserved risk differences both present at diagnosis and emerging thereafter (e.g., interim vascular burden), both modeled as present at diagnosis. Using this calibrated model, we mapped annual stroke risks (0.5–4%: HELT-E2S2 scores 0–4)2 from established AF populations back to the same-aged, stroke-free patients at the time of AF diagnosis (see https://github.com/TomokiNakamizo/mapping-clinical-risk for detailed mathematics and codes).

Parameters were set to reflect the HELT-E2S2 derivation cohorts:2 prior-stroke HR of 2.1 and 3.6 (95% confidence interval [CI] bounds) and a 2-year follow-up period. Because the time from AF diagnosis to cohort entry is typically unreported, we pragmatically evaluated “prevalent periods” of 1 and 3 years. To account for the exponential increase in stroke risk with age, we used a Gompertz baseline hazard function, setting the rate to 1.3 or 1.7 per decade (the 95% CI for the HR per decade).3

Results

The Figure illustrates how stroke risk estimates evolve after AF diagnosis, adjusted for aging during the interval. By reversing this evolution from the cohort entry time, we estimated the risk at AF diagnosis. Standard anticoagulation threshold-level risks (~1%/year)4 remained stable regardless of the assumed calibration targets, prevalent period, and frailty distribution. However, in high-risk scenarios, the implied risk at diagnosis was up to 1.6-fold higher than at cohort entry under high-heterogeneity and low-aging-rate conditions. Discussion

Despite the lack of empirical validation, the stability of threshold-level stroke risk across modeling scenarios, even under conservative calibration, supports the implicit assumption of risk score transportability and provides theoretical reassurance for score-guided anticoagulation decisions in newly diagnosed AF. The early rapid risk decline among high-risk patients varied across scenarios and may be overemphasized. Nevertheless, this potential decline underscores the importance of timely, albeit unaltered, decision-making, warranting further investigation.

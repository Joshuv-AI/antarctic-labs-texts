Avenanthramide C and chondroitin sulphate promote chondrogenic differentiation of adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells
Authors: Priscila Sun, Winston Lim, Shivatra C. Talchai, Veerapol Khemarangsan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Loss of functional chondrocytes contributes to cartilage degeneration. Autologous transplantation of adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASCs) offers a promising approach for cartilage repair; however, current differentiation protocols remain suboptimal. To improve chondrogenic outcomes, we supplemented the standard Chondrocyte Differentiation Medium (CDM1) with Avenanthramide C (Avn C), a compound primarily known for its anti-inflammatory and chondroprotective activity. Unexpectedly, Avn C enhanced COL2A1 protein deposition, the primary collagen type that is cartilage tissue-specific. Co-administration of chondroitin sulphate (CS), a major extracellular matrix component, further elevated the expressions of important chondrogenic genes including AGGRECAN, SOX9, and LRP1. Proteomic analysis revealed increased secretion of SERPINs and complement proteins, known regulators of cartilage homeostasis and chondrocyte function, in the presence of Avn C and CS. This work identified Avn C (besides for chondroprotection) and CS as synergistic enhancers of chondrogenic differentiation in ASCs, as evidenced by the expression of articular cartilage–specific markers LRP1 and AGGRECAN, supporting their relevance for regenerative applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42579-9.

Introduction

In the context of aging populations, the decline in functional chondrocytes, characterised by their limited regenerative capacity, plays a significant role in the progressive deterioration of joint pain over an extended period1,2. Chondrocytes, crucial cells responsible for the synthesis and secretion of various extracellular matrix components, including diverse types of collagens, are integral to maintaining the elasticity of cartilage3. Introducing chondrocyte replacement therapy could greatly enhance the quality of life for individuals afflicted by cartilage degradation.

Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs), possessing the capability to differentiate into multiple cell lineages, including chondrocytes, have sparked considerable interests in the development of MSC-based therapies for cartilage restoration4. By harnessing the patients’ own MSCs, such as bone marrow MSCs (BM-MSCs), adipose-derived MSCs (ASCs) or Wharton Jelly MSCs (WJ-MSCs) and orchestrating their directed differentiation towards the chondrocyte lineage5–8, this innovative approach offers a safe alternative that effectively circumvents the risks associated with cellular graft rejection by the immune system9–11. However, an effective and reliable method to induce chondrocyte differentiation in MSCs is required for practical applications. Existing approaches lack efficacy12,13, prompting the need for a novel inducer with superior outcomes.

Avenanthramides (Avns), polyphenolic compounds predominantly found in oats, have attracted attention due to their distinct biological properties. Specifically, it was found that the three major isoforms Avn A, Avn B and Avn C were able to elicit anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory effects in diverse pathologies such as coronary heart disease and skin inflammation14–16. Importantly, a recent study by Tran et al. demonstrated that Avn C is chondroprotective and could inhibit cartilage degradation by suppressing the expression of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) induced by proinflammatory IL-1β in mouse articular chondrocytes17. Although the authors also reported that chondrocyte markers such as Aggrecan, Col2a1 and Sox9 were not rescued under the influence of IL-1β, they did not evaluate the effects of Avn C on normal chondrocytes. However, it is unclear what are the direct effects of Avn C on mesenchymal stem cells as the majority of mSCs do not reside within cartilage. Given the many documented benefits of Avn C, we hypothesized that Avn C has potential chondrogenic effects and may even enhance the differentiation of MSCs to chondrocytes.

In this study, we investigated the effects of Avn C on the differentiation of human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASC) to chondrocytes. By varying the dosages of Avn C, we determined that the addition of 100 nM Avn C to a standard chondrocyte differentiation medium (CDM1) led to significantly heightened COL2A1 protein expression. Moreover, when chondroitin sulphate (CS), a major component of cartilage, was included into the differentiation medium along with Avn C, the chondrocyte mRNA characteristics such as AGGRECAN, SOX9 and LRP1 of the differentiated ASCs can be further enhanced. Together, these findings indicated that Avn C and CS can be utilized as novel ASC to chondrocyte differentiation factors, paving the way for its practical and facile implementation in a clinical setting.

Methods and materials

Materials

Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium low glucose (DMEM LG; 31600083), Penicillin-Streptomycin (P/S; 15140122), Amphotericin B (Amp-B; 15290026), Insulin-Transferrin-Selenium-Ethanolamine (ITS-X; 51500056), β-mercaptoethanol (21985023), Recombinant human epidermal growth factor (rhEGF; PHG0314) and Opti-MEM (31985062) was purchased from Life Technologies, US. Hydrocortisone (H0888), L-ascorbate-2-phosphate (49752), Avenanthramide C (Avn C; 36465), Chondroitin sulphate (CS; C4384), Linoleic acid-BSA (L9530), Dexamethasone (D4902), Alcian blue (B8438), Type B gelatin (gelatin; G9391) and Phosphate buffer saline (PBS; 56064 C) was procured from Sigma-Aldrich, US. Human platelet lysates (HPL; 06961) was obtained from Stemcell Technologies, CA. Fetal bovine serum (FBS; S181B) was acquired from Biowest, FR. Recombinant human Transforming growth factor – beta 3 (rhTGF-β3; 8420-B3-025) was bought from R&D Systems, US. Rabbit anti-Collagen 2 (COL2A1; ab34712) primary antibody, Rabbit anti-LDL Receptor Related Protein 1 (LRP1; ab92544) primary antibody and Goat anti-rabbit AF555 (ab150078) was purchased from Abcam, US. Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA; 0219989880) was procured from MP Biomedicals, US. 4% Paraformaldehyde (4% PFA; FSKJ19943.K2) was acquired from Fisher Scientific, US. Human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASCs), obtained from both biological male and female aged between 29 and 63, were acquired from Medeze Group Public Company Limited, Thailand.

Cell culture

Tissue culture plates and flasks were coated with autoclaved 0.1% (w/v) gelatin for at least 20 min before any cell seeding. All human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASCs) were cultured in an incubator at 37 °C containing 5% CO2 and expanded in medium containing DMEM LG, 0.5% HPL, 1X ITS-X, 0.2% BSA, 1X Linoleic acid-BSA, 100 µM L-ascorbate-2-phosphate, 100 µM β-mercaptoethanol, 10 ng/ml rhEGF, 1nM dexamethasone, 1% P/S and 250 ng/ml Amp B. Medium was changed every 2 to 3 days for cell expansion.

In vitro chondrogenic differentiation

ASCs (between Passage 4 to Passage 6) were seeded in gelatin-coated 24-well plates at a seeding density of 6000 cells/cm2 and expanded for 3 days prior to chondrogenic differentiation. The standard chondrocyte differentiation medium (CDM1) consisted of DMEM LG, 0.5 µg/ml hydrocortisone, 1% FBS, 1X ITS-X, 10 ng/ml rhTGF-β3, 50 nM L-ascorbate-2-phosphate, 1% P/S and 250 ng/ml Amp B. Depending on the experiment, varying dosages of Avn C and 500 µg/ml of CS was supplemented as required. Each condition, including the control group, consists of ASCs from 3 different individuals. For experiments that require integrated density quantification, the cells were differentiated in a 96 well flat black plate.

Alcian blue staining

On Day 21 of ASC to chondrocyte differentiation, samples were fixed with 4% PFA for 25 min. Following fixation, wells were rinsed thrice with 1X PBS and stained with 1% Alcian blue (pH 2.5) for 15 min. Stains were then regressively treated equally among all samples with 0.1 N hydrochloride acid (HCl) to obtain the desired intensity. Once done, the wells were washed thrice again with 1X PBS before viewing and imaging under a light microscope.

Immunofluorescence staining

Samples were first fixed in 4% PFA for 25 min and then washed with 1X PBS thrice. Each wash duration was 5 min. After washing, a permeabilization cum blocking buffer (5% BSA + 0.3% Triton-X in 1x PBS) was added to each well for 1 h. Once done, the buffer was aspirated out and, without washing, primary antibodies diluted in the very same buffer were added to each well. The primary antibodies used for this work were Rabbit anti-COL2A1 (1:100) and Rabbit anti-LRP1 (1:200 dilution). Once added, the well plate was incubated in the 4 °C fridge overnight. The following day, each well was washed thrice with 1X PBS and each wash duration was 5 min. After washing, secondary antibodies diluted in 0.22 μm pore size filtered 1X PBS containing DAPI (1:1000 dilution) were added to each well for 1 h. The secondary antibody used for this work was Goat anti-Rabbit AF555 (1:500 dilution). Once done, the samples were washed thrice with 1X PBS and imaged using a fluorescence microscope.

Quantification of integrated density normalized to cell numbers

The integrated density of an image was quantified using ImageJ. The value was then divided by the total number of cells within an image to obtain the normalized value for comparison between different conditions. For Figs. 2 and 3 that utilized this quantification method, one 20x magnification image was obtained from the center of the well of a 96 well flat black plate per condition per independent source of ASC. The image acquisition parameters for all images were kept constant. 3 independent ASC sources were used for each condition. Images obtained from 3 independent ASC sources per condition were quantified and the results were averaged and plotted. For Alcian Blue staining in Fig. 3C, the images were first processed with the Colour Deconvolution 2 plugin in ImageJ before obtaining their integrated density.

Reverse transcription-PCR (RT-PCR) and primer sequences

Cells were cultured in 24 well plates at a seeding density of 6000 cells/cm2 if they are intended for PCR. Once the differentiation duration has ended, cells derived from 3 independent ASC sources per condition were pooled for downstream processing and analysis. RNA extraction and cDNA conversion were performed as stated in the manufacturer’s protocol. For RT-PCR, the mastermix formula is described in Table 1, the thermal cycling protocol is described in Table 2 and the sequence of the primers used is listed in Table 3. Following the completion of the RT-PCR cycle for each tested gene, the solution in the reaction tubes were added to an ethidium bromide-doped agarose gel for amplicon separation and visualization. The full-length gel image for Fig. 4 can be found in Supplementary Fig. 1. Each band on the gel blot represents the total amplicons obtained from 3 different cell sources.

Cell secretome preparation, collection and analysis

Cells were first cultured to 70% confluency in a T75 flask and washed with 1x PBS followed by Opti-MEM. Subsequently, 7.5 ml of Opti-MEM was added to the cells and the flask was left to incubate at 37 °C containing 5% CO2 for 18 h. Once ready, the medium containing cellular secretome was collected in a 15 ml centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min. Thereafter, the supernatant was filtered using a 0.22 μm sterile filter. Secretome from each sample was concentrated using 3 kDa MWCO centrifugal filters before performing buffer exchange with 50 nM TEAB twice. Proteins were processed into peptides using S-TRAP Mini column according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. 1 µg of peptides were injected for analysis using SCIEX TripleTOF 6600 system. The data was processed by the ProteinPilot software against a database comprising entries from the SwissProt human reference proteome spiked with common contaminant proteins. Besides that, the data was also processed by the MASCOT software against the same database, to obtain exponentially modified protein abundance index (emPAI) values used to calculate the relative abundance of each protein identified in the samples.

Statistical analysis

All data analysis was performed using SPSS software. Data was first analyzed using Shapiro/Wilk normality test and Levene’s homogeneity of variance test. Data that was normally distributed and had a homogeneous variance were determined to be parametric and One-way ANOVA tests followed by either Bonferroni post hoc test (unequal sample per condition) or Tukey post hoc test (equal sample per condition) were performed. On the other hand, data that were either not normally distributed or did not have a homogeneous variance were determined to be non-parametric and Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Mann-Whitney post hoc test was performed. All data are presented as mean ± SD.

ASCs were first cultured with the standard chondrocyte differentiation medium (CDM1) to assess their differentiation potential towards the chondrocyte lineage. After 21 days of culture, it was observed that ASCs cultured in CDM1 became flatter and rounder (Fig. 1A). To prove that this morphological change was due to the effects of CDM1 and not due to the increase in cell density, a control culture was added. It was observed that, despite cultured to high confluency, ASCs exposed to normal expansion medium retained their spindle morphology (Fig. 1B). When the culture was fixed and histologically stained with Alcian Blue, dense blue stains in the background corresponding to deposited sulphated proteoglycans were detected. These findings indicated that chondrocyte-like cells were obtained when ASCs were cultured with CDM1 for 21 days.

As chondroitin sulphate (CS) was previously shown to promote chondrogenesis of MSCs18,19, it was added alongside 100 nM Avn C into CDM1 for differentiating ASCs. Based on the immunostaining intensity of LRP1, a receptor found in chondrocytes, it was first observed that naive untreated ASCs expressed low levels of LRP1. On the other hand, ASCs cultured in CDM1 for 21 days expressed significantly higher levels of LRP1. Additionally, when 100 nM Avn C and 500 µg/ml CS were supplemented into CDM1 and administered to the ASCs, the expression of LRP1 was notably higher as compared to just adding CDM1 (Fig. 3A and B). Alcian blue staining also showed more intense coloration when this combination was utilized for differentiation (Fig. 3C and D). Together, these findings revealed that Avn C and CS, when added into CDM1, enhanced ASC to chondrocyte differentiation.

Avn C and CS enhanced AGGRECAN, SOX9 and LRP1 mRNA expression in differentiated ASCs.

Besides immunostaining, RT-PCR was also performed on the differentiated cells after 21 days of culture. It was found that ASCs differentiated in CDM1 containing 100 nM Avn C and 500 µg/ml CS led to the highest mRNA expression of AGGRECAN, SOX9 and LRP1 as compared to CDM1, CDM1 added with only 100 nM Avn C and CDM1 added with only 500 µg/ml CS (Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. 1). This indicated that Avn C and CS, when added to CDM1 for differentiating ASCs, could promote the mRNA expression of chondrocyte markers AGGRECAN, SOX9 and LRP1.

Fig. 5The addition of Avn C and CS led to increased secretomic expression of SERPINs and complement proteins in chondrocyte-like cells.

The addition of Avn C and CS led to increased secretomic expression of SERPINs and complement proteins in chondrocyte-like cells.

Lastly, in order to elucidate the overall similarities and differences between ASCs, CDM1-differentiated cells and CDM1 + 100 nM Avn C + 500 µg/ml CS-differentiated cells, their secretomic proteins were identified and analyzed. When compared against a previously published comprehensive list of chondrocyte secretomes20, it was observed that CDM1- and CDM1 + Avn C + CS-differentiated cells were more notable for the secretion of proteins that were categorized as ‘Miscellaneous’. Between CDM1- and CDM1 + Avn C + CS-differentiated cells, the latter produced a higher percentage abundance of SERPIN proteins such as SERPINA3, SERPINE1 and SERPINE2 as well as complement proteins such as C1R, C3, CFD and CFH (Fig. 5). Taken together, these results validated that CDM1 was sufficiently potent to differentiate ASCs to the chondrocyte lineage and further addition of Avn C and CS led to higher abundance of SERPIN and complement proteins secretion.

Discussion

Chondrocytes lose their ability to regulate cartilage synthesis and degradation as they become senescent21. This usually corresponds to reduced production of essential extracellular matrix (ECM) components such as collagen and proteoglycans that are required for sustaining the structural integrity, thickness and elasticity of cartilage22. Clinical trials for resolving cartilage-related issues mostly include the injection of MSCs alone (NCT02118519, NCT02291926, NCT01873625) or MSCs encapsulated within an injectable hydrogel (NCT06078072, NCT01733186, NCT00850187) into the ailing site. While straightforward, utilizing MSCs as is does not appear to yield notable positive outcomes for cartilage regeneration, as evidenced from the complete lack of FDA-approved studies till date. Accordingly, this has prompted the scientific community to shift their focus to autologous23,24 and allogeneic25 chondrocyte implantation instead. Since MSCs can be differentiated to chondrocytes, they can still serve to replenish the senescent population of chondrocytes in patients. However, a limitation to this approach is the lack of differentiation efficacy due to MSCs’ innate heterogeneity12. In summary, there are two major findings in this study. First, we found that Avn C alone enhanced COL2A1 deposition in MSC-derived chondrocytes. Second, we demonstrated that Avn C and CS could heighten the chondrocyte characteristics of ASC-derived chondrocytes.

Type II collagen (COL2A1) is the major collagen synthesized by chondrocytes and is also a major component of cartilage. As COL2A1 is usually not deposited by ASCs, their presence in the background of the well plate (Fig. 2A), along with Alcian Blue staining (Fig. 1) and the expression of LRP1 (Fig. 3A) validated that the standard chondrocyte differentiation medium (CDM1) formulation, consistent with previous studies8,26,27, is effective in differentiating ASCs to chondrocytes. With ASCs differentiated using CDM1 as a baseline, we determined that the addition of 100 nM Avn C in CDM1 led to significantly higher amounts of COL2A1 deposition by the differentiated cells (Fig. 2). While Avn C has previously been reported to alleviate the expression of cartilage catabolic factors by chondrocytes in vitro when exposed to the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1β17, there were no other studies, to our knowledge, that investigated the influence of Avn C on MSC’s differentiation towards the chondrocyte lineage. Therefore, the finding that Avn C led to more COL2A1 deposition in ASC-derived chondrocytes is novel and significant since it suggested that Avn C can potentially boost the characteristics of chondrocytes. Importantly, this implied that since Avn C is usually administered in the 100 to 200 µM dosage range for anti-inflammatory purposes17,28,29 and 1000 to 2000 times more than the working concentration in this study, its utilization in cartilage-related diseases in addition to the implantation of ASCs, may function to not only ameliorate the degradation of cartilage in the inflamed tissue, but also promote the differentiation of the implanted ASCs to chondrocytes.

To further explore the possibility of increasing chondrocyte markers expression in the differentiated cells, we also added CS, an important constituent of cartilage and a molecule known to induce chondrogenesis30,31, along with 100 nM Avn C into CDM1 for differentiating the ASCs. As expected, we found that the combined supplementation of these two factors led to a significant increase in LRP1 protein expression as compared to CDM1 alone (Fig. 3). This observation was corroborated with RT-PCR experiments where the mRNA expression of LRP1 was found to be most prominent in the CDM1 + 100 nM Avn C + 500 µg/ml CS group (Fig. 4). To clarify, the marked increment of LRP1 mRNA and protein expression as compared to the control CDM1 group was not due to the addition of CS alone but rather, the combination of both Avn C and CS. Although LRP1 can also be found in many other cell types, it is especially highly expressed in the articular cartilage chondrocytes32. In chondrocytes, it primarily functions to protect the cartilage matrix by suppressing cartilage-degrading enzymes33. Recently, it was found to participate in biological processes such as autophagy and differentiation as well34. Correspondingly, the upregulation of LRP1 mRNA and protein in the cells indicated that the latter are becoming functionally similar to chondrocytes.

Accordingly, when ASCs were differentiated using CDM1 + 500 µg/ml CS, the LRP1 mRNA expression was not as pronounced as compared to the CDM1 + 100 nM Avn C + 500 µg/ml CS group. Moreover, the latter group also led to significant mRNA upregulation of other common chondrocyte markers such as AGGRECAN, SOX9 and COL2A135. It is currently unclear how or why Avn C and CS together resulted in this outcome in ASC-derived chondrocytes. Furthermore, given that both molecules have documented anti-inflammatory effects17,37,38, it would be interesting to find out if they can be administered in a combinatorial fashion to rescue bona fide chondrocytes and cartilage tissue challenged with a pro-inflammatory milieu. Future studies can elucidate the mechanism behind this synergy in promoting chondrocyte marker expressions as well as investigate if this effect can be reproduced in actual chondrocytes. Overall, this finding demonstrated that Avn C and CS can enhance the chondrocyte characteristics of ASC-differentiated chondrocytes.

Lastly, in the secretome comparison experiment, we found that ASCs differentiated using CDM1 and CDM1 + 100 nM Avn C + 500 µg/ml CS did not abundantly express the full range of proteins that were expected in normal human chondrocytes. Instead, the proteins secreted with higher relative abundance by the differentiated cells were mostly classified under ‘Miscellaneous’ (Fig. 5). Despite that, their consistent expression as compared to the ASCs datasets provided sufficient evidence of their differentiation towards the chondrocyte lineage. Besides that, it was observed that the addition of Avn C and CS into CDM1 resulted in the increased abundance of SERPINA3, SERPINE1 and SERPINE2 as well as C1R, C3, CFD and CFH. Generally, SERPIN proteins inhibit the proteolytic activity of serine proteases and prevent unintended degradation of the extracellular matrix39. During chondrogenesis, SERPINA3 was also shown to induce SOX9 expression, which in turn is crucial for maintaining chondrocyte lineage fate40,41. On the other hand, complement proteins regulate immune responses that directly or indirectly affect cartilage integrity. For instance, complement factor H (CFH), a component regulating the alternative immune pathway, binds to C3b to prevent unintended immune responses42. Studies have also found that the dysregulation of CFH correlated with cartilage-related diseases such as intervertebral disc degeneration, rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis43,44. Taken together, these findings suggested that the improvement of chondrocyte properties in ASCs differentiated using CDM1, Avn C and CS were due to the additional modulation of SERPIN and complement proteins. Future studies can validate this claim through loss- and/or gain-of-function studies, specifically targeting the SERPINS and complement proteins found to have increased abundance following Avn C and CS treatment. Furthermore, cells differentiated using the formulation reported in this work can be tested in vivo to evaluate clinical translatability.

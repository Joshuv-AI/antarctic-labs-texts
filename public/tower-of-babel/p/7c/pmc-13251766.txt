A clinical prediction rule for myotonia permanens associated with the SCN4A p.Gly1306Glu variant
Authors: Daniela Regonesi Polanco, Carlos Jaque Almendras, Marcela Lagos Lucero, Oslando Padilla Pérez, María de los Angeles Beytía Reyes, Ivonne Zamorano Valdebenito, Gabriel Bustamente Muller, Raúl Escobar Henríquez, Carmen Paz Vargas Leal, Marne Salazar Rivera, Daniela Avila-Smirnow
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMyotonia permanens (MP), a life-threatening condition, is prevalent in Chile, and is frequently associated with the SCN4A p.Gly1306Glu variant (exon 22), likely due to a founder effect in this population. Low clinical suspicion and the high cost of genetic testing contribute to delayed diagnosis and impaired quality of life. This study aimed to develop a clinical prediction rule (CPR) for early MP-p.Gly1306Glu detection specially in Chile, and confirm diagnosis through genetic testing.MethodsThis cross-sectional study was conducted at five Chilean hospitals and included healthy controls and patients with non-dystrophic myotonia (NDM). Participants were classified as controls, MP-p.Gly1306Glu, or non-permanens myotonia (NPM). Diagnosis was confirmed by SCN4A exon 22 sequencing or a gene panel (SCN4A, CLCN1). Fisher's exact test and logistic regression were used to identify clinical predictors of MP.ResultsA total of 19 controls and 17 patients with NDM (MP-p.Gly1306Glu/NPM = 8/9; mean age: 36 years) were included. Four predictors of MP-p.Gly1306Glu were identified: eyelid myotonia before age 2 (predictive value [PV] = 100%), myotonic discharges ≥28/30 s (PV = 95%), laryngospasm before age 2 (PV = 94%), and generalized myotonia before age 5 (PV = 88%). If all four predictors are present, MP-p.Gly1306Glu is highly probable and SCN4A exon 22 sequencing is recommended. If one to three predictors are present, targeted sequencing or a gene panel is advised, depending on clinical context.ConclusionThis CPR may facilitate early diagnosis of MP-p.Gly1306Glu and improve clinical outcomes, especially in Chile, and possibly other resource-limited settings, by guiding timely genetic confirmation and management. These predictors may support diagnostic criteria for MP/NPM, and guide future clinical trial design.Keywords: Founder effect, Laryngismus, Non-dystrophic myotonia, Potassium-aggravated myotonia, Sodium channels

Introduction

Non-dystrophic myotonia (NDM) is a channelopathy caused by pathogenic variants in CLCN1 or SCN4A, with the phenotype, inheritance pattern, and treatment varying by gene and variant (Stunnenberg et al., 2018a; Desaphy et al., 2020, 2021). Accurate differential diagnosis requires both clinical and genetic evaluations (Stunnenberg et al., 2020; Lehmann-Horn et al., 2008; Kullmann and Hanna, 2002). (Portaro et al., 2016; Caietta et al., 2013; Lehmann-Horn et al., 2017)NDMs are currently classified primarily within a genotype-based framework, encompassing sodium channel myotonias (SCM), and chloride channel-related myotonia congenita (autosomal dominant or recessive). SCM can be further classified in MP, myotonia fluctuans and acetazolamide responsive myotonia. Closely related SCN4A skeletal muscle channelopathies are paramyotonia congenita (occasional episodes of periodic paralysis [PP] with predominant myotonia), and PP (predominant PP with occasional myotonia) (Stunnenberg et al., 2020). Among NDMs, myotonia permanens (MP) represents the most severe form within the spectrum of SCM, and is not typically associated with episodes of PP (Stunnenberg et al., 2020). In contrast to other SCN4A-related phenotypes—such as myotonia fluctuans—which usually present with episodic myotonia, MP is characterized by frequent, generalized myotonia present at rest from early life, often leading to marked functional impairment (Portaro et al., 2016; Caietta et al., 2013; Lehmann-Horn et al., 2017).

MP is associated with gain-of-function variants in SCN4A, resulting in sustained sodium channel activation and muscle membrane hyperexcitability. At the molecular level, SCN4A encodes the Nav1.4 sodium channel located in the muscle membrane. Notably, in one of the earliest clinical descriptions by Lerche and colleagues (Rashaad Mohammed et al., 2023), MP was associated with the NM_000334.4:c.3917G > A (p.Gly1306Glu) variant in SCN4A, exon 22 (MP-p.Gly1306Glu), a finding later replicated in a case series identifying the same variant as a cause of MP (Portaro et al., 2016; Vicart et al., 2024; Fullam et al., 2022). Consistent with this, studies from Chile have shown that MP is frequently—and in most cases—associated with the p.Gly1306Glu variant, likely reflecting a founder effect in this population (Portaro et al., 2016; Vicart et al., 2024; Fullam et al., 2022). However, other SCN4A variants, including those located outside exon 22, have been reported in association with severe manifestations such as laryngospasm and MP ((Caietta et al., 2013; Lehmann-Horn et al., 2017). These observations support the concept that the biophysical impact of specific variants on sodium channel function, rather than their genomic location alone, is a key determinant of disease severity. Regarding the pathogenesis of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, the glycine 1306 residue is situated in the channel's hinge domain, where substitution by glutamate, slows channel inactivation and leads to muscle membrane hyperexcitability (Rashaad Mohammed et al., 2023; Matthews et al., 2008).

MP predominantly affects respiratory muscles, causing severe neonatal episodic laryngospasm, a life-threatening condition often requiring repeated emergency care and hospitalization (Dupré et al., 2009; Vivekanandam et al., 2023). Mortality rates of up to 20% have been observed within the first three decades of life (Avila-Smirnow et al., 2020), with anesthetic complications and myotonic crises occurring in approximately 40% of individuals with MP (Colding-Jørgensen et al., 2006). MP also significantly affects skeletal muscles, causing pain, functional impairment, and disrupted sleep, all of which impair quality of life (van den Berg et al., 2021; Diseño, 2009). Individuals with MP commonly experience limitations in recreational activities, sports, and writing; adults additionally report difficulty carrying objects, driving, and cooking (Stunnenberg et al., 2020).

Although the global prevalence of MP-p.Gly1306Glu is estimated at 0.01 per 100,000 (Lerche et al., 1993; Jurkat-Rott et al., 2010; Fournier et al., 2004), our research team found a prevalence of approximately 1 per 100,000 in Chile, a rate 100 times higher than elsewhere (Portaro et al., 2016). The recurrence of the same pathogenic variant in unrelated families may reflect a local founder effect contributing to genetic clustering in this population. This highlights the clinical significance of MP-p.Gly1306Glu in Chile, despite its rarity in other regions (Fullam et al., 2022). Unfortunately, MP-p.Gly1306Glu diagnosis is often delayed globally, by approximately two decades due to low clinical suspicion and limited access to genetic testing, contributing to reduced quality of life (van den Berg et al., 2021), and substantial financial burdens on families and the healthcare system (Fournier et al., 2006). Therefore, we propose that a clinical prediction rule (CPR) is necessary to facilitate the early diagnosis and treatment of MP-p.Gly1306Glu in Chile (Michel et al., 2007).

Electrophysiological testing, specifically the repeated short exercise test (RSET), aids in distinguishing NDM subtypes by identifying Fournier patterns I–III (Tan et al., 2011; Stunnenberg et al., 2018b). Typically, patterns I and III are associated with SCN4A variants, whereas pattern II is linked to CLCN1 variants (Vicart et al., 2021; Vivekanandam et al., 2024). Although RSET is recommended for evaluating patients with NDMs, its clinical utility in MP has not been thoroughly described.

Clinical guidelines recommend that individuals with clinical myotonia, but without muscle atrophy or systemic symptoms, undergo electromyography to confirm myotonia, followed by genetic testing of CLCN1 and SCN4A (Stunnenberg et al., 2020; Lehmann-Horn et al., 2008). In European populations, broad genetic testing has been proposed due to the absence of founder effects for pathogenetic variants.

Recent clinical trials have demonstrated the effectiveness of mexiletine (Andersen et al., 2017; De Bellis et al., 2017; Altamura et al., 2021) and lamotrigine (Altamura et al., 2021; Desaphy et al., 2004), in reducing myotonia severity in patients with NMDs, although no gene- or variant-specific treatments have been evaluated (Cea et al., 2020; Trivedi et al., 2013). MP may be refractory to these treatments, highlighting the need to explore alternatives such as flecainide (Dupré et al., 2009; Streib EW, 1987).

Patients with MP exhibit distinctive clinical characteristics, with an earlier and more severe phenotype compared with other NDMs, often including laryngospasm in young children and an increased mortality risk (Portaro et al., 2016; Vicart et al., 2024; Avila-Smirnow et al., 2020). Further, in our Chilean cohort, 82% of individuals with clinical signs of NDM and pattern III were found to harbor the pathogenic variant in exon 22. Given this high detection rate, targeted testing of exon 22 could be a cost-effective alternative, at least in the Chilean context, particularly given that comprehensive genetic panels are expensive, often unavailable in low- and middle-income countries, and generally not covered by insurance. Conversely, exon 22 sequencing is low-cost and available locally.

In this study, we proposed the development of a CPR to facilitate early diagnosis of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, especially in Chilean individuals, by identifying predictive clinical variables and confirming the diagnosis through targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A. Given the genetic heterogeneity of non-permanens myotonia (NPM) and prior evidence suggesting a more homogeneous phenotype in MP, we focused our analysis primarily on MP-p.Gly1306Glu patients.

Patients and methods

Ethical statements

This study was approved by the Pontifical Catholic University of Chile ethical standards committee on human experimentation (approval number: 210317003). Informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians.

Study design

This cross-sectional, case-control, multicenter study was conducted across five Chilean hospitals in three cities (Santiago [Hospital Dr. Sótero del Río, UC Christus, Roberto del Río], Chillan [Clínico Herminda Martin] and Puerto Montt [Dr. Dr. Eduardo Schütz Schroeder]). Given the rarity of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, a multicenter study approach was necessary to ensure adequate patient recruitment. Data were prospectively collected, and patients were recruited and evaluated between November 2022 and May 2024.

We reviewed the published literature and analyzed our institutional databases, corroborating an association between myotonia permanens in Chile and the p.Gly1306Glu variant, as well as associations between NPM and other variants in SCN4A and CLCN1. Accordingly, patients carrying the SCN4A p.Gly1306Glu variant were classified into the MP-p.Gly1306Glu cohort, whereas those with other SCN4A variants were grouped under the NPM cohort. The analysis focused on MP-p.Gly1306Glu patients due to their more homogeneous clinical profile and consistent genotype–phenotype correlations compared to the heterogeneous NPM group. Nonetheless, the NPM group was objectively analyzed using the same variables and methods applied to MP-p.Gly1306Glu to ensure comparability and methodological consistency.

Genetic ancestry was inferred from paternal and maternal surnames, which were classified as either of European or Indigenous American origin. For surnames of European origin, the corresponding country of origin was additionally identified.

Predictive variables

Based on a literature review and our database, 15 candidate variables were identified, spanning medical history, neurological examinations, and biochemical and electrophysiological tests. These variables were more frequent in patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu than those with NPM.

Participants

Both healthy controls and patients with NDM were included in this study. In the NDM group, the reference standard of diagnosis, a next-generation genetic panel encompassing the SCN4A and CLCN1 genes and Sanger sequencing of SCN4A exon 22 were performed, after collection of the 15 variables. Clinical evaluators were blinded to genetic test results, and if there were other members of the family with a genetic diagnosis, this was also blinded.

Patients and symptomatic relatives from participating hospitals were recruited through personal or email invitations, associated websites, and social media platforms.

Some participants were related (Table S1). However, all clinical, electrophysiological, and creatine kinase assessments included in the study were prospectively collected during a standardized study visit prior to genetic testing. Previously diagnosed family members were not included in the analysis because their data had been obtained non-systematically and were therefore not considered comparable with the prospectively evaluated cohort.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria for patients with NDM were being aged 5–60 years, with a clinical diagnosis of NDM made by a neurologist or a neuromuscular specialist, and no prior genetic testing. Healthy age- and sex-matched controls were also enrolled.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria included contraindications to venipuncture, intolerance to electrophysiological studies, non-cooperative behavior, psychiatric comorbidities, and pregnancy. Additional exclusions were made at the discretion of the investigator.

Sample size calculation

Recruitment aimed for 30 participants to detect a 50% difference in the presentation of a characteristic between one group and another, considering a power of 80% and a significance level of 95%.

Test methods

A standardized clinical form incorporating the 15 variables was developed for neurologists at each center, and training workshops were conducted for neurologists on the research team before patient evaluations began. Clinical and electrophysiological evaluations were conducted in four of the five hospitals. Owing to equipment limitations, Roberto del Río Hospital referred the recruited patients to the UC Christus Health Network for evaluations. Each hospital visit involved blood sampling for CK quantification, genetic testing, collection of clinical history variables, myotonia relaxation time measurement, and sequential electrophysiological studies. Electromyography studies were performed as part of the current study using a standardized protocol.

Clinical history variables were obtained from information provided by participants or their caregivers through a questionnaire. If the patients were using anti-myotonic medication (quinine and acetazolamide), it was suspended 24 h before evaluation.

Myotonia relaxation time measurement

To measure relaxation time, participants were instructed to close their eyes or to make a fist for 5 s, then quickly open them. This was repeated three times for the eyelids and dominant hand, with the average time calculated [34].

Electrophysiological study

Needle electrode studies were conducted on the abductor digiti minimi and tibialis anterior muscles, assessing myotonic discharge severity on a scale from +1 to +3, and evaluating burst duration [35]. The RSET involved repeated ulnar nerve stimulation with recordings from the abductor digiti minimi, performed at room temperature on the dominant hand and under cold conditions on the nondominant hand to analyze CMAP amplitude variation [24]. CMAP amplitude at baseline, and in the first, second and third repetitions of the RSET were registered. Two neurophysiologists evaluated the RSET curves, discussed and identified the patterns as Fournier I, II or III, or undefined if no clear pattern was observed, in patients presenting myotonic discharges. In the absence of myotonic discharges, RSET decrement, or any other electrophysiological abnormality, the study was classified as normal.

CK quantification

CK levels were measured from peripheral blood samples before the electrophysiological study and analyzed at each hospital.

Gene sequencing

The SCN4A and CLCN1 genetic panels were processed at a commercial Laboratory in the United States, whereas SCN4A exon 22 sequencing was performed at the UC-Christus Health Network clinical laboratory.

Statistical analysis

Three groups were analyzed: individuals with MP-p.Gly1306Glu, individuals with NPM, and healthy controls.

All variables were analyzed as dichotomic variables, and myotonic discharge duration (seconds) on EMG was also analyzed as a continuous variable. Data were analyzed using Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables, Fisher's exact probability test for dichotomic variables, odds ratio (OR), and logistic regression models. Comparisons were conducted between the Control and NMD groups, as well as between the MP-p.Gly1306Glu vs NPM groups. As this was a prospective study, data collection from participants was nearly complete, with minimal missing data. Specifically, CK measurements were unavailable for one healthy volunteer. For three patients with NDM, five out of six records were obtained in each RSET set. These missing data points were excluded from the analysis.

Mixed model analysis, which included second- and third-order interactions, was performed to evaluate CMAP amplitude (mV) in RSET according to genetic diagnosis (MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM), age, sex, and temperature (room temperature or cold). A raw model was applied to evaluate absolute values of CMAP, and a variation model was used to analyze the variation of CMAP amplitude compared to baseline.

Logistic regression was performed to define which variables had the better predictive values, and these were incorporated in the CPR.

Results

A total of 19 controls and 17 patients with NDM (eight with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and nine other NPMs) were recruited from five hospitals in Chile (Fig. 1). Table 1 provides a summary of the patient demographic characteristics. No significant differences were observed in demographic variables or comorbidities, except that patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu were younger than controls, and most controls were from the Metropolitan Region, owing to a recruitment bias. A total of eight patients with MP carried the SCN4A p.Gly1306Glu variant. Among patients with NPM, seven had variants in CLCN1 and two in SCN4A, with both homozygous, heterozygous and compound heterozygous genotypes identified (Table S1). This pattern reflects the genetic uniformity of MP compared with the heterogeneity found in NPM.

The median age of controls was 41 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 21–54), of patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu was 19 years (IQR: 8–30), and of patients with NPM was 35 years (IQR: 20–40). A significant age difference was observed between controls and patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (Mann–Whitney U test; p < 0.001), while no significant differences were found between controls and NPM (p = 0.227) or between MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM (p = 0.074). All 8 patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and 7 of 9 patients with NPM were diagnosed during the present study at the reported ages and subsequently started treatment.

Among controls, 57% were female and 43% male (11/8); among patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu, 37% and 63% (3/5); and among patients with NPM, 44% and 56% (4/5). There were no significant differences in sex distribution between groups (Fisher's exact test: controls vs. MP-p.Gly1306Glu, p = 0.42; controls vs. NPM, p = 0.689; MP-p.Gly1306Glu vs. NPM, p > 0.999).

All participants were of Latin ethnicity, with nearly all identifying as European (Spanish), except for one individual of mixed race in the NPM group (Spanish and Indigenous American) and one control of mixed Spanish Russian ancestry. There were no significant differences in ethnic distribution between groups (Fisher's exact test: controls vs. MP-p.Gly1306Glu, p = NA; controls vs. NPM, p = 0.321; MP-p.Gly1306Glu vs. NPM, p > 0.999).

Right-hand dominance was observed in 89% of controls (n = 17), 88% of patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (n = 7), and 100% of patients with NPM (n = 9). There were no significant differences in hand dominance between groups (Fisher's exact test: controls vs. MP-p.Gly1306Glu, p > 0.999; controls vs. NPM, p > 0.999; MP-p.Gly1306Glu vs. NPM, p = 0.471).

The most common comorbidities among controls were hypertension (21%, n = 4) and diabetes mellitus (16%, n = 3). Patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM did not present with significant comorbidities. No significant differences in the frequency of hypertension or diabetes were observed between controls and patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (Fisher's exact test: hypertension, p = 0.285; diabetes, p = 0.532), or between controls and NPM (hypertension, p = 0.273; diabetes, p = 0.530).

Clinical variables

None of the 10 clinical variables shown in Table 2 were present in the control group. A significant difference in the frequency of all these variables was observed between the control group and patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (Fisher's exact test), except for eyelid myotonia lasting >60 s (p = 0.080). In contrast, a significant difference between controls and patients with NPM was found only for autosomal dominant inheritance (p < 0.000), extraocular muscle myotonia (p = 0.026), and nocturnal myotonia (p < 0.000).

Other variables, including autosomal dominant inheritance (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 8/8; NPM = 6/9; p = 0.206), laryngeal myotonia triggered by cold foods (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 4/8; NPM = 1/9; p = 0.131), extraocular muscle myotonia (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 5/8; NPM = 3/9; p = 0.347), nocturnal myotonia (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 6/8; NPM = 7/9; p > 0.999), eyelid myotonia lasting >60 s (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 2/8; NPM = 0/9; p = 0.206), grip myotonia lasting >15 s (MP-p.Gly1306Glu = 4/8; NPM = 1/9; p = 0.131) did not show any significant differences between the groups. Table 2 present these variables across the three groups.

Two related patients (sisters) presented onset of myotonia before 5 years of age without apnea or eyelid myotonia before 2 years. Both carried a homozygous pathogenic CLCN1 intronic variant, c.1167-10T > C.

When applying the Bonferroni correction, dividing the significance threshold by the number of comparisons performed between the corresponding groups (yielding corrected significance levels of 0.0025, 0.0029, and 0.0026 for comparisons between controls and MP-p.Gly1306Glu, controls and NPM, and MP-p.Gly1306Glu versus NPM, respectively), we found that all associations remained statistically significant except for laryngeal myotonia, grip myotonia, and CK > 500 when comparing the control group with the MP-p.Gly1306Glu group. Similarly, when comparing the control group with the NPM group, statistical significance was lost for extraocular muscle myotonia. Finally, when comparing the two myotonia groups, NPM and MP-p.Gly1306Glu, only the association with onset of myotonia before the age of 2 years lost statistical significance (Table S2).

Plasma CK level

CK levels were normal in all controls, exceeded 500 U/L in four individuals with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and one with NPM, and exceeded 1000 U/L in one patient from each group. A significant difference in the frequency of CK > 500 U/L was observed between controls and patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (p = 0.020), while no significant differences were found between controls and patients with NPM (p > 0.999) or between MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM (p = 0.131). Similarly, no significant differences in CK > 1000 U/L were observed between controls and patients with MP (p = 0.308), between controls and NPM (p = 0.333), or between MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM (p > 0.999).

Electrophysiological variables

The assessed electrophysiological variables included myotonia severity (3+), Fournier Pattern and the duration of myotonic discharges (Table 3 and Fig. 2). None of the control participants exhibited myotonia, whereas nearly all individuals in the MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM groups met the criteria for myotonia severity (3+), except for one individual in the NPM group. There was a significant difference between controls and patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (p < 0.000) and those with NPM (p < 0.000). On the contrary, there was no significant difference between patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM (p = 0.111).

Mixed model for raw values of electrophysiological measurements

The time of measurement did not appear to be a relevant factor; values could potentially be averaged for each temperature condition and train over time (Table S3). Values were significantly higher in the cold condition compared to the room temperature condition (p = 0.39). In the cold condition, among females, values in train 3 were significantly lower than in train 1 (p = 0.025) and 2 (p = 0.029). In the room temperature condition, among patients with NPM, values in train 3 were also significantly lower than in train 1 (p = 0.029). In the cold condition, among patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu, values in train 3 were significantly lower than in the baseline (p = 0.021), train 1 (p = 0.028), and train 2 (p = 0.019).

Model of variations in electrophysiological parameters relative to baseline

The dependence of values over time was influenced by sex (p = 0.037). For males, variations relative to baseline did not show significant changes over time; conversely, for females, the percentage of variation relative to baseline tended to decrease over time (p = 0.049). The dependence of values on age is influenced by the temperature condition × train combination (p = 0.013). In most temperature condition × train combinations, there was no significant variation in values with age. However, exceptions were noted in the cold condition and train 1, where values significantly decreased with increasing age (p = 0.003). The dependence of values on the diagnostic group was not significant.

Assessment of clinical variables associated with MP-p.Gly1306Glu/NPM using odds ratios

Autosomal dominant inheritance (OR: 2.333; 95% CI: [1.274–4.272]) and onset of myotonia before the age of 2 years (OR: 56.000; 95% CI: [12.926–1071.635]) were both significantly associated with MP-p.Gly1306Glu. In contrast, laryngeal myotonia (OR: 8.000; 95% CI: [0.658–97.311]), extraocular muscle myotonia (OR: 3.333; 95% CI: [0.455–24.443]), nocturnal myotonia (OR: 0.857; 95% CI: [0.091–8.075]), grip myotonia lasting >15 s (OR: 8.000; 95% CI: [0.658–97.311]), CK > 500 U/L (OR: 8.000; 95% CI: [0.658–97.311]), and CK > 1000 U/L (OR: 1.143; 95% CI: [0.060–21.870]) were not significantly associated with MP-p.Gly1306Glu (Table 3).

The CI for onset of myotonia before the age of 5 years, apneas before the age of 2 years, eyelid myotonia before the age of 2 years, eyelid myotonia lasting >60 s, and myotonia severity (grade 3+) approached infinite values and were therefore not included in the OR analysis.

Predictive variables

Eyelid myotonia before the age of 2 years emerged as the most accurate predictive variable for MP-p.Gly1306Glu, being present in 9/9 patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and 0/8 patients with NPM. Additionally, the duration of the mean myotonic discharge (≥28/30 s of electrophysiological registry) was identified as a crucial predictive factor, correctly classifying 95% of patients.

In the logistic regression analysis, apneas before the age of 2 years were identified as the second most relevant variable, followed by the onset of myotonia before the age of 5 years, and onset of myotonia before the age of 2 years correctly classifying 94%, 88% and 88% of patients respectively (Table 4). Other variables had a predictive value bellow 80% and were therefore not included in the CPR.

CPR

Based on the results of the logistic regression, we propose a CPR that includes the most accurate predictive variables: eyelid myotonia before the age of 2 years, apneas before the age of 2 years, mean duration of myotonic discharges (≥28 out of 30 s of the electrophysiological recording), and onset of myotonia before the age of 5 years. If all four variables are present, there is a high probability of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, and targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A is recommended. If one to three predictors are present, the probability of MP-p.Gly1306Glu is moderate, and either a gene panel or targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A may be performed, depending on clinical context (e.g., cost, accessibility, and turnaround time). If none of the predictors are present, MP-p.Gly1306Glu is unlikely, and full sequencing of SCN4A and CLCN1 is advised (Fig. 3).

Clinical rule for patients with non-dystrophic myotonia (NDM) in Chile. If four or more predictive variables ar identified, targeted sequencing of exon 22of SCN4A is recommended. If no predictors are present, full sequencing of SCN4A and CLCN1 genes is advised. If one to three predictive variables are present, targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A may be recommended. However, full sequencing of SCN4A and CLCN1 should be considered depending on clinical context.

Discussion

This study identified several key characteristics that strongly predict MP-p.Gly1306Glu, which were incorporated into a CPR. The most significant predictors, ranked by importance, were eyelid myotonia before the age of 2, myotonic discharges observed in 93% of electrophysiological recordings (28 of 30 s), apneas before the age of 2, and onset of myotonia before the age of 5. Notably, eyelid myotonia and early-onset apneas and myotonia are clinical features readily obtainable from a patient's medical history. While eyelid myotonia before 2 years of age showed a predictive value of 100% in our cohort, additional clinical features remain relevant, especially in retrospective assessments. These findings highlight the potential for early suspicion of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, even in infants and young children, based on accessible clinical information. However, this CPR should be regarded as a screening marker, and genetic testing remains essential for diagnostic confirmation. This CPR is intended to be used in real-world clinical settings as a decision-support tool rather than a substitute for clinical judgment, helping to guide targeted genetic testing—particularly SCN4A exon 22 sequencing, motivated by limited access to comprehensive genetic testing in Chile and other low- and middle-income countries.

The features included in the CPR are well-recognized markers along the severity spectrum of non-dystrophic myotonias, particularly MP (Vicart et al., 2024; Vivekanandam et al., 2023; Vereb et al., 2021), in which early disease onset reflects increased membrane excitability and greater disease severity. Overall, the CPR yielded statistically robust results that were clinically and pathophysiologically coherent. Larger studies are warranted to further confirm differences between MP-p.Gly1306Glu and non-permanent myotonias.

Although early-onset eyelid myotonia was strongly associated with MP-p.Gly1306Glu in our cohort, similar clinical presentations have also been reported in other severe SCN4A-related phenotypes, supporting the notion that this feature, while highly suggestive, is not entirely specific to a single genotype (Lehmann-Horn et al., 2017; Castillo Taucher, 2015; 100 et al., 2021). After Bonferroni correction, onset of myotonia before 2 years lost statistical significance but remained near the corrected threshold, suggesting a potentially conservative adjustment.

The duration of myotonic discharges in electrophysiological recordings emerged as a sensitive predictor of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, which can be easily assessed by a trained neurologist. Interestingly, the RSET was not a sensitive marker for MP-p.Gly1306Glu. Although a type III Fournier pattern is typically expected in MP-p.Gly1306Glu and other NDMs related to SCN4A mutations (Stunnenberg et al., 2018b), many participants in this study exhibited a type III pattern regardless of whether their condition was linked to SCN4A or CLCN1 variants. Fournier originally described a type II response in patients with CLCN1 variants; however, subsequent studies have demonstrated variable RSET patterns, including normal findings or mild decrement without the classical pattern initially reported (Vivekanandam et al., 2024). Although RSET patterns have been used in the diagnostic workup of patients with NDM, prior reports lacked detailed statistical analysis and preceded the widespread availability of genetic testing (Tan et al., 2011; Stunnenberg et al., 2018b; Vicart et al., 2021). Interpretation of RSET patterns I–III remains subjective; therefore, we performed numerical analysis to explore significant associations with genetic factors. CMAP amplitude was significantly higher with cold exposure compared to room temperature, regardless of genetic diagnosis, sex, or age. In patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu, baseline, train 3 values, were significantly lower than train 1, and train 2 values under cold conditions, a pattern like that observed in patients with NPM. However, numerical variations of this kind may be difficult to evaluate in clinical practice, even if statistically significant.When comparing the genetic background of MP-p.Gly1306Glu patients in Chile with that reported in other regions, we found that most individuals were of European descent, consistent with previous reports in which most cases originated from Germany and Italy (Dupré et al., 2009; Vivekanandam et al., 2023; Avila-Smirnow et al., 2020).

Elevated CK levels were not identified as reliable predictors of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, although abnormal CK values were frequently observed in individuals with MP-p.Gly1306Glu and other forms of NDM [36]. This indicates that, although CK measurements, while reflective of muscle membrane instability, should not be solely relied upon for MP-p.Gly1306Glu diagnosis.

In contrast to European cohorts (Avila-Smirnow et al., 2020), we did not observe mortality in our cohort that could be clearly attributed to myotonic episodes, anesthetic complications, or exacerbations of myotonia. Although these findings should be interpreted cautiously given the sample size and follow-up duration, they suggest that mortality risk in MP may vary across cohorts and could be influenced by clinical context and management practices.

Access to genetic evaluations in Chile and other countries with lower resources, is limited, with a shortage of specialists. In Chile, most of whom are concentrated in the capital city of Santiago (39). Additionally, genetic testing is not covered by insurance providers, creating a significant barrier for patients. Only a few certified national laboratories offer limited molecular testing, and specialized analysis often require sending samples abroad at considerable expense.

The main limitation of this study is that it was conducted exclusively within the Chilean population, potentially reducing the generalizability of findings to other populations. Nevertheless, in other settings with limited resources—or even where genetic panels are available—this CPR may aid in the early suspicion of MP-p.Gly1306Glu and guide patient counseling and management prior to the availability of genetic results. This could be clinically useful in emergency situations such as laryngospasm, myotonic crisis, or anesthetic complications. Additionally, these clinical predictors could be explored as potential diagnostic criteria for MP-p.Gly1306Glu and NPM, thereby supporting both clinical practice and the design of clinical trials. Another limitation of the present study is that patients with MP-p.Gly1306Glu presented at a younger age than the broader NDM cohort, resulting in incomplete age matching for EMG assessments between groups. However, given the rarity of the condition, closer age matching was not feasible. A further consideration is that the practical utility of the proposed CPR may evolve over time as clinical recognition improves and comprehensive genetic testing becomes more widely available, as occurs with many clinical prediction tools.

Although the high frequency of the SCN4A p.Gly1306Glu variant in Chile might suggest prioritizing exon 22 sequencing, this approach should not be applied indiscriminately to all NDM cases. This CPR aims to identify patients with a high likelihood of MP-p.Gly1306Glu, thereby guiding targeted genetic testing and avoiding missed alternative channelopathies.

Most participants were adolescents and young adults (IQR: 14–46), which may further limit generalizability to other age groups. Nevertheless, the resting electrophysiological testing is feasible in infants and may guide suspicion of MP-p.Gly1306Glu. Although the CPR cannot be directly applied to newborns, the clinical variables identified herein can be assessed in infants and, given the high prevalence of autosomal dominant inheritance in Chile, may also be evaluated in parents of affected children, facilitating early molecular diagnosis in at-risk infants (Portaro et al., 2016; Vicart et al., 2024; Fullam et al., 2022). MP-p.Gly1306Glu, and MP in general, may represent life-threatening conditions during infancy, particularly in the presence of laryngospasm or apnea. In this context, targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A may represent an appropriate first diagnostic approach in high-risk patients. In patients with negative exon 22 testing but persistent clinical suspicion, escalation to full SCN4A sequencing remains appropriate. Importantly, the proposed CPR should not delay urgent diagnostic or treatment decisions but rather support early clinical suspicion and rapid targeted testing. Offspring of affected carriers should undergo early clinical and genetic evaluation.

The implications of these findings are substantial for improving early and cost-effective diagnostic approaches. Given the lack of previous reports on NDM distribution in Latin America, these results provide valuable insights and a foundation for future regional studies. By identifying patients whose clinical phenotypes strongly suggest MP-p.Gly1306Glu, targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A—which is available domestically and significantly less expensive than full genetic panels—could be prioritized. This approach reserves costlier and comprehensive genetic panels, which require international processing, for patients who do not meet key predictive characteristics (39). Considering that full genetic panel sequencing costs five to ten times more than exon 22 sequencing, this strategy could substantially reduce financial burdens on families and the healthcare system (40).

Overall, when testing is limited, we recommend that for patients with NDM presenting with four of the identified predictive indicators, targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A be performed. For those with one to three two indicators, targeted sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A or full genetic panel testing should be considered. Implementing this CPR by trained neurologists could reduce diagnostic delays for MP-p.Gly1306Glu in Chile and low-middle income countries, leading to earlier treatment and improved outcomes. However, in high-risk patients, immediate sequencing of exon 22 of SCN4A or full SCN4A sequencing may be considered as the initial diagnostic approach, depending on local availability and access to genetic testing. In broader diagnostic settings, comprehensive SCN4A screening remains essential, since in regions with lower MP prevalence, clinical features may overlap with other SCN4A-related NDM.

Conclusions

MP-p.Gly1306Glu is a severe disease posing a significant health concern, specially in Chile that, if left untreated, can be lethal in early life stages. In this study, we designed a rapid and cost-effective CPR. Furthermore, this approach promotes the development of national capabilities in molecular diagnostics, fostering greater technological and research autonomy, whereas reducing dependency on international resources. This work contributes to a global understanding of MP and sets the stage for future research and collaboration.

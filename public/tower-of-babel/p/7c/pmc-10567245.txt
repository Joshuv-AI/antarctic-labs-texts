Application of Internet hospitals for first-visit patients with COVID-19 during the pandemic in China
Authors: Ye Zhang, Ye Zhang, Jie Huang, Liang Dong
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere has been controversy over whether to carry out online medical inquiries and electronic prescription for first-visit patients. While Internet hospitals have become crucial to ensure providing online diagnosis and prescription needs for first-visit COVID-positive patients.ObjectiveWe describe the results and advantages of online medical treatment during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) outbreak and analyse the results and advantages of online medical treatment.MethodsData were collected from the patients who received online medical inquiry services through the COVID-19 Special Section of our Internet hospital. The user demographic characteristics, user behaviour, clinical symptoms and medical records were collected. Baseline data of offline outpatient patients were also collected.ResultsA total of 16 173 online medical inquiries were included. The daily online outpatient service volume reached a peak of 2446 person-times. The median age was 29.92 years (SD 15.59), ranging from 20–39 years (n = 8870, 54.84%). A total of 89.20% (n = 14 426/16 173) patients had epidemic-related symptoms. Moreover, 23.35% of the people were diagnosed as COVID-19-positive through antigen or nucleic acid testing. Furthermore, 86.61% (14 008/16 173) of the patients visited our hospital for the first time or had no offline physical outpatient service record within the past 3 months. The demand for medical inquiries was mainly for drug dispensing, followed by medication guidance and disease diagnosis. And 2.07% (334/16 173) of the patients needed to go to the offline hospital for further treatment.ConclusionThe service model of the COVID-19 Special Section based on Internet hospitals provided the public with the necessary medical support during the COVID-19 outbreak. It provides a reference for future pandemics or public health emergencies.

Introduction

After the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) outbreak, China adhered to the epidemic prevention and control strategy of dynamic zeroing, and dynamic containment control and precise isolation became common.1 The Chinese government encourages the development of innovative medical treatment models such as Internet hospitals.2,3 When offline treatment channels are blocked, the use of Internet medical care by people in closed and controlled areas becomes a real demand, and more and more people have now begun to recognize and understand Internet hospitals.4 Compared with physical hospitals, Internet hospitals have the advantages of contactless treatment, higher efficiency, lower costs and optimized resource allocation.5 Within some years of anti-epidemic time, patients’ medical behaviour modes have shifted from on-site to online, and the demand for Internet hospitals has gradually increased.5,6 Internet + medical centres dominated by public hospitals continue to emerge.3,4,6 Internet + medical centres and health businesses have gradually evolved.7

In December 2022, as China continued to optimize and adjust its epidemic prevention and control measures, COVID-19 infection was adjusted from ‘Class B disease under Class A control’ to ‘Class B disease under Class B control’.8 COVID-19-infected persons would no longer be isolated, close contacts would no longer be determined and high-risk areas would no longer be demarcated. In other words, epidemic prevention and control entered a new stage in China. Within a short time, the number of patients infected with COVID-19 had risen sharply nationwide. For most patients with mild symptoms, asymptomatic symptoms and stable basic diseases, the Chinese government suggested that isolation treatment at home should be the main approach. Because of the huge population base, how to ensure continuous medical services for isolated people at home has become a huge challenge. Simultaneously, the demand for medication among residents has increased sharply. There are serious shortages of cough medicines, antipyretics, antibiotics and antiviral drugs. China is facing the most severe test after the normalization of COVID-19 epidemic prevention and control.

There is a lack of research and experience on online diagnosis and treatment for first-visit patients. The Chinese government has proposed to provide medical guidance, electronic prescriptions and psychological support for COVID-19-positive patients isolated at home through the Internet diagnosis and treatment platform and encourages the delivery of drugs to patients’ homes.9 This policy has increased the demand for online medical care and has broken the previous taboo of ‘no Internet diagnosis and treatment activities for first-visit patients’.10 Simultaneously, qualified Internet medical service projects have been included in the scope of medical insurance payments.11 Internet medical service has ushered in a historic opportunity.

Our hospital established the COVID-19 Special Section of the Internet Hospital for the first time, providing online medical inquiries and offline drug delivery services for COVID-19-positive patients, including first-visit patients at home. Herein, we conducted a retrospective analysis to analyse the public’s demand for Internet medicine during the COVID-19 outbreak in China, evaluated the results and advantages of Internet medicine and provided a reference for response to public health emergencies in the future.

Methods

Construction and function of the COVID-19 Special Section

To further share the pressure of offline medical institutions and meet the medical needs of positive patients at home, the Internet Hospital of the First Hospital of Jiaxing established the ‘COVID-19 Special Section’ on 13 December 2022. Any user with a mobile phone can apply through the WeChat applet of the Internet Hospital. Doctors take advantage of their fragmented time to receive and provide medical services on mobile phones anytime and anywhere. The Special Section provides online medical inquiry services, electronic prescriptions, medication guidance, home isolation guidance and other medical services for COVID-19-positive patients at home. These services are not only aimed at patients who have been re-diagnosed at our hospital but also newly diagnosed patients looking for open online diagnosis and treatment services. To ensure that patients can obtain pharmacy services safely, quickly, conveniently and efficiently, we combined pharmacy services with intelligent prescription review and pharmacist review interventions. We also realized online appointments for medical examinations, offline contactless drug delivery services or self-fetching after medical insurance payment. The specific functions and service flow are presented in figure 1.

Service functions and the process followed at the COVID-19 Special Section of the Internet hospital

The COVID-19 Special Section comprises 124 clinicians and pharmacists, providing 24 × 7 online medical inquiry services. This section adjusts the timetable dynamically according to the number of inquiries, extending the service time and shortening the online waiting time of patients as much as possible. The section is divided into four teams for different patient groups: adult, child, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and pharmaceutical consultations. The TCM Special Section provides TCM-prescriptions developed by our hospital for the common symptoms of COVID-19, including 10 prescriptions for fever, cough, sore throat, fatigue, etc.

Data collection

Data were collected from all of the patients who received online medical inquiry services through the COVID-19 Special Section of our Internet hospital between 13 December 2022 and 10 January 2023. A retrospective analysis was conducted. We included online medical inquiries related to COVID-19. Duplicate, non-related to COVID-19, and incomplete inquiries are excluded. Patient variables, including sex, age, visit time, symptoms, medical inquiry process, medication records, historical visit records and offline visit suggestions were recorded. Epidemic-related symptoms included fever, sore throat, cough, myalgia, fatigue, loss of smell and taste, nasal congestion, runnynose, diarrhoea, conjunctivitis and COVID-19 symptoms.12 A patient could have had one or more symptoms. The medical inquiry process assessed the purpose of initiating the application, detailed records of doctor–patient communication and the evaluation records collected after the medical inquiry. Historical visit records referred to the records of patients who had visited our physical offline outpatient service before this online visit. Baseline data of offline fever outpatient patients were also collected.

First-visits were defined as never having visited the offline physical outpatient department of our hospital before the online visit. Patients who tested positive for antigens or nucleic acids were confirmed as COVID-19-positive. Offline guidance was defined as the presence of offline patient care records within the last 3 days. Each inquiry record was manually analysed by a trained researcher with a medical background and rechecked by another researcher. Any differences in the process of data collection would be resolved through discussion with all researchers.

Data analysis

Data were managed using Excel 2016. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM Corporation). The measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Frequency and percentage were the categorical variables analysed. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics statement

Ethical approval was obtained from the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Jiaxing (No. 2023-LY-022) prior to study initiation.

Results

Total medical inquiry volume

From 13 December 2022 to10 January 2023, 16 851 people in the COVID-19 Section applied for the online medical inquiry services. The number of daily online outpatients increased first and then decreased, reaching a peak on 17 December 2022. The number of daily online outpatients reached 2446, and then gradually decreased. During the same period, 18 082 offline fever outpatient visits were recorded. The number of online visits showed obvious fluctuations and peaks compared with the offline visits, as shown in figure 2. Through semantic analysis, 576 repeated inquiries and 102 inquiries unrelated to the epidemic were eliminated. Finally, 16 173 medical inquiry seekers were included for analysis. No missing data occurred.

Historical diagnosis and treatment records

By querying the historical visit records of each patient, 86.61% (14 008/16 173) of the patients were found to be on their first visit to our hospital or did not meet the conditions for online subsequent visits. Among those, 5912 were first-time patients, and there was no hospital visit record before this online visit. The hospital established online archives for these first-visit patients through the system. Moreover, 8096 person-times had not visited our offline physical outpatient service in the past 3 months.

Demographic characteristics

Of those 16 173 online patients, including 7279 males (45.01%) and 8894 females (54.99%). The youngest patient was 72 days old, whereas the oldest was 93 years old. The median patient age was 29.92 years (SD 15.59). Compared with the other age groups, users aged 30–39 years accounted for the largest proportion (32.91%, 5323/16 173), followed by users aged 20–29 years (21.93%, 3547/16 173). Additionally, 2957 patients were younger than 14 years, of which 45.52% (1346/2957) were children aged less than 6 years. All children aged under 14 years were provided an online medical inquiry by their guardians. Moreover, 622 patients were over 60 years old, and 441 patients were pregnant women. In contrast, the elderly account for a relatively high proportion among the offline patients. The proportion of 50–59 years old and over 60 years old was 11.3% and 14.5%, respectively, which was significantly higher than the offline group. There were significant differences in age distribution between the two groups (P < 0.05). No significant difference between the sex (P > 0.05). Characteristics of sections and disease distribution

Among the four sections we provided, the most popular one was the adult section, accounting for 61.82% (9998/16 173) of the total number of medical inquiries, followed by the paediatrics section at 24.39% (3945/16 173), TCM section at 8.58%(1387/16 173) and pharmacy section at 5.21%(843/16 173). The most frequently asked question during new medical inquiries was whether online drug dispensing and delivery services could be provided, followed by medication guidance and medical inquiries after COVID-19 related symptoms. Approximately 52.87% (n = 8550) of the patients clearly put forward the need for drug dispensing in their purpose of medical inquiry.

Distribution of users’ symptoms

The treatment contents were classified according to the patients’ symptoms, including fever, cough, muscle soreness, sore throat, fatigue, nasal congestion, headache, diarrhoea, etc. Among the 16 173 medical inquiry seekers, 89.20% (n = 14 426/16 173) had epidemic-related symptoms, and the distribution of their symptoms was similar to that of patients with COVID-19.12 Approximately 38.79% of the medical inquiry seekers had ≥3 epidemic-related symptoms. Fever (n = 9847, 60.89%) was the most common symptom, followed by cough and expectoration (n = 6012, 37.17%), myalgia (n = 4037, 24.96%), sore throat (n = 3432, 21.22%), nasal symptoms (n = 2418, 14.95%), headache (n = 1610, 9.95%), chills (n = 1457, 9.01%), fatigue (n = 1234, 7.63%), nausea and vomiting (n = 962, 5.95%), dizziness (n = 820, 5.07%), shortness of breath (n = 217, 1.34%), diarrhoea (n = 207, 1.28%) and eye discomfort (n = 173, 1.07%). The distribution of specific symptoms is shown in figure 3. Approximately 23.35% of all medical inquiry seekers were diagnosed as COVID-19-positive through antigen or nucleic acid testing.

Online prescriptions and drug distribution

A total of 4964 online prescriptions were completed, including 4316 for antipyretic drugs, 1631 for drugs improving dry throat and sore throat, 1488 for drugs against cough and expectorant phlegm and 359 for TCM. The qualified rate of artificial intelligence drug use audit was 99.58%. Approximately 0.42% of inappropriate prescriptions were approved after manual intervention by pharmacists. Moreover, 60.94% (3025/4964) of the patients chose online payment and offline contactless drug delivery. Geographical distribution analysis showed that 98.84% (2990/4964) of the patients came from Jiaxing City, Zhejiang Province; 13 patients came from other cities in Zhejiang Province; and 22 patients (0.73%) came from other provinces. Due to the impact on the logistics capacity during the epidemic, 39.06% (1939/4964) of the patients chose the ‘offline self-delivery’ method to obtain drugs from offline hospitals.

Offline visit guidance

Of the 16 173 patients receiving online medical inquiry services, 201(1.24%) were recommended by doctors to go to offline hospitals for treatment owing to their illness. Based on the visit record query, 334 (2.07%) patients had offline visit records within the last 3 days.

Discussion

The COVID-19 outbreak has caused obstacles in traditional face-to-face medical services, and the use of telemedicine as a long-distance and non-contact way of treatment has brought about unique opportunities and challenges.13,14 As a new type of outpatient service, Internet hospitals in China can provide online medical services.3 The Chinese government supports the development of newly emerging Internet hospitals,2,15 and encourages their inclusion in epidemic prevention and control. Internet hospitals can disrupt the geographical restrictions of high-quality medical resources,16,17 spread prevention and control knowledge in a timely and rapid manner,18 provide barrier-free medical services,19 and help prevent and control epidemics.20 COVID-19 has promoted the widespread use of Internet hospitals in China.21 Patients’ demands for online healthcare services have increased, and the mode of health-seeking behaviours has gradually shifted from on-site to online.21,22

We found that with the outbreak of the social epidemic, the number of online clinics has increased rapidly and exceeded that of offline fever clinics during the same period. Approximately 86.61% of the patients paid their first-visit to our hospital or had no offline clinic visit record within the past 3 months. Due to the large number of first-visit patients, the former model of Internet hospitals could no longer meet the needs of the public when emergency public health events occur. The decision of whether to open up online healthcare services and electronic prescriptions for first-visit patients can affect the mode provided for telemedicine service. By using the COVID-19 Special Area Service from the Internet hospital, our hospital has realized online inquiries and prescriptions for first-visit patients, online payment for medical insurance and drug distribution. The above achievements are rare in other Internet hospitals in China and have provided a new exploration of Internet medical services for many first-visit patients. Although these data do not directly explain that online medical service has reduced the total number of cases in hospitals, it is certain that telemedicine has reduced the burden of offline hospitals to a certain extent, avoiding hospital over crowding and reducing the risk of cross-infection.

To ensure medical safety, previously, the Chinese government clearly stipulated that online medical inquiry services are only open to patients who require subsequent visits at physical medical institution,10,15 and only provides a simple health consultation service for first-visit patients. However, there are still disputes on how to clearly define the first and subsequent visits. The vast majority of physical hospitals define subsequent visits as the ones with offline outpatient visit records within 3–6 months. The regulatory policies in the USA and other countries for the first visit also differ significantly from state to state.23,24 Faced with massive online demand during the outbreak, China has issued policies to encourage the provision of Internet medical services for COVID-19 patients isolated at home, including first-visit patients.9 Our research results showed that 89.20% of the patients had epidemic symptoms related to COVID-19 infection, such as fever, muscle aches, cough and sore throat, and that 23.35% of the patients were confirmed positive for COVID-19. Compared with other diseases, the risk of the first diagnosis of COVID-19 was not too risky when the patients conducted their first-visits to Internet hospitals. Additionally, approximately 2.07% of the patients in this study were directed to offline hospitals for further treatment owing to their conditions, which were not suitable for online medical inquiry. Internet hospitals operated by public hospitals can connect online services with offline medical visiting processes7 and strengthen the security of online medical services in terms of first-visit safety. The Internet medical service approach we explored to breaks through the first-visit limitation as a special response to a special period and has strong practical significance.

Our research provides an overview of the distribution of departments and diseases. We found that the public’s awareness of COVID-19 had increased significantly after many years of the anti-epidemic. Compared with the panic of being infected 3 years ago,20,25 the online medical inquiry content during the epidemic outbreak was more inclined to demand for drugs and medication guidance. We played a positive role in drug consultation, rational drug use, pharmaceutical examination and drug distribution. The vast majority of patients obtained drugs through contactless delivery services, consistent with that reported in other literature studies.26,27 Additionally, picking up medicines from the hospital also made up for the impact of the logistics paralysis during the outbreak of the epidemic. Pharmaceutical services could become a bridge between doctors and patients, saving patients’ time and economic costs and ensuring the safe and reasonable use of drugs.28 At the beginning of the epidemic, psychologists and paediatricians joined the online service,29 meeting an increased demand at the time. Our online services were also targeted at different groups of people. The results showed that the proportion of special needs children and pregnant women was increased during the study period, which is rare in ordinary times. During the epidemic, how to find public needs in time, adjust response strategies in time and improve the accessibility of Internet hospital applications were also detailed issues that could not be ignored.

With the advent of the information age, China now has 986 million mobile Internet users. Users aged 30–39 years account for the highest proportion (20.5%), followed by those aged 20–29(17.8%) and 40–49(18.8%) years.30 Our results showed that the age range of online outpatients was mainly 20–39 years, accounting for 54.84% of the total inclusions. In contrast, elderly patients accounted for a higher proportion of offline patients than online patients. In other words, young people are more adaptable to telemedicine and prefer to use mobile applications to solve less complex medical and health problems.14 Additionally, the proportion of women was slightly higher than that of men, and the sex distribution was consistent with the research results of Jiang et al.21,31 In other words, women were more likely than men to receive mobile medical services from online hospitals. These results describe the current user market of Internet hospitals and should be referenced when exploring new strategies in the market.

Additionally, evidence-based medicine has confirmed that the combination of traditional Chinese and western medicine may have clinical advantages in alleviating the symptoms and improving the prognosis of patients with COVID-19.32,33 In COVID-19 medical treatment, China has always adhered to a combination of traditional Chinese and western medicines to ensure full play to the unique advantages and role of TCM.34 However, TCM focusses on looking, listening, asking and feeling the pulse, and pulse diagnosis is not possible in telemedicine, so there are only a few reports in which TCM has been prescribed through telemedicine. Our study is a major exploration in this field. The Chinese medicine section is very popular in our field of research. Approximately 7.23% of the drug use comes from TCM-prescriptions, which has unique advantages and plays a complementary role in the situation of drug shortage.

Internet hospitals are valuable resources that help deal with large-scale public health emergencies.35 Of course, we also recognize some limitations of this study. First, online medical inquiries are an indirect way of communication. The audience includes mainly mild patients, and the ability to treat critical illnesses is limited. Given the lack of information and limited communication time, online doctors may only provide rough medical advice to patients. The efficiency of doctor–patient communication and the quality of doctors’ records were among the factors that influenced the results. In the future, whether Internet hospitals in China will be open to first-visit patients and in what form they can better meet the online medical needs of different people deserves in-depth consideration and exploration. Second, usually, young people apply for online treatment on the behalf of elderly and young patients; such patients may communicate inadequately and provide incomplete reporting. This can affect the study data and is one of the limitations of Internet medicine. How to help people with chronic diseases (the elderly) and those who find it inconvenient to seek medical treatment to better understand and use the Internet to receive medical treatment remains a big challenge that needs to be tackled in the future. Additionally, the number of online medical inquiries rises sharply and then declines gradually, which may be related to the slowing of the epidemic situation and the improvements in drug shortage. Such fluctuations are similar to the prediction results of the epidemic transmission dynamics published by Leung et al.36 Some studies have shown that 45% of all COVID-19 survivors will have long COVID-19 symptoms lasting for 12 weeks after initial infection.37 Therefore, COVID-19 may recur and last for a period. The research duration of this study was relatively short, and there was no long-term follow-up, including that for user experience analysis. The role of Internet hospitals in long COVID-19 needs to be confirmed through more prospective or retrospective studies in the future. Finally, a comparison between offline and Internet hospitals is needed to deepen our understanding of the characteristics of Internet hospitals, such as the studies from Jiang et al. and Zhang et al. This will be focussed upon in our future work.

Conclusion

Our study results showed that during the rapid spread of the epidemic, Internet hospitals could quickly establish online services in a short time. Internet medical treatment is the best form of contactless medical treatment for optimising the medical treatment process, meeting the public’s demands for medical services and avoiding excessive medical crowding. Taken together, Internet hospitals play a vital role in preventing and managing pandemics and are an important part of the new medical model of public health emergency systems.

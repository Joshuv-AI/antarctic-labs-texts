Nanopore full-length sequencing reveals Nudt21 knockdown drives genome-wide 3′UTR shortening and transcriptome reprogramming in mouse hepatocytes
Authors: Qiwei Jia, Haitao Dai, Yong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlternative polyadenylation (APA) fine-tunes gene expression at the post-transcriptional level by generating transcript isoforms with distinct 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) lengths. Nudix hydrolase 21 (Nudt21), a core subunit of the cleavage factor Im (CFIm) complex, is a key regulator of APA; however, its function in normal hepatocytes remains poorly characterized. This study aims to elucidate the regulatory role of Nudt21 in shaping APA landscapes and gene expression in normal hepatocytes.ResultsKnockdown of Nudt21 in the normal mouse hepatocyte cell line AML12 was performed, followed by full-length transcriptome sequencing using Nanopore Tail ISO-seq technology, which systematically mapped poly(A) site selection and tail length dynamics at single-molecule resolution. Integrative analysis revealed that Nudt21 knockdown led to differential expression of 1,386 genes and triggered genome-wide remodeling of APA patterns: 2,864 genes exhibited significant APA changes, among which 2,682 (93.6%) showed 3′UTR shortening and only 182 (6.4%) showed lengthening. Genes with 3′UTR shortening were significantly enriched in pathways related to cell cycle, protein transport and degradation, and RNA processing. Among the genes that exhibited both 3′UTR shortening and differential expression, the numbers of upregulated and downregulated genes were similar, suggesting that 3′UTR shortening exerts bidirectional regulatory effects on gene expression and also indicating that APA remodeling is an important mechanism driving transcriptomic reprogramming.ConclusionThis study provides the first systematic dissection of Nudt21-mediated maintenance of 3′UTR homeostasis in normal hepatocytes, demonstrating its role in modulating gene expression through APA regulation. These findings offer a valuable resource and novel insights into the contribution of APA dysregulation to liver physiology and pathology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-026-12929-y.Keywords: Nudt21, Alternative polyadenylation, Nanopore sequencing, 3′UTR shortening, Transcriptomic reprogramming, Hepatocytes

Introduction

Gene expression in eukaryotes is subject to multilayered and precise regulation, among which post-transcriptional control plays an essential role in determining the final protein output. Alternative polyadenylation (APA), a prevalent mechanism of post-transcriptional regulation, enables more than half of human protein-coding genes to generate transcript variants with distinct 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) lengths through the selection of alternative poly(A) sites [1]. The 3′UTR harbors a variety of cis-regulatory elements, including microRNA (miRNA) binding sites, RNA-binding protein (RBP) recognition motifs, and other regulatory sequences that modulate mRNA stability, translation efficiency, and subcellular localization [2]. Thus, by altering 3′UTR length, APA can influence gene expression levels and functions to a certain extent.

APA is tightly controlled by multiple trans-acting factors, among which the cleavage factor Im (CFIm) complex plays a critical role. CFIm is composed of three subunits: CFIm25 (encoded by the NUDT21 gene), CFIm59, and CFIm68. As the core recognition subunit, CFIm25 specifically binds to upstream UGUA-rich sequences and promotes distal poly(A) site selection, thereby preventing premature 3′UTR shortening [3, 4]. Studies have shown that Nudt21/CFIm25 expression levels correlate positively with 3′UTR length, and its downregulation leads to genome-wide 3′UTR shortening [5, 6].

Recent studies have revealed that APA dysregulation is closely associated with various human diseases, including cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, and metabolic dysfunction [7]. Genome-wide 3′UTR shortening has been widely observed across multiple cancer types and is associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis [8–10]. This APA switch is often accompanied by downregulation of Nudt21, resulting in oncogenes escaping miRNA-mediated repression due to 3′UTR shortening and consequently becoming aberrantly upregulated [11]. However, most current APA studies have focused on cancer cell lines or disease models, leaving the role of Nudt21 in maintaining APA homeostasis in normal cells, particularly hepatocytes, largely unexplored.

As the metabolic hub of the body, the liver relies on precise gene expression regulation for its normal function. Dysregulation of hepatocyte metabolism, detoxification, and synthetic functions is closely linked to the pathogenesis of various liver diseases, including hepatic steatosis, liver fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Emerging evidence suggests that APA dysregulation may contribute to liver disease pathogenesis, although the underlying mechanisms remain poorly defined [7, 12–14]. Therefore, elucidating the function of Nudt21 in normal hepatocytes is of critical importance for understanding gene expression control under both physiological and pathological conditions in the liver.

Compared with conventional second-generation sequencing, third-generation sequencing technologies such as Nanopore enable direct sequencing of full-length transcripts without fragmentation, allowing unbiased capture of transcript isoforms containing complete 3′ ends. This provides unprecedented resolution for precisely resolving alternative poly(A) site selection and the consequent 3′UTR length dynamics [15]. In this study, we employed Nanopore Tail ISO-seq to systematically profile the transcriptome and APA landscape upon Nudt21 knockdown in normal mouse hepatocytes. Our aim is to elucidate how Nudt21 governs 3′UTR length through APA regulation and consequently impacts hepatocyte gene expression, thereby laying a foundation for understanding the role of APA in liver biology.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and treatment

Alpha Mouse Liver 12 (AML-12) cells (Procell, Wuhan, China) are derived from the hepatocytes of 3-month-old adult mice. Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% insulin-transferrin-selenium (ITS), 40 ng/mL dexamethasone, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution. The cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. All cells used were within ten passages and were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) DNA profiling.

Nudt21 knockdown and RNA extraction

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) specifically targeting mouse Nudt21 (si-Nudt21) and negative control siRNAs (si-NC) were designed and synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology (Beijing, China). Each siRNA pool consisted of four distinct sequences (Table S1) to achieve robust and efficient knockdown. Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen) in 6-well plates according to the manufacturer‘s instructions. Cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). RNA quality was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, and all samples exhibited RNA integrity number (RIN) values greater than 8.5.

Western blotting

AML12 cells were lysed with RIPA buffer 48 h after si-Nudt21 transfection, and protein concentrations were determined using the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS–PAGE and transferred onto NC membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies: mouse anti-Nudt21 (1:2,000, Proteintech, 66335-1-Ig) and rabbit anti-β-actin (1:5,000, Proteintech, 20536-1-AP). After washing with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:10,000) for 1 h at room temperature. Signals were visualized using ECL chemiluminescence, and band intensities were quantified with ImageJ software.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from AML12 cells 48 h after si-Nudt21 transfection using RNA-easy Isolation Reagent (R701-01, Vazyme, China). cDNA was synthesized with the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR kit (R223-01, Vazyme, China). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using the Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit (Q712-02, Vazyme, China) on a LightCycler 480 system (Roche). mRNA expression levels were normalized to β-actin and quantified using the 2-ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences are listed in Table S1.

Sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

Sequencing data generation and preprocessing

Full-length transcriptome sequencing was performed using the Nanopore-based Tail ISO-seq platform. Raw data in pod5/fast5 format were subjected to base calling using Dorado (parameter: --no-trim) to generate FASTQ files containing sequence and quality information. Raw reads were filtered to obtain high-quality clean data by removing reads with an average quality score (Q) < 10. Full-length cDNA sequences were identified and trimmed using Pychopper (v2.4.0; parameters: -Q 7 -z 50), followed by additional filtering with NanoFilt (v2.8.0; parameters: -q 7 -l 50) [16].

Full-length read alignment and transcript assembly

High-quality full-length reads were aligned to the mouse reference genome (GRCm39) using Minimap2 (v2.17-r941; parameters: -ax splice -uf -k14) [17]. Based on the alignment results, consensus transcript sets were rapidly constructed using the Pinfish pipeline (v0.1.0; default parameters). Redundant transcripts were merged using StringTie (v2.1.4; parameter: --merge) to generate a non-redundant transcript set for subsequent analyses [18]. Using gffcompare (version: 0.12.1; parameters: -R -C -K -M), the non-redundant transcripts were compared with known transcripts in the genome. This pipeline outputs two types of transcripts: those that exactly match the reference annotation (labeled as “known”) and those not recorded in the existing annotation (labeled as “novel”). Through subsequent analysis (Supplementary Figure S1), these “novel” transcripts showed low expression levels and did not encode known proteins, contributing little to the core analyses of APA and differential expression. Therefore, we decided to remove all “novel” transcripts from subsequent analyses and retain only known genes.

Transcript quantification and differential expression analysis

Transcript expression levels were quantified using Salmon (v1.9.0) to generate a raw read count matrix [19]. Differential expression analysis between groups was performed using DESeq2 (v1.34.0) based on this matrix [20]. Differentially expressed genes/transcripts were identified using the following thresholds: adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05 and |log₂(fold change)| > 1.

Poly(A) tail length and poly(A) site analysis

Poly(A) tail length analysis: Poly(A) tail lengths for individual reads were directly estimated from sequencing signals using Dorado (parameter: --estimate-poly-a). Differences in poly(A) tail length between groups were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test, with significance thresholds set at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |Δmedian tail length| > 15 nt.

Poly(A) site identification and APA analysis: Given that Nanopore sequencing captures full-length reads, the alignment endpoints of reads correspond to poly(A) insertion sites. Poly(A) tags (PATs)—the 3′ terminal positions of aligned reads—were clustered within a 24 nt window using the Quantifypoly(A) tool and defined as poly(A) clusters (PACs). Gene 3′UTR lengths were calculated as the distance between the most distal and most proximal PACs. Differential APA analysis was performed using the Mann–Whitney U test, with thresholds of FDR < 0.05 and |Δmedian 3′UTR length| > 15 nt. Poly(A) site usage (PSU) was calculated as the proportion of reads mapped to a specific PAC relative to the total reads mapped to all PACs within the same gene.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) biological process and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed separately for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and for genes exhibiting significant 3′UTR shortening [21, 22]. Enrichment was assessed using hypergeometric tests implemented in the clusterProfiler R package (v4.2.2), with statistical significance set at FDR < 0.05 [23]. To evaluate directional changes in global transcriptional programs, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA): all genes were ranked in descending order by their log₂(fold change) values, and the enrichment of predefined GO and KEGG gene sets at the top (upregulated) or bottom (downregulated) of the ranked list was assessed. GSEA was also conducted using clusterProfiler (v4.2.2), with significance threshold set at FDR < 0.05.

Data analysis and statistics

In this study, statistical analyses for Western blotting and real-time quantitative PCR experiments were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 software. Other relevant statistical methods are as described above. All quantitative results are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM), with each group containing at least three biological replicates; the specific number of replicates (n) is indicated in the corresponding figure legends. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test. Statistical significance is indicated as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001; ns: not significant.

Results

Validation of Nudt21 knockdown and sequencing data quality control

We first validated the knockdown efficiency of Nudt21 in AML12 cells. Differential gene expression analysis showed that Nudt21 expression was significantly downregulated in the knockdown group, with a knockdown efficiency of 93.6%. Quantitative real-time PCR and western blotting further confirmed the knockdown efficiency (Fig. 1 A and B). Subsequently, Nanopore Tail ISO-seq was performed on six samples (three negative controls, Control1-3, and three Nudt21 knockdown samples, Knockdown1-3). Each sample yielded an average of approximately 5.5 million raw reads. After full-length identification and filtering, an average of approximately 4.8 million high-quality full-length reads were obtained per sample, with mapping rates ranging from 87.64% to 96.34%. Principal component analysis (PCA) showed clear separation between the control and knockdown groups along the PC1 axis, explaining 51.53% of the total variance. Notably, the three biological replicates of the control group exhibited a certain degree of dispersion, whereas the knockdown replicates showed markedly higher consistency. This may reflect intrinsic transcriptional fluctuations in hepatocytes under basal conditions, while Nudt21 knockdown, as a potent perturbation, drives cells toward a more unified, reprogrammed state (Fig.1 C). Inter-sample correlation analysis demonstrated that intra-group correlation coefficients were substantially higher than inter-group coefficients (Fig. 1 D), indicating good experimental reproducibility.

Nudt21 knockdown induces widespread transcriptomic reprogramming

To comprehensively assess the impact of Nudt21 knockdown on the transcriptome, we performed differential expression analysis. Using the thresholds of padj < 0.05 and |log₂FoldChange| > 1.0, a total of 1,386 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, of which 428 (approximately 30%) were upregulated and 958 (approximately 70%) were downregulated (Fig. 2 A; full list provided in Table S2). To further visualize the most significant expression differences between the knockdown and control groups, we selected the top 50 genes with the largest fold changes (ranked by |log₂FC|) and generated a heatmap with hierarchical clustering analysis (Fig. 2 B). The results showed clear separation of expression patterns between the two groups with good clustering, suggesting that Nudt21 may play differential regulatory roles under different physiological states.

To comprehensively dissect the functional orientation of transcriptomic reprogramming after Nudt21 knockdown, we separated the DEGs into upregulated and downregulated gene sets and performed GO functional enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on each set, respectively (Fig. 2C and D). The enrichment results for upregulated genes showed that the significantly enriched GO biological processes mainly involved tissue homeostasis and positive regulation of apoptotic process; the molecular functions were significantly enriched in glutathione transferase activity, peroxidase activity, etc.; KEGG pathway analysis indicated that upregulated genes were significantly enriched in cancer-related pathways such as pathways in cancer, viral carcinogenesis, small cell lung cancer, as well as the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and the Hippo signaling pathway. The enrichment results for downregulated genes showed that the significantly enriched GO biological processes mainly involved DNA repair, DNA metabolic process, and mitotic cell cycle process; KEGG pathways were mainly enriched in cell cycle and nucleotide excision repair pathways, consistent with the GO analysis observations of “mitotic cell cycle process”, “chromosome segregation”, and “DNA repair”. Notably, multiple neurodegeneration-related pathways were significantly enriched, including Parkinson disease, Alzheimer disease, Huntington disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and pathways of neurodegeneration – multiple diseases.

Genome-wide APA remodeling and 3′UTR shortening

To delineate the global impact of Nudt21 knockdown on alternative polyadenylation, we first performed cumulative distribution function (CDF) analysis. The CDF curve of the knockdown group exhibited a significant rightward shift relative to the control group (Fig. 3 A), indicating that a higher proportion of poly(A) sites were utilized at increased usage levels genome-wide upon Nudt21 depletion. This observation is consistent with a global switch toward proximal poly(A) site selection: proximal sites, which were originally low-usage, became the predominant output for many genes following knockdown, with their usage substantially elevated, manifesting as an increased fraction of high-usage sites at the global level (Fig. 3 B).

We next quantified 3′UTR length changes at single-gene resolution. Using a threshold of |Δmedian 3′UTR length| > 15 nt and FDR < 0.05, we identified a total of 2,864 genes with significant APA changes (Table S3). Among these, 2,682 genes (93.6%) exhibited 3′UTR shortening, whereas only 182 genes (6.4%) showed lengthening (Fig. 3 C). This highly skewed distribution strongly supports the conclusion that Nudt21 knockdown drives genome-wide 3′UTR shortening.

To identify high-confidence APA events directly regulated by Nudt21, we integrated analyses of differential poly(A) site activity (dePAC) and differential poly(A) site usage (deAPA). The results showed that the vast majority of genes exhibiting significant 3′UTR shortening were present in the core target set defined by the intersection of these two analyses, confirming that altered poly(A) site selection is the primary driver of 3′UTR remodeling (Fig. 3 D). Among the genes with significant 3′UTR shortening, E2f3 and B2m were representative examples with pronounced shortening. Further sequence analysis revealed that the 3′UTR regions of these genes are rich in canonical CFIm complex recognition motifs (i.e., U-rich elements such as UGUA) and core polyadenylation signals (e.g., AATAAA), providing a direct molecular explanation for the observed APA changes (Fig. 3 E and F). To experimentally validate the computationally predicted APA events, we performed qPCR-based APA assays to quantitatively analyze the 3′UTR length changes of E2f3 and B2m. The results showed that, compared with the control group, the proportion of long E2f3 transcripts was significantly reduced by approximately 90% in the Nudt21 knockdown group, and that of long B2m transcripts was reduced by approximately 40% (Fig. 3 G). These results are consistent with the 3′UTR shortening trend revealed by Nanopore sequencing, experimentally confirming the APA alterations induced by Nudt21 knockdown. To mechanistically elucidate how APA changes drive gene expression upregulation, we selected E2f3, a core regulator of the cell cycle that exhibited both significant 3′UTR shortening and upregulation, for in-depth analysis [24]. Based on the measured comparison of 3′UTR lengths between the experimental and control groups, combined with predictive analysis using the TargetScan database, we found that the 3′UTR of E2f3 was shortened from 2,274 nt to 265 nt, and the lost distal region was enriched for multiple potential miRNA binding sites [25, 26] (Fig. 3 H, Table S4).

This global 3′UTR shortening carries important potential biological consequences. To explore which biological processes are most susceptible to this APA dysregulation, we next performed systematic functional enrichment analysis on genes with significant 3′UTR shortening. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses collectively revealed a highly consistent regulatory network with clear biological orientation (Fig. 4 A).

GO analysis showed that biological processes (BP) were significantly enriched in intracellular protein transport, Golgi vesicle transport, mitotic cell cycle, and cell division; molecular functions (MF) were significantly enriched in ubiquitin-like protein ligase binding, mRNA binding, and ubiquitin-like protein transferase activity; cellular components (CC) were significantly enriched in ribonucleoprotein granules, spliceosomal complex, and spindle. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis (ranked by adjusted p-value) identified the top 20 pathways, mainly involving cell cycle, endocytosis, protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, ubiquitin mediated proteolysis, autophagy, mitophagy, fatty acid metabolism, ferroptosis, and various infection- and cancer-related pathways. These results indicate that genes with 3′UTR shortening upon Nudt21 knockdown are widely involved in cell cycle regulation, protein transport and degradation, vesicle trafficking, and disease-related signaling networks.

Association between 3′UTR shortening and gene expression

To explore the relationship between 3′UTR shortening and gene expression regulation, we performed an integrative analysis of the gene set with significant 3′UTR shortening and the differentially expressed gene set. The results showed that among the 401 genes exhibiting both 3′UTR shortening and differential expression, 213 were upregulated and 188 were downregulated (Fig. 4 B and C), indicating that 3′UTR shortening exerts bidirectional effects on gene expression, with similar numbers of upregulated and downregulated genes.

Notably, as many as 2,271 genes showed 3′UTR shortening without significant changes in expression levels, constituting the largest associated subgroup. This observation suggests that for these genes, key post-transcriptional regulatory elements may be located upstream of the proximal poly(A) site and thus retained after 3′UTR shortening, thereby maintaining mRNA abundance homeostasis [2]. For the upregulated genes, the changes are consistent with the classic “de-repression” mechanism: 3′UTR shortening leads to the loss of distal negative regulatory elements such as miRNA binding sites, enhancing mRNA stability and consequently increasing expression [26, 27]. This interpretation is consistent with the known function of the Nudt21/CFIm25 complex in promoting distal poly(A) site selection [28]. On the other hand, some genes showed downregulation despite 3′UTR shortening, which may result from the loss of positive regulatory elements [29, 30]. In addition, a large number of differentially expressed genes were not accompanied by significant APA changes, suggesting that Nudt21 knockdown may also trigger broader transcriptional cascades through indirect pathways (e.g., regulating transcription factor expression).

Poly(A) tail length remodeling

In this study, using the Nanopore-based Tail ISO-seq technology, we systematically evaluated the impact of Nudt21 knockdown on poly(A) tail length at single-molecule resolution for the first time, while precisely resolving changes in poly(A) site selection [31]. Using thresholds of FDR < 0.05 and an absolute difference in median tail length > 15 nt, a total of 160 transcripts with significant changes in poly(A) tail length were identified, of which 82 showed significantly increased tail length and 78 showed significantly shortened tail length. This observation indicates that Nudt21 depletion not only triggers a genome-wide switch toward proximal poly(A) site usage but also elicits specific remodeling at the level of poly(A) tail length, a critical layer of post-transcriptional modification [32].

We speculate that the tail length changes are largely derived from the coupled effects of poly(A) site switching (Fig. 4 D). As the core component of the CFIm complex, Nudt21 knockdown shifts cellular polyadenylation from distal to proximal site usage. Distinct cis-regulatory elements upstream of different poly(A) sites may differentially recruit poly(A) polymerase complexes or modulate their processivity, thereby establishing default tail lengths at the newly utilized sites that differ from those at the original sites [33]. Additionally, the extensive transcriptomic and metabolic reprogramming elicited by Nudt21 knockdown may indirectly influence tail length dynamics through altering global cellular states.

Enrichment analysis of liver disease-associated pathways

NUDT21 is a core subunit of the pre-mRNA processing complex CFIm and plays a key role in regulating alternative polyadenylation. Its functional loss in hepatocytes broadly affects the processing and stability of gene 3′UTRs, and may thereby be involved in liver homeostasis and disease progression. Although the data in this study are derived from a mouse hepatocyte cell line, the Nudt21-mediated RNA processing machinery and the signaling networks it regulates are highly conserved in mammals [7]; therefore, the above results provide a preliminary basis for future investigations into NUDT21 as a potential regulatory factor or therapeutic target in human disease contexts.

To evaluate pathway changes related to liver diseases following Nudt21 knockdown, we performed GSEA on KEGG pathways, selected three pathways closely associated with liver diseases, and extracted genes meeting the differential expression threshold within each pathway for presentation (Fig. 4 E-G). The GSEA results for the oxidative phosphorylation pathway showed an overall downward trend in the knockdown group; a total of 24 differentially expressed genes were detected, of which 23 were downregulated in the knockdown group, mainly involving multiple subunits of respiratory chain complexes I–V and the electron carrier Cycs, with only Cox15 being upregulated. The GSEA results for the hepatocellular carcinoma pathway also showed an overall downward trend; multiple differentially expressed genes were present within the pathway, some downregulated (e.g., Cdk4, Araf, Gstm1) and some upregulated (e.g., Cdk6, E2f3, Smad3). The GSEA results for the non-alcoholic fatty liver disease pathway showed an overall downward trend; its differentially expressed genes were predominantly downregulated, including multiple respiratory chain subunits, the endoplasmic reticulum stress-related gene Eif2s1, the apoptosis-related gene Bid, and the AMPK regulatory subunit Prkab1, while the upregulated genes were mainly the AMPK catalytic subunits Prkaa1 and Prkag2. The detailed expression patterns of the differentially expressed genes in the above three pathways are shown in the heatmap (Fig. 4H).

Discussion

In this study, using Nanopore long-read sequencing technology, we systematically delineated for the first time the transcriptome-wide and APA changes accompanying knockdown of the core APA regulator Nudt21 in a normal mouse hepatocyte cell line. We observed that Nudt21 depletion led to global 3′UTR shortening (ratio of shortened to lengthened genes approximately 16:1) and identified 1,396 differentially expressed genes. Functional enrichment analysis showed that these changes were enriched in pathways related to cell cycle, metabolism, inflammation, and liver diseases. These results provide a data foundation for further exploration of the role of Nudt21 in post-transcriptional regulation in hepatocytes.

The vast majority of APA changes observed upon Nudt21 knockdown in this study were 3′UTR shortening (93.6%), which is consistent with the classic mechanism by which CFIm25 promotes distal poly(A) site selection [7]. Sequence analysis of the 3′UTRs of representative genes (e.g., E2f3, B2m) revealed enrichment of the CFIm complex recognition motif UGUA, providing cis‑level sequence evidence for direct regulation by Nudt21 [2]. After Nudt21 knockdown, differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in multiple cancer-related pathways (e.g., PI3K-Akt, Hippo, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer), which aligns with previously reported roles of Nudt21 in various tumors including hepatocellular carcinoma, lung cancer, and bladder cancer [7, 11, 34]. Moreover, although this study used hepatocytes, several neurodegeneration-related pathways (e.g., Parkinson disease, Huntington disease) were also significantly enriched among the differentially expressed genes, an observation consistent with previous findings linking Nudt21/CFIm25 dysfunction to neurological disorders [35].

The present study has several limitations. First, regarding the cell model, the AML12 cells are derived from adult mice but are in an actively proliferative state, unlike quiescent primary hepatocytes. Therefore, the observed APA and transcriptomic changes may partly reflect regulatory characteristics associated with cell proliferation, and the conclusions apply primarily to this cell line model. Second, protein-level validation is lacking, so the impact of APA changes on protein expression remains unknown. Third, causal validation is absent; the observed pathway enrichments are merely associations and need to be verified by knockdown/overexpression or pharmacological interventions. Fourth, an in vivo model is missing; liver-specific Nudt21 knockout mice are a necessary step to assess physiological relevance. Fifth, the direct effects of APA changes on mRNA stability and translation efficiency require further validation by methods such as SLAM-seq, polysome profiling, etc. Future detection of NUDT21 expression and APA signatures in human NAFLD, liver fibrosis, and HCC samples will help explore its potential value as a disease biomarker or therapeutic target.

Conclusion

In summary, this study systematically unveils the multifaceted role of Nudt21 in maintaining transcriptome homeostasis in hepatocytes. Nudt21 serves as a primary determinant of global 3′UTR length, and its deficiency elicits extensive gene expression reprogramming through both APA-dependent and -independent pathways, collectively leading to metabolic dysregulation, cellular stress, and aberrant proliferation. These findings position Nudt21 at the center of the post-transcriptional regulatory network in hepatocytes, offering a conceptual framework and molecular basis for understanding the role of APA mechanisms in liver physiology and disease pathogenesis.

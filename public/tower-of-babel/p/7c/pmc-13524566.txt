HERDOO2 rule to identify women with unprovoked venous thromboembolism who can discontinue anticoagulation: Can I use non-VIDAS D-dimer with 2 cut points?
Authors: Harika Dasari, Grégoire Le Gal, Michael J Kovacs, Marc A Rodger
Published: 2026

Introduction

Venous thromboembolism (VTE), comprising deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, is a major cause of morbidity and mortality [1]. Although anticoagulation effectively reduces the risk of recurrent VTE [[2], [3], [4], [5], [6]], the optimal duration of therapy after the initial 3 to 6 months remains one of the most challenging decisions in thrombosis management [[7], [8], [9]]. While patients with VTE, provoked by major transient risk factors such as surgery, major trauma, or prolonged immobilization, have a relatively low recurrence risk after 3 months of anticoagulation [10], women with weakly provoked or unprovoked VTEs have substantially higher recurrence risk, making the decision to extend anticoagulation more complex [11,12].

The HERDOO2 (hyperpigmentation, edema or redness, D-dimer ≥250 μg/L, obesity [body mass index {BMI} ≥30], and older [age ≥65 years]) rule is a prospectively validated clinical decision tool that safely identifies women with a first unprovoked or weakly provoked VTE who can safely discontinue anticoagulation after completing 5 to 7 months of treatment [13,14]. A central component of HERDOO2 is a D-dimer concentration <250 μg/L measured using the VIDAS assay (Biomerieux). However, because VIDAS testing is performed in fewer than 5% of laboratories worldwide [15,16], clinicians frequently lack access to the assay required to apply HERDOO2 in routine practice.

In a previous work, we evaluated whether 4 widely available D-dimer assays (Innovance, Siemens Healthineers, Tina-quant, Roche Diagnostics, Liatest, Stago and HemosIL, Werfen) could substitute for VIDAS using a single optimized threshold corresponding to 250 μg/L. Even with assay-specific cut points, concordance with VIDAS was poor (κ = 0.30-0.38) and misclassification rates were clinically unacceptably high (14%-20%) [17]. Importantly, these findings highlighted that analytical differences between assays persist and that this approach cannot resolve the lack of assay standardization. Given these limitations, we investigated whether a 2–cut point strategy defining assay-specific low and high thresholds (ie, a low cut point predicts VIDAS D-dimer <250 μg/L, and a high cut point predicts VIDAS D-dimer ≥250 μg/L) could reduce clinically important misclassification and support safer application of HERDOO2 with alternative D-dimer assays.

Methods

We performed a secondary analysis of stored plasma samples from women enrolled in the HERDOO2 validation cohort study [14]. Plasma samples were retrieved from 248 randomly selected women selected from the 1213 women enrolled in the REVERSE II validation cohort with weakly provoked or unprovoked VTE who had consented to future use of stored samples [14]. No additional laboratory testing was performed for the current study, and the dual–cut point strategy was applied to existing 2018 assay data [17]. The study received institutional review board approval at all participating centers, and all participants provided written informed consent.

Plasma samples were collected during ongoing oral anticoagulation (5-12 months after VTE diagnosis), stored at −80 °C, and subsequently analyzed using 5 commercially available, automated, quantitative D-dimer assays: VIDAS, Innovance, TinaQuant, Liatest, and HemosIL (HS 500). All assays were performed per the manufacturer’s protocols at 3 accredited clinical and research laboratories in Ontario and Quebec, Canada, with laboratory personnel blinded to patient characteristics, other assay results, and clinical outcomes. D-dimer concentrations were reported in micrograms per liter using fibrinogen-equivalent units.

Definition of assay-specific dual cut points

For each non-VIDAS D-dimer (Innovance, TinaQuant, Liatest, and HemosIL), 2 assay-specific cut points were defined relative to the VIDAS reference standard (250 μg/L): 1) a low cut point predicting VIDAS D-dimer <250 μg/L and 2) a high cut point predicting VIDAS D-dimer ≥250 μg/L. Cut points corresponding to 90% and 95% sensitivity and specificity were derived using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis [17]. Values between the low and high cut points were classified as indeterminate.

Using these dual cut points, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of each alternative D-dimer assay in replicating VIDAS-based HERDOO2 risk classification, with VIDAS as the reference standard. For determinate results, sensitivity, specificity, negative predictive value, positive predictive value, and the proportion of indeterminate results were calculated.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize assay results. Agreement between alternative D-dimer assays and VIDAS results was assessed using Cohen’s κ coefficient with 95% CIs [18]. Agreement strength was interpreted using standard thresholds [18]. All analyses were performed using R version 4.2.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Plasma samples from 248 women with unprovoked or weakly provoked VTE were analyzed. Mean age and BMI were similar in the calibration cohort (n = 50; mean age, 50.1 years [SD, 16.8]; mean BMI, 31.7 kg/m2 [SD, 8.2]) and the validation cohort (n = 198; mean age, 49.8 years [SD 17.2]; mean BMI, 31.2 kg/m2 [SD 8.0]) . The proportion classified as low risk by the HERDOO2 rule was similar in the calibration (58%) and validation (56.6%) cohorts.

Using the calibration cohort, assay-specific dual cut points were derived for Innovance, TinaQuant, Liatest, and HemosIL relative to the VIDAS 250 μg/L threshold. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis showed the highest discrimination for Innovance (area under the curve, 0.87), followed by TinaQuant, Liatest, and HemosIL (Supplementary Figure). Discussion

In this secondary analysis of the HERDOO2 validation cohort, we assessed whether 4 non-VIDAS D-dimer assays could approximate VIDAS-based HERDOO2 risk classification using a pragmatic 2–cut point strategy. Using HERDOO2 misclassification proportion as a primary metric, Innovance and TinaQuant showed the closest alignment with VIDAS, whereas Liatest and HemosIL were limited by higher indeterminate rates and less consistent agreement despite similar overall misclassification at the 90% threshold.

The 2–cut point approach introduces an indeterminate zone intended to minimize clinically meaningful misclassification. Its feasibility, therefore, depends on local access to confirmatory VIDAS testing if non-VIDAS D-dimer results are indeterminate. Importantly, only women with an HERDOO2 score of 1 require a D-dimer result for final classification to categorize them as low-risk (0 or 1) or high-risk (2 or more), reducing the number of patients affected by indeterminate D-dimer results in practice.

Clinically, the direction of misclassification is also very relevant. Misclassifying a low-risk woman as high-risk may lead to unnecessary continuation of anticoagulation, exposing patients to bleeding risk and treatment burden without benefit [7,[19], [20], [21]]. Conversely, misclassifying a high-risk woman as low-risk may result in discontinuation of anticoagulation, with an increased risk of recurrent VTE. However, recent decision-analytic modeling has shown that reductions in recurrent VTE with extended anticoagulation do not necessarily translate into meaningful gains in quality-adjusted life years over a life-time horizon [20]. Also, in our data, most discordant classifications occurred among VIDAS D-dimer values close to the 250 μg/L threshold.

Strengths of this study include centralized, blinded testing in accredited laboratories; evaluation of widely used commercial assays; and a clinically grounded 2–cut point framework aligned with HERDOO2 decision making. Limitations include modest sample size and inability to assess preanalytical variability or broader assay standardization.

In summary, these findings support cautious extension of HERDOO2 to selected non-VIDAS D-dimer assays where VIDAS testing is unavailable. Innovance and TinaQuant demonstrated the most favorable balance of misclassification and feasibility.

Conclusion

Innovance and TinaQuant emerged as the most promising alternatives to VIDAS for HERDOO2-based risk stratification in women with unprovoked or weakly provoked VTE. Using a 2–cut point strategy, both assays demonstrated low overall misclassification rates and strong concordance with VIDAS-based classification. In contrast, Liatest and HemosIL were constrained by high indeterminate rates and lower concordance, limiting their utility within this framework. Prospective validation of assay-specific thresholds is warranted before broader implementation of HERDOO2-guided decision making in non-VIDAS settings.

One-stage posterior surgical treatment of the rare thoracolumbar spine process and laminar nucleus with incomplete paralysis: a retrospective study
Authors: Huijun Zhang, Zenghui Lu, Xiaotong Yue, Jinyu Yan, Xiaobin Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe study was to evaluate the results of the one-stage posterior approach in treating patients with the rare thoracic and lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae tuberculosis of the spine with incomplete paralysis.Materials and methods21 patients who were treated with bone graft fusion, debridement, spinal canal decompression, and posterior transpedicle internal fixation vie one-stage posterior approach were collected and analyzed. The data was collected at perioperative period and at the final follow-up visit.ResultsThe follow up time was at an average of 21.62 ± 2.17 months. The mean age of these patients was 44.81 ± 17.76 years. The intraoperative blood loss and operative time were 538.09 ± 180.21 mL and170.95 ± 20.08 min, respectively. The C-reactive protein (CRP) level and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) decreased to normal by the last follow-up evaluation. The Oswestry Disability Index (ODI)and visual analogue scale (VAS) were substantially improved 4 weeks postoperatively and at the last follow-up evaluation (P < 0.05). The incomplete paralysis had improved significantly at the last follow-up evaluation based on the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) grade (P < 0.05). All patients achieved the bony fusion criteria. The average fusion time was 11.200 ± 2.16 months.ConclusionsThe one-stage posterior approach is an efficient and safe surgical option for treating thoracic and lumbar spinous processes and vertebral laminae tuberculosis of the spine with incomplete paralysis. Surgical decompression is very necessary for the recovery of neurological function.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03200-4.Keywords: Vertebral laminae, Spinous process, Tuberculosis, Posterior approach

Introduction

Spinal tuberculosis (TB) is a chronic, specific infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which can lead to destruction of the bone marrow, spinal deformities, paraplegia, and/or incomplete paralysis [1, 2]. Basic nutritional support, systemic anti-tuberculosis therapy, and multidrug chemotherapy is essential to cure spinal TB [2, 3]. However, the thoracic and lumbar spinous processes and vertebral laminate TB of the spine with incomplete paraplegia are the most severe complications of spinal TB, which should be treated surgically [2–6].

Surgery for spinal TB helps to correct deformities, decompress the spinal cord, and restore spine stability. The anterior segment is the most common site for spinal tuberculosis infection [6–8]. The anterior approach has been shown to be effective in treating spinal tuberculosis [6–8]. However, the posterior approach has recently become widely accepted due to the decreased surgical trauma [4, 5]. Vertebral lamina and spinous process tuberculosis mainly manifest as destruction of the posterior segment of the spine. Vertebral lamina and spinous process TB of the spine can lead to back pain and paraplegia or incomplete paralysis and a worse quality of life. These findings are clear indications for surgery. Full neural decompression, enough deformity correction and reconstruction of ample spinal stability can be achieved through a posterior surgical approach at the same time [4, 5, 9–11]. The posterior surgical approach is superior because there is less trauma, fewer complications, and reliable fixation [9–12]. The posterior unilateral vertebral lamina fenestration debridement and bone grafting fusion combined with laminar reconstruction and internal fixation procedure has been reported to be superior to the conventional posterior-only approach [11]. However, studies involving this procedure are limited.

Therefore, this study investigated the clinical outcomes of the one-stage posterior approach among the patients with vertebral lamina and spinous process TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective study was proceeded in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and was approved by the Ethics Committee for Medical Scientific research of the Xi’an Chest Hospital (No. 2020-S0017). Written informed consent was obtained from each patient for this study.

Data from 21 Orthopedics Department patients (age range, 23–73 y) with thoracic and Lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis were retrospectively reviewed from January 2016 to December 2021. The diagnosis of spinal TB was confirmed by the following indicators: positive bacterial cultures, pathologic findings, clinical symptoms (weight loss, night sweats, back pain, and neurologic dysfunction), laboratory tests (C-reactive protein [CRP] and elevation of the erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR]), radiologic results (computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging and X-ray films), and imaging studies which showed spinous process and vertebral laminae destruction, para-vertebral abscesses, and spinal canal compression. Throughout the entire treatment cycle, preoperative bed rest, nutritional support, and effective anti-tuberculosis treatment were provided. Due to the clinical manifestations of incomplete paralysis in all cases, active surgical interventions were carried out in accordance with the standard procedures. After the operation, braces were worn for rehabilitation exercises, and anti-tuberculosis treatment continued until the end of the course.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: adults with thoracic and lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis; underwent the one-stage posterior approach; internal instrumentation and reconstruction; Radiologically confirmed thoracic/lumbar vertebral tuberculosis involving lamina/spinous process; Neurological impaire (ASIA grade B, C, or D); complete preoperative/postoperative follow-up records (≥ 18months). And the same spine surgery team performed the operations. Indications for surgery were as follows: neurologic deficits; incomplete paralysis; persistent pain; and bone destruction affecting spine stability.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: spine fractures; active pulmonary TB; and spine tumor(s); active systemic tuberculosis infection or simple vertebral tuberculosis; previous spinal surgery history; lost to follow-up; not thoracic/lumbar vertebral tuberculosis involving lamina/spinous process.

Preoperative management

Anti-TB chemotherapy was started immediately when the clinical diagnosis of thoracic and Lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis was made. No contraindications to surgery were noted during the preoperative examinations. At least 2weeks of first-line anti-tuberculous treatments (R rifampicin, 0.45 g; I isoniazid, 0.4 g; Z pyrazinamide, 1.5 g; and E ethambutol, 0.75 g) were given to the patients before surgery. Routine supportive therapy and symptomatic treatment followed the hospitalization [13]. Doses of anti-TB drugs were increased in patients weighing > 50 kg, or with TB in other parts of the body. Surgical indication: the body temperature came to normal or when the ESR decreased to < 60 mm/h and the CRP decreased gradually. Once the neurologic dysfunction worsened, the duration of drug therapy was shortened and surgery was proceeded as soon as possible.

The surgical techniques (the one-stage posterior approach of debridement, spinal canal decompression, bone graft fusion and posterior transpedicle internal fixation) and postoperative management were routinely done, and the details can be referred in the previous researches [4, 7].

Measures of effectiveness and safety

The data collected included at baseline and follow-up evaluations: blood loss, operative time, clinical manifestations, and imaging results, ESR, CRP, bone fusion time, follow-up time, the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), the visual analogue scale (VAS) score, and the neurologic status based on the American Spinal Cord Injury Association (ASIA) classification.

The assessment criterion is to measure the values of ESR and CRP during follow-up to evaluate the activity of tuberculosis lesions. The values of ESR and CRP were measured to evaluate the activity of tuberculosis lesions. A decrease in the values indicates the effectiveness of anti-tuberculosis treatment, while an increase indicates poor anti-tuberculosis effect.

The ASIA score was recorded respectively before the operation, after the operation and during the follow-up to evaluate the recovery of neurological function, and the ODI score and VAS score were recorded respectively to evaluate the improvement of postoperative pain. The positions of bone grafts and internal fixation were examined by conventional X-ray photography or CT, and the bone graft fusion status during the follow-up period was evaluated according to the revised radiological criteria established by Lee et al. [14]. The last follow-up generally refers to the data and information recorded during the reexamination 18 months after the operation.

Surgical procedure

Patients underwent one-stage posterior debridement, bone grafting fusion, and fixation. Generally, one or two adjacent normal vertebrae adjacent to the pathologic segment were fixed. Pedicle screws were implanted into the upper and lower vertebra adjacent to the pathologic segment in this study. Whether the screws were fixed to the pathologic vertebrae was based on the extent of vertebral destruction. The removal of the lesion is usually carried out after a temporary internal fixator is installed on one side. The removal of lesions, including TB granulation tissue, caseous necrotic material, abscesses, and dead bones, was performed using curettes of different sizes and angles until scraping of the bone surface produced bleeding. Rinse the surgical area repeatedly with normal saline, all potential residual lesions were debrided as radically as possible. The space between the decorticated transverse processes or articular process was carpeted with allogeneic particulate bone to promote bone fusion. Negative pressure drainage is routinely established before suturing the incision. The debridement specimens of each patient were subjected to mycobacterium culture and histopathological examination.

Postoperative procedure

The negative pressure drainage tube was retained after the operation until the drainage flow rate was less than 40 ml/d. Routine prevention and anti-infection should be carried out for 24 to 48 h, while enhancing nutritional support. The HRZE standard anti-tuberculosis regimen was adopted, and the treatment course was 6 months. Subsequently, pyrazinamide was discontinued and HRE chemotherapy lasted for 12 to 18 months. Liver and kidney functions should be evaluated regularly due to the potential adverse reactions of anti-tuberculosis drugs. Three weeks after the surgery, with the help of a spinal orthosis, one can gradually walk. The orthosis is usually removed after three months.

Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed using SPSS software (version 17.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). T-tests were used to compare the changes in the indices within each group at perioperative period and at the follow-up evaluation. The results are expressed as the mean ± SD and Student’s t-test was performed. A paired t-test was applied to compare the changes in indices in preoperatively, postoperatively, and during follow-up. Any discrepancy in the normal distribution was analyzed using the rank sum test. Normality was assessed using Shapiro-Wilk tests. All continuous variables (e.g., operative time, ESR levels) showed non-normal distributions (all p < 0.05), justifying the use of nonparametric tests. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used to validate and compare the changes in indices in preoperatively, postoperatively, and during follow-up. The multiple comparisons statistical test was proceeded to validate the results. Differences with a P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical results

Twenty-one patients with the thoracic and Lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis were included in our study. Patients had extradural tuberculous necrotic granulation tissue, caseous material, and tuberculous abscesses, and the spinal cord was either displaced or compressed in the 21 patients. All patients developed spinal Pott’s paraplegia. Bone destruction and marrow changes were observed in all patients. Most commonly, involvement was noted in the thoracic vertebrae, followed by the lumbar vertebrae.

The 21 Patients were followed for 18–24 months (average, 21.62 ± 2.67 months). The waist and back pain were alleviated and resolved 1–4 weeks postoperatively in all patients. No severe operative-related complications, including wound infections, were observed (Table 1). All patients achieved bony fusion with an average time of 11.20 ± 2.16 months.

ANOVA results and multiple comparisons statistical test

ANOVA results showed that, significant changes were observed in ESR (F = 17.571, P < 0.001), VAS (F = 156.654, P < 0.001), CRP (F = 36.992, P < 0.001), and ODI (F = 311.002, P < 0.001) postoperatively than that before surgery (Supplemental file 1). Multiple comparisons statistical test also validated the results (Supplemental file 1).

Discussion

This long-term follow-up study showed that the one-stage posterior approach of debridement, spinal canal decompression, bone graft fusion, and posterior transpedicle internal fixation was effective in curing patients with vertebral lamina and spinous process TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis. The surgeries were successful in all patients. All patients achieved the bony fusion criteria at 11.200 ± 2.16 months postoperatively. The last follow-up results showed that the CRP level and ESR had decreased to normal, the VAS and ODI scores had substantially improved, and the neurologic function of incomplete paralysis had also improved significantly. ANOVA results have validated these results.

In general, the front and middle columns of the spine are affected by M. tuberculosis [15]. Thoracic and lumbar spinous processes and vertebral laminae TB of the spine are quite rare in clinical practice. Spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine can cause intractable pain, sinus tract formation, paraplegia, or incomplete paralysis [6–9]. Imaging techniques such as CT, MRI play important roles on a diagnosis at an early stage [16]. Prompt medical and surgical treatment may prevent potentially catastrophic events in this situation [4–11]. The patients included in this study had variable degrees of incomplete paralysis, which is a severe complication of spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine. Few reports existed about the surgical procedures performed on these patients. A one-stage posterior approach consisting of debridement, spinal canal decompression, bone graft fusion, and posterior transpedicular internal fixation was performed in the current study and effective surgical results were achieved. As Liu et al. [11] reported, this procedure is minimally invasive with little trauma and quick recovery. At 4-week post-surgery, The VAS and ODI scores in the current study had significantly improved 4 weeks postoperatively and neurologic function of incomplete paralysis had also improved significantly. All patients met the bony fusion criteria at 11.200 ± 2.16 months, which was less than the bony fusion time (12.90 ± 3.91 months) in the study conducted by Zhong et al. [9].

All 21 patients with the thoracic and lumbar spinous process and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis collected in our study developed spinal Pott’s paraplegia with involvement was noted in the thoracic vertebrae, followed by lumbar vertebrae. These situations were similar with previous studies [16, 17]. Therefore, all 21 patients underwent posterior lesion removal, spinal decompression, and bone graft fusion internal fixation, which has achieved good intervention and positive follow-up results. Bone grafting supplemented by autologous or allograft bone graft achieved satisfying outcomes after one-stage posterior approach in our study, which is consistent with the report by Tang et al. [18]. At the last follow-up evaluation, all patients had recovered and achieved bone fusion, relief from pain, and neurologic recovery.

A previous study reported the effectiveness of the combination of 1-stage anterior focus debridement, interbody bone graft and anterior instrumentation and fusion for short segmental thoracic tuberculosis with paraplegia or incomplete paralysis [7]. The current study was different from the study by Lu et al. [7] in the following points. First, both series of patients have incomplete paralysis with similar mechanism of occurrence, but with different locations of occurrence [7]. Second, the study contents are different. The current paper studies the formation of tuberculosis lesions after spinal spinous process tuberculosis and lamina nucleus infection. The lesions compress the spinal canal from the rear, causing incomplete paralysis [7]. It is the obvious infection of the spinous process and lamina by tuberculosis bacteria that leads to bone destruction and the formation of lesions. But the surgical approach of the above published paper [7] belongs to the anterior transthoracic approach for thoracic tuberculosis. It mainly destroys the vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs of the spine, forming lesions. The lesions compress the dural sac from the front, resulting in incomplete paralysis, while the posterior lamina and spinous processes remain normal, have not been infected by tuberculosis bacteria, and have not been damaged. Third, the surgical approaches are different. The destruction of the spinous process and lamina nucleus was achieved through simple posterior lesion removal, bone graft fusion and internal fixation in the current study. But the published paper adopted a one-stage anterior approach via the thoracic cavity [7].

Moreover, the patients with incomplete paraplegia were treated later because spinal tuberculosis is a chronic specific spinal infection. Surgical treatment can be considered after more than two weeks of anti-tuberculosis treatment according to the Chinese guidelines for surgical management of spinal tuberculosis(2022 edition) [19]. To effectively control tuberculosis infection and alleviate symptoms of tuberculosis poisoning (such as fever and night sweats), and to reduce postoperative complications (such as skin sinus tract formation, tuberculosis recurrence, etc.), adequate preoperative preparation must be done [19]. Therefore, these patients with incomplete paraplegia in the current study were surgically treated after controlling and managing TB for some days, instead of immediate surgery.

However, this study was a retrospective study with small size. Selection bias could not be completely excluded without a randomized prospective cohort study. In addition, though the same intervention and anti-tuberculosis treatment were proceeded by the same doctor team, many confounding factors that may affect prognosis were not discussed and analyzed. In the future, prospective, randomized cohort studies among large samples with long-term follow-up and multi-factor analysis are needed. Our study did not systematically assess the impact of bone fusion due to heterogeneous follow-up imaging protocols. Future prospective studies should standardize fusion evaluation to clarify its role in neurological recovery.

Conclusions

The study results showed that the simple posterior approach for debridement, bone grafting fusion, and instrumentation can be considered for rare thoracic and lumbar spinous processes and vertebral laminae TB of the spine with incomplete paralysis. The posterior approach can reliably, safety, and effectively clear the focus, spinal canal decompression, improve incomplete paralysis, and rebuild spine stability. So surgical decompression is very necessary for the recovery of neurological function.

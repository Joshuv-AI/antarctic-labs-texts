Pillars of Peer Review
Authors: Matthias Nahrendorf
Published: 2026

Time flies—my first 6 months at the helm of JACC: Basic to Translational Science are already behind me. Last summer, after toiling as an author for a quarter of a century, I answered a call to serve and began changing sides—at least for part of the day—when logging onto the journal’s website as its Editor-in-Chief. My motivation for accepting this role was to channel some of the pain I experienced during peer review as an author into meaningful improvements for our scientific community.

My journey has only just begun, and I am grateful that I am not traveling it alone. One of the greatest rewards associated with this new role has been working with our outstanding editorial board—esteemed colleagues who agreed to join me and whom I now have the pleasure of meeting with every week. Thank you for your dedication and service, Andrew Lewis, Aneesh Bapat, Cameron McAlpine, Daniela Carnevale, Gustavo Ramos, Jason Roh, Kory Lavine, Maarten Hulsmanns, Nick Frangogiannis, and Sabine Steffens. I am also especially grateful to Kimberly Trevey (Managing Editor) and Monica Payne-Emmerson (Executive Managing Editor), who have ensured that the new board quickly found its footing and who keep us on track.

Together, we strive hard to make JACC: Basic to Translational Science an appealing venue for scientific discourse. We have broadened the journal’s scope to welcome all basic science papers addressing cardiovascular and neurovascular disease, as well as related work in hematology and immunology. We are committed to improving our interactions with authors and to providing faster, more collegial decisions. One particularly successful initiative has been the fast-track pathway for papers previously rejected elsewhere. We typically accept such submissions very quickly and without another round of external review. Please reach out if you have questions about this submission track. I am also eager to hear your general feedback and your ideas how we can do better—after all, I see my role as an Editor-in-Chief as service to you: our readers, authors, and reviewers.

The journal would be nothing without its diligent reviewers, who tirelessly help select and improve the papers we publish. At a time when some aspects of science have become big business, it is all the more remarkable that busy academics continue to volunteer their time and expertise.

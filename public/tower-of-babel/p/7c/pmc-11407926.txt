Tracking academic contributions to Women's empowerment in Malaysia: A bibliometric investigation
Authors: Sharmila Devi, Rajermani Thinakaran, Shamsiah Banu Mohamad Hanefar, Nazruzila Razniza Mohd Nadzri

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study implied bibliometric methods to investigate the research conducted on women’s empowerment in Malaysia from 1995 to 2023. Its main objective is to explore key trends and evidence in the field of women’s empowerment in Malaysia. This incorporates publication trends, identifying the most influential authors, evaluating the influential institutions, determining highly cited documents, and uncovering the most common keywords implied in the study in the field of women empowerment. Eventually, this study anticipated to provide a comprehensive idea of research encompassing women’s empowerment in Malaysia within a specified period. This knowledge will be useful to foster policies and initiatives pertaining to gender equality advancement and women empowerment in Malaysia.Keywords: Women empowerment, Gender equality, Bibliometric study, Economic growth, Gender mainstreaming, Reduced inequalities

Introduction

The concept of women's empowerment is complex, involving several aspects of autonomy and decision-making agency. Women's empowerment, according to Parveen and Choudhury [1], is the increased control that women have over their lives, especially in relation to financial independence, knowledge acquisition, political engagement, and economic resources. Prasad [2], who highlights the critical role of women in decision-making processes and life choices, supports this point of view. To expand on this, Oxaal and Baden [3] emphasize the cooperative aspect of empowerment, arguing that to advance gender equality and remove patriarchal barriers from systems of leadership and decision-making, women and men must collaborate. Power redistribution was the key focus of the empowerment discourse in the 1990s [4], but the term itself remained vague at times [5]. Kabeer [6,7] offers a thorough framework by emphasizing "the power within" people to manage resources and make choices. This multifaceted concept of women's empowerment is presented. This viewpoint is consistent with that of other scholars, such as Rowlands [8] and Sharifah Syahirah [9], who place an emphasis on women's self-generated empowerment, giving them control over their finances, access to information, and ability to participate in politics [1]. In addition, Prasad [2] emphasizes the cooperative elements of empowerment, contending that participation in groups can raise consciousness and spur action. Despite the diverse perspectives, a common thread among these scholars is the recognition of women's empowerment as a process that involves gaining control and autonomy in various spheres of life, whether through individual self-realization or collective action (see Fig. 1).

Significance of Women’s empowerment in Malaysia context

Women's empowerment has emerged as a crucial aspect of societal development within the context of Malaysia. Malaysia has made substantial progress in advancing women's empowerment on a national level, bolstered by legislative frameworks supporting equal rights and opportunities, such as the 1993 ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women [10]. The nation's commitment to gender equality is further evidenced by policy initiatives like the National Policy on Women and the establishment of the Ministry of Women, Family, and Community Development. As a result, women in Malaysia now boast higher literacy rates than men, and their presence in the workforce is steadily increasing due to these concerted efforts [10].

Despite these advancements, challenges persist, particularly in achieving comprehensive gender equality across various sectors. Women encounter barriers, including limited opportunities for high-quality employment and wage disparities compared to their male counterparts, even with higher education levels. The burden of unpaid domestic work disproportionately falls on women, affecting their overall well-being and hindering professional advancement. Implicit biases and cultural norms frequently erect invisible barriers that impede women from reaching their full potential. Additionally, new challenges have emerged with the growth of freelance employment and the evolving nature of work-life balance, posing difficulties for women balancing career goals with household duties.

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) make it critical to realize these challenges and the strategies employed by women to overcome them. Achieving SDG 5, which specifically demands gender equality and the empowerment of all women and girls, will involve achieving other SDGs, specifically those appropriate to economic growth, decent work, and poverty reduction. The purpose of this study is to conduct a bibliometric analysis of women's empowerment in Malaysia, directing to induce awareness about how improving gender equality can help the nation move toward a more sustainable and inclusive future. In addition to being a tool for scholarly investigation, bibliometric analyses can be used to promote effective practices, identify influential research, and direct future research. Consequently, this strategy is crucial for fully realizing and advancing women's empowerment in Malaysia.

In this context, bibliometric analysis plays a crucial role in fostering women's empowerment and offering insights. It makes it easier to identify research trends, influential authors, and persuasive institutions that support the field of women's empowerment, representing its development and emphasizing areas requiring further research. Bibliometric studies can measure the impact of numerous research contributions by looking at citation patterns and highly cited documents. This enables the study to identify important studies and cutting-edge work that have influenced the discussion on women's empowerment. Additionally, policymakers and practitioners can develop policies and initiatives that support women's empowerment by using the insights gathered from bibliometric analyses to learn about the current state of research, gaps in the literature, and emerging areas of interest. Moreover, the insights gained from a bibliometric analysis inform policymakers and practitioners about the current state of research, gaps in the literature, and emerging areas of interest, crucial for developing evidence-based policies and initiatives aimed at promoting women’s empowerment. Furthermore, identifying the main researchers and institutions can promote networking and collaboration among researchers, improving the quality and significance of future studies on women's empowerment.

Methods

Data collection

The Scopus database was utilized in the bibliometric analysis to investigate works and studies pertaining to "Women Empowerment in Malaysia" between 1995 and 2023. Using the search term "Women AND empowerment AND in AND Malaysia," articles were selected from 97 journal publications that were pertinent to the study's topic. To provide a comprehensive picture of the development of scholarly contributions and research trends related to women's empowerment in Malaysia over almost three decades, the period from 1995 to 2023 was selected. The study's aim of identifying long-term trends and shifts in the field is in line with this time frame.

Data cleaning

Rigorous data-cleaning protocols were utilized to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the study. In the beginning, author names were standardized, bibliographic data was cleaned, and keywords were refined using programs like OpenRefine and biblioMagika®. To preserve data integrity, OpenRefine was employed for manual verification and standardization of keywords. This process ascertains the consistency in the selection of scholarly studies aligned with women’s empowerment in Malaysia. To confirm comprehensive bibliometric analysis, biblioMagika® was used to track several metrics, containing h-index, total publications, and citations per paper. This tool guaranteed precise bibliographic data and resolved multivalued cells, assisting thorough bibliometric analyses.

Bibliometric framework and tools

The bibliometric framework proposed by Martí-Parreño Méndez-Ibáñez, and Alonso-Arroyo [11] was utilized to examine studies on women's empowerment conducted in Malaysia between 1995 and 2023. Identifying and visualizing research themes and trends over time periods, involved extracting keywords from the dataset and creating thematic maps.

Temporal segmentation was employed to clarify changes in the areas of research focus over time. The influence and productivity of individual studies and authors were assessed using metrics such as citations per publication and h-index, which provide a numerical representation of their contributions to the academic community.

Several tools were used in the bibliometric analysis to analyze the data. The R-based package Bibliometrix facilitated to perform quantitative analysis on the.csv file from Scopus, aiding in assessing co-authorship patterns, citation metrics, and publication trends. A user-friendly program for interactive data exploration was accessible through Biblioshiny, a web interface that supplemented Bibliometrix and offered more extensive analysis of publication trends and citation impact. Ultimately, using VOSviewer, graphical visualizations of co-authorship networks and keyword clusters were produced. These visualizations demonstrate the relationships and organization of authors and research themes in the literature on women's empowerment in Malaysia.

Consequently, Bibliometric instruments such as Biblioshiny and Bibliometrix are supported to perform a thorough quantitative analysis of the dataset. These tools catered to a thorough evaluation of the research output and collaborations by making it easier to examine co-authorship patterns, citation metrics, and publication trends.

To create a graphical visualization of co-authorship networks and keyword clusters in the literature, VOSviewer was utilized. The structure and associations between authors and research themes were discovered by these visualizations, which enhanced the examination of the topic of women's empowerment in Malaysia.

In this study, other researchers will be able to replicate the systematic processes employed in data cleaning and analysis. Scopus emerged as one of the prevalent and most widespread databases of peer-reviewed literature, comprising a wide range of disciplines and publications from respectable publishers worldwide. Critical reflection on findings and future direction

Research gap

The bibliometric analysis indicated several significant gaps in the literature on women's empowerment in Malaysia.

First, 83.72 % of the publications are single-authored, indicating an underrepresentation of collaborative research. This array necessitated the need for more cross-institutional and interdisciplinary collaborations that could yield deeper and more thorough understandings of women's empowerment.

Second, it's apparent that intersectional factors haven't been thoroughly explored. For instance, terms like "Developing Country" and "Ethnicity" rarely appear. This implies that essential components such as socioeconomic status, ethnicity, and the differences between rural and urban areas are not being fully investigated, thus highlighting the need for more inclusive and nuanced research in the future.

Ultimately, the focus of the occurring research is mainly on policy and academic settings, paying little attention to community-driven initiatives and impacts at the grassroots level. To tackle this, it would be beneficial for future research to incorporate empirical studies into women's lived experiences. These studies would offer valuable perspectives and promote a more comprehensive comprehension of women's empowerment in diverse settings.

Future research

Research on women's empowerment in Malaysia should concentrate more on collaborative and interdisciplinary studies in the future. This will generate diverse viewpoints from health, political science, economics, and sociology, and will make the study more thorough and reliable. A comprehensive evaluation of intersectionality is essential, as it would explore the ways in which gender intersects with other social constructs such as age, socioeconomic status, ethnicity, and geography. This would enable a more nuanced understanding of the experiences of empowerment that differ among various groups of women.

In addition, longitudinal research would provide insightful information about how policies and initiatives affect a population over time, making it easier to identify recurring problems and new trends.

Finally, a community-centric research approach that involves direct interaction with local women and leaders would provide useful insights into the efficacy of empowerment programs and highlight both areas that require improvement and successful strategies. This diverse strategy would help create a more comprehensive and meaningful understanding of women's empowerment in Malaysia.

Implications for policy and practice

Practical and policy implications highlight important areas that require additional attention. Because women's empowerment is multifaceted, policymakers need to create evidence-based policies that ensure inclusivity across a range of societal segments. Policymakers must create practical policies that guarantee inclusivity for a broad spectrum of societal groups and acknowledge the complex dimensions of women's empowerment. Encouraging collaboration between academic institutions, governmental agencies, and non-governmental groups can enhance the principles of empowerment programs by pooling resources and utilizing special mechanisms. Adopting an intersectional perspective during the policy-making process is essential to effectively tackle the various obstacles faced by women from a range of socioeconomic backgrounds, ethnic backgrounds, and geographic locations.

In a nutshell, noteworthy developments and patterns in the field of women's empowerment research in Malaysia were identified by the bibliometric analysis. It is critical to address the identified research gaps and focus on future directions to make significant progress in the field and inform practices and policies that support women's empowerment and gender equality.

Limitation of study

The study confronted two main limitations. Firstly, the study relied on data retrieved from the SCOPUS database, which may potentially neglect another source of study about women's empowerment study. Furthermore, some databases may have provided limited bibliometric data, affecting the comprehensiveness of the analysis.

Secondly, the analysis of the study and networking patterns such as keywords, citations, and co-occurrences were exclusively based on bibliometric data from SCOPUS. Consequently, the systematic literature review is crucial for a more comprehensive insight into topic evolution in Women empowerment study.

Since the focus of the study was women's empowerment in Malaysia, the results may not generalize to other countries. The inability to compare and extrapolate findings across various cultural, social, and economic contexts is hampered by this geographic focus. When applying research results outside of the Malaysian context, care must be taken to ensure that regional variations in women's empowerment issues and their solutions are considered.

Conclusion

In a nutshell, this study has provided a comprehensive understanding of the trends, patterns, and dynamics of women's empowerment research in Malaysia. Recognizing the research trend in this field is crucial for developing gender equality agendas and fostering women's empowerment effectively. By evaluating research trends over time, the study is equipped to perceive areas of progress, current issues, and gaps in knowledge, thus acquiring policy decisions, leading research agendas, and nurturing collaboration among stakeholders. The study’s widespread bibliometric analysis, which comprises 97 articles retrieved from the Scopus database, has illustrated countless aspects of women's empowerment research in Malaysia such as key contributors, influential institutions, collaboration patterns, highly cited documents, and prominent themes and trends within the literature.

The results from the study provide meaningful development and increasing impacts of women's empowerment research in Malaysia over time. As observed, a continuous growth in the number of publications, citations, and impact metrics, represents an increasing interest and recognition of the research. This highlights the significance of continuous research involvement and collaboration to further gender equality programs effectively. The study in addition revealed the essential role of certain institutions, such as Universiti Sains Malaysia, Universiti Malaya, Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia, and Universiti Putra Malaysia, towards influencing research and nurturing discourse on women's empowerment in Malaysia. These institutions have made extensive contributions to forward knowledge, advising policy initiatives, and promoting gender equity in Malaysian society.

Moreover, the analysis of collaboration patterns denoted a prevalent trend of single-authored works, signifying the dominance of individual contributions in the study of women’s empowerment. However, the study also detected a minimum level of collaborative endeavours, mainly in dual-author collaborations, considering interdisciplinary or cross-institutional collaboration in the research community. Likewise, highly cited documents and keyword occurrences in the study gave an understanding of the key themes and topics directed in the literature on women's empowerment in Malaysia. Prominent themes also have been traced such as gender equality, political participation, economic empowerment, and social indicators, reflecting the multidimensional nature of women's empowerment and its importance in societal development and progress. Notwithstanding this significant knowledge, the study asserts limitations with its dependence on a single database which is Scopus for data recovery and the incapability to assess the attributes of studies beyond bibliometric displays. Future studies could seize these restrictions by using several databases and applying systematic literature reviews to provide a more inclusive understanding of women's empowerment research in Malaysia.

Overall, this study contributes to the increasing body of literature review on women's empowerment and offers a basis for further research, policy enhancement, and advocacy efforts intended to promote gender equality and women's empowerment in Malaysia and beyond. By continuing to evaluate and examine trends in women's empowerment, the study can reinforce the determination to advance gender equity as evidence-based, informed, and significant.

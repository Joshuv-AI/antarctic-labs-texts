First‐In‐Human Study: Dose Expansion of E7130 in Urothelial Carcinoma and Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Head and Neck
Authors: Nobuaki Matsubara, Ken Saijo, Yasushi Shimizu, Susumu Okano, Kenji Nakano, Naoki Nishio, Tomoyuki Otsuka, Hirofumi Kuno, Takuma Miura, Tomoki Kubota, Hiroki Ikezawa, Yuko Umetsu, Ken Ito, Abraham Apfel, Yuanqing Ye, Yoshitaka Honma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe dose escalation part of the first‐in‐human study of E7130 in patients with advanced solid tumors determined the Recommended Phase 2 Dose (RP2D) of E7130 (480 μg/m2 Q3W). The dose expansion part (presented here) examined safety/preliminary efficacy in patients with squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck (SCCHN) and urothelial carcinoma (UC). Patients ≥ 20 years of age with previously treated SCCHN/UC were treated at the RP2D. The primary objective was to characterize tolerability/safety. Secondary objectives included preliminary efficacy (including objective response rate [ORR], progression‐free survival [PFS], and duration of response [DOR] by investigator per Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors version 1.1 [RECIST v1.1] and overall survival [OS]). Treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were recorded. In patients with SCCHN, tissue biomarker analyses were performed. Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate PFS, DOR, and OS. The Greenwood formula and log–log transformation were used to estimate 95% CIs. Eight patients (SCCHN, n = 2; UC, n = 6) were treated with the RP2D; 10 additional patients (SCCHN, n = 7; UC, n = 3) were treated at a reduced dose (410 μg/m2 Q3W). All patients had ≥ 1 TEAE; 66.7% had serious TEAEs. Patients with UC treated at the RP2D had an ORR of 66.7%; no other responses were observed. Median PFS/OS in patients with SCCHN treated at 480 μg/m2 were 2.8/7.7 months and at 410 μg/m2 were 3.5/5.0 months. Median PFS/OS in patients with UC treated at 480 μg/m2 were 6.6/10.7 months and at 410 μg/m2 were 2.6/11.9 months. Biomarker analyses indicated tumor microenvironment amelioration. Further evaluation is needed to optimize efficacy and safety of E7130 in patients with SCCHN and UC. Introduction

Squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck (SCCHN) and urothelial carcinoma (UC) represent major global health challenges. Despite established first‐line standard‐of‐care treatments for SCCHN (i.e., platinum‐based combination chemotherapy and/or immune checkpoint inhibitors) [1] and UC (i.e., enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab) [2, 3], both cancers are associated with aggressive disease progression, and patients have limited treatment options for advanced stage disease [4, 5].

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a complex ecosystem comprised of the extracellular matrix along with various cellular components and signaling molecules that collectively promote tumor growth/progression, metastasis, immune evasion, and resistance to therapy (including immunotherapy) [6, 7]. Cancer‐associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are an important component of the TME that drive progression and promote malignancy by secreting various proteins, such as transforming growth factor beta, which in turn fuel tumor growth, stimulate angiogenesis, and suppress the immune system [8, 9].

Targeting the TME has emerged as a promising approach to enhance the efficacy of existing treatments and to overcome resistance mechanisms in various tumors [10]. E7130, a novel microtubule inhibitor discovered through totally synthetic studies of halichondrin analogs [11], has been shown to modulate the TME by reducing levels of α‐smooth muscle actin‐positive CAFs [12] and suppressing the production of latency‐associated peptide‐transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGFβ1), a precursor of TGFβ1, in CAFs [13]. Additional preclinical data suggest that E7130 affects the TME by remodeling its vasculature. This process involves increasing the density of tumor microvessels, which can enhance the accumulation and delivery of macromolecular drugs [12, 14, 15]. A preclinical dynamic contrast‐enhanced MRI (DCE‐MRI) analysis with a clustering method showed increased regional tumor perfusion after treatment with E7130 [16]. By changing the vascular environment, therapies like E7130 may improve the effectiveness of other systemic treatments [17]. This dual action (i.e., enhancing drug delivery through vascular remodeling while also contributing to anti‐CAF activity) highlights the potential ability of E7130 to improve the efficacy of various anticancer therapies.

The dose escalation part (Part 1) of the first‐in‐human study of E7130 in patients with advanced solid tumors (NCT03444701) assessed dose‐limiting toxicity (DLT) and determined the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of E7130 (480 μg/m2 every 3 weeks [Q3W]) [15]. Furthermore, potential efficacy signals were observed in several patients with previously treated UC and SCCHN. Notably, UC and SCCHN are both characterized by hypoxia and CAFs, which are known negative prognostic factors [18, 19, 20, 21]. Based on these findings, along with preclinical evidence for E7130—which showed vascular remodeling effects that improved hypoxic conditions and anti‐CAF activity that suppressed tumor growth and promoted sustained therapeutic effects—UC and SCCHN were selected for further investigation in Part 2. Herein, we present the resulting dose expansion (Part 2) outcomes. The objectives of this dose expansion part were to further characterize the tolerability and safety of E7130 and to evaluate the preliminary efficacy of E7130 at the MTD in patients with advanced SCCHN and UC.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Patients

This Phase 1 Study of E7130 in patients with solid tumors was conducted in two parts (NCT03444701) (Figure 1). Part 1 assessed DLT, and the MTD of E7130 was determined to be 480 μg/m2 Q3W [15]. Part 2, reported here, consisted of two cohorts to further characterize the tolerability and safety and to evaluate the preliminary efficacy of E7130 at 480 μg/m2 Q3W (i.e., the recommended dose for Part 2) in patients with SCCHN and UC.

DLT, dose‐limiting toxicity; MTD, maximum tolerated dose; Q2W, every 2 weeks; Q3W, every 3 weeks.

Eligible patients were ≥ 20 years of age and had a histologically confirmed diagnosis of SCCHN or histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable UC (bladder, urethra, ureter, or renal pelvis). Patients must have had measurable disease with at least one lesion of ≥ 1.0 cm in the longest diameter for a non‐lymph node lesion or ≥ 1.5 cm in the short‐axis diameter for a lymph node lesion measurable according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) using CT/MRI. Patients with SCCHN were required to have a lesion suitable for pre‐ and posttreatment biopsies for biomarker and pharmacogenomic assessments. Patients with SCCHN must have previously received at least one but no more than three regimens of prior systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic disease, and patients with UC must have previously received systemic therapy with a platinum and immune checkpoint inhibitor. All patients were required to have adequate organ function with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0 or 1. A target enrollment of 31–71 patients was planned for the dose expansion phase (Figure 1).

Objectives and Assessments

The primary objectives of the dose expansion part were to evaluate the tolerability and safety profile of E7130 in patients with SCCHN or UC. Secondary objectives included determination of the recommended dose of E7130 for future studies, objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), progression‐free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), duration of response (DOR), and evaluation of the pharmacokinetics (PK) of E7130.

Tumor assessments by investigators per RECIST v1.1 using CT/MRI were performed every 6 weeks (±7 days) from Cycle 1 Day 1 through Week 24, and every 12 weeks (±7 days) thereafter, for patients who discontinued treatment without disease progression. Responses observed in the dose expansion phase were confirmed by the investigator ≥ 4 weeks after the initial response was observed. These assessments continued until disease progression, initiation of another anticancer therapy, death, or cutoff date, whichever occurred first. Safety assessments were conducted throughout the study. Adverse events were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 4.03. Hematology, blood chemistry, blood coagulation, and urinalysis; vital signs, arterial oxygen saturation, body weight, 12‐lead electrocardiograms, and ECOG PS; and performance of physical examinations were monitored at scheduled time points.

Pharmacodynamics and pharmacogenomic assessments were conducted. Blood samples were collected at predose on Day 1, on Days 8 and 15 of Cycles 1–2, predose on Day 1 and on Day 8 of Cycles 3–6, on Day 8 of Cycle 7 and thereafter, and at study drug discontinuation. For pharmacodynamic analyses, a multiplex analysis was performed on 79 analytes using AngiogenesisMAP (Myriad RBM; Austin, TX, USA).

In patients with SCCHN, MRI biomarker analyses were performed before treatment (screening) and Cycle 2 Day 8 (15 ± 7 days) after treatment with E7130. Images were acquired using 1.5 or 3 T MRI. DCE‐MRI was acquired using a 3D fast spoiled gradient recalled echo (scan parameters are described in Table S1). Following the acquisition of baseline images, a contrast agent (Gadobutrol; 0.1 mmol Gd/kg at the infusion speed of 2 mL/s followed by a flush of at least 10 mL of saline) was injected rapidly using a mechanical injector. Extractable parameters, including forward and reverse transfer rates (Ktrans, Kep), fractional plasma, and extravascular and extracellular volumes (V p, V e) from capillaries to extravascular space were estimated on the basis of the Tofts and Kermode model [22]. Tumor tissues were also collected from patients with SCCHN pre‐ and posttreatment (at screening and from Cycle 2 Day 3 to Cycle 2 Day 9), for tumor tissue biomarker assessments.

Electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) was used to measure TGFβ1 and CXCL5 (both per Sunplanet Co. Ltd.; Tsukuba, Ibaraki, Japan). RNA sequencing analysis was also performed using Illumina's platform (Nova‐seq X) with the Personalis ImmunoID NeXT assay (Personalis Inc.; Menlo Park, CA, USA). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA; based on results from modeling) and single sample GSEA (ssGSEA) analyses were performed to explore significant pathways.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 and R version 4.4.1. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate PFS, DOR, and OS. The Greenwood formula and log–log transformation was used to estimate the 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For binomial efficacy endpoints, Clopper and Pearson's exact CIs were presented. Pharmacodynamic changes were assessed using the Wilcoxon signed‐rank test; and a false discovery rate (FDR) criterion was also applied, as proposed by Benjamini and Hochberg [23]. Data for RNA sequencing analyses consisted of 22,955 genes from six patients (five patients with both pre‐ and posttreatment samples). Genes with low expression, defined as fewer than 10 raw counts (i.e., number of sequencing reads mapped to the gene) in any sample, were filtered out; 14,757 genes remained after filtering. The R software DESeq2 package was used to model changes in expression after treatment. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined based on testing effect of visit (FDR < 0.2 and |Cohen's d| > 0.8).

Results

The results described here include patients who received E7130 at the recommended dose (480 μg/m2 Q3W; n = 8) and at a reduced dose of 410 μg/m2 Q3W (n = 10). Based on safety signals observed in patients who received the reduced dose, the starting dose was further reduced to 350 μg/m2 Q3W. No additional patients were enrolled at the 350 μg/m2 Q3W dose level as the trial was prematurely terminated due to the sponsor's decision.

Patients

Overall, 18 patients (9 with SCCHN and 9 with UC) received at least one dose of E7130 Q3W and were included in the safety, efficacy, and biomarker analyses (Table 1). In the overall population, the median age was 67.5 years (range, 57–84), most patients were male (83.3%), and patients were nearly evenly split between ECOG PS 0 (44.4%) and 1 (55.6%). Most (72.2%) patients had received two or more prior systemic anticancer regimens. Additional details regarding patient demographics are shown in Table 1.

Safety

The first eight patients enrolled (SCCHN, n = 2; UC, n = 6) received the MTD (480 μg/m2 Q3W). Although no DLTs were observed at 480 μg/m2 Q3W in Part 1, more severe toxicities were observed for both the SCCHN and UC cohorts in Part 2. Specifically, TEAEs led to dose reductions in four (50.0%) patients, dose interruptions in four (50.0%) patients, and study drug discontinuation in three (37.5%) patients (Table 2). Additionally, seven treatment‐related serious adverse events (SAEs; grade 3 malaise, grade 2 and 3 decreased appetite, grade 3 peripheral sensory neuropathy, grade 2 subileus, grade 3 ileus, and grade 3 febrile neutropenia) were observed in five (62.5%) patients.

Given the relatively frequent occurrence of TEAEs resulting in dose interruption, dose reduction, or study drug discontinuation, along with treatment‐related SAEs in these first eight patients, the starting dose of E7130 was reduced to 410 μg/m2 Q3W. Ten additional patients (SCCHN, n = 7; UC, n = 3) received the reduced starting dose. However, TEAEs led to dose reductions in five (50.0%) patients, dose interruptions in two (20.0%) patients, and study drug discontinuation in two (20.0%) patients (Table 2). In addition, five treatment‐related SAEs (grade 3 decreased appetite, grade 2 ileus, grade 3 hyponatremia, grade 3 device‐related bacteremia, and grade 3 febrile neutropenia) were observed in two (20%) patients. Given the relatively frequent occurrence of TEAEs resulting in dose interruption, dose reduction, or study drug discontinuation, along with treatment‐related SAEs in these next 10 patients, the starting dose was further reduced to 350 μg/m2 Q3W; however, no patients were enrolled at this dose level. Safety analyses herein are summarized by dose level (Table 2; Table S2).

All patients had at least one TEAE (Table 2), most frequently constipation (77.8%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 87.5%, 410 μg/m2 dose: 70.0%), leukopenia (72.2%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 62.5%, 410 μg/m2 dose: 80.0%), neutropenia (72.2%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 62.5%, 410 μg/m2 dose: 80.0%), peripheral sensory neuropathy (72.2%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 75.0%, 410 μg/m2 dose: 70.0%), and hyponatremia (66.7%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 87.5%, 410 μg/m2 dose: 50.0%) (Table S2). Similarly, all patients had at least one grade 3–4 TEAE (Table 2), most frequently leukopenia (55.6%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 50.0%; 410 μg/m2 dose: 60.0%) and neutropenia (55.6%; 480 μg/m2 dose: 62.5%; 410 μg/m2 dose: 50.0%) (Table S2). Serious TEAEs occurred in 12 (66.7%) patients (5 patients treated at 480 μg/m2 and 7 patients treated at 410 μg/m2) (Table 2). The most frequent serious TEAEs, both occurring in the 480 μg/m2 cohort, were pneumonia and pneumonia aspiration (28.6% each). No patient had a grade 5 TEAE at either dose level.

Efficacy

Patients With SCCHN

In patients with SCCHN who received the E7130 480 μg/m2 Q3W dose (n = 2), both patients had a best overall response (BOR) of stable disease for an ORR of 0% (95% CI: 0.0, 84.2), a DCR of 100% (95% CI: 15.8, 100.0), and a CBR of 0% (95% CI: 0.0, 84.2) (Table 3). Tumor reduction from baseline was observed in both patients treated at the 480 μg/m2 dose (Figure 2A), and median PFS and OS were 2.8 months (95% CI: not estimable [NE], NE) and 7.7 months (95% CI: NE, NE), respectively (Table 3).

Patients With UC

In patients with UC who received the 480 μg/m2 Q3W dose (n = 6), one patient had a BOR of CR, three patients had a PR, and one patient had stable disease for an ORR of 66.7% (95% CI: 22.3, 95.7), a DCR of 83.3% (95% CI: 35.9, 99.6), and a CBR of 66.7% (95% CI: 22.3, 95.7) (Table 3); all six patients had evidence of tumor reduction from baseline (Figure 2B). Median time to response was 1.2 months (range, 1.2–2.8) and median DOR was 6.6 months (95% CI: 2.7, NE) (Table 3). The median PFS and OS were 6.6 months (95% CI: 2.8, NE) and 10.7 months (95% CI: 5.3, NE), respectively (Table 3).

In patients with UC who received the E7130 410 μg/m2 Q3W dose (n = 3), no patients had a CR or PR, and three patients had a BOR of stable disease for an ORR of 0% (95% CI: 0.0, 70.8), a DCR of 100% (95% CI: 29.2, 100.0), and a CBR of 0% (95% CI: 0.0, 70.8) (Table 3). Tumor reduction from baseline was observed in two of three patients (Figure 2B), and the median PFS and OS were 2.6 months (95% CI: 2.6, NE) and 11.9 months (95% CI: 5.7, NE), respectively (Table 3).

Case Study

Baseline and posttreatment MRIs of a patient with SCCHN treated with the 410 μg/m2 Q3W dose (350 μg/m2 Q3W at C2D1) for approximately 600 days are shown in Figure S1. This patient had a BOR of stable disease and maximum change from baseline in the sums of diameters of target lesions of −6.8%. A reduced Short Tau Inversion Recovery signal after treatment in the tumor region was observed, suggesting scar tissue. DCE‐MRI showed limited high perfusion in the tumor peripheral region at baseline. Posttreatment DCE‐MRI showed homogeneous tumor perfusion, suggesting TME amelioration and evidence of E7130 activity.

Biomarker Analysis

Five paired tumor biopsy samples were collected before the study and on Day 8 of Cycle 2 after E7130 treatment (one patient with SCCHN received E7130 480 μg/m2 and four patients with SCCHN received E7130 410 μg/m2). A baseline biopsy sample was collected from one additional patient who received E7130 410 μg/m2.

An overview of changes in TME‐related biomarkers in patients with SCCHN and UC is shown in Figure S2. Consistent with the dose escalation study [15], E7130 treatment at doses of 480/410 μg/m2 Q3W showed pharmacodynamic changes of TME‐related biomarkers in plasma such as TIE‐2 and VEGFR‐3 at various time points during the first cycle of treatment for patients with SCCHN and UC (Figure S2A). In the SCCHN cohort, E7130 treatment at the 480 or 410 μg/m2 Q3W dose showed a trend toward reduction of plasma levels of TGFβ1 in patients whose baseline concentrations were over the median (i.e., 2380 pg/mL) (Figure S3). Similar trends were observed in plasma levels of CXCL‐5 (a downstream target of TGFβ1 in patients with SCCHN) among patients whose baseline concentration was over the median (median CXCL‐5: 68.32 pg/mL). Changes in plasma biomarker levels from baseline to C1D8, C1D15, and C2D1 in all dosing cohorts are available in Tables S3 and S4. Of the 131 DEGs that were obtained (FDR < 0.2, |Cohen's d| > 0.8), 103 were upregulated and 28 were downregulated (Figure S2B). There were also significant changes in expression level for tubulin‐related genes following E7130 treatment, such as TUBB4B (p = 0.009) and TUBB4A (p = 0.016) (Figure S4). Since it is well known that tubulin dynamics inhibitors disrupt tubulin autoregulation [24], these data suggest target engagement by E7130 in the tumors in patients with SCCHN. Consistent with plasma TGFβ1 analysis, pathway expression analysis also showed a reduction trend of TGFβ signaling pathway (p = 0.01) (Figure S2C). Further, E7130 showed increased unfolded protein response and MYC target genes (p < 0.01) (Figure S2D), and ssGSEA showed a reduction trend in the CAF pathway for patients whose baseline CAF signature levels were high (Figure S2E).

PK Analysis

Preliminary population PK analyses were performed using PK data from 62 patients enrolled in Part 1 (dose escalation) and Part 2 (dose expansion) of the study. Clearance of E7130 was found to be statistically significantly lower in patients with SCCHN compared with those with other tumor types (based on covariate analysis; p < 0.01 in the forward selection step and p < 0.001 in the backward elimination step). However, the distribution of E7130 exposure among patients with SCCHN, UC, and other solid tumors showed substantial overlap, and no clinically meaningful differences between tumor types were observed.

Discussion

This Phase 1 Study of the microtubule dynamics inhibitor E7130, in patients with advanced SCCHN and UC, sought to determine the recommended dose of E7130 and evaluate its safety profile, preliminary antitumor activity, PK, and pharmacodynamics. Initial results showed the recommended dose of E7130 (480 μg/m2 Q3W) was effective; however, the dose was reduced to 410 μg/m2 Q3W based on safety data (including incidence of neutropenia, peripheral sensory neuropathy, and constipation) from the first eight patients treated in the dose expansion part of the study. A further dose reduction was planned, but no more patients were enrolled due to early termination of the study.

For the SCCHN cohort, no patients treated with either the E7130 480 (n = 2) or 410 μg/m2 Q3W (n = 7) dose showed a CR or PR. The 480 μg/m2 cohort had a median PFS of 2.8 months and a DCR of 100%, while the 410 μg/m2 cohort had a median PFS of 3.5 months and a DCR of 57.1%. Both doses were associated with TEAEs. All seven patients in the 410 μg/m2 cohort experienced SAEs, and one discontinued treatment due to a TEAE. Common side effects for both cohorts included leukopenia, neutropenia, constipation, malaise, and hyponatremia, with over half of the 410 μg/m2 cohort also experiencing peripheral sensory neuropathy (57.1%), and one patient (14.3%) experiencing a grade ≥ 3 severity peripheral sensory neuropathy event.

For the UC cohort, the E7130 480 μg/m2 Q3W dose (n = 6) achieved an ORR of 66.7%, with one patient having a CR and three patients having a PR. Median PFS was 6.6 months. However, 66.7% of these patients experienced SAEs, and 50% had TEAEs that led to study drug discontinuation, mainly due to peripheral sensory neuropathy (n = 2). The 410 μg/m2 dose (n = 3) was less effective, with no objective responses and a median PFS of 2.6 months. No SAEs were reported. Nevertheless, 33.3% of these patients discontinued treatment because of peripheral sensory neuropathy.

Although both doses were considered tolerable in the dose escalation part [15], the limited number of cases and differences in patient backgrounds may have contributed to the differing results between dose escalation and dose expansion portions of the study. In line with this, a comparison of baseline characteristics among patients who received the E7130 480 μg/m2 Q3W dose in both portions showed that, relative to the dose escalation portion, those enrolled in the dose expansion portion tended to be older and had comparatively poorer ECOG PS and renal function, including creatinine clearance, at baseline (Eisai data on file). Furthermore, peripheral sensory neuropathy observed with E7130 may be related to the disruption of microtubule‐dependent axonal transport in peripheral nerves, and is a common DLT seen with drugs that target microtubules [25]. Toxicities such as hyponatremia and ileus also appear to have limited the therapeutic window of E7130. In contrast, leukopenia and neutropenia were common TEAEs, but they were manageable and did not result in study drug discontinuation. Although additional cohorts at lower dose levels were planned to further explore the therapeutic window, no patients were ultimately enrolled, precluding completion of the assessment.

Multiplex pharmacodynamic analyses on TME‐related biomarkers showed significant changes in patients with SCCHN. A trend toward reduced plasma TGFβ1 was observed in patients whose baseline plasma TGFβ1 levels were over the median. Consistent with plasma TGFβ1 reduction, an ssGSEA using paired biopsy tumor tissue samples showed a trend toward a reduction of the CAF pathway, including the TGFβ signature. Furthermore, DCE‐MRI showed homogeneous tumor perfusion after E7130 treatment. Together, these results indicate TME amelioration after treatment with E7130.

E7130 showed clinical benefits (CR and PR) in patients with UC in the 480 μg/m2 cohort. These responders exhibited lower baseline plasma TGFβ1 levels compared with the median levels observed in patients with SCCHN (Eisai data on file). A similar trend was seen in Part 1, where patients achieving PR (UC and esophageal cancer) also had relatively low baseline TGFβ1 levels at the time of E7130 treatment (Eisai data on file). Although further investigation is required, these findings suggest that the TME in these responders may have been less severe, characterized by reduced CAF activation and a less immunosuppressive profile. In contrast, although E7130 reduced plasma TGFβ1 levels in patients whose baseline values exceeded the SCCHN median—indicating potential TME amelioration—clear clinical responses were not observed in this group.

Limitations to this study included low patient numbers (nine patients each in SCCHN and UC patient cohorts) and diversity of baseline characteristics, including different diagnoses, varying disease severity, and history of prior treatment, all contributing to a heterogeneous disease profile across this small population.

While the recommended dose of 480 μg/m2 Q3W demonstrated promising efficacy, particularly in the UC cohort, safety concerns were identified. Conversely, the next lower dose of 410 μg/m2 Q3W failed to achieve sufficient efficacy, and safety issues remained unresolved. Further evaluation is needed to determine the recommended dose of E7130 that provides the optimal balance between efficacy and safety, including exploration of new approaches such as modified dosing regimens or combination strategies with other agents.

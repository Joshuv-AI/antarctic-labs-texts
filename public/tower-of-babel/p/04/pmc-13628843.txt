Impact of below-the-knee runoff in patients undergoing drug-coated balloon angioplasty for femoropopliteal disease: a propensity score-matched analysis
Authors: Chunbo Li, Youpeng Zhu, Wenbo Yang, Ye Du, Qiong Song, Tingyu Wang, Xinyuan Wang, Leixin Li, Zibo Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn a real-world setting, the impact of below-the-knee (BTK) runoff status on outcomes following drug-coated balloon (DCB) treatment for femoropopliteal (FP) lesions remains insufficiently studied, and robust evidence is still lacking.MethodsBetween 2021 and 2024, a total of 1,574 patients undergoing drug-coated balloon (DCB) treatment for femoropopliteal arterial occlusive disease were retrospectively identified from 9 centers in China. Propensity score matching was applied to minimize baseline imbalances, resulting in 155 matched pairs for subsequent analysis. Outcome measures included 12-month amputation-free survival (AFS), freedom from clinically driven target lesion revascularization (CD-TLR), all-cause mortality, major adverse event rate, and amputation rate. Predictors of 12-month AFS were also analyzed.ResultsThe mean age was 69.82 ± 9.16 years, and 78.06% of patients were male. Hypertension (71.61%), diabetes mellitus (65.48%), and hyperlipidemia (27.10%) were the predominant comorbid conditions observed in the study population. The incidence of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease (CKD) was 22.58% and 17.10%, respectively. The mean lesion length was 19.12 ± 11.19 cm, with 56.77% being chronic total occlusions. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that the 12-month AFS was 93.67% in the group with 2–3 patent BTK arteries versus 84.93% in the group with 1 patent BTK artery, a statistically significant difference (log-rank test P = 0.028).In the overall cohort, univariable analysis indicated that renal insufficiency, chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI), lesion length, concomitant BTK intervention, and preprocedural BTK runoff status were significantly associated with AFS. In the multivariable model, renal insufficiency, CLTI, and preprocedural BTK runoff status remained independent predictors of AFS.ConclusionIn this multicenter real-world study, preprocedural BTK runoff status was associated with amputation-free survival after DCB treatment for femoropopliteal lesions. Compared with patients with a single patent BTK vessel, those with two or three patent BTK runoff vessels showed a lower event risk across different clinical and anatomical subgroups. Background

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) is a major clinical manifestation of systemic atherosclerosis. Globally, it is associated with substantial limb-related adverse outcomes as well as a higher likelihood of systemic cardiovascular complications and death in patients [1]. Current clinical practice guidelines consistently recommend endovascular therapy as a key treatment strategy for lower extremity PAD, particularly for femoropopliteal artery lesions [2–4]. Compared with plain old balloon angioplasty, drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty demonstrates superior mid- to long-term patency and has been widely adopted for the treatment of femoropopliteal occlusive disease (FPOD) [5, 6]. The status of the distal runoff, particularly the patency of below-the-knee (BTK) arteries, plays a crucial role in post-interventional hemodynamic reconstruction and mid- to long-term outcomes following femoropopliteal interventions. It has been reported to be associated with restenosis, repeat revascularization, and adverse limb events [7, 8]. However, previous studies investigating BTK runoff have predominantly focused on stent implantation or plain old balloon angioplasty (POBA), whereas evidence regarding the influence of preprocedural BTK runoff in the setting of drug-coated balloon (DCB) treatment remains limited [7, 9]. Although various studies have adopted composite scoring systems to evaluate BTK runoff, limited evidence is available regarding whether the preprocedural number of patent BTK arteries—a straightforward and clinically applicable measure—can predict outcomes after DCB treatment for FPOD [10]. Therefore, the present study aims to evaluate the association between the preprocedural number of patent BTK arteries and clinical outcomes after DCB treatment for femoropopliteal artery lesions.

Methods

Study design

The present analysis was conducted using data from the PROMISING registry, a real-world, prospective, multicenter cohort evaluating the safety and effectiveness of drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty in patients with femoropopliteal occlusive disease (FPOD). The registry enrolls consecutive patients undergoing DCB angioplasty for FPOD between 2021 and 2024, with a planned enrollment of 2,500 patients and a 4-year follow-up protocol. Detailed information regarding the PROMISING registry is available on ClinicalTrials.gov [11] .

Individuals enrolled during the period from January 2021 to December 2023 and with at least 1-year follow-up were eligible for this subgroup analysis. Patients without any patent below-the-knee (BTK) arteries on preprocedural imaging were excluded. Following propensity score matching (PSM), patients were categorized according to the number of patent BTK arteries into a single-vessel group and a two- to three-vessel group. Clinical outcomes at 1 year were subsequently compared between these cohorts in individuals with femoropopliteal lesions undergoing DCB intervention.

The major inclusion criteria were symptomatic PAD with Rutherford categories 2–5 and successful guidewire crossing of the target lesion. Regarding outflow, at least one continuous patent below-the-knee runoff vessel extending ≥ 10 cm below the knee was required. For inflow disease, patients with concomitant aortoiliac lesions were required to undergo proximal revascularization first, with residual stenosis ≤ 50%. Detailed eligibility criteria are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Angioplasty procedure

All procedures were performed by experienced vascular specialists according to the standard protocols of each participating center. Lesion crossing was preferentially attempted via an antegrade intraluminal approach. For long CTOs or lesions in which antegrade intraluminal crossing was unsuccessful, subintimal recanalization was permitted at the operator’s discretion, with adjunctive retrograde access used when necessary.

Vessel preparation was mainly performed using POBA. For severely calcified lesions, plaque modification could be selectively performed based on operator judgment, predominantly using directional atherectomy, with intravascular lithotripsy used in a small number of cases. Concomitant BTK intervention was mainly indicated for patients with CLTI and was determined by the operator based on wound status, ischemic severity, and runoff condition.

The choice of DCB devices (AcoArt, Micro, Ultrafree, and IN.PACT) depended on device availability and routine practice at each center, as well as operator preference. Detailed information regarding the DCB devices is provided in Supplementary Table S2. Bailout stenting was performed only for flow-limiting dissection or residual stenosis > 50%. Technical success was defined as successful completion of the procedure with final angiographic residual stenosis < 30% [12]. After the procedure, dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin 100 mg once daily and clopidogrel 75 mg once daily was prescribed for 6 months, followed by single antiplatelet therapy.

Definitions, outcomes and follow-up

AFS was defined as a composite of all-cause mortality and major amputation and served as the primary endpoint. Secondary endpoints included CD-TLR, major amputation, ACM, and MAE. CD-TLR was defined as any repeat intervention of the target lesion driven by recurrent symptoms or ABI decline ≥ 20% or > 0.15 from the postprocedural baseline. Major amputation was defined as amputation at or above the ankle, and MAE was defined as a composite of ACM, CD-TLR, and major amputation [13].

Patent BTK runoff was defined as preprocedural luminal stenosis < 50% on DSA. Lesion complexity was assessed using TASC classification. Calcification severity was evaluated on orthogonal angiographic images and classified according to predefined PROMISING registry criteria as none, mild, moderate, or severe: mild, unilateral calcification < 5 cm; moderate, bilateral calcification of 5–10 cm; and severe, bilateral calcification > 10 cm. Infra-ankle blood flow was further assessed using the Pedal-Plantar Loop score, ranging from 0 to 3, with higher scores indicating poorer pedal arch continuity. Lesion length was defined as the total length of the femoropopliteal target lesion treated with DCB; for tandem or multi-segment lesions treated within the same target territory, lesion lengths were summed. Health-related quality of life among patients with peripheral arterial disease was evaluated using the VascuQoL questionnaire [14, 15].

Outcome events were not determined solely by telephone self-report. Suspected events were verified using available source documents, including hospitalization records, imaging reports, operative records, discharge summaries, and death-related documentation. To improve the consistency of outcome ascertainment, suspected events were centrally reviewed by designated members of the PROMISING registry coordinating group according to predefined endpoint definitions. CD-TLR was considered clinically driven when repeat intervention was prompted by recurrent symptoms or objective deterioration, such as ABI decline, and supported by clinical or imaging findings when available. Major amputation and death were verified using operative records, hospitalization records, family report, or death-related documentation, as appropriate [12, 13, 16].

Statistical analysis

Propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted using propensity scores derived from a multivariable logistic regression model. The covariates included in the propensity score model are provided in Supplementary Table S3. Patients were matched 1:1 using nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, with a caliper width of 0.20 times the standard deviation of the propensity score logit. Covariate balance before and after matching was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with an SMD < 0.10 indicating adequate balance. A Love plot was generated to visually assess covariate balance after matching and is presented in Supplementary Figure S1.

Amputation-free survival, freedom from clinically driven target lesion revascularization, all-cause mortality, and major adverse events were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with group differences assessed by the log-rank test. Time-to-event outcomes were further analyzed by Cox proportional hazards regression, and the findings are presented as hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.3.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and GraphPad Prism (version 10.0; GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Statistical significance was set at a two-tailed P value of less than 0.05.

Results

Following PSM, 310 patients were ultimately enrolled in the final cohort, comprising 155 individuals with single-vessel below-the-knee (BTK) runoff and 155 patients with two to three patent BTK runoff vessels (Fig. 1). The mean age was 69.82 ± 9.16 years, and 78.06% of patients were male. Hypertension (71.61%), diabetes mellitus (65.48%), and hyperlipidemia (27.10%) were the predominant comorbid conditions observed in the study population. The incidence of coronary artery disease and chronic kidney disease (CKD) was 22.58% and 17.10%, respectively. The mean lesion length was 19.12 ± 11.19 cm, with 56.77% being chronic total occlusions.

Periprocedural outcomes

In the PSM cohort, bailout stent implantation after DCB angioplasty was comparable between the one-vessel and two-to-three-vessel BTK runoff groups (24.52% vs. 20.00%, P = 0.413), as was the use of debulking devices (13.55% vs. 12.90%, P = 0.997). The overall technical success rate was 99.0%. Concomitant infrapopliteal intervention was more frequently performed in the one-vessel group (47.74% vs. 17.42%, P < 0.001). Periprocedural complication rates were low and similar between groups. Postoperative ABI was also comparable between the two groups (0.84 ± 0.32 vs. 0.89 ± 0.21, P = 0.105) (Table 2).

Outcomes

Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated that patients with 2–3 patent below-the-knee (BTK) runoff vessels had significantly better amputation-free survival (AFS) than those with only one patent BTK vessel. At 12 months, AFS rates were 93.67% and 84.93%, respectively, with AFS events occurring in 9 of 155 and 21 of 155 patients, respectively (log-rank P = 0.028; Fig. 2A).By comparison, the two groups showed comparable freedom from clinically driven target lesion revascularization (CD-TLR). At 12 months, freedom from CD-TLR was 94.83% in the two-to-three-vessel cohort and 93.98% in the single-vessel cohort, with CD-TLR events occurring in 8 of 155 and 7 of 155 patients, respectively (log-rank P = 0.798; Fig. 2B).Patients with 2–3 patent BTK vessels also had greater 12-month freedom from all-cause death than those with a single patent BTK vessel. The corresponding rates were 95.10% and 87.60%, with all-cause death occurring in 7 of 155 and 18 of 155 patients, respectively (log-rank P = 0.038; Fig. 2C).

Patients without an event were censored at the date of last available follow-up or administratively censored at 365 days, whichever occurred first. Numbers at risk are shown below the curves

No statistically significant difference was observed in freedom from major adverse events (MAEs). At 12 months, freedom from MAEs was 88.61% in the two-to-three-vessel group and 81.55% in the one-vessel group, with MAE events occurring in 16 of 155 and 26 of 155 patients, respectively (log-rank P = 0.116; Fig. 2D). Overall, major amputation events were infrequent during follow-up, occurring in 2 of 155 patients in the two-to-three-vessel group and 3 of 155 patients in the one-vessel BTK group.

Cox proportional hazards regression analysis

Table 3 summarizes the results of the univariate and multivariable Cox regression analyses for amputation-free survival (AFS) in the overall cohort. Univariable Cox regression analysis demonstrated that 2–3 patent preprocedural BTK runoff vessels were associated with a lower risk of AFS events (HR = 0.38, 95% CI 0.24–0.61, P < 0.001). Additionally, renal insufficiency, CLTI, lesion length, and concomitant BTK intervention showed significant associations with AFS. In multivariable Cox regression, preprocedural BTK runoff status remained independently associated with AFS (HR = 0.41, 95% CI 0.24–0.71, P = 0.002), while renal insufficiency and CLTI were confirmed as independent risk factors. In summary, the number of preprocedural patent BTK runoff vessels was significantly associated with amputation-free survival and all-cause mortality in patients undergoing DCB treatment for femoropopliteal disease, while its influence on CD-TLR and MAEs was comparatively modest.

Subgroup analyses

Prespecified subgroup analyses were performed according to lesion length, concomitant infrapopliteal intervention, Rutherford classification, diabetes mellitus, renal dysfunction, and chronic total occlusion (CTO) status (Fig. 3). The association between baseline BTK runoff status and amputation-free survival (AFS) remained generally consistent across all subgroups. Patients with two to three patent BTK arteries consistently showed a lower risk of AFS events compared with those with a single patent BTK artery. No significant interaction was observed between BTK runoff status and any subgroup variable (all P for interaction > 0.05), including concomitant infrapopliteal intervention (P for interaction = 0.522) and Rutherford classification (P for interaction = 0.906), supporting the robustness of the primary findings.

Discussion

This study was based on the Chinese multicenter real-world PROMISING registry and explored the association between preprocedural below-the-knee (BTK) runoff status and clinical outcomes after drug-coated balloon (DCB) treatment for femoropopliteal lesions. The main findings were as follows. First, compared with patients with two or three patent BTK runoff vessels, those with only one patent BTK artery had lower amputation-free survival (AFS). Second, after adjustment for comorbidities, Rutherford classification, lesion characteristics, and periprocedural factors, BTK runoff status remained independently associated with AFS. Third, subgroup analysis showed that the direction of risk reduction associated with two to three patent BTK vessels versus a single patent BTK vessel was generally consistent across different clinical and anatomical subgroups, with no statistically significant interaction observed. These findings suggest that the number of patent BTK runoff vessels before the procedure may serve as a stable prognostic marker for AFS after DCB treatment, but should not be interpreted as evidence of a direct causal effect.

Previous studies have also suggested that BTK runoff status may be associated with prognosis after femoropopliteal intervention. Yamada et al. [9] reported that as the number of patent BTK vessels decreased, 24-month overall survival after DCB treatment progressively declined, with rates of 89.4%, 83.2%, 76.6%, and 61.2%, respectively (P = 0.007). Similar associations have also been observed in stent and plain balloon angioplasty cohorts. Park et al. [17] found that patients with poor baseline runoff had lower 5-year amputation-free survival than those with good runoff (45.1% vs. 56.7%, P = 0.03), as well as lower freedom from limb ischemia (48.9% vs. 65.4%, P = 0.003). Watanabe et al. [7] reported that, in patients with chronic total occlusion, favorable distal runoff was associated with a lower risk of major adverse limb events after PTA (80.9% vs. 43.5%, P < 0.001). Keeling et al. [18] also found that improved tibial artery patency was associated with a lower risk of adverse outcomes after SFA chronic total occlusion intervention (HR = 0.29; 95% CI, 0.15–0.59; P = 0.001). The findings of the present study are generally consistent with these reports and further suggest that, in a real-world DCB treatment setting, preprocedural BTK runoff status may reflect not only distal perfusion reserve but also a greater burden of lower-extremity arterial disease and systemic atherosclerosis.

From a potential mechanistic perspective, poor distal runoff may influence local vascular outcomes through hemodynamic alterations. Previous studies have suggested that poor distal runoff may promote intimal hyperplasia. Abnormal hemodynamics may reduce femoropopliteal flow velocity, promote turbulent flow, and aggravate shear stress–related vascular injury, thereby increasing the risk of proximal restenosis. An animal study by Nakamura et al. [19] also showed that poor BTK runoff may promote thrombus adhesion to stent struts and contribute to stent failure. Although the present study focused on patients treated with DCB, similar mechanisms may also be involved in restenosis, loss of patency, and limb-related events after DCB treatment. However, these mechanisms mainly explain local vascular and limb-related outcomes and should not be directly extrapolated to all-cause mortality risk.

In the overall cohort, Cox regression analysis showed that renal dysfunction, CLTI, and BTK runoff status were independently associated with AFS. Diabetes mellitus was not significantly associated with AFS in this study. One possible explanation is that the effects of BTK disease severity, CLTI status, and renal dysfunction on AFS were stronger and may have attenuated or masked the independent effect of diabetes. Notably, in the diabetic subgroup, two to three patent BTK vessels were still associated with a lower event risk, whereas the interaction by diabetes status was not significant. Therefore, this finding should not be interpreted as indicating that the prognostic role of BTK runoff exists only in patients with diabetes; rather, it may suggest that distal perfusion reserve remains clinically relevant in diabetic patients.

It should be noted that AFS is a composite endpoint consisting of death and major amputation. Because the number of major amputation events in this study was limited, the between-group difference in 12-month AFS may have been influenced, at least in part, by all-cause mortality. Therefore, poor BTK runoff should be regarded more as a marker of a high-risk patient phenotype, potentially reflecting a greater systemic atherosclerotic burden, advanced distal arterial disease, CLTI, renal dysfunction, and overall frailty, rather than as a direct cause of mortality.

This study has several limitations. First, although PSM was used to reduce baseline differences, residual confounding inherent to observational studies cannot be completely excluded. Second, BTK runoff status was defined according to the number of patent BTK vessels, which may not fully capture runoff quality, pedal arch integrity, diffuse tibial disease burden, or postprocedural improvement in runoff [20]. Third, multiple DCB platforms and vessel preparation strategies were used across different centers, introducing a degree of procedural heterogeneity. Finally, the study population was derived from a Chinese multicenter cohort, and follow-up was limited to 12 months; therefore, further validation with longer-term follow-up and broader populations is warranted.

Conclusions

In this multicenter real-world study, preprocedural BTK runoff status was associated with amputation-free survival after DCB treatment for femoropopliteal lesions. Compared with patients with a single patent BTK vessel, those with two or three patent BTK runoff vessels showed a lower event risk across different clinical and anatomical subgroups. These findings suggest that preprocedural BTK runoff status may serve as an important reference for risk stratification and prognostic assessment after DCB treatment.

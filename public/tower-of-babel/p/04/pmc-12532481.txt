Correlation analysis between frequency of gastrointestinal bleeding episodes and abnormal coagulation indexes in digestive system tumors
Authors: Ai Shaoqing, Gao Jun, Wei Jia, Liu Jie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis retrospective observational analysis was conducted to explore the correlation between the frequency of bleeding in digestive system tumors and various coagulation indexes, providing clinical data for predicting multiple bleeding episodes in patients. A retrospective collection was made of 41 patients with digestive system tumors who presented with gastrointestinal bleeding in our hospital from 2020 to 2024. The number of bleeding episodes ranged from 1 to 5. At the same time, the coagulation indexes at the time of admission were collected. The SPSS statistical software was used to analyze the correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and the coagulation indexes. A positive correlation was found between the number of bleeding episodes and the percentage activity of prothrombin (Pearson r = 0.199, p = 0.034), while there was no significant correlation with other coagulation indexes (prothrombin time, international normalized ratio, partial thromboplastin time, thrombin time, fibrinogen, and D - dimer). Also, no significant correlation was found between the number of bleeding episodes and other clinicopathological characteristics of the patients. The continuous increase in the elevated percentage activity of prothrombin may be associated with predicting re-bleeding in patients with digestive tract tumors.Keywords: Digestive tract tumors, Coagulation indexes, Percentage activity of prothrombin, Re-bleedingSubject terms: Cancer, Gastroenterology

Introduction

In recent years, the incidence and mortality rates of digestive tract tumors in China have been increasing year by year. Digestive tract tumors have become one of the most common malignant tumors in clinical practice1. Patients with these tumors often suffer from various complications, among which bleeding is a relatively severe complication that significantly affects the prognosis of patients2.Prothrombin, synthesized by the liver, is a key factor in the extrinsic coagulation pathway. Its activity directly reflects the liver’s synthetic function and the integrity of the coagulation cascade. Abnormalities in prothrombin percentage activity may disrupt the balance between coagulation and fibrinolysis, thereby affecting bleeding risk3. We hypothesize that increased prothrombin percentage activity is positively associated with the frequency of gastrointestinal bleeding episodes in patients with digestive system tumors.An in-depth understanding of the correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and prothrombin percentage activity in patients with digestive tract tumors can help clinicians better evaluate the patients’ conditions. This has important clinical value for early diagnosis, treatment, and prevention4.

Materials and methods

General information

This is a retrospective observational study conducted at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Medical University. The study population included patients with digestive system tumors who were hospitalized due to repeated gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis and/or melena) from 2021 to 2024. These patients were consecutively included if hospitalized for gastrointestinal bleeding episodes with confirmed digestive system tumors, with the inclusion period referring to such hospitalization time.Coagulation indexes (including prothrombin time, international normalized ratio, prothrombin percentage activity, partial thromboplastin time, thrombin time, fibrinogen, D-dimer, etc.) were detected within 24 h of admission and before hemostatic treatment to reflect the baseline state during bleeding. Collected data (name, gender, age, tumor type, differentiation degree, bleeding episodes, coagulation indexes) were representative.Inclusion criteria:

① Age ≥ 18 years;② Confirmed diagnosis of digestive system tumors (pathologically confirmed by surgical/endoscopic biopsy, or clinically diagnosed based on imaging [CT/MRI], tumor markers [CEA, CA19-9], and typical clinical manifestations for patients unable to undergo biopsy);③ Presence of ≥ 1 episode of gastrointestinal bleeding, defined as: Hematemesis, melena, or positive fecal occult blood test combined with a decrease in hemoglobin ≥ 20 g/L within 24 h; Bleeding confirmed by endoscopy (e.g., tumor surface erosion, variceal rupture);④ Coagulation indexes detected within 24 h of admission (before hemostatic treatment).Exclusion criteria: ① Recent relevant clinical treatments (including surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and biological therapy) within 1 month;② Missing clinical data (e.g., incomplete bleeding records, untested coagulation indexes);③ Complicated with congenital coagulation disorders (e.g., hemophilia A/B, von Willebrand disease);④ Acute infection within 1 month (body temperature > 38.5℃, white blood cell count > 10 × 10⁹/L) (infection can affect coagulation function);⑤ Severe liver or kidney failure unrelated to tumors (Child-Pugh class C liver function, serum creatinine > 300 µmol/L) (hepatic/renal dysfunction can alter coagulation indexes);⑥ Long-term use of anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin, heparin) or antiplatelet drugs (e.g., aspirin, clopidogrel) within 3 months (not covered by “recent treatments”);⑦ Complicated with hematological diseases (e.g., leukemia, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura) that affect coagulation function.

Research methods

A statement to confirm that all methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. A statement to confirm that all experimental protocols were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Medical University. It is confirmed that informed consent was obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardian(s).

All coagulation indexes (including prothrombin time, international normalized ratio, prothrombin percentage activity, etc.) were detected using a fully automatic coagulation analyzer and matching reagents, with daily quality control conducted using standard samples to ensure accuracy.Clinical data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system (HIS system) by two independent researchers. Key information (e.g., bleeding episodes, tumor diagnosis, coagulation indexes) was cross-checked, and discrepancies were resolved through consultation with a third senior clinician.The SPSS 22.0 statistical software was used for data analysis to analyze the correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and prothrombin percentage activity. Analysis of variance with P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Correlation analysis between repeated bleeding in digestive system tumors and coagulation indexes

Based on the data analysis of the correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and coagulation indexes in patients with digestive tract tumors, the results showed that the number of bleeding episodes was significantly correlated with prothrombin percentage activity (pearson r = 0.199, P = 0.034), while there was no significant correlation with prothrombin time (pearson r = − 0.172, P = 0.068), international normalized ratio (pearson r = − 0.171, P = 0.069), partial thromboplastin time (pearson r = − 0.087, P = 0.36), thrombin time (pearson r = − 0.145, P = 0.123), fibrinogen (pearson r = 0.128, P = 0.176), and D - dimer (pearson r = − 0.040, P = 0.670). This result indicates that there is a significant positive correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and prothrombin percentage activity in patients with digestive tract tumors. That is, as the prothrombin percentage activity increases, the number of bleeding episodes may increase; conversely, when the prothrombin percentage activity decreases, the number of bleeding episodes may decrease. Discussion

This study analyzed the correlation between the coagulation—related indexes of patients and the number of repeated bleeding episodes and found a significant correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and prothrombin percentage activity. The harmfulness of repeated bleeding and the difficulty of clinical treatment are self - evident. Therefore, early prediction of bleeding has important clinical significance.

This correlation opens up new ideas for the diagnosis and disease assessment of many hemorrhagic diseases. For patients with unexplained bleeding, monitoring prothrombin percentage activity can be used as a sensitive and specific indicator to assist doctors in quickly identifying potential causes of coagulation disorders5. Compared with traditional coagulation screening items, dynamic monitoring of prothrombin activity may be able to capture subtle changes in the disease earlier6.In patients with liver diseases, the liver’s function of synthesizing coagulation factors is impaired, and the prothrombin activity often shows a continuous downward trend. The magnitude of its decrease is closely related to the increase in the number of bleeding episodes, providing a quantitative basis for clinical judgment of the progression of liver diseases and early warning of bleeding risk, and helping medical staff to develop targeted preventive measures for bleeding in advance.

Prothrombin percentage activity is an index used to evaluate coagulation function, which measures the activity of prothrombin in the blood to assess whether the blood coagulation function is normal7. When the prothrombin percentage activity changes, it may affect the normal function of the coagulation system, thereby changing the bleeding risk8. In this study, It should be noted that the correlation coefficient (r = 0.199) indicates a weak association, suggesting that prothrombin percentage activity alone may not be a sufficient predictor. Its clinical value needs to be evaluated in combination with other factors9.

From a physiological mechanism perspective, a decrease in prothrombin percentage activity may mean that the process of prothrombin being converted into thrombin is hindered, preventing the coagulation process from starting in a timely and effective manner10. In this case, once there is vascular damage in the body, the hemostasis process may be delayed, increasing the number and severity of bleeding11. Conversely, an excessively high prothrombin percentage activity may also lead to hypercoagulability, increasing the risk of thrombosis12. In some cases, local hyperfibrinolysis after thrombosis may indirectly increase the bleeding risk13, but this complex relationship was not reflected in this study.

Moreover, the relationship between repeated bleeding and prothrombin percentage activity may also be affected by other factors14. The patient’s underlying disease status, drug use, and individual genetic differences can all interfere with the direct relationship between prothrombin percentage activity and the number of bleeding episodes15. For example, Wang Wei16 studied and believed that patients with liver diseases have obvious coagulation disorders, which are closely related to gastrointestinal hemorrhage in patients. Coagulation function indexes have a certain clinical predictive value for gastrointestinal hemorrhage, especially in patients with liver cirrhosis. This is mainly because the liver’s function of synthesizing coagulation factors is impaired, resulting in a decrease in prothrombin percentage activity, making it easier for patients to have bleeding symptoms and increasing the number of bleeding episodes. Certain drugs, such as antibacterial drugs, anticoagulants, and anti - platelet drugs, can also affect coagulation function, thereby interfering with the relationship between prothrombin percentage activity and the number of bleeding episodes17.

Most previous studies have focused on the association between coagulation indexes (e.g., PT, aPTT) and tumor prognosis, but few have specifically explored the link between prothrombin percentage activity and the frequency of gastrointestinal bleeding in digestive system tumors. This study fills the gap in this field, providing preliminary evidence for further research.Of course, This study has additional limitations: ① Small sample size and single-center design: The sample size (n = 41) is relatively small, and the single-center data may limit the generalizability of the results.② Lack of multivariate analysis: Confounding factors such as tumor stage, liver function, and medication use were not adjusted for, which may affect the interpretation of the correlation between prothrombin percentage activity and bleeding frequency.③ Lack of dynamic monitoring: Coagulation indexes were only measured at admission, and dynamic changes during follow-up were not recorded, making it impossible to assess the longitudinal relationship with bleeding episodes.④ Incomplete consideration of bleeding causes: Although we prioritized tumor-related bleeding, it is difficult to completely rule out concurrent non-tumor bleeding factors (e.g., stress ulcers), which may interfere with the results.⑤ Limited coagulation indexes: Other potential coagulation-related markers (e.g., platelet count, von Willebrand factor) were not included, which may provide a more comprehensive understanding of coagulation-bleeding relationships.

In conclusion, this study found a certain correlation between the number of bleeding episodes and prothrombin percentage activity, suggesting that clinicians should pay attention to the prothrombin percentage activity index when evaluating patients’ bleeding risks. At the same time, it is necessary to comprehensively consider the overall situation of patients, further study the relationship between the two and its influencing factors, and carry out secondary prevention treatment in internal medicine, which can effectively reduce the re-bleeding rate and related mortality18, so as to better guide clinical practice, reduce patients’ bleeding risks, and improve treatment effects.

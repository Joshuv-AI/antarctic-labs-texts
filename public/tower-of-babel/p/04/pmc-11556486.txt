Correlation of Pseudocholinesterase Level with Clinical, Biochemical Parameters Including Cardiac Profile and the Outcome in Organophosphorus Poisoning
Authors: Arvind Kumar, Shyam Chand Chaudhary, Kauser Usman, Vivek Kumar, Kamal Kumar Sawlani, Munna Lal Patel, Abhishek Singh, Amit Kumar, Gyanendra Kumar Sonkar, Shiuli Rathore
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Poisoning is a significant health hazard and a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. India, being a predominantly agrarian country, routinely employs organophosphate (OP) pesticides in farming, and they are readily available “over the counter.” OPs exert their toxicity by interfering with the normal function of acetylcholine, an essential neurotransmitter throughout the autonomic and central nervous systems. Due to the limited availability of facilities and resources in health-care systems, and economically restraining patients, it is necessary to rely more on clinical features to assess the severity of poisoning and manage the condition properly.Methodology:It was a hospital-based prospective observational study that included patients aged >13 years in a tertiary care hospital. All patients were clinically evaluated based on their history and examination. The diagnosis was made based on characteristic clinical manifestations or evidence of exposure to organophosphorus compounds (corroborative evidence such as empty containers and the odor of gastric aspirates). Clinical severity was assessed and categorized according to the Peradeniya Organophosphorus Poisoning Scale (POP scale). A score of 0–3 is considered mild poisoning, 4–7 as moderate poisoning, and 8–11 as severe poisoning.Results:Out of the 50 patients enrolled in the study, 17 (34.00%) were aged <20 years, 19 (38%) were in the 20–30 years age group, and 14 (28%) were aged >30 years. Ingestion is the only mode of exposure to poisoning. None of the patients had history of contact or inhalational exposure. Of the 50 cases, 12 (24.0%) were in the mild category, 26 (52.0%) in the moderate category, and 12 (24%) in the severe category on the POP grading. A comparison of the mean serum pseudocholinesterase, troponin-T, and pro-BNP levels with severity was performed. In mild OP poisoning, the mean serum PChE level was 2766.58 ± 1120.44; in moderate, it was 1969.35 ± 1330.07, and in severe, it was 701.83 ± 961.17. Pseudocholinesterase levels decreased progressively with increasing clinical severity from mild-to-severe cases, and this association was statistically significant (P < 0.001). Two-dimensional echocardiography screening done in all patients did not show any significant abnormalities.Conclusion:This study shows that serum PCE is reduced in OP poisoning and correlates with the clinical severity grading done by the POP scale and is also associated with an increase in the duration of intensive care unit stay. No significant evidence of direct cardiac injury was observed in this study. A low Glasgow Coma Scale score and an increased respiratory rate at presentation are associated with poor outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Poisoning is an important health hazard and one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. The World Health Organization estimates that one million serious unintentional poisonings occur every year, in addition to two million cases of suicide attempts with pesticides.[1] It is one of the preferred means of committing suicide in India, and organophosphate (OP) poisoning continues to be a frequent reason for admission to hospitals and intensive care units (ICUs). India is a predominantly agrarian country where pesticides are routinely used for farming; as such, they are readily available “over the counter” despite being a major cause of morbidity and mortality.[2] Its incidence is higher in adolescents and young people in developing countries, with mortality rates ranging from 10% to 20%.[3]

Organophosphorus compounds are antiacetylcholinesterases, which exert their toxicity by interfering with the normal function of acetylcholine, an essential neurotransmitter throughout the autonomic and central nervous systems. Plasma cholinesterase (pseudo or butyrylcholinesterase), which is found in the plasma, liver, pancreas, and intestinal mucosa, is suppressed in OP poisoning and is easier to estimate.

The significance of estimating serum pseudocholinesterase (PCE) in the assessment of the severity and prognosis of patients with OP poisoning has been elusive in previous studies, and there is a lack of data on direct cardiac injury in these patients. Owing to the limited availability of facilities and resources in health-care systems and economically restraining patients, it is necessary to rely more on clinical features to assess the severity of poisoning and to manage the condition properly. Therefore, the present study was designed to establish a prognostic correlation with clinical severity and evidence of cardiac injury in patients with OP poisoning.

The aims and objectives of the study were to identify the clinical and biochemical profiles, estimate serum pseudocholinesterase, serum troponin-T (TROP-T) levels, and pro-BNP levels in OP poisoning patients, and determine their prognostic significance in the outcome.

METHODOLOGY

The study was conducted in the Department of Medicine in collaboration with Department of Cardiology at tertiary care hospital. This was a hospital-based prospective observational study that included patients aged >13 years. After obtaining approval and clearance from the institutional ethics committee, patients who fulfilled the inclusion criteria were enrolled in the study after obtaining informed consent. Data were collected from each patient using preformed proforma.

Inclusion criteria

Patients aged >13 years with organophosphorus poisoning were included after obtaining written informed consent from either the patient or the attendants.

Exclusion criteria

Patients or attendants not giving consent, patients with pregnancy, chronic illness, or history of use of certain drugs (succinylcholine, Codeine, morphine, and antimalarial drugs) were excluded from the study. Patients with double insecticide/multiple poisonings with other drugs were also excluded from the study.

All patients were clinically evaluated based on their history and examination. The diagnosis was made based on characteristic clinical manifestations, including miosis, fasciculations, bilateral crepitations in the chest, excessive salivation, and improvement of symptoms and signs with administration of atropine or evidence of exposure to organophosphorus compounds (corroborative evidence such as empty containers and odor of gastric aspirates).

Treatment was commenced as soon as the patients attended, and electrocardiography (ECG), arterial blood gas, complete hemogram, serum electrolytes, liver function test, blood urea, serum creatinine, and chest X-ray were performed routinely in all patients. Serum PCE level by butyrylthiocholine potassium hexacyanoferrate (lll) method (Biological reference range 5000–12,000 U/L), TROP-T, and pro-BNP levels using the electrochemiluminescent method (Reference range for TROP-T 0.0–0.014 ng/ml, for pro-BNP 0–125 ng/ml) was estimated for all patients at the time of admission.

Clinical severity was assessed and categorized according to Peradeniya Organophosphorus Poisoning scale (POP scale) [Table 1].[4] The score was obtained at the initial presentation before any medical intervention, and it represented the muscarinic, nicotinic, and central effects of the acute cholinergic manifestations of OP poisoning. A score of 0–3 is considered mild poisoning, 4–7 as moderate poisoning, and 8–11 as severe poisoning. The Peradeniya Organophosphorus Poisoning (POP) Scale is a scoring system introduced by Senanayake H J de Silva and L Karalliedde in 1993.[4] The most common clinical manifestations of OP poisoning were selected as parameters, and each was assessed on a three-point scale.

The patients were assessed for respiratory failure, and, if so, intubated and transferred to the ICU for mechanical ventilation. Rapid atropinization, with doubling doses of atropine at 5–10 min intervals, starting at 1–3 mg, until improvement of muscarinic signs, was performed for all patients.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were organized and tabulated in Microsoft Excel 2021 (Office 2021 package), and statistical analysis was done using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 21.0 (IBM Corp., Chicago, IL, USA). The data collected were analyzed statistically using descriptive statistics, namely mean, standard deviation, and percentage, wherever applicable. Appropriate parametric and nonparametric tests were used to determine the association between severity category on the POP scale and pseudocholinesterase, TROP-T, and pro-BNP levels. Pearson’s correlation coefficient between the POP scale and duration of hospital stay was used.

RESULTS

Fifty patients with organophosphorus poisoning were included according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Of the 50 patients enrolled in the study, 17 (34.00%) were aged <20 years, 19 (38%) were in the 20–30 years age group, and 14 (28%) were aged >30 years. Their ages ranged from 14 to 56 years, with a mean age of 26 ± 9.7 years. The maximum number of cases (72%) belonged to the age group ≤30 years. Twenty-six (52%) were male and 24 (48%) were female, with a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.

Among the studied population, the majority were students (38%), followed by homemakers (26.2%) and laborers (12%). Twenty-three (46%) cases belonged to the lower class and 27 (54%) cases belonged to the middle class of the socioeconomic group. Ingestion is the only mode of exposure to poisoning. None of the patients presented contact or inhalational exposure. In ingestion, 98.0% of the patients took poison with suicidal intent, whereas 2.0% accidentally ingested poison. The organophosphorus compounds were identified as chlorpyrifos (32%), chlorpyrifos + cypermethrin (16%), dichlorvos (2%), and unknown OP agents (48%).

Of the 50 cases, 12 (24.0%) were in the mild category, 26 (52.0%) in the moderate category, and 12 (24%) in the severe category on POP grading. A comparison of the mean serum pseudocholinesterase, TROP-T, and Pro-BNP levels with severity was performed. In mild OP poisoning, the mean serum pseudocholinesterase level was 2766.58 ± 1120.44, in moderate, it was 1969.35 ± 1330.07, and in severe, it was 701.83 ± 961.17 [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Organophosphorus pesticide poisoning is one of the most common modes of poisoning fatalities. The younger population is most commonly affected (72%) between 13 and 30 years, with a mean age of 26 years; both sexes were equally affected, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.1. Similar observations were made by Yaduraj et al. and Honnakatti et al. and others.[56]

The most common mode of exposure to poisoning was ingestion and none of the patients had history of contact or inhalation exposure. This may be due to the increased use of better and newly developed equipment for pesticide spraying in fields compared with traditional or conventional methods and increased awareness of using facemasks and gloves, following the manufacturer’s instructions for application, personal safety, and use of personal protective equipment while working in fields.

Suicidal intent was the most common cause (92%), followed by accidental ingestion (2%). This indicates an increased incidence of organophosphorus compound poisoning among young people. This age group is the most active physically, mentally, and socially; therefore, it is more prone to stress during life because of unemployment, poverty, depression due to various causes, and the main reason for suicidal ingestion. Similar observations were observed in a study by Tejas et al. and were supported by other studies.[567] There was a significant correlation between the degree of derangement in serum PChE levels and the severity of OP poisoning (P < 0.001). A statistically significant decline in serum pseudocholinesterase levels was observed with a higher clinical POP severity grade. We also observed that patients with severe POP had higher mortality rates.

The correlation between cardiac injury and OP intoxication is unknown. We are unaware of any study demonstrating higher serum levels of cardiac markers as predictors of death in patients with OP poisoning. In our study, the mean pro-BNP level was slightly higher in the death group than in the surviving group, but the difference was not statistically significant. 2D-ECHO screening done in all patients did not show any regional wall motion abnormalities or significant decrease in ejection fraction. These findings are similar to those of a study conducted by Anand et al.[8] Although these findings do not allow us to determine whether cardiac injury is a cause of death, this may add to the understanding of the OP and its effect on the heart.

ECG changes were analyzed; out of 50 cases, 25 (50%) were in sinus rhythm (11 in mild, 13 in moderate, and 1 in severe grade), 13 patients (26%) had sinus tachycardia (7 in moderate and 6 in severe grade), and 12 patients (24%) had sinus bradycardia, one patient showed T wave inversion in V1–V4. Of the expired patients, three had sinus bradycardia and two had sinus tachycardia with QTc prolongation. Similar observations were reported by Reddy and Reddy who studied ECG changes in 80 adult patients with OP poisoning and found the rate to be normal in 53 (66.25%), sinus tachycardia in 15 (18.75%), and sinus bradycardia in 12 (15%) cases.[9] Prolongation of QTc in severe cases associated with mortality is also shown by Prabhakaran et al.[10]

The mean potassium level was significantly higher in the death group than in the survival group. However, the other biochemical parameters were not significantly different between the groups.

The mean GCS score among the death cases (7.80 ± 0.84) was less than that in the surviving cases (12.56 ± 2.94), and the difference was found to be significant (P = 0.001). Similar observations were made by Davies et al., in their study, they concluded that GCS was a good predictor of outcome, and a study by Shashank and Acikalin et al. also supported similar results.[111213] In the surviving group, 18 (40.00%) patients required intensive care and 13 (28.88%) required mechanical ventilation. High severity grade on the POP scale required intensive and ventilatory care, similar findings were made by Chaudhary et al. in their study.[14]

The mean hospital stay was 3.6 ± 0.62 days in the mild category, 6 ± 2.21 days in the moderate, and 10.25 ± 13.10 days in the severe category. Severity was significantly positively correlated with hospital stay (P = 0.050), with the longest stay of 52 days in one patient who developed ventilator-associated pneumonia, with intermediate syndrome and polyneuropathy, and required 44 days of ventilatory support, tracheostomy, and one session of plasmapheresis. Severity was significantly and positively correlated with hospital stay duration.

CONCLUSION

The principal limitation of the current study is its small sample size, which was limited by the COVID-19 pandemic. However, more research is required to prove this result, and a larger sample size is needed for better statistical analysis.

Nevertheless, we conclude that reduced levels of serum pseudocholinesterase associated with clinically severe grades as per the POP scale along with longer duration of ICU stay. All five patients who expired belonged to the severe category. Death was significantly associated with severe POP grade (P = 0.0002) and can be used as a clinical tool for the assessment of severity in remote areas. No significant evidence of direct cardiac injury was observed in this study. A low GCS score and an increased respiratory rate at presentation are associated with poor outcomes. Outcomes in patients who have been rapidly and adequately atropinized are favorable and good.

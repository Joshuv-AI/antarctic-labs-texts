Adolescent literacy, trust, and acceptance of vaccines before and after educational intervention: protocol for a mixed methods research
Authors: Bruna Hinnah Borges Martins de Freitas, Jaqueline Costa Lima, Ana Lucia Sartori, Rafael Haeffner, Camila Rodrigues Barbosa Nemer, Ana Roberta Vilarouca da Silva, Carolina Maia Martins Sales, Erica Marvila Garcia, Juliano Bortolini, Scott C. Ratzan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHealth literacy is essential for understanding and using health information, influencing both individual and collective decision-making. In the context of vaccination, vaccine literacy helps individuals comprehend the risks and benefits, combating vaccine hesitancy. Adolescents, in particular, face challenges related to misinformation and negative perceptions about vaccines. This study aims to describe the protocol of a mixed-methods study designed to analyze vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents before and after an educational intervention, considering their sociodemographic, economic, and health characteristics.MethodsThe quasi-experimental mixed-methods research will be structured into five phases: (1) Pre-intervention quantitative phase; (2) Pre-intervention qualitative phase; (3) Development, validation, and application of Care-Educational Technologies (CET); (4) Post-intervention quantitative phase; (5) Post-intervention qualitative phase. Adolescents aged 10 to 19 years, from public and private schools in five Brazilian capitals, will participate in the study. Data collection will include the Health Literacy Vaccinale (HLV), Vaccine Trust Gauge (VTG), Vaccine Acceptance Instrument (VAI-SV), and a sociodemographic questionnaire. Qualitative interviews will explore adolescents’ perceptions of literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance. The CET will be validated in another municipality before its implementation. The intervention will focus on adolescents with varying levels of literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance, assessing the implementation’s impact using the same instruments. The post-intervention qualitative phase will include participants intentionally selected to represent improvements, stability, or reductions in these levels.DiscussionThe educational intervention is expected to increase vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents. The results may provide valuable insights into the factors influencing these aspects, contributing to more effective public vaccination policies. The validated CET could become a crucial tool for improving vaccine adherence, promoting a positive impact on public health.

Background

Health literacy refers to an individual’s set of abilities, within a specific context, to observe, understand, interact with, and respond effectively to their surrounding environment. Being literate means having the capacity to engage effectively with the world around oneself. Health literacy is essential for understanding, evaluating, and applying health information to make informed decisions about well-being [1, 2]. It encompasses the skills to acquire and critically assess knowledge, enabling informed choices that promote both individual and collective care [3]. In the context of vaccination, it translates into the ability to understand and use immunization information, as well as to manage personal and community health through vaccination [4–6].

When immunization information is not understood correctly, there may be a misperception of risk estimation, leading to vaccine hesitancy [7]. The rise in vaccine hesitancy and the decline in vaccination coverage have raised concerns about the risk of outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases, particularly since the Covid-19 pandemic [8–10].

In this context, vaccine literacy is seen as a promising technique to combat vaccine hesitancy. Previous studies have associated health literacy with Covid-19 vaccine acceptance and a “non-hesitant” attitude towards vaccines [11]. Improving vaccine literacy may be essential for increasing trust and vaccine acceptance, especially when providing information tailored to people’s needs.

For adolescents, vaccine acceptance faces significant challenges, such as misinformation and negative perceptions about immunization. Studies show that adolescents often have limited knowledge of vaccines and feel that immunization information is more directed at parents than at them. This is particularly concerning for a digital generation that spends much of its time online. Their greater exposure to misinformation can foster distrust and resistance to vaccines [12].

Understanding trust as a network of factors related to vaccination can offer valuable insights into the drivers of vaccine acceptance [13], as identified in a recent study conducted across different countries [14]. However, research on the relationship between literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance among adolescents is limited.

Educational interventions may be effective in improving health literacy, increasing trust in vaccines, and enhancing vaccine acceptance among adolescents. However, there is still a need for studies exploring which types of interventions are most effective and how to tailor these strategies to adolescents’ specific needs. Care-Educational Technologies (CET) provide a promising approach, combining scientific and everyday knowledge to promote critical and reflective awareness. The rationale for choosing CET lies in their ability to integrate educational and care processes, fostering deeper engagement with health topics, especially among adolescents. CET offer opportunities for broad diffusion and scalability, ensuring adaptability to diverse sociocultural contexts. Their promise as an intervention tool is grounded in their potential to act as a ‘rising tide,’ improving vaccine literacy and trust across all levels of adolescent understanding and reinforcing key health messages in a cohesive manner [15].

Mixed-methods studies, which integrate quantitative and qualitative data, can offer deeper insights into adolescents’ literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance, as well as inform strategies for promoting these aspects through CET. The main objective of this paper is to describe the protocol of a mixed-methods study designed to analyze vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents before and after an educational intervention, considering their sociodemographic, economic, and health characteristics.

Methods/design

Study design and setting

This study will be conducted using a quasi-experimental mixed-methods design, adopting a complex sequential approach that involves multiple phases and procedures to capture the complexity of the phenomenon being studied [16]. As outlined in Fig. 1, the mixed-methods design will be structured into five main phases: (1) Pre-intervention quantitative phase (QUAN); (2) Pre-intervention qualitative phase (qual); (3) Construction, validation, and application of CET (methodological); (4) Post-intervention quantitative phase (QUAN); (5) Post-intervention qualitative phase (qual).

This sequence will allow for the integrated collection and analysis of quantitative and qualitative data, enabling a comprehensive understanding of changes in vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels among adolescents, before and after the educational intervention.

The study will be conducted in five Brazilian capital cities: Cuiabá (Mato Grosso), Macapá (Amapá), Teresina (Piauí), Vitória (Espírito Santo), and Curitiba (Paraná). The validation of the intervention materials will take place in the municipality of Sinop (Mato Grosso).

Study population

Study participants will be adolescents aged 10 to 19 years, enrolled in both public and private schools, ranging from the fourth grade of elementary school to the third year of high school. To be included in the study, participants must be able to read or communicate verbally, and provide informed consent. Adolescents with reported cognitive impairments or those who transfer schools during the research period will be excluded. These criteria ensure that participants can either complete the self-administered instruments or respond to questions during data collection, guaranteeing the reliability and comprehensiveness of the information gathered.

Study procedures

Pre-intervention quantitative phase

Participants will be recruited from public and private schools in the five selected capitals. The sample size calculation will be based on an expected proportion of 50%, with a 95% confidence level and a 5% margin of error. Stratified sampling will be conducted within each capital and carried out in two-stage clusters. The strata will be defined by the four geographical regions, type of school (public – including municipal, state, and federal networks – or private), gender (male or female), and age group (10 to 14 years or 15 to 19 years). The two cluster stages will include the selection of schools and, subsequently, the selection of classes within those schools. A correction factor of 1.5 will be applied to adjust for the sampling plan effect. The distribution of the sample within each stratum will be proportional to the size of each.

Quantitative data will be collected using the Brazilian versions of the following instruments, which are currently undergoing cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric property analysis in another study conducted by the same research group:

Health Literacy Vaccinale (HLV), adapted by Biasio et al. [17] to assess vaccine health literacy.Vaccine Trust Gauge (VTG), developed by Masoud et al. [18], to measure vaccine trust.Short Version of the Vaccine Acceptance Instrument (VAI-SV), the short version of the vaccine acceptance instrument adapted by Sarathchandra et al. [19].

Additionally, the Questionnaire on Sociodemographic, Economic, and Health Aspects, specifically developed for this study by the authors, will also be used.

Pre-intervention qualitative phase

Participants for the qualitative phase will be selected from those who took part in the pre-intervention quantitative phase. Initially, 15 adolescents from each capital will be recruited, distributed according to the levels of literacy, trust, and acceptance of vaccines identified. Semi-structured interviews will be conducted to explore in-depth adolescents’ perceptions of vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance, as well as the factors influencing these perceptions.

Construction, validation, and application of the CET

The CET will be developed based on the data collected in the quantitative and qualitative phases preceding the intervention. After their construction, the CET will undergo a validation process in the municipality of Sinop, before being implemented in the participating capitals. This validation will be conducted by specialists in adolescent health and public health, involving a detailed critical analysis. The process will include careful reviews of the methodologies used, ensuring that the CET are based on solid evidence and can be effectively applied in practical contexts.

The CET implementation will target adolescents with varying levels of literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance, as identified in the pre-intervention phase. Designed as a “rising tide,” it aims to benefit adolescents across all levels of literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance. By fostering positive attitudes and providing tailored content, the CET seeks to improve literacy, trust, and vaccine acceptance, even among those with higher baseline levels, creating a cascading effect throughout the target group. The intervention will be applied to groups of adolescents in five selected capitals and standardized through a specific orientation guide, ensuring consistency across regions.

The sample size for the intervention phase will be determined based on the expected effect size of the intervention. A minimum sample will be calculated to detect a significant increase in vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels, ensuring adequate statistical power. The calculation will consider a significance level of 5% (α = 0.05) and a power of 80% (1-β = 0.80), with adjustments for potential participant loss. Post-intervention quantitative phase

The same adolescents who participated in the CET implementation will be included in the post-intervention phase. After the intervention, the HLV, VTG, and VAI-SV instruments will be re-administered to assess the impact of the intervention on vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels. Data collection will take place at two time points: immediately after the intervention and 30 days later.

Post-intervention qualitative phase

Participant selection will be based on the changes observed in vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels in the post-intervention quantitative phase. Adolescents who showed improvement, stability, or a reduction in these levels will be included. The selection will be intentional, aiming to obtain a representative sample of the different experiences and perceptions of adolescents regarding the intervention.

New qualitative interviews will be conducted to explore adolescents’ perceptions of the CET and the impacts of the educational intervention. The interviews will be transcribed. These interviews will aim to understand the changes in vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels, as well as assess adolescents’ perceptions of the efficacy and applicability of the CET.

Data analysis

Quantitative analysis

The quantitative analysis for this study will be conducted through tables and graphs presenting descriptive measures such as mean, standard deviation, and proportions, along with visualizations such as boxplots and histograms to examine the distribution of variables of interest. The relationship between vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance levels will be explored using Pearson or Spearman correlations, with 95% confidence intervals calculated using the BCa bootstrap method. The comparison of these indicators across the different participating centers will be performed using a generalized linear model, with the instrument scores as response variables.

The instruments used in this study, particularly the VTG, HLV, and VAI-SV, include overlapping variables that may provide predictive insights into adolescent outcomes. These overlapping dimensions, such as vaccine literacy, trust and acceptance, will be analyzed to identify correlations and redundancies, enhancing the interpretation of results and the design of future interventions.

To assess the impact of the educational intervention, a generalized linear mixed-effects model will be used, accounting for instrument scores over time (pre-intervention, immediate post-intervention, and delayed post-intervention) and the participants’ sociodemographic, economic, and health characteristics. This model will include a random intercept for the adolescents and will use F-tests and t-tests with Bonferroni correction to evaluate the significance of the intervention and perform multiple comparisons. This approach will enable a robust and detailed analysis, considering the hierarchical structure of the data (adolescents grouped by participating centers). All analyses will be performed using the R software.

Qualitative analysis

The qualitative data will be analyzed using Codebook Thematic Analysis, a structured approach that facilitates integration with the quantitative data collected in this mixed-methods study. A preliminary codebook will be developed based on the study’s objectives and the dimensions measured by the HLV, VTG, and VAI-SV instruments [20].

The analysis will follow six steps, namely: (1) Familiarization with the data; (2) Generating initial codes; (3) Searching for themes; (4) Reviewing themes; (5) Defining and naming themes; and (6) Producing the report [21]. The codebook will be iteratively revised to ensure it reflects both pre-defined aspects and emerging insights. Two researchers will independently analyze the data, using triangulation to ensure the validity and reliability of the findings, while maintaining the flexibility required to capture the complexity of the data.

Data integration

The integration of quantitative and qualitative data will occur in three main stages. In the first stage, quantitative data from the pre- and post-intervention phases will guide the development of corresponding qualitative interviews, allowing for a deeper exploration of the results identified in the quantitative analyses. In the second stage, the combined quantitative and qualitative data from the pre-intervention phase will inform the creation of CET, ensuring they are evidence-based and aligned with identified needs. In the third stage, qualitative themes will be integrated with quantitative findings using the joint display technique, as described by Guetterman, Fàbregues, and Sakakibara [22]. This structured approach will facilitate triangulation of the results and the generation of robust meta-inferences about vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents. The mixed methods research report will follow the recommendations of the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool [23].

All content generated by the model was carefully reviewed, edited, and validated by the authors to ensure accuracy, clarity, and compliance with academic standards. Ethical considerations

This study will strictly adhere to the ethical principles established for research involving human subjects in Brazil and has obtained the Certificate of Ethical Appreciation (CAAE) No. 82788524.6.1001.8124. All participants, or their legal guardians, will be informed about the objectives, procedures, and potential risks of the study, providing written informed consent prior to their participation. Confidentiality and anonymity of the participants will be ensured, with data securely stored and accessible only to authorized research team members.

Participants will have the freedom to withdraw from the study at any time without any penalty. The results will be presented in aggregate form, without individual identification of participants, ensuring respect for their privacy and dignity. These measures will ensure ethical integrity and respect for participants’ rights throughout the entire research process.

Discussion

Given the study’s objective, its importance lies in the fact that vaccine hesitancy among adolescents can directly impact public health, especially in critical moments when vaccination campaigns are essential for controlling disease outbreaks [7]. The lack of vaccine literacy and trust among adolescents represents a significant challenge for acceptance, and understanding the factors influencing their decisions can be crucial for developing more effective public policies.

It is expected that the results of this study will increase vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents following the educational intervention, in addition to providing a deeper understanding of the sociodemographic, economic, and health factors influencing these aspects. The validation and development of CET aim to provide an effective tool for promoting vaccine literacy, applicable in different contexts and regions. The documentation of changes in adolescents’ perceptions and attitudes toward vaccines, based on an integrated analysis of quantitative and qualitative data, will provide a solid foundation for future interventions and public policies focused on improving vaccine acceptance.

Thus, this study has the potential to contribute significantly to public health and the improvement of vaccination policies by providing insights into how to enhance vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents. Identifying effective educational interventions can guide more impactful practices, promoting greater vaccine adherence and consequently improving public health.

Regarding practical implications, it is anticipated that the CET, developed and validated throughout the study, will play a crucial role in increasing vaccine literacy, trust, and acceptance among adolescents. These technologies, integrated into school and community health programs, have the potential to offer a practical approach for disseminating evidence-based information. The implementation of these CET is expected to not only increase the knowledge of participating adolescents but also create a cascading effect, engaging the community and families in the educational process due to the adolescents’ multiplier effect. Additionally, comparing literacy, trust, and acceptance levels across different participating centers will allow for identifying regional patterns and variabilities, guiding tailored strategies for future interventions. However, to effectively stimulate behavioral change, these technologies must be structured clearly and objectively, and aligned with the needs of the target audience [23].

Throughout the research, efforts will be made to empower adolescents as active agents in health self-care, raising their awareness of the importance of immunization. In this context, it is crucial to develop technologies that are easy to apply and cost-effective, capable of reaching the expected number of adolescents. These tools should establish themselves as an effective means of promoting knowledge and best vaccination practices, both for adolescents and, indirectly, for their families [24].

Furthermore, the application of the results could support new mass dissemination strategies of scientific information, from government media to social networks, which are widely used by adolescents. This approach could be one of the most effective ways to combat vaccine misinformation [25].

Nevertheless, some obstacles are expected during the study’s development, such as vaccine hesitancy in certain regions and the resistance of some guardians to authorize adolescent participation, both during recruitment and follow-up. To mitigate these risks, additional schools will be randomly selected if the number of participants is insufficient. Identifying these barriers will provide valuable insights into the cultural and social influences on the vaccine debate.

A previous study showed that parents exert significant influence on family decisions, while adolescent involvement in this process is limited. In general, adolescents spend less time reflecting on vaccination decisions compared to their parents [26]. In this study, the predictive factors for vaccination were: trust in the vaccine, having people close to them who support vaccination, and a clear perception of the benefits of vaccines [26].

On the other hand, the involvement of healthcare professionals, schools, and communities, alongside CET, could be an important facilitator for the successful implementation of the study. Studies indicate that, to strengthen vaccination programs and improve acceptance, strategies must include adequate information for adolescents. These actions need to actively engage the community, schools, and healthcare units [27].

As for the study’s limitations, the potential for sampling bias is considered in regions with high vaccine hesitancy, where guardians may be less likely to allow adolescent participation. Additionally, the use of self-reports by adolescents may introduce social desirability bias. To mitigate these risks, the study was structured to include additional schools in case of low initial adherence, ensure anonymity, administer the instruments in an environment that guarantees privacy, reducing social pressure on participants, and utilize the triangulation of quantitative and qualitative data, ensuring robust analysis. The integration of mixed-methods data will provide a more comprehensive view of adolescent perceptions, minimizing possible methodological limitations.

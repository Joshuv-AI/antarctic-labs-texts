The functional role of exosomal miRNA-675-3p derived from patients with hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Chi Liang, Xiang Ji, Shan Liu, Liancheng Xu, Yicheng Xiong, Guanyi Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common solid tumors, resulting in poor survival and high mortality worldwide. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) contained in serum exosomes are being increasing used as targets for disease diagnosis and treatment. However, the role and the diagnostic potential of exosome-transported miRNAs in HCC remain largely underexplored. Therefore, this study aims to identify differentially expressed exosomal miRNAs in the serum of HCC patients and healthy individuals, clarify the biological function of the key miRNAs in HCC progression, and explore its potential as a diagnostic and therapeutic biomarker for HCC.MethodsIn this study, the serum-derived exosomes of patients with HCC and healthy individuals were characterized via transmission electron microscopy (TEM), Western blot assay, and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA). The serum-derived exosomes were labeled with PKH-67, and the transport to recipient HCC cells was observed with confocal microscopy. Differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRs) encapsulated in serum-derived exosomes were screened via miRNA sequencing, the expression of which was verified through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). The association between miR-675-3p and clinicopathological features was investigated via the chi-squared test. The gain and loss of function of miR-675-3p in HCC cells were examined through transfection with mimics and inhibitor. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8), colony formation, 5-ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EdU) staining, and Transwell assays were conducted to test the biological effects of overexpressed and inhibited miR-675-3p on HCC cells in vitro. Tumor xenograft models and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed to assess the malignant progression of HCC cells with downregulated miR-675-3p in vivo.ResultsUptake of exosomes from patients with HCC promoted the malignant phenotype of recipient cells. miRNA-675-3p within exosomes was confirmed to be upregulated in the serum of patients with HCC as compared with that of healthy donors. The chi-squared test results showed that miRNA-675-3p was associated with poor survival. Overexpression of miRNA-675-3p promoted the proliferation, invasion, and colony formation of HCC cells in vitro, while downregulation of miRNA-675-3p had the opposite biological effects on HCC cells and inhibited tumor growth and malignant progression in vivo. Furthermore, bioinformatics analysis indicated that miRNA-675-3p was involved in certain cellular processes, especially in lipid metabolism and regulation of several signaling pathways such as PI3K/AKT pathways and PPAR pathways.ConclusionsmiRNA-675-3p was upregulated in the serum of patients with HCC and exerted an oncogenic role in HCC cells via the targeting of downstream genes. These findings may constitute novel insights into the development of HCC, with miRNA-675-3p potentially serving as a biomarker for the diagnosis and treatment of patients with HCC.Keywords: Exosome, microRNA (miRNA), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), biomarker, oncogene

Highlight box

Key findings

• Exosomal miR-675-3p is significantly upregulated in the serum of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), correlating with advanced tumor stage, poor differentiation, and reduced survival. Functional studies revealed that miR-675-3p promotes HCC cell proliferation, invasion, and colony formation in vitro, while its inhibition suppresses tumor growth in vivo. Bioinformatics linked miR-675-3p to lipid metabolism and oncogenic pathways (PI3K/AKT and PPAR), indicating its role in metabolic reprogramming.

What is known and what is new?

• Exosomal microRNAs (miRNAs) drive cancer progression and serve as biomarkers. Prior studies have suggested that miRNAs such as miR-1247-3p are involved in HCC metastasis and PI3K/AKT pathway dysregulation.

• In this study, through the analysis of clinical cohorts (199 HCC vs. 99 controls) and functional assays, miR-675-3p was established as a novel oncogenic driver in HCC. This is the first study to link miR-675-3p with lipid metabolism, offering insights into HCC metabolic rewiring.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• miR-675-3p is a potential noninvasive biomarker and therapeutic target. Clinically, integrating its detection could improve early diagnosis and prognosis. Future steps should include validating findings in larger cohorts, targeting downstream pathways, and developing miRNA-based therapies. Translating these insights requires collaborative efforts so that diagnostic tools and combination therapies can be optimized.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the third deadliest cancer in the world (1). HCC treatment strategies include surgical operation, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, ablation therapy, and traditional Chinese medicine. Despite the substantial progress that has been made in HCC prognosis and treatment in recent years, the overall 5-year survival rate of patients with HCC is only approximately 18%, which is due to the advanced stage disease at initial treatment and frequent relapse (2,3). Therefore, clarifying the cellular and molecular mechanisms that regulate occurrence and development of HCC and identifying novel clinical markers for HCC diagnosis are critical to improving patient treatment and survival.

Exosomes are membrane-bound vesicles that range in diameter from 30 to 150 nm and that have a special lipid bilayer structure which protects the elements within from nucleases and proteases (4). These exosomes are released into the extracellular environment and can be detected in various body fluid such as plasma, urine, cerebrospinal fluid, serum, and bile (5-8). Recipient cells identify and assimilate the exosomes and absorb them, which exert an influence on the physiology- and pathology-related cell functions of recipients (9).

Thus, exosomes participate in intercellular communication and the function of certain diseases, including cancers, leukemia, and myocardial infarction (10-13). Studies have demonstrated that exosomes are involved in tumor proliferation, autophagy, angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), ferroptosis, immune evasion, chemotherapeutic drug resistance, and metastasis (14-16). Exosomes comprise a number of bioactive substances, such as DNA, proteins, and noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs). Among the ncRNAs are included circular RNA (circRNA), microRNA (miRNA), long noncoding RNA (lncRNA), and messenger RNA (mRNA) (9). The accumulation of numerous genetic and epigenetic aberrations containing dysregulated mRNAs and ncRNAs eventually lead to tumorigenesis or other malignant behaviors of HCC (17).

miRNAs, a group of short ncRNAs, negatively regulate mRNA expression posttranscriptionally through binding to their 3′-untranslated region (3′UTR) and that of certain genes (18). Dysregulated miRNA expression has been demonstrated to play an important role in various malignant tumors, functioning as oncogenes or suppressors. miR-1470 is abundant in the serum of patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) and may function as a prognostic marker. Gain-of-function experiments have indicated that miR-1470 promotes the proliferation and metastasis of CRC cells, indicating that exosomal miR-1470 may regulate the invasion and progression of CRC (19). Extracellular vesicle (EV)-carried miRNAs are emerging as valuable tools in HCC management. Their key advantages include remarkable stability in bodily fluids and inherent targeting capacity to specific cells. These properties make them promising for enhancing HCC diagnosis, monitoring disease progression, and optimizing therapeutic strategies (17,19). One study reported that HCC-derived exosomal miR-1247-3p plays a vital role during the transition of fibroblasts to cancer-associated fibroblasts by regulating the B4GALT3 and NF-κB signaling pathway in the lung premetastatic niche of liver cancer. Furthermore, upregulated serum exosomal miR-1247-3p may be positively associated with lung metastasis in patients with HCC (20). Exosomal miR-335-5p derived from metastatic CRC cells was found to promote the invasion and metastasis of CRC cells by promoting EMT via the targeting of RASA1, suggesting it as a therapeutic target for CRC metastasis (21). However, few studies have examined the role of miR675-3p in patient-derived exosomes in modulating HCC. Exosomal miRNA is not only a promising noninvasive biomarker for HCC emergence and metastasis but is also a target for HCC anticancer cellular immunotherapy (22,23). This study aimed to identify the serum exosomal miRNA that could aid in the diagnosis and treatment of HCC. We found that exosomal miR-675-3p could promote the proliferation, invasion, and metastasis of HCC, and thus the detection of this miRNA may serve as diagnostic tool or a target for therapeutic interventions in HCC. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR and ARRIVE reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1372/rc).

Methods

Clinical specimens

Serum samples were obtained from 202 patients with HCC and 102 healthy individuals treated at Nantong University Affiliated Hospital. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments, and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Nantong University (No. 2022-L062). All patients agreed to the use of their samples in scientific research and provided written informed consent.

Cell culture

Human immortalized hepatocytes (THLE-2) were purchased from Zhejiang Meisen Cell Technology Co., Ltd (Zhejiang, China). HCC cell lines containing Huh7 and HCCLM3 were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences’ Shanghai Institute of Biological Sciences (Shanghai, China) and Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Huh7 and HCCLM3 cells were cultured with Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-neomycin (PSN; Gibco). The THLE-2 cells were cultured with BEGM BulletKit (Lonza, Basel, Switzerland). All cells were incubated at 37 ℃ in a humidified 5% CO2 environment.

Exosome isolation

The collected blood samples were processed by gradient centrifugation at 500 ×g for 5 min, 1,000 ×g for 10 min, and 10,000 ×g for 10 min, which was followed by filtration. The supernatant was then ultracentrifuged at 100,000 ×g for 2 h at 4 ℃ to obtain exosomes. The exosomes were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and stored at 4 ℃ for subsequent experiments. The exosomes were examined via transmission electron microscopy (TEM) to determine the typical morphology, Western blotting assay was applied to identify markers, and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) was used to measure diameters.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA in cell samples was isolated with the RNA-easy Isolation Reagent (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Serum total RNA was extracted with the Total RNA Pure and Isolation Kit with rotating column (BioTeke, Beijing, China). Reverse transcription of miRNAs was completed with a miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (by stem-loop) (Vazyme), while quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed with SYBR Green ChamQ Universal SYBR qRT-PCR Master Mix (Vazyme). Results were analyzed via a 2−ΔΔCt methodology. Each biological reaction was repeated three times. The expression of miRNAs were compared to U6. Primers were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China).

Cell transfection

The miR-675-3p mimics/inhibitor and corresponding negative control (NC) mimics/inhibitor were synthesized by RiboBio. Transfection was performed with the riboFECT CP Transfection Kit (RiboBio) in six-well plates in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. The transfection concentration of the miR-675-3p mimics and inhibitors was 50 nM and 100 nM respectively. After transfection for 48 h, the cells were collected for subsequent function and mechanism assays.

Cell Counting Kit-8 assay (CCK8)

CCK8 assay was performed to measure the HCC cell proliferation. Cells were seeded on 96-well plates at a density of 2×103 cells/well and cultured for 24 h. The cells underwent coculture with exosomes or transfection with mimics/inhibitor before detection. Next, the cells in each well were incubated with conditional culture medium supplemented with CCK-8 Cell Proliferation and Cytotoxicity Assay Kit (SolarBio, Beijing, China) for 2 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured with a TECAN Spark microplate reader (Tecan, Männedorf, Switzerland).

Colony formation assay

HCC cells were digested into single cells with trypsin (Gibco) at confluence of 90%. Subsequently, 1×103 cells were seeded in a six-well plate to form colonies. After 14 days, the plates were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution and then stained with crystal violet solution (SolarBio). The analysis for cell clones was performed via ImageJ software v.1.47 (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

EdU staining assay

HCC cell proliferation was investigated using BeyoClickTM EdU Cell Proliferation Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, HCC cells were incubated with conditional medium supplemented with EdU (5-ethynyl 2’-deoxyuridine) working solution (10 µm) for 2 h at 37 ℃. Next, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.5% TritonX-100 (SolarBio). Their nuclei were then stained with DAPI (SolarBio). The images were captured with a Nikon Ti2-E microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Transwell invasion assay

The invasion ability of HCC cells was measured in Transwell chambers with a porous polycarbonate filter. HCC cells at nearly 90% confluence were digested into single cells. Approximately, 2×104 cells in 200 µL of serum-free medium were cultured in the upper chamber coated with Matrigel (Becton Dickinson Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), while 600 µL of medium (10% FBS) was placed in the lower chamber. After 2 days of culture, cells in the upper chamber were discarded and cells on the lower surface of the membrane were fixed and then stained with 0.2% crystal violet. The number of migrated cells was determined with ImageJ v. 1.47 software.

Western blotting

RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) supplemented with 1% protease inhibitor cocktail (Epizyme, Cambridge, MA, USA) or with 1% protease inhibitor cocktail and 1% phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Epizyme) was used to extract protein from HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells or exosomes, respectively. The bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method based on Omni-Eas Ready-to-use BCA Protein Assay Kit (Epizyme) was conducted to measure the concentration of protein. Protein samples were separated, resolved via sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, and then transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes, which were blocked with 5% nonfat milk or bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 2 h. Next, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 ℃ overnight and then the corresponding secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Protein bands on membranes were detected with the Omni-ECL Enhanced Pico Light Chemiluminescence Kit (Epizyme) and the Tanon 5200 Multi system (Tanon, Shanghai, China). The primary antibodies included anti-CD9 (Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA), anti-CD63 (Proteintech), and anti-TSG101 (Proteintech, China); meanwhile, the secondary antibodies included goat anti-rabbit IgG (H+L) (Jackson ImmunoResearch, Philadelphia, PA, USA).

Exosome uptake analysis with PKH67 labelling and immunofluorescence

Freshly isolated exosomes were labelled using the PKH67 Green Fluorescent Cell Linker Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Next, labeled cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with PBS with 0.3% TritonX-100 (SolarBio) and then blocked with 5% BSA Blocking Buffer (SolarBio). Phalloidin (SolarBio) and DAPI (SolarBio) were used to stain F-actin and the nucleus of HCC cells, respectively. Images were obtained using a SP8 confocal microscope system (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

miRNA sequencing of exosomes

RNAs were extracted from the freshly isolated exosomes of healthy individuals and patients with HCC via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The miRNA sequencing of exosomes was performed by Gene Denovo Biotechnology (Guangzhou, China). Differentially expressed miRNAs with log fold change (FC) >2 and P values <0.05 were screened. Following this, Gene Oncology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis, conducted through Omicsmart (www.omicshare.com/tools/), were employed to analyze the differentially expressed miRNAs and potential target genes. The data of the miRNA sequencing are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Establishment of tumor xenograft models

Experiments involving animals in this study were carried out in accordance with the Guide to Nursing and Use of Experimental Animals and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Nantong University (No. S20220228-004). A protocol was prepared before the study without registration.

Male nude mice, aged 6 weeks and weighing 16–20 g, were purchased from GemPharmatech (Nanjing, China) and subsequently maintained in the animal facility of Nantong University. Mice were randomly assigned to various groups and underwent subcutaneous injection under the axillary skin with a total of 1×107 Huh7 cells transfected with miR-675-3p inhibitor or inhibitor control. Three weeks later, the mice were killed via cervical dislocation under anesthesia (1% isoflurane inhalation), and the tumors were then harvested for further analysis. The maximum length (L), minimum length (W), and weight of tumors were measured. Tumor volume was calculated as follows: volume = 1/2 × L × W2.

Immunohistochemistry

The samples were fixed in formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 4- to 5-µm slices. Slides are deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was then performed via heat-induced epitope retrieval (HIER) in citrate buffer (pH 6.0), with samples subsequently being cooled to room temperature. To minimize nonspecific signals, endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% H2O2, and nonspecific binding was blocked with 5% goat serum. The primary antibody, diluted in an appropriate buffer, was applied, and incubation was conducted overnight at 4 ℃ and followed by incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody for 1 hour at room temperature. Detection was carried out using a DAB substrate, which produced a brown precipitate upon reaction, and nuclei were visualized via hematoxylin counterstaining. Finally, slides were dehydrated, cleared in xylene, and mounted with a resinous medium for bright-field microscopic analysis with a Ti2-E microscope (Nikon). The primary antibodies included Ki-67 (Proteintech), PCNA (Proteintech), and Bcl2 (Proteintech); meanwhile, the secondary antibodies included HRP-goat secondary antibody (Proteintech).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS software version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (Dotmatics, Boston, USA). The Student t-test was used to analyze the statistical significance of the differences between two experimental groups. The Pearson χ2 test was used to evaluate the correlation between exosomal miR-675-3p and the clinical and pathological parameters of patient with HCC. Quantitative data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Kaplan-Meier curves were drawn, and the log-rank test was used to analyze the survival data. All experiments were repeated at least three times. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The characterization of serum exosomes from healthy individuals and patients with HCC

In this study, exosomes were isolated from the serum of healthy individuals and patients with HCC based on gradient centrifugation. NTA analysis revealed that the diameter of the extracted structures from the two groups predominantly ranged from 50 to 120 nm, consistent with the classical size range related to exosomes (Figure 1A). Results of TEM analysis also confirmed that the serum exosomes from healthy individuals and patients with HCC had a typical cup-shaped morphology (Figure 1B). According to Western blotting, the CD9, CD63, and TSG101 proteins were all present in the serum exosome protein samples, while typical exosomal proteins were not detected in the cell protein samples (THLE-2 and Huh7) (Figure 1C). To determine the effects of incubation with serum-derived exosomes from patients with HCC on cells, malignant phenotypes of two HCC cell lines (HCCLM3 and Huh7) were evaluated after incubation the exosomes with cells. First, both HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells were incubated with PKH67-labeled serum exosomes isolated from healthy individuals and patients with HCC, and then immunofluorescence (IF) imaging was applied to assess the uptake of exosomes in HCC cells (Figure 1D). Subsequently, results of CCK-8 and Transwell invasion assays showed that both HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells with HCC patient-derived exosomes had significantly increased proliferative activities and invasion capabilities than did the cells with exosomes from healthy individuals (Figure 1E,1F).

Overall, the presence and absence of typical protein, a cup-shape structure, and the particle size indicated that the exosomes were isolated successfully from the serum of both healthy individuals and patients with HCC. Moreover, the data also confirmed that HCC patient-derived exosomes could promote the malignant function of tumor cells.

Differentially expressed miRNAs present in the serum exosomes of patients with HCC

To determine the expression pattern of miRNAs in the serum exosomes of patients with HCC, we performed miRNA-sequencing analysis on the extracted RNA from isolated exosomes in the serum of healthy individuals and patients with HCC. In total, 59 differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRs) between the two groups of exosomes were selected on the basis of the screening criteria of a P value <0.05) and logFC >2. Heatmap and volcano plots were employed to visualize the DEmiRs (Figure 2A,2B). According to P value, miRNA-675-3p was selected further validation and analyses. The miR-675-3p in the exosomes was detected via qRT-PCR, which revealed that amplification of miR-675-3p was successful and verified the miRNA-sequencing analysis (Figure 2C and Figure S1A,S1B). RNA was extracted from both exosomes and exosome-depleted culture medium, and the expression levels of miR-675-3p in the two were compared. The results demonstrated that miR-675-3p was predominantly localized in extracellular vesicles, whereas its expression level was significantly lower in exosome-depleted culture medium, suggesting that miR-675-3p is mainly enriched in exosomes (Figure S1C,S1D). The expression of exosomal miR-675-3p in the serum of 199 patients with HCC was compared with that of the exosomal miRNA in the serum of 99 healthy individuals, and the results indicated that miR-675-3p was highly expressed in patients with HCC (Figure 2D). To assess clinical applicability, we ensured the stability and integrity of miR-675-3p. On a melting curve graph, amplification of miR-675-3p yielded a clearly single peak that was highly similar to that of U6, indicating that qRT-PCR detection for this miRNA was specific (Figure S1A,S1B). Moreover, the complement DNA (cDNA) of miR-675-3p was diluted proportionally, and a linear relationship was confirmed via qRT-PCR. The R2 value (U6: 0.9980; miR-675-3p: 0.9983) and regression equation (U6: y=–2.007x+8.037; miR-675-3p: y=–1.934x+10.42) of the U6 and miR-675-3p standard curve indicated that qRT-PCR could detect the serum exosomal miR-675-3p at different concentrations (Figure S1E,S1F). Furthermore, the results of repeated freeze–thaw and incubation at room temperature demonstrated that miR-675-3p had good stability (Figure S1G,S1H). The 120 patients with HCC and a histological prognosis were divided into two groups on the basis of exosomal miR-675-3p expression level (high-expression group: n=60; low-expression group: n=60). The clinical and pathological parameters associated with the expression level of exosomal miR-675-3p were determined via the χ2 test, which indicated that the expression levels of miR-675-3p in the serum exosomes of patients with HCC were significantly correlated with histological differentiation (P=0.010), TNM stage (P<0.001), an Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage (P=0.014). Meanwhile, no significant differences were observed concerning serum AFP levels (P=0.132, Table 1). Additionally, Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that high exosomal miR-675-3p expression was related to shorter overall survival (Figure 2E). These analyses suggested that the qRT-PCR detection of exosomal miR-675-3p expression level in the serum of patients with HCC may have clinical application prospects. The expression of exosomal miR-675-3p was associated with HCC tumorigenesis and poor 5-year survival. In short, we found that miRNA miR-675-3p expression was significantly upregulated in the serum exosomes of patients with HCC and may thus serve as diagnostic and survival biomarker for these patients.

Oncogenic role of miR-675-3p in HCC

To investigate the role of miR-675-3p in HCC, mimics and inhibitor of miR-675-3p were used to upregulate and inhibit its expression level, respectively. Results from qRT-PCR included a marked increase and decrease of miR-675-3p after the transfection of mimics and inhibitor, respectively, to HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells (Figure 3A,3B). Subsequently, CCK8, colony formation, Transwell, and EdU staining assays were conducted to examine effects of miR-675-3p overexpression and inhibition on HCC proliferation and invasion. The CCK8 and colony formation assays revealed that the overexpression of miR-675-3p significantly promoted HCCLM3 and Huh7 cell proliferation as compared the NC mimic condition; meanwhile, miR-675-3p knockdown led to the opposite effects, with an attenuation of proliferation (Figure 3C,3D). Colony formation and EdU staining assays further demonstrated that the upregulation of miR-675-3p increased the proportion of EdU-positive cells. Conversely, there was an obvious reduction of cell growth and EdU-positive proportion in miR-675-3p-knockdown cells, indicating an inhibitory effect of miR-675-3p on colony formation and proliferation in a single cell state (Figure 3). Furthermore, Transwell assays were performed to examine the potential function of miR-675-3p in HCC cell invasion. Invasion in both HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells with upregulated miR-675-3p was strengthened as compared to the negative control condition. Whereas, knockdown of miR-675-3p significantly diminished the invasion abilities of HCC cells (Figure 3G,3H).

Collectively, these results indicated that miR-675-3p facilitates the proliferation and invasion abilities of HCC cells and may function as an oncogene in the carcinogenesis of HCC.

Downregulation of miR-675-3p inhibited subcutaneous tumorigenesis in vivo

To determine the function of miR-675-3p in vivo, xenograft models with implantation of Huh7 cells were established (inhibitor NC or inhibitor miR-675-3p). After implantation of Huh7 cells, mice with mock and inhibitor NC Huh7 cells showed the first sign of subcutaneous tumorigenesis at 7 days and a linear enlargement in tumor volume during later observation. In contrast, tumor in mice with inhibitor miR-675-3p HCC cells was not observed until 12 days after transplantation (Figure 4A,4B). Moreover, tumor volume and weight were both reduced in the downregulated miR-675-3p group as compared to those in the control group (Figure 4C,4D). Immunohistochemical (IHC) results indicated that the expression level of Ki-67 and PCNA protein, both markers of proliferation, and Bcl2, a marker of antiapoptosis, was significantly lower in the miR-675-3p-downregulated group than in the control group (Figure 4E).

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of miR-675-3p target genes

To clarify the molecular mechanisms underlying the effect of miR-675-3p on the proliferation and invasion of HCC cells, TargetScan, miRanDa, miRDB, and miRPathDB were used to predict the potential target genes of miR-675-3p. The analysis for downstream targets of miR-675-3p involved overlapping the results from the four databases, which revealed that the 3′-UTR sequences of 14 mRNAs were highly base-paired with miR-675-3p (Figure 5A). Additionally, GO enrichment analysis was performed of these 14 common mRNAs, and these common target genes could be categorized into 3 ontologies, including molecular function, cellular component, and biological process (Figure 5B). KEGG pathway analysis was performed to further characterize the signaling pathways that the miR-675-3p-targeted mRNAs were involved in. KEGG analysis indicated that the mRNAs that indirectly interacted with miR-675-3p were significantly enriched in choline metabolism, fatty-acid metabolism, steroid hormone biosynthesis, PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, PPAR signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway, among others (Figure 5C). Taken together, these results suggest that exosomal miR-675-3p may play a pivotal role in the occurrence and progression of HCC by regulating downstream target genes and signaling pathways.

Discussion

Exosomes function as an intermediate in in cell-cell communication by transporting their bioactive components between different cells (24). There are a large number of miRNAs in exosomes (25). Thousands of miRNAs have been identified in extracellular vesicles derived from human body fluids, such as blood serum, bile, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid (26). The vesicular membrane protects EV-miRNAs from degradation, ensuring consistent detectability in clinical samples and overcoming the insensitivity of traditional markers like AFP for early HCC. Their natural biocompatibility allows safe delivery of functional miRNAs to HCC cells via cell-specific targeting, effectively inhibiting tumor growth. This targeted approach reduces off-target effects, boosting the efficiency of miRNA-based therapies compared to free miRNA use. Moreover, EVs can work with gene editing tools, broadening their role in personalized HCC treatment (9). Shu et al. discovered a type of self-driven cancer cell progression that relies on bile exosomal miR-182/183-5p secreted by cholangiocarcinoma cells, which and promotes stemness and angiogenesis by activating PTGER1 and increasing VEGF-A release (8). Meanwhile, Qiu et al. found that miR-519a-3p in patients with liver metastasis of gastric cancer (GC) was significantly higher than that in patients without metastasis. GC cell-derived exosomes induce angiogenesis and facilitate intrahepatic premetastatic niche formation by delivering exo-miR-519a-3p into intrahepatic macrophages and then activating the DUSP2/MAPK/ERK pathway, thereby causing M2-like polarization of macrophages (26). In our study, we discovered an upregulated miRNA (miR-675-3p) in the serum of patients with HCC by sequencing and subsequent experimental verification. Exosomal miRNA sequencing was used to select the DEmiRs in serum-derived exosomes from patients with HCC via comparison with healthy individuals. There were 40 upregulated miRNAs in exosomes from patients with HCC, indicating a significantly role in HCC progression. Meanwhile, there were 19 downregulated miRNAs that may function as suppressors for tumor development. The verification experiments indicated that miR-675-3p in exosomes from the serum of 102 patients with HCC was significantly enriched as compared to that from 202 healthy individuals. A few studies have indicated that miR-675-3p could facilitate the malignant biological behaviors, such as invasion, proliferation, and chemotherapy resistance in various cancers (27-30), yet the biological functions of HCC-derived exosomal miR-675-3p remains unclear.

In the analysis of other serum samples from patients with HCC, exosomal miR-675-3p expression was significantly associated with the clinical pathological characteristics of the patients with HCC. HCC Patients with a higher expression level of exosomal miR-675-3p had significantly increased tumor volume and poor survival. Moreover, uptake of high miR-675-3p expression in exosomes increased the proliferation and invasion of HCC cells both in vitro and in vivo. These results indicate that exosomal miR-675-3p may play an important role in the progression of HCC. Subsequently, the biological functions of miR-675-3p were examined. To clarify the impact of miR-675-3p on cell proliferation, CCK8, colony formation, and EdU staining assays were implemented. The functional gain experiments conducted on miR-675-3p in HCC cell lines revealed marked augmentation in cell proliferation upon its overexpression. Conversely, the functional loss experiments revealed that the suppression of miR-675-3p significantly impeded the proliferation of tumor cells. Additionally, overexpression of miR-675-3p also facilitated the migration and invasion abilities of HCC cells. Overall, these results support the notion that miR-675-3p could function as an oncogenic regulator in the development of HCC. Typically, miRNAs exert biological functions by causing target genes to degrade or undergo translational repression (31). Next, several databases were used to the predict potential downstream mRNA targets of miR-675-3p. KEGG and GO enrichment analyses were performed to clarify the molecular mechanisms by which miR-675-3p influences HCC. GO analysis indicated that miR-675-3p may participate in certain critical cellular processes and molecular interactions, such as metabolic process, cellular anatomical entity, binding, and transporter activity. Meanwhile, the KEGG signaling pathway enrichment suggested that miR-675-3p may exert biological functions by regulating PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, fatty-acid metabolism, PPAR signaling pathway, and biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids. It is highly likely that miR-675-3p activates metabolic reprogramming, especially lipid metabolism. Cao et al. reported that knockdown of miR-612 in HCC cells led to the initiation of β-oxidation of fatty acids and the promotion of cholesterol biosynthesis, thus facilitating proliferation and metastasis (32). The PPAR signaling pathway was confirmed to play a key role in the β-oxidation of fatty acids in HCC and to supply cancer cells with energy and biosynthesis products for their proliferation, metastasis, and signal transduction (33). Our study indicated that miR-675-3p may act as a mediator in the β-oxidation and reprogramming of fatty acids, but more studies are needed to elucidate the related molecular mechanism and signaling pathways. In addition, the PI3KA/KT signaling pathway, a well-known pathway for the regulation of proliferation and invasion (34), has been recently reported to promote proliferation via metabolic reprogramming in HCC (35). Further experiments should be conducted to clarify, in detail, the molecular mechanisms by which miR-675-3p and its targets regulate the signaling cascade. Moreover, clinical trials on small-molecular AKT inhibitors such as GDC-0068 and AZD5363 demonstrate good therapeutic effects (35,36). However, whether nucleic acid drugs targeting miR-675-3p enhance the therapeutic effects of small-molecular AKT inhibitors or play a role in metabolic reprogramming needs to be clarified in further vitro and in vivo research.

Overall, our findings shed light on the involvement of exosomal miR-675-3p in HCC and suggest that it may be a key diagnostic and therapeutic target. Additional investigations are needed to characterize the molecular mechanisms by which miR-675-3p facilitates the pathogenesis of HCC.

Conclusions

This study identified an upregulated miRNA (exosomal miR-675-3p) in the serum exosomes of patients with HCC. We observed that exosomal miR-675-3p could be packaged into exosomes, transported to recipient cells, and then promote the malignant progression of recipient cells. Moreover, upregulated exosomal miR-675-3p expression was associated with the poor prognosis of patients with HCC as compared to patients with upregulated exosomal miR-675-3p expression. This study identified an alternative biomolecule for targeted therapy, which may serve as ab avenue for the advancement of noninvasive diagnostic tools in HCC.

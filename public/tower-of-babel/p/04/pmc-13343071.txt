Effects of tegileridine versus sufentanil on postoperative nausea and vomiting in female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy with a multimodal antiemetic approach based in Chengdu, China: protocol for a dual-centre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xi Wang, Yuanhong Li, Jiaqi Yang, Xiaoqiang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) remains one of the most common complications after laparoscopic cholecystectomy, especially in female patients faced with high baseline risk. Although multimodal antiemetic strategies are routinely used in perioperative care, opioid exposure remains an important contributor to PONV. Tegileridine, a novel G protein-biased μ-opioid receptor agonist, may provide effective analgesia with fewer gastrointestinal adverse effects than conventional opioids such as sufentanil. However, evidence on its role in preventing PONV in female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy remains limited.Methods and analysisThis dual-centre, randomised controlled trial will enrol 148 female patients undergoing elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy at two tertiary hospitals in Chengdu, China. Participants will be randomly assigned individually (1:1 ratio) to receive either tegileridine-based or sufentanil-based perioperative analgesia within a standardised multimodal antiemetic framework. Randomisation will be centrally performed using a web-based system with allocation concealment. Participants, postoperative outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded to treatment allocation. The primary outcome will be the overall incidence of PONV within 24 hours after surgery. Secondary outcomes will include the incidence and severity of nausea and vomiting at predefined postoperative time points, pain scores, use of rescue analgesic and antiemetic medication, quality of recovery, opioid-related adverse events and recovery-related time-dependent outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the ethics committees of West China Hospital, Sichuan University and West China Tianfu Hospital, Sichuan University (ethical review no. 1775.2025). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and academic conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500111528.

Introduction

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy is a standardised minimally invasive procedure for the treatment of gallbladder diseases and represents a key surgical modality within the framework of enhanced recovery after surgery programme.1 However, despite its minimal invasive nature of laparoscopic cholecystectomy, the presence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) and postoperative pain remain common and is a clinically relevant postoperative concern.2 Previous studies have demonstrated that female patients are at a significantly higher risk of developing PONV after laparoscopic cholecystectomy than men. Moreover, the occurrence of PONV is often associated with delayed postoperative recovery, reduced patient satisfaction and an increased risk of complications such as dehydration, electrolyte imbalance, wound-related complications and prolonged hospital stay, thereby imposing an additional burden3 on healthcare costs. With this in mind, identifying effective strategies to reduce the incidence of PONV in female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy remains a pressing clinical concern in perioperative management.

The pathophysiology of PONV is complex and involves multiple factors, including patient-related characteristics, surgical-related concerns and the types of anaesthetic medications to be used. Among these, the perioperative use of opioid analgesics is considered one of the key risk factors for the development of PONV.4 Although opioids play an indispensable role in intraoperative and postoperative analgesia, they exert pronounced effects on gastrointestinal motility and central emetic pathways, effects that are particularly evident in female patients.5 Consequently, reducing opioid-related adverse effects while ensuring adequate analgesia has become an important focus of contemporary perioperative anaesthetic management.

At present, the prevention and management of PONV primarily relies on multimodal antiemetic strategies, including the combined use of 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor antagonists, corticosteroids, dopamine receptor antagonists and neurokinin-1 receptor antagonists.6 Although these strategies reduce the incidence of PONV to some extent, they mainly target downstream components of the emetic pathways and do not adequately address opioid-induced PONV at its source.7 Therefore, optimising analgesic regimens themselves by reducing or replacing opioids with a high emetogenic potential is considered a promising approach for further lowering the risk of PONV.

Tegileridine is a novel G protein-biased μ-opioid receptor agonist whose mechanism of action differs from that of conventional opioids.8 Evidence suggests that on activation of the μ-opioid receptor, tegileridine preferentially engages G protein-dependent signalling pathways to produce analgesic effects, while relatively sparing the activation of β-arrestin-mediated pathways, which are closely associated with various opioid-related adverse effects.9 Based on these pharmacological characteristics, tegileridine may preserve effective analgesia while exerting less inhibitory effect on gastrointestinal function, thereby offering a potential advantage in reducing the risk of PONV.10 However, clinical evidence regarding the perioperative use of tegileridine—particularly for the prevention of PONV in female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy—remains limited, and well-designed randomised controlled trials are still lacking.

Based on the above considerations, this study is designed as a dual-centre, randomised controlled trial to evaluate two perioperative analgesic strategies implemented within the same standardised multimodal antiemetic framework in female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Rather than isolating the pharmacological effects of two individual drugs, the trial compares a tegileridine-based analgesic approach with a sufentanil-based analgesic approach comparing PONV and recovery-related outcomes. The primary objective is to determine whether the tegileridine-based strategy reduces the 24-hour incidence of PONV compared with the sufentanil-based strategy. We hypothesise that optimisation of perioperative opioid selection within a multimodal antiemetic framework will reduce PONV while maintaining adequate analgesia and recovery quality.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study is designed as a dual-centre, randomised controlled clinical trial to be conducted at West China Hospital of Sichuan University and West China Tianfu Hospital of Sichuan University. Only those of the female gender scheduled for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy are considered eligible participants for this study. A unified protocol for anaesthetic management, study interventions, outcome assessment and data collection will be implemented at both centres to minimise potential inter-centre variability and associated bias. Participant recruitment started on 1 November 2025 and is scheduled to conclude by 31 December 2027. Final follow-up for the primary endpoint will be completed within 24 hours after surgery. All participants will sign an informed consent form (onlinesupplemental files 1 2).

Participants withdrawn after randomisation will remain in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population whenever outcome data are available; if primary outcome data are missing, they will be handled according to the pre-specified missing-data strategy.

Randomisation and blinding

Randomisation will be performed centrally using a web-based randomisation system. Participants will be allocated individually to the tegileridine group or sufentanil group using stratified block randomisation, with the hospital study centre as the stratification factor and variable block sizes to maintain allocation concealment. The random allocation sequence will be generated in advance by an independent statistician who is not involved in patient recruitment, intervention delivery, outcome assessment or data analysis. The concealed allocation sequence will be generated centrally using a secure web-based randomisation system. After eligibility confirmation, group assignment will be implemented by an independent research assistant using a pre-arranged opaque, sealed, sequentially numbered envelope that is prepared according to the centrally generated randomisation sequence. All participants, postoperative outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded to treatment allocation. Because of the differences in intraoperative study medication administration, those anaesthesiologists responsible for intraoperative management cannot be fully blinded; however, they will not participate in any postoperative follow-up or assessment of outcome.

Interventions

Study interventions

To maintain participant blinding, both groups will receive a pre-induction infusion and an induction-phase opioid/placebo administration.

Tegileridine group

Patients will receive tegileridine 1 mg diluted in 20 mL of normal saline via infusion pump over 10 min before anaesthesia induction. During induction, an equal volume of normal saline will be administered as a placebo in place of sufentanil.

Sufentanil group

Patients will receive 20 mL of normal saline via infusion pump over 10 min before anaesthesia induction as a placebo for tegileridine. During induction, sufentanil 0.3 µg/kg will be administered intravenously as the active comparator.

Anaesthesia and perioperative management

Apart from the study drug/placebo allocation described above, all other anaesthetic and perioperative management procedures will be standardised between groups.

All patients will receive standardised preoperative education within 24 hours before surgery. Patients will undergo fasting and abstain from drinking for 8 hours before surgery. No sedatives, analgesics or antiemetic medications will be administered preoperatively. On arrival in the operating room, routine monitoring will be initiated, including electrocardiography, pulse oxygen saturation, heart rate, noninvasive blood pressure and bispectral index (BIS).

Anaesthesia induction will be performed as follows. In the tegileridine group, after completion of the pre-induction tegileridine infusion, anaesthesia will be induced with remimazolam 0.2 mg/kg, placebo normal saline in place of sufentanil, propofol 2 mg/kg and dexamethasone 5 mg. In the sufentanil group, after completion of the pre-induction placebo saline infusion, anaesthesia will be induced with remimazolam 0.2 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.3 µg/kg, propofol 2 mg/kg and dexamethasone 5 mg. After loss of consciousness, rocuronium 0.7 mg/kg will be administered to facilitate airway establishment.

Anaesthesia will be maintained with inhaled desflurane, with the concentration adjusted to maintain BIS values between 40 and 60. The infusion rate of remifentanil will be adjusted as required within a range of 0.1–0.3 µg/kg min. Intraoperative haemodynamic fluctuations will be maintained within ±20% of baseline blood pressure, with atropine or phenylephrine administered when necessary; 30 min before the end of surgery, flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg and ondansetron 8 mg will be administered intravenously. At the completion of surgery, remifentanil and inhalational anaesthetics will be discontinued, and the surgical incision will be infiltrated locally with 10 mL of 1% ropivacaine. Neuromuscular blockade will be fully reversed using sugammadex, and the airway device will be removed once the patient is fully awake.

Postoperative management and follow-up

After transfer to the post-anaesthesia care unit, patients will be assessed for PONV and pain. If the numeric rating scale pain score exceeds 4, sufentanil 0.1 µg/kg will be administered intravenously as a rescue analgesia strategy, with repeat dosing permitted as necessary and recorded accordingly. In cases of vomiting or severe nausea, droperidol 1 mg will be administered as rescue antiemetic therapy. Postoperative follow-up assessments will be conducted at 6, 12 and 24 hours after surgery by investigators who are blinded to group allocation.

Sample size calculation

This trial is designed as a superiority study. The sample size calculation is based on the primary outcome, namely the overall incidence of PONV within 24 hours after surgery. Previous studies have shown that the 24-hour incidence of PONV after laparoscopic cholecystectomy is approximately 25%, whereas in high-risk female patients undergoing laparoscopic gastrointestinal surgery, the overall incidence of PONV may still reach about 50% despite the presence of double prophylaxis.11 12 Given that the present study will enrol female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy who will still require perioperative opioid analgesia, the baseline risk is expected to be higher than that in the general laparoscopic cholecystectomy population but lower than that in high-risk female patients undergoing laparoscopic gastrointestinal surgery. Based on the above evidence, the characteristics of the target population and our centre’s clinical experience, we assumed a 24-hour PONV incidence of 50% in the control group and 28% in the intervention group. Under a two-sided alpha of 0.05, a power of 80% and a 1:1 allocation ratio, 73 participants are required in each group, yielding a total sample size of 146. As the primary outcome will be assessed during hospitalisation within 24 hours after surgery, missing primary outcome data are expected to be minimal; therefore, no additional inflation for loss to follow-up was applied. Considering balanced group allocation and study feasibility, the final planned sample size is 148 participants, with 74 participants in each group.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be pre-specified before database lock and performed using SPSS V.27.0 and R V.4.3. The primary analysis will follow the ITT principle and include all randomised participants according to their allocated group. A per-protocol (PP) analysis will be conducted as a sensitivity analysis, excluding participants with major protocol deviations.

Baseline characteristics will be summarised descriptively by group. Continuous variables will be presented as mean±SD or median (IQR), as appropriate, and categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages.

The primary outcome is the overall incidence of PONV within 24 hours after surgery. This outcome will be analysed in the ITT population using a generalised linear model with study group and study centre included in the model. Because the primary outcome is binary, the main analysis will use a modified Poisson regression model with robust variance estimation to estimate relative risks and 95% CIs. Risk differences and their 95% CIs will also be reported.

Repeated binary secondary outcomes, including the incidence of nausea and vomiting during different postoperative time intervals, will be analysed using generalised linear mixed-effects models, with study group, time, group-by-time interaction and study centre included as fixed effects, and participant included as a random effect. Repeated continuous outcomes, including postoperative pain scores measured over time, will be analysed using linear mixed-effects models with the same fixed-effects and random-effects structure. Binary secondary outcomes assessed at a single time point will be analysed using generalised linear models adjusted for study centre, whereas continuous secondary outcomes measured once will be analysed using linear regression models adjusted for study centre. Time-to-event outcomes will be described using Kaplan-Meier methods and compared between groups using Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for study centre.

Missing data will be minimised through standardised in-hospital follow-up procedures. Because the primary outcome will be assessed during hospitalisation within 24 hours after surgery, missing primary outcome data are expected to be minimal. Should missing data occur, multiple imputation by chained equations will be performed under the missing-at-random assumption. The imputation model will include treatment group, study centre and relevant baseline variables associated with the outcome or the missingness mechanism. Complete-case analysis and PP analysis will also be performed as sensitivity analyses.

All tests will be two-sided, and p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant for the primary outcome. As this trial has a single primary outcome, secondary outcomes will be analysed as exploratory outcomes without formal adjustment for multiple comparisons, and their results will be interpreted cautiously.

Quality control and data management

To ensure consistency in study implementation and data quality, a unified study protocol, anaesthetic management procedures, outcome assessment criteria and data collection forms will be applied across both participating centres. Before study initiation, all anaesthesiologists, follow-up personnel and research coordinators involved in the trial will receive standardised training to clarify study procedures, group allocation principles, outcome definitions and data recording requirements.

During the study, the principal investigator will regularly review participant enrolment, protocol adherence, follow-up completion and data integrity to promptly identify and correct potential deviations. All study data will be entered and managed by designated research personnel, stored in a de-identified manner and accessible only to authorised members of the research team. After data entry, logical consistency checks will be performed, and source documents will be reviewed when necessary to ensure data accuracy and traceability.

A formal independent Data Monitoring Committee will not be established for this trial because it is a short-term, investigator-initiated perioperative study with a short follow-up period and no planned interim efficacy analysis. Safety oversight will instead be undertaken by the principal investigator, the local research team at each participating centre and the institutional ethics committee. Any serious adverse event will be reported promptly according to local requirements.

Monitoring and management of adverse events

All participants will be continuously monitored for adverse events throughout the study period. Adverse events are defined as any unfavourable clinical manifestations occurring during the study that may be related to the study interventions.

Respiratory depression will be defined as a respiratory rate <8 breaths/min and/or SpO2 <90%, or any episode requiring airway support or naloxone administration.

Hypotension will be defined as systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg, mean arterial pressure <65 mm Hg or a decrease of >20% from baseline requiring vasoactive treatment.

Bradycardia and urinary retention will also be recorded according to pre-specified clinical criteria.

All adverse events will be documented in detail, including the time of onset, severity, duration and management measures.

This study has been approved by the ethics committees of West China Hospital, Sichuan University and West China Tianfu Hospital, Sichuan University (ethical review no. 1775.2025, contact email: 39006900@qq.com). All participants are required to sign a written informed consent form prior to enrolment. The study will provide participants with a detailed explanation of the study objectives, procedures and potential risks, and offer ample opportunity for consultation to ensure informed consent. All data will be anonymised, and personal information will be managed confidentially in strict compliance with data protection regulations.

Study results will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed academic journals and presentations at academic conferences and will be shared with healthcare professionals and policymakers to guide future clinical practice. To ensure data accessibility, this study adopts an open-access publication model, and de-identified data that do not involve patient privacy will be available for sharing. Researchers interested in obtaining the data may contact the corresponding author via email and submit a formal written request. On approval by the ethics committee, the anonymised dataset will be provided.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the development of the research question, study design, recruitment plan, choice of outcome measures, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study.

Discussion

This study proposes a dual-centre, randomised, controlled clinical trial focusing on female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy, a population at high risk for PONV. Within the context of a standardised multimodal antiemetic strategy, the protocol is designed to compare the perioperative effects of two different analgesic regimens. Unlike previous studies that have primarily focused on various combinations of antiemetic agents, the present study places the analgesic strategy itself at the core of the intervention. By approaching PONV prevention from the perspective of comprehensive perioperative management, this design more closely reflects real-world clinical decision-making.

A key feature of this study lies in its comparative framework. Rather than simply comparing the efficacy of different antiemetic or analgesic agents, the study contrasts a biased μ-opioid receptor agonist that may reduce opioid-related adverse effects with a conventional opioid known to be associated with a higher risk of PONV. This design aims to address a critical question in perioperative care: in the era where multimodal antiemetic strategies have become routine practice, can optimisation of analgesic selection itself further reduce the risk of PONV? Such a strategy-level comparison expands the current research paradigm, which has largely emphasised symptomatic antiemetic treatment, and offers a novel framework for integrated perioperative management.

From a methodological perspective, this protocol adopts a dual-centre randomised controlled design, with standardised anaesthetic regimens, outcome assessment criteria and data collection procedures implemented across both centres before study initiation. This approach helps to mitigate systematic bias commonly associated with single-centre studies. The dual-centre design not only enhances the external validity and generalisability of the findings, but also allows for the inclusion of a broader range of clinical practice patterns while maintaining protocol consistency, thereby increasing the practical relevance of the study conclusions. In addition, multicentre implementation is expected to improve patient recruitment efficiency and facilitate timely study completion.

Regarding outcome assessment, this study designates the overall incidence of PONV within 24 hours postoperatively as the primary outcome, while also systematically evaluating multiple secondary outcomes, including the incidence and severity of nausea and vomiting at different postoperative time intervals, postoperative pain control, consumption of rescue analgesic and antiemetic medications and quality of postoperative recovery. This multidimensional outcome framework enables a comprehensive assessment of the clinical value of different analgesic strategies in perioperative management, rather than limiting evaluation to a single adverse event.

This trial is intended to evaluate clinical effectiveness at the analgesic-strategy level within a standardised multimodal antiemetic framework. Therefore, any between-group differences should be interpreted as reflecting the combined perioperative effects of the allocated analgesic strategies rather than the isolated pharmacological effects of tegileridine or sufentanil alone.

Several limitations of this study should also be acknowledged. Although a dual-centre design with standardised protocols is employed, subtle differences between centres—such as anaesthesiologists’ prescribing preferences and surgical team experience—may persist and introduce centre-related effects as potential confounders. These factors should be appropriately considered in the statistical analysis. In addition, because of objective differences in intraoperative medication regimens, complete blinding of anaesthesia providers is not feasible. To mitigate potential assessment bias, outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded to group allocation. Furthermore, pharmacoeconomic evaluation is not included as a primary outcome in this study. Given that tegileridine is a novel agent with higher costs than conventional opioids, future studies incorporating cost-effectiveness analyses are warranted to provide more comprehensive evidence for clinical decision-making and resource allocation.

In summary, this study protocol adopts a rigorous randomised controlled design and focuses on female patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy, a population at high risk for PONV. Within the context of a multimodal antiemetic strategy, the trial is designed to systematically evaluate the perioperative effects of different analgesic approaches. The findings of this study are expected to provide methodological evidence to inform opioid selection and optimise PONV prevention strategies in perioperative care, while also laying a foundation for future-related research.

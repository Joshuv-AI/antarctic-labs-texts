Clinical Outcomes of a Monofocal, Optimized, Aspheric IOL in Eyes with Different Degrees of Corneal Spherical Aberration
Authors: Francisco Pastor-Pascual, Rafael Pastor-Pascual, Pedro Tañá-Sanz, Santiago Tañá-Sanz, Pedro Tañá-Rivero
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the clinical outcomes of a monofocal, optimized, aspheric, intraocular lens (IOL) implanted in eyes with different degrees of corneal spherical aberration (SA) after cataract surgery.Methods54 eyes implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL were analyzed at 3 months post-surgery. Refractive error, corrected-distance visual acuity (CDVA), uncorrected distance visual acuity, contrast sensitivity (photopic and mesopic), photopic and mesopic defocus curve and patient-reported outcome measures with the Catquest-9SF questionnaire were recorded. Eyes were categorized into 3 groups (18 eyes per group) based on their corneal SA (6-mm pupil): group A (0 to 0.2 μm), group B (>0.2 to 0.4 μm), and group C (>0.4 to 0.6 μm).ResultsCDVA was similar between groups, showing mean values of –0.02±0.04, –0.04±0.05, and 0.01±0.11, for groups A, B, and C, respectively (P=0.094). In group A, 88.89% of eyes were within ±1.00D of spherical equivalent (SE) and with a refractive astigmatism of ≤1.00D, in group B, it was 94.4%, and, in group C, 88.89% of eyes were within ±1.00D and 94.4% of eyes ≤1.00D (SE: P=0.431). Contrast sensitivity functions were good and similar for all spatial frequencies (P>0.01). The area under the 7‑point defocus curves, both photopic and mesopic, did not differ between groups (photopic, P=0.596 and mesopic, P=0.548). All patients reported being either very or quite satisfied with their vision.ConclusionDifferent degrees of corneal SA measured in our study do not affect the visual performance of eyes implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL through the visual metrics assessed. This IOL may be used clinically to restore visual acuity in eyes with different corneal SA after undergoing cataract surgery.

Introduction

The introduction of aspheric intraocular lenses (IOLs) represents a major advancement in cataract and refractive surgery. Now widely used by cataract surgeons, these lenses are designed to correct the positive corneal spherical aberration (SA). The correction of higher-order aberrations (HOAs), particularly SA, by commercially available aspheric IOLs provides optimized retinal image quality1,2 and improved vision after implantation.3,4 A systematic review with meta-analysis studying the impact on vision of aspheric IOLs versus spherical IOLs in cataract surgery reported that a patient may achieve better contrast sensitivity (CS) with an aspheric IOL than with a spherical IOL, especially under low lighting conditions.5 This study analyzed data pertaining to eyes (n=2076) implanted with aspheric IOLs and eyes implanted with spherical IOLs (n=2034). The same authors concluded in another study that aspheric IOL implantation resulted in less ocular SA and fewer ocular HOAs than spherical IOLs, which may explain the better CS observed in patients implanted with aspheric IOLs.6

An aspheric IOL with a non-constant aberration design is commercially available: the CT LUCIA 621P IOL from Carl Zeiss Meditec AG (Jena, Germany). This lens was designed with an optimized Zeiss Optic aspheric profile, featuring a central zone with negative SA to balance the positive SA of the cornea, with a gradual transition to positive SA in the peripheral zone. The aspheric profile varies with dioptric power, correcting the positive corneal SA, and increasing tolerance to lens misalignment following implantation. Several clinical studies have reported the clinical outcomes of cataract patients implanted with this lens.7–13 Both retrospective and prospective studies with different samples and follow-ups (from 1 to 12 months) have demonstrated high refractive accuracy and excellent visual performance, in addition to high patient satisfaction and good CS under different lighting conditions, following implantation of this lens in cataract patients. It is well known that SA is related to age and ethnicity.14–20 However, no previous studies have reported how different degrees of corneal SA may affect the visual performance of eyes implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL. Therefore, the main objective of the current study was to assess the subjective refractive error, uncorrected and corrected distance visual acuities (UCVA and CDVA), CS (photopic and mesopic), photopic and mesopic defocus curve, and patient-reported outcome measures using the Catquest-9SF questionnaire, in a cohort of eyes with different degrees of corneal SA at 3 months post-cataract surgery.

Methods

Study Population and Criteria

This prospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Hospital Clínico San Carlos in Madrid (Spain), C.I. 23/567-O_P) and registered at the DRKS German Clinical Trials Register (clinical registration number: DRKS00032562). This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. The inclusion criteria considered patients providing informed consent, age-related cataracts, an age of ≥50 years, corneal astigmatism of up to 1.00D, and a calculated IOL power ranging from 0 to 34.0D. The exclusion criteria were: patients not able to/willing to give informed consent, irregular cornea, previous corneal refractive surgery, ocular anomalies, or pathologies that may reduce postoperative IOL stability or visual function, and patients participating in another clinical study.

Intraocular Lens and Surgical Procedure

The same lens, the monofocal optimized aspheric CT LUCIA 621P IOL, was implanted in all eyes. This lens was supplied fully preloaded in a BLUESERT injector. The IOL is a heparin-coated, hydrophobic acrylic lens with a benzotriazole UV-absorber. It has a refractive index of 1.49 with an Abbe number of 51, and is available in powers from 0 to 34D in 0.5D increments. It shows an optical diameter of 6 mm, and an overall diameter of 13 mm. It has a modified C-loop with a step-vaulted haptic angulation (0 degrees). Standard cataract surgery with phacoemulsification was performed by an experienced surgeon (FPP) using the Centurion device from Alcon Labs (Fort Worth, TX, USA) via a temporal-corneal 2.2-mm incision and targeting a 5 mm-diameter capsulorhexis. The ophthalmic viscosurgical devices Z-Hyalin and Z-Hyalin Plus (viscous-cohesive) from Carl Zeiss Meditec AG (Jena, Germany) were used.

Data Collection and Parameters

Patients recruited in this study were assessed preoperatively by slit-lamp and dilated fundoscopic examination. The following measurements were recorded: monocular photopic logMAR CDVA (Snellen test at 4 m), contrast sensitivity (85 and 3 cd/m2) using the CC-100 system from Topcon (Tokyo, Japan), subjective refraction, intraocular pressure (IOP), and biometry using the IOLMaster 700 device from Carl Zeiss Meditec AG (Jena, Germany). Specifically, data on anterior corneal SA were obtained using the Pentacam device from Oculus (Wetzlar, Germany) by a single expert, who performed all examinations in the same room, and under the same conditions. Eyes were then categorized into 3 groups based on their corneal SA (for a 6-mm pupil): group A, with a value ranging from 0 to 0.2 μm, group B, with a value ranging from >0.2 to 0.4 μm, and group C, with a value ranging from >0.4 to 0.6 μm.

The Barrett Universal II formula was used to for IOL power calculation (emmetropia being the target refraction in all eyes). Postoperatively (at 1- and 3-months), the following parameters were recorded: monocular logMAR UDVA and CDVA, subjective refraction, IOP, ocular biometry, monocular CS (photopic and mesopic), monocular defocus curve (photopic and mesopic, ranging from +1.50D to –1.50D in 0.50D increments). For the defocus curve, the area under-the-curve (AUC) was calculated using the trapezoidal rule, a standard numerical integration method for discretely sampled data. Since defocus was tested in uniform 0.50D increments (from +1.50D to –1.50D), the AUC could be derived by summing the areas of consecutive trapezoids formed between adjacent defocus levels. Sample Size and Statistical Analysis

The measurements obtained for the different parameters were evaluated using Excel (2019, v. 16.43 from Microsoft Corporation [Redmond, WA, USA]) using means, standard deviations (SD), and ranges for quantitative variables. Normality was checked by means of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and Shapiro–Wilk tests for each variable within each group. Normally distributed variables were analyzed using the one-way ANOVA test, and non-normally distributed variables using the Kruskal–Wallis test, to evaluate possible differences among the groups. A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. For CS, because multiple frequencies were analysed, a Bonferroni adjustment was applied to control the familywise error rate, resulting in an adjusted significance threshold of 0.01. Sample size was calculated considering a 95% confidence interval (CI), 80% power, with a SD of 0.06. 16 eyes per group were required to detect a 1-line difference (0.1 logMAR23). Thus, a total of 48 eyes were needed and, accounting for a 10% attrition rate, a total of 54 eyes were recruited.

Results

Our trial analyzed the outcomes of 54 eyes implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL. Table 1 shows the preoperative characteristics of the eyes. There were no statistically significant differences between the different groups regarding age (P=0.128), spherical equivalent (SE, P=0.837), UDVA (P=0.054), CDVA (P=0.457) and IOL power (P=0.292). Standard cataract surgery was performed in all cases, and no intra- or post-operative complications or IOL-related adverse events were reported either during surgery or up to the 3-month follow-up visit. For visual acuity outcomes, the mean monocular logMAR UDVA and CDVA were 0.18±0.31, and –0.02±0.04, for group A, 0.04±0.10 and –0.04±0.05 for group B, and 0.08±0.13 and 0.01±0.11 for group C, respectively. Both UDVA and CDVA were comparable among groups without showing statistically significant differences (P=0.316 and P=0.094, respectively). Regarding refractive accuracy, Figure 1 shows the refractive accuracy for the three groups at the 3-month postoperative visit. Regarding accumulative outcomes, in group A, 88.89% of eyes were within ±1.00D and with a refractive astigmatism of ≤1.00D. In group B, this value changed to 94.4%, and in group C, 88.89% eyes were within ±1.00D of SE and 94.4% of eyes with a refractive astigmatism of ≤1.00D. For group A, the mean values of the SE and refractive astigmatism were −0.56±0.0.60D and −0.49±0.46D, respectively. For group B, the values were −0.32±0.49D and −0.25±0.36D, respectively, and, for group C, they were −0.38±0.0.42D and −0.33±0.49D, respectively. No statistically significant differences were found between the three groups regarding the SE value (P=0.431). Figure 3 shows the log CS functions recorded under photopic (Figure 3A) and mesopic (Figure 3B) lighting conditions for the 3 groups. The results registered under both lighting conditions were similar for most spatial frequencies (see P values in the figure). Overall, CS did not differ significantly between the groups at any of the tested spatial frequencies under either photopic or mesopic lighting conditions. Figure 4 shows the defocus curve (photopic and mesopic) from +1.50D to –1.50D for the 3 groups. Descriptive statistics showed comparable mean photopic AUC values across the 3 study groups. Group A presented a mean of 2.36±0.09, group B a mean of 2.40±0.12, and group C a mean of 2.23±0.41. A similar pattern was observed under mesopic conditions. Group A exhibited a mean mesopic AUC of 2.35±0.08, group B 2.38±0.13, and group C 2.22±0.42. Since the Shapiro–Wilk and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests indicated that several groups violated the assumption of normality, group comparisons were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. No statistically significant differences were found among the 3 groups regarding AUC under either photopic (P=0.596) or mesopic conditions (P=0.548). Discussion

There are previous clinical studies analyzing the clinical outcomes of patients implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL.7–13 Table 2 was created to exhibit the main characteristics of these studies, indicating authors, year of publication, sample, type of surgery, follow-up, age of the patients, IOL power implanted, and the main clinical measurements recorded. A key limitation of these studies is that none specifically measured preoperative corneal SA; therefore, the impact of differing aberration levels on visual performance of eyes implanted with this IOL remains unknown. Consequently, this study was designed to analyze visual outcomes across three patient groups categorized by their level of corneal SA. Visual Acuity

We have obtained good outcomes for distance vision, with mean CDVA values of about 20/20 or better in all 3 groups. No statistically significant differences were obtained among them (P=0.094). With regard to previous studies published with this IOL model, it should be noted that Hernández-Martínez et al7 found slightly worse results with a mean CDVA of 0.89±0.13 (Snellen decimal); however, the values for García-Tomás et al8 were better, with a mean value of 1.13±0.11 (>20/20). Our mean CDVA values (–0.02±0.04, –0.04±0.05 and 0.01±0.11 logMAR for groups A, B and C, respectively), were similar to the 0.00±0.11 logMAR (20/20) found by Schallhorn et al. Han et al10 reported mean values of visual acuity at different distances, namely 0.00±0.03, 0.05±0.08, 0.28±0.09, 0.38±0.08, and 0.58±0.09 logMAR for CDVA, UDVA, UIVA (80 cm), UIVA (60 cm), and UNVA (40 cm), respectively. These authors compared the CT LUCIA 621P lens with the Tecnis Eyhance lens of Johnson & Johnson Vision Care, Inc. (Jacksonville, USA) showing comparable outcomes at these distances (P>0.05). Schallhorn et al11 in another study, found a mean CDVA value of 0.00±0.09 logMAR (20/20), also supporting our results. Eom et al12 also measured visual acuity at different distances reporting mean values for CDVA, UDVA, UIVA (66 cm), UNVA (40 cm) and UNVA (33 cm) of 0.02±0.04, 0.09±0.08, 0.19±0.12, 0.41±0.14, 0.59±0.11 logMAR, respectively. They compared this lens with the Tecnis ZCB00 IOL of Johnson & Johnson Vision Care, Inc. (Jacksonville, USA) showing comparable visual acuities except for UIVA at 66 cm, which was statistically significantly better for the CT LUCIA 621P (0.19 vs. 0.32 logMAR, P<0.001). They indicated that the improved intermediate vision may be attributed to its non-constant aberration aspheric design. And, finally, Casazza et al13 found a mean value CDVA of –0.08±0.07 logMAR and –0.08±0.08 logMAR for the CCS and the FLACS group, respectively (P=0.989), which aligns with the outcomes reported in our cohort. The absence of significant differences (P>0.05) in UDVA and CDVA among the 3 groups indicating that varying degrees of corneal aberration do not affect visual acuity.

Refractive Accuracy

Refractive outcomes in both groups revealed a similar percentage of eyes with a SE within ±1.00D and with refractive astigmatism of ≤1.00D, and mean values between a quarter and a half diopter for both values (see Figure 1A and B). The distribution patterns in the double-angle plots were similar among the 3 groups (see Figure 2A–C). Hernández-Martínez et al7 in a retrospective study of 99 eyes at 1-month follow-up found 90% of eyes within ±1.00D and 73.33% of eyes within ±0.50D, with a mean SE of –0.18±0.55D. They also found that 37.78% and 76.67% of eyes exhibited a refractive cylinder of ≤0.50D and ≤1.00D, respectively. The mean value of refractive cylinder was –0.81±0.50D. García-Tomás et al8 in a prospective study of 55 eyes with a longer follow-up (3 months) found that 92.73% of eyes were within ±0.50D and all eyes within ±1.00D, with a mean SE of –0.03±0.30D, and 80% of eyes with a refractive astigmatism within ≤0.50D and 96.36% ≤1.00D, with a mean value of –0.36±0.34D. Schallhorn et al9 in a large sample of 191 eyes, retrospectively, assessed this cohort, with a mean follow-up of 1.7 months, obtaining 84.8% and 98.4% of eyes within ±0.50D and ±1.00D, respectively (with a mean value of +0.15±0.38D). In a series of 60 eyes at 1 month, Han et al10 reported a mean SE value of –0.29±0.34D. Schallhorn et al11 in the largest sample published to date using this IOL model, retrospectively analyzed 194 eyes with a mean follow-up of 2.7 months, obtaining 82.5% and 97.4% of eyes within ±0.50D and ±1.00D, respectively (mean value: +0.05±0.46D). Eom et al12 in 32 eyes at 3 months, obtained a mean SE of –0.09±0.31D, and, finally, Casazza et al13 with the longest follow-up (12 months) evaluated 73 eyes prospectively in order to assess the possible difference in position and refractive stability of this lens after conventional (CCS) or femtosecond-laser assisted cataract surgery (FLACS). This study revealed a mean SE value of 0.23±0.38D in the CCS and value of 0.21±0.46D in the FLACS group, with no statistically significant difference among groups (P=0.180). Overall, the mean value was 0.24±0.42D, with 79.73% of eyes within ±0.50D and 98.65% within ±1.00D. Our results revealed that there were no statistically significant differences in the SE value across the 3 groups analyzed (P=0.431), showing comparable outcomes in terms of refractive accuracy.

Contrast Sensitivity

Regarding CS, the three groups performed similarly at all spatial frequencies under both lighting conditions (P>0.01 for all spatial frequencies, see Figure 3A and B). These good outcomes correlate with the good visual acuity outcomes. Note that visual acuity is related to high spatial frequencies. This is evidenced by our results since there were no statistically significant differences in UDVA and CDVA among the three groups (P=0.316 and P=0.094, respectively). García-Tomás et al8 who also measured CS under both conditions in their patients showed similar values the different spatial frequencies evaluated (P>0.05). The comparative study of Han et al10 showed that the CT LUCIA 621P lens demonstrated superior CS at 12 and 18 cpd for unilateral eyes (P<0.001 and P=0.009, respectively) and at 18 cpd for bilateral eyes (P=0.044) in relation to the Eyhance lens. These authors suggested that the CT LUCIA 621P IOL could provide better overall CS, which may contribute to enhanced visual performance in low contrast environments. This is in line with our outcomes being comparable to the CS values obtained between the 3 groups at the highest spatial frequencies of 12 and 18 cpd. Casazza et al13 also measured photopic CS but using another test known as the Pelli-Robson test, obtaining comparable mean values of 1.51±0.12 and 1.52±0.13 for the FLACS and CCS groups, respectively (P=0.331).

Defocus Curve

We found a similar trend for the 3 groups with a best visual acuity value at 0D of vergence (distance vision), with a certain degree of depth-of-focus around this value (Figure 4A and B). Overall, both photopic and mesopic AUC were similar across the 3 groups, with no statistically significant-differences (P=0.596 and P=0.548). Note that the visual acuity was ≥20/25 up to –0.75D of vergence and about ≥20/32 up to –1.50D of vergence. Han et al10 measured defocus curves (unilateral and bilateral eyes) under photopic and mesopic lighting conditions from +1.00D to –3.50D reporting that the CT LUCIA 621P and Eyhance lenses showed comparable visual performance across a wide range of defocus values, meaning that both lenses are appropriate for providing consistent good visual acuity at various distances. These authors discussed that the CT LUCIA 621P IOL was designed with an aspheric concept optic that modifies the SA, which is negative in the central optic and gradually changes to a positive value in the peripheral zone.24 This transition extends the depth-of-focus due to the residual SA value from the peripheral aberration-neutral zone of the optic.25 In their study, Eom et al12 reported that the CT LUCIA 621P and ZCB00 IOLs were similar in their unimodal shape; however, the CT LUCIA 621P lens allowed for a wider depth-of-focus range compared to the ZCB00 lens, showing significantly higher mean visual acuity at +0.50D, −0.50D, −1.00D, −1.50D, −2.00D, and −2.50D (P<0.02). The CT LUCIA 621P IOL maintained mean visual acuity of <0.1 logMAR in the range of +0.50D to −0.50D and mean visual acuity of ≤0.24 logMAR in the range of −1.00D to −1.50D. Casazza et al13 measured the monocular defocus curve showing a mean visual acuity of ≤0.20 logMAR (20/32) within a range of +1.00D to –1.00D. Our results are fully consistent with these outcomes (see both graphs in Figure 4A and B).

PROMs

Regarding the Catquest-9SF questionnaire, patient responses were similar across the 3 groups for all questions (see Figure 5). It is noteworthy, for example, that all patients in the 3 groups were “very” or “quite satisfied”, and almost all of them responded with “no, no difficulty” when they were asked to perform visual tasks. Several of the visual tasks included in the Catquest-9SF questionnaire rely on functional intermediate vision, such as for example seeing the prices of goods when shopping, hobbies, handicrafts, and reading text in newspapers. In our study, we found a range of focus from 0D to −1.50D, maintaining a Snellen visual acuity of 20/40 or better, supporting the high rates of satisfaction observed. García-Tomás et al8 used the same test and found that 100% of patients reported being “fairly satisfied” or “very satisfied” with their vision after the surgery, while 54.54% did not report any difficulty in their everyday life. Furthermore, no difficulty was reported at all regarding recognizing faces, in line with our results. Schallhorn et al11 used an ad hoc questionnaire and found that 63.9% of patients reported being “very satisfied” and 30.9% reported being “satisfied” with their vision after the surgery. 93.8% reported that they were “much more satisfied” or “more satisfied” with their vision after the surgery compared to their vision before surgery using their contact lenses or glasses, and 97.9% answered they would have the surgery again and 99.0% would recommend the surgery to their friends and relatives. Most patients reported that they did not experience any postoperative visual phenomena (72.2%, 89.7%, 85.6% and 89.7% for glare, halo, starburst, and ghosting or double vision, respectively). Casazza et al13 also used the same questionnaire with 83.78% of patients reporting no difficulties in their everyday life, and 97.30% reporting that they were “very” or “fairly satisfied” with the outcomes after the surgery. Considering our results and those previously reported by other authors in the peer-reviewed literature, we believe that the CT LUCIA 621P IOL demonstrates favorable patient-reported outcomes, as measured by standardized PROMs.

We must acknowledge that our study has certain limitations, such as the relatively short follow-up (3 months) or the lack of a control group with other monofocal IOLs for comparison purposes. Thus, longer follow-ups and the benefits of using this IOL in relation to other competitors in the market requires further analysis. Future studies should lengthen the postoperative period for the examinations, include other monofocal lenses available in the market and larger samples of eyes in each of the different groups analyzed.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that different degrees of corneal SA measured in our study do not affect the visual performance of eyes implanted with the CT LUCIA 621P IOL after cataract surgery through the visual metrics assessed. Therefore, the investigated IOL may be clinically used to restore visual acuity in eyes with different corneal SA after undergoing cataract surgery.
